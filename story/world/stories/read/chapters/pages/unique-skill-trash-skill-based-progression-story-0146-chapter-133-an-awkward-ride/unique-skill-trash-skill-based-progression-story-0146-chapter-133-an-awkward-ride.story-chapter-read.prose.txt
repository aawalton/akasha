Chapter 133

An Awkward Ride

I never thought I would be one to almost prefer the anonymity that the veil provided as part of my healer’s garb offers me. That is the thought I had while wearing my extra layer of clothing. Clothing that is light, breathable, and provides just enough protection to make it seemingly worth the hassle.

I’ve also found that there is a second bonus to this uniform, in that it helps to hide some of my facial features and reactions. Particularly when listening to people who ask seemingly stupid questions. As was happening now.

While I don’t know why Matriarch Florence Livanathan and her lackies chose to ride to the Ancestral Cathedral with Madame Bloom and I, I was thankful that we had to leave already dressed in our ceremonial healer’s garb. At first, I thought our riding together was either an economy thing, as there might not have been enough vehicles from Unity University’s travel delegation to move everyone properly. However, this was quickly disproven as Matriarch Livanathan purposefully abandoned her vehicle at the Bloom estate to ride in our carriage.

“I see you still favor the old methods of transportation,” Matriarch Livanathan stated, her words dismissive, despite her imposing on Madame Bloom’s hospitality to travel. As the Matriarch, and undisputed highest ranked member of the delegation, it fell upon Madame Bloom to be here and capitulate to the demands of the Matriarch and her entourage.

“I believe this form of travel has a certain aesthetic appeal that is hard to match with modern conveniences,” Madame Bloom replied, her words equally dismissive of the obvious provocation Matriarch Livanathan was trying to establish. Then again, I thought I might be biased. At least, at first I thought I could be biased and assume that there was some deep seeded animosity between the two. But after seeing their interactions over the past day and this morning, it was crystal clear to anyone watching that these two despised each other.

“Well, let’s hope we can get more progressive thoughts out of your apprentice,” the Matriarch continued, and then before anyone could protest otherwise, she turned to me and continued with her belligerence, almost expecting me to be some untutored backwoods healer. “Is this your first time wearing the healer’s uniform?”

I shake my head, keeping the presence of a silent healer while wearing the healer’s uniform. A stand that was taught to me on the first night of wearing this uniform, and one that my mind instantly reverted to. Once the hood is up, I am quiet as death.

The Matriarch looked confused for a moment, but then paused as she turned from me to Madame Bloom, who still wore her own hood up, yet had no issue with talking through the hood.

“She has?” Matriarch Livanathan asked, her voice denoting that she needed to reevaluate how she thought of me.

“She has; she is the healer that was on duty at the Arean last week. The one who slew a Death Eater in front of everyone,” Madame Bloom commented, her words seeming to beam with pride. I could tell Madame Bloom was especially pleased with the way that Matriarch Livanathan seemed to deflate at her revelation. Until finally the Matriarch sat up and seemed to conclude on her own.

“Regardless, it is my job to make sure the wisdom of ages is past to the next generation of Healers,” Matriarch Livanathan stated gesturing to the healer who sat next to me. The one who seemed to think she would die if her spine relaxed for even a second. “Tell me child, do you know why we wear black?”

It was something I had wondered at first, but it wasn’t until fighting a Death Eater that I realized exactly why. Still, this was likely as good of a chance to test out my theory as any, so despite my almost vow of silence, I decided to answer.

“We dress as Death Eaters, knowing they are drawn to our healing arts especially. By hiding as them, the hope is that we can both hide from them in plain sight, while also allowing us a chance to leave. Given our shapes and the fact, we can’t sink into shadows, a living person used to our appearance will be able to differentiate the two of us easily. I also assume, it has a psychological factor to it as well. One where fighters see us enough times and are no longer frightened by the idea of fighting a Death Eater.” I reply, pausing as I see the look of shock on Matriarch Livanathan’s face even through her veil.  Seeing that there is a pause, I also choose that moment to fill in the gap with something else that I have been contemplating.

“It likely also acts as a reminder to everyone, that once you remove these outer layers we are all vulnerable. Remove the dark cloak of a Death Eater and they are nothing but brittle bones. Likewise removing our hoods and cloaks will show us as mere mortals, trying to find a place in this world.”

There is a deafening silence that fills the cabin, as we continue to roll our way down the cobblestone streets of the city. Off in the distance, I can already see the towering spire of the Ancestral Cathedral reaching like a giant finger of resistance to the heavens.

My gaze is so captivated by the sight of the Ancestral Cathedral, and the seeming motes of energy that are radiating off of it, that for a moment I forget that we have guests with us. Unfortunately, those very same guests don’t forget that they are here, or the fact that they are apparently supposed to establish authority through knowledge.

“Yes, it seems that you have both fought with Death Eaters and have even come up with your own theories about them. This is good, and this is something that we will cover more at Unity University,” Matriarch Livanathan spoke. Hearing her speak, I almost wondered if she thought I was going to Unity. If I was, this was likely something that Madame Bloom was brokering on the back end. From what I understood before, my acceptance was no sure thing. But maybe that had all changed.

“Her admission is not confirmed, at least not yet,” Madame Bloom spoke up, her words seemed to deflate the old Matriarch.

“You know you have already signed, meaning that you could force her fulfill your contract for you as her master,” Matriarch Livanathan commented.

“I could, if I had clout in our contract. However, I still owe her one Skill, and her obligations to me have already been fulfilled. Meaning, she is the one with clout in our relationship,” Madame Bloom replied. Her words seeming to have an immediate reaction on Matriarch Livanathan. While I wasn’t clear exactly what, nor why Matriarch Livanathan was suddenly interested in me, I felt it had to do with my confirmed kill of a Death Eater. That and the fact that Madame Bloom seemed to know exactly what buttons to push to get the most emphatic response from the Matriarch.

“You have already signed though, and you know at your age that such an obligation would be a death sentence,” Matriarch Livanathan replied back. Hearing that, I leaned forward, suddenly wondering just how close to death Madame Bloom was. Seeing my reaction, Madame Bloom held up two fingers towards me, a sign for me to sit back and relax, while she began replying almost immediately.

“I do, and I also know that nothing more will fill my final days with joy than knowing that the princesses Lucia and Luna are taken care of, while you realize the prize you had before you all along.” Madame Boom stated, gesturing to me with the same hand that she used to warn me a second ago to remain quiet with. Had one not known, it might have looked like it was part of her normal mannerisms, but I knew differently. I knew what she had meant by the gesture form the start. This was no longer a debate, but rather a negotiations being played out before me. A negotiation, where I was apparently the prize being bartered for.

“Prize? I see that you, as usual have too much respect for your apprentices,” Matriarch Livanathan replied.

“Yes, though to my defense, my last apprentice might tarnish my legacy for how most would view the competency of my past choices,” Madame Bloom responded. That’s when I saw it, the slight clenching of gloved fists, the minor trembling caused by angrily restraining oneself that I realized Matriarch Livanathan was offended by that last comment. Which made me wonder if she had in fact been a former apprentice of Madame Bloom. Their apprenticeship would have had to have been a while ago, but if this was true, then a lot of their personal animosities would make more sense. The level of betrayal that Madame Bloom seemed to have radiating from her when merely being around Matriarch Livanathan all made more sense if there was a deep connection at first, and then a fall out.

“Well, no doubt she, like your past Apprentices will go on to learn from their Master’s mistakes and finally make something worthwhile of themselves and their gifts they have been given,” Matriarch Livanathan shot back. If I had any questions before about their past relationship, the clear provocation being shot forward now was all I would need to remove any lingering traces of doubt. Hearing the words I again shot forward, but I was again waved off before I could interject myself into this conversation on behalf of Madame Bloom. While I had not had many highlights to this new life, I will say that Madame Bloom is genuinely one of the good people of this world, and one of the people that I would gladly risk my life for.

Only now, once I am at the edge of my seat, ready to fight on Madame Bloom’s behalf, do I realize why so many others all come when she calls. If she treated them half as well as she treated me, then she deserves their respect. Leaning forward, I see the looks of Markan, Pierre, Mr. Stevens, Mr. Yi, Madame Voyou, Mr. Karl, and more all rising to my mind. Each one powerful in their own right, but much stronger by the fact that they will all come together and rally behind Madame Bloom. Only now do I realize that she has pulled me into her orbit as well, making it so I would gladly join in any fight to defend Madame Bloom, even if it meant ruining my chance at entering the most prestigious university in the world. It is that last thought, that last realization that if I said or did anything now, I could ruin my chance at entering Unity University with Luna and Lucia I pause. I pause long enough for Madame Bloom to continue her verbal assault on Matriarch Livanathan.

Sadly, it is only now that I realize why Madame Bloom reached the rank of General while in the military, for it is only now that I realize how she is a tactical genius. Using the heightened emotion and perceived time crunch left in our journey. A journey that was almost certainly coming to a close given how close the Ancestral Cathedral is, that would all but end any chances at negotiations. This moment, this time, this exact place, this is where Madame Boom makes her declaration.

“I’ll admit, right now you have me. But if you want my Apprentice to go in my stead. A proven killer of Death Eaters, a finder and destroyer of shadow stones, someone capable of imbuing and activating sacred sigils, then you will need to offer much more compensations than you have offered to me. An old washed-up healer, whose days are past their best and has already shown signs of regression. So much so that her old Apprentice questioned her competency and had her evicted from her previous place of leadership,” Madame Bloom commented.

At that declaration, the rolling of the wheels slowly died down, until the carriage came to a complete stop. Only once the stop was completed did Matriarch Livanathan looked shocked for a moment. Turning from Madame Bloom and then looking at me, then back to Madame Bloom.

“She can do Sigils?” Matriarch Livanathan asked.

“What do you think her unlocked seals are for? Taking up a placement on her?” Madame Bloom asked.

At that, I felt an intense sensation of someone gazing deeply into my very soul. I also saw whisps of unmistakable blue light pouring from under Matriarch Livanathan’s veil.

“What would you want to take the place of your Master, and attend Unity University in her stead?” Matriarch Livanathan finally asked. Her body moving was the only movement as our entire carriage had come to a complete stop and it was clear we were all queuing up to be let out as one group of healers that would all enter the Ancestral Cathedral in unison.

“Tsk-tsk-tsk,” Madame Bloom clicked her tongue for a moment, before speaking. “I want you to take whatever the most amount of compensations you can possibly convince about giving up for a promising student. Then double it,” Madame Bloom asked.

“Double it?” Matriarch Livanathan asked, a note of annoyance in her tone as she seemed to say that now it had become ridiculous.

“Yes, double it, and realize that by the end of today, it will be a steal. Particularly after what you and everyone present will see my Apprentice truly be able to do,” Madame Bloom stated, a note of pride in her voice as I could hear her almost beaming about me. Which filled me both with hope, and with a sense of trepidation as there was no way I could do as much as she was now referring to.

“You speak as if—” Matriarch Livanathan began but was cut off by a quick raising of a gloved finger by Madame Bloom.

At that, there were two crisp knocks that came from the door, before Mr. Stevens opened revealing lines of people that were all assembled to watch us depart from our carriage and enter the Ancestral Cathedral.

“We have arrived—” was all Mr. Stevens said, to let us all know that any conversations were over, and we were now onto the pageantry portion of our main event.
