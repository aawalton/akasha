Alric took another sip of his beer and frowned at it, as if the beer was to blame. It was still good, but somewhere between Stromni’s campaign to keep score in the war against sobriety, Alric had forgotten he was hungry.

“I was getting food.” Alric said, pointing at Stromni, “How am I getting mixed up in your schemes?”

Stromni had just taken a sip of his beer when his cheeks puffed out. He managed a decent glare at Alric, though the effect was ruined by puffy cheeks and the beer running through his beard. "Lad, ya best be joking. My whole life is just getting mixed up in your schemes."

“This whole thing was your idea at first. I’m going to get food before you get Vale to figure out how to do drinking accounting,” Alric said, laughing as he set off.

“Aye, I ain't fighting this dragon with en empty stomach,” Stromni said, lengthening his stride to catch up. Behind them, new arguments flared about marks on tankards.

They headed toward the row of innkeepers; the field was filling up since they arrived, and the approach had a steady stream of people coming from the city. Berrin spotted Alric and waved him over with a big smile. Alric grinned and headed for his area; Stromni peeled off toward one of the other wagons.

“Are you eating now, Mister Alric?” Berrin asked with a wide grin. Behind him, Berrin had found assistants, but Alric didn’t recognize anyone else.

“Yes, porridge for me, please. Anyone else from the inn coming later?” Alric asked, glancing behind again.

Berrin shook his head. “Couldn’t miss this, but we have other guests,” Berrin explained. He passed Alric a board with a generous bowl of porridge, a large lump of butter melting in the middle. Honey had been scattered over it as well as nuts and chopped dried fruit.

“Thanks, Berrin. I’ll pop around as we go.” Alric said, taking the bowl. He couldn’t help but notice how the porridge had come leaps and bounds from the time he had stayed there. He added two sausages and some dried fruit.

Stromni approached with his own board; he had picked a different selection. Sausages, several meat skewers, a slab of bacon, and something dark that Alric couldn’t identify.

“Balanced breakfast you got there,” Alric said, using his board to gesture to Stromni’s. He was getting heartburn just looking at it.

“Aye, ain’t eating fruit if there is this much meat,” Stromni said with a grin. He gestured with his head, and the pair began walking.

They found space at one of the long tables with the innkeepers behind them and the muster yard in front. Alric settled as his guards rotated to get food. Alric put his beer off the board and away from his elbow; he took a spoonful of the porridge to discover Berrin had done well.

The porridge was far thicker than before, the nuts adding just enough crunch to stop it feeling pasty. The fruit also added a satisfying flavor with the honey.

“This is good,” Alric said, tapping the porridge bowl and nodding toward the field.

“Aye,” Stromni said, holding up a sausage.

For a while, no one spoke; they just ate. People-watching became the entertainment; the beer wagon was a constant source of argument, and lines were beginning to form around it. No one had to wait long, but most had a small debate over the lines.

One man walked away holding two tankards.

“Cheat!” Someone nearby shouted at him.

“Ain’t both mine!” The man shouted back.

Three more people called him a cheat. The man put a beer to each ear and walked away. Stromni chuckled, while Alric laughed. By the time Alric had finished his porridge, the sun had climbed high enough that the mist was starting to burn off. The air was cold, but the sun felt good. Alric took off his cloak, putting it into his item box while he noticed others removing theirs.

A strange ripping sound caught Alric’s attention; he glanced off to the side. Four people were stretched out onto the grass in the sun. One had bundled his cloak under his head; another was lying on his cloak. The largest lay on his back, both hands on his stomach, and repeated the ripping sound in the form of a snore.

Alric’s eyes widened as he glanced closer; none of them had tankards. Stacks of empty bowls sat there, each with a board nearby.

“It’s not even midday?” Alric said, pointing to it.

“Ate too much, tempted ta join em.” Stromni nodded; he pointed to two other groups doing the same. Alric looked where indicated; one group of adventurers was passed out over a table, and another was soldiers, each resting on their shields.

“A small nap in the sun doesn’t sound terrible,” Alric said, taking a sip of beer. His guards nodded.

“Might do one after lunch, I reckon,” Stromni said, adjusting his belt and pushing his board away.

One of the sleeping adventurers sat up when a cheer erupted near the beer wagon. He looked around briefly before lowering himself again. Alric noticed he was smiling.

The smell was changing over the field as the inns prepared for lunch and eventual dinner. Several of the inns were now turning whole pigs over in the spits; one had a side of beef roasting, another had mutton arranged over many fires. Alric turned back to glance. Large boards were now being put out as a help-yourself spread; some had cloth over them. Alric spotted piles of pies, cheeses, something that could have been mustard that would warrant careful investigation later, several types of breads, and one common theme that drew Alric’s attention most: Sausages. Every inn seemed to be serving different kinds of sausages.

“Who do you think makes the best sausage?” Alric said, glancing at Stromni and his guards.

“Easy, three barrels does it best.” Came from the guard Simon.

“Nuh uh, Dancing Gale.” Came from Stromni. The rest of the guards were pointing in different directions.

Alric grinned. “Seems we have a problem. Every inn has sausages, and we don’t know whose is best,” Alric said. He turned himself around, putting his back to the muster yard, facing the inns.

“Aye, we need ta figure this out,” Stromni said with a nod.

Simon only had to nod at the guards before four of them scattered in different directions. It didn’t take long for each to return with a board from two inns. The judging began.

Denner's Three Barrels sausage had more pepper; Alric clocked that immediately and grabbed two. Berrin’s was fattier, which one considered a positive and another immediately challenged. One was packed too tightly and slightly dry, which Stromni preferred. They discussed the merits of each sausage at length.

“Didn’t know you’d care this much,” Alric said, relishing the over-peppered sausage. The guards just shrugged.

By the end, Denner was narrowly ahead, pepper and some slight herbs winning the day; that was when the brewery carters arrived.

“Borsen! Glad you made it.” Alric said, waving them over. They all walked over, glancing at the boards of sausages and guards still pointing.

“Wouldn’t miss it, Mister Alric. What’s all this?” Borsen said, pointing to the boards.

Alric gestured to the innkeeper wagons, “Important work. Every innkeeper has a sausage. We need to know whose is best. Denner so far.”

“Denner? Naw, must be the stag.” Came from one of the carters.

“As ta be the gale?” Came from Borsen. Alric gestured to the boards, and the sausage judging got that much more serious.

The carters argued with the guards, pointing to different fires. Each tried to outdo the other; Alric was sure there were theological debates that were less passionate than this. Borsen held the overly packed, slightly dry sausage from the Gale and one of Denner’s. Taking a bite of each, he glared at Denner’s as though it deliberately complicated his morning.

The argument moved on to who had the best casings, which meant another sample. Stromni seemed passionate about that, too. Alric took a sip of his beer when he noticed the bundles at the carter's feet.

“You brought bedrolls?” Alric asked, interrupting and pointing.

Stromni and the guards noticed, and an uproar of laughter broke out.

“That we did, Guv. I mean Mister Alric.” Came from one of the other carters. Alric waved it off.

“Makes sense, it does, gonna have a huge meal, small kip on the grass. Later tonight we aren’t stumbling home.” Borsen said with a wide grin.

Stromni scratched at his beard, “Ya know, Borsen, can't fault that. But we always gonna give ya lip ‘bout bringing a bedroll to a party.” Stromni said and got another laugh; the carters all smirked.

“Reckon thems gonna ave the whole day ta stretch their backs?” Borsen said, still smiling and using his chin to gesture to the other sleepers.

Alric glanced over and gave a shrug.

“We gonna go stuff our faces, Mister Alric, be seein’ ya.” Borsen said after some small talk, the carters eagerly heading off to the wagons.

A loud shout came from the muster yard; they all turned around to see it. Three men were walking toward the yard carrying what looked like a tree.

“Ahh, caber tossin',” Stromni said, leaning forward.

Alric grabbed a sausage and settled in to watch. “Had something similar,” Alric said to Stromni, who got the hint that he couldn’t talk about it but was familiar.

Alric had heard of this before, mostly clips and pictures, but the basic idea carried. Huge man picked up huge log, huge man threw it. Like shotput but with extra splinters.

People began gathering and watching; one large adventurer stepped up, removing his cloak and shirt. Alric wasn’t sure if being bare-chested was part of it, but who knew who was watching. With help, the man got a grip on the bottom of the caber.

Once carrying it, he awkwardly jogged up to a line someone had drawn in the mud before yeeting it with all he was worth.

The log tumbled end over end before landing with a hollow thump; a cheer rang out, followed by a groan as the log fell sideways.

The crowd quieted; people patted the adventurer on the back as he slumped forward, and many started shouting advice. Alric couldn’t help but notice many of the advisors were thinner than he was.

After recovering the caber, a second adventurer was prepared, also bare-chested, Alric noted; he briefly wondered if you got extra points for having a lot of chest hair or impressive nipples.

The adventurer took his run-up and heaved. The trunk went up, turned once in the air, and landed with a dull thump. The crowd cheered as it hung in the air, but they collectively sucked in a breath as it landed; slowly, it fell forward to a massive cheer, and the adventurer raised both arms.

Alric pulled his lips to the side. “That first guy threw it further?” Alric said, glancing at Stromni.

“Nah, lad, ya don’t want far.” Stromni said, pointing to it, “See it ‘as a thick end? Ya want it ta tumble once, land on that end. Then how it falls is what matters. Ya want it ta fall straight over.” Stromni explained with his hands.

So, you want the thick end down, then it must fall to twelve o’clock? Well, that makes more sense.

Alric briefly considered criticizing the game but then remembered golf from Earth and decided this made more sense.

The third contestant was a soldier; his throw was very straight, but the log fell backward. The fourth had the caber spin many times in the air and land on its side. Each got a cheer from the crowd. The Pavise stepped up.

“Well now?” Stromni said, watching him approach.

Alric couldn’t help but notice how well-toned Pavise was, and briefly wondered if there was a secret protein source only he knew about. Pavise took his time, hefting the caber and shifting it around while the crowd fell silent. Then he ran.

Just as he reached the line, Alric felt something like a flash rather than seeing it. There was a collective shout rather than a cheer from the crowd; the caber, however, had damn near been launched. It tumbled end over end; it could have cleared the walls, Alric suspected.

Stromni and the guards burst out laughing as the log landed with a hollow thump, roughly five times farther than anyone else had managed. It landed on its side, which apparently was not the goal, but at least it was memorable. The crowd shouted and pointed, but mostly they just laughed.

“Bloody cheat, using mana,” Stromni said, still chuckling, looking at where the log had landed and laughing again.

Alric looked back from the log to the many people pointing and mock glaring at Pavise, who was putting his shirt on again and shrugging, “You can use mana like that?” Alric asked.

“Aye, some of ya guards can do it. It makes ya tired quick. Pavise is built a bit different. Most mana fighters don’t ‘ave the stamina, but that one ‘as both.” Stromni said, pointing to Pavise.

“It’s easier if you don’t have too much mana, Mister Alric.” Simon pointed out.

Alric nodded. That door was shut forever then.

A group had recovered the caber and were bringing it back. Another soldier was taking off his shirt; Alric, Stromni, and his guards leaned forward to watch.

Another soldier started stripping down for the caber pole, giving the crowd the distraction it wanted, though people still called Pavise a cheat as he put his tunic back on. He just grinned.

The soldiers' throw seemed less impressive to Alric, but it was better by the rules. The caber barely turned through the air, landed on the thick end, and fell correctly. People cheered; even Stromni did.

“That’s how ya do it!” Stromni shouted in approval.

Alric just nodded, not knowing enough to argue, took another bite of sausage, and settled back.

The next hour passed quickly; Stromni took a turn with the caber. He managed to turn it over in the air, but it landed badly and fell sideways. A long dispute followed about the rules, and how they were prejudiced against short people who had managed to throw a tree over his head.

Other games materialized around the muster yard with all the planning of a stampede. Archery sprouted near the river, while the ancient sport of 'who can throw a rock the furthest' briefly captured hearts before being unceremoniously dethroned by tug of war. Adventurers challenged each other, soldiers challenged anyone who looked available, and everyone else watched, because nothing says entertainment like people voluntarily falling into mud.

Over time, the games grew more organized; wooden boards appeared, and tournament brackets formed out of nowhere. The fastest-concluded tournament was an arm-wrestling tournament; it drew a large crowd and betting. One contestant got a bye, and several people demanded to know who his relatives were.

That’s when Pavise got involved. He had initially refused, until someone accused him of cowardice and a rule dispute was brought up. Eventually Pavise sat down, and the tournament got worth watching. Alric and his group got up to watch it.

Pavise eventually won, which didn’t seem to surprise anyone, although some gave him a strong run for his money. One large adventurer had pushed Pavise halfway before the guild master forced him back. What Alric enjoyed most, however, was his prize.

A wooden spoon and a wreath, both clearly intended for a roast pig, but now repurposed for athletic glory.

Pavise accepted both with the gravity of a man receiving a knighthood. Alric joined the applause as the wreath was placed on Pavise’s head and he brandished the wooden spoon like a scepter.

They turned back for their table when Alric noticed something interesting: no one had taken the table.

A steady trickle of people was coming in now; the approach even had guards. The muster yard was filling up; table space was premium real estate, and no one had taken the table they were using.

Alric shrugged, lifted his stein, and discovered it empty.

“Need another, Mister Alric?” Simon asked, holding his hand out.

“I can get it, thanks, Simon,” Alric said, pointing to the wagon.

“We don’t doubt that,” Simon said with a smile and still holding his hand out.

Alric stared at him for a moment before shrugging and handing it over. “Fine, thanks, Simon.”

Simon headed for the Beer Dragon; Alric remained standing by the table and watched. The wagon had become busier with more beer pourers now, more lines, and more cheering. Simon approached, and people began moving aside. One noticed, pointed, and people shifted sideways. Simon marched right up to the front, handed the stein over, got the mark, and headed back. No one commented or called him a cheat as had happened before.

“Thank you,” Alric said, taking the stein from Simon. He took a sip and nodded.

“Course, Mister Alric.”

Alric frowned at the beer wagon for a moment.

Another group of four guards approached from the city side; they waved at Alric’s guards and approached. Brannick had mentioned they would be rotating the men, though Alric had forgotten; Brannick was always on top of things. The guards exchanged a few words, nodded, and the relieved group grinned widely.

“Go enjoy yourselves,” Alric told them, lifting his stein to them.

That got several nods, the group quickly heading for the beer dragon.

Alric decided it was time to wander around a bit, both to take a look and have the new guards see the whole thing. He wandered down to the riverside; an archery contest was in full swing. Alric watched as an adventurer put three arrows into a target from a distance Alric thought was rude. Someone shouted the third shot was wide; the archer replied with a gesture that had also somehow crossed worlds.

Alric laughed and moved on. Borsen and the other carters had executed their plan to perfection, sprawled on bedrolls in a display of professional relaxation. One managed a lazy wave; the rest remained dedicated to the art of napping. Alric waved back, not wishing to disturb.

A brewery worker called his name from one of the tables, lifting his tankard. Alric lifted his stein in response, and many tankards came up in reply.

“Sixteen!” Came a shout to Alric’s left.

He lifted his own stein with a pout, showing the two lines, “Two, you can't be on sixteen!” Alric replied.

There was a chorus of good-natured boos, followed by shouts of three and four. Alric lifted his stein to them too.

Alric approached the center and just stopped to admire how well the entire affair had worked out.

People stood around games or sat by fires, eating from boards nearby. Tankards went up frequently; cheers were even more frequent. People shouted between groups and laughter rang out. The cooking fires were filling the field with delicious smells, and one of the inns started carving one of the pigs, and a crowd had formed around it.

Some small groups sat further from the games, drinking and talking quietly. One told a story; the group raised their tankards without cheering, drank, and sat down again. When Alric noticed a tankard without an owner, he remembered what Pavise had told him: some would spend the day trying to forget, and others would try to remember.

A wave near one of the fires caught his attention. Seren stood close to one of the fires with Brannick; she held a board and picked up a cup as Alric headed over. Even on the walk, groups raised tankards to him; he returned the gesture.

“You two looking comfy here?” Alric asked as he approached. The fire felt too hot, so he moved away from it.

Seren grinned, “We are managing, Mister Alric.” She had a pie on her board and a cup of what looked like tea.

Brannick had a mound of sausages and a tankard. “Good day so far.”

“Yes, I’m kind of surprised. Have you seen Pavise?” Alric asked with a grin.

Both started chuckling. “The wreath,” Brannick said. He pointed with a sausage; Pavise was talking to someone thirty paces away. To Alric, he could have looked something like a Roman god of his old world, but the wreath was clearly meant for a roast pig.

They stood together for a while, discussing the games and how Stromni’s chalk system had sparked serious debate. Three people had approached Seren to ask whether the marks had to be on the same tankard.

“Of course they do,” Alric said. He turned his tankard, showing his two lines. Brannick had one line on his; they both grinned.

Seren raised her eyebrows. “You’re making rules now?”

“No, Stromni started this. I’m just an innocent bystander,” Alric said, sipping his stein and displaying the mark. “But obviously, it has to be the same tankard.” Brannick nodded, wielding his sausage in agreement.

Seren just shook her head with a smirk at the pair.

“Need anything while I’m here, Mister Alric?” Brannick asked, eyeing out the guards behind him.

“No, and you're meant just to be enjoying yourself. You aren’t working today, remember?” Alric said with a frown. Brannick just shrugged. “There are hundreds of armed people here, Brannick. If something gets through all of them, not sure anything could stop it.”

Brannick nodded, “Fine.” He said, taking a bite of his sausage. Brannick pointed to something.

Alric turned; several men were carrying a long, stripped pole into one of the open spaces in front of the innkeepers. They set it into a hole with a brazier over it; one man followed with a stool, another with an iron pot.

“And now?” Alric asked, frowning.

“That’s King’s Perch,” Brannick responded with a nod.

The top of the pole had a crossbar with small pouches that swayed like grapes on strings. The man with the pot began splashing what looked like oil onto the pole. People were gathering quickly.

The innkeeper responsible stepped out. “Coins! Food! Sausages! Meal Tokens and even a small Silver!” He shouted, and the crowd cheered loudly.

“So, they climb up and take one?” Alric asked, to which Brannick nodded.

The man with the iron pot dipped a cloth into the oil and began applying it more liberally. By the time he finished, the whole pole shone.

The first contestant was a large adventurer. He handed his tankard to a friend, slipped off his shirt, wrapped both arms around the pole, and jumped.

He slid down immediately before the crowd exploded; he glared at everyone, then tried again. He got maybe halfway up before he started to slide again. He attempted to overcome this by doing a worm motion as fast as he could while sliding down slowly. The crowd enjoyed this even more; by the time his boots hit the ground, he was laughing too.

The second man did better; he jumped higher, wrapping his legs, and managed a few feet before stopping. Advice was shouted with enthusiasm. The contestant tried to move one hand and immediately began sliding. Two people caught him as he fell off.

The third contestant nearly reached the pouches, missing by a hand. The fourth was so bad it almost seemed deliberate. By now, Alric, Seren, Brannick, and the guards were doubled over.

The fifth, a brewer of the more aerodynamic, thinner variety, squatted, jumped, and wormed his way up. Just as he began the inevitable slide, he snatched a pouch. The crowd cheered, and the man with the pot prepared the pole for its next victim.

Opening the pouch, the brewer's shoulders slumped. He retrieved a sausage, held it up in triumph, and got a laugh and a cheer before taking a bite.

As another contestant got ready, Alric saw Simon approaching, carrying a board loaded with meat. “Mister Alric, thought you should know, the lonely stag? They got a pepper stew. You liked the pepper sausage,” Simon said, pointing to the innkeepers.

Alric was taken aback. “Thanks, Simon. I’ll remember that.”

Simon nodded with a smile before leaving.

As Alric was watching him leave, he noticed something else. Another man was coming toward him, his steps purposeful. Before he even got close, another adventurer intercepted him.

They spoke briefly; the man gestured to Alric, but he could barely hear over the noise. Finally, as the noise from a cheer subsided, he heard a snippet from the adventurer who intercepted the man: “If he wanted a cobbler. Trust he’d have three. Give him his peace.”

The other man rolled his eyes and left. Alric knew neither of them.

As the morning gave way to afternoon, the air felt brisk as it warmed slightly, not enough to explain some of the inn girls moving between various tables, however. Many carried platters, dropping food off with groups of adventurers or moving between fires offering whatever was on them. Most of the inn girls, however, were wearing far too little considering the weather.

Alric watched one as she dropped off a platter with a group. One of the soldiers said something to her; she giggled, then handed him something small. She then bent forward and kissed him on the cheek.

“Seren, is that… is that what I think it is?” Alric said, gesturing with his stein.

Brannick guffawed and scoffed, raising a hand to his mouth. He had to hold his board close.

Seren raised an eyebrow and asked innocently, “What do you think it is?” Brannick was still struggling.

“Shall we call them women of negotiable affection?” Alric asked.

Seren started laughing, but it was just too much for Brannick; he had started laughing with beer in his mouth, trying to swallow, but the final line finished him off, and the beer ran out his nose. Both Alric and Seren pointed fingers at him and laughed far harder.
