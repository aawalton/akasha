Their uneasy camaraderie was interrupted by a sudden, distant roar that echoed across the desert. The ground trembled beneath their feet, sending ripples through the sand. Ada’s horse, tied loosely to a nearby rock, neighed in terror and bolted into the dunes.

“Oh, come on!” Ada shouted, chasing a few futile steps after it before stopping. “I knew I should’ve used a teleportation circle, but noooo, the ‘corner cases’ are too dangerous! And don’t even get me started on the faulty pyromancy scrolls! Do you have any idea how hard it is to find decent-quality scrolls? I wasted an entire day because the last one fizzled out halfway through the mining process! Do you know how much ore I could’ve collected if it hadn’t failed? A lot, that’s how much!”

Sigurd’s eyes were fixed on the horizon, where a massive shadow began to take shape. The dragon’s silhouette loomed, its immense size defying belief. The ground quaked with every step it took, and the air grew heavy with the stench of churned earth.

“You jinxed me,” Ada accused, pointing at Sigurd. “This is your fault.”

“My fault?” he shot back, incredulous. “You’re the one mining near the thing’s den!”

“How was I supposed to know it was a dragon’s den?” Ada snapped, panic creeping into her voice. “It’s not like there was a sign that said, ‘Beware of giant mountain-sized lizard.’ And don’t even get me started on the mining itself! I couldn’t even get half the ore I needed because that stupid pyromancy scroll fizzled out halfway through! Do you know how much time I wasted trying to rework my setup? Hours! Hours that I could have spent collecting enough ore to actually make something useful!”

Sigurd raised an eyebrow. “Maybe your mining is what woke the dragon up.”

Ada gasped, her indignation flaring. “My mining? Oh, sure, blame the person just trying to make a living in this gods-forsaken desert! Maybe the dragon should’ve left a note saying, ‘Please don’t dig here, I’m sleeping!’ Or maybe the guild should’ve warned us that their maps are outdated and don’t mention, oh, I don’t know, a giant dragon’s den!”

She threw her arms up in frustration. “Honestly, why is it always me? I just wanted to mine some ore and get out of here, but noooo, faulty scrolls, missing horses, and now dragons! The whole system is broken, I tell you, broken!”

Sigurd sighed, already moving toward his horse. “Come on. We’re leaving. Now.”

But Ada was far from finished. She jabbed a finger in his direction, her voice rising as she launched into another tirade. “And another thing! Do you have any idea how insufferably attractive you are? Honestly, it’s almost criminal! You walk around with those broad shoulders, that chiseled jaw, and those stupidly perfect muscles, and expect the rest of us to just act normal? Gods above, it’s infuriating! You’re like some sort of divine punishment for women everywhere. Do you know how many times I’ve caught myself staring? And don’t even get me started on your hair! What, do you bathe in moonlight or something?”

Sigurd blinked, taken aback, but Ada plowed on, her words gaining momentum. “And then there’s me! Look at me! I’m just an ugly, greenish-gray halfling with no prospects and even less luck. My parents—oh, let’s talk about them for a second—decided it was a great idea to saddle me with this delightful skin tone and these stubby legs. Thanks, Mom and Dad! I can’t even dream of marriageable prospects. You think anyone’s lining up to marry someone who looks like she belongs in the background of a goblin raid? No! I spend my nights alone, dreaming about, well, you know, night activities! But who am I kidding? I’m a twig, a joke, and the gods just love reminding me of it!”

Sigurd crossed his arms, a bemused smirk tugging at his lips. “You done?”

“No, I’m not done!” Ada shot back, pointing her staff at him. “And now you’re standing there, all smug and perfect, acting like you’re too good for this conversation. It’s infuriating! If you have a single decent bone in that overly attractive body of yours, you’d—”

“Alright, alright,” Sigurd interrupted, stepping closer with a sudden, mischievous glint in his eye. “If it’s such a problem, maybe I should take some of the guilt off your shoulders.” His voice dropped, laced with teasing as he leaned in slightly. “What if I said I found your greenish skin… alluring?”

Ada froze, her eyes narrowing as her cheeks darkened with embarrassment. “Don’t you dare patronize me, you—”

“Patronize you?” Sigurd grinned. “Not at all. You’re fascinating, Ada. But maybe the reason you’re still single is because you keep scaring people off with all this nagging.”

Ada gasped, clutching her chest in mock horror. “My womanhood! How dare you insult it! You think this is about nagging? You’re insufferable! And for the record, it’s not nagging; it’s passionate, articulate expression!”

“Whatever helps you sleep at night,” Sigurd replied, mounting his horse. “Now, are we leaving, or do you want to keep venting until the dragon eats us both?”

Ada’s eyes narrowed, and she crossed her arms, refusing to let the comment slide. “What helps me sleep at night? Oh, you mean besides wishing I could drag some perfectly built, mouth-watering adventurer like you back to my witch’s cottage? Do you know how often I imagine some towering, rippling mountain of a man—yes, someone like you—completely at my mercy for, let’s just say, nightly activities that last all day long?” Her voice dripped with sarcasm, though a faint blush betrayed her honesty.

She jabbed a finger toward the sky, her rant gaining steam. “But do I get that? No! Instead, I wake up every morning to realize it was all just another cruel joke from the God of Dreams. It’s not enough that they mock me with fantasies I’ll never have, but they make it so vivid! It’s like they’re actively taunting me, reminding me I’m stuck as this scrawny, greenish halfling with zero prospects!”

Sigurd turned to face her, his brow raised in amused disbelief. “You fantasize about dragging adventurers to your cottage?”

“Oh, don’t look so shocked!” Ada snapped. “I may be an ugly halfling with stubby legs, but I have needs, Sigurd! Desires! Unfortunately, no one is interested in fulfilling them because I look like a walking goblin sidekick! The gods really outdid themselves when they created me, didn’t they?”

A mischievous grin spread across Sigurd’s face. “Well, maybe if you didn’t scare everyone off with your delightful rants, you’d have better luck.”

Ada gasped, her indignation flaring anew. “Oh, so now it’s my personality that’s the problem? Unbelievable! And here I thought you were supposed to be charming, not insufferable!”

Ada huffed, climbing up behind him. “You’re lucky I’m desperate enough to take this ride. But mark my words, Sigurd. You’ll regret underestimating me.”

Then, as if unable to stop herself, she launched into another tirade. “Do you know what helps me sleep at night? Not that it’s any of your business, but I’ll tell you anyway. About three days ago, I had the most vivid wet dream. In it, I was this incredibly respected magical instructor back at the academy, surrounded by adoring students. These young, eager mages came to me for advice, but things took a, uh, unexpected turn. Next thing I knew, they were… seducing me in my wizardry laboratory!” She fanned herself dramatically, her cheeks flushing a deep green. “Not that I’m complaining… well, maybe a little. I mean, I’m the type who likes to dominate! But I… kind of didn’t hate the idea of some young, fresh-faced boy taking control.”

She scowled, waving her hand as if brushing away the thought. “But do you think I got to enjoy it? Of course not! Just when things were about to get interesting, one of my cheap magical artifacts—a heater I bought to keep the lab warm—malfunctioned and started burning! I had to scramble to put out the fire with my water magic before the whole lab went up in flames. And when I took that piece of junk back to the magic crafter who sold it to me, do you know what they said? ‘Oh, we’re not responsible for what happens after purchase.’ Not responsible? It’s literally their job!”

She threw her arms up in frustration. “Honestly, is it too much to ask for a little respect and accountability in this world? Even in my dreams, I can’t catch a break!”
