The pair had touched on trivial matters and shared snacks that Xara had procured from her storage ring, and before long, it was nighttime. Both had been lost in their own thoughts as they navigated the forest. Out of the corner of his eyes, Levi could pick out a few quirks of hers, like the way she clenched her reins and occasionally gritted her teeth. She seemed to be fighting internal battles of her own that he was not aware of. He was forced to ponder his own thoughts, and he did not like the things that floated around in his mind, so he shut them down and focused on the path ahead.

“It’s getting late, we should camp for the night.”

Levi looked around the area, using the rays of Bylin's glow that slipped through the space in the canopy above to scan the forest. “You're right. I think it's best we make camp off the path.”

“Right,” Xara nodded.

The pair broke off the path and into the forest proper and stopped once they’d found a sufficient location. The spot that they’d chosen was just a few minutes from the path, close enough that they could cover with minimal exertion, yet far enough to conceal them from any unsuspecting traveler.

With a location secured, the pair quickly dismounted and tied their steeds to a nearby tree, then stared at each other, wondering who would breach the conversation about their sleeping arrangement and who would keep watch.

“Ommpf.” Levi wheezed as the air was suddenly knocked out of his lungs, as the bedroll Xara had pulled out of her storage ring had hit him in the chest. “You could've warned me.”

“I could've, but that was more fun,” She chuckled. “You’re welcome, by the way.”

“Yeah, thanks,” Levi said sheepishly.

Xara smiled and then yawned slightly. “You can thank me properly by keeping first watch. I’m tired.”

“Sure thing.” He flashed a thumbs-up.

She smiled back and pulled a tent out of thin air, which made Levi go green with jealousy. One look at the thing and he could tell that it could’ve easily housed the pair. Then again, she was of nobility, and their leanings towards comfort were unparalleled. He stared at his bedroll and then at the mage's tent and couldn't help but press his lips into a thin smile, a bit of dissatisfaction seeping into his bones.

I might as well manage the bedroll; besides, beggars can’t be choosers.

“Ohh, and one more thing, Levi.”

“What?”

“Please don’t abandon me during the night.”

Levi was startled by the mage's request, and before he had a chance to respond, she had closed her tent. The rogue would be lying if he said the thought hadn’t crossed his mind once or twice, not because he wanted to leave her but because that was just the kind of person he was. He was the type of person who considered every option. If he felt he’d be better off without the mage, then he’d have been long gone. But that wasn’t the case. He needed her more than she did him.

The rogue sighed at the sight of the closed tent before he got to work setting up his bedroll. Of course, the setup only involved him tossing it on the floor, but he had to do it in such a way that the entire camp was visible to him. Once he found a suitable spot, he sat upright on his bedroll, crossed his legs, and kept his eyes peeled for danger.

With droopy eyes, he stared at the rays of dawn as the day broke. The night had come and gone without any unwanted surprises. Levi had been the only one to keep watch. He told himself it was because he didn’t trust the mage with his life, but he knew deep down that there was another reason, one that he didn’t want to think too much about at the moment. Besides, there were more important things like his forehead, which was still tingling from his recently completed brand.

He hadn’t screamed or shouted when it had happened because he’d expected it. He could tell that he had a couple of messages waiting for him in his system logs, but he'd chosen not to open them yet. The rogue wasn’t sure he was ready to see what the stone had done to him..

“I can’t believe you let me sleep through the night!”

At the noise, his head snapped to the side to the sight of a groggy-looking Xara stepping out of her tent, staring at him with confusion and pity.

“Don’t sweat it,” He said, rubbing his eyes to dispel his droopiness, even if momentarily. “The way I see it, you needed the sleep.”

“Yes, I did, but so did you,” She countered.

Levi pushed himself off his bedroll and stretched, and then locked eyes with the mage.”Don’t worry about me. I’m fine.”

The sounds of the insects and birds in the forest were the only thing that could be heard as the pair got into a staring contest. One that Xara ultimately lost. She huffed in annoyance at the rogue and then returned to her tent, closing it behind her.

“Well, what did I do wrong?“ Levi whispered to no one in particular. His gaze turned to their steeds, and the pair had blank expressions on their faces.

The flapping of the tent tore his focus from the mata to the mage who’d emerged from it. Her regal demeanor had returned.

The confusion and pity seemed to have disappeared into thin air, and he was left looking at nobility. The lack of emotion on her face was accompanied by a hard glint in her eyes. He watched as the mage turned her attention to the bedroll and tent, recalling both into her storage ring. When she was done with those, she turned to face him.

“I’m not just a human coin purse. I can help. That’s why I joined you. To help.” She said. “I don’t want to be pampered.”

“I’m not—”

“Don’t you dare lie to me!”

Levi stared at the mage for a moment, noting how her nostrils flared and her chest rose. She was angry, and that was something he wasn’t interested in dealing with, so he turned and walked to his mata, untying it and tuning out her protests.

Too much drama. I can’t deal with this.

“Hey, do you even know the way?” Xara called out, arms crossed.

“No.”

“So you're just going to get on the road to a place that you don’t know all by yourself rather than apologize to me?”

“Yes.”

“B-But I’m nobility… You have to apologize to me.”

“Like I told you before, I don’t have to do anything that I don’t want to,” Levi said as he mounted his mata. “I am too tired to argue, and if that's all you want to do, then you can just pick any tree and say whatever you want to it. It’ll surely give more of a listening ear to your grievances than I would, but if you want to do the more productive thing, then I suggest that you get on your steed so that we can be on our way. The choice is yours.“

“Y-You … argh,” She huffed. Still glaring daggers at him, she untied her own mata and mounted it.”I’m beginning to hate you.”

He turned to stare at the mage. Making sure their eyes locked before he gave her his response. “So am I.”

The rogue kicked the side for his steed, coaxing it into a trot. It seemed like the beast was a good reader of emotions. The black mata had refrained from trying to kill him like it had done the previous day. A more than welcome development, especially since he wasn’t in the mood for more shenanigans.

Levi glanced at the mage out of the corner of his eye; her annoyance seemed to fade into nothingness. Her face had become a blank canvas as she followed closely behind him on her own mount, an ironclad grip on her reins. He wondered if he’d been wrong in the way he’d spoken to her; it wasn’t nice, and he didn't actually hate her. It was better to deal with the effects of his words now than to let them fester in her heart. “I’m sorry.” He said.

“So am I,” the mage said after a heartbeat.

The pair needed no more words; they both understood that they’d overstepped a bit, but the relationship had been mended, and now they could focus back on getting to the path.

Levi surveyed the path thoroughly before nudging his mount onto it. The area was clear …too clear. Since the previous day, the number of people they’d seen on the path had dwindled to the occasional one or two travelers they spotted on the road. He wasn’t sure whether to be relieved or alarmed, and in the end, he settled for a mix of both.

The tiredness he felt was something that could be dealt with while he was on his mount, and traveling like this could give him some time to get himself back to his best. The rogue had only stayed up all night because he’d doubted that they’d be allowed to get a good night's sleep. A decision he half regretted. If he’d known that no monster or person would attack them during the night, then he would've woken up the mage to have the second watch and then caught some sleep. He sighed at the thought, then shrugged his shoulders. The deed had already been done, and now it was time for him to live through the consequences of his decision.

“You said you want to help, right?”

“Yes. I do.”

“Good, I have a couple of questions that I need you to help me answer.”

“Ask.”

“What does being the obsidian rogue mean?”

“The obsidian rogue… I’ll admit that I don’t know much about the specifics of the First, but what I do know is that the stone is equivalent to the Emperor's crown.” She said. “It signals you out as the most powerful of your class…At least it's supposed to”

“What does that mean?”

“To put it plainly. Those stones are only to be wielded by the strongest. Hence, the free-for-all that exists.” She said, “Those who aren't strong enough to keep their stones die, and those who are stronger replace them. According to rumors, the stone supposedly fast-tracks leveling up or at least gives its wielder some sort of edge over the whole process.”

“Rumors?”

“Yeah, no one knows for sure. Information on what the stones do is almost non-existent.”

Seems like a lot of secrecy surrounds the stone.

“You keep saying stones? Just how many are there?”

“Six, there are six stones in total, five for each combat class and one stone for all non-combat classes that exist. That stone is called the Opal stone.”

“Opal stone, got it.“ He said. “What about the other stones?”

The mage stared at him for a moment, seemingly considering her words before she spoke. “The others are the obsidian, which you possess, carnelian for the warrior class, diamond for the priests, jade for the rangers, Citrine for mages.”

“And the opal stone is for non-combat class, right?”

“Yes, and if you want to be factually correct, you’d say that the opal stone belongs to the administrator,” she said. “All six fully branded synced in possession of the stones form the council of six. It is their duty to protect all of Rinesta from all threats. They are the frontline against any and everything.”

“Protect Rinesta? From what…” The rogue trailed off.

The image of the female rogue he’d seen back at the Sapphires popped up in his mind unbidden. She had obviously been high-levelled, and yet something had mauled her. What sort of monster could've done that to a synced… What kind of monster could've done that to someone on that so-called council? It didn't make any sense to him.

He wasn’t sure, but his mind didn't believe that his predecessor had died in a dungeon. It was very well within the realms of possibilities, but it didn’t seem like the case to him. The puzzle just didn’t fit.

What the hell have I gotten myself into?
