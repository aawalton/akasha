Time seemed to slow, but I knew that was just an illusion.

It was a real phenomenon, apparently, where your subconscious processed thoughts a split-second before your conscious mind, making it feel like your brain was working faster. I’d read about it back on Earth once.

Whatever it was, though, I was thankful for it as I scanned the compound, noticing details I never had. The defensive emplacements weren’t just positioned to cover approaches from outside. They had overlapping fields of fire inward too, covering the checkpoint, the main plaza, and every spot anyone might try to hide. Furthermore, the skeleton guards patrolled with a ton of overlap between their routes.

I had to hand it to whoever had come up with the defenses. Overkill was their middle name.

There were at least three tentacle commanders, their grotesque forms perched on elevated positions like organic watchtowers. Their psychic control meant the skeleton soldiers weren’t just following programmed routes. They were being actively directed by a mind far greater than theirs, and likely even mine.

Then there were the several tanks that sat within the compound, their main guns pointed outward. Their machine gun turrets, however, were on a 360-degree swivel and could rake anything within the compound.

This wasn’t just a military installation. It was a killbox. A trap designed to slaughter any enemy stupid enough to enter with overwhelming force.

And of course, Yashas and I had walked right into it.

The checkpoint ahead had two skeleton soldiers that stood at a makeshift barrier, checking what looked like ID cards or tags from every soldier passing through. Behind them, two heavy machine gun emplacements pointed directly at the checkpoint itself.

Going through was obviously not an option. Even if our IFF transponders were somehow valid, we were flesh and blood. The moment we got close enough for the people at the checkpoint to see us properly, those guns would light us up, and we’d be dead before we hit the ground.

“Fuck,” I muttered again.

Running was out. We were too far inside the compound, surrounded by patrols and automated defenses. The moment we broke from our current route, we’d be flagged as hostile.

But fighting was suicide. Even with my Vigor suit, and even with everyone out of my inventory, we couldn’t take on an entire military compound. Not with tanks and artillery and fucking helicopter gunships hovering overhead.

Which left… what? Bluffing our way through and hoping we didn’t get close enough to the guards for them to see our faces? That was barely a plan. That was just delaying the inevitable.

No, all that was left was Plan C, and it was Plan C for a reason.

I wasn’t in my Grace suit, but I did have [Hotswap]. The ability let me switch between suits instantly, but it also let me equip a suit when I wasn’t currently wearing any. Especially handy during late nights in bed with Aerion in hostile terrain… or when masquerading as skeleton troopers in the middle of a dungeon stronghold.

I felt a lot less naked when I knew I could be in my suit and ready to fight at a moment’s notice.

Of course, the second I did, the skeleton armor would vanish, and I’d very obviously not be one of them anymore, which meant every gun in this compound would turn on me.

“Yashas,” I whispered, hoping for a miracle. “Best escape route from here?”

He didn’t turn his head, maintaining the appearance of a casual patrol. “Forward. Through the checkpoint, up the stairs of the Sensō-ji, through the main doors, then down to the underground levels where the generator is located.”

Because of course the only way out was through.

“And the enemies along that path?”

“Numerous.” His voice was grim. “Two at the checkpoint. Four guards at the base of the stairs. Six inside the temple proper, with at least two more on the path to the underground entrance. And that’s not counting the patrols that could converge on our position once the alarm is raised.”

I did the math in my head. [Spacefold] had a range of about twenty feet per use, and I could trigger it maybe once every few seconds if I pushed it.

That meant at least three, likely four consecutive jumps, and I’d have to stash Yashas, change, and activate my powers in such quick succession that I got inside without them noticing.

That was the ideal scenario, and I knew better than to hope it would go that well. More likely, we’d be detected, and I’d have to whip out the big guns to fight our way in.

“Whatever you’re about to do,” Yashas said quietly, “you’ll have to do it fast. We’ve been standing here too long. We’re starting to draw attention.”

Sure enough, I could see a patrol on the far side of the plaza slowing down, one of the skeletons turning to look in our direction.

Shit. That was it for planning. Now it was time to execute.

“I’m putting you in my inventory,” I said. “Be ready to use your detection ability when I bring you out.”

To his credit, Yashas didn’t question my decision. “Then go with the speed of the gods, Greg.”

I touched his shoulder and pulled him into my inventory, activating [Hotswap] in the same breath.

The skeleton armor vanished, replaced instantly by my Grace suit, and the surge of returned stats hit me harder than snorting cocaine ever could. My speed and balance multiplied, and every muscle that dealt with finesse grew visibly stronger, giving me a near-divine command over my body I’d so sorely missed.

I didn’t give the enemy the chance to notice.

[Spacefold] activated, and I blinked past the checkpoint, reality distorting as I appeared at the top of the stairs. The guards at the bottom turned towards me, but I’d already activated the ability again, disappearing through the open temple doors.

The interior was beautiful. Or at least, it would have been if it hadn’t been converted into a military staging area. Traditional architecture was hidden by modern tactical equipment, crates of ammunition stacked against the ornate pillars, with communications equipment set up on what had probably once been the main altar.

Six guards were inside, just like Yashas had said, and this time, there was no avoiding them. Never mind that even stretched [Spacefold] to its limit, I appeared in front of them—I’d never have made it to the internal stairs without them noticing anyway.

I didn’t doubt my ability to take them out, of course. Light of the Fearless could cut through them easily enough. But that would take time, and time was the one thing I didn’t have. The moment those guards shouted for help, reinforcements would flood in. Machine guns would swivel over, or one of the tanks would get a bead on me. All splendid ways to die.

No, I needed something faster. Something that would maintain the element of surprise I’d miraculously held onto thus far.

And so I reached into my inventory and pulled out my ultimate secret weapon.

A British guy.

The friendly face popped into existence in front of me, his expression shifting impressively quickly from the disorientation of being pulled out to combat-ready awareness. I was glad I had told him to be ready to fight the moment he came out, because he didn’t waste any time looking around or peppering me with questions.

The same couldn’t be said for the skeleton guards, seeing a second person appear from nowhere. That left them stupefied. Just for a split second, but a split second was all Richard needed.

His hand came up, and six skeleton guards collapsed, their bones losing cohesion and clattering on the stone tiles along with their combat armor and their helmets, which went rolling.

I had to admire how much Richard’s ability had evolved since Basecrest’s Trial. He didn’t even need flesh to act on anymore, let alone hearts. Now it could affect the magical animating force that kept the skeletons moving, disrupting whatever passed for their vital functions.

“Nice!” I threw him an appreciative nod, then pulled him back into my inventory, along with the six skeleton corpses and their armor for good measure. Couldn’t leave evidence lying around, after all. Not while we still had the element of surprise.

Activating [Spacefold] again, I was at the stairs and descending into the complex.

The stairs led down into an empty hallway lined with doors on both sides. Doors I had no visibility into. Time to rectify that.

I pulled Yashas out, and given that he’d only been in for minutes, there was nearly no disorientation to speak of.

He turned to me and raised an eyebrow.

“Find us an empty room,” I muttered, to which he nodded, scanning the area before pointing to a door.

Finding it unlocked, I opened it to find what looked to be a maintenance closet. Except the closet was easily larger than my entire apartment had been on Earth, with all sorts of industrial vacuums, mops, workbenches, and maintenance supplies on shelves that rose to its two-story roof, illuminated by white LEDs embedded in the ceiling.

Considering the size of the operation they were running, I supposed it made sense that they’d need something this large.

Yashas eased the door behind me.

“Well?” I muttered. “How bad is it?”

“Truthfully, I am amazed you managed to come this far without detection,” he said slowly. “The compound is on alert.” He paused, closing his eyes in concentration. “They know something happened, but they don’t know where you went. From what I am able to tell, their sweeps are focused around the perimeter for now, likely assuming you escaped rather than penetrated deeper.”

“As any sane person would,” I said, letting out a breath I didn’t know I’d been holding. “So we haven’t been detected yet, then?”

“Not yet. But we will be when the guards you offed fail to check in.”

I raised an eyebrow. “You know about that?”

“I no longer sense the six that were at the top of the stairs,” Yashas replied, giving me a smile. “The conclusion was obvious.”

Yep, I was glad to have someone as smart as him on our side.

“We have minutes at most before they start searching the temple itself.” He looked at me, and I saw something like respect in his eyes. “That was an insane gambit.”

“Yeah, well.” I leaned against the wall, trying to stop my arm quivering from the adrenaline. I’d fought monsters before, but trying to go in secretly made my heart pound like nothing else. “Insane is kind of my specialty at this point.”

We were closer to the shield generator than we had any right to be, but I didn’t let that delude me from the monumental task we had ahead.

I could hear boots in the halls and shouts echoing down from the levels above. The compound was on alert, and we still had to find the generator, blow it up, and somehow get out alive.

“Come on,” I said, nodding to the door. “Let’s move before our luck runs out.”

Assuming, of course, we had any luck left to lose.
