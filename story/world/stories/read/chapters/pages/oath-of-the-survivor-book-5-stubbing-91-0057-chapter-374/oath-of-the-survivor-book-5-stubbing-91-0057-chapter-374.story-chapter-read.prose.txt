Duroc and his retinue stared, slack-jawed at the troop of chaddlings arrayed before them.  “I didn’t realize your second class was a summoner variant, and at such a high level,” Duroc breathed out, eyes fixed on the chaddling legionnaire at the head of the group.

It was supported by nine of the standard melee chaddlings and two of the ranged variants.  Kyle intended to deploy the rest of his retinue to keep Mangalica’s forces busy while he and Duroc focused on the C Grades.  Still, he wanted to make sure that the civilians had some protection should the fighting spill over to them through the course of their battle.  Duroc didn’t bring many fighters with him, with the strongest of the orcs among them in the early stages of D Grade.  The legionnaire on its own exceeded the whole group’s combat power, and with the extra support of the chaddlings behind it, they’d be able to hold against most threats shy of C Grade, at least for a while.

“Come on,” Kyle said, ignoring Duroc’s comment.  “If we don’t go spring the trap, they could get bored and bring the fighting to us.”

Duroc nodded, hefting his tower shield as he and Kyle took off down the path at a brisk pace, swiftly approaching the location of the ambush.

“How do you want to play this?” Kyle asked.

“I will call him out when we are closer, and give him an opportunity to retreat.  And when he doesn’t take it, I will challenge him to a duel in front of you and all of his troops as witnesses.  When he turns that down, I will engage him directly, assuming you still feel confident in handling his pet.”

“I’ve killed enough of these creatures already, what’s one more?” Kyle joked.  “And I’ll be using more of these summons to hold back the rest of the troops, so unless he’s got another C Grade in reserve we should be able to focus on our fights.”

“I never expected the backwater D Grade I met on a dying planet to turn out so scary,” Duroc joked.  “I’m glad you’re on my side.”

Kyle chuckled.  “That’s the pot calling the kettle black.  You hardly showed any of what you’re capable of at the Tournament of Ascension, and still held on enough to keep the other War Chieftains happy.”

“Well let’s hope we’re both scary enough to make him think twice,” Duroc said.

The moment C.H.A.D.D. signified they were close enough, they stopped.  Auric Perception could faintly detect their presences, though the drone noted that they were using some sort of portable formation to block the majority of their mana signatures.  It clearly wasn’t high-quality, but without any sensory skills it would have done the trick.  As they came to a halt, Duroc let out a bellow that echoed through the stony ground.

“Mangalica!  I know you’re here.  Don’t insult either of us with your pathetic ambush.  Come talk face to face, if you’ve the stomach for it.”

Kyle was taken aback by the intensity of the orc’s words.  Duroc’s laid-back attitude toward most things was something he’d taken for granted, and the sharp orders felt very out of place.  For a moment, nothing happened.  Then twin auras swept over the land as the formation suppressing them was dispelled, and a massive, scaled paw reached over the nearest rock formation.

A horned beast covered in dark green scales clambered over the stone, thick membranes running between its forelimbs and sides, with vestigial wings sprouting from its shoulders.  A tail nearly the size of its body thrashed behind it as a low growl bubbled in its chest, flaming orange eyes fixed on Kyle and Duroc.  The creature was unmistakably a hodag, though it almost looked to be crossed with a dragon.  What drew Kyle’s attention the most, however, was the figure riding the creature.

Mangalica was wearing a thick war kilt made of leather and studded with metal, and what looked to be the remains of shredded and tattered leather armor across his torso.  His bursting muscles and bristly hide were unable to be fully contained by the material, making it look almost unremarkable.  Identify told him a different story.

“The armor is fused with him,” Kyle whispered to Duroc, not shifting his eyes from the massive orc.

The mana signature from the armor was ever-so-different than Mangalica’s own; something Kyle hadn’t noticed with the suppressive effect of the formation.  It looked like the renegade War Chieftain had tried to do another round of absorption, and only partially succeeded.  This remaining “armor” was, in fact, the hide of another C Grade hodag, one that Mangalica was in the process of assimilating into himself.  It was both horrifying and relieving at the same time, as Kyle realized that there were dramatic diminishing returns.  The already massive orc was even larger than before, but his aura didn’t feel significantly more powerful.  Kyle expected that would change when he activated his boosting skill, though it was a relief that he wasn’t able to spike his personal power infinitely, at least at this grade.

Hefting his oversized blade, Mangalica spoke for the first time, his voice carrying undisguised malice.  “I see you and the others have finally finished your play fighting.  Apparently you’ve let your games go to your head, if you think your performance there means anything about your ability to survive what’s coming.”

“Your ambitions only end one way, Mangalica,” Duroc said, a hint of exhaustion in his voice.  “If you truly want reform, it isn’t too late to engage in conversation.  The others are open to it.  But revolution?  Disrespecting all the sacred traditions of our people?  That isn’t the way.”

“That way of thinking is what has kept our people so weak for so long!” Mangalica snarled.  “Tell me, when did the last War Chieftain rise to B Grade?  How long has it been since an orc reached A Grade?  And those that did chose to abandon this worthless world.  I’ve been shown a new path, a better path.  And you will all be fuel for it.”

Duroc’s beady eyes narrowed as he looked at the power-mad War Chieftain.  “If you truly believe that, then face me here.  One-on-one, in front of these witnesses.  Prove that your path is as mighty as you claim.”

A wicked smile crossed Mangalica’s face, revealing sharpened teeth behind the oversized tusks.  “I have nothing to prove to you.  Besides, your friend here has caused me enough trouble as it is.  I look forward to breaking him and bringing him back as a trophy.  You and your village will fall this day.”

He turned to his followers, who snapped to attention as he barked.  “Go up the path to find his people, and kill them all.  Then meet the strike force heading to his village.  We’ll engage after I clean up here.”

As one, Mangalica’s followers shouted their assent, rushing down the far side of the road to give their leader a clear path to Duroc and Kyle.

“Bastard,” Duroc snarled, grip tightening on his spear.

Kyle just sighed, looking at the horde of charging orcs.  He began summoning the chaddlings, and was about to activate Aura Force, when he felt a surge of mana from beside him.  A huge chunk of the attacking force suddenly shot skyward; no warning, no fanfare.  All that they managed was a brief call of surprise before they were little more than dark spots in the sky.

The cry caused some of the others to pause and turn toward the newly formed hole in their formation, making them entirely unprepared when the chaddling forces crashed into their lines.  His remaining legionnaires punched holes through the disorganized rabble with brutal efficiency, only slowing down when Mangalica’s own elites managed to get to the front and engage them.

Kyle wanted to watch their performance, but was forced to duck as Mangalica, still riding the scaled hodag, closed the distance between them in a heartbeat, swinging his massive blade in an arc to behead him.  Duroc interposed his shield at an almost impossible angle, deflecting the blow as his spear shot out directly toward Mangalica’s heart.

The renegade War Chieftain tried to twist his body out of the way, only to find himself drawn toward the tip of the weapon by Duroc’s gravity manipulation.  The hodag veered violently at the last moment, causing Duroc’s attack to narrowly miss, cutting a deep line across Mangalica’s chest instead of impaling him.

Kyle had already reached out to the creature with Parasitic Resonance, but quickly encountered a problem.  These scales are no joke.  They seemed to possess mana-nullification properties even beyond the stonebloom’s, making it difficult to even maintain Parasitic Resonance in their vicinity.  A quick test with Aura Force showed him that it was similarly hampered. This will be a tough one.

The hodag let out an earth-shaking roar as it lunged again.  Kyle twisted his hips, already withdrawing Model: Spear from the World Tree Seed.  He was aiming for the creature's open maw, but Mangalica intercepted the swiftly-extending weapon with a stroke of his blade.  Kyle swore as he narrowly dodged the charge with Aura Force.

Duroc took to the air, and took advantage of the opening to strike toward Mangalica’s neck from above. It was a beautiful strike, which by all rights should have landed.  However, at the last moment the living armor rippled across his body before rising to slap away the piercing blow, turning it into another light graze.

Duroc was undeterred.  He took the momentum from the glancing blow and pivoted in the air as his shield glowed with deep silver light.  A shockwave capable of killing some C Grades outright smashed into Mangalica and the hodag alike, blasting them dozens of meters away.  The hodag flared its wings as its claws scrabbled against the stony ground.  Silvery light surrounded both of them as the remnants of the skill floated around them, and Kyle felt a surge of mana as Duroc activated the second layer of his technique.  Mangalica was lifted into the air as the hodag was shoved toward the ground.

The impact on the creature was far less significant; the silver light already dissipating as the hodag’s scales destabilized the mana from Duroc’s technique.  It was still enough to separate the hodag from Mangalica, which was what they needed.  They’d known going in that fighting the War Chieftain alongside any of the hodags would be dangerous.  Beast Masters were at their most deadly when they could empower their companions while being empowered in turn.  Duroc was confident in fighting Mangalica head-on, which meant that Kyle’s job was to keep the hodag out of the way.  The big question for him was to figure out how exactly he was supposed to manage that.

He didn’t need Identify to show him that this was the most powerful hodag he’d faced so far.  Its attributes all across the board were exceptional, only deficient in Intelligence and Willpower.  Ordinarily, that would give him an easy opportunity to leverage Parasitic Resonance, but its scales protected against that tool quite effectively.  The hodag tensed as it prepared to leap toward the still-flying Mangalica, and Kyle reacted by switching his prosthesis to Model: Cannon and launching a massive explosion directly in the hodag’s path.

Before the dust could even clear from the explosion, a flash of light from C.H.A.D.D. and Synaptic Barbs warned Kyle of a fast-moving tail aiming for his head like a guillotine blade.  He barely ducked underneath as Genesis Engine fully activated.  He looked up at the monster already mid-leap, aiming to crush him.  Here we go.
