Risha drew Crimson, the blade shining with the light of the moon above. In the sword, she felt the young spirit, sister to the one forming in her other blade. She wondered how the spirit in the blade was different from Igni. Was it already bound to her like Igni was?

She placed her finger on the flat of the blade, tracing the metal that had tasted so much blood.

The sound of voices drew her attention away from the blade and to the entrance of her tent, where a furious Elder Kulos stormed in, followed closely by the warrior who guarded her tent.

Risha sheathed her blade, as she faced the glaring Elder, the tips of his teeth showing in frustration.

“Grand Mother, you’ve asked me to be the voice where you are blind, but you cannot expect me to clean up every mess you make. Their deaths are not on my hands alone!” The Elder growled with adrenaline.

Risha straightened her back, her eyes narrowing at the Elder as she exposed her own teeth.

She growled, the sound long and low.

The Elder stepped back, and visibly regained control of himself.

“Elder Kulos, what are you talking about?” Her voice stayed low.

“Grand Mother.” With a practiced motion, the goblin lowered himself, bowing in apology. “With Mystic Klu, our people had time, time to know him, goblins to protect him. With the humans, their Saint protected you in a battle all could see. With these creatures… you cannot expect them to kill each other one day, and fight together the next. Scaled Warriors will be challenged and die and I cannot protect them all.”

Risha watched the Elder for a long moment, then let out a breath, her tenseness leaving her. “You’re worrying about them.”

Elder Kulos paused, caught off guard by the comment. He cleared his throat before speaking. “I’m worrying about the death of your people.”

“What do you think I should do then?” Risha asked.

The Elder sighed, “The damage has been done,” He stepped over to where her black cloak hung from the wall, “and it isn’t as bad as it could’ve been. Your hold on the kobolds isn’t as tight as it needs to be. If Sun-Chaser hadn’t been in the right place at the right time, you would’ve had a massacre on your hands.”

Risha’s eyes narrowed, she hadn’t realized they would go against her wishes so directly.

Elder Kulos’s hand reached out, feeling the panther fur that covered her hanging cloak. “In the future, when making such a decision, let us help you. We have learned to keep peace amongst our people, and you are not alone as a leader.” He lowered his hand and looked back at her, “You are many things, Grand Mother, but delicate diplomat is not one of them.”

Risha laughed, a large smile blooming across her face. “No, Risha is not.” She walked over to the Elder and looked at the cloak with him. In a quieter voice, she continued, “I’m glad you see that.”

Elder Kulos turned to her with eyes that saw far too deep. Deep enough that Risha began to worry that he saw to the core of her. He lowered his eyes, and bowed his head, “Do you put everything on your shoulders, Grand Mother?”

The question hung over the room like a blanket, stifling Risha, smothering her. She couldn’t answer, any words suddenly stuck in her throat.

“Kulos will continue to remind Risha that she is not alone.” The Elder fell to his knees, his head lowering, “Kulos will help Risha carry her burdens, always.”

Risha felt the tension leave her like the wind from a smith’s bellows. She placed her hand on top of the older goblin’s head, then she leaned down and kissed the top of his head. “Risha will remember the words of her Elder.” She stood to her full height, and offered the Elder her hand. “So what should Risha do now to protect her people, Elder Kulos?”

The Elder took the hand and rose to his full height. “There are goblins who see your vision, assign them to oversee the Scaled Warriors.”

Risha nodded, “Inform Glo, and make it happen.”

Elder Kulos turned and walked to the door of the tent. He opened the flap, then paused, “I am… sorry for my disrespect, Grand Mother.”

Risha looked away from her Elder, her eyes looking into the distance. “Your anger came from a good place, Elder. Do not forget that anger when it matters.”

The Elder bowed his head, then left the tent.

Risha stood there for a long time, staring at nothing.

“Kliks.”

The spiderling crawled to her shoulder, and rested a leg on her cheek.

“How long before the spiders will need to burrow?”

Kliks paused, then lifted herself to feel the air. “Four weeks, maybe? Many can push past that.”

Risha nodded, “Tell Glo we have one more day of rest, then we march forward.”

The spiderling leapt from Risha’s shoulder, then skittered off towards the command tent.

Risha opened her eyes to her home in the council of Grandmas. The tent was as she’d left it after her last transformation. Bloodied spears marked the entrance, while her chain swords hung from the walls amidst the tapestries of her many battles.

She noticed with surprise that not only had the spirits of her swords joined her here, resting in the blades on the wall, but Igni also rested inside her, giving her an inner warmth.

She turned away from the tapestries, and walked out of the tent, and onto the stone pathway she’d made for herself. Around the bloodied and broken swords and spears that marked the edge of the path, thorny vines had grown, their flowers readying for their first bloom.

She walked forward, up to the statues. Here too, life could be seen, as plants began their ascension of the statues of her people. Behind the kobold, the goblin, and the orc, another statue was sculpted, this one depicting a featureless scaled warrior, his head facing the sky as his chains broke around him.

She looked away from them to her doorway, the source of the vines that had taken over her garden of metal and stone. It was vibrant, with its first flower in full bloom. With a start, Risha realized that she recognized the flower, it was the same one The Ancient Shaman had gifted to her from the world of his lost friend, Elly.

She walked up to the flower, and reached up to gently feel it. Spirit traveled through it, doing things that Risha didn’t understand. Still, Risha could tell that they were not harmful, if anything, they were the exact opposite.

Risha laughed. She should’ve known, that his gift would be anything but simple.

She had to admit, she liked the green that the flower brought to her path of broken blades.

With a shake of her head, she stepped through the doorway, and into the void of the council.

Moments later, her feet stepped through the snow of a mountaintop, a steaming cave just visible.

“You’re getting better, dear.”

Risha turned to the voice, finding Iris there, her ancient eyes sparkling with mirth.

Risha bowed her head to the other Grand Mother. “I’ve been practicing.”

“Is that so?” Iris looked her up and down, then turned to the hotsprings, her feet leaving no mark on the snow as she walked. “Is the spirit of flame you have bound to yourself part of that practice?”

Risha paused, then hurried to follow the woman to the warm baths. She shouldn’t have been surprised that Iris could sense the spirit, but still she was. “Yes, I was taught how to bind Igni.”

“Igni?” Iris looked back at her, “Quite the name your spirit chose for itself.”

“Does it mean something?” Risha asked.

“It is an old name.” Iris disrobed without a care before stepping into the baths with a long sigh. “It simply means fire. Beyond that, it has no meaning.”

Risha joined her in the baths, the heat of the hot spring immediately relaxing her after what had been a long couple days.

They relaxed in silence for a long time, both of them enjoying the break from their own stressors. Risha sank into the waters, not realizing how exhausted she’d been.

“I found out what leads the Scaled Warriors.” Risha opened her eyes, the words coming out of her, no matter how much she wished to simply enjoy the water.

Iris opened her eyes, their icy blue color glowing with a piercing light before the feeling faded. “Oh? Who leads them then?”

“A Spark.”

Iris straightened, “A Spark?” a note of anger entered her voice, the air freezing and the pool boiling with heat before she calmed herself. “They should all be bound. For one to take over the sacred bindings… Are you certain, Risha?”

Risha nodded, “I fought with one of his servants. The power he wielded was… too much for me alone.”

Iris was silent for a long moment, “If what you are telling me is true, little one. then I must warn you. There was a roost there, long ago, not of drakes, and not of serpents, but of Dragons. I thought it was one of them who opposed you, but if it isn’t… They too might have fallen to those bindings.” She went quiet, but something in the air told Risha that she should wait to speak. “If they have, then… call me. I will make my decision then and only if they truly have fallen to another.”

“Iris, are you-?”

The look in Iris’s eyes silenced Risha.

“I have removed myself from the affairs of mortals, Mother Risha. I do not claim my children as I once did, but I will not let a Spark disrespect me. Just… do not call me without proof.”

Risha saw it then, for the briefest of moments, the spirit that rested behind Iris. Shining blue scales covered her, and the eyes of a serpent sat above the muzzle of a great beast, teeth sharp and capable of swallowing even Klaz’zks whole.

Then it was gone, and the woman with blue skin rested in the waters with Risha again. She smiled softly, “I apologize for letting my anger get the better of me for a moment, you do not understand the importance of the bonds that belong to the Scaled warriors. I call them sacred for a reason. They are meant to be given freely, not taken.”

Risha felt a rush of relief that she had not taken the bonds of her prisoners, however briefly she’d considered the possibility. “Don’t apologize.” she finally said.

Iris sunk into the hotspring, then created bubbles with her breath as she stared into the distance.

Risha watched for a second, then smiled as she sunk into the pool and blew bubbles as well.

Iris did her best to cover a smile at the goblin’s antics.
