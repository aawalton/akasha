The Adventurer’s Guild Hall was bustling with activity when Sigurd walked in, Ada trailing behind him with her ever-present staff and the mini-dragon in tow. Adventurers swapped stories of glory, bounties, and dangerous quests, but the chatter died down when Sigurd approached the mission board.

The Guildmaster raised an eyebrow. “You’re taking the thieves’ job, Sigurd? Thought that’d be too small for you.”

Sigurd scanned the mission details, his brow furrowing. The targets were infamous for their escapades, but something about their descriptions felt… off.

“They’re troublemakers,” The Guildmaster continued. “Haven’t killed anyone, but they’ve made a lot of enemies among the nobles.”

“What’s the bounty?” Sigurd asked.

The Guildmaster snorted. “Enough to make anyone forget they’re basically stealing from the worst people around.”

Ada piped up, leaning against the counter. “So, thieves wannabes? Oh, this should be fun. I’m coming.”

Sigurd sighed. “It’s not a field trip, Ada. Stay here. I don’t need you getting hurt.”

Ada’s eyes narrowed, her ears twitching with irritation. “Getting hurt? Really? You think I’m some damsel in distress?”

Sigurd held up his hands defensively. “That’s not what I meant. It’s just—this isn’t your fight.”

Ada stepped closer, jabbing a finger into his chest. “Listen here, big guy. I don’t need a man to protect me. I’ve taken down more threats with my staff and brain than you have with your oversized sword.”

The mini-dragon chirped in agreement, nudging Ada’s leg. She crossed her arms and added with a smirk, “Besides, someone’s got to make sure you don’t screw this up. So, like it or not, I’m coming.”

Sigurd groaned, pinching the bridge of his nose. “You’re impossibly stubborn, you know that?”

“You’re impossibly handsome, you know that?” Ada shot back, already packing experimental potions into her satchel. The mini-dragon wagged its tail happily as Ada grinned triumphantly. “It’s decided. Let’s go.”

Their first destination was the mansion of a merchant named Gary, a man whose complaints about the thieves had reached the Guild’s ears. Gary met them at the door, wringing his hands dramatically. “My precious jewels! They stole them right out of my vault! Those hooligans must be punished!”

Sigurd’s eyes narrowed. He gestured to Gary’s study. “Mind if we take a look around?”

Gary hesitated, his face twitching with reluctance, but eventually relented. Inside, Sigurd’s sharp eyes quickly found something suspicious: hidden ledgers beneath a false drawer.

“Tax evasion, illegal trading, and…oh, importing cursed artifacts. A real upstanding citizen,” Ada quipped, flipping through the pages. “I’m starting to like these thieves already.”

Gary, who had been hovering nervously, suddenly blustered, “Those are private documents! You have no right—this is slander!”

Ada turned, fixing Gary with a withering glare. “Oh, sit down, Gary,” she snapped, waving her staff for emphasis. “Private documents? What kind of merchant keeps cursed artifacts on the down-low? Are you planning to sell those to children next?”

Gary’s face turned an unflattering shade of red. “I—I am a respectable businessman!”

“Respectable?” Ada’s voice rose, her sarcasm sharp enough to cut. Then, without missing a beat, she launched into a tirade so rapid-fire it could have been mistaken for a spell incantation. “Let me guess, Gary: you overcharge the poor, underpay your workers, and probably skimp on basic sanitation in your shops. You whine about thieves while hoarding cursed artifacts—are those for sale, or just part of your personal ‘aesthetic’? Speaking of aesthetics, what is that outfit? Did you get dressed in the dark? Or are you just trying to scare away customers with that hideous sense of fashion? I mean, honestly, those colors together? My eyes are bleeding.”

Gary, already pale, turned a deeper shade of red with each insult. “I-I-” he stammered, but Ada steamrolled over him.

“And don’t think I didn’t notice the ledgers. Tax evasion? Illegal trading? What’s next, Gary, counterfeit potions? You’re not a respectable merchant; you’re a walking scandal waiting to happen. Honestly, it’s a miracle the city guards haven’t carted you off already. You’re like a bad play that just keeps getting worse!”

Gary’s lower lip trembled. “Stop… please…”

But Ada wasn’t finished. “Oh, and let’s not forget the whining! ‘My jewels! My vault!’ Boohoo, Gary. Maybe spend less time crying and more time fixing your sham of a business. Honestly, I’ve met frogs with better backbone than you!”

That was the breaking point. Gary collapsed to his knees, tears streaming down his face. “Mother! Help!” he wailed, clutching his head as if Ada’s words had physically struck him.

From a back room, an elderly woman bustled in, her soft demeanor a stark contrast to her son’s pitiful state. “Oh, my sweet boy!” she cooed, kneeling beside him and cradling his head in her lap. “What happened?”

Ada crossed her arms, raising an eyebrow. “Just a reality check, ma’am. Don’t worry; he’ll live.”

Sigurd, who had been watching the entire scene with a mix of amusement and disbelief, cleared his throat. “Let’s go, Ada. We’ve got what we need.”

Gary continued to sob in his mother’s arms as Sigurd and Ada walked out of Gary's mansion, Ada muttering something about “some people just can’t handle constructive criticism.”

Sigurd chuckled.

“What?” she asked. “Someone had to put him in his place.”

Their next stop was an orphanage on the outskirts of the city. The caretakers greeted them warmly, though the elderly woman who oversaw the orphanage spoke with a mix of emotions.

“They came in the dead of night,” she began, her voice tinged with both gratitude and shame. “Dropped off sacks of gold and food. Said it was for the children. Who does that if they’re truly evil? But…” She paused, glancing at the children playing nearby. “It’s hard to admit we needed charity from thieves to survive. It makes me wonder what kind of world we live in that leaves us so desperate. Still, I can’t deny their help saved us.”

Ada glanced at the children, her usual snark softening. “Not every hero wears shining armor,” she muttered, earning a nod from the caretaker.

The children, overhearing the conversation, ran up eagerly to join in.

“They always wear masks!” one boy exclaimed. “The man’s in sparkling gentleman’s clothes, like a prince! And the lady’s dress is so fancy, like a princess!”

Another child piped up, giggling. “But they’re kinda clumsy. They keep dropping things! Last time, they spilled all the gold coins, and we had to help pick them up.”

A girl chimed in, covering her mouth as she giggled. “And they’re always bumping into each other, but then they start yelling about how much they love each other! It’s so awkward!”

Despite their laughter, the children’s eyes sparkled with admiration.

“They’re our saviors,” one of the older kids said firmly. “Without them, we wouldn’t have food or warm clothes. They’re the best people we’ve ever met.”

Sigurd nodded thoughtfully as he took in the descriptions and the stories, his resolve to understand the thieves growing stronger. Ada, meanwhile, smiled faintly. “Sounds like the-thief-that-steals-from-the-rich-to-give-the-poor trope is alive and well,” she said softly, her usual sarcasm laced with a hint of respect.

In the slums, several families revealed they had received help from the thieves. Gold to pay off crushing debts, food to stave off starvation, and even medicine for the sick.

“They wore masks,” one man said. “But they weren’t like the other nobles. They cared. They didn’t ask for anything in return.”

Sigurd’s jaw tightened. The thieves weren’t just thieves—they were trying to right the wrongs their victims had inflicted on others.

Ada crossed her arms, raising an eyebrow. “Think we’re chasing the wrong bad guys?”

“We’re chasing people who made reckless decisions,” Sigurd replied. “That doesn’t mean they’re beyond help.”

Using the information they had gathered, Sigurd pieced together the thieves’ movements. Their trail led to the desert, to a remote cave system hidden among towering rocky outcrops.

The journey was grueling. The sun blazed overhead, the sand reflecting the heat back in suffocating waves. Ada complained incessantly, her boots kicking up clouds of dust. “This is absurd. I’m not built for desert adventures. I’m a witch, not a camel.”

Sigurd glanced over his shoulder, raising an eyebrow. “Really? Because not too long ago, you came out here by yourself to mine ores, and I don’t recall you complaining about it then.”

Ada scoffed, waving him off dismissively. “Annoying details. That was completely different.”

Sigurd stopped walking, turning to face her. “Different? You woke up a dragon, Ada. That dragon nearly destroyed Grimroost.”

Ada’s ears twitched in irritation as she brushed past him. “Ugh, you’re so dramatic. It all worked out in the end, didn’t it? Besides, I saved your life during that mess, so you’re welcome.”

The mini-dragon chirped approvingly, waddling after her. Sigurd shook his head, muttering, “Unbelievable.”

The mini-dragon seemed unbothered, happily waddling through the sand and occasionally chirping at passing lizards.

When they finally reached the cave, Sigurd’s sharp eyes immediately spotted signs of habitation: footprints leading inside, discarded supplies, and a faint trail of smoke from a dying campfire.

“They’re here,” he said, drawing his weapon. Ada stayed close behind, her staff ready.

As they crept closer, voices echoed from within. The thieves were oblivious to their approach, lost in their own dramatic world.

“My darling Connie,” a man said, his voice dripping with theatrical passion. “If the stars themselves were stolen, I would craft a new sky just to see you smile.”

Connie swooned, clutching his hands. “Oh, Kyle! Your love is my light, my compass in this cruel, dark world!”

Kyle spun her in a dramatic flourish, narrowly missing a stack of crates and almost knocking over a lantern. Connie giggled as she steadied herself. “Oh, my love, your passion knows no bounds, but do try not to set us on fire!”

“Forgive me, my angel,” Kyle replied, his voice quivering with emotion. “Your beauty distracts me from even the most basic of tasks!”

The pair clasped hands again, gazing into each other’s eyes as if the world around them didn’t exist. Kyle attempted another poetic declaration but stumbled on his words, prompting Connie to finish the line for him with a dreamy sigh.

Ada’s muffled laughter nearly gave them away. She leaned toward Sigurd and whispered, “Are we sure they’re thieves? Because this feels more like a bad romance play.”

Sigurd shot her a glare but couldn’t help the small, exasperated smile creeping across his face. “Let’s end this,” he whispered, stepping into the cave to confront the star-crossed thieves.
