Don woke up and for a long moment didn’t move.

Not because he was tired. Quite the opposite. He felt… good.

That was great. Whatever kind of System bullshit made his sleep this sweet was worth all the pain and struggle he had to go through.

Every time he woke up he was rested, energized, happy. It was awesome.

Don stretched indulgently and rolled his shoulders. No strain, no pain, fresh as if he hadn’t spent the last days in constant fights.

“Damn,” he muttered, sitting up. “Could get used to this.”

He stood, stretched some more and glanced out the window of the Grimoid shack. Morning light didn’t exist down here, but something about the stillness made it feel early. Like the world hadn’t quite started yet. That liminal hour when everything is just waking up.

It made him think about mornings back on Earth. Before all this. Before the System.

Back then he used to go running in the mornings with Baxter. The good old boy had loved those runs. He’d been a competitive little bastard. Don felt a pang in his chest.

Anyway, the runs had been as good to Don as they had been for Baxter.

The runs were short at first. Then longer. Then harder. It had become a ritual. They’d gotten a lot faster and stronger. Baxter had looked like an absolute killer. The runs had certainly started pulling Don into shape as well.

Don used to like those morning jogs. Even when the runs got hard. Actually, thinking back, especially when they got hard.

But then his back had started acting up. They needed to dial down the intensity. It was no longer fun. Then it kind of just dropped off.

Like another candle snuffed out. Just like that life had been heavier again.

Don shook his head. Different life. Different man now. But some things carried over. And morning practice still felt right. He loved to have no excuse of his aching back anymore.

So he stepped outside and started his training.

The swamp was quiet. Still damp, still fetid, still dripping from somewhere you couldn’t quite place. But it didn’t stink quite as badly anymore. Or maybe Don was just used to it. Either way, it was fine.

He pulled two of the old Grimoid shields from the pile and focused.

Float. Orbit. Pull. Stop.

The shields lifted into the air and hovered.

This time it was different. Easier. Cleaner. His mind didn’t scramble to keep the paths steady. He didn’t feel like he was juggling glass plates on strings made of smoke.

He just… did it.

One shield circled left. The other right. Don walked forward slowly, then picked up speed. He jogged, then sprinted, then rolled and sprang upright again. The shields followed him like loyal dogs. They weren’t perfect, not yet. But they stayed afloat and under Don’s control.

He launched one into the sky, curved it like a boomerang, caught it again mid-flight and set it into orbit without blinking.

Don grinned.

It was coming together.

He reached out and picked up two spears, then a third, balancing all five objects in the air at once. The strain was there, a dull pressure behind his temples, but it wasn’t sharp. Just enough to know he was working.

Then he pushed it further.

He grabbed a chunk of stone rubble with his mind and added it to the orbit. That tipped the balance. The shields wobbled. The spears jerked. The stone resisted. But Don adjusted. Slowed everything down, redistributed force, aligned the pull.

Then he ramped the speed back up.

Everything snapped into motion.

Objects whirled around him, precise and controlled. A satellite field of weapons and debris spinning faster than any human hand could hope to manage. Don stood in the center, completely still, as wood and iron and stone traced invisible lines around him.

And then the message popped up.

[Racial Skill Increased: Telekinesis] [Telekinesis Level 7]

Don exhaled. A calm smile tugged at the corner of his mouth. “About time.”

He flicked the rubble toward a nearby shack and sent the spears flying like javelins in five different directions. Each one struck exactly where he aimed. Then he pulled the shields back into orbit, spun them in opposite directions, and accelerated them until they whined against the air.

He kept spinning the weapons in the air, getting a feel to his increase in level. He leveraged his Meta-Awareness, and really poured his focus into the Telekinesis, squeezing every ounce of leverage that the upgrade could give.

His control was sharper. He didn’t try to pull maximum velocity stunts, but he did try accelerating and slowing down the flying objects. It was much easier than before. Good. He would need every edge. But now it was time to move on. Don extended his arms and made all of the floating objects in the air stop.

Then he slammed them together.

The crack echoed across the swamp like a thunderclap.

Don dropped the pieces and stood there, breathing hard but steady.

That was enough of a warm up. No more stalling. It was time to prepare.

First thing Don did was use some Evolution points he had acquired from the bats.

[1000 Evolution Points spent]

[Evolution Upgraded!]

[Psychic sense (D-grade)] -> [Psychic sense (C-grade)]

[Psychic Sense (C-grade)]

[Psychic sense: Your pineal gland has activated and transformed into a third eye. You are now proficient in wielding psychic energy. You have acquired the ability to resist mental effects from enemies. Upgrading this Evolution increases the potency of all Psychic abilities. +10 Mental]

That was a hefty +4 boost to Mental. Not trivial for his psychics. He additionally spent the three free Attribute points from his last level all on Mental. He would really have to push his psychics this fight. His raw physical strength would be sapped the moment he stepped into that dark water.

Don returned to the shack he had slept in and checked his gear.

Cloak: secure, flexible, stealth boost still active. Good.

Necklace: mental clarity already doing work. He could feel it even now, how decisions came easier. His reactions felt cleaner. No hesitation. And boy had it helped him in the Telekinesis training…

Sonic Disruptor Bombs: three in total, still packed tight in the black case. He checked each fuse line. No cracks. Trigger wires solid. He didn’t know if these would be enough to kill Morshoff outright, but they’d do something. And that was all he needed.

Fire flask from the Grimoids:  the crystal flask was still in the satchel. The Oil of the Everburning Embers. Don wasn’t exactly sure how it worked, but he suspected it could start a really big fire, even in a wet swamp.

Don tested the rope slings on the bundles of spears. Two dozen left after training. He arranged them into shoulder rigs he could flick over one arm and grab with Telekinesis mid-combat. Not elegant. But it would do.

He picked up one of the remaining shields and strapped it across his back. The edge wasn’t sharp, but the thing was heavy and solid. Maybe it could work as a battering ram or last-ditch weapon.

That was his kit.

Now came the terrain.

Don crept up to the edge of the swamp and crouched low.

He focused his Echo Location. Let out a sharp whistle and listened.

Morshoff hadn’t moved. Still in the center of the bog. Still anchored to the bottom like some deepwater parasite. The stalk was long and flexible. The shell on its back was thick and slick. Those eel-like tendrils had some kind of awareness. They didn’t just flail. They hunted.

The good news? Don knew where it was.

The bad news? It knew he was here.

Don clicked his tongue and let the mental map settle.

He had to pull it out of the water. That was the entire plan. If Morshoff could retreat, he’d never kill it. So he had to engage hard, fast, with no mercy. Break the stalk. Use the bombs. Set the whole goddamn bog on fire if he had to.

Simple plan. High risk.

But if it worked? The rewards would be massive.

He’d take another boss trait. Another few hundred Evolution Points. Maybe more. Maybe it would unlock new powers.

And most importantly: Don would know. He would have the experience and the confidence that came from beating impossible odds.

Two wills would clash. Morshoffs will to consume him. And Don’s absolute, unyielding hunger for victory.

I’ll win.

Don stood and adjusted the cloak around his shoulders. He stepped on the edge of the wooden platform. Once he took this one step, his fate would be sealed. He would either die in this fight, or emerge as something he had not realized he could be.

But what happened after the fight didn’t matter.

What mattered was now.

And now, he was ready to kill a goddamn swamp monster.

He dropped down on the moist ground. The tentacles awoke immediately and surged at him. Don smiled.

It was showtime.
