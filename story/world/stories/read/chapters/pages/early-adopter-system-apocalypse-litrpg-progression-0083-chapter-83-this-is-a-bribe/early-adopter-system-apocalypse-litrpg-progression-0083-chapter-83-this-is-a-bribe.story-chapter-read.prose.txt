Just before Ms. Maggie turned to leave, Scarlet reached out with her telekinesis and gently stopped her.

“Ms. Margaret,” she began.

“Ms. Bourne,” Ms. Maggie’s eyes glinted in good humor.

“Before you leave, would you consider allowing us to use your venue for a meeting tomorrow morning?”

“Oh?” Ms. Maggie raised an eyebrow. “You want me to play host? And catering I presume.”

“Not at all. Well, if you’d be amenable to providing refreshments, I would be happy to supply ingredients for the meal and as compensation. However, if possible, I would like you to participate. I’d also appreciate if you could ensure the mayor and Forester are in attendance.”

“Would you now?”

“I would,” she confirmed.

Then, Scarlet held out her hand. She could feel the myriad reactions from around the table as an obviously magical book materialised on her palm. A brief flicker of shock passed through Ms. Maggie’s aura before curiosity smothered it.

“What’s this, now?” Ms. Maggie asked.

Scarlet’s lips turned up in a smile as Ms. Maggie reached out and gently took the book from her still outstretched hand.

[Introduction to Enhanced Cooking: Volume 1]

It was one of the few Knowledge Tomes Scarlet had purchased from the System Store. She had a few mundane-book version of the Tome as well, however, she saw the Tome as an investment, and a favour more than repaid.

Even in the unlikely event that Ms. Maggie decided not to join her faction, the book would still be put to good use.

“This is a bribe,” Scarlet responded. Her smile was sweet and her face radiated transparently affected sincerity.

Ms. Maggie barked out a laugh before nodding and raising the book in a mock salute.

“I’ll see you in the morning, then. Come an hour before we open with what you want cooked. We can meet in the private room after the morning rush,” Ms. Maggie said.

Scarlet was honestly less surprised than she should have been that Ms. Maggie was still running her diner in the middle of the apocalypse.

“And you’ll bring—”

“I’m sure I can persuade the boys to make themselves available for the meeting,” she said before finally heading back to the kitchen, Tome in hand.

The rest of the meal proceeded in tense silence, with Dani’s eyes constantly bouncing between people. They landed most often on Scarlet and Maus, however, occasionally Myka and Kain would get caught in the crossfire.

The rest of the table wasn’t much better. It was obvious several of them wanted to say something but either couldn’t or wouldn’t.

Finally, Raj spoke up.

“How have those knives been treating you?” she asked.

“They saved my life more than once in the beginning. Truly.” Scarlet turned to face Raj and nodded in genuine appreciation, her expression settling into something relaxed and gentle.

“I didn’t know you two were so close,” Kain said.

There was an incredibly faint undercurrent to his tone that Scarlet only barely picked up on. It was enough to pique her interest.

With his words, something more fluctuated in the air around the table. Scarlet felt mana brush against her, the result of an active Skill. She doubted any of the Tier 0s around the table had the Perception necessary to detect it. However, with Myka sitting so close, Scarlet didn’t even have to rely on her innate defences. His Trait passively dispelled the effect.

Scarlet had noticed his Skill ‘Rapport’ back on the road when she’d scanned the group. She’d assumed it was some type of social Skill, but with no passive effect she’d been able to detect, she’d reasones it was an Active Skill. She’d also figured she’d catch it if Kain tried to use it.

Apparently, she’d been right.

Scarlet pulled Kain’s Skill list back up.

Rapport was the most likely culprit.

She’d have to observe the effects without Myka’s unintentional intervention. That, or get a better investigative Skill.

Kain’s eyes widened before narrowing. Then he shook his head and went back to his food.

She didn’t fault him. It truly was delicious.

“I thought my brother would have some trouble finding you,” Scarlet said. Raj bobbed her head in response.

“It was such a strange coincidence. Apparently, my shop’s only about a block away from his house. I usually stay in the apartment above it. Kain and I actually ended up meeting before we even knew you had reached out.”

Scarlet nodded.

It really wasn’t a coincidence. She’d seen the shop’s address on Raj’s business card when they’d met at the convention. She simply hadn’t known the two would meet organically before she’d sent the request to her brother.

Not seeing it necessary to mention any of that, however, Scarlet simply nodded along as they conversed.

“It is good to see you well.”

Then her eyes went distant, her smile faltering as she spoke. “Actually, I was wondering if it would be possible to go check on Dadaji.” Raj laughed, but it was obviously forced. “I haven’t heard from him in a while. Not with,” she made a vague, all-encompassing gesture in the air before dropping her hand back onto her lap. “Where is he? I remember you saying he lived in the Interior.”

“He’s about an hour northeast from here, just outside of Rockmore.”

“That would make sense,” Scarlet mused. “If I’m not mistaken, it’s an old mining town?”

Raj’s lips tilted up. “Yeah. He has a house there. The forge is there too. I know there’s a rail-line that goes up that way but–”

“You don’t need to explain it to me,” Scarlet said before her gaze turned to Kain. “I’m sure you can spare an appropriate vehicle. My own truck is inappropriate for the post-Integration terrain.” She didn’t wait for a response. “If you head off tomorrow morning before the meeting, you can be back by the time all the tedium has been concluded.”

Kain’s lips pursed minutely as his eyes remained on Scarlet. She could see the muscle in his temple shift as his jaw clenched and unclenched. His expression would have appeared thoughtful to anyone who couldn’t recognise the tension. However, to Scarlet the tells were loud. She didn’t need super Perception to see what she’d spent years learning to look out for.

“That’s fine,” Kain said, turning to face Raj. To his credit, the smile he gave her was genuine. “We’ve got a few off-roaders in the garage. I can take you to pick one in the morning. Sombra?” He turned to face the man.

“I can go with them,” Dani said, her fingers tracing idle patterns on the surface of a recently cleaned Skillit.

Two pairs of eyes pinned her to her seat. One ringed in violet, the other a pale blue. Simultaneously, the look broke as the siblings met each other’s gaze, a silent conversation passing in a moment. Then Scarlet glanced away, sipping at the now tepid cup of tea Ms. Maggie had been kind enough to bring over. Kain turned back to Dani.

“You’ll be smart,” he said. It was a statement, not a question.

Dani grinned at the acknowledgement while Scarlet drained the last of her tea and pulled her gloves back onto her hands.
