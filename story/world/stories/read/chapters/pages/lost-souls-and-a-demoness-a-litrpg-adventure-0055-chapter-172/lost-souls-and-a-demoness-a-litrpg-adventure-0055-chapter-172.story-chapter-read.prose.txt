Unlike the transport vessel, the stealth ship looked almost exactly like Jade expected it to. She, Tara, and Seris strode purposefully across the hangar towards the craft in question, doing their best to act like they belonged. It helped that the latter two, both members of the expeditionary taskforce funding this research center, were perfectly authorized to be here. Only Jade was not, as the privilege granted to her by virtue of being one of Tara's team members did not extend to a free pass to wander through military testing labs. For that reason, Jade was currently disguised as one of Seris's colleagues, a female tek'kalla with light violet skin and short lavender hair. Her shape shifting, combined with one of Seris's spare flight suits and a hastily modified nametag, had been enough to get her through the door. Now they just had to hope that none of the technicians took too close a look.

"I have to admit, Seris." Tara commented, looking the aircraft up and down as they approached. "That's a nice-looking ship."

"Tara? Saying something positive about a magitech device? I never thought I'd see the day.""Oh shut up, I’m not that bad. I'm not!" Tara huffed, scowling at her friend's skeptical expression. "I've never had a problem with magitech."

"Right, it was never a problem."

"Can we talk about this later?" Jade hissed, eyeing a nearby group of technicians. They were working on a device that looked to Jade like a jet-engine studded with glowing gemstones. One of them glanced their way as they passed. "We don't want to draw attention."

The two tek'kalla fell silent, perhaps remembering what they were here to do. Jade, as the uninvited guest, was acutely aware of the risk. At any moment she expected to trip some kind of alarm or sensor that would reveal her nature as a demon and send the entire plan crashing into ruins. Her upgraded shapeshifting ability should render her disguise immune to most forms of magical detection, but 'most' wasn't the same thing as 'all'.

Despite her fears, they reached the ship without incident. The vessel was 'U' shaped, with the crew compartment in the middle and two wings that swept forward like the points of a trident. The bright purple color of the composite exterior didn't quite fit her idea of "stealthy", but she'd seen enough tek'kalla technology by this point to reserve judgement. Two ground crew stood together at a workbench nearby, gesturing at a set of schematics between them. Seris approached, with Jade and Tara trailing just behind.

"Fraya, Junli, would you mind getting her prepped for a shakedown flight?" The pilot asked calmly. Jade was impressed. She sensed the frayed nerves hidden behind his friendly expression, but none of it showed. She wasn't convinced that she was doing as good a job of looking at ease.

"Seris?" One of them, the man, asked. He frowned, walking over to greet them. "I thought the shakedown flight was scheduled for next week."

"The deck officer bumped it up. Said she wants the Phantom to be ready to help with the demon incursion."

"Ah, that makes sense." He said, tapping a few times on a crystal tablet on the workbench. "I don't see it on the schedule, though."

"We were just talking about it." Seris explained, and Jade thought she heard a note of strain in his voice. Okay, maybe not quite so unflappable after all. "The system probably hasn't updated yet."

"Well, I can't get her released for takeoff before everything is signed off." He said with a shake of his head. "Sorry, Seris. I can call her, if you like."

"No, no." Jade said, holding up a hand to stop him. It was time for plan B. "We shouldn't bother her over something like this. A war could be starting out there, and we just want to be ready to help." Jade infused the words with magic, feeling them sink into his mind as the tek'kalla met her eyes. "You'll help us with that, right?" She finished sweetly.

The man nodded slowly as the enchantment took hold, but Jade wasn't done yet. They hadn't counted on two techs being present when coming up with the fallback plan. At Allure's current level, Jade could only charm groups when they were tightly packed together. When enhanced by her succubus form, the A-rank spell's effective radius was much more forgiving. She was disguised as a tek'kalla, though, and its power was limited to B rank. The other woman was too far away to be effected by it and judging by the strange look she was giving them, the remaining tek'kalla was beginning to realize that something was off.

Fortunately, Jade had a workaround.

Ability (Glamourblade): Everlasting Appeal

Effect: When a charm spell successfully affects a target, the spell doesn't go on cooldown.

In the past, Allure's cooldown had prevented Jade from being able to chain-cast it. But with the power of her advanced class at her disposal, that was no longer a problem. Even then, her glamourblade ability wouldn't have been enough to save them if not for another ability she'd picked up along the way:

Ability: Enduring Temptation

Effect: Solidify the links between yourself and creatures you have charmed. While a creature is under the effect of one of your enchantments you may touch them to transform the duration of the condition to 'until dispelled'. Doing so requires you to commit a portion of your maximum mana to maintaining the enchantment proportional to the creature's power. This mana will be unavailable to you while you maintain this bond.

Normally, casting Allure while a previous cast's charm was still in effect ended the original spell. Enduring Temptation allowed Jade to sidestep that restriction and maintain as many charm effects as she had the mana to power. She activated the ability, linking the charm spell to her mana pool. Fortunately, the technician's level was low enough that the cost of maintaining it only ate up about ten percent of Jade's mana bar. That allowed her to switch her attention to the woman, who was just beginning to speak.

"Hey, what's going on he--"

"Everything's alright." Jade said smoothly, meeting her gaze with a friendly smile. "Just help your friend get us ready to leave, and don't talk about this to anyone else."

The woman hesitated, her eyes glazing over. Finally, she nodded.

"Right… Right, the test flight." She looked back at Seris. "We'll get right on that, Pilot Seris."

The two technicians walked off without another word, beginning the process of making the airship flight ready, and Jade breathed a soft sigh of relief. One crisis averted.

"Let's get inside." Seris said, his expression unreadable. "We'll be ready to go soon. Looks like they already had her on standby."

A few minutes later, Jade sat beside Seris in the Phantom's copilot’s seat. Despite the ship's large size, it was at least forty feet from nose to tail and almost as wide, the crew compartment was relatively cramped. Other than the two seats in front of the flight controls there were a handful of jump seats in the rear compartment, plus a small storage locker filled with emergency supplies. Jade judged that they'd have just enough room for everyone to be aboard, so long as nobody minded a lack of personal space.

"Almost ready." Seris said, flipping a switch on the main console as another status light blinked from amber to blue. "Once they disconnect the charging cables, we'll be ready for takeoff."

Jade nodded, eyeing the confusing array of lights and displays. She was useless here, and the feeling made her anxious.

"Doesn't it bother you?"

This wasn't the time to have this conversation, but Jade couldn't help it. If she and her friends would be entrusting their lives to this man's skills, she needed to clear the air between them.

"Doesn't what bother me?"

"My charm magic. How I used it on the ground crew."

Seris frowned at her.

"That was the backup plan, wasn't it?"

Jade didn't sense any animosity from him. For some reason, that only made her anxiety worse.

"Most people react differently when they see the kind of enchantments I can cast. They worry I'll do it to them."

"Ah." Seris nodded and then flipped two more switches. A subsonic hum thrummed through the deck, felt more than heard. "I understand. But no, it doesn't bother me. As far as I can tell, you haven't used it for anything bad. Tara'lynn trusts you, and she wouldn't if you hadn't earned it."

"You trust her that much?"

"She's always had a strong sense of justice. It got her into trouble in the academy, but it wouldn't lead her astray here. Besides, I know what compulsion spells feel like, and I'm not under the effect of one."

Before Jade could think of a reply, Tara ducked her head into the cockpit.

"Um, are we ready to go? Because it looks like we've caught the attention of the deck officer."

Jade craned her neck, looking back through the canopy. A trio of uniformed tek'kalla were hurrying across the hangar in their direction. She doubted it was to approve their flight plan.

"Seris?"

"Hang on…"

Another indicator blinked blue. The pilot threw two more switches and then frowned. He went back to the first one, flipping it repeatedly back and forth while the indicator above it stubbornly remained amber.

Flick, flick, flick.

"Screw it." He let it be, waving the still-charmed ground crew away through the transparent canopy. "We don't need that one."

"Don't need what?" Jade asked, giving the unlabeled switch a suspicious look.

"Ahh… don't worry about it."

That wasn't particularly reassuring, but they were committed now. Jade could only watch as Tara shut the rear hatch and Seris gently tugged back on the throttle, lifting the craft into the air.

"Strap in."

They coasted forward, angling towards the wide-open mouth of the hangar and the clear blue sky beyond. Jade thought she could hear muffled shouts from behind them, and she half expected spells or weapons-fire to strike the craft, but none did.

Best to be on their way before their luck changed.

"Let's get out of here. Luis and the others should be ready for pickup by now."

"Here we go!"

With a burst of acceleration that jammed Jade back in her seat, they shot from hangar and into the open sky. There was a flash of cityscape beneath them, and then they were out past the edge of the enormous city ship, the distant landscape of the Labyrinth stretching to the horizon.

"Are they coming after us?" Tara asked, poking her head around the cockpit door. "Did we make it?"

"Nothing yet. Let's engage stealth before that changes."

Seris maintained course, and they cut through the twilit sky like a shooting star. A familiar sense of wonder and freedom welled up within Jade, and she had to resist the urge to spread her wings and take to the sky herself. The daydream abruptly ended when Seris cursed beside her.

"What is it?" she asked, regaining her senses with a jolt of alarm. She shifted back to her human form as she spoke, the familiar body helping to provide a sense of stability.

"We might have a small problem here…"

"Define small." Said Tara.

"Define problem." Jade added.

Seris keyed in a command, and a message flashed on the center console. It took a moment for the Labyrinth's translation system to kick in, shifting the characters into something Jade could understand.

"Stealth unavailable, please wait?!" Jade exclaimed. "That sounds like a big problem."

"The capacitors are still charging." Seris explained, cycling the screen through various status displays. Although he sounded strained, the pilot's actions were calm and deliberate. "Damn it, we haven't tested the stealth function so quickly after liftoff. It'll probably take a few minutes."

"Do we have a few minutes?" Tara asked. "They're not going to just let us fly away."

Seris adjusted the throttle, and Jade felt the craft accelerate. He was trying to put distance between them and the city ship. Her mind quickly cycled through ideas, but she didn't know how any of this technology worked.

"Is there any way we can charge it faster?" Jade asked.

"No, we… wait." Seris shook his head. "Tara! How's your mana supply?"

"I'm a C-rank mage, I've got plenty!"

"Great! See that hatch? No, that one. Yes! Open it and start pouring everything you can into the mana batteries!"

"What's that light?" Jade asked, pointing to a red blinking light on the center console.

"Communications. They're hailing us." Seris said distractedly, still working on the main display while keeping one eye on the flight instruments. He scooped up a headset and thrust it into Jade's arms. "Get on there and stall them."

"What?!""Just, do your best!"

"Like this?" Tara called from the passenger compartment. There was a metallic clatter followed by what sounded like rustling cables. "Am I doing it right? Oh! That tingles."

"Yes! Keep it up!"

Jade swore under her breath and donned the headset. It took her a moment to find and adjust the volume controls. Soon after, a voice crackled through the earpiece.

"-unscheduled launch. Repeat, Phantom, you do not have a flight path on file. Return to base immediately."

"Sorry, uh, negative." Jade said, holding down the transmit button. Being put on the spot like this was making her mind completely blank out. "We have a slight… thrust malfunction. Nothing bad, but we'll need a minute to get it fixed."

"What happened, Phantom? Who gave you launch authorization?"

"Just a, um, mana capacitor overload. We're working on bypassing it." Jade had no idea if what she was saying made any sense and didn't particularly care. "But everything will be fine, we're all fine here, ahh… how are you?"

She cringed. So much for sounding natural.

"Who is this? What's your callsign?"

Jade clicked off the headset and groaned, ending the transmission.

"Please tell me you're ready."

"Almost there…"

"Seris, I see something back behind us!" Tara called from behind them. "I think they're sending another airship after us!"

"Annnnd, got it!"

Seris flipped two more switches and then clicked a button. The cabin lights turned blue and, to Jade's amazement, the front prongs of the airship she could see from the canopy flickered once, and then completely vanished. Seris banked as they continued to coast, changing their heading away from where they'd last been seen.

"There." He sighed, leaning back heavily in his chair. "We're clear."

Jade let out a sigh of relief.

"Let's never do that again."
