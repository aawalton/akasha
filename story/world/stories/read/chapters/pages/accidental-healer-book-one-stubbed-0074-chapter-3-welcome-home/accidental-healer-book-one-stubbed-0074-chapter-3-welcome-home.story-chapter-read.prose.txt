It wasn’t hard to find my parents. The streets of New Boise were wide and mostly empty. Following the celebaration of victory many of the people just wanted to be with their families. How could I blame them?

Seeing my parents from above, dad waving his hands around in grand gestures, I couldn’t help crack a grin. He’d no doubt be sharing all his grand plans in excruciating detail to Jared, already planning ways to put our faction to work helping those dreams come to life.

Well technically we were going to be in the same faction now.

My feet scraped the dirt, and I took three quick steps to absorb my landing. All three turned at once. My mom beamed, hugging me for what must’ve been the hundredth time since I entered through the portal.

“Back already?” Dad asked.

“Yep, back already.” I glanced at Jared. “Didn’t you see the notification?”

Jared chuckled. “I haven’t opened it yet. We’ve been busy here.”

“Well, the Gree-Cek are gone.” I shrugged. “I placed portal in the city, and I still have one more, I figured we could use it in the old Tannerite city, if anyone decides that they still want to settle there.”

Saying it didn’t feel great. I knew that Boretek had to go but I didn’t have to like it.

Martin shook his head.

“I still can’t believe you’re here.”

By now my mom had released me from her clutches and was just looking at me like I might float off if she looked away.

“Honestly, I still can’t even believe I’m still here.” His eyes took a far-off look.

Jared smiled warmly. “Why don’t we reconvene tomorrow, Martin?”

“That sounds great.” Martin nodded appreciatively.

My mom hugged Jared. “Jared, thank you so much for taking care of our little boy.”

Jared scoffed. “It was more like he took care of us. You should be very proud of your son.”

“Alllrigghht, that’s enough of that.” I said grabbing my my moms shoulders and directing her away. “Oh, before I forget. Can you get an objective set up for Damon with this as the reward?”

A hammer dropped into my hand with a silver head mounted on richly marbled red wood. In the head were several intricate runes that looked burned into the metal.

“It’s called temperance. I’m fairly sure its some kind of blacksmithing hammer.”

Jared took it, and it vanished to his storage.

“Consider it done.” Jared took it, and it vanished to his storage. He waved. “I’ll see you all in the morning.”

Together we watched him disappear. My dad cleared his throat.

“Well, should we give you a quick tour?”

The sun was just about to dip away behind the horizon, darkness already taking hold on the city. With enhanced stats, darkness wouldn’t be a problem. Only, I wasn’t in the mood.

I was still decompressing. Earlier this morning I was still in the scourge, fought three chaos spawn that had no business being there, killed a warlord, saved two factions, acquired three more, added hundreds of thousands to my own, found out my best friend was a traitor to his kind and honestly...I was tired.

“Dad, I can’t wait to see everything, but how would you feel if we just sat down for a little while?”

He must’ve finally realized just how exhausted I felt and he wrapped an arm around me.

“You know, that actually sounds pretty good.”

Together my parents guided me to the small log cabin that they’d been calling home. A path of stone led from the dirt streets to the rustic door, bordered by well-maintained dark green grass that looked as though it was recently trimmed.

I shot my dad a look that said “are you serious?”

He grinned sheepishly.

“Did you mow your lawn yesterday?” I asked accusatorily.

“He did.” Mom said flatly.

“It helps me think!”

“I thought lawnmowers were part of technology, how did you even do it?” I wondered aloud.

Mom groaned.

“You had to ask..”

Dad beamed and before I could walk my question back, an old rotary mower appeared.

“I had it specially made by one of our crafters. It’s uncommon grade.” He informed me proudly, hands on hips. “It cuts better than my old mower and I don't even need to bag the grass, this little darlin gobbles it right up.”

“Let me get this straight.” I said slowly. “You were convinced you and your entire faction was about to be swept from the face of the earth, and you still found the time to mow your lawn?”

He lifted his hat and scratched his head.

“Huh...”

I smacked him on the shoulder. “I’m glad to know the apocalypse hasn’t changed you.”

If there was one thing that my dad always stressed to me growing up, it was that you could usually tell a measure of a man by the way he kept his yard. And it was necessarily the beaty either.

It was attention to detail, the care and effort that mattered. A good man demonstrated care to that which was in his stewardship. It showed in the humble space that him and my mother called home.

“Who’s that?”

Martin pointed over my shoulder.

I looked, already suspecting who it might be. Waiting a few cabins down in the fading twilight was Norso, spear out resting at his shoulder.

“That would be Norso.”

“He was in the fight with you against Boretek’s wizards, I remember him.”

I nodded. “Yeah. You might as well get used to seeing him, he’s basically my shadow.”

“Does he always seem so angry?” Mom asked in a whisper.

“He’s had a troubled past. Why don’t we go inside?”

She watched him with sad eyes.

“You want to just leave him out here?” She asked and before I could stop her, she was already waving. “Norso? We are about to head inside, why don’t you come with?”

The dark elf looked up sharply, his eyes passing between Claire and me.

My parents and I hadn’t seen each other in months. Most of that time was spent wondering if the other was still alive. Now, after all that time, we would finally have a night to our own where we could wind down and enjoy the reunion just the three of us and my mom invites Norso.

For just a heartbeat there was a swelling of frustration in my chest for the invited intrusion, but a glance toward my dad was all I needed to shove the feeling right back where it belonged.

His eyes were fixed to his wife of over twenty years. You’d have to be blind to miss the admiration behind those eyes.

He knew just the kind of woman my mother was. She was the kind who didn’t care about the imposition. Her doors were always open. It didn’t matter who it was or when it was.

Knowing that any resistance was futile anyway, I waved a gesturing hand toward Norso.

“You might as well.” I shrugged resigned.

Norso’s eyes moved between the three of us, but he didn’t budge.

“I...uhhh...don’t want to impose.” He grunted awkwardly.

“Oh please.” Mom chirped. “Let’s all head in.”

Norso and I shared a look and before any of us could argue she was already through the door. The scent of something freshly baked wafted into the night from somewhere inside.

“Cookies?” I asked excitedly.

Dad smiled, and we all followed.

My nose hadn’t deceived me. A pile of what looked like oatmeal cookies was situated in the center of a small table in the center of the room.

There was a small desk in the corner and a simple partition of cloth separating the sleeping area.

It was as basic as any dwelling could be.

“I take it our old house didn’t survive the transition?” I wondered aloud.

“Oh no honey, it did. We can go see it tomorrow if you want to.”

I cocked my head confused. “So why are you living here?”

“Well, it just felt a little crowded after your dad invited twenty other people to live there.”

I turned to my dad for an explanation.

“They’re refugees. And now that we have more manpower, we should be able to get more homes put up.”

Hearing that my parents turned their forever home into a refugee camp didn’t surprise me one bit.

I recalled walking into Jordan’s faction and finding his people living in basic slums, while he posted up in the nicest home there. That just wasn’t my parents.

“I think this new place is nicer anyways.” I said cheerfully, taking a seat around the table and snagging a cookie. “It’s less to clean.”

It wasn’t like I thought everyone had to have a bigger house than my parents did. It just felt good to know that they put others first.

“SOOOO...” I said loudly, taking a bite of the cookie. It was delicious. “That was quite the note you sent me dad. A bit dramatic don’t you think?”

He palmed his face.

“I only had five minutes, OK? I thought I was saving your life at the time.”

Claire folded her arms.

“How was your dad supposed to know you were waiting on the other side of that portal with an army of super soldiers?”

“Super soldiers?” I asked while munching on a mouthful of cookie. “I don’t know about that...”

My faction had come a long way, but it was hard to erase the image of when Jared and his people first found me in the clearing all those months ago.

“Layton.” Martin said, lowering his tone, getting serious. “Boretek and his faction wiped out entire cities of people. He would’ve done the same to us. Until today, I wasn’t even sure he could be stopped.”

I recalled my fight with the four-armed faction leader. Boretek was a decently high level, but he wasn’t much stronger than some of the regular bruisers in the scourge trial.

“I guess he was pretty strong.”

“You guess?” He said, grabbing his own cookie absentmindedly. He broke a piece off and shoved it into his mouth. My dad turned his attention to Norso. “Do you think you could’ve taken him?”

Norso’s head shot up from staring holes in the cookies on the table, surprised to be included in the conversation. I shifted my body to face the dark elf, feeling curious about what he’d say.

He wasn’t quite level 50, still needing a few more levels last I checked.

“Not with the spellcasters supporting him, no.”

“And if it was one on one?”

Norso didn’t answer right away. His attention drifted back to the cookies at the center of the table.

“Yes, I believe so.”

“Oh, for heavens sakes!” My mom blurted out, shoving the plate across the table. Norso jumped at the outburst but finally gave in a took his own cookie.

My dad continued unfazed. “Where would you say you rank in total strength within your faction?”

Norso closed his eyes as if he was actually counting the number of people who he considered stronger than himself in the faction.

“Maybe within the top thousand?”

“Within the top thousand.” Martin muttered. “So, you’re saying, there are a thousand more fighters at least as strong as Boretek?”

I could see where my dad’s mind was heading, but I really didn’t think it was worth using Boretek as the litmus test.

“And that panther, Mischief...he called you his brother?” My dad visibly shivered.

“He’s technically a mountain lion.” I corrected.

“Right, ok, mountain lion. Why did I get the feeling that if Mischief wanted, he could’ve handled the entire Gree-Cek on his own?”

“He most certainly could.” Norso said, bits of cookie tumbling from his scruff. “As could Layton.”

Both my parents fixed me with questions in their eyes. The intensity of their stares made me shift uncomfortably.

“Well son. I think I’ve done enough interrogating, it's story time.”
