I felt the crack through my hands. The warmth of the shell I was used to. The movement, I was not. The movement came as a pressure from the inside of the egg, pushing outward. A line split the gold from one side nearly to the other. I did not breathe. Whatever exhaustion I had felt cracked as soon as the egg had.

Then there was a second crack, one that rose up and joined the first. The shell where the two met shook, just faintly.

"Ash," I said quickly. I could hardly recognize the sound of my own voice. "Ash."

Frantic footsteps came, and then Ash was beside me, kneeling on the floor next to the bed. "It's happening?"

"Yes."

More cracks joined the first two. Each new line let more light through. Then pieces of the shell began to fall away, one after the other. The creature inside pushed, and the egg came apart in my hands. And I could see the beast that had been inside all this time.

It was small. That was the first thought. It fit in my cupped palms with room to spare. It was wet, its feathers slick against a body so fragile I was afraid to close my fingers.

And the creature had four wings. Two of them were gold. Two of them were black. The golden wings were bright, even beneath the dampness. The black ones were so dark they seemed to drink in the light. My heart sank. Four wings. I had feared this might happen. That the beast inside would come out wrong just like its predecessor had. And that I would then have to-

I paused. The four wings were not wrong. Not bending in ways that made no sense. These were perfectly aligned, as if they had always been meant to come in fours.

Its body was a soft gold, the exact color most of the shell had been. It had two eyes, round and enormous for its size, and they were open. They found me.

The tiny creature looked up at me, and I looked down at it. It did not move. I barely breathed. Its golden eyes had dark pupils, the same color as its feathers. Almost the same color as the mark on my right arm. Those big eyes held my gaze steadily. Very steadily.

I slowly raised it to my face. My hands were steady. I had to make them so. It was perhaps one of the hardest things I had ever done. The creature's head tilted abruptly to one side, and one of its wings twitched. Several drops of fluid fell onto my palms. "Hello," I said.

Those big golden eyes of hers regarded me for several long moments. Then her head straightened and tilted upwards, towards the ceiling. She chirped. The sound was small and thoroughly indignant. The bird was offended to have been kept waiting for so long. I was sure of it. "A commanding voice. That will serve you well," I said. Ash laughed beside me.

Soon, the creature dried. Its wings twitched, and each time drops of fluid fell onto my palms, my arms, even my face. I let them. As it dried, its wet feathers fluffed outwards. I went from holding something wet and fragile to holding something that was absurdly soft.

The tiny phoenix sat in my palms and surveyed the room. Her head turned in quick, almost abrupt movements. Left, right, up, down. Each movement was followed by a chirp. I believed she was cataloguing her surroundings and finding them lacking. "She surveys her domain," I said. "As is proper. It is my shame as a Queen to have not prepared something better for her arrival." She was a she. I felt that, somehow, through whatever mental prickling we now shared.

"She's looking at the lantern," Ash said.

"She is surveying her domain," I corrected her. Her mistake was foolish. This proud creature would not do something so simple as stare at a mere lantern. Yes, there was a greater purpose to it. To everything she did.

The phoenix let out another chirp and turned its attention to me. Specifically, to my left horn. She regarded my horn with intense focus. I brought her closer to my head, more out of curiosity than anything else.

The creature regarded my horn, before she reached out towards it with one of her talons. It gripped my horn near its end, where it was more narrow. There was no pain. I hardly even felt her. Still, the indignity of this was considerable. Many would have been turned to dust for daring to do a tenth of what this baby phoenix had done. I decided to allow it. She was a new subject, and so she did not know her place. She would learn, in time.

Ash was watching with an expression I refused to look at directly. "Not a word," I said.

"I wasn't going to say anything."

"Your face is saying things. Tell it to stop." I lowered my hands.

It took some time for the phoenix to release my horn. Why she had been so fascinated with it at all, not even the Gods could have said. When she did, she made her way down my arms. She moved in short, uncertain hops, her four wings spreading for balance with each step. She moved down my left arm first, towards the green Line that hid the Cradle. The tiny creature regarded the green Line and pressed herself against it immediately. Then she let out a soft and high sound that she never had before. A trill. She folded her legs entirely and sat on my mark, and for a moment I worried she might just fall asleep there. That would certainly be…inconvenient. Another liberty I might permit, for now.

Instead she rose and moved on. She hopped toward my right arm, toward the red Line that hid the Requiem. She slowed as she approached, and her head tilted far to one side. She was showing hesitation. She had shown none of it for the Cradle. Perhaps she knew what the mark really was. The same one that had slain her predecessor.

I was very still. Of course she was afraid. My chest was tight. There was no reason for it to be. This was a feeling I had no name for, though it sat close to the place where sadness lived. Then the tiny bird hopped forward and closed the distance. She pressed herself against the red Line just as she had with the green, and she let out another trill. One no different from the one she had made last. She folded herself and sat on the line.

"Foolish creature," I said in a whisper. My eyes might have blurred. I used my left hand to wipe them dry.

From there, the naming began as a matter of necessity. I did not wish to call this thing beast, and minion was a poorer moniker still. "Pyratheon," I said. "Pyratheon. It is a name befitting one born from divine fire. It carries weight, and-"

"No."

"You did not even consider it."

"I did not need to." Ash sat cross-legged on the edge of our bed. She was enjoying this far too much, judging by the look on her face. "It's the size of your hand. You are not naming that golden bread loaf Pyratheon."

"It will grow!" I protested. Perhaps the name was ill-fitting now, but it would not be forever.

"Then name it that when she does."

I held the phoenix up. It chirped and pecked at my thumb. "Very well. I shall name her Obsidian: Requiem of the Eternal Dawn." Ash stared at me. "It captures both aspects of her nature," I explained.

"That is six words, Lys…and why are you giving her a title?!"

"It is one word. I will not say her full name all the time."

"Still no."

I thought of two more names. Proud names. The first was Ashara Nox, the kind of name that belonged to a creature with two wings the color of the night. The next was Moltress Duskwing, another even more proud name. Either one would have been apt. Ash rejected both. As if this insufferable woman had any right over what I named my own familiar. For some strange reason, I reluctantly obliged. I did not want to pick a name she would not approve of.

"You should go with something simple," Ash said. "Something simple enough that you can say it quickly."

Ash wanted a simple name? This baby chick did not deserve simple. I looked at her. She was an entity of gold and black. One whom I had made from a beast considered myth even to me. A hatchling now bound to the strongest mortal that had ever lived. Simple? Such a name would not fit. Or perhaps…perhaps there was a certain elegance in simplicity. That brought its own issue. How was I to come up with a simple name?

I thought about it for a time. I came up with many names, and none of them were simple. All of which Ash would have rejected if I voiced them. She watched me, and the baby phoenix watched me too, her tiny head tilted very far to one side. Finally, I had one. A name even this fool Hero should find no objection with. "Solara," I said.

Ash considered it without dismissing it immediately. It seemed the name had been simple enough after all. Even the phoenix went still, as if listening. "Solara, huh? That works. I like that one."

"Solara," I said again, feeling the taste of it on my tongue. The phoenix chirped when I said it. "That is her full name. To be used at all times."

"Sol," Ash said.

My eye twitched. "That is not-" The phoenix chirped at 'Sol'. Loudly and with great enthusiasm. "…Solara," I insisted. Sol chirped again. My new subject was already a traitor.

Time passed, and eventually Sol found herself perched on my right knee. She was attempting her first flight. I knew this because she had been preparing for it for the better part of five minutes. Her four wings were moving in increasingly quick bursts. And she had reared herself up, making herself more angular. I was paying more attention to this display than I ever had to a military exercise. Even this preparation of hers with her wings beating as they did was mechanically impressive for a creature barely an hour old.

"She will be a formidable flier, one day. She might fly to all the heavens with just her wings," I observed. Ash did not answer me. She was watching Sol with almost the same amount of concentration I was.

The wings beat a few more times, with more vigor than they had before. She craned her neck, let out a small chirp, and launched herself. Her four wings all flared wide, the gold and the black. The gold ones shone in the light. Somehow, I was certain the baby phoenix aimed for my head. It was a lofty goal, one she had no hope of reaching. She managed to fly only perhaps a dozen inches before gravity reclaimed her and she fell.

The phoenix hit my chest face first, and then fell onto my thigh. Her wings crumpled beneath her and a muffled chirp came from somewhere under the pile of feathers. My heart stopped.

I reached for her with both hands, already channeling the Cradle, already preparing to mend whatever damage the fall had-

Sol popped up. Her feathers were askew, one of her black wings was sticking out at an angle, and she was chirping furiously. She shook herself, spread her wings again, and launched. She made it two inches this time.

She faceplanted again, and marked this with another furious chirp. She got up and tried again. My hands were trembling.

The baby bird tried a fourth time, and then a fifth. None of these attempts went any better than the last few had. Each time the bird fell, she immediately rose, and chirped loudly. There was a queen's indignity in her voice. I could not watch this any longer.

I reached down and lifted her gently, before setting her on top of my head, directly between my horns. Small talons found purchase on my hair, and I felt Sol sit herself down. I looked up, and saw her feathers puffed out in what was surely satisfaction. "You will be a proud steed one day. But for now, accept my assistance."

Sol chirped from atop my head. This time, it sounded almost regal. "That," I said to Ash, "was a roar."

"Of course it was." Ash had perhaps the widest grin I had ever seen on her.

Sol was very warm atop my head. I supposed there was no greater throne in all the world than atop my crown.

It had started to run late. Well, later than it already was, after what we had done for the boy.

I took Solara from my head and set her on the pillow. She turned in a half-circle, and then began to peck at the blanket. I believed she was testing the defenses of her new sleeping place. Then the tiny bird yawned, and I could see a faint glow at the back of her throat. Her big eyes slowly opened and closed. "Even the mightiest require rest. You are but a hatchling, so you might require more rest than most."

Sol turned in three circles on the pillow and sat down heavily, her four wings tucked against her sides. "And now she establishes her throne. The pillow is a worthy seat of power."

Ash watched all of this from the other side of the bed. She had pulled her knees up and rested her chin on them. The expression on her face was one I had seen before, a few times. I could guess what expression she wore. My retainer was…bemused. "You know," Ash said, "you take to motherhood very naturally."

"I am not her mother. She is my familiar and I am her…Queen."

"You have checked on her nine times in the last four minutes."

I had. Every now and then I would move my left hand over her, letting the Cradle reach inside. Sol seemed to enjoy the sensation a great deal, though I did not believe there was any damage for me to heal. "Vigilance is a virtue. I would have thought that a Hero would know that."

Sol chose this moment to begin fussing. She squirmed on the pillow, chirping in short, sharp bursts. Her wings flapped uselessly and she pecked at the fabric, at the air, at my finger when I extended it.

I lifted her and examined her. She seemed healthy. None of her many falls had done any damage to her at all. I held her closer. She pecked at my collarbone, hard enough that I felt it through the uniform. "What is the matter with you?" I demanded, even as I raised her up enough to glare at her from eye level. Sol squawked. It was the loudest sound she had made, and it was entirely graceless.

"She's hungry," Ash said.

I looked towards her. "What?"

Ash was already on her feet, pulling on her boots. "She's hungry, Lys. She just needs food. I'm dumb. Why didn't I think of that right away?"

"How do you-" I did not even have time to voice the question. Ash was already through the door, and judging by the sound of her footsteps, she was running, and running fast.

That left me alone, holding a very loud bird. I had no idea what a phoenix ate, and I doubted Ash knew either. Did…did anyone know? "Please stop screaming," I said. My voice was almost pleading. The baby phoenix regarded me for one moment, and decided that she would not stop screaming.

It took perhaps five minutes for Solara's loud chirps to grow softer. Each chirp came out softer than the last, and those big golden eyes of hers slowly opened and closed. Her chirping was exhausting what little energy the bird had. Her last chirp was so soft it was closer to a squeak. She pressed herself against my chest and closed her eyes.

Without her chirping, the room was perfectly quiet. I continued to hold Solara close to my chest. "I will learn how to care for you," I whispered to her. The words were only for her. "I will not be adequate at this at first, but I will learn." How I would accomplish this, I did not know. Then again, I had reared a beast before. It should be no more difficult the second time.

The tiny bird adjusted herself in her sleep. One of her golden feathers twitched, and I adjusted her against my chest. She weighed nothing and she was so very warm. That was when Sol sneezed.

I did not know phoenixes could sneeze. It was no more than a tiny huff of air. It was almost nothing at all. If only it had not been accompanied by a flare of light so bright it turned the room white.

I blinked and stared as a blast of yellow and orange left Sol's beak. It travelled in a straight line, struck the door, and kept going. The wood of the door burned, leaving behind a smoking hole perhaps the size of my fist. That…was remarkably like a smaller version of [Great Fire Annihilation].

Sol blinked and looked around as if mildly confused by her own power. The smoke cleared. There was a figure on the other side of the door, one I only saw through the hole. She was illuminated by the glow of the lanterns outside. Red hair, peering through the hole at me, and at the tiny bird waking up on my chest. It was Ren. She stared.

"…Wasn't me this time."
