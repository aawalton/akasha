The colossal remipede didn’t give Gael so much as ten seconds to properly appreciate how catastrophically interesting it’d become. One moment, Saint Mercy’s Widow was rearing above the lake with a hundred armored legs tearing through the water and its regenerated carapace still steaming from eighty-five cannon shells—the next, it simply dropped its entire body forward and charged. There was no warning beyond the abrupt lowering of its armored head, and no little courtesy scream to inform the surrounding ships that they were about to become debris. It simply hit the water with enough force to shove two nearby fishing vessels sideways, then surged straight across the lake towards the eastern canal with all hundred legs working in hideous coordination beneath the surface.

“Ah,” Gael said.

Then the colossal remipede smashed through three Saint’s Hands cutters, and wood, steel plating, men, and ammunition crates went flying everywhere.

Fergal’s voice immediately thundered from the fortress bridge behind him.

“Turn the starboard batteries! Cutters twelve through nineteen, close that canal”

But they were all too slow to respond. The colossal remipede had already slammed through the canal mouth before the first cannon could finish rotating, and then it wriggled between the floating buildings lining the district, followed by several violent crashes and several rising clouds of splinters in the distance. Fergal swore loudly enough for half the deck to hear him, and Gael understood exactly why: their lovely arrangement of eighty-five cannons had become considerably less useful now that their target was sprinting through a highly populated ward. One shell missing the remipede by inches could erase somebody’s house, and a full volley could easily erase an entire neighborhood. At this distance, their cannons were practically useless.

So Gael looked at Maeve, Maeve looked at him, and Jin shared a knowing glance with Vivi.

Then their new serrated wings snapped open behind them, and they jumped, catching the wind that carried them much, much farther than they would’ve managed otherwise. Gliding away from the floating fortress, they landed on the roof of the nearest cutter, took a few running steps, and launched off again. Gael was taking the lead, but his jumps and glides were pretty clumsy compared to Maeve’s smooth glides, and Jin and Vivi were visibly more in control of their jumps and landings as well. Damnit, he probably shouldn’t have downed so many bottles of alcohol on his way here.

“Find a way around, boys!” Gael shouted at the Saint’s Hands crews as they hopped from ship to ship. “Cut it off wherever you can, but don’t fire unless you’re two hundred percent sure you have a clean shot! We’ll try to intercept it while you surround it!”

He left it to the Saintess whether they heard him or not. Once the four of them skipped across their fleet of ships and landed on the roof of a dock warehouse by the edge of the lake, they began chasing after the colossal remipede.

Fortunately—or unfortunately—the Myrmur wasn’t difficult to follow. One simply had to pursue its corridor of destruction. While the colossal remipede continued barreling through the ward, its segmented back rising and dipping beneath the waves every now and again like a serpent, they hopped and glided across dozens of wooden shops that’d been smashed apart, walkways that hung broken over the canals, and several fishing barges that’d been tossed completely onto the floating platforms. Gael’s heart hammered.

“Pale,” he breathed. “It’s going straight through everything.”

More buildings collapsed ahead as the remipede plowed through them. People scattered across the floating streets. A fishing vessel trying to reverse out of the canal got clipped by three armored legs and spun around hard enough to throw half its crew into the water, and for several more seconds, Gael felt a dozen cold hands pressing down on his shoulder.

… Then he began to notice something very peculiar.

Nobody was screaming.

There was shouting, certainly. Lots of it. But Gael knew panic. He’d seen and heard panic in operating rooms, plague wards, gang battles, and Mymur attacks—and this wasn’t panic. He watched as a drunk fisherman dove away from the remipede’s tail, rolled across a roof, then came back up laughing so hard he nearly fell off again. A woman whose fish stall had just been reduced to scattered planks stood knee-deep in dead trout and screamed, “That’s a big ‘un!” with unmistakable delight. Several dozen children were even cheering at the remipede from a second-floor balcony while their caretakers dragged them inside by their collars, almost like… this was nothing but a normal happenstance.

Confused, he glanced past the remipede and into the fog hanging over the rest of endless Wraithpier.

He wasn’t expecting to see much, but boy, was he way off. Far to the west, a smaller leviathan had erupted out of a canal and was currently being chased by six tiny boats throwing harpoons into its back. To the south, another enormous fish thrashed beneath a bridge while people on both sides beat it with fishing poles. Somewhere even farther away to the east, a long-finned beast tore through an open market square while several hundred locals pursued it on foot with sandals, toothbrushes, forks, and spoons.

The entire ward was battling their own leviathans, though only on much, much smaller scales compared to his colossal remipede.

Gael stared at the rest of the ward for only another moment before he began to laugh.

“Crazy bastards!” he shouted. “You’re enjoying yourselves, aren’tcha?”

A nearby fisherman confirmed the observation by raising both fists as the remipede demolished the front half of his neighbor’s building. “Get the damn thing outta here already!”

Gael’s grin widened. If Wraithpier considered urban destruction an acceptable outcome of fishing, who was he to impose his southern morality on them?

“Dearest wife! Let’s give them something worth watching!”

The four of them caught up several streets later. The remipede had slowed slightly while trying to force itself between two narrow rows of buildings, which gave them their opening to hop at it from a distant roof, catch the wind on their wings, and slam onto its carapace simultaneously. All of them landed badly, though, and they slipped for a fraction of a second before the microscopic setae on their feet snagged onto the wet chitin, letting them stick.

Gael was on the ‘neck’ of the remipede, far in the front. Maeve was four segments behind. Vivi came down near the middle, and Jin was near the tail, all of them scattered along its fifty meter length.

“Give it hell!” Gael roared.

Gael threw out his right hand, and the three hungry flowers wrapped around his glove swelled in size, stems thickening as their mouths unfolded into enormous tooth-lined blooms. They lunged down towards the nearest gap between the carapace plates and began chewing furiously at the softer tissue underneath. Meanwhile, Maeve jammed Mistrender between two plates and fired cannons of toxic blood away, and Vivi brought her new bioarcanic rifle down, firing explosive fire ant bullets through her gap. Jin’s ‘hell’ was much less subtle. He simply clenched his gauntlet and started punching down, attempting to bash through the remipede’s plates with brute force.

And for nearly a whole minute, they battered the remipede from four different points while it tore through Wraithpier beneath them, completely oblivious to their presence. Gael’s flowers chewed until their petals started tearing. Maeve fired blood cannon after blood cannon until sweat started beading on her temples. Vivi kept emptying explosive rounds into the same exposed seams until she started running out of bullets. Jin pounded one segment so many times that the entire plate had visibly deformed, and yet…

The remipede wasn’t slowing. Not even a little bit. So when Gael’s flowers suddenly recoiled and snapped back to his glove—one of them actually groaning in pain as they did—he shook his glove and glared at them.

“Oh, come on! Aren’t you guys hungry?”

One of his hungry flowers opened its mouth weakly, then closed it again.

“Fuck you, then! I’ll do it myself—”

A hard buckle from the remipede knocked him off balance, and he staggered a few steps back while Maeve also staggered a few steps forward, sending them crashing into each other.

“It’s no use!” Maeve shouted, ripping Mistrender free from her gap and breathing hard. “I can get my toxic blood into the seams, but the plates are overlapped too deeply! I’m barely reaching anything important!”

“My rounds aren’t penetrating either!” Vivi shouted behind them. “There’s too much armor protecting the organs underneath, I think!”

“Can’t break it,” Jin grunted, straightening as he stepped back from his plate covered in shallow dents. “We can probably kill it if it doesn’t have any armor.”

Gael grimaced as he looked straight down. He supposed that only made sense for a Myrmur with over twenty-six levels in toughness and a core mutation that gave it particularly tough chitin to boot, but if they couldn’t do any damage without stripping its armor first, well, what could they do about it? He’d used up most of his chemicals making the lure mixture for the giant eel. If he could somehow hop back to his warehouse for a resupply, then maybe he could make something that could melt through its carapace. Maybe. But there was also no way anything he could whip up on the spot could melt through its armor within three business days, so—

The colossal remipede suddenly swerved again, twisting away from the Drowned Parish ahead, It apparently realized that ramming directly into the gigantic cathedral might actually hurt it, which meant as it turned and barreled down another canal, all four of them were whipped sideways with so much force even their setae couldn’t keep their feet glued to its back.

All four of them were sent flying through a giant window on the second floor of the building. For Gael’s part, he only saw glass for a split second before he smashed through it head-first and crashed into a pile of stacked fish tanks inside. Cold water swallowed him. Something bit his sleeve. Something else slapped him in the face.

Ow! Fuck off!

Gael clawed onto his feet, sputtering and peeling off the tiny eels biting at his arm. Maeve staggered up from another shattered tank nearby with seaweed hanging from her hair. Jin struggled to tear his way through the rack of nets he’d been flung into, while Vivi was still lying in a puddle filled with flopping silver fish with an expression of shock and surprise. This was probably her first time getting flung through a window like that, though, so Gael wouldn’t give her too much shit for not recovering immediately.

But eventually, all four of them managed to stumble back to the massive hole they’d created in the wall of the Parish, and they looked through it with their breaths held.

The colossal remipede was still rampaging freely through the ward, but now it was headed towards a building they all recognized.

“The warehouse,” Maeve breathed.

“Miss Umbrage’s clinic,” Jin and Vivi whispered.

There were people still in there. The sick and the young. Jin immediately flared his wings and prepared to leap out, followed by Vivi and Maeve, but—

Then a fleet of Saint’s Hands cutters burst out of a side canal in the distance, and Rebus, Bran, and Nixy—standing on the bowsprit of the leading ships—jabbed their fingers forward.

“FIRE, YE SLOW BASTARDS!”

The cutters fired, and a volley of armor-piercing shells slammed into the remipede’s side. Several plates cracked, and for the first time, the giant remipede buckled and screamed, recoiling away from the clinic to swerve somewhere else instead. Gael raised a brow. That wasn’t the end of the assault on it, though. Before the remipede could get far, ten massive strands of seaweed erupted from beneath the waters, their stems hardened with a metallic sheen as they wrapped around it and restrained it in the canal.

The culprit, of course, was Liorin lying completely flat across a wooden plank far behind the remipede, both of his arms submerged to the shoulders.

That’s… his Art, isn’t it?

He’s touching the underwater vegetation and controlling them.

The remipede thrashed, and several giant weeds snapped, but an even more familiar voice came screaming from overhead.

Then a familiar voice came screaming from overhead.

“Cutters twenty one through thirty, close the eastern canal and don’t let the ugly bastard get round the clinic!” Evelyn shouted, streaking through the air on her flight-capable wings and pointing the Saint’s Hands crews into position. “Move yer arses and fire again!”

And when the remipede snapped through the rest of the seaweed and pounced up at her, its jaws opening, another volley of shells struck it directly across the head. The impact knocked it sideways, making it scream in pain again. More armored cutters flew in from the next canal over, and on the bowsprit of the leading ship, Fergal stood with six massive cannons in six arms, each barrel still hissing with gunpowder.

“Reload!” Fergal roared at the men behind him. “Keep it moving, and don’t let the Myrmur settle!”

The remipede shrieked and tore free from Liorin’s remaining vines, only to be driven down another canal by successive shell impacts. More Saint’s Hands ships appeared from different directions with Rebus, Bran, and Nixy shouting shortcuts and routes for them to intercept the remipede with.

Gael watched the remipede retreat, eyes widening.

“That’s it,” he whispered. “The cannons can break the plates. We just gotta immobilize the remipede long enough for one concentrated volley to shatter its armor all over at the same time so it can’t regenerate quickly, so…”

Vivi suddenly turned toward Jin. “Could you hold it still?”

Jin tilted his head. “How?”

Vivi started speaking quickly, her hands moving as she laid out her proposal. Gael listened quietly, and so did Maeve.

It was… a dangerous maneuver. And possibly awful for Jin’s bones as well. But Gael liked it, and Jin stared through the broken wall towards the retreating remipede for another moment before looking back at Vivi.

He nodded then.

“I can do that.”

Vivi’s entire face brightened. Jin either didn’t notice or pretended not to as he turned towards Gael and Maeve next.

“If I can pin it down and let the Saint’s Hands’ cannons break its armor, can you two deal the finishing blow?”

That was a stupid question. Gael simply spun his cane once while Maeve tightened her grip on Mistrender, and nobody needed to say anything else then.

They leapt through the hole in the wall, gliding after the colossal remipede once again.

… Come on, now.

Round two.
