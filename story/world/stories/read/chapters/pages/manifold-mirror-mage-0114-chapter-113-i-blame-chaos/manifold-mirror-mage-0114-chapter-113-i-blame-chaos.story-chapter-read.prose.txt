Once again, Gio found himself with a head full of disjointed thoughts swimming between his ears like colorful fish behind the murky glass of an overgrown aquarium.

He and many other students had chosen not to stay in the testing room, especially not after Saint Vaught had promised a punishment for the next person to interrupt the ceremony.

Now back in his dorm room, Gio fiddled with a nice shirt that had been generously given to him by Celeste, the tall elven girl from Sewing Club. He hadn’t had a chance to catch up with the upperclassmen in weeks, but she and a few others had been invited to Jean’s gathering. He had a bit of time to kill before the party, and he wanted to try and use that time to attempt to make himself appear less disheveled, if at all possible. Gio was sure that there would be a crowd, after all.

Jean has certainly been inviting everyone. He seems excited to be done with the exam… but isn’t this a bit much? I wonder, will Papyrus and Vito end up coming, after what happened with Minette?

Gio thought back to the moment that the door closed behind Minette. He tried to suppress the thought of her walking into that candle-lit stairwell, but his eyes ended up landing on the research station where he had placed the ripped page that Minette had handed him. Her domain.

Gio had taken great care to uncrumple it and was currently in the process of trying to smooth out the creases with gentle weight and a clean cloth, a technique taught to him by his mother. The shamanic domain looked less like a magical formation and more like an artist’s rendering of a thriving ecosystem- the spell felt alive in a way that Gio had not been prepared for.

What am I going to do with you?

His bet with Minette was… stupid. He didn’t really know what he was saying, and it was mostly a floundering attempt at getting her to come back safely. Now that he was looking at the strange spellform, the significance - and also the foolishness - of what he had asked of her came into sharp focus.

She’s facing a life-or-death situation, and I decided to turn it into a petty game. What is wrong with me?

Gio couldn’t even look himself in the eyes as he started his retrospection, as his reflection had yet to return from the foolish task Gio had sent him on.

And where is Rio? I’m starting to get worried. He didn’t get caught, did he?

Rio.

(About an hour ago.)

Being that he was an (almost) identical copy of his original, Rio had the same impulse as Gio, at nearly the same moment.

A Legendary spell. Something that most mages won’t ever see. A potentially once-in-a-lifetime opportunity.Gio had hastily scrawled the words ‘GET IT’ with the grease of his finger on the surface of a compact mirror that he had started carrying with him at all times. Rio had been contemplating it as soon as he saw the lavish carrying case, but his counterpart’s endorsement was the implicit permission that he needed to spring into action.

Admittedly, he probably wouldn’t have tried, had Gio not asked.

Rio had been a little worried about his own mental state, especially after what had happened in the fight against the construct. The influence of primordial chaos on his mind had somehow managed to overtake him and Gio without setting off any mental alarms in their combined form at all. Without Trio’s timely intervention, who knows what would have happened?

Rio was determined to start regularly checking himself to make sure that he wasn’t being overly influenced by chaos. So far, he’d judged himself to be relatively fine… but without outside input, or some sort of early warning system, how would he know if he wasn’t?

But, the main body is on board with this plan, which means that it’s a good idea… right?

Both Gio and his reflection were aware that it was, in fact, a stupid plan.

Rio had ventured deep into the fraying edges of the testing chamber’s infinite reflection. He was currently several layers deep into tesselating, mind-bending vistas, and he had to watch his step lest he slip into the Between accidentally. He had started by diving into the reflection of a puddle of water left over from Gio’s domain, and then slipped from there into the distorted surface of a belt buckle that was just barely visible at the puddle’s edge. Here, in the third layer of reflections apart from the real world, there would be no possible way for someone to spot him or notice what he was about to do. To an onlooker in the real world, even if Rio were to summon a massive sun, there would only be a particularly bright dot on some random student’s belt buckle. That, combined with the fact that he only planned to take a reversed copy of the spell, should mean that nobody would detect their meddling. The stored reflection would have none of the properties of the materials that the original was printed on, and might not even be altogether too useful as an object of study- only time would tell.

But the mitigating factors didn’t stop the plan from being stupid. A zany, hare-brained scheme, even. Rio was anticipating some sort of hiccup because there’s no way that getting their hands on a Legendary spell would be this easy.

But, at the same time, maybe it will be! Who is going to stop us? I doubt anyone could follow me out here, layers deep into the mirror dimension. It’s not like-

“Greetings!” Baphelus said, startling Rio out of his jinxing thoughts.

Rio balked at his mentor, sitting and sipping tea at a conjured table, three layers deep into another realm.

“S-Saint Baphelus,” Rio bowed. “I was just-”

“I honestly don’t fault you for trying,” Baphelus said. “But no. You may not copy that spell.”

It was a quicker stop to his scheme than Rio had expected, but it became less and less surprising the more he thought about it.

“If I may ask… how did you know?” Rio puttered.

“A few reasons, really. The first and most obvious reason is that it’s predictable, so I was waiting for you to try. Both you and your other parts are mirror mages. Mirrors copy and reflect. I’m not upset at you for using your unique advantages, but you should know that there will be those who are waiting for you to slip up and reveal that you have been using the mirror dimension to dissect their spells. So far, you’ve done a good job at sticking to spells that are ‘public domain’, for lack of a better term. This is relatively unproblematic, and something that most noble houses are already doing - the only difference is that they’re just buying copies of these spells to study, rather than going through the hassle of doing something so convoluted as stealing reflections.” The lich instructed.

“So… you know that we’ve been copying domains.” Rio sheepishly replied.

Somewhat unsurprising, but embarrassing to have to admit to.

“Dear boy… you’re in my domain. I know all that occurs within.” Baphelus replied.

“You certainly couldn’t be aware of everything that goes on in your domain, right?” Rio asked. ”And I thought that the Manse didn’t extend into the Mirror dimension.”

“Yes, and no,” Baphelus answered. “I try not to micromanage every aspect of the school, but I get the highlights from my servitor spirits. As for my Manse… I’ve learned from your trip to Hart’s Casino.  Just because we’re in a reflection of a reflection of a reflection of my domain does not mean that I am without claim to this place. You, more than most, should know that a reflection of something does not necessarily diverge far from that thing itself. Just as you are essentially another part of ‘Giorgio deGloria’, the reflection of my domain is something akin to another part of my domain itself. The difference is that a scrying plane would not work the same here, nor can I come and go as easily as I can in what you might call ‘the material plane’. Magic, especially true magic, has plenty of soft, fuzzy boundaries in territories such as these. It’s a fascinating field of study, and one that would take us many, many years to educate you on fully. I hope to have more time to elucidate you on this subject and more in the future… but right now, I would like to start by having a long lecture on why taking that Legendary spell, had you succeeded, would have ruined you.”

Rio blanched. “Ruined me?”

“Take a seat.” The lich said, summoning a comfortable chair into existence.

Rio lowered himself into the seat cushions, watching the reflected form of Sybil Quinta gingerly cast her spell. The color of the mana, even through the distorted lens of the mirror dimension, was like a diffused sunbeam peaking through grey clouds. It was hypnotic.

“Have you ever heard the expression, ‘never steal from a witch’?” Baphelus asked.

“I can’t say that I’ve heard that expression. I’ve heard of… cautionary tales, mostly folklore, about vengeful witches and curses, though.” Rio answered.

“‘Never steal from a witch’ is an old turn of phrase,” Baphelus said. “It’s up there with another that you might have heard more frequently used - ‘never take a dragon’s gold’ - both are old, ubiquitous aphorisms - little sayings that everyone uses - And both of these sayings essentially mean  ‘don’t take what isn’t yours, especially from powerful people’. In this particular case, I would append those sayings by letting you know, in a very literal sense, that certain beings, like witches and dragons, have a supernatural sense to tell when they are being exploited, or when someone is profiting off of them without offering something in return.”

Rio cocked his head to the side. “I’ve gotten pretty good at reading lips. Unless I’m mistaken, didn’t the princess say that she isn’t a witch?”

Baphelus smiled.

“That is correct! However, young Sybil is not the witch whom I refer to. [A Perfectly Rainy Day] rightfully belongs to Sybil’s mentor and guardian, Alecto Stradivari. Madame Stradivari is a dangerous woman to owe any debts to. I hope that my wife one day succeeds in wooing her over to our court of friends, but for now, I know not where her allegiances truly lie.”

Rio quirked an eyebrow.

So a witch would be able to tell even if I only stole a reflection from her. Good to know.

This was a new factor that he and his counterparts would need to account for when messing with things in the mirror dimension going forward. But… one thing was still bothering Rio.

“I’m a little lost, to be honest,” Rio admitted. “How would she know? How did you sense me here? Even if you knew that I would try, we’re very far out into the mirror dimension. Even I would have a hard time navigating out here… and I’m a reflection.”

“Justification.” Baphelus intoned.

“Not this again.” Rio deadpanned.

Baphelus smiled. “It’s true, though. I built this testing chamber myself- not by hand, no, but with magic and money. It’s deeply symbolic of the Crystal Ring Academy’s contract with the spirits, as well as my personal relationship with the system. I’d know if anyone in that room was doing anything untoward, even if I try to pay as little attention as possible to the proceedings of the test itself. I brought in Vaught because, from experience, it is better for my students not to be accused of getting special treatment from me. It wouldn’t be true, of course- I’d fail you in a heartbeat if necessary… but ‘perception is reality’, and all that.”

“So you’re saying that you would know if anything bad would happen in your domain?” Rio asked.

“There are always exceptions,” Baphelus replied. “And I would not be so bold as to tempt the spirits by claiming omniscience.”

”So things can slip by you… like an undead dragon rotting in a pocket dimension, perhaps?” Rio quipped.

Baphelus sighed.

“Gio- or Rio, whatever you’re calling yourself in this guise,” Baphelus said, placing a hand on Rio’s shoulder, disregarding the violent fizzing noise of chaotic mana. “I’m sorry about what happened to you and your friends. It was an odd situation, and one that we endeavor to stop from happening ever again. The only thing that the gaps in my armor prove is that this school is, and will always be, a maelstrom of stories and quirks of fate. To answer your unasked question - ‘how did I let that happen’ - The ‘Diamond Collective’ that once ruled the city before the Goldwatch Empire warded those spaces tightly, and they had potent skills and enchantments specifically designed to keep me out. Trust me when I say that nothing remains hidden in the old noble district. My wife and I have made certain of it.”

Rio stopped for a moment.

“I… I didn’t mean to sound so accusatory.” Rio backtracked.

“It’s okay,” Baphelus replied. “One of the harsh lessons one learns about power and responsibility is that no matter how many lives you save, or how powerful you become, there is always some new way to fail. Our world is hungry for conflict, and danger has a way of inventing itself whenever powerful magic is involved. Power, even when used responsibly, often leads to inviting trouble to those around us. I trust that you have begun to see this, given your own circumstances.”

Baphelus punctuated his last point by pulling his hand free of Rio’s shoulder, revealing sizzling flesh and gruesomely exposed bones riddled with cracks.

“I understand,” Rio replied. “I’m sorry that I tried to take the Legendary spell.”

Baphelus blinked, simultaneously allowing the skin on his hand to re-knit.

“What? That’s not the point that I was trying to make.” Baphelus laughed.

Rio returned the lich’s confused stare. “You were telling me that I shouldn’t reach for power because it’s dangerous, and letting me know that you’re always watching, right?”

“Is that what you took from all that?” Baphelus chortled. “No! I’m letting you know that you need to be smarter and sneakier if you’re trying to pull off stealing a legendary spell from a powerful witch. I’m not letting you take this one because Cyr’s spell would teach you terrible spell-crafting habits. I simply won’t lose another student to the madness contained in that domain. You want to know what you would have learned? I’ll spoil it for you- Cyr was an expert at putting himself into his spells, literally. You would take apart that domain to find that it was suffused with an echo of a cocky, snooty- if not legendary- [True Archivist], and that echo would have taught you how to imbue your spells with a unique resonance that would make them like written images, nay - reflections, of you. It’s a shortcut; a stupid way to artificially inflate your spells with intent rather than doing the hard work of learning how to forge that intent into the spells the old-fashioned way via knowledge and planning.”

Rio blinked again.

“But… all of that sounds great to me. Wait, you’re speaking about the man who made that spell as if you knew him personally. You probably did, didn’t you?” Rio guessed.

“Knew him?” Baphelus croaked. “I taught the famous ‘Cyr of Thornborough’ everything he knew about true magic. He was one of my first students when we founded this school. The little ingrate went on to prance around the continent, peddling his misconstrued notions of identity as wisdom.”

“So.. I take it that you didn’t get along with him after he graduated.” Rio said.

“Well, I’m being overdramatic.” Baphelus smiled. “It is a privilege to give rise to one’s own nemesis. He was a fantastic student- and he could hold his own in a debate, not shying away from lambasting even his own mentor in public settings. He viewed me as a curmudgeonly old skeleton with antiquated views on spell formulae, which, to be fair, I am. It’s not my fault that the classics just work, is it? Let me say this: If you want to branch off and invent some sort of arcane-gobbledegook-inspired style of spellcasting, you will do so only after you have entirely mastered the basics of Unified Codice.”

“U-understood,” Rio muttered.

Baphelus’s smile froze.

The testing chamber’s reflection had gone murky, and changes were happening all over the mirror dimension. Baphelus transported both of them out of the collapsing reflection and into a reflection of the main room, where they hung suspended in the tapestries.

In the real world, Sterling Sundleah had just cast his distorted domain, and putrid water was filling the testing area.

“That’s… abnormal,” Baphelus muttered.

Rio, however, was focused on a cascade of blue threads that were attempting to close in on his main body. As he watched, they burst into grey flames and corroded into dust. His eyes widened as Gio invoked a piece of all-too-familiar magic, the Spellflower.

“Ahh, I thought I detected a strange flicker in your soul.” Baphelus preened, turning his gaze back upon Rio. “Tell me, trusted student, why have you neglected to mention that you’ve recently attained forbidden magic?”

“Uh…” Rio hummed. “Chaos gave it to me.”

“Of course.”
