It was painful to have the unlock for the second stage of the Soul-Forged-Body in his hand, but unable to use it.

There was an explicit warning in the body cultivation manual that the order mattered.

And it made sense to Jianyi, too. You wanted to strengthen your body from the inside out. He didn’t even want to imagine what would happen if you strengthened your muscles without your bones being able to sustain the power you would exert.

Still. It was painful.

Especially since he had been roaming ever farther from their camp for days now and hadn’t really found any signs of other spirit beasts, much less an Earth-aligned one.

He kept faithfully to his various trainings and exercises, though, and took pleasure from the incremental progress he made in many of them.

It was a long list of things, but it kept him busy. At the center of it was, of course, the Eternal Spear Meditation. With the boost from his Celestial Core Card, he could see the changes in his lower dantian—there was no guarantee that he would make it to stage three in the remaining weeks, but he was hopeful.

Then there was the qi control training. He was less convinced that he could make it to three threads, but he never forgot about the armband Elder Sun had given him, and he was close to being able to control three threads within his dantian. Once he had managed that, he would just need to expand through his pathways and then outside of his body.

Meditating in his soul space to improve his control over his full will was the most relaxing activity of all. He realized now that he had made the most progress with his will when in truly challenging situations, but the meditation still helped, and he would not give up on it.

Despite the importance of all the facets of his cultivation, dearest to his heart was and would always be the spear.

No day went by that he didn’t train the Fivefold Spear Art with Minghao. The progress was a lot slower than it had been when he had started out, but that didn’t make it any less real. The fight against the beaver had shown that he still struggled to translate his proficiency with the movements and forms into an effective fighter.

He was determined to change that—and the time in the wilderness was the perfect opportunity.

That thought brought him back to the moment.

He had just finished his morning presence training with Minghao, who had laid a trail for him that he needed to follow. Every day, those became trickier to find, but he had mostly succeeded.

After that session, he had headed out again, choosing to explore the south on this day, for no other reason than a bird that had flown away from that direction.

Most likely it was just happenstance, but maybe there was something there that was worth following up on.

***

Jianyi rested his head on the forest floor.

His robes were singed, his shoulder bleeding, and, after just having applied Verdant Mending to his own body, his lower dantian was empty.

His hand wasn’t, though.

It held a beautiful card.

Calming Flame

Technique

Common

1 x Fire

Hold the temperature in a quarter-pace cube constant, lasts ten breaths

The Flaming Antler Deer had maybe been too much of a challenge, but in hindsight, Jianyi was fairly sure that he had misjudged the creature's advancement. It was probably in the third, maybe even fourth stage of Qi Gathering.

But he had won.

What made him especially happy was that it had been largely a victory on the back of his spear craft—and to a lesser extent, his presence.

He had managed to get relatively close to the beast before he got noticed, and the first Blazing Arc had left a deep wound. Then, with the help of Force of the Forest, he had delivered two Piercing Fangs, which had increased the beast’s blood loss to unsustainable levels.

Unfortunately, the deer had managed to get a couple of techniques off on its own.

But Jianyi would live.

The card he had gained was one that he had heard of. It was apparently quite sought after by some crafters, blacksmiths especially. The ability to hold the temperature of a forged item constant for a bit longer was valuable to them.

The combat applications were limited—or at least Jianyi didn’t see them.

It didn’t matter.

He would happily trade the card for the formation that Minghao had offered him on day one of their time in the forest.

Things had worked out so far, but the formation seemed like a spectacular investment in his own safety in the long run.

Of course, there was the option to keep the card to trade it as part of a deal for a Fire-aligned card in his body cultivation, but that was the fourth stage of Body Tempering—a long way away, and he needed to survive to make it there in the first place.

He slowly got up from the ground, dusted himself off, and started with the field dressing. The deer was too large to go into his storage ring whole, but there were a few things, its antlers first and foremost, that he did not want to leave behind.

For the first time in a few days, he thought of his mother.

She would certainly not be happy with him getting into fights that served little purpose other than to prepare himself to get into more fights.

He smiled ruefully.

Despite that, he was sure she would be proud of him. Proud of the discipline with which he conducted himself and the progress he had made.

She was likely worried that she hadn’t heard from him in a while, and he resolved to write to her the moment he was back in the sect.

From his mother, he came to the tailors in the sect.

It had not occurred to him to bring leather armor when they had left, but it became ever more clear to him that the normal sect robes were not necessarily the best combat equipment.

But neither could he reconcile his image of a proper cultivator with one wearing leather armor—that was not what the stories had said—nor did it align with his aesthetic sense.

He didn’t think he was vain—at least not more than other boys his age—but one needed to project a certain level of… dignity at all times, and latching some piece of leather over his nice silk robes just didn’t seem appropriate.

The answer was clear: there was a need for better robes.

It didn’t feel like something that he could address in the short-term—he neither had the time nor a teacher—but should he ever have the opportunity, maybe once he had defeated the demons, he wanted to explore what he could do.

Combining the capabilities of a cultivator with his mother’s craft resonated deeply with him.

Maybe there would be an opportunity even sooner.

***

After Jianyi had returned to camp and cleaned up a bit, Minghao offered to practice forms with him.

As they went through Flowing Current, Minghao stopped him mid-motion. “Hold it right there. Close your eyes and tell me what the next move will be?”

Jianyi did as he was asked, thought about it for a moment, and answered. “Following through the motion to the end.”

“That is correct. For a form. And you have made progress on those over the last half year.” His teacher paused for a moment. “What is different in a real fight, though?”

He tried to visualize his recent fights with the two spirit beasts. “The beasts move and evade.”

Minghao chuckled. “Yes, that is correct. But even if they don’t and you hit, there will be resistance; you won’t be able to follow the motion to the very end.

“There is enormous value in learning the movements and forms to perfection; you should continue to do so for your whole life. However, as I said to you in the past, a fight is not a sequence of forms—and there is little that can be done out of battle to truly prepare you for it, but still, every move you make in training should carry with it the intent to kill your opponent.”

He felt Minghao’s spear poke him in the hips and the shoulder. “If you really tried to kill an opponent, there would be a lot more tension in your body. You need to understand, deep down in your gut, that fighting with the spear is not about beauty; it is not about smoothness for appearance's sake. The smoothness will follow if every move builds on the others with one objective only: killing your opponent. Do you understand?”

Jianyi gritted his teeth; he had just beaten a spirit beast on his own, yet Minghao criticized him.

That wasn’t fair.

He engaged his core muscles and exhaled violently. Fairness was not something that existed when fighting demons. When fighting for survival.

All these years, he had trained in his backyard on his own and wished for nothing more than a teacher.

Was he becoming arrogant just because he had some small success?

Instead of getting hung up in his own head, he considered what Minghao had said, his eyes still closed.

He returned to the Rooted Stance, then executed Flowing Current. This time, his hands gripped the spear as if it were to impact a tough opponent; his hip led the move, his shoulder followed through with all his strength.

“I understand.”

“Very good.”

***

“You know, Jianyi, I never asked what happened on that day half a year ago. On your birthday. And I never will.” They were sitting around the campfire in the afterglow of having eaten Flaming Antler Deer steaks. “But I do know that killing spirit beasts on your own at your age is special. What is even more special, Elder Sun expecting you to do that. He is old, and sometimes struggles to put himself in the shoes of somebody at the beginning of their Path, but he does know the simple rule that early on, cultivators are weaker than spirit beasts in their realm.”

Jianyi felt heat rising to his cheeks.

It was good to hear that Minghao acknowledged his success—though he knew that his ability to kill the beasts was largely due to his Celestial Core Card and the opportunities given to him. Though he did hope that he was doing these opportunities justice.

“Thank you, Minghao. I genuinely couldn’t do any of these things without your teachings,” he said, meaning every word.

“The reason I am saying all this is to remind you that, no matter how strong you are, no matter how much pressure you feel, there are risks not worth taking. Not for Elder Sun, not for me, not for power. So make sure that you never stop weighing the odds and whether it truly is worth it.”

Jianyi nodded slowly. He had taken a lot of risks since the day of his trial. Trusting Elder Sun on that day, going for the Martial Meridian Gate Opening Art, standing up to Elder Fang, and most recently, his fights against spirit beasts that would likely kill most of his sect brothers and sisters, who had started with him.

In his mind, the gate opening technique had been the biggest risk. But he had taken it consciously, and he would do it again. Without it, he wouldn’t have been able to go beyond his natural potential, which in turn would not have allowed him to use the Eternal Spear Meditation, and would certainly have many downsides along his path.

Still, Minghao’s warning was timely. Just because he had mostly succeeded so far—he had not forgotten about the failed gate opening attempt—didn’t mean that the risks would pay off in the future.

He nodded again, this time more decisively. “I understand, Minghao. I will be careful where I can.”
