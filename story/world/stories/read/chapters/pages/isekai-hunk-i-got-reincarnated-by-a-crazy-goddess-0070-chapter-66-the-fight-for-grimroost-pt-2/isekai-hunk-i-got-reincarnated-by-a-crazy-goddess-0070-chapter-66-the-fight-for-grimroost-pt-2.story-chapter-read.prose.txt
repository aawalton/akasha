Frustrated by the lack of effective magical support from the city guard, Ada clenched her staff tightly, her pink eyes darting between the rampaging dragon and Sigurd’s increasingly frantic attempts to hold his ground. She muttered under her breath, cursing herself for ever getting involved. “Why am I here?” she groaned. “Oh, right, because if he dies, so does my business.”

With a resolute sigh, she raised her staff, summoning a bright surge of light that wrapped around Sigurd. “There,” Ada called. “Speed and agility buff. Now try not to trip over yourself!”

Sigurd felt the effects immediately, his movements sharper and faster as he darted forward. His strikes landed with precision, but the dragon’s thick, stony scales absorbed much of the impact. The beast retaliated with a swipe of its massive claws, forcing Sigurd back onto the defensive.

“Stop hacking at its legs!” Ada shouted from behind a crumbled wall. “You’re just chipping rock! Aim for the exposed underbelly—it’s not reinforced!”

“Easier said than done,” Sigurd growled, leaping over a sudden spike erupting from the ground. The dragon let out another deafening roar, the sound reverberating through the battlefield.

Ada rummaged furiously through her satchel, pulling out a glowing blue vial. “Fine. If you’re going to be stubborn, let me handle this part!” She hurled the vial with all her strength. It shattered against the dragon’s snout, releasing a blinding burst of light.

The dragon recoiled, shaking its massive head and letting out a guttural growl. Ada smirked. “That’s right, look here, you oversized gecko! Let’s see how you like it!”

Using the opening, Sigurd darted beneath the dragon, his enhanced speed allowing him to dodge its frantic thrashing. With a powerful thrust, he drove his blade into the vulnerable underbelly, piercing the softer scales. The creature roared in agony, its massive body convulsing as it tried to dislodge him.

Ada wasn’t finished. She muttered another incantation, this time targeting the dragon directly. A haze of magical energy surrounded the beast, slowing its movements and making its swipes clumsier. “Let’s see how you like feeling like you’ve pulled an all-nighter,” she quipped.

The dragon, visibly drowsy, stumbled as it attempted another swipe. Sigurd took full advantage, driving another strike into the exposed flesh. Even so, the beast’s sheer size and strength remained daunting, its thrashing sending shockwaves through the battlefield.

“This is insane,” Ada muttered, wiping sweat from her brow. She reached into her bag again, pulling out a handful of magical components. “Alright, one last trick.” Combining the items, she crafted a spell that generated a series of illusory flames around the dragon’s head, further disorienting it. “How about a little chaos to keep you busy?”

The dragon roared again, snapping at the phantom flames, giving Sigurd just enough time to reposition for another attack. Despite her exhaustion, Ada managed a faint grin. “Alright, big guy,” she called out, her voice tinged with reluctant admiration. “Finish this so I can go collapse somewhere.”

Despite their combined efforts, the dragon showed no signs of slowing. Ada’s buffs and debuffs were starting to drain her energy, and Sigurd was visibly tiring, sweat trickling down his face as he gritted his teeth against the beast’s unrelenting attacks.

A familiar, playful voice echoed through the battlefield. “Oh, this is *rich*. I should’ve brought popcorn! Seriously, Sigurd, you couldn’t make this more entertaining if you tried.”

Sigurd groaned, the sound filled with equal parts frustration and exhaustion. “Not now,” he muttered, dodging another swipe of the dragon’s massive claws. He cast a quick glance upward. “Unless you’re here to help?”

The Goddess’s laughter rang out, light and teasing. “Help? Oh no, darling, I wouldn’t dream of interrupting. This is peak entertainment! It’s like an action movie and a videogame rolled into one. Look at you—sweating, dodging, almost getting flattened! It’s perfection.”

Sigurd crouched behind a crumbling wall, and managed to glare up at the sky despite the fatigue. “Can you at least *pretend* to take this seriously? I'm about to die down here!”

“Die?” the Goddess mused, her tone amused. “Oh, don’t be so dramatic. You're my champion. I wouldn’t let you die before the third act. That’s just bad storytelling.”

“I hate you,” Sigurd muttered, swinging his sword to deflect another incoming attack.

The Goddess ignored him, her tone turning sing-song. “Buuut, since I’m feeling generous, I suppose I could throw you a bone. Call it… a mid-episode power-up! Enjoy, darling!”

A surge of golden light enveloped Sigurd, and a new skill manifested in his mind. The Goddess’s teasing voice continued, “You’re welcome, by the way. This one’s on the house. Don’t waste it!”

Reluctantly, Sigurd activated the skill, muttering under his breath about meddling deities. A strange, glowing sigil appeared beneath the dragon, its runes shifting and swirling with an eerie glow. The dragon let out a final, guttural roar as its massive body began to shrink. Its scales shimmered, the once-mountainous form folding in on itself until it stood no taller than a small dog. However, its weight remained unchanged, causing it to waddle awkwardly, each step sinking slightly into the ground.

Sigurd watched in stunned silence as the dragon, now a comically diminutive version of itself, attempted to roar. The sound came out as a high-pitched squeak, drawing an incredulous laugh from Ada.

“Oh my gods,” she said, clutching her sides. “It’s… it’s adorable! Look at it waddling! It’s like a deadly paperweight!”

The dragon blinked up at Sigurd, its fiery eyes now wide and almost innocent. It let out a small snort of steam, tilting its head as if confused. Sigurd groaned, running a hand down his face. “I... I… I don’t even know what this is.”

Ada pointed her staff at the dragon, still giggling. “Well, congratulations, big guy. You’ve got yourself a magical pet. I hope you like cleaning up after it. Do dragons even need litter boxes?”

The Goddess’s voice echoed once more, laced with mockery. “Oh, bravo, Sigurd. Truly inspired work. You’ve gone from dragon slayer to dragon babysitter. I’ll admit, the comedic potential here is just *chef’s kiss.*”

Sigurd stared up at the sky, exasperation written across his face. “You planned this, didn’t you?”

“Planned?” the Goddess replied, her tone dripping with faux innocence. “I merely provided you with the tools. The fact that it turned into this masterpiece is all you. But don’t worry, darling. I’ll be watching. This is better than any reality show.”

The dragon sniffed at his boots, letting out another squeaky roar. Sigurd sighed deeply, rubbing his temples. “This is my life now, isn’t it?”

“Absolutely,” Ada quipped, stepping closer to poke the dragon experimentally. “But hey, at least it’s not trying to kill us anymore. I’d call that progress.”

The Goddess clapped enthusiastically. “Oh, bravo! A truly inspired performance. Ten out of ten. Well, maybe nine—the pacing dragged a little in the middle.” Her laughter echoed as her presence faded, leaving Sigurd to face the aftermath of her ‘help.’

“I hate you,” Sigurd said flatly to the Goddess.

Ada froze, her face falling into a mask of horror. She dropped to her knees, prostrating herself before Sigurd. “Please reconsider,” she wailed dramatically. “Don’t hate me! I’ll do anything to make it up to you!”

Before Sigurd could respond, Ada grabbed one of his boots and began kissing it fervently. “Oh, mighty dragon tamer,” she muttered between kisses. “I was wrong to doubt you. Please don’t cast me aside!”

She moved on to his hands, clutching them as she pressed her lips against his gauntlets. “And these muscles!” she exclaimed breathlessly. “Oh, yes, I love these muscles.” Her fingers started tracing along the curve of his biceps, her voice dropping to a reverent whisper. “You’re a walking masterpiece…”

Sigurd’s eyes widened in disbelief. “Ada, what are you doing?” he demanded, trying to pull his hands away.

But Ada, lost in her own theatrics, ignored him entirely. “I was blind before,” she declared, still clinging to his arm. “But now I see the truth. You are perfection given form!”

The mini-dragon tilted its head, watching the scene with what might have been curiosity. A faint squeaky roar broke the awkward silence.

Sigurd sighed, exasperation and embarrassment warring for dominance on his face. “This is not happening,” he muttered, wishing the ground would open up and swallow him whole.

The strident and incessant laughter of the Goddess echoed in his mind, sharp and relentless. "Oh, this is priceless! Truly, Sigurd, you deliver entertainment on a divine level! Bravo!"
