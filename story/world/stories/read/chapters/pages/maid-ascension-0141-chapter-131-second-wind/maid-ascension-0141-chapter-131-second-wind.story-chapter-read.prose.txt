Aric and Deren hadn't shown their faces once since the ultimatum. Usually, on any given week, they would spend at least ten hours in the library, so it felt surprisingly strange to know that they were somehow avoiding her. Lira would never admit it to herself, but she missed them, since both of them had become a big part of her life. Lyth, on the other hand, kept showing up regularly, though Henry seemed to be watching her more closely than usual.

She had less than a month now, and if things soured even further, she needed more magical proficiency and more spells in her arsenal than what she currently had, just incase she needed to make an abrupt exit.

So she focused on her breathing exercises, in through the nose, hold, out through the mouth. She was getting the hang of it, reaching the state of mind where she could visualize the shroud of her soul extending over her body, though she couldn't tell if she was close to running out of mana or not.

Once she was done, she decided to consider what she should work on next. It needed to be something useful and easy to learn. She couldn't afford a long tangent that would eat up weeks of her time, the way her healing spell had.

"Pika. Selan. Tureth."

"Pika. Moren. Kaldris."

Meditation settled over her while her thoughts slowed into the familiar rhythm, allowing her to parse through the enormous amount of knowledge she had read or magically absorbed, until a memory surfaced that made her pause, the Sword Saint's manual she had gifted to Deren. While the book explained movement and strikes in detail, it also had whole chapters spent on breath, on timing a strike to the exhale, on holding air in the chest to steady a hand before a thrust.

This gave her her first idea. She let the meditation fade and went looking through her books instead, using book divination to curate the relevant knowledge.

Over the next few hours, while visitors came and went, Lira worked her way through every physical training manual the library had, old cavalry texts, a monk's writings on meditation, two fencing manuals written by rival masters who agreed on almost nothing except the importance of breath.

She understood, intellectually, that breathing was important, people died without it after all, but there seemed to be a lot more to it than that.

One author linked breath to the heartbeat, claiming a trained man could slow his own pulse by slowing his breath to match it. Another tied it to circulation, to how quickly blood carried warmth to cold fingers on a winter march. A third insisted that proper breathing even helped with digestion.

She considered what it would mean to create a spell that improved her breathing, more air drawn in with less effort, and better blood circulation, maybe. Her stamina and her endurance would probably improve along with it. That would matter a great deal if she ever needed to run or climb a few walls in one night, and more importantly, it would allow her to train her body harder and for longer without succumbing to exhaustion.

Would a spell like this improve her rate of mana recovery? Would the mana used to improve her breath be offset by an increase in mana recovery?

Probably not, though it mostly depended on how efficient the spell was. She couldn't spend her magical reserve propping up her stamina, not without running out and ending up in a worse state anyway, out of mana and out of strength both, left with nothing but a headache for her trouble.

Whatever this spell became, it needed to cost her almost nothing while it ran, which meant it needed to be passive. She already had two passive spells that she could hold for hours without ever running out of mana, her Warm Pocket spell and her Danger Sense spell, and she saw no reason this one couldn't work the same way.

It wouldn't be as mana efficient as her runic spells, which drew only on environmental mana with no personal cost for her to sustain. She considered that route for a second, and at first she couldn't picture how that would even work. A rune sat somewhere fixed and acted on whatever crossed it, so unless she enchanted her own body using tattoos... she shook her head. The idea, while fascinating, would need a year of research before she would even consider attempting it.

No, she would settle for a passive.

In the end, she wrote five words onto the page.

Paldina. Yorad. Cipos. Kalaba. Riad.

Passive. Body. Amplification. Life. Wind.

"Pika. Selan. Tureth."

She entered meditation again, and this time she contemplated the words and their relationship to the effect she wanted.

Paldina, because she wanted a constant improvement to her stamina and recovery, where every single breath was worth more, every inhale giving slightly more energy, and every exhale releasing slightly more exhaustion, all of it at a sustainable pace, in terms of mana consumption, so that she could keep the spell running almost indefinitely, and sustainable to her body as well, so that she didn't overwhelm her own fortress.

Body, Amplification, Life was the base of every healing spell, and what she was attempting was a form of healing. Exhaustion was an ailment of the body caused by overexertion, and this spell would help prevent it and recover from it. If it had another benefit for her mana regeneration as well, that would simply be a bonus.

If Life and Water was blood, then Life Wind is breath. It made sense to her and fit very well with how she had been doing things up until now.

She worked through every breathing exercise she knew, some from Emberborn's magical manual, others from the Sword Saint manual and other physical manuscripts.

She linked it all back to her body-as-fortress model, imagining that it probably helped the red peasants become more efficient at their jobs, since Tony had linked them to the body's maintenance.

Paldina. Yorad. Cipos. Kalaba. Riad.

Click.

Lira opened her eyes and smiled. Hopefully, the testing phase would go well.

"Paldina. Yorad. Cipos. Kalaba. Riad."

For a moment, nothing felt different at all, which almost disappointed her after everything she had just put into it. She flexed her fingers, checked the familiar weight of her own breathing, and found no obvious change.

There was only one way to know for certain.

She left her alcove and made her way to the stairwell at the far end of the tower, the one that spiraled up through all three floors to the storage rooms above. It was steep enough that she had never once climbed it without feeling her thighs burn by the second landing, and without a slight pant settling in by the third.

So she started climbing.

The first flight passed the way it always did. The second flight, too, her legs working against the familiar ache that crept in by this point every single time. She braced herself for the third, the one that usually had her gripping the rail.

It never came.

She reached the highest landing and stood there for a long moment, waiting for her chest to start heaving the way it was supposed to, and it simply didn't. Her breath came even and unhurried, in through the nose, out through the mouth, exactly the rhythm she had spent all afternoon studying, except now it seemed to be happening on its own, without her needing to think about it at all.

"Huh."

She went back down and climbed it again, then a third time, taking the steps two at a time on the way up, daring her own body to complain. By the fourth climb, she was genuinely searching for the fatigue she knew should have been there, probing with her fingers at her own legs and lungs the way she might have prodded a wound to see if it still hurt, and finding only the faint, pleasant warmth of exertion.

She thought about it for a second. What she was feeling was a sort of equalization in the level of exhaustion, where every subsequent effort felt relatively similar to what she had felt after the very first flight of stairs, the familiar wall she usually hit after a while was simply nonexistent.

Or was it? There was, she supposed, only one way to find out.

She started climbing again, at her fastest speed, up and down, up and down, having activated a sound-dampening rune so as not to alarm any passing visitor. Fifteen minutes in, the point where her lungs would normally have started to burn and her stride would have begun to shorten and stumble beneath her, she felt nothing worse than a mild warmth spreading through her chest. She kept going. Twenty minutes. Thirty. Her mind, with nothing urgent left to occupy it, drifted to Aric and Deren, and whether they would truly make it all the way to Almara, then to Kavard, and whether she should find a way to kill him before he left for the countryside, and then back to Kelroth, and whether it might be worth burning this identity away with him entirely and returning to being Lira Velora.

By the time she finally slowed to a stop, an hour had passed. Her legs had begun to shake, just slightly, and she was sweating, more like completely drenched, her shift clinging to her back, and her hair stuck to her temple. But her breath came steady, and she could tell that she probably had another thirty minutes left in her, if she needed to.

Lira allowed herself a slow, satisfied smile. Combined with her body obfuscation, she could probably outrun almost any pursuer, provided it wasn't a horse on an open road, and provided the night gave her cover.

More importantly, she could dedicate a few hours a day to improving her physical condition, without looking like a dying corpse when she was done.
