The forces of the alliance didn’t exactly rush up to the new ‘wall of horrors’ in their way. The core approached warily, as did the great wedge of champions, including the likes of Zadkiel, Redberry, Keinauz, Bast, Canmore, Oddyseus, and all the golemized Knights of the Round.

Long-range effects were fired into the bone structure to test it out. Damage was dealt and the contours disrupted, but they were like rocks tossed into a lake, and the bones quickly regenerated themselves from whatever destruction was done. In particular, it was very strong against AoE, barely cracking against it. Fire was useless, and even acid did next to nothing.

Samantha tested her localized explosions via Seal of Devastation, and they worked well even from a distance. She also tried a couple Pillar of Force casts for some continual damage, but something within the bones shredded the spell after a few slams in each. Clandestine, she couldn’t pinpoint the source — perhaps it was something innate to the living wall.

The spirit beasts of the horn that were left in the wake of Levi’s attack had mostly all survived, though the water and the destruction in general had scattered them all throughout the remaining enemy army. They were causing more casualties on the other side of the wall, but in pockmarked fashion, many were reduced to only individuals, while the Dominion side was getting in order. The spirit beast numbers were dwindling rapidly.

The worst part is, I’m not sure making bodies of their soldiers much spares us against bone sorcery.

Flyers tested flying high over it and dropping attacks down, and it worked. Samantha was leery, however. Even if they could fly hundreds of thousands of soldiers over, this was not something she wanted to have at her army’s back and cut off from backline support. She’d rather force a breach and funnel through that, maintaining it if necessary until the source could be squelched.

If that is even feasible.

Because they weren’t much use against the bone wall, Samantha sent her Firebirds over to cause as much damage as possible on the other side, aiming to support the spirit beast in their final moments. She knew the summons would all perish. She included some Resemblant support as well, to extend things for as long as possible.

Inevitably, ground forces had to engage from close range and see what could be done as a whole. So they marched, the common soldier frontlines composed of rotated members who still had the cloud buff going.

Just before a good charging distance away, the bone wall shifted and contorted all along its length, and out popped form after form of independent, giant bone constructs, already charging for them and more being spun out by the minute. They were spiky and jagged, unnatural in every discernible way, and moved with tremendous speed. Each had a comparatively tiny skull somewhere in the mass, with the pits of their eyes like voids.

Every soldier? Was every soldier turned into this?

No one, including Samantha, had much time for rumination. She unleashed what she’d been waiting and primed to: throwing her javelin, Contempt For The End. It surged over the heads of the keystone vanguard and the elites in the middle of them, as it buffed them with tremendous toughness advantages, the likes of which they were clearly about to need.

The bone constructs shrugged off most oncoming magical support fire but at least had their charge broken up here and there from the most intense concentrations.

The hyper-buffed frontlines of the alliance counter-charged.

The elites smashed back with authority, breaking bones with impunity, whether from weapon strikes, special skills, or close-range magic unleashed.

Meanwhile, the bone constructs ripped into the lesser ranks and shredded through them by the dozens, sweeping powerful serrated arms through and bowling people over, often severing weapons and body parts.

However, these more common soldiers functioned more or less like meat walls that the bone constructs’ charge inevitably blunted against, and they began to peter out.

As the elites pivoted and began taking different angles to cut off more and more of the enemy charge’s progress, this became even more pronounced.

Within this core zone, casualties accrued, and the giant bone-things dove deep into the lines before slowing, but it was inevitable with the numbers differences.

With her magical bombs from above, Samantha focused on one flank, blowing apart the front lines of the enemy right before impact, ruining their charges in as many pockets as she could manage. Azure, Bast, and a few others did similar on the other flank, more or less extending the breadth of their side’s effectiveness.

It became a melee, as the constructs were difficult to destroy and regenerated continuously. On the other hand, so did the soldiers, from a combination of regen potions and healing support. Thanks to the buffs, on top of many others, the vanguard were very hard to put down, even as their numbers nonetheless slowly depleted.

Word passed around quickly about the suggestion of being more precise and accurate to go for the head, wherever it was. Often, it was heavily protected. Teamwork paid off, as some would break through intervening bone guards and then the ‘headhunter’ went for the skull. Any time the skull was heavily damaged, the bone constructs flopped and stopped moving, and the bones stopped regenerating entirely.

Beyond the core zone where the elites and hyper-buffed fought, however, things were just worse. The bone constructs simply had too many advantages, and their charges went deeper, even pushing back the army somewhat, though they had to fight for every little inch.

There wasn’t an easy fix. They had to keep pushing where they could make progress.

As always, soldiers have to die.

In the core, the enemy bunched up and concentrated the forces of their constructs to push the elites back and keep them from the wall. Though the real wall was ahead, they were a terrible wall themselves, so heavily condensed and focused.

But if they were indeed such a wall, they inevitably encountered a relative wrecking ball.

They faced the collective might of the Knights of the Round, ripping apart dozens of them a second, whether by the overpowering might or speed of this or that sword, or by sheer strength, or by craftiness and guile. The golems waded in with impunity, as if the enemy was nothing new. It likely wasn’t, to them.

They faced Keinauz and his elite cadre of Daenriar, carving through even these enemies like butter, his elated yet fierce warcry building intensity as he went, becoming a bigger and bigger problem with each swing of his greatsword, fueling his fellows in the process.

They faced Leiande and her badass cadre of Elven Radiancers, blasting and incinerating their Entropy-heavy forms into dust with impunity, leaving everyone who observed them wishing they had ten times their number.

They faced Zadkiel and Redberry, fighting in terrifying coordination despite far different styles, one entangling and jamming up a whole segment with vines and growths, the other taking advantage, jumping and smashing apart the sitting ducks over and over. This was but one very abusable trick in their bag, of course.

They faced Odysseus, who was once Palthennius, the best of the Heaven Knights, flowing through the fight like a ghost, barely ever detected, much less touched by the enemy, and needing no assistance to cut through skulls, assassinating them one by one, like clockwork.

They faced Canmore, wielding his blade of light, once the sword of a butcher. Now it carved through bone and black-pitted skulls like it had been made for it, glowing bright and hot, as the [Luminary Knight] of Samantha dominated, untouched and unstoppable in perhaps his finest hour.

They faced the sorceress Nyomel, on the surface a Naugite, but the spells she wove were cast by no other, as giant snakes of raw spirit flowed through the air at her bidding and ripped apart bone with singular fury and rage. Deep hatred burned in her disturbing, alien eyes as she fought like one of the scorned out for revenge. Many understood that she forever was, ultimately — not for herself, but for the death of her lover.

They faced Bast, detonating the air with impunity, atomizing bone in small, but continually rolling zones of undeniable potency. His sphinxes darted around him, a blur to the natural eye, defending their master and striking opportunistically, occasionally dropping a skull at Bast’s feet and getting praise each time for the delivery.

They faced Gelmak, wielding two throwing axes that cut through bone explosively, as he howled continuously in excitement, and seemed to be at once distracted, watching the battlefield continuously, as if he could not decide which was better: fighting or watching his allies work in this, the greatest of battles; the last battle.

They faced the walking meteor, Six Moons Falling, diving into the constructs as she smashed their bones simply with her body, or flung her fist contemptuously and bashed skulls into dust, or reached in and ripped them out. Meanwhile, several stone spheres the size of boulders rotated around and above her, occasionally smashing down and crushing a construct wholesale.

They faced Bob. He got one, for sure. What a trooper!

And these were hardly all — on and on the heroes went, from every creed and land. From the forests of the elves and fae, to the swamps of the frogmen, to across the water where the foreign felines ruled, the world as a whole had focused its champions here and now, for the best of all showings.

In short, the wall of bone constructs failed utterly.
