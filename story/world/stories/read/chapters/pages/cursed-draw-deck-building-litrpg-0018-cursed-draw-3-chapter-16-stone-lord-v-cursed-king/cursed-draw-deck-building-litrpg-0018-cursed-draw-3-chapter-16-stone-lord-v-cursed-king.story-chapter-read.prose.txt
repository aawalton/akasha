Alley was putting together the beginnings of a plan. He would have preferred to be further along into enacting a strategy than that, but the fact of the matter was his ninety-Card Library was simply too big and too unreliable to have that luxury.  Instead, Alley almost always had to tailor his play around what his opponent was doing, and today was no different.

The opposing side of the field was absolutely swarming with creatures, so much so, in fact, that Alley wasn’t sure what would happen if the Terracotta Warriors had the ability activated again. It was something that had never come up before him, and he was curious if there was some sort of limit to how many individual creatures someone could have in play at once.

As things stood, Alley’s Brutish Zombie and Necrotic Fiend were safe from the Terracotta Warriors, though for how much longer was hard to say. On top of that The Shard of the Living Mountain Relic had spawned another Crystaline Barrier, which Vern could sacrifice to negate an attack. Considering the Terracotta Warriors were up to twenty-eight hundred Power, only the Necrotic Fiend was strong enough to actually defeat one of the clay soldiers, which was a major problem, as Alley desperately needed to start clearing them out. That was where his plan came into the situation.

If Alley attempted to attack a Terracotta Warrior with the fiend, he wouldn’t get anywhere, but they weren’t the only thing on Vern’s side of the field. He also had a fifteen-hundred Power Ursine Prospector, which Alley was confident the older man wouldn’t bother to protect, and if he did well, then that would open up Alley’s ability to attack the Terracotta Warriors anyway.

Alley had a rule about always inspecting his Hand carefully if there was time, something he believed had become even more important and effective now that he had Denderra to read the effects of creature Cards when he was uncertain about them. Of course, with his Hand as it stood, he was actually extremely familiar with every Card in it, having played all of them either in real matches or while practicing against Kyera.

That didn’t mean he was quick to act during his Creation Phase, just that he didn’t need to speak to his ghostly companion about it. At the start of this turn, Alley redrew to fill the gap of having discarded Lynilian and found himself holding a Lesser Shade Hound in addition to his existing Hand. He considered playing the shadowy dog creature, but eventually settled on Manifesting Cursed Gravedigger. Both the shovel-wielding skeleton and the Shade Hound were on the weaker side and represented a path for Vern’s clay soldiers to start attacking Alley, but he still considered both worth playing while he was attempting to break a stalemate.

The gravedigger appeared as it always did: a skeleton wearing the half-decayed remains of a black coat and boots while carrying a rusted shovel. Despite not being a Unique Card, Alley felt a sort of affection for the weak monster and offered it a little nod.

“Glad to see you could make it,” he said to the skeleton and then chuckled at himself. Speaking to a Unique Card was weird enough. Speaking to a non-unique was an exercise in utter futility.

Moving on to the War Phase, Alley redrew and ended up with a Mouldering Mage in his Hand. It wasn’t a bad draw, but it wasn’t going to be helpful with his plan for the turn.

“Brutish Zombie Rend Ursine Miner.”

Empowered by Bone Weaved Brutish Zombie was sitting at two thousand two hundred and fifty Power, more than enough to overwhelm the fifteen hundred Power Ursine Miner. As predicted, Vern made no effort to protect the bear creature, and the purple-skinned zombie easily strode over and knocked its target over with a wild swing of its muscular arms. Normally, the Ursine Protector would have been defeated but still survived the encounter since it had two Vitality. This was not the case here, as Brutish Zombie’s effect was that if it defeated a two-Vitality Creature, it immediately killed it.

So, rather than moving on to attempt to attack Vern as soon as it won the clash, the zombie stopped to grab the humanoid bear by the neck with both hands, opened its zombie mouth far wider than it should have been able to, and bit into the top of the bear’s head. The Ursine Miner vanished in a flash of blue light, and the Brutish Zombie turned to charge Vern.

Since a creature Card had been destroyed, Alley immediately activated the effect of Necrotic Fiend before he lost the opportunity. The horned creature required him to discard a Card, so Alley sent Lesser Shade Hound from his Hand to his Crypt before pausing to watch his Brutish Zombie launch itself at Vern.

Alley was curious how this was going to play out. The old man from the Shattered Archipelago hadn’t shown anything to suggest he was particularly quick or agile, but he also hadn’t made use of any linked items or the abilities that a Deck imbued its wielder with as they grew in ranks.

The Brutish Zombie wasn’t what Alley would call agile either, but the monster’s top speed when moving in a single direction was fairly impressive. So much so, it seemed that Vern didn’t even attempt to avoid the assault from the zombie. Rather, the white-bearded man set his feet and took on a defensive pose. A second before the Brutish Zombie collided with him, he seemed to tense up. As he did, the older man’s skin changed, clearly shifting from flesh to the color and texture of dark stone.

Vern’s transformed skin only lasted a second or two, but it was enough time that the zombie, which was only allowed a single attack, bounced off the man and loped back over to Alley’s side of the field. The large man hadn’t activated a play any time Card or a linked item, so Alley was confident he had just witnessed an ability that Earth Resonance had granted Vern. It made him wonder about what ability the Crown of the Cursed King had bestowed upon himself after reaching his current rank. Now wasn’t really the time to think about it, but he would have to do some tests when he could to figure out what had joined Alley’s ability to see ghosts.

Unlike a Trials Deck, which could be relied upon to grant the same abilities at the same ranks no matter who was using it, a True Deck like Alley’s granted something based on a combination of the Resonance and what the wielder devoted themselves to doing with their time.

Necrotic Fiend did not possess an ability where Alley was able to look at his Library or collection, so Alley's eyes didn’t shine blue, and the world around him wasn’t replaced with a pocket reality. Purple Resonance flowed into the Necrotic Fiend from where the Ursine Prospector had been standing, and the grey-skinned monster gestured towards Alley.  A beam of that same purple Resonance flew from the monster’s hand and struck the four Card images that presently made up Alley’s Hand.

‘Come on,’ Alley quietly prayed, hoping that the Spell Card the Necrotic Fiend’s ability would fetch him would be one he could make use of. As per what Denderra had explained to him, Alley knew he could activate the effect of the Necrotic Fiend once per turn when a creature was sent from the Field to the Crypt. When the effect was used, he needed to discard a Card, and the fiend would move the Spell Card closest to the top of his Library into his Hand.

Soul Exchange was the Spell Card he ended up with, leaving Alley with ‘Soul Exchange, Mouldering Mage, Phoenix Urn Phylactery, Shadowed Eater, and Wraith Master.’

Soul Exchange was fairly unique, as Spell in Crown of the Cursed King went, not because it was especially powerful, though it was a strong Card. What made the Card stand out amongst the Spells in Alley’s Deck was its image and its nature. The Resonances the Card held were Curse and Charm, but also the image depicted on the Card was actually rather sweet looking, with a man and woman interlinking their hands as their spirits flowed from their own body into the other person, as though two lovers who were truly swapping their souls.

As always, the image was far less important than the effect of the Card, and this was one of the few play-any-time Spells he possessed, so Alley could play it right now if he wanted. Deciding that would be an inefficient use of the Spell when he could use it during Vern’s turn and activate Necrotic Fiend again instead.
