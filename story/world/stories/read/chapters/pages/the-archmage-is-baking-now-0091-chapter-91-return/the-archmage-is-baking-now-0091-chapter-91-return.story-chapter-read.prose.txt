The captain signaled to the driver, and the carriage lurched into motion. Brand settled back into his seat and turned to look out the window, watching the timber town fade into the distance behind them. He did not talk about what he had said to the woman, and nobody asked.

The carriage picked up speed once they cleared the town limits. The horses were fresh, and the driver pushed them hard. Jane felt every bump and rut in the road, but she was too tired to care much. The events of the last day had caught up with her during the last day, but she hadn’t stopped. Now she had no choice at all in the matter. As the carriage rolled forward, she slept.

When she woke, the sun had climbed well above the horizon. The carriage was still moving. Across from her, Dave was slumped against the wall, dead to the world. The captain was also asleep, she thought, though his posture didn’t reveal much for certain. He had his legs crossed, one hand on his knee and the other resting on his sword. Asleep or not, Jane had no doubts he could get into action pretty quickly.

Brand was awake. He sat by the window, looking out at the passing landscape, thinking.

He was a good kid, she thought. A good man, really. There hadn't been any malice in his getting-lost experience, and there was no sense that he had made a deliberate attempt to cause trouble. He had simply seen good to do and had done it. None of it had been a joy-ride or an abdication of duty in favor of fun and freedom. It had all cost him something.

Now he was, she supposed, in trouble with the king and the queen. Jane thought about that for a while. She hadn't had much in the way of parents after hers had passed, mostly being raised from childhood by Cecelia and the academy. Her aunt had been many things, but she had never been particularly protective in the keep-them-inside-and-safe sort of way. If anything, she had encouraged Jane to take risks.

Cecelia probably would have been pleased in most ways if Jane had managed to get lost like this at the academy. She would have seen it as a good sign about the young lady's exercise of judgment and tendencies for adventure. Spending a few days in the wilderness helping someone in need while her teachers fretted and wrung their hands was exactly the kind of experience Cecelia thought built the right kind of character.

Brand wasn't a child anymore, either. He was nearly a man, yet still being treated like a child in odd ways that even Jane could see. Nobody would tell Allen or Brit they didn’t have a right to a few days here and there outside of the all-seeing eye of a dozen very highly trained guards. If Dave had gone off on a journey and hadn’t come back for a few days longer than he said he’d take, nobody would have blinked. Not Jane, not his relatives back in his hometown. He was an adult, and his time was his own.

She wondered if all this protection was really necessary. What would happen, really, if Brand was hurt? Would it have to be a huge international incident? Did it have to be a crisis? Or was there some way around that?

Jane didn't know. Politics had never been her strong suit, and she had managed to squeak by learning the bare minimum about them. She still felt a pang of sympathy and support for Brand. Whatever all this was, it clearly wasn’t right, once she thought of him as a person rather than a prince. He had done the right thing. Whether his parents would see it the same way was another matter entirely.

"You're awake," Brand said.

"So are you."

"Couldn't sleep. I keep thinking about what I'm going to say to my parents."

"What are you going to say?"

"The truth, I suppose. That we found a dying woman and stayed with her until the end, then buried her properly, like anyone decent would do." He shrugged. "I don't think that's going to matter much to them."

"Why not?"

"Because they'll just hear that I disappeared for days. They’ll care that they were scared and that they had to use resources to get unscared. That’s it.”

"You might be surprised."

"I doubt it."

"Your father apologized to me, you know. For trying to push us together without either of our permission."

Brand looked at her with genuine surprise. "He did?"

"He did. Apparently my aunt had some strong words for him about it." Jane smiled. "The point is, he's capable of noticing that he was wrong when he’s pushed into it. Even if he does have to be pushed."

"Maybe." Brand didn't sound convinced. "But even if he understands, that doesn't mean things will change."

They reached the halfway-point inn sooner than Jane expected. Captain Harris called for a brief stop, just long enough for everyone to use the outhouse facilities and stretch their legs. Jane took the opportunity to splash water on her face from the inn's pump, grimly aware of the day or two’s worth of sweat and dust building up on her. She hadn’t even changed clothes over this period of time. This late in the trip, she could hardly remember if she had any with her to change into.

The innkeeper provided a hastily assembled lunch of meat and bread, enough for the group to eat in a hurry on the road. The captain paid him generously for it, then started calling for everyone to load back into the wagon.

"We're not stopping?" Dave asked. Jane guessed that however serious he thought of this experience as being, he was underestimating it.

"Not today," the captain said. "We can sleep when we reach Glenfall and get all of this resolved."

They piled back into the carriage and set off again. Jane ate her share of the lunch as the others did, just looking out the windows and watching the scenery pass. Dave fell asleep again almost immediately after eating, and Brand laughed as he began to snore.

"What happened to the horse?" Jane asked. "Dave's horse, I mean. The one that pulled his wagon. And his wagon, for that matter."

"Still at the town," Brand said. "We couldn't bring it with us. The wagon was too slow, and the captain wanted to move fast. I’m sure he’ll have it inside of a few days. The royal purse is good at making that kind of thing happen."

"Someone will need to go back for it."

"I know. I'll make sure someone does. They’ll be paid well for it. Nobody will fight me on that, at least, so long as I make sure they remember what a burden it would be on a poor common person to have to walk there to retrieve it."

"Nice of you to make sure that happens, at least."

"It's my fault we're in this situation. The least I can do is clean up after myself."

The afternoon wore on. They were getting closer to Glenfall, and Jane could see the mix of plants, trees, and even the color of the dirt getting closer and closer to what she was used to from home.

The sun was beginning to set when Glenfall finally came into view. They had made the trip in record time, and even the horses looked exhausted from the experience.

The carriage didn't slow much even then. They moved at the briskest pace Glenfall’s mostly-for-walking streets would allow, and made it to the center of the city in almost no time at all. The carriage finally ground to a halt in front the city’s meeting place, and the weary guards immediately began to untether the horses from its yokes. It would move no further today, it seemed.

The door to the carriage opened, and Captain Harris stepped out first. He offered his hand to Brand, who took it and descended to the street. Jane followed and was in turn followed by Dave. The four of them stood in front of the building for a few wordless moments, soaking in the conversation to come.

The king and queen were waiting, and Jane hardly expected them to be happy.

—

The meeting hall was quiet. The rider Harris had sent in advance of the carriage had likely arrived before them as expected, meaning that anyone present already knew the crisis was over.

The king and queen sat in normal chairs, the remains of a meal and tea strewn before them. They were attended by a minimum of guards, no city leaders, and Cecelia.

Jane did a double-take when she saw her aunt. Cecelia had been in the cold northern reaches of the kingdom just days ago, dealing with whatever kind of crisis very cold places tend to have. The journey back should have taken longer, Jane felt, even if she didn’t know where Cecelia had been when the king and queen managed to contact her. Then Jane remembered who she was thinking about. Cecelia did impossible-seeming things every day. Traveling faster than normal people could travel wasn’t outside of her skillset one bit.

Cecelia did not rise to greet her. Jane understood why. This was not the time for a reunion. There were more important matters to address first.

The king and queen did rise from their chairs as the group approached. The queen's face was tired. She looked Brand up and down as if checking him for injuries, found none, and seemed to take a breath. If Jane had to guess, she had gotten everything she wanted in that one look. The king was a bit harder to read.

"Captain Harris," the king said. "Your report."

Harris stepped forward and snapped to attention. His uniform was dusty from the road, but that hardly mattered once he took up a more official posture. This was an important man, a man who had risen through the ranks of the elite until he sat at their top. It was the kind of thing you could see in his shoulders alone.

"Your Majesty. We made excellent time to the destination. Lady Jane's magic and investigative skills made it possible to locate the prince quickly once we arrived." He paused. "I must apologize for the initial failure of the investigation. I instructed my men to search for the prince, in those words, when we should have been looking for a freight worker. The people we asked responded in kind, since they hadn’t seen anything they imagined to be a prince. Lady Jane untangled that mess and brought everything to a satisfactory conclusion."

The king turned to Jane. "Is that accurate?"

"Yes, Your Majesty." Jane stepped forward to stand beside Harris. "The captain's men did good work. They simply weren’t used to looking for a commoner, so to speak. Even if they had been, only one person in town knew where Brand and Dave had gone. They would have had to get lucky. I feel the captain would have sorted it out himself, if I had given him enough time to."

The guards in the room went a little stiffer, perhaps realizing that they, too, simply would have asked if anyone had seen the prince. Cecelia looked, if anything, a bit amused at the way things were turning out.

"I should add that when we found the prince and Dave, they had spent the last few days comforting a dying woman and putting her to rest. That's why they didn't return when expected."

The room went silent. The king's expression shifted. The queen's hand went to her mouth. Even Cecelia looked surprised by it, a feat Jane tucked away for later enjoyment.

"A dying woman,” the king said. “You observed this?”

"In a sense, Your Majesty. She was the sister of the laundress in town who put us on the right path. They stayed with her until she passed, then buried her properly. I did not meet the woman, but I observed her grave."

"I see.” The king shook his head. "Well then. Lady Jane, you have acquitted yourself admirably. We are in your debt. Captain, your work in this matter is also appreciated. Under the circumstances, you couldn’t have done better.."

Harris bowed. "Thank you, Your Majesty."

The king's gaze moved to Dave, who had been standing quiet and thoroughly daunted at the rear of the group. His eyes narrowed, as if he was trying to determine exactly where this young man fit into the good-or-bad-behavior picture.

"Let him alone. He never left me," Brand said. "Even when he should have, to get help for that woman. He obeyed your commands to the letter."

The king considered this for a moment, then nodded one last time, leaving Dave uncommented on after all.

"Well then. That's that. You've all done good work, and you have our thanks. I'm sure you're all exhausted from your journey."

"No."

Brand had stepped forward again.

"That's not that," he said. "I have more to say."
