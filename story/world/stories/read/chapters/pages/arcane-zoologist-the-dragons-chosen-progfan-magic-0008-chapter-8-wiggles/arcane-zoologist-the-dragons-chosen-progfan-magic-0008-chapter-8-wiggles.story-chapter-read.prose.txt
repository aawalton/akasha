A variety of emotions passed through Callie in the following few seconds. Awe, horror, excitement, and fear all mingled together to the point that her stomach felt queasy.

The first thing she did, however, was look at Mister Blackthorn. He was still sharpening the sword, his eyes focused on the motion of the whetstone sliding over the blade. Callie exhaled in relief. He hadn't seen her create fire out of nothing.

She then looked down at her forearm, wondering whether her mark was shining through her tunic. Much to her relief, the glow had completely disappeared.

Once the fear of being discovered was gone, Callie felt herself filling with joy. She had used magic to light a fire! It was completely by accident, and the timing was incredibly poor. Had Mister Blackthorn been watching… Callie shuddered to think what kind of disaster would follow.

But he hadn't!

"M-Mister Blackthorn. I got the fire going," Callie said quietly, trying to hide her excitement.

The gruff man turned his gaze to her, his eyes widening slightly. "It appears I have been distracted. I didn't notice the moment it started to smoke."

"It happened rather fast…" Callie said, squeezing her hands together. They were getting clammy. She desperately hoped that he couldn't somehow tell it was magic. Was there a difference between real fire and magic fire? She certainly hadn't noticed any.

Mister Blackthorn approached her, looking at the tiny flame burning on the plank. He appeared skeptical. Then again, he always looked skeptical. But Callie was shaking internally and doing everything within her power not to let her body shake for real.

"A fire is a fire. Your technique is lacking, but we'll practice more in the future. You may go home for today," he finally said. With a swift motion from his boot, he pushed soil onto the little flame, extinguishing it.

Callie felt a little disappointed, seeing it go out. It would have been nice to look at the magic for a bit longer. Nonetheless, she thanked him for the lesson and ran home. She wanted to see whether she could get the fire going again.

How could she make it appear again? She had felt rather frustrated and angry the moment magic appeared. Maybe that was the key to fire magic. Feeling angry? It would make sense. Dad was often angry, and he could use fire magic. Mom was calmer and more flexible, so perhaps that was why she could use water magic.

Don't jump to conclusions, Callie! I need to get home and try to make fire with magic again. Understand how it works exactly.

However, on her way home, Callie noticed something gleaming in the sunlight. It was on the trail that led to the flower meadow. She would have dismissed it as nothing more than a large snail trail if it didn't have a light green sheen. Curious, she bent down to inspect it, seeing that it was far larger than a snail trail.

There were also little flower stems poking out of the ground along the trail. A snail wouldn't consume so many flowers in their entirety. They preferred eating the soft leaves, especially when they had a choice. This was undoubtedly the job of a mireling.

Callie's heart filled with excitement. This was a chance to meet the little arcane beast again. Without a second thought, Callie began following the thin slime trail.

But in her excited haste, Callie forgot an important detail. She only remembered it when she felt herself being roughly yanked by the arm and pulled behind one of the village houses. She tried to struggle against the grasp, but it was too strong.

"Where do you think you're going, Calluna!" Aurelia asked as she roughly shoved Callie against the wall. "Crawling on the ground, following some nasty slime. Are you trying to endanger the whole village by luring monsters closer?"

"W-What do you want?!" Callie tried to say fiercely, but her voice sounded much squeakier than she had intended.

"I want you to stop obsessing with monsters! Hunters already have their hands full and here you are following a mireling trail," Aurelia barked. She tried to appear tough but Callie could see that her lip was trembling a little. Was she afraid of mirelings?

Gerda, a girl who was just a year older than Callie, but acted like Aurelia's most devout follower, was glancing around nervously.

"We should tell hunters about this. Have them eradicate these nasty pests once and for all!" Aurelia announced.

"It didn't do anything wrong!" Callie protested, feeling anger rise within her again. If they only stopped to think, they would realize that mirelings were quite harmless.

Callie opened her mouth to argue against Aurelia, but then she noticed that just behind the girls, the mireling was wobbling nervously in the nearby shrubbery.

"What are you looking at?" Aurelia narrowed her eyes, throwing a glance behind herself. The mireling disappeared in the bush, faster than Callie could blink. When Aurelia didn't see anything and focused on Callie again, the mireling once again peeked out of the bush.

Callie didn't even hear what the girls were saying anymore. She had to warn the mireling to run away! Should these meanies actually see it, they would call the hunters for sure.

But before she could do that, the mireling emerged from the bush and slid closer to the girls. Callie shook her head at the mireling, hoping that it would somehow understand her.

Don't come! Don't come!

Sadly, the mireling didn't. It extended its tendril and reached towards Aurelia, touching her calf, making the girl jump up from sudden pain.

Aurelia let out a sharp yelp. "Ow, what the hell? Did you step on me, Gerda?"

"I didn't step on you!" Gerda shook her head. The mireling then extended another tendril and touched Gerda's leg too, making the girl scream.

Then it promptly slipped back into the underbrush, hiding from everyone. A blistering red mark could be seen on Aurelia's and Gerda's legs where the mireling had touched them. Callie watched their injuries in horror. Had the mireling just burned them?

"Ow, ow, ow! That really hurts," Aurelia whimpered, then glared at Callie. "What did you do?"

"I didn't do anything!" Callie shook her head. It was true, although there was no way she would admit that it had been the mireling.

Gerda was clutching onto her leg, tears streaming down her face. "I-It hurts! She must have done something! Nobody else is here."

"It looks the same as mine," Aurelia noted, seeing that Gerda's sock also had a hole burned into it.

"Look over there!" Callie suddenly called, pointing in the opposite direction.

The girls, likely dazed by the situation, actually turned and looked in the direction she pointed. Meanwhile, Callie rushed towards the bush where the mireling was hiding. She hesitated for a second. It had left horrible burns on Aurelia's and Gerda's legs.

But should they figure out that it was this little one that did it, they would undoubtedly tell the hunters about it. So Callie braced herself for pain and grabbed the mireling in her arms, running away from the scene as quickly as possible.

"Hey! Come back here!" Aurelia yelled, but didn't pursue her. The burn must have been too painful for her.

Nonetheless, Callie didn't look back. Gripping the squishy, lightly moist mireling in her grasp, Callie ran as quickly as she could, jumping over roots and bushes until she finally reached the part of the forest that was near her home.

She had expected her hands to start hurting and burning, but they didn't. The mireling slime was definitely coating her hands, but it felt cool and pleasant, almost like an ointment. Could it somehow control whether its slime was painful or not?

Panting and gasping for breath, Callie looked around. Seeing that nobody was following her, she sat down in the soft moss and gently lowered the mireling to the ground.

"That was really dangerous, you know! What if they had seen you? They could have reported you to the hunters," Callie chastised the mireling, then added. "You… You really hurt those girls…"

The slimy creature looked completely unapologetic. In fact, it seemed proud of its accomplishments, its slimy form stretching high and wobbling slightly. Callie couldn't help but let out a giggle.

"No, no, you shouldn't have done that! Burning people is bad," Callie said sternly.

The mireling deflated. It seemed to understand that it had done something bad. However, seeing it like that, Callie's heart quickly melted. Its intentions were in the right place, even the actions weren't the best.

"Thank you," Callie said and reached out to pet the mireling. "You really saved me back there."

It leaned into her touch, seeming pleased by the praise.

"But!" Callie raised her finger in the air, just like Mom did when she was chastising her. "You can't burn people. That is really bad, you know."

The mireling just wobbled slightly. It felt like an agreement. Callie dearly hoped so. She didn't want it to hurt people just to help Callie get away from Aurelia. Perhaps it was best to switch the topic. "How did you find me?"

The mireling didn't seem to understand her question either, but it slid closer to her. Callie smiled. This situation did prove that arcane beasts weren't monsters everyone believed them to be. In fact, this little creature had shown compassion by helping Callie out.

"I guess it doesn't matter. Thank you…" Callie paused. "Do you have a name?"

The mireling didn't understand her. It merely reached out its tendrils, poking curiously at her fingers. Like it was trying to understand what they were.

"I suppose you wouldn't have a name. Would you like one?" Callie asked.

The mireling wiggled, which Callie took as a sign of approval.

"Very well. From this day, you shall be known as Wiggles!" She announced proudly.

The mireling stopped its poking, going still for a moment. Then it wiggled two tendrils in the air. It seemed to like its new name.

At that moment, the mark on Callie's hand glowed, gentle warmth spreading from it. She gasped, hurriedly rolling her sleeve up to look at it. She didn't feel the heat within her like she did before, but something was definitely happening to her mark. Not only was it glowing, but it was also growing.

From the little dot, a petal sprouted on Callie's skin, drawn there by magic. She and Wiggles both watched it grow for a minute, slowly but surely becoming more defined. Once it was just as clear as the initial dot, the glow faded, and Callie was left with a tiny flower with a single petal on her forearm.

"Did you do that?" Callie asked.

Wiggles tilted its upper slime to the side somewhat, which seemed to indicate that it didn't have a clue how it happened. But Callie was certain. It was thanks to Wiggles that her mark had grown larger. But why? Was it because it was an arcane beast and she touched it?

But she had touched Wiggles before, and the petal hadn't grown then. Why did it grow now?

"Ughh! There are so many questions, yet so few answers!" Callie tousled her hair in frustration.

Wiggles extended its tentacle and seemingly tried to set it straight again. Despite the mireling's best efforts, her hair remained completely unruly, going in every which direction.

"Thank you, Wiggles. It'll be alright. I'll pass my lessons and get to the library. I'm sure I'll find all the answers I need in the books," she said with determination. "But for now, you should head home. It wouldn't do you any good to be seen by my family. They don't like arcane beasts very much."

Wiggles poked at Callie's mark one last time, before retrieving its slimy tendril and sliding back into the forest. But unlike the last few times, Callie was sure that she would meet Wiggles again very soon.
