They tumbled into the tent, getting caught up in the fabric as the entire structure collapsed.

Elion squirmed, trying to untangle himself from Keyla and the tent as Elfron laughed. A blast of power washed over Elion, burning away ropes and fabric.

Suddenly freed, Elion searched for Keyla’s rifle but couldn’t find it. Keyla lay unconscious beside him, clothing scorched and smoking.

Elion turned to face Elfron. The big warlock laughed again, casting another spell. Ribbons of violet streamed from his wand, twisting and wrapping around Elion.

“So,” the big man said, “I’ve caught you at last.”

The warlock’s spell entangled Elion, twisting into knots and sinching down tightly. Elion recalled back to the time he’d been ensnared by warlocks, trapped by the same spell in the Walker’s back yard.

This time Praxis didn’t give him the option to use a Zelian spell to break free. But he fought against it, his enhanced strength allowing him to rip the binding power away from his arms. The same way that Ryfer had struggled against Elion’s ‘Save a Friend’ ability.

He managed to free his arms, as his legs were ensnared.

“Elion!” Yaret yelled from the other side of the arachnatronic. He stood unsteadily on an injured leg, wielding the sledge. He raised the weapon, then swung it down on the sword capture machine, eliciting a howl of fury from Dave.

The distractions worked, though, and the warlock’s spell on Elion faded. He scrabbled over the rocks, picking up Yaret’s long rake, and whirled it over his head.

Two jolts of pain stabbed into the flesh of his arm, and a third struck him just between his collarbone and the top of the breast plate.

Gasping from the pain, as exhaustion surged through him, Elion pressed forward. He fired the shotgun, catching Elfron’s left shoulder in the spray of pellets.

The warlock staggered backwards and Elion pressed his advantage, driving the dark robed man back toward the edge of the cliff.

Keyla’s bondhold—her mother’s bandana—was tied around Elfron’s left wrist. As the warlock backed up to the edge of the cliff, his hood fell away. His eyes, glowing violet, exposed his fear.

Reaching out, Elion snagged the man’s wrist, trying to prevent him from doing any of the gestures that helped him cast spells. Blood dripped from the wounds in Elion’s arm, and the ground wobbled beneath him.

“Wait, stop,” Elfron hissed, glancing over his shoulder as Elion drove him back toward the cliff, barrel of the shotgun to his chest. The man gasped from exertion, weakening as he resisted.

“Please,” Elfron said, searching for a means to escape. Elion ripped the handkerchief off of Elfron’s arm, then pulled the trigger.

The shotgun blast took Elfron full in the chest, sending his body tumbling off the edge of the cliff and down toward the pools of acid below.

Elion powered his armor down, trembling as he lost the added strength. He sank to his knees, trying to regain control of his body.

Blood ran down his hand, soaking into the handkerchief he’d wrenched from Elfron’s arm.

The distant sounds of fighting pulsed in and out rhythmically, his heart drowning out the noise as it beat.

He had to get the bondhold back to Keyla.

Elion leaned on the barrel of the shotgun like a cane, pushing it down into the dirt, to help him rise. Stumbling back toward the camp, he searched for the damaged Arachnatronic and the collapsed tent.

“I can’t kill you,” she said. “I had to stop the fighting. I wouldn’t do what he wanted me to do.”

Dave had disappeared, running to who knows where. The warlock Elion had sledged still lay on the ground, the dent in her skull leaking blood into a puddle around her head.

Elion crossed the ground in a daze, coming to kneel beside Keyla.

She lifted a hand and grabbed his arm, turning to look into his eyes.

“Elion!” she gasped.

“I got it for you,” Elion said, taking her hand, and pressing the handkerchief into it. “I got your bondhold.

“You came back for me,” she said.

“It’s a clean wound,” Yaret said. “With proper medical treatment she’ll recover soon enough. Faster if you can get her to a Heranan healer.”

“Is she going into shock?”

“Partially, yes,” Yaret said. He looked up from the wound, worry lines creasing his brow.

“Let me stabilize her,” Elion said, placing his hands against Keyla’s shoulders.
