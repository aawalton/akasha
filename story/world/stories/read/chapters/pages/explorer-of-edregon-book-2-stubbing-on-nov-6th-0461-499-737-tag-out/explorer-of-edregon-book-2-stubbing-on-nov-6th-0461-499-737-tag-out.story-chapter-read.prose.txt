When it came to magic, there were a lot of things Vin prided himself on. Whipping together runic formations, navigating around runic backlashes, and pulling spells from artifacts were all things he was naturally quite good at. Not at the level of a prodigy, as Shia so often reminded him, but still to an impressive degree. That said, there were plenty of areas involving magic he was far less good at, such as creating artifacts or runic engraving.

Or anything involving rituals.

Vin sat at a table across from three neilans, sitting in silence while he thought over what he’d just been told. Their too-long fingers and large heads made them appear almost alien in a way, but he wasn’t one to judge another based on appearances. Now, he still very much judged them for their almost total disregard of any other race of people beyond themselves, but he felt that was more than fair to do so.

After hearing from Theodore that the neilans had agreed to help out, Vin had turned around and run straight back into the Underside, rushing over to the neilans’ fragment to sit in on this meeting. On one side of the table were him, Tom, Vanessa, and Theodore, while on the other were three neilans who had supposedly agreed to assist with Terra’s newfound problem.

Only as per usual, it wasn’t quite that simple.

“Just to make sure I’m getting this right. In exchange for your assistance, you want us to sign a magically binding contract that we’ll never enter your fragment ever again?”

“That is correct,” the one Vin had designated as the leader said, nodding his large head. “We wish to have nothing to do with the rest of Edregon. We are more than content to sit within what remains of our world, safe from others. You appear to be the only ones capable of bypassing our barrier, and we want no more of it. Those are our terms. There will be no negotiation.”

Vin briefly wished he had Shia or Alka with him to play the role of ‘bad cop’ and remind the neilans that the only reason they hadn’t all been killed already was because he alone had stood up for them and stayed Alka’s hand. After he and his team had buried the remains of the blue fur clan in a mass grave, they had discovered it was the neilans who were responsible for shunting all their own monsters off into the crystal desert fragment. Even if they weren’t directly responsible for killing the other people, they didn’t have the slightest sliver of remorse for what they’d done. The neilans had once conquered even death prior to coming to Edregon, and because of that, they looked upon everyone other than themselves as lesser beings.

“We cannot abide by those terms,” Vanessa said, giving the neilans her best award-winning smile as she shook her head. “We know you are immensely skilled Ritualists and could shift the output of your ritual to a fragment other than the crystalline desert at any moment. We would feel more comfortable being able to check and ensure that doesn’t happen. Not to mention such a contract would prevent us from coming to your aid in the event that another people figured out a way to breach your barrier. As I’ve been saying for weeks now, we want a true alliance between our people. One that we can both benefit from.”

Vin could practically feel the charm in the Ambassador’s voice, and he held in a shudder as her words reached him. While classes like Diplomat and Ambassador didn’t actually do anything along the lines of mind control people or even really influence their actions, they did often give people pause and almost force them to second guess themselves as they went back over the reasons why they were disagreeing with someone in the first place. It made persuading people to do things easier, though it was still impossible to influence anyone to do something they felt too strongly opposed to. Otherwise, Tom would have gotten the guards manning the Citadel’s wall to open the gate months ago.

“We care not for an alliance, and we care not for you and your people,” the lead neilan said, his frown deepening. “And for the twelfth time, your class’ weak abilities will not work on us. If you desire our aid, your leaders will enter a ritualistic contract with us that will prevent either side from going against their word.”

“And you won’t settle for a simple promise on our end?” Vin tried yet again.

“Your word means nothing to us,” the neilan scoffed. “The contract ritual will utilize soul magic to ensure that both sides remain true to the bargain. Naturally, as the ones more experienced with rituals, we will take charge in preparing it.”

Vin drummed his fingers along the table, matching the neilan’s stare. The problem was that the neilans truly had them in a bind. There were no other ritual masters for them to turn to, which meant the neilans had all the power of the negotiation. The infernals were good, but they weren’t ‘interfere with a working of the Gods’ level of good. The eighth wave was getting closer with every day, which meant they really didn’t have any time to waste. And even if he was willing to try and force them to help out, which he wasn’t, there was no telling what the neilans would do if they were forced to conduct some sort of grand ritual. Vin didn’t doubt they’d be able to create something that would destroy all of Terra if it came to that.

Just before he asked them to give him a minute to run their demands past Terra’s council, Vin received a surprising suggestion.

“I have dealt with people like this before,” Ovord spoke in his head, causing Vin to blink as the quiet mage spoke up. “It is like watching the major races of my world force the lesser ones to do as they command. If you want their help, you should allow me to assist you.”

“Seriously? You want me to trust you with this of all things?” Vin muttered, ignoring the neilans’ confused glance while he whispered to himself. Theodore, Tom, and Vanessa looked just as confused as to what was going on, and Vin ignored them as well. “You know how important this is to us.”

“Which is precisely why I am offering my assistance. You are going to have to give me control of your body to aid your golem friend soon enough. It would be wise for us to start practicing. It has been centuries since I last had a physical form.”

“This is a pretty big test,” Vin said, eyeing up the neilans across the table. “If you mess this up… Frankly, I don’t know what I’ll do, but you won’t like it.”

“Even if you have refused the offer of my soul bond, I still consider myself your vassal. I will not fail you.”

“Alright…” Vin muttered. Clearing his throat, he turned toward the other Earthers. “Theodore, I need a minute alone with the neilans. Can you three wait outside?”

“Sure…” Theodore said, giving him a concerned look. Leaning closer, he lowered his voice. “Vin, what are you going to do?”

“I’m honestly not sure,” Vin admitted, praying he wasn’t making a bad decision. The only reason he was okay with this in the first place was because Ovord knew the stakes at play here. If the mage messed up something this important, he knew that would affect his chances at getting a real body for himself. Not to mention it was true that Ovord had experience dealing with the whole lesser-versus-major races of his home world. Seeing as the neilans acted like they were Gods and everyone else was beneath them, it made him perfect for the job. “Just trust me on this one, alright?”

“You’re the boss,” Theodore shrugged. Giving the neilans a quick bow, he then followed Tom and Vanessa out of the room. Once they were gone and the door closed behind them, the lead neilan gave Vin a pointed look.

“Is this where you threaten us? Try to make us assist you by force?”

“I am not planning on doing anything,” Vin shrugged, taking a deep breath before triggering the active portion of Human Vessel for the first time in months. “Though I can’t say the same for him.”

Bourhon raised an eyebrow when the human spoke his cryptic words, clearly trying to unnerve them in some manner. While he disliked not knowing what it was the human was planning to attempt, whatever his inane plan was, it wouldn’t change anything. Bourhon was well aware of the powerful position that he and his people were in, and he was determined to take advantage of it to force the humans to leave them alone. The fact that they had to put up with such pitiful creatures who were still enslaved to such forces as death and pain was bad enough, but actually talking to them?

This conversation couldn’t end soon enough.

Bourhon blinked when the human’s eyes shifted without warning from a light blue to bright silver. Not only that, the human seemed to sit up straighter, the corners of his mouth turning up as he held up his hands before him in awe and flexed his fingers. Something about the way he was moving was different, almost unnerving in a way, as though the simple act of breathing filled him with immense, incalculable joy.

“Oh, how I have missed this,” the human said, his voice strangely musical. Laying a hand gingerly on his face, he sighed, looking like he was basking in nothing more than the sensation of touch. “So long have I waited…”

“I don’t know what you are trying to accomplish by this disgusting display of baser instincts, but we are through putting up with your feeble attempts at negotiation,” Bourhon said, getting to his feet and glaring at the strange human. “You have our demands. Either follow them or get out of our world.” Bourhon was half tempted to ‘agree’ to the humans’ demands just to design a ritual that would wipe them all off the face of this new world, but he’d rather not leave his house if at all possible. Being forced to deal with these sad creatures was bad enough. The last thing he wanted was to venture to what they considered a civilization, even in order to destroy it.

“That’s right, I did promise I’d deal with you,” the human muttered. His silver eyes shone as he glanced at them, almost like he’d forgotten that they were even in the same room as him. “Well, above all else, I am a man of my word. I prefer to be a bit more subtle with my workings, but I am on something of a time limit here, so you will have to forgive me for my more primitive methods.”

With a snap of the human’s fingers, shadows rocketed out from all corners of the room, binding the three neilans in place and yanking them back down into their chairs. Bourhon’s eyes widened when his mouth was covered, preventing him from screaming out in fear as the human casually stood up and leaned over the table, tilting his head curiously as he peered deep into Bourhon’s eyes. Somehow, Bourhon knew in his core that the human wasn’t actually looking into his eyes, but into his very soul, and he shuddered in fear of what was to come.

“Unfortunately for you three, it would appear I am able to shunt my Lord’s presence into his own mana void just as he is able to do to me,” the human explained. Tilting his head this way and that, he continued staring down into Bourhon’s wide eyes, all the while humming to himself. “He is quite displeased with me at the moment, and I have no doubt this little stunt will cost me, which means I will have to be quite thorough and ensure you do exactly as he demands of you. Now, my Lord doesn’t quite seem to understand how things are done between those who view the world in black and white terms such as you and I, but fortunately for you, I am more than schooled in such methods. However, you are mistaken as to which one of us is superior, and which one is lesser. A mistake I intend to correct.”

The human smiled as he spoke, his silver eyes gleaming in the light as his flesh-and-blood hand turned ethereal. Without warning, he plunged it into Bourhon’s chest, and the neilan tried to scream as the sensation of ice water filling his veins spread from his torso throughout the rest of his body. Yet try as he might, the shadows binding his mouth prevented him from uttering so much as a peep.

“If it were up to me, I would slaughter you and your whole people for what you have already done,” the human hissed, leaning in close and staring daggers at Bourhon as he rooted around within his chest. “I do not just see it on your faces. I can feel it in your soul. You and your people are rotten to the core. Thinking about wiping out an entire race of people because their existence annoys you? You are almost worse than the major races of my own world, and I did not think such a feat was possible. My Lord is far too merciful, even with one such as myself. If he cannot see the rot that needs to be pruned from this new world, I will take it upon myself to follow in his shadow and do what needs to be done. The divide between races I spent my entire life trying to end is no more, and I will not stand idly by and watch as you work to create it anew. You will change. Or you will die. It is that simple. With that in mind…”

The human stopped rooting around within Bourhon’s chest, his fingers clutching something that made Bourhon freeze in place. As he sat there, bound in both body and soul, the human flashed him one last horrifying grin, his silver eyes boring into the neilan’s own.

“...Which will it be?”
