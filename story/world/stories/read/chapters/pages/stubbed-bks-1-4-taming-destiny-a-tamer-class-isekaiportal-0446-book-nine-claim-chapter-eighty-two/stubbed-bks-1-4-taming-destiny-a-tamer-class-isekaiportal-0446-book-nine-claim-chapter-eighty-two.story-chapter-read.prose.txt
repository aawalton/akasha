"Do we try, my lord?" Ilsys asked.

Elarion is silent for a long moment, his eyes studying the beasts between his party and the settlement.

Had he been with a group of elites or even combat classes, he wouldn't have hesitated. Punching a hole through these beasts wouldn't take much with such a party. As it is, however, he's mostly accompanied by non-combat Classers and even some non-Classers entirely. He’s not confident he will be capable of escorting them through the overly-aggressive beasts unscathed.

He considers leaving them alone in the forest while he re-enters the settlement with only those capable of fighting, but dismisses that possibility after a moment – the forest is hardly safe, and he would be required to take all the most combat-capable of them with him.

Ultimately, he cannot risk even one of his current charges – each of them is precious to the food generation efforts. In different circumstances, he might trust the healers within the settlement to save those who might be injured, but with the current crises and limited healing potions, he’s not willing to take the chance.

He's still considering the possibilities when movement in the forest catches his eye. His dismay deepens as he makes out a large group of beasts approaching – even bigger than the one currently assailing the walls. And unless his eyes are mistaken, one of them looks to be big enough to be a Tier three.

"Back," he hisses.

Those around him obey without question and they retreat into the treeline. Given how aggressively the beasts are throwing themselves against the wall with no concern for the damage the defensive enchantments are doing to them, he has no desire for any of them to catch sight of his party. He'd rather face ordinary forest beasts than risk that.

"My lord–" a tremulous voice reaches him.

He turns sharply to find one of the non-Classer farmers backing away from a helven-like beast. Elarion's hand moves instinctively to his staff, then relaxes as he recognises the figure, and the second one beside it.

"They are Lord Markus's Bonded," he tells the group. Then he narrows his eyes at the two creatures. "Were you not supposed to be scouting for the source of these beasts?"

"We guard," the slightly-smaller of the beasts says, its projected voice unsettlingly human. Elarion reflected that at least the samurans look as though they ought to be capable of speech. These creatures look like they should speak in growls – or, rather, not speak at all.

"Guarding what?" he asks, mildly curious.

The creature simply bares its teeth at him and doesn't answer. But Elarion doesn't need it to. Now that he's paying attention, he can feel a node of nature magic to one side. He steps toward it.

He's met immediately with growls from the helven-like creature.

"I do not intend harm," he says, sending it an impatient glance. "I am simply curious."

"Packleader didn't say to let you close," the creature warns.

Elarion sighs, then reaches out along the Bond to Markus. It's still faintly unnerving to have a Bond with the man, though he has to admit it's far less onerous than he expected. That's probably because it isn't the kind of Bond his father holds, but Elarion suspects it also has something to do with the kind of man Markus is.

He hates to admit it, but he’s gained a great deal of respect for the expedition leader – both as a person, and a lord.

Markus. Are you available?

A moment passes, and then the response comes, his mental voice sounding distinctly harried.

If I must be. What is it, Elarion?

Elarion is briefly tempted to take offence at the brusqueness, but decides against it. He hasn't spoken much during the discussions the others have been having over the past few days, but he's been listening. It's abundantly clear that the settlement leader is being pulled in multiple directions at once. Not for the first time, Elarion is quietly grateful he isn't the one in that position. A little brusqueness can be forgiven.

We have encountered two of your Bonded in the forest, he tells the man succinctly. They indicate they're guarding something and refuse to let me approach. Yet I can feel nature magic. Will you allow me to investigate?

I thought you were trying to make it through to the settlement? Markus questions, not answering him.

We assessed the situation, Elarion relates with a touch of impatience. I might have risked an attempt before the reinforcements arrived. Now is another question. I do not believe the healers need a new influx of patients.

No, we don’t, Markus agrees, sounding disappointed but accepting. You'll remain in the forest until the beasts have either been killed or dispersed?

Elarion gives a short, unconscious nod, despite knowing logically that Markus can't see it.

That is my intention. And I noticed that the nature magic your Bonded are guarding feels like what we’ve been encountering in the fields, only far stronger. Studying it may be useful for our farming efforts.

Markus hesitates.

My two companions are currently guarding another of our companions – one who seems to have gone too deeply into a massive root network. I’ll speak to them, but if you do investigate, please be careful. We don't want you becoming trapped as well.

With every word, Elarion feels both excitement and incredulity rise within him. He is an Ice Druid, and yet this is the first time he’s hearing of this?

However in deference to the pressures of the situation and how busy he knows Markus is, he decides to point this out to Markus later.

I will be careful, he promises. Then, not quite capable of avoiding any digs entirely, This is, after all, exactly what my Class is good at.

Good to hear, Markus replies, sounding distracted. Elarion, I really need to go. Is there anything else?

No, Elarion replies, pushing away his instinctive offence at the disrespect. He is wise enough to know Markus does not intend it.

Then good luck, Markus tells him finally and Elarion feels his attention shift away.

He's gratified, however, when a moment later the helven creature before him stops growling.

"Packleader says you can approach," it tells him, sounding faintly disgruntled.

Elarion nods imperiously and steps toward the source of nature magic. As he does, two others move with him, their eyes fixed on the same point – Mage Tiel and Mage Rithalis, both Plant Mages, both just as sensitive to this kind of magic as he is.

He quickly updates them on what Markus told him – the warning as well as the history.

"But this makes so much sense," Mage Rithalis says, her eyes sparkling. "No wonder we've had such trouble making a foothold in the earth. There's a consciousness underpinning it."

"The consciousness must be vast," Mage Tiel says thoughtfully. "If I had felt its edges at any point, I would have known it existed. But it just felt like the earth to me."

"That may very well be the case – and it may be hostile.” Elarion gestures towards the pile of vines. “Apparently one of Lord Markus’ companions is trapped there in body and mind. However,” he adds, stepping forward. “If we can better understand its nature, we should be able to better work with or around it in our farming efforts."

"This could change everything," Mage Rithalis says, excitement in her voice.

Elarion nods curtly.

"Markus was clear that it is not to be trifled with. I shall attempt to examine it, but I would appreciate your support and your readiness to pull me back if I'm drawn too deeply."

It would be foolish to dismiss Markus's concerns about falling into the same state as his companion, even if Elarion suspects it's less likely in his case. Non-Classers, in his experience, tend to move without nearly the care that mages do – a beast probably even more so – and Elarion has had decades to learn how to stay safe. As a Druid, he's also significantly more protected from the tendency of conscious plants to strangle or take over those they sense as invaders.

Mage Rithalis and Mage Tiel place their hands on his shoulders, forming a basic anchor triangle. Elarion steps close to the mass of vines and, as he does, confirms what he suspected – there is indeed a creature beneath them. The yelna. It shouldn't surprise him, given that he was aware the yelna is capable of plant manipulation, and yet somehow it does.

He sets his emotions aside, places his hands on the vines, closes his eyes, and concentrates on his Skill. Plant Meld, he invokes silently and sends his mind into the body of the plant.

Plant Meld combines him with the plant to a large extent, giving him a strong sense of at least his immediate surroundings, and usually a lot more. Though plants certainly don’t have the same kind of senses a human does, his Skill enables him to retain a kind of shadow of his usual senses even when melding with a plant. Thus, he can almost see the tendrils of the network extending before him like pathways even while he simply knows the shape of the maze surrounding him.

His first impression is underwhelming. The network he can observe from this node is less complex than the fungal networks he's studied back home. Fewer pathways, simpler connections.

However, as he steps forward, he realises that his Skill hasn’t fully mapped it as he was expecting.

Walking for a while, he finds another node, and then another one. Each time, his sense of the network grows wider and wider, more nodes meeting his awareness with each one he discovers.

Nonchalance is quickly replaced by awe. It's like standing at the foot of a mountain, dismissing it as unimpressive because all one can see are the foothills – until the cloud clears and one realises the peak goes on far, far higher than one had ever imagined.

Markus was right. This network is absolutely vast, Elarion murmurs to himself, feeling abruptly like an ant rather than the giant he usually feels when wielding this Skill. Even more than just being vast, he can tell from just how old some of the growths are that it is ancient. He’s practised in being able to tell approximately how old a section of a network is, but in this case, he can barely believe his senses. The section where he’s currently walking is relatively new – perhaps only a decade or two old. However, the further to the south-west he steps, the older the growth appears, each node approximately half a decade older than the one previous to it – and there’s a lot further to go.

There’s something else towards the south-west – something powerful. The origin root, he guesses, but something tells him it’s more than just that – and it’s something he doesn’t want to alert to his presence, for fear of being labelled a pest and removed with prejudice.

Instead, he focuses on observation. On understanding what the network is and how it works. He notices that what he took for a simple network at first quickly proves far more layered than anything he's encountered before. The nodes appear to be focal points of mana, acting as storages for their surrounding area, and each one is a different plant entirely – something Elarion has never seen before. This network uses trees as its canopy, fungal networks as its roots, bushes as its lower foliage. It is one organism made of thousands of different species.

How, by the gods, is this even possible? he wonders to himself, awed. He has worked with grafted trees, has planned and seen constructed vast gardens of innumerable plants that each interweave with their neighbours. Yet never has he seen a network that actually encompasses them in the way this does.

It is like any plant that is old enough or big enough or deemed worthy by some unknown standard is converted into an offshoot. Yet this seems to be mutually beneficial as the plants connected in are far stronger than the ones he vaguely senses that are not.

Fascinated, he digs deeper, soon discovering that the nodes are even more interesting than he had thought. Not only mana storages, they act like hearts, pulsing the mana through their surrounding area, then gathering it again, and more besides. The connected plants, felt only faintly in the network like small pinpricks on its surface, match it almost imperceptibly, the source of the extra mana. It gives Elarion an idea.

I wonder whether we could improve the survival of our own plants by matching this pulse? Or maybe the position of the plant in relation to a node would change the amount of Energy stored in the plant? What would happen if our plants connected in with this network – would that be positive for our purposes or negative? Or what if we could remove the network completely from an area and claim it for ourselves?

He has no way of knowing yet whether any of these ideas are feasible, but they're directions he wouldn't have considered without this experience. They're worth pursuing.

Satisfied with what he has learned, Elarion begins to draw back toward his body.

And realises, with a sudden shock of cold clarity, that he has a problem.

He's gone deep enough that he is no longer certain which direction leads back. He reaches out for the Plant Mages anchoring him, searching for the signature of their magic, but the thick sap-like mana of the network drowns out any other sense he can bring to bear.

He keeps his composure. Panicking will do him no good. His body is his. He has an indisputable connection to it that he should be able to find at any moment. He simply needs to be calm enough to sense it.

He calms himself and searches.

Then he feels a presence in a node nearby. Relief surges through him, and he moves toward it. It’s only when he’s closer that he realises it is neither his exit point nor the sense of his own body.

It is another consciousness, less distinct than his own. It has an overall shape, but its details are blurry and lines of green network connect to it – like roots feeding on a carcass, he realises.

Nonetheless, it reacts to his approach with projected violence and a familiar screech.

Immediately, Elarion knows what – and who – it is.

"You are Markus's companion," he murmurs, studying the creature within the node.

At his words, it stills, seeming to consider him carefully.

You are the cold plant human, the beast's presence replies finally.

Elarion is not entirely pleased with having his class reduced to "cold" and "plant," but that is considerably less important right now than finding his way out.

"Markus has been worried about you," he says plainly. Markus hasn't explicitly said as much, but he's easy enough to read.

The beast sends back a sense of discomfort.

I have been trying to find my way back, it admits, but I cannot.

Elarion sighs.

"Nor can I, at present." He considers for a moment. "Come. Perhaps working together we will find our respective bodies more easily."

The yelna studies him for a long moment, then chuffs quietly. I have nothing else to do.

Elarion chooses not to take too much offence at that, reminding himself it is a beast. Proper manners can hardly be expected.

Though the yelna is slow at first, the green tendrils seeming to sap at its strength, the more it moves, the faster it becomes. Soon, the tendrils begin to fall away and its blurriness begins sharpening, details appearing once more. Elarion assumes this is a good sign.

The only idea Elarion has is to follow the newer growth – he had walked towards older nodes on the way in; it’s logical that he should reverse his pathway on the way out, though it might still take a good deal of searching depending on in how many directions the plant network has been extending. With any luck, he will run across landmarks that he recognises were on the pathway that he took into the network.

Pathway…he muses to himself, the word reminding him of something.

He walks in silence, searching for exactly what has made his subconscious react. Meanwhile he searches for traces of familiarity or the signature of his body or anchors, becoming increasingly discomforted as he finds none.

Suddenly, it hits him and he freezes in place, the words of his father from the first time he’d used Plant Meld echoing in his ears.

Elarion, remember – while we use this Skill, there is always the risk of being lost. But, while we meld to an extent with the plants, we do not become them. Our mana is always different from theirs and will leave a pathway for us. Follow the mana, and you find your way home.

What is it? the yelna asks warily, pulling him out of his memories. Elarion waves at it to indicate it should be silent even as a satisfied smile tugs at the corners of his mouth.

He closes his eyes, focuses, and imagines the trail of mana he has been leaving behind. The sap is so slow here that his trail should still be mostly intact.

Opening his eyes, his smile widens as, behind him, a pathway of glowing icy blue stretches behind him. And there, somewhere far enough in the distance that he can barely see it, a point where the lines cross.

He glances over at the yelna.

“I know how to get out of here.”
