The seabed groaned under the weight of the walking mountain.

Each step the titan took sank deep into the silt, vitrifying the ground beneath into smoking glass, a fate that had also been suffered by its cousin up in the desert, leaving behind a luminous scar dragged across the ocean floor.

With each footfall, the surrounding water flashed into a furious white hiss that billowed upward in boiling plumes. It bubbled and churned, visibly pushed back by an invisible wall of heat radiating outward in every direction. The sea itself seemed to surrender before the titan’s advance, parting reluctantly around that colossal column of rock and flame, and only daring to creep back to its original place once the titan had already passed.

“Damn it,” Viktor cursed.

He had assumed water would win against fire, and he had been wrong. Reality was not so simple. The two opposing forces merely canceled each other out where they met, and whichever side happened to bring the greater quantity to that meeting point came away the victor. And, as it turned out, the heat radiating from the titan was so overwhelming it vaporized any approaching water long before that water ever had the chance to actually touch its burning flesh.

Well, it was not like he lacked quantity of his own to throw into the equation either. He had an entire sea at his command, after all. The fire might reign supreme in a localized clash, but the water here was so vast—especially now that he had gone to the trouble of expanding the third floor to accommodate that trebuchet project—he refused to believe he couldn’t defeat that oversized bonfire in the long run.

From a safe distance, the Acolytes of the Deep floated in a loose formation above the waves, their webbed hands weaving intricate patterns through the water. Behind them, the mermaids raised their voices in a hymn, drawing on power passed down from their ancestor, the God of the Sea. Globe after globe of compressed water arced high through the air, each one the size of a carriage, hurled with devastating force at the titan’s body. They hit with explosive concussions that could have pulverized stone, only to evaporate into clouds that did little more than obscure the monstrosity’s outline for the span of a single breath.

The titan didn’t slow down in the slightest. It just allowed the incoming attacks to strike wherever they landed, knowing full well they would vanish before they ever properly touched its enormous frame. There seemed to be only one thing it cared about: reaching its destination.

This might look like a hopelessly futile assault, no more effective than pelting a volcano with handfuls of snow and hoping for the best. But that was fine. Every globe of water sacrificed to the flames meant a little more heat the titan no longer had. This was a battle of attrition, plain and simple, one that would grind the enemy down slowly, bit by relentless bit, the same patient way wind and rain could wear a mountain down to nothing given enough time. So on that score, Viktor wasn’t too worried. Instead, his more pressing concern was: could he actually keep the thing trapped on this floor? That depended entirely on Celeste, on her ability to keep the enemy from forcing its way through to the lower floor.

He took a breath. The answer to that question was coming, whether he liked it or not.

The titan had reached its destination at last. The long march across the water realm had brought it to a small door on a small island. The titan loomed over it, filling the sky above that tiny patch of dirt, the way a grown man might loom over an anthill he had stumbled upon in his garden.

It knelt down and raised one hand, from which extended a single, colossal finger. The tip blazed as its hue shifted from molten orange to searing white, then into that unbearable, sky-blue intensity that made Viktor’s borrowed vision throb in protest.

Here we go again.

The finger pressed against the door, and needless to say, that piece of wood was vaporized instantly. Then came the old Core Room behind it, and then everything else in turn, layer after layer simply ceasing to exist under that blinding touch, until the staircase leading down to the fourth floor was fully revealed.

That was it. The access point between two Folded Chambers, the Domain of Water and the Domain of Light. In order to descend any further, the titan would have to physically squeeze its colossal body through that tiny hole.

And so, it pressed its finger into the gap, attempting to force the opening wider, the same trick it had used on the chasm above.

But this time, the entrance did not yield so easily.

Viktor could feel Celeste’s presence beside him, her focus absolute, her power humming through every facet of her crystalline form. She was building the passage back up as fast as the titan tried to unmake it, the two of them locked in a brutal, grinding contest of attrition that neither seemed willing to concede.

Meanwhile, the Acolytes and mermaids maintained their relentless assault, throwing volley after volley of water at the titan’s back. In addition to attacking the enemy directly, they were also working to draw the heated water pooling near the titan’s body away so it could cool down, while simultaneously channeling fresh, cold water in from the farther reaches of the sea to replace it, keeping the supply of ammunition steady and unbroken.

He opened his eyes, pulled the bracelet from his wrist, and placed it on the table in front of him.

“Bring this to Orloth,” he said. He had shown a handful of his minions on the third floor how it worked beforehand, so the guy should be able to handle it well enough.

Seconds later, the door opened and a goblin walked in. It bowed to him, crossed the room to pick up the Reliquary, then got teleported away.

That was an artifact that could turn water into ice, so it should help cool the heated water. Not by much in the grand scheme of a battle this size, but every small advantage counted.

Closing his eyes once more, Viktor sent his vision back down to the third floor, and found, to his relief, that the titan had pulled its finger back from the passage. It appeared Celeste had won this duel.

That was when he saw the thing open its mouth.

The lower jaw unhinged with a crack that split the crust of its rocky cheek, and from somewhere deep within that impossible throat a roar tore free. A cascade of liquid fire and semi-solid rock came pouring forth in a thundering deluge, crashing onto the shore below. And—

—those globs of molten rock were moving.

They stretched, hardened, and took shape. Dozens of them. Hundreds. Though they stood tiny compared to the titan looming above, they were still tall enough to dwarf a fully grown man. They had limbs of cracked obsidian and cores of blinding white flame. They flexed their molten fingers, their bodies dripping with lingering heat. Then, almost in perfect unison, they turned toward the opening the titan had spent so much effort trying and failing to force its way through.

Ugh.

Fire golems. Not too different from the one Dimitra had once conjured. Except this time, what he had to deal with was not one single construct, but an entire army.

So that’s the game, huh?

The titan might not fit through that narrow passage, but its children certainly could.

They swarmed forward without hesitation, running down the stairs in a chaotic rush. They began their descent in earnest, a relentless procession of living embers marching down and down.

Viktor opened his eyes again. “Sebekton is still on the fourth floor, right?” he asked, turning to Celeste.

[Yes, Master. He remains at the cathedral’s gates with the Tengu and the Templars. I have also teleported the Ettins and Cyclopes, along with the remaining tomb guards and the rest of the minions of the great tomb, to join them.]

“Let me go there as well,” Galatea said, her projected form having returned to his side after finishing the errand of delivering his message to Renee. Her expression had turned serious, and there was an edge of impatience in her voice. Clearly, she wanted to go back as soon as possible to defend her cathedral from what was now bearing down on it.

Well, yes. He could send her to the fourth floor to fight in her armored form. But was that the best way to use her?

Sebekton and the rest should be able to hold against this wave well enough. The problem was, even if they managed to repel this initial batch, what would happen when the next one came? After all, he doubted this was the full extent of what the titan could do. Far more likely, the thing was going to vomit out an endless tide of fire golems that would grind through everything in their path. Until eventually, inevitably, they reached the Core Room.

It looked like his plan to wear the titan down through attrition had failed. A waiting game was no longer feasible. He needed to find a way to bring the titan down directly, and fast.

But how?

What could he possibly do against a colossal monstrosity on this scale?

Even the Apostle, troublesome as that confrontation had been, was still someone he could put a blade through. This, on the other hand, was something else altogether. Something he genuinely didn’t believe he could confront head-on, even with “Rekindled Ember” activated.

Viktor sighed.

So this is the power of a god, huh? A tiny, fractional sliver of it, and that alone had been enough to render the entire situation this utterly hopeless. All Xander did was utter a single name, and with that one act alone the whole board had flipped over.

The true name of a god...

He glanced at his rapier. Perhaps he could attempt something similar. Galatea should know her god’s true name, which meant, in theory at least, he could ask her to speak it aloud and trigger the divine residual lying dormant inside this blade.

There were several glaring problems with such a plan, however.

First, unlike the Abyss, who had almost certainly engineered the whole thing, he had no control whatsoever over what might result from doing the same. The whole affair would be, by every measure, truly and dangerously unpredictable. Best he could hope for was to treat it like a bomb, a horrifying and thoroughly unreliable one.

Which brought him to the second problem. For the bomb to accomplish anything, it had to be detonated as close to its target as humanly possible. And that, given the target in question, was rather easier said than done.

And third, uttering a god’s true name was an act most blasphemous. Galatea, devoted as she had shown herself to be toward the Lord of Light, would very likely refuse to do something so disrespectful to the deity she had been serving for who knew how long.

Wait.

What about a god she felt no such reverence for, then?

And so, he turned to his Guardian. “You once told me you knew the true name of the Great One of the Deep, right?”
