Olethros turned to me with a cunning smile. “I would ask that you grant these two their wish, Captain.”

Captain?

His eyes opened with a bright emerald flash. As he leaned forward slightly, his voice dropped to a commanding tone. “I request you give Bodhil and Beigaldi the General’s eye immediately and without a second thought.”

“…What if it’s already too late?”

He tilted his head, his hand caressing his chin. “​I don’t believe you’re under any obligations to share your concerns. It’s not like they’d listen to your warnings.”

He was certainly playing games, but it was clear I was on his team.

“But Relias knows we have it," I objected. "Once they use it and go off on their own to free him, they’ll be—”

“The target of any traps,” Olethros confirmed. “A win for us, to be sure.”

Beigaldi and Bodhil stared hard at each other, their dark auras pulsing.

“We should wait until we are a little stronger,” Olethros advised, more to me than them. “But not so long that he has time to reset his traps. It would be nice to attack as one when we are ready, but that is an option that would require time and—”

“A month!” Bodhil snapped angrily, pushing herself away from the table in disgust. “And only that long!”

“How generous,” Olethros muttered, despite a small smile forming once again on his lips. “But surely you will help us recruit?”

Bodhil looked around the room, though it was hard to decide what her flames had settled on. “I’ve seen worse pits to gather hordes…”

“We’re taking anyone we can get,” Nora advised. “We don’t care what they’re made of, as long as they show it.”

Bodhil’s eyeflames narrowed as she sneered at her. “What does that mean—”

“Show me what you’re made of!” Nora shouted, showing off her bicep guns.

I started to laugh, but everyone else gave me a dour look.

“It’s… Okay, look, it doesn’t matter what she’s parodying, because she’s on target.” I gestured broadly to emphasize my point. “Don’t discount someone just because of what they are. We need all sorts of sentient beings out there, and we’re not all subject matter experts in everything. Just make sure they’re not going to stab us in the back, okay?”

Bodhil hesitated, then muttered. “First the General, then Relias…. And now you.”

I blinked. “What’s this?”

She looked in the opposite direction. “Recruiting outside the ranks,” she replied.

Tetora’s ears flattened. “Relias isn’t recruiting. Forcing demons into the form of an Angel isn’t—”

“They’re not all demons.”

“What?!” came from several of us.

Bodhil turned smug, but her posture suggested it was more a surface-level instinct than any deep-seated conviction. “So I’m one of your first rescues? I can see how you’d want to get a powerful being like me on your side.”

“What else is he turning into Angels?!” Tetora bellowed, his face full of well-earned fear.

Bodhil’s flames narrowed on him. “You might just be the second-to-last tiger left in Speranza.” She pulled absently at her pointed ear. “Your sister was too fast, though.”

“Taika!” he cried.

Nora was already on it, her phone clicking along with her finger taps. “She’s in a place called Nearhaven.”

Everyone exchanged glances, but no one had any firm ideas about the location.

Nora looked up at the ceiling. “Galenus!”

He appeared with a soft buzz. “I’m already searching,” he advised as he adjusted his wire-rim glasses. “I will contact you with any leads.”

Nora turned to a very rigid Tetora, who was now two minutes into a bout of personal crisis. “I have some ideas. But I’m going to need your help with Vernie to pull them off. Do you think you can hold yourself together?”

Tetora snarled, bearing his teeth. “I’m Iron Tiger Tetora!”

Nora nodded. “Grandmaster, even. So come help out a squishy, okay?”

Tetora shook his head, his ears swiveling back. “You’re not a squishy. You have thorns. Magic ones.”

Nora grinned.

“I can help too,” I added. “Just tell me what—”

“I think the three of us can handle it,” Nora replied as she tucked her phone back into her pocket.

“But shouldn’t I—”

“Delegate smaller tasks to the group members?” She spun back, pointing at me with one finger. “Excellent idea! That’ll leave you time to go see what’s up with your horse.”

My heart thundered for several beats. “Aurum?!”

“Yes, that one. He’s in Castle Amantia Town.”

“What’s he doing all the way out there? Is Lieutenant Arlena with him?”

“Dunno, and nope. She’s in Chairo.” Nora stretched up on her tiptoes and clasped her hands behind her back. “Maybe you want to go pick him up?”

I thought about what happened to Cinder, my first horse, and my hands clenched at my sides. “What if he gets hurt because of me? I...”

Olethros stepped forward, his gold staff forming in his hand. “I’ll go with you.”

I turned to him, searching his unreadable face. “But aren’t you still feeling a little off?”

He twirled his staff before planting it on the floor. “I believe the three of us can handle stealing a single steed.”

“Who else is going to help?”

“Galenus,” he advised, as Nora handed me my old disguise pin. “He’ll portal us in and out.”

Even a glare as suspicious as it could be elicited no confessions of collusion from either of them.

“Alright...”

***Castle Amantia Town’s main street was choked with citizens awaiting something from one end, but the air felt more suffocating than festive. Banners hung from the buildings limply in the cold, dead air, as if the wind couldn’t be bothered to play with them. People stood shoulder to shoulder with forced smiles never reaching their eyes, and their dull roar was quieter than their numbers demanded.

One woman turned to the man next to her. “I know he’s the oldest,” she said with a harsh sniff, pulling her shawl tight. “But isn’t it too soon?”

He shook his head, quickly flashing a forced smile to a nearby soldier in crimson livery. “Not now, Maude.”

Olethros waved me back from the edge of the road, his expression darkening.

“Prince Victor is expected to arrive shortly for his coronation,” he said once we tucked ourselves away behind a building. “King Saulus passed on last fortnight.”

No...

“What about Prince Mito?” I asked, uneasily.

His eyes flashed. “The bad man has declared him missing.”

I frowned, my mind racing through the implications. “No. He was on Nora's contact list. I think we’d better—”

A loud, angry whinny came from the main street.

Without preamble, I rushed forward, pushing myself between a couple to get a better look.

The crown idiot dared to ride my warhorse!

As I frothed at the mouth, Olethros pulled me back once more.

“We need a plan!” I whispered tersely. “Just look at what they did to him!”

“I not only see, but I also hear it as well,” he agreed.

It was just awful. His mane had been curled and covered in countless bows tied with bells, the clamor of his movements reducing him even below a prancing show pony. They’d also taken his coloring too far, painting his hooves a tacky gold.

“A distraction,” I mumbled. “Maybe we can—”

“Pull off your pin,” he suggested. “You’ll be perfect.”

“I was thinking about something to distract them away from us.”

Olethros extended his hand. “Trust me. There is simplicity in a stealthy strike.”

I grasped the pin. “Alright, have it your way.”

Shadowy vines encapsulated us, only to spit us out just a few feet in front of our target.

“Aurum!” I cried, just as the warhorse surged upward.

His bells shrieked as he reared to full height, his forelegs whirling. Prince Victor didn’t even manage a full curse before he hit the cobblestones, as Aurum twisted hard to the left and bucked him off.

The crowd gasped and screamed as I lunged for his reins. “It’s me! It’s me.”

He tossed his head a few times before slowing, catching my scent.

I heard several twangs of crossbows, but Olethros’s summoned barrier protected us.

“Let’s get out of here,” I declared, even as the shadow vines reformed around us.

“He wants to know why you abandoned him,” Olethros explained once we were back in what I called The Parlour, the first point of entry into Aziza's Bastion. “Perhaps a few confessions might put him at ease?”

I was still failing to untangle even a single bow from Aurum’s mane.

“I couldn’t take you to the Tower of the Demon King,” I said, my palms raised in surrender to the skittish steed. “It would have been too dangerous.”

Olethros tilted his head, listening to something Aurum wasn’t visibly saying. The horse only flicked an ear, refusing to sniff me.

“You didn’t explain that to him,” he concluded.

“I don’t know how to explain something like that to a horse.”

Olethros darkened slightly. “He says you left without saying anything at all.”

“Oh.” My gaze dropped to the floor. “He’s right. I do that sometimes, don’t I? I’m not very good at goodbyes.”

“He wants you to promise you’ll never abandon him again.”

That landed harder than it should have.

“I can’t promise that,” I admitted quietly. “It would probably be a lie.”

I rested my forehead briefly against Aurum’s, his bells chiming softly between us.

“I might have to leave again. I won’t risk your well-being to make myself feel better. That would be selfish.” I swallowed. “But if I do have to go, I promise I’ll say goodbye properly. No more sudden disappearances, I promise.”

Aurum huffed, relaxing a little.

My fingers slid into his mane, working at the first of many ribbons. “Let him know I’ll pair it with a whole sack of apples, so he understands it’s not because of him. And until then… Well, we’ll just have to make good memories of our time together. Can you ask him to agree to that?”

Olethros was quiet for several moments.

“No.”

I blinked. “No? He doesn’t agree?”

“No, I will not convey your proposal.”

“Olethros?”

I turned, but he had already faded out of view.
