“Hey, cool hat, Clay! You guys are done faster than I expected!” Lora greeted as the party of triumphant adventurers arrived at the cafe on the outskirts of Locker.

It was a quaint place with outdoor seating, where the woman had been studying a schedule while she sipped her smoothie. The shack was only big enough for three or four people inside, but the two workers were consistently busy with the crowd that was visiting. While not even close to the center of town, it still seemed very popular.

Trevor and Adabelle sat down across from Lora while Clay expressed interest in the beach behind them. They were still in a mostly humanoid form, and tipped their new pirate’s hat like Wayne would in greeting before leaving to go dig through the sand.

The teleporter closed her schedule and smiled. “How did it go?”

“Very well,” Adabelle answered.

“Yup,” Trevor agreed. “Got a level up and everything.”

“Awesome! Those are just the best,” Lora said, and the courier nodded at her wise words.

“How long were you expecting to wait on us?” Trevor asked curiously.

“Oh, a couple more hours,” she replied with a shrug. “It’s a new dungeon, ya know? I figured you’d be more cautious, especially with Trevor here. No offense, of course.”

“None taken,” the courier chuckled. “If it wasn’t for the good match up, I’d have been dead weight.”

“That’s a little harsh, Trevor,” Adabelle admonished. “Tons of adventuring parties would love to have someone like you on their team. You have a lot more versatility than you think, and I’m not just talking about Clay. I wish I had you for more than a few dungeon dives I’ve gone on in the past.”

“Thanks, lady,” he replied, smiling. “I might not be an adventurer, but I do have some tricks up my sleeves. Clay is definitely most of them, though. They figured out the right path forward and safely set off a lot of traps that could have really hurt us. And, of course, my wife was the MVP, which should surprise no one.”

“Oh, stop,” she said, putting her arm and tail around her husband. “It was a team effort. Everything went so smoothly because we worked together.”

“Yup. I especially liked being danced around the bad guys,” Trevor teased before looking at Lora. “I mean that literally, too. She dipped me under a sword at one point.”

The woman had been smiling, elbows on the table and her face in her hands, as she watched them. She giggled at the image he put in her head. “That certainly sounds like an unconventional fighting style. But, hey, if it works it works, right?”

“Precisely,” Adabelle agreed.

Lora sat up and clapped her hands together. “Alright! So! You have two options. We can either go to Paladona proper and you can get right into the festivities, or I can take you to the place you’re staying at. Considering I heard the ghost dungeon can be a little… gross, I’m going to assume the latter?”

“I’ll be the first to admit that I’m beat, so that second one does sound good,” Trevor said as he shifted his feet.

Both his and Adabelle’s armor had been wet and gross by the time they left the dungeon, but they had taken the time to change inside Arny’s hut with his permission. While he was as clean as he could be without having taken a shower, he didn’t feel that way at all. His wife also nodded at the sentiment.

“Sounds good! Give me just one moment before we go,” Lora requested. She downed the rest of her smoothie, before standing up and dashing to the counter to return the glass.

Leaning against Adabelle, Trevor sent a small nudge through his connection with Clay. He could feel the earth elemental’s acceptance as they stopped, turned around, and started coming back. They were getting strange looks from the locals, anyway, so it was probably better not to let them wander around.

“How do you think we did compared to the usual crew?” Trevor mused as he watched his familiar return, Tanazi’s hat still on their head.

“Considering you have over sixty pristine armors in your inventory right now?” Adabelle asked with a light laugh. “I think whoever is going to receive those will be sad that we’re leaving after only one haul.”

He thought about that for a moment before nodding. “That’s fair. I’m still surprised they gave up like that in the end.”

“I think Tanazi is softer than he puts on,” she replied as she rubbed her husband’s back. “Arny mentioned that he knew their armor was used as materials, and now we know he used to be a Summoned. He certainly fought like one. I think he was entertained, knew that it was better if they stayed on schedule, and gave up to help more people.”

“Yeah?”

“Yes, I think so,” Adabelle confirmed. “You can learn a lot about the kind of person someone is by how they fight. Aside from his first strike, Tanazi didn’t really try any feints or tricks. He was straightforward and didn’t complain about my mobility or strength. I would hesitate to call him honorable, but his crew adores him and that says a lot about his leadership. He plays the bad guy, but he’s not.”

“That’s some good insight, lady.”

“I’m a people person, too, you know,” she teased.

“Alright, all done here!” Lora announced as she returned. “I’d offer to get you something, but there’s plenty back at your temporary Paladona residence so don’t you worry about a thing.”

“Sounds good. I had a snack earlier, but some real food would really hit the spot,” Trevor said. “Preferably after a shower, though.”

“Definitely after a shower,” Adabelle agreed.

“Perfect. Shall we?” Lora asked, holding out her hands.

Clay had already separated their husk into separate pieces for Trevor, and they were collected along with the hat and Adabelle’s gear. Once every piece of magic had been stored, they took Lora’s hands so they could be teleported.

The area they found themselves in next wasn’t so different from the one they just left, but it was far more secluded than a well-known cafe. There were only two buildings near the road they stood on. Both of them looked like colonial farmhouses that were sitting between the beach and the jungle.

Lora stumbled slightly, and Adabelle caught her. “Are you okay?” she asked.

“Peachy keen, jellybean!” the woman answered before standing up straight and shaking herself off. “We’ve gone a long way today. I don’t generally hop around like this so much. But, I promise I’m fine! Nothing a little sit can’t fix. I’m going to take it easy for the rest of the day.”

“I’m glad you get a break, at least,” Trevor said as he summoned Clay’s stoat husk for them. They quickly jumped inside and clambered up to their master’s shoulder. The earth elemental wasted no time rubbing their face against his cheek, and he petted them. “What should I do about the armor?”

“Keep them for now,” Lora replied. “I’ll have the local magicsman stop by tomorrow morning and he’ll do the whole quest giving thing for you. He’ll also have the stuff to take to other places, so you don’t gotta worry about that. Going to be super easy.”

“Sure sounds like it.”

The woman nodded before gesturing towards one of the buildings. “So, this place is yours, along with this whole beach. There’s food, beds, shower, other stuff. I’ll be in the next house over, probably reading, so don’t hesitate to reach out if you need anything. And I mean anything. I can get you stuff lickity split if it’s not already there, and I aim to please.”

Turning, Lora pointed down the road. “Paladona is that way, about a mile. I was told that you have Clay, which is true, and they can be really fast so we didn’t get you, like, a cart or anything. The city is on the coast and you’ve got a couple days to explore it. Or not! I’m not the boss of you, you’re the boss of you. That means you can do what you want.”

Finally, she turned to the beach and gestured towards the ocean. “This is all private property so feel free to go swimming, build sandcastles, bury your significant other, or whatever it is you’d like to do. That island over there with the big mountain is Mount Cruon, which is sacred ground because their goddess used to live there, but it’s also the backdrop for the festival's grand finale. I’ll let that surprise you, though. Or, you can ask around! You have today, tomorrow, and the day after to enjoy things.”

Trevor whistled as he looked over the island. It reminded him of Hawaii and its volcanoes with an obvious dip in the top. There was no smoke coming from it, but his curiosity led him to ask a question. “Is that active?”

“Nope!” Lora answered. “Not in many, many, many years. The boss wouldn’t put you next to an active volcano, but an inactive one is fair game.”

“Cool. I wasn’t really worried about it erupting or anything. We’re far enough away that I don’t think it could hurt us, I just think it’s neat.”

“I agree,” Adabelle said, wrapping her tail around Trevor’s hand. “Shall we go inside and get that shower?”

“Sure. Do you want to stay out here and explore the beach, Clay?” he asked, looking down at his familiar. The courier immediately laughed. “I see you’ve already started.”

The earth elemental was already buried up to their neck and appeared to be swimming around. Their little stoat head bobbed up and down as if they were treading water. They turned when Trevor called, and waved before shifting so they were stomach up and utilizing a perfect backstroke through the sand.

“What an adorable little guy,” Lora said, holding a hand to her heart. “Really. Just an all around cutie. But I won’t keep you. Freshen up, explore, or don’t, just have a good time. Bye! Oh, right. If you need me, call me. Okay, bye for real now!”

Trevor and Adabelle waved at the other Summoned as she backed away, and watched once she turned and skipped to their house. The demonkin was the first to move, leading her husband to their own.

There was a pair of keys and a note inside saying that they belonged to the front and back doors, and to come and go as they pleased. The inside was decorated with plenty of pictures of the beach as well as aerial shots of the area. They explored quickly, finding that someone had put together a charcuterie board of crackers, meats, and cheeses for them in the kitchen.

“You know, Trevor,” Adabelle said as she eyed the tray of food. “I’m sure there’s a minotaur-sized bath tub in here somewhere. How about we have a quick shower, see what they have available for oils, and have a soak while we eat? It’ll help loosen our muscles up after all that work earlier.”

“Lady, you had me at bath,” the courier replied, kissing her cheek.

He quickly put together a small stack of food and offered it to his wife. She smiled as she bit into it, eating half, and he ate the rest. The tray, along with everything on it, was sent into his inventory. They went upstairs when they didn’t find where they were sleeping at ground level.

The second floor was a huge space that was almost all bedroom. There were a few doors nearby that led to closets, and another that took them into a large, luxurious bathroom. Though, like before, the tub was near the bed.

“I’m starting to think that Klar really likes these,” Trevor chuckled as he looked at the selection of bubbles and oils by the jacuzzi. “Not that I’m going to complain. We’re going to enjoy them, too.”

“We sure are,” Adabelle said. “I know it’s only noon, but we’ve already had an action-packed day. Would you mind if we simply enjoyed the beach today and wandered into the city tomorrow?”

“Not at all. In fact, I think I’d prefer it,” he answered easily. “That dungeon sure was something else. Very different from the earth elemental one.”

“I believe it. It might not be too much to say that it’s probably one of a kind,” the demonkin said as she leaned against her husband. He wrapped his arms around her, and her tail moved to encircle his leg. “How are you feeling, really? I know it was a little scary at first, but you seemed to get over it fast enough.”

“It was pretty eerie,” he confirmed, smiling as he rubbed her shoulders. Adabelle melted against him. “The heckling made it more absurd than scary, though. I’m glad we got to go and make some unlikely… friends? I guess? I think they’re still going to try to hurt us if we bring that hat back, but they might be good-natured about it next time.”

“I’m glad, too. I’m especially glad that I got to see the measure of the man I married,” she said in a low tone as she turned to face him. The noblewoman wrapped her arms around his neck, and he found his hands wandering to her hips.

“Did he live up to expectations?” the courier- asked, his curiosity shining through.

“You always do, Trevor. You always do,” Adabelle reassured him as she nuzzled her nose against his. “Now, about that shower…”
