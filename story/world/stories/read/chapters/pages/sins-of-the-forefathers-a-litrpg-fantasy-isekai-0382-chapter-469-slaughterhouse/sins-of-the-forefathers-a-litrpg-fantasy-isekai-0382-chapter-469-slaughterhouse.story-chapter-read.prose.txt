God, the sheer amount of bodies that we were encountering…it was insane. I’m not sure I had ever seen this many dead all in one place, even after everything I’d been through. The level of death on display here was sickening.

I’m not sure how long the Korvoks had been assaulting the Varlayans, but it couldn’t have been recently. Not with the sheer density of corpses our column had to wade through, as we approached the entryway into the city. And how had it all started in the first place? It was odd that we had seen absolutely no evidence of battle outside of the Gates of Anur Sur. It was only after we had passed the tunnel and descended the steps themselves that the first pool of blood and the first slaughtered Dwarf had appeared. There was a distinct smell of rot in the air, as well, like these bodies had been lying here for at least a few days. For as much light as the gargantuan sun stone in the mountain ceiling gave off, it wasn’t quite the sun. These bodies weren’t baked by the heat of it…but I could tell they weren’t fresh.

As arrogant as it might have been to consider, we hadn’t arrived right in the nick of time, right at the commencement of war, to prevent slaughter. No, we had somehow stumbled into a greater conflict, something still in the midst of occurring.

But it would be our job to finish it. And if it hurt the Korvoks in the process?

Well.

All the better. They wouldn’t be able to stop us when we sought out Nehushtan, then.

As focused as I was on the task at hand, Middle still had enough presence of mind to note the actual entryway into the city itself. It wasn’t quite as grandiose as the Gate of Anur Sur, as Olag had called it, but it was impressive enough. Massive, fluted marble pillars marked the delineation point from the bridge to the city proper, stretching to an admirable height to tower above us. An intricately carved archway sat upon them, bridging the gap ahead, and at a cursory inspection, I could see the relief of a rearing bird of some kind. I couldn’t tell what at a glance, and frankly, it didn’t matter.

The pillars were marred by the blood that had been splashed upon their surface, anyway.

Through them, stretching out directly before us, was what appeared to be a large plaza. Perhaps for the welcoming of guests, whoever they expected those to be, or perhaps as a local gathering place. It certainly would have been one of the most idyllic places to gather, I suppose, what with its surprising greenery in carefully cultivated patches, myriad benches, and impressive fountains.

But if I had thought the bridge had been the sight of fierce battle, this place must have been the sight of a war. It was absolutely littered with bodies, and to my dismay, not all of them looked to have been either Varlayan Legionaires or Korvokian berserkers.

I did my best to put the shattered trees and red-flowing, tainted fountains out of my mind. Instead, I started to bark out orders. “We’re splitting up! Two platoons to a battle-group! Gustave, you’re going to hold this position for us. Bleddyn, Maria, you two take your soldiers and go explore down the western path!” I said, pointing toward one of the three exits that existed in this plaza. “Sylvia, Bella, you’re taking the eastern path! Olag, you’re with me! Our goal is to find and link up with the Varlayan forces, anyone who’s survived this! Each group takes one of my Sprites, and if you find something, it’ll report to me! I’ll act as our communications for this operation.”

With that, I concentrated on Umbra Gemina Exactoris and created three of my more advanced Sprites, each with a simple slave mind that could transmit what it knew back to me after dispelling itself. But if the Sprite did that, the battle-group that had done so would be left without a means of communication. I highly doubted one of my incredibly stupid Sprites could track the group back down by itself if I sent another after them. This was a tactic we, as a group, had discussed in the past, so my commanders knew what to expect.

And what was on the line.

Each of my Sprites flew over to their groups, one to Gustave, one to Sylvia, and one to Maria, and then…they were gone.

God, I hated to split our forces like this, but there was no other option. The streets of the Hold didn’t look big enough to accommodate more than two platoons in the first place, much like the entrance tunnel had been. If, no, when we got into a fight, that many people in such confined spaces would have been tripping over each other, and onto the blades of our enemies.

It had to be done, unfortunately.

That left me alone with Olag, our soldiers, and Gustave barking orders at his platoon as they scrambled to set up a defensive position. With Skills, Spells, and Arts, it wasn’t as difficult as it seemed.

I motioned Walter, Meia, and Renauld closer as Olag and I conferred. As far as I was concerned, they were my Lieutenants for the battles to come. “Please tell me you have an idea where the seat of Government for Karag Varla is.”

The Dwarf grimaced, as the soldiers all around us didn’t even bother to pretend they weren’t eavesdropping. “An extremely vague one. I…believe, from lessons in my youth, that the Varlayans call it the Hagia Dynamis. Supposedly, it was one of the martial houses that Yorgunn would rule from whenever he was in residence. After his passing, my distant cousins adopted it as a government center. There’s a problem, though.”

I suppressed a sigh of frustration. “Just spit it out.”

“I don’t have a description,” Olag said bluntly. “Nor do I know where it could be. It could be any one of the domed buildings around here, and you’re right to ask after it. That is likely one of the places where the defense must be fiercest. But we’re going to have to check nearly every single dome we see if we want to find it.”

Almost as one, our heads stretched to look out into the ‘skyline’ of the Hold, and I noticed I wasn’t the only one.

“Son of a bitch,” I heard Renauld mutter, and I didn’t blame him.

That was a lot of domes.

I shook my head with a scowl. “We’ll take things as they come. Enough standing around. Everyone, move out.”

………………………………..

I wish I could have seen this city in any other state than its current one. The combination of the carefully sculpted marble, the bright light of the artificial sun, and the reflections of the crystal blue waters was genuinely so beautiful.

It was a damn tragedy that all of it was marred by the evidence of war. It surrounded me on all sides as Olag and my battle-group carefully scouted down what seemed to be one of the central avenues of Karag Varla. Death and destruction were all around us, and as I had suspected, not even the civilians of this city seemed to have been spared. We had only been marching down the lane for maybe ten minutes of the canal-laden Hold, and I had seen more savaged bodies here than I had even in Rhoscara.

For all of the brutality of Anguis’s mind-controlled soldiers, there had at least been a degree of cold efficiency to their slaughter. His culling of Rhoscara had been about wiping out even the memory of resistance to his reign. There had been no joy in it, for all of his many, many faults.

The Korvoks appeared to have had no such compunctions. They had rampaged through these streets in open battle with the defenders of Varla with a degree of savagery I found deeply sickening. I couldn’t focus on it too deeply, or I would fly off the handle in a way that was simply not conducive to the task at hand.

There were only so many skinned and butchered corpses I could handle before I started to actively hate these barbarians. I could tell it was affecting the men and women under my command, as well. Even if they were mostly hardened warriors, more than one of them had still needed to stop and vomit at the atrocities we stumbled across. I didn’t blame them. The displays that these monsters had strung up of their victims were enough to make me queasy myself. Thankfully, though, while their stomachs may have been tested, their wills hadn’t. None of them looked ready to call it quits.

Good thing, too, because it didn’t take us long to figure out just how the Korvoks had gotten into this city. After little more than a few more minutes of scouting, we found it.

And them.

In the center of what looked to be a central plaza, in comparison to what had essentially been a park earlier, the middle of it looked to have been hollowed out. The plaza itself was easily nearly half a mile in diameter, and I could well imagine it as the central business and shopping district for the populace of Karag Varla. But now there was a gigantic tunnel, with visibly mined edges, right in the center of it, almost like a kind of sinkhole. It was large enough that I think it would have easily been able to swallow the Final Line…

And all around it, throughout the plaza, were Korvoks. Hundreds and hundreds of them.

When we had stepped foot into the plaza, I’d been allowed a moment to see these…creatures in their natural environment. It did nothing to endear them to me. There was little sense of discipline among their ranks, with most either loitering about aimlessly or engaged in obscene acts. Dozens of skinning stations had been set up all across the pavement, and the bodies of both Varlayans and some of their own Korvok fellows were being processed every few minutes. If that wasn’t bad enough, after those poor people had been ‘relieved’ of their skin, the bodies were then being given over to literal butchers.

The sheer amount of blood staining this once pristine stone…

As dozens and dozens and dozens of mad, feral eyes fell upon our battle-group, I coldly returned the examination.

And did not find people. These were just mad animals, and the most merciful thing to do with beasts like this…

Was end them, and spare us the suffering of their existence.

In the absolute silence that ensued, I tapped my staff against the stone of the plaza three times.

Rap.

Tap.

Tap.

“No. Quarter.”

At my order, our battle-group, already on edge and ready to eke out punishment for the sins of the Korvoks readied themselves to do so. Olag’s group was composed mostly of heavily armored warriors, while mine was more caster and ranged heavy. Ranks of plate-mailed Dwarven bruisers arranged themselves into neat lines before us, while those of us more Magically inclined readied our little ‘gifts’ for the cannibals.

For a single, brief moment, I considered sending Middle and Core off into my Sprites to request backup from the other battle-groups. While they could only send a single message to me with the constructs, I could relay orders much more easily.

But frankly, I didn’t think it would be necessary.

Olag and his heavies began to beat their shields to a rhythm only they knew, and the provocation worked.

The Korvok’s howled as if they were little more than wild beasts, and as a single, frenzied mass, charged straight at the front line of our column. They tripped over not only the myriad corpses of their victims to reach us in their mad bloodlust, but each other as well.

To my surprise, I hear Fade snort disdainfully off to my side. Leisurely glancing down at him as I prepared a surprise for the berserkers, I saw his short, stubby little growing horns start to glow a telltale ghostly green. “Amateurs.”

And then he inflated his chest and showed the Korvoks what a true howl sounded like. A particularly strong showing from him, really. The sound echoed long and strong out across not only the plaza, but I believe the city itself. There was something in that cry that spoke of a Mystical quality it to, a depth that went beyond the physical realm. It was entirely possible that everyone and anyone who still lived in this city would hear it.

Good. Maybe there was one person in particular here who would recognize the sound and make a connection.

Might even spare us the trouble of finding him.

The effect on the Korvoks, though, was immediate. The front row of charging berserkers suddenly collapsed, as if they had lost control of their very bodies, twitching where they suddenly lay on the blood-soaked stone. Those behind them clutched uselessly at their mutilated skull, as if they were trying to block out a sound. Many of them carelessly trampled their ‘fellows’ that had collapsed, saving us the trouble of killing them.

All in all, with a single howl, I think Fade had somehow managed to break the Korvok’s charge.

I couldn’t help but laugh at the chaos he had wrought. “Since when could you do that?”

Fade shrugged his lupine shoulders faux-nonchalantly, though I could see a measure of tiredness in his body line. “Just something I’ve been working on. I call it ‘Disorienting Cry’. Not bad, yeah?”

I smiled at the verbal tic in his voice. I knew where that had come from. “Not bad at all. Now…you handled their front line, let me handle the back.”

With that, I raised my staff, concentrated, and triggered my capstone Skill.

Grasp of Calithorne.

The cookfires shifted first, swaying as if they had been disturbed by an unseen wind. Then, the previously merry orange turned a crimson red, not unlike the innocent blood they had spilled so callously. From nearly a dozen different fires, snaking vines of a similar shade emerged, covered in thorns and hooks to hover behind the rushing lines of barbarians like waiting, hungry serpents swaying in the breeze.

I raised my free hand to hang in the air beside my staff…and clenched it into a fist.

At once, over two dozen different barbed vines speared toward the back of the Korvoks, and screams filled the air as they found their targets.

Between Fade’s lingering disorientation ‘Spell’, and my own vines wreaking havoc through the Korvoks ranks…it didn’t take long for the battle-group to mop up the rest of these beasts.

More mercy than they had shown.
