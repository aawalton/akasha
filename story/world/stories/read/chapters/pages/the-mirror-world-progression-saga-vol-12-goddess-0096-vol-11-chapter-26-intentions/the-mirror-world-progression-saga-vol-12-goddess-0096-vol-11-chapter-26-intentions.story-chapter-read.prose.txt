Such was her shock, caught with her mouth open, and Azure looking at her in puzzlement, that Samantha had no immediate response to the communication.

Glae seemed to sniff derisively in her mind. Now in a calmer tone, he continued, “There’s no use denying it. That thing simply existing in the world again would be unsubtle as it is, and you were foolish enough to brush your will across it, weren’t you? I suppose it can’t be helped. A foolish child must touch the hot stove.”

So close to her ‘angry self’ recently, Glae’s commentary was fit to fan the flames and reignite it. Scowling and looking away from Azure, though mentally cluing her in about what had just occurred, Samantha replied, “As if I wouldn’t investigate such an obviously powerful artifact before carting it off to you? I may be a fool, but not that much of one.”

“Only fools think of degrees to foolishness.” There was a pause as he seemed to ponder his words carefully. “Congratulations on your success at Matrazere. I should thank you for leveling up so many of my Negators in that affair. I always thought the siege of that place would be a grueling affair, but it seems you had a trump card. A shame it was one-use.”

As always, every compliment is backhanded. I seriously need to not stoop to this douchebag’s level.

Deliberately getting a handle on her aggravation, Samantha finally replied, “I’ve got plenty more trump cards for the Capital, believe me. What are your designs in that regard, by the way?”

“I haven’t decided yet.”

Samantha resisted rolling her eyes. “Now that the less relevant unpleasantries are concluded, we should get to the point, hmm? I cannot in good conscience hand this item off to you without firm understanding and guarantees about your intentions with it. I need to know everything.”

“Do you really seek to test me, whelp? Do you think you’ve reached an equal footing with me?”

Despite that Samantha remained calm, there was no way she was backing down at this point. She could do it with tact, regardless of his douchebaggery. “If this is a sticking point, you’ve proved mine, and I will withhold the First Page from you with all of my power. I think I’ve proven what I have collectively. If you think it’ll be easy to take Itha Ranon, I’ll be happy to disabuse you of the notion. As for conquest otherwise, not only do you lack numbers, but you’d disrupt my march and thus your own interests. I doubt you’d do it.”

There was a dangerous pause from his side, and then sudden, rippling laughter. “What you have collectively — a wise choice of words, and soundly presented. You still fear a duel with me, and you choose to lean on what strength you’ve magnified externally. So be it, and fair enough! I won’t comment on whether I believe I could take the artifact despite your protestations, but I’ll acknowledge the strength of your position as more formidable than I believed you were capable. Indeed, we remain pointed at a mutual heading, and it’s far better to keep us that way.”

There was the communication of a mental shrug from him. “I’ll grant your wish, Sam: that item is the focal point of a breaking. Thus, it must be broken. Destroyed. That is its role in the ritual I’ve prepared in every other way already.”

Samantha felt her heart sink, despite the fact that it was technically good news, if true. Ultimately, she’d suspected it all along. Her eyes found themselves meeting Azure’s, whose hand moved to take hers and squeeze supportively. Samantha smiled faintly and nodded.

To Glae, she said, “So be it. Sharing this isn’t enough, though. I need a guarantee. Perhaps via your contest-making.”

Glae seemed annoyed. “Why are you so obsessed with this? Why would my word not be enough for it? What do you think you know about the First Page?”

“If you don’t know, I’m certainly not going to tell you.”

Glae barked a bitter laugh. “I know it better than you, I reckon. I know enough. If used haphazardly or with deliberate nihilism, it could confuse and befuddle the entire world and throw civilization into chaos. If tediously experimented with, it could change the way people think, steer them toward desired tendencies. ‘Them’ being everyone… all sapients, who rely on the same basal translation foundations.”

Samantha was a bit stunned. “I see.”

“Do you? I cannot emphasize enough how much I despise such manipulations. This is the sort of thing our mutual enemy loves, and I hate with a passion.” A pregnant pause. “You mentioned your conviction about me having it and using it. Double it, and that's me, toward you having it, Miss Cult of Personality. By no means will I allow you to continue experimenting with that vile object, as I suspect you seem to enjoy, from your evident dabbling in the darker arts.”

“I didn’t fucking exper-” Samantha began in annoyance, but cut herself off and restarted. “Never mind. Look, what you say is all fine and dandy, but-”

“I’ll do you one better, then, tower-raiser. Instead of explaining, how about I show you? Bring me the artifact, and stand in witness to what is done with it.”

Samantha didn’t respond immediately, as she felt a sense of paranoia about the invitation. It could be a trap; it could’ve all been staged to get her wherever his ritual chamber was located. But… he was going to destroy the artifact — something in her knew his words were true, as she’d sensed that was the object’s destiny before even speaking to him.

She didn’t need to go personally just to witness things, either, but something in her couldn’t back down from Glae, not in a situation like this. Aside from that, she wanted to personally be there to fully absorb what he was doing.

Finally, Samantha replied, “Very well — I trust I will be received with guarantees of hospitality and allowed an escort.”

“Of course,” Glae said, his words laced with mockery. “Bring your whole village if you like. They might even be useful. You have my word that you’ll be treated as an honored guest and unharmed so long as you refrain from deliberate violence yourself. Meant in the spirit spoken, not the letter, lest you think I’m being a devil about it.”

“Perish the thought. When do we do this?”

“One hour. I’ll send you the location fifteen minutes prior. I’ve done much of the work in my laboratories, but the ritual requires the open air. I’ll have my best around as guards, so you know. Once begun, the ritual can’t be interrupted. On that note, I need your word that you won’t interfere with the ritual.”

“You have it. As long as it destroys this thing in the process, as we’ve agreed, and you’re truthful that you aren’t using it to warp the collective unconscious or something, I won’t stop it.”

“So be it. Rest assured, you’ll see its spectacular end. I’ll see you in an hour for the conclusion of a World Spell, Sam.”

As Glae disconnected, her mouth was left hanging open yet again.

A World Spell? Could he tell me more about them? It would rankle to ask him, and he might not tell her, regardless. It was hard to resist doing so anyway. Little public information was available other than that they did ‘big things.’ Ironically, she’d sort of been involved in ending one and spawning another, despite knowing so little and not having an understanding of deliberate genesis. It was well-known that the Sage had conducted multiple of them.

If Glae is doing something to even the odds, that’s good, because as of now, I can’t, not in that particular sphere. I doubt I have time for anything this big, even if I had the capability.

Turning her perceptions back to the real world, her eyes fell on the First Page, still floating in the ritual chamber. “Prepare the artifact for transport. We’re headed for Traesh.”

Samantha and her crew were ready and waiting when Glae sent the location information to find the meeting place and ritual spot. They had mirrors distributed, surrounding Traesh, so Samantha would have the best angle to begin moving. Unsurprisingly, the location was in the mountains. It was in a small, sheltered valley within them, and specifically an unassuming grove in the middle of the wilderness. Interestingly, though perhaps not surprisingly, four antimagic towers could be seen flanking the location at high points in a diamond shape, unusually close compared to most areas she’d seen.

Hrm, does overlap matter? Not sure, but it shouldn’t, not for antimagic zones.

Samantha mirror-walked into the region by herself, then rocketed through the air most of the rest of the way. Once close enough for her purposes, but not in the immediate vicinity of the grove, Samantha unleashed a slurry of small mirrors and dumped out her crew from their containment via [Mirror Maze]. Azure also popped in afterward, and they all proceeded by flight to the location.

Old stones were arrayed in a circle, reminiscent of depictions of Stonehenge as originally constructed, though with enduring, perfectly-cut, preserved stones. The center inside the circle also differed, making a tighter diamond to surround the ritual grounds.

Clusters of guards were around, whether groundside or hovering in the air as ever-vigilant sentries. Most were Antimages, she was sure, but there was little question he’d have a variety hanging out. Many who were groundside had the aura of martial fighters, too. A few champions in armor leaned on the hilts of enchanted greatswords.

This has the strong look of expecting trouble. Hmm.

Glae was obviously waiting dead in the center of it all, standing in front of a floating platform with various accoutrements atop it.

Steeling herself, Samantha took a deep breath and began floating down. She mentally directed her formidable crew of many stripes and colors to form a perimeter synchronized with Glae’s own people, whether to support or counter them, if need be.

Here we go.
