Walking down the path to the C-Ranked dungeon he remembered from his last scouting expedition on the island, Nathan realized that he’d come a long way from the tired teenager who'd been flipping signs for Mr. Wong.

He had grown into whatever this system of a thing was. He’d battled monsters, killed them, and bathed in their blood, but the leader of the ogres was different. And it felt like there was more to unravel in that single fight than any other he had had since the apocalypse had begun, and yet he found himself wondering if he could ever understand its decision.

The dying ogre had given away its entire stash of Ra’ahal coins to Nathan on its dying breath. It had also urged him to seek out and fight a Demon King, and with the way things had been going so far, Nathan had a sneaking suspicion it was connected to the imps, Demon Lord, and Demon Prince he had fought most recently. At least it should be, considering the fact that those titles made up a hierarchy in his mind.

Well, that's one piece of the puzzle, Nathan thought.

Nathan let out a tired breath and cleared his mind. Thoughts about the Demon King and the remainder of hell could come much later, as even the Invasion would have to take a back seat in his mind while he focused on this dungeon.

He was only a few months into the apocalypse, but he was becoming a lot more bloodthirsty than he had been at the start. Nathan hadn't gone on a rampage yet, but he could feel the urge bubbling up within his subconscious.

The need… no, want to kill monsters.

There was also the fact that he had just a few skills, and no matter how mature he was, Nathan was still a teenager. And so, the thought of using his new skills excited him. It was the type of excitement that bubbled underneath his stoic façade even as he stared at the entrance to the dungeon.

Nathan hadn't been able to go higher than D-rank dungeons all this while because he had been running around for so long, dealing with threats to his stronghold and his people. Not that Nathan would complain, but he would still prefer his privacy every now and then. All that meant was that his outings in dungeons were as much alone time as he could get these days, so Nathan would not be opposed to maximizing the opportunity.

Dungeon Rating: C

Fully stepping past the threshold means you accept the challenge.

Wyverns, that ought to be interesting.

As Nathan crossed the threshold into the dungeon, his eyes instinctively widened, and his tight grip on Oblivion loosened a bit as he looked at the sky of the dungeon he had supposedly just walked into. Nathan wasn't even sure this was supposed to be a dungeon because, even though he’d crossed the threshold and received the system notification confirming that he had indeed walked into a dungeon, it was more likely to Nathan that he had crossed dimensions into… Earth.

[Welcome, challenger.

The Wyverns Nest awaits.

The dungeon is locked and will remain locked until you fulfill the clearance requirement.

Clearance requirement: Eradicate the Wyvern Nest.

Status: 0/3 Wyverns Slayed.]

I had almost forgotten what it looked like. Nathan thought, before dismissing the system notification.

The sight of the blue sky, white, fluffy clouds, and a beaming yellow sun left Nathan unbalanced. It had only been months since the apocalypse had begun, but it felt like years to Nathan since he'd last seen the natural skyline of Earth, and he couldn't help but admire its beauty.

It seemed like he was in a forest, although ‘forest’ was a strong word, as the surrounding area he’d appeared in once he’d crossed the dungeon threshold had very few trees, which allowed him an almost unobstructed view of the blue skyline.

Come on. Nathan thought and bit his tongue just to feel something… anything. It seemed to have worked, as the pain blossoming from his bleeding tongue snapped him out of this spell of awe, and he looked away from the skyline, reminding him that he had come here to hunt for monsters, not ogle at the skyline.

Guess what they say is true. You only value something in its absence.

With his gaze turned away from the skyline, he focused on the ground and what was right in front of him, which was, to a large extent… nothing.

Nathan tightened his grip on oblivion as he surveyed the area before quickly coming to a conclusion about where he was, or more aptly, where he was supposed to be. An island. And it was the smallest island he could ever have imagined.

The island was so small that his eyes could track its beginning and its end. In fact, Nathan believed the island's shore on every side was less than a five-minute walk away, which meant it was basically nothing more than a patch of land surrounded by vast waters on every side.

I’m standing on an island within another island… poetic.

That aside, it seemed like the Wyverns he was supposed to fight wouldn't come to him, so it fell to him to find them. “I guess it's time to go hunting.”

Nathan had been dropped in the center of the island, so it was up to him to pick a direction and begin walking. But before he did that, Nathan decided to take a moment to still himself and use his ears to listen for anything, but he found nothing except for the eerie silence, which was starting to get on his nerves.

It was almost as if he was the only living thing on the island, which wasn't making any sense unless... Nathan’s eyes shot up to the skyline once more as he scanned for the monsters that had been bred for his sword, but once again, nothing—just an endless stretch of blue in the sky.

“Hunting it is,” Nathan breathed as he picked a random direction and began walking.

He should have known that a C-Rank dungeon would be a lot different than the lower-ranked dungeons. Then again, maybe different wasn't actually quite the word he was supposed to use, but he was certain that there was something off about this dungeon as compared to the other ones he’d fought in.

Thinking back, the closest dungeon he’d entered that could compare to walking into another dimension was when he had fought in the Naga's Lair, but that hadn't even been as extreme a change of environment as this one he was currently in.

I guess I'm going to have to make do with what is in front of me. Hopefully it's not an illusion, and I am not fighting a monster that's trying to bite off my head.

That was the fear Nathan had at the moment because the scene in front of him resembled more of what rich people would consider a vacation on Earth, before the apocalypse had come. And the sudden change in environment, which had a striking likeness to Earth, had thrown him off balance a bit.

Still, he had no choice but to focus on finding the nest. Nathan could tell that the Wyverns were here, but they were surprisingly quiet. And while he wasn't quite a walking bestiary of monster names and their sizes, he did have a faint inkling of what a Wyvern was.

He supposed they were what one would call the children of dragons? Or maybe their distant relatives in some form.

They were definitely alike if the creature he had just spotted was anything to go by. The Wyvern, which seemed to be at the far side to his right, seemed to have blended with some of the trees high up.

The beast looked a bit like a dragon. And while Nathan would have to admit that he wasn't quite sure what a dragon itself looked like, because he had never seen one in the flesh. He’d read the books and seen the depictions that this apocalypse had brought to life in the form of so many mythical creatures in the corresponding shapes and sizes of folklore stories.

It’s only fair to assume the human stories did have some truth to Wyverns as a whole.

Then again, any RPG gamer knew what they were, and at some point they had either fought with or beside one.

[ Young Wyvern

Level: 93  ]

“Almost a hundred, shouldn’t be too hard to handle.” Nathan breathed, the berserker absentmindedly dismissing the system screen.

For something as mythical and feared as the Wyvern, Nathan had expected a much bigger creature. But to be fair to the Wyvern, it was a huge creature, but it wasn't massive on a scale that would prove too difficult for Nathan.

From what he was seeing, the Wyvern itself was about the size of the average family car. Maybe a bit bigger, or somewhere around that.

It was hard to see its full form and intricacies, as far up as it was, and how it blended into the trees. Nathan supposed that wasn't going to be a problem for much longer, as the creature itself was soon going to drop from where it had hidden itself, judging by the way its green slit eyes locked onto him, sensing prey.

Or predator.

There were two things involved right now as he considered what had happened. The Wyverns themselves were skilled hunters who preferred to attack their prey from their hiding spots, or they were creatures who had sensed him and his power once he had stepped into the dungeon and had instantly marked him as a predator higher in the hierarchy than they would rank themselves.

The latter thought seemed to bloat Nathan's ego, which he quickly squashed down in a heartbeat. Whether or not they considered him a threat or just regular prey should not concern him. What should matter to the berserker was how fast he could sink both his sword's hilt deep into the hides of each and every Wyvern on this imitation of glorious mother Earth.

It seemed like he wouldn’t have to wait too long to find out because, for the first time in a while, there was no fanfare, no words, no growls, no roars, just two different creatures of different races with different views, locking eyes with the other and, without a moment's notice, they launched themselves at the other.

Naturally, Nathan was faster. The blood berserker's size gave him a unique advantage the Wyvern did not have, despite its wings and powerful hind legs that seemed to explode with a mini boom as it launched itself up from the trees, tucked in for a dive towards the berserker, who had already begun his charge towards it.

The Wyvern had the aerial advantage, but Nathan wouldn't count on that being the case for much longer as the two creatures finally clashed. Oblivion in his right hand, Nathan barged into the Wyvern shoulder first. The force behind the berserker’s and Wyvern's coming together was enough to knock both creatures backward, sending Nathan skidding a few feet, with the Wyvern itself having to concede ground in a bid to reorient itself from Nathan's barge.

As far as the contest of strength was concerned, it seemed like the pair were somewhat level or as close as level could be, considering their different body types.

Now up close, Nathan could see the Wyvern in its entirety. Truth be told, the Wyvern looked more like a scaled lizard than anything fierce. It had a barbed tail, two legs behind, and wings at the front instead of two front legs, which meant all in all it had four limbs. The Wyvern's head

sported two horns, and it had a forked tongue.

The more he looked, the more it seemed like he was about to fight an imitation of the real deal, which in a sense was actually correct, as Wyverns themselves were not actual dragons, which made them kind of… meh to the berserker.  Then again, he supposed they did pose a reasonable threat, considering the sharp rows of teeth, the barbed tail, the physical strength it possessed, as well as any skills the creature might have, which Nathan was yet to find out about.

Nathan pushed thoughts about what the Wyvern could and couldn't do and turned his focus to killing the creature itself. The Wyvern seemed to have taken its time to take him in, no doubt, as it weighed him up as prey.

What a mistake that would be. Nathan thought, a grin appearing on his face even as the Wyvern charged at him. Its wings beat hard against the air as it rushed towards the berserker, sending a strong gust of wind Nathan's way.

The gust of wind was not enough to make him budge, but Nathan found it mildly annoying, considering all the dust, dried leaves, and fine grain of sand the Wyvern's gust of wind raised up around him. Come to think of it, he supposed that was the point of that line of action by the Wyvern.

It had sought to obstruct and obscure his vision and sense of coordination while it attacked.

Unfortunately for the Wyvern, Nathan had a friend called the wind, so even with the gust of wind kicking up, Nathan could easily read its movements. And it must’ve come as a surprise to the Wyvern, as the blur of abyss that was Oblivion descended toward it as soon as it made its move.
