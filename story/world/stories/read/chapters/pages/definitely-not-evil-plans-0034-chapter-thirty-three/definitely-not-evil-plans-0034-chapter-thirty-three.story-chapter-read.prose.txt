Monday November, 21

The last week had gone by fairly uneventfully. Every lawyer that Greg Harrison reached out to refused to take his case and several were advising he face the consequences for the email he sent out and by backpedaling it’s only made his situation look worse.

The news was reporting that the government had gotten involved and there was an on-going investigation into the legitimacy of the claims. While this should have made me happy, instead I kind of felt hollow and empty. The holidays were encroaching and all around me people were making plans, buying turkeys, fighting over travel arrangements. And I was…not.

Scott picked up on my attitude pretty quickly.

“So, what are your Thanksgiving plans?” he asked.

Even though I had told him at the beginning of our dating about being uninvited, I’ve skillfully managed to avoid talking about my mom or family over the past ten days. We were at the coffee shop. Kate and my coffee shop. I was maybe not so secretly hoping that I’d accidentally run into her here. So far it hadn’t been successful.

“Sam?”

I held a bite of pie in front of my face, apparently I’d zoned out rather than answering. “Oh! Sorry, I was considering. My mom uninvited me, remember?”

“I remember but I thought maybe you’d have your dad or other family or friends to see?”

“My dad?” I asked confused. It had been so long since I let myself think of him. And as his memory bubbled to the surface I tamped it back down.

“Do you not have a dad?”

“No.” I replied without further clarification. He didn’t need to know that I’d killed him.

“Sorry. Friends then?”

I wrinkled my nose. “Not any who are in the mood for me as a dinner guest.”

He was quiet for a moment. “Have you tried Kate?”

Hearing her name outloud hit me like a gut-punch and I may have even winced.

“I’m sorry.” He continued seeing my reaction, “I just figured with the Holidays it might be a good opportunity to fix things.”

“No.” My response came out far sharper than I intended. “She blocked me. She’s done with me.”

He reached across the table and took my hand, “Hey. I’m sure she’s not done with you. Sometimes people just need time to process on their own. Don’t give up.”

“It’s fine.” I said before pulling my hand back to scoop up another bite of pie. I popped it into my mouth and chewed slowly hoping to avoid anymore of this topic.

“It’s not fine. It’s Thanksgiving and you’re going to be alone. That’s not fine. Come to my place?”

“What?” I asked, genuinely shocked.

“For Thanksgiving. I’m not doing anything fancy. Maybe Chinese food, watching football.”

“Weren’t you going to your mother’s in Ohio?”

“Oh,” he said before trailing off. “Yeah, work needs me to stay close. I had to cancel at the last minute.”

I looked up and searched his face for signs of deception. All I saw was that goofy half smile and earnest glint in his eyes. “I really couldn’t impose.”

“It’s not an imposition, it's an invitation. Plus, why should we both spend Thanksgiving alone?” His smile widened and that dimple distracted me from my brooding. Then he had the audacity to add, “Jay-C misses you.”

I laughed in spite of myself. “Jay-C the stuffed alien?”

“He’s been asking about you. Says you’re his favorite human.”

“Tell Jay-C I’m flattered.”

“Tell him yourself Thursday. My place. Noon.”

Spending Thanksgiving with a guy I’d been seeing for less than two weeks seemed like something that only my mother would approve of. I guess her plan was working afterall. That wankclown. But what was the alternative?  Sitting in my apartment spying on Greg? That seemed unhealthy. Even to me.

“Okay,” I relented. “But I’m bringing something. What goes with Chinese Food?”

“More Chinese food probably.”

Tuesday November, 22

My nerves were shot. Between Greg, Kate, Scott and my mom I didn’t know what to worry about. Instead I played with my plants.

I’d heavily researched each of these plants thanks to a hand plant identification app. What that app did not tell me, was how to fix a sad plant which I had changed the colors of. I turned the pot in my hands using codevision to look at every single leaf trying to puzzle out what made this plant different from anything else I’d changed. From a code perspective nothing jumped out at me. Sure I still didn’t understand the vast majority of the symbols on the plant but color was still bolded and could still be adjusted. I watched the code looking for differences as I nudged the color of the wilted plant from an anemic green-brown to a lively kelly green.

More symbols changed to compensate than I could keep up with. I blinked and rubbed at my eyes before sitting back. The plant visibly wilted further. I tried to encourage the stem to straighten by lifting it gently with a finger. When it finally looked stable I moved my finger away. It was not stable and immediately fell and as the stem hit the edge a leaf snapped off.

“Oh no!”

I tried to reattach the leaf to the stem. I examined the symbols where the stem and leaf intersected on a still attached leaf and those where the no longer attached leaf had come off. I felt like I was looking at advanced calculus. None of the symbols were bold indicating I had or could easily adjust them.

I saw a set of symbols that varied between the two and attempted to push the symbol on the disconnected leaf to match the other. A white hot ice pick went straight into my left eye. I dropped the potted plant and reached up to put pressure on my head in hopes that the pain would relent.

The pot clattered to the ground with a crash. When the pain in my head finally began to subside a strange cold sensation on my knee and dripping on my leg drew my attention. The leaf that I had been attempting to alter had become a mess of goo.

Wednesday November, 23

In between baking a pumpkin pie I spent the day internet stalking Scott. I grew up in the nineties. I know a thing or two about stranger danger. LinkedIn looked fairly standard, he was listed as self-employed, had years of history engaging with others it even looked like he had participated in a coding camp for kids for a few years at his old company. That was pretty sweet.

Facebook was private, but as we’d talked about earlier, nothing is truly private on the internet. A single symbol change was all it took and I was able to see everything. I browsed through his photos. Most of them were tagged images of him and various relatives. A kindly looking older woman had her arm around him in front of a Christmas tree. Two kids and he were rough-housing in the wrapping paper scraps from what I could only assume was an unwrapping extravaganza. There were a couple tagged in from a guy named Tomasz who also regularly shared some kind of mechanical puzzle box games with him.

He checked out. Pretty normal dude. Let’s hope his air B&B didn’t give off the same serial killer vibes as Daniel’s.
