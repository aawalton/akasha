It never ceased to amaze Vir how revelations always seemed to come in the strangest of places at the oddest times, and yet somehow never while actively working at the problem.

Vir had been lying on his back staring at the ceiling of their chambers with Maiya asleep against his side after their experience together when the thought surfaced. Not the grand epiphany he'd been hammering at for weeks in the mindscape, but something so obvious and quite frankly stupid that he felt like a grakking fool for not seeing it sooner.

Assuming, of course, that it worked. He could very well have been wrong, but his intuition told him he wasn’t, and that had rarely failed him over the years.

The issue was that he had been treating the machine as something separate from himself. As a terrifying alien contraption that he was supposed to survive by being strong enough to weather the onslaught of prana it threw at him. He'd approached it the way he'd approached his own development until now—looking for ways to better himself to rise to the challenge.

But the communion capsule had shown him something profound. Or more accurately, had reminded him of something he already knew.

His consciousness separating from his body wasn't a new experience, considering Demonic Overlord had shown him that years ago, but the flavor of it had been different. Instead of controlling a dozen or a hundred minds, he’d simply shifted his perspective, growing beyond his body.

He closed his eyes and re-entered his mindscape without making a sound, and by the time Maiya turned over and hugged him like a pillow, his mind was elsewhere.

Vir rode the lift to the top in what had quickly become a ritual.

Except this time when the prana began to build, he activated Demonic Overlord, pushing his awareness outward and into the machine itself, seizing control of it the way he'd once seized control of his Asura in the field.

He was both surprised and wasn’t when it held.

It not only held, but he didn’t immediately die, either.

Aspect of the Demon God had, of course, been active the entire time. It was the first thing he activated at the start of every attempt, but in every prior run, the sheer volume of prana had simply overwhelmed the ability before it could protect him.

But now that he was in control of the machine, he stopped the prana from pouring into him.

That was just a stopgap, however, and it was only once he began cycling the prana through the machine into his body, and back out again, that the Ultimate bloodline Art finally shone, holding his body together exactly as Janak had said it would.

Even with the art active and sucking up prana at a terrifying rate, his body wouldn’t have lasted long without Yuma’s Embrace to rapidly heal the wounds as they formed.

The Third Eye Chakra and Iksana's Clarity worked in tandem to steer him away from the futures that ended the simulation early. The combination was something he'd relied on in combat for years, seeing bad outcomes a second or two before they arrived and adjusting to avoid them. It worked the same way here, except with such awe-inspiring amounts of prana, Clarity let him see a full thirty seconds into the future, though his mind wasn’t nearly sharp enough to take advantage of it.

Haste, at least, bought his mind more time, slowing reality to a crawl and letting him avoid the millions of ways he could die from even the slightest flaw channeling the prana. The firehose had to be aimed perfectly, with every cell in his body pushed to its capacity and slightly beyond. Too little and the cells next to it would overload and rupture. Too much and the same would happen, leading to a cascading failure that resulted in his death.

Never before had he concentrated so much simply on the act of cycling.

Yet despite all these advantages, and despite the fact that Vir’s tattoos’ powers surged to easily tenfold anything they would have been capable of without that near-infinite reservoir of prana, the machine’s prana density grew by the second as it continued to absorb the Ashen Realm.

In just seconds, his body would crack under the strain and his mind would no longer be capable of making the proper calculations.

The prana needed an exit. A relief valve to blow off the mounting pressure.

Entirely unprepared for what was about to come, Vir activating Aindri's Jungal Beckons, and that was when the machine opened up entirely.

The information hit him without warning. Concepts so dense that he nearly fumbled with the prana cycling to absorb them.

It was, mostly, a primer on the fundamental structures of living matter, but its breadth was all-encompasing.

Vir absorbed everything about the composition of organisms so tiny as to be invisible to the eye.

But that, it seemed, had only been the first wave. Information of their structure, of the building blocks of life, and a thousand other things flooded into his head, and his mind gave out.

He once again found himself back at the base of the machine.

He’d failed. But this time, Vir knew he would win. This was, after all, familiar territory.

And so he went in again. And again, Clarity and the Third Eye Chakra guided him through absorbing the knowledge that seemed just as infinite as the prana the machine was accumulating. All while guiding an ever-increasing torrent of prana through his body, directing his healing arts to mend only the most important wounds, lest they fall behind the rapidly accruing damage.

It was the ultimate balancing act, and perhaps the greatest challenge Vir had ever faced.

Just as he thought he was getting a handle on things, Jungal Beckons hit him with multicellular life and the near-infinity of more complex organisms it brought with it.

That was, of course, enough to overload Vir’s mind and trigger a reset.

Still, he’d lasted an instant longer than before. A span less than the blink of an eye, but it was, without a doubt, progress.

With grim determination, Vir plodded into the machine and began the process anew.

After going at it countless times and after countless resets, Vir thought he was actually beginning to grab ahold of this juggling act.

Yes, he still perished when the machine injected knowledge of plantlife into his mind, but he’d progressed so far from those single-celled organisms when he had first started.

During that time, the primary issue facing him had returned full circle to that of prana poisoning. Or, more accurately, prana explosion, given what would likely happen to his body if this were to happen for real, outside his mindscape.

His initial epiphany had bought him time, allowing him to cycle that prana being injected into his body throughout the whole machine, thus easing the burden. But as his mind lasted longer against the rush of knowledge, his body once again became unable to endure the force acting upon it.

He needed another solution. A better one, and the answer was both simple and obvious in theory. It was, however, anything but in execution.

Vir began the process, bracing for the knowledge that flooded into his head, having grown as accustomed to the discomforting sensation as one could possibly get.

His Arts guided him through the onslaught of prana, and despite the terrifying quantity, despite the fact that it did eventually overwhelm his body, at least it no longer overwhelmed his mind.

One could only stand in awe of a spectacle for so long, after all, no matter how great it was.

Except this time, before the deluge of prana became unworkable, Vir siphoned off just a bit. Instead of flowing it through his body into the machine, he sent it to the machine’s core, where the illusory reality was to be created.

First, he focused on the structure of the new realm.

He’d decided almost immediately upon hearing Janak’s plan that he would keep the Human and Demon realms identical to their current state. Not only was it simpler to copy directly, it minimized the risk of the result being a failure. With how risky this operation was bound to be, Vir thought it a prudent path.

Reality, however, was not so kind.

No matter how much he wished not to influence the world, he’d be forced to make some decisions of his own.This new realm, after all, would lack the Ash.

He had to make decisions about Mahādi and every other Imperium outpost. Whether to include them or not, and if he did, decide their locations within this new unified realm. About how the borders of what was once the Ash met with the human and demon lands—no longer realms of their own, but joined by geographical borders.

If he’d learned anything about those with power, it was that just because one could didn’t mean they should, and so Vir made simplest choices, maintaining as light a touch as he thought possible as he created the rock and the soil of the new realm and meticulously shaped the land with Balancer of Scales to mimic the world of his mindscape.

The machine-enhanced Balancer of Scales that was, seemingly without limit to its power or reach. The version of the ability Janak had had in mind, back when he created it and the Akh Nara as two pieces of the same whole.

With it, Vir pulled mountains from the ground and crushed land into valleys, and while he might ordinarily have shuddered at the prana required for such a task, he reveled in just how absurdly expensive it proved to be. Easing, if only for an instant, the burden on his body.

How exactly the machine created the land he envisioned, he couldn’t say. Some Imperium magic, surely.

But it worked, and with the blueprint of his Crown Chakra mindscape, Vir meticulously crafted the realms in their entirety.

It was not lost on him how strange it was to create an illusion of a world within his mindscape, which was itself an illusion. Nor was the fact that the task that felt like it took hours had taken no longer than a handful of seconds.

A handful of seconds longer than he’d ever lasted in any prior iteration.

Now, information about trees, about animal life, amphibians, and reptiles flooded into him, and Vir wasted no time.

For in addition to the knowledge came the awareness that his mindscape was no longer barren. No longer a lifeless and lonely expanse.

From the lowliest life forms to Ash’va and everything in between, all the knowledge that had been poured into his mind manifested in his mindscape. In forms which, he presumed, perfectly mimicked the locations and forms of their counterparts in reality.

Vir funneled as much prana as he dared out of the loop, sinking it into the new creatures he conjured up in his head as quickly as he could.

It wasn’t enough. Prana built faster than ever, and Vir knew he’d reset in moments if he didn’t plunge headlong into the next, terrifying step.

Despite his reservations, despite not feeling even close to ready, he began creating the first humans and demons of the land.

As it was, he focused on those he knew best. Maiya. Cirayus, Ashani, Saunak, Greesha, and Ira. Everyone he had interacted with on a regular basis, whose forms and personalities he knew best. He sought them out in his mindscape, finding them instantly. Maiya sleeping with him in the Mahādi’s central spire.. Cirayus in the demon realm, battling Ash Beasts that were currently spilling through an especially large Ash Tear. Ashani spending time alone with Janak.

It felt wrong to see the world like this. To see everything.

But Vir didn’t have the time to dwell.

He dove into their bodies, analyzing them down to their most basic components and creating exact copies in this new world.

But when Vir attempted to bring life to them, when he fueled their cells with prana, the quantity that surged through his body far outstripped anything he’d expected.

The simulation reset, and Vir stared up at the machine.

Janak was right. Vir wasn’t designed to survive this process because it was impossible. How could anything survive such an onslaught? All he could do was delay the inevitable until he’d achieved his mission.

Worse, the more his mind bulged with the information, the clearer it became that, just as with the prana ruining his body, mortals were never designed to learn this much information, and certainly not at this rate.

At least here, in the mindscape, the damage to his body and mind were erased when he reset and the knowledge was, if not entirely forgotten, then buried deep where it could not hurt him.

But what of the real thing? Not only would he have only one chance to absorb all the information of life and creation, not only did he have to channel the torrent of prana that burned his body away by the second, causing untold levels of agony that was thankfully absent in the mindscape… but he had to create an illusory realm and form a copy of every living soul at the same time!

It was akin to running the toughest mental calculations imaginable while shouldering the weight of a mountain on one hand and juggling with the other.

Was it even possible to achieve all that, even if he sacrificed his soul?

Vir couldn’t help but feel that he was missing something. Some key piece of this puzzle that might allow him to succeed at this insane task.

And there was something else as well. A question that had nagged him about this whole thing. If Janak was so sure that the Fateweavers were aware of his gambit, why would they allow him to succeed when they had placed hundreds of seeds that took thousands of years to mature, all to foil his plans? Somehow, he doubted they’d be willing to simply give up after fighting so hard for so long.

What is Janak not telling me? He thought darkly.

He very much intended to find out.
