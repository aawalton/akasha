Celene's office had not changed since the last time Lira had been inside it. A desk was positioned so that whoever sat behind it always faced the door, while her bed was on the left side, hidden behind a privacy screen.

Celene moved in first and sat in her chair. There was one more seat facing the desk, which Lira decided to leave to Aric; instead, she stood to the left of the headmaid in a subordinate position.

Aric took the seat as expected, while Deren shut the door before taking a similar standing position behind the prince.

After a brief pause, Aric finally spoke.

"I would like to start by saying that I owe you an apology, Celene."

"An apology for what?" she asked, her face shifting into a frown.

"My father wanted to give you the title of Lady Grand Stewardess," Aric said with a sigh. "He trusts you more than he trusts anyone else who serves him, myself included."

"And yet," Celene said, "he promoted Taren instead."

The prince nodded.

"I am no fool, Aric" said Celene. "It was almost impossible from the get-go for a woman to be granted such a title. This is Halthar, not Almara."

"I believe in time he could have made it work," said Aric with a shrug. "You might be illegitimate, but you are still a daughter of a duke. He could have granted you the title of Dame and put you in charge of the castle, especially since you were already doing most of the work."

"But for that, he would have to give it to me over Harwin and a few other nobles who were vying for that position."

Aric nodded hesitantly.

"By sidelining me, he knows I am loyal enough not to cause him problems," added Celene. "It is a win-win for him."

"That is not it," protested Aric. "He was willing to leave the matter until winter, since he was not eager to promote anyone over you. But last week, I may have accidentally forced his hand into making a choice."

Lira blinked at that. She had not been aware that Aric cared about the Grand Steward's succession that much.

"You see, I went to him to talk about Cormenfield, after I healed the children in Westside. I told him something had to be done about Taren. Either he had to remove him from his position, or I would have Deren challenge him to a duel over his mishandling of the famine."

Deren grinned at Lira and gave her a wink. It seemed the knight was starting to enjoy the role of attack dog and executioner after the whole Salvek debacle.

"So you forced his hand," Celene said.

"I did." He met her eyes when he said it. "I was thinking about the starving children. At the time, I did not care that my father could not simply dethrone a loyal Viscount, even if the wretch was incompetent."

Lira could start to see the shape of the plan now, how the need to remove Taren from Cormenfield had led to Celene losing her chance at the position.

"Argon could not strip a Viscount of his title with one hand and grant a woman real power with the other," Celene said slowly. "He would look like a populist. What is next, would he introduce an elected council?"

Aric winced but nodded. Lira could not help but smile at how thick the sarcasm was, coming from Celene, who was usually so proper.

Truthfully, the kingdom could benefit from an elected council, given how well the nobility was driving it straight into ruin. The Magari republic had one, and they were doing just fine.

"Rather than removing Taren from Cormenfield entirely," Aric went on, "he decided to promote him out of it, while putting Noah in charge instead. He, at least, is the most capable of Taren's potential successors, since he proved himself a competent manager when he served as quartermaster in the military."

"That part I understand," said Lira. "But if he trusts Celene more than Harwin, why not place Celene as Taren's second?"

"Because Harwin is already Taren's second in his role as Master of Coin," said Aric.

"Because Taren does not respect women, and would never let me take over his duties the way he lets Harwin," added Celene, shaking her head.

"No offense meant, my lady," Deren said, shifting his weight, "but it was always a long shot. No one in this castle would have expected the king to put you in Atropos's chair. Not with how things are done here, it's not only Taren; half of the old guard wouldn't listen to a woman."

"None taken," she said, though her mouth went tight. "I made my peace with it long ago. Though, some part of me thought Argon might still find a way, for the sake of what we shared in our youth."

"Maybe he could have," Aric said, grimacing, "if I had not intervened."

"No, the situation in Cormenfield had already lasted long enough," Celene said, shaking her head. "You only made your father do something he was reluctant about, but already planned to do regardless."

Lira let the silence sit for a moment, before she asked the thing that had been picking at her since the garden.

"Lady Rosalind?" she asked. "How much warning did she get?"

"Most of the planning happened yesterday," Aric said. "I sent for Lord Noah in a letter before heading to the ball, while my father held a meeting with Taren to inform him of both his own promotion and Harwin's. During that meeting, my father ordered Taren to bring all four of his daughters today, so Harwin could pick the one he liked."

That explained the matching dresses, then. Four girls, none of them knew which one was to be wed. Being a noble girl could sometimes be its own kind of trouble.

"His Majesty ordered the eldest to step forward, though," Lira said.

"Yes. When Harwin was told of the arrangement this morning, he asked specifically for the eldest," Aric explained.

"Really. Did they have a prior attachment?" Lira asked, curious.

"Not that I know of." Aric shook his head.

"It was a calculated choice," sighed Celene. "Everything Harwin does has an underlying reason."

"Does it have to do with the scandal?" asked Deren.

"Probably," said Celene. "Rosalind is twenty-five, and has more or less resigned herself to being an old maid. Her father got drunk and made a scene at her debut, which lowered her prospects considerably. She was still able to get engaged to a knight afterward, but before things could be finalized, the groom-to-be witnessed Taren harassing the man's own younger sister. The engagement was broken off. The young man refused to marry into a family with such an odious person at its head."

"So it was out of kindness that Harwin picked her?" asked Deren.

Lira thought of the girl's face in the garden, composed and dutiful, resigned maybe, but not angry or resentful.

"He picked the one who would not resent him," Lira finally said.

Celene nodded. "He chose Rosalind so she would be grateful to him, rather than challenge him every step of the way."

"The second sister looked relieved too," Lira added. "When she was not picked."

Celene nodded slowly. "She debuted two seasons ago. She is secretly engaged to a midshipman in the navy, who is meant to come forward once he receives his commission. Her father does not know of this, but her mother does."

"If Harwin had chosen her instead," Lira said, "it would have ruined her arrangement entirely."

"Harwin is nothing if not reasonable," Celene said. "Why marry a woman who would hate you, when you can pick the one who will be grateful to you instead?"

Beauty, love, compatibility, none of that entered the equation to him at all. Still, Lira could respect the reasoning. And at least he had not chosen the sixteen-year-old.

"So Harwin has a castle to run, a title to assume, and a wife who is grateful to him," said Deren. "All he has to do now is keep Taren from mucking things up. It's a very good arrangement."

"It is," Aric said. "I cannot pretend I have a problem with Harwin. I would have welcomed this with open arms, if it had not come at your expense, Celene."

Aric did not seem suspicious of Harwin. Maybe the coin magic was something known to the royal family, and was not something worth worrying over. But for some reason, whenever Lira touched one of those coins, an unease settled over her that she could not quite shake, a feeling that something about them was off.

"Who is going to run the bookkeeping tower now that Harwin has moved up?" Lira decided to ask, instead.

"Cedric," Aric said. "For the moment, until the new Master of Coin arrives."

"Hopefully someone competent, rather than another Taren," said Celene.

"Yes," said Lira, with a smile. "No offense to Cedric, but I doubt he could manage the tower on his own."

"Oh, he is competent." Aric looked directly at Lira. "Lord Kenth Kelroth is going to be the new Master of Coin."

Lira froze, her face gone visibly pale. There was no hiding it. Aric definitely noticed it.
