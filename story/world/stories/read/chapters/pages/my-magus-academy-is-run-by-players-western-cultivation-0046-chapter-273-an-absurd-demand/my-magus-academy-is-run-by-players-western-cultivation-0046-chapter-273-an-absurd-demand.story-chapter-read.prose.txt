“Oi, oi, what’s with that stuck-up lady?” ButaYaarou grumbled, his magic weapon resting on his shoulder as he stood on top of the wall of the eastern bastion.

SirLaughsALot snorted beside him, spinning his magic sword lazily. “Yeah, she called us peasants and said she wanted to meet ‘our so-called headmaster.’ Real diplomatic, huh?”

The eastern bastion, usually filled with the lively chatter of players heading out to explore the forest, was unusually tense. Around one hundred players had gathered, their eyes fixed on the woman floating high above the wall. She wore a crimson Magus robe emblazoned with the insignia of the Arcane Radiance College. Immense mana pressure was being emitted from her on the surrounding players.

“I demand to see your headmaster, Victor Asteriscus!” she shouted, her voice cutting through the murmurs below. “Inform him that Envoy Selene Vaelor of the Arcane Radiance College is here to take custody of the Sanctum and its resources. Anyone who resists will be treated as an enemy of the College!”

“Custody my ass,” NuclearCode muttered. “She’s trying to turn us into slaves!”

SirLaughsALot grinned, a glint of mischief in his eyes. “Well, you know what they say — no negotiation survives contact with the player base.”

Several players burst out laughing, drawing their weapons and casting spells in anticipation of a fight. Although there was no official update regarding this storyline, they had long since known that Magus Academy Online was a very dynamic game that could cause events like this unexpectedly.

“Lady, this is player territory! You can’t just waltz in here and act like a final boss!” someone yelled.

Selene’s expression darkened. “Insolent rabble…”

Her mana flared violently, shaking the ground beneath them. The players staggered back as an overwhelming pressure unlike anything they’d felt before swept across the bastion. Even the professor NPCs froze in place, trembling as they watched from afar.

“Holy crap! She’s beyond Elemental Adept rank, likely a Nexus Temporal Magus!” Inghelta exclaimed, eyes wide. “We can’t fight that!”

“Too late for that!” ButaYaarou roared, casting a Pyro spell and launching a massive fireball. Several other players followed suit, filling the sky with a dazzling barrage of spells and projectiles.

The attacks struck in a brilliant explosion — only to vanish harmlessly before touching her. Selene raised her hand lazily and, with a single wave, unleashed a crushing shockwave that tore through the air. Despite the academy’s magic barrier flaring to life, absorbing part of the impact, dozens of players at the edge of the blast were thrown violently backward, while the weaker ones were torn apart instantly, their avatars disintegrating in flashes of light.

“Damn it! I just joined the game a week a—!” Inghelta shouted as his body was cut into pieces.

ButaYaarou crashed hard into a wall, groaning. “Yeah… okay, that was a bad idea…”

SirLaughsALot coughed, barely staying on his feet. “We might need a… uh, Headmaster intervention here…”

Selene descended closer to the wall, her cold gaze sweeping over the fallen players. “Pathetic. If these are the so-called defenders of the Sanctum of Interdimensional Magi, then you’re hardly worth conquering. Perhaps razing this entire place to the ground would be more fitting.”

Suddenly, the sky above the Sanctum darkened, the space rippling with distortion as mana and elemental particles converged in thick, oppressive waves.

ButaYaarou’s jaw dropped. “Whoa! Such power… That’s not her power, is it?”

“No,” SirLaughsALot said, swallowing hard. “It’s the Headmaster. And he’s mad.”

True enough, from the heart of the academy, a handsome young Magus with blue hair and a Magus robe flew toward where they were. The oppressive tension in the air changed as both forces locked onto each other.

It appeared that the clash of two giants was about to begin.

From high above the academy, Victor surveyed the chaos below. The once-bustling eastern bastion was now scarred with cracks, rubble, and the remains of fallen players. Broken fragments of weapons littered the ground like debris after a storm. Dozens of players were scattered across the area — some barely clinging to life, others reduced to little more than mangled flesh. Although the players were numerous, fighting a Nexus Temporal Magus was tantamount to suicide.

Victor’s gaze turned cold. “So, this is the ‘new envoy,’ huh? Already causing havoc the moment she arrives.” Though it couldn’t help that such a thing happened when the hard-headed players came face-to-face with cold and pragmatic Magi.

[A powerful extraordinary life form has been detected!]

[The life form is expected to have early Attuned-stage Nexus Temporal Magus combat capability.]

Still, he could feel the sharp fluctuations of the woman’s mana — calm, composed, but brimming with arrogance. Even without reading the alert from the System, he could tell she was a strong Nexus Temporal Magus. The kind of power that could flatten half the academy if left unchecked.

But that didn’t matter.

He spread his arms slightly as mana surged outward, and the air trembled around him. The atmosphere thickened, pressing down on the intruder below. Even Selene’s expression faltered for an instant as her senses registered the overwhelming force radiating from him.

Of course, none of it was real. At least, this power wasn’t his own.

Hidden within Victor’s Shadowlink Mark, the Cosmic Rubik was amplifying and projecting a false aura — a perfect illusion of unrestrained power. It wasn’t just a trick of mana; it distorted spatial and elemental perception itself, convincing even the most seasoned Magi that what they felt was genuine. That Victor was a strong Nexus Temporal Magus.

In truth, he was still suffering from the effects of the Soulrot Curse, and his strength was far from its peak. But as long as the Cosmic Rubik concealed the instability within him, no one would dare to test it. After all, Victor was a master at playing pretend. This whole academy was built by tricking a bunch of players from Earth, to begin with.

“Master…” Lizbeth’s voice echoed in his mind through the Shadowlink Mark. “Should I inform the other professors or Valeria to engage?”

“No,” Victor replied calmly. “Not yet. Let her show her hand first.”

He watched as Selene hovered arrogantly before the battered players, her mana flaring once more and causing the academy’s barrier to ripple. If she was truly here to make the Sanctum submit, as her words implied, then she wouldn’t be attacking the academy so recklessly — unless she had another motive.

“Envoy Frederick came not too long ago to negotiate, and we reached an agreement,” Victor called out. “Are you perhaps an impersonator of the Arcane Radiance College’s envoy?”

“Impudent!” Selene retorted angrily, “I am the true envoy of the Arcane Radiance College! This so-called Frederick you speak of is an impostor! Is this how your insignificant academy treats an envoy of my standing?”

Oh? So she’s pretending ignorance rather than accusing me outright of killing him, Victor thought. This meant that Selene, or whoever sent her, truly had the intention to force Victor’s academy to submit, likely to uncover his secrets and claim them for themselves.

Victor’s eyes glinted with amusement. “How fascinating. The Arcane Radiance College must have become rather disorganized if two of its envoys are claiming the same post.”

Selene sneered with open disdain. “Watch your tone, Headmaster. You should be welcoming me, not asking pointless questions.”

“All right,” Victor said evenly. “If you claim to be the true envoy, then state the Arcane Radiance College’s terms.” He gestured lightly for her to continue.

“Ha, it’s quite simple.” Selene’s smirk deepened, her voice dripping with arrogance. “All we require is for your little academy to submit — open your protective magic barriers and allow our forces to occupy the Sanctum when the time comes.”

“Impossible.”

What she proposed wasn’t negotiation; it was total surrender. To accept such terms would be no different than handing the academy over and becoming a lapdog of the Arcane Radiance College.

“Then know that if you refuse, the Arcane Radiance College, along with the surrounding Magus factions, will join forces to raze your academy to the ground!” she threatened.

Her mana flared again, and faint cracks began to spread across the Sanctum’s outer barrier. Several players who had been slowly recovering flinched instinctively, retreating behind the inner walls for safety.

“So, the Arcane Radiance College dares to send an envoy that thinks she can threaten my academy?” Victor said coldly, enunciating each syllable.

Even as he spoke, Victor secretly sent a secret magic transmission to Selene: “Envoy, I’m not opposed to cooperation with the Arcane Radiance College. But please, save my face in front of my acolytes and staff. We can discuss the details through magic transmission.”

Still maintaining her arrogant and furious expression, Selene replied through magic transmission, “So, the Magus rumored to have destroyed the Merlin family is this cunning, huh? Very well, Headmaster. Let’s speak this way.”

Of course, Victor wasn’t going to submit just because he told her to. But the pretense would fool the other party and give him time to prepare the Sanctum against the surrounding Magus forces while also giving him time to continue working on the curse. On top of that, it would give him the time to clear up the “Buy Your Academy Time” main mission. If anything, doing this would kill three birds with one stone.

“Envoy. Grant my academy three months to prepare for submission. I need time to convince my staff and acolytes that coming under the Arcane Radiance College is a blessing,” Victor said smoothly through the magic transmission.

“No way. Three months is far too long. One month is the absolute limit. Otherwise, forget this deal — we’ll simply take the Sanctum by force,” Selene shot back.

“Two months. I only need two months. That should be reasonable for Magi of our standing,” Victor countered.

“One month and a week.”

“Miss Envoy, that’s still too short. At least one and a half months,” Victor pressed, his expression adamant.

Selene hesitated, then snapped, “Fine. One and a half months. But mark my words: any sign of you breaking your word, and the Arcane Radiance will march immediately. No more negotiations.”

“Agreed.” Victor nodded slightly. “We will comply. I expect the Arcane Radiance College to honor its side as well.”

“Good.”

“Also, Envoy,” Victor added evenly, “could you at least pretend that you’re being pushed back by me?”

“Hmph.” She let out a short, annoyed snort but didn’t bother to argue.

A moment later, she turned sharply and flew toward the horizon, turning into a crimson streak like a comet. Her voice echoed across the skies as she departed: “I will return in forty-five days. Prepare yourself!”

As her presence faded, murmurs rippled through the crowd below. Some players were in awe that their headmaster had seemingly driven away such a powerful Magus without even lifting a finger. Others fumed at the envoy’s arrogance for daring to make such bold threats on the Sanctum’s grounds.

Either way, one thing was certain: the players were stirred. And when the time came, they would be ready for the inevitable clash in a month and a half.
