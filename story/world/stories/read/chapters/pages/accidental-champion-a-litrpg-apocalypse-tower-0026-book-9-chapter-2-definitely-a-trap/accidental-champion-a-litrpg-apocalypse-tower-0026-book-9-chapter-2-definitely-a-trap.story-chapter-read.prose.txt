Twin suns hung high in a purple sky. Xavier stood on a large, green plain, wind swaying the ankle-high grass. Beyond the plain was a small village with houses made from wood and stone. The village was silent.

He took a breath—the air was sweet, something about the plants on this planet.

Xavier was in the Orin sector, awaiting a rendezvous with Palini. For him, a month had passed since he’d defeated the Soul Amalgamation and the World Destroyer. Dealing with the World Destroyer in Xavier’s Prime’s universe hadn’t taken long, either. He’d watched from the other side of the universe-spanning portal, pushing his consciousness through it to observe the World Destroyer meet its fate a second time.

For the rest of the universe, not even a minute had gone by since that had all happened. At least, until he’d set foot on this planet. He’d done a fair few things in that time—but mostly, he’d taken a chance to breathe. Yes, there were important things happening in the universe, especially after what he’d said to the old monsters who’d been watching, but Adranial had reminded him that once in a while he needed to stop and enjoy the fruits of his labour and not just keep labouring.

And so that was exactly what he’d done, until he’d been contacted by Palini.

Palini had told him this was where they were to meet. A small world on the edge of the Orin sector with a population of less than ten million.

Xavier had known something strange was going on when he’d gotten the man’s message. The way he’d spoken—it hadn’t sounded like Palini.

When he’d used his spiritual sense and The All-Seeing Eye to scan the planet, he’d found that not a single person was here. The world had been evacuated. It wasn’t that their veils were too strong for him to pierce—he would have seen them with The All-Seeing Eye if that had been the case, and there would have been plenty of people under System age who lacked veils.

Yeah, this is definitely a trap.

What he couldn’t ascertain was whose trap.

Palini had been sent back to the Orin sector to keep an eye on things. Xavier had defeated Sovereign Rewke Fouran when the man had challenged him in the arena for the Ventorin sector. While Xavier had won, Fouran’s soul hadn’t been inside his body—which meant the sector was technically still under his control.

Of course, the late sovereign wasn’t in a position to defend it. Hundreds of rulers from the surrounding sectors had seen the fight go down, instantly becoming aware of Orin’s vulnerability. Xavier didn’t think any of them would be able to move fast enough to capitalise on that vulnerability, but he’d still wanted eyes on the ground.

The other reason he’d sent Palini here was because he’d needed someone who knew the sector better than him to find where the sovereign had hidden his soul. Finding and destroying that soul would be the fastest and easiest way for Xavier to take control of the sector.

Xavier intended to rule the entire universe, in due time, but even with such a goal he didn’t want unnecessary bloodshed along the way. There would always be death—even when he’d taken over the Ventorin sector, he’d needed to dispense justice—but he wanted to avoid as much of that as he could. The sectors he wished to rule weren’t his enemy.

He wanted them united.

And the more inevitable his rule felt to the populace, the less bloodshed there would be.

Xavier glanced up at the purple sky, the twin suns, then looked over the plains at the small village nestled at the border of dense woods beyond.

This really was a beautiful place.

Xavier went over the message he’d received from Palini again.

Palini: [Meet me on the planet Batrah. Come alone. There’s something I need to show you.]

That message had been instantly suspicious. Xavier could have asked questions, but he had no idea what position Palini was in. If someone was there, able to intercept the messages he sent through their linked Communication Stones, and Palini was forced to tell the truth… it might very well lead to his death.

Which meant whoever was behind this—assuming it wasn’t Palini himself, and Xavier had more trust in the man than to worry about such things—then they would have to be more powerful than the B Grade swordsman.

A B Grade Swordsman who happened to have the ability to use spell patterns and possessed a Reality Core.

Xavier tapped his foot on the soft ground. His wings twitched. He’d been standing there for ten minutes of SUT—Standard Universe Time, as he’d come to think of it. That might not seem like long, but a lot was happening in those ten minutes. A lot he was missing out on.

His spiritual sense was pushed outward, encompassing the entire planet. In theory, he should be able to sense any spatial distortions in the area.

All was quiet.

Xavier: [Palini? Are you here yet?]

Palini: [I’m sorry, Xavier. I didn’t have a choice.]

Xavier tilted his head to one side. He felt the emotion in the man’s message as his words sounded in his mind through the Communication Stone.

It’s his family, then.

Xavier: [Oh, don’t worry about me.] At the same time as he replied to Palini, he messaged someone else in the Orin sector. [Have you got them?]

Xavier Prime: [One moment… All right. They’re safe now. The Damascus Estate was locked down. Some rather nasty people were keeping his family hostage.]

Xavier: [What happened?]

Xavier Prime: [The nasty people will no longer be an issue. To anyone.]

Xavier inclined his head. He adjusted the time dilation field wrapped around him and pushed his senses beyond the planet and into space above.

There.

A spatial distortion. He pinpointed the area with The All-Seeing Eye and saw three beings floating in space in front of a large portal. They moved slowly compared to him, but they moved faster than SUT. A time dilation field was wrapped around the three of them. Xavier saw the caster—the time mage had their eyes closed, intense concentration marring their face, crystal-topped staff glowing brightly.

Xavier estimated the time difference at roughly ten-to-one in his favour.

Well, well, well. I wonder who sent you three.

He didn’t need to pierce their veils to tell that each was B Grade. A hit team sent by a neighbouring sector ruler? He couldn’t imagine any of them were sector rulers themselves—they didn’t tend to leave their domains for such dirty work. They had people for that. Besides, the majority of them were too afraid of leaving their sectors in fear of their enemies moving in.

That was another reason the old monsters moved so slowly. No matter how powerful they became, the same was true for them—if they left their domains the sectors they ruled would become instantly vulnerable to attack, so they sent their agents. Powerful Denizens, but not ones as powerful as their rulers.

Xavier had a similar issue, but he wasn’t as worried about it with how fast he could move.

Time dilation was one of his ultimate cheats, and he would continue to use it to the best of his ability.

The other two Denizens hovering in space in front of the portal seemed to be powering up. Energy gathered around them. A lot of energy. Xavier didn’t recognise the spell, but it appeared to be combining their power. The two Denizens looked almost identical, though something told him they were twins not clones.

Though he didn’t know what the spell was, he anticipated its destructive power.

He wondered if it was Palini who’d managed to convince them to evacuate the planet, or if whoever had ordered this hit squad here had wanted to limit collateral damage.

Xavier found it hard to imagine it was the latter.

He tilted his head to the side and further examined the energy patterns of the spell that was being combined. It kept growing, becoming ten times as powerful as he’d anticipated.

It wouldn’t just destroy the world but obliterate it, turning it into nothing more than dust. He thought of the quiet village, those wood and stone houses, and the millions more like them all around the planet.

Xavier’s wings twitched.

He leapt into the air, pumping his wings with violent speed. He could have portalled or teleported straight into space where the Denizens had arrived, but he wanted them to see him coming.

He dropped his veil. Power radiated off him in waves, shattering the hit squad’s attention as he hurtled toward them.

Fear lit the twins’ faces as they sensed his power and saw how close he suddenly was. The time mage opened her eyes shock-wide. The glow of her staff flickered.

Then something shifted.

That shocked face turned eerily calm. The crystal-topped staff glowed brighter than before. And a smile touched the time mage’s lips.

The twin Denizens didn’t look afraid anymore either. He couldn’t sense even a small amount of fear coming off them. In fact, he hadn’t sensed fear from anyone. They’d only looked afraid.

The time dilation field encompassing the hit squad changed. He felt the time distortion strengthen rapidly, though it didn’t appear as though the time mage was straining anymore.

Before Xavier could reach them, the time dilation field matched speed to his own.

Then, it became faster.

What the hell?

Xavier’s mind worked. Fast. He manifested half a dozen spell patterns and rapidly increased his mental processing power.

He’d come here knowing this was a trap. He’d even brought Xavier Prime into this universe to ensure Palini and his family were safe—in his universe, a rendezvous like this would be happening soon after.

They hadn’t wanted to chance anything.

But Xavier hadn’t seriously expected whatever threat greeted him on the corner of Orin space to actually be, well, threatening.

Over confidence rearing its ugly head again.

The spell’s destructive energy continued to build until it finally reached its peak. The spell slingshotted toward him, hurtling through space. Even with all Xavier had gained over the years he wasn’t sure this body would survive a direct hit from that much combined power—enough to turn an entire planet into dust.

He wasn’t in a sector he ruled. He wasn’t backed by the power of two galaxies from alternate universes. He didn’t have his allies at his back.

Once again, it was only him.

While Xavier hadn’t expected the time mage to be able to push their time dilation field this fast, they weren’t the only ones who’d initially been holding back, because even when Xavier was alone… he wasn’t actually alone.

The mass of power slammed into Xavier and instantly ripped through his defences, tearing through his Force Armour and then through his actual armour. It burned his skin, melted his bones, liquefied his organs.

The pain was obscene. The pain of death always was.

He was right—that body hadn’t been able to survive that much power.

Xavier cut his consciousness out of the drone as it died, its atoms scattered like dust in the great expanse of space.

Xavier Collins—the real Xavier Collins, not the drone he’d been inhabiting as a decoy for whoever had laid this trap—hovered a few hundred miles away from the three Denizens and the portal they’d come through. He narrowed his eyes, peering at them, watching for how they might react.

The planet had been spared. Those spell patterns Xavier had manifested inside the drone’s body had ensured the energy from the destructive spell had all been pulled toward it. Every last drop of that energy had been spent on the drone’s demise.

Xavier altered his own time dilation field. It was faster than the one the drone had been using, and faster than the time mage’s as well, but not by as much as he’d expected.

With the drone taking their attack, Xavier was able to examine the memory and get a good idea of just how much power the twin Denizens had been able to bring to bear.

Peak B Grades.

His wings flapped lazily as he tilted his head, birdlike, and examined each of the Denizens more closely. He’d expected any hit squad that came after him to be B Grade, but if he was reading the energy right, each of these Denizens was more powerful than Sovereign Rewke Fouran had been, which meant the likelihood of them being agents from a nearby sector was low. They must have been from farther away—none of the sectors close to this one would be able to field such a powerful hit squad.

He supposed he should have expected nothing less.

What he’d done to Sovereign Rewke Fouran inside that arena had been no secret. There had been enough sector rulers there that some of them surely would have been powerful enough to send a hit squad like this, and he wasn’t surprised that after they’d seen what he’d done that they would want him dead.

He could hover there and theorise for ages about who might have sent these people—it could even have been someone from the inner sectors, after the display he’d put on for them, but he doubted they would have moved this fast.

And they would have sent more powerful Denizens than these.

Instead of thinking about it, he moved, teleporting through space to appear directly behind the three Denizens. A spell pattern manifested inside his body and he cast it on the portal, locking it in place so whoever had created it shouldn’t be able to cancel it. At the same time, he activated a second spell to examine the portal’s properties to get coordinates to where it led.

His veil was up, and he had several other spells concealing his presence. Even if the Denizens were utilising an ability similar to The All-Seeing Eye, they wouldn’t be able to spot him right behind them through his superior concealment.

Moving silently through space wasn’t exactly an issue, and so as he hovered right up to the time mage, now inside their time dilation field.

Xavier touched a finger to the back of her head and cast a variant of Willpower Infusion.
