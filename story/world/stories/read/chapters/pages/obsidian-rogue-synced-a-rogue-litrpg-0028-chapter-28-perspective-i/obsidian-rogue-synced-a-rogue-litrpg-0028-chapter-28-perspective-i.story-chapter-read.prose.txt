Levi smiled as he watched his stats update to show the changes that he had made to his mana. While the points he’d pumped into the stat wouldn't be enough to take them the entire way, it could very well be the difference between life and death for the mage. From what he had seen, Xara had about three to four hours before she died, and he intended to use that time wisely.

He turned to the mage who was on the floor, still bleeding and desperately clinging to life. “I think I might have a way to get us there faster.”

“How?” She coughed.

“I just used the obsidian stone. I've gotten my subclass, and I've been offered a rare subclass, Void-stepper. It's the synergy between my portalist subclass and my rook class.”

Xara stared at him in confusion. “I'm sorry, I couldn't pick up anything that you just said.”

The rogue was puzzled. He was certain that he hadn't minced his words, but he decided to go out on a limb and repeat himself. “I said, I have picked my subclass, which is a synergy between the portalist subclass and my rogue class.”

Unfortunately, his words did not have the desired effect. If anything, her face seems to have scrunched up in frustration. This caused Levi to recall the time when the system had informed him that he would not be able to discuss what he had seen with anyone else.

Understanding finally dawned on him. He turned back to the mage and said, “Sorry, I meant to say that I have a way to get us there faster. Don't ask any questions. It would just be incoherent to you.”

This time, the mage nodded in agreement, an action that surprised him a bit, but he didn't show it. Instead, the rogue hoisted the mage to her feet and pulled her good arm over his shoulder.

Steadying her even as she hissed in agony. He would've let her stay on the floo, but they both knew that his actions were necessary.

“[Void step(II) Tear]” Levi said.

A tear appeared in the air right in front of them. It was the size of a doorway and shaped like a diamond. In the middle was a profound darkness that felt chilly. It looked beautiful and at the same time ominous, but neither of them minded, as they both knew that it was his skill in action.

“Come on,” he said, as he helped her into the void tear.

A cool wave washed over them as they stepped into the void.

The movement was instantaneous. They reappeared thirty meters ahead of where they were before. Looking at his status screen, he wondered how many times he could use Void Step before he collapsed.

“Are you all right?” He asked, to which Xara nodded weakly, a glint of steel in her eyes.

Levi took that as his cue to use [Void step(II) Tear] again.

A new void tear appeared in front of them, and just as they’d done before, they walked through it. The chilly sensation was just as potent as the first time, but it wasn’t anything the pair couldn't handle, and within a breath, they’d reappeared thirty meters ahead.

It took several void steps for them to cover a reasonable distance, and by that time, the rogue had exhausted his mana; he had a pounding headache. He also felt the sudden urge to puke, which meant his body also seemed to be reacting negatively to the constant use of mana.

Unfortunately, there was nothing he could do about either symptom, but he gritted his teeth in agony as he walked the mage. Doing his best to keep her upright as they walked the remaining length of their destination.

Their movement without using void-step was agonizingly slow. Every step the mage took was accompanied by a pained grunt and hot air from her lips, hitting his face. He expected one or two complaints about the injury, but she hadn't uttered a single word about it, and even in the obvious agony that she was currently in, she continued to move. While the mage did not match him stride for stride, she did do her best to keep up, and that was something Levi found commendable.

By the third hour, the mage was audibly gasping for air. A soft groan of agony accompanied every step she took, beads of sweat dripped down her face in rivulets, and her every breath was labored. There was a glassy look of determination in her eyes.

To be honest, after the first two hours had passed, he had expected the mage to have given up, but she hadn’t, a testament to her core strength. He’d have commended her for that, but he knew she was fading. The mage would not survive more than another hour of this.

Against his better judgment, he wrapped his right hand—which still had the odd arrow sticking out of it—around the mage's waist, pressing her closer to his body in his bid to keep her from falling over.

“Don’t you die on me,” He whispered. As closely pressed together as they were, he was sure that his words would easily carry to her ears.”We’re almost there, stay sharp.”

A weak smile formed on her face as she turned to him. Her bloodstained lips parted as she took a pause from her labored breaths and gave him a sarcastic reply. “Look at that… He does have a heart.”

“Of course I do,“ Levi retorted. “I need something to pump my blood, don't I?”

A slight vibration blossomed in his side as the mage let out a small laugh at his words. A half smile appeared on her face, but was quickly replaced by a bloody cough as the mage tried to catch her breath. With a grunt, Levi refocused on the path, preferring to pull up his status screen than to spend the time staring at the wheezing mage. He needed to check how much mana he had now.

To counter the effects on his body, he'd either have to improve his stamina or intelligence, or perhaps both.

Wordlessly, they both stepped through the void tear, reappearing thirty meters further and repeating the process one more time, with the last of his mana.

His mana wasn’t the only thing that was depleted; his stamina seemed to be waning. His steps were shorter, and he was now matching the pace of the injured mage, not out of pity but because he couldn’t do more than that without struggling to breathe.

In their current state, they’d both be easy targets, and he wasn't sure that either of them would be able to survive another attack if any came their way. That line of thought had him questioning if he had made the right choice, going for portalist over healer.

Levi’s pondering was cut short as he caught sight of a settlement up ahead. There was no doubt that this was the village that Xara had spoken about. A wave of relief washed over him at the sight. He turned to the mage, who seemed to be half dead. Her eyelids were constantly fluttering, and her head drooped.

The only thing that made him certain that she was alive was the way her nostrils flared as she took in shallow breaths. It was obvious that she was on her last legs, and it was up to him to save her. Gritting his teeth, he shouldered her weight and managed to get them to the gate. His chest heaving and his breathing ragged.

It’d taken everything he had to get him close to the wooden gate that served as the entrance to the village, where two guards intercepted them. Levi wasn’t fooled by the two stationed there; he could tell that there were more guards in hiding, especially since he'd seen a few trailing them on top of the trees. He’d ignored them and walked up to the two guards, hoping they’d offer aid, but before he could utter a single word, one of the guards beat him to it.
