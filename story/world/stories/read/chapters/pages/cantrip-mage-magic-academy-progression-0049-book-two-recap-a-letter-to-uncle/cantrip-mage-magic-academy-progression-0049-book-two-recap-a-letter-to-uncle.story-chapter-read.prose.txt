Dear Uncle,

So much has happened in the last year that I simply must send this letter. Please know that I’ve done and learned so much more than I could put into words, especially on only one piece of parchment, front and back.

Varin’s Academy is like nothing in Greenarbor. Many of the students are nobles’ children, and they act like it—but more of that later. My classes ended well. High marks in my apprenticeship and Ideograms, of course, but also acceptable ones in Magical Dueling and Introduction to Monsters. All four of those classes helped me survive the term exams. I was even able to pass Mana Studies, despite the professor being a hag of a gnome.

Classes have taught me a lot, of course. I understand how runes are put together better than any other student in my year, most likely—in fact, Instructor Tarik had me marking runes for his more advanced classes. And I’ve learned the Taron-Li well enough. I’m no elf, that’s for sure, but I can perform a sword dance passably with one.

I even managed to summon and bind a familiar. Her name is Atta, and she’s a cat. Just a cat. Definitely nothing strange about her at all.

I’ve also joined the combat track. More importantly, the library has been instrumental in understanding magic. And what a library it is. The first two floors are safe. Mostly. Far into the second is a little more dangerous, but no more so than hunting monsters with you. But I’ve only ventured into the third floor twice, and both times have been nearly lethal. I won’t be pushing that far again without my friends at my back.

Oh, yes, I have friends here!

Colin, Patrice, and Roth are my suitemates, and have been since Y’aer’s Day and the midterm apprentice-choosing ceremony. Colin’s a sweet boy, but a bit of a slob. It’s surprising, though. He seems to know exactly who to talk to in Varin’s Town. It’s a skill I don’t understand, and don’t want to. He’s been my suitemate the longest. Yes, we share common spaces with boys here. No, no one makes a big deal of it.

Patrice Clerk—of House Clerk up in Foglight—is an oddball. She’s usually asleep if she’s in her room. Sometimes, she sleeps in classes, at lunch, or deep in the library. It’s actually impressive how easily she sleeps. She’s the worst student at Varin’s Academy, according to her. I’m not sure I agree with that, but she insists it’s true.

And Roth…Roth is a mystery. He’s from the far north. He won’t even name his village, but he’s covered in scars. It must be a dangerous place. He’s a genius with instruments, though. Harps, flutes and pipes, and his voice. It’s amazing. I just wish he wasn’t so angry all the time—and I also wonder what chip he’s got on his shoulder.

Patrice and Colin away for the term break, though, so it remains to be seen if it’ll become awkward and uncomfortable when they get back. There’s been some of that already between my old suite mates but it’ll be different this time. Colin and I are courting.

I’ve had a few problems, though. They’re pretty minor, and you don’t have to worry about them. It’s just one professor and one other student.

The professor is Madame Reyanna. She teaches Mana Studies, and she’s got such a flawed way of teaching it that I can’t bring myself to care about the subject. I did my best, but she’s still teaching Mana lines, for Y’aer’s sake! And she’s got it out for me like no one I’ve ever seen. I don’t understand.

She’s not the only gnome I’ve met here. There’s a cook, a blacksmith, and a few students. But she’s definitely the worst of them all. The headmaster says that next year’s professors may be worse. I find it hard to believe that, though.

But the worst person in all of Varin’s Academy—with only one possible exception—is Catrine of House Andrese.

She’s everything Lord Card is, but in a smaller, less clever, and more spiteful package. If it weren’t for Patrice and Garreth Skaget, I’d be pretty much convinced that nobles were the worst people in all of Alemia. She’s had it out for me since the beginning of the year. Even beating her in a duel didn’t help. To be fair, though, I don’t think I was supposed to win that fight. I only really planned on surviving the spell-trading and getting to the swords-and-spells part of it. Nobles’ duels are strange—nothing like the Taron-Li or exhibition dueling.

Anyway, Catrine. She won’t even stop when people’s lives are in danger. She was at my throat in the middle of the Library Exam.

Right. The Library Exam.

It was supposed to be the final test, and the one that keeps the combat track small. If you pass it, you get to join the combat classes. If you don’t, you don’t. I’m not supposed to tell anyone what the test was, but you’re not going to be a student, so it’s probably fine. We were supposed to gather five books from around the third floor.

We only got four.

But we also got separated from our staff escorts, lost in the library, attacked by pretty much every monster Instructor Clearance warned us about in Introduction to Monsters—and one she didn’t—and trapped in a floating study room by a cultist. I had to pull some tricks out of my sleeve to drive him off and defeat his pet monster, but in the end, we escaped with four of the five books. Colin and Roth both ended up in the hospital, but we escaped.

(As an aside, being in that situation makes me even more impressed with Mom and Dad. They’ve survived in Lord Card’s dungeon for four years now. I know they have. We barely survived for a few hours. I’ve got a long way to go still.)

Once we got out, I had to meet with the headmaster himself. He talked to me about what happened and quizzed me a little about magic, then passed three of us. Roth’s taking classes with the headmaster instead of the combat track.

It’s been a busy first year of school at Varin’s Academy, and I’m looking forward to it quieting down a little.

I hope things are well in Greenarbor, and that the city’s lord hasn’t put too much pressure on you. I hear through rumors that the city struggles, and trust that you continue to make your own way. If you hear anything about my father or mother, please write me at once. Otherwise, I suspect this letter and your reply will be our only correspondence this year.

Your loving niece,

Eola

Eola set her pen in its holder and poured sand across her letter. Even sending mail from Varin’s Academy to her uncle in Greenarbor was a risk. Lord Card had trapped her parents in his dungeon, after all, and Y’aer only knew what he’d do if he found out what she was up to.

But one every year wouldn’t hurt.

Or maybe it would. But it was worth the risk. She waited for the sand to do its job, then, when the ink had dried, folded the parchment carefully and slid it into an envelope. She’d had to borrow it from Instructor Tarik; stationary wasn’t among the things she’d brought to Varin’s Academy, and buying a set in town didn’t make any sense.

The academy’s rookery was all but empty. The students who could had all left for the three-week break between terms, and so had most of the professors. She found a bird, sketched the spell to implant it with a destination—Greenarbor, the town she’d grown up in—and let the raven fly.

It beat its wings, wheeling due east and flying into the cool, late summer wind. It’d be fall soon; summer didn’t last long this high in the mountains. And with fall would come her second year at Varin’s Academy.

Then, when the black bird finally disappeared, she turned and hurried toward the cafeteria. If she was quick, she could finish off a bowl of porridge before her morning study session.
