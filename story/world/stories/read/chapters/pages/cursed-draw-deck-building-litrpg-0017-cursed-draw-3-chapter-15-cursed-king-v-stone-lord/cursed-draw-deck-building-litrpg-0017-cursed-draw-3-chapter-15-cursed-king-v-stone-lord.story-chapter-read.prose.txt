Alley’s turn began, and he redrew to fill the missing Card slot in his Hand. Unlike when you played a Card, discarding one at the end of your turn meant a player had to wait until the start of their next turn to replace it. While it was far from ideal to endure an entire enemy turn while down a Card, Crown of the Cursed King didn’t sport many play-anytime spells anyway.

The image that joined the rest of his Hand made Alley pause to consider his options. With a slight turn of his head, he looked to Denderra, who let out a sigh and filled him in on what the Card’s effect was. That made him stop and consider his options again. He had drawn Necrotic Fiend, a monster with reasonably impressive stats, an even more impressive ability, and a cost in Vials to match. Playing the monster would mean he could no longer afford to Manifest Lynillian this match, a prospect that had grown more and more tempting with each Terracotta Warrior.

Considering Alley's maximum capacity was eight Vials anyway, playing just about anything that had a Vial cost would prevent him from making use of the Carrion King anyway,

“Keeping promises made easy, I suppose.” He muttered to himself and Manifested the Creature he had just drawn.

“Necrotic Fiend!”

​

​

As always, spending almost half his Vials at once made Alley feel like his body heat was leaking away, but by this point, he was more than used to the sensation.

While powerful and expensive, the Necrotic Fiend wasn’t a unique Card, so it simply appeared in a burst of blue light.

The monster, though human-shaped, was grey-skinned and emaciated with an angular face, red eyes, sharp horns protruding from the sides of its head, and a similar set of spikes coming from its back. The fiend was dressed in the ragged remains of a robe, and its feet, which were unshod, looked more like someone had maimed a bird of prey than those of a human being.

Alley had to admit he had no idea where Anaten had found half the creatures that made up his Library. He understood that Undead things weren't going to necessarily look like they had in life, but he'd never even heard of a monster that had an appearance like this one.

Alley concluded that it didn't matter since this was a Challenge Match, not a beauty contest. That thought made him pause with a pang of sadness at the loss of his sister, who had won every such contest she had ever entered. Alley missed his sister, missed his mother, and wondered about the fate of his father. Still, no amount of mournful regret was going to win matches for him.

“I end my creation phase.” The moment he said it, the gap left in his Hand by the Necrotic Fiend he had just Manifested was refilled. The new Card was an old friend in the form of Cursed Gravedigger that had frequently been instrumental in Alley's victories.

Now that his War Phase had begun, the choice was obvious. He needed to get attacking sooner rather than later.

“Brutish Zombie Shred,” he commanded as the attack phrase popped into his mind. The purple-skinned zombie, which was being empowered by the Bone Weaved armor that now covered almost its entirety, loped towards the barrier between players with one of the Terracotta Warriors in its sights.

Even with the Power from their effect, the Terracotta Warriors only had nineteen hundred Power, while the Brutish Zombie currently stood at two thousand, two hundred and fifty.

“I activate Crystaline Barrier!” called Vern just as the muscular zombie closed in on the wall of blue energy in the center of the Arena.

​

The wall of crystal that sat at the front of the old man’s side of the field. The Relic that had been spawned by the large cube at the end of Vern’s last turn glowed briefly before shattering and then vanishing entirely. What was left behind in the wall’s place was a barrier of familiar crimson energy that the charging zombie collided with and then bounced off of.

Having witnessed his opponent play multiple matches earlier in the tournament, Alley had already known what to expect from the Relic, but he’d still ordered the attack anyway as he knew he needed to keep the pressure up or the Relics would keep multiplying until there was simply no way to reduce the number of Terracotta Warriors at all.

Ending his War Phase, Alley looked at his Hand and considered playing or even discarding Phoenix Urn Phylactery. The Relic was extremely powerful but required the loss of a Hand size, something that was an extremely high cost in this situation, especially as it would require a Vial to be spent as well.

‘Phoenix Urn Phylactery, Shadowed Eater, Wraith Master, Cursed Gravedigger, and Lynillian The Carrion King.’

There was nothing he could play this turn in his second Creation Phase other than the Phoenix Urn, which he opted to leave in his hand. Alley’s creatures were out of range of the Terracotta Warriors for now anyway, and even if the army of clay soldiers got enough Power together to threaten Brutish Zombie and Necrotic Fiend, the zombie was protected by the Bone Weaved armor, and Alley still had his Buckler Ring waiting in reserve if he needed it.

As much as it hurt to do so, Alley ended his turn and discarded Lynillian when he did. The Carrion King was too expensive to play anyway.

The large old man stroked his thick white beard for a few seconds as he observed the field at the beginning of his turn.

“Ursine Prospector.” He eventually said Manifesting a Card from his hand. Alley hadn’t actually seen this one yet and would have been curious about the effect if Vern didn’t immediately explain it as the creature appeared in its burst of blue light.

​

The creature was a towering mass of brown fur dressed in an oversized tunic of deep green and brown pants. It wore a leather belt with a pouch full of gold nuggets hanging at its hip and a pickaxe slung over the bear-creature's back. Honestly, the beast looked more intimidating than the fifteen hundred Power would suggest, but really, Alley knew certain breeds of bear were almost like huge dogs rather than the threats they looked like.

“Damnit.” Grunted Vern as his eyes ceased glowing bright blue, and three Card images rose out of his torc-Dealer only split apart, and presumably be sent to the man’s Crypt.

“Unlucky?” Alley called across the Arena with a pair of raised eyebrows and a small smirk.

“Won’t matter,” Vern called back. “ We can stalemate all day, and it will only help me, as my army keeps growing.”

Alley wasn’t about to say anything to agree with the older man, but the fact of the matter was that the statement was true. When he ended his turn the Shard Of The Living Mountain would activate again and Alley would be looking across the field at an awful lot of Terracotta warriors. Even as he thought it, Vern did just that and ended his turn.

Another Card image left Vern’s Dealer and became another Crystaline Barrier, which, of course, triggered the four Terracotta Warriors. Slate grey Earth Resonance flowed from the clay soldiers into the ground, causing the number of statue-men to double in a mere few seconds. That would be bad enough, but for each new warrior, the Power stat floating above the clay soldier’s heads grew by another three hundred. Once all four additional Terracotta Warriors had pulled themselves from the ground, the contingent each had twenty-eight hundred Power.
