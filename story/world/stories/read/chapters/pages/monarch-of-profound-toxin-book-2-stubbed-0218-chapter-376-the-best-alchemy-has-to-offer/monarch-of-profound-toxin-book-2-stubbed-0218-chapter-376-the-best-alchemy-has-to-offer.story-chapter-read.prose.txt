Chapter 376

The number of people Eik brought to Toxin with the teleporting effect of Admittance to the Realm this time was far, far higher than anything he had done before.

He had brought the kids and Ihasu all at the same time before, but that had been the extent of it as far as he could remember. Luckily, as he had expected, there had been no real problems with the process. The biggest issue, really, had been making sure everybody was accounted for before leaving.

In the end, he had given the teleportees, most of whom were either friends of his or members of Eik’s Great Group of Guys, a short tour of the world. Entirely on purpose, he had made absolutely certain to give Profound Toxin’s house a wide berth, lest the blue bastard talked someone’s ear off about petunias or his enthusiastic plan to eventually get to the point where he can establish an entire field of wheat out back.

During his first visit with his father, Goo had become overwhelmingly convinced that this was his new home. He claimed to feel an attachment to the world that was so strong that it was as if he was being pulled down toward the center of the planet by an invisible force.

Ihasu did not like the sound of that at all, and Eik himself wasn’t exactly thrilled about it either, although he doubted anything catastrophic would come of it. The kid was literally made of the stuff of Toxin, so it seemed only natural that he would feel a sense of belonging here.

***

“Worked like… a… charm… and then a little smiley here at the end,” Eik sounded as he wrote the results of the test down in his note book back at the laboratory. Meri, Guild Master Ganyak hir Gawak, and the rest of the alchemists stood clustered around him as he wrote. “There!”

“Wait, wait, wait,” Meri insisted, slamming a palm down onto Eik’s now closed note book before he could get up to put it away on one of the countless shelves in the laboratory where he would surely forget about it within the next one to two business days. “What did you just do?”

He looked up at her, blinking with puzzlement. “What do you mean what did I just do? I recorded my findings from the testing of the grade seven Potion of Mighty Strength. Hey, Meri, is there something wrong with your memory? Guild Master, maybe it would be good if you went and fetched your granddaughter a cool, damp cloth to put over her eyes so she can lie down for a little bi—”

“That’s not what I was talking about and—although I can never be a hundred percent certain when it comes to a lunatic like you—I’m pretty sure you know that as well! That potion just outperformed all other potions ever made put together, for crying out loud! With a single hammer blow, you just hollowed out the underground of the entire area, causing a region-wide natural catastrophe, if it can still be called natural! And then you almost killed us all!” she barked as one particularly prominent vein below her eyebrow thumped in agitation.

Eik stared at her, slack-jawed, while she yelled at him.

“And all you have to say about it in these notes that will be sold as one of the most revered artifacts in the entire history of alchemy a few thousand years from now is ‘worked like a charm’ with a dumb drawing at the end? Four words, Mr. Magnasen!”

Eik pursed his lips for a second before he began to speak. “All right, first of all. That smiley was cute as all hell and you know it. Second of all, what else do I need to write in this?” he asked, holding up the note book. “I watched each and every one of you scribble away like mad people throughout this entire process. As a matter of fact, if I’m not mistaken, that meter-high stack of documents on the table in the corner over there is nothing but notes from this project.”

“It’s the principle of it, Eik! You’ve accomplished something amazing, so you should record it properly for the generations to follow!” she raged.

Eik glanced at the guild master. “She just switched from calling me Mr. Magnasen to Eik, didn’t she? Is that, like, okay, or…?”

“I would prefer to have no part in this pointless argument.”

“Honestly, same,” Eik said. “Listen, Meri, Do you know what happened when I fought the Lord of the Moon a little while ago?”

Suddenly caught off balance, she just swallowed.

“I lost. That means we all lose, okay? Do you know why I’m not looking for him again right now? Because I can’t win, and I need as much time as I can get with these potions before he comes looking for us. Every pill I can make before he attacks is another hope for survival. And he will attack, I can promise you. He saw what I can do and would be right to be cautious. It’s frankly a stroke of luck that he hasn’t invaded already, just to snuff out my life before I can become a threat.”

Soberly, Meri nodded her understanding. “I’m sorry,” she whispered.

Eik smiled. “No worries at all. I love professional enthusiasm. But I’d much rather have you guys tell me about the possibilities for some of the other potion types at grade seven?”

One of the other alchemists pulled a small note book out from somewhere and ran a finger down a page. “Certainly, Mr. Magnasen. We’ve discussed this already among ourselves, and we believe it would best serve your current goals and schedule to attempt to create a Potion of the Metal Body next. If we’ve understood correctly, your skill set is only moderately suited for defense, so having an alchemical solution would be wise.”

“Yeah, Metal Body sounds great. Have you figured out what it requires?”

“Fortunately, yes. We haven’t been able to figure out exactly why, but it turns out that your body parts are exceedingly valuable alchemical ingredients. Most monsters with viable body parts can only be used in one type of potion, with a few that can provide materials for two different potions.”

“Do you have to call me an ingredient?”

“Sorry,” she said sheepishly.

Eik chuckled. “Nah, it’s okay. So how many am I useful in?”

“With various parts harvested from your body, we have figured out four different grade seven recipes that can be made.”

“Seriously? That’s a lot!”

“Indeed.”

“So Potion of the Metal Body can be made by and with me? What does it use?”

She paused as she leafed through a few pages. “Ah, here it is. It requires marrow from your femur as the active agent.”

“My femur? Oh, man. I remember that one really sucking… Okay, I know I’m probably screwing myself by asking this, but what are the two other recipes and… what do I have to pull out?”

Again, she flipped through the pages while the rest of the team watched. “I apologize,” she said with a glance up. “I was primarily involved in general analysis, and didn’t make most of these.”

Instead of waiting, Guild Master Ganyak hir Gawak spoke up. “They are the Potion of Magical Empowerment and one you probably haven’t heard about before called Kiq’s Temper.”

Eik frowned. “Kiq’s Temper, Kiq’s Temper. Where have I heard that before?”

The guild master tilted his head to the side. “Perhaps you’ve stumbled across it in your studies? It’s mostly used by lower ranked Awakened on exploration or for travel and leisure in the outdoors.”

“Kiq’s Temper! I remember it now! It’s an insect repellent!”

“Exactly right.”

“If I’m not mistaken, that was the first potion I ever made! Man, how long it’s been,” Eik said with a chuckle.

Eyes widened all around. “Your first potion was Kiq’s Temper?” Meri asked. “What kind of idiotic teacher pushed you to try that? Despite its low rank and relatively simple recipe, it’s notorious for blowing up in peoples faces and causing harm to eyes and skin. Some unfortunates even go blind when it’s really bad.”

Eik winced. “No one pushed me into it. I used to sell homemade medicine before I Awakened and felt an instant attraction to magical alchemy, so I spent some money on a recipe book and an encyclopedia. It was just one of the recipes I could actually find the ingredients for. I guess I was a little reckless back then.”

One of the alchemists couldn’t hold back a snort of amusement, all eyes suddenly trained on him. Suddenly the center of attention, he spluttered nervously. “Ah, n-no, I’m sorry. It’s just… you said you were reckless back then, but…” he trailed of hesitatingly, voice growing dimmer as he lost confidence. “you’re also reckless… now?” he said, ending on an upward inflection, as if asking if that was okay to say.

Eik’s eyes were hard as he gazed at the man, making the poor alchemist nearly faint where he stood. But after a couple of seconds, he couldn’t keep it up and clapped the man on the back repeatedly, hard enough to send him into a coughing fit for his life. “You’re not wrong there, mate. Even my daughter tells me I’m too reckless sometimes.”

“And by sometimes, she of course means always,” Meri added.

Eik sent her a fiery glare but didn’t shoot back. “Anyway, what the hell would an X-ranked Kiq’s Temper even be like? Just, what, more stink?”

“More stink is probably about right, yes,” the guild master said with a nod. “And more persistent, most likely.”

Eik narrowed his eyes with trepidation. “Exactly how persistent are we talking here?”

The guild master shrugged lightly. “Well, we can’t really assume too much before we have the actual, finished work in our hands, of course, but if we extrapolate a little from what you’ve told us about the strength of the grade seven Potion of Mighty Strength, and given the fact that both would be made from your body parts, I would guess that it might remain effective from anywhere from six to nine years, or something in that area.”

“Nine years?” Eik gasped. "You mean not only would you be bathed in X-ranked stink, you would be walking around like that for nine years? That sounds like a substance that would be pretty high on a bunch of lists.”

“Lists?”

“Yeah, like lists of harmful chemicals, weapons of biological warfare, and stuff like that. Basically like an ultra strong surströmming.”

“I don’t recognize that word, I’m afraid.”

Eik winced. “It would probably be best that you don’t dig further. It’s an evil substance that has felled countless powerful men through the times. Only a few old geezers have been able to overcome it and even find pleasure in its consumption.”

The alchemists gaped at this revelation. “There were people on Earth before the initiation into the Unified Mass who would eat what others would call a weapon?”

“Oh, yes. I’ve only ever been subjected to it once before myself, but even I had to admit defeat in the face of overwhelming aversion.”

Hushed words were exchanged between the alchemists as they contemplated the notion of such a horror. Finally, one of them looked at Eik. “Mr. Magnasen, where might one locate such a weapon, if it even still exists in this world.”

Eik hesitated. “Uuh, I would sometimes see it in the supermarket, I suppose.”

“Supermarket?” the guild master repeated. “A market trading only in extraordinary treasure? Truly, old Earth was such a magnificent society.”

“Ah, no, that’s actually not quite…” Eik hurried to say but trailed off as he imagined the chain of explanations he would have to give. Considering that, in addition to having his ass cut off a thousand times in a row, he could now look forward to having his femur removed a thousand times as well, he really wasn’t in the mood for a lecture on grocery conventions. “Actually, sure, let’s go with that. Let’s just get on with the potion stuff.”
