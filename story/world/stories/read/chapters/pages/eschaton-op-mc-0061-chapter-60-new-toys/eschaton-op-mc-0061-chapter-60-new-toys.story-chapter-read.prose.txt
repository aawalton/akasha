Sergeant Castillo of the United States National Guard angrily eyes the thunderstorm that just soaked her and her entire squad. Colorado weather is unstable at the best of times, and these certainly aren’t the best of times.

“Damn it, Sarge, couldn’t we have hung out in the Strykers?” Jericho moans. “I’m soaked to bone!”

“Shut up, Corporal, and keep your eyes peeled. We’re supposed to be training for the real thing. You wanna get your face eaten by a monster?”

He grumbles as all of them keep slowly advancing over the empty field.

“What monsters? This place is empty as fuck!”

Castillo just slaps him on the back of the helmet. “Take your fireteam and hold the other side of the Bradley! Move, dipshit!”

He grumbles, but takes his fireteam to the other side of the Bradley advancing in step with them, holding the center of their formation. Their two Strykers with top mounted .50 Cals follow just behind their squad.

As their group crests a small rise, they can see a shot up car off in the distance.

“Contact front!” Callisto calls out.

“Armor engaging,” the newest addition to 2nd Squad replies. Lieutenant Matterson is now their commanding officer, and leads from the squad’s newly attached Bradley.

The IFV shakes and the main 25mm autocannon hammers away at the blown-up, shot-up, and set-on-fire target car. 2nd Squad drops to the ground and lets loose with their new battle rifles. Thor takes a knee and shoulders his massive .50 caliber rifle. It thunders out, the enchantments on it—and his strength—allowing him to barely control it.

“Cease fire, Cease fire!” Lieutenant Matterson calls out over the radio. “Infantry, mount up!”

2nd Squad hustles into the two eight-person troop transports and the six-seater Bradley. Once their rear doors shut with a clank that would send their ears ringing without their ear protection, all three vehicles rumble off. Diesel engines roar as the Bradley leads the way over a flat field. Just a few miles away, the rapidly growing walls of Denver take over the horizon.

Eric’s helmet clicks against the bare metal interior of the Stryker and he sighs.

“Whew. Hey, Sarge, I had a thought.”

“Wow, try not to pull something Eric,” Richardson, the squad’s medic, snickers.

Callisto smacks him on the chest, rattling his chest plate. “Shuddup, Doc. Eric, what’s up?”

“You know, I was just thinking about what Machina might be getting up to these days.”

Callisto rolls her eyes. “He’s very busy, I’m sure. He’s saving the world and all. These days I’ve heard he’s been spending even more time in Fractures, too. Somehow.”

“Maybe we’ll get paired up with him again. He is hanging around Denver, you know,” Richardson chimes in. “I heard he cleared a Fracture in Denver just a few days ago, too.”

The gunner of their Stryker, Thomas, looks at them with shock.

“Wait, you guys know Machina? The big metal robot guy. The guy who saved DC and New York?”

“No, we don’t know him,” Callisto sighs. “We were around when he was around. A lot. It was luck, sheer chance, and nothing more.”

“Don’t tell the brass that,” Sandra mutters. “They still seem to think we’re the best of the best.”

“At least we get all these fancy new toys!” Eric says cheerfully.

“Yeah, that means they expect us to need and use them,” Sandra points out. “The reward for a job well done is more work.”

“Oh. Right, that makes sense,” Eric says more thoughtfully. “Suddenly I’m a lot less happy about it.”

“No, no, I’m serious guys,” Thomas says. “You guys, like, talked to Machina. The American Hero. Haven’t you seen the friggin’ news? They plaster his face all over it every damn day.”

“Eh, I guess?” Eric says. “I don’t think he’d remember us. I’m sure he’s met a lot of people. We can’t be that memorable, right? We’re just…us, right?”

The Stryker jumps as it drives over a gulley, and Eric goes flying into the metal roof. He slams into it with a clang and a yelp. Everyone else was either holding onto something or belted in.

“Moron! Doc, check him out,” Callisto orders.

“I’m fine, I’m fine,” Eric says, rubbing his head. His helmet protected him from the worst of it.

“Try not to get injured on a training exercise,” Callisto says dryly. “You’ll make me look bad.”

The squad begins the next day with a training session on some low cost drones. They showed promise during the short-lived war Russia started in Ukraine. While the conflict is still officially on-going, the presence of monsters infesting the tundra wastes has forced Russia to worry about other things. Not to mention the internal threats bulletproof Empowered pose to the regime.

Sandra is working with the trainer on a fixed-wing drone, with a wingspan about six feet from tip-to-tip. Sandra will carry the pieces into the field, while her assistant operator, Dillion Anders, will carry the launch rack and communication equipment required for Sandra to control it.

After assembling the drone, they lift it onto the launch rack. With a rush of compressed air, it flings the drone into the sky. Its propeller flicks on with a hum, and Sandra beings to take it through some basic maneuvers under the careful eye of the trainer.

“Sarge,” Eric asks. “What’re we doing out here? I sure ain’t gonna try to fly that thing.”

“Eric?” She says.

“Yes, Sarge?”

“Shut up.”

“Yes, Sarge.”

A car pulls up, and a middle-aged man who clearly isn’t military steps out. The man dresses like a college professor stereotype. Tweed jacket with patched elbows and all. He starts by cleaning his glasses and clearing his throat.

“Hm, yes, hello. I’m Doctor Patricks, head of the Fracture crash research program based out of DARPA. I’m very busy, and so will keep this brief. For reasons beyond me, you’ve been selected to become one of the United States’ first dedicated Fracture Dive Teams. That means you’ll need to be trained on a variety of scientific devices and methodologies. With me so far?”

“Yes, sir,” Lieutenant Matterson says. “What do you need us to do?”

A truck rolls in and comes to a stop on the dirt field, in one of Denver’s brand new military airbases.

“First, unload the truck, and once you’re done I’ll give you all a walk through of what you’re going to do.”

Long after the sun has gone down, 2nd Squad staggers into their barracks after being run ragged.

“That fuckin’ drone is too damn heavy,” Sandra groans. “I swear to God my back is gonna kill me tomorrow. Fuck, it’s killing me right now.”

“At least you got to learn to fly!” Eric says, throwing himself onto his bed. “They spent all day teaching us how to dig dirt and chop a tree. I’ve been doing that since I was six. If I wanted to dig holes and chop trees, I’d just go home.”

“Didn’t you hear what the egghead said?” Jericho says dryly. “It’s special dirt or whatever. Very important.”

“It is important.” Thor intones.

“Well,” Doc says after a brief pause. “Anything else to add, Thor?”

“No.”

Everyone laughs at Thor’s taciturn nature. Lieutenant Matterson steps into the barracks, and everyone snaps to a salute. Even Eric scrambles off his bed.

“At ease, 2nd Squad. I just wanted to let you all know that you can expect another early start. I’ve been told there’s still more they want to teach us. When they mean we’re becoming one of the first Dive Teams, they mean it. Expect to get a lot of toys and a lot of training. And stay ready. We’re likely to get dragged into a Fracture if it looks like it might rupture.”

He gives Callisto a nod before leaving.

“Right, well, you heard the man,” she says. “Lights out in five, come on, move it. I ain’t gonna let you sorry pack of idiots make me look bad.”

The Bradley is bouncing down the road, followed by the two Strykers. Callisto’s fireteam, Fireteam Diamonds, is in the Bradley instead of the Stryker on this training exercise. Callisto leans in towards Lt. Matterson in order to be heard over the roaring diesel engine.

“Lieutenant, I heard you knew Machina before all this magic shit went down. That true?”

After mulling over an answer for a minute, he finally replies. “Uh, yeah. I did. I don’t really think it’s my place to talk about him, though. I ain’t that kinda guy, get me, Sergeant.?”

“Yes, sir,” she says. “I just have one question, if you don’t mind, sir.”

“Shoot, but no promises.”

“Every time I’ve met him he’s always been running towards a fight without a care in the world. Was he always that kind of guy?”

The Bradley shakes and jumps as their convoy rolls out towards their next exercise location. It takes a couple of minutes before Matterson decides on a response.

“We’ve all got demons,” he says carefully. “He’s no different. Whether he’s running from them, or fighting them, I can’t say.” He turns around from his seat to look at her. “That’s all I’ll say about him. That’s his story to tell, hear me?”

“Loud and clear, sir.”
