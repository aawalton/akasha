Once more we drifted over the small city. Oh how it had grown over the years. When first my daughter and her team had built it they'd made the walls far out, well set with enough room for people to expand, but now? Now it pushed nearly all the way to the edges, and around it were several outlying settlements, a novel idea.

That was one I would have liked to use back home, but there were some differences here. Back on our continent we had the issue of monsters that seemed almost non-existent in these lands, ones that would fully destroy any unprotected little outpost. However here it was possible to keep such things, the people who lived there retreating if there were signs of trouble. Those signs would come with many deaths back in the bulk of my kingdom, but different setups weren't anything too strange.

No, the elves of the far north had had their Icehome before we'd retired it. Nobody lived in that region now so far as I knew, though I was told a few went there for hunting trips when it was warmer, going after furs and the like which were harder to find further south. The elves who lived in wetter areas too had houses often placed on stilts, a smart choice if your home was likely to flood.

“She's done well,” Isha said from beside me.

“Indeed, I'm proud of her. It's going even faster than it did among our own people.”“Less fighting I imagine, these humans were so far behind us even before you.”“Not too far. Sure they did things differently, but they did things we didn't. Their hounds for example, none of us ever did that.”“Before you came along.”“Well yes, but I'm an oddball for the century, hardly one to compare to. Though the small number of magic users is odd is it not? I wonder why that is.”“Not sure how to find that out husband,” Isha said.

“That may well be beyond me, seems more like a thing to ask our daughter. We know magic passes from parents down, and that it runs in lines like that. Though it is odd in a few ways.” I shrugged, not really sure what to do with that.

It didn't take long for the blimp to drift down, slowly coming to a sort of rest as the lines were tied in and we were made ready to exit. Of course I didn't much care for waiting so I took Isha in my arms and leapt from the edge, gently floating us down to the city below, where my daughter, and a few others as well, were waiting for us to join them.

“An entrance as always,” my daughter said with a shake of her head.

“Of course, why not? After all, if one can make a grand entrance they should.” A few of the humans gathered round, and for their benefit I was speaking in their language, since I rather doubted they spoke mine.

“Well, come along then, no need to dawdle.”“Oh love,” my perfect wife answered. “We're mostly here to dawdle. If we don't drag your father from his work once in awhile he gets too deep into it, a bit of play is a main purpose of this whole journey.”

“Indeed, gallivanting without any unruly chores to do. Okay, well a few chores to do, but most of that is deliveries and checking up on my favorite daughter,” I said with a shrug.

“I'm your only daughter,” Adia sighed. “Right?” She shot Isha a look and I could see her itching to cast a spell, but she held off, knowing how impolite that would be.

“Alas, yes, though not for my lack of trying,” my wife pouted.

“Forget I asked,” Adia said, grimacing, nobody cared to hear about their parents love life.

“Consider it done, now how do things go?” I asked.

“Well enough, some issues in the East recently, but I sent some people to deal with it.”“Oh? What?”“Not sure father, but a steady stream of refugees, seems there are several tribes fighting, and perhaps some monsters. Nothing we haven't seen before, but never something pleasant.”“Indeed, if any further help is needed, I could...”“Absolutely not,” Isha said. “Adia can handle things, if and only if they get out of control are we getting involved.”

“Ah well, I tried,” I said with a smile.

“Actually, if you want to be useful,” Adia said. “We have a number of refugees, and a small expansion would be helpful, some homes, a bit of an expansion to our infrastructure.”“You want one of the most powerful mages in the world to do construction work?” I asked, feigning incredulity. “That's a wonderful idea, I love it!”

We bantered for a bit longer, her telling us of the past ten years. Surprisingly little had happened with her. There was an elf in another settlement who'd taken a human as his wife, and that led to a bit of discussion. There were concerns that she was so much younger than him, and what would happen when she gold older. Nobody was really sure what to make of it, but she was now with child, and we also had no idea what to make of that.

Personally I worried about the idea of mixed kids. Not because there was anything wrong with loving someone of another... species? I wasn't sure. No, my concern was how they would age, and the fact that I knew that in some species things got... weird, when you mixed them. Mules and hinnies were among those, and they were quite different, and sterile. Was it wrong to have children when you knew those children would never be able to have their own? How would a father who was an elf react to his wife and child getting old and dying? We had no cultural context for that. And what would we do if the children of such unions should turn out to come out malformed, in pain, or never quite right? Perhaps the only answer was to pray, and plan to aid in whatever way we could.

“You look distracted again,” Isha said.

“Ah, nothing we can do anything about now. At any rate, where exactly is this expansion needed? And have you considered your own accommodations Adia dear? Living here for so long in such a small home, I'm sure we can do something about that too!”
