“There is no time to waste. Your duel is tomorrow. Let’s train,” Colorful said, bursting with energy.

“Is there even a point, Master?” Gary asked. “It’s not that I don’t want to, but how much can I improve in a day? Isn’t it better to rest so I can go into battle with a fresh mind?”

“Do you trust me?”

Gary tightened his lips. “Yes.”

“Okay. Step in that empty space over there.”

Gary crossed Colorful’s garden to arrive where he’d been indicated. He stood, feeling weary. He chose to trust.

“Today, you managed to use the third song in the arena. Was it a fluke?” Colorful asked.

“I don’t think so,” Gary replied. He drew his spear, using the first three songs in succession. His application of the third wasn’t masterful, but it was viable. “I’ve gotten it down,” he said. “I focus on something else, which limits the black lightning in my aura. Then, I just use the technique you taught me.”

Colorful nodded repeatedly. “Your third song is trash for now, but it wouldn’t work against Sui Duang, anyway. The fact that you learned it is enough. We can now move on to the fourth song!”

Gary hesitated. Finally, he spoke. “Master… I get you want to keep trying, but we only have a day. I cannot learn the fourth song in time…”

“Idiot disciple. Who said anything about learning?”

“Excuse me?”

Colorful grinned. “I had hoped you’d learn all four songs in time, but that was unrealistic. You were too unsuited for rain. Therefore, while we weren’t training together, I worked a little extra to deduce a way to teach you the Song of Lightning.”

“Really!?” Gary exclaimed with joy before his voice was clouded with suspicion. “That sounds sus, Master.”

“What is the meaning of sus?”

“Suspicious.”

“Why so?”

“You’ve told me this is an old technique. Why would you need to discover anything extra?”

“Ah. Because what we’re about to attempt is completely unsafe and ill-advised.”

“Of course.”

“I’m a practitioner of the Nine Songs of the Spear myself. There are records of previous cultivators using this teaching technique, only without passing it down. I assume because it’s needlessly dangerous. It took me some time to rediscover it, but I’m confident it’s mostly right.”

“Mostly?”

“I can only know so much without experimenting, disciple. I will be frank with you. You have a Duel of Honor tomorrow morning against a powerful opponent. If you go in as you are now, you’ll most probably die. If you let me try this teaching technique on you, you still might die. If it succeeds, you’ll suffer a fate worse than death, but you’ll be able to have a somewhat decent chance of beating Sui Duang.”

Gary chuckled bitterly. “All this effort, all these tricks, a fate worse than death or death itself, all to have a somewhat decent chance…”

“You challenged someone much stronger than you. What did you expect?”

“I expected to have three months before the day of the duel.”

“Man plans, and the Tower laughs. Enough chatter. We need every second we can get.”

Gary sighed. He braced himself, eyes hardening. “What do I have to do?”

“Survive and learn.”

Before Gary could ask more, a stick flew to Colorful’s hands, and he fell into a thrusting stance. Aura built up around him. He was aiming at Gary.

“Um, Master?” Gary asked.

“Song of Mud,” Colorful said, releasing a slow, brown thrust that shot past Gary’s head and into the sky. It was the first time Gary saw his master’s demonstration after learning the song himself. Suddenly, he was humbled by his lack of mastery. Even in the first song, he had so far to go.

“Song of Life.” Colorful barely missed Gary with a regenerative green strike.

“Song of Rain.” His stick flicked upward, releasing drops of blue aura into the sky. Unlike Gary’s Song of Rain, this was the real deal. It contained genuine water vibrations. Gary was lost in the sky, watching the drops and trying to see the laws between them. He lowered his gaze to find Colorful staring fire at him.

“Song of Lightning.”

Next thing he knew, Gary awoke on the grass, body covered in burning pain. He thought he was in the Golden Bolt’s heavenly lightning trial for a moment. He managed to stand, though his nerves were wrecked. “Master?” he asked. “What happened?”

Colorful sat in his chair, gazing in the distance. He turned at Gary’s words. “That was a failure, disciple. Prepare yourself. We’re just getting started.”

Sui Duang practiced the spear. Elegant moves flowed off him like water down the creek. Waves of tiny thrusts followed his every strike. When he stopped, panting and sweating lightly, Elder Sui nodded from the side. “Very good,” he said. “You are in top shape. Even if Gary Steel was hiding something, the gap between you is too large to close with tricks.”

“He’s nothing, Master. Just a no-family drafter with less than six months of cultivation. If he could come even close to beating me, I wouldn’t deserve the last name Sui.”

“Never underestimate your opponent,” Elder Sui said strictly. “Drafter or not, family or not, Gary Steel has reached the tournament semi-finals. He defeated the fifth-ranked disciple without much trouble. The fact that he’s been cultivating for such a short time yet has reached these heights should be a warning to you, not a comfort. His progress is rapid. We don’t know how much he’s improved. Even a lion tries its hardest to hunt a rabbit—with a Duel of Honor coming up, you should do the same.”

“You are wise, Master. Duang apologizes,” Sui Duang said, clasping his fists. His stature was so much larger than the Elder’s that the sight was almost comical.

Elder Sui’s face softened. “The family has entrusted you to me, Duang. This is a test for both of us. Don’t take risks.”

“I understand, Master. I will try my absolute hardest. Tomorrow, no matter what, I will win.”

As Sui Duang returned to practice, Elder Sui moved away. He couldn’t hide the discomfort in his face.

Yes, Duang was stronger than Gary Steel. That much was clear. Based on all the battles Elder Sui had witnessed, Gary Steel held no tangible advantage over Duang, not the tiniest bit.

But the drafter was mysterious. He was backed by Colorful, a mentor admittedly more capable than Elder Sui himself. His rise had been too fast, too sudden. Why? How? What had he experienced in the Floor of Trials? What benefits had he reaped? What treasures?

How was it possible for a man of such low birth to advance so swiftly?

Elder Sui didn’t know, and that troubled him. His years of climbing the tower had made him a cautious man. The unknown held danger—and even the tiniest risk could be lethal. Blades hid in the shadows.

I have a way to ensure Duang’s victory…but can I really ask him to do it? Is it worth planting a bomb in his path just to stomp a little Gary Steel?

This question played around Elder Sui’s mind as he poured himself some tea.

I don’t like Gary Steel. He has a certain temperament—the kind all elders want to see, the kind that grows until it dies. These people are like weeds—unless you thoroughly and unclimactically exterminate them, they always find a way to survive in the cracks, then come back to bite you.

Duang should be the absolute winner. Why does my heart worry so?

Should I really…

“Brochor,” Elder Sui said, and a man walked out of the bushes. He fell to a knee.

“Sir,” Brochor said.

“What’s Gary Steel doing now?”

“The same as ever. Training. All day, every day. It’s almost admirable.”

Elder Sui’s brows twitched. “Save your compliments for our own people,” he said, glancing at where Sui Duang was taking a break. He was only slightly sweaty.

“I apologize.”

“Have you managed to ascertain whether he knows the fourth song?”

“I haven’t been able to watch since he started training in Colorful’s manor. Seven days ago, he was still practicing the second song. It would be impressive if he managed to learn the fourth already.”

“The third song should trouble him, but the fourth should be smooth sailing…” Elder Sui shook his head. “If it was anyone else, I would claim it’s impossible, but this man has been training non-stop for weeks. Who knows how far he’s progressed. With the amount of hours he’s put in, it just might be possible…”

“Is the fourth song enough to beat Duang, sir?” Brochor asked.

Elder Sui glanced at his young disciple, who was still resting. “No,” he said. “But it can give him a fighting chance. Since he cultivates lightning, especially black lightning, the fourth song will be a double power spike. Those are always hard to estimate.”

“Sir, should I try to—”

“You’ll never get past Colorful. Not in a million years,” Elder Sui said. “He makes me uneasy, Brochor. The amount of training he does, the secrets he hides, the tutelage he receives, the fourth song… They’re all question marks. Duang’s victory should be a given, but I can’t shake the feeling that if we don’t try our hardest, something might happen…”

“I’m sorry, sir, but I don’t follow. Is there something you’re not doing?”

“I could teach him the Meat Grinder.”

Brochor’s eyes widened. It was a tiny movement, but still more than this man should ever show. “I apologize. I understand now.”

Elder Sui sighed. “It’s unwise, Brochor. I don’t like teaching risky moves to juniors. They think nothing bad can happen to them, they grow arrogant, and eventually, the move I taught them backfires at the worst possible moment. It would be trading a tiny risk for a real one, but if everything goes wrong, that tiny risk might cost him his life…”

The two men sat in silence. “May I speak my mind, sir?” Brochor asked.

“You may.”

“Trading long-term risk for short-term certainty is always a tough decision. Both options are risky for Duang. However, sir, of the two worst outcomes, only one will get you in trouble with the family…”

Elder Sui looked up sharply. His eyes cleared. He gave a wicked smile. “Please don’t say such things, Brochor. It’s highly improper.”

“Of course, sir. I apologize,” Brochor said lightly.

“You are dismissed. Make sure Gary Steel doesn’t skip town.”

Brochor slipped into the bushes, while Elder Sui paced over to the resting Duang with a clear mind. He changed his carefree expression to a considerate one. “Come here, Duang. I have something to show you.”
