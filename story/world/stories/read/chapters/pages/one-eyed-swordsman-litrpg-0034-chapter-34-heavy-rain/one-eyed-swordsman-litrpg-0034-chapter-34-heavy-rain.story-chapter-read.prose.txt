Killing was nothing new to Oswald. Rain clung to his body, trailing in steady rivulets from his hair, his jaw, the edges of his cloak. Droplets gathered at his fingertips before slipping free, one by one, vanishing into the ground.

The storm had scoured the blood from his face, but it hadn’t washed away the ache in his bones or the heaviness around his chest. His eyes fixed on Cecilia.

She knelt on the ground, arms trembling with the effort of holding herself up. Where Selene’s blade had pierced her chest, black ichor still oozed in thick, sluggish pulses, mixing with the rain.

Her lips parted faintly, twitching around half-formed words she didn’t have the breath to speak. She didn’t fight, more accurately, she couldn't.

She just knelt there, swaying, slowly unraveling beneath the downpour. Oswald stopped a few paces away.

Zareth hung low at his side, its flames dimmed under the constant battering of rain. Steam hissed where water met lingering fire.

Oswald’s grip tightened around Zareth’s hilt. The blade flickered weakly in the stormlight, its once-roaring fire now reduced to little more than embers.

Rainwater sizzled along the edge, whispering into the wind as it hissed and steamed. Every part of him ached, but none of it matched the weight pressing into his chest.

Cecilia knelt there like a statue worn down by time and rot. Her arms shook under her own weight. Her head hung low, strands of soaked hair clinging to her face like seaweed. The black ichor dripping from her wound had slowed, but it still spilled in sluggish pulses, mixing with the rain, feeding the pool beneath her knees.

She swayed, barely upright. Not resisting. Not pleading. Damn it...

The storm pressed in around them, thunder growling overhead like a beast too tired to roar. Smoke curled behind him from the ruin they'd left behind, the fire struggling to keep burning in the downpour.

Oswald shifted his weight, boots sinking into the muck. The silence itched at his skin.

Then, Selene’s voice cut through the rain. “You must kill her, for your own sake.”

He turned slightly, just enough to catch her silhouette through the mist. Selene stood still, the horns crowning her head like thorns of judgment, her scaled skin catching the rain in faint glimmers. Her eyes fixed not on Cecilia, but on him.

Oswald looked back at Cecilia. She ain’t even moving much.

He took a breath through his nose, jaw clenched. The rain was cold, but the thoughts in his head burned. But she tried to gut me, didn’t she? And she damn near pulled it off.

She’s probably killed dozens. Maybe more. How many people died just because she was a little thirsty? How many believed her lies while she drained ‘em dry?

His fingers tensed. Whatever good she did… it doesn't wash that kind of blood off.

Oswlad stepped forward, slow, one foot after the other. Zareth’s fire stirred, faint but alive, as if it too understood the decision being made.

Cecilia’s head twitched. Just a fraction. Her eyes lifted halfway to meet his.

She didn’t speak as Oswald raised the blade. Then he drove the sword down.

Zareth’s edge cut clean through flesh and bone, plunging into her back, right through the heart. A sharp breath escaped her lips, followed by a low, choking sound. Her body convulsed violently, muscles spasming as fire raced through her, devouring her from within.

She collapsed fully, hands digging into the wet earth, clawing without aim. Rain soaked her face as her back arched off the ground. Black ichor sprayed across the mud, sizzling where it hit the blade. The fire within Zareth flared brighter, golden and furious despite the rain.

Oswald stepped back, chest heaving. Cecilia writhed.

Her body twisted and jerked, bones cracking beneath the skin, grasping at nothing. Her breath rattled, then stuttered. The light in her eyes dimmed.

Then, finally… she stopped. No last word, just stillness.

And then came the light. Her body, once weighed down by rot and decay, lifted faintly. Glowing red particles rose from her chest, soft and luminous. They shimmered, suspended in the rain, before slowly drifting toward him.

Oswald stood still, stunned, watching the glow seep into his right eye. The sigil in his iris pulsed with a dull, aching heat. The souls pressed into him, one after the next, each brushing against the edges of his thoughts like whispering fingers in the dark.

[Congratulations, you have gained 2,000 souls!]

The message burned across his vision before vanishing into nothing. Oswald lowered his sword. At least it's over now.

Oswald stared at the empty space where Cecilia had once knelt. Rain washed the last traces of her from the earth, but the weight she’d left behind clung to him like wet ash.

He turned toward Selene, still catching his breath. “It’s done.”

“No, there is one more that must be killed.” Selene said.

That's when Oswald heard the footsteps. Lily stepped out of the house, limping. Her cloak hung in shreds, her hair matted with blood and rain. One eye had nearly swollen shut, and her arms were streaked with bruises. Yet her face held no rage, only grief.

She dropped to her knees beside what remained of Cecilia’s cloak. Lily reached out with trembling fingers and gathered it into her arms like it were a child. Her lips quivered, but no words came, only a choked gasp, followed by a sob that tore loose from somewhere deep.

Then another. And another, until her body shook beneath the weight of tears.

Despite the pitiful display, Selene’s voice pierced the moment like a blade. “Kill her.”

Oswald blinked. “Huh?”

“Pureblood vampires are dangerous, Oswald, I've at least told you this much."

“Yeah... I remember. I'll finish the job."

Oswald turned back toward Lily. She hadn’t heard him talking with Selene. Or if she had, she didn’t care.

Lily's fingers clenched tighter around Cecilia's cloak, pulling the fabric close like it could hold together whatever pieces of her had been shattered. Her head hung low, shadowed by damp strands of red hair that clung to her cheeks. Her sobs were quieter now, less violent, but just as raw.

You've killed people before; this is no big deal. Oswald thought, tightening his grip on the sword in his hand.

The boy was keenly aware that he had spilled blood many times before, and often for reasons that were far from justifiable. Part of him believed that killing Lily would do nothing to absolve him of the wrongs he had committed.

Still, Lily and Cecilia were far from virtuous. They may have saved lives, but only to keep feeding on their victims' blood.

Besides, Oswald had killed for far less. Seeing Lily cry shook him a little, but it certainly wasn’t enough to stop him from doing what had to be done.

Oswald drew in a breath through his nose and stepped forward. His boots sloshed through shallow puddles as he closed the distance. Rain continued to fall, steady and unrelenting, washing the blood from the ground but not from his memory.

“Sorry for this,” he said.

Lily’s head twitched at the sound. For a heartbeat, he thought she might ignore him again. But then, her shoulders trembled. A soft giggle bubbled out. Then louder. Then louder still, until it was no longer a giggle but a full-bodied, broken cackle that echoed across the rain-slicked ruins.

“You’re not sorry, do not lie to me! I can hear the hollowness in your voice. You say the words, but your heart feels nothing.”

Oswald took an instinctive step back, raising his sword. The fire along its edge flared in response, casting a wavering orange glow across his face. He braced himself, half-expecting her to lunge, to attack with the same frenzy as before.

But Lily just sat there, her arms falling limp at her sides. “I’ve no will to fight left in me."

She looked back at the ruined house, before turning her head towards Oswald again. “Congratulations, by the way, you saved the city. A glorious victory, isn't it? You should feel proud.”

Oswald’s throat felt tight. He took a cautious step forward, then another, lowering his weapon slightly. “Do you want to say anything else? Last words, or… something?”

Lily’s eyes drifted down to the sodden ground. “My light has faded, and soon, so will I.”

Oswald looked down at her, at the torn cloak, the trembling hands, the lifeless weight in her posture. But he raised his sword regardless. Just do it, it'll be over soon.

The blade cut through the silence with a hiss, and Lily’s body went still. Her head tilted to the side.

A few seconds later, light bloomed. Her form shimmered with a red glow, dissolving into motes of crimson, like sparks trailing from a dying fire. The particles rose and twisted in the air before spiraling inward, drawn to the sigil in Oswald’s right eye. The X-shaped mark flared as it drank in her soul, the tendrils of red vanishing into its depth until nothing remained.

Oswald just stood still after that. Rain continued to fall, tapping against the stones, the steel, his skin.

He didn’t feel triumphant. He didn’t feel regret, either. Just… numb. He lowered his sword and stared at the space where Lily and Cecilia had been. At least it's all over now.

[Steel your heart, or be devoured by doubt when the hour is dire]

Oswald's eyes widened. That was the first time the system ever said anything like that to him. But just as quickly as he saw those words, they vanished, and then replaced.
