A town between two major empires—that was the scenario of this trial. The city we were placed in—an insignificant speck of dust in the grand scheme of things—was located between the edge of the jungle elf territories and an aelf stronghold. Both sides despised humans, though were willing to tolerate them should they assist in bringing victory to the respective side.

Normally, we’d be sent in by the time the battle was ongoing. I used to think that it was only to provide entertainment for the deities. Now, I suspected that we had been the “afterparty”. The skilled participants were sent first to set the scene and reach the climax. The trash was only there to mop up the remains.

The more things change, the more they stay the same, I thought.

What does that mean? Serp asked.

The Listener card had helped me learn a great many things. The first was that, apart from the old man, the other participants were keeping to themselves. After what Marcus had done, it was normal for Sparta’s group to distrust me. As for the rest, they likely had their own agendas. After the last trial, we were no longer a group and, as Harlequin had shown, only first place mattered.

“I say we go against the savages,” I heard a hushed discussion between city guards. “They’re easier to kill than the castle.”

That was a stupid assumption, but the implication was clear. I had heard hundreds of similar discussions whispered all over the city. The local ruler hadn’t decided which side to join, while various factions were pulling for joining a different enemy. What they didn’t know was that either side they chose, they’d lose. During similar trials in my previous run, our goal had only been to kill a certain number of enemies. Occasionally, we’d aim for a general. Each time, we had been slaughtered by monsters on both sides.

What does that mean, old man? Serp repeated his question.

“Nothing.”

My divine blades didn’t detect anything remotely strong within the city, so I went to the second-best source of information: inns.

I made sure to pick one in the third circle, as close to the gates as possible. Mercenaries, travelers, and soldiers frequented those places the most. In order to boost my odds a bit more, I also chose one that was owned by a retired soldier.

“Something to drink,” I said, slamming a silver coin on the counter.

“Just that?” The man gave me a stern look.

From what I could see, he had definitely been a soldier. In his mid-thirties, he had the scars and attitude to prove it. No missing body parts, so he had done at least something right. I immediately spotted a few blades hidden behind the counter. Old habits clearly died hard.

“And some information.” I looked about. “Any interesting beasts in the area?”

“You want work? Check at the adventurer guilds,” he grumbled as he poured me a mug.

The alcohol in the place was weak as piss, that’s why I enjoyed it so much. After last time, I had no intention of getting drunk ever again. At least not until I had settled my debts with those who betrayed me.

Casually, I whistled a tune, activating my Bard card. Aether threads came out of my mouth and attached to his ears.

“I’m sure you know something.” I held the mug with both hands. “I just want to sharpen my skills.”

During the past runs, a few combat veterans had shared that long-trials were full of additional rewards. Anything from loot caches to rare creatures to sphere-holding monsters were scattered about. All one needed to find them was to ask the right questions at the right places.

The innkeeper looked at me, probably trying to determine whether I was skilled, stupid, or suicidal. I must have passed his trial, for he shook his head without walking away.

“Not my business if you’re wasting your silver.” He shrugged. “They say a turtleclaw’s been hanging near the southern-side caves.”

“Turtleclaw?” I thought back to my previous run.

The creature was among those to be avoided, which meant there was a chance for it to release something nice. With a bit of luck, I could even get a card out of it, provided Harlequin’s generosity hadn’t spontaneously ended.

“And no one has gone for it?” I knew that the locals wouldn’t, but there was a chance some other participant might have.

“Too close to the pales,” he said with a hiss. “Only idiots go there. Idiots and people who want to sharpen their skills.”

I laughed. No one wanted to mess with the aelves. Compared to them, the elves were outright cordial.

“How long to get there?”

“On horseback, maybe two days. More if you’re sneaky about it.”

“And on foot?”

The innkeeper looked at me as if I had pissed in his beer kegs.

“Maybe a week. You don’t look like someone who couldn’t afford it.”

I could afford it, though I found it useless without a Rider card. Any participant with a bit of strength could outrun and outlast a common horse.

“Save me a room,” I ordered using my Bard card.

“Right.” He nodded. “Don’t die.”

I had no idea whether I’d return to this place, but it was always better to have a room ready.

The night was calm and unusually cold. The streets were filled with guard patrols, especially this close to the gates. The war had everyone on edge, and the local nobility was probably worried that might give people ideas.

We’re going to hunt it, right? Serp asked.

Yes, I mentally replied. If it drops a sphere, it’s yours.

Finally! It was painful watching you waste them on cards last trial.

Better than—

“You’ve certainly made an impression,” a familiar voice said from a nearby alley.

I didn’t need to turn around to know who it was.

Sparta. “I didn't intend to.” I turned around.

The red mist had transformed her into a cross between a low-level assassin and a high-end thief: a cloak, leather clothes, and a rather fetching pair of boots. It didn’t escape me that she had no weapons on her person, suggesting that she had likely moved them to her inventory.

“I wasn’t able to find you,” I said and walked up to her.

From the Observer card, I knew that she welcomed it.

“I guess I was being a bit more subtle.”

I couldn’t help but smile. She was flirting with me. Some things didn’t change even if our power levels were reversed. I could see her cards, her specials, even the new stats that had appeared on the status box. With the exception of divinity, I could safely say that I beat her in every aspect. Yet, everything in her stance suggested she was confident she held the upper hand. If so, I saw no point in disappointing her.

“What about the rest?”

“Half are in the dark. There’s a chance they might have been sent somewhere else.”

Or they didn’t want to be found. By the sound of it, I wasn’t the only one who had issues with other participants. Then again, that was only true if she was telling the truth.

“Are you really thinking of going hunting?” Sparta asked.

“It’s never too soon to gain an advantage. I’m sure you know that.”

Her smile widened slightly, showing more of her teeth. As before, they were flawless. Mercury abhorred imperfection, so he made sure to sculpt away even the smallest flaws among his followers.

“Want to join?” I probed the situation.

It was a terrible thing to ask. With everyone being on their own, there was no guarantee that the turtleclaw would drop more than one copy of cards. There was a good chance that we’d have to share. That was unless she decided to steal my prize.

“You’re not inviting anyone from your group?” Sparta narrowed her eyes.

“Couldn’t find them.” That was the truth. Of course, I would have asked even if it wasn’t. “So, how about it?”

Are you sure about this, old man? The serpent blade whispered. I don’t like her. Besides, her blades are nasty and—

“Sure.”

Shit! Serp yelled in my mind. I’m not sharing the sphere with her! You see it, you get it!

“Of course,” I said out loud.

Even if I had hoped for this response, it surprised me. The Sparta I knew was a lot more cautious. The only times she had agreed to a request was if she were to make it first. Could that mean that she aimed to get back together with me from the start?

“Good,” I managed to say. “Let’s go hunting.”

Just like that, a team that had taken years in the making had been reformed in less than a day.

Neither of us gave any stock in any animals we could find in the city. With a war approaching, the only ones worth anything belonged to the army, the nobles, or those who weren’t willing to part with them. Besides, unless the horses were granted any blessings or special abilities, they would be a lot slower than us sprinting.

Breaching the city walls proved easier than expected. Having the thief card allowed Sparta to climb over without making a sound. On my end, I relied on my physical strength to just jump onto one of the lower sections.

Once outside, we dashed in the direction of the mountains.

“Any chance you can ask your god for some help?” I probed.

“To give me horses?” She laughed, elegantly avoiding the question. “I think we can manage that on our own.”

“Quite confident of you. I’m Greg, by the way.”

“I know.”

Harlequin had made certain of that. Of course, that wasn’t why I had mentioned it.

“And you are?” I pressed on.

I knew her name perfectly well; I was looking at it even now. What I really wanted was for her to say it.

“Call me Sparta.”

Just like last time. “That’s a weird name. Greek?”

“No. Just a nickname I got in college.”

That was different. Last time she had said that the nickname originated when she was a child. Supposedly, her grandfather had been a huge fan of ancient mythology and had started calling her that. Whatever the truth was, I suspected both explanations to be lies.

The innkeeper had said that it would take us two days to reach the mountain caves. Thanks to our skills and blessings, we managed to reach it in three hours. Getting brief flashbacks from my past trial, I expected my chest to explode in pain the moment I exerted myself. It was a relief to see that didn’t happen.

Once we neared the mountain, I took the divine blades out of my inventory. The innkeeper hadn’t been very specific about directions, though it wasn’t that I had asked. In truth, I didn’t care what I had to face. As long as the blades considered it strong enough to merit attention, it was almost certain to have a prize.

“What’s wrong?” Sparta slowed down.

The blades moved a fraction to the left.

Got you! “Get ready!” I moved the blades up to the point I felt no resistance.

They were pointing towards the foot of the mountain. Using my Eagle Eye, I could see the opening of a cave.

Never gets old, I thought.

In my past run, Harlequin would send us on cave hunting trials rather often. The goal was always the same: find and kill a set number of monsters. In this case, we’d only be facing one. Of course, both of us were considerably weaker.

“The cave.” I pointed with my left blade. “It’s there.”

“I’ll take your word for it.”

With the direction clear, I sped up again. Reaching the mouth of the cave, I stopped. I could clearly feel the presence of several beasts. Going by basic logic, this has to be a lair. The sounds coming from inside confirmed my suspicions.

“There’s several,” I said.

“I know.” Sparta couldn’t help herself. She was just as competitive as any veteran in the Grand Arcana. “How do we split the rewards?”

“My lead, my pick,” I said without hesitation. “In case of doubles, even split.”

“Works.” She nodded. “Can you see?”

Now, it was her probing me. She knew from the last trial that I could see in the dark, so likely she was trying to determine the source of my skill.

“Yes,” I replied. “Ever fought a turtleclaw before?”

Seeing her shake her head seemed strange. Even if she hadn’t made a pact with a hunting deity, she was supposed to have that knowledge. Apparently, her connection to her patron deity was truly restricted, which meant there was a chance during this trial she was completely free of his influence.

“I’ll take the lead,” I said. “You just make sure that nothing else interferes.”
