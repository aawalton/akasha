Ten days until the tournament,Twenty-seven days until the invasion.

Gary reached the end of the Heaven Reaching area. The forest thinned here, becoming a plain farther on, but the border—and the area he was interested in—was indicated by a crack on the ground, a canyon stretching a mile long but only a hundred feet wide. Mist twirled around the edges, hiding its contents from invasive eyes, while runes swam inside that mist. A single end of the canyon was on ground level, leading to a steep descent between the stones.

This wasn’t the first time Gary visited the Valley of Broken Spears. He was, however, even more awed than before, precisely because he knew what lay inside.

“Greetings, Elder,” he said, bowing light. The shirtless, rough, battle-scarred Elder guarding the entrance grunted in response.

After spending over three months in the clan, Gary had come to recognize some of its symbolisms. Most clan resources were guarded by deacons. Only sensitive places like the Cliff of Seclusion, the Spear Hold, and this valley were guarded by Elders. The Cliff and the Hold needed Elders for the extra security—this valley, Gary assumed, had an Elder standing guard out of pure respect.

Gary looked around. It was more crowded than usual, with a dozen cultivators meditating around.

“Is there a line to enter, Elder?” he asked respectfully.

“There is no line,” the Elder replied.

“Oh. I thought everyone was waiting for their turn.”

“This is a prime meditation spot. Everyone is excited to get stronger with the tournament coming up.”

“I guess the best time to train is right before you need it.”

The Elder cracked a grin. “You may enter.”

“Thank you, Elder.”

Gary paid the Elder thirty aura stones and walked into the Valley of Broken Spears. The atmosphere changed instantly. It was colder now, and darker. The tall stone walls blocked direct sunlight on all hours but high noon. A faint mist hung in the air, limiting visibility to only a few dozen feet.

Before long, the steep downhill evened out, and Gary found himself before a plain tombstone. Empty stone stretched in all directions, while the mist coagulated fifty feet ahead.

Clear writing decorated the tombstone: “Hay Dragonfault, high earth talent, Inner Elder of the Heaven Reaching clan. He fell in a Duel of Honor on the 73rd floor before his potential could bloom. May his spirit ever climb.”

“Gary Steel greets senior Dragonfault!” Gary said, clasping his hands.

White vapor emerged from the tombstone, forming the ghost of an old man. His hair was caught in a ponytail, and his jaw was sharp and angular, eyes carrying the resolve of a warrior. Transparent robes blew around him to an unfelt wind. His gaze rested on Gary with the calmness of mastery.

“I remember you,” he said.

“Thank you, senior.”

“You have become stronger.”

“I have.”

“Strength has given you confidence. By challenging me, you stand to lose it. Are you certain?”

“I don’t need confidence, senior. I need drive. I need fire.”

“Then I shall fight you at my best.”

The ghost glowed for a moment, forcing Gary to avert his gaze. When he looked again, a handsome young man stood there. He looked slightly older than the previous time Gary had fought him, maybe eighteen years of age. His body was more filled out. His gaze was steadier. His spear remained golden and decorated with dragon scales.

As for the ghost’s aura, it was now at a similar level to Gary’s.

Spear Phantom of Hay Dragonfault, Level 21

Ever tried kebab from a poor street merchant? The ones that char their meat so you can’t taste how bad it is? That’s exactly how he’ll cook you—and exactly how you’ll feel when you’re done.

Don’t underestimate this guy just because he has no rank. He’s easily a field boss.

The last time Gary was here, he’d been level 12, but senior Dragonfault had fought him with a level 10 cultivation—a realm below Gary. Gary had barely won.

This time, Gary had a legendary spear in place of a wooden one, a far mightier vibration, and greatly increased stats.

But Dragonfault was no longer a realm below him.

The ghost attacked. Gary met him bravely, blow for blow, but quickly found himself pushed back. Their stats must have been similar. It was their skill that varied. Dragonfault flowed between the moves like he’d been born with a spear, prevailing in every exchange, gaining small advantages that slowly but inevitably built into greater ones. Gary’s momentum was exhausted, and he had to start backstepping.

The moment he took that first step back, the battle’s momentum turned entirely against him. The spear excelled in offense but lacked in defense. In a battle of spear-wielders, whoever controlled the distance controlled the battle.

But, Gary had more tools than last time.

“Thunder Step!” he yelled, blasting sideways. Dragonfault’s spear met air. Gary thrust, but Dragonfault blurred, stepping into the ground so hard he cracked it below his foot. He dashed a short distance to dodge, then nailed his foot into the ground again, forcing himself to a still.

“Dragon Dash!” he cried out. His voice held hints of amusement. Gary reactivated his movement technique, as did the ghost. They zapped around each other, their spears moving in wide slashes that never found purchase.

“That’s decent footwork, junior, but mine is better! Among short-distance movement techniques, my Dragon Dash is king!”

“How do you even have knees after stopping like that?”

“Shielding my knees with aura is the tricky part. It’s a powerful but dangerous technique. In my generation, only I mastered it—that’s why I was called the Golden Dragon!”

Thunder Step gave Gary a sudden burst of speed. He could then use the principles of electricity to instantly decelerate, expending the excess energy as a short-range lightning burst. It had been Gary’s strongest technique so far, but as Dragonfault had said, his was slightly better. It took less time to activate, mere fractions of a second, and achieved similar acceleration and top speed. Its greatest advantage over Thunder Step was that it consumed significantly less energy at the cost of harming the wielder’s legs.

“How can lightning not be the fastest element?” Gary growled.

“It is! There are faster techniques than the Thunder Step, junior, but they’re a little advanced for the current you.”

They stopped dashing around to face each other. Dragonfault was panting, but Gary was panting harder.

“Dragon Drill!” Dragonfault yelled.

“Nail Gun!” Gary shouted back.

The Golden Bolt thrust rapidly and repeatedly until the real spear blended with afterimages, and it looked like a dozen weapons rather than one. Dragonfault wrapped his arms around the spear, then sent it spinning forward like a drill. Gary was confident he’d prevail.

When their attacks met, however, he realized he had been blind. The spear span more than itself—it span the aura around it. Gary’s thrusts were pulled off course, while the Dragon Drill held its trajectory. He jumped back and brought his spear to block—the clash sent him flying, nailed into the cold stone walls.

He thunderstepped away just before a golden spear stabbed into the stone. Dragonfault leapt in hot pursuit.

“Dragon Dash! Dragon Drill!” he yelled.

Gary gritted his teeth. He grabbed the spear from the middle and span it in his hands, coating it with aura. He’d practiced this move intensely—he didn’t always get it right, but this was his first time using it combat. The spear span. It threatened to escape Gary’s hands, but he managed to hold it in. The technique activated. He sensed his aura field spinning alongside the spear.

When Dragonfault’s spear entered the area, something weird happened. Its attacking rotation weakened, but the spear stayed perfectly on course. It rammed the center of Gary’s spear, barely missing his fingers, and sent him flying again. He bounced on the floor before sliding to his feet.

“Do you accept pointers, young man?” Dragonfault asked, laughing.

“I’m all ears.”

“Fan the Spear is a great counter to my attack, but you span it the wrong way! Had you matched the spinning of my Dragon Drill, my spear would have flown completely off-course. You went the wrong way, canceling out the spins but leaving my spear intact!”

Gary hesitated. “I can’t spin it the other way,” he said.

“You need to learn,” Dragonfault replied. He attacked again.

Gary jumped back. “Black Bolt!” he roared, thrusting at the air and unleashing bolts of black lightning towards Dragonfault. He swiped them out of the air.

“Thunder Thrust!” Gary yelled. He thunderstepped directly forward, combining it with a rapid thrust. Using lightning in his arms and legs simultaneously was hard, but he managed. His speartip flew for Dragonfault at tremendous speed. The golden spear blurred—he parried, using some technique to massively accelerate his hands, but the trajectory of Gary’s body remained unchanged. The lightning bursting from his legs struck Dragonfault cleanly, slowing him down for a split-second, in which Gary resumed his dash and rammed his shoulder into Dragonfault’s chest. The ghost went flying.

“That was uncouth,” he said, standing up with a hand on his chest.

“I appreciate the pointers, senior Dragonfault, but I’m here to test my limits. Let’s fight for real,” Gary said.

The ghost’s eyes narrowed. “As you wish.” He lunged at Gary like a dragon. His spear span wild. Before he even arrived, invisible bursts of spinning aura reached Gary, disturbing his own aura. There was too little time between them entering his aura field and reaching his spear—he had no time to adjust. The thrust he’d been about to throw flew wide. He thunderstepped away, dodging Dragonfault’s strike, but the ghost dashed after him, cracking the ground with every step. His movement was rapid and unpredictable. He could change directions with a thought and accelerate instantly. Gary focused on his legs to barely keep up, but Thunder Step took a toll. He smelled burnt flesh.

Gary blocked a slash and went flying. He thunderstepped mid-air, appearing over Dragonfault’s head. The lightning bursting from his legs was going to strike the ghost at almost point-blank. Gary thrust down simultaneously, pouring aura into his spear until the runes glowed and the head hummed.

There came a pulse of aura from Dragonfault’s waist. He suddenly floated horizontally, facing up, opening the distance between his head and Gary’s legs. His spear thrusted up. It split the lightning as rock would split a river. “Dragon Dawn!” he yelled.

“Thrust of Mercy!” Gary yelled back.

Their spears clashed, aura meeting aura. It was like two meteors colliding. A shockwave spread to the sides, angled more up than down, and Gary’s spear was pushed back. The aura struck him from below. He was blown twenty feet into the air, spewing out a mouthful of blood. His high vitality kept him in the battle. He looked down and prepared to thunderstep, but Dragonfault was nowhere to be seen.

Where is he!?

A spearhead entered his aura field from above, followed by a wave of aura so potent it directly invalidated Gary’s aura field. He used a burst of lightning to spin himself, swinging his spear wide to parry. He failed. The spear found him in the chest, its tip blunted by a cushion of dragon aura, and nailed Gary into the ground from twenty feet in the air. A wave of dirt went flying. Pebbles rose. When Gary recovered, a golden spear was on his throat.

“Are you okay, junior?” Dragonfault said, retrieving his spear.

Gary tried to speak but coughed. A bit of blood came out. “I thank senior…for his mercy…”

“I’m here to humble, not kill. I knew your body could take it.” He reached out a hand, which Gary accepted to help him stand. “You can rest for a moment,” Dragonfault said.

“I’ll be fine. Nothing is broken. Thank you for the guidance, senior. Our duel today gave me a lot to consider.”

The ghost’s face fell. “I hope I haven’t discouraged you.”

“The opposite, senior. This was the most thrilling duel I’ve ever had. You gave me pointers and inspirations for my moves. My fire burns bright as ever. I can’t thank you enough,” Gary said, raising his head to show a bright gaze.

Dragonfault nodded. “Train well, young man. We weren’t as far apart as it seemed. Perhaps next time, you can best me.”

“I’ll certainly try!” Gary said, bowing with a smile. The ghost dissipated. Gary turned and hobbled his way to the entrance of the valley. By the time he reached it, his regeneration had kicked in, and he could walk almost normally. It was a good thing he hadn’t broken bones, or he’d be forced to recuperate inside the valley for hours.

As he exited, the Elder nodded at him. Gary held his ribs and suppressed a grunt to bow.

“Finally,” came an annoyed voice. Gary looked up to find a man with long, flowing golden hair and a yellow spear behind his back, his arms crossed and his fingers drumming his elbow with impatience.

“Excuse me?” Gary said.

“A man should know when to yield. Why overdraw yourself to face senior Dragonfault? If you’re too weak, not only will you be injured, but you’ll force true challengers to wait until you’re well enough to walk.”

Gary’s brows dropped. His face darkened. “You sure are arrogant for someone who doesn’t know what he’s talking about,” he said.

“Arrogance is for the weak. The strong have confidence. Now step aside—you’ve delayed me long enough.”

Many cultivators present had opened their eyes from meditation to watch the exchange. The blond man ignored them to walk towards the valley entrance. Gary was still there, and he did not step aside. The blond man’s brows tightened. He shoulder bumped Gary on the way to shove him aside.

Gary remained as still as a rooted tree. It was the other man who stumbled instead. Chuckles came from the meditating cultivators.

The blond man’s brows spasmed like he was about to say something, but finally, he kept his mouth shut. He took a small sidestep and made to enter the valley. A hand fell on his shoulder.

“Hold it,” Gary said.

The blond man turned around and squared up. “What’s your problem?” he said.

“My problem?” Gary said, chuckling darkly. “You insulted me and tried to shove me unprovoked. How am I the one with the problem?”

“What are you going to do about it? Fight me in front of honorable Elder Square?” the blond man asked. The hard-faced Elder to the side was completely ignoring them, meditating with his eyes closed.

Gary snorted. “I want to know your name.”

“What for?”

“Are you afraid to tell me? I’m Gary Steel.”

“Hmph! The only thing I’m afraid of is wasting my precious breath talking to trash. My name is Rat Himake, of the Rat family. Now that you know my family name, feel free to apologize.”

“Do you know Rat Wei?”

The blond man’s face changed. “You knew my cousin?”

“I killed your cousin. He was about as shitty as you, except he actually had a reason to provoke me. You’re just an idiot.”

While Rat Himake stood frozen, Gary started walking away.

“You will pay for messing with the Rat family!” the blond man yelled behind him, his voice filled with barely contained anger, but Gary only laughed.

“I used to live above a rat-infested basement in university—I’ve killed a whole lot of them, and some put up a better fight than your cousin. Please trip and break your neck on the way down to save us all the trouble.”

Rat Himake was so stunned he couldn’t speak. By the time he managed, chuckles were coming from the group of meditating cultivators, and his face went completely red. “Fuck you!” he said, stomping his way into the valley. Gary shook his head and kept walking.

“Well said!” a person called out. He turned to find two women and one man walking his way. “That asshole got what he deserved. My name’s Caramel,” a woman said, shaking his hand.

“Are you really not afraid of his family? They aren’t pushovers. I’m Pop Ear, by the way,” the man said. He had oddly large forearms.

“They aren’t even the largest family after me,” Gary said.

“Thank you for that. Lots of us have been bullied by the families, but few dare to talk back,” the second woman said. She seemed the angriest of the three.

“Why not? It’s not like they’ll send Elders down to kill you,” Gary said.

“It’s not that. Family cultivators are stronger than us. They’ve been training since they were children. Even the talented ones among us, who might have been able to compete, can do nothing about the inequality of resources…” She shook her head. “They have an even bigger stick up their asses with the tournament approaching. They know they’ll dominate the rankings, as they do every year. To them, it’s just fuel to underestimate us more.”

Gary smiled. “I do what I can.”

“I’m Helen. Thank you, anyway. We’re all happy to see those idiots humbled occasionally.”

“He really brought it upon himself,” Gary said. “I appreciate the gesture. It’s nice to see I’m not alone. Give them hell in the tournament.”

“You too! Let’s make this the year where we, the non-family cultivators, can finally match them!” Helen said.

Gary felt the heat. Passion spilled out of these people’s eyes, and their uphill battle touched him. They would most likely fail—but they tried.

I worked hard, but I also got lucky. The Muscle Sap, the Seed, the spear… Without resources, the odds are so low…

The three people said their goodbyes and were returning to meditation when Gary had an idea. “Let’s walk together,” he said.

“We aren’t leaving yet,” Helen said.

“Just keep me company for a while,” he replied.

The three glanced at each other, then followed Gary into the woods. When they were out of earshot, he spoke.

“I know someone who can help you guys,” he said.

“What do you mean?” Caramel asked.

“The families dominate not only because of their training and inherited talent, but also because of their resources. If Rat Himake grew up eating Muscle Sap and who knows what else, all the hard work in the world might not be enough to catch up.”

“That’s right,” Pop Ear said.

“I know a guy who can help you get some Muscle Sap.”

They froze in their tracks. “That’s illegal,” Helen said.

“Very illegal,” Caramel said.

“It’s against the clan itself,” Pop Ear said.

“The clan is good, but it’s also corrupted. It’s unfair. If you guys want a fighting chance in the tournament—a real fighting chance—I can help. If not, forget I ever said anything.”

They glanced at each other again. They discussed with their eyes. Sometime later, Helen turned to Gary. “Who?” she asked.

“Her name is— Um, actually, wait a moment.”

He ignored their stares to dive into the chat.

Gary: Are you here?

Chloe: Yeah, what’s up?

Gary: I have some people from my clan who might be interested. They’re passionate to fight against the families. Where should I refer them?

Chloe: Awesome. Tell them to visit the Mayhem Magical Museum today in the afternoon and look for Bob. He will be wearing a flowery black robe.

Gary relayed the instructions.

“Isn’t that at the Academy?” Caramel asked.

“You know those wizards. They can’t keep their hands to themselves,” Gary said with a shrug. “This guy sells Muscle Sap at thirty aura stones per drop, with a minimum purchase of five drops.”

“We don’t even know if we’re going,” Helen said.

“Whatever you prefer. But, if you really want to get them back—if you really want to make them pay—you need strength. We all do. Imagine the families’ surprise when their sons and daughters are eliminated by random people like us.”

“We’ll think about it.”

“Good enough. Happy meditation, guys. Let’s give them hell.”

Gary walked away, feeling very weird about what had transpired. Am I a drug dealer? he wondered. A good one, but what does it matter? Fuck their families, and fuck their corruption. We’ll break their broken system. We’ll make them pay.
