Before leaving, Milla had given them strict orders not to just rush in headfirst and attack every single Singer all at once. Luke wasn't one for strategy, but it didn't take a genius to figure out that it was sound advice. Their guildmistress also told them to retreat if it got to be too much. Now the problem at hand was how to even manage some semblance of a strategy in the remains of a city where not even a single building had survived. The bridges were gone too, of course, and there wasn't even a tree to see.

"They'll reach the edge of the city in a couple of hours," Curtis said, shielding his eyes from the sun with a raised hand. "It'll still be light out, and most of the dust will have settled."

"We should rush them," Nash said, nodding as if agreeing with himself. Sam and Mas stared at him with their signature expressionless looks, but it was Savannah who slapped the back of his head.

"Shut up," she said. "You think you can act stupid just because Milla isn't here to watch over you?"

Martin stepped up in front of the group. Having lost Rolf looked like it had aged the Phase Blade ten years, but he put up a brave front. "We need to go around and attack them from the side. If we can push a wedge through their formation, we might be able to deal a strong blow to the back of their large group."

Luke and Curtis exchanged a glance, then shrugged and nodded their agreement.

"Let's do that," Luke said. "What Martin just said."

Smudge stepped up next to Luke, and he petted the voidling and fed it another monster core. The little bastard had an endless appetite, but so far Luke was far from running out. The proposed tactic would need stealth, and while Smudge was hard to see in a way, he was also far too impulsive and enthusiastic. If he saw a butterfly, he'd chase it, revealing their position. Luke hated to do it, but he placed Smudge into the Stable in his interface to keep him and the group safe. "Curtis and I will act as the wedge and cut them off. Once we've isolated a group of them, you swoop in and do your thing."

"Just the two of you?" Lemira asked. "I would like to join."

Luke shook his head. "It's too dangerous, and your magic lionesses will be great for an ambush-type attack, don't you think? Besides, we'll have Smudge with us."

Smudge let out a couple of soundless barks, wagging his tail. If he could even fight, Luke didn't know. Once, long ago, he'd attacked an intruder, one of Alan's people, and kept him pinned, so there was some hope there. It was clear as day that Lemira didn't like his answer, but she accepted it anyway. The power difference between Luke, Curtis, and the others was like a chasm, and Luke wondered again if he'd made the right call, even allowing them to join. At that point, only time would tell.

About two hours later, they were all in position. Finding the long column of Singers was child's play, and staying hidden was just as simple. They didn't look like an army on the move, but more like a large family coming to take up residence. Other than a single scout, whom Luke took care of without issue, their target didn't seem to care about anything other than reaching what remained of the city, and they moved fast to get there.

Luke, hidden behind a boulder in a small patch of trees, frowned. "Why are they in such a rush?"

"The portals," Curtis grunted.

"What do you mean?"

"If there are no Integrated, there will be no portals. Remember? They think we're all dead from that blast. Now, they've come back to keep the portals going. More than anything, they want to gain levels."

"Right," Luke said. "Let's wait a little more and then go?"

Curtis stared down at the long column with a quiet intensity in his eyes. "No. We should get down there now, before too many pass by."

"That's the whole idea. We need a manageable number of them."

Luke was about to argue further, but his Weaver's Perception picked something up, something that made him think that maybe, just maybe, this whole thing had been a setup.

"They're coming," he said, turning to face the slope.

Curtis craned his neck. "Who?"

"Singers. The paladin."

"Oh," Curtis said, his blank face splitting into an eerie smile. "That's nice."

"We need to abort," Luke said. "They know we're here." That made him think of the others. "Shit, what if they know about the others waiting down there?"

"Who?"

The more than hundred-strong group of Singers kept pace on the highway below, as if nothing had changed. Movement near the side of the road caught Luke's attention. It was Nash and Martin, getting ready for the ambush. Luke snapped his fingers in front of Curtis's face. "Wake up, asshole! We need to go down there and help them."

"How nice of you two to join us yet again," a booming voice called from over by the crest of the hill.

It was over. Perwen had come. With him knowing their location, the ambush would fall apart in moments unless they charged the Singer lines right away, driving the wedge in. At this point, the others wouldn't be able to pull back without being spotted, and the Singer coming toward Luke and Curtis would spot them from this angle. Their tactic was about to blow up in their faces, and it would soon turn into a bloodbath.

"You choose," Curtis said, keeping his gaze in the direction of the Singers they'd fought in the goblin dungeon. "Can you take these guys alone, or do you want to go down to the road and fight?"

Luke considered for no more than a split second, since time was running out. It wasn't about making the right call. He just needed to make a decision to get them moving. "I'll deal with Perwen first."

Disappearing down the slope, moving as if in a cloud of darkness that blended into the undergrowth and tall grass, Curtis didn't even bother to reply. A figure appeared on the crest and dashed toward Luke, but rather than attack, it blurred past, hunting other prey. Zelaui, the Starlinker. That meant Perwen wasn't alone. Having dealt with her again recently, Luke figured Curtis wouldn't have a difficult time dealing with the aggressive Singer, but she would reveal his location and, in doing so, also put the others in danger. Luke wasn't about to let that happen. She might be fast, but she was far too slow to outrun Weaver's Perception. With her location, speed, and direction held in his mind, all Luke needed to do was reach out with Threads of Mana while having Weaver's Whip activated. Zelaui took care of the rest herself. Charging past him, the Starlinker crashed into a bush a few steps later, leaves rustling, her body sliced in two right below her shoulders. Not a single sound escaped her.

Luke wondered if the pain had even registered before she died as he made his way up the hill, readying himself. He might have grown a lot stronger since they fought in the goblin dungeon, but their progress wouldn't have halted either, and there were at least four of them left. Five, if Perwen found a window to resurrect his wife. Cresting the hill, energy blasted Luke from his right, but he managed to weavestep through the wave, coming out on the other side, right in front of the one called Moonad, their mage. Reaching forward with several threads of mana, Luke intended to destroy her Meta-Heart in one go before the others reacted. Unfortunately, Perwen was quicker than he looked and charged in, a glowing, round metal shield held out in front of him. Not wanting to be touched by that glow, Luke weavestepped again, then again, launching himself at the back of their ranks. There, he came upon one Singer he expected and one he did not.

Lei, he had fought against in the dungeon, but he didn't remember her class. Shock at his sudden approach was all over her expression, an expression he must have mirrored when he came face to face with the other one. Again, he'd failed to notice her presence. Once was an acceptable mistake, but he wouldn't have made that same mistake a second time. She had some trick to hide herself from him. Luom, back again, and she had led Perwen and the others right to him. Weaver's Echo, that had to be it. She'd learned a couple of new skills, then, or hidden some of her abilities before. Luom must've gone through a different portal, because he doubted she'd figured out how to use the weave for travel.

Hesitating for a moment, he lost the initiative, and the other Lifeweaver used The Healer's Moment. In many cases, that could have been the end of the fight. Luke could come back from sustaining a lot of damage, but disabling him for a moment may have been enough for the other Singers to gain enough of an upper hand. But Luom wasn't a fighter, and her threads didn't have the reach. Instead, she'd just panicked and wasted the skill. Luke came out on the other side of her jarring time freeze without losing a step. That moment of hesitation could have meant his doom, and he wasn't about to repeat it, not with so much at stake. Fighting against Perwen, who was able to bring back his friends from the dead, as well as a Lifeweaver who could keep them going indefinitely, was not a winning scenario.

Luke hated to do it, even after she had led the Singers to him, but he needed to even the odds. He let momentum carry him forward and reached Luom with his threads of mana. Her eyes widened in fear even as a barrier formed around her. Moonad was too far back for Luke to cut through the spellweave, but he didn't have to. The Singer barrier mage had been too late. Luom used her will in an attempt to fight off Luke's invasion of her mana channels, but his own washed her resistance away like it was nothing. The other Lifeweaver didn't stand a chance. It felt wrong, another effect of the mental block being gone, perhaps, but that didn't mean Luke would spare her.

Weaver's Rupture with Luke's will as backing tore through her Meta-Heart, reducing all her resistance to nothing. Most people would have been dead on their feet, but Luke didn't take chances with a Lifeweaver. As she fell and the barrier formed, he lashed out with another thread, this one focused on the physical realm. With Weaver's Whip, Luke separated her head from her shoulders, killing the Singer Lifeweaver he'd spent a whole dungeon getting to know.
