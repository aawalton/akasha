The Survivors had scattered throughout the ship, which made the task of picking them off much easier. Rend, Thal al ta Rine, was dealing with the Dreamspawn's brother and the fake prophet. Now all the rest could be purged of sin without interference—a divine mission.

It was an honor and a duty.

All the Survivors must be purged. No exceptions.

The ship had three floors in total.

The bottom floor was half embedded into the dirt and held mostly storage.

The middle floor held the cells; there were rooms as well, but those in reality were just nicer cells.

The top floor was more sophisticated, being made up of luxury living quarters, a small ballroom, a dining area, and the kitchen.

"Above" that was the ship's deck, but if any Survivors were out there, they'd quickly come in. The Red Moon had been a surprise, a frightening and awful surprise that made the last Visitor balk, but he took it for what it was—a sign. A sign that meant they were going too slow, that more Survivors needed to be eliminated, and faster. It felt … it felt bad. They cried when they killed Talin, but there was no choice. They had revealed themselves to her, spouting similar words that Nico and Orlan had both heard, but unlike them … unlike Orlan and now Nico, she didn't believe it. She pretended to, but it didn't reach her eyes. She was going to reveal everything, ruin everything that their God had planned again.

She wasn't supposed to die this early. She was supposed to be handed over to Appraise, but it ended up being too risky. She had defied the will of God once, unknowing as it may have been, so she would do so again. And her eyes. They were in denial. So, the third Visitor summoned Rend from their Restriction. She didn't get to scream. Rend was too fast for that and went for the neck first. They were glad it was quick. It wasn't quick enough. The fear had taken hold of Talin's eyes before she died, but it was still quick.

It was awful.

The Visitor just wanted to go back, back to feeling Joy and not the feelings of this Fiend-forsaken place. They were supposed to be here, so God had made them a divine tool for the works. The Visitor didn't need to know the why—didn't even need to agree with the how—they just needed to act as a messenger this day.

The sound of footsteps drew their attention.

It was the girl that Alder had accidentally shot. Of course, it wasn't the Gladus' fault, it was the Fiend—the Dreamspawn—who was responsible. If that Visitor hadn't stolen Nothing, this woman wouldn't be injured. The Visitor paused, the feelings of fear and sadness growing at the sight of the woman. They focused on something else, anything to get their heart away from the feeling. Dreamspawn. They didn't actually know what that word meant, but God had used it, and it sounded bad, worse than the word Fiend, so it had to be something awful. He remembered the words.

"Above all the Dreamspawn must lose."

The philosophy that the body was a temporary endeavor, and it was the soul that made one true, that it filled the shape of the body it inhabited. Nico had once taught them that a body full of sin was just that, and by stripping a sinful person of their body, they could be born anew.

The Visitor wasn't a Fiend—it was the one comfort they could afford to repeat. A Fiend would destroy the soul; all he was doing was breaking the Vessel and returning Water to the River. They weren't doing anything wrong, as bad as it felt.

If she had done a poor job, then perhaps... Perhaps that would be enough? They didn't know, but the woman was here, and that meant there was punishment owed for something.

Dorian

I didn't have a plan.

That was just another in a series of mistakes, but at times like these, I couldn't afford to think. My start in the arena informed me of that much. The monsters would never stop, and if I slowed for even a second, everything would go to shit. It had been reinforced even more when I had let Jeren take the lead and introduce me as a Fiend.

It wasn't a bad plan. It was just that there was no way to know how the Survivors would react, and maybe it ended for the best as I retrieved Maerin and Platra, but I couldn't shake the wriggling feeling in the back of my skull. The one that said "you could've done better".

So, as I leapt out of the ship, into the waiting maw of Rend, I had no expectations of Jeren's plan working. I'd try to get the monster in position. If his plan worked great, but not for a moment did I plan on relying on him to succeed. Which meant that I had to come up with a way to kill this thing. I had my trump card, but using [Open the Path*] to teleport this thing to the moon would likely kill everyone, as the full force of the red light would be on us.

Rend didn't even allow me to hit the ground before it leapt at me, jaws wide to close around the lower half of my body. I wasn't afraid. I curled my body, letting all of it be enveloped by the Shroud. The monster was forcibly tunneled through to the opposite side of me and rammed headlong into the ship with a splintering crack.

My mind kicked into overdrive.

How to kill it?How would I kill a normal wolf?Stab it in the neck.What if I couldn't break through its fur, in this case, stone?Hurl it off a cliff.There was no cliff.That wasn't the point.The point was to have it kill itself or have the environment do it instead.Was there anything I could use against it?It hadn't revealed any special abilities, and there was nothing else to use against it.

The monster blindly rushed again, biting upwards with its jaw, just to find itself teleported closer to the forest. In frustration, the creature stamped the ground with its paws, angling its limbs at off angles to burrow, elongating claws underneath. The black marble rippled like muscle as it upended dirt in an explosion of force, sending chunks of soft earth and jagged rock hurtling towards me. It all teleported through the Veil and peppered the ship instead. The wolf with the upside-down head growled at me, snarling and salivating.

I couldn't pin them down. The Veil would act as a moveable solid, and this thing was definitely stronger than me. A thought hit me. That's if I was over it, but what if it was the other way around? Yeah. That might work.

I stepped backwards to the ship's hull. My eyes never left the creature, which slowly plodded towards me. Two steps forward for every one step I took back. That was fine by me.

I fell backwards, pretending to trip, and sending the Veil away from my neck.

The beast saw its opportunity and snapped forward in a single burst of power, down and forward like a fox in the snow. I pressed myself up against the hull of the overturned ship. I needed it to come in at a downward angle. It leapt.

Deep breath in.

Deep breath out.

I shoved the Veil underneath my back and up through my stomach. The teeth descended. Drool dripped down onto my face as foul odor wafted down my throat. I almost gagged. Rend writhed. It pushed, it roared, but my Shroud held, pinning it between my Boon and the ship.

"Got you." I couldn't smile, even though I wanted to. My head was too woozy, and the stench too strong.

Its pounce had sealed its fate. It came up and down at an angle, towards me and the ground, but mostly towards the ground. There wasn't any space for it to teleport to on the other side, which means it would go nowhere at all. The Shroud was a solid, and since there was just enough space above me, every time it moved up, it would break contact with the Veil, thus no teleport. As long as I didn't move my hand an inch, it was trapped.

I twisted myself into the Veil, fighting the urge to flinch at every snap of the creature's jaws. This was only the first step. I bought myself some time, now I needed to actually figure out how to kill it. This was also as good as it was going to get for Jeren. I ran through some options. Maybe I could lead it to the ravine and drown it in—

The creature's mouth opened, and from the jaw I saw blinding white, human teeth smiling down at me. They opened, revealing the rest of the creature's mouth beyond. Hands. Tens of pulsating, tiny hands, and beyond even that, a pin-sized gullet.

It spoke, and all the warmth drained from me in an instant.

"Dorian," it said in my Rowan's voice. "Help me."

I froze. I didn't move. I didn't think. I didn't feel. I just sat there, pinned inside the Shroud as the monster whispered to me.

I fought not to cover my ears. It didn't stop.

I started yelling, trying to be louder than it. It didn't stop.

I told it to shut up. It didn't stop.

A shimmer of golden light.

The world engulfed me.

In the absolute safety of the Shroud, I found myself entombed in dirt.

I saw a golden film above the wolf and above me. It vanished a second later, and—

Deeper darkness, the golden film was back. The wolf was still on top of me. It yelped, and I could see cracks in its back forming.

Again.

It cried out in that same voice. No words, just the sound of painful yelling.

Again.

I had never heard my brother scream, but it must've sounded like this.

Again.

Did he cry out in pain the same way, alone in the dream?

On the fifth impact of that golden film, only a fine layer of vibrating dust and darkness surrounded the Veil. All around me was a massive wolf-sized imprint in the dirt. I looked up, seeing the red light of the moon hitting the edge of the crater a good ten feet up, and at the top of it, Jeren, standing there.

What he did hit me like a truck, and I was forced to recontextualize just how powerful [Wayfarer] was. It said that it would shunt an enemy to the edge of the sphere, but what happened when there was something in the way of that? I looked at the shattered stone covering my Shroud. I had my answer.

He stared down at me, I looked up at him, and I swallowed hard. I was scared. If he wanted to, he could trap me down here in an instant. It didn't matter if I had [Shroud of Nothing]. He could bury dirt on me, and there'd be nothing I could do. He had no reason to. He wouldn't, right? But if that were true, why was I also shunted out of [Wayfarer]? It only affected enemies.

I gritted my teeth, ready to use [Open the Path*] if I needed to. Then a rope hit me in the face.

"Come on. We need to go." He spoke quickly, shooting glances behind him.

I didn't argue, just began to climb up as fast as I could with Jeren bracing himself against the rope. I resurfaced gasping for air, not realizing that I had been holding it that entire time.

We both stared into the hole with the Spirit called Rend. We looked at each other uncertainly; neither of us said it, but I knew we were thinking the same thing.

That it was too easy. Too straightforward, that a supposed Great Spirit could die so easily.

I peered deeper, and my eyes widened. The vibrating specks of dust shifted, attached to one another, and coagulated into clumps. I pointed, Jeren followed my finger, and there was no need to explain. We began kicking at the edges of the hole, filling it with dirt and hoping the hole was deep enough that if the creature reformed, it couldn't escape.

We made it inside quickly after that.

We found the remaining Survivor in the dining room, everyone seated around a table. They all looked on edge. I met Maerin's and Krevith's gaze, and frowned even deeper when I saw the man with spectacles sitting with Alder. They weren't able to get him. Damn it. I counted again. No. Not all the Survivors were here.

Alder stood and cleared his throat, looking between me and Jeren. The way he looked at me communicated all that I needed to know about what he thought of me. Less than dirt. "Why is he here? He summoned Rend to kill us!"

Jeren rounded on the man, "He is the only reason any of you survived. I saw a vision that this one gets us out of here alive. Do you deny me my sight?!" He sounded actually angry, which surprised me.

Alder flinched back and looked away.

It was someone I didn't know who spoke next, a woman in a well-fitted three-piece suit. "Rend came from somewhere. That's not the most pressing matter." She looked around the room, meeting everyone's eyes. "The important part is that Biancca is missing, no one can find her."
