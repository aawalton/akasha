The calm before the storm. That’s what I’d call this.

It was…an apt term.

It spoke of a period where men sought to brace themselves, lest they be caught in a howling, pitiless tempest…

And were rent, limb by limb, by a machine of pure destruction.

Uncaring. And…

Indiscriminate.

Speeches had been given, orders received, and soldiers prepared. All across Rhoscara, a hush had fallen over the city. The civilians had been given their own instructions, of course. Back on Terra, I would have assumed they’d be told to evacuate to something like a shelter, or perhaps a bunker. History lessons from my youth reminded me of how, in the great wars of the past, shelters had been prepared for just that eventuality. Great steel cages forged for the sole purpose with which to protect a nation’s most precious resource from the bombs that sought to decimate them. Even the Netherim, those ancient, hidden forebears from which the ‘gods’ of this world had arisen, had such things.

But not now. Not in modern Vereden.

It just wasn’t advanced enough. Not technologically. Not materially.

And certainly not socially.

That was…something I hoped to change. Both with the hands of the Polaris Reach, and with the immeasurably valuable knowledge, hidden within a child’s autonomous instructor, back in Blutstein.

But that wasn’t now.

And so, the people of Rhoscara had been told to hunker down in their homes and their businesses. Perhaps to barricade themselves in and board up the windows so the Savoy hounds at their door didn’t tear out their throats. They had been assured that soldiers, both foreign and domestic, would be patrolling their streets, endlessly hunting and seeking for those who sought the death of their loved ones. And it was even true.

But the city was vast, and there were only so many Rangers…and only so many members of my expedition. We would never be able to be there for all who cried out for a savior.

It was an outright impossibility.

Innocent blood would be shed this day, I was certain of that.

As it always was, in war. The cycle would undoubtedly continue. Great powers would struggle against each other, unable to come to terms that would prevent the scourging of the innocent. Blades would be bared, and life's blood spilled in the name of great concepts. Of soaring, towering ideals, fed to the wide-eyed and young in search of glory and stability.

Their mothers would weep over their ravaged corpses, wailing into the motionless, dead grey skies ‘Why? Why did the blood of my family have to be spilled? Why did my heart have to be broken?’

And on and on and on it would go, until the last fire died and the last cup of water turned to dust in our hands.

This nature of ours…this endless, fractious contention of wills would never cease, would it? Even here, upon the soil of a green world named Vereden, I had found it to be true. Not even those born of a different line than Humanity could be spared from this curse. Not Dwarf nor Gnoll nor Elf of land could be driven from a path where spilled blood awaited, walking together with man into a blood-drenched horizon.

We were all brothers and sisters in devastation. In conquest.

In a grief that stretched across a tapestry of millennia.

This was the world I struggled so hard to save, from an endless, grinding death of corruption unto our very spirits.

What bitter ironies we weaved, that salvation was only possible through the birth of misery.

And yet…for all of the dour cynicism that had crept over me, here in the hour that remained to us, I knew there was no other choice. There truly was nothing else that could be done. Battle would be joined against the forces of those who sought nothing less than total annihilation. I had never once been given the reason behind this campaign, born from the will of Anguis of House Savoy. No motive unveiled to me, as to why he forged such a path of misery.

What could possibly be worth unleashing an apocalypse like the Skyfall upon an entire planet? Of fouling the very air that drifted upon the breeze? Of stealing even the light of the cleansing sun? I had always been told that Anguis, himself the Father of my once tormentor, was a cold man. Callous, yes, but in reality, not given to personally inflicting torment. Some had described him as a beast of logic.

What magnitude of bound flesh was needed to harvest a given yield? What size force was required to squash this uprising?

Like a great glacier of callous wealth, pushing forward enormous boulders of influence over the land that ground his enemies to so much gristle.

What had pushed a man like that to these lengths, if he was truly the mastermind behind it all?

I…had my suspicions, quiet and hidden as they were.

Denied even the certainties of day and night, there was an emptiness to be found in all of us living through this grey nightmare. Undying grey skies…I hated them so deeply it had wounded me. And I’m certain I wasn’t alone. All of the generations upon Vereden who lived through this crisis would suffer the same haunting lesion upon their soul.

The grinding nature of this anguish would never fade from all who survived the crisis.

And yet, we fought on.

For those who would follow us.

May their struggles be lightened. May our labors bear fruit in a shade born of suffering.

All these thoughts and more spiraled through my mind as I sat, alone and unbothered, upon the roof of a Rhoscaran watch tower. In fact, it was the same watch tower I had first sighted the coming Savoy fleet. I really was truly alone, as well. While I sat upon this roof, nominally watching the coming of the fleet, but in reality collecting myself for the battle ahead, both my Core and my Middle Lattice’s were with the expedition. The benefit of having a multi-part mind and a clone Skill was that I could both prepare myself mentally and perform my duties at the same time.

My other thirds were even used to it, these days.

More importantly, though, three hours previously, the fleet had been far enough away that I’d only been able to see them through the use of a far-eye, with eyes enhanced by a high Perception.

No longer.

Now, the fleet born on the backs of bondage was visible on the horizon with bare eyes. I’m sure that even one of the frightened, huddling civilians in the city below would have been able to see them.

They flowed down the breadth of the Fiume d’Oro like a mass of molten iron. Plated hulls of surpassing size, borne forward by green and gold sails, themselves bearing a coiled serpent. Oozing across the water with an ill purpose, threatening to crush all that lay within its path.

Wonderful. Really, I was so incredibly happy to see them.

At the very least, the waiting would soon be over.

Still, I had one last task to see to.

It was time to update my Status. I’d been so busy after taking on the Savoy saboteur ring, followed by checking up on matters down south, and then helping with the last-minute organization of the defenses that I hadn’t done so yet.

But it would be incredibly foolish not to do so. What scraps of power the System might hand out to me might mean the difference in one day returning home to Aveline…

Or dying at the end of a Savoy blade.

And yet…for the first time since I had gotten my Status, I was truly wary of it. I know I hadn’t always been either strict or punctual when it came to actually accumulating power from the System. I was forgetful, and I was, at times, a bit…dismissive. No matter how much I enjoyed gaining Level and Skills and Talents, there had always been a portion of myself that was…almost disbelieving of it.

This strange, half-broken spiritual mechanism was meant to grant me the power to stand against literal monsters?

I well remembered quiet, doubtful, hidden thoughts I’d had about that, in the first few days since I’d been given a Status by Grey. I had, mostly, gotten over it. If not without my almost reluctant moments, of course.

But now…

From my estimates, this Level up was going to be special. I was near to something that was important, after all. Something I had been warned about some time ago.

At level two hundred and fifty was something other than a Breakpoint. For reaching the quarter point to the Level cap of one thousand, you were granted a special boon by the System.

A new Class Selection.

It was at level two fifty, five hundred, and seven fifty that you were granted the ability to take on an additional Class, exponentially increasing your abilities. For a Paragon, for instance Alveron, you would have a total of four Classes, when it came time to reach the absolute peak of power the System could give.

It changed things. Your Aetherial weight became denser, as another archetype was layered over your soul. You gained strange new Skills and Talents to mark the occasion, much in the way I had at level ten. Three brand new Skills or Talents that would come with the new Class. A new Class that would be influenced by the amount of Impact I had generated in the nearly two years now since I had been dropped on Vereden.

Surely…surely I had done enough with Aetherial Melding to qualify for a higher tier, a higher quality of Class.

That was one of my fears, of course. And truthfully, I doubted I would reach the point of Class Selection with the update I was about to do.

Instead, I would be given something else.

My Capstone Skill.

It was my belief that I was likely going to Level up to, or even a little past Level two hundred and thirty. That was the last point in someone’s Class where you would gain a Skill, and never a Talent, from your current, first Class gained at Level ten. The next time you gained anything other than Virtue points from the System would be when Class Selection occurred at two fifty.

From everything I had been told, a Capstone Skill was meant to be the consolidation of everything that defined your current Class. The quintessential ability that was the essence of whatever it was that you chose when you first gained it. The pièce de resistance, as it were.

The main dish.

This Skill was meant to change your fighting style for the rest of your life and be a cornerstone of your new one.

For me, the first Class I had gained, all the way back in the forests outside of Addersfield, chased by a rogue serpentine Prime…was Thornblade Acolyte.

I had lucked out in a very minor way, at the time, with that Class. It was Superior in quality, something that was relatively uncommon. Most people who became serious Classers made the decision to do so after receiving an Uncommon quality Class.

It wasn’t much of a boon, but it had been enough.

In the time since, sometimes when I really stretched myself, when I was tested in battle, I almost felt like I was…chafing against it. I wasn’t even speaking metaphorically.

I was being literal.

Thornblade Acoylyte was a perfectly fine Class for a down-on-their-luck slave seeking to evade the notice of their masters, and then strike out against them. But it wasn’t quite as powerful as I needed it to be, if my ultimate ambitions lay in the direction of deicide.

However…

Something had changed in me in the time since I’d been granted that Class. Something that had ultimately worked out for me, of course. But it had been very touch and go at the time.

My first Breakthrough, back at Level one hundred. Where Grey had needed to infuse me with his own Mana to save my life, and in the process granted me a secondary Affinity.

I had been born with a Terrestrial Affinity, and that had likely influenced the System’s decision to grant me Thornblade Acolyte. But in the time since I’d been granted the additional Celestial Affinity, it hadn’t escaped my notice that I tended to gain both Terrestrially aligned abilities…

But new, azure fire inspired ones, reminiscent of distant, cold stars in the outer heavens.

All the while, my Class had remained the same.

As I’d grown in strength, I could sometimes feel the impression of Thornblade Acolyte, its complex weavings intertwined with my soul by the System.

They felt a little like clothes worn past their prime, the edges of the thread fraying at the seams. It worried me, I wouldn’t lie. There was only so much power left in the Class, pushed past its limits.

And so, I was worried about just what would happen, trying to reach for the Capstone Skill of Thornblade Acolyte. Would it shatter, and take my Status at the same time? Would I be left to fight with nothing more than my own flesh and blood, bereft of the gifts of the System?

Or was I just being a dour little shit right now, because I was only a few minutes away from the largest battle of my life?

Probably that one, I conceded.

Christ, I was a moody bastard these days. The combination of the circumstances and the Skyfall were stealing what joy I had in my heart with each passing day.

Not even a few hours with Aveline were enough to dispel it, I suppose. Only push the melancholy away for a time.

Enough of this.

I reached for my Status with Hidden Amidst the Spheres.

Knew it.

I skimmed the rest of the dialogue, finding I hadn’t even really leveled up any of my General Skills or Talents. The things that mattered were starting to plateau.

But there, at the end of the level-up message.

I found it.

I hesitated for only a moment more…

And then clicked Yes.
