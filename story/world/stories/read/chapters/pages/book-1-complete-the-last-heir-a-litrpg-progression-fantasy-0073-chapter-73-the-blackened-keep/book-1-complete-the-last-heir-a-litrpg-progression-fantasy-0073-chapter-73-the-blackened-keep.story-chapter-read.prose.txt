The moment Rowan's finger touched the edge, the world around him disintegrated into a burst of chaotic light. For a heartbeat, everything blurred—a dizzying meld of sensation that was oh so different from the teleportation he'd become accustomed to.

The taste of burnt ozone hit his tongue, followed by a piercing shriek that rattled his bones. The sound vibrated through his skull, leaving his ears ringing as if a bell had been struck inside his head.

Up was down, left was right, forward was back, and Rowan had a feeling that if his [Fire Shield] wasn't somehow keeping him anchored, the transition would have been even harsher.

The magical protection wrapped around him like a second skin, its familiar warmth providing stability amid the chaos.

All around him, reality seemed to reassemble itself, threads stitching together to form a mesmerizing tapestry of something Rowan couldn't quite make out. Colors he had no names for blended and separated, forms taking shape only to dissolve again.

Then, from one moment to the next, the tumult stopped.

Rowan staggered to his feet, immediately flaring [Iron Will] to get his bearings. Like being plunged into an icy bath, his mind sharpened, the nausea and disorientation making way for a familiar focus.

He'd expected an ambush, but what greeted him instead was an old, abandoned corridor. Torches hung from the walls, illuminating the well-worn stone beneath his feet. The flames flickered with an unsettling bluish tint, casting long shadows that seemed to move of their own accord.

Rowan waited a few beats longer before withdrawing his shield.

His mana flowed back up his body, pooling to cover just his neck and back. Having a full suit of Fire armor was certainly useful, but the mana draw was staggering, even with his enlarged reserves.

Suddenly, a notification appeared.

[System Notification]

You have entered a Corrupted Rift: [The Blackened Keep – Tier 2]

A fortress lost to time, swallowed by the Abyss. The Blackened Keep stands as a bastion of forgotten horrors, where Demons carve their will into stone and blood fuels the echoes of the past.

Objective: Defeat the Rift Guardian.

Rewards: ??? [Unstable Rift: Rewards may fluctuate]

WARNING: Rift Anomaly Detected.

Rowan read it over once. Then twice. And then a third time just to be sure, his eyes lingering on each word like they might change if he looked away.

There was more than one thing that caught his eye in the notification, but there was only a single thing that demanded his attention.

"Swallowed by the Abyss?" he repeated, the hairs on the back of his neck standing on end. "Well... fuck."

Out of everything that happened today, this was by far the most surprising—and not to mention alarming.

The Abyss was the void between the Material Plane and the Nine Hells. It was a place few ventured into—and even fewer returned from. The stories he'd heard as a child spoke of endless darkness and twisted beings that defied comprehension, of laws of reality bent beyond recognition.

"That's why the teleportation felt so rough," Rowan realized, his mind racing as the pieces fell into place. "It had to pierce through the gods' damned veil between realities."

He took a moment, letting that particular fact wash over him. His heartbeat quickened, his hands shaking in a strange combination of both exhilaration and fear. Sweat beaded on his forehead despite the chill that crept along his spine.

I'm in the Abyss, he told himself. Hunting a Demon.

Rowan let out a strained laugh, shaking his head as the tension pressing down on his shoulders rose. The sound echoed down the corridor, bouncing off the ancient stone walls in a way that made it almost seem like something was laughing back.

He was by no means an expert on Rifts, but he did know that they usually didn't involve this much strangeness. They were like bubbles connected to the Material Plane, enclosed spaces with their own rules but still fundamentally tied to the world he knew.

This, however, felt different. It seemed that this Corrupted Rift acted more like a bridge, spanning the gap between planes.

Another moment passed, and Rowan let out a slow, deliberate breath.

"It's not like where I am changes things," he muttered, cracking his neck. "This is still a Rift, corrupted or not."

Rowan doubted he was in the actual Abyss, because if he was, there was a very real chance he'd already be dead. The 'Unstable Rift' part was worrying, but there was little he could do about it. He came here with a goal, and that hadn't changed either.

Objective: Defeat the Rift Guardian.

Rewards: ??? [Unstable Rift: Rewards may fluctuate]

Firming his resolve, Rowan started walking down the corridor, his footsteps echoing softly against the stone.

Things would have been much easier if he only needed to fight a single opponent, but besides the strangeness of it all, this appeared to be a fully functioning Rift. Which meant monsters. Plural.

At the end of the hallway, a large gate barred his path. There were old runes carved into the stone, pulsing with a malevolent light that seemed to throb in time with his heartbeat. He stepped closer, his eyes tracing the sharp, jagged script.

Va'khir wasn't a language humans could speak, but reading it was another matter entirely.

Rowan's lips curled up into a smile. "I guess all those hours spent in the library are finally paying off," he muttered, closing his eyes and trying to recall the Demonic lexicons he'd read.

Unfortunately, it wasn't as simple as comparing words.

Va'khir was a runic language, meaning Rowan needed to puzzle it out. The symbols combined and separated in ways that made little sense, each one containing layers of meaning that shifted depending on context. But thankfully, he'd always been good at puzzles.

I feel like a proper Delver, he thought, unable to hold back a smile.

After spending most of his life thinking he'd never be able to advance, Rowan needed a moment to truly process where he was.

I'm the first person inside this Rift. Or well, at least the first person actively delving it, he thought, a spark of excitement mixing with the apprehension. There aren't any guides for me to look over or experts to ask. He reached out, his fingers a hair's breadth away from touching the stone. But if this isn't a clue, I'll tangle my circuits.

There was no doubt that something big was going on here. The Greater Demon had been luring people in here for who knows how long, and the chances of someone in the Storm Crown not noticing were slim. So if Rowan wanted to stop whatever plan was currently unfolding, he needed answers. And those answers lay beyond this gate, guarded by whatever horrors awaited within.

Nowhere to run now, he thought, steeling himself for what was to come. I don't care how many pests I have to take out. That Demon and I are going to have a conversation.

His finger made contact with the stone. The rune flared beneath his touch, its light intensifying for a brief moment before fading to a dull throb.

Suddenly, the walls shook, dust falling from above. The entire structure seemed to groan, small fragments of stone clattered to the floor around his feet.

Rowan's eyes widened in alarm, and before his conscious mind could catch up, mana started moving through his channels. The familiar heat surged through his body, instinct taking over where thought was too slow. His shield grew in size, moving to the front as he took a step back, a whip of tightly coiled flames erupting from his shoulders.

A low growl reached his ears as the gate opened fully, revealing a large chamber with three forms standing on the other side, each one distinct in its appearance.

The first one clung to the ceiling like a grotesque spider, its elongated limbs bending at impossible angles. Its skin was leathery and stretched too thin over a frame that was both emaciated and unnervingly strong. Every joint bulged unnaturally, as if struggling to contain something larger than itself. Spittle dripped from its gaping maw, jagged fangs clattering as it skittered forward.

The second was the most humanoid of the bunch, but that only served to make it more unsettling. Its body was covered in patches of hardened chitin and raw, pulsing flesh that seemed to beat with a life of its own. Too many joints lined its limbs, each finger tipped with a thin, elongated claw that gleamed wickedly in the torchlight. A second mouth yawned across its throat, lined with needle-like teeth that clicked together rhythmically, as if tasting the air. Its eyes tracked Rowan with an intelligence that the others lacked, calculating and cold.

Level: 41

Race: Lesser Demon

Sin: Envy

The last beast padded forward on all fours, its gait eerily smooth despite its size. It was the largest of the three, its form a grotesque mockery of a hound. Its fur was sparse and matted, patches of exposed muscle twitching with every step. The beast's jaw hung open, saliva dripping from its fangs.

Rowan exhaled, flexing his fingers as mana burst through his channels. He started to cast [Fireball], but thought better of it. There was no telling how many more of these monsters waited for him further in. He needed to be smart about this. Efficient. Not just with his mana, but with the opportunities this Rift brought.

Instead, with a slight working of Intent, Rowan reshaped his whip, flattening the edge into something resembling a blade.

His mana molded easily under his touch, the change [Storm Touched] wrought on his capabilities apparent. Before, something like this would have been beyond him, but now, it felt as natural as breathing.

The humanoid Demon snarled, tilting forward and bolting towards Rowan at an alarming speed. Its muscled legs pounded against the stone with each furious step, leaving small cracks where its claws dug into the floor for purchase.

Rowan fed more mana into his spell, making it grow longer, brighter, more powerful.

The sudden change didn't seem to register on the creature's gnarled face, its eyes focused on Rowan with an almost manic intensity. There was something in that gaze beyond simple bloodlust—a hunger that spoke of deeper needs, of desires that transcended physical wants.

His whip lashed out, whistling as it cut through the air. The sound it made was like a blade being unsheathed, sharp and promising.

In response, the Demon raised an arm, attempting to shield itself from Rowan's attack.

The whip slashed across the hardened chitin covering its forearm, cutting through the armor and into the pulsing flesh beneath. A screeching sound filled the air as flame met demonic flesh, along with a familiar stench that Rowan had grown used to over the last few months.

But that didn't stop the monster. It didn't even slow it down. If anything, the pain seemed to fuel it, driving it forward with renewed vigor.

The Demon growled as it dodged to the side, using its speed to duck under another slash. Its movements were fluid, almost graceful. It circled Rowan, looking for an opening, testing his defenses with feints and quick lunges.

Rowan narrowed his focus, his spell lashing through the air like an enraged serpent, cutting off the Demon's approach with each rapid strike. The closer it got, the more condensed Rowan's attacks became.

He lost himself in the exchange, feeling that familiar thrill coursing through his veins. This was what he was made for, what he was working towards. The challenge, the danger, the raw exhilaration that only came in moments like this one.

The Demon let out an ear-piercing shriek from the mouth on its throat as its rush failed, launching needle-like projectiles at Rowan.

They shot through the air with blinding speed, each one glinting with a substance that Rowan didn’t want anywhere near him.

He didn't even need to think about moving his shield, it just happened. Like his mana knew what he wanted before he even thought it. The shield expanded, catching the projectiles with a series of dull thuds.

Rowan pushed down his excitement, knowing he needed to be focused for the fight ahead. These were monsters a team of capable Silver-ranks would have trouble dealing with, and while he didn't feel particularly threatened, he hadn't against the Wyrmlings either.

Overconfidence was a danger he was all too familiar with.

With the needles dealt with, Rowan moved his shield out of the way, eyeing the group of Demons with a challenging expression.

The ease with which he'd handled them so far finally seemed to register to the Demon, causing it to take a cautious step back. Its eyes narrowed, reassessing the threat.

Rowan's smile widened.

Three on one, he thought, watching as the spider slithered across the ceiling and the hound bent its knees. I guess that's a start.

Two more whips erupted from his back, one for each of the approaching monsters.

For a moment, Rowan thought that alone would be enough to make them falter. But then, the humanoid Demon let out another shriek, a sound that carried command instead of pain or fear, and all three of them charged.
