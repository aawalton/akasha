The heavy iron doors ahead stood slightly ajar, white lamplight bleeding through the gap—or what his mind insisted was white—and through the opening, he stole a brief look.

His mystic eyes did what they did best and stripped away the obvious to reveal what was actually there. The feedback, as usual, came in a chaotic mess—essence signatures layered over one another like tangled thread, various essence residue clinging to skin and clothing, the faint tracework of sorceries that had been cast and dismissed and cast again until the air itself felt greasy with spent power—and buried in all of that noise were four signatures that burned brightly against the mob.

« Located, » Angel confirmed before Jin could even ask. « There are three Order II essence signatures and one Order III. Cross-referencing against Corren's memories now. »

The overlay snapped into clarity, and Jin saw them.

One cultist near the center of the room crouched behind a cluster of civilians. Two more flanking the sides and trying to blend into the press of bodies like predators hiding in tall grass. The fourth—the lone Order III—pressed against the far wall with a dozen people between him and the door.

They'd scattered themselves through the room with care, which meant someone had been thinking. Jin focused on the Order III to get a feel for the threat and found the man's aura was a wreck—unstable, poorly controlled, leaking essence in uneven pulses—and he had no doubt the cultist was carrying some kind of artifact to keep it from collapsing entirely.

Nothing like Orodain.

This one wouldn't last ten seconds against someone who knew what they were doing.

"Veric," Jin said, and his voice came out flat and empty. "Four cultists. Only one at overmortal. I'll take the point and engage with them."

"Understood," the older man replied from just behind him, and Jin felt Veric's stare on the back of his head—probably trying to figure out if he was really enough to take care of four cultists—but it didn't matter. Veric would keep up, or he wouldn't. Jin had already moved past caring.

"Wait for a minute and then move in after me," Jin said, letting out a slow breath as he reached and flipped the switch on the regulator; immediately, the [Thermokinetic Engine] roared to life.

Clock's ticking, Jin thought, and he shoved the doors open.

Jin raised Iron Howl toward the ceiling as his lips moved in a wordless murmur. “Essence, condense and flare—let blinding light shatter the dark!”

The sorcery infused with the essence bullet, and Jin pulled the trigger.

[Spellshot Synthesis] activated, and the spell fused itself to the bullet as he pulled the trigger, and the gun kicked hard against his palm.

BANG.

The bullet detonated somewhere up near the ceiling—bright white light blooming in a sphere of blinding brilliance followed immediately by a pressure wave that rattled teeth and shook dust from the beams—and the mob of civilians recoiled as one, hands flying up to cover their eyes, bodies stumbling backward into one another.

Jin moved before the echo faded, letting [Thermokinetic Engine] drink the recoil from Iron Howl's discharge and convert it to a burst of kinetic charge that he poured straight into his legs and dashed.

He blurred forward, stepping straight into the air and sailed over the first wave of civilians entirely, angling himself toward the peripheral blind spot of the nearest Order II cultist who was still blinking away afterimages.

Before the cultist could raise his weapon, Jin’s aura slammed down on the man.

The cultist—barely into Order II by his fingernails—lost his footing under the weight of it, and Jin blinked to the man’s side, bringing Iron Howl beneath the man's jaw at an angle that would send the round straight up through the skull and into the ceiling instead of through the crowd behind him.

He faintly uttered, “Goodbye.”

BANG.

The gun roared, and the cultist's skull burst open like a watermelon.

As the body hit the ground, Jin dissolved into the shadows, blinking out of sight.

He appeared among the turned civilians as they looked for him. He swept his gaze across the remaining three targets, pulling at the [Inevitable Doom] skill, and the mark latched onto all three simultaneously like hooks dragging through flesh.

« All of them under the first stage of the mark. None of them has noticed any abnormalities. »

« Attack within next—»

I know.

Jin could see it even if no one else could—a faint shimmer in the air around their throats and chests—and he poured the accumulated kinetic charges from his Engine into his raised leg and stomped.

A shockwave of kinetic force tore outward, radiating across the floor in a visible ripple that threw everyone off balance—and in the half-second of confusion that followed—the weak, insignificant attack had Mark of Doom triggered.

The two Order II cultists buckled as the multiple afflictions took hold, curses blooming like rot, and then the panic set in.

Jin jumped just as a spike of earth rose from the ground at his location, and then a loud, gruff voice echoed through the room. “He’s right there, you bastards! Go and finish him, or else I’ll have you fed to the ritual fire as fuel!”

It was the Order III that had commanded, and Jin saw the man immediately go into summoning a shield around him and drawing a circle.

« Most likely a ritualist. »

Jin found himself bombarded with dark spikes, and so the moment he landed, he fell straight through the shadows.

He materialized behind the two cultists and kicked one straight in the chest. The man was sent flying away, and before Jin could take aim, more spikes homed in on his location.

Jin sidestepped the spikes without breaking stride—they weren't fast, not by his current standards, nowhere near the speed he'd need to worry about—and brought Iron Howl up to track the Order III cultist at the far end who looked like he was preparing a nasty ritual. No doubt a summoning type, unfortunately, Jin had no interest in playing for long.

He overcharged the next bullet, feeding it more essence and his cryo affinity, before pulling the trigger.

BANG.

The bullet screamed across the room and collided with a transparent shield that the Order III cultist had. The barrier shattered on impact, and the cultist grunted in surprise as he threw himself sideways to avoid the bullet.

Jin turned his attention back to the two Order IIs—one still crumpled on the ground from his kick, the other charging straight at him—and made his decision.

His essence pooled, flowing outward into the space directly in front of him, and he activated [Essence Constructs], manifesting a sturdy invisible shield wall. He kicked off the ground, dashing towards the cultist and using the shield as a battering ram to intercept the charging cultist mid-stride.

The man let out a grunt of surprise as he collided with the construct, and to his horror, Jin didn't stop. He poured more kinetic charges into his body—enhancing his strength, speed, and momentum—and drove the cultist straight into the wall behind him hard enough that the stone cracked.

The cultist burst like overripe fruit from the impact, and Jin turned away from the bloody mess.

And just then, he felt a familiar essence flare.

"KNEEL!" Veric’s voice cut through the chaos, and everyone in the room, Jin included, felt a compulsion to kneel.

The effect barely registered on him, but it did wonders for the turned civilians and the remaining Order II cultists.

Raising his gun, he shot at the downed cultist’s legs with a cryo-infused round, and the cultist’s lower half was frozen solid in place.

Veric and the others could drag that one back for questioning later.

Jin's focus snapped to the Order III, and he took a step forward, and his instincts screamed as the world exploded beneath his feet.

The trap detonated with enough force to crack stone, and a blast threw him back across the room.

His Helldiver’s Mark flared to life immediately, and the shield absorbed the worst of it, and he used [Thermokinetic Engine] to devour the rest.

He hit the ground rolling and came up in a crouch.

"You hesitate, Gunmage!" the cultist spat, and his aura flared outward in a pressure wave that made the air feel thick and heavy. "Your puny aura can't hold off against mine!"

« The man sure speaks a lot. Doesn’t he realize we took like zero damage from his attack? »

Angel—

Jin paused as he felt the essence in the room twist, and he realized it was a sorcery taking shape. Suddenly, the civilians in the room were picked up and shoved forward by invisible hands into his path like living barricades.

The cultist's voice cracked like a whip. "Kill him! Rush him! Tear him apart! Bring him to our Lord’s Darkness!"

Jin watched the civilians' expressions shift as they turned toward him, a dozen bodies moving with the jerky coordination of puppets on strings, and his mind processed the situation with cold clarity.

Through the sovereign’s indifference, Jin knew the optimal solution would be to release the hold over [Thermokinetic Engine] and generate short kinetic bursts.

That would clear the immediate space in under two seconds. But that would have every civilian caught suffering from broken bones, ruptured organs, and internal bleeding at the very least.

He knew most of them wouldn't survive.

There will be casualties. Minimal but…

No.

The thought came from somewhere deeper. The Mind Star muted his emotions, stripped away fear, hesitation, and guilt, but it couldn't completely erase his choice.

He could feel the difference now—the gap between what the detached state suggested and what he actually wanted to do—the indifference state offered efficiency without morality.

And Jin rejected it.

Not completely, but the choice would be his. Always his.

And this time he wasn’t alone.

"Veric!" Jin barked, his voice cutting through the chaos. "Shift left! Give me a funnel through this crowd!"

The older man didn't hesitate. "Kneel!"

The compulsion hit harder this time, and every civilian in front of him dropped to the ground. And from the ground earth rose to bind them in place.

That works.

Jin planted his back foot and surged forward, threading through the gap Veric had created, and found himself face-to-face with the Order III cultist at maybe ten feet of separation.

Jin raised his gun, and the cultist's eyes went wide as he grabbed a civilian from the side and threw him in front of his body like a shield.

Jin slowed a little and shoved the man lightly across the floor, using a small essence construct to get him out of the line of fire.

With a clear view, he raised Iron Howl and fired three times in rapid succession—BANG BANG BANG—and the cultist threw up another barrier that stopped the first two rounds, but the third punched through and caught him in the shoulder.

The man screamed and stumbled back, blood welling up through his robes, and grabbed another civilian by the hair.

Oh c’mon.

"Back!" His voice was ragged, one hand pressed against the bleeding wound while the other held the civilian in a chokehold. "Back, or I snap her neck! I know you people care too much for your weak."

“Jin!” Jack’s voice echoed, and he saw a circle of light flare under the cultist's feet, and the woman disappeared.

The cultist spat blood and squealed in surprise. Jin realized the cultist, being an Order III, allowed him to resist a foreign forced teleport.

Oh well.

Jin stomped his foot to release another concussive blast that threw the man off balance, and using the blast as a push, he blinked forward and fired.

The bullet—fully overcharged with cryo essence—caught the cultist square in the chest. Ice bloomed across his torso in spreading fractals, freezing flesh and blood and bone in the span of a heartbeat, and the bullet punched through the frozen mass to detonate inside his chest cavity.

The cultist's eyes went wide, his mouth opened, but no sound came out—just a thin trickle of blood that froze before it could drip—he swayed once and toppled backward to hit the stone floor with a sound like breaking glass.

Jin landed and felt the heat in his chest start to fade as the [Thermokinetic Engine] throttled back down to passive absorption.

His body was running hot—not dangerously so, but enough that he could feel sweat beading on his forehead despite the Indifference's dampening—and his essence reserves had taken a hit.

Nothing a quick harvest won’t solve. Jin nodded to himself as he turned to survey the room.

Veric and the others had subdued most of the mind-controlled civilians, binding them with restraints, and Jack was going around giving each of the civilians a good whack on the head to knock them unconscious, with Gleeward helping.

Veric stood at the center of the room, and when their eyes met, the older man gave a single approving nod.

"Clear," Veric called out, his gaze sweeping the space one final time before landing on Jin. "No friendly casualties."

Jin pointed at the half-frozen cultist still twitching on the floor. "That one's still breathing. Your call if you want him for questioning or if you'd rather finish it now."

Veric moved toward the cultist without answering, and Jin let the Sovereign's Indifference fade.

Color bled back into his vision slowly—gray walls becoming blue, faces shifting from monochrome to flesh tones, blood going from black to red—and with it came the first stirrings of emotion.

Relief that no one had died. Exhaustion from the kinetic burn in his muscles. A faint thread of guilt that he'd had to consider killing civilians at all, even if he'd rejected the idea in the end.

He took a slow breath, let it out, and felt his heartbeat start to settle back toward normal. Then his perception flared with a warning that made every hair on his body stand on end.

Angel's voice cut through his thoughts like a knife. « Jin! »

I know. I felt it.

Essence.

Massive essence signature spiking somewhere beyond these walls.

"Jin?" Veric started.

"Eleanor," Jin said shortly. "She's fighting. We need to move."
