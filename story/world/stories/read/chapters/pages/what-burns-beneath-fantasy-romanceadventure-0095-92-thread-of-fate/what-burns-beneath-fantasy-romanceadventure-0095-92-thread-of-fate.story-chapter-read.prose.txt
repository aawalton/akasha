“Renner,” I breathed, “do you still have my letter? The… the one to Durst?”

He took in my doubtlessly excited expression and offered a strained, “Yeah.”

I let go of his hand as he swung his pack down, delved into a pouch, and produced the folded parchment. I knelt, flattened the letter with the blank side facing up, and examined the cavern floor.

Hm. I doubt anyone’s got an inkpot with them. Maybe I could use some mud?

“Searching for a pattern, lady Brin?” Joselia paced towards us. One gauntleted hand pulled a small leather pouch from her belt. “I had a similar thought, though I lack any parchment.”

“Something like that,” I offered. “I thought… I don’t know. Perhaps the lines… er, mean something?“ My voice sounded incredibly meek and small in the vast, empty air. Dust swirled as heavy boots paced to and fro, though no disparaging or suspicious glances were cast my way. “Or could be… studied?”

“Any thread is worth following.” She opened the pouch and handed me a palm-sized lump of charcoal. “Here. Part of my, ah, emergency wound kit. I use it far too often, these days.”

I accepted the lump, turned it til I found a somewhat sharp point, and began tracing a crude sketch of the rune.

Joselia watched quietly. Then, drifting past me and so softly it was barely a breath, “Are you alright?”

Renner grunted.

I winced, glancing up at his rigid features. He looked back silently, eyes dark.

“You can wait outside,” I murmured. “I won’t be long.”

He grunted again. It conveyed very little, but the set of his shoulders seemed to indicate that he wasn’t about to leave.

Because I’m down here. The thought hit like a mallet. I froze for a breath, searching his face and wondering if that truly was the reason. Because he won’t leave me alone in a cave, not when he’s thinking about Baltha and his head cracking against the wall and her strangling me and-

“Think about something else,” the whisper tumbled out without a thought. I wasn’t sure if I was talking to myself or him and hurriedly scrubbed my face, sucking in a breath of thick, fetid air. Then, after a moment of steadying myself, I caught his eye and gave a shy smile. “Something… er, nice.”

Slowly, so slowly it ached, he smirked down at me.

I turned my burning face down and resumed scrawling. My fingers trembled.

“Y-you… you grind it up when there’s risk of infection, right? Then mix it with water and flour?” I cleared my throat, not daring to look up but hoping the chatter would continue to distract Renner. And me. “The charcoal?”

“I do.” Joselia sounded vaguely impressed. “Jareth said you tended a mortal wound in the marketplace today. Were you trained as a healer, lady Brin?”

I chewed my lower lip for a moment, finishing a curve along the parchment. “I apprenticed with our town cleric. Well, sort of.”

“She shot a rabbit.” Teela chirped as she and Royce drew near. Her voice trembled with restrained laughter. “Then learned how to make salves so he’d recover. Something like that, right?”

My face grew warm. “It was a she.”

“Rosie. I remember.” Royce chuckled. “Saints, that was an ugly rabbit.”

“She was not!” I gave a huff, etching the lower segment of parchment. “She was just… er, different looking-”

“She had buckteeth and was missing four toes, Brin. And half her hair.” He leaned towards Teela and whispered loudly, “Mange.”

She shuddered.

“Yes, and that made her even more special. Besides, helping Clem come up with a treatment for her fur made me want to keep learning about all of it, and when he showed me half of the ingredients in his recipe books were things I could grow, well…” I stood, brandishing my completed picture with a little grin. “There you have it.”

Royce nudged me with his elbow, mirroring my expression. “Uh-huh. Pretty sure Durst started going gray while you two were struggling to keep that poor rabbit alive.”

My cheeks grew even hotter. He wasn’t wrong.

Renner’s soft chuckle drew my attention. The sound made a little jolt of triumph flood my chest; he was still tense, and currently had his eyes closed, but the set of his jaw was softened. The chatter had indeed seemed to help.

Joselia interrupted my thoughts. “Well, now that you four have had your curiosity sated, are you ready to return to the city?”

My three companions looked towards me. I folded the parchment carefully and put it in my pack. “Yes. I… it’s dark, Dame, and we were hoping-”

“Oh, I asked because I’m old and I like going to bed early.” One sea-green eye winked. “May I accompany you?”

My shoulders sagged with relief, then tensed a bit as Jareth strode towards us. Metal groaned and every step sent little puffs of dust into the air.

“I’ll come, too.” He gave a tight smile and inclined his head. Then, looking a bit uncertain, he glanced between me and Teela and added, “Ah, if that’s… alright?”

No one argued, so the six of us turned and hurried out of the tomb and into the cold shadows of a pine forest at twilight.

I practically held my breath for the first leg of our journey. Not from fear, exactly; Jareth strode ahead of me and Joselia was to my side, and… well, and Horace was still presumably somewhere nearby. That fact brought a curious mixture of anxiety and bizarre reassurance. Teela and Royce walked in the front of our little group, chatting in low voices.

My worry was aimed directly ahead, towards the two men walking shoulder to shoulder through the pines. Jareth’s cloak shone with a mixture of white, black and silver thread whenever he crossed through a patch of moonlight. Renner moved like a shadow beside him. I waited for the barbs, the biting anger, the frozen glares… none came.

They walked in easy strides. The few snippets of conversation I overheard were calm. No glares were leveled.

“You seem troubled.” Joselia’s gentle voice drew me from my thoughts.

I ducked under a branch. “I… a bit.” Patient quiet ensued until I added, “They were… angry at each other earlier. Jareth and Renner.”

She made a soft ‘mm’ sound, seemingly unsurprised.

“I’m worried that… I don’t know, exactly.” I searched the shadowed forest floor, feeling a bit helpless. “It was nice to see him happy. Just for a bit. And now…”

“Those two fight, my lady.” Her voice was as soft and mild as the twilight breeze. “Then they remember they’d die for each other, and nearly have on more than one occasion. Then they fight, then they remember, then they fight… I could go on. Like rowdy twins. They’ve always been like that.”

My ears pricked at that. “Always? Did they come to the Order together?”

Her lips thinned. “Mm. No. They met on Jareth’s first time in the field, actually. Well, I suppose they mostly met in the aftermath.”

“Oh? What happened?”

She was quiet for a while, green eyes cutting to me with an assessing weight. Finally, in the measured tone of someone choosing their words very carefully, “Has Renner told you why he joined the Order, lady Brin?”

I couldn’t help but wince at the memory of him glowering at me in Baltha’s cage. Reluctantly sharing fragments of information, though not painting a whole picture.

“Not… not exactly. He’s only told me a little bit. I know it was after his… injuries.”

She inclined her head. “Ah. Yes. The last real bit of good magic I’ve seen, truth be told.”

“He said someone in the Order healed him. Are there Thalessian runekeepers?”

She shook her head, giving a bemused little huff. “There are… or, were. We haven’t any for a good few years, and none with more than scraps for longer than that.”

“Then how-”

“It was me. Well, me following some very odd instructions. Truthfully, the whole tale’s a bit of a mystery. And I say that having been right at the center of it.”

“What do you mean?”

She cocked her head, eyeing the back of Renner’s cloak thoughtfully. “It’s not a story I share often. But… oh, let’s see. About… twenty five years ago, I think it was, I met a young woman on the road. She was about your age and just sitting there, like she’d been waiting for me. I was heading north at the time, on my way to join the Order. She was very pleasant, though a bit… weary, I think. She had very sad eyes. She gave me a little satchel and a letter with some peculiar instructions. She claimed I wouldn’t need them for quite some time, but that they were of the utmost importance. She said she’d dreamt of… well, of a man she desperately needed in fighting form, to use her words.”

My steps slowed. Then stopped. Frosty air pricked at my skin as I stared at the paladin. I opened my mouth to ask for more, but all words failed me.

It… it couldn’t be. It can’t be.

She stopped with another disbelieving little laugh. “You can imagine how strange I found the whole thing. And this was a long time ago, as I said, during… well, peace. No Fae for decades, and no one had ever heard of a pact before. I thought she was mad, but didn’t think much more than that. I laughed her away, and she… mm. Truthfully, what happened next was the strangest moment of my life. She looked at me very gravely and said… ashes, I’ll never forget it. She said ‘by all the love a flower bears the dawn, I charge you.’”

Joselia grew still beside me, eyes distant and lips pressed together. Then she gave her head a little shake. “I don’t think I’ve ever been so rattled. That was something my father used to say, you see. He was a merchant, and his travels often kept him from home. He fancied himself a bit of a bard, too, though not a very good one. Before he’d go away for a long time that was always how he’d… say goodbye. ‘I love you as a flower loves the dawn, Josie.’ Mm. I can still hear him say it.”

My heartbeat swelled to a roar. I couldn’t seem to move.

“She left without another word. I stood there stunned for quite some time before I opened her gift. The satchel held a little crystal bottle wrapped in cloth. It was full of water, but… stars, bright. Like a jar of sunlight. Her letter said that in about fifteen years, I’d be called to investigate a little village to the southeast. Bellmere. She said it would be burned to the ground, with a young man lying half-dead in the rubble. The letter described Renner perfectly and said the bottle was for him. He was unconscious, and of course he barely remembers a thing from the aftermath, but… by all the saints, his healing was miraculous. Divine. An act of true, holy magic, right before my eyes. By all rights, his hands shouldn’t work.”

The world was spinning. Every breath was a ragged rush of frosty air.

Joselia sighed, watching the man in question- who was now quite some ways ahead, along with the others- fondly. “I think about that woman quite often, truth be told. I’d love to meet her again, and be a bit more… well, respectful. And thank her, of course. By the stars, I could spend the rest of my life thanking her and it wouldn’t be enough! I often wonder-”

She continued speaking, trailing after our companions. I followed on numb legs, not hearing a word. Her voice was a muffled, distant hum swallowed by crickets and owls and rasping pines. Silent tears spilled down my face as the dark forest swam and a hundred other things filled my vision.

Renner, on his knees between me and a nightmare.

Renner, drawing Baltha away from me with a dead rabbit and a broken scrap of metal.

Renner, holding me in the woods, smelling of smoke and iron while I cried and laughed and babbled nonsense.

I remained quiet as we walked. There was nothing and far too much to say, all at the same time, and I didn’t think I could manage a word of it amidst the tide of emotion sweeping through me. Grief and despair, hope and joy, humbled awe and childlike longing for a woman I’d never known but who suddenly seemed more real than ever before. Not just a piece of Durst’s memory and a parchment full of scrawled ink, but a flesh-and-blood figure who’d walked the world. Who’d sought out a confused young woman on the road twenty five years ago to ensure that, on the darkest night of my life, I was not alone.

I wept silently, turning my face towards the shadows every time someone glanced my way and swallowing every sob before it dared make a sound. I couldn’t bear to hear the questions my companions would ask, nor offer any answers.

This moment was… for me. Only for me.

Each step became more sure than the last. My heartbeat pounded in a steady, strong rhythm. Every thrum sang out that blazing promise, fiercer than ever before.

I was beloved.
