Outside, the crowd was still gathered around the newly displayed spirit ores. Most people seemed content to watch after the morning's terrible results, though the occasional decent find was enough to stir them up again.

Ning, Xiao Fan, and Sun Jing slipped back into the crowd and waited. A short while later, Shopkeeper Feng emerged with another batch of spirit ores, including the one prepared for Sun Jing.

Sun Jing glanced at the other two. "Time to earn those free ores."

Xiao Fan's expression immediately soured.

Ning, on the other hand, found that an excellent way of putting it.

The three made their way toward the front, and it didn't take long before someone recognized Sun Jing.

"Young Master Sun is here again!"

"Sun Jing? The one who's always finding good things?"

"I saw him open an artifact last time."

The reaction quickly spread through the nearby crowd. Apparently, Sun Jing had been doing this long enough to build something of a reputation around the pavilion.

Naturally, not everyone was convinced.

"How does the same person keep getting lucky?" someone muttered suspiciously. "Don't tell me the pavilion is paying him."

Ning slowly turned toward Sun Jing, who promptly avoided his gaze.

"...You really have been doing this for years."

"It's not like I win every time," Sun Jing whispered. "That would be too obvious."

Ning had to give them some credit for that, at least.

Beside him, Xiao Fan looked increasingly uncomfortable with every word.

"Can we just get this over with?"

"Of course." Sun Jing immediately straightened and raised his voice. "Shopkeeper Feng! Bring out the new batch. Today, this young master feels particularly lucky!"

The crowd stirred.

Ning stared at him.

So much for subtlety.

...

Sun Jing moved decisively. He had already memorized the shape and patterns of the prepared ore, so after scanning the newly displayed batch, he quickly picked one that seemed to match.

Shopkeeper Feng raised the spirit-breaking hammer and brought it down.

Crack.

The spirit ore shattered across the platform, revealing absolutely nothing inside.

Sun Jing's smile stiffened. Perhaps he'd picked the wrong one.

He searched again and soon found another with nearly the same shape, texture, and faint vein patterns. This one looked even closer to what he remembered, but when Shopkeeper Feng shattered it, the result was exactly the same.

By the third empty ore, whispers were already spreading through the crowd.

"That's the third one..." Shopkeeper Feng stared at the fragments with a strained expression. "And it's empty again."

Sun Jing slowly looked up at him. The two exchanged a long stare, their equally stiff expressions making it obvious that something had gone wrong.

Xiao Fan noticed as well and moved closer. "Don't tell me you forgot what it looked like."

"No. I remember it clearly." Sun Jing glanced at the shattered ores. "Those should've been the right ones. The shape, the markings, even the faint grain lines matched."

Which meant the prepared ore wasn't where it was supposed to be.

Either the wrong batch had been brought out, or someone had made a very costly mistake.

Meanwhile, the crowd had reached a much simpler conclusion.

"So even Sun Jing got nothing?"

"I told you this batch was terrible."

"Three empty ores in a row. Who would waste spirit stones on this?"

Shopkeeper Feng's face twitched with every comment. He kept shooting meaningful looks at Sun Jing, clearly hoping he could somehow salvage the situation, but there wasn't much Sun Jing could do when the supposedly guaranteed winning ore had vanished.

"Well..." Sun Jing rubbed his nose awkwardly. "I suppose even luck needs a day off."

That did absolutely nothing to improve the crowd's confidence.

He quickly pulled Ning and Xiao Fan aside and explained what had happened. After hearing the story, Ning glanced at Xiao Fan and found himself strangely unsurprised.

A normal trip to gamble on spirit ores should have been simple. Enter the shop, pick a few rocks, lose some money, perhaps win something if heaven was feeling generous, then leave. Instead, they'd uncovered a small conspiracy, nearly fought the shopkeeper, agreed to participate in said conspiracy, and somehow managed to break the conspiracy before it even started.

Ning looked at Xiao Fan again.

Protagonist luck really was something.

With the plan already ruined, Sun Jing suggested that Ning and Xiao Fan give up their chances and exchange the promised ores for spirit stones instead. Xiao Fan seemed perfectly happy with that arrangement, but before he could agree, Ning patted him lightly on the shoulder.

"It's fine. I came here to try my luck anyway." Ning looked toward the remaining spirit ores. "Who knows? Maybe mine will be better."

He walked over and chose one without much thought. To his eyes, most of the spirit ores looked similar enough anyway.

"Shopkeeper Feng, this one."

The shopkeeper wasn't particularly enthusiastic about continuing after everything had already gone wrong, but he'd promised them the ores. He picked up the spirit-breaking hammer and struck along one of the natural lines running across the shell.

Crack.

The stone split apart, and this time, something actually fell out.

Ning raised an eyebrow. He had only intended to use one of his chances before finding an excuse to let Xiao Fan choose the other, so he hadn't expected to get anything himself.

Shopkeeper Feng immediately brightened and hurried forward to retrieve the object. After brushing away the dust and unfolding it, however, his excitement slowly disappeared.

It was a sock.

For a few seconds, nobody seemed to know how to react.

"...A sock?"

Someone in the crowd finally burst out laughing. "Hahaha! I've heard spirit ores can contain anything, but this is my first time seeing someone pull out a sock!"

That broke the silence, and laughter quickly spread through the surrounding crowd.

Spirit ores were famous for containing ancient techniques, precious medicines, rare materials, and powerful artifacts left behind countless years ago.

Ning had received footwear.

Singular.

Xiao Fan looked between the sock and Ning's expression before failing to suppress a smile. "Your luck really is something. There's spiritual qi coming from it, so it might even be an artifact. Maybe you can stuff some rocks inside and use it to hit people."

Ning looked down at the sock and seriously considered the suggestion. "That does make sense."

Xiao Fan's smile faded.

He had been joking.

"You're not actually thinking of doing that, are you?"

"I've already thought of a name," Ning replied. "The Sock of Doom."

If the rocks didn't kill them, the bacteria surely would.

Judging by Ning's increasingly thoughtful expression, Xiao Fan decided it would be safer to change the subject before he actually went looking for someone to test it on.

"What are you going to do with your other chance?"

That reminded Ning of his original purpose.

"How about you choose one for me?"

Xiao Fan pointed at himself. "Me? I don't know anything about spirit ores."

"Neither do I." Ning gestured toward the rows of rocks. "But you and Sun Jing encountered a Foundation Building beast and somehow escaped alive. You even improved your martial arts afterward. I'd say your luck is pretty good."

After all, Ning couldn't exactly tell him, You're the protagonist. Pick the damn rock.

Xiao Fan gave him an incredulous look. "You call encountering a Foundation Building beast good luck?"

From his perspective, he'd always considered himself unusually unlucky. Trouble seemed to find him every few days regardless of what he did.

Sun Jing, however, immediately sided with Ning. "But we survived something that should've killed us, and Brother Fan even improved his martial skills afterward. That sounds pretty lucky to me."

Xiao Fan wanted to argue, but after thinking about it, he couldn't completely deny the point. Trouble did seem to follow him everywhere, yet somehow he usually came away from it with something in the end.

That still didn't mean he possessed some miraculous ability to choose spirit ores.

Eventually, however, he gave in. "Fine. But don't blame me if there's another sock inside."

"If it's the matching one, I'll give it to you."

"...Keep it."

With that settled, Xiao Fan stepped forward and began looking through the remaining spirit ores. His decision quickly attracted the crowd's attention, especially after they'd just watched Sun Jing open three empty ones.

"Another one's trying?"

"After even Sun Jing got nothing?"

"He's just throwing away spirit stones."

Ning listened to the pessimistic chatter with growing amusement.

Wasn't this the perfect setup for a face-slapping?

Xiao Fan, meanwhile, found himself agreeing with the crowd for perhaps the first time in his life. He knew nothing about spirit ores, and Ning's confidence in his supposed luck made even less sense. Still, this was the first time someone had believed in his luck so earnestly, so after some hesitation, he carefully chose one.

Shopkeeper Feng brought down the spirit-breaking hammer.

Crack.

The shell shattered, and something pale green slipped from among the fragments.

A jade slip.

The nearby crowd fell silent until someone quickly recognized it.

"That's a Jade Talisman Slip!"

Ning's eyes lit up.

Unlike ordinary jade slips, a Jade Talisman Slip could directly imprint the technique stored within it into someone's mind. Even the most ordinary one was valuable, and depending on the technique inside, its price could easily reach thousands of spirit stones.

"See?" Ning nudged Xiao Fan happily. "I told you that you were lucky."

Xiao Fan stared at the Jade Talisman Slip, clearly just as surprised. "I... didn't expect that either."

Then the crowd finally erupted.

"He actually got something!"

"A Jade Talisman Slip!"

"After all those empty ores, someone finally got something good!"

The pessimism that had hung over the pavilion vanished almost instantly. People who had been reluctant to spend a single spirit stone suddenly began pushing toward the displays, shouting over one another as they tried to buy ores before someone else could.

"I'll take three low-grade ores!"

"Give me five!"

"Move! I was here first!"

Shopkeeper Feng stared at the Jade Talisman Slip as though Xiao Fan had personally descended from the heavens to save his business. Within moments, he was shouting instructions at the attendants and ordering them to bring out more spirit ores.

Ning watched the sudden chaos with some amusement.

Their original plan had somehow succeeded after completely failing.

Xiao Fan was still examining the Jade Talisman Slip when he seemed to remember something. He turned and tossed it toward Ning.

"It seems I didn't fail your expectations. Take it."

Ning caught the slip. "Didn't I tell you that you were lucky?"

He examined it briefly before adding, "Once I see what's inside, I'll make copies for both of you."

Sun Jing's eyes immediately lit up. "Really? But I didn't even do anything."

"You brought us here."

Sun Jing considered that for all of half a second before happily accepting the logic.

Xiao Fan was more surprised. Techniques were rarely shared so casually, especially when they came from something as valuable as a Jade Talisman Slip, but Ning seemed to consider the matter settled already.

More importantly, Xiao Fan found himself looking back toward the spirit ores.

He had originally planned to exchange his own two promised ores for spirit stones, but after watching the ore he'd chosen for Ning produce something worth thousands, that suddenly seemed like a waste.

Maybe Ning was right. Perhaps he really did have some luck with these things.

After tasting the sweetness once, it became considerably harder to walk away.

Xiao Fan pointed toward another spirit ore. "Shopkeeper Feng, I'll try that one."

Shopkeeper Feng's attitude toward him had improved remarkably quickly. "Of course!"

The hammer fell, the ore shattered...

And contained absolutely nothing.

A few disappointed sounds came from the crowd.

"So the first one was just luck."

"I thought he'd found some trick."

Sun Jing gave Xiao Fan a sympathetic pat on the shoulder, but Xiao Fan merely stared at the empty fragments.

Maybe that one had just been a bad choice. The thought came surprisingly easily, and Ning almost laughed when he noticed the change in Xiao Fan's expression.

That was fast.

Xiao Fan hesitated for only a moment before looking over the remaining ores again. He still had one promised ore left.

"I'll try once more."

"Again?" Sun Jing asked.

Ning, however, suddenly remembered something. "Didn't you say you felt something from these ores earlier? Don't choose randomly this time. Follow that feeling."

Xiao Fan paused as he remembered that strange sensation from earlier. Closing his eyes, he concentrated and gradually let the noise of the pavilion fade into the background.

After several moments, he felt it again—a subtle pull at the edge of his perception, almost like something was quietly drawing his attention. He opened his eyes and followed the feeling until his gaze settled on an unremarkable spirit ore near the edge of the display.

It wasn't particularly large, nor were its surface patterns especially impressive. Judging by the dust around it, plenty of people had passed it over already.

Naturally, that only made the crowd more doubtful.

"That one?"

"It's been sitting there all morning."

Xiao Fan ignored them and pointed. "I'll take it."

Shopkeeper Feng brought down the hammer.

Crack.

The shell split apart, and something metallic struck the table with a small clink.

A dull metal token rolled free, no larger than Xiao Fan's palm, with a single ancient character carved into its surface.

The crowd leaned closer before quickly losing interest.

"...A token?"

"That's it?"

Even Shopkeeper Feng looked somewhat disappointed.

Xiao Fan picked it up and turned it over in his hand. Despite its unimpressive appearance, the token was surprisingly heavy and cold to the touch.

Old tokens weren't particularly unusual finds. Spirit ores sometimes preserved remnants of sects and cultivators that had vanished hundreds or even thousands of years ago, but that rarely made the tokens valuable. Their origins were difficult to identify, and even when someone succeeded, whatever sect or organization they belonged to had usually disappeared long ago.

Most ended up as little more than curiosities.

Xiao Fan sighed. So much for following his instincts.

"Don't look so disappointed," Ning said. "Maybe it'll be useful someday."

Xiao Fan glanced doubtfully at the token, but Ning wasn't merely trying to comfort him. Xiao Fan had sensed this particular ore among hundreds of others, and considering who they were talking about, Ning wasn't foolish enough to dismiss that as coincidence.

As for trying to take the token for himself, the thought barely crossed his mind. Even if there really was some hidden opportunity attached to it, chances were that only someone with Xiao Fan's ridiculous luck would ever figure out what it was for.

"If you say so." Xiao Fan tucked the token away, though he still sounded unconvinced.

He couldn't help feeling somewhat wronged. When he chose an ore for Ning, out came a Jade Talisman Slip worth thousands of spirit stones. When he chose one for himself, he got an old piece of metal nobody wanted.

Xiao Fan looked at Ning, then at the token in his hand.

Was his luck good or bad?

For once, he genuinely couldn't tell.
