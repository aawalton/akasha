The vision ended and I was sent back to reality. The world started from where I left. I was still bent over my knees, hacking and coughing up the blood that spurted into my open mouth.

Even though it felt like entire days had passed while I was caught up in Zortana’s memories, the actual time here had barely moved.

A trail of spittle hung on my lips while I studied the hard packed wet dirt.

I was aware of metallic feet scraping behind me. A second later a hand patted my back. I knew it was my dad from the deep green metal boots.

Streams of water slithered their way through the uneven portions of the road like rivers for ants. I did my best to keep my breathing steady. Strands of red bled into the water like thin whisps. The whisps became thicker and soon the streams of water were crimson.

My eyes followed the paths of flowing crimson to where Zortana’s headless corpse lay. His fingers twitched, but he was, without a doubt, dead.

I tried to heal him anyway.

Why wouldn’t I?

If I was somehow able to reverse the execution of his son, the man with the red beard would have no vendetta against my faction. Obviously, my healing magic didn’t work.

I wiped my mouth and forced myself to stand up straight. Nick used my shadow and teleported next to me. He took a few steps and used his bow to prod at Zortana’s corpse.

“Well Daevon...I think you got ‘em.” Nick stowed his bow and spun to face me. “Is it just me, or was that waaaayyy crazier than it should’ve been?”

Instead of responding to Nick, I turned to my dad. He watched me closely with his hand resting on my shoulder. I knew he was worried about me.

I gave him a halfhearted crooked smile that I hoped was at least semi-reassuring. In time, I would tell him about everything that I saw in the vision. For right now though there were other more pressing matters.

The first order of business.

What should we do about the Gabordian’s?

At some point Tenor had ended up on the ground. I couldn’t recall when it happened, but he still hadn’t gotten up. He, like the other Gabordian’s, seemed locked in place. No doubt they knew running wouldn’t be an option, meaning they really didn’t have much choice besides staying put and awaiting their fate.

I took a few steps to stand over Tenor.

“Spare my faction. I am the leader here. The other people are only guilty of their loyalty to me.” Tenor said, head bowed.

“Yeah, alright.” I agreed, reaching out a hand. “Might as well spare you too while I’m at it.”

Tenor looked up, an expression of surprise evident on his face. He regarded my hand suspiciously.

I shook it, urging him to take hold.

He did, and I pulled him onto his feet. Hand still clasped, I asked him.

“Would you still consider joining our faction?”

“Would I consider joining your faction?” Tenor parroted, looking at me like I just sprouted a second head on my shoulder. His eyes drifted to the headless corpse and then to the destroyed temple entrance. “After...all that happened?”

I shrugged.

“I don’t blame you, Tenor. You were only making the best of a terrible situation.”

I released his hand. He took a couple of steps back.

“You don’t have to. I’ll leave it up to you, but I don’t have any hard feelings if you don’t.”

Tenor seemed to be too stunned to speak. With a smile, I turned to leave, gesturing for the rest of my faction members to follow.

“You know where to find us once you decide.”

The Gabordians let us pass without any trouble.

Once we were out of earshot, I signaled Samson to my side.

“They’ve lost most of their strength. Can you and a few others patrol nearby? I don’t want anything else to happen to this faction even if they don’t join us.”

I was fairly confident they would join us, but they’d been through so much already I didn’t want to force anything on them.

“Consider it done.” He said before bolting off with several other wolfmen.

“As for everyone, feel free to take off. I’m going to walk back with my dad.”

People dispersed and not long after that, it was just my dad and I.

There were no trails to guide me or my dad as we made our way home, but it wouldn’t be hard to navigate.

The rain had grown from a misting to a steady drizzle. It didn’t bother me much though. Whether it was my spirit robe or just the increased stats, I hardly noticed the chilly weather. If anything, the gloomy weather seemed like a reflection of my mood.

My dad must have noticed because he cleared his throat.

“Are you ok Layton?” He wasn’t wearing his armor anymore. “That, back there, it couldn’t have been easy.”

“Heh.” I laughed cynically. “It’s just how things go.”

He echoed my cynical laugh.

“How is what just happened back there.” He used air quotes. “Just how things go?”

I shook my head and scoffed.

“You don’t know the half of it.”

He stopped. “What do you mean?”

And then I just told him. All of it. We took our time, strolling through the trees, just me and my dad.

I told him what I saw in the vision. Explained how Zortana was an actual being from another planet, but that he was a kid like me. That Stacy was just lonely and afraid. How she was manipulated and used for some kind of weird off brand opportunity for Zortana to enter our world.

I even told him about the bearded man. How small he’d made me feel, how he’d threatened me.

It felt good to speak the words. Like saying how I felt out loud was therapy in and of itself.

My dad just listened to me. He didn’t judge me for feeling unsure of myself. He didn’t tell me I was wrong for wishing I could’ve helped Stacy more or critique me for judging the situation without having all the facts.

“Are you worried?” He eventually asked.

“Worried?” I asked, tossing a branch I’d been fiddling with. “Maybe a little. But not really.”

My dad raised an eyebrow.

“Yeah, well I mean, if the bearded man could’ve hurt me, he would’ve.”

“Is that so?” He pressed.

“I think so. But I’m pretty sure the system won’t let him, at least not through a raid or anything like that.”

Then again, maybe a world as old as theirs didn’t need raids? Was it possible that a faction like red beards had the technology to travel that kind of distance in a space craft of some kind?

Probably.

But that would mean he’d have to find our world first.

Right?

“Honestly dad, I already figured there would be guys like red beard out there.”

I felt like I’d already met a few of them, albeit they were much lower levels.

“I realized pretty early on that I was barely a spec compared to whatever else is out there. It’s why I work so hard to get stronger. What use is worrying going to get me? If Red Beard shows up, he shows up.” I shrugged. “In the meantime, I’m going to keep helping people.”

I looked towards my dad.

He was smiling at me.

“What?” I asked, with narrowed eyes.

His hand rested on my shoulder, squeezing it.

“Let’s go find your mom, she’s probably starting to get worried.”

Mischief rested his head on his forepaws, stretching his bulky body longways along the dirt. As good as Mischief was at stealth, this new land provided little in terms of cover besides the short sage brush and lazy rolling hills.

Even if he moved in total silence, in the daytime, it would be hard to miss a black mass nearly the size of a horse.

It wasn’t like he could just turn invisible.

So, he waited patiently in the shrubs. Eventually the daytime would leave, giving way to the darkness.

He could be patient.

Waiting didn’t bother Mischief though. He was curious.

For his life, all he’d ever known was the forest. Then he followed Layton through the portal and for some reason it felt like his world became so much bigger.

It felt good in this new part of the world. More open space.

Mischief wanted to see it. He wanted to see everything.

Which is what led him to where he was now.

He wasn’t really sure what had drawn him to the Tria Calama. Maybe it was the number two next to their rank on the territory power rankings? Would they be strong? Compared to faction Layton Mischief’s numbers on the rankings it wasn’t promising.

But what Mischief really hoped was that they were doing something bad. Or at least bad according to Layton. Mischief trusted Layton’s judgement, plus he didn’t want his brother to be angry with him if he killed someone he shouldn’t have.

Killing didn’t bother Mischief. He still didn’t totally understand why it bothered Layton. Layton was strong. He might not have been born as a predator like Mischief had been, but he’d become one.

Layton’s strength made Mischiefs chest swell with pride. Of course, even if Layton was weak, he’d still be his brother. He was still glad he wasn’t though.

The thought was comforting, and he felt his eyelids growing heavy, so he let them sag.

Mischief was carried away into a dream world, where he was once again in the fight with the three-chaos spawn. He felt the exhilarating rush of adrenaline, and his lips curled back in a smile.

When he finally drifted back awake, the sun was well past the horizon, and the world was drenched in the purple and grey of early night.

Mischiefs maw opened in a silent but mighty yawn. He stood and arched his back in a deep, satisfying, stretch.

Lights flickered in the distance, like little hands beckoning for him to come and see.

Mischief could’ve used a shadow to teleport from where he napped. He didn’t want too though. He wanted to feel the earth moving under the pads of his feet as he bounded quiet as a summer breeze.

Ever since he’d evolved his class from Erebos Shadowborn to Erebos Shadow Lord, he’d felt a new kinship with the darkness. As a Shadowborn, he’d used the darkness, harnessed its innate characteristics to his advantage when hunting his prey. Mischief had always felt at home in darkness, but now, as the Shadowlord, that relationship had changed.

There was more depth, more understanding. Mischief had always assumed the darkness was kind only to him, the hunter. But that wasn’t true. He was merely seeing it from his own eyes. The darkness was a friend to the crafty, the coward, the hungry, the ashamed, the bold. It did not ever discriminate.

It obscured them all, each to their own end.

He respected the shadows for that. Because of that, he wanted to become a benevolent lord. For now, he had no idea what that would actually look like. Until he learned and understood more, he would use the shadows sparingly and be grateful for what they gave.

Mischief wasn’t the most well versed in human structures. But he’d definitely seen enough to know that this faction's city...was a disaster. Most of the structures weren’t structures at all anymore. They’d been reduced to little more than scattered walls and piles of tinder.

He rested his foot atop one of the piles of ruined building and felt the debri shift under the his weight causing a loose piece of the former buildings wall to break free of its tenuous hold and land with a crash.

Despite its ill repair, the city itself was not deserted. There were campfires all throughout the ruins, casting flickering jagged shadows into the night.

Mischief retreated a few steps, concerned that the noise he’d caused might have drawn unwanted attention.

He shrank into the shadows and watched.

A few days ago, Layton asked Mischief a question.

“You know, I was thinking. How come you're always sneaking around? I mean do you really need the element of surprise anymore?”

To Mischief, it seemed like such a silly question. The answer was obvious.

“Because it’s more fun sneaking around.”

Killing had become as easy as breathing. It wasn’t all that long ago that hunting and killing was a life or death situation. He’d spent days wandering the forest hoping to find his next meal. Back then if he didn’t kill, he didn’t eat, there was a thrill in the simplicity.

Now, he needed more to feel satisfied. By this point, he enjoyed stalking his prey more then he enjoyed the actual kill.

The experience points were nice though.

Eventually, when no one came to investigate, Mischief hopped casually over the debris, careful not to disturb it again.

He listened actively. The city was not abandoned. Voices could be heard chatting near campfires, their tone casual and friendly.

Mischief prowled closer, careful to remain hidden.

As he ventured deeper into the city, he noticed that not every building was completely destroyed. There was one building in particular that stood out.

It reminded him of the building that Jared called “town hall” from home. It was much bigger then any of the other sturctures and looked untouched by damage for the most part. Well, it was mostly untouched, besides the large double doors that were lying on the ground inside the building rather then on the hinges where they belonged.

The voices faded into the back of Mischiefs mind, as all his attention fell on the open doorway of this building.

Why was it preserved while all the other buildings were left in shambles? Mischief couldn’t help himself.

He wanted to look inside.

Luckily for Mischief, there didn’t appear to be anyone nearby. Neither were there any campfires.

Any light that might betray his approach was the light of the crescent moon which was half obscured by fluffy grey clouds. Even that light was more than what illuminated the open chamber behind the doorway.

His paws pressed soundlessly against the course stone like material that lead in shallow steps towards the opening.

Mischief knew the building wasn’t empty. His skill, whispers of the Abyss, was more than mere telepathy. It also provided him with the passive ability to somewhat sense the emotions of beings nearby.

His head crossed the threshold of the doorway, followed by his shoulders, then finally his tail. He found himself standing at the end of a long hallway, with more doorways on each wall.

Mischief tilted his head, intrigued.

Because at the end of the hallway, was a man, sitting on the edge of a four-poster bed resting his head in his hands.

Then the man spoke.

“I know you're there.” He said, almost lazily. “In case you didn’t know, the system store has all kinds of handy security systems for sneaks like you.”

The man yawned and arched his back, before reluctantly getting to his feet.

Although Mischief was an unknown intruder entering his home, he could feel what could only be described as an utter lack of any ounce of concern from the man at the end of the hall. If anything, there seemed to be traces of excitement.

“Your either quite the stealth build, or very, very small. Either way, why don’t you show yourself so we can get more acquainted?”

Mischief's eyes twinkled. He itched to oblige the confident stranger.

Instead, he turned around and walked back out the way he came.

As soon as Mischiefs body had fully left the building the man shouted after him.

“Aww...leaving so soon? Didn’t want to stay and play?”

Mischiefs teeth glinted in the moonlight as he left the man at the end of the hall with one parting thought.

“Another day.”
