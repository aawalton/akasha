Day 359, 10:16 AM

I gape at Edna, and a lone word escapes my mouth, “What?”

“It took over and ordered me to attack you. I’m so sorry.” Her voice shakes, tears welling in her eyes.

“What are you talking about?” I ask, not daring believe my ears.

Edna draws a breath to collect herself, balling her hands into fists. “Whatever that golden light was, it took control of my body. It felt like a nightmare. I knew what was happening, but I had no way of stopping it. The loss of control felt horrible. Please believe me, I didn’t mean to hurt you.”

Well, that confirms it. But why does she remember? What could be the reason? Her high intellect or wisdom? Some skill? The concentration of mana within her body? Or maybe is it the fact that she is connected to the Guide? That seems the most reasonable, somehow the Guide connects her to her future memories.

No, wait, that’s not it. I’ve been doing loops, everyone with Guide would have gone crazy if that’s the case. The key part is probably dying to Vengeful. Vengeful, Redo, and the Guide are all BSD features or derivatives. A crazy thought strikes me that I should research the phenomenon, but that’s insane, even by my standards.

“It’s fine,” the grounded part of me says while parallel thoughts wildly gallop on a different track. “What do you think happened? Not just in the dungeon, but in general, history-wise. Why is Hadriuse there?”

I think the clarification was warranted and even necessary. Edna’s thoughts on the chain of events that got us here interest me more than what happened during our brief scuffle against the eldritch monsters from beyond the golden crack and their puppet.

How did my life even reach this point? I can’t recall any moments where I made such disastrous decisions which would lead to speculations on history feeling more interesting than the fact that I’m fighting what seem to be outer gods.

“My guess is that whatever those entities were, they had possessed the archmages when they created the dungeons. Maybe they even killed them all, and took Hadriuse as a hostage because he was useful.” Edna pauses, then shakes her head. “I don’t know how the Church of Holiness fits. Maybe they knew from the start, maybe they became corrupted through the Guide, maybe the Guide created them. I don’t know.”

Gila’s concerned look grows more concerned as the tank-tread treants pass us by. Right, she has no clue what’s happening. I should explain, but the Guide is here, listening. It can’t invade my thoughts, but it probably can hear the spoken words, so I must limit the amount of information I share.

“I can travel back in time, and this time I took Edna with me. Somehow.”

Gila doesn’t seem any less confused. “Is that some sort of spell?”

“Unique skill,” I answer while still listening to Edna’s guesses about the outer gods’ influence until she shrugs.

“But all that is pure speculation. The first individual spell which created a personal guide could have been the monsters’ invention.”

Surprisingly, the conversation turns lighter. Both Edna and Gila make guesses and fantasize about what they would do with a skill like mine, and they are as naive as one would expect. To them, Redo is a game.

They would do everything perfectly, no matter how many redos it took. They would save lives and earn a fortune. Become the best tailor ever. I didn’t tell them about my skill’s limitations both because the Guide is listening and because I can’t help my paranoia.

The eldritch horrors already know I’m time-hopping, so I guess letting them hear the woman talk about me living entire lives and then starting over should scare them even more. But what if they have similar power? And they called me a demigod, what did that mean?

I let the women talk, keeping an ear out for useful ideas, but all their suggestions thus far would drive me mad after a handful of loops. With a flick of my will, I summon BSD and assign all my points into physique. The thirteen points amount to a nearly quadrupled mana pool. This way, I can just blast my way through the dungeon, no need for Edna to die permanently.

We keep walking for six hours, the endless waves of abominations from the previous timeline completely gone, the tunnel and its forks which lead outside eerily silent. Finally, we break for food, and Edna looks at me.

“Their tactics have changed. They aren’t randomly sending abominations after us.” She’s right, of course. The eldritch creatures have changed their strategy, probably gathering their forces so they could overwhelm us in a single attack.

On one hand the plan is decent. It took considerably more than half my mana to devolve a hundred abominations. If they forced my hand two or three times, they would be safe. Unfortunately, if they are relying on the previous redo’s information, they are in for a nasty surprise.

Do they have my BSD information if I don’t access it through the interface they control? Probably not. They don’t know about the anarchist information.

“Do you guys think there’s a reason to do anything differently this time?” I take a bite of my bug jerky, not really sure myself. I don’t see a need to change our approach. “They can’t fill the dungeon with abominations, since they are too big to pass between the floors. That means they can either cram the first floor full of them, or unleash them at us.”

The planning session bears no fruit, and the days of constant fighting have turned into uneventful days of constant walking. I have a bad feeling about this, and I was right. On the thirteenth day of the loop, an hour before reaching the dungeon, all hell breaks loose.

The stone pavement shakes, the skittering sounds growing from a distant buzz to a stampede. Gila illuminates the tunnel, just in case some abominations have the ability to avoid scanner, and Edna splits the treants ahead and behind us.

A massive praying mantis octopus slams into the front row of treants, another mirrors its attack behind. Their numerous scythe-like blades hack at the trees like axes, each felling one in a matter of moments.

The masterminds’ tactic has changed completely. Last time they sent waves of minor abominations, seemingly just throwing them away. Now, these things are at least abominations if not elder abominations, and, unlike last time, they are specialized in decimating our fodder.

“Gila stay here, Edna, handle the one in the back.”

I rush forward, and dissolve the abomination slightly before Edna turns her target to ashes. Six treants died in the brief clash, and there’s still an army of abominations surging towards us. I sing of recovery and melting snow, weaving my hands as I cast devolve, this time, the targets are huge, each blocking the tunnel in its own right.

I guess the eldritch entities believed bigger, more powerful creations would force me to expend more mana, but that’s not how I designed my spell. It only needs to touch my target, then instead of destroying anything, it starts a chain reaction in which it unleashes mana from the flesh it’s infusing.

And by sending several dozen massive monsters instead of hundreds of smaller ones, they are actually conserving my mana.

The spell is done, and like an x-ray, devolve pierces through abominations two hundred yards in front of me. I run forward, casting the spell again and again for nearly five minutes until I sense no abominations ahead. Then I turn around and rush to help Edna.

Half a minute later, the tunnel is quiet, the last of the elder abominations dissolving on the ground. The whole affair cost me a concerning twenty percent of my mana. If they really have improved their defenses by this much, the descent might prove trickier than I expected.

“Where did they get so many elder abominations?” Edna asks, and I have the answer.

“They had the minor abominations eat each other and evolve. That’s what they spent these two weeks on.” It’s a good strategy, one which must have cost them a huge number of monsters, but it would have worked if we only relied on treants.

“Let’s go.”

Soon enough, the tunnel ends in the vast open space of the dungeon’s first floor. It’s completely empty, not a trace of the hatchery.

“They had them eat the hatchery,” Edna and I say at the same time.

No wonder the monsters coming from the back were so few, while the onslaught from the front felt endless. But even a hatchery couldn’t explain those numbers.

“Gila, you wait here with the treants, we’re going down.”

Edna nods, and we descend the stairs, finding the second floor in complete darkness, the abominations have devoured the second hatchery as well.
