Jin groaned and let his head fall underwater in the bathtub, bubbles escaping from his mouth as the screens materialized in the corner of his vision—bright, insistent, and impossible to ignore even with his eyes closed—he sighed and pulled up the notifications.

He stayed under for three more seconds, enjoying the muffled quiet, before his lungs demanded air and he surfaced with a gasp.

« The second round is done, » Angel's voice resounded in his head, calm and professional. « The next milestone ETA eleven hours. Also, you could stay underwater for a long time, Jin. »

Jin frowned, water dripping from his hair as he stepped out of the bathtub. A pulse of essence into his thermokinetic engine, and the accumulated moisture shot away from his skin in a fine mist that left him dry and slightly warm.

"Why that late?" He grabbed a towel anyway, more out of habit than need.

« Because the earlier absorptions were focused on strengthening your base and stats as a whole, » Angel explained. « Without a proper foundation to absorb all that power, you would have gone ka-boom. »

"Alright, alright, Angel, no need to be that graphic." Jin pulled on clean clothes from his spatial storage—wait, no, he'd given that to Joe—he grabbed the spare outfit hanging in the room's small closet instead.

« Also, just so you know, these two absorptions were easy because this was Kiyon's powers, which were already converted into something digestible by the lovely friend you formed a soul pact with. »

"Huh? Only Kiyon..." Jin paused mid-motion, shirt halfway over his head, then pulled it down with a smirk. "Are you getting jealous, Angel?"

« Sod off! »

Jin stopped. Made an amused expression at thin air. "That's an odd choice of curse words."

« ... »

"Well, whatever." He shrugged and moved toward the kitchen, opening the fridge and pulling out the apple juice. He collapsed onto the small sofa, the furniture creaking under his weight. "Anything else? I have a feeling Trish's powers would be a hell of a lot more invasive and substantial."

« Without any doubt, the pain should be bearable. These two absorptions have strengthened, expanded, and carved the necessary channels in you. But yes, the next one would lock the changes in place. »

« Jin, after this… the final absorption would take days, if not more than a week, to fully assimilate. And when it does, it would be a massive change. »

"That long?" Jin frowned, unscrewing the apple juice cap. "And I bet as the deadline approaches, I'll have that same 'stuffed' feeling?"

« Expect pain, too, Jin. In your words, it would be invasive and painful. »

"Great..." Jin rubbed his temples with his free hand, taking a sip. "Well, that means I'm grounded for most operations before the final battle."

« Indeed, » Angel said. « But look on the bright side—this would give us ample time to actually look at yourself and train your expertise. Not just that, we could also bring as many people up in ranks, effectively increasing our combat power to use against the cult. »

Jin nodded slowly, the tactical implications settling into place. "Yeah. Any messages?" He took another sip. "Oh, and send messages asking for updates from Veric, Joe, Reyana, and Rudy."

« Understood, done, but before we do anything, there are some concerning things you should look at. »

"Where?" Jin began to ask when screens popped up in his vision, translucent panels floating in the air like accusations.

•••

[QUESTS]

◈ Available Quests: 3

•••

“Three? When in fucking hell did I get more?” Jin frowned and then pulled up the details.

•••

Jin scrolled past the familiar quest—that one he knew about, had been planning around—and stopped at the second entry.

The text made his stomach drop. Where did this come from? He didn’t remember doing anything that would trigger this.

"What in the fuck is this shit! I don’t even know most of the things this is telling me to do!" Jin's voice cracked through the empty room, loud enough that anyone in the adjacent quarters probably heard him.

« My thoughts exactly, » Angel said, his tone grim. « None of your memories of reading the books—which are fragmenting and fading away, Jin—say anything apart from the chosen ones and Primes. »

Jin froze.

The apple juice bottle slipped from nerveless fingers, hitting the floor with a dull thunk that spilled liquid across the tile.

"Wait, wait, hold up." His voice came out tight, controlled only through sheer force of will. "What do you mean fragmenting and fading?"

« Haa… I was delaying bringing this matter, but… go on… try to parse through your memories to see how much you recall. »

Jin set the bottle upright with trembling hands, ignoring the spreading puddle, and moved back to the sofa. He sat carefully, spine straight, hands on his knees, breathing measured and controlled.

The Path of the Eternal Sovereign's breathing pattern came naturally now after weeks of practice. In through the nose, hold for three heartbeats, out through the mouth. Each cycle pulled essence through his channels, settling his racing heart, preparing his mind.

His consciousness turned inward.

Memories should have been easy to access. His [Learning and Remembering] skill was at Adept 93—near-perfect recall of anything he'd experienced since awakening in this world—before that should have been equally clear, his past life as… as.

Huh?

But when Jin reached for those memories, he hit resistance.

Not a wall. Walls could be broken. This was more like... reaching through fog that grew thicker the deeper he went.

White walls materialized around him, and he remembered where he was instantly. It was his room, or to be precise, his hospital room. He could recall that, but not his name?

Ryujin Amane.

This time the name came easily, along with the faint smell of disinfectant and the distant beep of medical equipment, creating a rhythm he'd lived with for years.

Jin looked down.

He was in bed. White sheets pulled up to his chest. His body—Ryujin's body—was thin and wasted from months of illness, IVs trailing from his arms like the roots of some parasitic plant.

He tried to move.

Chains materialized around his wrists, his ankles, binding him to the bed with links that felt cold and wrong against his skin.

What—

He pulled harder, but the chains didn't budge.

A door opened to his left. An elderly nurse entered—gray hair pulled back in a practical bun, kind eyes behind wire-rimmed glasses—moving with the efficient care of someone who'd done this ten thousand times before.

Jin knew her. Had to know her. She'd taken care of him for years.

But he couldn't remember her name.

He tried to focus on her face. The details slipped away like water through cupped hands, leaving only a blur where features should be.

"Hey!" Jin called out. His voice sounded… too weak, it was Ryujin's lifeless voice instead of Jin's. "Hey, I need—"

The nurse walked past him without acknowledgment. Checked the IV drip, made a note on her clipboard, adjusted his pillow with hands that passed through him like he was a ghost.

Terror spiked cold and sharp through Jin's chest.

"Please!" He screamed. "I'm right here! Hey! I'm right here!"

She hummed a tune under her breath—something soft and old that he couldn't quite place—and left the room without a backward glance.

The scene shifted.

Not smoothly. Reality lurched, and suddenly there was someone else in the chair beside his bed.

A man in his late twenties. Dark hair, holding a book open in his lap with a very familiar cover and the words “Mantle of Gods” written on the spine. The man was reading aloud, and Jin felt a pang of pain in his heart as he struggled to remember who this person was.

The man was reading to him. Had been reading to him for hours, maybe days, the same patient tone carrying through passages Jin knew he should remember but couldn't quite grasp.

Who is this?

He knew this person was deeply, fundamentally important. Someone who'd sat by his bedside through the worst of it, who'd refused to leave even when the doctors said there was nothing more to be done.

But the face was wrong. Blurred. Like someone had taken an eraser to a photograph and scrubbed away the details until only the outline remained.

Jin tried to speak. Tried to reach out.

The chains tightened, biting into his wrists hard enough to draw blood.

He fought anyway. Pulled against the restraints with everything he had—but this wasn't Jin Winters' power—this was Ryujin Amane's failing body, and he had nothing.

"Who are you?" He gasped. "Please, I know—"

And like a floodgate, images and flashes of memories splashed his mind.

Ren.

Ren Amane.

His older brother. The person who'd stayed when everyone else had given up, who'd read books aloud until his voice went hoarse because silence made the hospital room feel like a tomb.

The moment the name crystallized in Jin's mind, the blur vanished.

Ren's face came into sharp focus—handsome, tired, eyes red-rimmed from crying he'd done in the bathroom where Ryujin couldn't see. The same face Jin had seen in the mirror for twenty years before this life began.

Relief flooded through him so intensely that it hurt.

"Ren—"

Ren's eyes lifted from the book and met his gaze.

But when he spoke, his voice came out... wrong. Gone was the warmth and love; instead, it was a lifeless voice like that of a puppet.

"You… left me. Ryujin is dead," Ren said. Each word fell like a stone into still water. "You moved onto a new world, leaving me behind."

His expression didn't change. Didn't flicker with recognition or warmth or any of the thousand small tells that made Ren Ren.

"Now be that person. And let me grieve Ryujin Amane… my little brother’s death."

Black tears ran from Ren's eyes.

Black tears??

He watched, alarmed as his mind struggled to make sense of what it was seeing. The tears were thick and viscous as oil, trailing down his cheeks and dripping onto the book in his lap, where they sizzled and burned through the pages.

His irises turned white.

Colorless and empty. Like someone had reached into his skull and scooped out everything that made him human, leaving only holes where a person used to be.

The voice that came out of Ren's mouth wasn't his.

It was wrong—layered, echoing, speaking in frequencies that human vocal cords couldn't produce—and it drove straight into Jin's mind like a sword through flesh.

"DEAD DEAD DEAD YOU LEFT HIM YOU ABANDONED ME! YOU SELFISH—"

Pain exploded behind Jin's eyes. His eardrums burst, blood pouring hot and wet down the sides of his face, and the voice kept screaming directly into his brain without need for sound.

Ren—no, not Ren, whatever the fuck this was—reached up with both hands.

Grabbed his own face.

And tore it off.

Skin ripped away in wet strips. Muscle followed, peeling back like layers of rotted fruit. Bone cracked and splintered as the thing wearing Ren's body destroyed its skin, and underneath was—

« JIN! »

Jin woke up screaming.

The sound tore out of his throat raw and ragged, and his hands clawed at the sofa cushions hard enough to shred fabric. His heart hammered against his ribs like it was trying to break free, and he couldn't breathe, couldn't breathe—

« Are you okay, Jin? » Angel's voice cracked through his panic like thunder. « I couldn't access your mind—I couldn't pull you out, and you were screaming—! »

For the first time since Jin had awakened with Angel in his head, the guide… his friend sounded panicked.

Jin tried to respond. Tried to form words. But his throat was too tight, his lungs too empty, his entire body shaking so violently he thought his bones might snap.

"Sorry, Angel," he managed after what felt like hours but was probably only seconds. "But give me a moment."

He reached for his mind star with desperate mental fingers.

The constellation of Body, Mind, and Soul blazed to life in his inner sky, spinning faster than he'd ever pushed them before. The Mind star pulled hardest, dragging his consciousness through its filter, and the grey world of Sovereign's Indifference washed over him like diving into arctic water.

The panic drowned—the grief, the sorrow, the horror—all of it pulled down beneath the grey calm and left cold clarity in its place.

Jin took a deep breath and then another until his hands stopped shaking and his heartbeat slowed to something approaching normal.

Later. Deal with it later.

"Angel?" His voice came out cold and detached.

« I'm here. » Angel sounded shaken, which was... concerning. « What happened, and how did we get cursed? »

"Curse?"

Jin pulled up his status screen with a mental command.

»»»» You have been afflicted with soul identity degradation. Seek immediate help!

»»»» ??? is crawling towards you through the cracks.

•••

« Jin? » Angel called out softly. « What happened? »

Jin heard the question, but stayed in the grey world where emotions couldn't touch him, and began recounting the experience with the detached precision of someone filing a mission report.

He described the white walls. The chains. The nurse whose face he couldn't remember despite years of care. Ren reading to him. The moment of recognition when the name crystallized. The cold pronouncement of death.

The black tears.

The white eyes.

The thing underneath.

When he finished, silence stretched between them for three full heartbeats.

"Can you read my recent memories?" Jin asked.

« ... »

« If you're talking about what happened while meditating... I can't. » Angel's voice was careful, measured. « There's no memory, or at least no memory I can see, which is not a good sign. You know what that means, right? There’s only one instance that this has happened before. »

“Yeah…” Jin leaned back against the sofa, still wrapped in Sovereign's Indifference like armor.

This opened a very disturbing can of worms. Severe complications that he didn't have time to unpack but couldn't afford to ignore, especially now that he only had less than twenty days to defeat the cult and stop the ritual.

« A Pandora's box. »

"Heh... Regardless, we need to have a talk with the Eternal One, Angel." The words came out cold, clinical, burning with purpose despite the grey filter. "And we need to talk to him soon."

« Jin, listen, we need to—

Jin's head twitched to the left. It was not a conscious movement. Just a small jerk, like someone had tugged an invisible string attached to his skull.

His head hurt, likely from the curse, he guessed.

—immediate action. The soul identity degradation warnings are stacking, and that last one— »

He blinked. Frowned. Was Angel saying something? He tried to tune out the pain, but it was there… present even through the grey world.

Which meant the pain was likely a serious issue.

The grey calm of Sovereign's Indifference wrapped tighter around Jin's mind, and finally, after a breath of relief now that the pain was manageable, he focused on Angel. He was saying something before—

Angel's voice felt... distant. Intrusive, almost. A buzzing that interfered with the clarity he needed right now.

He reached for the mental equivalent of a volume dial and tried to turn it up.

Just a little.

Just enough to think and hear him, but he felt his vision blurred at the edges.

Jin blinked hard. Once. Twice. Rubbed at his eyes with the heels of his palms, but the blur didn't clear completely. It hovered there at the periphery, making the apartment's walls seem less solid somehow. Less here.

Like looking through water.

Like looking through fog.

Like the memory, all blurry and forgotten.

Jin's eyes itched.

He rubbed them again, harder this time, and when he pulled his hands away, the blur had spread. Not just at the edges anymore.

“Angel!” He croaked.

« You should—

Jin's head snapped up. "Angel? Can’t hear you! I should what?"

« You should—

He turned the mental dial further up despite the surging pain.

« You should write it down. »

« Everything you remember. Before it fades further. Write it all down. »

That made sense. Perfect sense, actually. His memories were fragmenting—Angel had said so himself—and if he didn't record them now, they might slip away entirely. All those details about Ren, about the hospital, about his past life.

He called someone he didn’t remember for some notebooks or sheets and cursed at the world for giving his whole damn ring away.

He didn’t remember when he got the stuff, but he remembered being hunched over his desk, filling page after page with handwriting that grew progressively less neat, letters cramping together, words running into each other into a mess for anyone else but perfectly readable for him.

This was his bible… his codex… his life.

And in the corner of his status screen, where warnings piled up in red text, a new message appeared.

»»»» ??? is here.

But Jin didn't see it.

He'd turned the volume so far up that the pain and noise were too loud in his mind to hear the alarm, and his eyes—still itching, still burning, still seeing the world through that spreading blur—were fixed on the paper in front of him.

Writing.

Writing.

Writing everything down before it could fade away, just like Angel had suggested.
