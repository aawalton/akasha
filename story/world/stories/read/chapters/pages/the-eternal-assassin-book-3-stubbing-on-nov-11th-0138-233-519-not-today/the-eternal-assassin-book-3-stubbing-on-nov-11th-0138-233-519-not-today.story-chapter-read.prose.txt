Sever the Thread - Lvl 28

Asher could only stare in shock at the fallen lich before him as her head rolled to a stop across the carpet. All this time, he’d mentally prepared himself to go after The Council of Death. All this time, he’d imagined the horrifying battles they’d have to face. All the blood, sweat, and tears that would go into taking down each and every one one of the fourteen liches. And while the freaky abyss monster had in fact been a pretty unnerving opponent to fight, never in a million years had he expected one of the liches on the Council to fall so easily. There was no fanfare. No burst of brilliant colors as an evil of the world was snuffed out. No ethereal cheering as her soul was severed from this mortal realm after all these years.

She was just dead. Dead, dead. Just like that.

“How intriguing," Zygrul said, the fires in his eyes burning brighter than ever as he stared at his fellow lich who had been reduced to no more than a regular corpse. Asher didn’t miss how the flames were once again burning their regular purple, almost as if the black fires that had replaced them for a brief moment had been a figment of his imagination. “Witnessing your skill up close is truly different from watching it from afar. You severed her soul through the connection she had to her phylactery with only a single cut. Outstanding.”

Recovering from his own shock, Asher didn’t bother engaging the lich in banter. As quickly as he could, he reached out with another compressed use of Distortion, aiming to plunge Crescent directly into Zygrul’s own neck and take it out as well. But almost as if the lich knew what he was thinking, its eyes burned black once more, and Asher gasped as his energy was again ripped from his soul. Though this time, rather than target an artifact on his person, Zygrul appeared to target Asher’s skill itself. Distortion collapsed without warning, the very skill ripped away from his control and consumed.

And his arm along with it.

Asher stared in shock at the bleeding stump that sat where his right arm used to be. The entire length of his arm that had been shoved through compressed space was simply gone, as though it had never existed to begin with. It all happened so fast that the missing limb didn’t even hurt, even as blood began spurting everywhere.

“Unless you want to lose the other arm, I would not recommend trying that again,” Zygrul said, its eyes already back to their standard purple once more. “You are going to need those in the near future. Along with this as well.”

Again, Zygrul’s eyes flashed black. Only this time, rather than rip out any more of his energy or consume any more of his skills, Crescent appeared out of nowhere, as if summoned from Asher’s own Personal Rift. Asher could only stare at the lich in shock as the dagger floated over to him, landing on the carpet at his feet without Zygrul so much as moving a muscle. Slowly, Asher reached down and picked it up, the knuckles on his left hand going white around his dagger. Already Eternal Regeneration was getting to work closing up his severed arm, aided by his new Amulet of Improved Healing. Asher briefly thanked his past self for choosing to wear his Ring of False Death on his left hand, before he shook his head.

“You set her up,” Asher said blankly, finally putting together everything that had happened. “Letting her secure me with her own artifact… Drawing her attention away from me… Breaking me out of her control with her back turned… You wanted me to kill her. Why?!”

“That is certainly a theory,” Zygrul said, sounding no different than it had just a few minutes ago. As far as Asher could tell, the lich didn’t even seem to have emotions. It merely stood there, continuing to watch Asher like he was some sort of interesting lab experiment. “Why would I want you to do that?”

“You…” Asher paused, surprised to find the lich actually talking with him. He’d expected it to vanish into the aether, leaving him with more questions than answers. Yet seeing as it was still here, Asher decided not to waste the opportunity. “You want to take over The Council of Death? Take down this Lethinal guy and replace him?”

“A reasonable hypothesis,” Zygrul said, continuing to stand as still as a statue, other than the flickering flames in his eye sockets. “Though incorrect. I will be taking this base with me, but it will take some time for my ritual to be completed. You are welcome to take whatever you find within it before it vanishes, though I would recommend having vacated the perimeter before the ritual is finished. Even for a realm wanderer, where I am going, you stand no chance of survival.”

“You’re just going to up and leave?” Asher asked, staring at the lich in shock. “Just like that?”

“Indeed I am,” Zygrul said. “I would recommend starting your search down the hall, three doors on the right. Perhaps after you save your associates.”

Actually conversing with the lich and killing one of the members of The Council of Death had been jarring enough for Asher to forget about everything that had happened previously, and he swore as he remembered the greater demon that had grabbed him and brought him before the liches before heading back out at Zygrul’s orders.

“If you’re on our side, why order the demons to attack us at all?!” Asher demanded, pointing at Zygrul with his dagger. “You burned down Horntho village!”

“I am on no one’s side. It was the simplest way to encourage your growth,” Zygrul said, as casually as if he were stating that Asher’s mithral dagger was blue. “The risk of you choosing to live a simpler life within that village was too great, and regardless, the other members of the Council would have come for you if I had not. I made the optimal choice.”

“Your optimal choice hurt people I cared about!” Asher shouted, the faces of George and Martha flashing through his mind. Memories of Logan running around playing with Brian on the outskirts of the village. Nights spent consoling Samantha as she cried about the only home she’d ever known burning to the ground around her.

“Your emotions are irrelevant,” Zygrul said, tapping its staff on the ground and summoning a swirling black portal behind itself. “You have five minutes.”

With a shout, Asher desperately threw up a Spatial Lock, locking down teleportation and trapping the lich inside with him. Without hesitation, Zygrul’s eyes flared black flames once more, and Asher doubled over as a hole was torn straight into the side of his skill, ripped out like a wolf tearing into a deer carcass. By the time he’d recovered and straightened up, the lich was nowhere to be found.

Damn it! He hissed, rubbing at his missing right arm. Zygrul had even taken the other lich’s body and the gemstone-studded tiara with it, leaving Asher in the room with nothing more than some furniture and a carpet stained with his own blood. Asher hadn’t missed how the lich didn’t actually drop any shards upon death, which meant either she’d been hiding them somewhere, or liches couldn’t hold shards.

Or the shards dropped out beside her phylactery, where her soul is actually stored. That would explain why they’re not here as well.

Deciding that was a question for later, Asher turned and ran out of the study, doing his best to double back the way the greater demon had taken him. Zygrul’s base was a lot larger than he’d originally realized, and he cursed his past self for not paying better attention.

To be fair, I was being dragged by the head. Not exactly the easiest method for paying attention to one’s surroundings.

Asher tried not to imagine the greater demon grinning as it tore its way through the rest of his team, but having personally been on the receiving end of just what those monsters were capable of, it was hard not to. The only thing that kept him reasonably calm was the fact that Samantha hadn’t sustained any serious injuries according to the Eternal Mark on her, and from what he could tell with the Lesser Realm Sight skill he’d used Permanence on, the realm of ages was pretty calm within Zygrul’s base. So far it seemed their theory that the less activity there was in the material realm, the less fluctuations there were in the realm of ages, was holding true. It wasn’t all that surprising that a lich’s secret base tucked away on the far edges of the kingdom didn’t really have many people wandering about within its walls.

Asher cursed as he tried Recalling to Samantha yet again, only to have his skill fail for the third time. On the one hand, that meant he was close to the greater demon. But on the other, that meant the greater demon was also still alive and presumably fighting.

Thankfully, with Samantha’s mark informing him of her precise location, he managed to figure out where to go quickly enough. Throwing open one last door, he stopped and stared at the scene of carnage before him.

The large room that once held a good fifty lesser demons and at least twice that in imps was absolutely covered in blood and body parts. Dead demons were scattered all over the floor with arrows having been blasted through their heads or limbs cleaved cleanly off by, presumably, a giant battle axe. Most of the imps appeared to have been burst open, and Asher recognized Moxy’s handiwork in an instant. But even with all the blood and gore scattered about, that wasn’t even the most eye-catching thing.

No, that would be his three teammates going head-to-head with the greater demon.

Asher watched from across the room as Rulfar roared, the man still in his battle form as he brought his battle axe down on the demon with all his weight. The demon merely grinned with its black teeth, catching the blade in its claws. Yet as it went to retaliate, moving to shove its other clawed hand straight into Rulfar’s chest, Samantha blocked the blow with the edge of her emerald wing, the hunter darting out from behind Rulfar to fire a glowing arrow point-blank into the demon’s face. The demon, closing its eyes to let the arrow bounce off a heavy lid, staggered as Moxy blasted into it from behind, using her full body weight to ram her shoulder into the back of its legs and cause it to stumble. Asher heard an unpleasant pop that he was pretty certain was Moxy’s shoulder dislocating rather than any part of the demon breaking, but the warrior simply leapt back, already healing herself with a glowing violet hand and a manic grin on her face. At the same time, her pelts came to life, leaping off her and running up the demon’s body to try and start gnawing away at its throat.

All of this happened in the blink of an eye, and Asher stared in wonder at the sight of his three teammates not only taking the fight to one of the greater demons, but winning as far as he could tell. They’d manage to back the greater demon into the corner of the room with the weakest fluctuations in the realm of ages, which meant the greater demon could do little more than harness slight bursts of speed here and there rather than pretending to teleport around.

It was Moxy, recovering from her own reckless attack, who spotted him first. The warrior’s eyes widened, and she laughed out loud. “Guys, look who’s back!”

“Asher!” Samantha shouted, giving him a relieved smile before quickly bringing her focus back toward the demon, just in time to block a lightning-fast kick with her wings. Based on how their emerald green form flickered from the impact, Asher could tell Samantha had to be running on fumes energy-wise. “Help us finish this!”

“You’re finally done talking?” the greater demon asked, staring across the room at Asher and giving him a black smile that would haunt his dreams for days to come. “Took you long enough. In that case, I’ll see you in hell.”

Before any of them could say or do anything, the greater demon reached up with its jagged claws and sliced its own head clean off.

Or at least, it certainly tried to.

“Not on my watch!” Rulfar roared, the bloodhound dropping his axe and catching the greater demon’s wrists just in the nick of time. Even in his battle form and with his Strength element, it was clear that Rulfar was straining with everything he had as he struggled to keep the greater demon from severing its own head, and it was a battle he was rapidly losing as the demon’s claws dug deeper and deeper into its own neck. Looking over his shoulder, Rulfar shouted at him. “Asher, do it!”

Not wasting a second, Asher sprinted forward with Distortion, closing the distance between him and his team in a flash. He watched as the demon’s face morphed from confidence to fear as whatever plan it had in place quickly fell apart. With a roar, the demon reared back and kicked Rulfar in the gut, snapping bones and sending the bloodhound hurtling backwards and crashing to the ground in a heap.

But by then it was too late.

Before it could finish taking its own life, Asher lunged the last few feet, using a thin tunnel of compressed space to drive Crescent straight into the greater demon’s eye socket. Having only managed to cut two-thirds of the way through its own powerfully built neck thanks to Rulfar, the demon staggered back as Asher’s dagger entered its brain, before toppling over and crashing to the ground. Panting, the four of them looked at one another, before Moxy let out a ragged chuckle.

“Well thank the realms for that! Now we can sell it!”
