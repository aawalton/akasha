Adon reached the entrance to the fort town just as the gates were swinging open.

He saw the figures slip through the entry as it had barely parted, and a little flash of nervousness flitted through his mind for a moment.

Then the feeling was gone.

It was just the way these people moved, their postures making their bodies look smaller than they were, almost like they were trying to conceal themselves. But they were moving out in the open, and they even had their hands visible as if to reassure their allies that they were friendly.

There is a type of person who naturally moves that way, Adon told himself. He had reference to memories of such people. Often, scouts and spies could not easily shake off being good at hiding, good at lying, and naturally inclined to take up less space when it was physically possible to do so.

Such behavior became a reflex.

It was only in the present context, walking openly through a gate that they were being permitted through willingly, that their body language felt slightly ridiculous.

Why did a group of scouts and spies show up here? Adon wondered uneasily. He remembered that these professionals had been dispatched—probably entirely by King Janus, since Rosslyn’s own spy corps was likely in shambles after the fall of Wayn—but had no idea what sort of information they might be carrying.

Adon was tempted to ask. A quick, telepathic question would make them think of the answers, and then, even if they did not respond verbally, he would scoop the information from their brains.

He was still a little reluctant to violate individuals’ privacy more than was necessary, though. He’d occasionally used his powers to smooth over interactions between Rosslyn, King Janus, and the other nobles and officer class members in meetings over the last several weeks, and he had no regrets about that. He could tell himself it was for the greater good, and no one really noticed that he was helping Rosslyn navigate social situations. She just looked like an unusually socially intelligent leader.

Stealing secrets out of spies’ heads seemed like the sort of thing that would be difficult for him to explain or excuse in the event that someone else found out, though. If Adon told anyone else what he knew, there was some possibility that the information would leak in some way, causing a lot of problems for him and whoever he had spoken with, and possibly fracturing the delicate trust in the alliance. And if he never intended to discuss what the scouts and spies knew, there was probably little point in taking the information from their heads.

So Adon just set down beside the new arrivals and transformed into his semi-humanoid form, with the human number of arms and legs, pointed ears—mostly because he just liked that aesthetically—and some additional eyes and subtle bits of exoskeletal armor protecting him underneath the clothing that he manifested over his body.

Their reaction to him was something between startled and pleased.

He could actually feel their mental defenses sort of harden at the same time that they smiled at him and seemed to be genuinely—as conveyed by Telepathy—pleased to see him.

Being a spy must be hard, Adon assessed. They have to feel unsafe all the time when they’re out on a mission, and even while they’re here, their minds aren’t at ease.

“Revered butterfly,” said the spy who seemed to be the leader of the group. “Would you kindly bring us before the King and Queen?” He lowered his voice. “The smallest circle of trust possible is ideal right now, you understand?”

Adon nodded. “I understand. Thank you all for the risks you take in traveling back and forth and gathering information.”

“The men taking the most risk are the ones we have left behind,” the spy replied. “But your kind words are much appreciated, of course.”

The butterfly allowed his body to snap back to its insect form, and he flew back across the fort town as quickly as he could, settling on Goldie’s shoulder once again. She was almost to Adon’s destination anyway, and he expected that she would be within whatever circle of trust ended up being permitted to hear the spies’ intel.

“What was the commotion about?” she asked curiously.

The people Janus and Rosslyn had out there gathering information are back, Adon sent, going to Telepathy to avoid the possibility of being overheard. They want a private conversation with the inner circle.

“Oh, well I’ll take you to Rosslyn so you and she can discuss it,” Goldie replied.

Adon sensed her lack of desire to be part of the conversation to come—the spider really hadn’t liked the number of unnecessary meetings she had attended over the last few weeks.

I’m pretty sure we’ll want you there, too, Goldie, he sent. But if you want to get out of it, I can go on my own, and you could turn into a spider and go out hunting again. I’ll pretend I didn't see you.

“No,” she said quietly, resigned. “I will be strong and endure the boring strategy conversation. For the good of the country.” She gave him a small smirk.

At least Frederick will be there, Adon replied.

Her smile turned more genuine.

“That is true,” she said.

You can’t have a strategy meeting without Frederick, she added in her mind, a note of pride in her inner voice.

The two of them entered the tower at the back end of the fort town and ascended the stairs to find Rosslyn in her study, reviewing a map of Claustria intently. She sat up as soon as the mystic beasts entered the room, her eyes falling on Goldie with a hint of surprise.

“To what do I owe the pleasure?” the Queen asked, smiling. “You hardly ever come up here unless we explicitly ask you to, Goldie.”

“It’s not you,” the spider replied. “But I prefer action over these long discussions over the wooden table. I even prefer the wild-caught food over the meals that they serve.”

“The cooks will be devastated to hear that,” Rosslyn said, amused.

“Nothing personal,” Goldie said. “Probably not even a reflection on their skills. Just personal taste. Food that I killed myself tastes the best.”

We are actually here for a reason, though, Rosslyn, Adon sent telepathically.

Her eyes landed on him with obvious affection.

“Come over here and give me a kiss?” she said lightly. “Before you get into your reason?”

Adon would have blushed if he was already in human form. He and Rosslyn had kissed before, more than once or twice now, but it still never felt like something he was allowed to do. In his mind, he was still going to wake up back at the palace any day now, and she would be looking at him with her head tilted, wondering what sort of perverted dreams had been passing through his mind.

The butterfly obligingly transformed into his humanoid shape, approached the Queen, and gave her a long kiss on the mouth. Goldie stood back, watching, clearly basking in the glow of Adon’s slight embarrassment at her presence.

“So, we had some spies and scouts come back,” Adon said immediately as soon as his and Rosslyn’s mouths had separated. “They want to have a meeting as soon as possible, need to know personnel only.” He lowered his voice. “I gather they have some new, strategically important information, based on how quiet they want to be about it.”

Rosslyn nodded and rubbed her hands together.

“Well, we have not been idle,” she said eagerly. “If they give us good news, our preparations of the last few weeks might finally bear fruit.”

Adon felt how excited she was about the idea of going back to Wayn and reclaiming her country’s capital from the Empire. He forced himself to be somewhat excited with her, instead of worrying about what might happen during such an attack—to Rosslyn, to Goldie, to Frederick, to the population of the city. The risks were endless, but there was no gain in life without some risk or sacrifice.

With his new skill, hopefully the butterfly could mitigate the dangers.

That’s right, I haven’t told Rosslyn about the Butterfly Effect yet, he thought. I guess I’ll have to wait and do it sometime after this meeting.

“I do think it will be good news,” said Goldie from behind them. “I did not speak with them alongside Adon, so I don’t know. But I have a good feeling.”

Rosslyn called for one of her aides to have the key members of the combined military command assembled.

Then she chattered away pleasantly for the next ten minutes with Adon, mostly focused on her war plans. She was genuinely energized by the thought of personally leading the fight. The more time he spent around Rosslyn, the more Adon recognized that she was almost as eccentric and different from others of her own class as Adon was separate from the others around them.

It was a little thing, but it made him feel slightly more confident in the relationship. They weren’t an “odd couple,” two people who did not make sense together. They were a couple of people who both happened to be a little odd in different ways.

The time flew by briskly, and Adon had no awareness at all of how much had passed when a knock came at the door.

Rosslyn’s aide had returned.

“Your Highness, everyone is gathered as you ordered,” he said quietly, bowing.

“Very good,” Rosslyn replied. “Thank you, Logan.”

She rose from where she had been sitting beside Adon on a loveseat.

“Ready to go?” Adon asked Goldie.

She frowned and shook her head. “This had better be as important as I think it is.”

The trio left the room and passed through four layers of guards—far more security than the tower normally kept inside—before they arrived at the meeting place where the spies, scouts, and a handful of key figures sat around the great table.

There was King Janus, Frederick, Spymaster Scott, and a handful of generals.

All of them rose as one body when Rosslyn entered the room.

“Your Highness,” King Janus said, his eyes sparkling.

Adon could sense that he had a good feeling about this, as much as Goldie did. He hadn’t asked the spies to divulge anything to him outside the presence of the Queen, but he was anticipating very positive news of some sort. Something that would finally allow him and the Claustrians to take the much-delayed action that everyone was anticipating.

“Everyone, please be seated,” Rosslyn said, smiling. “Good to see you, Your Highness. Frederick. General…” She greeted everyone in the room by name in order of rank, going to the Spymaster last. Adon sensed that she did not know the names of the front line spies themselves, which was partially by design. They kept a tight circle at all times, as much as they could, and they recruited new members in the field wherever necessary.

“And I sincerely thank the group of you for returning here with your insights,” she added to the group of spies collectively, lowering her head in a nod that was almost a bow.

The spies let out a little gasp as a group, before the leader spoke up.

“You honor us too much, Your Highness,” he said a little nervously. “But thank you very much. We will all cherish this moment, when we received our Queen’s gratitude, until our dying days. Shall we close the door and begin the meeting?”

“One moment,” Rosslyn replied. She ordered an attendant to bring them refreshments and then to seal the door.

Finally, after some food and wine had been placed before everyone, including the group of spies and scouts, the door closed, and the meeting could begin.

“Thank you for gathering so quickly and discreetly to meet with us,” said the leader of the little group of spies. “We appreciate you keeping the circle to this minimal number of people, for the safety of myself and our other men in the field. My comrades and I bring you a report on the movements and intentions of the Demon Army and its troops…”
