In twelve seconds, Clarissa touched down at the far edge of the boss clearing.

If he’s alive, Clarissa promised herself. I WILL strangle him.

What’s he thinking?!

But he isn’t. He’s an Outworlder, it’s all still a dream to him.

She wouldn’t have allowed Alice and Jason, two well-geared Level 5’s with Awakened pedigrees, and as such years of specialized training, to take on a two-star, F-rank boss.

You absolute idiot!

He’s so new he doesn’t realize what his limits are.

Even if he’s gained a level, or two levels, since the start—she was struggling to imagine what Beckett was thinking; she was trying to imagine the most likely scenario. Even if he has a few extra levels, even if he can save himself, what happens to his teammate?

It was very, very rare you’d trust an incomplete team of Level 5’s to so much as attempt a two-star rift boss.

As for the individuals she’d trust to comprise a team of only two Level 5’s, across time, against a two-star boss—she and the rest of Awakened society were very aware of their names. These were the talents the world knew, your Devereaux’s, your Arden’s, your St. Clair’s; Clarissa was thinking specifically of what Adrian was at Level 5. Two such transcendental talents might have a chance.

Clarissa herself was said to be one such potential talent, but she was well aware potential was all she was. She’d yet to truly establish herself.

The reason she’d been assigned as one of Beckett’s peer mentors was precisely because he was one such potential talent. It was this talent that gave her a glimmer of hope.

But talent was much more than a grade on a talent card, and if Clarissa felt she was unproven,  what was Beckett?

Clarissa glanced around desperately, and she froze.

Beckett’s body was bleeding from dozens of places. His lips were pale with ice mana as he gasped, lying there, eyes unfocused, blood dripping from open lips.

Before him, an ice knight was raising its blade.

She felt, paradoxically, a stab of relief—she was in time.

To last twelve seconds solo against a Level 7 high-quality rift creature, such as a rift boss, especially with four days of Awakening experience, was already incredible.

I can’t let him know that, she resolved. A spell formed within a gorgeous diamond-tipped staff, an instance item retrieved from the Antarctica Tower in its first cycle, between 2016-2019, before the items reset for its second cycle; as such there were and would always be less than 300 identical replicas of this staff in existence. Each was worth a fortune, and Clarissa’s was by far the most expensive of her gear items.

It was called Time-lost Diamond Staff, and it was tipped with its namesake ‘Time-lost Diamond’ which dramatically reduced casting times for E-rank Skills—among other properties. It was, like many Tower-delved instance items, widely considered a pinnacle item of its class. It didn’t merely allow for Skill spamming, it also essentially meant anything Clarissa cast was essentially instantaneous.

It was also why Clarissa was certain she was in time.

Except a Skill struck before hers.

A blur of—is that Swordforce?

Two more such Swordforce arcs followed; it succeeded in gaining the boss’s attention, at least.

It also succeeded in gaining Clarissa’s attention. She pursed her lips.

“…What?”

It was Allen.

Or was it Alex?

It’s Alex.

Alex was at the edge of her vision from this angle, and his eyes were almost unnervingly wide, like he was trying to take in the entire clearing at once.

He’s a Swordforce specialist, she realized. The Swordforce arc was quite well-done for his level, especially since it seemed to be a Mana Manipulation.

What struck her was—he clearly felt pressure; it was clear in the intensity on his face, but there was also a strange lack of fear. It was like he’d decided to drown out everything except his focus.

What is he—?

An instant later, she saw the two Pure mana constructs hovering a few feet away.

The ice knight attacked.

Clarissa felt Force mana rising in Alex’s body. “Wait—!” She began to say.

Sword struck sword.

CLANG!

The sound reverberated through her mind.

A second strike, a third.

Clarissa was silent.

Clarissa watched as Beckett staggered up, muscles crackling with infused Lightning. She watched as the two exchanged a glance.

Beckett respects him. As an equal.

She watched as the two began a lurching back-and-forth with the knight in which Alex wielded Force within his body, and Swordforce on his blade, and unwieldy mana constructs condensed in the air. And Beckett wielded constructs of Fire and Lightning she was certain he didn’t have at the start of all this, constructs he’d placed in a shell of Pure mana.

“Miss St. Clair!”

It was Alice, looking alarmed—at both the fight, and at Clarissa herself. Clarissa’s evaluatees had arrived.

“Stay behind me,” said Clarissa instantly, stepping in front of them.

She was fairly certain Alex and Beckett were Level 5, like Alice and Jason. She didn’t identify the two; if a person were identified, they could feel it. I don’t want them to know I’m here.

Her thinking had changed very quickly. She was certain she could save them. She was quickly beyond that.

I should step in, said a tiny part of her.

Clarissa didn’t.

Beckett chose this, he has to understand the consequences. She was very clear on this. If I save him this quickly, I’m teaching him the wrong lesson.

There was a second reason, which was simple.

She’d glimpsed something startling in these two, a glimmer she wasn’t certain was real.

If it was, then stepping in was absolutely the wrong decision.

Clarissa watched as a few exchanges passed, exchanges that looked absolutely desperate.

The ice knight was simply overwhelming them with the sheer speed and intensity of its attacks; a particularly forceful exchange caused Alex to spit blood, and she was certain Alex’s body, like Beckett’s, was starting to break down.

Beckett’s fire-lightning lances helped relieve Alex between flurries of slashes. When the ice knight attacked him, Beckett wasn’t parrying at all anymore; instead he was using sharp spurts of lightning infusions, and his intuitive sense, to slip out of the way. Damaging his body slightly as he did.

Beckett also attacked with his Lightning Spear Skill, freezing the Knight, but also chaining to the few minions that managed to get in close.

Alex was also slipping in Mana Shields to help himself stay afloat.

But it wasn’t enough.

The two of them were clearly starting to break, even as they held the Knight, and its few debilitated minions, to this equilibrium with razor-thin margins.

The Knight also debuted an area-of-effect attack in which it stabbed the ground, sending dozens of pillars of ice shooting up around the clearing, pillars which also instantly exploded into shards of jagged ice. It was impossible to avoid them all, and several shards injected new freeze into Alex and Beckett’s bodies.

All the while, Alex was flooding his sword with Swordforce; he was also having to circulate Force within his body to break the accumulating ice clusters.

Is this all you have?

After what she’d just seen—especially from Alex—she couldn’t bring herself to believe it.

I know you have more in you. Even as, in her mind, the two of them had less than thirty seconds of these exchanges before either Beckett or Alex’s bodies gave in.

Come on! Clarissa clenched her teeth. Was it her imagination, or was Alex holding a little better against the ice knight—was the Force in his body a little denser?

Alice and Jason were silent, and when she glanced at them, they were wide-eyed, slightly uncomprehending.

A few things happened in quick succession.

First, Beckett slammed a nearby minion with potent two fire-lightning lances, finally finishing it. A minor victory.

And the wear on the ice knight was also increasingly visible—its overall health was still likely higher than either Alex’s or Beckett’s, but at least pieces of its ice armor were now partially melted and slashed. It was also faintly crackling with lightning mana, which Beckett had managed to load into it across dozens of stabs and sparks. Including, presumably, a few sparks he’d thrown before she’d arrived.

But this wasn’t a positive.

Because the degree of damage seemed to have reached a threshold, and Clarissa identified what was happening before either Alex or Beckett seemed able to.

The mana in the ice knight’s body, and especially its sword, flared brilliantly; it slashed its sword at the skies. An arc of ice mana flew directly upward.

The skies were partly cloudy, with sparse sunlight filtering through.

The arc seemed to rip this sky wide open, exposing a dome of artificial bright-blue.

The sunlight grew blinding; she saw Alex and Beckett both step back a little, shielding their faces.

Sunlight seemed to condense on the ice knight. It kept its sword lifted, a sword that seemed to become a prism of light.

CRACK-CRACK!

The heavy armors on the knight’s body shattered. CRACK-CRACK-CRACK!

The heavier pieces sloughed off the knight, melting as they did, revealing a scarcely scratched, more slender physique. A few lighter pieces remained. The ice mana’s melting back into its body…

Its giant cleaver of a sword also partially melted away in the light, revealing a sleeker, glistening saber.

The boss leveled its sword at Alex and Beckett once more, its transformation complete; the two braced, their eyes trained on it.

And the knight, in this final stage, began to move.

Or more accurately, it began to dance, and Clarissa’s heart sank.

It has a swords dance?

As the knight stepped, its movements were very fluid, and its sword began to glow. And the intensity of the ice mana in its body increased; it began to exude an icy aura.

The intensity of the freeze in Alex and Beckett’s bodies, in its presence, also began to increase.

And with this swords dance, it struck.

First at Alex, with three blindingly fast attacks—one of which easily shattered a Mana Shield, a second of which Alex barely blocked, a third of which slashed his right arm, leaving an iced-up cut.

Before turning on Beckett, and delivering a torrid sequence. Only with two Mana Shields were the two able to hold it off, if barely.

And with every step, its mana was only growing brighter.

Clarissa felt a little reluctant, but—It’s time to step in.

She’d given them as much a chance as she could, but with every exchange it was clear the two couldn’t keep up, were barely surviving. She watched as Beckett staggered, and a sword whipped for his neck, a shield spell already in her staff—

PING!

An arc of Swordforce sliced into side of the ice knight’s face.

An arc that, unlike every prior instance of Swordforce, sliced two inches into its icy helm.

The ice knight danced back, turning as it did—

PING!

It deflected a third slash.

And by then, she was certain.

Alex’s Swordforce had leveled up.

Alex’s face didn’t change at all. The ice knight leaped toward him, diving into a beautiful slash which Alex barely held off with two consecutive Mana Shields. With his deadened right arm, Alex now seemed much less keen to deflect big strikes head-on.

The ice mana was nearly wiped all the way off Alex’s sword. Resisting all that ice mana had to have helped.

There’s the glimmer.

“You just—” Beckett spat blood, grinning weakly.

His body surged with Lightning.

Except rather than striking at his own body—crack-crack-CRACK!

It began breaking up the ice in his muscles.

“You just had to beat me to it.”

“This—” said Alex, deflecting a blisteringly fast blow. “—isn’t over!”

Alex was right. The boss’s powers, the brilliance of its mana, was still increasing. Its aura was beginning to affect the mana in the air; as it danced, an icy wind slowly began to rise.

Beckett engaged it again, as did Alex.

A little more evenly, at first, except for all the two breakthroughs did, it only managed to return the fight to near-parity.

A parity that still began to slide; they still can’t keep up—since the knight had its swords dance, and the winds were beginning to rise into a vortex, a vortex funneling mana to its center as it entered the next stage of its—

A subtle glow of essence.

Beckett Leveled up, and he bared his teeth.

Alex also began to grin.

And it seemed around this point that the ice knight began to understand, what kind of Awakened had walked into its zone.

Clarissa knew because its movements grew a little more erratic; faster, like it was trying to force the dance further, force the later stages, where she guessed it’d swallow all the ice mana in the clearing, and if it accomplished that, even Beckett’s Level—

CRACK.

CRACK-CRACK!

It was like the sound in Beckett’s body, except this time in Alex’s—

Again?! Clarissa was incredulous.

It wasn’t just the Force; two Pure mana constructs screamed through the air, clearly much faster than anything prior, as every single component, from Force, to Pure, to Swordforce seemed stronger—

That upgrade also broke through to the next Mana Manipulation Skill rarity, she realized.

The ice knight deflected a construct, and dodged a second, but it didn’t seem to like what it felt at all.

The ice knight danced, its movements no longer fluid, almost manic; it seemed to show genuine panic. All the while, Alex and Beckett began flowing in, from its perspective, what had to be a frankly terrifying degree of synchronicity.

As the two of them—in their own ways—so clearly understood the creature they were facing.

They can’t keep up, they can’t keep up—

It can’t keep up.

Fire and lightning flashed within the rising vortex, as fluid light and dark blues converged from the other side.

Four consecutive breakthroughs under life-or-death pressure—contributed to by life-or-death pressure—back, to back, to back, to back—and as she watched the way the final boss was moving now, and the way the two of them were moving—which of you is dancing?
