Ryland wished he had more time. There was so much else he would have liked to explore and investigate, so much else he’d like to understand before he made a proper move.

Sadly, there was no real time for any of that. Mavar might have gone insane with his determination to ascend as a Great God, but he was no fool. He wasn’t without power. The effects were there for all to see. The truth was laid before them.

After all, [Mad God’s Cage] could no longer contain the extraplanar sorcery descending upon them.

Gleaming, shining, golden cracks pockmarked the entirety of Ryland’s barrier, the harshest of sunlight injecting into the reality Ryland inhabited like burning spears. They carried with them motes of virulent power, ironically the same kind of energy that Ryland himself had used to construct [Mad God’s Cage] in the first place.

But the shining cracks were only one source of the divine energy polluting the Realm. The far more potent source was Mavar himself.

And the Living Mana he was coopting into his forced ascension.

The great titan Ryland had fought against had now collapsed completely. No longer were those two conjoined figures recognizable in the amorphous blister and bump of Living Mana that it had become. Instead, pustules exploded with chaotic Living Mana, jets shot this way and that, frothing tendrils tried to escape only to be pulled back.

More golden energy ran through that entire amalgamation, lending the whole Living Mana abomination an eerie halo that clashed against its opalescence.

The sight wasn’t pretty at all.

“Sir…” Acrypha said. She couldn’t get more words out. Her face was twisting with anguish. Not for Viren, whom Ryland had confirmed was alive with his Inscribed Glyph of Detection. It had to be for her parents. For the guardians formed from their spirits and taken over by the Living Mana. “What do we do? Why is everything breaking apart?”

Ryland could only shake his head. She was right. Chunks of reality were literally falling away as an intense pressure assaulted them. He had cast [Oneself World] a while back, yet it wasn’t going to last long under this level of duress.

Moreover, those chunks of reality…

Ryland frowned. The masses of void-like darkness that tore off the “ceiling” of reality, leaving behind a pocket of blistering golden energy streaming into the universe, were different sized. But each was still large enough to form apocalyptic meteors when landing upon Rhel. While most people had already left the Realm, he couldn’t be certain that everyone had.

If he didn’t stop those would-be meteors of broken reality, who knew what sort of untold devastation would sweep across Rhel.

“Sir,” Acrypha said. She had steeled herself, her stance and her words now filled with conviction. “Are you going after those… things?”

Ryland nodded. “We must put a stop to them before anyone gets hurt.”

“Can you stop them all?”

That wasn’t a terrible question. Not even for Ryland. The cracks were numerous, and so were the chunks of reality. Each one was streaking towards Rhel with the speed of a comet. Each impact would be enough to wipe out unprepared civilizations.

And by a quick count, there were at least three dozen of them.

“I will have to,” Ryland said.

Acrypha nodded like she had expected that answer. “Then before you go, sir, please cover me up with your barriers. I don’t want to take up too much of your time, but just a few will be enough.”

“Why?”

She turned to stare where the Living Mana was pulsing and rippling as it radiated off tonnes of energy while being compressed to an incredible degree. “I need to stop that madness. And just like how Viren saved me, I need to protect him too.”

“The task will be difficult. Nigh on impossible.”

“Impossibility didn’t stop my parents, and it isn’t going to stop me, either.”

Ryland could have pointed out that her parents had been full-fledged Restorers, mages who were several orders of magnitude stronger than her. Acrypha was no slouch, but strong Restorers were at least Primordial-ranked. At just Silver, Acrypha couldn’t hope to match them.

But there was no time to argue. Things were already far too precarious.

“Alright, then,” Ryland said, then began casting. “[Eternal Bastion]. [Oneself World]. [Soulburn Transfer]. [Inevitability Escape].”

She looked at him as transparent planes of intense energy hardened around her body. Her eyes screwed up in suspicion. “Sir, those other spells…”

“Don’t worry,” Ryland said. He was worried, but he managed a smile regardless. “You have nothing to fear. The other two are merely precautions that will ensure you at least live to fight another day in case things go horribly south. For now, good luck, Acrypha. Let us both succeed.”

“Good luck, sir.”

They separated. Even as he disappeared with a [Translocate], Ryland cast [Omniscience] on one eye to ensure he always had Acrypha under close watch. All his precautions would indeed ensure her safety, and they already had practice with him transferring his power to her when they had fought against the Guardian.

So, for now, Ryland focused on stopping those meteors.

The first one he had rifted to would reach his location in half a minute at most. There was no time for him to do anything fancy. Plus, this was a literal chunk of reality. Most of his spells wouldn’t even work. Even if he tried to just use a giant rift to swallow it up, it would fail.

So, just as with [Mad God’s Cage], he had to crack open the Overrealm itself and send those chunks back where they belonged.

Ryland tamped down on the urge to scowl. There was another reason Mavar had reacted so poorly when Ryland had cast [Mad God’s Cage]. Using spells that channelled divine energy, that poked and prodded the plane of the Great Gods themselves, would always cause issues.

One such instance was the very destruction of reality he was currently facing, where an apocalyptic chunk was now heading for Rhel.

“[Gods’ Tangle],” Ryland cast.

His Incarnation form flared with brilliant energy. Ryland was outshining stars with the sheer amount of mana he was pushing out. With this magic, he crafted a crack across the Overrealm, much like the ones that had appeared across his [Mad God’s Cage] barrier. Except, instead of breaking and unleashing more unstoppable meteors, these debris of the Overrealm fell inside.

All thanks to that meteor of a reality chunk striking them with such force and fury.

Ryland had to make some effort to close the breach then, but it was only a second or two of overwhelming mana. He could manage it. True, it was one of the few spells that he considered taxing, and his spirit needed just a moment to recover its ability to handle those levels of mana.

But in no time at all, he had [Translocated] to the next reality chunk.

The act of stopping them became rote. It was the same deal every single time. Ryland had to cast [Gods’ Tangle] to ensure the meteors broke back into the Overrealm, leaving Rhel entirely unaffected.

Where the problem lay was in the stress it was causing even his manasoul. Magic of this level was accessible only to Incarnations, and though he was treating it with blithe practicality because the situation called for it, in truth, it went beyond mere magic.

This was divine energy. This was what made up the Overrealm itself. No truly mortal mage, no matter how strong, could ever hope to channel it.

For when even Incarnations built to channel it, like Ryland, were struggling after a while, then its exclusivity went without question. His spirit was sparking, sending off uncontrolled little bolts of energy as it faced great difficulty in maintaining its form. Yet another sign that channelling the divine energy was causing him great stress.

He’d need to do something about it. At this rate, even he would fail to stop the last few of those reality chunk meteors.

“[Sorcerer’s Graveyard],” he incanted next.

Several rifts popped open, though nothing emerged from them. Well, apart from a flood of mana that sank into Ryland with a rush like an explosion going off. He was instantly refuelled, his Incarnation form regaining its stability thanks to the rift to Realm 51. The repository of mana stored by an ancient Archlich was vast indeed.

It took only moments for Ryland to take care of the last of the reality meteors heading towards Rhel. Sadly, that was only the first batch. More cracks had popped up, and they had sent their respective stoppers flying towards Rhel as well.

Ryland sighed, then dealt with them in much the same fashion. He didn’t want to keep casting [Gods’ Tangle] all the time. It was destabilizing reality around Rhel. Now, if the influence of Mavar’s ascension stretched this far, then there would be far more cracks all across the airspace of Rhel.

Which meant the only way to stop it all in truth was by stopping Mavar himself.

“[Reality Scar],” Ryland incanted. “[Translocate].”

He had carved the first spell in a netlike fashion to ensure that it caught all the reality chunks. While it wouldn’t end them as surely as [Gods’ Tangle] was doing, it would at least prevent them from reaching Rhel or getting anywhere close.

Which meant Ryland was now free to approach the site of ascension. He had already taken far too long to finish dealing with the meteors.

“[Translocate],” he incanted.

Acrypha could feel a strange presence weighing on her now. She didn’t understand the intricacies of what was allowing her to move and breathe and just continue to function. Acrypha was literally in space. There should be no air, no warmth, nothing that could sustain her life.

Whatever spell Ryland had cast on her and whatever magic she still contained from all that Living Mana was what kept her alive. Kept her from feeling any of the negatives that she should have.

As such, she focused on her task.

The mass of Living Mana around that crazy bastard called Mavar looked far too impenetrable. She wasn’t sure what Tier of spell would generally be needed to get through something so powerful. After all, if Mavar was crazy enough to start an ascension, then was the Living Mana turning into some sort of god as well? That sounded even more insane.

But it didn’t matter because Acrypha had a way through that didn’t need magical firepower. The Living Mana would allow her through on its own.

Well, she needed magic to reach it, so she had to cast her magic. “[Future Flight].”

The spell made her summoned cloak flutter behind her. Since Acrypha was already floating in the air, she had no trouble magically propelling herself forward. It was just convenient that the pocket of pressurized air and stable atmosphere followed her.

“Please, open up,” she said as soon as she reached the barricade of Living Mana.

There was no response. Of course. It wasn’t like she was yelling into gigantic ears. There was no real proof that her words were reaching any sort of sentience that could actually comprehend what she was saying.

But that wasn’t the whole truth. If the Living Mana really wanted to protect her, then it would listen to her.

“I know you can hear me.” Acrypha said. “I know you can understand, and I don’t care what you’re pretending. I’m getting inside one way or another.”

She clawed at the barrier of Living Mana. It felt thick. Heavy and smooth and cool, like metal, but fizzing ever so slightly so that there was a buzz at her fingertips. Acrypha swallowed. If she didn’t have Ryland’s incredible barrier around her, she might just have lost her hand.

But because she had it, she continued attacking. One way or another, she was getting through.

The exertion was causing her to run out of breath. It shouldn’t have occurred so quickly. Acrypha had years of training to condition herself and ensure her stamina was far higher than a girl of her age should have.

But her current circumstance was far from normal. Ryland might have trapped a decent amount of air for her inside the cosmos, but it wouldn’t last forever. Plus, because she was exercising her muscles and raising her breathing rate, it was running out all the quicker. She didn’t stop, though. Acrypha needed to keep it up.

The stress from the strange golden energy got unbearable. Audible cracks thundered through the space she inhabited. Her heart began pounding. Drawing breaths suddenly turned into an impossibility and she was—

She was tumbling through the sudden opening in the wall of Living Mana, the air around her stabilizing again as she reached a hard platform. The wall had rapidly closed behind her again.

Something told her that wasn’t what was supposed to have happened. She had broken laws of physics, lessons her parents had more or less forced her to take, regardless of how magic tended to violate those laws every so often. Just like now. But she shook her head as her panic receded. Focus. She had to focus.

Mustering her courage, she forged onwards. Everything was constructed from Living Mana now. The constituent matter of the artificial moon appeared to be gone entirely.

That was a good thing. The Living Mana felt her and responded to her. It forewent her words or commands or intentions, focusing only on her goal. Her desperation to reach Viren before something irreversibly horrible occurred.

“I need to get to him,” she all but growled. “I need to—”

A tremor nearly threw Acrypha off her feet. She got the distinct impression it had come from behind her for some reason, but it alarmed her too, so she rushed onwards as fast as she could. Thankfully, with how compressed and condensed the collapsed artificial moon had become, she didn’t need long to reach her destination.

The Living Mana parted like a mournful curtain. Her heart quavered when she saw what lay before her.

Viren was frozen. He was covered in the same kind of liquid film that had covered her up inside the pod.

The Demiurge was glowing so brightly nearby, her eyes probably would have been blinded if not for Ryland’s protective spells. She could feel the energy trying to burn out her eyeballs, only to get transported away through some foreign link she didn’t fully understand.

But the point was, she was surviving. Now, all she needed to do was reach Viren.

So, Acrypha surged onwards. There was nothing and no one there to stop her. Mavar was too busy glowing brighter than a Calamity-cursed star and appeared not to care about the sheer insignificance she presented. She couldn’t fault him for it.

Strands of the most condensed Living Mana Acrypha had ever beheld led away from Mavar to a storm of the same magical substance all around them. Ignoring it all wasn’t easy, but she reached Viren in no time.

When she reached out a hand to touch him, to drag him free from this net that the Demiurge had him trapped in, things unravelled disastrously in moments.

Well, it wasn’t surprising. Acrypha was merely one lone girl against this entire apocalypse of magic. That she had come this far was an outright miracle, even with all the help she was receiving. No surprise, then, that she finally reached the limit of what she was capable of in this situation.

The link between Viren and the Demiurge began deleting her.

Acrypha was so utterly shocked by it, she couldn’t even muster up a scream at first. Her arm was literally breaking apart into smithereens too small for her to see, starting from her fingertips then digging into the flesh, then blood, then bone.

Then she screamed. She shrieked out, trying to jerk her limb free, but the link had possessed her.

Blood rushed up her face as bile climbed up her gullet, her eyes flecked with tears as her heart clenched in her chest. Her hand was now a misshapen blob of flesh and blood. And it was still disappearing.

But Viren was getting freed. Somehow, the link between him and the ascending Demiurge was transferring to her, and that was causing her to get consumed.

She had to free herself. She had to—

Blackness started dancing in her line of sight. Acrypha opened her mouth, to scream or to cast a spell she wasn’t sure, but no sounds emerged. If she didn’t free herself, her whole body was going to get bloodily decimated and—

Reality opened up behind her like a gaping maw. A wound she couldn’t even begin to fathom. Two arms grabbed her. One heavy and solid, the other light but firm still. One flowing like paint. One rippling with possessive, devouring strength.

Somehow, they pulled her free from the link with the Demiurge. They freed her from his eradicating grip.

But before Acrypha was aware of anything else, the maw of reality swallowed her whole.

Space was convulsing badly when he reached the same location he had started from. There was just too much divine energy everywhere. All those openings into the Overrealm had stuffed this universe with the kind of power it fundamentally couldn’t contain, which left it highly unstable and in great danger of collapsing entirely.

Ryland scowled. An uncontrolled breach into the Overrealm would cause massive problems that could easily spread to every corner of the Accorded Realms.

Mavar had really grown far too self-absorbed if he failed to see that.

With no time left, Ryland headed back with [Translocate]. Except, what he saw made him freeze.

Acrypha had just managed to free Viren at great cost to herself. He should have been there to help her. To save them both. But before he could even begin channelling his magic, a Wound Corridor opened up and took her away.
