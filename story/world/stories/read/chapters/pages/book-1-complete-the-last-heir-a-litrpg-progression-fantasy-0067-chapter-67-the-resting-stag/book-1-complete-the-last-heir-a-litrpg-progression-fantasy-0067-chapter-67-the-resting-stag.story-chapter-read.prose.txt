The Imperial Road was a marvel of magical engineering, one that took dozens of the kingdom's best mages centuries to build. It wound its way through the regions, the enchantments engraved into the stones keeping the denizens of the Wilds at bay.

Rowan smiled. “Thank you, Wraiths.”

The detour had probably saved him days of walking—not to mention, it confirmed his destination.

There were dozens of settlements in the Stormspire Heights, ranging from the size of Litwick to cities with walls high enough to keep out even the most dangerous monsters. But the Imperial Road only led to one place in a region.

Its Capital. The Storm Crown

It was a mountain range with three peaks, each one ruled by a Lord.

Rowan thought back, recalling his lessons.

The Veilspire, Mount Darkhaven, and Duskridge.

Lord Varyn Stormholt ruled the first, Lady Ysara Draven the second, and Lord Kaelen Thane the third.

All of them were powerful. They were Yellow-Core mages with Gold-ranked bodies, but they still gave their fealty to Baroness Jalani of House Duron. She was the only Mythril-ranked warrior in the city, and along with a few Green-Core mages, the true powerhouse of the Capital.

Baroness Jalani ruled Aether’s Rest, a floating island nestled between the protective mountain range. It was a place Rowan hoped not to visit, because if he did, something had gone terribly wrong.

He would have preferred the token to teleport him somewhere slightly more remote, but this was also fine. Especially after the increase in power [Storm Touched] gave him. Rowan wouldn’t be fighting the Baroness or the Lords quite yet, but their retinues weren’t out of the question.

It was a ridiculous thought. Him winning against Yellow-Core mages and Gold-ranked warriors seemed hard to process, especially considering he’d cast his first spell less than a year ago. But Rowan felt it wasn’t just hubris talking.

His spells were stronger than ever, his mana pool larger, and his efficiency higher. Not to mention that his stats had grown by leaps and bounds. He was only level twelve, yet if someone saw them, they’d think his level was three times that.

But most of all, it was the fact he had so many avenues of power to pursue that made him so confident.

Advancing his Core, working on his body, mastering Chants for Fire and working towards doing the same with Wind, and finally, trying to find the Lightning hiding somewhere inside him.

If Rowan had half a year of peace, there was no telling how much he could grow.

He shook his head, pushing that thought away.

No tempting Fate.

They continued their walk. The clouds started closing in, the familiar weather of the Heights once again making itself known. It didn’t take long for the rain to start. Rowan pulled his coat tighter, raising his hood.

Kai hunkered down on his shoulder.

“So, we’re going to need an alias when we get there,” Rowan said, savoring the feeling of the smooth cobbled road beneath his boots and the fact there were no monsters in sight. It was a massive improvement over the last week they spent in the uncharted Wilds, and even the rain couldn’t dampen his mood.

Kai tilted his head to the side, clicking his beak twice.

“And I think I have an idea,” Rowan said, scratching his feathers. “It’s a little bit out there, but it should work.”

There was no way he’d be able to hide his capabilities. The Storm Crown wasn’t Litwick. He might have been able to avoid questions there, but as soon as he casts a single spell anyone with eyes would know how capable Rowan truly was. So instead of hiding, he was going to lean into it.

“I’m going to tell people I’m the apprentice of Archmage Rylan.”

Kai pecked his hand.

“Hey!” Rowan yelped. “I don’t really have that many other options, okay?”

He sighed. “Rylan took on an apprentice a few years ago and disappeared, so no one is going to be able to confirm or deny it. And with my current level of power, it should be believable enough.”

That would hopefully work as a shield against the stronger citizens of the Crown. There were few people willing to risk the ire of an Archmage, and messing with one's apprentice was a surefire way to get it.

“Archmage Rylan is known to send his apprentices out into the world to gather life experience, and it’s not like they can just go and ask him if I’m spouting bullshit,” Rowan said, continuing his walk down the cobbled road.

Kai nuzzled against his cheek, letting out a slightly disinterested trill and getting comfortable.

It didn’t take him long to fall asleep, and even though Rowan would have appreciated the company, he decided not to wake him. Instead, he let his mind wander, enjoying the peace and quiet as the patter of rain lulled him into a sense of calm.

As the sun set on the Stormspire Heights, Rowan saw lights in the distance.

“Thank the gods,” he muttered, quickening his steps.

[Heat] kept the chill away, but there was a difference between being warm and spending a night in a cozy bed with a nice fire burning.

Kai stirred to life, shaking himself awake just as the tavern came into focus through the rain.

Nestled on the side of the road, the building looked sturdy and weather-worn, its stone walls darkened by years of storms and its roof steep enough to shed even the heaviest snowfall. As they got closer, Rowan read the wooden sign that swayed gently in the wind, creaking with each gust.

The Resting Stag, it read, the faded image of an antlered beast visible beneath the peeling paint.

A soft glow spilled from the windows onto the cobblestone road, casting pale circles of light that barely held back the surrounding darkness.

“Looks… cozy?” Rowan muttered, yet something pricked at the back of his mind.

Kai trilled softly, his feather ruffling.

“Well we’re not just going to walk past,” he said. “This is the first building we’ve seen in a week, and I’m getting tired of being wet.”

Still, he hesitated for a moment longer before opening the door and stepping inside.

The first thing that hit him was the silence.

The common room wasn’t anywhere near empty. Merchants, travelers, adventurers, all of them sat around wooden tables with half-eaten meals in front of them. Yet their eyes were downcast and their faces shadowed in the dim firelight. The air was thick and heavy, carrying the scent of damp stone and something faintly metallic hiding beneath the aroma of cooked meat.

None of them turned to look at Rowan as he walked in.

He glanced at the bar, a tall man with hollow cheeks standing behind it, polishing a glass with slow, deliberate movements. His eyes flicked up to Rowan, holding his gaze for a second too long before offering a thin-lipped smile.

“Welcome,” the barkeep said, his voice smooth and practiced. “Rough weather, isn’t it?” He laughed a little bit too loud. “But then again, when is it not?”

Rowan nodded, scanning the people in the room as he walked up to the bar.

It was a smattering of people at low to high Silver, with a few peak Iron’s thrown into the mix. Rowan focused on the barkeep.

His smile widened, though it didn’t reach his eyes. “What can I get for you?” he asked. “A room for the night? A warm stew?”

Rowan glanced around the room again as he sat on an empty chair by the bar. The fire burned low, the embers crackling. The other patrons didn’t speak, barely moving as they nursed their drinks. One man in the corner seemed to be watching him, his head tilted at an odd angle, but when Rowan turned to look, the man quickly lowered his gaze.

“Just a room is fine,” he answered, feeling more than a bit on edge. “How much?”

“Two silver.”

Rowan fished out the coins and placed them on the wooden counter. The barkeep slid them over, carefully putting them in his pocket.

Kai stayed on Rowan’s shoulder, unusually still. His sharp eyes scanned the room, his feather puffing as if sensing something Rowan couldn’t see.

Now, Rowan wasn’t an idiot. There was definitely something weird going on here, and he was curious to find out what. He leaned back in his chair, his gaze sweeping the room again. This time, he noticed the walls—old, scratched-out carvings etched into the stone, faded but still visible in the firelight.

Symbols. Wardings.

Rowan’s breath slowed, his pulse quickening.

He recognized those marks, and the fact they were scratched-out screamed danger.

Kai tensed on his shoulder.

Rowan met the barkeep's gaze. The man smiled again—too wide this time, his teeth just a little too sharp.

“We haven’t been getting many travelers as of late. Especially mages like yourself,” he said softly, still polishing the glass. “It’s always such a treat when someone decides to… stay with us.”

[Iron Will] activated, Rowan’s body instinctively preparing for a fight.

Then, the fire flickered, the shadows lengthening along the walls like grasping fingers.

Rowan rose slowly, his fingers twitching, aching to cast. “I have no idea what’s going on here, but I’m pretty damn sure it’s not good.”

The barkeep didn’t move from his spot. The other patrons turned in unison, their eyes locking onto Rowan. For a moment, the only sound was the crackling of the fire. Then, the door to the tavern swung shut with a loud thud.

“Great.” Rowan sighed, mana already moving through his channels.

If this situation had happened just a week before, the chances of him getting out alive would have been slim to none. There were more than a dozen Silver-rank warriors here, and it only took a single wayward thrust to spell his end. But with the changes [Storm Touched] had wrought upon his body, the prevailing emotion Rowan felt was excitement.

A crack drew his attention back to the bar, and what he saw changed that excitement into a deep, scorching anger.

The barkeep shuddered, his frame convulsing as something beneath his skin shifted. Bones snapped, twisting into new angles, his limbs stretching unnaturally. The thin, too-wide smile remained as his jaw elongated, sharp teeth pushing past human lips. Then, from his skull, two curved horns burst forth—scarred, ridged, and unmistakably familiar.

For the first time since that fateful day in Eiseylth, Rowan laid eyes on a Demon.
