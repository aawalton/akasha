Chapter 76 - Picturesque

The streets of the city still smelled faintly of ash.

Ezra walked with his hood up, the fabric pressed close to his damp forehead. Morning had only just broken, but already the air was heavy with heat, thick with the lingering weight of summer storms. Around him, both members of the public, and the academy's students moved in groups, rushing around with little care for their surroundings, and even less for him.

He preferred it that way.

Today, he was seeing everything as if for the last time.

The cracked cobblestones beneath his boots, once silver-veined but now dulled by years of footfall and soot, seemed to shimmer in the pale light of dawn. Morning mist clung to the edges of the wrought-iron fences that framed the dormitory grounds, each droplet catching the sun like a dewdrop diamond. The city’s sharp spires stabbed into the sky, casting long shadows down the backstreet boys alleys where ivy climbed the brick walls in crooked lines.

Ezra passed the old stone bench where he and Marcel used to skip training to share a bottle of cheap liquor. The hollow beneath the city’s east tower still whistled faintly with the wind, it used to carry the voices of the mayor, fanatics, and other obnoxious individuals, swirling their lessons into the air like forgotten prayers. The smell of damp parchment drifted from a vendor’s tent, the ink barely dry on the newest batch of newpapers. Across from it, a bakery had begun early; the scent of cinnamon and warm bread wafted into the road, so thick and rich it nearly dragged him off course.

Every corner, every scent, every glint of morning sun was something he knew he would never see again.

He paused at the edge of the forge district.

Marcel’s presence in his mind pulsed with mild unease, neither fully dormant nor active, like a sleeping beast with one eye half-open. Ezra kept his jaw clenched as he approached the narrow street leading to the weaponsmith’s forge. The scent of coal and hot metal drifted on the breeze before the first glint of the smithy came into view. The doorway was cracked open. Light from the hearth flickered inside like the glow of a beast’s maw.

As he stepped inside, a familiar clang rang out.

“Ezra?”

The weaponsmith looked up from behind the counter, a hammer still in one gloved hand. Her soot-streaked face was drawn in surprise, her red braid tied back and speckled with black dust. She blinked once, then frowned.

“You’re back.” Her eyes narrowed. “Where’s Marcel?”

Ezra hesitated.

There was a flare of discomfort in his mind, Marcel stirring, his awareness sharpening instantly.

Let me handle this, Marcel said inside Ezra’s head.

Ezra didn't respond aloud. He let his breathing settle and loosened the tension in his shoulders as the subtle shift began. His fingers twitched. His posture changed. When his eyes opened again, they held a different focus.

Marcel spoke through Ezra’s lips, his voice softer, with a familiar confidence laced in. “He's alright, don’t worry.”

Rikka’s brow furrowed. “What’s that supposed to mean?”

Marcel, still in Ezra’s body, smiled, raising both hands in mock surrender. “It’s not like I've done anything! He’s just out on a hunt. Decided to separate from him for a while. Shouldn’t be dangerous, he’ll cope.”

The weaponsmith stepped out from behind the counter slowly, her boots scuffing against the blackened floor. Her expression was unreadable, arms crossed beneath her leather apron. “There've been rumours going around about you two.”

“Really now...” Marcel sighed. “All good things I'd hope.”

She studied his face, Ezra’s face, and the smile wavered slightly under her scrutiny. Then, slowly, she exhaled and nodded.

“You’re an idiot,” she muttered.

“That’s fair.”

The words hung between them, charged with an uncoordinated tension. Then, she turned sharply on her heel, walking toward the cluttered workbench near the forge. She rummaged through a pile of cloth and oiled leather before pulling something free, a dagger, short-bladed and familiar, the twin to the one Ezra favoured before he’d… before they’d become one.

“Thought you might not be coming back for this. I had kept it aside for you.”

The Weaponsmith held it up, the weapon catching the light.

“Didn’t let anyone buy it. Figured if you ever did come back, you’d want it.”

Marcel stepped forward, reverence in every movement as he reached for it.

When his fingers closed around the hilt, something shifted inside him, like a memory stirring, a phantom limb returning. The blade hummed faintly in his palm, as if recognizing its rightful owner.

“It’s perfect,” Marcel said, voice almost a whisper.

The Weaponsmith nodded, not meeting his eyes. “Take it. No charge.”

Marcel blinked, confusedly.

“You sure?” he asked.

She looked up, brushing a streak of hair from her cheek with the back of her glove. “Ezra, I made it as a set, might as well keep it that way. It’s yours. I don’t need the money.”

Something in her tone made Marcel pause.

“I—thanks.”

She waved a hand like she was brushing away smoke. “Just try not to die.

“No promises.”

A smile, thin and fleeting, crossed her face.

“Then go. I’ve got to finish a sword for this impatient noble brat.”

Marcel nodded, letting Ezra slowly return to control as he stepped out into the morning light. The sun had risen a little higher now, painting the edges of the academy’s spires in gold. The dagger weighed comfortably in his hand.

He turned his gaze back one final time.

The city’s rooftops shimmered in the sun, their copper tiles glowing like dragon scales. Banners fluttered atop the citadel, their colours dulled from long months of exposure. In the distance, a bell tolled, its deep hum resonating through his chest like a heartbeat. Somewhere, far off, laughter rose from the student gardens.

It all felt distant. And heartbreaking.

Inside, Marcel’s voice returned, quieter than before.

She still thinks I’m alive out there somewhere.

Ezra adjusted the blade into its sheath beneath his cloak. You kind of are.

Not in any way she’d understand.

They walked in silence for a moment, the hum of the city around them, the pulse of something shared beneath Ezra’s skin.

“You miss it?” Ezra asked aloud.

What?

“Being… yourself.”

There was a long pause.

No doubt.

Ezra didn’t push further. He didn’t need to.

Their minds intertwined more seamlessly than they had weeks ago when they’d first tried the meld, the jagged friction between them softened by shared pain and necessity. Still, there were lines neither crossed. Feelings they didn’t name. Truths they hadn’t yet faced.

“I’ll fix this,” Ezra said after a while, quiet and certain.

I know you will, Marcel answered. But until then… don’t forget I’m here. Even if it's weird as hell.

Ezra cracked a faint smile.

“It is weird as hell.”

They walked on.

The dagger at his side felt like the closing of a loop. A thread of the past returning to the present, reforged. And he’d find a way to make this right, with Marcel at his side—or rather, within.

They’d do it together, and they were willing to do whatever it takes.

Kaelin woke with a violent gasp, water spilling from her lips as she choked on the rain that had been pouring into her mouth. Her chest convulsed, breath ragged and shallow. She coughed again, rolling to her side, fingers sinking into slick mud. Rain lashed down on her in heavy sheets, soaking her to the bone.

She blinked through the haze, vision swimming. Everything was dark, blurry edges, water in her eyes, pain like a spike behind her forehead. Her head throbbed, and every beat of her pulse sent another wave of nausea through her.

Slowly, shakily, she sat up.

The stone beneath her hands wasn’t random. It was carved. Patterned.

She looked around, heart stuttering.

She was in the courtyard. The large one, the one near the old library and the outdoor gym.

The one where Aric had—

She hesitated.

Had what?

The memories slipped like water through her fingers. She clutched at pieces: his voice, cold and low. A glint of something silver. The way the sky had darkened. Was it a storm?

She glanced at her arms. No wounds. No blood. Just mud, bruises, and the rain.

Had she dreamt it?

The courtyard looked untouched. No scorch marks, no ruins, no sign of a fight.

Maybe it had all been in her head.

She pressed her palms against her eyes, trying to force the headache away. Instead, flashes came. Names.

Ezra.

Marcel.

Two daggers. Both with straight hilts and blades curved like a kris.

A symbol, a snake swallowing its own tail.

The ouroboros.

The very same one she had seen in the ruin.

She didn't know what any of it meant, how they each connected.

It felt like it had happened in another life. A dream inside another dream.

Her breathing slowed, though her chest still ached. No one was around. No professors. No students. Just darkness and rain. The buildings surrounding her loomed like silent sentinels, their windows black. The only sound was the steady, punishing rhythm of the storm.

She stood on trembling legs, wobbling slightly, arms wrapped tight around herself. Her uniform clung to her skin, heavy and cold. The rain dripped from her chin, her hair, the tips of her fingers.

A gust of wind howled through the empty courtyard, and she turned sharply, but saw nothing.

No Aric.

No Kana.

No one.

Kaelin swallowed hard.

She was alone.

She took one more breath and stepped forward.

Not knowing what had just happened.

But she was alive.

And that had to count for something...

Right?
