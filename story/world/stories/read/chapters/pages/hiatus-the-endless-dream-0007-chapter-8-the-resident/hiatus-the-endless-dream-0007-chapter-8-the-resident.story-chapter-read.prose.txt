When I fully awoke, it was still bright outside. My throat wasn't parched at all. I went to raise my arm but was met with nothing. Not resistance, but the complete absence of movement. I frowned before the memories of the statue came crashing back.

I sprawled out, head whipping from one spot to another as I took in my surroundings. I was lying in a small glade. The running water I had heard before was nearby, flowing next to a gazebo and a few flower gardens.

I frowned, unsure of how I got here before nausea caused me to keel over. My shoulder fucking hurt. A sharp, full ache that expanded much further than just my shoulder. I swore I could still feel pressure on a non-existent wrist. The cold stone cupped on my elbow. My vision ebbed, and I failed to keep a cry of pain in as tears welled in my eyes.

By the time I was able to look around again, I had locked eyes with the woman I had seen right before passing out.

So much for second impressions.

There was a tension in the air as the blonde-haired woman hesitated. She sat under a tree, a stone tablet next to her, and eyed me worriedly. Her mouth was slightly ajar, and I could tell she was thinking about what to say as I followed her eyes.

"It may be best to rest. You are still recovering," is what the woman went with, and I was all too keen to follow those directions specifically.

After a moment to catch my breath, I asked the burning question. "Is this your Instance?"

"Ah?" She let out a surprised sound before realization dawned on her, and she blushed lightly.

"No. Though the fact you could mistake me for a God is flattering. I'm just a Resident of this place."

My frown and confusion could've split my face. I hadn't even known that was possible. I got the impression that I had no idea what was happening. Where could I even start? Probably with my win condition for leaving this place. The faster I could win, the faster I could leave. I could always just die, but I didn't know what the Lose condition consequence meant, and I didn't want to risk it. Escape first. Mental breakdown later.

"The mourning circle. What can you tell me about it?" my voice came out shaky with so much desperation that I didn't have the energy to hide.

She looked at me with pity, and I struggled not to bite my tongue. "This entire place is in the Mourning Circle. A self-looping garden. Each statue tells a story of mourning. You can leave once you've placated the spirit within one of them."

I laughed weakly. How dumb of me. Did I really just saw my arm off for nothing? All I had to do was placate the statue right when I entered the Instance. I brought my knees to my chest.

I pushed the self-deprecation to the side. I had made the best decision for the information I had. There was no way for me to know. Moreover, I was given the [Bad Start] Restriction. It wouldn't have been a bad start if the spirit inside that statue was easy to placate.

I was at a loss. It was hard to think, much less hold a coherent conversation. I had so many questions, but right now, I just wanted to go home. To lie down in my bed and go to sleep. Except I couldn't sleep. I couldn't rest because every time I tried, I'd come back to this hell.

I didn't yell, didn't scream. My fists didn't even clench. There was so little point.

"Do visitors usually appear held by a statue?" I eventually asked, half joking.

"No. Seeing you like that. Well. It was quite the shock." She tried her best to match my humorous tone, but it just didn't land.

I looked at the stump of my arm and found that it wasn't bleeding; that skin had already grown around it as if it had healed years ago.

"You do this?" I asked

She nodded.

"Thanks" is all I managed. I really wanted to just curl up in a ball and die, but I couldn't. If I stopped moving for even a second, I would never start again.

"If you don't mind me asking," the woman started, "why are you here? This world doesn't get too many Visitors."

Oh, the implications of that. I simply shrugged, "Wish I knew. I was dragged here from my world."

It was now the woman's turn to frown deeply as thoughts clashed behind her eyes. "That's odd. Our God would not resort to something so cruel as kidnapping. So why then–" Her eyes narrowed at me in suspicion.

I raised my one remaining arm as I failed to stamp down my frustration.

"I don't know! Fuck. Okay? I have no god damn clue. I don't know anything that's happening. I don't know where I am, I don't know what an Instance is, The Endless Dream is a complete mystery to me. All I know is I was visiting my brother's grave and then I was forced here."

The woman seemed taken aback at my outburst, and she looked away for a long time. "I'm sorry."

My heart ached. How frustrating it was to almost break down in front of this stranger over two simple words.

I composed myself, "It's alright." I collected myself further. I needed information. "You said world, but then this is also an Instance. How does that work?"

"Your world does not have Instances?" She gave me a sad smile as if she was looking at someone destined for hell. "Instances are holy places, locations touched by the Gods. They offer trials and tribulations in exchange for strengthening our souls. They are locations in the world."

I actually laughed. I shouldn't have, but I couldn't help myself. Yeah, fat chance. Holy? I hadn't been in many Instances, but I saw their bleed-over. A diner where the owner cooked his own body was the furthest thing from Holy I could imagine.

The woman seemed flustered at my laugh, strands of indignation seeping into her tone. "It is hardly a laughing matter. You are on sacred grounds."

My laughter finally faded, and it was naturally my turn to apologize. I didn't.

I would synthesize all the information into a theory later, but stripping away the propaganda from the woman's words, all I could really glean was that there were other worlds, and they were different from Instances.

The thought of this all being an astral projection of my soul or something caused another wave of laughter.

"Are you quite done?" the woman practically hissed at me with a light, almost joking tone, and I nodded through shaky breaths.

"Yes," she stared at me expectantly, but I just continued, "are you challenging this Instance then? If so, we could team up."

She shook her head, "I can't. I'm not a Visitor."

I frowned but nodded slowly, "Then why are you here?"

"I'm a Resident visiting my cousin's statue. Since I'm not a Visitor, I'm free to come and go without fear for my safety."

Ah. Yeah, I supposed that made sense. I don't know how she healed my arm then, but I didn't really care. If I understood her right, this was another world, and that meant the rules were likely completely different.

"Well, thanks anyways. My name's Dorian. You are?"

"Talin," she smiled at me as if she had never had a day of sadness in her life, "No need to thank me. It was the will of my God."

I controlled a snort, which came easier to me because something about the woman was deeply unsettling to me. I was in shock and focused on surviving this Instance. This woman just hadn't stopped smiling since I had met her, and I was not being charming.

"Do you mind showing me around? This place is … beautiful" in a horrifying type of way, but I didn't say that last part. It felt like everything was trying to trick me into relaxing, and more than once, I felt the desire to smile despite having just brutalized my arm.

The woman—Talin, considered it for a moment before nodding, "If you are not in a rush. I still must visit my cousin's statue."

I waved a dismissive hand before scowling and realizing the arm I raised was the wrong one. Phantom pain shot through a forearm and fingertips that weren't there as the neural pathways screamed that the limb should be in pain despite its absence. "Yeah, that's fine," I said through grit teeth.

The more we walked, the more thankful I grew for Talin's healing. I was still in pain, and my gait shifted. The lack of weight was disorienting and now every small motion I would've made before unconsciously was laid bare and obvious now that I couldn't move it at all. At least I was alive. I tried not to think about it anymore—after I left the Instance, I'd have my arm back—it wasn't something I had to learn to live with.

We reached the statue of Talin's cousin after a silent thirty-minute walk. I recognized some of the surroundings, at the very least, the sound of far-off running water. A morbid curiosity filled me. Should I go get my arm? Surely not. Looking at it wouldn't hurt. I opened my mouth to ask but saw Talin standing silently by a statue of a young boy, not more than 16. His stone visage was lying down, face contorted in pain with marble-etched tears growing from its eyes. His chest was open. More ripped than the word made it sound, with the majority of his ribs hanging in the air without support. Stone flesh and organs had erupted from the boy's body and hung in the air.

I turned away, looking towards Talin. The sight of her made me flinch back. She was smiling. She was happy. Still, after seeing the sight of her cousin being vivisected she looked on like nothing was wrong in the world.

"Talin?" my voice came out hesitantly. She turned to me.

"Yes?" her honeyed words drew me in.

"Are you okay?" I asked, desperately hoping the answer would be no.

"Why, of course! I wish he wasn't a heretic," she said, turning back to the statue. "But I'm happy he's here."

Psycho. This woman was crazy. Actively malicious and liable to kill me. It was time to leave. I could imagine her trying to force me to interact with her cousin's statue and placate the spirit trapped there. I could also imagine the thought never having crossed her mind, and I didn't know which one was worse.

"I'm going to give you space and look around a little bit. Gonna see what's over those mountains."

She frowned for a brief moment and shook her head. "That's ill-advised."

I felt cold sweat running down my back, and my heart rate picked up. I glanced towards the tree line. Maybe I could kill her if we were closer, but she had healed my arm without making a fuss. I didn't know what else she could do, and I didn't want to find out. So running was the only option. If she chased me, maybe I'd be able to push her into one of the sappy trees. It seemed viscous enough to trap her, at least for a moment.

"Why would that be?" I took a casual step closer to the edge of the clearing.

"There's nothing out there."

That got my attention, but I still took another step. "You're going to have to be a bit more specific."

"Hmm, I don't know how to put it another way. There's just nothing." Her eyes quirked up as she saw me inching my way further and further, but she mentioned nothing about it. It made me stop moving.

"Like void or a hole or–"

She cut me off, "You misunderstand. There's nothing there. No color, no absence of color, nothing beyond, no boundary, just nothing."

What?

But before I could ask any more questions, static danced in my eyes and formed words that downloaded the information directly into my mind.

[Due to unforeseen circumstances, your journey into: Garden of Stone has been temporarily halted. You will be ejected from the instance for the next 24 hours while judgment is delivered. You will be made aware of any changes that occur.]

"What?" I shouted, in equal parts surprise and frustration at how I was being yanked around. I was already feeling a static building in my skull, the sign that I was about to be moved. "What's going on?" I hissed out, wishing I could have a physical representation of the System to spew venom at. Ultimately, it didn't matter. I was shunted out of the Instance and awoke still kneeling next to my brother's grave.
