The elf’s face contorted multiple times, running through a whole gamut of emotions in the span of just a handful of seconds. Beth was silently amused, comparing all the other elves she knew, which was quite a few at this point, and their stoic and taciturn natures in stark contrast to this elf, one who wore his emotions on his sleeve and struggled to act appropriately. She was watching that struggle play out in real time, hoping that he lost this miniature battle with himself, but it looked like he was just a cut above the last Ascended she had engaged in a little bout of fisticuffs.

Meanwhile, Beth’s group was working like a well-oiled machine at this point, grinding through the dungeon at a rapid, but assured, pace. They were very good at every part of the instance by now, even with little variations that such instances and dungeons could have. A level five hundred dungeon could have a lot more variance, though that was typically restricted to the layout and size, not to any other factors. They had, however, done the spiked version enough they they had encountered plenty of oddities. The worst they had had so far was a run where it was storming, which would have actually been a bit of a relief, but the storm had caused all the beasts to have some kind of lightning element added to them and their attacks, many of them still with fire aspects as well. On top of that, it wasn’t raining water, but some kind of sulfur-based acid that ate away at the group’s equipment and flesh, proving very annoying to have to constantly heal through.

The run they found themselves in now was normal, at least at first, but their familiarity with the instance led them to discover a key difference very quickly. Normally, they would have to be constantly worrying about the wurm beasts attacking them; even though they were few in number, they were a real pain in the ass to kill, and so they were a constant annoyance for the first half or more of the clear. It became clear that this instance was something different, and likely very special, when they weren’t attacked by a wurm even after nearly two hours had passed, which would be a record for any time the instance had wurms, which had been every run so far. This time, they were pretty sure there weren’t any of the burrowing beasts at all, as the team had become quite good at sensing the vibrations in the ground and air that the wurms made, though the disturbance in the air was so minute Beth was the only one who could sense it when the wurm wasn’t literally right on top of them, and that was only using concepts from her mastery of space to detect the changes in air pressure.

It took a long time, surprisingly, for the change to really make itself known, but when it did was when they knew this instance in particular was going to be a very special run of the dungeon. Sometimes, environmental changes, things like the storm that added lightning, were massive deals that really changed the whole context of the dungeon, which included massively changing the rewards structure. Other times, it was inherent changes in the beasts or the enemies, whether in type or number that did it. Sometimes, however, it was a change to everything within the dungeon, but a change to one beast in particular, or the addition of one beast in particular, that would radically alter the experience. It was also changes like this, the thing that Beth was staring at right now, that often led to inexperienced or under-leveled team being entirely wiped out by using a spike. She was sure that it wasn’t the boss, either, because they had cleared almost half the dungeon and knew where the boss was, even if they hadn’t gotten to its location yet. No, this was not the final boss, but it would likely be a far more difficult challenge, though something that was an even greater reward, given everything that went into it. Beth was just worried that her wife might have a few qualms, but that was quickly put to rest in the next few moments.
