“Let the Heaven Reaching tournament begin!” Elder Feng announced, to the most deafening round of cheers yet. Gary and the others jumped to the empty segment of seats, which were right over the arena walls. He, Alicia, and Cog sat together.

“I wonder who we’ll fight,” Alicia said.

“I hope I go first. The waiting is killing me!” Cog said.

“Are you serious? I want to go last!” she replied.

“I just hope I don’t fight Sui Duang today,” Gary said.

“Give a warm round of applause for the four brave deacons who will be acting as referees for the tournament’s first phase,” Elder Feng said. The crowd clapped.

After the arena sands had been vacated, the four deacons moved. They used their spears to carve clear lines through the sand, splitting the arena into four equal parts. Blue energy walls shimmered around each smaller stage, separating them from the audience and each other. Two of the deacons moved away the platform Elder Feng had been standing on, then each deacon stood in one of the smaller stages.

The Elder herself jumped gracefully to a higher area of the stands demarcated by a red rope—it looked like a VIP area, with an empty throne placed prominently in the middle. Most of the seats were full; Gary recognized several Heaven Reaching Elders, along with many non-clan members.

He then spotted a hand waving from just below the VIP area—it was Chloe’s, and Asim sat next to her.

Are they eating corn? Gary thought.

“My name is Deacon Flower, and I will be announcing the pairings for today. For the first phase of the tournament, we’ll be hosting four battles at a time,” one of the deacons said—a particularly beautiful man. “As I announce the pairs, please step into the stages starting from mine and moving clockwise. Dean Stun versus Lyra Overdawn. Wang Shi versus Heili Green. Pop Ear versus Rat Shing. Pat Cute versus Olivar. Disciples, please attend.”

Of the eight names, Gary only recognized Pop Ear and Pat Cute—the charismatic feline with whom he’d interacted in the past. All eight cultivators jumped to their respective stages. Most were humble. Rat Shing, who came from the Rat family, raised his gray spear proudly to the air.

“Fight!” Deacon Flower said. Four battles erupted at once. Spears flew wide. Aura outbursts occasionally struck the energy walls, causing not a crack. The audience cheered.

Gary didn’t find any of the eight particularly impressive. He kept his eyes on the Rat family member. The fight was about equal, but the longer it dragged on, the more Rat Shing seemed agitated.

“Mouse Trap!” he yelled, bringing the spear down on his opponent with a wide, overhead motion.

“Spinach Burst!” Pop Ear shouted, slashing up. The spears met in mid-air, and after a tense collision, Rat Shing was pushed back. His spear flew up. Pop Ear’s green spear struck him in the face, sending him flying into a wall. When the sand cleared, Rat Shing was unconscious, bleeding heavily from the nose. The deacon crossed his arms in the air. Pop Ear pumped a fist. The audience cheered.

“Well done!” a voice came from the disciple seats, and Gary spotted Caramel cheering heavily. It had been a close match. Had the Muscle Sap made the difference?

Was Gary indirectly responsible for that victory?

When all battles were done, the deacons announced the names of the winners, who jumped back into their seats. The next round of battles held no one important. On the third round, however, Gary heard a familiar name.

“Gary Steel versus Rat Himake. Disciples, please attend.”

“Hey, that’s you!” Cog said. “Go get them!”

“Finally! Let’s do this, Gary!” Goldie said.

Gary stood up and jumped to the corresponding arena. The attention of thousands of people weighed on him. Opposite him landed a man with flowing blond hair, a yellow spear, and a wicked grin. “Fate works in mysterious ways,” he said. “We meet again, Gary Steel!”

“Who exactly are you?” Gary asked.

The person’s face fell. “It’s me, Rat Himake! You made fun of me outside the Valley of Broken Spears nine days ago. Now, you’ll pay!”

“Oh, yeah.”

“Don’t pretend like you don’t remember me!”

“I honestly forgot about you. I have bigger problems.”

“You’ll pay for your arrogance, Gary Steel!”

“Fight!” the deacon said, and Rat Himake charged. His spear glowed yellow. Gary adopted a stance, ready to thrust, and suddenly realized he’d never used the black lightning against a person before. The only exception was Sui Duang back during the expedition, but it wasn’t like he’d ever struck the man.

With his opponent bearing down, Gary realized he didn’t know how destructive his vibration was. He could easily pierce holes in trees. What if he accidentally killed this man?

Gary’s hesitation translated into a delay. Instead of attacking, he blocked Rat Himake’s strike and was blown away. The crowd cheered. “Forget that, Gary Steel!” the opponent cried out, charging again.

Gary slid to his feet. Rat Himake wasn’t too weak, thankfully. He could survive. Charging his spear to half capacity, he met his opponent head-on. Black lightning clashed against what resembled…wheat? The two auras met in front of the speartips, grinding against each other until Rat Himake emerged victorious. Gary was once against pushed back, but he only slid a little this time.

“It’s over!” Rat Himake exclaimed.

Gary observed his opponent. He wasn’t unscathed. Some of Gary’s black lightning had seeped into his body, and he was panting. A few strands of his flowing golden hair floated in the air. He didn’t seem particularly troubled, however, which Gary took as a sign that he could use more power.

“The Mill Spills!” Rat Himake exclaimed. He thrust at the air, each thrust releasing a yellow aura projectile towards Gary. They were as dense as a wall. If Gary faced this move a week ago, he’d have quite some trouble dodging everything.

He span his spear, infusing it with aura, creating a spinning aura field in the projectiles’ way. They all flew harmlessly to the side.

I did it! he exclaimed inwardly.

“Block this. Wheat Grows From The Dirt!” Rat Himake screamed, charging with a thrust. At the last moment, he changed it into a thrust from below. The thrust had been a decent feint, which Gary found pretty stupid since he’d literally called out the direction of his attack in its name. He easily parried the blow and sent Rat Himake flying. He’d struck him properly this time, using a good part of his power. He held his breath, hoping he hadn’t accidentally killed the man.

Rat Himake bounced thrice on the ground. He stayed still. For a moment. Then, he stood, clasping his ribs. Gary heaved a sigh of relief—it seemed like his power wasn’t too destructive, so he could use it to fight freely. He wouldn’t accidentally kill anyone.

As he looked at Rat Himake, however, something weird happened. Gary’s relief evaporated. His jaw hung open.

“You got in a lucky shot, Gary Steel, but prepare for retribution! I will show you the might of a Rat clansman! I will— Hmm?” Rat Himake said, stopping mid-sentence. He frowned at Gary’s hanging jaw. He glanced at the deacon, who held a similarly stunned expression. The audience had grown oddly quiet.

“What’s wrong?” he asked, checking himself for hidden injuries but finding none.

“Are they for real?” a voice came from the audience.

“Do you see that? Is it fake? It has to be fake,” another voice asked.

“Oh my god!” a woman squealed, and suddenly the entire audience stand erupted with peals of laughter.

Gary scratched his head. “I’m sorry…” he said.

Rat Himake looked around and frowned. “What are you—” He cut himself off mid-sentence. His mouth widened. “No,” he muttered.

Golden locks fluttered in the air around him. Too many of them. He looked at the sand below his feet to find it covered in golden hair. “No!” he screamed again.

Gary also struggled to believe his eyes. His attack hadn’t injured Rat Himake more than expected. However, the moment he stood up, all his hair had fallen off. Not burned, not crisped, just fallen. His head, once covered in flowing golden hair, was now completely bald. Even his eyebrows had fallen off, and so had the hair on his arms and legs.

Rat Himake was now completely and utterly hairless.

“No freaking way,” Gary muttered.

Rat Himake took a moment to digest the scale of his disaster. His face grew so red it resembled a tomato. “I’LL FUCKING MAIM YOU, GARY STEEL!”

“I can’t believe it, he went completely bald!”

“What a sinister attack!”

“Way to go, Gary Steel! You’re my new favorite!”

The audience was sprouting exclamations of wonder, while many held their bellies and couldn’t stop laughing.

Rat Himake leaped into the air, holding his spear with both hands, ready to bring it down on Gary’s head with all his strength. A sun appeared on its tip—though the real sun was the blinding lights on the ceiling behind him. “WHEATHER!” he screamed, falling like a hairless comet.

“Black Thrust!” Gary said. Now that he wasn’t afraid of accidentally killing anyone, he could strike with his full power. He stabbed up. Black lightning lit up the runes on his spear shaft, then gathered in its head. His thrust met Rat Himake’s full-blown overhead swing. Spears and aura clashed in mid-air, unleashing a shockwave at the point of impact. Gary’s lightning proved superior. Rat Himake’s momentum was reversed. His power was extinguished, his spear flew out of his hands, and his perfectly hairless body flew straight up until it hit the ceiling of the energy walls, twenty feet off the ground. The deacon caught him before he could reach the ground.

“Winner, Gary Steel!” he announced. The crowd went wild.

“Don’t you dare lose, Gary Steel!”

“Now these are real stakes!”

“Give us a good show!”

He stood there, washed in their cheers, really not knowing how to respond. He hadn’t planned for this. Were all his opponents going to become bald?

“Did you know this would happen, Goldie?” he asked.

“They love it! You should do it more!” she replied.

“I don’t even know how I did that.”

“It’s black lightning, dummy. The body instinctively protects the important bits, but not hair follicles.”

“Are you saying this is a real thing? I can’t stop it?”

“Yep. If you strike people with black lightning, they become bald.”

He sighed, unable to form words, the nonsensical cheers still washing over him.

“I call foul!” a voice cut through the clamor. Rat Himake had woken up, a line of blood dripping from his mouth as he pointed a vexed finger at Gary. “He crippled me! He crippled me on purpose! Elders, eliminate him and expel him from the clan at once!”

“Careful with the stress, you’ll lose your hair,” someone in the audience said, leading to widespread laughter. Rat Himake turned even redder.

In the VIP stands, Elder Feng stood up. “Losing your hair is not crippling, disciple. Would you complain if an attack clipped your toenails? Instead of obsessing about your appearance, focus on becoming a stronger cultivator.”

The blood got to Rat Himake’s perfectly round head. “This is absurd! I refuse to accept this! He did this to humiliate me, he—”

A snort came from elsewhere in the VIP stands. The moment it sounded, it resounded powerfully across the arena, and the audience stopped laughing except for a random guy that had gone hysterical with laughter. Everyone else stared at an Elder with a long, blond beard that Gary hadn’t seen before.

“You are a disgrace,” the Elder said, still sitting. “Forfeit the tournament and go repent at once. You must meditate in seclusion until your hair grows back.”

The Elder’s voice completely killed Rat Himake’s anger. His red face grew pale. His lips trembled. After a moment, he turned towards the Elder and bowed, his head accidentally reflecting the blinding ceiling light on the Elder.

“This disciple obeys,” he said, sounding like he could barely keep himself in check.

Elder Feng cracked a smile. “Elder Rat is wise,” she said, then sat back down. After a moment of silence, someone in the audience whistled, and the heckling returned at full force.

Gary jumped back to his seat. Rat Himake exited the arena directly, finally sparing his Elder from the ray of light he was accidentally creating.

“That was literally a headlight!” the guy who’d been laughing hysterically yelled, breaking down again.

“What was that?” Alicia asked.

“The funniest shit I’ve seen in my life,” Cog said.

“I honestly had no idea that would happen,” Gary said.

All the surrounding disciples watched him with a mix of amusement, disbelief, and fear.

“Your revenge runs deeper than I ever dared to imagine, comrade,” Caramel said, approaching to shake Gary’s hand. “That was superb. I hope you meet family cultivators every step of the way.”

“I hope I wake up and this was all a dream,” Gary replied.

As the disturbance died down, Deacon Flower announced the next pairings. For three rounds of pairings, no one important appeared. On the fourth, however, Gary sat up straight.

“Sui Duang versus Caramel Strawberry. Disciples, please attend!”
