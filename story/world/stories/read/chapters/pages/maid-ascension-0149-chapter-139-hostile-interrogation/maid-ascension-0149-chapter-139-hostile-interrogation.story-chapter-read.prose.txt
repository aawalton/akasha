Lira stayed beside Lyth at the front gate while the footmen carried the coffin up the steps. When the procession passed into the gardens and made its way to the main hall, she followed the royal family, since the young prince refused to let go of her sleeve.

That was how she ended up close enough to see King Argon stop at the top of the stairs and beckon to a man waiting by the wall. He was lean and short, with sandy hair, somewhere in his thirties, and wore a pristine green tunic rather than armour.

The man crossed the floor at a half run before dropping to one knee in front of the king.

“Sir Garrick,” the king said quietly, but the hall had gone silent enough that every word carried. “You are the fastest rider I have. Take Thunder and go to my son Aric in the Holtarry Mountains. Tell him that his brother is dead and that he is to abort his expedition and return to the capital at once. Do not stop for anything.”

“As you command, Your Majesty.” Garrick bowed his head and then left at a run.

Lira watched him disappear through the gate while she wondered how Aric would take the news. The timing was terrible for him. He had been looking forward to this expedition all month, and now he was going to have to abort it. Add to that the fact that he now had a brother to bury and a responsibility to a throne that Lira suspected the new heir never wanted.

Lira’s thoughts were interrupted as Estophon crossed the hall toward her, still dusty from the road, with the mage who had handled the Truth Stone during Taren’s investigation walking behind him. They stopped in front of Celene first.

“Follow me,” Estophon said in a gruff voice. Then he turned toward Lira. “You too.”

Unlike last time, the interview was not going to happen in the library, a safe space for her. Instead, they led both women past the stairs and deeper into the main hall, where the air felt strangely still.

Lyth was left behind with Henry. Unlike last time, the bodyguard wasn’t invited as a witness. Henry simply placed a hand on Lyth’s back and gently guided the boy back to his room, giving Lira one last glance before they parted.

Celene was taken through a door at the end of the corridor first, while Lira was shown into a small side room with a single bench, where four guards took positions along the walls to watch her.

The library caretaker sat with her hands folded in her lap for what felt like an hour, though she suspected it was closer to fifteen minutes. She couldn’t hear a thing coming from the room Celene had entered, probably thanks to thicker walls or sound-dampening enchantments.

When the door finally opened, Celene came out with her face pale and her mouth pressed into a line. She looked at Lira for a moment with an expression somewhere between worry and apology before the guards took her away.

The guard gestured for Lira to follow, and she stood and went in. The room was plain, with a long table, and the Truth Stone sat in the middle of it on a square of black cloth, perfectly still. The mage stood next to it, a young scribe sat in the corner nearest to her, a quill in hand ready to write everything being said, while Estophon sat at the far end with his arms crossed. The jovial man who had apologised and joked through Taren’s investigation was gone. The man watching her now had the flat, tired eyes of someone having a bad day and wanting something or someone to blame for it.

“Miss Lira Tenlan,” the mage said.

“Yes.”

“Is that your full name?”

“Yes.”

“Have you ever changed your name?”

“No, not that I know...”

“Answer only with yes or no, please.”

“Understood.”

“Did you kill Prince Kavard?”

“No.”

“Did you order the prince killed?”

“No.”

“Did you poison the prince?”

“No.”

“Did you cast any spell on the prince that led to his death?”

“No.”

“Did you cast any spell on or around the prince, such as something to improve perception or a divination spell?”

“No.”

“Do you have any magical spells imprinted?”

“No.”

The stone did not move, and the mage nodded to himself before continuing.

“For the next questions, use as few words as possible,” he ordered. “Where were you last night?”

“I fell asleep at my desk after sweeping the floor of the library.”

“Do you often spend the night alone in the library?”

“Yes.”

The mage tapped the table with his finger, interrupting the sound of the quill on paper coming from the scribe.

“Why do you hate Prince Kavard?”

“He caused the deaths of three maids, attempted to rape a friend, and threatened to kill me.”

“Why did he threaten to kill you?”

“He thought I was Aric’s mistress, and he wanted to hurt his brother for exposing the murder of the three maids.”

“Are you Aric’s mistress?”

“No.”

“Did you conspire with Aric to put him on the throne?”

“No.”

“Did you conspire with anyone against Kavard?”

“No.”

The stone moved, buzzing like a fly. Lira’s heart started thumping.

“Try again,” the mage said.

“In a way...” Lira hesitated. “I conspired with Celene to help a maid escape from him.”

“Did that conspiracy extend to hurting Kavard in any way, shape, or form?”

“I found some documents from the former Lord Head Steward’s estate that might have alluded to exposing Kavard, but I only shared them with Celene, who only shared them with the king.”

“Did you hurt Kavard in any other way?”

“No.”

“How did you help maid Maren escape?”

They already know Maren’s name... They must have gotten it from Celene.

“I hid her in the library, and then I used a rope to lower her from the wall at night.”

“You could be arrested for harbouring a fugitive,” Estophon said at last, his voice rumbling through the room.

“I thought the king had exonerated her.”

“That does not change the fact that you were willing to help her before you knew that.”

“I guess that is true.”

“Is that all you have to say for yourself?”

“That is all there is to say.” Lira shrugged. “I helped a friend in the moment, one I was sure was innocent, and I was proven right after the fact.”

Estophon kept looking at her with eyes like a storm about to break. Then he asked the last question, almost casually. “Are you happy that Kavard is dead?”

Lira hesitated, since an honest answer was not one she could say aloud, and she felt the heat of the captain’s eyes piercing her soul.

“I won’t pretend I’m sorry,” was the answer she settled on.

“Yes or no,” Estophon insisted, his hand tightening on the table.

“No.”

The stone buzzed again.

“I feel conflicted for the sake of Lyth,” she hastily added. This time, the stone didn’t move.

“We are wasting our time,” the mage sighed. “She clearly has nothing to do with it.”

“That might be true,” Estophon said, “but a commoner like her doesn’t get to act this way about the death of her prince.”

“You are aware that he actively threatened me. We even talked about it,” Lira clarified hesitantly.

“I know he slipped away from me to find you, which is why, deep down, I feel like you had something to do with it.”

“This is absurd,” Lira protested. “He was allowed to kill me, while I can’t hate him for it.”

“Yes,” Estophon said with a nod. “He was a royal.”

“I have nothing against the royals,” Lira insisted. “I respect Aric, and I would lay down my life to protect Lyth.”

Estophon looked down at the Truth Stone. It did not move. Something about her words seemed to have worked on him, because after letting out a long breath, the captain waved a hand toward the door.

“You can go.”

Lira left the main hall feeling the tension drain out of her shoulders with every step. It was finally over, and she was not in trouble.

Kavard was dead, and the world was a better place without him.

She went straight to the library, which was empty, with no one showing up after the news of the prince’s death had spread. She sat down at her desk and opened her journal, where her long to-do list spread before her.

Her eyes landed on a line written at the bottom of the page: After a stressful day, remember to relax with the help of Calligraphic Foundations by Master Jorel Velora.

She did not remember writing that down, but it did seem like a good idea. There was nothing like imitating a master’s brushstrokes to wash away a day’s stress.

She headed for the secret alcove where she had once hidden Maren, slid the false wooden wall aside from behind the tapestry, and stepped in. The book was resting on the small table next to the chair, exactly where she must have left it. She sat down and opened it, and as she did, a slip of paper fell out of the pages. Lira frowned, picked it up, and turned it over. There were only a few words written on it.

Magic Incantation:

Pika. Cognar. Luneth.

Mind. Knowledge. Purification.

The handwriting was unmistakably hers, the same hand as in her journal. A line on the list was one thing, but this...

She hesitantly sounded out the three unfamiliar words aloud.

“Pika. Cognar. Luneth.”

The fog that had been settling over her mind all day lifted all at once, and every memory that had been buried came back in a rush: her name, her father’s death, Kelroth’s involvement, her life with Pallan afterwards, her work as a forger, joining the palace, Thadwick, Hesta, and Salvek’s deaths.

Then the memory of last night surged forward: the prince, his long night ride, the brothel, the bath, the Canary spell, and the linked paper burning in the wind.

And with that, Lira Tenlan was once again Lira Velora.
