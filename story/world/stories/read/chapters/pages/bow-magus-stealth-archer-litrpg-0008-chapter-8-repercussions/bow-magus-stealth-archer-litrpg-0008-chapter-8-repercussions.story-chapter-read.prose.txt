The hunters had seen the smoke well before Rex got back. The fire had quickly spread out of control. As it turned out, goblins were smart enough to come up with a quick, albeit blunt solution to their human problem, but not smart enough to plan for what came next.

“Is this your fault, boy?” Mulvaney barked as Rex came sprinting out. Not only was he running from the direction of the fire, but he smelt like it too.

“Me? Ah, well. Not really.”

“Speak plainly, boy! Lie to me again, and I might just cut you down where you stand.”

“Well, it’s not like I started the fire!”

“Oh?”

Rex felt like shoving a boot down his throat. He’d given away too much, and there was no backtracking now.

Think this through. They know I came from the fire, but nothing else.

“Yeah, it was the goblins!” Rex swung his hands about. “They tried to ambush me. They used fire to cut off my escape route and tried to box me in. They’re not too fast, though. I managed to slip past them.”

“Is that so, you outran multiple goblins?” Mulvaney raised a suspicious brow.

“The dumb little monsters are always doing stupid shit like that,” one of the hunters said. “Seen a couple light themselves on fire once.”

“Hmph. I have my eye on you, newbie!” Mulvaney shook a finger at Rex. “But for now, I’ll choose to believe you. But if I find out you’re lying, they’ll be hell to pay.”

Rex kept his head down as they waited for the rest of the hunters. They all returned early after spotting the fire.

In the end, the fire destroyed a decent chunk of forest. They were saved thanks to an evening shower and the cool night that followed. Still, it was good hunting grounds they lost, and if not for the massive expanse of the forest, Rex probably would have ended up with a knife in his gut.

“Oh-oho! There you are! You're lucky, newbie!” Mulvaney said, interrupting Rex’s meal in the mess. “We’ve confirmed that the fire is out. If I were you, I’d be making an offering to the rain goddess. If not for that storm, who knows how long the inferno would have raged. And I can tell you, Jango would’ve wrung your neck out had that fire kept going. Even if the goblins are to blame. You need to watch your back. We still lost a good chunk of forest. Not to mention all the beasts that took flight! We’re almost certainly going to catch less game for a while. And that comes at a cost. Let’s say, two hundred gold coin.”

“Two hundred gold?” Rex blinked. “Huh? What are you talking about?”

“That’s how much you owe Credo now. And consider yourself lucky. Something like this happens again, and saving your sorry ass won’t be worth the effort. Got it?”

“Yeah, got it,” Rex groaned.

That was absolute bullshit. But could he say? He had a feeling Mulvaney would happily skin him alive if given reason. That didn’t mean he was just going to accept it, though.

Rex had had just about enough of every asshole trying to extort him, and it was seriously getting old.

“Good. And don’t fuck up again. Prove yourself and bring back some game, and you might just pay that off. But good luck if you can’t. If Jango reassigns you to guarding farmers, you’ll be an old man before Credo is happy,” Mulvaney said, and left with a half-satisfied huff.

He gritted his teeth. It was even worse knowing that they couldn’t prove anything.

Fuck these guys.

But as pissed as he was, he took a deep breath and exhaled. There was no point getting worked up. He needed to take his time and plan. An opportunity would arise, or he would make one. But he certainly wasn't going to just accept paying that debt.

At the mess hall for dinner, Rex glanced over at Freyja. She was smiling as she talked to a couple of people from her room.

“Rex? Are you there?” Andy said, waving a hand.

“Huh?”

“You can’t drop something like that on me and then zone out. Do you really owe two hundred gold? That’s a fortune.”

“I know.”

“That’s… that’s rough. And I thought my first day was bad.”

“What, you didn’t burn down half the forest?”

Andy smiled. “Nope. Just sat around.”

“Where are the other guys anyway?” Rex asked, glancing around their empty table.

“From our room? They were assigned to the caravan. They won’t be back for three days,” Andy said. “Probably out by the mill sipping whisky by now. And we’re stuck here.”

Red nodded his head, half listening. He wasn’t trying to be rude, but he remembered that he still had a pending level-up. And now that his punishment had been dealt out, it was unlikely that any more of Credo’s thugs would bother him. And Andy seemed content detailing his day of standing around doing nothing as he nodded along.

Now’s my chance.

Rex opened up his System page. He was dying to know what Proficiency options he had. Versatile Classes were a mix of different things. The main reason they were rare was that you needed to show multiple skills to the Shrine to get one, which was stupid, as it made the guardian that much stronger.

Versatile Classes weren’t inherently better than the others, and so there was little point in taking on the risk of fighting a more difficult guardian to get one, unless you had to.

Proficiencies were generally for Vigor Classes, which normally got one at level one. Although Veil Classes also usually got one at later levels. Or at least that was what the tomes back on Dorog said.

Please select one Proficiency from the following options!

Swordsmanship

Take your first step toward becoming a master swordsman. +1 Agility when wielding swords. Gain the Feat [ Always Sharp ]: Swords you wield will not dull. Although not unbreakable, your sword will not dull, even against magical surfaces.

Polearm

Learn the art of keeping your enemies at a distance. +1 Strength when wielding polearms. Gain the Feat [ Pushback Attack ] A small amount of additional power aids your attacks, helping to push back your opponents.

Archery

Shieldarm

Hold the line with your shield held high. +1 Vitality when using shields. Gain the Feat [ Immovable ]. You are a bulwark. While not entirely unmovable, you are far harder to push or pull when your feet are flat against the ground.

Rex groaned. He needed to take Archery. The bow was the only thing he knew, but Mystic Arrow sounded bad. Like the worst Feat of the bunch, by his reckoning.

“Something wrong?”

“No, nothing. Keep telling your story.”

“Right then,” Andy said. “Like I was saying, I was leaning against the tree, watching as a line of ants made their way up and down, and that was when—”

Why does archer get screwed? Mystic Arrow? Does it even do anything? I can buy arrows. What good is summoning one? And only one? Great, just great.

The thing was, even if he chose to branch out and use a melee weapon as well, Proficiencies could be upgraded. Mystic Arrow might be disappointing. But the next upgrade might not be. Not that it made the bitter taste any better.

These other Feats actually sound useful!

The shield dude practically couldn’t be moved, and while not the most interesting, having a sword that couldn’t be dulled was pretty useful. But one extra arrow a day, even for a broke boy like himself, was just lame.

Then again… I suppose I can save some coin. I guess it’s better than nothing.

There was also the extra point of Strength while wielding bows. Even if he could draw his current bow easily enough already, more Strength would make it easier and less tiresome. Also, he had read about bow artifacts with strings so hard to draw that even grown-ass men couldn’t make them budge. Some bows even required many levels of Strength.

Oh well, at least I got a Proficiency, right? That’s pretty cool.

Up until now, Rex hadn’t actually known if his Class was going to suit his bow skills. And unlocking Proiciencies at least confirmed that his Versatile Class was both martial and magic-oriented.

Rex opened his status page and took a look.

Homeworld: Dorog

Everything looked in order, but he really wanted to see if his Strength changed when holding his bow.

“Eh, not great.”

“It wasn’t any better. Didn’t even spot any game.”

“At least you got freedom instead of being stuck here. Oh, I know! This tastes just like merefox!”

Rex shook his head and took a bite. It was a little gamy, he thought.

“So what’s the deal with the mill anyway?”

“Oh, that?” Well, every three days, a caravan of supplies travels between the farmstead and the mill and distillery. It’s not actually that far, but apparently there are enough monsters out there to make the trip dangerous.”

“I see. Well, I hope they’re okay.”

“To be honest, I wish I’d gone. I dunno how many more days of standing around I can take. The moment I get some coin, I’m going to get some nice boots, I tell you.”

“Well, at least you’re settling in and don’t have to worry about debt,” Rex said and stood. “Well, I’m done.”

“Settling in? You mean not dying of boredom?” Andy said, stacking up his tray and following him.

“Something like that,” Rex replied, dropping his tray off at the kitchen and heading up to their room.

“Look, I get it. It could be worse,” Andy said. “But I don’t want to be here forever. I just want to gain a couple of levels. Get some new boots, pay off the debt for my sword, and go do something interesting.”

“Yeah, same,” Rex said and threw himself on his bunk. “Oh, that feels good.”

“So, you’re just going to hang out around here until you pay Credo back?”

“I um… I dunno. There’s a lot I gotta think about.”

“Like? I got all day to think. Hit me with your worst.”

Oh, come on, man. Just leave it.

“Like I said, I dunno.”

“Rex, I thought we were buddies. You have to have thought about your future a little.”

“Yeah, I guess. But not too deeply. I mean, obviously I want to get another level up.”

“Another? What do you mean by another?”

Uh-oh.

“Nothing. Slip of the tongue. I meant to get my first level.”

“Ah, I, err…”

“Rex?”

I don’t think he’s going to tattle on me. He seems like a nice guy. Then again, I don’t really know him. But I don’t want him to distrust me…

Rex considered whether sharing his secrets or running the risk of losing Andy’s trust was worse. The problem was, he really didn't have anybody else to confide in.

I really hope this isn’t a mistake.

“Level 2? No fucking way! You actually hit level 2? Do you get experience if you start a fire and it kills a bunch of monsters? Wait, is that your big secret? You started the fire? To be fair, that’s not a bad way to grind, actually… maybe I should—”

What’s this?

Great, just great.

No need to panic. Dorog wasn’t any better, and I got myself out of that shithole. This place won’t be any different. I just need some time to think things through.

He just needed a plan.
