Yan De sat in quiet meditation, as he had for the last sixteen years. His mind was empty and his body was a distant memory as he drew in the abundant qi of the Coiling Dragon Peak. Had he the thoughts to spare, he might have been excited or even anxious about his attempted breakthrough. He was a peerless cultivator of the realm, second only to the God-Emperor himself—and soon, not even to him.

But of course, he thought none of those things. He thought nothing at all, having left such things behind him to focus whole-heartedly on his cultivation. Besides, he had a long way to go before breaking through and summoning the heavenly trial of ascension, and he wasn’t foolish enough to think he would truly be a match for the God-Emperor—at least, not right away.

Suddenly, Yan De’s concentration was broken by an anomaly—an intruder on the mountain. An attacker? That should be impossible, anyone who hadn’t yet formed their spiritual body would have died to the wards, and those who had would have suffered grave injuries. Yan De remained calm, shedding a layer of ice and snow from his naked body as he rose. He summoned a fine mystical robe and donned it, adopting an imperious posture in time for the intruder to show himself.

As expected, it was one of his own sect’s disciples—nobody else could have gotten through the wards unharmed. Yan De didn’t recognize the face, but from the cultivation he estimated that it must be one of his grand-disciples. Yan De was not upset at the interruption, though he had made it clear he was not to be disturbed. He trusted his sect not to bother him without good cause.

The disciple knelt down into a deep kowtow and did not speak for a full minute—good, this one was trained in proper etiquette. After another minute, Yan De finally addressed the disciple.

“Speak.”

Only when given permission did the disciple address his grandmaster, and he did not raise his head to do so.

“Lord Grandmaster, there has been a disturbance in the spiritual tablet! Master sent me to inform you.”

Quick and concise, good—but this disciple had a lot to learn about giving comprehensive answers. A grandmaster should not have to ask questions.

“What kind of disturbance?”

“This lowly disciple cannot answer, as I was not permitted entry to the inner sanctum.”

Yan De frowned, fractionally. Not the messenger’s fault, then—one of his disciples should have come to inform him themselves. Years of governing the sect had made them fat and lazy, no doubt.

“Very well, you are free to leave.”

Without waiting for a response, Yan De used a sacred art to transform himself into a gust of wind and arrived within the inner sanctum of the Awakening Dragon Sect’s temple in the blink of an eye. Several inner disciples were so startled by his sudden appearance that they nearly collapsed before they remembered themselves and dropped into a kowtow.

“These unworthy disciples greet you, ancestral grandmaster!”

Yan De ignored them, except to note that none of them were above the third stage. That core disciple messenger hadn’t been allowed access, but these worthless inners were? Well, someone had to wipe the floors, he supposed. Yan De turned to the one person in the room who had not been startled—a rotund man who had merely dropped to one knee and placed a hand over his fist rather than kowtowing.

“Yan Hao, why have I been summoned?”

Yan Hao was not a true member of the family by blood, but as one of Yan De’s direct disciples, he had been granted the name as an honor.

“Master! One of the spiritual jade tablets has cracked. One of the inner disciples discovered it while cleaning, and I sent for you right away!”

“Show me.”

Yan Hao bowed before moving to the shrine that held the spiritual jade tablets, which had been linked to the core members of the sect. Only direct disciples and family members had their tablets kept here in the inner sanctum—the rest were kept elsewhere. Yan Hao opened the shrine to reveal a cascading arrangement of brilliant white tablets, each one glowing faintly. Yan De’s own spiritual jade sat at the very top, radiating power.

One tablet, however, was not glowing. Now dull and gray compared to those around it, the crack ran straight from the top of the tablet to the bottom—neatly bisecting it. That could only mean one thing—death. Yan De’s expression was ice cold as he read the two characters adorning the tablet—Zhihao.

Yan Hao coughed up blood as the pulse of power swept over him, concentrating for a moment to restabilize the disruption of his divine core. As for the inner disciples, without a strand of divine energy between them—they could only curse their misfortune as their souls departed for reincarnation. Yan De—who had shown no outward signs of his outburst—turned and regarded Yan Hao coldly.

“Gather your best disciples and depart at once for the Grand Academy.”

“But master, the sect—”

“The sect will survive without you, Yan Hao. My scion has been slain. This is a direct insult to my honor, and it will not stand. You are to leave for the Grand Academy with all haste and bring the perpetrator to justice.”

Yan Hao hesitated for a moment, but quickly bowed.

“Yes, master! Um, if this disciple may be so bold, what of the other nations? The imperial family—”

“If Qin Zhao could not protect my son, then the imperial family is more decadent and corrupt than even I realized. I will see to the arrangements. You’ll have one—no, three tokens and a personal invitation to attend the academy as an instructor by tomorrow.”

Yan De was furious! He had only begun his breakthrough when he was confident that he had left an heir. He had known about the Grand Academy project, and had arranged for his son to attend, thinking that he would be safe from harm under the protection of the imperial family. Now, not only was his breakthrough ruined, but he would have to sire another heir.

His wife—no, her cultivation wouldn’t have recovered from birthing Zhihao yet. A concubine, then—finding someone suitable and arranging a dowry was another headache Yan De did not want to deal with. Yan Hao had nearly left the room when Yan De called out to him.

“Yan Hao! Once I finish making the proper arrangements, I will be returning to closed door training. I’ll make sure you get into the academy without starting a war, but once you’ve left—I don’t care if you have to burn the place down to the foundations—do not return without the head of whoever killed my son.”

“Yes master!”

Yan Hao bowed deeply and fled the room. Only once he was completely gone and Yan De’s was alone with the dead inners did he allow himself a snarl of frustration. So close! He had been so close to ascension, and now it could be years before he could once again make the attempt to join his Emperor’s heavenly host. He vowed that whoever had done this would pay dearly for their transgression.
