[Tutorial Task 1: Fiat lux - Decree the existence of a box.]

The color drained from my face.

I took the words for what they really meant.

I had been haunted about seeing the box suddenly appear through Peeler and then again at the perfectly preserved church despite the destruction around it. They weren't natural, they had been created. I hadn't really thought about where Instance Holders came from. They were monsters playing gods for reasons I couldn't possibly understand. Now I knew better. They were just Visitors who had crossed a threshold of Presence.

I had skipped over so many steps by killing that moon.

I let the static in my vision fade. The hand of my mother held mine. I was sweating. She didn't ask. I could see the worry in her eyes.

I'd deal with it later. For now, I was eating food with my family. I remembered how we'd always eat outside on that very patio. We'd hide under the awning when it rained or snowed and just listen. We'd usually talk. I remembered a particular conversation on one misty evening. We couldn't even see our neighbor's house with such poor visibility.

Mom had made tea—she always added honey. Dad had the newspaper from exactly a year ago, a hobby he had developed in college. Rowan wrote in his notebooks, one of the rare occasions he was around, sketching this or that. Mom broke the silence. "Either of you have any book recommendations? The book club just finished talking about Piranesi and are looking for another one." She directed the question at Rowan and I.

Rowan closed his notebook, "You finished it? What'd you think?"

She tilted her head back and forth in a so-so gesture. "I liked it. It was good."

Dad rolled his eyes and rubbed my mom's back. Rowan caught the motion, "but?" he questioned.

"No buts. I liked it." She persisted.

"Okay, not but. And?" Rowan pushed.

Mom took longer to reply, "I thought it was really sad. Especially the ending."

I had raised my eyebrow at that, "Sad? It was pretty unambiguously happy."

"Not ambiguous if half the people at this table disagree." Mom laughed. I looked to Dad, who nodded, setting down his year-old newspaper to join the conversation. I glanced towards Rowan to see if he was on the same page.

"Juries still out," he grunted at my glance. "Sorry, Doe, looks like you're outnumbered."

I gave my parents an incredulous look. "How was it sad?"

Mom had sighed, "I mean. He died in a way. Two separate times. Once when he was trapped in the house and another time when he left. Even after he left he could never go back, not really."

I snapped back to reality, my mouth mimicking the words my dad had said right after, "It reminded me of the cave allegory."

"What?" my mom said at my random outburst.

"Nothing," I started, realizing I had been lost in the memory and let it slip, "was just thinking about Piranesi."

She nodded. "Want to sleep in the living room? We can make a pillow fort."

I laughed—a fond thing. "I think I'm good."

She hesitated before rephrasing her question, "Could you sleep in the living room for the next couple of days? Just to keep a better eye on you."

My instinct was no, and the crawling of my skin pushed me to that reaction even stronger than usual. I managed a breath and a nod, "Yeah, that's fair."

I saw the relief, the tension in my parents' shoulders loosen just a little. It made me glad I had agreed.

We continued to eat and make small talk.

I didn't hate small talk. In a lot of ways, I preferred it. I could care for someone, listen to them, and talk without being dragged down by everything beneath the surface. I thought about it. Hmm. I liked talking to my parents more likely. When Joey, Maerin, Alexi, and Illia tried for small talk, it kind of pissed me off.

My parents were reluctant to leave me alone to the point that they hovered in my doorway when I collected some blankets to sleep on the couch that evening. The only isolation I could manage was the bathroom. I didn't need to go, but it was the only place I was alone to look at the static. I had gone back and forth about whether to look at it in the hours after eating, but I kept thinking about it. It gnawed at me.

I let the static take my vision and focused on the task.

[Do you wish to undergo the tutorial now?]

I manipulated the static to form words, just like how the System had taught me before. The words were easier to form now. Much easier, despite the fact I hadn't practiced at all. I didn't know what this tutorial would look like, so I layed down in the bathtub just in case. It wouldn't do if I passed out on my feet and brained myself on the counter.

[System, how long will it take?]

[An hour. I can dilate time on your end if needed.]

[By how much?]

[Up to a ratio of 10.]

[Agreed. Max dilation.]

Everything slowed. The world flickered in static. There was no physical change in my surroundings, but I knew. I was in the Dream now. I stepped out into the hall, walked to the nearest room, and looked out the window. Sure enough, a shattered sky showed itself.

[Congrats on making it to the tutorial. It was … faster than expected.]

"Yeah well I'm not working on your schedule." I meant to put more venom in my voice, but it didn't come through. "So, what changes. You can tell me more now right."

[In time, but yes. Many of the things I've kept hidden have been for your benefit. Just knowing some things can break you.]

I took in a deep breath, thinking about the Spinner who could rewrite my memories and of the Dream who had taken time itself from me. I thought of my brother's notebook that asked, 'Who is Dorian?' And of what Nate had told me about people being puppeted by this creature in the static.

"I need to know. No lying or bullshit. Is Rowan even real. Is he even my brother or was he just another puppet." I held my breath as soon as the words left my mouth. I didn't dare move, or even start trying to exist as I stood in uncomfortable apprehension.

[Rowan is real. He is not under my control.]

I kept my eyes pressed shut. This thing could still be lying. It could do that, but I wanted to believe it more than anything else. "How do I get to him," I asked.

[500,000 Presence. You'll be able to meet him if you get that much.]

I didn't even flinch. The numbers went up. They'd always just go up, wouldn't they?

Even if I pulled what I did before with the moon, I'd have to do that, what? 100,000 times. It'd take years, decades, even, but I knew I could pull it off. It was possible to kill Instance Holders. I had seen it twice now, once by the System and once by my own hand. If I went this path, I wouldn't have to wait as long. It'd be more dangerous, but everything I had seen told me those fuckers deserved to die.

"Okay," I finally said. "So, what's this tutorial."

I could sense hesitation in the static. I couldn't hear it, and its words came typed out in my vision. There were signs, though, like how long it took the creature to respond. It felt surprised, like it had expected a stronger reaction.

[It's how you'll gather the needed Presence. We'll start simple, with the creation of a box.]

I saw where this was going. Peeler had shown me, but the creature continued to dance around it. I wasn't going to let that happen. "You're going to turn me into an Instance Holder. That's your plan."

More silence. More hesitation.

[Yes. You need not change. Instance Holders are not a type of creature, just an additional ability. Like your Title.]

"This is how it happens, isn't it?" I questioned the air. I didn't know what I meant, not exactly. Still, I felt the words apt for the situation. I had already crossed so many lines. What was one more? I hated the Instance Holders I had interacted with, but I understood their tactics. The underhanded nature of it all to stack the deck in your favor. I saved myself so many times simply because I thought, 'What would I do in their situation?'

If I did this, I'd be just as bad as them. Probably worse. Not right away, but I knew who I was and what I was capable of. I hadn't before I entered the Dream. I could feel the tug this shortcut offered. If I took it, there'd be no going back. I thought of what Jeren said: how I had more concern about hurting bad people than helping those who needed it.

He was right.

I wasn't above hurting people. I'd do what I had to every single time ... but I didn't want to drag others into it. I wouldn't be like them. I refused.

I made my decision. "I'm canceling the tutorial. Send me back."

[Don't be stubborn, Dorian.]

"I'm not gonna be an Instance Holder," I reaffirmed.

[Why?]

I paused at that, not because I was set in my decision, but because the question surprised me. What even was the System? Was it just another layer on top of the Visitors and Instance Holders too. I thought that they weren't human, they certainly didn't seem to understand certain reactions or have respect for my autonomy. So, I had thought it a machine, but I wasn't so sure now. "You've been watching me. You know why."

[You don't have to be like them.]

"Then how would I get Presence? Would I not take it from the Visitors. I thought the lost Presence might be going to you or the Dream, but it's the Instance Holders that take it, right?"

[You're inability to think of other solutions shows a lack of creativity.]

"No matter what I'll have to take. Unless you're telling me I can get Presence from nothing."

Silence.

"Yeah, that's what I thought. I'm not gonna do it."

[You don't have a choice.]

Fear and spite ran through me in equal measures. Spite won out. Like hell I didn't have a choice. I stepped towards the kitchen, aiming for one of the knives. My legs vanished, and I violently crashed into the floor. I yelled, opened my mouth, and stuck out my tongue to bite down. It too vanished. My arms followed. Then my chest. It took more and more from me until the only parts of me left came in the form of two eye stalks, my heart, and a brain.

I had forgotten in all the events and tension of the Instances, and the Systems' relative silence that it wore the mantle of the Dream itself. No Instance holder could compete with the cruelty it could and would casually dish out whenever it felt like.

The situation reminded me of the healing Boon I used to recreate my body when I was blown to pieces.

If I could've grinned, I would have. I still had the Boon equipped. I activated it.

The sensations were the same as before, but this time I could see flesh knitting itself from thin air. It helped ground me as my nerves screamed against the prospect of being born. I could hardly think. I was on my feet. I dashed forward, reaching the kitchen and grabbing a knife before my legs vanished. I brought the knife up. My arms disappeared, and the knife fell down, digging an inch into my chest before losing balance and clattering to the floor.

My eyes went right after, then the rest. Despite the lack of sight, the static still formed, which should've been impossible.

[So fucking stubborn. What's it going to take, Dorian? What's it going to take for you to agree and stop being a screaming toddler?]

I oriented myself, imagining the world where I had last seen the knife on the floor. I only had one charge of my Boon left in the Dream. If I wanted to escape, I had to make it count. I imagined reforming—flesh coming into existence from nothing. I pictured my hands gripping the knife. It felt like solving a puzzle box as I mentally twisted and contorted my body for the perfect angle.

I activated the Boon. I felt the superficial chill of the hardened grip on my palms. Before I could register anything else, I slammed the blade into my eye.

I sputtered. Twitched. I tried to think, but the thoughts came half-formed and sporadic. A strong odor hit my nostrils. I tasted copper through my fingertips. I jerked, twisted ... and died.

I woke up in the bathtub vomiting blood. I started to spasm. I activated my Boon before it could get worse. To my horror, nothing happened. I wretched again, my arms shaking. I scrambled for the faucet. I tore it all the way left, and the uneven flow of water covered up the torturous sounds escaping me.

The pain and disorientation weren't as bad this time around. Not nearly as harsh as it had been the first time in Joey's dorm. I could get used to it if I tried hard enough. If I pushed through the pain and discomfort, I could desensitize myself to it. I had the rest of my life with the Dream. I could do it. I had to do it.

I turned the temperature to scalding hot and lay on the floor of the tub. I pulled that little plug that turned it into a shower and let the heat stop my shaking.

"A better deal, that's what I need," I said to no one, but they were there, watching me. It felt like the only thing I knew.

Static formed in the steam.

[Fine. The hard was it is. There's always next time. Sleep well, Dorian.]

I dismissed the words.

If the Creature in the Static wouldn't give me answers, I'd have to get them myself. It was time to go over Rowan's journals and VHS tapes again.
