Stacy, the Soccer Mom, took advantage of Badlan’s foolishness.

The leader of the Gabordian faction and brother-in-law of Tenor was transformed into an unrecognizable abomination along with three others.

What was left of the four men immediately turned onto their allies.

“Badlan, or what was left of him, used his bare hands...” Tenor closed his eyes and took a deep breath. “....Meklin had no chance. Badlan reduced his body to pieces. The skill, or curse, not only transformed its victims. It also greatly enhanced their strength.”

He shivered.

“Some tried to fight, myself being one.” He shook his head. “It was no use. What little damage we inflicted was completely ignored. Many died and we were forced to flee.”

I imagined the gory scene playing out. The fight probably happened not too far from where we were speaking now. Behind Tenor, the horned monkey men no longer kneeled.

One by one I studied their faces.

Droopy, bloodshot eyes seemed uniform throughout their ranks. Who could blame them?

Tenor’s story was an unfortunate one.

It could also have been easily avoided. The fact that if Badlan hadn’t been stupid enough to try and use the witch, he would’ve fought us, probably resulting in his death anyway.

To be fair, it wasn’t all that long ago that Stacy used her magic to try and kill me. Obviously, the situations were different. I was ambushed, whereas Badlan knew about the witch and still tried to use her.

Men like Badlan weren’t long for this world.

“It sounds like you were lucky to get away.” I said, thinking out loud.

Tenor’s story made me wonder though. Why hadn’t the witch used that same spell on me?

I could think of two possible reasons.

Either, she didn’t have the spell yet, or she didn’t have the time needed to cast the spell.

The fight with the cyclops wasn’t an especially long fight, but I also don’t know how long Stacy was lurking in the trees watching me.

“I hope you don’t mind me asking.” I scratched my chin. “But you mentioned before that a witch needs two things to cast its spell. Time and visibility. How long did it take for Stacy to curse your leader?”

Tenor's face scrunched up in thought.

“I can’t say exactly, but if I were to guess, it might have been two hours.”

Two hours?

I was definitely not fighting the cyclops for that long. So that could’ve been the reason. Two hours was a very long time.

Casting such a spell in a fight seemed very unlikely. Yet if all she needed was visibility, she could easily just sit outside of a village and cast her spell. But she didn’t. So, my gut told me, it must’ve been a skill acquired by a recent evolution.

Most likely, she was caught before she was able to take advantage of the spell. At least she wouldn’t have been able to, until Badlan gave her everything she needed.

Speaking of which...how long had Tenor been telling his story now?

“Whelp!” I clapped my hands together, and the snap made Tenor jump. “I’ve made up my mind. You can’t be slaves but were happy to help.”

Tenor blinked.

“So, if you’d be so kind as to wait here, were gonna head back to let Jared know were bringing people in.”

He shifted uneasily.

“I uhh...thank you. You want us to wait here?”

I grinned.

“Yeah, we’ll be right back, and then you can take us to the rest of your faction.”

“So just wait here?” Tenor repeated.

“We’ll see you soon!” I said, turning away.

Nick turned with me and whispered in my ear. “Are you sure you want to leave them?”

I wrapped my arm around his shoulder as we left the horny monkey men behind.

“I’m sure.”

Tenor wasn't sure what to do.

He thought to call out to the strange young man, but he couldn’t find the right words before the boy and his entire group was gone, disappearing between the dark green pines.

Should I follow?

He wondered, but then quickly dismissed that idea. If he wasn’t careful, it could be misinterpreted as an act of aggression.

And that was one thing he absolutely did not want.

Tenor would have to be a fool not to see how horribly outmatched he and his men were.

Instead, he just stood in stunned silence.

Even now, with the group long gone it was hard to shake the image of the hooded wizard with storm clouds for eyes and prominent antlers, like baren trees casting shadows on a cloudless night.

Trees rustled peacefully. The air was thick with the smell of impending rain.

The day was chilly and it was nearing the night. Tenor breathed through his nose feeling the warm air escape into the chill.

He cursed Badlan.

It was because of him that he was in this impossible situation.

Although at least now he knew for certain. If they had somehow managed to penetrate the barrier, like Badlan had originally planned, they would all already be dead.

Maybe that would have been better...

“Tenor?”

Tenor swayed out of his stupor at the sound of his name.

"Huh?”

He pulled his eyes away from the trees where Layton departed.

“What now?”

It was Thorpe. Concern etched in the corners of his eyes.

“I suppose now...we wait?” Tenor concluded, not sure what else they could do.

“Do you think they suspect something?”

Tenor scratched the dark brown fur on his cheek.

Things had not gone the way that he’d expected. Afterall, who would refuse willing slaves that delivered themselves to your doorstep? He knew things might not work perfectly, but to be outright rejected? That he hadn’t expected.

“Perhaps....but it's too early to tell. The important thing is getting him to the village. What happens after that is up to more powerful forces.”

Thorpe grunted his agreement. Offering themselves as slaves was only ever just a means to an end. There were other ways to entice the faction leader out, but they’d settled on slaves because it seemed the most rational.

He’d been more concerned about what would happen once they arrived in their village. Now that he’d met just a few members of Layton Mischiefs’ faction, he had no idea how things were going to go.

No doubt about it, they were in the middle of a rock and a hard place.

That was just the way it worked for weak factions like Tenors. The powerful made the world shift, while the weak were just along for the ride.

Thorpes' ear twitched, and he tilted his head towards where Layton had gone. Tenor turned in the direction and sure enough, Layton was already returning with his men.

How long had it been? Surely, no more than a few minutes?

Yet there they were.

A shiver crawled up his spine at the reappearance of the wizard draped in dark blue robes. A spark jumped between the antlers protruding from its raised hood. Tenor felt the spark should’ve been brighter than it was, as if the light was somehow being subdued.

With effort, he peeled his eyes away only to notice that the group seemed smaller than it had before. For one thing, several of the tall canine-looking companions were missing. The shadowy archer was also notably missing.

Where would they have gone?

It didn’t make sense that they would send such a large group back to their city. His thoughts were cut off though as Layton sauntered up, the bottom of his healers’ robe brushing just at the top of the low grass.

“Sorry for making you wait.” The young man said pleasantly, with a bright smile.

“It was nothing.” Tenor replied truthfully.

“Ok then. Should we head out?”

Tenor frowned. “Head out?”

“We’re going to help you, but this isn’t everyone, is it? Shouldn’t we get the rest?”

Some of the tension in Tenor’s shoulders drained.

“Yes, I’ll show the way.” He said, tempering his emotions, afraid to spoil his good fortune by appearing overeager. Tenor hadn’t planned on his offering of slavery to be rejected.

On his home world it was entirely unheard of to turn away slaves, but in the end, this would still work.

What would happen once he got him there?

Tenor was far too weak to concern himself with that. So, they left, and Layton followed with his men.

Together they wound through the trees. Not long into their journey the rolling dark clouds above finally gave. Rain began to patter onto the trees and bushes. Not a downpour, just a calm drizzle that seeped through the short, cropped fur on the top of Tenor’s head.

It was already a chilly day, and the rain only made it seem colder.

Beside him, Layton walked with his hands behind his back like he was on a pleasant stroll. His breath puffed as he spoke to the man in armor at his side.

Of all the newcomers, Layton was by far the least intimidating. His face appeared youthful compared to many of his soldiers and he was likely the shortest Tenor had seen from his faction so far.

His healer's robes and small stature likely meant he hadn’t invested much into his physical stats like strength, agility or constitution.

That made sense. Most healers used wisdom and intelligence to cast barriers on themselves for protection and relied on their allies for offense.

Good healers were highly sought after and made for high value slaves. But Tenor hadn’t heard of a dedicated healer ever becoming the leader of a faction.

At least not in his home world.

Maybe in the early days some might have risen through the ranks, but inevitably they’d fall or get replaced by someone with more power than themselves. Tenor felt like he remembered a story where a healer became ruler of a mighty empire only to get betrayed by his own brother who’d help build it.

His curiosity got the better of him and he found himself asking...

“I hope it’s not too presumptuous to assume but are you indeed a healer like your robes suggest?”

“Hmm?” Layton's attention was locked on the clouds, misting the trees. “Why does everyone immediately assume these are healer's robes?”

He asked in what Tenor hoped was mock annoyance.

“If I had to put it simply, they just seem like robes someone who performs healing magic might wear.” Tenor’s brows furrowed, had he got it wrong? “Are you not?”

“No, I am a healer. And they are healer robes.” Layton cracked a half smile. “I was just curious what gave it away. But I guess you're right. They do give off healer vibes, don’t they?”

Tenor studied the young man, who kept his attention on the clouds. He felt strange. Like talking openly with the young man was the most natural thing in the world.

“It's rare to see a healer leading a faction.” Tenor found himself saying.

To his surprise, it wasn’t the boy who responded but the man in armor at his side.

“Is that so?” His voice came out slightly muffled but could still be heard clearly through the helmet covering his face. “Why do you think that might be?”

“I bet I could guess.” The boy said, finally pulling his face away from the sky. Rain drops cascaded from his soaked brown hair down his youthful face.

“It’s because they get killed.” He said, turning to face Tenor.

“Am I right?”

The boy's response wasn’t terse. It was just him stating a fact. His sincere response to observations of a violent world.

Still, Tenor chose his next words carefully.

“I’m not sure they are all killed. But generally, they are overthrown, yes.”

Layton shrugged. “That doesn’t surprise me.”

“You’re not worried about being overthrown?” The man in armor laughed.

“Me?” Layton said, pointing at his chest. “Nah, people love me.”

“Is it love? Or are we scared Mischief will eat us?” One of Layton’s soldiers said from behind them.

Layton laughed.

“Mischief hasn’t eaten anyone yet.” Then Layton paused. “Or at least it’s been a while, so it has to be love!”

Tenor observed the exchange and quietly tucked the name Mischief away. A member of their faction who they feared might eat them? How was that something to joke about?

So, the boy was naive.

This Mischief must’ve been how Layton gained his station and was also how he maintained his status. If Mischief were willing to eat his own faction members for harming Layton, what might he do to a stranger?

Tenor shoved the thought out of his mind and decided he would take the rest of their journey in silence, content to listen to the casual banter of the soldiers in Layton’s army.

Tenor’s men walked in silence.

Not everyone within Layton’s party had universal translators. It was still easy to divine that they were at ease. To some effect that bothered Tenor.

Why should they be so careless? Even if they suspected themselves of the superior force, shouldn’t they still be more wary? Or did they just believe they were that much stronger?

They didn’t know what they were getting themselves into though.

There was a break in the tree line and through it, Tenor saw a familiar landmark. A rock formation that resembled a watercraft from his home world.

It meant they were getting close.

“So, Tenor, what would you put the odds at that Stacy shows up while we are moving your people?”

Tenor’s breath caught in his throat. He looked towards the mountain range as if making sure they were moving in the right direction while he reigned in his composure.

“I’m not sure....I suppose there is a small possibility.”

“Hmm.” He pushed a branch out his path and let it snap back to hit the man in matte green armor.

The man swatted the branch away with a snort.

“I guess it would be a decent time to ask, but it would make things a lot easier once we get there if you give up your territory to our faction. That way we can place portals.”

Tenor nodded slowly. “Sure, sure, I’m sure that won’t be a problem. However, I’d like to explain the situation to my people first if that is acceptable?”

Layton was about to respond when a small pine tree whipped up from between the armored man's legs aimed directly at the young healer. The ploy was clearly retaliation from Layton’s earlier attack with the branch.

Using a small tree was a clear escalation from his subordinate.

Layton’s hand moved so quickly that, to Tenor, it almost appeared to teleport. It flashed from his side to the juvenile tree trunk, arresting its movement in an instant.

Tenor blinked.

Layton pulled a branch from his mouth that managed to reach his face in spite of his incredible reaction.

With a grin, he spit a few pine needles from his mouth and brushed the tree to the side.

“I hope you can excuse Martin’s childish behavior.” He said with a twinkle in his eyes. “He still has a lot of growing up to do.”

The man, who Tenor now knew was named Martin, snorted even louder.

“But yeah, I have no problem with you explaining anything you need.”

Had Tenor really seen what he thought he did?

Almost certainly not. Layton only seemed to move that quickly because he’d caught the movement from the corner of his eye. Yes...that must have been it.

“Hey! Looks like we’re here, isn’t that your village?”
