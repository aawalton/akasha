“Did you really survive out here by yourself?” Freyja asked as she led him back to the farm.

She had gone through his stuff and put the ring he looted on her own finger, and Rex was eyeing his new bow, which she had over her shoulder.

Dammit, I just got those. I am definitely getting them back, one way or another. At least I still have my emerald tucked away safely.

“You mean, out in the forest? Yeah? I mean, who else would have helped me?” Rex replied.

She turned to him, her brow creased and deadly serious. “You’re not working with any lords, are you?”

“Me? No?”

She glared at him for a moment. There was clearly more going on than Rex understood.

“Information is what’s keeping you alive, Rex. Your aura senses intrigue me, but not enough to save you. You disappoint me, and I guarantee that you won’t be making it out of here alive,” she said, pointing at him.

She wants information? This might be my chance…

“Right. No worries. I’m an open book. Just let me see Andy first so that I know he is okay, and I’ll tell you everything you want to know,” Rex lied.

“So, you are working for someone?”

“Andy first,” Rex chimed as if dangling a carrot before her.

“Fine! But I’m serious, Rex,” she said, tugging on the rope hard, almost pulling him off his feet. “You better not be screwing with me. Let’s go!”

It turned out that there was a sizable cellar that doubled as a dungeon beneath the farmstead. Freyja led him down the stone steps and into the cool, damp chamber, where they met Jango standing beside a cell, with Andy and Ernie sitting hunched over inside.

“So, you caught the troublemaker? Impressive. It seems Credo chose well when he picked you.”

“Yeah, and your men are pretty much useless,” Freyja said, leading Rex up to the cell. “You, get in.”

Andy and Ernie looked up as Rex was pushed in beside them, and he saw how bruised up their faces were.

“Rex?” Andy groaned.

“Hey buddy,” Rex said. “You guys okay?”

Freyja snapped her fingers. “You can catch up later. Now, you owe me an explanation. Who do you work for?!”

Oh shit. What do I say now?

“They’re not working for a lord, are they?” Jango asked Freyja.

He speaks to her like an equal. What is her deal? Is she actually a newly Classed or what?

“Dunno. About to find out. Rex?! Answer me! Now!”

I’d better say something before she changes her mind about sparing my life.

“Um—I work for Lord Castarian,” Rex said, remembering the name from back in The Red Rose. The only other lords he knew by name were back on Dorog, and he doubted their reach would make it here.

“Lord Castarian,” Jango murmured. “You think he’s telling the truth?”

“I… I dunno,” Freyja said, looking puzzled.

“What do you mean you don’t know? I thought reading auras was your thing?”

“It is! Rex, I need more. Why did Lord Castarian send you here?” Freyja demanded. “What does he want with us?”

Shit. Looks like I’m going to have to bullshit my way out of this.

“I don’t know the details. I’m not important enough to know what kind of stuff. He just wanted me to figure out how many men you had, and where they were stationed.”

“Frejya,” Jango hissed. “Is he telling the truth, or not?”

“I can’t say. His control over his aura is different,” Freyja rubbed her temples, and her brow ceased. “There’s something odd about it.”

“A Feat?” Jango suggested.

“Maybe. But no Basic Feat could do this. Not by itself, at least. And he’s not high enough level for something more advanced. There’s something else about him.”

Did—did that actually just work? They bought that?

“What do we do?”

“Leave him here. I need to think.”

“Freyja,” Jango said insistently.

“Give me some time to meditate on his aura. Either that, or we call my boss.”

“Credo?”

“No. The other one.”

“No, no, no! That won’t be necessary. Take your time. They’re behind bars now anyway. They’re not going anywhere.”

“I’ll be back for more details later, Rex. If you’re lying to me, I will kill you myself,” Freyja said and turned to leave.

“One thing,” Jango said, eyeing Rex. “What did you do to my men? We found scraps of their gear and signs of battle. They’re obviously dead. Otherwise, those whining bastards would have come back by now. And I doubt you have the muscle to capture them. So, where are they?”

I guess Slimer ended up eating them all after I left?

“Monster,” Rex said. “Shadowbeast. It ambushed us when the fight broke out. It’s the only reason I survived. I think it ate them.”

“Hmph.” Jango eyed him for a long moment and then just shook his head. “Now we have a fucking shadowbeast to deal with as well? Curse my luck.”

Whew, that actually worked? I guess shadowbeasts do eat people. Well, well, well. Turns out I’m pretty good at lying.

Then again, it wasn’t a hard lie to sell. It would have taken any normal person a long time to dispose of all the corpses without a trace. Even his [ Corrosive Touch ] would have taken multiple attempts to completely melt them, and likely would have drained his mana multiple times before he got done with the job. Not to mention, he had a feeling no one even suspected he could cast spells yet.

I really owe Slimer now.

Rex felt his entire body relax as the two of them disappeared up the stairs and turned to the boys.

“You guys look banged up. How are you doing?”

“Alive. Could be better, though,” Andy flashed a crooked smile. “Somehow, we ended up much worse than you. That’s so unfair.”

“Ha. Yeah, it is actually,” Rex smiled. “Good to see you’re alive.”

“Y-you didn’t have to, Rex,” Ernie sniffled and looked up, one side of his glasses broken. “Come here, that is. We didn’t all have to be stuck in here together.”

“Nah, I did,” Rex said, dropping to his side and wrapping an arm around his shoulders. “Don’t worry, though. I totally considered abandoning you.”

“Hey!” Andy said.

“Look, I’m not dumb enough to come here without a plan,” Rex said. “Or selfless enough, for that matter.”

“You’ve got a plan?” Andy sat up, his puppy dog eyes glimmering.

“Yeah, of course I do. I’m Rex of the Waste, aren’t I?”

Andy and Ernie looked at each other and shrugged.

“Well, I am. Remember that name, it is going to be famous one day. Now for saving your sorry asses.”

Rex had been working on instinct and hadn’t exactly known how he was going to free his friends when he let himself get captured. But now, in an empty cellar, looking at the iron bars of their cell, he had a pretty good idea.

First, we get out of here, then I figure out how to get my gear back.

There was no way he was just going to give up on his first artifacts as soon as he got them. But survival took precedence.

He got up, walked over to the bars, and grabbed them.

“What are you doing?” Andy asked.

“One moment,” Rex said, and within seconds, he cast [ Corrosive Touch ] twice, melting away and removing two bars. “Think that’s enough space to slip through?”

“Did you? Wait, y-you’re a spellcaster?”

“Well, kinda? Technically, Versatile. But I’ve got a few tricks up my sleeve.”

“Wow, Rex. Didn’t know you could do stuff like this. You’re amazing,” Andy said.

“Yeah, it’s cool,” Ernie said, looking around. “But they’re going to see it when they get back, and then we’ll be in really big trouble.”

“Not if we escape first. Come, let's get out of here before they return. Just try to relax. Take deep breaths. It’ll help keep your auras down—”

Footsteps. They were coming down.

“Shit! Not now! Okay, we gotta go hide,” Rex said, pointing down a corridor that was the only exit from the room, besides the stairs.

The corridor was short, leading into the supply room, which was filled with food and drink.

“Shit, shit, shit!”

“There’s no way out!” Ernie said.

“What do we do, Rex?” Andy asked.

There was a small stone vent up beside the ceiling, but there was no way they were slipping through it.

Rex turned. Whoever was coming down the stairs was about to reach their destination, and he still hadn’t recovered his bow.

I’m starting to think [ Seeker Dart ] would have been a good choice.

His friends were scared. Andy hid it better, but neither had seen combat as he had.

I need to do something.

“You two stay here. I’ll deal with this.”

“You sure?” Andy said.

“Yeah. I dealt with the hunters, didn’t I?”

Andy held his gaze for a second and nodded.

Leaving them behind in the storage, he crept to the edge of the corridor and eyed the base of the stairs. And a second later, two of Jango’s men entered.

Rex knew he needed to do something, and quickly. But what? He was a ranged combatant, and they had sharp weapons.

“Look at that, the bars.”

“Shit! We gotta tell Jango.”

“You go, I’ll stay here. They can’t have gone far.”

Shit. Not good!

There wasn’t going to be an easy way out when Jango arrived. But launching himself at a melee combatant was suicide. He needed to be smart. He needed to think; besides, one of them had already escaped up the stairs, and there was no way to stop him.

The other man stepped down into the cellar, drawing a small hand axe. He wore light leather, a scar over one eye, and his boots had a strange gleam to them.

Rex focused on dulling his aura as the man walked up to the cell and pressed himself against the wall of the corridor. Hidden, he couldn’t see the man, but he could sense and hear him.

“If ya know what’s good for ya, ye’d better show yourselves. Jango ain’t gonna be as forgiving if he’s gotta come down here drag ya back into your cell himself.”

There was only one way to go, and the man quickly finished searching the cell room and turned to the corridor.

“I know ya ain’t got anywhere to run. Come on, make it easy for yourself.”

He could sense Ernie and Andy in the storage room, and no doubt the man could too. It was the perfect situation; he just had to be invisible—reduce his aura to nothing.

The man stepped into the darkness of the corridor, and—

“Ack!”

In an instant, Rex had sprung out, his left hand reaching out to push the axe away, as his right hand shot towards the man’s chest. It all happened so fast, and the moment he felt something against his palm, he activated [ Corrosive Touch ].

“Bah-argh!” The man coughed and spat as a hole was melted through his chest.

It was over in seconds, and the others came out as they heard his body drop.

“That’s so gross,” Andy said.

“Here, take this,” Rex said, grabbing the axe and handing it to Andy. “The boots and coin pouch are mine, though,” he added as he looted.

“You killed him! What are we going to do now?” Ernie chattered. “There’s no way to escape. They’ll kill us for sure now.”

“They were going to kill us anyway, Ernie,” Andy said.

“You got a good aim?” Rex asked Andy.

“What?”

“Tell me, if you threw this axe, would you be able to hit your target?”

“Err, I dunno, maybe?”

“Andy, this is important. I’ve got an idea, but it’s going to be risky.”

Andy nodded. “Alright. I guess my aim is okay. It’s not like we have much choice, anyway.”

“Good enough. I’m going to walk over there,” Rex pointed to the base of the stairs. “When our captors come looking for us, I’m going to do something. It’ll take too long to explain. But it’s going to look like I disappeared. And you’ll see a blub blob, or rather, a slime, appear where I was standing. When it appears, I need you to throw the axe at it.”

“And that’ll save us?”

Rex shrugged. “It’s a long shot. But unless you want to get back in the cell—”

“I’ll do it.”

“Andy! The cell wasn’t so bad,” Ernie said, but shrank the moment Andy turned to glare at him.

“Listen. This part is important. The moment you throw the axe, get behind cover. There’s going to be a boom. I’m going to come back here as soon as I can, but it might take me a while. If there were any other options, I would try them.”

“It’s fine, Rex. I’m sure they were planning to kill us. Now quit yapping and get moving before they get here.”

Rex nodded and rushed over to the stairs.

The moment he got to the base of the stairs, he spotted boots rushing down, and a second later, Jango leading several of his men.

“You! I knew I couldn't trust leaving you alive,” he said, drawing duel sabres. “I’m going to skewer you! You won’t get a second chance to make a fool of me!”

“Won’t I?” Rex challenged and immediately regretted it.

Jango activated some kind of Skill and, in an instant, rushed down the stairs, appearing only a meter or two away from Rex.

It took less than a second to activate [ Transference ], yet that was enough time for his blades to swing out at him, reaching within a couple of inches of his neck before he disappeared.

Rex blinked, and he was in the forest, reflexively rubbing his neck.

“That was waaay too close. Wait, where’s the burrow…”

He looked around and noticed a structure. It was the farm. He could see the edges of it through a thin line of trees. That little bastard had followed him!

“Slimer, you—” Rex wanted to scold his monster companion, but bit his tongue. The slime had been disobedient, but it had turned out for the best. He was much closer to the farm now.

He placed his new boots down and started running for the farm. A second later, he heard a bang reverberate out from it, and a moment after that, screams.
