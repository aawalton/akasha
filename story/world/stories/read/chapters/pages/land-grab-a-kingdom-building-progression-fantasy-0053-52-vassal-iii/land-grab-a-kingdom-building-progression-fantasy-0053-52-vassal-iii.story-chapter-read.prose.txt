The only sound in the courtyard was a crying child, as Lady Donath arrived, bringing their children with her.

Tavian exhaled softly, seeing them; Donath wouldn’t have brought his family if he intended to shed blood here.

“You expect me to believe that this is one of the twelve Conqueror’s Cores?” Donath asked, incredulous, staring at the silver spike from across the courtyard. “Impossible. If you had such a thing, then why not use it to stop Lord Anarvia?”

“Inspect the Core for yourself if you don’t believe me,” Tavian said.

Lord Donath stepped up to the Core. He removed a riding glove from his hand, and ran his finger over the engravings.

Tavian glanced over at Donath’s family. He had ten children, from about fourteen down to the baby Lady Donath was cradling. They looked nervous, hungry, and tired. The man was risking a lot by bringing them here, rather than fleeing to the south.

Donath’s face changed as he inspected the Core, softening from a wry desperation to a look of awe. He examined the Core more closely, placing a hand on its side, and scanning the glyphs and reliefs carved there.

Tavian watched his eyes carefully. Was there a hint of envy in the man’s gaze?

“Calli found scrolls in the library at the Temple,” Tavian explained. “They mapped out a location where one of the ancient Conqueror’s Cores was supposed to have last been seen. We traveled to Endara, searching for relics, yes, but we were searching for this one in particular. We found it.”

“I don’t believe it,” Donath said finally. “This can’t be real. It must be a fake, something crafted as a decoy or something.”

Tavian crossed his arms, scowling. “It’s real,” he said. “Look at the markings.”

“I’m not a coresmith,” Donath countered. “I could be deceived.”

“Lea, how do we prove that this is a Conqueror’s Core? Even if I grant him a class, he could still doubt it.”

“People are are capable of doubting everything.”

“If only you could show yourself to him,” Tavian said. “I think he’d believe it, then.”

Lea was silent.

“Do that thing that you did before, for Vanal,” Tavian suggested. “Make the Core shake and speak?”

“If he thinks the Core is a trick and an illusion… I don’t think that would persuade him.”

“I think he’d believe if he saw you,” Tavian said. “Is that even possible?”

“It’s painful,” Lea said. “And hard. The more Influence you have, the better.”

“Let me show you something,” Tavian said, turning to Donath.

“Tavian, please, can you at least get influence up above three first? It’s going to be brief, and everyone will see.”

“Show me what?” Donath asked, hands straying to his hips. Near his sword hilt.

“Proof,” Tavian said. “It requires a bit of preparation.” He crossed over to Vanal, who was still working through sifting the stockpiled treasures collected from the refugees.

Vanal and his assistants [Extracted] Essence, pressing it into the Core. Donath watched curiously. Most people didn’t get to see this often. Watching gold trinkets and finely crafted plates dissolve into elemental Essence and condense into spheres was a captivating sight.

The treasure pile dwindled.

“How’s that?” Tavian asked. Their influence was now 3.3.

“Get ready,” Lea said.

“Here’s your proof,” Tavian said, turning Donath toward the Core.

In a flash of light, Lea appeared, hovering over the ground as her wings fluttered. She glowed with a golden light, her dress and hair wreathing her like a halo.

“Donath,” she said. Her voice shook the ground, and light shone from her. “I am the spirit of this Conqueror’s Core. I forged it alongside Galwyn.” Her voice strained. “I am not what you fear.”

Lea faded away, leaving a glowing afterimage on Tavian’s retinas.

Donath dropped to both knees, staring at the Core, his mouth hanging open.

“Thank you, Lea,” Tavian sent.

She didn’t reply.

Donath’s mouth moved, but words didn’t come out. People in the courtyard were staring at them now, stunned by the miraculous appearance.

“Why didn’t you tell us?” Donath breathed. “This changes everything.”

“I told my father,” Tavian said. “I was expecting a more enthusiastic welcome when I returned home. I was surprised to find that he’d told nobody, and he ordered me to stow it in the vault.”

“He was afraid of upsetting the balance of power,” Donath observed. “But he was already too late. I see now that the wheels of Anarvia’s coup had been turning for some time.”

“Lord Anarvia betrayed us months ago,” Tavian agreed. “You don’t prepare and land troop ships in a matter of days.”

“You coming home with this,” Donath gestured at the relic. “You forced Lord Anarvia to act before he was ready.”

Tavian nodded grimly. “He was too late. This Core is only reason that we’ve survived.”

“Is it as powerful as the legends say?”

Tavian closed his eyes and nodded slowly. Then, his voice a little over a whisper, he spoke. “More.”

Perhaps it was a slight exaggeration.

“I can’t believe it’s real,” Donath said, looking back at the Core. “But there’s no other explanation for how you’ve held out so long. Why didn’t you take it and flee?”

“That is what my father ordered us to do,” Tavian said. “Flee to the southern stronghold. But I couldn’t abandon my people.”

Donath bowed his head toward Tavian. “I’m sorry about your father,” he said. “He should have listened to you. We could have had a united front facing the invaders.”

“What’s past is past,” Tavian said, though he felt a surge of pride at having won Donath’s respect. Everyone was watching them now, and Tavian felt a shade of self consciousness. He didn’t want this to become a scene.

“Come with me, and we’ll discuss in private,” he said.

Donath followed Tavian into the command tent. He greeted Erond warmly, and Braun slipped in behind them. Cormath had made himself scarce, which Tavian decided was for the best.

As they entered the tent, Tavian started explaining his problem. “We were just discussing a surprise attack against Anarvia’s compound,” he said. “With you and your men, we might be able to do it.”

Donath did not acknowledge the comment. He pointed at the Aegis, scowling. “Is that Tavirost’s Shield?” he asked. “What have you done with it?”

Tavian pursed his lips, his heart suddenly reminding him that Donath commanded a superior force. They weren’t out of the woods yet.

“There was a dungeon, beneath the fortress, and the temple,” Tavian said. “Calli worked out a way to drain miasma from the dungeon, but we needed a vessel. The shield was convenient.”

Tavian held his breath, hoping that Donath wouldn’t push harder. Once the man had sworn fealty, Tavian would be able to trust him better.

“So you draw on the power of miasma also,” Donath said, his stance suddenly tense. “Do you have an army of ghouls as well?”

Tavian was suddenly glad that his swordmaster wasn’t present.

“Donath,” Tavian said, soothingly. “You’re right to be nervous about miasmic power. It has nearly destroyed me and my sister. We’ve both been marked by it. We know its danger. But we had to do something about the dungeon. That is what tore my father’s kingdom apart. Now that we’ve dealt with it, we’re unstoppable.”

Donath crossed his arms, glancing nervously at the other men in the room. “Marked how?”

“Calli’s hair has changed,” Tavian said. “I have a scar, a stain on my side from a wound.”

“Does it… influence you?”

“No,” Tavian said, a little too quickly. The man didn’t seem to notice the lie.

“Lord Anarvia tried to… do something to my mind,” Donath said. “I heard voices.”

“That’s the power we’re trying to seal away. With the miasma drained from the dungeon beneath us, we can now access the full power of the Core,” Tavian said. “Together, I believe that you and I can reunite the Houses and defend the city from the Karath Amon.”

Donath nodded slowly. “Wouldn’t it be wiser to take the Core somewhere else?” he asked. “If it falls into the hands of the Karath Amon…”

Tavian shrugged. “Practically speaking,” he said, “we need a more experienced coresmith if we’re going to attempt something like that. Calli is afraid she might damage it if she attempts a deactivation.”

Lea was afraid of that too.

“What of the guild?” Donath asked. “Halin?”

“All dead or missing in the coup,” Tavian said. “We are afraid that Halin has been working with Lord Anarvia, and helped him create the Witherstone.”

Donath’s face soured. “I understand your position,” he said. “It is dangerous.”

“Everything is dangerous.”

“What are we going to do, then?”

“Please, take a seat. We’ll start getting your people added to the kingdom, and I’ll create a class for you that lets you lead them.”

Donath sat down, cautiously.

“I’m not sure, Tavian,” he said. “We could destroy the Core. It would be a tragedy, but if it falls into Lord Anarvia’s hands…”

“So far we’ve managed to keep it from him,” Tavian countered. “The Karath Amon will similarly find themselves impotent. And if we must destroy the Core, we will.”

Donath exhaled, long and slow. Tavian could tell he was weighing his honor against the lives of his wife and children. The possibility of a brutal death against the disgrace of a shameful retreat.

“Then we will join you,” Donath said. “We’ll fight to defend this city.”

Donath swore fealty and was added to the kingdom. Tavian created a new class for the man, and granted him the Binding Oath perk, which would allow him to administer the oath to his men on behalf of Tavian.

⬣ New Class Created: [Vassal] ⬣

⬣ Perks: Binding Oath ⬣

It was the first Tier III class that Tavian had granted, and it cost him a total of thirty seven essence.

⬣ Kingdom Status ⬣

Lord Donath thanked Tavian, standing taller and moving more easily in the influence of his new kingdom.

“Let’s discuss strategy,” Tavian said. “Can we destroy Lord Anarvia’s Core before the Karath Amon arrive?”

“That gives us roughly two more dawns,” Donath said. “According to my scouts.”

Erond and Braun nodded, faces grim. “Not a lot of time,” Erond observed.

“Lord Anarvia’s compound is not as secure as Tavirost Fortress,” Tavian said.

“But he has walls,” Donath said. “And ghouls. Thousands of ghouls. And his Core is in his personal safe room.”

“You’ve seen it?” Tavian asked.

“Yes,” Donath said. “When he was trying to win my allegiance. He has his Core, and his Witherstone there, surrounded by hundreds of his most reliable men at arms and walls a dozen feet thick.”

“I have an idea for the walls,” Tavian said. “And the ghouls can be drawn off.”

He explained the plan to them, addressing their concerns. Eventually all four men were in agreement.

“My lord,” Donath said. “I’ll go administer the oath to my men.”

Donath walked out of the room, and Braun eyed Tavian curiously. “Not affected by the miasma?” he said. “Valliroth might disagree.”

“Valliroth is more of a zealot these days,” Tavian said. “But even he has sworn fealty. What matters is that we have Donath on our side now. We need him, and his men. This gives us options. We can strike back at Anarvia.”

“I see that you gave him a tier three class,” Braun said, folding his arms. His own class, [Regent II], granted him some permissions to see the kingdom organization in the system.

“I’ll upgrade yours too,” Tavian said. “First we need to get our forces ready.”

Tavian prepared [Regent III] for Braun, but he was thinking about their next move. He felt a tightness in his chest. Donath wasn’t going to like seeing the way that Tavian planned to use the Aegis.
