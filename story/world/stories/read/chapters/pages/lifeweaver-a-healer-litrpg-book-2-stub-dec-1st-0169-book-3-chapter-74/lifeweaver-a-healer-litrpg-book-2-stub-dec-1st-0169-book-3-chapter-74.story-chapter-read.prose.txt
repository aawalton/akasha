Windpipe sliced through, both lungs punctured, heart opened in several places, eyes popped, and several punctures inside the brain were enough that Luke would have died on the spot if not for the healing mana in his Meta-Heart. Rather than death, every ounce of those stores emptied to keep him going. The Singer had even found a new and inventive way of hurting Luke, cutting through a very sensitive place several times. Healing mana restored it all before running out.

Through new-formed eyes, he saw a follow-up blow coming. The staff whirred through the air with a trajectory that would once again crack his skull. The pain from all the damage he'd suffered during The Healer's Moment was immense, since he hadn't been able to pre-emptively numb himself, but in that moment, he couldn't allow any of that to get to him. This opponent would not give him time to recover.

Threads of Mana were not strong enough to block one of Trypten's blows, not even when woven into a shield, but they didn't need to be. Forming the shield was near instantaneous, and Luke held it with both hands while extending several more threads to intercept the staff and guide it across the shield and then past it. The weapon slammed into the floor, and Luke stepped in without hesitation.

Trypten's attacks had all been physical, even those during the time freeze, and judging by the pathetic amount of mana in his Meta-Heart, this opponent had discarded the strongest aspects of the Lifeweaver class in favor of a pure melee-oriented version. A mistake, if ever there was one. Not only because it meant a lack of metaphysical offense options, but because with so little mana, there was no way this bastard could even heal himself with any real skill.

With that in mind, Luke reached in and opened a long gash in the aorta while also severing the main mana channel going out of his Meta-Heart. The effect wasn't long in coming. Trypten coughed up blood, dropped the staff, and sank to his knees, clawing at his chest with a single thread of mana coming out of the palm of his hand. Luke watched the Singer work, standing over him and ignoring the not-nurses still rushing back and forth.

The Singer was a true amateur in the art of healing. Working with a single thread of mana, there was no hope of him closing the wounds. Judging by the way Trypten used Needle of Life to make stitches a grade schooler would be embarrassed to show his teacher, he wouldn't even finish the aorta in time.

"Luke!" Leom shouted, coming around the corner. Her eyes widened, and she started running. "Trypten!"

Luke held up a hand to her, palm forward. "No."

"But he'll die!"

He nodded. "Yes."

Luke saw the warring emotions on her face. Leom knew she didn't stand a chance if she attacked in an attempt to save her fellow Singer. Rather than throw her own life away, she made another attempt at pleading. "Please, Luke! You're a healer, aren't you?"

Another human-looking monster nurse rushed past. Rather than allow it through, Luke grabbed it and drained every speck of mana within it before pulling the monster core free. By the time he'd finished, Trypten was dead, as he'd failed to heal himself. Impressed with the Singer's skill with the weapon, Luke took the staff, cursing his indecision about the weapon all the while.

Not wanting to antagonize Leom more than necessary, he would need her later, after all, he didn't loot the Singer's corpse. Instead, the motes of light soon came to claim it. As they did, and Leom shed tears over her fellow Singer, Luke frowned, looking down at the staff. Something about it was different. Different in a good way.

"Channels for mana?" he said, unable to keep the bewilderment out of his voice as he slid a thread of mana inside the weapon, extending it through and out the other side, adding to the thread's range without wasting any more mana. "How?"

Mana-Infused Staff: A metal staff crafted by a master Mana Smith, allowing the channeling of spells. Once mana courses through the weapon, it becomes nigh indestructible.

Luke couldn't believe what he was reading. What the hell was Trypten doing with a weapon like that? Why hadn't he used it? Yeah, the durability had been insane, but that benefit was nothing compared to the boon to Lifeweavers. Allowing him to run threads through it would be a game-changer for fighting.

He cleared his throat and gave a silent thanks to the murderous Singer. If he hadn't attacked, Luke might never even have learned of the existence of weapons like this.

"Ready to go?" he asked Leom, looking up from the dull-gray metal staff. She wasn't there. The corridor had changed, too. Perhaps he'd moved this time? Checking in on Dot's echo confirmed it. She was below him now. What a pain.

Another nurse came running straight for him, pushing a wheelchair in front of him. The patient inside was another injured Singer. This one looked electrocuted, with singed clothes, blackened skin in places, and white scarring showing where lightning had struck. Luke had an inkling of what that meant, but didn't dwell on it. His fight was here in this dungeon, and he needed to focus on getting out before helping his friends even became an option.

Luke raised the new staff and held it out in front of him. Just as the nurse reached it, he acted, extending a thread of mana through the weapon and out the other side. Rather than push with the physical weapon, he widened Needle of Life into something akin to an arrow and punched it through the monster core, killing the nurse in an instant.

Shaking his head, Luke looted a few credits off the fallen monster. This was what he had been missing. A weapon he could use to combine staff combat with all the possibilities of metaphysical assault. Perhaps now his offensive capabilities would catch up to his somewhat insane level of survivability. A slow grin spread across his face as his hands squeezed around the staff. He couldn't wait to test it out some more against proper opponents. The monster-nurses weren't much of a challenge, but like with most dungeons, he was sure this one would have a boss of some kind. Once he'd found Dot, it would be time to see what he was capable of with this new addition to his arsenal.

Walking down the corridor and avoiding the hospital staff, he peered into rooms here and there to heal the occasional human Integrated. With Weaver's Perception, identifying them through walls and even the floor and ceiling wasn't an issue. The nurse-monsters gave ample mana, so he didn't risk running out. A few tried attacking him, but they were never a threat. No matter how far down the corridor he went, it never ended. No hospital rooms had windows showing the outside world, and Luke was pretty sure it didn't exist. This whole dungeon was just infinite corridors with perhaps just as many floors.

Since Trypten's death, he hadn't run into Leom again either, but after a while, her echo joined Dot's. The two of them together didn't bother him much, since neither was the fighting type. If he had to guess, the two were busy healing anyone and anything they could find, human Integrated, Singers, and perhaps even monsters. In that regard, those two were a lot alike.

Finding staircases to move up or down floors wasn't difficult, but it didn't matter. Either the dungeon kept moving him up and down in relation to their location, or they were the ones moving. He couldn't tell which, and in the end, it didn't matter either. What mattered was the fact that he couldn't get to them by normal means. Time was wasting, time he didn't have. To get out of the endless loop, Luke decided it was time to try something else. He'd weavestepped using the echo as an anchor for a location several times now. While never pleasant, using the skill didn't end up with him half dead anymore, or not quite as half dead, at least. It still required a lot of mana, but with all the hospital staff running around and offering themselves up to him, that wasn't too much of an issue.

He found an empty room and barricaded the door by pitching, dragging a cabinet over to block it, then turned off the light and got up on the blood-spattered but empty hospital bed. Closing his eyes, he calmed himself to the best of his ability. Entering a meditative state, mana regeneration kicked in, and this system's weave appeared before his senses. Earth's weave was like that of an intricate carpet, where it might appear chaotic if you were too close, but once you pulled back a little, it became clear there was a method to the madness. In the dungeon, that was not the case. Here, it was pure chaos. Threads twisted in on themselves and connected to other threads at random, or just ran off into the distance with no rhyme or reason. Even attempting to perceive part of the weave made Luke's head spin, and he soon gave up. It was impossible. That didn't matter, though, since all he needed was an echo. Concentrating, he let Dot's thread come to him, wavering through the air while glowing. Other threads almost seemed to reach out, and it got stuck in a tangle, but Luke reached out and grabbed hold of it for just long enough to use Weavestep.

Pain wracked his body as he traveled through the weave, but it was nothing like the first couple of times. He didn't even puke up blood or bleed through his eyeballs.

Like all other times, he appeared right next to Dot. She stood over a hospital bed, with Leom on the opposite side. Both worked on healing a patient in the bed, not-nurses clustering around them so he couldn't make out who was in the bed at first. When he'd pushed past and saw, Luke couldn't believe his eyes. Reaching out with threads of mana, he cut off both of theirs, pushing them off their patient.

"What are you doing, Luke?!" Dot shouted, not showing an ounce of surprise at his appearance.

"What the hell are YOU doing, Dot?!" Luke shouted back, grabbing her shoulder and pulling her away from the hospital bed. "Why are you trying to heal The Fallen Shepherd!?"
