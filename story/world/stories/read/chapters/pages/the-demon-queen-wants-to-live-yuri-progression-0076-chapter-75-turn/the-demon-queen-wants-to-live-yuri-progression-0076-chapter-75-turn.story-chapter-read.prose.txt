"When you said you would rest for today," Ash said, "I thought you meant longer than a single day." Ash sounded amused. She leaned against the trunk of a tree at the edge of the clearing, the morning light finding the gold in her hair.

"Even a single day of rest is more than we can afford, Ash. And besides." I cupped my hands and let a thread of Ruin gather in the right, a thread of Bloom in the left. "I consider this to be quite relaxing, in its own way."

"I suppose you would."

The chamber the Archon had given us today was a forest. This was not a true forest. This was one of the Academy's many resonance chambers, where all of the trees rose tall toward the sky and the light beamed down from somewhere far above. If I did not look too closely I could almost forget the walls. A narrow stream ran along the eastern edge of the clearing. The mana here was thicker than it was outside, but only just. It reminded me of another forest. One where I had woken beneath a blue sky for the first time.

We had come here because the Archon had refused me. I had gone to his office hours ago and demanded the Crest chamber -the one he had once dangled before me. The old man had apologized. Its last occupant, he said, had left it in no state for anyone. Not for a few days. I remembered who the last occupant was well, and so I was forced to believe him.

In its place he had offered this, the chamber for a class called Basic Survival. It was not terrible. Had he offered the desert, there would have been disagreement.

A small chirp came from the stream. Sol waddled along its bank, three steps and a pause, three steps and another pause. She pecked at a smooth stone and chirped. The stone failed whatever standard she had imposed upon it. She chirped more loudly and struck it again. This time, the stone broke under the might of her beak.

Then she stopped. She drew herself up and tucked her wings. She rolled. She rolled until she struck another small stone with a small bump. She righted herself, and continued her waddling.

I did not understand her fascination with rolling. I had watched her do it a dozen times now and understood it no better than the first. Perhaps I would attempt it myself someday, when there was no one to observe. Not even Ash.

I returned my attention to my hands. Ruin ate Bloom, black swallowing white. Bloom pressed back against the black and did not swallow anything at all. "This power," I said, "is far stranger than either Ruin or Bloom."

I had learned that much already. The two streams had to be perfectly matched in power, or the thing between them would not show itself. A hair too much of either, and there was only the eating. It was harder than it looked, which annoyed me. I could manage the precision required perhaps one time in three.

"It felt strange to me too when you used it then," Ash said.

I looked at her then. "You mean from the stands?"

"I do."

I considered this. All Essence carried a taste. The Requiem tasted of endings and the Cradle tasted of beginnings. I had assumed the third thing would feel like both at once. It had felt like that to me, in the moment. I hadn’t considered someone else would think differently. "And what," I asked, "did it taste of?" The fact that power had a taste was still a somewhat alien concept.

Ash considered for long enough that I almost repeated myself. "Like tomorrow could be a new day," she said. "Like tomorrow didn't have to be the same as today." She shrugged. "Everyone probably felt the same thing."

A strange answer. At least it was less poetic than those texts I had suffered through. "That is marginally useful," I said at last. Somewhere by the stream, Sol trilled, as if she too remembered the taste.

There was a large boulder at the clearing's edge. I turned toward it. I channelled Ruin and Bloom together and fired.

The air between my hands cracked and mended. The world split along a hundred lines in one moment, and reformed again in the next. A beam of every color and every shade slammed into the boulder and did…absolutely nothing. The boulder was exactly as it had been before I had used my power.

"Well," I said. "That is progress." It had manifested at all, which was more than could be said for two firings in three.

Ash walked over toward the stone. She tapped it once with her knuckles and eyed it. Then she shrugged, and drew a line in the air above it with her left. Sure enough, the top of the stone split away from the rest of it and hit the ground with a small thud. The boulder's cut was smooth enough to carry a reflection. Ash looked at me and grinned.

"You are showing off, Hero," I said.

The Hero's grin grew wider. "Sure am."

I looked at her grin. It was not a Hero's grin at all, and I decided to let her have it.

I turned my attention back to the larger boulder. This new power of mine presented a strange problem. Ruin always did something when it touched an object. It ended or ceased whatever it touched. Bloom always did something too. It restored. Both would doubtless do as they always had if I fired them now at this rock, and yet this third thing had done nothing, when in the arena it had turned a girl's lightning into pale light. Something about the scenarios was different, but what?

Ah. When I thought about things in those terms, the answer was almost too obvious. These powers of mine, the Beginning and the Ending, both were some form of Concept Magic. Magic that read intent. Why would this third power that came from both of them be any different? Right now, against the boulder, I had not wanted anything. I had no picture in my mind of what should happen, and so nothing did.

I faced the nearer half of the split boulder. I fixed in my mind exactly what I intended and I gathered both powers and fired. Pain flared from the Ember Core and I staggered.

Ash was at my side before I had finished the stagger. "Are you okay?"

"I am well enough, Hero," I said sharply. The Hero went still. I should not have spoken to her so. "I apologize. I'm fine, Ash."

She nodded slowly. We both looked at the boulder. It was exactly the boulder it had been. "Did you try to do anything different this time?" Ash asked.

"I tried to will that miserable lump of rock into becoming a diamond."

"Huh." She considered it. "Well. That would have been a rather convenient power."

"Indeed it would have."

How very vexing. In the old world, every power came with the tools needed to understand it. There was certainly room for experimentation, yes, but experimentation was not guesswork.

"Maybe the change you were trying to make was too much," Ash said. "That could be it."

I looked at her. "Too much?"

"You tried to turn a normal rock into a diamond…that's a big change, isn't it? Maybe you need to try something smaller." She frowned, reaching for it. "Or something similar?"

I thought on the Hero's words. The one time my power had worked, it had turned lightning into light. That was a big change in consequence, perhaps, but not a big change in nature. One brightness had simply become another, however odd. Yes, perhaps there was some merit to this.

I knelt. There was grass beneath me, ordinary green grass. I took a deep breath and loosed this new power. And as I did, the change I wanted to make was very small.

My power struck the grass, and this time it did something. Red spread from the center of the beam and spread outwards across the grass. That color spread blade to blade, until eventually, a patch of the grass was only red and nothing else. During all of this, the Ember Core had been silent.

Ash's eyes were on my face. Then they went to the grass. "That's beautiful," she said.

It was. The red I saw now was the red of my old sky. The perpetual red that had hung over the Demon Realm every hour of every day. I had not pictured this exact shade when I had used my new power, and yet this was what the Becoming had made of the grass anyway. I reached down and touched it. Yes, it was unchanged in every way save for one.

There was a loud chirp. Solara rolled over towards the patch of grass, stopped, and regarded it. She let out another chirp now, one that was surely approving.

"When I used Sever on a flower, the color came back," Ash said. She looked at the red grass. "Yours is staying. I did figure out how to do it right, eventually. I'm surprised you figured it out so quickly. It took me hours."

"So it is. And that is not much of a surprise. This style of magic did make me the most fearsome mortal to ever live, after all." My power changed what the thing was. The grass was red now, and it would be red until I decided otherwise.

There had been an impression there, the same way there were always impressions with Ruin and Bloom. It had been so small. Almost nothing, almost no more than a shrug. As if the blade of grass was content to be grass, and did not care if I chose to make it red grass instead. "This will need far more testing," I said.

"Want me to bring you things to test, then?"

I looked at her. "Perhaps you know me too well, Hero." She smiled, and went to find me things.

The Hero brought me many stones. Some of them were small, some large. Some were so round they were almost spherical. I used my power on all of them with great care. One boulder became a hundred uneven stones, and then a thousand rough pieces of gravel that I had spread into a road to nowhere. From some of the stones, I fashioned two poorly made seats. I could turn the stones into different shapes and forms, but I could not completely transmute them.

For my next test, I sat down at the edge of the stream and cupped water in my hands and tried to turn it into a blade. The test achieved nothing save to waste an almost absurd amount of Essence. The stream of water had an impression, one that came strongly. It had never had any interest in becoming a sword. Apparently, even water had interests in this new world. "The stream," I informed Ash, "is insolent." She laughed.

Eventually, I tried to make a vase out of mud. I had done this once before with my hands. It should have been simpler with my new power. It was not. What I ended up with was a lump with a dent in it. I suspected this was not a failing of my power, for I had been able to fashion a boulder into stone seats. This felt like a failing somewhere within me. Some things, it seemed, still had to be made rather than willed.

I turned the red grass green again, and it went as easily as it had come before. My successes climbed as I learned my new power. My requests had to be precise, and I could not turn a thing into something it could have never been. There had to be continuity and recognizability. Even then, I could not make a single boulder into a great highway capable of marching an army. Those laws still persisted. After some time, I thought I had a good grasp of my new power. At least as far as the inanimate was concerned.

Then Ash brought me a brown rabbit. She held it out to me, and its absurdly large ears looked no more normal now than they had that first night. It trembled in the Hero’s arms but she held it steady. I reached for it with the third thing, and willed its color to change.

The cost was far, far greater. This single test drained more Essence in seconds than the entirety of the tests I had done so far. My body almost shook from the effort. The change took, in the end. The rabbit's brown deepened toward a red it had never worn. And from the impression I understood why it had cost as much as it had.

It was because the creature was refusing me unlike the grass. The grass did not care about what color it was. And while the water had certainly been smug, it had not offered anything akin to resistance. This creature knew what it was, and it did not want to be changed. And so it pushed back against my power, in some way. My Essence had overpowered its own wanting, but the overpowering had cost me.

"I apologize," I said as I reached for the third thing again. I turned the rabbit back to brown. Ash set it down. It sat still for a moment, then bolted for the trees. I watched it go.

Ash steered me to one of the stone seats, though I could have walked well enough on my own. Sol had waddled to a place beneath my feet. She looked up at me and chirped mightily. I sat down, and as soon as I did, the baby phoenix climbed onto my thigh. Her wings flared. I brushed her head, and only then did she sit down.

Ash crouched in front of me. She looked at my chest.  "Your core stops hurting when you use that power, doesn't it?" Ash asked. I said nothing. "I've been hearing it all day. It stops when you get that power to work, and it comes back the second you stop. Don’t tell me I imagine it."

"That is a trivial side effect, Ash, nothing more," I said.

"Is that why you turned grass red then green then red then green again?" she asked. "Or was that supposed to be a 'test' I don't understand?"

I stared at Ash, and she stared back at me without saying a word. The Hero's jaw was set, and her eyes were fixed. She was showing me a Hero's stubbornness.

"This silent siege of yours," I finally said, "is too effective a technique." Still she waited without a word. I looked at the red grass I had made green again. "Yes, Ash. You have not imagined anything. Maybe I was chasing that quiet without realizing it."

The Hero's hand reached for mine. I let her. She waited several seconds before she spoke, and when she did, her voice was hardly louder than a whisper. "When it's bad, I want you to tell me, Lys. Don't just keep it to yourself."

I knew what this was. Ash was asking me to make a promise, like she had once before. That vow had been far grander than this. It was therefore easier to grant. "Very well. I promise," I said.

The Hero slowly nodded. Solara climbed up to my stomach and trilled. Ash squeezed my hand tight for a moment, and then rose. She moved over to the second of the stone seats and sat down. "You know, Lys. If it's quiet you want, I could simply talk less."

"Do not dare," I said at once.

Ash laughed brightly, and Solara let out a loud chirp to match it. Neither sound was disagreeable. Eventually, Solara began to groom one of her black wings. I began to brush at the side the baby phoenix wasn't grooming.

Ash rose from beside me. She took a few steps forward, rolling her shoulders all the while. Then the Hero began a series of stretches. I recognized some of them, for they were the same she had once inflicted upon me. Most of them I had never seen before. I watched Ash do them. There was nothing else in this forest to look at, after all. Her body folded and unfolded. Pressed and unpressed.

Ash's shoulders were broad, and throughout her many stretches, her balance remained excellent. She bent herself forward, and I saw the line of her back. It was just an observation. The Hero turned her head and looked at me. Her face went faintly red, but she did not look away. My own face had some measure of heat, though it was not warm in this chamber. It was I who looked away from her first. It was because of the light coming from above. It was uncomfortable at this angle.

Sol looked from Ash to me, and back to Ash. She tilted her head very far to one side and tilted her head farther still. "What are you looking at?" I asked her. This time she did chirp, loudly. Even I did not know what this particular chirp meant.

"So, Lys," Ash said. "Have you decided?"

"Decided what?"

Ash rolled one shoulder. "The name of your new power, I mean. You've been thinking about it all day. I can tell that much."

The Hero was rather sure of herself. I almost told her that she was wrong. I did not. For as luck would have it, she was correct, on this one occasion. "Coincidentally, I have just now thought of a name."

I cleared my throat, and drew myself up as much as one could upon an uncomfortable stone seat. "I shall name this power: Divine Reconstitution."

The Hero groaned immediately. She even stopped her stretching to do it. "Lys." The Hero's voice sounded pained.

"It is only two words this time. Last time, your issue was the number of words, so I have chosen a name that has only two."

"And what two words they are. Ruin, Bloom, and now Divine Reconstitution. What a trio they make."

"Perhaps it is a little out of place," I conceded. "Very well. I shall simply call it Reconstitution."

"Lys, that single word is the length of three."

"You can just call it Turn," Ash said. I went still. "You're turning one thing into another, so just call it Turn. It makes sense." She paused and looked at me. "But well, you can call it Reconstitution if you want. That's not a bad name either."

I considered Ash's suggestion. This Turn was a rather simple and small word. There was no majesty behind it, nor could I find any hidden meaning. This new power lived between Beginning and Ending, and so surely it deserved more than one syllable-
