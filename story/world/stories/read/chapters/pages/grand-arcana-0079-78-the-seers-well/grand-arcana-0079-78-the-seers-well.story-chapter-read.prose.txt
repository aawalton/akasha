“What will you do if you could see the future?” Avon asked as he took a puff from his pipe.

I let out a sigh. We had known each other for a year now, and he still surprised me with a random question that made no sense. The others used to call it the privilege of the strong. Being fairly certain he would survive the trials, he had the time to worry about things of no importance… or, as some liked to call it, “philosophy.”

“Do all the good things and avoid the bad,” I replied, stoking the fire.

We were fortunate enough to find a cave that had been mostly empty, so could spend the night in relative safety. To my surprise, the entire group had managed to survive the fight, even the rookies.

“If you can change the future, it’s no longer the future,” Avon continued. “It’s just a prediction.”

“I don’t see the point,” I replied, thinking about other things, namely how to survive the rest of the trial.

“That,” Avon pointed his pipe at me, “is the real question.”

Idiot. I shook my head. On the other hand, he was the strongest person in the group by far.

“Sir, sir!” A boy of seventeen ran in from outside. “I found firewood!”

“Oh?” Avon cracked a smile. “Good work, Pup. Enough for the night?”

“More than that!” he dropped a pile of sticks on the cave floor. “Enough for a whole week! I’ll go get more.”

“Don’t,” I said firmly. “You’ll just attract attention.”

“But, sir. I thought the smoke of the fire—”

“Get some sleep,” I ignored him. I knew perfectly well that the fire had made every monster within miles aware of our presence. I just didn’t want to lose anyone pointlessly. If he were so eager to die, he could at least do so for something useful.

“Yes, sir,” the boy said and went to a corner of the cave.

Supposedly, he was a prodigy, gaining a Grand Arcana in his second year. Meanwhile, here I was five years in without any to show for it. From my perspective, there was nothing prodigious in him; the boy had just been lucky, nothing more, nothing less.

“Greg, is there anything that can make you happy?” Avon asked.

“Same as everyone else,” I evaded the question. “Live through the trial.”

“Assuming you live through all of them, what then?”

“The trials will never end.” I stood up and moved away from the fire. “Not until we die.”

I shook my head. There was no reason for me to think of that particular scene. Learning that Eleonor was the Delphi Oracle had triggered it, maybe because it was the first time Avon had forced me to think about future predictions. The funny thing was that he wasn’t really interested in that sort of thing, not specifically in any event. He only enjoyed talking about things that had no answer.

“Where are we going?” I asked.

We had been riding for over an hour, which on these horses meant that we had crossed five times the distance we would if we were sprinting. Curiously enough, we didn’t seem to be heading towards the jungle or the aelf territories.

“The Seer’s Well,” the goth said, as if I were familiar with the term. “It’s a set of ruins,” she added a few moments later, as if that explained it all.

“It’s always ruins,” I muttered.

“Legend claims that a seer’s chained at the bottom, cursed to tell the future of all desperate enough to see it. In this case, part of the legend is true.”

I was tempted to take out my divine blades to check whether there were any strong creatures in the vicinity. So far, we hadn’t come across anything remotely dangerous, and that concerned me.

“How did you become an oracle?” I asked.

“That’s not part of our deal.”

You promised information! “Just making conversation,” I lied. “You used to be the chatty one.”

“Only when you couldn’t talk.”

Despite myself, I had to admit that was a rather good comeback. A bit more practice and she might even reach Serp’s level.

“A seer really is locked in the well,” Eleonor said. “If we reach her, she’ll be able to tell us where our target is. More importantly,” she paused, “she’ll tell us the deity that’s behind it all.”

“Sounds too simple.”

Eleonor glared at me over her shoulder.

“The way to her is protected. Do you think I called you for your looks?”

Once again, I was getting mixed signals. Now that I knew she was an oracle, it made sense. It was said that in order to see the future, they had to be stoned out of their minds. There were also rumors of wild orgies that accompanied their seances, which, given how often she played around with people in their dreams, wasn’t difficult to believe.

You know, maybe she’s not that bad, Serp said like a child promised a truckload of chocolates. You get to keep all the treats from your kills, right?

We rode in silence for half an hour longer. The sun had reached its zenith and was starting its way back down to the horizon. A sharp chill cut the air, making me wish I had brought warmer clothes. Of course, Eleonor had omitted to mention that part of the trip.

If there were ruins, they had to be hidden underground. Our destination turned out to be a circular hole in the ground surrounded by a low wall of black marble tiles. It was notable that time and nature hadn’t had much effect on them, even if they had tried. Dirt and plants covered the greater part of the circular well.

“This is it?” I asked.

It looked a lot more like a pit than a functioning well. If there had been water, it had retreated to the bottom long ago, leaving nothing but the entrance of a very wide shaft behind. Looking down, the bottom was several hundred feet away.

“We'll need more than a rope to reach that,” I said.

Eleonor crosses her arms expectantly.

Of course you’d want me to do the work. I shook my head.

Reaching in from the edge of the wall, I cast an earth spell. A ring of steps emerged, spiraling down towards the bottom. They didn’t continue for long; my current magic only allowed for this much, but with enough persistence we could easily make it.

“You could have done that on your own,” I noted.

“It’s fun watching you do it. Besides, I’m just fulfilling my part of our deal.”

Our deal… Hypnos was already abusing our agreement, just as I thought he might. Or maybe this was all Eleonor herself? After all, the trial’s limitation was in effect for him as well.

“I’ll take the lead,” I climbed up on the edge of the well. “Warn me if anything shows up.”

The further we descended, the more the air filled with the smell of salt. Likely, this must have been a sea at some point. Either that, or Harlequin had a twisted sense of humor. Every twenty steps, I’d pause and cast one of my four known spells again, slowly transforming the inside of the well into a stairwell. Reaching halfway down, I took out my divine blades. The weapons tugged me down almost instantly.

“What’s down here?” I whispered. Whatever they were pointing at was definitely strong.

“Guards,” Eleonor replied. “Black statues, to be more specific.”

“Statues?” This was a new one.

Since the start of my second trial, I had only fought statues once. More appropriately, it was correct to say I had dashed away from them like an idiot.

“Any chance you have some gold on you?” I looked over my shoulder.

The glance she gave me said it all. As far as the goth was concerned, she had already given me my payment and now it was entirely up to me to get her to where she needed to go.

Nothing special awaited us at the bottom of the well. Other than the layers of salt on the walls and the shallow pool of water, there was absolutely zilch. I used my Observer card to examine the marble bricks, even then nothing stood out.

“Move aside,” Eleonor said, noticing my confusion.

Deliberately walking by me, she placed her hand on the wall. There were no threads of aether, no spell circles, or any other type of magic I knew. Rather, it was as if she were searching for the combination to a safe.

“How much do you know about the sides?” she asked as her fingers slid along the rows of salt-covered tiles.

“I get the feeling you’re not talking about the trial,” I said, carefully observing her.

“There’s only one Grand Arcana at the end of this.” She pressed a tile. It sank with an audible click. “Yet it can’t be obtained by a person alone.” Another click.

“In short, an alliance is needed.”

Something felt off.

I didn’t sense any pheromones in the air and, according to the Beastmaster card, there were no animals nearby, and still something wasn’t right. For one thing, the divine blades were acting irrationally, directing me to different parts of the wall. It was as if several strong creatures surrounded us.

“No alliances, not about this,” Eleonor said as another tile clicked. “I thought you knew that.”

Just being polite. “Is that a philosophical question?” I asked. “I’m not into that.”

“You should be.” The goth’s hand slid along the wall as she continued several steps along the wall before pressing the next tile. “It’s better than just practicing in the physical department. Tell me, how is she? Good enough that you’d drop everything just for a fuck?”

Another provocation attempt… Lately they were getting worse and worse. It was as if she had some idea what was important, but was pushing in the entirely wrong direction. I was almost willing to bet that the next comment was going to be about my family outside, or my sister in particular.

“Curious how you jumped into her bed so fast,” Eleonor pressed on. “All the time before you were so reserved, and—” as the tile clicked, a section of the wall moved aside.

The head of a large stone centipede leaped out.

There was no time for words. I dashed forward, shoving Eleonor out of the way as I struck the creature with my left blade.

Red Stone Centipede Statue

Level: 25

Cards: NONE

Special: NONE

Weapon: NONE

The divine swords slammed into the monster’s head. Even with all my current strength, all I was able to do was push it slightly back.

If I had enough gold, I would have started the third awakening trial of my weapons without a second thought. Fighting statues was never a good idea. They were strong, heavily defended, and offered nothing when defeated.

Restrict! I struck the creature again.

Stone legs extended, eager to pierce my flesh. Before they could, a series of force strikes fell upon it from the side, sending the upper part of the centipede into the well wall.

Eleonor had shaken off the initial shock and joined the fight.

Any advice on this, Serp? I asked as I slammed the statue’s head from above.

It’s stone, the serpent blade noted in annoyance. The other swords can handle it.

“How do you kill it?” I shouted.

Of all the many creatures I had faced in my previous run, this was one of the few I had no idea how to defeat. Statues had always been a mystery. Other than their fascination with silver, I knew next to nothing about them. They were frequently present in many of the trials, were difficult to destroy, and didn’t seem to serve any particular purpose.

I put away one of my blades, then took out a handful of silver coins and tossed them at it. The pieces of silver bounced off its head without any effect.

So much for bribery, I thought.

“Cower!” I ordered, using the second skill of my Emperor card.

The force of the order pushed back the surrounding air. In a single instant, both Eleonor and the creature froze, then fell to the ground, trembling like flies sprayed with insect repellent. This wasn’t exactly what I planned, but there was no doubt that it worked. Apparently, cards could affect statues.

Restrict! I struck the statue. Restrict! Restrict! Restrict!

At first, nothing seemed to happen. My actions were no different from those of a child slamming a stick on a boulder. As I continued, however, signs of the effect started to be noticed. The legs of the centipede spontaneously stopped trembling. Shortly later, the rest of its body followed, rendering it a lifeless statue once more.

Unwilling to take a chance, I kept on hitting.

This wasn’t the first time I had seen the full effects of the Gatekeeper card, but it was the first time I had brought an enemy to complete paralysis. There was no escape for it now. Although invulnerable, I had removed the centipede’s desire to move, restricting it to lie there motionless until the next time I lost consciousness.

No longer seeing it as a threat, I turned to the opening. A paved tunnel continued further in. Thankfully, there were no other entities—statues or otherwise—as far as I could see.

Nice way to start the search. I turned to Eleonor.

“Hey.” I put the blade away and bent down over her. “You’re fine,” I whispered, grabbing hold of her.

The woman was shivering like a scared kitten.

“It’s fine. Just stay calm and think through it,” I said, using the Bard’s ability to fix the damage caused by the Emperor. “We won this one,” I added. “We won.”
