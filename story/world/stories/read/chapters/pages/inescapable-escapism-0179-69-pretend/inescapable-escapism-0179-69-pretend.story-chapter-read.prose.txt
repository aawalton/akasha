My palms were sweaty as I came to a stop outside Seth’s door, unable to work up the courage to knock. I wiped my hands on my jogging bottoms, hoping that would help for some reason, but it did not. My hands were less sweaty, but I was still too scared to just reach out and tap on his door.

Noise travelled down the corridor towards me, and I glanced in the direction of the common room before looking forward again. There were people walking towards me. The boys’ dorm was busy, and there were lots of other trainees wandering about. They could all see me standing there, staring at his door without doing anything, and sooner or later, someone was going to stop and ask if I was okay. I could already feel them giving me weird looks.

And if Seth were to open the door for any reason, he’d immediately spot me, and that would probably weird him out. He might think I’d been lurking outside his room for ages, and I assume he’d want to know why I didn’t just knock, but I didn’t have an answer for him. How was I meant to explain that I just couldn’t bring myself to do it for some reason?

It was just Seth, though, I tried to tell myself. I knew him. He was my friend, and I liked spending time with him. I always had a good time when I did, so there was no need to panic or worry about anything. I was just overthinking things, but it was unnecessary.

All I needed to do was gather my courage, reach out, and knock on his door, and then I’d be fine. I’d stop feeling so scared. Part of me knew that was a lie, but I ignored it as I forced myself to take a deep breath before clenching my hands into fists and reaching forward with one.

A grimace flitted across my face as the far too loud knock echoed down the corridor, and scuffling sounds came from behind the door. Before, it had been silent. There had been nothing to indicate whether or not Seth was out of the shower yet, but the moment I’d knocked, that had changed.

My heart pounded loudly in my ears, the sensation wholly unnecessary, as I heard footsteps move closer. Quickly, before he could open the door, I wiped my hands on my trousers again, trying to hide any trace of my anxiety.

“Hey,” Seth said as he opened the door, a smile on his face.

The sight of him made my chest tighten, but I forced myself to ignore the sensation, keeping my face as neutral as possible as I returned the smile.

“Hey,” I replied. “I didn’t keep you waiting too long, did I?”

I hadn’t planned on asking, but his hair appeared almost dry already. I wasn’t sure whether it just dried quickly because of how short it was or how much time I’d taken, but I was concerned.

“No, no, don’t worry,” he told me with a wave of his hand. “Oh, hey! We’re matching.”

Confusion pulled at me for a moment, and I glanced down at myself before looking back at him. He was right. We’d both chosen the navy jogging bottoms and t-shirt, and I couldn’t help but think they looked much better on Seth. Rather than being oversized and baggy, the t-shirt clung to Seth’s arms, making it hard to look away.

“Oh, yeah. We are,” I said, once I’d managed to rip my eyes away from his muscles.

Seth grinned at me again before speaking.

“Shall we go check the common rooms to see if any of the TVs are free, then?”

I wasn’t sure whether I hoped there was a television that we could use or not. As much as part of me did want to spend the evening alone in Seth’s room, I also didn’t. I knew that it might make the way I felt about him even more complicated, and I was worried about that. I was struggling enough already, and I didn’t want that to get any worse, but at the same time…

“Sure,” I replied, my tone much more casual than I felt.

“Cool.” Seth stepped out into the hallway, pulling his door shut behind him. “Did you check the one at the top of the stairs?”

Thankfully, even though I’d been distracted by anxiety, I had remembered to glance into the common rooms I’d passed on the way to Seth’s room.

“Yeah. People were using them all.”

Seth glanced at me, and I felt a spark of anxiety flare within my chest. Did he think I was lying? It kind of sounded like a like, but it wasn’t. The room was busy, pretty much full, and there were other trainees huddled around every TV.

“Oh really?” he asked. “That makes sense. I mean, it is kind of the right time for people to be chilling and watching something.”

Relief washed over me, and I almost let out a sigh.

“Yeah.”

“I guess that’s why the Academy has so many iPads and stuff for us to use. The common rooms are big, but they can only fix so many people,” Seth mused. “So, what kind of movie are you feeling?”

I hadn’t thought that far ahead. It hadn’t even occurred to me that we’d need to choose a film to watch. I’d just been so fixated on the fact that we were going to do it that I hadn’t been able to think of anything else.

“Uhhh, I’m not sure. What do you think?” I asked.

Seth cocked his head to the side.

“I don’t know either,” he said. “I feel like there are so many options here, you know?”

“Yeah, for sure. It’s kind of harder to decide on anything when we have like… every streaming service to choose from.”

“Right?” Seth asked as we came to a stop just inside the doorway. “Ah.”

Looking around quickly, I felt a trill of excitement and anxiety shoot through me. Just like in the other common room, all of the TVs were in use. There wasn’t even any space on the sofas. If Seth and I had wanted to join any of the groups, we would have needed to sit on the floor.

“No space,” I pointed out, even though it was clear Seth had already noticed.

“Yeah,” he said, glancing around the room. “Do you want to check whether there are any free TVs on the girls’ side too? I assume there’s probably not, but there might be?”

“Mmmmm… I don’t really mind. I did look in at the one I passed on my way to yours, but I think people were using them all, so it’s up to you,” I told him. “What do you want to do?”

Seth looked around the room again, seemingly considering it, and I tried not to watch him too closely, but I wanted to know what he was thinking. His expression was inscrutable, though, and I wished that I could hear his thoughts, but that wasn’t possible in the reality I was in.

“We can just grab one of the iPads maybe, then?”

My chest grew a little tighter.

“Sure,” I said, adding a shrug to appear more unbothered than I was.

Seth didn’t seem to care too much about what we did. Either that or he was just much better at hiding it than I was. His face didn’t give away any emotion, and his posture was relaxed. The only hint that he might have been feeling anything was in how quickly he moved towards the cabinet on the wall behind us. Pretty much as soon as I finished talking, he’d stepped towards it and grabbed an iPad.

“Shall we?” he asked.

“Let’s go.”

Neither of us spoke as we walked back along the corridor we’d just been in, but my thoughts were racing. I watched Seth out of the corner of my eye, hoping to see something else that would confirm how he felt about me, but there was nothing.

“Well… welcome to my room,” Seth announced with a slightly awkward smile as he pushed the door open again before hesitating and looking at me. “Or… welcome back, I guess?”

A laugh slipped from my lips, and his smile grew wider and more natural.

“Thanks,” I said. “I like what you’ve done with the place.”

I wasn’t sure where that comment came from. It seemed to just flow out of my mouth without conscious thought, and it didn’t feel like me. It seemed like something I’d heard in a television show or something, but it made a smirk pull at Seth’s lips.

“Why, thank you. I really put a lot of work into making this place feel like home, you know?”

Another laugh escaped me as I looked around again. Seth’s dorm room hadn’t changed at all since the last time I’d seen it, and he’d only just gotten out of the induction period then. He was seeing the room for the first time, just as I was, and it looked identical.

It was just as spotless as it had been then, too. I wasn’t sure whether Seth had tidied it because he knew there was a chance I’d be going into his room or if he always kept it like that, but I was impressed. He’d even made the bed. It was perfect. I couldn’t work out which one he’d been sleeping in, and which was the empty bed, still waiting for Scott to get out of the induction wing.

Seth hadn’t personalised the room at all, but that didn’t surprise me. There wasn’t much we could do. Someone had said something about us being able to hang posters or art on the walls, but I had no idea where we’d even get them from. I suspected that was just something we could bring back from assignments, and I knew that I could have probably asked someone about it, but I just hadn’t gotten around to it.

Plus, the idea of decorating my dorm room made me uncomfortable. The room wasn’t mine. Not entirely, anyway. I was going to be sharing it with Katie and Abbie, and I didn’t want to do anything to it that they might not like. It wouldn’t be too bad. I could always take the poster down if needed, but it still made me anxious, and maybe Seth felt the same way about his room.

“So, uhh…” Seth started as he walked towards the bed closest to the window and dropped onto it. “You can sit down too, if you want?”

“Oh, sure!”

I was still hovering by the door, I realised before forcing myself to take a step towards him. Nerves raced through me as I moved closer to his bed, slipping my shoes off before sitting down and crossing my legs. I was painfully aware of how close we were. Our knees weren’t touching. There was still a gap between us, but I could have closed it easily.

“Wow,” Seth muttered, seemingly more to himself than to me. “They really do have every streaming service, huh? I’ve never heard of some of these. What even is Hayu?”

Leaning closer, I scanned the screen in search of the one he was looking at before finding the reddish logo.

“No clue,” I said. “I’ve not heard of that one either. What’s on it?”

Seth tapped on the app and waited for it to load. A furrow appeared between his eyebrows as he scrolled through the home page.

“Seems to be… mostly reality TV?”

“Oh,” I replied. “Makes sense why I’ve never heard of it, then. I don’t really watch reality TV.”

The only exception was the wild game shows Phoebe and I watched sometimes, where people tried to complete obstacle courses that I was pretty sure were impossible. They were great, but I wasn’t sure if they even counted as reality TV. They were more… game shows? But then again, they were reality, so I wasn’t sure.

“Really? Me either,” Seth said, a smile on his lips. “So, what streaming services did you have before you came here? Or did you not really have any?”

Hesitation slammed into me. I wasn’t sure why, but I had no clue how to answer that question, and I really didn’t want to. It was a normal question, an innocent enough one, but I just couldn’t think of what to say to him.

Perhaps it was that I couldn’t really remember it? I had some memories from my life before. I could access most of them, if not all, but I couldn’t remember whether or not I’d had Netflix or anything like that, and I didn’t want to say the wrong thing. I was scared of accidentally lying to Seth and being caught out later without even realising.

“Uhh,” I started to say.

Seth glanced away from the iPad, his gaze finding my face, and the smile dropped from his lips.

“Oh, shit,” he said. “That’s a specific question, right?”

My hesitation was replaced with confusion. I had no idea what he was talking about or why he’d said that.

“What?”

“We’re not meant to talk about the specifics of our life before we came to the Academy, right?” Seth checked before adding, “Or was that just until we passed the induction period? I can’t remember what Rodgers said about that.”

“Oh,” I replied as realisation dawned on me. “Yeah, I can’t remember either, actually. I think it was just whilst we were in the induction wing? I mean, the whole purpose was to make it harder for us to find each other if one of us got kicked out, right?”

“Yeah. I guess we can talk about that all now, seeing as… you know, it’s unlikely we’re going to get kicked out now,” he said with a laugh. “Unless the whole Academy gets shut down or something.”

I forced myself to smile, but a flare of terror burst to life in my stomach. I didn’t want to even think about the Academy being shut down. Then, I’d have to go back to my parents, and I couldn’t. How could I return to normal life after my time in training?

It would be impossible. I wouldn’t be able to do it. I hadn’t been in the Academy for long, and I was still adjusting to it. I wasn’t used to everything yet, but it felt like home to me. It was more like home than my actual home had ever been in that world, and the thought of giving it up felt like cutting my heart out. I’d be nothing. A shadow of a person, forever chasing the life that had been ripped away from me.

But there was more than that too. I had a future at the Academy. Once I chose my specialism, I had a path mapped out for me. All I had to do was follow it, and I didn’t have that back home. Or in my reality. There was too much choice, too much freedom, and it scared me. I didn’t know how to even begin to narrow it down, but there were people at the Academy who could help me. The teachers could help me figure out what I wanted to do with my life. They were better than the teachers at my school. I was still a little bit scared of them, but I could actually talk to them.

The fear in my stomach burned brighter. That was not the only thing I’d lose if the Academy shut down. I’d never see my friends again. Seth, Katie, Abbie, and Scott. If something happened, and I was sent back home, I’d go back to being alone all the time. I hadn’t spoken to Phoebe in years. She wasn’t my friend, and neither was Duncan. I didn’t even have Rex. There would be no one for me to talk to. My dad would be the only person who actually cared about me, and the thought of that made my heart ache.

That wasn’t true, though. It felt true, but I knew it wasn’t. That was just how I felt in that reality, but it wasn’t my reality. There was a whole other world where I had people who loved me. In my real life, I spoke to Phoebe every single day, and Duncan too, so I wouldn’t be totally alone if something happened to the Academy.

I still felt like I needed it, though. I didn’t want to go back to not having a safe world that I could dip into whenever I wanted to, and there was nowhere else. It seemed like every other world I’d gone to, every other reality, was filled with pain and terror. Even in the worlds that seemed normal, the ones that felt completely safe at first, monsters seemed to lurk just below the surface, waiting to ensnare me. The only exception was Mitch’s world. That was the one other beacon of light amongst the horrors, but I couldn’t go back there. No matter how much I wanted to.

But it wasn’t happening, I reminded myself, trying to force the pain down. Nothing was happening to the Academy. It wasn’t being shut down, and it never would be. The programme had been running for so long, and it was too useful to the country. They needed us. That’s what we’d been told. The assignments we went on, the information we gathered and passed on. It was crucial to the ongoing safety and prosperity of the country. There was no way they’d just give up on that.

“Yeah, I guess we’re stuck here,” I said, aware that the silence between us had gone on for far too long.

Seth glanced at me out of the corner of his eye again, and I tried to force a neutral expression onto my face. I didn’t want him to know what I’d just been thinking about. It was silly, and there was no need for me to be worrying about that. I was in the Academy, and I would stay there until I went out on assignment or graduated, so it was fine. I could continue escaping into the world whenever I wanted.

“Sometimes,” Seth started, “I kind of wish I could forget about my life and pretend it started when I got here.”

His tone was soft, and there was an almost wistful note to it that made me look at him again. His eyes weren’t on mine, though. He was staring into the distance, his expression strangely melancholic.

“I get that,” I said, my voice just as quiet.

Seth’s lips pulled up into a slight smile that didn’t match the look in his eyes, and I felt a wave of guilt wash over me. It felt wrong for me to be agreeing with Seth. It made me feel like I was comparing my life before to his, like I was pretending mine had been just as tough as his was, and I wasn’t. It wasn’t.

My life at home was quiet and depressing. My mom didn’t treat me well. She was mean to me, but Seth’s father was abusive. He’d not told me everything, but he’d said enough that I knew that. He was a monster, and my mom wasn’t. She was just… my mom.

“We can do that, if you want?”

“Do what?” I asked.

Seth’s gaze found me, and I could feel myself being sucked in. Even if I’d wanted to, I don’t think I would have been able to look away from him.

“Just… not talk about it,” he suggested. “Pretend life started the day we got to the Academy, and the stuff that happened before just… didn’t happen.”

The urge to agree with him swept over me immediately, but part of me hesitated. It whispered that it wasn’t right. I felt as though I was betraying my dad by pretending to forget about him, and I didn’t want to do that. I loved him in every world, and I loved my mom too. It was just… harder.

But Seth seemed to need it. He looked like he needed me to agree. I could see the hope in his eyes. It bordered on desperation, and I knew that he wanted me to say yes. He didn’t want to talk about his past or think about it ever. The Academy was a fresh start for him, a place where he could be unbothered by everything else that had happened. It was just as much an escape for him as all of my other worlds were for me, and I couldn’t take that away from him. It would be too cruel.

Surely, that couldn’t be healthy, though. I wasn’t certain where I’d picked it up from, but I knew that it wasn’t good to ignore all of the bad things that had happened. It meant that they would just become repressed, and they’d never actually be dealt with, so they’d continue to be a problem. They’d linger in the back of our minds forever, until we actually did something about them, and I knew that, but I couldn’t bring myself to say that to Seth.

Maybe one day, he’d deal with it. Pretty much all of the trainees at the Academy saw a therapist, so perhaps Seth would open up to them, and they’d help him deal with it. That would be good. It was the therapist’s job to help him with that, but it wasn’t mine. I didn’t need to force Seth to face it if he didn’t want to.

“Sure,” I said. “So, what do you want to watch?”

Seth’s gaze remained fixed on my face for a few more seconds, and I could see the relief wash over him. His posture sagged slightly, becoming less tensed, and the desperation drained from his eyes. Seth’s lips pulled into a smile, and he looked away from me, glancing down at the iPad once more.

“How about this one?”
