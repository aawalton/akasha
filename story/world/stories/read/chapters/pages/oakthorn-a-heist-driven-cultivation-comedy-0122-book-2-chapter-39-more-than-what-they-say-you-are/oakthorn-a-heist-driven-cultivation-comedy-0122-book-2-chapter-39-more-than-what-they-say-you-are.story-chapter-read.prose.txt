Cade and Bunny reached the top of the crumbling bricks and moss-infested stones with a final heave of effort. The ancient cathedral’s vaulted ceiling concealed a network of stone ledges and crumbling buttresses—architectural remnants of a civilization that understood power flows upward.

The young thief pressed himself against the weathered stonework, his breath a shallow whisper as Bernard’s ritual unfolded beneath him. From this height, the green-tinged glow from the seven sigils formed a macabre constellation on the cathedral floor, with Chubbs at its heart like a sacrificial star.

Step one: Don’t die. Step two: Save Chubbs. Step three: Figure out step four.

The ritual circle below pulsed with malevolent purpose, drawing tendrils of golden light from Chubbs’ small form. Each pulse corresponded with one of the seven floor sigils—a magical circuit designed to channel her essence into something ancient and terrible. Bunny nipped at the end of Cade’s quill nervously.

C’mon. Tell me your secrets, Cade silently prompted the complex runework, pulling his writing utensil out of the dragonling’s mouth.

He pulled out his Lifekeeper tome and started to compare various sections. But it was like trying to translate a dead language into another tongue that he was barely conversant in. Which, in a way, was exactly what he was trying to do. But with each passing moment, his eyes started to make sense of the swirling green lines and jagged edges of the seal.

Cade traced the patterns in a spare journal, committing each line and curve to memory. Unlike most modern magic that relied on intuitive flows, this ritual was mathematical in its precision. He had to give it to the Progenitors who made this place and the seal therein. It was masterfully crafted.

He counted seven anchor points, five internal gateways, three containment barriers, all feeding into the central dais.

“Okay, now comes the hard part,” Cade said as he circled a section in his recreated diagram.

Bunny growled nervously and kneaded his claws into the stone next to Cade’s face. The thief turned to give the white dragonling a gentle brush down his scaly neck, calming him immediately. His gaze returned to the unfolding scene below. His crew had started their stealthy approach, and he could see them lurk between pews and toppled pillars toward their targets. Gav moved on silent feet while Nora twisted and prodded the Pandemonium Box from her hidden alcove. Evie, staff in hand, comically rolled and danced toward the lumbering figure of Augustus Vale near the entrance to the cathedral. Despite her odd and totally unrealistic attempts at going unseen, it worked. It was like someone was offering her advice on exactly when and where to move.

“You lucky siren,” Cade breathed with a warm smile tugging at his lips.

His gaze returned to his journal. He tried out several runic patterns, erasing some and trying out others. After a few painstaking minutes atop the ledge, he figured out the right combination. The change he was planning to make to it was simple. Just a few altered lines and that should do it. But to make those lines without burning his flesh from his bones… That was going to be a challenge.

He put the two books back into his pack and nestled as far as he could into the shadows of his perch. Black clouds laden with rain gathered overhead. With a deep breath, he got into his meditative stance, legs crossed and hands open-palmed toward what one could loosely argue was the ceiling.

And I didn’t tell anyone that part of the plan, Cade thought, wincing at his own stubbornness. Because why make things simple when you can add an extra layer of idiotic secrecy?

But he knew why he neglected to mention this final part of his plan to the others. He feared they would tell him to forget it. To focus on rescuing Chubbs. But they didn’t understand just how overwhelmingly outmatched they were. If he stood any chance against Evil Bernard and stopping this ritual, he needed to do this.

He needed to reach gold rank.

“Okay. It’s time. Go get ‘em, troublemaker,” Cade whispered, giving Bunny a quick hug around the neck. He pulled back so he could look the dragonling in his adorably large green eyes. “Remember the plan—distract, don’t engage. Bernard’s way out of your weight class.”

Bunny gave him a look that somehow managed to convey both deep offense and absolute confidence.

“I’m serious,” Cade murmured. “No heroics. Just keep his attention while I...” He trailed off, still uncertain how to articulate what he was attempting. “While I level up, I guess.”

The dragonling huffed, then nuzzled briefly against Cade’s cheek—a gesture so unexpected that Cade nearly lost his balance. Before he could respond, Bunny leapt from his shoulder, gliding on injured wings across the cathedral’s vast open space.

Cade turned his attention inward, even as his eyes tracked the movements below. His destruction magic thrummed beneath his skin, silver-gold energy. Nora’s meditation techniques had helped, but they weren’t enough. He needed to go deeper, to understand the fundamental relationship between these two forces. He knew it had something to do with order and chaos, but beyond that, he was stuck.

Below, Bunny had reached the central dais. With characteristic audacity, the dragonling simply hopped onto the altar beside Chubbs, chittering at her reassuringly before turning his attention to Bernard.

The possessed crime lord froze mid-incantation, the six emerald eyes embedded in the crown that had fused with his skull widening in shock.

“What is this?” Bernard’s voice resonated with ancient timbre, confusion evident in its layered tones. “A Cloudrift dragonling? Impossible. Your kind were extinguished during the Culling War, were you not?”

Bunny chirped, cocking his head with apparent curiosity.

“Clearly, you are no foul illusion nor conjuration of my ancient mind,” Bernard continued, his voice softening to something almost reverential. “Truly, before me is a child of the Progenitors, preserved across the millennia. Most fortunate! Most fortunate indeed, little sire.”

Cade nearly lost his concentration. Progenitor? Bunny is a Progenitor?

“Join us, little brother,” Bernard extended a hand toward Bunny. “Help us shatter the chains of these usurper gods. We shall remake the world in the image of those who came before—our true ancestors. Your ancestors.”

The revelation hit Cade like Destruction’s backhand. Bunny—his chaotic, occasionally destructive, unfailingly loyal companion—was apparently descended from the same ancient race that had once challenged the gods themselves.

Focus, Cade commanded himself. Questions later. Survival now.

The ritual’s intensity was increasing, the sigils pulsing faster as Bernard’s dark work progressed. Chubbs whimpered, her small face contorted in pain as more golden essence was drawn from her.

Time was running out.

Cade plunged deeper into meditation, seeking the twin core of his magic. The sphere of silver-gold energy materialized in his mind’s eye, though there was a good deal more more gold mixed in there than he’d remembered from even just a little bit ago. The crimson threads were more visible now, forming patterns that spiderwebbed in swirling patterns around and through the Destruction magic.

Through half-lidded eyes, he watched the battle unfold below. He caught glimpses of Evie, her delicate frame belying the steel beneath as she called out to Augustus. The brute lunged for her with corrupted earth magic crackling along his massive frame.

“Oh my!” A theatrical voice that was decidedly not Evie’s emanated from her direction. “What an uncouth display of aggression from the gentleman. One might suggest anger management techniques, or perhaps a proper education in courtly manners!”

Guildford, Cade realized with a mix of amusement and concern. The semi-incorporeal faun floated around Evie, potbelly wobbling as he prepared to meet Augustus head-on. Evie dodged under an arc of green and brown stone, retreating down the steps of the cathedral.

“Come back here, you little bitch!” Augustus bellowed, his voice distorted by corruption.

“Language!” Guildford admonished through Evie’s lips, every syllable dripping with aristocratic disdain. “There is a CHILD present, you unconscionable brute! Have you no sense of decorum whatsoever?”

Across the cathedral, Elena moved like shadow given form, engaging Nash with enough ferocity to make a fully changed lycanthrope pause. The corrupted fire-mage hurled emerald flames that left scorched trails across ancient stone, but Elena was always just beyond their reach, her whip-dagger singing through the air. She used it to redirect his wrists just enough to make him miss.

Behind a fallen column, Nora hunched over the Pandemonium Box, her fingers tracing its intricate patterns while occasionally glancing up to ensure no threats approached. Her lips moved silently, working through the riddle inscribed on its surface: Born in fire but fearing wind, I am as strong as a dragon’s breath yet as delicate as a fairy’s wing.

“You can do this, Nora,” Cade whispered from his perch.

Meanwhile, Gavin stalked Rufus through the shadows, amber eyes gleaming as partial transformation rippled across his features. The debt collector’s corrupted water dominion left sizzling trails across the stone floor, but Gav matched his movements with inhuman grace.

They’re buying me time, Cade realized. Now I need to use it.

He returned his focus to his inner landscape. The silver-gold sphere pulsed rhythmically, but the crimson threads remained constant, their patterns forming a lattice that somehow contained without constricting.

Right. They’re not fighting against the destruction magic, he realized. They’re working with it. Guiding it.

As this insight settled once again, a memory surfaced from the darkness of his mindscape—his father’s face, calm and resolute, tracing glowing symbols on infant Cade’s chest.

“Remember, Cade,” his father’s voice echoed across time. “Pure magic lacks purpose. It is only through change that power finds meaning.”

The vision shifted—his father kneeling before him, hands on Cade’s tiny shoulders. “What flows must be guided, not controlled. What burns must be directed, not contained. Do you understand what I’m trying to tell you, son?”

He watched his younger self blush in shame but shake his head. His father sighed and ruffled his hair.

“You will. By the Dominions, I hope you do someday, son.”

Below, the battle intensified. A roar of triumph drew Cade’s attention to Nora, who had sprung to her feet, the Pandemonium Box glowing in her hands.

“A CANDLE!” she shouted, her voice echoing through the cathedral. “It’s a FUCKING CANDLE!”

The box responded instantly, ancient mechanisms clicking and shifting beneath her fingers. Sections that had seemed immovable rotated freely, glowing sigils aligning in perfect symmetry.

With a final twist, Nora completed the sequence. The box shuddered in her hands, then burst open with explosive force. Instead of chaos, however, it released what appeared to be origami soldiers—hundreds of them, crafted from paper so thin it seemed translucent, yet moving with deadly purpose.

“What the actual hell?” Elena’s voice carried up to Cade’s perch.

The paper soldiers formed ranks, their tiny weapons gleaming with unexpected menace. At Nora’s gesture, they charged toward a section of wall that had begun to bulge inward. The stone cracked and shattered, revealing corrupted abominations summoned by Bernard—skeletal creatures with too many limbs and emerald fire burning in empty eye sockets.

The origami army met them head-on, paper-thin blades somehow slicing through bone and corrupted flesh with impossible efficiency. Each fallen monster collapsed into dust that the paper soldiers seemed to absorb, growing more substantial with each victory.

Cade’s first instinct was to abandon his meditation and join the fray. His crew was fighting for their lives while he sat in relative safety, watching.

The worst part of leadership, Hugh had once told him in a rare fit of paternal advice, isn’t making the hard calls. It’s trusting others to handle their piece while you handle yours.

With painful effort, Cade forced himself to remain still. The paper army was holding the line. His team was executing their tasks. Nora directed her unexpected forces with tactical precision while Elizabeth, the corrupted paladin, charged toward her with righteous fury.

“Elena!” Nora called out, parrying Elizabeth’s massive blade. “Evie needs backup! Augustus is pushing her toward the marshes!”

Elena immediately disengaged from her nearly-defeated opponent, sprinting across the cathedral floor with inhuman speed. “On it!”

They’re doing their part, Cade reminded himself. Now do yours.

He sank deeper into meditation, past the surface fluctuations of his magic to the very core of his power. The silver-gold sphere of destruction magic pulsed with increasing intensity, while the crimson threads wove more intricately through it, forming patterns that reminded him of constellations.

Bernard’s voice carried up from below, filled with ancient rage as Bunny continued to evade his grasp. “Ungrateful spawn! You would side with these vermin against your own kind?”

Chubbs cried out as another pulse of golden energy was drawn from her small form. The sound cut through Cade’s concentration like a physical blow.

I’m out of time.

With desperate resolve, Cade reached for both energies simultaneously. Instead of trying to force them into alignment, he surrendered to their natural flow, allowing them to move through him without resistance.

The effect was immediate and overwhelming. Power surged through him, not in chaotic bursts but in controlled waves. The crimson threads and silver-gold energy no longer competed for dominance—they complemented each other, creating a harmony that transcended their individual strengths.

Chaos, a voice whispered from the depths of his memory. His father’s voice. In you, is Chaos, my son. Wield it. Make it yours. Save them, son.

The revelation hit Cade with physical force. Not some nameless crimson energy, but Chaos itself—the primordial force that predated even the gods.

And his father had somehow given him this gift, knowing what it would mean.

Understanding flooded through him, crystallizing years of confusion into perfect clarity. He remembered this moment when his dad spoke with such soft intensity. He thought he’d been talking metaphorically, assuring his son that it was okay to break the molds the world tried to put him in.

But that wasn’t it—or all of it, at least.

This clarified everything in Cade’s life concerning his magic. It explained his inability to fully control destruction magic, especially in the beginning—it was because destruction was only half of his power. The crimson threads of Chaos weren’t meant to constrain destruction, but to transform it, guide it, give it purpose beyond mere annihilation.

He’d been wrong. This power wasn’t order. It was merely giving a way for the destruction to go. It wasn’t trying to control it at all. It was freeing it.

With newfound certainty, Cade allowed the two magics to flow together naturally, like tributaries joining to form a mighty river.

The effect was immediate and staggering. Power surged through him, more intense than anything he’d experienced before. His skin glowed from within, silver-gold veins intertwined with a brilliant crimson as the two magics finally, truly, merged.

And with that final convergence, the barrier between silver and gold—the final threshold of his rank—shattered like glass.

Energy rushed through him as he felt his core condense, this time as twin powers danced to form a single perfect sphere. Red wove through gold. But where Destruction’s magic had been an ocean from which he could only take single drops from, now it was like the world had been opened up. He could already tell that he’d have to be careful. Chaos might’ve freed his core from any inhibitors once on it, but that didn’t mean his body could withstand that level of power coursing through his mortal form.

Not yet, at least.

The core solidified inside of him in that darkness of his mind.

Gold rank. He’d reached gold rank.

Before he could emerge from his internal proprioception, another flash snaked across his thoughts. It seemed to be sourced in those clusters of memories around his father. This time, though, the images crashed into him like a dam not of magic but of his own making, had been destroyed.

He was young. The vision focused in on his father’s neck, exposed as he bent to tie young Cade’s shoe. A tattoo of broken chains and shackles, identical to the one he’d seen on Hugh.

His thoughts froze, victory temporarily forgotten as he lost himself in this repressed memory. That tattoo. How had he missed it? His dad had one, Hugh did, but that wasn’t what stabbed through his mind like a rancid claw.

No. That tattoo was identical to the ones worn by the men who had killed his father.

“They fear what they cannot understand,” his father whispered as he frantically tied Cade’s left shoe. “So be sure to not let their fear define you. Once I’m gone.”

Young Cade looked at his dad. “You’re leaving, papa?”

“Just until Uncle Thaddeus says it’s okay,” his father replied.

The memory faded, leaving Cade breathless with unexpected grief and clarity. Whatever his father had known—whatever he had done to Cade’s core—it was connected to these crimson threads that now wove through his destruction magic.

Cade opened his eyes, seeing the world with new clarity. The ritual circle below was no longer just a pattern—he could perceive the flows of energy within it, the vulnerabilities in its design. Bernard’s corrupted power was substantial, but not infallible. The ritual could be altered, redirected. And now, his magic was more than up for the task.

His gaze settled on a particular section of the ritual circle—a junction where three lines of power converged. If he could modify that node, he might be able to disrupt the flow without collapsing the entire structure.

Cade dropped silently from his perch, using his newly controlled magic to cushion his descent. He kept to the shadows, moving with supernatural stealth as Bernard focused on fending off Bunny’s persistent harassment.

The battle raged around him—Nora’s paper soldiers cutting through waves of skeletal abominations, Elena helping Evie and Guildford lure Augustus toward the crypt entrance, Gavin locked in mortal combat with Rufus. Each member of his crew fought with everything they had.

Cade reached the ritual’s outer perimeter undetected. Up close, the magical geometry was even more complex, but his enhanced perception allowed him to see the underlying structure. He identified the weak point—a section where the power flowed toward Chubbs rather than away.

Kneeling beside the glowing lines, Cade extended his hands. Crimson-gold energy danced across his fingertips as he prepared to rewrite the ancient magic. His hands trembled, not with fear but with the weight of potential consequences. One mistake could accelerate the ritual instead of disrupting it. One error could kill Chubbs instead of saving her.

But doing nothing meant certain failure.

Here goes everything, Cade thought, pressing his palms against the stone floor. Chaos and Destruction flowed through him in perfect harmony as he began to reshape the ritual’s foundations.

The ancient magic resisted, fighting his intrusion with malevolent purpose. Cade gritted his teeth, sweat beading on his forehead as he forced his will upon the ritual’s structure. Inch by inch, line by line, he bent the flow to his design—not breaking the circle outright, but subtly altering its purpose.

Please don’t let this kill us all, he prayed silently to whatever gods might be listening, as his golden-red energy sank deeper into the ritual’s heart.
