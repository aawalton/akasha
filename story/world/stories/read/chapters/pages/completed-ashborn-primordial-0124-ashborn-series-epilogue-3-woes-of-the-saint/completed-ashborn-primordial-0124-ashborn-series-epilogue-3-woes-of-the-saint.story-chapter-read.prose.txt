Saint Tiyana Matali could not believe the amount of paperwork that came with prosperity. The number of sleepless nights that would be required to ensure harmony, even in an era of peace and prosperity.

Especially in an era of peace, it seemed.

“My office is a Vimana! Half the Asuras in the realm report to me, and some lowly scribe in New Kaia requires rework? From me? This would never have happened during the war.”

And thank the gods the other Vimana continued to remain dormant, she thought.

“A good sign, Tia,” Haymi consoled, handing her yet another stack of papers due to be signed. “People have the time and the will to ensure things are done right. You know you have a small army screening requests, and then us. I can assure you only the most important requests get set upon your desk. ”

“I’m aware,” Tia seethed, staring at the stack of papers. She knew that every one of those represented progress. The first steps to prosperity.

Yet even a prana-enhanced constitution could only go so far, to say nothing of her mind. The realm had mourned. They’d accepted their losses and moved on. When would she?

“Tell you what,” Haymi said, coming behind Tia’s chair to massage her shoulders. “Why don’t you take a break? Go make some rounds around the Vimana. We’re above uncharted lands now. The view is quite spectacular.”

“Yeah?” Tia asked, unconvinced. Any break now would just mean burning the midnight prana lantern later.

“Absolutely,” Haymi replied. “Take a bath. I’ll schedule you a massage. The gods know you need one. Look at how stiff your shoulders are!”

“Hmm,” Tia said, reveling in the sensation of Haymi’s massage. “Maybe… maybe I will, then.”

Tia left her office finding herself at a complete loss as to where to visit.

Yes, there were any of the fifty floors, each chock full with busy accountants, Sawai lords in residence, military officials, city planners, and more. The list went on, and each would absolutely love to get even a moment of Saint Matali’s time… but Tia had no desire to see them.

She had no desire to see anyone right now.

What she wanted—what she needed—was a friend.

Someone who understood her woes and who sympathized with her complaints. As lucky as she was to have Haymi and Vason, they hadn’t dealt with the responsibility Tia had. They didn’t truly comprehend the weight upon her shoulders.

No, what she needed was someone like… someone like Maiya.

But Maiya was dead and gone. Hopefully resting in peace somewhere with her late husband.

And so, it was in a daze that Tia found herself walking the catwalks among the clouds. Exposed to the wind, all alone.

It was there, at the balcony of the Gods, high above the realm she now led and impossibly alone, that she allowed the roiling storm she’d kept bottled within to break loose.

Where none saw her shake in sorrow. Where none heard her bare and naked wails. Where none judged her confessions to the enemy she’d slain.

To the friend she’d betrayed.

“Why?” she whispered to the air. “Why did it have to end like this, Vir? Why couldn’t I… I wanted to say sorry. To apologize for what I did to you! Not to ki-kill you! I wanted to show you how far I’d come. Of the amends I made! Why!? And now I will never be able to. I will live with this regret for the rest of my days as I fulfill my last oath to you. As I shepherd humanity and demonkind into a new Age.”

She was shouting at this point. Baring her soul for no one to hear.

“And you won’t be there to see it. This world you’ve built. This legacy of yours… and you will never know the fruits of your labor, Vir. This realm without you is… lost, and we both know I can’t hope to guide them. You died and left me alone. And I will curse you forever!”

“Oh?” said a voice. “And is that all?”

Tia froze in place. Every muscle in her body going stiff, pinning her in place.

There shouldn’t have been anyone up here, so how!?

Her heart threatened to burst out of her chest, never had it beat so fast or so hard.

Slowly, mustering her will, Tia turned.

Fully expecting to see someone.

Perhaps an aide, sneaking up on her. Perhaps a demon, come to take revenge.

What she saw was…

“Prana?” she breathed, staring in wonder at the swirling energy, simultaneously every color in the rainbow and none at all.

White and black and neither at the same time.

She stood transfixed as the energy coalesced, taking form.

Taking the form of a figure. A figure she recognized, despite the unusual garb. A figure with night-black hair and bloodred eyes and pale gray skin. Wearing a loose white-button shirt, his hands tucked in black pant pockets.

Her eyes bulged. Her heart leaped.

“Vir…”

The demon gave her a wry smile. “Did you really think it would be so easy… to kill a god?”
