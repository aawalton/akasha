“I managed to plant a high-grade spiritual plant, but who would’ve thought a monkey would appear out of nowhere and destroy it?”

“I had a similar experience. Fortunately, I learned a combat martial art in my spare time, so I was able to fend it off.”

“In the end, though, the number of weeds was just too much.”

Ning's vision snapped back into focus amid the scattered complaints of the other candidates.

The stone floor was beneath his feet again, and the familiar scent of damp soil and crushed leaves filled the Spiritual Plant Hall. After spending what had felt like several days inside the illusion, returning to reality so abruptly left him momentarily disoriented.

Ning slapped his cheeks lightly and shook his head. "Must be a side effect of the illusion."

Even with the lingering dizziness, though, the experience had been more than worthwhile.

The Serpent Flower alone had taught him quite a bit. Reading about its cultivation in a book was one thing, but actually managing several plants at once had made him appreciate just how troublesome their roots could become.

His original plan of creating separate trails of concentrated spiritual qi had worked well enough in the beginning. Each root naturally followed the richest path available, allowing Ning to control where it spread without interfering with the plant directly.

The problem was that plants grew.

As their root systems expanded, the carefully separated paths became increasingly crowded. A route that had been perfectly safe earlier could bring two plants dangerously close together several hours later, forcing Ning to constantly alter the distribution of spiritual qi beneath the field.

There had been other surprises as well. When the elders began spawning weeds throughout the field, Ning initially relied on Scorching Purge to remove them, but the mature Serpent Flowers eventually began handling much of the problem themselves. Their roots aggressively competed for spiritual qi and nutrients, while the poison they released into the surrounding soil inhibited many of the ordinary weeds trying to grow nearby.

The manuals had mentioned both characteristics separately. Ning simply hadn't appreciated how strongly they affected each other in practice.

Of course, that aggressiveness created problems of its own. If he ever planted Serpent Flowers in a real field, he would have to pay much closer attention to neighboring crops and the condition of the soil. Letting their roots spread freely might save him some effort with weeds, but it could just as easily poison half the field.

Fortunately, none of those mistakes mattered here.

That was probably the greatest value of the Hundred Illusion Treasure Mirror.

Ning could experiment, push plants harder than he normally would, and even deliberately try methods he wasn't confident in without wasting months of work or a single real seed. Some details of the experience were already becoming slightly hazy now that he had awakened, but the practical understanding remained.

It was basically a cultivator's farming simulator, except frighteningly realistic and actually useful.

Ning glanced back at the bronze-framed mirror.

Illusion techniques always seemed to get treated rather poorly in the novels he'd read. Some protagonist would grit his teeth, remember his tragic backstory, shout something about his Dao heart, and suddenly become immune to everything.

Yet an artifact like this made it obvious how wasteful it was to think of illusions purely as combat techniques.

"Maybe when I'm stronger, I can forge something similar," Ning murmured. "Or at least borrow this one."

This was probably the first artifact he'd encountered that he genuinely wanted for reasons completely unrelated to fighting.

With something like this, who would have to worry about their proficiency numbers not going brrr?

Before Ning could begin calculating how many techniques he could practice inside the mirror, a sharp voice cut through the hall.

"Enough. Stop crowding like geese."

The chatter died instantly, and everyone quickly straightened themselves.

“Now,” the elder continued, “I will announce the results.”

“Ni Lunxia takes first place. He successfully cultivated three high-grade plants: Blood Spirit Rice, Grey Wisteria, and Flame Spirit Herb. He has reached great accomplishment in four basic farming spells, excluding the Golden Finger Technique, and has achieved minor accomplishment in the Earth-Shaking Hoe Technique.”

Ning blinked.

As expected. No matter how confident he was in his skills, there was always someone better.

He glanced sideways, his gaze landing on an older man who appeared to be around fifty. Wearing a straw hat, he carried the unmistakable air of a seasoned farmer.

Sensing Ning’s gaze, Ni Lunxia nodded. Ning returned the gesture.

He remembered now that this senior had placed highly in the first test as well. It seemed practical farming was where he truly excelled.

“Second place: Ji Ning. He cultivated three high-grade plants: Frozen Breath Grain, Dragon Qi Grass, and Serpent Flower. He achieved great accomplishments in the Withering and Flourishing Technique, Small Rain Cloud Technique, and Soil Refining Technique. He has also reached minor accomplishments in Golden Finger, Scorching Purge, and Hidden Ice Mist.”

“Third place: Fang Tian.”

“Fourth place: Cui Xia.”

“Fifth place: Fei Shin.”

“Sixth place: Xin Fu.”

As the names were read out in rapid succession, some rejoiced while others despaired.

“How is this possible?!”

“No… no way…”

“I did it…”

Ning watched quietly, struck by how familiar the scene felt. It reminded him strongly of Earth.

“He’s so young; how could he have mastered so many techniques?” someone shouted.

Ning recognized the voice.

Xin Fu.

The man truly was something else. Even after repeated setbacks, he never forgot to say something discordant.

This time, however, his complaint gained traction.

After all, aside from Ning, who honestly looked barely fifteen, the others were all middle-aged or elderly men with long white beards. Standing among them, Ning’s youth was glaringly obvious.

The elder, however, snorted coldly. “Look at you all, sore losers who’ve learned nothing but to grow old!"

His words instantly silenced the hall.

Ning blinked at the elder’s crude phrasing, then spoke calmly. “The origins of my spells are clear. All of my basic spells, as well as the Hidden Ice Mist Technique, were obtained from the Spirit Hall. As for how I learned them, isn’t consistent practice enough for improvement to come naturally?”

The elder gave Ning a long look before nodding.

"Regardless of how you learned them, being able to use so many techniques proficiently at your age is impressive."

"Thank you, Elder."

Ning accepted the praise without arguing. There wasn't much else to say. He had spent years practicing these techniques whenever he had the opportunity, and the results were there for everyone to see.

The elder turned his attention back to the other candidates.

"That is enough. This assessment exists to judge your ability as spiritual farmers, not to give you excuses for why another disciple performed better."

His gaze settled on Xin Fu.

"Especially you. This is your final warning. If you have evidence that the assessment is unfair, present it. Otherwise, stop disrupting the examination."

Xin Fu's expression tightened, but this time he lowered his head.

"...Understood, Elder."

"Good."

The elder swept his gaze across the remaining candidates.

"If you have time to concern yourselves with how another disciple cultivated his plants, use that time to improve your own methods. The assessment is not over yet."

The hall remained silent.

Ning watched Xin Fu wilt once again, but his attention soon shifted to the two disciples standing behind him.

They were snickering.

Ning remembered seeing them talking with Xin Fu earlier, and if his memory served him right, one of them had placed fifth in the first test.

Huh. That was interesting.

He couldn't say for certain that they had deliberately pushed Xin Fu into confronting him, but they certainly hadn't stopped him. Every time Xin Fu caused trouble, he was the one who shouted, the one who got scolded, and the one whose reputation suffered. Those two simply stood behind him and agreed.

And with only five certificates available, Xin Fu losing his composure benefited quite a few people.

Ning looked at him again.

Xin Fu was arrogant and hotheaded, but Ning was beginning to suspect that at least some of his hostility had been encouraged by the people around him. Not that it excused his behavior. Nobody had forced him to open his mouth.

Still, if Ning had noticed it, there was no reason not to point it out.

Soon, the next test began.

"The third test is disease observation! Enter the room when called!"

The crowd gradually thinned as candidates were called inside. This time, rather than finding a corner to wait in, Ning walked toward Xin Fu.

The man was standing near a stone pillar with his arms crossed, looking thoroughly miserable.

"Senior Brother Xin."

"What?" Xin Fu replied harshly. "Here to mock me too?"

"No. If I wanted to mock you, I wouldn't bother coming over."

That made Xin Fu pause.

"...Then what do you want?"

"I just want to talk." Ning glanced toward the two disciples from earlier. "Those two behind you. Are you close with them?"

Xin Fu followed his gaze. "We've talked a few times. They agreed with me about you."

"Did they?"

Xin Fu frowned. "What do you mean?"

"They were letting you shout," Ning said. "Big difference."

"What?"

"Think about what happened. You accused me, the elder got angry, and you were the one who got warned. What happened to them?"

Xin Fu didn't answer.

Ning waited.

"Nothing."

"Exactly." Ning nodded.

Xin Fu looked toward the two disciples again.

"I'm not saying they're responsible for everything you've done," Ning continued. "You shouted those words yourself. But every time you get angry, they agree with you, you speak up, and then you take all the consequences."

Xin Fu's expression gradually changed.

Ning shrugged. "Maybe they're deliberately using you. Maybe they just realized you're easy to rile up and decided to take advantage of it. Either way, you're the only one paying for it."

For a while, Xin Fu said nothing.

Then he asked, "Why are you telling me this?"

"Because I'd rather settle this now than have you keep blaming me if you fail." Ning smiled.

Xin Fu remained silent for a moment before taking a deep breath. The anger that had followed him throughout the assessment finally seemed to settle.

"...I won't forget this."

"Ji Ning! Enter!"

His name had finally been called.

"That's my turn. Good luck, Senior Brother Xin."

Ning gave him a casual wave and headed for the examination room.

There wasn't any complicated reason behind helping Xin Fu. Their conflict was still minor, and Ning would rather end it here than let something this stupid turn into an actual grudge.

Besides, Xin Fu was still sixth. Whether he regained his composure and earned one of the five certificates was his own problem.

Ning had already said what needed to be said.

Thinking about it, half the conflicts in the stories from his previous life could probably have been avoided this way. People misunderstood something, refused to explain themselves, and somehow spent the next fifty chapters making everything worse.

Romance stories were particularly bad about it.

Communication. Truly an underrated art.

...

Ning stepped into the room, and before he could even take a second look around, the elder spoke.

"Identify the diseases affecting these plants. Write down the cause and treatment where you can."

There was no preamble or further explanation. With a casual flick of his sleeve, fifteen potted spiritual plants appeared before Ning, divided into five low-grade, five mid-grade, and five high-grade First-Tier plants.

Ning's gaze swept over them, calm on the surface, though his confidence had already dropped somewhat.

Truth be told, this test wasn't his strongest area.

Most of his experience in spiritual farming involved cultivation itself: preparing the soil, planting, nourishing, controlling the environment, and improving the final harvest. He had naturally studied diseases and pests as well, since anyone managing a spiritual field needed to know how to recognize problems before they spread, but his practical experience with them was much more limited.

After all, the entire point of maintaining a field properly was to prevent such problems from appearing in the first place.

Treatment was another matter. Common diseases and pests had well-known methods of dealing with them, but the more troublesome ones often required specialized medicines, techniques, or experience that ordinary spiritual farmers might not possess. Some treatment methods were valuable enough that their owners had little reason to spread them freely.

There was good money in saving a spiritual field that someone had spent months or even years cultivating.

Fortunately, the test seemed to focus more on diagnosis than whether they could personally cure every disease placed before them.

Ning exhaled quietly and stepped forward.

He examined the plants one by one, beginning with the low-grade specimens. Most of their problems were relatively obvious. One had small insects hiding beneath its leaves, while another showed signs of root rot from excessive moisture. A third had begun yellowing from a problem with the soil.

The mid-grade plants took considerably longer.

Many of their symptoms could have several different causes, forcing Ning to inspect more than what was immediately visible. He loosened the soil around the roots of one plant and found several small bite marks beneath the surface. Another had reddish-yellow spots spreading across its leaves, while the roots of a seemingly healthy specimen had already begun to soften and discolor.

By the time he reached the high-grade plants, Ning had slowed down considerably.

One of them had withered despite having sufficient water and spiritual qi. Another had tiny insects hiding along the veins beneath its leaves, almost impossible to notice without careful inspection. The last few were even less obvious, and Ning had to repeatedly compare the condition of their leaves, stems, roots, and surrounding soil before settling on an answer.

This was where his lack of experience began to show.

Knowing the symptoms from books was one thing. Seeing a half-withered plant and deciding which of several similar problems had actually caused it was another.

Still, Ning didn't panic. He simply took his time and worked through what he knew.

Where he was confident, he wrote down the diagnosis and the appropriate treatment. Where several causes were possible, he recorded the most likely one along with what he would need to check before taking further action. If he didn't know the exact treatment, he focused on what could safely be done first: isolating the affected plant, slowing the spread, preserving its vitality, or correcting the surrounding conditions.

It was better than confidently applying the wrong treatment and finishing off the plant himself.

Time passed quietly as Ning worked through the fifteen pots. The room remained still, broken only by the occasional rustle of leaves and the soft scrape of his brush against the paper.

Strangely enough, the test was... straightforward.

After the weeds and the Howling Monkey in the previous trial, Ning had been expecting something to happen for a while now. Perhaps one of the plants would suddenly worsen halfway through, or two diseases would be deliberately made to resemble each other.

But nothing happened.

After a while, Ning couldn't help becoming suspicious.

Maybe not having a surprise is the surprise.

He stared at the plants for a moment before shaking his head. If he kept thinking like this, he would only end up second-guessing everything and making mistakes on his own.

Ning returned his attention to the remaining plants and continued working carefully. This was already his weakest of the three tests, so there was even less reason to rush.

When the allotted time finally ended, he put down his brush.

The elder collected his answers and glanced through them briefly before giving a small nod.

"The results will be announced shortly. You may leave."

Ning cupped his fists. "Thank you, Elder."

He stepped out of the room, still thinking about a few of the plants he hadn't been completely certain about.

Even if his result wasn't as good as the previous two tests, the experience itself had been worthwhile. Disease diagnosis was clearly one area of spiritual farming where he still lacked practice.

Perhaps he could find a way to work on that later.

...

Waiting was always the worst part.

Ning had done the best he could on the third test, and fortunately, his performance in the first two had been outstanding. That should be enough to carry him through.

Rather than continue worrying about it, he practiced his spiritual control exercises, carefully controlling a thread of qi, drawing it thinner and thinner. Distracting himself was one of the best ways to make time pass.

About three hours later, the third test concluded.

“Now, I will announce the candidates who will receive the spiritual plant certificate. When your name is called, come forward.”

The elder’s voice echoed through the hall.

Everyone held their breath. Ning listened calmly; at this point, everything depended on fate.

“Ni Lunxia, Ji Ning, Fang Tian, Cui Xia, and Fei Shin. You have passed the tests. Congratulations.”

The hall erupted into noise.

Ning, however, simply let out a quiet breath and smiled.

At last.

The spiritual plant certificate was his.
