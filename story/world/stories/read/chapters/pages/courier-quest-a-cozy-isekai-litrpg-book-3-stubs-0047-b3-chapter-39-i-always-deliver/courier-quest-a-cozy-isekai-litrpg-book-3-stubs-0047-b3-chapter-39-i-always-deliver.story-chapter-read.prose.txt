The garage the Summoned arrived in could have been more adequately described as a hangar due to how large it was. Bright lights shone down on mostly empty space, and the bulk of the activity focused around three of the vehicles that had been brought here.

The first one was something like a semi-truck, but wide and bulbous. It made Trevor think more of a sci-fi inspired submarine or spacecraft than something fit for the streets. Still, there were at least eight tires on one side to indicate that it was road worthy. He knew a few people who would have jumped at the chance to drive it.

In the middle was something smaller, reminding Trevor of an armored personnel carrier mixed with an RV. There were rectangular slits instead of glass for the driver to look through, and the door that made up its back end was lowered to the ground with people coming and going. Protective slats opened up in their direction, but they weren’t large enough to see much inside.

The final vehicle was the most normal one. It was a sleek convertible that had been painted silver with red racing stripes. The cab was round and the glass windshield was bubble-shaped, which seemed strange to Trevor. There were three seats; two in the front, one in the back.

“You have a choice of these three cars,” Lora explained as she approached the vehicles.

“Only one of these is really a car,” Trevor pointed out.

“Yes, but they’re all called cars here,” she replied with a shrug.

A demon woman with short horns stepped out of the APC with a clipboard and immediately noticed them. She turned their way. Her plain clothes were covered in grease and oil, and her prehensile tail flicked sharply behind her. It wiped against her clothes before she handed her clipboard to it.

“You must be our guests!” she announced with glee. Her attention skimmed over them before settling on courier specifically. She reached out to shake his hand. “You must be the Earth guy who’s driven before that I’ve heard so much about.”

“I don’t know about what Lora might have told you, but yeah. I’m Trevor,” he replied as he returned her greeting. “This is my wife, Adabelle, and my familiar, Clay. We’re pretty excited to get out there tonight.”

“It’s a real dream,” the demon replied with a grin. “We’ve got Rambunctious C all set up for you today. That’s the convertible, by the way. Tanky B has some repairs going on, and we were advised that you probably wouldn’t want to take Delores for a spin.”

Trevor glanced at the other vehicles. “I’m assuming the really big one is Delores?”

“That’s right,” she said, only to be interrupted by Lora clearing her throat.

“It’s good to see you again, Matilsa,” the teleporter said. “Just wanted to get your name out there since you haven’t introduced yourself.”

“Ah, my fault. I just get into a groove sometimes,” the demon said, her tail waving her clipboard towards the blocky vehicle. “Lora here assumed you would want one of the smaller ones, which is fine. Tanky B is just acting up.”

“What’s going on with it?” Trevor asked curiously, earning him an appraising glance.

“There’s a clicking noise and the lights come on, but the engine won’t start.”

“Might be a battery issue,” he suggested. “Maybe the starter, but that won’t turn on if there’s not enough power.”

Matilsa looked at him with renewed interest before stepping closer into his personal space. Beside him, Adabelle bristled. The demon didn’t seem to notice. “Were you some kind of mechanic back on Earth?”

“No,” Trevor hurriedly answered, holding up his hands as if that would placate the woman. “I had to fix some things on my own car myself because it was cheaper and wasn’t so involved, but I wouldn’t call myself any kind of expert.”

“Disappointing,” the demon sighed, taking a few light steps back. “I’ve been wanting to speak with someone from Earth who actually knows how to fix cars professionally. Not that it would do much good, but it’s a different perspective.”

“Why wouldn’t it do much good?” Adabelle asked, stepping into the conversation as she took her husband’s hand.

“Sammie the Gamer made these herself, but she didn’t leave any schematics, documents, or anything more than a few napkin scribbles,” Matilsa lamented, looking genuinely upset. “Then, bless her soul, she passes away! Everything we’ve learned has been from observing how they work because, of course, they’re considered national treasures!”

“If they’re national treasures, then how did we get permission to drive one of them?” Trevor asked incredulously.

“Deep pockets and the fact that letting them sit for too long is bad for them and we demons think that things should be used, not tucked away just to be looked at,” she answered easily before returning to her tangent. “The biggest leaps in understanding came when Duck E stopped working about fifteen years ago and they let us do a full autopsy on the engine. I remember being a new little mechanic back then, standing next to the greats as they cracked that baby open.”

Trevor and Adabelle shared a glance. Matilsa’s tail twitched erratically. A pen on a chain dangled off of the clipboard, occasionally clacking against the floor. She didn’t notice that, either.

“And we learned so much! Then, Racer A broke down and we suddenly had two! Two whole engines that we could play with and learn as much as we could before putting the old girls in the museum! That’s why we can fix Tanky B, though, so their sacrifices weren’t in vain,” she said, putting a hand over her heart.

“Did you learn enough to fix Racer A and Duck E?” Trevor asked.

“Technically, yes,” Matilsa answered before sighing. “But it requires manipulating metal in ways that we can’t replicate yet to build new parts. Sammie the Gamer’s Power allowed her to make it, and there’s no way we can emulate what she could do at this moment. Technologically, it’s out of our reach. We make motors—that’s easy—but we haven’t succeeded in building an engine like hers yet. I know we will someday, though. It's my lifelong dream.”

As the mechanic spoke, Trevor glanced up at the earth elemental on his shoulder. Clay was looking around, nodding when they saw everyone else do it to appear polite. The moment Matilsa mentioned manipulating metal, the courier knew there might be a way to help.

“You up for some metal manipulation, little buddy?” he asked. Both Clay and the mechanic turned to look at him. “It could be an interesting little challenge for you.”

It seemed like Matilsa was about to say something when Lora motioned for her to stop. She still looked like she wanted to grab Clay, but refrained and began bouncing on her heels instead. The familiar looked back at her before reaching up to pat their master on his cheek.

“Yeah, you would miss out on riding in a car,” Trevor replied, interpreting Clay’s feelings through their bond. “Afterwards, though? Give these engines a little peek and try to figure out if you can make something identical with what they have?”

“Such a thing might take a few days,” Lora warned. “Not saying that Clay couldn’t do it, just that it would probably be a lengthy process. They’d have to learn what to do, then produce a working model, then do it again to make sure the first time wasn’t a fluke.”

The engine on Tanky D revved to life, filling the hangar with sound. Clay lowered their body against Trevor as they stared at it.

“Fixed it, head mechanic!” a demon announced after leaving the vehicle.

“Excellent work, Aaran. Go take a break” she replied, not taking her eyes off of the earth elemental. “It is starting to get late, so how about we get you into Rambunctious C and send you off so you can arrive at the viewing point to see the sunset in the mountains. That will give you plenty of time to convince your familiar to work with us.”

“There won’t be any convincing,” Trevor said with a thin smile. “They’ll want to do it or they won’t, and it’s up to them to decide if they’re going to.”

“That… is fair,” Matilsa conceded, but not without a sour face. The courier could tell that these vehicles were her whole life, and he didn’t begrudge her that. “Please, follow me.”

Trevor squeezed Adabelle’s hand as they walked towards the convertible. Hearing that it was a national treasure had thrown him off, but he couldn’t deny that he was giddy at the prospect of driving again. Especially for his wife and familiar.

Matilsa went through a clearly rehearsed safety lesson on what was expected of them while they operated the Rambunctious C. Hands on the wheel at all times, don’t open the doors while in motion, always wear a seatbelt. Trevor nodded at each point. It wasn’t anything he hadn’t heard before and there weren’t any curve balls waiting for him.

The safety talk went on for nearly ten minutes before he was allowed to sit in the driver’s seat. While excited, he passed Clay off to Adabelle first. It wouldn’t be good for him to hold his familiar while driving.

He settled into the black seat and immediately started adjusting both it and the mirrors. The wheel, knobs, switches, and levers were all where he expected, as were the pedals, and were only slightly different. It was comparable to what he had once driven, and memories were flooding back. The only thing he didn’t touch was the start button.

“I think it’s safe to assume Sammie the Gamer was a human who chose to be a goblin, because this reminds me of Earth cars,” Trevor stated as he turned on the lights to check that they worked. They did.

“That’s right,” Matilsa confirmed as she reached down and ran her thumb across the hood, removing some dirt. “She often lamented not being able to reach things anymore, but then she’d simply invent something to act as longer arms and legs. Then she’d try and get away with sleeping in small places because she could fit. She was a very chaotic woman.”

While he had been quick to enter the car, Adabelle took her time. She sat down daintily, threading her tail through the hole in the seat. Setting Clay down on her lap, she pulled her seatbelt on, prompting Trevor to do the same now that they were getting closer to leaving.

“It’s very comfortable,” Adabelle praised.

“Sammie the Gamer was a big believer in comfort,” Matilsa replied, though she was obviously pleased. “Why don’t you turn it on?”

The start button was big, red, and made him feel like he was about to launch a missile with it. Trevor didn’t have to be told twice. The engine roared to life before settling into a low rumble.

Then, he pressed it again to turn it off.

“You okay, little buddy?” he asked, turning to his familiar.

Clay didn’t have any fur to poof out when startled, but they took on the shape of Mr. Tiggles for that. They had stood up on Adabelle’s legs, back arched as they stared at the car’s engine. Trevor could feel turbulence through their bond.

“What’s going on?” Lora asked, concerned.

“They don’t like the vibrations,” Trevor responded before reaching over and petting the earth elemental. “It’s okay. That’s going to keep up for the whole drive, but it’s not going to hurt us or anything. Do you think you can handle it?”

There was no response for a moment before Clay stepped over to their master’s lap and laid down, still staring at the front of the car.

“That’s not very safe, Clay,” Trevor gently told his familiar. “But I want to see if you can get used to it. I’m going to turn it back on, okay?”

Another pause, this time followed by a nod.

Trevor started the car again. He felt the earth elemental flinch and shake as the car idled. It was almost as if they were trying to vibrate in the opposite way Rambunctious C did. Like they were trying to cancel it out. This went on for about twenty seconds before he turned the car off.

He scooped Clay into his arms and held them. “I don’t think this is going to work out.”

“What’s going on?” Adabelle asked, concerned. She reached out and stroked Clay between the ears.

“I think the vibrations are messing with how they’re seeing the world?” Trevor offered, though he wasn’t really sure. “It took them a bit to get used to wracor rides and wagons before they learned to transform into them, but I think a car might be a bit much. Too much vibrating and too chaotic. I think we should call it.”

The earth elemental shook their head and pointed towards the car before placing their paws on Trevor’s chest and pushing.

“We… should go?” the courier asked. “Are you sure?”

Clay wiggled out of his arms and onto his shoulders before opening his paws for Lora. She gasped. “You want me to hold you?” the teleporter asked. When the familiar nodded, she squealed and snatched them up. “Oh, we’re going to have so much fun together! I’ve always thought you were super duper cute, you know.”

Trevor smiled at his familiar and sent a round of concerned but thankful feelings through their bond. Clay reciprocated them, and their master got the impression that they thought he would be okay because Adabelle was there. The thought made him place a hand on his wife’s leg.

“You take care of them, okay?” the courier told Lora. “Clay better be all in one piece when we get back.”

“Roger roger, cap-i-tan,” she replied, shifting the earth elemental in one hand so she could snap off a salute. “We’re going to rest, recuperate, and be all set when you get back.”

“And there might even be some tests we can attempt while you’re gone, so take your time,” Matilsa added, a calculating look in her eyes as she stared at Clay.

“Watch her, too, will you?” Trevor requested, pointing towards the head mechanic.

“I’m not going to do anything!” she said quickly. “I am a professional.”

“You’re an enthusiast, and I’ve seen those before,” he replied with a chuckle before shaking his head. “Last chance, Clay. Are you sure?”

His familiar nodded without hesitation and even went so far as to make the shooing motion he had seen Wayne do on occasion.

“Well, if that’s not a ringing endorsement, then I don’t know what is,” Adabelle said with a smile.

“Seems like it,” Trevor agreed. “Alright, little buddy. Don’t let Matilsa get the better of you, and stick around Lora. We’ll be back before you know it.”

“But not too soon,” the mechanic said. “It is a long drive, and the sunset out there is pretty romantic.”

“I’m sure,” he chuckled. “But if they get too stressed out, I’ll know anyway. We’re bonded, so I can feel what they’re feeling. If something happens, I’ll know.”

“Nothing’s going to happen,” Lora said. “Now get out there and drive. Stop wasting time with us.”

Trevor nodded and started the car again. There wasn’t any more distress from Clay other than concern for the newlyweds, so he had the feeling that the vibration theory was right. He turned to Adabelle. “Ready, lady?”

“I am,” she answered with a mischievous smile. “Now, I believe you told me that this could be thrilling. I’m holding you to that, so you better deliver.”

“Please, I’m the inventoryman,” he playfully scoffed before shifting Rambunctious C into drive. “I always deliver.”

Adabelle laughed. “I set you up for that one.”

“Yes, you did, please still love me,” Trevor replied, grinning as he hit the gas. It had a lot more power than he thought it would, and they zoomed off towards the open door that led to the mountain course.
