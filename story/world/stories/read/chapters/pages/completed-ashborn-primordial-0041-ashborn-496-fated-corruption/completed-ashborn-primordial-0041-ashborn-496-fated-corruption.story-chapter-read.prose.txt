Escaping with the Vimana was not as difficult a proposition as Vir had initially feared. While far too high to reach via Balancer of Scales, even for Cirayus and his incredible mastery of the art, procuring an airship proved simpler once Cirayus and Tia’s forces had claimed most of the camp, subduing what meager resistance they encountered.

Vir helped here and there with lingering stragglers, but always from the shadows, and they didn’t reunite with Cirayus and Tia after.

The fewer their interactions and the fewer the explanations given, the safer everyone remained.

Luckily, with the chaos of battle, remaining undetected was not nearly as difficult as it might have otherwise been. With the wolves’ help, they threw the panicked crew off an Altani fast-attack skyship and took to the air.

Normally, piloting such an airship required fifteen mejai working in tandem. Fifteen skilled Altani mejai.

They just had Maiya, who, with her S-rank magic and the prana batteries gifted to her by the Imperium, managed the feat all on her own.

She ran around the deck, charging the hundreds of Lighten Load utility orbs placed all along the ship before blasting a near-continuous stream of Wind magic through the series of tubes at the stern, sending them sailing up through the sky, though she did nearly cause the ship to flip upside down in the process.

“You try learning how to fly an Altani airship on the spot!” she snapped, despite neither Vir nor Ashani making a single comment. Vir didn’t have a death wish.

It was only when the Vimana’s bay doors responded to the same prana override Vir had used back in his universe that he let out a breath of relief. He’d worried that Saunak had changed it somehow, considering he’d found some way of not only entering, but reactivating and commanding the Vimana.

Luckily, the passcode remained intact. Even still, Saunak must have discovered the same key combination to get through the first time. How exactly, Vir couldn’t begin to guess. Even after knowing the demon for years, his genius still unnerved him to this day.

It was once they were inside that the truly difficult part began. Their skyship’s landing went unnoticed for the most part—such occurrences seemed commonplace here, and someone even directed Maiya on where to land.

The ruse, however, would not have lasted long. The moment anyone came over, they’d have noticed the missing crew, to say nothing of their lack of uniform.

Anticipating this, Vir had already ferried Ashani to a nearby empty storeroom even before they’d landed. Maiya was the last to go, and he took her the moment they set down. Now, all anyone would find would be an empty skyship.

Let them make of that what they may, Vir thought wryly. By the time they had a chance to investigate, Vir and his group would be long gone.

That said, it was one thing to storm through a Vimana and ascend its levels leaving corpses in their wake. Doing so without being seen, however, was something else entirely.

Even with Dance of the Shadow Demon, Vir struggled to move Maiya and Ashani through empty areas without detection. Anyone could be an agent of the Fateweavers, and on a marvel like the Vimana, there was little doubt they had many eyes and ears.

Oftentimes, Vir was forced to jump out into the open before quickly sinking back into the shadows to reach a safe spot.

This Vimana, like the others, was a vertical structure that sloped inward to a point at the top, and was as ostentatious as it was awe-inspiring, though this particular one happened to be conical rather than angular, and decorated in blue-silver tones instead of the usual gold. As focused as he was on his task, though, Vir scarcely had the opportunity to appreciate its beauty, which he felt was a little tragic.

After what felt like forever to Vir, but no time at all to Maiya and Ashani, the group slipped into a lift carrying them toward the top.

“Good thing everyone’s panicking about Andros,” Maiya muttered, crouching low to avoid being spotted as she stared up at the peak and the catwalk ringing it—a circular ring instead of triangular, to mirror the conical shape. “Dunno if we’d have made it otherwise. But do you really think this is going to work? There’s bound to be guards up there.”

“Worst case,” Vir said, “I take them out before they can see us.”

“I don’t doubt your skill,” Ashani replied, “but I must say the risk of detection is quite large.”

“What do you suggest?” Vir asked.

“Perhaps another distraction might be in order,” the goddess replied.

Vir considered the idea, then nodded. “Very well.”

He set his sights on the rapidly approaching catwalk. Though barely twenty paces across, Andros had seen fit to guard the ornate double silver doors with two burly-looking guards, and to Vir’s dismay, not even the chaos rocking the Vimana below made them budge.

The moment the lift neared the catwalk, Vir fired three of his Artifact chakrams, energizing them with only the smallest trickle of prana so as not to raise suspicion. The Imperium-forged chakrams he carried did not exist in this world—they hadn’t even existed in his own—and would almost certainly raise eyebrows if he used their full potential.

But he did not send them careening towards the guards. That would have killed them without a doubt.

Instead, he directed the chakrams straight upward into the platform’s ceiling above them. The spinning discs bit into the exotic metal, spraying sparks everywhere. It was a tiny distraction, earning him a handful of seconds at most, but it was enough.

Vir emerged from one of the guards’ shadows, striking both of their throats to drop them without a sound, easing their unconscious forms to the floor.

“Well done, mister,” Maiya said, strutting up to Vir and patting his shoulders with a grin. “Never doubted you.”

“Sure you didn’t,” Vir muttered, returning her grin. The expression felt foreign to him. Wrong. How long had it been since he’d seen such joy on her face?

Too long, and that went for them both.

Vir input the prana sequence to unlock the doors, preparing himself for whatever might lie beyond.

Unfortunately, the chamber beyond was shielded from Prana Vision. Likely the same shielding that hid what transpired within from the eyes of the Fateweavers, but it worked against him now.

They held their collective breath as they stepped into the room, only relaxing when they saw it was indeed empty. Which made Vir wonder why they even bothered to guard the room. It served no obvious purpose.

Maiya and Ashani quickly shut the doors behind them, and only then did Ashani create a Gate—an Ash Gate at the top level—connecting the chamber to the grounds outside and allowing their half dozen Ashfire Wolves to enter.

As stealthy as they could be, a pack was impossible to hide completely. Vir had even considered leaving Maiya behind, but her skills were required to pilot the skyship, and leaving her at the hangar was hardly safer than bringing her along.

The moment the door slammed shut, inscriptions lit up across the room, and a familiar projection formed, bringing with him a bit of relief in this strange place.

As familiar as everything might seem, they truly were in another universe, with Janak as their only lifeline.

Which was why that relief vanished when parts of Janak’s avatar flickered, disappearing only to reappear seconds later. His voice came broken, garbled and oddly distorted.

“No…” Ashani whispered, staring at the projection, but it wasn’t the distortion that frightened Vir. It was the look on her alabaster face. The look of panic he had never once seen on the normally serene goddess. Whatever this was, it shook her to her core, and anything that shook a being from the Prime Imperium terrified Vir.

“Cal—cal… measures… corrupted,” the projection sputtered. “Control… them all…”

“What is he saying?” Maiya asked, looking between the avatar and Ashani.

“I cannot be sure,” Ashani breathed, frowning. “This isn’t supposed to happen!”

“What do you mean ‘supposed to happen’?” Vir asked, whirling towards her.

“Survive!” the avatar shouted suddenly. “At all costs! Do not reveal—who—you are—so close—now—can’t ruin—ruin—ruin—”

The projection shuddered, then vanished entirely, leaving the room in silence.

Vir turned to Ashani. “What is going on here? You know something about this, don’t you?”

Ashani clutched her head. “I… I can’t… I’m sorry—”

Seeing Ashani so distraught left Vir speechless. Her panic seemed to grow, but then, in the span of a breath, it vanished entirely. Her expression smoothed as though wiped clean.

“I apologize for that display,” she said in her usual tone. “That was unbecoming of me. It seems that Janak’s plans have gone awry. From what I could interpolate from the projection, it seems that, while secrecy has always been important, this world we are about to enter may be under Fateweaver control.”

She gestured toward the Ash Gate rippling in the corner. To call it a Gate was generous. It looked more like a torn, unstable Ash Tear that bridged dimensions. Regular Ash Tears were dangerous enough. Vir didn’t even want to begin thinking of how poorly something like this could end.

“We must go,” Ashani said. “I cannot be sure how long this Gate will hold.”

Maiya eyed the warped opening with deep suspicion, and voiced what was on both their minds. “Is that really our best idea? If it’s corrupted, how do we know it doesn’t lead into some abyss we can’t escape?”

“We have little choice,” Ashani replied. “We cannot remain here.”

“What about the other Vimana?” Maiya pressed.

Vir looked to Ashani, hopeful.

Ashani shook her head, extinguishing that hope. “All Vimana are linked. The recording would play in any of them, and the Gates Janak set up will all lead to the same destination. With how corrupted everything is… I fear another Vimana may fail to generate a Gate at all, thereby stranding us.”

Vir stared through the unstable Gate. Beyond it, storms churned in scenery that looked hauntingly similar to the Ashen Realm. The deluge of prana pouring through all but confirmed it.

“Let’s go,” Vir said quietly. “Whatever waits for us on the other side, we face it together. And gods willing, we make it through.”
