Spoiler - Spiders at the end of this chapter

The trek back to the relative safety of the main northern road was a grim, silent affair. The adrenaline from the Plague Bear fight had completely faded, leaving the party with heavily aching muscles and a profound sense of the monumental task ahead of them.

They did not face any major threats on the return journey. They encountered a few scattered, shuffling groups of corrupted deer and a singular, rotting forest cat that Perberos dispatched with a single arrow before it even realised they were there. The deep woods were thick with dark mana, but the larger predators were keeping their distance for now.

When they finally broke through the edge of the treeline and stepped back onto the cracked, frost-covered cobblestones, they found Torin and the other party leaders already waiting.

"Any casualties?" Torin asked, his gravelly voice cutting through the freezing evening air.

"None," Josh reported, leaning his heavy steel shield against his leg. "But we encountered a mutated, highly saturated native beast deep in the woods. A Plague Bear. It took all five of us to bring it down. The deeper we go, the worse the corruption gets."

Torin nodded grimly, his scarred face tight with worry. "We found similar resistance on the eastern side. We nearly lost our scout to a pack of skeletal hounds. We need to fall back and regroup."

They marched a mile south down the road, putting a comfortable distance between themselves and the densest wall of the necrotic forest. They found a defensible, slightly elevated patch of ground near a dried-out riverbed and began the tedious process of establishing a camp.

It was an utterly miserable evening.

The most basic survival tasks proved incredibly difficult in the corrupted environment. Bhel and a few of the other fighters ventured to the edge of the woods to gather fallen timber for a fire, only to find the wood completely saturated with dark, foul-smelling moisture. It was not simply damp from the winter frost. The necrotic mana had soaked into the very grain of the timber, turning it into a spongy, rotting mess.

They piled the damp wood in the centre of the camp, completely unable to spark it with standard flint and steel.

"Stand back," Brett sighed, looking at the pathetic pile of wet timber with deep offence.

The mage knelt in the frozen mud and pressed his bare hands directly against the damp bark. He pushed his mana into the wood and forced raw thermal energy deep into the logs, attempting to boil the unnatural moisture out from the inside.

The wood hissed and popped violently. A thick, foul-smelling steam billowed upward, the stink of old graves and stagnant water filling the air. It took Brett nearly ten minutes of sustained, focused magical exertion before the necrotic dampness was finally purged and the wood caught fire with a reluctant, sputtering orange flame.

"That should not be so difficult," Brett complained, wiping a sheen of sweat from his pale forehead as he sat back on a rock. "The ambient dark mana is thick even this close to Ashenfall. This environment is becoming hostile to life."

They set a strict, overlapping guard rotation, pairing members from different parties to ensure no one fell asleep on watch. The night passed in a state of tense, shivering vigilance, but the undead did not press an attack. They remained in the shadows of the treeline, a silent, looming threat.

The next morning broke with a biting, bitter wind.

Josh, Torin, and the three other party leaders gathered around a large, slightly crumpled map of the northern territories spread over a flat boulder. The map showed the Crypt nestled deep in the mountains, with the sprawling, infected forest acting as a massive buffer zone between the dungeon and Ashenfall.

"We cannot just wander blindly through the trees," Josh stated, tracing a gauntleted finger across the map. "The woods are too thick. If we push forward randomly, we will leave pockets of undead behind us. They will eventually encircle us and cut off our retreat to the road."

"We use a grid," Torin agreed, his scarred finger tapping the parchment. "We divide the forest leading up to the mountain base into five distinct corridors. Each party takes a designated lane. We sweep north simultaneously, clearing everything in our path. We maintain an even pace so no group pushes too far ahead and exposes their flanks."

It was a slow, methodical, and highly dangerous plan, but it was the only tactical option that made sense.

"We also have a logistical problem," Josh added, looking around the small, miserable camp. "We cannot survive out here sleeping in the freezing mud every night. If we are going to spend weeks clearing this forest and eventually pushing into the Crypt itself, we need a secure base of operations. A few campfires will not stop a horde."

Torin crossed his arms, his expression serious. "What do you propose?"

"We send a runner back to Ashenfall immediately," Josh said. "We tell Mich at the Guild to send us labourers, carpenters, and stone masons. We need a proper, fortified palisade built right here on this ridge. We need tents, reliable supply lines for food and dry wood, and we need any other high-level parties the Guild can spare to rotate into the clearing effort."

The other leaders nodded in firm agreement. The threat of the Crypt was far too massive for five isolated parties to handle alone. They were the tip of the spear, but they needed the full weight of the Guild behind them to hold the line.

A fleet-footed rogue from one of the other groups was selected for the run. He packed a light bag, took the official request sealed by all five party leaders, and sprinted south towards the city.

With the camp logistics settled, the parties prepared to enter the dark woods.

Josh’s party was assigned the far-left corridor of the grid pattern. This meant their left flank would be protected by a steep, impassable mountain ridge, leaving them only to worry about coordinating their right flank with the neighbouring party.

"Keep your spacing tight," Josh instructed as they approached the treeline via a completely different path from the previous day. "Perberos, you take point. Mark our boundary line clearly so the others know where our sector ends."

The elven scout nodded, producing a large spool of bright, crimson fabric from his pack. He tore off a long strip and tied it securely around the trunk of a dead oak tree at the edge of the road. The bright red ribbon stood out starkly against the grey and black rot of the forest.

They stepped into the shadows.

The atmosphere was just as oppressive as before. The dead silence pressed against their eardrums, and the smell of decay coated the back of their throats.

They moved methodically. Perberos drifted ahead, his silver eyes piercing the gloom, pausing every fifty yards to tie another crimson ribbon to a prominent branch, establishing a clear, visible boundary line for their grid sector.

The initial combat encounters of the morning were predictable. They clashed with small groups of resurrected native wildlife. Starving, rotting wolves and skeletal boars charged them with mindless aggression. The party dispatched them with brutal efficiency. Bhel hacked them to pieces, Josh crushed their skulls with his shield, and Brett incinerated the remains to prevent the ambient dark mana from reanimating the broken parts.

Because these were native beasts raised by the corrupted environment, they did not dissolve. They left behind a horrific, foul-smelling mess of rotting meat and shattered bone that the party had to carefully step over.

However, as the morning dragged on and they pushed a mile deeper into the grid, the nature of their enemies began to shift.

They entered a wide, shallow ravine filled with dead, thorny bushes. Shuffling through the mist at the bottom of the ravine was a group of seven heavily armoured, skeletal warriors. They carried rusted, ancient broadswords and moved with a terrifying, coordinated military discipline that the mindless animal corpses entirely lacked.

"Crypt Guards," Brett noted quietly from the ridge, a small spark of fire already dancing across his knuckles. "I bet those are not native to this forest. They have marched directly out of the dungeon."

Josh raised his shield and led the charge down the slope.

The fight was intense but brief. The skeletal warriors possessed a rigid, predictable combat style. Josh caught their rusted blades on his steel shield, trusting his heavy armour to absorb the glancing blows. He triggered his Stormforged Adamantite skill, channelling raw lightning into his sword, and shattered their ancient ribs with heavy strikes.

When the last skeletal guard collapsed, its skull crushed beneath Bhel’s heavy boot, the pile of shattered bones did not remain in the mud. The dark, necrotic magic holding the system-generated dungeon monster together completely evaporated. The bones rapidly broke down, dissolving instantly into a shimmering pile of golden dust, leaving only a few rusted iron buckles behind.

"I guess that makes it very easy to tell how far the dungeon has regurgitated its forces," Perberos noted, retrieving an arrow from the mud.

The grid search rapidly evolved into a brutal, exhausting slog as they pushed into the second hour. The deeper they went, the more the lines between dungeon spawn and corrupted native wildlife began to blur.

They were navigating a dense patch of rotting birch trees when a loud, frantic crashing sound echoed from their right flank.

Perberos instantly dropped to one knee, an arrow nocked. Josh raised his shield and stepped in front of Carcan.

Bursting through the dead, white trunks came a herd of Razor-Horn Stags. In life, they would have been majestic, towering beasts of the deep woods. In death, they were nightmares. Their flesh was sloughing off their massive frames, and their wide, deadly antlers were tangled with black, necrotic vines.

But they were not charging blindly. They were being driven.

Directly behind the rotting stags, hissing and snapping like twisted sheepdogs, was a pack of six Crypt Ghouls. The ghouls were completely hairless, their skin a sickly, bruised purple, possessing elongated arms that ended in jagged, filthy claws. The system-spawned dungeon monsters were actively herding the reanimated native beasts forward, using them as heavily armoured shock troops.

"They are coordinating!" Brett shouted, his hands already glowing with intense heat. "The dungeon spawn are using the native corpses as a meat shield!"

"Then we butcher the meat!" Bhel roared happily.

The massive stags hit the party's line with the force of a cavalry charge. Josh planted his boots, activating his Titan's Anchor skill. The shimmering blue energy shield flared to life just as the lead stag lowered its massive, rotting antlers.

The impact was bone-jarring. The stag's neck snapped backward with a wet crunch, its decaying body crumpling against Josh's immovable barrier.

Bhel did not wait for the others. He dove beneath the swinging antlers of a second stag, his twin axes glowing with Brett's magical fire. He drove the heavy iron blades directly into the beast's front knees, severing the joints completely. The stag collapsed into the mud, shrieking a horrific, gurgling cry, before Bhel casually buried his right axe into its skull.

With the physical wall of the stags broken, the Crypt Ghouls surged through the gaps. They moved with the terrifying, unnatural speed of system-generated monsters, their claws swiping frantically.

"Step back!" Brett commanded.

Josh and Bhel instantly retreated a single pace. Brett unleashed a devastating, wide-arc cone of pure thermal energy from his palms. The fire washed over the six ghouls. They lacked any dense, saturated physical mass and simply shrieked as they vaporised within the flames, turning into six separate piles of glittering golden dust that settled softly over the bloody, physical corpses of the stags.

"That is a horrific combination," Josh breathed, shaking the gore from his shield. "If they start mixing their forces like that on a larger scale, the city guard will be completely overrun."

They barely had time to recover their breath before the forest tested them again.

Another mile into their designated sector, the dead canopy overhead grew incredibly thick, blocking out almost all of the pale morning light. The air grew stagnant and completely still.

Perberos halted abruptly, his silver eyes snapping upward.

"Shields up!" the elf yelled, drawing his bow string back with blinding speed.

Dropping silently from the dead branches above them were half a dozen massive, corrupted birds of prey. They resembled giant owls, but their feathers were rotting away to reveal grey, diseased skin, and their talons dripped with a foul, black sludge.

They did not swoop. They simply released their grip on the branches and fell like weighted stones directly toward the party, their talons extended.

Carcan reacted instantly. She slammed the base of her amber-topped staff into the mud. "Light protect us!"

A brilliant, golden dome of holy magic flared to life over her head, expanding just enough to cover herself and Brett. Two of the rotting birds slammed into the magical barrier. The holy energy hissed and burned their necrotic flesh, sending them tumbling into the mud where they thrashed wildly.

Josh raised his steel shield flat above his head just as a massive bird crashed into him. The impact drove him to his knees. The beast's talons screeched against the metal, trying to find a purchase, its beak snapping frantically at the edge of the shield.

Josh shoved upward with all his strength, throwing the creature off balance. He rolled to his feet and brought his electrified longsword around in a tight, upward arc, cleaving the bird neatly in two.

Perberos was a machine of lethal precision. He fired straight upward, taking one bird out of the sky before it even cleared the canopy. He dropped his bow, drew his twin curved blades, and intercepted a second bird that was diving for Bhel's exposed neck. The elf sliced the creature's wings cleanly off, leaving Bhel to casually crush its head under a heavy iron boot.

Brett, protected beneath Carcan's dome, pointed a single finger at the two birds struggling in the mud. He did not waste a large spell. He simply fired two condensed, pea-sized bolts of white-hot plasma that pierced their skulls with a sharp sizzle, ending the threat instantly.

"They are coming from every conceivable angle," Brett noted, rubbing his temples as Carcan dropped the golden shield. "This is going to be exhausting."

"Keep tying the ribbons, Perberos," Josh ordered, his chest heaving slightly as he checked his party for injuries. "We are not stopping until midday."

The volume of combat was draining their stamina, but they held their rigid formation, leaving a neat, undeniable trail of crimson ribbons behind them to mark their progress.

As the pale sun finally reached its highest point in the sky, signalling the arrival of midday, the air in the forest grew significantly, unnaturally colder.

They broke through a thick wall of dead pines and stepped into a massive, circular clearing.

Josh stopped dead in his tracks, raising his hand to halt the party.

The clearing was not empty. The towering, dead trees surrounding the open space were completely coated in a thick, unnatural substance. It looked like a cross between grey spider webbing and hardened, calcified bone. Thick, ropey strands of the material crisscrossed the clearing, creating a massive, chaotic net.

Suspended in the centre of the web, roughly ten feet off the ground, was a true nightmare.

It was a Crypt Weaver. It was a massive, arachnid-like monstrosity spawned directly from the depths of the dungeon. Its bulbous body was constructed entirely from fused human skulls and ribcages, glowing from within with a sickly, pulsing green light. Its eight long, spindly legs were made of sharpened femurs.

But the Weaver was not alone.

Standing perfectly still in the mud beneath the suspended monster were three massive, corrupted dire wolves. These were native beasts, their flesh rotting and falling away from their bones.

What made Josh’s blood run cold was the connection between them. Thin, glowing threads of pure necrotic mana extended from the Weaver’s bony abdomen, dropping down to attach directly to the spines of the three dead wolves.

"It is a puppeteer," Brett whispered, his eyes widening in pure horror as he recognised the magical structure. "The dungeon beast is actively channelling its own dark mana to command the corpses. It is using them as a physical shield."

As if hearing Brett’s whisper, the Crypt Weaver shifted in its web. The skulls forming its body clattered together horribly. The glowing green light within its abdomen flared.

Instantly, the three dead dire wolves snapped their heads towards the party. They did not move with the sluggish, mindless aggression of the other native undead. They moved with terrifying, perfectly synchronised speed, driven entirely by the intelligence of the Weaver above them.

The three massive wolves charged as one, completely silent, their jaws opening wide to reveal rotting fangs.

"Burn the webs!" Josh roared, stepping forward to intercept the charge. "Bhel, take the flank! Cut the puppets down!"

Josh planted his boots and raised his shield, bracing for a devastating three-pronged impact as the lunch hour officially turned into a fight for survival.
