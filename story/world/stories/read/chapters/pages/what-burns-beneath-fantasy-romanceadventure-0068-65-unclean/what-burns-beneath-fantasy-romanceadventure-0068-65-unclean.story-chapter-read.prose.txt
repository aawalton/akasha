The road north was a wide, tamped-down path strewn with rocks and creeping grasses. Towering, creaking pines and sloping tors framed either side. The sky was a dull gray haze, threatening either rain or snow, but fortunately neither one fell.

The horse- Barley, Royce announced, named by old farmer Jeth years ago when he’d discovered the creature’s insatiable appetite for the grain- was a welcome, good-natured presence. Barley was a sturdy beast with thick legs and a placid disposition. He tirelessly bore the weight of several packs without complaint.

Teela, Royce and I made pleasant conversation as we walked. Teela bounded along, animatedly regaling us with stories of her life in Respite. It had been a kind one, though sometimes lonely, from the way she spoke of it. Royce bobbed his head along, an enthusiastic listener, then swapped stories of our very unexciting childhoods in Fellbrook.

Renner wandered ahead, shoulders stiff and one hand often trailing to his sword hilt. I trotted forward several times, attempting to engage him in the conversation. This, quite problematically, led to thoughts of this morning. Of limbs twined practically together, of husky laughter, of the way his eyes were calm and fierce all at the same time and just held mine and… and it all ended with my face burning and my lips stammering nonsense and him giving me a mystified, vaguely annoyed look before I retreated from each meek attempt at conversation.

Get it together, Brin.

It was becoming my new mantra. I growled it to myself silently as the clouds darkened to purple and blue. Teeth gritted, fists clenched, gaze boring into Renner’s back.

You’re acting like a salted teenager. Yes, he saved your life. Yes, he’s handsome. Yes, he keeps saying suggestive things and getting far too close and he smells nice and… get it together! A few days ago he wanted to kill you! And… and things have changed, but… ashes, there’s no time for those sorts of thoughts. Not now.

The realization, and the truth behind it, was sobering. My eyes trailed to the shadowed, rock-strewn dirt beneath my feet. Every step suddenly felt quite heavy.

I’m still a criminal. I’m still bound to a Fae. I’m still in danger every night, and even if he doesn’t hurt me I might still be locked up or killed as soon as we get to the Order.

It wouldn’t be fair to him. Saints, especially not with Royce here. The two of them already don’t seem to like each other, and it would make everything awkward. He’d be crushed.

“Finally!” The cheerful boom shattered my maudlin thoughts, making me jump. Royce was craning his head up, peering ahead. “I’m starving!”

I followed his gaze, breathing my own sigh of relief at the sight. There was a break in the rustling treeline around a bend up ahead. A squat, gray-sided building sat nestled within dark firs and hissing pine nettles. There was a narrow lean-to beside it with stalls, watering troughs, and brown sacks of- presumably- horsefeed.

Teela, who had been lagging behind a bit, bounded forwards. “Oh, I hope they have something warm to drink. Tea or mulled wine or… honestly, I’d take anything at this point.”

The thought brought a spring to my step, too. Our attire was well-suited to the cold, thanks to captain Rell- and, in my case, my mother- but the bitter wind was still unpleasant. My nose was starting to go numb.

The building loomed silently beneath darkening clouds and a distant, midnight-hued horizon. The windows were black, and as we drew closer I noticed the front door was ajar. Royce led our stoic horse to the stalls, disappearing inside.

Renner reached the entrance first. I hurried in after him, ready to stretch my fingers out to the warmth of a hearth… and was immediately disappointed. It was indeed a small inn. There were a few rickety tables and chairs scattered around, with unlit candles set atop each. The room’s far side held a barren stone fireplace, a long countertop set with pewter dishware, and a closed, dark pine door. To our right lay several more closed doors.

The room was empty.

“Hello?” Teela called, stretching as she entered. Her voice resounded off the thick, grainy walls. There was no answer. “Innkeep? Anyone here?”

Renner prowled to the side, one thumb resting on a sheathed dagger. He spent a moment tapping on each closed door. Then, when silence was the only answer, creaking them open and inspecting the interiors.

“They must be out,” I offered, yawning and settling into a chair. “Drawing water, hunting, something like that.”

“Probably.” He sounded unconcerned, but his prowling didn’t cease. Sharp copper eyes flicked around, tracing every shadow.

“Looks like there’s a pantry over here,” Teela chirped. She was half-in an enclosed space near the back doorway, tail winding this way and that behind her. She stepped further and her voice echoed alongside the sound of rustling burlap, “Not much food in it, though. Let’s see… flour, oats, a big bag of grain. Guess we’re having rations again. Yay.” She reappeared and peeked out the back door. “Oh, there’s a well right out back! And a little garden.”

My ears pricked up at that. I hurried over and peered out the back window. Sure enough, a little plot of neatly-spaced, brittle-looking plants and herbs sat outside. A round stone well sat beneath the shaded canopy nearby.

“Probably won’t have anything decent, this time of year,” Renner muttered.

“Oh, don’t be so sure!” I chirped, practically skipping towards Teela and out the back door. “Some vegetables are resilient. I’ll bet you anything I can find us some tenacious little potatoes. Maybe a parsnip or two, some carrots… maybe even a squash.”

His boots thumped along behind me. “Wow. A whole squash.”

I flashed him a smile as we stepped outside, undeterred by the obvious sarcasm. “If we’re lucky!”

He leaned against the outer wall, arms folded and eyes scanning the treeline as I inspected the garden. Most of the plants were in poor condition. Weeds had choked out all the herbs, and most of the vegetables held curling, droopy leaves.

“Alar.” Two fingers traced. I spent the next few minutes pacing back and forth, repeating the rune as needed to ensure each plant got a nice long drink.

Teela bounded along, watching hopefully. “Find anything worth picking? I really don’t wanna just eat seed cakes and nuts for dinner.”

“I’m working on it,” I murmured, gaze trailing along the rows. I stopped to kneel quite often, poking and prodding into the foliage. “Looks like no one’s been tending to any of this lately. Oh, but… let’s see, this might be… ah-hah!”

I rose, victorious, my prize clutched between both hands.

“Wow,” Renner drawled, watching me from nearby. His mouth twitched. “A whole squash.”

Teela laughed aloud.

“And I see some little beans over here! Oh, and a carrot!” I gathered more bounty, dimpling at the feel of hard soil and crisp, cold leaves.

“They’re vegetables, Brin.” Teela paced alongside me, shaking her head and snickering. “Salt, I’ve never seen you so giddy.”

I held up a fistful of pearly-shelled beans. “Well, look at them! No one caring for them, weeds everywhere, hardly any rain lately, the weather so cold… and they did so well. All by themselves!”

“Uh-huh.” She accepted the handful, though she seemed far less interested in my enthusiasm and more in merely helping me collect some dinner. “Sure. Whatever you say.”

“Laugh all you want, but if one of you draws some water and finds me a kettle you’ll…” My brow furrowed in sudden thought. I frowned. “Oh. I’m sorry, I… stars above, this isn’t our food! What am I thinking?”

“Oh, I’m sure it’s fine!” the young woman swatted a narrow hand through the air. “It’s not that much food, and for all we know this place might be abandoned.”

I gnawed my lower lip, not fully convinced that we weren’t… well, stealing seemed like a strong term, but…

“You said no one’s tending to these, right? Not for a while?” She swept her hand out, gesturing to the plants around us, and gave a shrug. “Whoever owns it probably doesn’t mind.”

“I… I suppose so. And I can leave a coin to cover the cost. It’s probably fine.”

Teela’s horns gleamed crimson as she bobbed her head. “It’s definitely fine. And I definitely would rather eat fresh vegetables than a pile of oats. You said you need, what, a kettle?”

“And some water. One of those would do.” I nodded towards the well and the tethered wooden bucket atop its rim.

“I’ll do it.” Royce came loping out the back door. He stretched both long arms above his head, making joints pop. “Barley’s all set, and our things are inside.”

“Thanks!” Teela flashed him a grin and, much more quietly to me, whispered, “He’s awful handy.”

“I heard that!” He caught hold of the rope, lowering it with a creak and a grunt as Teela’s cheeks tinged pink. A distant splash echoed from deep within the stone. “Whew. Smells weird over here.”

I gently nudged her in the side and, loud enough for Royce to hear, proclaimed, “Very handy. You should see him chop firewood.” I cleared my throat as he dimpled. “As in, tonight? So I can cook? Right after the water’s drawn?”

“Salt, have mercy!” he laughed, glancing my way, then turning to peer down into the well. “Never a moment’s rest with this-” he fell silent abruptly, eyes going wide, and took a step back from the stones. His hands went white around the rope.

“What?” Renner’s voice was sharp.

“Um…” was all Royce managed. He glanced our way, face ashen.

I hurried over, Teela and Renner at my heels, as he slowly drew the wooden bucket up to the ledge. Wood creaked and water sloshed as he set it down.

A pungent, sickeningly sweet smell hit my nostrils. I peered at the bucket, eyes rounding and one hand covering my mouth.

The liquid inside was rust-colored and oddly thick. Silt and misshapen white flakes floated on the surface, alongside what was assuredly a large clump of long blonde hair. Every strand snaked lazy trails through the water, and they all seemed to connect at one end to a large wad of slick, slimy-looking flesh.

I gagged, stumbling back. My squash hit the ground with a dull thump.

Renner swore before turning to eye the treeline with renewed vehemence.

“Oh, salt, that can’t be…” Teela clapped both hands over her mouth, staring down in horror. “Is that from a person?”

I barely heard her. The seeping dusk around me was being swallowed by impossibly bright lines. Blazing across my vision, purest white amidst the shadows… graceful swirls and curves that wound through my mind, asking-

“Halaeth.” The word slipped out thoughtlessly as my feet drew me forwards. Vague horror echoed through the back of my mind as one hand traced the lines, carving white light into the frosty air, and the other hand dropped to the water.

“Brin!” Royce snatched my arm, pulling me back.

I barely felt his touch. My very skin was vibrating, burning hot and ice cold all at once. It felt as though I was being held against a fire even as a frigid wind swept across my flesh.

Spots filled my vision as the water shimmered and rippled. I slumped into familiar, strong arms, blinking down. Watching breathlessly, heartbeat roaring in my ears, as the bucket of reddish filth glowed white. Pearly and bright, so bright it was blinding, and then-

“Blood and bones,” Teela breathed, watching the sight warily. “You… cleansed it.” Her voice trembled, as though she could hardly believe the words.

I swayed against Royce, the sky spinning and dancing around me. “Huh?”

She grabbed the bucket with shaking hands and tilted it towards me. The water was crystal-clear and absent any debris.

“Brin? You okay?” Royce’s baritone, also trembling, steadied me as surely as his touch. “You look like you’re gonna faint.”

I blinked rapidly as he waved one broad hand in front of my face. “I’m… y-yes. Sorry. I… s-sorry.”

“Why are you sorry?”

“For making you… worry,” I mumbled, fighting to steady myself. My gaze, and fingers, held fast to the frosty stones.

A growl ripped from the back of Renner’s throat. “Everyone get inside. Now.”

No one moved. Teela gave a little whimper, staring at the dark building in obvious fear.

“Now!” he barked, and the sound jolted me into movement.

I stumbled inside in a daze, walls spinning around me. Royce’s warm hands remained clasped around my upper arms, keeping me upright until I made it to a chair.

Thumping, shuffling, and harsh voices echoed all around. The world settled back into focus as Renner snapped commands that were quickly obeyed. He and Royce rushed out to gather armfuls of firewood as Teela grabbed anything edible from the pantry. Once everyone was back inside, every bedroom and door was closed, locked, and had a table shoved in front of it.

“Surely it’s… someone must have fallen in. An accident, of course,” Royce insisted as he dragged a table across the room. Its legs grated and squealed against the wood. He was shaking his head. “Not a Fae. That’s madness to think… it must have been an accident-”

“Maybe.” Renner glowered as he attended to the fireplace. Dead pine boughs lay stacked inside the rocks and he held a tinderbox in one hand. “Could’ve been a monster, could’ve been some scum passing through. Could’ve been an accident. We’re not taking the chance. Everyone’s sleeping in here, and we’re keeping watches.”

Teela moaned, fists dragging through her hair. “Not again!”

“This area’s been a hag’s hunting grounds for ten years.” Thick, dark brows furrowed in thought. Red-orange light flickered as embers smoldered into flame, casting the planes of his face in a fierce hue. “And she seemed territorial, so this was probably just bandits. There’s probably nothing to worry about.”

“That’s what you said last time!” She stamped one boot on the floorboards, tail snapping like a whipcrack. “You said there was probably nothing-”

“Well, I was wrong!” His shout rang off the walls. Ugly, tense quiet followed in its wake.

“There’s… there’s another option.” My voice sounded very small in the darkening room.

Renner’s lips thinned. A muscle in his jaw twitched and, without looking away from the fire, he gave a short, sharp nod. He’d clearly already had the same thought as me. Salt, it might’ve been his first thought.

“What?” Royce looked between the two of us. Silence snaked through the room. “What’s the other option?”

Renner stood, mouth twisted into a grim snarl. His eyes met mine and we answered together, “Ward.”
