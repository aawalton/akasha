“I wonder what he’s trying to achieve,” Alycia said.

“We’ll only know when he comes and speaks for himself,” Cedric said with a shrug. “But if I had to guess, he probably wants to stop the investigation of that secret lair.”

“But you’ve found nothing so far that could actually accuse him or any of his men. On the other hand, by coming here and attempting to halt the investigation, he’s practically admitting that he’s guilty.”

“We haven’t found anything yet, true, but that doesn’t mean there’s nothing there to be found. So perhaps he’s stopping us precisely because he knows if we keep digging, we will eventually find it.”

Alycia gave a snort. “If there’s indeed something problematic buried inside that place, what he should do is send his men in to steal or destroy it, whatever it is. He has at least two Mithril-ranked adventurers at his command, does he not? Instead, he is doing... this. Again, what exactly does he expect to happen? Does he seriously believe we will stop just because he tells us to?”

“He might try to bribe us,” Cedric said.

“If bribery is what he has in mind, then he should’ve arranged a quiet, private meeting with your mother behind closed doors, not staged some sort of public spectacle that would attract attention from everyone in Daelin.”

To that, Cedric had no answer.

Well, Viktor had been thinking exactly that ever since he heard the message yesterday. He couldn’t imagine what Khalid thought the outcome of this so-called parley would look like. But then, the man himself would arrive soon enough, and when he did, Viktor supposed he would have his answer.

“By the way,” Cedric asked, glancing around, “Jeanne is not coming today?”

“Someone has to stay at the Sanctuary with Claire,” Viktor said. “We can’t all be away, after all. Yesterday it was Alycia, and today it’s Jeanne.”

“Also,” Rhea said, “She herself did not want to come. That man... Xander. He could very well be here, accompanying the Guildmaster of Iskora.”

“Right.” Cedric slowly nodded. “Seeing him again might traumatize her. No one could blame her for wanting to stay away.”

Actually, what she had said was more along the lines of, “If I go, I might not be able to control myself. I will fucking murder that bastard if I see him.”

But...

That was her; how about him?

What would he do if Xander walked in through that door right now?

Yesterday, he had spent quite a bit of time discussing with Celeste and his Guardians whether he should attack the bastard if he indeed came back to Daelin. He would not do it inside the Guild, of course, but he could ambush him on the road before or after this parley took place.

But then, Celeste told him that if Khalid was coming, there was a real possibility that Nephra would be somewhere in the vicinity as well, and starting a fight in her presence would be unwise. Better to hear what the man had to say first and get a clearer picture of what was happening before making a move. And, begrudgingly, he had agreed.

Now, though, standing here in this hall with the door not yet open, he found himself returning to the question.

If Xander walked through that threshold and appeared in front of him, in the flesh, what then?

He believed that he could control himself. That he could stop himself from doing something stupid.

But what if—

Suddenly, the door swung open, cutting through his thoughts.

The Guildmaster of Iskora entered, still absurdly tall, still absurdly thin, still expensively dressed, though something in the air around him had changed since the last time Viktor had seen him. The self-importance that clung to the man like a second coat had diminished considerably, and in its place was something else. He now looked... anxious?

However.

Viktor immediately lost interest in Khalid the moment he registered who was coming with him.

The man had only a single escort today, and it was neither Xander nor Zubayr. Instead, three steps behind him, there was a woman.

A woman with lustrous black hair sculpted into a sharp, straight bob that grazed her shoulders, a neat fringe of bangs sweeping across her forehead.

A woman whose face looked young but whose eyes were ageless.

A woman with skin of bronze.

Nephra.

Why? Why was she here?

The Guildmaster of Iskora stepped forward, doing his best to project authority as he walked into a hall full of people staring at him with open hatred. Nephra, on the other hand, followed at an unhurried pace, a faint smile resting at the corners of her lips.

Calyssa stood at the center of the chamber. “Guildmaster,” she said, her face completely neutral.

“Where is Guildmaster Isadora?”

“She’s waiting in the meeting room.”

“Then lead me there.”

The bespectacled woman smiled. “There are still fifteen minutes until the appointed time. Please, wait here. I’ll bring you through when the time comes.”

A bit of power play, isn’t it? Viktor thought, amused. Normally, he would have found such things unnecessarily theatrical. Petty, even. But this guy had earned it, so he was not going to complain.

And so, Khalid scurried to a corner. There, he studied the walls, he studied the floor, he studied the ceiling, he studied basically anything that was not the scornful face of anyone in this hall.

And the worst part—or the best part—was that his escort didn’t even bother to come and stand with him. Nephra drifted to a pillar, leaned against it, and let her gaze move across the chamber at her leisure.

When her eyes met Viktor’s, she smiled, raised a hand, and waved.

Just like last time, huh?

Fine.

Let’s see what she wants.

Without a word, he left his group and walked toward the woman.

“How is your sister?” she asked when he reached her.

“Same,” he said. “She’s still unconscious.”

“Let me make it clear. What was done to her was the action of that man and his alone. Neither my lord nor I had any part in it, and it was never our intention to allow such a thing to happen. We would never have permitted it had we known it was coming. It was, in the end, a tragic misfortune. Even so, I’m sorry that it happened. It was a failure of control on our part. Please accept my apology for this oversight.”

Viktor stared at the woman. She was a powerful Ascendant. An ancient princess who had lived for thousands of years. The most fervent servant of Nakhran, the Second God of Death. Why was she apologizing to him?

“You called me over just to say that?”

“That’s one of the reasons,” Nephra said. “The other is that I believe there are things you want to ask me.”

True enough.

“When you say ‘my lord,’ you mean Khalid, or someone else?”

Nephra smiled. “That you’ll know eventually.”

So, questions about who she served and what she really was were off the table. Fine. He would just move on.

“Are you my enemy?”

Nephra let out a soft laugh. “That’s rather blunt. Well, I can only say that I bear you no ill will myself. Everything I have done, and everything I will do, is in alignment with my lord’s design.”

Which meant, read plainly, if her lord became his enemy, she would follow without hesitation.

“You do everything for your master?” Viktor asked. “You have no desires of your own?”

A shift crossed her expression. Barely, but not unnoticeable. Then she looked—not at him, not at anyone else, not at anything in this hall—but at something far away.

“You’re right. Not everything I do is for him. Just as he’s playing his game, I have a game of my own that I’ve been waiting to play for a very long time. It hasn’t started yet; I’m still preparing the board and setting the pieces where they need to be. But before long, she will arrive, and when she does, our game can finally begin.”

“Who?”

Nephra smiled again. “That you’ll know eventually.”

Ugh.

Didn’t it mean this other game would involve him as well? Why the hell had he been drawn into two different games?

Can’t you people just play games by yourselves without randomly forcing other people to participate?

Oh, well. There was nothing to be gained from complaining about it. Instead, he should take the opportunity to gather as much information as possible. Surely, she wouldn’t have called him over just to say “No” to all of his questions. She was willing to answer some of them, only not those she decided he wasn’t meant to know yet.

Let’s ask about something more immediate, then.

Should he inquire about Xander or Khalid, perhaps? The parley was about to begin, though, so asking such questions before hearing what that Guildmaster had to say felt a bit premature. And if he asked, “What does he want to parley for?” her answer would probably be, “That you’ll know in a few minutes.”

So he chose a different topic. “What’s the deal with Clovis?”

“He’s resigned. But I think you already knew that.”

“Why?”

“Because I told him to,” Nephra said, smiling.

“What?”

“Did you know that I have complete control over the space surrounding Iskora? No one can open a rift into that city, or out of it, without my knowledge. And if I choose to, I can shut down the link instantly.”

What kind of ridiculous power was that? It was far beyond the abilities of even the greatest Riftwalker in this world. She wasn’t even trying to hide what she was anymore, was she?

“One day, a rift opened, connecting Daelin to Iskora. It was made by a bald Riftwalker, and through it stepped a man. A curious man, carrying a curious gauntlet. So I told Clovis to resign.”

She was talking about Pimm, wasn’t she? And the gauntlet was the detonator Viktor had used to destroy Iskora’s portal and construction site. She merely looked at one man stepping through a rift and deduced an attack was coming from just that? And that was the reason Clovis had abandoned his post right before the strike?

“By that point, he was Guildmaster in name only. The sponsors of the project had been gradually losing patience with him after his repeated failures. Accordingly, his authority had been stripped away over time. When Rennald declined his offer and chose to work with Isadora instead, thanks to you and your sister, that was the final straw. The project was no longer in his hands, and Khalid took over from there.”

So the monster attack was indeed the new Guildmaster’s idea. Viktor had suspected as much, but it was nice to have it confirmed.

“I asked Clovis, ‘Why should you remain here, to carry responsibility for something that was never yours to begin with?’ So he resigned. That was not even passing the blame. It was simply returning it to where it belonged.”

“Why was he so obsessed with this dungeon?” Viktor asked.

Nephra giggled. “That you would have to ask him yourself. I’m sure you and he will meet face to face at some point in the future.”

“Where is he now?”

“East,” Nephra said. “He’s currently a guest of the Iron Khagan.”

The Iron Khagan, huh?

A direct descendant of Tzhara, and the reigning monarch of the Tzharan Khanate. One of the five remaining Key Holders.

Viktor looked at the woman, turning over the implication in his mind. Was she telling him that after Dagnar, the next target would arrive from the East?

Nephra, on the other hand, moved her gaze past him, shifting toward the center of the hall.

“It’s time,” she said. “Let’s go. The parley is about to begin.”
