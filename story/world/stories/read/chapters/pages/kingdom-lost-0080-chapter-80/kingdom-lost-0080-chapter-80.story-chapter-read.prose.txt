The Clawborn army pressed deep into the north. Black rock rose sharp beneath a thin crust of snow that crunched under thousands of boots. The ground felt unforgiving here, nothing like the softer southern lands the soldiers knew. Grey skies stretched overhead, heavy and low, while a single bright line clung to the distant horizon and turned the far trees into dark silhouettes.

Cold bit through every layer of wool and leather. The wind carried a raw edge that clawed at exposed skin and made men hunch their shoulders as they marched. Mountains flanked the column on both sides, their slopes steep and shadowed, hemming the army into a narrow valley that offered little shelter.

This was the bulk of the Clawborn force. Most soldiers moved on foot, their steps steady but slowed by the weight of winter gear. Cavalry rode stocky horses bred for harsh ground, shorter and thicker through the chest than the sleek mounts used back home. Infantry carried long spears that swayed in rhythm with their stride. Archers and other ranged troops kept formation behind them. Every man wore heavy cloaks and fur-lined boots suited for the biting weather.

The entire host followed a small group at the front. In the center of that group rode one figure who stood out clearly. A blood-red cloak draped over his shoulders and stirred with each movement of his horse. On his chest the standard Clawborn mark appeared, but crossed by a second set of diagonal red claw marks that intersected the first in a sharp X pattern. General Khal-Vor of the Clawborn Elite sat straight in the saddle, his gaze fixed on the frozen path ahead.

The army continued its march without pause, the only sounds the steady crunch of snow underfoot and the occasional snort of a horse against the chill air.

General Khal-Vor raised one gloved hand and shouted a sharp command in the Clawborn tongue. The words rolled down the column as officers repeated them, each voice passing the order until the entire army halted. Boots stopped crunching against the snow. Horses stamped and blew clouds of steam from their nostrils.

Khal-Vor sat tall in the saddle, shoulders squared and reins held loosely in one hand. His eyes moved slowly across the ranks, taking in the disciplined lines of infantry with their long spears grounded, the stocky cavalry mounts shifting their weight, and the archers who stood ready with bows unstrung against the cold. He scanned the black rock and thin snow underfoot, then lifted his gaze to the mountains that boxed the valley on both sides. A faint smile touched the corner of his mouth, the expression of a man who had easily measured every advantage and explored every risk with the same effort other used to do the simplest of tasks.

The valley stretched straight ahead for a short distance before it bent sharply to the left. The turn hid everything beyond from view, leaving only the grey sky and the shadowed slopes visible. Khal-Vor studied that blind corner for several long moments, his posture relaxed, as though the unknown terrain were merely another piece of ground to be claimed.

He gave a single nod, satisfied with what he saw and what he did not see. The army waited in silence behind him, breath visible in the frigid air.

A ripple passed through a cluster of Clawborn soldiers gathered near the center of the halted column. Several rode stocky horses that suddenly tossed their heads and snorted, ears flicking nervously. The animals shifted their weight and stamped at the snow. Within moments the horses grew more agitated, eyes rolling white as they danced sideways. One reared sharply, front legs kicking upward, forcing its rider to yank the reins and spur the mount clear. Others followed, their riders pulling back to create space.

The soldiers on foot stepped away from their neat ranks, spears tilting as they retreated several paces. All eyes fixed on the same spot just above the ground where the air began to warp and shimmer.

An oval portal took shape, standing perpendicular to the snow. Grey and gold swirled together at its edges while threads of deep blue wove through the center, laced with thin wisps of black that drifted like smoke. The colors pulsed once, then settled into a stable frame.

Edrin Kavos stepped through the opening with calm, measured strides. His dark robes brushed the snow as his boots touched solid ground. The moment he cleared the threshold the portal flickered, its colors fading rapidly until nothing remained but empty air and the faint scent of ozone.

The Edrin stood still for a breath, adjusting the hood of his cloak against the biting wind. He turned his gaze toward General Khal-Vor at the head of the vanguard and offered a respectful nod, then shuttered with the sudden gust of cold wind.

General Khal-Vor remained mounted a short distance away, and ask Edrin approached the General looked off into the distance for a moment then slowly looked back in Edrin's direction. The blood-red cloak shifted slightly in the cold wind while the intersecting claw marks on his chest caught the weak light. Edrin met the general's gaze and walked steadily through the snow toward him.

"What news do you bring from the empress?" Khal-Vor asked, looking down from the saddle.

Edrin stopped beside the horse and lifted his head. "She sends no word."

Khal-Vor sneered, his eyes sliding away from Edrin without meeting them again.

Hooves pounded against the frozen ground as a scout rode up from the rear. The man began speaking rapidly in the Clawborn tongue, his words urgent. Khal-Vor listened for a moment and seemed slightly disinterested, then his face hardened.

Edrin glanced sideways at Khal-Vor. The general turned his head briefly toward the mage, then looked back at the scout. "Speak the common tongue," he ordered.

The scout straightened in the saddle and switched languages. "It is beyond the next bend. It has changed."

"How has it changed?" Edrin asked.

Khal-Vor waved the man away with a sharp motion of his gloved hand. Edrin turned and looked at the general. The scout rode away without another word.

"What will a scout tell you that your own eyes cannot?" Khal-Vor grunted. He fixed his stare on the sharp left turn in the valley ahead. "What is this?"

Edrin stepped closer until he stood beside the general’s horse. He followed Khal-Vor’s gaze toward the hidden bend and spoke quietly. "Something more than I thought."

The army remained halted behind them, the cold wind stirring cloaks and manes while every soldier waited in silence for the next command.

General Khal-Vor grunted once and shouted another command in the Clawborn tongue. The soldiers nearest to him immediately began moving forward toward the bend in the valley. Officers repeated the order down the line until the entire army stirred back into motion, boots crunching through the thin snow as the long column advanced.

"A horse?" Edrin called out sharply toward the general.

Khal-Vor glanced back over his shoulder and sneered. After a moment he raised his arm in a reluctant wave. A cavalry soldier broke from the ranks, rode quickly to Edrin, and dismounted with a low grunt. The man thrust the reins into the mage's hands.

Edrin smiled as he took them. He swung up into the saddle with practiced ease and urged the stocky horse forward until he rode level with General Khal-Vor. Together they continued toward the sharp left turn, the vanguard moving ahead of the main host.

The moment the small group rounded the bend they saw it and pulled their mounts to an abrupt stop. The valley opened before them into a wide, frozen expanse that had clearly changed. Black rock and snow gave way to something far more unnatural, and every rider in the lead stared in silence at the sight that now filled the space ahead.

A massive spiral of energy hung suspended in the air above the frozen expanse. It turned slowly on a slight tilt, dense and enormous, like a galaxy caught in the moment of birth. Black and red dominated the vortex, streaked with thin whisps of cream, gold, and grey that circled endlessly into its core. The entire formation twisted upon itself, a visible distortion in the fabric of reality.

In the distance a lone bird altered its path sharply, wings beating hard as it veered wide to avoid the anomaly altogether.

The soldiers behind the vanguard shifted in place. Shoulders tightened. Spears were gripped a fraction higher. A few men glanced at one another before quickly looking away, their postures rigid beneath heavy winter cloaks. The stocky cavalry horses stamped and blew nervous breaths that misted in the cold air.

General Khal-Vor swung down from his saddle and planted his boots firmly in the snow. He stood tall, shoulders squared, and tilted his head back to stare up at the swirling vortex with open contempt.

Edrin remained mounted for a moment longer, then dismounted slowly. He stared at the spiraling mass. His eyes narrowed and the skin around his jaw tightened as he studied the unnatural sight.

The army stayed halted at the bend, the cold wind whispering across the black rock while every man waited for the general or the mage to speak.
