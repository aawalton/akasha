Chien

Chien lounged, lounged in a suite deep in a city he'd never actually been to. Not because he didn't want to, but because he'd legitimately never had a reason, and he had to admit, it was stunning.

Outside streets bustled with people, moving to and fro, all going about their business, all enjoying their lives. A couple walked hand in hand a few streets away from the window beneath a tree before kissing, it brought a smile to his face. A man sold fresh fruits another street down, his voice indistinguishable but loud even from here.

And the trees, oh the trees, worked into a few of the buildings in ways he'd not seen in so, so long. It was a wonder, and he knew where that idea had come from. Of course she would know how to do that, of course she would feel some need to.

“You're here,” he said, as he felt the woman in question open the door and step inside, bits of her aura flowing past him.

“Enjoying yourself Chien?” she asked him, even as he didn't turn to look at her.

“Immensely, I should have visited this place years and years ago.”

“Yes, you should have, and visited me. That's not why we're here now though Ambassador, or Councilor, or what are you calling yourself now?”“You could always call me uncle like you used to Adia. Those were better days.” I turned to face her, one of the few beings in this world who was nearly as old as myself, and one of the few who was a genuine threat to me.

The room was richly enough appointed, if simply. The furniture here, and there were chairs aplenty, was well made without being gaudy, just properly worked wood and cushions. It was how these people did things, avoiding greed and the like. Both of us moved towards the table in the center of the room, yet neither yet sat down.

“I'm not here to play games or reminisce,” she began.

“And yet you invited me.”

“Invite and angry demand for audience are not the same thing.”“We both know you're not that angry, and we both know if I didn't want to come I wouldn't have. You're a brilliant woman now these days Adia, but that does not mean you command me. So what is this about.”

“The old king,” she began.“Your father.”

“Came to Hediza, a clear violation, and the second such time he has done so in the last thousand years.”“I'm surprised you heard of that, though technically he didn't, an island off the coast only.”“That is sophistry and we both know it!” she almost yelled.

“Perhaps, but still close enough to the letter of the law to not worry me. Nor does the previous visit, as it was effected by that charming young woman, whose death he had come to that island to investigate.”“Chien.” It was clear she was frustrated.

“No, you're acting like a spoiled brat, which I know for a fact you aren't. You've got your issues, and I understand them, I do, even if I think they're foolish. This though, your over-aggression here, about an island, where I was with him, and we literally did nothing of note, enough of it. You don't believe it, and neither do I.” I finally took my seat, breathing deeply to steady myself.

“Justice,” she tried.

“Should be tempered by mercy and understanding. You seem to have forgotten that lesson. I know how many your order has killed without trial or chance, yours and the others. Don't think you can hide that from me. I thought you knew better, even if all the people your people killed were guilty as they could possibly be.”

She too circled around her seat, her long ears brushing the back of the seat much as mine did.

“If he comes back to the mainland it will start a war. There's little I can do about that.”“There's plenty you could dear, but mind if I make a suggestion?”“Is it a joke?”“Actually no,” I replied. “Go and visit him. Not as this,” I commented, waving at her robe and formality. “Go and see your father.”“It's been a long time...” she said, looking off to the West.

“Indeed, all the more reason to do so.”“Where do you even start after so long?”“With 'Hello.' generally.” That one got me a small chuckle, almost a laugh. “He's hurting.”“We all do,” she nodded. “And it's not like he's been going around trying to make amends.”“People change, even your father. As for amends, he has fled from power, done what he can to revitalize what he can, and to protect others. So far as repentance goes that's about the best he might be able to manage, that and studying in an attempt to do, whatever it is he's trying to do.”

“I'll think on it uncle,” she finally said, using the old term I'd loved so much.

“Try to take less than a millennium,” I replied, getting a snort at least.
