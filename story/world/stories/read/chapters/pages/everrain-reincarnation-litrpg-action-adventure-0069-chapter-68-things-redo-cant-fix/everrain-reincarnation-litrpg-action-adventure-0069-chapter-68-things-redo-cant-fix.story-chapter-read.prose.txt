Day 182, 08:00 AM

Ten redos, and my mind is fraying, so it’s about time I stopped. The twenty weeks have been fruitful, I have discovered the level up conditions for two archmage paths, but a more important discovery is that black mana is not related to objects, but matter in general.

For a mage to advance into a path of matter archmage, they need to fuse two materials into a single one, but there’s a caveat. Fusing two metals or metal and wood won’t work. They are too similar, or the fusion is too simple to register. However, fusing a stone bowl with the water inside it to create a new, mind-boggling substance, which is cool, liquid rock with a water’s boiling point is a feat worthy of becoming an archmage.

Path of matter has similar skills and level up conditions as the path of life, but they are focused on matter. It makes sense, and I tried to experiment with all other mana colors, but the twelve weeks I had set aside for experimentation had proved insufficient.

“This is enough. I’m going crazy, and it’s time to stop.” I shape Batsy into a spear and pierce my heart.

My chest feels tight as I’m about to leave Hadriuse’s abode for the eleventh time. I turn around in the corridor and head for the kitchen, where Lucy and Gila are chatting.

“Why did we turn the kitchen into a hub?” Blunt asks something that’s been on my mind for years.

The girls look at me in confusion as the door closes behind me.

“Sorry, I was hasty when I said I would handle the dungeon alone. While it’s true that it takes much more time to do it with you, we should do it together. But how about we wait two weeks before heading out? The monsters will grow a bit stronger, but I don’t think it will impact us.”

Their confused looks grow more confused. The problem of doing redos after interacting with people, I need to find a balance to it. One option was to have the same conversation with them over and over again, the other was to do it once, and then change my mind in what seems like five minutes to them.

Best to avoid both.

I’ll need to consider the matter in the future. A possible solution is not sharing plans with others and just doing things, then there’s nothing to confuse them.

Stop! They are staring at you, even Lucy seems afraid.

And really, their pupils are open wide, like scared animals looking for a way out.

“I’m sorry if I worried you.” I take a seat at the table. “Do you know where Millie and Elie are?”

“Probably in their room,” Lucy says.

“Do you have any problems with them? Did they do something?” I try to use the most unthreatening tone I have at my disposal, and the girls shake their heads.

“In that case, would you mind if I invited them to the kitchen?” Yes, this is what I need. Grounded, human talk. Something to anchor me back after running through deaths and experiments with no heed for my safety or my mental health.

The girls hesitate, the kitchen has become our place, where we hang out and chat or cook or whatever, and they don’t want to let outsiders in. Bet, after a brief pause, they nod.

“Perfect, I’ll call them over.”

Is exploring level up options really that important? Is it worth risking my sanity? The answer is probably no. I could leave and reenter classes to achieve what I want without resorting to Redo, but the circumstances with Edna are fairly unique. Her life is at stake, as are ours. Despite being unhinged myself, leaving my fate in the hands of a crazy person is not something I want to do when I have the power to change it.

I will spend the next two weeks, socializing, helping the girls and my apprentices in any way I can. Hopefully, that will help me regain my balance.

On the third day, Elie casts cleaner, and becomes a journeyman mage.

BSD offers me a choice between Initial Mana Affinity and Expert Mana Ambivalence, with hardly a thought, I upgrade my skill. When I reach the third level of archmage, I will push it to master tier, but until then, I shall remain a mage and push the class until I reach the cap.

The next level up condition is to help my apprentice become a mage, and I dedicate my days to that goal. All five of us head into the dungeon once a month to clear it and to grab some attribute points for the girls. And while we are at the mansion, I answer their questions, help them pick which books to read, and enjoy swimming.

Three months have passed without redos, and my mind has mostly recovered. I’m swimming in the guest pool, when Lucy comes in. The girl’s face is marbled red as she disrobes and enters the pool.

She swims towards me with the most comically embarrassed face I have seen in a long time. She stops next to me and looks me in the eye.

“Griff,” her voice trembles, “It’s my twentieth birthday today, and…” she loses her thread, but quickly regains her wits.

“I love you. I want to be with you. Twenty years is prime marrying age.”

She squirms, and staying silent would be cruel towards her. I don’t love her the same way she loves me. I don’t even love her as a child. More like a friend or a pet, which is horrible to say to a person, but goes to show my mental state. Also, what kind of pickup line is, “I’m of a marrying age”?

“Lucy…” Damn, I don’t know what to say, and there’s no way I’m redoing this. “I like you. I really do. The timing is horrible. Edna will march against the church in some six months, possibly less, and I plan to join her. I can’t drag you into this, nor would it be fair to leave you behind if I died fighting at the capital.”

The girl swims closer, until we’re inches apart, and rises from the water, her breasts staring at my face. It’s a good move, one which would have captured inexperienced youths, but I’m not an inexperienced youth.

Which makes it even worse. I know she won’t back away, and my options are to go with the flow or to push her away. She doesn’t seem the type, but having to think about a psycho mage cooking my pet cockroach is more trouble than the alternative.

She will get pregnant. Another part of me whispers. She takes a step forward and buries my face in her breasts.

Half an hour later, Lucy is sleeping on a towel, her happy grin a mockery to my conflicted mood. I told myself months ago I wouldn’t do this, and yet here I am. I jump back into the pool, but somehow I still feel filthy.

She’s twenty years old, I’m what? A thousand? Two thousand? Close to a hundred times her age either way. Maybe I should redo—

No. Life’s choices are something I can’t alter like that, unless I die to unforeseen circumstances. Otherwise, my last tether will disappear, and I will descend into madness. I dry myself with a spell, and don my servant clothes before heading out.

Maybe Edna will kill me while we chat. I wouldn’t even hold it against her. Then, if I redo, I can hide in the secret lab for a few days so that Lucy forgets her crazy idea.

“Hey, Edna,” I greet the only person crazier than me for miles around. “How’s your army going?”

“I’m making good progress.” Her voice is flat, void of emotion, as if she’s speaking while dreaming. “A hundred inquisitors and two dozen minor abominations attacked three weeks ago, and the tree soldiers destroyed them. I lost only two of them. Soon, I will be ready to attack the capital.”

She shows no emotion whatsoever. I don’t particularly care about the dead inquisitors, nor the lost treants, but she should. She should be happy with her victory, or at least satisfied. We have removed nearly a hundred and fifty inquisitors so far. Her revenge is at hand, and she’s completely hollow.

“Call me before you depart, I wish to head for the capital with you and help as much as I can.”

She focuses on me, seemingly seeing me for the first time. “You’re growing nicely. You will make a small difference in the siege, but I guess it doesn’t hurt to have an extra soldier. I’ll call you in about three months’ time.”

That’s all she has to say to me. I’m not sure what is a bigger disappointment, Edna’s mental state or the fact that she didn’t kill me. It’s probably the latter. I can fix Edna’s insanity, but what can I do about my own?
