Over the past week, Sakura had grown to hate the bunk where she had to spend most of her day. Ater was a good healer, but an absolutely unbearable person when it came to making sure she followed his instructions. He forbade her from lifting anything heavy, walking for too long, or even turning too sharply. The last restriction was especially irritating. Sakura kept forgetting about it and only remembered when the wound beneath her ribs started hurting.

Still, she was feeling better now. Her bandage needed changing less often, her thigh had almost stopped bothering her, and for the past few days she had even been helping Lir with chores without overexerting herself. Gilbert was also gradually recovering from his injury, although Ater still would not allow him to do anything too strenuous.

Faerd was recovering more slowly than the others. After the incident during training, he rarely approached Sakura unless he had to, and she was not particularly eager to change that. Gilbert could already discuss everyday matters with Faerd without any trouble, but trust was still out of the question. Still, Sakura and Faerd had managed to exchange a few words once, and neither of them had tried to kill the other. Not bad.

That day, Ater returned from a short walk with a bundle of old papers tucked under his arm. He placed them on the table where Gilbert was studying a map and began searching through his belongings. For the past week, the mage had been planning their next route. They needed to leave the area before their pursuers reached the hideout and restock their supplies before the journey.

"The Night of Other Faces starts tomorrow near the town," the old man announced. "The merchants I usually buy medicine from will be there. If you still need those ingredients, you should take advantage of the opportunity."

Gilbert raised his head.

"I thought you said they wouldn't arrive for another few days."

The old man gave his usual irritated snort.

"Then they're arriving earlier," he snapped. "The festival starts in the evening, but many merchants arrive in the morning."

Sakura, who had been sitting near the stove, turned toward them.

"What festival?"

"Did you injure your ears along with your ribs? The Night of Other Faces," Ater repeated. "Once a year, people put on masks and pretend they have something worth celebrating."

"How cynical."

"A pointless festival! But at least there's plenty of food, music, and drunk people. So there's something to see. If you can see..."

"Oh!" Sakura straightened. "Now that sounds much more interesting."

Gilbert did not even look up from the map.

"No."

"But Gilbert! Can't we go too?"

"To buy medicine? We can."

"No. To the festival."

"No."

Sakura gave him an offended look, then turned toward Lir, who was sitting at the table peeling an apple.

"What will be there?" the young man asked.

"A fair, performances, dancing," Ater listed. "They usually organize games and competitions too. Nothing particularly remarkable."

"Dancing?" Lir repeated.

"And lots of alcohol," Sakura added, well aware of which part of the description would interest him more. She figured a fisherman's son had to be interested in drinking, at least a little.

Gilbert set the map aside.

"You both realize we're wanted, don't you? And not just in Mebrel."

"That's exactly why we'll wear masks!" Sakura declared. "Isn't that a wonderful opportunity? Nobody knows who's hiding underneath them."

"Sakura, a mask doesn't make you invisible."

"And sitting in the forest all the time doesn't make our lives particularly enjoyable. Listen, we're leaving soon anyway. When are we going to get another chance like this?"

Gilbert looked at her, then at Lir, who had already stopped peeling his apple and was waiting for an answer.

"Are you two going to keep trying to convince me until tonight?"

"If necessary," Sakura assured him.

"I can help too," Lir offered.

Ater chuckled quietly.

"If you're worried about the city guards, the festival takes place outside the walls. There's a large market square near the old road leading toward the Lake of Six Mists. Anyone can enter the fair."

Gilbert turned back to the map.

"There's an old road to the square," Ater added. "The locals use it to transport goods. If you approach the main road from the east, you won't have to pass through the city gates at all."

"And how many guards will be there?" Gilbert asked.

"A few patrols near the market stalls. They're mostly watching for drunken fights and thieves. Nobody's going to check every visitor."

Sakura smiled with satisfaction.

"See!"

"I haven't agreed yet."

She was already preparing another argument when Faerd, who had been sitting silently against the wall, raised his head.

"I can stay here."

Sakura looked at him. After everything that had happened, the man had barely left the guardhouse except for walks near the canal. He seemed so quiet, distant, and withdrawn that it was sometimes frightening. Though no more frightening than everything else associated with his name. Sakura still had not forgotten what he had done. However, she was no longer as angry as she had been that day.

"Why?" she asked Faerd. "You can come too."

He was silent for a moment.

"I haven't been to anything like that in a long time. I'm not sure I even remember how to behave in places like that..."

"All the more reason to come. It'll give you a chance to remember and practice being sociable."

Gilbert sighed and pulled the map closer. Sakura watched him study the road leading to the market square and several nearby side routes once more. As always, the mage was meticulous about such things.

"Fine. But we're only going if everyone follows a few conditions."

Sakura threw her hands up in triumph, though she immediately winced and carefully lowered one of them toward her injured side.

"I'm already starting to regret this," Gilbert remarked.

By the following evening, Sakura was thoroughly convinced that Gilbert had absolutely no idea how to relax.

He had studied the road in advance, asked Ater about the layout of the market stalls, chosen a meeting place in case anyone got lost, and even set a time for their return. They could reach the square from the guardhouse along a relatively flat road, without having to climb slopes or make their way through a swamp. The only thing left to find out was whether Sakura could handle that much walking.

"No fighting," the mage said as the others prepared to leave.

"What if someone attacks us?" Lir asked.

"Then we leave quickly."

"And if we can't?"

"Then we deal with the situation."

"Great plan," Sakura remarked sarcastically. "Very detailed."

Gilbert ignored her.

"No using magic. I think that should be obvious, but I'm going to say it out loud anyway."

At those last words, he looked directly at Sakura.

"Why are you telling me?"

"Because I know you."

"Seriously? I've never used my skills without a reason."

Sakura was almost offended. Gilbert raised an eyebrow, and she immediately understood what he was remembering. She snorted and crossed her arms over her chest.

"And no getting drunk," the mage continued. "One mug each if you want. Two at most, if the drink is weak. I'm not carrying anyone back through the forest."

Lir drew out his complaint.

"Only two?"

"You don't have to drink at all."

"Two sounds wonderful," he said, suddenly changing his mind.

Sakura grinned. By then, Gilbert had already taken out the familiar tin of "Gray Veil." They still had a small supply of the ointment left after Ogrom. He checked the contents, closed the lid, and put the tin back into [Inventory].

"We'll apply it before entering the fair," he explained. "Just don't get it wet or rub it off. It won't protect you from someone looking closely. It only interferes with System recognition. And don't even think about drawing attention to yourselves."

Sakura remembered that nasty gray stuff and its unpleasant smell all too well.

"Do we really have to pretend we're completely boring people?" she asked.

"Preferably."

"One look at Faerd is enough to tell there's something wrong with him," the girl muttered.

Faerd looked up at her but said nothing. Ater, who had been sitting near the stove, finally got up and approached Sakura. He checked the bandage on her side, carefully pressed near the wound, and nodded in satisfaction.

"You can walk. But no jumping, running, or any other foolishness."

"I'm going to a festival, not a battle."

"Then try not to confuse the two. Especially after drinking."

There was nothing she could say to that. Pampli saw them off with an extremely displeased growl. He clearly had no desire to stay at the guardhouse with Ater, especially when he realized Sakura was leaving without him. She had to spend several minutes persuading him and promising to bring back something tasty. This had almost become a tradition, although it always hurt Sakura that she couldn't take her beast out into the wider world.

"And try to make sure all four of you come back," Ater said before they left. "I have absolutely no desire to go around collecting your Inventories from the surrounding area."

"How touching," Gilbert replied.

They reached the fair shortly before dark. The first festival lights were visible from the road. Dozens of paper lanterns hung above the market stalls, colorful ribbons stretched between wooden posts, and music was already playing somewhere ahead. Sakura could make out individual voices, laughter, and the loud shouts of merchants, but wagons still blocked her view of the square itself.

Not far from the fair entrance, Gilbert stopped the others beside an empty wagon. This was where they finally used the "Gray Veil." Sakura rubbed a thin layer of ointment onto her wrists and winced at the familiar smell, then quickly hid her hands beneath her sleeves.

"Now the masks," Gilbert reminded them.

They were being sold nearby. The selection was so large that Sakura spent almost fifteen minutes at the stall.

In the end, she chose a small red fox mask with pointed ears. It covered the upper half of her face, and even her eyes were barely visible beneath it. Gilbert bought himself a plain black mask without decorations. His light-colored hair was now hidden beneath a dark hood.

Lir found something absolutely unbelievable: a green mask with enormous yellow feathers sticking out in every direction.

"Are you seriously going to wear that?" Sakura asked.

"What's wrong with it?"

"Nothing. But if you get lost, I don't think we'll have much trouble finding you again."

Lir proudly pulled on the mask and adjusted the feathers. Meanwhile, Faerd stood a little farther away, showing little interest in the selection. Sakura noticed a white mask decorated with small flowers and silver butterflies. She thought about it for a moment, then bought it.

"Here."

Faerd looked at the gift.

"Is this for me?"

"Well, yeah. You still haven't picked anything. And going to the festival without a mask isn't an option."

The man examined the mask carefully, then calmly put it on. Lir made a strangled sound, turned away, and covered his mouth with his hand, apparently trying very hard not to laugh. Sakura had to make an effort not to burst out laughing herself. The silver butterflies looked especially funny on Faerd's serious face.

"What?" he asked.

"Nothing," Sakura answered with a broad smile. "It really suits you."

Gilbert studied him for a while.

"I think nobody will recognize you now."

Faerd shrugged.

"Then the mask is serving its main purpose."

Lir could no longer hold back and burst out laughing.

Still, once they entered the square, Sakura realized that her companions' masks were nowhere near the strangest ones there. Some visitors wore enormous horns, long beaks, or even entire wooden animal heads. One man proudly carried a pumpkin the size of a small barrel on his shoulders, while his friend appeared to be dressed as a bird with four eyes.

Compared to them, Lir with his feathers and Faerd with his butterflies looked perfectly respectable and almost inconspicuous. Sakura and Gilbert looked positively ordinary by comparison.

The fair turned out to be much larger than Sakura had expected. Market stalls occupied almost the entire square outside the city walls, and crowds of people in all kinds of costumes moved between them. Sakura kept stopping to examine someone's mask or the goods on a stall, and Gilbert had to remind her several times that they needed to buy the necessary supplies first.

The air smelled of roasted meat, sweet pastries, and spiced drinks. Musicians performed near a fountain, and not far away, several people on tall stilts entertained children by waving long ribbons. One of them leaned toward Sakura and greeted her with an elegant bow. She laughed and waved back.

Meanwhile, Gilbert was already heading toward the healers' tents.

"Come on," he called.

"I'm coming!"

Shopping took about half an hour. Gilbert bought several medicinal herb mixtures, bandages, and small vials of ingredients he planned to use on the road. After moving away from prying eyes, he stored the packages in [Inventory], checked their remaining money, and finally looked at his companions.

"That's everything. Now we can walk around for a while."

"How generous of you," Sakura replied.

Lir immediately dragged them toward the nearest food stalls. They sold hot flatbreads with cheese, roasted pears, meat on wooden skewers, candied nuts, and countless other things with complicated names Sakura did not even try to remember. She bought a flatbread, then couldn't resist getting a small serving of sweet nuts as well. Walking past a stall that smelled so delicious had proved completely impossible.

Meanwhile, Lir had acquired two meat skewers, a pie, and a large bundle of pastries.

"Are you planning to feed everyone at the guardhouse?" Gilbert asked.

"No. This is for me."

"All of it?"

"I didn't eat much today."

Sakura looked at him with genuine respect.

"You have... a really impressive stomach."

Faerd lingered near a pastry stall. He studied the selection for quite a while until the woman selling them finally asked what exactly he wanted. The man chose a small pie filled with meat and mushrooms, paid for it, and returned to the others. Sakura noticed that he first tried a very small bite, then took another with noticeably more enjoyment.

They found an empty table near a drinks stall. Sakura ordered a mug of warm spiced apple cider, which turned out to be surprisingly delicious. It was sweet, slightly tart, and pleasantly warming.

"Oh, this is wonderful," she announced after her first sip.

Gilbert tried his drink.

"Not bad."

"Not bad? Are you even capable of enjoying anything?"

"Right now, I'm enjoying the fact that you're not trying to get yourself killed."

"What a romantic answer."

Gilbert looked at her over his mug but said nothing. A moment later, Sakura realized how her remark had sounded and quickly took another sip.

Fortunately, Lir changed the subject just then. He started explaining why one of the roasted pears had seemed suspiciously small for its price. Gilbert replied that nobody had forced him to buy the pear, and Sakura suggested that next time he choose fruit by its size rather than how pretty the glaze looked. Lir tried to argue, but ended up laughing himself.

It had been a long time since she had heard him laugh so freely. After the Castle of Justice and the news of his father's death, Lir had rarely been in a particularly good mood, so Sakura decided not to keep teasing him about his purchases.

Nearby, they discovered a fairground game that tested accuracy. For a small fee, visitors received three wooden balls and had to knock down figures on a distant shelf. The figures stood in tightly packed rows, one behind another, so a good hit could knock several down at once. The grand prize was an enormous stuffed bird with a ridiculously long neck and round eyes.

Lir immediately declared that he was going to win it. Of course, he had jumped to that conclusion far too early. The first ball missed every figure, the second knocked down one, and the third hit the edge of the wooden stand and bounced back so unexpectedly that Lir had to duck.

"Almost," he said.

"Very close," Gilbert agreed. "Especially the first attempt."

Sakura couldn't help laughing at the mage's words. Then she decided to try it herself. She was sure she could do better, but the first ball knocked down only one figure, and the second missed everything. She threw the third too sharply and immediately regretted it when a sharp pain pulled at her side. She had to straighten carefully and wait for the pain to fade. And even that sacrifice failed to bring her the result she had hoped for.

Gilbert noticed Sakura wincing from the pain, which showed no sign of fading.

"That's enough for today."

"But I almost hit it!"

"Almost? Yes, just like Lir," the mage remarked with obvious sarcasm. "You two are practically gods of accuracy."

Faerd, who had been watching the game the entire time, unexpectedly placed a few coins on the counter.

"May I try?"

The merchant immediately perked up at the sight of the imposing man and handed him three balls. Faerd weighed the first one in his palm, aimed, and threw. The ball struck the front row, knocking down three figures at once. The second knocked down the remaining figures on the upper shelf, and with the third, Faerd finished off the last ones near the edge.

Lir slowly turned his head toward him.

"Wow! How did you do that?"

"Threw it."

"Yeah, sure... Threw it..." the young man muttered in admiration.

The merchant carefully examined the empty shelves.

"Something's not right here," he said, narrowing his eyes.

Faerd turned toward him.

"I can do it again. But for another prize."

"No!" the merchant answered with surprising speed.

Reluctantly, the man took the enormous bird down from the upper beam and handed it to the winner. Faerd examined the prize, then turned toward Lir.

"You wanted it."

Lir looked confused.

"Well... yeah."

"Here."

He handed him the bird. Sakura burst out laughing when she saw the bewildered expression on Lir's face as he accepted the gift. The enormous stuffed neck immediately drooped over his shoulder, while the bird's ridiculous head stared off to one side.

"Wonderful," Gilbert said. "Now we have two birds in our group."

Lir adjusted his mask.

"Keep your jealousy to yourself."

By then, almost all the lanterns in the square had been lit. The musicians began playing louder, and people started gathering near the fountain. Sakura moved closer and saw the participants taking each other's hands, forming a large circle. As a cheerful tune played, they separated into pairs, changed places, and then returned to their neighbors.

The movements did not look particularly complicated to Sakura, so she decided to join in. She didn't know it yet, but this was her worst idea of the evening...
