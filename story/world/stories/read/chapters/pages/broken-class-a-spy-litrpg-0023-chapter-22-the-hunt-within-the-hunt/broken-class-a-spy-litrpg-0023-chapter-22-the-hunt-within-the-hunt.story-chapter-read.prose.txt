The team following the princess consisted of 15 members. Five had stealth capabilities, trailing her from behind the large redwood trees but never revealing themselves. The other ten followed further behind - nine of them clad in heavy armor, advancing with practiced caution. The tenth, however, stood out. He wore light armor, his movements more fluid and unburdened. But what truly set him apart was the mana signature. A spark larger and more volatile than the others. Most likely a mage, Arthur thought. A dangerous one.

Every now and then, a man from the stealth team would jog back to the rear group to relay updates. Arthur watched with growing curiosity. “No enchanted comms. No signal flares. No psychic links. Just good old-fashioned jogging.” Arthur smirked. “Charming, in a Stone Age kind of way.”

They followed the princess’s team for the entire day, never drawing close, never making a move. Arthur expected them to attack under cover of darkness. But night came and went without bloodshed. Their restraint elevated his opinion.

After all, the deadliest blades were always the ones you didn’t see coming.

The next day, everything changed.

Braska sighed. The heavyset tank adjusted the straps on his shoulder guard and glanced at his teammates. “Tenth day of the Hunt, and we’ve killed… what, half of Arthur’s score?”

Thorne, the taller of the two warriors, shook his head. “He racked up those kills in the first few hours. It’s like he turned the Hunt into a warm-up routine.”

Braska grunted. “Then why stop? He could’ve hit two hundred by now.”

“Maybe he got hurt,” Thorne suggested. “Or another team jumped him.”

“Doubt it.” Braska snorted. “He’s still topping the leaderboard. You think someone who soloed a hundred monsters went down easy?”

Yudursh’s voice cut in, calm but commanding. “Save it. Kaelin’s back.”

From behind a redwood trunk, a lithe figure emerged - Kaelin, their rogue. He dropped to one knee and bowed his head. “Princess, I’ve found an Earthen Dreadspine resting ahead. Shall we change directions?”

Ysara didn’t hesitate. “No. We hunt it.”

They followed Kaelin back towards the monster. After a few minutes, they sighted it resting under a tree. It was about the size of a cow, covered in dense, jagged quills made of mineralized bone. When they were a few yards away, it opened its small beady eyes and slowly got up.

Ysara instructed her team to move to formation three.

Immediately, Braska the tank moved in front of the others. Kaelin and Thorne flanked the monster from each direction. Yudursh remained behind Ysara, his eyes scanning for any threat from the rear.

Braska drew aggro, and the Dreadspine lumbered toward him in a low-slung gait. The rogue and warrior attacked from the flanks, prompting the creature to reflexively eject several spines. Kaelin’s agility allowed him to evade them, but Thorne wasn’t as lucky. A pair of spines embedded in his thigh and arm.

Grimacing, Thorne yanked them free. Each was the size of a thick dart. "Princess, healing," he called.

Ysara responded with practiced precision. Her healing skill surged forth, sealing the wounds rapidly and allowing Thorne to re engage.

The Dreadspine posed a different kind of challenge. It had few vulnerable spots - the eyes, the underside, and the narrow gaps between its quills. It instinctively shielded its eyes whenever Braska targeted them, and attempting to strike its underbelly risked being trampled. That left Kaelin and Thorne to carefully chip away at its softer gaps.

Fortunately, the monster was slow and its offense limited. And with Ysara on overwatch, their injuries never lingered.

After nearly an hour of attrition, the Dreadspine bellowed and unleashed its last-ditch defense. All of its quills fired in every direction.

Even Kaelin couldn’t evade it completely. Thorne took the worst of it, riddled with spikes. One even jutting from his eye socket.

Yet he stood his ground. With a snarl, he pressed forward, intent on ending the monster now that its hide was exposed.

Then came the ambush.

Yudursh intercepted an arrow from behind that was targeting the princess. He bellowed a warning to his teammates, but it was a bit late. As he saw Thorne go down with an arrow to his throat.

Ysara, quick to respond, called her team to regroup. With practiced motion, they abandoned the monster, now bleeding and immobile, and closed ranks around the princess.

Kaelin, the one with the sharpest perception, reported immediately, "Princess, there are at least twelve people, maybe more. We need to run."

Ysara nodded and ordered the retreat. But they couldn’t afford to turn their backs; doing so would expose them to a hail of arrows. Instead, they backpedaled, still facing the direction of their attackers. Ysara pulled a mana potion from her storage ring and drank deeply. Her mana pool, which had dipped to sixty percent, began to rise steadily.

Their attackers gave no warning, no grand speeches or theatrical delays. Eight warriors charged toward them while arrows continued to fly. Ysara stayed composed, her training and instincts kicking in.

But then she saw Garran.

He walked behind the charging warriors with eerie calm. His presence alone turned Ysara pale. Garran's talent wasn’t widely known, but nothing remained hidden from her father for long. He was a blood mage, an unnatural counter to her healing arts. Where she sealed wounds, he could reopen them, intensify the bleeding, and nullify recovery.

Braska shouted to draw aggro, engaging the frontline. Kaelin veered left toward stealth attackers approaching the princess. He warned Yudursh of incoming rogues on the right flank.

Ysara healed Kaelin before he moved out of range, then turned her focus to Braska.

Yudursh moved fast. Too fast for the rogues to handle. He weaved past short swords and countered with two clean slashes across their necks. Both dropped, bleeding out in seconds. Without pause, he sprinted back toward Braska.

Braska, meanwhile, held the line. He relied on his large shield to guard his left and a short sword to counter from the right. He deflected several attacks, but for every two or three he blocked, one got through. His armor slowed the bleeding, but not the pain. A sword pierced his thigh, destabilizing his stance. As he staggered, he glimpsed Yudursh just a few yards away.

Yudursh rammed into one attacker, sending him crashing into two others. He stabbed under the arm of a fourth and slashed the neck of a fifth. But he was too late. Two enemies had already plunged their blades into Braska.

Yudursh looked for Kaelin. The rogue was already down. But not before taking one of the enemies with him.

Yudursh didn’t let grief distract him. He moved toward another attacker, only to hear Ysara cry out.

She had taken an arrow to the stomach, just inches from a fatal wound.

Another arrow struck Yudursh’s shoulder, but he ignored the pain. He rushed back to the princess.

He had wanted to grab her and flee from the start, but it would've been suicide. Now, escape wasn’t even an option.

So he planted himself between Ysara and the oncoming enemy.

And prepared for his last stand.

While the battle raged on, Arthur had already crept behind the enemy archers, silently taking down the two long-range threats with ruthless precision. With their vantage gone, he slipped into the chaos just as the clash reached its peak.
