As the stranger within the tower looked upon them with a mixture of horror and shock, Vin took the opportunity to more closely examine them. Beyond the flexible wings which were now splayed out behind the being as if to try and make itself look larger, they looked quite similar to an elf. Sharp features and a thin frame spoke of a build for speed and agility over strength, and the being’s clothes told a similar tale, looking like a series of leather pieces secured to their person with a dozen different small belts along their limbs and torso. Their face was androgynous enough that he couldn’t tell if they’d stumbled upon a man or a woman, but from their expression, either way it was clear whoever this was hadn’t been expecting to see anyone outside their window.

“Maybe they're deaf?” Scule suggested, pulling a pen and some paper out of his cape and offering them to Vin. “Want to try writing a message?”

“Give them a minute. I think they’re just trying to come to terms with suddenly finding three people and a rat standing a good quarter-mile up on the outside of their giant tower,” Shia snorted.

Sure enough, that seemed to be the case, as the being managed to collect themself before too long. They began rapidly speaking, and Vin could only sigh and shake his head when he realized the window was completely soundproof.

“Figures. They couldn’t hear us. I might need that pen and paper after all.”

But before Vin could start writing, the being seemed to realize the problem as well, as they desperately held up two hands and pushed at them, in the universal gesture of wait right there. Once they were sure the message had gotten across, they turned and ran off out of the large room that looked like it ran around the entire tower.

“Guess they’re coming to get us,” Scule said, tucking his tools away. “Vin, did your Common Ally pick up anything about these people? What are we dealing with?”

“They’re called divigels. They’re lightweight, capable of flight, and are quite fragile.”

“We could sort of tell all of that just by looking at them,” Shia chuckled. “Anything else?”

“They value community quite highly,” he added, still trying to get used to the new information he received from having evolved his title. “Like, really highly. I think being alone is almost physically painful for these people.”

“Makes you wonder how many divigels are tucked away within that giant tower,” Scule muttered, before glancing up toward the top of the tower. “Oh look, they’re here.”

Sure enough, the being they’d spotted was quickly approaching, showing that they were capable of flying far quicker than the avisul were. Flaring out their wings at the last moment, they came to a sudden stop barely a dozen feet away, slowly flapping to hover in place as they stared at the four of them. Now that he’d had a moment to parse through what Common Ally was telling him, he knew the newcomer was in fact female.

“…Are you real?” the divigel asked, her high-pitched voice shaking slightly. “Or did I finally go crazy after all this time?”

“We’re real,” Vin confirmed, continuing his steady breathing as he drew on the mana around him to replace the amount he was burning through keeping Redirect Gravity going. He felt the mixture of sadness and hope practically radiating off of the lone divigel, the sudden emotions slamming into him as though the flier had come at him with a hammer in an attempt to knock him off the tower. “We’re also a little tired of climbing. Any chance we could talk somewhere less… sideways?”

“Oh, of course!” she stammered, recoiling back in the air as if only just then realizing they were standing at a ninety-degree angle hundreds of feet above the ground. “Please, follow me!”

With that, she shot upward, directing them toward what Vin could only assume was an entrance in and out of the tower. Beyond curious, he and his friends jogged after her, quickly coming upon what looked like a vault door of all things set in the side of the tower. The door itself seemed to be made of six inches of pure metal, and Shia whistled when they spotted it.

“Think you could pick your way through that?” she teased Scule.

“I’ll be the first to admit it would certainly be a challenge,” Scule muttered, narrowing his eyes at the tower security.

“In here,” the flier said, nimbly tucking in her wings as she stepped through. Figuring they could take her easily enough in a fight should the need arise, Vin shrugged and followed after her, ending his spell before he and his team could go falling sideways through the tower.

“What is this place?” he asked, glancing around at the small, empty corridor they now found themselves in. It was sterile and without any decoration, with nothing more than another thick door set in the far wall to look at.

“This is decontamination,” the divigel explained, quickly sealing the outer door and letting out a small sigh of relief the moment it was locked once more. “Normally we have procedures and rules that have to be followed for this sort of thing, but seeing as the people in charge of punishing all that are already gone…” she laughed, a little too hard in Vin’s opinion, before leading them over to the second door. “Anyway, you don’t need to hear about any of that! Welcome to the Beacon! The last hope of the divigel and our plan to wait out the apocalypse in comfort and style!”

While she walked, she continued throwing glances at them every other second, as if terrified the moment she took her eyes off them they’d vanish into the aether or prove to be just a figment of her imagination. Before Vin could try to reassure her that they weren’t going anywhere, she pushed open the second door, and he blinked as he realized what she meant.

The colossal lighthouse was a bunker for the end of the world.

“By the Gods,” Scule muttered. Tightening his grip on Vin’s hair, he peered down the central area of the lighthouse. The door their host had just brought them through led out onto a large walkway wrapping around the edge of the tower in a ring. The dead center, however, was completely free, allowing one to look down over the railing all the way to the ground at the very bottom. Counting the number of similar rings stretching down, Vin guessed there had to be a full fifty or so floors making up the entire lighthouse, each one comprised of the same gray, dense stone that looked like it could withstand a missile without too much difficulty.

“Yeah, it’s home sweet home,” the divigel chuckled nervously, her wings settling and unsettling against her back in what Vin took as her version of wringing her hands. “Oh, and I’m Tabetha! Sorry, I forgot to introduce myself! I’m… well, I’m the Beacon’s Keeper! I guess!”

“Tabetha… how many people live here in the Beacon?” Shia asked, giving the divigel a worried look. Vin figured she’d probably already come to the same conclusion he had and was gently trying to broach the elephant in the room.

“Oh, the Beacon was designed to hold thousands of divigel without issue, so don’t feel as though you’re intruding,” Tabetha said, letting out a bark of laughter as she vigorously shook her head. “Here, let me show you some of the wonderful things our Architects put together for us!”

Tabetha all but dragged them over to a set of stairs, which Vin thought was a tad strange to have added for a race of people capable of flight. He didn’t know if flying around all the time had to be tiring, or older divigel couldn’t fly as well as their younger counterparts, but either way, he was glad he wasn’t going to have to keep playing with gravity to maneuver around within the lighthouse.

“So this is one of the recreation areas,” Tabetha said, gesturing through a doorway at a truly massive floor that looked like it held an artificially grown forest complete with large trees and a trickling brook. “It gives us a chance to stretch our wings and try not to go crazy while cooped up in here!”

Vin didn’t miss the fact that it looked as though someone had chopped down one of the trees and had tried to poorly turn strips of it into wooden planks in order to build the world’s shoddiest treehouse. He shared a look with Shia, but before he could say anything, Tabetha dragged them away and down another level.

“Oh, here is one of the kitchens! The Chefs really let this one go, but there are plenty of others!” she exclaimed, her plastered smile never leaving her face as she gestured toward a large kitchen that looked like a typhoon had gone off inside. There were pots and pans everywhere, and food residue somehow not only on the floor and walls, but even the ceiling. “Don’t worry about the waste! The Beacon was stocked well enough that we’ll be able to survive the next hundred years without concern! So long as you don’t mind dried rations and preserves!”

“Tabetha—” Vin tried gently, getting silenced as the divigel continued without pause on the tour, immediately dragging them down another floor and speaking up over him.

“This is the library!” she all but shouted. Panting slightly, she wiped loose strands of hair out of her eyes and grinned at them, gesturing toward the rows and rows of books all throughout the room. Vin didn’t miss how the large library was just as empty and vacant of other people as every other room in the Beacon. Nor how books were strewn about all over the place. Or the large fort in the center that appeared to have been constructed from some of the thicker tomes. “Don’t worry about keeping your voice down! As the Beacon’s Keeper, I can pretty much do whatever I want, so it’s totally fine! No need to worry about bothering anyone, not even in here!”

“I’m starting to think Tabetha is a few silver shy of a coin purse, if you catch my drift,” Scule muttered in Vin’s ear as the divigel pivoted to the next floor before any of them could get a word in edgewise. “Do you think… you know…”

“Yeah,” Vin muttered, his heart going out to the poor divigel who didn’t look like she was much older than he was. “I think that’s exactly what happened.”

“This is one of the nooks!” Tabetha said, continuing the tour as though she wasn’t simply dragging them around the lighthouse and shouting over them. “This is where we like to get together and just talk to one another! Share our thoughts and feelings and whatever else we want to get off our wings!”

The nook was a large lounge area, complete with everything from solid chairs to fluffy beanbags. It looked as though it had seating for all levels of comfort, but most noticeable was the fact that a good half of the chairs had been knocked over or partially destroyed, as though someone had gone through a fit of rage with a hammer at some point in the past. In fact, Vin even spotted the tool in question leaning against the wall, which looked like a meat tenderizer taken from the kitchens.

“I can’t say this room has gotten too much use in a while,” Tabetha giggled, kicking at an errant chair leg that lay on the ground near the entrance. “But that’s okay! Maybe I can reopen it again now that you guys are here!”

“Tabetha,” Vin said. This time he reached out and physically stopped her from rushing off to the next floor, gently but firmly laying his human hand on her shoulder. The divigel flinched at the unexpected touch, her eyes widening and lips trembling as she stared down at his hand like it was made of fire.

“…Are you guys really real?” she asked after a moment, her words barely louder than a whisper. Her whole body began shaking like a leaf in a storm, all the way out to the tips of her wings.

“We’re real,” Shia confirmed, stepping forward and pulling the divigel into a tight embrace. “You’re not alone anymore.”

Tabetha managed to hold out for about three more seconds. Then her eyes filled with tears and she began sobbing at the top of her lungs, her legs giving out as she all but collapsed against Shia. As the Beacon’s Keeper dug her fingers into Shia’s back and let out what looked like months of pain and anguish, Scule sighed.

“Damn it all... It’s things like this that make it really hard to steal from people sometimes.”
