Ascalon blinked a few times as the world around him regained its shape, waiting for his senses to stabilize from the dimensional traveling. The familiar tent stood above his head, a wave of relief washing over him—after all, this was the first time he had been teleported out of a dungeon by the dungeon itself.

“Ascalon!” An unmistakable voice called out, drawing his attention.

“Are you alright?”

None other than Lilithra would ask that, he thought with a smile. Ascalon shook his head slightly, his grasp on reality strengthening as he glanced over at the voice’s direction.

The rest of the group were there, and while he felt overjoyed by their presence, Wrath’s last words lingered. Frantically, his eye darted around their frames, trying to discern any visible wounds or signs of harm. They looked at him with a mix of curiosity and confusion, a sigh escaping his lips as soon as he realized they were fine.

“I am fine, how are you all?” Ascalon asked with a gentle grin, taking a step toward his friends. Selorien nodded lazily at his words, while Lyra and Lilithra approached to meet him midway.

“We’re fine, I guess…” Lilithra spoke with an embarrassed voice, her downcast expression causing Ascalon to worry about her.

Perhaps she had non-visible wounds, after all. In her arms was Moonstone, sleeping peacefully, which reminded him of Athanya’el somehow—likely because of their adorableness. As he wondered about random thoughts, Lilithra’s voice rang out again.

“I’m sorry… I was acting strange… and we weren’t of any help, again…” Lilithra stated softly, looking a bit regretful. Her eyes were set on the ground, her frown clearly giving away her emotions as she sighed.

“It could not be helped.” Ascalon began, his tone firm yet soft. “I mean no offense, but… that enemy was beyond any of you.” The knight spoke with no reluctance, even if he knew his words could be perceived as hurtful.

“Even I could not finish her in time…” Ascalon admitted, clenching his fists. The rest quickly perked up at his words, a mix of surprised and worried expressions on their faces.

“Not even you…? Damn…” Selorien chimed in with furrowed brows, his visage turning thoughtful as he stroked his chin.

“Well, to be fair, she was helped out by Wrath… the vile man distracted me just before I could vanquish her…!” Ascalon lamented, another sigh escaping his lips. With these words, the tension among his friends seemed to lighten.

“But I am glad you are all unharmed.” The knight added, grinning from ear to ear. After all, his friends’ safety was his first and foremost priority.

“Ugh… we gotta get stronger… but first!” Lilithra spoke up, her eyes burning with conviction. “We have to tell Mr. Ron about Ravanna, quickly!” She urged with a nervous tone, her words making a lot of sense.

They had to let headquarters know about those dangerous persons and their cult, now that they had clear evidence of their involvement with New Lumingard. Ascalon nodded in agreement, with Lyra and Selorien quickly joining in.

“Let’s split up.” Selorien suddenly suggested, earning confused looks from his friends. “I’ll tell Mr. Ron, you guys finish up the quest!” He explained, a confident smile on his lips.

“Don’t worry, I’ll be careful.” The elf raised a thumbs up, trying to reassure them, though his laid-back aura was anything but!

“But…” Lilithra muttered, her face overcome with worry. Ascalon placed a hand on her head, softly patting her a few times. He had an idea about what could've been preoccupying her.

“Do not worry—I do not believe they would be so brazen to attack in the open, especially after almost suffering a loss.” Ascalon declared, his unwavering tone carrying undeniable wisdom. Lilithra’s face lit up a bit, her lips curling up into a small smile.

“Alright… but be careful, just in case…!” Lilithra pleaded, punching Selorien playfully in the shoulder. The elf smirked and nodded, before turning around and heading for the exit.

“S—should I go with him?” Lyra asked, her eyes darting from Selorien’s figure to the rest of the group, urging them to answer quickly.

“I believe he would be safer by himself.” Ascalon responded straightforwardly, exuding confidence. Selorien was a man of the shadows, after all—being alone would be way safer for him. Lyra, seemingly understanding the intention behind his words, nodded in relief.

Lilithra then let out a slow breath, her shoulders drooping slightly. She seemed tired, so perhaps it would be wise to finish up the quest and go back to The Chalice. With that in mind, Ascalon stepped toward the tent’s exit.

“Shall we go?” He asked in a gentle tone, a soft grin spreading across his face. Both Lilithra and Lyra exchanged glances and a small smile, with Moonstone’s gently snoozing adding to the now relaxed atmosphere.

“Alright… let’s go!” Lilithra chirped with more energy, likely more at ease now. Lyra giggled at her side, and soon they all left the Howling Chasm’s portal behind. It was time to finally finish their first quest as a Guild!

Meanwhile, deep within the city's underbelly, Wrath stood impassively at the center of their ‘meeting’ room. The candles were all lit up, bathing Pride’s tattered form in a dim glow—her frame shivering in clear anger, her defiant stare focused sharply on Wrath.

“I—” She began to speak, but Wrath raised a hand, gesturing her to stop.

“I don’t need explanations or excuses, Pride.” He answered coldly, the temperature in the room seemingly dropping at his words. Pride clenched her fists, yet her gaze remained unwavering. At the sight, Wrath could do nothing but sigh deeply.

“I know it can’t be helped… you’re ‘Pride’ after all…” He said in a low voice, walking closer to her.

“But this time, your actions will hurt the Septem greatly.” Wrath declared in a serious tone, echoing around the room, undeterred by his mask.

“I know, but…!” Pride began to retort, but once again, Wrath signaled her to stop.

“Listen to me, Pride.” He spoke slowly, yet his words carried certain authority.

“You gave away your identity—you cannot stay in New Lumingard any longer.” Wrath crossed his arms, his words more of an order rather than an observation.

“I know… but before that, we can go and defeat the wanderer! Listen, I—” Pride spoke with more enthusiasm, but suddenly stopped, her entire body tensing up. Wrath’s presence expanded, encompassing the whole room with an oppressive, chilling aura. Pride’s eyes widened, her breath caught on her throat.

“I have a vague idea of what you’re planning, Pride… but have you learned nothing?” Wrath spoke with clear irritation, his calm voice feeling incredibly overpowering.

“Hastiness has led us to lose against their group three times in a row now.” He continued, clearly exasperated.

“We cannot let ourselves be consumed by our sins—not yet, at the very least.” Wrath sighed, calming down a bit. As the weight of his presence vanished, Pride breathed once more, sweat rolling down her bare, red skin.

“I hate repeating myself… but, once again: all in due time, Pride.” Wrath mused, slowly walking around her. She looked up at him with a frustrated glare, biting her lips, unable to reply.

“Well, let’s move on.... Since ‘Ravanna’ is no longer of any use to us, I’ll have to switch things up a little.” Wrath let out a thoughtful ‘hmm’ as he paced around the room. Pride looked at him with curiosity, arching an eyebrow.

“I’ll have to send you farther away—fortunately, you’re not that well-known in the upper continent.” He spoke plainly, his words causing Pride to jolt in place.

“But that’s…” She began, clenching her teeth. “That’s not where I was supposed to go… it's like you’re casting me away…!” Pride admitted, frustration more than evident on her visage.

“I know it might feel like that, but no.” Wrath replied with a confident voice, Pride’s expression shifting to a more suspicious one.

“I have some… acquaintances we could—to put it simply—use.” He revealed, a wide grin spreading behind his mask. Pride tilted her head, her face tensing up in pain as her burned skin stretched slightly.

“You’ll need some more time to recover, it seems.” Wrath pointed out, stopping in place.

“But we cannot waste any more time. I’ll tell you what you’ll have to do, later…” Wrath said as he walked toward her, dark, deep fog exuding from his frame. Pride quickly perked up, urgency clear in her face.

“Wait…! I swear…!” She began to speak frantically, Wrath’s hideous fumes enveloping her entire body. “I won’t lose again…!” Pride declared in desperation.

“See that you don’t.” Wrath murmured, his voice carrying a clear ultimatum. Pride frowned, helplessly vanishing along the black smoke. Wrath sighed as he finished casting his skill, with Pride now being far, far away from New Lumingard.

“Stupid child.” Wrath whispered coldly, the entire room warping under his malice.

“If only she weren’t so attuned to her sin… I would’ve disposed of her…” Wrath kept complaining in the empty hall, the candles around him flickering and extinguishing, his entire surroundings plunging into darkness.

“Ha… no matter…!” He clenched his fists, resolution burning in his chest. “The rest are already in their respective places… It’s time I move out, too…” Wrath added, looking around one last time.

“But I’ll be back, New Lumingard… and next time I won’t hide in the shadows anymore…!”

He paused, a wicked smile warping his face.

“Perhaps… a little parting gift wouldn’t hurt…”

And with these parting words, his frame blended with the dark, his presence completely disappearing from the city as a hollow laugh echoed in the now vacant room.

The group managed to complete the quest without any issues, earning their payment and the gratitude of the alchemist’s Guild. Nothing out of the ordinary occurred on the way back, which allowed Ascalon to relax a little, his steady demeanor influencing the two girls that walked at his side.

He was glad to see them behaving more like usual—a tad more cheerful and at ease. He smiled gently at them, chit-chatting as they navigated the streets. Yet beneath his calm exterior, a shadow of worry lingered. Wrath and his henchmen gnawed at the back of his mind—these people couldn’t simply be ignored.

Unfortunately, he knew it was going to be impossible to find them. Ever since he had ‘met’ Wrath for the first time, Ascalon had been on the lookout for his presence—yet not even once had he perceived him among the many citizens of New Lumingard.

Still, the city was big, so perhaps he just had to take a stroll through the entire territory—after all, Ascalon would go to any lengths if necessary! For now, Lilithra and Lyra’s peaceful expressions calmed him down, so he decided to focus on them rather than dwell on negative thoughts.

Time passed uneventfully, and they finally reached The Chalice. They were greeted by a cheerful Raphael, and a small Goddess chomping on sweet delicacies—as usual! Though this time Athanya’el’s attention had been piqued.

“What a cute animal!” The Goddess chirped as soon as she caught sight of Moonstone, who barked at her with clear eagerness. Lilithra, who had been taking turns in carrying the pup—alongside both Ascalon and Lyra—placed it on the tavern’s floor, to Raphael’s dismay.

Moonstone darted straight to Athanya’el, jumping at her and starting to lick her and bark playfully.

“W—whoa! That tickles, you…!” The small Thany muttered as she grabbed the fluffy dog, flustered but visibly happy.

The sight was so sweet Ascalon swore to only eat salty food for the rest of the day—though that wasn’t for him to decide, but for the chef behind the counter.

“You guys just keep bringing in new, uhm, members to this old place, huh?” Raphael spoke with a bit of reluctance in his tone, perhaps not entirely convinced of the dog’s presence and carefully picking his words.

“I’m sorry, uncle! But Ascalon wouldn’t budge!” Lilithra giggled as she approached the counter, a playful grin on her face. Ascalon sighed at her words, realizing she was teasing him, and both him and Lyra followed after her.

With everyone at the counter, they started to chat animatedly about the small pup and how they found it, while Athanya’el played with it in a show of pure adorableness.

Eventually, Selorien arrived, his carefree demeanor letting them know he hadn’t had any issues on his end either.

Reunited at last, it was time to discuss serious matters.
