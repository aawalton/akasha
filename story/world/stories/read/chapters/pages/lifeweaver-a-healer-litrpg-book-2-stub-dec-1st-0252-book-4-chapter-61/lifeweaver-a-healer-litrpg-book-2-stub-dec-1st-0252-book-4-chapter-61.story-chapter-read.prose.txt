Three separate attacks by void creatures at the same time. This time, Luke didn’t mess around with Source. Instead, he offed the two at the first location and moved to the second without pause. Here, two more advanced opponents waited for him. They were well into the weave already, having worked with feverish glee to gain access to their beloved food source. Luke’s attack didn’t go unanswered, and he felt one pushing into his mind with a prepared spell of some kind. In rebuffing the attempt, he bought himself some time. Not being one to squander an opportunity, Luke lashed out with threads and pushed into one creature. It did its best to repel the threads, but he was ready for it.

Submit.

The will-infused command wouldn’t affect the target for long, but he needed no more than a second. Even while killing the first creature, Luke numbed his pain receptors in anticipation of a blow he saw but knew he wouldn’t be able to dodge. Rather than latching on this time, the tentacle sheared through the weave, cutting threads and limbs. Having both his legs severed right above the knee while floating in his mind’s representation of Earth’s system was… an experience, but he recovered and steeled himself for the next attack. The tentacle returned, but this time, with ample time to react, Luke refused to let it hurt him.

Having its attack blocked by nothing more than a combination of Weaver’s Bulwark and Luke’s will stunned the remaining void creature, allowing Luke to finish the fight without much resistance. Weaver’s Renewal returned his legs, but he decided it was perhaps best if he didn’t come in the flesh next time.

Appearing in the third location, he found Hannah. She was locked in physical combat with an undeveloped void creature outside the weave while Dimitrios stood right next to her and shouted encouraging words.

“Luke!” Dimitrios said without turning away from the fight. “Your girl said she was ready, so I figured I’d let her prove it!”

Hannah’s focus was complete. Tentacles of shadow enveloped her arms and extended to her opponent, who met them with its own. Locked in combat, neither made much progress toward winning. Her even being here in the weave was a massive development, and pride filled Luke, who joined in with the whoops, egging her on. While she didn’t look like she was anywhere near losing, it was also clear she hadn’t grasped the concept of disrupting the creature's Source. Tearing large chunks off the void creature, like she was doing, would perhaps make it retreat in time, but it wouldn’t award her the kill.

“Should we step in?” Luke asked.

Dimitrios pursed his lips and scratched the stubble on his cheek. “I think that is best. Falzyxi is not far. Popov informed me of your plan.”

“And?”

“It’s risky.”

Luke nodded. “Well, yeah.”

“I will help you.”

“Just like that?”

“Of course,” Dimitrios said. “I will not touch the power inside them unless it is to cut, but I will keep your back free of intervention from other creatures.”

“Source.”

Dimitrios looked away from Hannah’s fight to glance at Luke. “What?”

“The power inside them. I’ve named it Source.”

“Like Counter-Strike?”

Luke blinked. “What?”

“Never mind,” Dimitrios said. “I like it. We will beat them by cutting their power from the source.”

“Something like that,” Luke said.

Hannah faltered and found herself pushed back. In a fight like this one, she should not have lost the exchange of blows. She was stronger than this.

“Mind lending a hand?” Hannah called out, and Dimitrios was there in an instant, killing the creature.

“Good job,” Luke said when she noticed his presence and approached with a smile.

She pressed in tight. “I can’t believe this place. There’s so much.” Sniffing him, she added, “You need a shower.”

“Grand indeed,” Dimitrios said, before pointing to the shredded end seam. “Do you mind repairing that, Luke? I… never learned how.”

Once it was done, all three turned to stare into the void. Hannah’s voice wavered a little when she spoke. “That… feeling. Is that?”

Luke nodded. “Falzyxi.”

“It won’t be long now,” Dimitrios added. “Best make peace with it, no matter what comes.”

“When?” she asked.

Dimitrios looked over to Luke. “Tomorrow?”

“Time is strange out here,” Luke said. “But yes, I would think so. Tomorrow.”

“That means we have at least another night,” Hannah said.

Dimitrios laughed. “Best make it one to remember!”

It was. Hannah was still asleep when Luke slipped out of bed and grabbed a second shower. Two in less than twenty-four hours, a record for these last couple of strange, horrible, and wonderful months. Waking up with the sense of dread in his chest having reached new heights meant the enemy was near. It wouldn’t be long now.

After some serious arguments from Luke, Hannah had agreed to stay with Popov and his group for the coming attack, since Luke and Dimitrios would be too preoccupied trying to ensure Earth’s continued survival. She wasn’t pleased, but saw reason. As it stood, she wasn’t strong enough yet to contribute on her own. In a group, things would be different, but neither Luke nor Dimitrios would be able to divert their attention to her safety, and things wouldn’t go well.

During a quiet moment the night before, Hannah had confessed the reason she hadn’t been giving it her all against the void creature. It had to do with her dominion over The Deep Dweller and her connection to The Others. Something inside her wanted to consume the void for herself and charge forward into the unknown to claim all Source for herself. Something out there called to her, and if she lost control during the fight, she wasn’t sure she would be able to hold herself back. Neither of them was sure what to make of that, but they agreed it was best for her to hold herself back and assist the others.

With Hannah still fast asleep, Luke needed to get something over with. Time was running out, and he was supposed to meet the others soon. Even so, there was a weakness he needed to eliminate. Moving through the weave without an echo thread was not beyond his ability. What he needed was to find a way to identify and find threads to anywhere, not just random types of destinations, like with the portals. It was time to remove the limiting factor on Weavestep, and like with many other obstacles, it was his own mind standing in his way, and it was through his mind he would find the solution. Meditation was the key.

Sinking into the familiar state of detached calmness, Luke got to work. Threads of all kinds moved, swayed, and rippled, as if disturbed by invisible gusts of wind. Focusing on one, he knew what it represented without having to touch it: a fox on another continent. There was no rhyme or reason to the chaos of the weave. All threads existed on their own, but also together, and while his brain interpreted some as closer than others, he knew that wasn’t the case. Proximity was not a thing in the weave. Everything was everywhere, and all was right there, if he only knew how to shift his own thinking to allow deeper interpretation. What Luke needed was another way to look and sense, and this time Weaver’s Perception would be of no help, at least no more than what it was already accomplishing. The problem, as always, was finding the right thread.

Threads for Integrated, for Singers, for portals. They were all there, but it was not enough. The echoes didn’t help either. Reaching out to a thread at random, he willed it to lead to a specific place, forcing reality to shift and mold itself to his will. That made his brain sort of pop, and blood spewed out, filling his skull. Gasping, he fell out of meditation. Clinging to consciousness, he panted and grabbed for an old, rusted, and bent bike rack, just waiting for healing mana to do its thing. Once he’d calmed down and realized what he had just attempted was beyond stupid, Luke opened his eyes and relaxed as best he could. Before his eyes, everything on the street before him came alive with threads. This was nothing new, of course, but as he blinked, the world came into focus, and it hit him then: the error in how he had been looking for an answer. It wasn’t the thread he should be looking for. It was the object, or even the location.

Grinning, he dove deeper into meditation. The weave spread out before him, but rather than look for and grasp a single thread, Luke sought a place. To make it easier, he sought one with strong emotional ties, one he knew well. Focusing on that location, he let the threads come to him rather than the other way around. His smile stayed as he reached out and grasped the thread that had drifted out of the weave to meet him.

A Weavestep later, he found himself on his bed in the house he grew up in.

“This is it,” he whispered. “This is what I’ve been looking for.”

The house was empty. His parents had been moved to a secure location a long time ago. Using his newfound ability, he could find them if he wanted, but he decided against it. Time was always running out, now more so than ever, but he would make time for them soon. He promised himself as much. He and Milla would go for dinner or something. Perhaps they could even bring Hannah and Lana. They’d like that.

A sort of rumble made the air itself shudder, and it was as if the weave trembled under the sense of hunger coming from the predator as it approached. Falzyxi had arrived. No, not yet. The void being was close, so very close, but it hadn’t arrived yet. They still had a little time, but it could be counted in hours, not days.

Penny: “Oh shit, did you feel that?”

Hannah: “Where are you? It’s coming!”

Dimitrios: “I am not looking forward to this.”

Ella: “Make it go away!”

Popov: “We are gathering. There is still time for you to see reason.”

Curtis: “...My Seeker profession just picked something up.”

Luke sent out messages, then submerged himself into the weave again. Time was running out, and there were still preparations he needed to complete. Soon, they would see if what they had learned would be enough to stand up against something that had just destroyed an entire system and consumed all its Source. Luke would not let it do the same to Earth.
