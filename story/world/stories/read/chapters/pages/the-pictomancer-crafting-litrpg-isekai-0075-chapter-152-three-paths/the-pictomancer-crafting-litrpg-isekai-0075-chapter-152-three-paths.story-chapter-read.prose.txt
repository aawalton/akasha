An applause came from behind, startling him.

He turned. Certainty was leaning against the boundary wall with her arms folded, slow-clapping with satisfaction. He wasn’t surprised to see her. She had a habit of appearing after moments like this. Still, they hadn’t spoken since she gave that awkward mission to terminate Jill, which he had refused. What did she want this time? It was always difficult to tell when it came to Certainty.

"Nicely done," Certainty said. "A few hours to match a color temperature the people of this world were unable to classify." She pushed off the wall and walked toward him, eyes on the device. "And you built the apparatus yourself. Marvellous Clive. I always knew you could do great things."

“Why are you here?” Clive interrupted. He still viewed her with suspicion.

“You know why. It's that time again.” She opened her palms and materialized above them, rotating slowly. “Its time for upgrades.”

[RANK ADVANCEMENT]

[HP +200]

[MP +200]

[Power Level +200]

Current Rank: Artist

Choose your evolution path:

The notification hung in the air longer than usual. He read it twice without meaning to.

"Congratulations Clive," she said. "Three paths lie ahead of you. It’s time to select the next step of your evolution."

He looked at the options. This was new. He had never been offered him a branch before. The system always just handed him the next rung and expected him to climb it.

There had been a time when something like this would have mattered enormously.

Early on, when the system was still new and every rank felt like progression.  He’d chased those moments the way he’d once chased the feeling of a drawing finally coming together.

But that had been before. He had Jill now. She was asleep inside, probably, or watching the moon from whatever window she’d claimed as hers. They weren’t quite back to what they were, but they were warming up again.

So what was the point of all this?

"I want out," he said.

“Out?” Certainty tilted her head with a quizzical look.

“Done. Finished. Whatever word works.”

Certainty shook her head. “There is no out, Clive. That's not how this functions.”

“You tried to get me to kill Jill...”

Certainty paused. She was silent long enough that he thought she might not answer. Then she spoke, her high-pitched voice lowering into something deeper.  “So that’s what this is about.”

"I don’t understand you," Clive said. “Just what do you want from me?”

She looked at him for a long moment. The theatrical edge she usually wore dropped away, She still bore the body of a child, but her deposition had shifted. There was nothing childlike in the way she was watching him.  "Do you trust me, Clive?"

The honest answer was complicated. Certainty was many things — theatrical, possessive, occasionally exhausting in the specific way of someone who had decided they knew what was best for you and couldn't understand why you kept disagreeing. She had appeared uninvited at more moments than he could count, always with her own agenda half-hidden behind whatever she was pretending to offer.

But she had also been there. From the beginning, through all of it. And whatever her methods, whatever the missions she'd sent him on, it had always led to him getting stronger. He hadn't always liked the sense it made. But it had always made sense.

"I don't know," he said. Which was more honest than yes, and more honest than no.

"That's fair," she said quietly. She was looking at her feet. He hadn't seen her do that before.  Certainty, whose presence usually occupied whatever space she was in like she'd always owned it, was standing slightly contracted, her shoulders hunched over.

He'd had a list of things he'd wanted to say to her.  But looking at her now, he couldn't find the opening for any of them.

"Why did you give me the mission?" he asked instead. "To kill Jill. I want to understand it."

She didn't answer immediately. Her fingers fidget slightly at her sides, and her eyes were glued to her feet.

"Do you really want to know?"

"Yes."

She was quiet for another moment. When she finally looked up, her expression looked vulnerable. The child's body she inhabited had always seemed like an affectation, a costume she wore to keep people off-balance. But in this moment there was nothing performative about it. She just looked small.

"I was afraid," she said.

“Of what?”

"Afraid that if you found her —" she stopped. Started again. "You have always moved forward, Clive. Even when it cost you. Even when you didn't want to. That quality in you is —" she seemed to search for the word and settle for a lesser one — "rare. And I thought that if you had her back, you would stop. That she would be enough and everything else would just —" a small gesture, something dissolving — "fall away."

Clive went silent. He had been thinking about it more than he'd admitted, even to himself. Now that he had Jill, everything else had lost its edges. Vandiel. San Dioral. The Dragon Knights. None of it mattered to him anymore.

He just wanted somewhere quiet. Somewhere with good light in the mornings and no one asking anything of him. Jill across the table. Paint on his hands.

"I won't deny it," he said. "I want to go home."

Certainty looked at him steadily. "There is only one way home."

“How?”

"The same way I’ve told you right from the start. The Ascended rank."

Right. He had forgotten about that. The ability to traverse the fragments once he crossed the ascended threshold. Something about being able to navigate the fragments once he reached that rank. It had felt important when she'd first explained it, back when the shape of this world was still new to him and everything she said carried the weight of revelation.

Then, he got distracted by the politics of this world. One thing led to the next until the original goal had sunk below the waterline.

"You're closer than you think." She said it without theatrics, which made it land differently than her usual pronouncements. "You're already stronger than most mortals in this realm. The gap between where you are and where you need to be is —" she paused, measuring something he couldn't see — "smaller than it looks from where you're standing."

He didn't say anything.

"One more push," she said. "And you wouldn't just be going home, Clive. You'd be going home as something that could challenge the gods themselves if it wanted to. Imagine what Jill would think of that."

It was a cheap line and she probably knew it. But she'd said it anyway, which meant she was running low on better ones.

"Is that supposed to motivate me?" he asked.

"I don't know," she said. "Does it?"

He looked away. Across the courtyard, the moonlight barrier still hung in the air. He'd built it in a single night. Painted with light itself. Just a year ago, he was still sitting in a cramped apartment in Los Angeles with paint under his fingernails and nothing to show for it but canvases no one would ever see. Art had been the one thing he’d kept after everything else had been taken from him.

Look at him now. He had never imagined anything like this would have been possible before. Which meant he didn't know what else was possible yet. How far could he bring this. He wanted to know as well.

He didn't answer Certainty's question out loud. He didn't need to. She'd been watching him long enough to read the answer in the two seconds he'd taken before deciding not to answer.

The ghost of a smile crossed her face. She had the grace not to say anything about it.

“Alright, tell me about these evolution paths,” he said. “I see this to its very end.”

Something shifted in her immediately. The contracted, careful version of her from a moment ago folding away, and the usual Certainty reassembled itself like a coat being shrugged back on. Her posture lifted. Her eyes brightened with the particular energy of someone who had been waiting to have exactly this conversation.

"I knew it," she said. "That's what I've always liked about you, Clive. You look at the edge and you can't help wondering what's past it." She clasped her hands together. "That's why I chose you."

He let that sit without comment.

"The evolution paths," she continued, "are a departure from what you've been doing. Up until now you've been a generalist. A little of everything, adaptable, functional across many situations. That served you well in the early stages."

"But."

"But generalism has a ceiling. Every craftsman learns this eventually. The blacksmith who also does carpentry and also does cobbling will always be outpaced by the blacksmith who does nothing but blacksmithing." She tilted her head. "You've hit that ceiling, Clive. These paths are what's above it. Specialized routes, each one taking a core part of what you already are and asking — what if you went further? What if that was all you chased?"

He looked at the notification still hovering faintly at the edge of his vision.

Elemental Artist. Moonlight Artist. Architect of Life.

“Tell me about them.”
