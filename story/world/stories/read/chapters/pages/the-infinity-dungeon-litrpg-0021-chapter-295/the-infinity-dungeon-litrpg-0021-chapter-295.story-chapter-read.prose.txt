Chapter 295

The air tasted like iron. Patrick woke up with a gasp, his body spasming and pulling itself up almost on its own, sending the bedsheets flying everywhere. He looked around, eyes wild with adrenaline, searching for anything out of the ordinary and finding nothing.

Nothing save for the strange electric taste he felt in the back of his throat.

The light coming from the windows was the same, usual harsh light of a city that never slept. Sometimes a car passed by, its high beams turning the bedroom into a strobe party. The sharp shadows were in a strange shape, a bump in the sheets, half covered by the mess of fabric he had caused when he was roused from his sleep.

His heart beat wildly in his chest. No matter how hard he tried, he could not fall asleep again. A sensation of pressure made him break out into cold sweat, and moments later the feeling of being watched accompanied it. It came from all around, outside and inside his house, but there was a direction to it, like a magnet drawing him somewhere.

His legs carried him to the basement. The door had been locked long ago, but through the gaps in the frame he could tell that something was not quite right. Light came from below, bright azure light. He fiddled with the door handle, fumbling with the rusty key in the lock just below it and struggling to get the door to open.

When it did, it revealed dusty air streaked by light. He pinched himself to confirm that he was not dreaming. For some reason, the earlier panic had transformed into a strange sort of calm. Instead of running away or calling the cops, he took a step forward. The old wooden steps creaked beneath his feet, one after the other as his hand glided over the handrail until he came to a stop.

There, behind old boxes he had not opened since he had moved in, most of them still full of his old stuff from his parents’ house, was the source of the light. Most of it was hidden, but jagged edges of torn space could be seen above the boxes, and the source of the light was something beyond them, beyond the point of no return.

“Is anyone there?”

Nothing.

Transfixed, he found himself drawn toward the strange rift in the world. And after he moved the boxes, he finally saw the alien landscape that existed on the other side and gasped.

He had seen things on social media. Strange sightings all over the world. But this? This was right in front of him. This was real. Tangible.

Pressure emanated from it, the source of the strange gaze and of the tingling latent power in the air. Like strong gusts of wind, it escaped from the portal and flooded the basement, searching for a way out, up the stairs, into his house. He was about to take a step, to see the other side in person, perhaps foolishly.

Patrick froze.

It was not fear. It was not pressure. All of a sudden, it was like the air had become solid, preventing him from crossing through. Trapping him in the old, grey mundane world of his life. All he could do was move his eyes around, all he could see was the portal. It was bright, making the rest of the basement disappear in blackness.

“Hello, kid,” a voice said from behind. “Don’t panic, alright? I’m going to release you.”

“Who are you?” Patrick said, some feeling returning to his face.

“Name’s David. You are not going to do anything stupid, are you?”

More feeling returned to his body, and he managed to shake his head. The panic was back in full force, because even though the air was thawing and letting him move again, a new source of pressure had appeared, feeling like the weight of a mountain. It was suffocatingly strong, and with it came another, subtler sensation. Like eyes, watching.

Patrick turned around, making sure that his hands were visible, palms facing the man who had appeared in the middle of the basement without him noticing. The door at the top of the stairs was closed, and he was sure he would have heard it creak on its hinges if the man had come through there. He had the suspicion that he had not.

“It’s your lucky day, kid.”

The man in front of him was tall, impossibly so. He had to bend down not to scrape his head against the ceiling. Despite his young looks, his voice was deep and bore the weight of wisdom, yet at the same time Patrick could feel the impatience as the man searched for the right words to say.

“You know what that is, don’t you?” David asked, chin moving up to point at the portal behind Patrick.

Of course he did. He nodded.

“It’s just like in the games you love to play in your free time,” David continued. “A dungeon, a portal to another world. Now, the deal is simple, do you want to become an adventurer like in the games and mangas, or don’t you?”

“I do!” he replied with way too much enthusiasm. Sweat was already dotting his brow, and his heart was palpitating. This… this was a dream! But he wasn’t dreaming. He had checked.

“Good,” the man said, drawing out the word.

Patrick watched him create a literal chair made of the darkest stone he had ever seen, looking like he was pulling it straight out of the foundations of the house. The man sat on it, creating a small table much in the same way and finally a smaller seat for Patrick and motioning him to sit down.

“Here’s the deal,” David said, crossing his legs. “Have you heard about Unity Corporation?”

There was a glint in the man’s eyes. He leaned closer, studying Patrick’s face.

“You did, didn’t you? Perhaps read about it on those online forums. The anonymous boards. You spend a lot of time there, theorizing. Well, Unity’s paranormal division, Candle Light, is very interested in anomalies such as that portal behind you.”

He smiled, a gold tooth catching the azure light of the aperture. “Oh, did I say paranormal? I meant charity division. Officially.”

Patrick gulped.

“Thing is,” the man continued. “We are a bit short staffed these days. With the devil invasion and all that. The world is changing quickly, and we need to adapt in order to keep up. We have our ways, but the one thing we need is people. Machines can’t level up, after all.”

“It’s–It’s real?” Patrick asked. His throat was dry, and it hurt to swallow. His heart felt right about to explode, and the tips of his fingers felt cold. “The devils are real? Magic is real?”

“I’m sure you saw some leaked pictures. You saw our operators doing strange things around the world, didn’t you? Heard the rumors.”

“I thought they were AI generated!” he said, words leaving his mouth rapid-fire like bullets from a rifle.

“Yeah, by this new cutting edge AI model called Daedalus,” David chuckled. “Guess who owns it.”

There was a pause, silence deafening.

“The world at large ain’t ready for shit like that,” he said. “But you…” he let the word hang in the air, once again leaning closer.

Patrick glanced behind him. The thought of riches and magic made him salivate.

“Ah, what am I saying?” David scoffed. “Dungeons are not for the weak. There are monsters and traps and dangers. You wouldn’t last a minute in there, trust me. Better to leave exploring and securing anomalies to trained operators.”

The seat beneath him crumbled as he got up. Patrick did the same, and not a moment too early because his seat too disappeared. Only as David turned to leave did he notice that the stone table was still there.

“Wait!” he cried out.

“Unless…” The man turned around, a grin on his youthful face. “What? Feeling brave, kid?” he asked.

Patrick nodded weakly.

“Do you perhaps feel like you might have what it takes to seize your destiny, to make a name for yourself, to finally take charge of your life… if only someone gave you a little push? A fateful encounter, a pivotal moment when everything changes?”

“Yes!” he said, nodding more and more vigorously. This was it. This was his pivot point!

“Very well, kid. So be it. I will give you a tool, all the push you are ever going to get. It comes with responsibility, and limitless potential.”

He placed something on the table. A simple digital watch, just like the one Patrick always wore at his wrist.

“Wearing this will give you access to the Origin System. It will give you Quests, magical skills, XP points and levels. Do you know what it means?” David asked, then chuckled. “I know you do.”

“And the responsibilities?” Patrick asked. He had no idea how he even managed to have the presence of mind not to immediately jump on the table and take the watch. Perhaps it was the man’s petrifying presence.

“Ah,” David scoffed. “Simple stuff. The Quests will explain it better. Just explore the rift, level up and upgrade the Domain emitter. You think you can do that, kid?”

Patrick didn’t know what to say.

“Perhaps I should give you a taste,” David said. “Put that watch on.”

He did. The licence agreement kind of scared him, but David did something and the message window disappeared. Another took its place–the Status. After only a few moments, barely long enough to have a glimpse of what the Origin System could do for Patrick, he took it away.

“Think about it, alright?” David said. “You have until tomorrow to accept the terms. Between you and me, kid. I think you can do it and honestly, I’d hate to have to come back to close the portal just because you turned out to be a wimp who couldn’t rise up to a challenge that could change your life forever.”

With that, the man was gone. Only the strange stone table remained, the watch, and the licence agreement he had already decided he was going to accept. The moment he did, many windows appeared one after the other. A tutorial, a place to sell items in exchange for XP, wiki entries about magic coins, his status screen complete with levels, skills and an artificial intelligence whose name he knew very well. Quests to explore the rift, see where it led and what awaited inside. The need to protect the emitter and, like David had said, to upgrade. The necessity to keep magic a controlled secret, for now. A chance to become a part of the worldwide network of operators acting behind the scenes, in the shadows, shaping the future.

He did not waste time. He was born just a wimpy kid, bullied at school, a life of grey. But he was going to become much more. He was an adventurer, an operator. The rift beckoned.
