Three more days had passed in my imprisonment, and I must say, my cell had improved considerably.

It was now full of pillows, books, a fine mattress, actual food supplies, and even a few drawings and makeshift decorations. To be honest, it was starting to look better than my old room — which, also to be honest, I didn’t leave much either.

Except for the toilet hole in the corner. That was still a toilet hole.

I really didn’t enjoy doing my business in a literal pit, but surprisingly, it didn’t smell that bad. I couldn’t tell how deep it was—it was pitch black down there. Was this a portal to some shit dimension? I’d investigate someday. But not today.

Today I was busy.

I actually had things to do in this cell.

First: fitness training.

I decided to level myself up the mundane way. Not as cool as magically leveling up, but still important. There was a high probability of me getting into a physical confrontation at some point soon, and being less of a weakling would dramatically improve my survival chances. As much as I hated push-ups and squats, it was now a matter of life and death.

Which, by the way, is a surprisingly effective motivation for working out.

Another nice way to pass the time was the books Juliette brought me. She smuggled them from various shelves in the mansion. I asked her to focus on useful topics like magic and history, but since the books weren’t categorized, she had to guess.There was this history book called The Dark Emperor that I actually found myself enjoying. It was about an emperor who saved villages from monsters, gaining their alliance and expanding his empire that way. Each time, the story focused on him rescuing a specific beautiful farm girl from danger—and her... expressing her gratitude in certain ways.

Ok, this book probably wasn’t a history book. It was more like a romance novel—actually, more like a trashy erotic romance. At that point, I should’ve stopped reading and done something more productive. But I didn’t. I mean, by then, the emperor was basically my role model.

Another book was more practical, more like a basic guide to magic.

According to it, the magic system here had two parts. The first was called Rune Manifestation, where you had to summon magical symbols—magic circuits—into a kind of half-real, glowing, holographic form.

These runes had to be precise, with exact shapes and flawless structure. The second part was Activation. Once the rune was stable, you could then use it to cast actual magic.So, say you wanted to cast a fireball. You’d need to draw three complex circuits—for fire, condensation, and movement—and then combine them.

Even the most basic fire circuit was a mess of triangles, spirals, and weird runes that needed to be drawn flawlessly.

In other words, before you could even think about lobbing a single little fireball, you probably had to practice rune-drawing for years.

Still… I tried.

You’re supposed to start small. Imagine a simple shape—a line, for example. As a beginner, you’d say a specific word out loud to help form it; for a line, it was something like “Najaaa.” Each shape had its own unique word, but more advanced users didn’t need to say them anymore.

I tried it a few times. And to my awesome surprise—a line appeared! Only for a moment, but it was there. Glowing, magical, and sort of real.

The hard part was stabilizing it. Which, according to the book, was basically impossible to learn without a proper teacher. As cool as it was, trying to do it alone would probably be a waste of time.But that little flicker of a glowing line was the first magical thing I’d ever done in my life. And It made me so happy.My favorite part of the day, though, wasn’t the workouts or the sketchy emperor erotica.

It was Juliette’s visits.She brought me food, told me about her day, and we’d talk for hours. She’d been using her free time to visit the tavern, talk to NPCs, and gather info about the world. We’d go over everything she learned and try to piece together what kind of system we were stuck in. She was also on the lookout for more players like us.

She told me that most of the NPC groups hanging around the tavern had some kind of problem. A missing child. A mine full of monsters. Obvious quest bait.

She also scouted a forest near town. She spotted strange creatures at the edge—low-level monsters, probably. One looked like a giant jumping spider. Another was like a small bear with scales and horns. Not your typical fantasy monsters—definitely not from any game I knew.

So the possibility of slaying weak monsters and leveling up was back on the table—though Juliette would have none of this senseless animal slaughter.

She also found out that past the forest was a bigger town called Hermiuly, known for its magical shops and a mage guild. But multiple NPCs warned her about the forest. Not that the NPCs were all that helpful. Most of them just repeated the same lines, no matter what she asked.

She did think my little flickering magic line was really cool, though.

Honestly, it was fun—planning, talking, sharing ideas. Juliette was smart, thoughtful, and curious. She asked good questions. And we were both stuck in a magical adventure together. No other way to describe it.

She was also cute—not very… developed-cute, But still pretty. And she was my friend. Back in my old life, I didn’t have many friends—especially not any female ones. So this was kind of magical in itself.So when I heard her footsteps coming down the hall, I put the seductive emperor book aside and sat up straighter.

But then I heard another set of footsteps. Heavier ones.

A grown man appeared beside her. Tan skin, short beard. Friendly face.

“This is Ramon,” Juliette said cheerfully. “He’s from the Philippines.”

Damn. What the heck? Why is the third group member a dude? Is this... could this be... reverse harem!?
