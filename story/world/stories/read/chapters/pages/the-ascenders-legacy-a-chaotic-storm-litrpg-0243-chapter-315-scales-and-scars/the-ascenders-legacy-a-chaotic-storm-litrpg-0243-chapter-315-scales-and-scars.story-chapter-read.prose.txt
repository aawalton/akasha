Still reeling with the thought of appointing Crixis and the others as his underlings, Aodhán pushed the front door open and walked into his mansion, a huge smile blooming on his face as he took in the beautiful space.

The entrance hall stretched before him like a throne room, the ceiling soaring high above and crowned by an intricate corona of crystallized lightning that pulsed with soft violet light, casting the space in shifting shades of gold, crimson, and violet.

The walls themselves were the color of midnight, their surfaces rippling and shimmering with a dense array of runic etchings that seemed to pulse with each breath he took. The floor appeared to have been made from polished gold marble, its mirror-like surface reflecting the corona's light and doubling the sense of space, which was already massive to begin with.

But perhaps most beautiful were the scatterings of dragon flame flowers placed decoratively at every corner of the room—their black and crimson petals perfectly complementing the beauty of the space.

It was beautiful in a very somber, dangerous way that Aodhán really liked, like a sleeping dragon.

But beyond its physical grandeur was something far more intoxicating: its essence quality.

While the entirety of the special sect was already dense with storm essence—the atmosphere so rich and full it would require a few days of adaptation to finally overcome the discomfort—the essence inside the mansion was cleaner, sharper, richer, and far easier to manage.

Every breath Aodhán took felt like inhaling raw, crisp lightning, the energy buzzing through his nostrils and then tingling down into his lungs, bathing his pathways and core in raw power.

Shuddering in pleasure, Aodhán turned around to take in the whole room in amazement, fingers brushing against one of the couches scattered around the space. He glanced at Elder ThunderWind then and said, "Wow."

Elder ThunderWind smiled. "I know exactly how you feel, Special Disciple Aodhán. But if you're this amazed now, then you're in for a shock when you see the other rooms."

Aodhán chuckled, shaking his head. "I can't believe this is only one of eighteen rooms. It's like a throne room."

"And the least impressive, in my opinion," Elder ThunderWind teased as he pointed to the left side of the hall. "The kitchen, storage, and dining are over there, and to your right are three bedrooms, which you can check out in your own spare time."

Aodhán raised an eyebrow. "I take it they aren't special then?"

Elder ThunderWind shook his head. "They are just normal rooms for your underlings or guests. Though, considering your unique situation, I doubt you'll be having many of the latter."

Aodhán's mind flashed to his family in Lutia, and he shook his head. "Maybe not."

Elder ThunderWind nodded somberly. "I don't know what your situation in Lutia was like, but you can think of us as your new family, Aodhán. Everyone in the sect is one family, bonded by our affinity."

Aodhán smiled. "I thought blood makes family."

Elder ThunderWind snorted. "Love makes family, Aodhán. Love and magic, and here in the sect we have both."

Aodhán nodded. "I'll keep that in mind."

"Oh no, don't take my word for it. You'll experience it yourself soon enough. Now come on, we have eleven other rooms to see. I suggest we begin with the basement."

Elder ThunderWind led him to the basement stairs, and together they descended, Aodhán bearing a slight grimace as he thought of the elder's words.

He hadn't sensed any anger or envy from his fellow disciples during his proclamation, but a part of him just couldn't accept that in a sect of thousands of individuals, not one had a negative reaction to his status as a transmigrant.

Perhaps he was overthinking things. Perhaps Lutia had traumatized him. But Aodhán was just waiting for the other shoe to drop, and with a war looming, he had a feeling it wouldn't be too long before it did.

They entered the basement a few seconds later, and the gloomy feeling that had begun forming within him suddenly evaporated at the sight spread out before them.

Elder ThunderWind smiled widely when he noticed Aodhán's expression. "Welcome to your Runic-Forge Station. The architects got information from High Elder Al'ruhn that you were very interested in runes, so they scrapped the alchemical station to make a bigger space for you to work."

"I…" Aodhán tried to speak, but words failed him in that moment.

Before him was a workshop just like that of Professor Caldwell, only much larger and far more advanced. Grand-rank ice and wind runes covered the roof, and despite the lack of windows, a steady stream of light bathed the forge in gold.

At the heart of the chamber stood the forge itself—a runic ecosystem of interlocking black-steel platforms streaked with veins of crackling violet and gold lightning.

It wasn't active yet, but Aodhán could already feel excitement bubbling beneath his awe as he imagined all the things he could create here. Aside from the forge, he recognized a ton of equipment he had seen back in Professor Caldwell's workshop, and though he had very little experience with forging weapons, he could already tell he would be spending a lot of time down here.

"Do you have a lot of experience in runic forging?" Elder ThunderWind asked, and Aodhán finally tore his gaze away from the forge.

"No. No, I don't." He smiled. "I assisted a teacher of mine once or twice, but I never got the chance to learn it properly. Can I learn it in the sect? Will I be taking classes?"

Elder ThunderWind shook his head. "No, you will not be taking classes. This is not a school, Aodhán. However, there are certain elders interested in passing down their extensive knowledge to the younger generation. They hold what we call apprenticeships. If runic weaponry is something you're interested in, then I can put in a word for you with Master Sedgwick. He's an apprentice of the foremost runic expert in the sect. I'm sure he'll be happy to take you. I probably won't even have to convince him."

"Thank you, Elder ThunderWind. I would appreciate that."

Elder ThunderWind nodded, and Aodhán glanced back at the forge, still reeling. If he wanted to create anything of worth, he had to improve his runic inscribing skills greatly. And that meant he needed to begin inscribing adept-rank runes very soon.

The thought lit a fire inside of him, and his gaze hardened in determination. "What other classes can I take?"

"Any class you want," Elder ThunderWind responded as he began climbing back up from the basement. "The sect doesn't restrict or try to control your advancement. We simply make available any resource you might need. Some people advance by gaining and accumulating knowledge, while others are more interested in gaining experience out in the wild."

"Missions."

"Yes." Elder ThunderWind nodded. "For those who have lived their lives on a knife-edge like you have, rift delving can become very boring very quickly. I hear people like you have to stare death in the eye at least once a week to feel alive."

Aodhán laughed. "Oh, no. I'm not that extreme. In fact, I would prefer to live the rest of my life without staring death in the face."

"Hmm," Elder ThunderWind hummed, not quite believing him. "Whatever the case may be, the sect can cater to your needs. You only have to name it."

They reached the living room a moment later, and Elder ThunderWind began climbing up the stairs. "We only have a few more important rooms to go through. But before I lead you to your master bedroom, how about I show you—" He pushed a door open, and Aodhán was immediately accosted by a dense wave of water essence, so powerful it was nearly tangible.

His eyes widened, and Elder ThunderWind announced proudly, "Your Aspect room."

Aodhán stepped through the doorway, breath catching as the chamber revealed itself. It was smaller than the entrance hall—perhaps twenty meters across—but what it lacked in size, it made up for in sheer presence. The walls were no longer midnight-black but a deep blue that reflected light as if it were underwater.

The floor was covered in a thin layer of water—ankle-deep, perfectly clear, and warm to the touch despite looking frigid. And arranged on the walls was an array of water-awakened plants, their auras the main contributors to the density of essence in the room.

But it wasn't the essence density that stole Aodhán's breath.

It was his Aspect.

This time, it didn't just hum; rather, it sang, resonating within him so tangibly that Aodhán could feel it refreshing and re-energizing his spirit. Almost instinctively, he reached a hand to the liquid around him and pulled—a single tendril of water rising to curl around him like a serpent.

"Impressive." Elder ThunderWind's voice echoed from behind him, and that was when Aodhán realized he now stood at the center of the chamber, leaving Elder ThunderWind at the doorway.

"You truly are a genius, Special Disciple Aodhán," Elder ThunderWind continued. "To have unlocked an aspect at the advanced tier. I wouldn't have believed it had I not seen it with my own eyes."

Aodhán smiled at the man and waved him in, but Elder ThunderWind shook his head. "I can't follow you in there. I haven't yet unlocked the Aspect of Water, so such dense essence would be quite a discomfort—not unlike drowning to a man who can't swim."

Aodhán nodded in understanding. "Is it polite to ask what Aspects you have unlocked?"

Elder ThunderWind grimaced. "In your case, it would be very impolite, seeing as you have gained what many new Mythics still haven't. Perhaps that is a question you should refrain from asking Mythics to avoid seeming disrespectful."

Aodhán nodded and glanced back at the room, feeling a bit overwhelmed at the sight of it. An hour of meditation here would do wonders for his control over water, and if he kept at it, perhaps he could gain full control over the aspect before the 50th tier.

He sighed, shaking his head. The number of resources the sect had poured into this one mansion was unimaginable, but Aodhán also knew that to whom much was given, much was required.

As beneficial as all these resources were to him, it was more beneficial to the sect that he was strong—a strength he needed to gain very quickly. According to Zarxan, as soon as the other sects found out about him, his life would be plunged deeply into danger, and he would need all his strength to survive.

Aodhán left the Aspect room a few minutes later, and for the next hour, they moved from one room to the other, his amazement only rising with each one—from the library to the training hall to an observation deck and finally the rooftop: a cave-like chamber made specifically for Varéc.

Varéc came out to play then, wings flaring as he settled comfortably in the chamber, the open sky high above him.

It was an overall amazing experience, and by the time they were done, nearly two hours had passed since they'd left the mountain.

"I'm blown away, to be honest," Aodhán said to Elder ThunderWind as they descended the stairs to the living room. "I had expected something grand for the special sect, but I must confess my expectations have been exceeded."

"As they should be." Elder ThunderWind nodded in understanding. "Before the formation of sects hundreds of years ago, cultivators had to fight for every resource—bloodlines, knowledge, treasures—tearing each other down to maintain strength and keep themselves alive. Everyone chose a combat path, desperate for survival. Very few people had the time or chance to dedicate themselves to research and knowledge acquisition. The little knowledge they gained, they kept secret for themselves and their allies. Power was individual, and people perished for lack of knowledge."

"Until the sect came," Aodhán whispered.

"Until the sect came." Elder ThunderWind nodded. "It gave us a community and forced us to see that everyone of the same affinity is family. It made us understand that the power of a group could achieve far more than any single individual."

He smiled. "In many ways, the outer sect is just like that pre-sect era, though to a much lesser extent, because even they have access to the wisdom of elders and a ton of great resources at their fingertips. But you are a special disciple, Aodhán, and thus will be treated specially. I suggest you get used to it quickly, because more gifts are only going to come. We are entering a new era, Aodhán, and you more than others will have to be prepared for it."

Aodhán nodded somberly. He understood Elder ThunderWind's words perfectly. As the fourth transmigrant, and thus the one tilting everything out of balance, he would be the target of every other sect. The weakest link. And the sooner he became strong, the better he could protect himself.

Elder ThunderWind regarded him for a moment before nodding. "As a disciple of the special sect, you are entitled to a salary of two thousand sect points as well as an unrestricted communication chip that gives you access to everyone in the sect other than the Calamities and High Elders."

He pulled a violet-streaked ring from his robe and handed it over to Aodhán. "My contact is already saved in there in case of any questions. The ring also serves as your sect interface, allowing you to receive news, access missions, and track your progress."

Aodhán accepted the ring and channeled a small strand of willpower into it. Immediately, a violet screen flashed before him.

Disciple Interface

Name: Aodhán Brystion (Alias not yet chosen)

Stratum: Special sect

Available Points: 2000 SP

[MISSIONS] [CONTACTS]

Aodhán frowned as he read through the interface. "What does this alias mean?"

Elder ThunderWind smiled. "I'm sure you'll find out soon enough. That brings me to the last thing on my agenda, which is a matter of your underlings. I'm certain you've decided whom you want to choose by now?"

Aodhán grinned. "I do."

"Good." He pulled out a pen and pad from his robes. "Tell me their names, and I'll get them to you before sunset."

Aodhán did, and when he was done, Elder ThunderWind pocketed the pad. "I'll let you get some rest now. You strained your spirit during the mountain climb—it would be wise to let it heal before engaging in anything strenuous."

"I will."

Elder ThunderWind smiled. "See that you do, Special Disciple Aodhán. And welcome to the sect. I hope you find among us a family like never before."

He left soon after that, and Aodhán was finally left alone in the mansion. He turned around to take in the large space in much more detail, then decided to give his control over the building a test.

Channeling a strand of energized willpower into the point in his spirit where he could feel the bond of the house, he muttered, "Activate voice command."

And then, like a distant rumble of thunder, a response echoed: "Voice command activated."

Aodhán grinned, feeling a giddy excitement surge through him at the blend of magic and technology. Varéc must have heard the sound too, because he appeared almost immediately, phasing through the ceiling with a confused expression on his face.

The living room wasn't nearly as wide or as tall as his cave at the rooftop, though, and the moment he arrived, the room suddenly felt small and cramped.

Aodhán knew the first command he wanted to issue in that moment, and with a grin, he said, "Increase vertical height to twenty feet in all rooms."

Space hummed around him in response, runes gleamed, and then, so subtly that Aodhán wouldn't have noticed had he not been watching for that exact reaction, the space around him began to expand.

Varéc growled in surprise as he raised himself to his full height and found the ceiling still a few inches away. Aodhán's grin widened, and like a child with a new toy, he proceeded to expand the width also, until Varéc was able to move around the living room without issue.

Varéc growled in excitement, and Aodhán chuckled. "I'm glad you like it."

His expansions had converted the mansion into a palace with far more space than was necessary for human beings, further heightening its majestic beauty and danger.

His stomach rumbled before he could fully appreciate the beauty of the room, and that was when Aodhán realized he hadn't eaten anything since his time in the pub with Rhetta and her friends. He made his way to the kitchen then, searching the huge space for anything to eat, but he soon found the entire place empty. Apparently, his special treatment didn't stretch toward stocking his cabinets full of food.

Grimacing, he glanced back at Varéc, who was now lounging at the center of the living room. "I guess we'll have to go to the market to stock up. Would you like to come with me, or is that center rug too comfortable?"

Varéc perked up, tail swishing in excitement at the prospect of being outside. However, just as Aodhán began moving toward the front door, he suddenly sensed the cores of four individuals arrive within his compound, each one blazing with an intensity very similar to his own but far more powerful. There was no doubt as to who they were.

His newest neighbors.

Aodhán paused immediately, core sense delving deep into their cores to take inventory of their strengths. Only one of them was a transmigrant, while the others were double inheritors. But that wasn't the disturbing part.

Apart from being the newest special on the block, it turned out he was also the weakest, with the least of his visitors at the 57th tier and the strongest at the 70th.

Almost instinctively, Aodhán prepared himself for a battle he couldn't win. Elder ThunderWind had asked him to treat the other disciples as family, but Aodhán had been in this exact situation before.

The new kid disrupts the balance of power and needs to be put in his place by a group of jerks. Unfortunately for him this time, he was vastly outmatched and outnumbered.

His fingers brushed against the communication ring Elder ThunderWind had given him, itching to call for help, but he hesitated—not out of fear, but out of the awareness that he was traumatized.

These people hadn't given him any reason to mistrust them yet, and calling Elder ThunderWind for help when it wasn't necessary would only send the wrong signal to his new neighbors.

So, despite his screaming instincts not to trust them, Aodhán released his hold on the communication ring, drew in a deep breath, and waited.

The knock came a moment later—three sharp raps against the door, each one sending a spike of adrenaline through his chest. Beside him, Varéc tensed, scales rippling as he sensed Aodhán's unease, a low rumble building in his throat.

"Easy," Aodhán whispered, more to himself than Varéc, eyes closed as he forced his breathing to slow, his mind sinking deeply into the calm of {Eye of the Storm}. The calm rippled through his mind like a cleansing wave, pushing his fears and worries to the back of his consciousness.

When he opened his eyes next, his mind was as still as a lake, his expression placid.

With one final steadying breath, Aodhán pulled the door open, bracing himself for the leering snarls and aggressive posturing of his supposed superiors here to teach him his place.

Instead, he was met with an explosion of glitter and cheers.

Sparkling violet and gold confetti burst into the air as four voices shouted in unison: "WELCOME TO THE SPECIAL SECT!"

Aodhán froze, blinking against the cascade of shimmering particles now settling in his hair and on his shoulders. His mind stuttered, unable to process the disconnect between expectation and reality.

"Uh…" he managed. "Thanks?"

The group erupted into laughter—genuine, warm, completely unthreatening laughter—and their leader, the transmigrant, stepped forward with a grin so bright it reminded him of Yurin.

"Sorry about the ambush," the transmigrant chuckled, not sounding sorry at all. "We took the liberty of throwing you a welcome party. I am Sophran Thunderstride; a pleasure to meet you."

"And you too." Aodhán grasped his outstretched arm. "I am Aodhán Brystion.

"Well, we would be fools not to know who you are." One of the double inheritors stepped forward, silver-blond hair packed in a high ponytail. The boy playfully elbowed Sophran out of the way and stretched out his own arm. "I'm Seamus Calcirra. But you can call me Seamus or Cirra like these other fools do. You cannot, however, call me Cal. I hate Cal."

"Okay." Aodhán chuckled as he grasped the boy's arm. "Nice to meet you, Seamus."

"And you too." Seamus's smile widened. "It is your pleasure. You must be honored to stand in my presence."

"Get out of the way, Seamus." The only girl among them slapped Seamus on the arm and shoved him aside. Seamus protested as he staggered back, but she barely spared him a glance as she turned to face Aodhán, a wide smile on her face.

"Hi, Aodhán Brystion. I am Mirelle Vaskoff. And it is a true pleasure to meet you. I watched your climb up the mountain of tempest, and I must say, I am impressed."

"Thank you so much." Aodhán smiled, feeling his lips begin to ache slightly. "I appreciate that."

Mirelle pulled away, and then the last boy stepped up, dressed all in black. He had raven-dark hair and black Lichtenburg scars hugging one side of his pale white face.

The manifestation wasn't quite as severe as Aodhán's, but it was significantly more severe than that of the others, and as he stretched a hand to Aodhán in greeting, Aodhán felt a subtle sense of kinship pulse between them.

"Hi, Aodhán." He spoke softly, almost airily. "I am Darien. And I'm very happy to meet you."

"You too." Aodhán smiled, genuinely this time, as he grasped Darien's palm. "I'm happy to meet you."

Darien nodded and pulled back, his gaze still lingering on Aodhán as he pulled out a deck of cards from nowhere. "We brought cake, drinks, and cards. Do you play?"

Aodhán glanced at the unfamiliar cards and chuckled. "I don't think I've played this kind of card before, but I'm happy to find out." He opened the door wider. "Please come in."

The specials walked in, chatting and laughing as Darien began explaining the game mechanics. Aodhan watched them with a slight smile, not quite sure how to react to the surprising turn of events.

The last thing Aodhán had expected of his neighbors was niceness, and he found himself at a loss for how to deal with it.

But it wasn’t a bad feeling at all.

Slowly, a smile tugged at his lips, and he wondered, maybe—just maybe—Gordon really was different from Lutia.

Maybe he could let himself believe it.
