Ryland had to admit that this was perhaps one of the best coordinated groups of mages he had ever faced. Not just in terms of the sheer magical power they were capable of outputting. It was also just how well they worked together.

To call them well-drilled was a tremendous understatement.

One mage attempted to slow him down. It took only a fraction of a second for Ryland to decipher the vector of slowdown. It wasn’t time as he had originally thought. The grey-armoured Guardian’s spell circle had executed too quickly even for him to read the Centreglyph, but that was fine.

Air. It was evident that the Guardian was compressing and condensing the air itself to turn much of Ryland’s reality into a sluggish, slow miasma for him to pervade through.

And while it took him another fraction of a second to banish the slowness with the right counterspell, the Guardian had already fulfilled his purpose. A deadly golden lightning bolt blasted through the air to land upon Ryland with the force of a smiting Great God. It was a bit of an exaggeration, but it certainly didn’t seem so at the moment.

Ryland was actually forced back a few handspans in mid-air. The impact had been reduced because of the lack of air around him after his counterspell, but it had still been hard enough to leave a few cracks on his barrier.

Credit to the Guardians, rather than gloating about it, they continued their deadly barrage of magic.

It wasn’t long before Ryland grew increasingly tired of the sheer volume of spells coming his way. A torrent of molten metal shot out from a gargantuan spell circle thrown up by Nesmel. The lightning-caster had sent another spray of thundering bolts, this time bouncing along each of the bits of red-hot, liquid metal shooting at Ryland with the speed of cannonballs.

Someone else had called forth an entire sea in the middle of the air, towering tentacles shooting up out of nowhere.

A bow-wielding Guardian was taking pot shots at Ryland from atop a cumulonimbus cloud. Each pillar-sized arrow was wreathed with the power of the storm, unleashing a gale of wind, rain, and lightning upon Ryland whenever they got close.

The worst was the portal-wielder, however. For, like how Ryland himself was fond of doing, she could pop either herself or her compatriots out through annoying portals with no warning.

He supposed it was high time he received another taste of his own medicine one of these days.

But Ryland was quickly growing tired of simply reacting. The air was splitting apart with the sheer endless blast of magic, the world itself shaking and throbbing as violent mana pounded through reality. A cataclysmic battle was underway, one the Guardians were desperately trying to keep contained.

All because they valued the Crypts below them more than their own lives, or so Ryland assumed.

He raised a hand when he got a blink of breathing room. “[Extinction Event].”

His manasoul surged, turning his entire form as incandescent as a newborn star. Far above them all, the sky ripped apart with golden cracks as a gigantic rift opened, spewing out a meteor that could rival a small moon. One heading straight for the Crypts far below.

Immediately, the gathered Guardians’ attention went to the descending meteor, which was gaining a corona of blistering heat and unstoppable pressure as it shattered through Rhel’s atmosphere. Spell circles shot up, magic intensifying among every single mage, furious power emerging with volcanic intensity to stop the devastating plummet.

Ryland kept an eye on things with [Omniscience] and disappeared with another [Translocate]. With their attentions focused temporarily elsewhere, Ryland could start on the real offensive.

“[Emergent Depth Chaos]!” shouted the Guardian with the glaive.

A mountain-sized whirlpool emerged from her glaive, sparking with lightning and crackling with thunder. It ascended where the meteor fell, striking hard enough to actually begin slowing its fall. Ryland was impressed. To possess enough power to slow down an apocalyptic meteor certainly took some doing.

The others had been searching for him as he had disappeared, but they were quick to locate him. He wasn’t hiding, after all. Plus, he couldn’t tamp down his presence in his current form.

As such, a portal opened to his right.

Daring, really. Ryland was out in the open cosmos, where no air existed, where the temperature was low enough to cause flesh to freeze instantly, where the lack of stabilizing forces would have devastating effects on any mortal body.

And yet, a mage was forcing himself through, determined to take down Ryland. They were either quite brave.

Or too controlled by Mavar to resist.

“[Moon Eater],” Ryland cast.

Just as the elderly Guardian with the mace burst out of his compatriot’s portal, an army of white tentacles with black spikes attacked. He began smashing them apart, but he couldn’t stop them all. Several of Ryland’s summoned tentacles evaded the Guardian and shot into the portals.

But neither the older Guardian nor the one casting the portal was Ryland’s target. The Guardian approaching across the surface of the meteor wasn’t his goal either.

“[Awakened Avatar],” Ryland cast. His mana turned several degrees further intense. Sheer might and power tore apart reality in his vicinity, a corona of blistering magic ripping apart the enormous meteor’s ground he was situated on. Then— “[Dead Comet].”

Ryland fell. He shot through the meteor right beneath him as though he had been fired by a mountain-sized ballista, his Incarnation form ripping through the centre of the meteor in a blinding flash. He wasn’t actually tearing it up as he went, though he could have had he wished to do so. Instead, he was simply removing one Conceptual Glyph through the entire meteor.

The Conceptual Glyph of Substance.

Now, while the meteor existed in reality, it was as though it had suddenly become constructed of mist and smoke. There was no longer anything solid about it. Ryland fell straight through, as did several startled Guardians attempting to assault him.

Really, they had no reason to be shocked. Had they truly believed he’d have allowed a gargantuan meteor to land on Rhel itself?

Of course, this meant that the enormous whirlpool sent by the glaive-wielding woman was also rising far higher, straight through the smoky meteor. But Ryland was the real meteor now. He crashed into the whirlpool, experiencing the slightest of tickles at the meteor-stopping spell the Guardian had unleashed

Then he charged right through.

Too fast. Too powerful. Too explosively unstoppable. The sheer energy with which Ryland cut through the Guardian’s spell left no time or room for anyone to respond.

He collided against her with blistering power, his whole form exploding and sending out several shockwaves of increasingly more compressed energy in rapid seconds. The tops of the obelisks were sheared right off, the air rippling and warping, the atmosphere of Rhel thumping at the detonating impact.

Ryland had stopped, but his target had been flung through the sky at the momentum transfer. The Guardian’s armour rained off her in dozens of pieces, crashing onto the Crypts. Her body struck with fatal finality somewhere farther off in the distance.

There wasn’t a single sign of magic emanating from her location afterwards.

“No!” one of the other Guardians cried. “Fazirha! Khadrun!”

The glaive wielder wasn’t the only Guardian taken out. Ryland had reversed the Conceptual Glyph of Substance on the summoned meteor. The two Guardians who had fallen into it were now suddenly trapped within thousands of acres of solid stone.

Not that it meant Ryland could relax.

“[Absolute Timefort],” he cast.

It was good he had done so, for the spells from the enraged Guardians arrived in short order. Beams of frightening potency, waves of rippling energy, claws of compressed night and stars. A storm of magic so deadly and potent, it was perhaps a blessing for Rhel that they were all so high up in the air.

Ryland’s barrier with his Soul Glyph of Time ensured he remained entirely unaffected. The spells crashed against him like the tide of a restless ocean, attempting to wear him down, though they would undoubtedly need the assistance of aeons to have any effect.

They had bare seconds.

“[Temporal Reflection],” Ryland cast.

All the spells hammering on him now returned back to their casters, flying through the reality of time to attack their masters. Explosions rocked both sides of the conflict now.

It was far from enough. Already, the Guardians were moving on to other plans of attack. Other stratagems were already underway. By this point, their pride had been completely forgotten. They were all in on destroying Ryland together, by any means necessary.

Which explained why only one of them had tackled the reflected storm of spells, leaving the rest free to pursue Ryland’s demise.

“[Piercing Heavens],” cried the blood-armoured Guardian with the golden scimitars.

He turned into an actual drill, lines of gold rotating around his glowing form while power crackled outwards from his wake. But before he launched himself, there were two other Guardians enhancing him.

One was Nesmel, casting a spell that surrounded the spinning Guardian with deadly pellets of molten metal. The second was creating a strange, twisted corridor that connected the scimitar wielder and his target—Ryland. A blink later, the Guardian was charging through the world to rush down Ryland, the noise ripping into his ears despite [Absolute Timefort].

“[Translocate],” Ryland cast.

No sooner had the scimitar wielder thrown himself forward than Ryland had rifted right to his location. To a point right behind him, to be exact. For while he charged to where his target had been a second ago, Ryland could take care of his compatriots.

“[Alchemic Reconstruction],” Ryland incanted.

It was perhaps not the most correct choice just then. While both Guardians were momentarily surprised at Ryland’s appearance, they were recovering with blinding speed. They were Guardians of Rhel, after all. Hardened barriers encased their already armoured bodies, brutal magic ready to retaliate against whatever they faced.

Ryland’s spell converted the front of the descending meteor into a gargantuan, fang-filled maw, which was erupting outwards to consume the two Guardians he had targeted.

But it wasn’t just the maw of rocks and stone they had to contend. The other two Guardians, the ones Ryland had trapped within the meteor, had been blasting their spells in an attempt to get out of their impromptu prison. Now, with Ryland’s magic reshaping the meteor, their power was guided out in a particular direction.

Alighting right against their supposed companions.

Both Guardians shouted and cursed as they were swamped by their own allies’ magic. With [Absolute Timefort] ensuring his safety, more or less, Ryland took advantage of the chaos.

“[Everlife Spectre],” he incanted.

Through the storm of magic that had confused the two Guardians, Ryland rushed to them. His arms had shifted into that silver, spectral and skeletal form. Before either of his targets was aware, he had stabbed both his hands into their chests, clutching their very manasouls.

“[Soul Serration],” Ryland cast. Both Guardians froze, their minds unable to come up with even an instinctive reply thanks to how fast it was all transpiring. “[Soul Imp—”

Sadly, Ryland didn’t get to finish his second spell. Two interferences popped up first.

An arrow bearing a storm alighted upon his location, apparently uncaring that there were two Guardians caught up in things too. Ryland supposed getting blasted by rain and lightning was better than getting the soul stolen.

Then the elderly Guardian with the mace smashed in too, surrounded by an aura of pure belligerence made manifest into miasmic energy.

And if all that wasn’t bad enough, that silly drilling Guardian had returned in record time too. Ryland had thought he was only taking a second or two at most, yet even that was too long for the scimitar wielder. Something to keep note of.

“[Translocate],” Ryland incanted, taking himself away from the chaos.

The thing with the interruption was that, while [Absolute Timefort] would almost entirely guarantee his safety, it would still be affected by the wide variety of effects it had been about to be subjected to. Far too many kinds of magic impacting it all at once from all sorts of angles was a recipe for unintended consequences mucking up Ryland’s intentions.

So instead, he focused on taking care of the Guardians in singular fashion. While the rest were too caught up in the situation he had left them in—the world-rumbling detonation far behind him was proof enough of that—he concentrated on the one left alone.

The archer on the cloud.

A hundred arrows greeted him in an instant, each glowing and crowned with a hurricane. This spell [Absolute Timefort] had no problems tanking.

Which allowed Ryland to focus on his cast. “[Void Seal].”

He got the briefest look surprise from the archer before he was trapped inside the black void. Well, that took care of that pest. So long as Ryland dealt with the next pest, of course.

Another [Translocate] with another [Omniscience] soon got him next to the portal caster. For an infinitesimal moment, it was worth contemplating that the man was next to where the glaive wielder had fallen, with a portal active at her exact site of her perhaps-death.

But Ryland had no time for mercy right then. “[Fate Lock].”

A simple [Dimension Lock] wouldn’t be enough. Assuming his portals were advanced enough, the caster could have escaped spatial chains. Temporal chains, however, were an entirely different matter altogether.

The moment he felt Ryland’s spell take effect, his eyes widened. Even from a distance, his surprise was palpable.

But he was done, so Ryland turned his focus elsewhere.

He was forced to. The rest of the Guardians were already attacking him yet again, entirely ignoring how he had summarily dealt with three different Guardians in just a few minutes. A storm of spells was once more descending upon him, massive magic at the back of the man with the auric scimitars with their deadly edges.

“Enough of your rampage,” he shouted down at Ryland. “This ends here and now!”

Ryland couldn’t have agreed more.

Thunder fell with terrifying prowess. Magic rained down like an apocalypse. Sheer power assaulted Ryland instantaneously.

He noted, as [Absolute Timefort] yet again did its utmost to keep him perfectly safe, that the rest of the Guardians were staying behind. And the scimitar wielder’s power had multiplied as well. Hmm. Were they empowering one comrade in the hopes he’d be strong enough to defeat Ryland?

A foolish idea.

“[Dragongod’s—”

Yet again, Ryland’s spell casting was interrupted. The Guardian arrived with flashing speed, curved swords slashing at the Incarnation-level barrier around his target. It left no mark, but did push him back a few fingerbreadths, causing Ryland to frown.

“[Reality Slash],” the Guardian cried, perhaps a little too late. It seemed they could both be affected by the rush of battle.

Once again, [Absolute Timefort] proved absolute when defending Ryland, but it failed to stand up to proper scrutiny. Especially against a spell clearly designed to get through seemingly impassable barriers.

Space cracked, the world broke apart, and—

And Ryland stopped paying attention. No doubt, they were trying to mess with his rifting capabilities, trying to keep him locked down just as the archer and the portal-caster were.

But it was all for naught.

“[Broken Realm],” Ryland incanted. More magic surged out of him as reality obeyed and shattered further, far worse than whatever spatial slices the Guardian of Rhel was capable of, even with the assistance of his comrades. “[Bleeding World].”

Unreality started breaking out from the cracks in the cosmos that Ryland was setting forth. Where the scimitar wielder had been slashing at Ryland to peg him back, now he was desperately trying to defend himself against it. Ryland was actually impressed with how his blades were deflecting the cracks in reality sent out by [Broken Realm].

Thankfully, there was the second spell to overcome it all. It ensured that unreality wasn’t simply a widening crack threatening to consume everything it encroached upon.

Now, it bled out, seeking to take over whatever it touched actively like a parasite.

And there wasn’t a thing any of them could do about it. Ryland had spread his spell out far and wide. The easiest way to win was by keeping the Guardians separated, so, he had let the effect of his spells spread out over the entire mountain range. Obelisks toppled, peaks shattered, landslides roared down mountainsides, only to be swallowed into unreality.

Far above them, where the miasmic cloud of ruinous magic was falling upon his location, the other Guardians were struggling to stop Ryland’s spell as well. It seemed they had no direct counter to spatial magic that manipulated reality to that level. Although, now that Ryland looked closer, he spotted some of them successfully repairing bits of reality. How intriguing.

“What is this magic?” the Guardian nearest to Ryland said. “How do you stop this?”

Ryland was almost tempted to take pity on how the man was struggling. “You brought this on yourself, you know. In fact, you’re fortunate I haven’t taken any further action.”

Whatever mental calculations went through the Guardian’s head clearly landed on one specific conclusion. Pride didn’t matter in the face of annihilation by unreality.

“Then will you cease this—this spell if I tell you where the girl is?” the Guardian asked.

As if that signalled their defeat, the Grand Frame popped up as confirmation.

[ Achievement Recognized: One Mage Nation

You’ve defeated six Empyrean-ranked opponents despite being merely Titan-ranked.

Rank up from Titan [31] to Titan [55].

Reward: Glyph of Guardian [Tier 26]. ]

Ryland ignored it and felt a flicker of something, some emotion, trying to derail his thoughts. But he ruthlessly focused on the present. On his goal. He aimed a hand at the Guardian, magic coming alive with such frightful intensity, it turned his hands to Glyphs.

“I’m willing to hear you out,” Ryland said. “But understand that, if there is any trickery, I will simply wipe you out from existence.”

The Guardian gulped as unreality stopped expanding. Then he spoke.
