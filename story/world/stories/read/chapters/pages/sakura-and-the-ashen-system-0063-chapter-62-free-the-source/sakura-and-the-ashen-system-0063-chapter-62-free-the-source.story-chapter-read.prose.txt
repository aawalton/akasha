Sakura stared at the trembling surface of the pool for several more seconds, not believing her own ears.

"You finally heard us," the quiet voice repeated.

Now there was no doubt left about it. This was not splashing that had accidentally formed familiar sounds. The water was really speaking to her.

The next element.

Sakura remembered Gilbert warning her about this when she had shared the story about the voices of the wind with him. The mage had told her about Crowlis, who had also heard the elements one after another. Back then, it had seemed like a strange story about a person who had lived a very long time ago and had a completely different connection to the world. Sakura had thought the wind might have been a single case. Maybe some side effect of her class, or simply another local anomaly.

But now the water was speaking to her. Sakura got to her feet sharply and almost immediately stumbled. Keeping her balance, she went over to the mage and shook him by the shoulder.

"Gilbert."

He did not react.

"Gilbert, wake up."

The mage opened one eye, looked at her, and closed it again.

"If they found us, wake Pampli," he mumbled.

"They didn’t find us."

"Then don’t wake me."

"The water is talking to me."

Gilbert opened both eyes. For several seconds, he silently looked at Sakura as if trying to understand whether he had really woken up or was still dreaming.

"It’s doing what?"

"Talking. The water. The one in the pool."

Gilbert slowly sat up and ran a hand over his face.

"Of course. Why wouldn’t it start talking right now, when I’m resting somewhere other than a prison cell for the first time in several days?"

"I didn’t choose the time."

"And the water apparently did."

On the neighboring bench, Lir stirred. He lifted his head and looked at them sleepily.

"What happened?"

"Sakura heard the water," Gilbert answered.

Lir looked surprised.

"You mean she heard it flowing?"

"I mean it’s talking to me," Sakura said.

Sleep vanished from Lir’s face instantly. He sat up, pulling his bruised leg closer to himself.

"Like a person?"

"Not exactly. The voices are coming from everywhere. From the pool, from the gutter, even from the walls. One of them spoke more clearly than the others."

"And what did it say?"

Sakura turned toward the water. The voices had not disappeared. They kept whispering, but now they had layered over one another again. In every splash, she heard fragments of words, separate sounds, and sensations she understood more than heard:

"Open."

"Below."

"Tight."

"Dark."

"Free."

Sakura approached the pool and crouched by the edge.

"I’m listening," she said quietly.

Gilbert stood behind her. Lir also lowered his feet from the bench, but stayed where he was for now.

"What are they saying?" the mage asked.

"They’re asking me to release the water. To free some source. It’s somewhere under the bathhouse, but I don’t understand exactly where."

"Then don’t try to force them to speak like people," Gilbert said. "It was the same with Crowlis. Elements don’t always use words. Sometimes they convey images or sensations."

The water at the edge of the pool trembled. Several thin streams rose above the surface, stretched toward Sakura, and wrapped around her fingers. They did not fall down like ordinary water should have, but held in the air like transparent threads in weightlessness.

Lir drew in a breath.

"You see that too?"

"Unfortunately, yes," Gilbert answered.

Sakura carefully raised her hand. The streams followed. The water was warm and did not fully wet her skin, instead sliding over it as if barely touching.

"I’m not doing anything," she said.

The streams suddenly stretched in one direction, pointing beyond the pool. Then they fell onto the floor and flowed in thin paths between the stones of the mosaic.

Sakura followed them. Behind the pool was a section of wall they had barely looked at before. The mosaic had survived poorly here. Blue and white stones ended at a large square slab with an image of a fish. It was twice as large as the other drawings, but looked like an ordinary part of the decoration.

Water gathered in the grooves around the fish’s tail.

"Here," Sakura said.

Gilbert came closer and ran his fingers along the edge of the slab.

"This isn’t just a mosaic."

He pressed one of the stones near the fish’s eye. Nothing happened. Then the mage checked the edge of the slab, tapped it with his knuckles, and crouched, studying the floor.

Lir finally rose and slowly came over to them, limping.

"Maybe it needs to be pulled?"

"Possibly," Gilbert said. "But first we need to understand in which direction. If this is an old technical entrance, the slab may be part of a mechanism."

Pampli, who had been lying near the arch until then, lifted his head. He watched them for several seconds, then stood and came closer.

"Don’t break it," Sakura warned.

The beast pushed his muzzle into the slab, and the stone shifted several finger-widths with a heavy scrape. Gilbert looked at Sakura.

"It seems we didn’t need a complicated mechanism after all."

Pampli pressed his head against the slab again and moved it even farther. Behind it, a narrow dark gap opened, and warm damp air immediately drifted out. Sakura heard the water voices grow louder.

"Below."

"Closer."

"Open."

They managed to move the slab far enough for a narrow stone staircase to appear behind it. The steps went down under the bathhouse. Old pipes and shallow gutters covered with mineral deposits ran along the walls.

"I’m going with you," Lir said at once.

"No," Sakura and Gilbert answered at the same time.

Lir frowned.

"I can help."

"You can barely stand," Sakura said.

"I already feel better."

"You’re saying that while holding on to the wall."

Lir removed his hand from the wall, immediately swayed a little, and pretended nothing had happened.

"It might be narrow down there," he continued. "I can crawl through places you can’t."

"You’re skinny, not liquid," Gilbert replied. "If anyone can fit through a narrow opening, Sakura can. And if she can’t, then you definitely can’t either. And we don’t need a second person we’ll have to pull out of an underground channel."

"Then what am I supposed to do?"

Sakura looked toward the entrance of the bathhouse.

"Keep watch."

"Watch what?"

"Everything. The entrance, the things, Pampli. If the pursuit shows up, shout."

Lir clearly wanted to object, but Gilbert nodded toward the arch.

"It’s an important task. We don’t know how long we’ll spend below or whether the technical tunnels have another exit. Someone needs to stay up here."

After those words, Lir calmed down a little. He still did not like the task, but it no longer looked like a way to get rid of the weak companion.

"All right. I’ll keep watch."

Pampli tried to squeeze into the opened passage before Sakura. His head and front paws somehow fit, but his massive shoulders got stuck between the walls. The beast pushed harder, and the stonework trembled.

"No," Sakura said. "Don’t even think about widening the passage."

Pampli growled unhappily and lunged forward again. His claws scraped over the steps, and his hind paws braced against the bathhouse floor.

"Pampli, back."

The beast ignored her.

"He’s about to bring half the wall down on us," Gilbert noted.

Sakura grabbed one of the ridges on Pampli’s neck and pulled backward. It did not help at all. Lir came over from the other side and carefully braced his palms against the beast’s side.

"Maybe together?"

"He weighs more than all three of us."

"Then let’s try to convince him."

"Pampli," Sakura said sternly, leaning toward his ear. "If you destroy the bathhouse, there won’t be any water anymore."

The beast froze.

"Exactly. No warm pool. No bathing. Only mud."

Pampli stood still for another second, then reluctantly backed away. Once he had pulled himself out of the passage, he shook himself off and looked at Sakura.

"You’re staying with Lir," she said.

Pampli snorted.

"I’m happy too," Lir responded.

Sakura and Gilbert went down. The steps were slippery, and the ceiling gradually grew lower. Soon they had to walk almost bent over. The voices of the water sounded louder and louder, but Sakura still could not make out full sentences.

The tunnel led them into a small technical room. The floor was flooded ankle-deep. Wide stone channels ran along the walls, and in the center stood an old mechanism made of darkened metal. Several large wheels were connected to shutters in the walls. Most of the structure had rusted and become overgrown with white mineral deposits.

The water barely moved. It stood in the gutters as a warm motionless mass, and from beneath the far wall came dull pressure, as if a trapped current was beating against the stone.

"There," Sakura said and pointed ahead.

Another shutter could be seen on the far wall. It had been completely thrown out of alignment. A piece of collapsed masonry was wedged between the stone guides, and thick roots had tangled over everything from above.

Gilbert came closer and examined the obstacle.

"If we simply break the stone, the current may hit too hard."

"The water wants out."

"Water doesn’t have bones it can break against a wall. We do."

Sakura listened. The voices hurried her, but another sensation appeared among them. Not just a request to open the shutter, but a sequence: first the upper channel, then the iron, and only then the stone.

She looked at the mechanism.

"We need to open something above first."

Gilbert followed her gaze and noticed a small wheel under the ceiling. A thin chain went down from it, connected to a side shutter.

"Pressure release," he guessed. "If we open it, part of the water will go into another channel, and the main current won’t hit all at once."

The wheel did not move. Gilbert tried to turn it by hand, but the rusty metal only creaked.

"Step back," he said.

Sakura took a step back. The mage raised his hand. A thin discharge flashed between his fingers. It was not as bright as before, and for a moment it went out, but Gilbert gathered himself and directed the lightning into the metal axle. The discharge ran through the mechanism. Rust crumbled away, the chain jerked, and the wheel slowly turned.

Water began to murmur in the side channel.

Gilbert exhaled heavily and lowered his hand.

"Are you all right?" Sakura asked.

"Yes. I have enough strength for old plumbing. That’s enough."

Sakura listened again. Now the iron.

"We need to free the shutter from the roots."

Gilbert used another short discharge. The roots smoked, split, and began to slide down from the wall. The mage had to repeat it twice before the last thick offshoot fell into the water. After that, he leaned his palm against the wall for several seconds.

Sakura said nothing, only stood closer in case he needed help.

The stone remained. It was wedged between the guides too tightly to pull out by hand. Gilbert placed his palm on the masonry beside it and closed his eyes.

"I’ll split it into pieces. When the water comes, stay against the wall."

"Maybe we should move farther away first?"

"Then I won’t reach."

Sakura grabbed a protrusion in the wall. Gilbert gathered magic. This time, the discharge was short, but it struck exactly into the crack. The stone split with a dry crunch.

The first pieces fell into the channel, and the shutter jerked. Gilbert managed to take one step back when water struck it from the other side. The metal bent, thin streams burst from the gaps, and then the current rushed into the room.

Sakura was pressed against the wall. The water rose almost to her knees, spun around her legs, and tore through the opened channels. Gilbert held on to a metal support. For several seconds, the noise was so strong they could hear nothing else.

Then the level began to fall as the stagnant water went into the gutters, carrying away dirt, silt, and small fragments. From beneath the wall, a clean hot stream poured out with a bright blue glow. It filled the main channel and ran upward, toward the bathhouse pool.

The voices around Sakura merged into one common sigh:

"Free."

"At last."

"Thank you."

Gilbert looked at the shining water.

"I assume we did what they wanted?"

"Yes."

"Did they say that directly?"

"You could say that."

They went back up. Even on the stairs, Sakura heard splashing, and when she climbed out into the bathhouse, she stopped.

The entire room had changed.

Water ran noisily through the old channels along the walls. The pool had cleared of dark sediment and now shone brighter. Blue light reflected off the ceiling, the mosaic, and the wet stones. Even the faded fish on the floor seemed more alive.

Lir stood by the pool, and Pampli was circling around, irritated that he had been left upstairs.

"What did you do?" Lir asked.

"Freed the source," Sakura answered.

"The bathhouse started glowing."

Gilbert lifted his gaze to the wall.

"Look."

Thin blue lines were spreading across the mosaic. They appeared between the stones, connecting the images of fish, waves, and the sun. At first, Sakura thought they were simply streams of water, but the lines did not flow. They formed something. Sakura understood that it was a map. It marked underground streams, springs, and small buildings similar to this bathhouse. Some signs had almost gone out, while others barely flickered. One was here. Another was glowing much farther away, to the east.

Gilbert came closer.

"This is an old network of water collection stations. They were connected by underground currents and served the road."

"Most of them are destroyed?" Lir asked.

"Looks that way."

Sakura ran her finger along a glowing line. It went away from the bathhouse, curved around the main road, and stretched toward the place where the Lake of Six Mists was located.

"This stream goes toward the lake."

"Not directly to it," Gilbert clarified. "But close enough. If we follow the channel, we can stay away from the road."

"And the next station?"

He looked at the bright mark.

"If the map is still accurate, it’s here. And if we’re lucky enough, there should be a spring and shelter there."

They had a route. Sakura felt relief, but the water spoke again. The voices changed, becoming quieter and colder:

"Black hunter."

"Blood in the water."

"Searching."

Sakura froze.

"What else?" Gilbert asked at once.

She closed her eyes, trying to make out the images. A shallow stream, unfamiliar boots in the water, dark shards fallen between stones. The smell of blood. A tall man walking along the streambed and paying no attention to the cold.

The black hunter. He had touched one of the currents connected to this source. This man was asking about the Obsidian Hierophant and following their trail.

Sakura opened her eyes.

"They’re looking for us."

"Castle servants?" Lir asked.

"No. Someone else."

She looked at the glowing lines of the map. A barely visible dark ripple was slowly spreading along one of them.

"The water calls him the black hunter. It says he touched a current connected to it. He had blood on him and obsidian magic."

Gilbert immediately grew more serious.

"Is he close?"

Sakura listened.

"Far."

"Going the right way."

She already knew the answer. Even before the water showed her a tall silhouette, a heavy stride, and the face of a man who would not stop on his own. Who did not know how to stop. They had managed to lose the trail when they infiltrated the Castle for Lir, but their head start was over. He was here again. He had found them.

"Still far," Sakura said quietly. "But he’s going the right way."

Lir looked at her with alarm.

"Do you know who it is?"

Sakura did not look away from the dark ripple on the map.

"His name is Faerd."
