By the time Edge took his seat, the proceeds from their sales had arrived.

Since Trapper had arranged everything, the Credits were transferred to her account. She sent Sakura and Edge their share, leaving him with a whopping 1,059,500 creds to spend during the second half of the event.

The auctioneer and his assistant ascended the dais soon after. He waited for a few stragglers to arrive before addressing the crowd, slowly turning in a circle so he was facing everyone for a portion of his spiel.

“Welcome back, esteemed aficionados of quality goods. I hope you enjoyed the complimentary refreshments provided by our talented staff. We’ve had some exciting exchanges during the first round, but what’s coming next promises to be even better. The hottest skill gems, rarest resources, and most powerful devices will be displayed during this portion of the proceedings. If your eyes are keen and your wits are sharp, you might walk away with the item of your dreams.

“Moving right along, we’re going to present today’s offerings by category. Exotic materials will be showcased first, followed by top-grade weapons, armor, and skills. After displaying a fabulous selection of custom-crafted magitech devices and implants, we will end the auction with a small, carefully curated selection of one-of-a-kind finds—the details of which won’t be revealed until we’re ready for the bidding to begin.

“With that out of the way, it’s time for some fun! We’re kicking off this session with a collection of elementally aspected jewels. They boast a wide range of uses, including crafting…”

Edge tuned the auctioneer out, since he wasn’t planning to purchase anything in this block. While it was interesting to check out an assortment of exotic materials he hadn’t seen before, he didn’t need to listen to the man sing their praises or track the play-by-play of the bidding wars that followed, although he memorized their properties and appearances so he could spot them while hunting in the field.

He spent most of his time watching his fellow attendees. Privacy devices and active skills weren’t allowed during the auction, which meant people had to school their expressions the old-fashioned way. Some of them were much better at masking their emotions than others. He realized that part of the art of getting a good deal involved reading the competition, bluffing, and the like, just like the card games he’d played when his father was alive.

He savored the electric tension filling the air when the First Among Equals entered an economic duel with one of the leaders of the merchant guilds. A satisfied smirk lit up her face when she lost the bid, leading Edge to suspect she’d ramped up the price on an item she wasn’t interested in to keep the guildmaster from claiming the prize she truly wanted.

The back-and-forth was fierce between the assembled crafters and traders, but they weren’t the only ones interested in raw resources. A handful of hunters threw their hats into the ring for materials that were ideal for crafting weapons and armor.

The exchanges got even more interesting when the auctioneer moved on to equipment. For this portion of the event, Edge paid close attention to every word. It was a fascinating opportunity to check out some of the best gear World’s End had to offer. Some of the weapons were even more expensive than Opulence, although they couldn’t compete with the polearm’s raw value when viewed through Dragon’s Greed.

His friends placed bids on several weapons but gave up when the prices soared above their means. They had agreed on a signal ahead of time to indicate must-haves, so he knew nothing critical had slipped through their fingers.

When the weapons transitioned into armor, Edge’s adrenaline started flowing again. He was in desperate need of something better than his current set. It was a decent stopgap but wouldn’t hold up against stage-four opponents, particularly the gigantic creatures straddling the apex of the biome’s ecology.

He almost went all in on a set of magitech armor that resembled the suit he’d seen Corpse wearing but decided not to at the last second. The prizes for the Carnage Cup had been unveiled that morning, and the winner would receive a Legendary set of runic armor known as Living Mail.

They hadn’t released the details of what it could do, but considering its rarity and the fact that the match was one of the most competitive exhibitions of the season, he suspected it was better than the suit on display. He knew his odds were long, but he wanted to take his best shot before blowing his savings on another set. Besides, I can attend an auction every month going forward. There are bound to be other options even if I don’t win the Cup.

Sakura wound up purchasing the next suit the auctioneer displayed—an amalgamation of pale-pink leather and pure-white chitin reinforced with a delicate mesh of orange chainmail. It wasn’t runic or magitech, but it was made by one of the best armor crafters in the settlement, and the set was strong enough to carry her through stage three. It cost the scion 300,000 Credits, which no longer felt like a fortune after seeing how much some of the other items sold for.

When the auction transitioned to skill gems, magitech devices, runic gear, and implants, a state of intense focus came over Edge that was usually reserved for life-and-death battles. He supposed it made sense. This was a battle in its own right, and many of his opponents had him outgunned in wealth and combat prowess.

While there were several items that caught his eye, there was only one he seriously wanted to buy. He patiently waited for it to appear while watching his friends have fun.

Trapper came away with a Rare gem containing an unusual skill called Sentry Ward. The Wards could be placed in midair in addition to being bound to the ground like Manifest Trap or Jab. The skill didn’t do any damage, but it could be set to trigger under a complex range of conditions and could manifest in various ways, unleashing everything from a blaring horn loud enough to be heard for miles to a heads-up display for her eyes only.

While Ward had some overlap with the Alarm skill she’d picked up a few days back, it offered quite a few novel features, had a considerably longer range, and could detect both incorporeal creatures and mental projections along with physical threats. Once she ranked up both skills, Trapper planned to merge them with a token, which would free up a slot and, with any luck, create something even better.

Sakura was interested in some high-end magitech devices that could facilitate the analysis of mana and aether. There wasn’t anything that could compete with her mana sensitivity, but she purchased an interesting item called a Mana Filter. Once she programmed it to detect a specific frequency of energy, it would block everything else, making it easy to focus on the source she wanted to investigate—ideal when hunting for minuscule traces in an environment saturated with other forms of magic.

At long last, the auctioneer reached for the item Edge had been eagerly awaiting all night. He was in the middle of reviewing his reasons for wanting it when the man began his spiel. His intense state of focus shot straight to eleven, scanning the crowd while schooling his features.

At the same time, he pulled up the details on the auction-interface menu provided by his Guide.

Arcane Jammer (Rare). Tier: 1.

Size: Small (1 implant point). Aether type: Platinum.

This implant detects various forms of tracking and observational skills targeting its owner or an item in their possession. The Jammer will render the energetic link visible to its wielder’s eyes (additional information will be available at higher tiers). It cannot detect existing magic that is targeting a specific location. If the wielder desires, the implant will expend aether to sever the energetic link, forcibly canceling the skill.

“As you can see,” the auctioneer continued, “the Arcane Jammer is a perfect device for anyone who values discretion and privacy. It will ensure that you are not being followed or observed via magical means. At tier one, the device can detect skills up to rank five, and that number will climb if you manage to upgrade it.”

Edge frowned at the appreciative murmurs that rippled through the audience before Sakura elbowed him, and he put his game face back on. If he wanted to claim this implant for his own, he would have to overcome fierce competition. As he waited for the man to reach the end of his presentation, Edge made sure this was a wise decision while deciding how much of his wealth he was willing to part with.

Since the Jammer was Rare at tier one, it would max out at Legendary, which was superior to any of the implants available on the silver ring or the Mortium exchange. The custom-crafted device was guaranteed to be incredibly good at its job, and it would improve substantially once he sourced the materials required to upgrade it. Better still, the Arcane Jammer only filled a single implant point, which was significantly better than the inferior options he could acquire through other means.

It was the only implant he’d heard of that ran on Platinum aether. A full charge was included with the purchase, as were the services of the establishment’s surgeon, who would install the implant under the cover of a privacy device. It would take years before it ran out of juice unless he used it to counter function, even if he left the Jammer running around the clock.

The more he mulled the matter over, the more certain he became that this was an ideal implant for his needs. Going forward, Edge had to watch over his shoulder for the Wardens, the Coalition, and anyone else who was seeking the scions of the fallen Lords of Ord. He was hunting the Ancients, and suspected they were tracking him too, with an insane bounty posted on his head as a cherry on top.

Even without those pressing concerns, the Arcane Jammer would ensure that no one could determine his whereabouts via magical means. It would come in handy when hunting apex predators and exterminating monsters, beings that were perfectly capable of utilizing tracking and observational spells.

Better still, the detection and counter functions were separate, which meant he could pretend that he didn’t know he was being watched. It would be invaluable if he wanted to lure his pursuers into a trap, and he could use the device to follow spells to their source while the link was active. His last bastion of reluctance gave way when the auctioneer mentioned that one of the tier-three upgrade paths let the Jammer create a magical backlash, damaging the caster of any spell it unmade.

“For such an exquisite item—the first of its kind to be sold in years—why don’t we start the bidding at an even 100,000 creds?”

Edge sighed in relief when he heard that number. Maybe I can afford it after all. Before he had time to confirm his bid, the price jumped to 200,000, followed immediately by an offer of 500,000.

Determined to take the device home, he placed his first bid, increasing the price by the minimum 50k. Excited whispers broke out as the number steadily climbed. Before long, the bids had skyrocketed to a whopping 750,000 Credits.

Cold sweat ran down his neck when he upped it to 800k, grinning when he realized he was embroiled in one of the fiercest fights of his life, even if its nature was distinctly different than anything that had come before. His pulse pounded as he watched the thirty-second bid window tick down to zero, but just before the clock ran out, someone raised their offer all the way to one million.

Edge didn’t have much more than that, even after his recent windfall. He immediately raised the bid by 50k, desperately trying to convey the impression that the amount was insignificant and he could play this game all day. Whether his ruse worked or his competition simply wasn’t willing to go any higher didn’t matter to him at all. One way or another, when the clock hit zero, his bid remained on the screen.

“And there you have it, ladies and gentlemen. After a delightful exchange, the Arcane Jammer has found a new home for a sum just north of a million creds.”

The attendees applauded the thrilling round. It wasn’t the largest sum an item had sold for this evening, but it was the most an offering had gone above its starting bid.

Trapper squeezed one hand, and Sakura clasped the other, congratulating him on his victory without looking his way. It took everything he had not to celebrate the moment, contemplating the devastation he’d wrought to his savings instead. I haven’t been this poor since the start of my adventures. I need to go hunting tomorrow.

Edge’s Guide let him know the surgeon would be available as soon as the auction was over. He was relieved to read that the winners would be awarded their items in a manner that guaranteed complete anonymity. He sat there, secretly savoring the biggest purchase of his life while other offerings were presented and sold for considerable sums.

It seemed like the evening’s excitement had come to an end, but he couldn’t have been more wrong.

“Esteemed patrons, tonight has been a wonderful event. We’ve watched amazing items change hands and witnessed intense rounds of commercial combat. Before we bring the auction to a close, I have one final item for your consideration.”

Something in the man’s tone drew every eye in the room. The chatter died down, and Edge waited on pins and needles. Trapper and Sakura felt it too, staring at the dais while the auctioneer reached for an object covered by a silken scarf.

“This is a special offering whose details were hidden until this moment. For your consideration, allow me to reveal the only Legendary item on tonight’s docket. It is one of the finest runic items I’ve ever seen—obtained while conquering a stage-four dungeon in a biome that was only discovered a few weeks ago.”

There was more, but Edge couldn’t focus on the words. Every thought had been driven from his head when his Guide displayed the details.

Lens of Truesight (Legendary)

Type: Runic item.

Strain: Low (1 implant point).

This lens is imbued with the power to pierce illusions and reveal the true nature of creatures that can change their forms.

He heard Trapper and Sakura inhale sharply, and it was clear they had been struck by the same revelation. This is exactly what we need to discover the Ancient’s identity, along with any of its foul brethren lurking elsewhere on Ord.

Trapper flashed one of the hand signs they’d come up with in preparation for the auction, indicating that she planned to go all in, even if she had to spend the Credits she was holding for the rest of the team. Sakura nodded her agreement, and Edge knew the scion was transferring her remaining funds to the crew’s leader.

He berated himself, upset that he’d squandered his savings and couldn’t contribute, but it wound up being a moot point. The lens opened at a million creds, then quickly shot to two. Before he knew it, the runic item had surpassed what the crew could have afforded by pooling their resources.

Everyone started talking at once when the lens hit five million and kept right on going. The back-and-forth started slowing around six. Edge had the impression that most of the buyers had backed out, leaving only a handful competing to claim the Legendary lens. The air was thick with tension as everyone tried to figure out who was involved.

The offers jumped time and again until the price broke seven million creds. When the countdown clock hit zero, the auctioneer extolled the victor and the crowd went wild.

That was when he caught the reek of rotten meat. A wave of tainted magicytes filled the room, flowing from somewhere high above. The corrupted magic seemed furious to his skill senses, as if the filth was saturated with killing intent.

When the women beside him went rigid, he realized they felt it too. The full implications of what was happening hit him just as Skill-Eater’s voice echoed within his mind.

“Beware, my scion. The Ancient stands before us.”

The monster was involved in that bidding war. Whether or not it won, it was angry enough to let its mask slip for a moment.

No one else seemed to have noticed anything out of the ordinary, which meant Monster Slayer had detected motes of corruption that were invisible to everyone else. He started at his Guide and read random messages as a pretense, certain that his life was on the line. His instincts were screaming that scanning the balconies would be courting catastrophe. A creature that careful would have already covered its tracks, and his gut was telling him it was watching.

While he waited for his heart to stop racing, Edge pondered the implications of the event. The Ancient was sitting in one of the VIP balconies. It must be one of the city’s elites. When the audience returned to the reception area for a nightcap while waiting to pick up their items, the trio stepped into an empty room and activated their privacy devices.

“We need to find out who was involved in that bidding war,” Trapper read Edge’s thoughts verbatim. “One of them must be the Ancient. If it wanted that lens, the device must be capable of piercing its disguise. We need to get our hands on it.”

“I already tried hacking into the building’s magitech server,” Sakura replied, “but the defenses are potent, even for Lore-Weaver. I’ll keep working on it, but it’s going to take more than one use of Bypass to get the job done, and I have to wait for my ultimate to cool down before I can try again.”

“When we meet up with the crew,” Trapper said, “we can start working on the problem from multiple angles. I’ll investigate the owners of this establishment, but they’re known for being reclusive, and tracking them down won’t be easy. On the bright side, we’ve confirmed the monster is still in the city and finally have a lead on its identity.

“For now, we need to collect our winnings and do everything in our power not to draw attention to ourselves. The Ancient is an apex predator that’s been living among us for centuries, likely peak stage-four or above. A single mistake past this point, and we’ll be dead before we know what hit us.”
