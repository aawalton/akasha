After flying to a suitable spot, the Mirabelle descended and landed in a large clearing.

Theo ran ahead of everyone else. “Master!” he shouted, slowing to a jog once the dwarven smith came into view.

Veronica and the others followed behind at a more leisurely pace.

“Master,” Theo said again with a huff. “I finally found you.”

Orban remained motionless on the rock, his face plastered with a stern expression. Unlike Theo, he didn’t look at all pleased to be reunited.

Instead, he simply growled. “I told you not to search for me, boy. Why the hell are you all the way out here? Did ya not read my note?”

Theo straightened, catching his breath. “I-I did. But I still wanted to find you anyway.”

The dwarf’s eyes sharpened, glaring at Theo as though he had just given the wrong answer to a question. “I have nothin’ left, boy. Your contract’s done. You were free ta go. You even have all the remaining vix from the auction.”

“I don’t care about the money, Master.”

Another wrong answer. Orban’s face creased further as he glared at him. “Well, I do, you idiot! That money’s enough for you to live on. But for me, it’s dead weight. It ain’t enuff for me nor the Steelhand. Coming to find me—yer wastin’ your time.”

Veronica, Gwen, and Nasha slowly approached, having finally caught up.

Orban’s harsh expression didn’t let up as he stared at them. His eyes focused directly on Veronica, the exact person who had outbid him at the auction.

“And you even went together with those damn nobles. The ones who took the starmetal right from under our noses.” He turned toward Theo, who stood nervously nearby. “Have ya no damn shame, boy? Just ’cause they look pretty, you forget how we ended up in this situation? Trustin’ and askin’ help from filthy nobles now?”

“Um, we’re not nobles,” Gwen said.

“No one asked you, girl,” Orban spat, causing Gwen to pause. “Tch. Whatever. Nobles or merchants, who cares what the lot of ya are? It don’t change nothin’.”

“Your master certainly has a spiteful tongue,” Veronica said, keeping her expression neutral, though there was a faint warning in her eyes.

He looked back toward Veronica. “The hell are you three even here for? You work for Twistwell?” He clenched his fist.

Veronica’s ears twitched. Something beneath the ground was rumbling. It wasn’t exactly sculpting magic, but it was similar. The earth and stone beneath them seemed to churn, ready to erupt at a moment’s notice.

“No. We’re not from any of the merchant groups. We came here to talk,” Veronica said, eyes narrowing. “But if it’s a fight you want, I’d be happy to give you one.”

Orban scoffed. He hopped down from the stone and swung the shield strapped across his back into his hand in one smooth motion. “Big words for a spoiled brat.”

“Small words for someone who has to glare up at me.”

“Hah. Talkin’ about bodies? Yours looks like a twig ready to snap.”

Veronica smiled, mana pouring into her hands. “I’m watching my figure. I’d be surprised if you can even see your feet.”

“Um, hey. Aren’t we supposed to be asking for his help?” Gwen whispered.

Veronica ignored her.

Nasha grabbed onto Gwen’s arm and pulled her aside. She leaned in, whispering into her ear, “Come on. Let’s back up. Veronica mentioned something like this might happen before we got off the ship.”

Gwen looked between her and Veronica with arched brows. She relented and stepped several paces back, giving the two room. Theo followed them.

Orban grinned. He reached down and unhooked the hammer strapped to his waist, gripping it in one hand. “And you’re the one who said I had the spiteful tongue.” The ground rumbled behind him, ready to explode.

Veronica’s arms rested at her sides, but her fingers were tense. Two spell circles appeared before her palms. “You going to keep talking?” She tilted her head forward slightly. “Dwarf.”

Two giant stone hands erupted from the earth behind Orban, scattering dirt and rock. “Yer brave. I’ll give ya that. I’ll even give ya the first move.” He tightened his grip on his shield. “Elf.”

With a raise of her palm, Veronica unleashed a fire spell, summoning a large orb of flame that barreled forward. Three luminous white wings flashed behind her hand as the spell tore through the space ahead.

Before it could hit, Orban swung his hammer down. The giant stone arm behind him extended overhead and slammed its fist into the ground before him. The flames struck the stone directly, whipping around the fist and licking up the curved length of the arm, but otherwise leaving him unharmed.

By then, Nasha, Gwen, and Theo had all moved far off to the side to watch the battle.

“Why do I feel like that ‘dwarf’ and ‘elf’ comment has a lot more meaning than I know?” Gwen asked Nasha.

“I’m assuming elves don’t like dwarves and vice versa,” Nasha said.

Gwen hummed. “So… why are we standing here? Shouldn’t we be helping her or something?”

Nasha crossed her arms, watching the two fight. “No. I don’t think Veronica wants us to step in. I think she just wants to fight him. She mentioned that there's a chance they’ll fight one another. I just wasn’t expecting it to be this quickly. She should beat him pretty soon.”

“Beat Master Orban?” Theo asked, looking at her in confusion. “I’m sorry, but Master Orban won’t lose. Don’t you see the ground around us?” He gestured toward the ruined earth and the mysterious black-cloaked bodies scattered nearby. “I think you should tell your friend to apologize for the insults. She might get hurt at this rate.”

Nasha and Gwen looked at one another, smiled, then turned toward Theo.

“Wanna make a bet?” they both asked at the same time.

A couple of minutes passed. The ground, already wrecked by the time they arrived, had been destroyed even further.

Orban dashed forward, swinging his forge hammer at Veronica. A blue barrier shimmered into view, blocking the attack and sending ripples across the dome-shaped shield.

“Hmph. So you’re at least Third-Tier,” Orban said.

Veronica pointed two fingers at him. “So the smith knows a bit more than just swinging a hammer against metal.”

The tip of Veronica’s finger flashed brightly. Twin trails of lightning twisted along her arm in a double helix, racing toward her fingers before she unleashed the spell.

Orban raised his shield. The lightning-infused spell slammed against the metal, rebounding and tearing up the ground around them, carving chaotic crevices through the dirt. As the spell dissipated, they both leapt away from one another.

Orban’s boots struck the ground hard. The moment they did, the earth beneath Veronica’s feet twisted.

Her eyes narrowed.

Stone hands burst from the ground on either side of her, each large enough to wrap around her entire body. Their fingers curled inward, aiming to crush her between them before she could move.

She let out a slow, controlled breath; the world responded by slowing ever so slightly.

The dwarf’s fingers twitched around the handle of his hammer. His shoulders subtly shifted back. His knees bent, and his body turned, shifting his weight toward his left side. He wasn’t going to swing his hammer from that distance. That meant he was preparing a follow-up spell.

The stone hands weren’t his real attack. They were a decoy—a cage meant to trap her.

Veronica smiled.

Instead of retreating, she stepped forward and enveloped her body in mana using Ascended Body. Mana concentrated around her feet, coalescing tightly before she launched herself forward in a sudden burst.

Orban’s eyes widened in surprise, but he quickly composed himself and shifted his stance.

A white spell circle bloomed beside Veronica as she ran. Light gathered from the air, merging into a large, immaterial lance of pure radiance.

Orban grunted and stepped forward, swinging his hammer upward. A large stone wall surged between them, but Veronica had already predicted it. She poured more power into her legs, focusing entirely on speed. She slid along the side of the wall, one hand brushing against the stone as she passed, her other arm raised to control the lance.

Orban raised his shield, bracing to block the spell.

But Veronica didn’t use it. Not yet.

Instead, she kept running and drove a straight punch into his shield. The impact knocked him off balance. Orban hit the ground hard, and when his eyes opened again, the white lance of light floated mere inches from his face.

Veronica stood above him, looking down.

For a moment, Orban only caught his breath. Then he closed his eyes, remained silent for a long moment, and finally opened them again. “Fine,” he said. “I guess we can call this a draw.”

“A draw?” Veronica scoffed. “What part of this looks like a draw to you?”

His eyes flicked upward.

Veronica followed his gaze and noticed the two giant stone fists poised above her, both ready to slam down.

She chuckled. “Nice try. But my barrier is strong enough to block them.”

Orban clicked his tongue, his bluff called. “Fine. You win this time.”

Then he muttered beneath his breath, “Damn elves.”

Gwen focused on healing Veronica and Orban’s injuries after the fight.

“Looks like we won the bet,” Nasha said, standing beside Theo.

His face fell into a frown. “Fine… a deal is a deal.”

“So?” Orban grunted, sitting on a raised stump of stone he had created. “Why’d ya come all the way out here to find me? If you were with the commerce groups, you would’ve put me in chains already.”

“We already told you,” Veronica said, flexing her fingers. Of course, Gwen had healed Veronica first and Orban second. “We aren’t from any of the commerce groups. We simply came to talk and make a deal with you.”

His lips flattened as he stared at her. “This is about the starmetal, ain’t it?”

“That’s right. Considering you wanted to bid on it, I assume you’re capable of forging it?”

“Of course I am,” he said. “But I won’t be able to do it for any of you.”

“Hey, stop being stingy,” Gwen said, hands on her hips. “You lost that fight fair and square. Don’t tell me you’re going to hold a grudge now.”

“That’s not it,” Orban said, waving her off. He sighed. “Although I can forge it for you, I won’t take a losing job.”

“What do you mean?” Veronica asked.

“I assume you know about my scuffle with three of Maftem’s biggest commerce groups?”

She nodded.

“Well, smithing is still a job. I’ve spent nearly a decade workin’ that forge. But those three damned greedy whelps snaked their way into my life, bitin’ and infectin’ me with poison little by little. Now? I got nothin’ left. Even if I take your commission, I still won’t have enough money to buy my life back. I already sold the forge, and my reputation in the city is damn near washed up. Ain’t no one trusts me anymore.”

“Then why did you bid on the starmetal?” Nasha asked. “If your career is as finished as you make it sound, why spend so much on it?”

“Because starmetal is rare. Ore like that ain’t found just anywhere. Takes years for it to show up on the market. If I bought it, I would’ve had the only supply around for years ta come. Could charge whatever price I wanted. Ain’t no one could accuse me of overchargin’. Either you buy it, or you don’t.”

“Your apprentice here mentioned something about that. So you only bid on it because you could control the price?” Veronica asked. “If someone else bought it—like me—you couldn’t justify charging such a high commission.”

“Because there are other smiths in Vitian who would do it for less, right?” Gwen chimed in. “They aren’t as desperate as you and wouldn’t charge nearly as much.”

Orban nodded. “Aye.”

“Then let’s say I can afford it,” Veronica said slowly. “How much are you charging?”

Orban looked at her sharply. “Ain’t gonna be a price you like.”

“Try me.”

He studied her for a moment before answering. “Twenty million vix. Other smiths capable of workin’ starmetal probably won’t charge ya more than six or seven million. That’s why I said you won’t like—”

“Fine. Deal.”

Orban paused, mouth open. “What?”

“I said we’ll take it. I might not be able to pay you immediately, but I can promise you’ll receive twenty million vix worth of value, either directly in coin or through a similar business arrangement.”

The dwarf’s bushy brows rose until they nearly formed a miniature mountain peak between his eyes. He looked at her as though she were insane.

After a moment, he found his words. “The hell do ya mean, a similar business arrangement?”

“How about I hire you as my personal smith? I’ll even put you in contact with a rising merchant company that’ll become Vitian’s wealthiest in the near future.”

Orban’s expression fell. His gaze dropped to the ground beneath him. “Damn… you’re just like all of them.”

“What do you mean?”

He looked up. “Don’t ya know about my feud? The only reason they’re targeting me is because they want to put me in a position where I’ll be forced to work under them, following their every beck and whim. No different from a damn slave with meager pay. Your deal ain’t nothin’ different. You’re just another greedy merchant house in my eyes.”

Veronica crossed her arms and hummed. “How about freedom to do as you wish, then? I’ll buy you out, but you can do whatever you want. You’ll only need to complete a commission for weapons or armor here and there. No more than a few times a year, most likely.”

His eyes narrowed as he studied her. “What’s the catch?”

She shrugged. “No catch.”

A few moments of silence passed. “How can I even trust those words?” He pointed at Gwen. “That girl said none of ya are nobles. How the hell am I supposed to trust you’ll pay me? Even after hearing other blacksmiths in Vitian would charge less?”

“Twenty million won’t be much in my eyes if you’re willing to wait… one year?”

“What?” he said. “Like hell it ain’t. Even the big three wouldn’t dare spend twenty million on a deal like this. It’s damn stupid. Are ya missin’ some cells in your noggin?”

“You… don’t know who I am, do you?” Veronica asked slowly.

“Am I supposed to? If ya aren’t nobles or merchants, you’re a nobody. You’re good at fightin’, but nothin’ impressive.”

Veronica pointed south, past a small line of trees where the Mirabelle was parked. “You saw that we came in an airship, right? You left the auction before the final special lots.” She turned toward him. “That thing cost twenty million vix by itself.”

He furrowed his brows, slowly coming to an understanding. A ‘nobody’ wouldn’t be casually flying around in an airship.

“You paid twenty million vix for that thing?”

“Indirectly, but yes.” Veronica smiled faintly. “Though I’m sure the person backing me might give my words a little more credibility.”

He looked toward her again. “And who’s that?”

She smiled. “An Archmage.”
