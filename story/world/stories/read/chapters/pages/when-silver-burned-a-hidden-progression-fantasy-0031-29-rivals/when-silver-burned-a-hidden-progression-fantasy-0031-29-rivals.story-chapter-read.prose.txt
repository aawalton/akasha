Swords are more than just pointy sticks. You wave them around, arms flailing like a hysterical octopus and hope the metal rod hits the thing in your general vicinity that's bothering you. Simple, in theory. Problem is, the waving around part requires a hell of a lot more finesse than you'd think at first glance. That's why, if you aim to improve said arm-flailing, you better find someone who's not afraid to show you the error of your ways with a generous dosage of blunt pain all over your body.

And some people are more generous with dealing out pain than others.

Kalsin cupped his jaw, opening and closing his mouth like a stranded fish and hoping all his teeth remained intact. Over the last few days he'd become a little more intimate with Ringtail's elbow than he'd ever meant to be.

Ringtail circled an arm as if to stretch her muscles, but the seemingly casual move didn't fool Kalsin. He scored a decent smack to her forearm with the flat of his blade that must've smarted more than his jaw. Her pain tolerance or her pride; Kalsin wasn't sure which one was the culprit.

"That one's on me. Should've seen it coming," Kalsin said over the noise of sparring recruits around them. Though it was still dark with only the faintest lines of dawn lighting up the horizon, they'd been at it for long enough to fill the open farmland with a sweaty tang.

"Why didn't you?" Ringtail said back. "I dropped the spin after you said it was, quote, 'too predictable', so you shouldn't be one to complain."

After confirming he didn't suffer any permanent dental injuries, Kalsin got back into position. "Not complaining, no, just surprised. When'd you ever take my suggestions to heart?"

"Never said I did. Maybe I was already gonna do that without your input."

Before Kalsin could point out the unlikely chances of that being a coincidence, Ringtail came at him. Her strikes blurred quickly as Kalsin parried one after another, keeping her offense at bay. They traded back and forth, pushing the other to give up a few steps of ground before being driven back. They had become familiar with each other's styles enough that, for all they knew, there could've been an unseen puppeteer hanging above their heads and directing each move and motion.

Each swipe Kalsin blocked had just a little more strength behind it, growing in concert with Ringtail's always mounting frustration. It gave her a frightening edge in terms of resilience, but made her more predictable to those with enough perception to see one step farther than the blade coming at them, and solve the puzzle that was the person wielding it instead.

In contrast, Kalsin kept his emotions locked in a far-away box as his sword whistled through the air, his face a calm surface. No intimidation, no mental edge, only the consistency of someone confident in the skill of their craft. It was a lesson life had taught him; someone with a complete lack of surge could not afford to make mistakes in a duel. Practice or no, he never fought for the thrill of it. That was not an indulgence he could afford to make.

They separated for a moment, giving Kalsin barely enough time to switch positions. He held his sword below the waist, blade lowered toward the ground in a so-called fool's guard.

As Kalsin expected, her strike came at his upper body, and he raised his sword into a hanging parry, blade aligning with his body almost vertically. Their swords connected with bone rattling force, but Kalsin already had his free hand shooting toward Ringtail's two-handed grip. As she was about to disengage from her overreached position, instead of trying to pry her blade out of her hands, Kalsin leaned in and added some extra force to her pull. Suddenly realizing her sword’s pommel was sailing at her own face a little faster than she would've liked, Ringtail twisted her body out of the way. Or tried to.

The good news was, the pommel of her sword made a glancing connection with her cheek, still hard enough to leave a mark. Bad news was that before he could call his victory, Kalsin lost his balance and bodily collapsed on top of her smaller form.

That in itself didn't cause Kalsin much grief. He tried avoiding it, but it wasn't for the first time spars ended up in a pile of tangled limbs. So as he laid on his sparring partner—who only managed a groan through her hand plastered to her face—he muttered an apology and rolled himself off her to the side. He caught his breath for a moment before he pushed himself into a sitting position, then looked over at Ringtail, hoping he didn't give her lasting brain damage.

Ringtail's shirt had ridden up, exposing a lightly muscled stomach that Kalsin might've been able to appreciate for a fraction of a second. That is, if there wasn't a rotten black line ruining the tanned landscape of her skin.

A dizzying force of nausea hit Kalsin harder than the elbow minutes ago. It was a good thing he wasn't fully upright because he felt his legs go weak, promptly flattening him back on the soft grass.

Fuck. He blinked at the recruit slowly regaining her wits next to him. Not trusting his legs, Kalsin performed another inelegant roll to put more distance between the two of them. In his state, it was a miracle his breakfast remained where it belonged, though it only did by a slim margin. He stared at the gray sky, focusing on the ringing of steel to settle his squirming insides.

Too close. He'd come way too close to disaster. If it wasn't for a single layer of fabric separating them, he'd have a third scar added to his collection right about now. And a suddenly, mysteriously Clenn recruit raising hell in a thousand mile radius. He bit back a fresh wave of sickness.

"What the hell," Ringtail spat from somewhere nearby. Roast him on a spike if Kalsin was going to look again. "I had you, I did, and then come your spontaneous bullshit again. Handgrabs, tackles. You got a sword, how hard is it to just use the damn thing!"

"Says the one who just slammed an elbow in my face not two minutes ago."

"'Least that was an honest hit, not some smoke and mirrors trick. A bait that obvious would never work on the field."

Kalsin pushed himself to a sitting position, meeting Ringtail's glare. "Seeing how it worked on a moron like you, I wouldn't bet on that."

"Who cares," she said, then gave a smile that was all teeth. "Next time I'll just come without a shirt and watch you run off crying to mommy and daddy."

He barely noticed when or how, but a moment later he was standing face to face with and equally pissed off Ringtail. He felt his body shake all over, but it didn't stop him from shoving her roughly. "That's all you can do, isn't that the truth? 'Ghost, that's cheating! Ghost, I'm so much better than you!' Brute force till you get what you want. Please, go on and rush ahead with that attitude so I can celebrate over the worthless carcass of a Scarrd who got impaled by the first sharp object in her way. Preferably right through the gut."

Something resembling uncertainty flickered through Ringtail's face, as if she didn't expect to be on the receiving end of an outburst. Probably thinking, Ghost had never spoken back to her, so why the sudden change of heart? Or maybe she was weighing her chances if things escalated past practice. One or the other, Kalsin was past the point of caring. "You know, where I come from, people have fucking standards. See this face, hm? The rot between my eyes, the one right here underneath? I could tie a cloth over them, cover them up. Sure as shit would make my life a lot easier, but I learned manners growing up, manners enough to have the decency and let people know which part of mine they should avoid."

Ringtail stared at him with visible shock. Several recruits have stopped their training around them.

"But no, you, you're above that. Why's that, I wonder. Maybe you think if people don't see that hideous thing on you, they'll treat you like a Clenn? Parade through the underworld as Scarrd defer to you, not knowing you're just as much of a human garbage as they are? Probably the reason you're here in the army, I'm thinking. Messed over a good deal of people before they found out and tried throwing you in a cell like you deserved. And those people, they had it right. They're better off without you."

Ringtail blinked once. "Fuck you," she whispered before quickly turning.

She picked up her sword and marched away.

Kalsin ran a hand through his sweat slicked hair, eyes shut as he let the early morning wind wade through him. What on earth was he doing? He lost control, that's what, and now he had hushed whispers directed at him all around. Drawing unwanted attention was the last thing he needed, especially not by insulting all and every Scarrd in earshot. Stupid, him and his mouth.

He was tired of improvising. Without a solid plan, things would careen off the road and leave him crushed under the wheels sooner or later, and soon had long passed by now. What was he doing here, going off to the end of the world only to play soldier and defend a country he didn't care for? He almost ripped off his uniform then and there, making a run toward Jack and Cinder, and to the depths with the consequences. Almost.

"Here," Fals said, holding out his dropped sword to him hilt first.

Kalsin didn't even notice her creep up on him. He needed to get his shit together. He mumbled a thank you as he slipped the weapon in his scabbard, though not before swiping the dirty blade clean on his trousers. "Enjoyed the show?" he asked.

Fals gave a so-so gesture. "Eh. Would've preferred a frothing tankard of grog to go along with it, but we take what we can get. Just be glad Brownweather likes his beauty sleep, or you'd be getting a historical dressing down. Both you and Ringtail, probably."

"Probably," Kalsin allowed. He hesitated. "Just, you know. I didn't mean all I’ve said. A Scarrd bad mouthing other Scarrd while serving in the Scarrd Division, no rationale can rightly justify that. Lost my temper. Lashed out at the easiest target. Sorry."

"Hah, you gotta try harder than that to hurt the feelings of little bold me," Fals said. "I've said my fair share of things I didn't mean, so yeah. We're good. And I get it. Phobias are twisted things just by themselves, but..." She grimaced. "Say me, for example. I'm overweight, I know it, and I still think I'm the most incredible tavern rat that has graced the world with its voice. I hate my fat, but I don't hate me for my fat, if that makes sense. And I don't hate others for theirs. That's all good and dandy the way it is."

Fals had a dreamy expression as she started chewing on a fingernail. "Now, if someone harbors full-blown hatred for a select group of people, and this is a group of people that he also belongs to… then where is the line between others and the self? The distinctions blur together. Things become jumbled. Like a tumor, something part of the person but separate from the whole." She gave Kalsin a smile, still nibbling away. "Eeeh, twisted things. Anyway, you have to be a special kind of fucked-up to exist like that. While I think you could use some lessons in tact, you're a decent bloke. No need to convince me otherwise. I don't believe a guy like you could really hate Scarrd. That, and himself alongside it."

Kalsin watched the brightening clouds for some time after she'd left.

"'Course not, Fals. Of course not."

He adjusted the sling of his scabbard and joined the dispersing crowd. They broke camp, and by the time Warmlight cast its first rays down from the sky, the recruits were already moving east.
