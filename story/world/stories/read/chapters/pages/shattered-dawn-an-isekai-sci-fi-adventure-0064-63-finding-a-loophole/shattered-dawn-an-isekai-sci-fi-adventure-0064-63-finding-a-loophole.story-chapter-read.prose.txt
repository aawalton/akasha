That evening, Elion, Keyla, and Yaret hiked back up to Boff’s lodging house. Men loaded a large, armored transport vehicle with crates of refined Thrandicite.

Among the last of Boff’s owned to return to the house, they pushed in through the back door and headed down the hall to the dining area. Elion hung back, searching for the limits of the order they’d been given.

“Come back for dinner when you’re done,” Boff had said.

Keyla and Yaret disappeared in to the dining area, and Elion walked slowly down the hall past Boff’s front office.

The door was cracked open, and he heard Boff conversing with a man. Elion peered in, and saw Prator leaning back in Boff’s chair. He’d dumped a handful of purple Cends onto Boff’s table, and was saying something about payment for increasing production.

The urge to continue on to the dining area pulled Elion away from the door. He walked down the hall.

That night Elion lay awake in his bunk. Any time someone else moved, the whole stack of bunks jiggled, so he tried to keep his own restless shifting to a minimum. The other owned in the hall had fallen silent when Boff turned out the lights, conversations cutting off abruptly. Silence ruled the room.

Elion lay in his bed for a long time, unable to fall asleep on the lumpy, uncomfortable mattress despite his exhaustion. Stacked 4 bunks high, very little space remained between Elion and the ceiling.

Keyla shifted in the bunk below him. Elion rolled, leaning his head over the edge of the bunk. Keyla’s eyes shone in the darkness.

“Hey,” Elion whispered. He didn’t want to disturb the other people sleeping nearby.

“Hey,” Keyla whispered back.

“I’m thinking about how these Bondholds work,” Elion said. “I think that we are only bound by our own understanding of the orders. Remember when I picked up the chain, but you couldn’t because you thought Bortim told you not to touch it?”

“Yeah,” Keyla said. “I remember.”

“Well I think it applies to everything. If I could convince myself that when Bortim says ‘come back here and eat dinner’ that what he really means is ‘go to the warlock’s camp and reclaim your knife,’ then I’d be able to go do it.”

“How is that working out for you?” Keyla asked.

“It’s not,” Elion said. “I tried my best to convince myself of it, but… well, here I am. We need to figure out a way to talk to one of the Aterfelians. Hol, or Ryfer, or Remy.”

“I don’t know how we’re going to do that,” Keyla said. “Maybe you can. It’s hard for me to do anything other than exactly what Boff orders me to do. I don’t even know how you’re sticking your head off the edge of your bunk right now.”

“What do you mean?” Elion asked.

“Boff told us we had to stay in the bunks all night,” Keyla said. “You’re partially out of yours.”

“Naw, I’m still in my bunk,” Elion said. “If I were to try to get down to the floor, or climb into a different bunk, then I’d be in violation, but this is fine.”

“I’m just saying that you seem to have a less strict definition of everything than I do,” she said. “You can lean out of your bunk, you can hold back from going straight to dinner as long as you’re ‘on your way,’ but that’s harder for me.”

“That’s interesting,” Elion said. “That’s what I’m saying; it matters how you interpret things.”

“Maybe I just have a stronger bond, to my bandana than you do to that pendant,” Keyla said.

“Are you trying to say I don’t care about my sister?” Elion hissed. He’d jumped through a warlock portal to come to Kylios and try to save Liora. He wasn’t about to let Keyla question his commitment.

“No, no, I just mean that my mother gave me that bandana, and I’ve been wearing it every day for more than a year, thinking about her.”

Elion thought about it, and decided that it could be true. “Possibly,” he said. “I’ve only had Liora’s pendant for a short time.”

“But you’re right,” Keyla said. “I think most of Boff’s crew works in the refinery. I’m pretty sure Hol, Ryfer, and Remy all lived in New Kairn Tol before the Breaking of the Sky. I bet they’re here because they know how to run things in the refinery.”

“Makes sense,” Elion said.

“So they probably know what happened to my mother. She was in charge of the refinery. If what Yaret said is true, about Prator going out to trap people to come work here… He probably met Mom and realized that he needed to bring her back here to help run the refinery.”

“I just can’t believe he tricked so many people,” Elion said. “It doesn’t seem like a very Aurelian thing to do.”

“They probably helped trick themselves,” Keyla said. “They’d been telling the story of Alandar’s Beacon for so long, that it made sense for New Kairn Tol to be lead by an Aurelian.”

What Keyla said made sense. “I thought you said he married her.”

“The best way to convince her he was serious about going to New Kairn Tol, I guess.”

“We need to find out what happened,” Elion said.

“We need to get away from here so we can find her.”

“Hey, you promised me you’d tell me the story of Alandar. What did he do?”

“Alandar was an Aurelian hero in the early days of civilization, just after the Cataclysm,” Keyla said. “He was among the first of Aurelia’s followers, on the Path of Dawn. In those days, incursions by Tephalians were far more common, since it was before the Celestial Sphere was constructed, to keep them away.

“At the time, the way they dealt with Tephalians was a drawn out affair. They would locate one, surround its position, and then charge in from all sides with overwhelming force. This method took a lot of coordination and effort, but more importantly, it took a lot of time. They would do a lot of damage as the surround was built up, and Tephalians were landing around Kylios at a rate faster than they could be driven out.

“So, in a time when most were afraid to be the first to confront a Tephalian, Alandar decided that he would always be first. He had developed a skill for locating the enemy, and would search out Tephalian warriors, then go charging in to battle them, alone. He was strong, and he could go toe-to-toe with a Tephalian.

“But after a particularly difficult fight with a Tephalian that left him injured and recovering for months, he realized that a new tactic was needed. The best and safest way to destroy a Tephalian warrior was still with overwhelming force, forming a circle, supporting one another, and destroying the warrior in one strong push.”

“Why?” Elion asked. “It sounds like Tephalians always come alone, and then get surrounded and overwhelmed. Why would they do that? Why not form a united front?”

“I don’t know,” Keyla said. “I’m just telling you the story I was told.”

“How long ago did this happen?”

“A couple hundred years ago I think.”

“Hmm…”

“If you’re done, I’ll continue.”

Elion lay on his back, staring up at the ceiling just in front of his face. Keyla continued the story.

“Alandar began forming a team who would take on Tephalians with him. Each member of the team would search for the hidden warriors. Most of them weren’t strong enough to fight a Tephalian alone. So when they would find one,”

“What do you mean, ‘find one’?” Elion asked. “They are good at hiding? Why would they be so hard to find, if they’re destroying things?”

“It’s not hard to know a Tephalian is in the area,” Keyla said. “They disrupt and distort things, simply through their presence. But to find their source? That is far more challenging.

“Anyways, Alandar decided to take the best parts of both strategies and join them together. He developed a new technique. When one of the members of his team would find one, they would light up a beacon. Alandar and every other member would drop whatever they were doing and come charging in as fast as possible.

“Knowing that support was on the way gave even the weakest Knight the courage to charge in when they found a Tephalian. So they would ignite the beacon, and charge. Over the next several hours, more and more Knights would rally to the beacon, bringing overwhelming force to bear on the Tephalian warrior, quickly defeating it.

“So, in time, Alandar’s Beacon became a rallying cry. A powerful symbol of hope amidst the darkness. That’s why they chose it as a symbol for New Kairn Tol.”

They lay in silence, in the darkness for a time. Elion imagined the story, rolling Keyla’s words around in his mind. He wondered if if anyone else might still be awake, listening in to them.

“That’s a good symbol,” he said. “I wish I had one of those beacons right now.”

Keyla didn’t reply.

Elion rolled over onto his back, and eventually fell asleep.
