The corridor ended twenty meters ahead of Patel. The Living Wall had sealed it, root mat and stone grown across the full width of the passage in a solid barrier of tissue that pulsed faintly with the same gold light blazing behind Ethan’s shoulders, the network closing its fist around the man who’d sent a cancer through its veins. The seal was three meters thick. CelestOS read the density without being asked and didn’t bother reporting it because the number was irrelevant. Patel wasn’t getting through.

Patel’s armor scraped the narrowing walls as he slowed from a run to a jog to a walk. The passage had tightened to three meters and was still contracting, the root mat thickening on both sides with the patient insistence of something that had all the time in the world because the man inside it had none. His left pauldron was stripped bare where the Resin shield had cannibalized it, the composite plating uneven, the armor’s silhouette lopsided. Scorch marks from the gauntlet corridor ran along his right side. Resin fragments from the shattered shield were embedded in the chest plate, dark red against matte black, the remains of his own weapon lodged in his own protection.

He reached the seal and put one gauntlet against it. The tissue didn’t respond. No veins brightened. No passage opened. The Wall had nothing for him.

He turned around. Ethan was ten meters away with the cannon aimed at his chest. The gold veins ran continuous behind him, five kilometers of blazing light stretching back to the cathedral where his son was breathing and his wife was holding the crib and the planet was broadcasting Frederick’s heartbeat through every meter of Living Wall tissue on Veslaya. The light stopped at Ethan’s position. Beyond him, toward Patel, the corridor was dark.

Patel’s helmet was still open. His face was visible. The boardroom composure that had carried him through thirty-seven dead clones and a corridor full of turrets was cracked along every line, the underlying expression showing through like structure beneath plaster. His eyes went to the cannon, the sealed passage behind him, then Ethan.

“Cross.” He found his register. Corporate calm, reassembled from pieces. “You need to think about what happens next. Celestitech doesn’t forget an asset. When I don’t report back—”

“You won’t,” Ethan said.

“—they’ll send more. Not clones. Not a retrieval team. A military operation, full scale, orbital support. Everything I brought was a scouting force. You’ve seen the beginning. What comes next—”

“I’ll build for it.”

Patel’s jaw worked. The pitch was collapsing, each word landing with less weight than the last. His right gauntlet twitched toward his hip where a sidearm would have been if the Resin shield hadn’t stripped that section of armor to feed itself. The gesture was instinct, not strategy, and it died as soon as it started. He shifted registers instead. His voice went harder, closer to the real thing beneath the polish.

“Julian is alive. He’s degrading. Every clone cycle costs the source body, and his genetic integrity is at forty-three percent. Without Celestitech’s medical infrastructure, he has two years. Maybe three. You kill me and Julian loses the only person advocating for his treatment program.” Patel’s eyes locked on Ethan’s. “You’re going to murder your brother’s only chance at survival because he’s standing in a corridor you can’t see past.”

The words landed where they were designed to land, the calculation transparent: Patel had saved Julian for last because Julian was the card no building plan or threat assessment could counter. Julian was blood.

Ethan looked at the man who’d sent thirty-seven copies of Julian through a gauntlet to die and walked through a corridor of his brother’s faces without looking down. Julian’s only chance at survival. Julian’s advocate. The man who’d appraised Julian like livestock, then fed his body into a production line and his loyalty into ammunition.

“Julian volunteered because I stole his seat,” Ethan said. “That’s what you told me. He signed the consent forms. He called it his legacy. He was enthusiastic.” He took one step forward. The gold veins brightened under his boot. “You didn’t advocate for Julian. You cashed him in. Every copy on that corridor floor is a receipt.”

Patel’s back was against the sealed wall. The root mat pulsed faintly behind him, the Living Wall’s tissue contracting with a slow rhythmic pressure that pushed him forward half a centimeter, the planet nudging him toward the man it had chosen. The walls were three meters apart and closing. The air between them smelled like copper and warm stone, the cathedral’s atmosphere leaking through five kilometers of Living Wall to fill the space where its enemy was cornered.

“You need me alive,” Patel said, his voice stripped raw. “Cross. You need what I know. About Julian, about the program, about what Celestitech has planned for this planet. I’m the only source you have. Kill me and you’re blind. You’ll have a maxed-out skill set and no intelligence on the force that’s coming for you, and they are coming, Cross, with or without my report.”

“Let them come,” Ethan said. “I’ll build for that too.”

Patel’s composure broke along the last line holding it together. His voice went thin. “Think about your son. Think about what kind of world you’re building for him if you start it with—”

“Don’t.” Ethan’s voice was quiet and the quiet was worse than shouting. “Don’t you dare mention my son. Don’t talk about what I’m building. You sent thirty-seven copies of my brother to kill my family while my wife was giving birth. You walked through a corridor of Julian’s face and you didn’t look down. You called him a product.” He took another step. Five meters. “All that time chasing me through this planet. And here you are. Cornered.”

Patel’s face changed. The last calculation ran behind his eyes and the answer came back empty. Every angle was sealed. The man standing in front of him wasn’t negotiating. He was finishing.

Patel opened his mouth to say something else. Ethan didn’t wait to hear it. He fired.

The cannon thumped once. The Resin sphere crossed ten meters of dark corridor in less than a heartbeat and hit Patel’s chest plate at the point where the shield had stripped the composite, the weakest section of armor on his body, the section Ethan had been aiming at since he’d raised the weapon. The Resin hit the compromised plating and did what Resin did to everything it touched that wasn’t strong enough to hold it.

The composite destabilized. Cracks spread outward from the impact point in a web of dark red lines, the molecular structure failing in layers, each layer curling back from the one beneath it. The process took two seconds. Patel watched it happen. His hands came up to his chest plate as if he could hold the armor together with his fingers and the armor came apart under his palms and the Resin ate through the last layer and reached the man inside.

In the final second, Patel looked up from his failing armor and looked at Ethan. Whatever he saw in that last moment stayed on his face as his knees buckled. His gauntlets hit the stone. His body folded forward and the ruined chest plate struck the corridor floor with the sound of something heavy and expensive landing on ancient rock.

The gold veins behind Ethan pulsed once, hard, and held. Five kilometers of light carrying his son’s heartbeat through the dark. The corridor was quiet. Patel was on the floor and the fight was over and Ethan’s hands were steady on the cannon and for two full seconds he believed it.

CelestOS: Hostile neutralized. Threat assessment: zero. Logging defensive action under planetary authority—

The log entry stopped.

CelestOS didn’t stop mid-sentence. In eighteen months of sardonic commentary and clinical updates and corporate-grade sarcasm, she had never cut herself off in the middle of a word. Ethan’s hand tightened on the cannon.

“Cel?”

CelestOS: Don’t move.

Patel’s body twitched, and the twitch was structural. His left arm locked straight at the elbow with a sound like a ratchet engaging. His right hand clenched against the stone and didn’t open. His spine arched backward in a slow, deliberate curve that lifted his ruined chest plate toward the ceiling, and the dark red cracks in the composite began to glow.

CelestOS: Entropic signature detected. Active and expanding. The Resin fragments embedded in his armor. They were never inert. He was carrying a live culture in his chest plate this entire time. The sphere destabilized the containment. It’s loose.

The cracks spread. What had been structural failure became something else, something that pulsed with its own rhythm, a heartbeat that didn’t belong to Patel because Patel’s heartbeat had stopped four seconds ago. The Resin he’d harvested and weaponized and worn against his skin was waking up inside the armor that had contained it, and the armor was the only thing left holding a shape.

Ethan raised the cannon for a second shot. The weapon hummed, cycling.

Patel’s head came up. The eyes were open. They weren’t Patel’s anymore. The irises had gone the color of raw garnet, deep red light boiling behind them, the same light that had burned in the clones’ sockets but concentrated, older, fed on months of harvested Resin instead of days of forced growth. The mouth opened and the sound that came out was not a word. It was pressure venting through a throat that was being restructured from the inside.

The composite armor began to move. The plates shifted across Patel’s torso like tectonic plates, pushed from beneath by something growing underneath, red tendrils threading through the seams and locking the pieces into new configurations that had nothing to do with how the armor had been designed. The left pauldron, the one stripped bare by the shield, erupted in a spray of dark vapor. Resin filaments burst from the exposed joint, whipping outward, testing the air, tasting the corridor walls. Two of them found the Living Wall’s tissue and the tissue recoiled, gold veins dimming where the tendrils touched.

CelestOS: Ethan. The host transformation is active. This is not Reyes. Reyes was involuntary infection over hours. Patel cultivated this deliberately. He harvested the Resin, wore it, integrated it into his combat systems. The culture has been feeding on his biosignature for months. Whatever comes out of that armor will be an order of magnitude beyond anything we’ve encountered.

The cannon finished cycling. Ethan fired.

The sphere hit Patel’s chest dead center and the armor absorbed the impact and the Resin inside the armor absorbed the Resin in the sphere and the glow intensified, the red cracks flaring bright enough to throw shadows on the corridor walls. The shot had fed it. The weapon Ethan had built to kill Patel was feeding the thing that was eating Patel from the inside out.

CelestOS: Negative effect. The entropic culture is metabolizing the Resin projectiles as fuel. Cease fire immediately. You are accelerating the transformation.

Ethan lowered the weapon. His best gun was useless. Worse than useless. Every shot he’d built, every round he’d fabricated, every hour of production he’d poured into the Resin cannon was now ammunition for the thing on the corridor floor in front of him.

And it was getting off the floor. The legs straightened with mechanical precision, the Resin tendrils inside the suit inflating like hydraulic lines, pressurizing, forcing the frame upright with an efficiency that suggested the culture had been rehearsing this for months. Waiting for the containment to break. The composite plates groaned and separated along new fault lines, revealing gaps packed with wet, glistening tissue that hadn’t existed thirty seconds ago. The armor was becoming an exoskeleton. The Resin was becoming the body.

A sound rose from the thing’s chest. Low and wet, with its own pulse. A heartbeat. Faster than human. Getting faster.

The sealed corridor pulsed gold behind Ethan’s shoulders. Five kilometers of Living Wall carrying his son’s heartbeat in one direction. And ten meters ahead, a second heartbeat starting, red and wrong and accelerating, pulling in the other.

The thing that had been Patel turned its head toward Ethan. The garnet eyes locked on. The mouth opened wider than a human mouth should open and the jaw kept going, the mandible splitting along a seam that ran from chin to throat, the bone separating like a hinge forced past its design limit. From the opening something emerged. A stalk. Segmented. Barbed at the tip. Slick with dark Resin that steamed where it dripped on stone.

It tasted the air. It swung toward Ethan, past Ethan, toward the gold light stretching behind him. Toward the cathedral.

Toward Frederick.

The stalk held that orientation for two full seconds, locked on the signal of a newborn’s heartbeat broadcasting through five kilometers of planetary nervous system, and the red pulse in the thing’s chest accelerated to match.

CelestOS: It knows he’s there.

The thing took its first step. The stone cracked under the weight of composite and Resin and whatever was left of Patel, fused into something heavier than all of them combined, something the corridor hadn’t been built to hold. The gold veins in the floor beneath it went dark. The root mat peeled away from its boots, curling back from every step. The Living Wall was pulling away from the thing wearing Patel’s armor.

Ethan stood between it and the light. The cannon was dead weight in his hands. Five kilometers of corridor behind him and his family at the end of it and something wearing his enemy’s body taking its second step toward both.
