Judging by the way that the demon's gills flared, Nathan had to hazard a guess that the demon had an inkling of what his words meant, and it definitely wasn’t pleased by what it had heard. And if Nathan was being honest, he’d have to say that it looked very offended, and it was now up to him to deliver his retribution to it.

“Well, don’t just stand there,” Nathan said, waving his sword in the direction of the demon. “Come and try your luck against the king.”

Needless to say, his taunts were unneeded as the demon was already headed straight for him. The ground shook as the fish-faced demon stomped towards a waiting Nathan, its gills flaring in a way that made its feelings clear.

As far as Nathan was concerned, that was all inconsequential to him. The demon lacked even a spark of an aura, which made Nathan feel less threatened by it, although he had to admit that his inability to scan the monster’s level successfully still weighed on the back of his mind.

The easiest explanation was that the monster was over level 40, and in that case, it was not surprising that he was way below what exactly he would’ve liked to fight.

That was something that he hadn’t assumed he’d have to think too much about, and even if the monster was higher levelled than him, Nathan wasn’t going to run.

Either way, this would be a hard-fought battle, compared to his fight with the harpies, and a hard battle was something that Nathan could get behind.

So, he readied himself, gauging and watching the monster's movements as it closed in on him, and even then, he barely managed to sidestep the demon's opening attack. His face morphed into a wince as the claws of the Xharnton tore into his side, tearing into his flesh.

Having claws tear apart my body seems to be the agenda for the day. Despite the wince, Nathan was far from being worried; if anything, his heart raced at the thrill of an actual battle against a foe that was close to him in power level, compared to his fight against the harpies and the demon prince.

Fuck ending this quickly, I’m definitely going to savor this. A bloodthirsty grin wiped away Nathan's wince as he repositioned for a swift counter.

The endless darkness of oblivion blurred as Nathan caught his footing, the black blade slicing at the Xharnton's exposed chest, but it sank only two inches before all the momentum behind his swing disappeared, and Nathan took that as his cue to pull back his weapon.

The berserker bounced on the balls of his feet, moving backwards just in time to avoid a wild swing at his neck. Whoa, that was close.

Constantly fighting foes that were far beneath his level had made him sloppy. However, his reaction time had improved slightly since the first exchange with the Xharnton. To some, small improvements like that might seem irrelevant, but to those at the level of Nathan, the Xharnton, and the demon prince, a slight improvement was deadly.

And that was why he had to be sharp as ever, and for that reason, he fully tuned out the constant thrumming of oblivion at the back of his head. Even through the thrumming, he could clearly understand what of the blade was begging for souls to feed on.

More than once, Nathan had considered switching to King's Wrath, even if just to silence oblivion, but he refrained from doing so. If anything, it made sense that the sword would be so active as he faced the Xharnton, given that oblivion was so effective against demons.

Nathan put a lid on his thoughts as he focused on the Xharnton. Both he and the demon had sustained wounds from the opening minutes of the fight. Nothing fatal at the moment, but Nathan knew that the longer this fight wore on, the more likely he’d sustain a serious injury.

And there’s no telling what demon I’m going to fight after this one, so it’d do me some good to avoid getting badly injured. Even though Nathan felt that he was close to his next level, he knew that he couldn’t afford to gamble on the fact that he’d level up after killing this monster, and if that was the case, then he’d have to be smart.

His priorities in check, Nathan moved. His blade went up in a deadly arc as it flashed towards the Xharnton's neck, only to be blocked by the monster, which crossed its elbows in an ‘X’ format as it successfully blocked his attack.

Nathan snickered as he pushed his blade against the demon's forearms, pressing the attack and watching with satisfaction as oblivion slowly bit into the demon's forearms. Seeing progress on that front, the berserker put his weight behind the soul-sucking blade.

A pained scream left the fish's lips of his opponent, but Nathan ignored it, content to try to cut through the demon’s forearm, but the demon seemed to realize what would happen to its forearms if it kept trying to win the battle of strength.

With another screech, it hopped back, receiving a slash on its left thigh for its decision. A small price from its perspective to escape Nathan's onslaught, but its breather was short-lived, as Nathan quickly closed the distance Xharnton had just created, unwilling to let his advantage slip from his grip.

Unfortunately for Nathan, his opponent was equally competent. The Xharnton dug its heels in as it prepared to meet Nathan’s charge.

The berserker had chosen a deceptive overhead strike as the move to continue their clash, and the Xharnton stood still even as oblivion arched downward towards its skull. It was a sight that unnerved Nathan, and his instincts screamed at him to abandon the strike.

Nathan was of two minds on what to do. As far as he was concerned, this was the cleanest strike he could hope for, and maybe he was just overthinking things. Even if the demon had some sort of trick under its skin, oblivion was already two-thirds of the way to the Xharnton’s skull, and Nathan was ninety percent sure that he could kill it before it had a chance to do whatever it could have planned.

In that split second, his eyes and his instinct warred, and in the end, Nathan chose his instinct as he pivoted mid-attack. His hips protested the abrupt movement, but the berserker persevered.

His split-second decision turned out to be in his favour as the demon spat out green slime towards the position that Nathan would’ve been a breath ago, and the berserker could only watch in horror as the ground sizzled wherever the green slime fell. Acid!

Nathan couldn’t help but squeeze his face at the realization of what the demon had spat out, and, if he was being honest, he wasn't quite sure what to think of the green slime sizzling in front of him.

He wasn’t even sure that his constitution, even as high as it was, could handle the slimy acid hitting his exposed body. Besides that, there was no telling if the healing he got from levelling up would heal that.

I hate variables. The introduction of the slimy acid by the Xharnton had thrown a wrench in Nathan's skeletal plan, and now he couldn't help but wonder how exactly he was supposed to fight a monster like this up close. And even if he already had it at the back of his head, he knew that he had to be extra careful.

“You sly demon, you almost had me for a second there,” Nathan chuckled, increasing the distance between him and the demon.

Nathan focused on the Xharnton as he began to put together a plan to take the demon down. He considered the information he’d gleaned from fighting the monster so far. It spits acid, its skin is thick and hardened, but oblivion cut into it.

There was also the fact that the Xharnton was fast, which was surprising for a monster of its size. Those three were already signs that this monster wasn't one to mess with.

Well, I’ve battled worse creatures, power-wise, in my early days, so this shouldn’t be a problem. Nathan still had to fight the demon prince in the future, and that demon was on a whole other level compared to the Xharnton.

It already been fifty minutes since they’d started this dance of death, and yet, neither party had delivered a serious hit to the other, so by all accounts, both sides were still fresh, and that meant that this fight was far from reaching its end. Unless one of the two could come up with something special, the fight would drag on, but at this moment, Nathan was betting on himself to pull through.

He had way too much at stake to fail here. My life and hers.

Nathan refused to believe that the priestess wasn’t still alive, regardless of the probability of that being false. He couldn’t abandon her, not now. Besides, he and the demon prince had personal scores to settle, and all Nathan had to do was just get past the Xharnton. And discover my aura.

Tristan had told him he had to figure out how to get his own aura, even to stand a chance of fighting the demon prince. Because from the onset, it was clear that the demon prince massively outclassed him to the point that even getting an aura wouldn’t close the significant power gap, but it’d definitely help. As far as Nathan was concerned, this monster was the first real step toward getting his aura and fixing his sore shoulders.

With an exhale, he cleared his mind, tightened his grip on oblivion, and charged like a bullet toward his foe. His footsteps were thunderous on the narrow path the maze offered.

The black blade in his right hand was nothing but a blur as Nathan began his relentless onslaught on the Xharnton.

An errant forearm deflected a wild slash to the demon's side. His lightning-quick follow-up punch also failed to reach its intended target as the Xharnton side-stepped the attack. Nathan gritted his teeth in frustration as the Xharnton evaded attack after attack.

The monster seemed to prefer letting Nathan tire himself out before it would launch an attack of its own, and Nathan had to admit that it was a good tactic against any other foe, but Nathan wasn’t any other foe.

He’d bathed in the blood of many monsters; he’d punched, slashed, and diced monsters of different species. He’d fought monsters way stronger than he was, and even when he was on his last legs, he’d never given up, and he wasn’t about to start now.

If anything, the monster was playing with fire, and Nathan just needed one good strike with oblivion for the demon to have its soul shredded into nothingness, so Nathan fired on rather than pulling back.

Oblivion moved in dizzying patterns as he pressed his attack, much to the surprise of the Xharnton, who’d expected him to take a breather. But Nathan wasn’t a quitter. He always worked hard, regardless of the task at hand. And even then, the rewards had been meager compared to now, when the evidence of his hard work was easily visible.

Nathan hadn’t asked for the apocalypse, but he’d made a promise back at his cabin that he’d get as strong as possible. Demons, snakes, spiders, bunnies, it didn’t matter to the blood berserker. His resolve was stronger than tungsten, and if he kept up the pressure, he wouldn’t fail. Not now, not ever.
