“Is it abnormal?” I asked, jogging to her side.

“Not necessarily. No,” she paused, “but most of the time the fourth floor is buzzing with life; it’s definitely not this quiet often,” she said.

Mabel’s words made sense. There were even fewer monsters compared to the third floor… which made little sense. I mean, the monster spawn rate is supposed to increase the deeper we go.

“Hmm…” I murmured. “There might be a dungeon boss that had broken through his floor and eaten all the monsters!?” I added, half serious, half joking.

“Dungeon boss, huh?” Mabel repeated.

Suddenly she turned her head toward me, her blue eyes locking onto mine.

“That could be true!” she exclaimed, her eyes widened. “I mean, not the dungeon boss part, of course; only an idiot would think that a dungeon boss would move to another floor.”

“I was joking!” She was totally taking the chance to call me an idiot!

“But the part about a strong monster? That could totally be true.” So, she decided to ignore me.

“You know what, Arnos, a Minotaur escaped onto the third floor recently.”

“Wait, really!” I widened my eyes, feigning ignorance. “How could that be? Floor three! I didn’t see any Minotaur when I was camping in the bush.”

“Well… good thing you didn’t. According to the adventurers at the guild, it was undergoing Mad Rush. It even broke the gate between the second and third floors!”

“Wait, really!”

“Yeah… but if he’s waiting for us, say, near the gate to the fifth floor, it would be awesome! I’d easily kill it.”

I mean, if I could kill a Minotaur, I was pretty sure Mabel could kill one too. Though her words made me curious.

“Which attack would you use?” I questioned my eyes turning to the front.

The corridor we walked through this time was certainly longer; by now we had covered half its distance. My eyes looked at the turning. It was another single turn to the right, though more light seeped in from that corridor.

Bright orange light. I wonder what kind of grass produced such illumination.

“What do you mean?” Mabel asked, her question breaking my concentration.

“I glanced at her. Like, let’s say you were to hypothetically meet one Minotaur, what attack would you use on it?”

“Depends…”

Her answer was ambiguous.

“On what?”

“Whether it’s undergoing Mad Rush or not. If it is, I’d probably choose giant Lance—a spear perhaps,” Mabel answered casually, looking straight ahead.

Then, she looked into my eyes.

“If it’s not undergoing one, I’d probably use Cryo night, and freeze its internal body after which I’d use a lance to shatter its body.”

Her answer made sense, using lance to kill a mindless beast, and blitzing the tamer one and freezing it completely. Wait… is Lance needed after she has used Cryo Night? Isn’t that her strongest spell? I wondered.

Not wanting to stay clueless, I asked, “So you’d use Lance, regardless?”

“Yeah,” Mabel nodded.

“Why?” I pressed.

“It looks cool! Imagine it—a humongous Minotaur shattering into many pieces of ice… it’s amazing! It looks like snowfall, but prettier!” she exclaimed.

I turned my gaze away. Of course she uses Lance because it looks cooler.

The corridor was now just a few steps away; the orange light looked brighter and brighter as we approached closer.

“And honestly… despite all the increase in attack power, the one undergoing Mad Rush is dumber, easier to hit with Lance. The normal one might dodge it during its charge; it also might have a weapon—” Mabel suddenly paused as we turned to the right.

My eyes widened, senses tightened. In front of us stood five goblins, two small ones with scrawny bodies, two perhaps as big as us, evolved goblins (hobgoblins).

And in the centre stood a large imposing creature, which I presumed was the goblin commander.

His muscles were well toned, his body bulky, eyes glowing green, and a large unnerving grin was on his face. He was holding a fire torch in his hand, his bloodied hand.

Bloodied hand, not injured, it had probably killed someone and claimed their torch as its own.

[Khekhikhi] the goblin commander laughed, as if it found amusement, as if we had walked into its trap.

The two smaller goblins lunged forward, while the hobgoblin laughed beside their commander. Did they think we were weak because we were small? I mean… perhaps they did.

Unless I am mistaken, that is.

I raised my right arm. Goblins can’t really sense magic.

“Lance—!” Mabel yelled, or technically was about to yell.

But I interrupted her.

No, I didn’t have a plan, nor did I want to interrupt. I just shouted.

“FIREBALL.” Since I was terrified, a large ball of flame covering half the area of the labyrinth emerged from the palm of my hand; it was hot, hot enough for Mabel to take a step back.

Didn’t affect me much though, I was used to my flame.

The ball of fire… or perhaps the boulder of fire, surged forward, tearing through the dungeon’s floor as it moved.

[Arghh] the goblins caught in the flame yelled, so did the hobgoblin and the commander as the fireball pushed them to the end of the corridor before finally dispersing.

“You use this to fight numbats?” Mabel asked, blinking. The smoke from the fireball entered my nose. The smell of burnt flesh was in the air.

“I don’t do it, no. Numbats aren’t dumb enough to fly in groups; also, large fireballs are tough to hide,” I replied flatly.

“Is that so?” Mabel added, turning her head to the surviving goblins.

The smoke from the giant fireball settled. The skeletons of the two goblins that charged us lay in front. I turned my gaze up, looking at the other goblins.

The commander still stood; he held the hobgoblin by their throat, perhaps using them to take the blunt of the force. “Firebal—” I was about to cast another spell when the commander threw a hobgoblin in my direction. It came crashing down where I stood.

“Quick-step!” I yelled, moving back, barely avoiding the throw.

[You have gained 4 credits for slaying a hobgoblin]

[Your total credits are now - 96/150]

“I’ll take him on,” Mabel said, her left hand pointing downward as if she was telling me to stay out of it. Raising her wand, “Lance,” she murmured.

The goblin commander grunted; his teeth brought the unconscious body of the hobgoblin in front as he backstepped.

I looked at her, my eyebrows raised. She said, Lance, but no projection appeared… then she vanished all of a sudden. It was a bluff!

I turned my head to the commander. Behind him, she stood, her wand lunged forward; her movement was sharp, and the wand too fast to see.

She had used Quick-Step spell.

“Arghhhhh,” the commander yelled in pain, releasing the body of the hobgoblin in the process. The commander elbowed backward to attack Mabel… or at least attempted to.

“Cryo Night,” Mabel mumbled, a smile curling on her lips. Instantly, the commander froze—his hand suspended mid-swing. Within seconds, the green of his skin frosted over, icy deposits spreading across his bulk. His beady eyes glazed white, lifeless. No sound escaped, even his throat had frozen solid.

The monster’s body was like a statue, and then a crack appeared, followed by another.

Its body broke into smaller shards with a sharp [Crackle]. The frozen leg wasn’t going to cut it; the torso of the monster split from the main body and fell forward, breaking into smaller pieces in the next moment.

Red dust emerged from the ground with it, covering everything in a metre radius.

I glanced at Mabel. She had vanished from her place; a stream of icy air grazed my ears, and I glanced beside. Mabel had placed a hand over her nose and gestured for me to come with her.

The last hobgoblin had died.

I took a breath, then nodded. “Achoo—” I sneezed. “Damn, it’s cold;” A tingle ran down my spine.

“Don’t breathe!” Mabel yelled.

With her words, I placed my hand on my nose, clutching it shut just like hers, and walked behind quietly. I wanted to breathe; my lung capacity wasn’t that great, but I fought through.

“Ahhhh,” I sighed, taking a large breath finally as we moved back to where we had walked in from.

“Phew, that was close,” Mabel murmured, with a relaxed smile spreading across her face.

“How was it close? The fight seemed pretty one-sided to me,” I said, my brows raised in confusion.

“The Goblin commander’s body isn’t strong enough to resist shattering. And his blood is too weak; when it shatters, it forms red icy dust—breathe it a little and it will cause you a sore throat.”

Sore throat, huh? That’s not too bad.

“Too much, you die,” Mabel added nonchalantly, eyes on the ceiling as she pressed her hands together in a quick, mocking prayer.

“That was dangerous!” I yelled. “Shouldn’t you have told me sooner?” My eyes widened. “I didn’t breathe too much, did I?” I asked outloud, looking down at my chest.

I felt her hand on my shoulder, my eyes turned to her.

“Do not fret, I saved you…” she said with a calm, angelic smile only an enlightened fellow may have.

However, she lacks enlightenment.

“You don’t have to thank me, okay; I would’ve done the same for anyone else,” Mabel added, as she turned back and began walking.

I wasn’t going to thank her, though; that thought didn’t even cross my mind.

“What are we going to do now? Don’t we have to get goblin flesh to use as lure?” I asked.

“Hmm, we would,” Mabel paused. She took a moment to think.

She glanced at me as if she had an idea; her face filled with excitement, then turned away with a somber expression.

“I don’t know, she murmured. I should’ve just used Lance, I just… ahh, sorry.”

I knew what she was about to say, maybe something about how she thought it would look cool or something. Can’t blame her though; if I were in her shoes— I glanced at her feet, my eyes squinted—heels, I corrected myself, I might’ve done something like that too.

“I should not have used that massive fireball too,” I said with a small understanding smile. “I am the one at fault.”

Still smiling, Mabel looked at me, then turned her eyes away.

“Yeah, right,” Mabel nodded sincerely.

“If we look at the number of goblin corpses destroyed, you are the one at fault,” she added, nodding.

“Yeah, yeah. Let’s change our plan. I’ll be the lure and you will be the attack? No… she shook her head. Attacker!”
