‘Typha!’ I shouted internally, whirling around just in time to see her desperate face as the stone wall slammed shut. My heart dropped. Had she just been crushed?!

That was pretty damn hard to do after seeing one of our own die right in front of our eyes! My kneecaps were shaking!

“Ah, quite a diversified bunch, an unusual spectacle,” a strange voice boomed, my eyes suddenly turning to the open room before us. There was another area with torches, a circular chamber with multiple doors—it was vast, still, and eerily quiet, except for that talking statue right in front of us.

He looked like a god, posed like one, too. The statue was carved from some kind of iridescent stone that seemed to shift colors as the torchlight played across its surface. It depicted a man with six arms, each hand holding an orb with a different elemental symbol. He had a long robe on, long hair, and sculpted eyes. His face was serene yet intimidating, and already I knew that the statue was no other than King Salzer.

“I am King Salzer, Guardian of the Elemental Nexus,” the statue continued, its mouth not moving despite the voice clearly emanating from it. “Or rather, an echo of him. You have entered my domain unbidden, unprepared, and unafraid of the trials that await you. As a master of the six elements, allow me to inform you that your worth shall be judged. You may advance—if you prove yourself capable of understanding the true nature of balance….”

The stone eyes of the statue flashed with six different colors, cycling through them rapidly before settling on a faint green hue.

“Earth—your first trial.” Suddenly, the ceiling of the area began to descend in circular layers, until the center of it pointed downward a few meters. It opened up to a block of stone with someone trapped in it!

‘Typha!’

She was suspended upside down as she squirmed and writhed, trying to break free. Just when I thought I saw the worst of it, the center of the floor began to open up in two, and a platform of large-sized stone spikes opened up in the ring.

“Help me! Please!” she begged. Lun was on it, but as he swung his scepter upward, the glow in his gem faded.

“What the?”

“Before you are six doors,” the statue said, ignoring our efforts in saving our party member. “Each door represents an elemental challenge. Solve the earth element trial, and your companion shall be spared. Fail, and….” The statue’s eyes flashed menacingly. “Well, I believe her current position speaks for itself.”

I examined the six doors arranged in a perfect circle around us. Each door had a distinct symbol—a flame, a droplet, a mountain, a swirling gust, a radiant sun, and a crescent moon.

“Good luck,” he said, his voice fading before the light in his eyes went dark again.

I thought that this guy was a benevolent king?

“Stop standing there and help me!” Typha shouted down at us.

“If it hasn’t been clear to you already, we can’t,” Nolan retorted. “There is no bypassing the challenge.”

“Nolan is right, unfortunately,” Lun said as he flicked the crown of his scepter with his finger. “The magic in this chamber prohibits this loophole.”

“Then how are you so sure it’d work in the trial?!” Typha cried.

“Tier five dungeons are rigged, but not that rigged,” Nolan reassured her. “Hang tight. We’ll finish the trial and come back for you.”

“As if I have a choice!”

“Should we leave Zaiden here with her?” Lun suggested, and Typha was quick to reject the idea.

“No! There could be more traps!” she cried. “I don’t want him here where no one could help him!”

“I hear you loud and clear,” Lun said as he looked up to Typha. “But we are in a tier five dungeon. Nolan would be seriously disadvantaged if he—”

“You’re not hearing me!” she shouted. “Don’t leave him behind. Promise me….” She was serious, the tremor in her voice giving me chills.

“Come on,” Nolan said, leading the party to the door with the mountain symbol on it.

The moment we stepped through the mountain-inscribed door, the world around us transformed. Instead of a confined chamber, we found ourselves standing at the edge of a vast valley, surrounded by jagged peaks that stretched impossibly high into a white cloudy sky. The mountains weren’t just tall—they were colossal, their summits vanishing into swirling clouds.

‘Whoa,’ I breathed through our bond. ‘Are we still in the dungeon?’

“Elemental pocket dimension,” Lun explained, his eyes wide with scholarly excitement. “Fascinating! The dungeon creates miniature realms for each trial, separate from the main chamber but still connected to it.”

‘This place is massive.’ I marveled at the depth of the landscape before us. Deep ravines cut through the earth, and massive boulders were scattered across the valley floor like toys discarded by a giant child. Speaking of giants….

What appeared to be solid rocks moments before were now transforming. The ground shook as loose scree elevated in the air and fused with these rocks that began to take on humanoid forms. We tried to distance ourselves by turning back, but there were two problems with that. One, the door we came from disappeared, and two, we ran into an invisible jelly-like barricade that bounced us back into an unavoidable fight.

I turned back around and noticed three massive stone golems, easily five times our height. They had blank faces, broad layered shoulders, and various weapons for upper limbs: a mallet, a sword, and a flail. Their other arm had fingers, but fewer than the standard five, and their bodies were built crafted from the same rust-colored rock as the mountains.

“The welcoming committee doesn’t look too friendly,” Lun muttered.

I checked their levels, and came to a terrifying realization:

As to be expected from a tier 5 dungeon, but being here in the moment, experiencing it, felt like something else entirely. It humbled me real quick, making me realize how small I really was in this world. Dragons might be powerful, but I was still a baby among titans.

“Freya, you need to focus,” Nolan’s voice cut through my panic. “Remember what Lun said—you’re an elemental conduit. This is the earth trial. You need to connect with the weakness of that element. In your case, that’s your acid breath. Faster erosion process.”

“Acid breath?” Lun repeated. “A breath weapon, yes. That could work.” Lun turned to Nolan. “I need you to distract them while I conjure an amplification spell. I will bubble our dear unconscious friend here while you work your magic, gunslinger.”

I swear I saw a sweat drop along Nolan’s temple! Distract three high-leveled stone golems with a gun, a sword, and a whip?!

Nevertheless, he didn’t hesitate. Before the stone golems could march closer to us from over the horizon, Nolan placed Zaiden down and Lun encased him in a protective Magneto bubble.

“You better make this amplifying magic fast,” Nolan chimed as he pulled out Scarlet and approached the rock-headed trio. “There’s only so much ammo I can use for one trial.”

Lun grinned as he waved his scepter over my body. “Don’t worry, hotshot. I’ll try my best.”

Nolan played it safe. The range game was our best option. With the scrolls he stole from Zev’s private collection room, I expected him to have more ways to subdue these monsters while Lun ramped up his scepter. Sure enough, he did. He aimed the barrel of his shotgun right before their feet. The first golem—the one with the mallet—swung its massive weapon before sending it crashing into the ground. The impact caused a massive fissure that ran its course toward Nolan, but the gunslinger continued to point Scarlet in the same position.

I began to worry, not understanding what his game plan was. But I should have known by now that this one-eyed mercenary was filled with surprises. The tats on his arms glowed, and just before the fissure swallowed him up, he fired his shot. A pulse dispersed in the air, its radius massive for one shot. Before I knew it, the fissure was gone, and so were the three golems!

‘What the heck?!’

Nolan lifted his gun as smoke continued to escape the barrel. He inspected it with a, “Huh, that worked out better than I expected.”

Lun snorted. “Time Rewind Skill Scroll, adaptable weapon variety. Neat trick.”

So what? The golems were g—oh, I spoke too soon. They were materializing from the rocks again!

“Almost done here,” Lun whispered as he continued to rotate his scepter over me.

The scepter began to hum, its crystal crown pulsing with a translucent blue light that wrapped around my scales like liquid moonlight. I felt a strange tingling sensation in my throat, as if something dormant was awakening.

“There!” Lun exclaimed, stepping back with satisfaction. “The lunar essence in my scepter should magnify your natural abilities tenfold. Now, young dragon, show these stone behemoths what real power looks like!”

I stepped forward, my chest swelling with new energy. The connection between Lun’s magic and my draconic nature felt like a circuit completing as electricity ran through my veins. The golems lumbered toward us again, their massive bodies shaking the ground with each step. I could feel something different in my throat—a burning, acidic sensation building up like pressure in a sealed container.

“Any time now,” Nolan called out, rolling to avoid the sword-armed golem’s downward slash. The blade cut into the earth where he’d stood moments before, sending chunks of rock flying.

I opened my jaws and released a stream of vibrant green acid that looked different than my normal breath—more concentrated, more potent. The stream arced through the air and splashed across the chest of the mallet-wielding golem. The effect was immediate and dramatic—the acid hissed and bubbled, eating through the stone like it was butter, creating deep pockmarks that spread quickly.

The golem roared—a sound like grinding boulders—and staggered backward, its mallet arm disintegrating where the acid had made contact. Chunks of dissolving rock fell away, revealing hollow cavities within.

‘It’s working!’ I mentally shouted to Nolan through our bond. But my celebration was short-lived as the sword-golem charged toward me, surprisingly agile for something so massive.

“Watch out!” Nolan shouted, firing three quick ice shots at the golem’s knees, causing it to stumble but not fall.

I dodged right as the sword came down, the impact sending tremors through the ground. Quickly pivoting, I unleashed another stream of acid, this time aiming for the joints where the golem's limbs connected to its torso.

The acid struck true, sizzling as it melted through the stone joints. The sword-golem’s arm dangled for a moment before separating entirely, crashing to the ground with a hard impact that sent cracks spider-webbing across the valley floor.

“That’s it!” Lun exclaimed. “Target their structural weaknesses!”

The third golem—the one with the flail—swung its weapon high and wide. The spiked ball whistled through the air as it gained momentum, and as it swung in a circle, that whistling noise grew louder and louder until—WHAM!

I steadied myself and focused on the flail-golem’s center mass, ignoring Nolan’s barking at me as he ordered me to quit my shenanigans. With a deep breath, I summoned another acid stream, more concentrated than before. The viscous liquid splashed across the golem’s chest and began eating away at its core. The monster stumbled backward, its movements becoming erratic as the acid penetrated deeper.

The flail-golem roared in frustration, its movements growing more desperate as the acid continued to dissolve its stone body from the inside out. Chunks of corroded rock fell away, revealing a glowing amber core pulsing within its chest cavity.
