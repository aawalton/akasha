The Adventurer’s Guild was packed.

It was a much larger building than the one in Ronswick, roughly twice the size. The floor was made of polished dark wood, while the walls were built from stone. Two sets of stairs along the sides of the building led up to a second-floor balcony. Several stone pillars stood beneath it, separating the guild clerks from the rest of the hall while supporting the balcony above.

Voices filled the space from every direction. Adventurers, both solo and in parties, crowded around tables, counters, and request boards. From what Veronica could see, even the second floor had filled tables. People sat and moved about, some drinking, others eating. It was a place full of arguments, but also laughter. It felt more like a tavern than a guild.

Auction season had certainly gathered a large number of adventurers, all vying for money or prestige, hoping to connect with the various influential figures who had arrived in the city. Noble houses could always use strong fighters, whether as personal guards or commissioned adventurers sworn to their service.

In the past, even Veronica had received requests from various mercenaries, escorts, guards, and hunters, all seeking to work under her. Of course, she had never needed people like that.

Veronica ignored the clamor and walked along the side of the hall, navigating through the crowd.

Naturally, there were far more adventurers clustered near the lower-ranked boards.

Most of them looked new. Young swordsmen with poorly fitted armor. Spearmen with nervous eyes. Mages carrying staves that looked more expensive than their robes. A few archers leaned against the wall, their bows wrapped in cloth or slung across their backs.

She received a few leering stares. Adventurers were always some of the more rowdy bunch. And considering how many watched her walk by—their gazes all over her—she could tell she was in the right place.

A little farther away, several elven archers stood together near the E-rank board. Their gazes found Veronica as she drew closer. Their expressions twisted immediately.

Disgust.

Veronica looked back at them the same way.

She had never been fond of other elves. She didn’t have a direct problem with them, but elves carried a searing hatred for dark elves. Even Melinda, the elf clerk from the Ronswick Adventurer’s Guild, had looked at her with disdain.

Veronica had nothing but disrespect for those who showed her the same.

The archers narrowed their eyes. They quickly grabbed a commission and walked away, refusing to stand near her presence. Veronica simply rolled her eyes as she stepped up to the request board.

Compared to the rest of the guild, this section was almost disappointing. Roughly twenty requests had been pinned across the board, most written on plain parchment and marked with guild stamps. Some were old enough that their edges had curled. Others looked freshly posted.

Veronica scanned them one by one.

Herb gathering. F-rank.

No.

Assist with unloading merchant cargo. F-rank.

Absolutely not.

Escort a pottery wagon to a nearby village. E-rank.

Too slow.

Investigate missing chickens from a farmstead west of Maftem. F-rank.

Veronica stared at that one for a moment. The pay was oddly high, a thousand vix for such a simple request. Though perhaps there was more beneath the surface than it appeared.

Regardless, she ignored it and moved on.

Veronica wasn’t here purely for money. Not entirely. If vix were the only concern, she could have found several less annoying ways to earn it. The commission needed to be useful. Something low enough that her current condition wouldn’t turn it into suicide, but troublesome enough that Gwen and Nasha could practice moving as a group.

Her gaze slowed near the lower right side of the board.

A newer request had been pinned there.

Silverwolf Pack Extermination. E-rank.

Veronica leaned forward, reading the request.

A pack of silverwolves had migrated unexpectedly into a rocky creek area east of Maftem. The location was approximately eight kilometers from the eastern gate, roughly an hour’s travel by wagon or horse depending on road conditions. The monsters had been sighted near the waterline and surrounding stones, harassing smaller caravans and forcing gatherers away from the area.

The estimated number of wolves was six to eight. The reward was two hundred vix per silverwolf kill. A left ear was required for each confirmed kill.

She read it over twice.

Silverwolves were not particularly dangerous monsters on their own. Fast, coordinated, and aggressive in packs, but still well within the limits of an E-rank commission. A normal group of adventurers could handle them with decent positioning and basic discipline. Veronica herself could handle the commission even at second tier.

A rocky creek also meant uneven terrain. It would be open enough for them to monitor their surroundings, but troublesome enough to force movement and positioning. Useful for a beginner like Gwen. It was the perfect commission for training.

Veronica reached for the request, only for a hand to slam down on the corner of the parchment.

She turned, frowning.

It was a large man, nearly a head taller than her. He had bulky muscles and a greatsword strapped across his back. He wasn’t wearing armor. Not exactly. A few straps crossed his chest, but he wore no shirt beneath them.

It was apparent he was someone who relied on aura to protect his body instead of armor.

A fool’s method of survival.

“Hey, little lady. Interested in some silverwolves, huh?” he asked. “Want me to tag along? Might be a bit dangerous for a pretty girl like you.”

Veronica narrowed her eyes. He was one of the few that had been staring at her since he came in. She just hadn’t expected he would actually come forth and try his luck.

She wasn’t that easy.

At least Nasha had sung her an entire performance.

He leaned closer, hovering over her head. “Aw, don’t glare at me like that. You’ll make me freeze. I’m just being nice.”

She reached out, grabbed the commission anyway, and tore the top corner free from where his hand had pinned it to the board. “Not interested,” she said plainly.

Veronica turned and walked toward one of the lines for the guild clerks.

She could hear his footsteps behind her.

“Oh, come on. That’s an E-rank quest. Seeing as you don’t even have a party, why not join mine? I’m a C-rank myself. I’ll carry you through the commission. We could always use a porter.” He chuckled as he spoke.

Veronica stepped in line, refusing to look back. “Again. Not interested. I already have a party.”

“Oh?” he said behind her. “You’ve got friends, then? Where are they?”

Veronica didn’t answer.

The line moved slowly.

In front of her, a pair of adventurers argued with the guild clerk over payment for a goblin extermination quest. Apparently, the ears they brought were too damaged to confirm the kills. It seemed the clerk had already repeated her explanation three times and was one more complaint away from kicking the two out.

“Come on, don’t be like that,” the man behind her said. “I’m just offering some help. You’re just an E-rank, right?”

He leaned in closer to her ear.

“It wouldn’t be wise to make enemies of someone several ranks above you.”

Veronica narrowed her eyes. “Walk away and I won’t kill you.”

The man chuckled, still too close to her ear. “Cold. I like that.”

Suddenly, a faint disturbance brushed against her barrier, the one Sage always maintained around her. It shuddered, blocking his hand from touching her. Based on where the ripple had come from, she immediately knew where he had tried to reach.

A spot far too low to be a simple tap.

“Hm? Why’s it hard? Is this a barri—”

The guild floor shook as the man came face to face with a bright orange explosion. Fiery destruction erupted behind her, causing her hair to whip backward. The man was lifted straight off his feet and launched several meters away, dragging across the floor before crashing into an occupied table. The two men sitting there immediately stood and backed away.

Everyone in the guild froze. Conversations stopped at once. The only sounds that lingered were the creak of wooden debris falling from the shattered table and the searing embers fading in the air.

A groan came from the broken heap. The man grabbed the side of the ruined table, pushing himself back up to his knees. The front of his chest had been singed, burning away most of the fastened bands wrapped around his body, including the one that held the sheath behind his back. There was no blood or other obvious wound, but his face was red, livid with rage and humiliation.

“You fucking bitch,” he snarled.

Veronica’s gaze tightened on him.

[Minor strain on mana cores has been detected. Please refrain from excessive mana usage.]

The man reached over, fingers fumbling until they finally closed around the handle of his fallen greatsword.

Around them, the closest adventurers stepped back. A few others simply stood still, brows raised as if watching a spectacle. Some looked worried, but none appeared willing to step in.

But just as the man was about to stand, a sudden pressure coiled down from the second floor.

Veronica felt it settle across her shoulders first. Then her arms. Then her legs. It was as if gravity had doubled. It wasn’t enough to crush her, but it forced her knees to bend ever so slightly. Even the man across from her was kneeling, struggling to rise.

To counter the pressure, Veronica fueled more mana into her barrier alongside Sage’s. She recognized the sensation. It was similar to a mage’s domain. But instead of mana, it was aura.

Everyone in the guild shifted their eyes toward the left staircase. Veronica followed their gazes.

A man walked down the steps slowly, one hand resting lightly against the railing. He had a handsome face, sharp features, and a clean-shaven jaw. He wasn’t bulky, but he certainly had a soldier’s physique. He looked to be in his late twenties, still quite young, but his presence was something else. Not like a noble’s authority, but something similarly respected.

That was how everyone looked at him.

His armor was thin and polished, fitted close to his body in sleek silver plates rather than large ornaments. He carried no sword, but a shield was strapped across his back.

As he reached the bottom of the stairs, he looked over at the man kneeling by the shattered table. Then his gaze drifted to Veronica.

“What happened here?” he asked. His voice was smooth and regal, like a commander placed in charge of a small infantry.

The man with the greatsword grunted through clenched teeth. “That bitch attacked me.”

“I see,” he replied. He regarded the man for a moment, then turned back to Veronica. His attention lingered on her briefly before his eyes seemed to shift past her, toward the counters.

She didn’t miss the subtlety.

But why? What was he looking at?

A moment later, the armored man’s attention returned to the large brute. “Respect shown,” he said, voice calm, “is respect received.”

“What the hell are you talking about?” the idiot replied, as if those words weren’t meant for him.

The armored man released Veronica.

The weight vanished from her shoulders in an instant. At the same time, the aura pressing down on the brute multiplied.

The man’s eyes widened.

His knee slammed harder against the floor, and the boards beneath him groaned. His fingers tightened around the hilt of his greatsword, but he couldn’t lift it. The weapon scraped once against the ground, then stopped as his arm was forced lower.

“Harassing another adventurer inside the guild,” the armored man said. “Attempting to touch her without permission after being rejected. Drawing a weapon after being struck for it. Quite bold of you.”

The pressure increased again.

The wooden floor cracked beneath the brute’s knees. His words collapsed into a choked grunt.

Adventurers all around them began to chuckle, laughing and taking delight in the scene unfolding before them. It was classic entertainment, seeing someone put in their place.

Eventually, the fool stopped resisting. His body went limp as he succumbed to the pressure and passed out.

Veronica looked at the armored man with renewed interest.

His control over aura was precise. It wasn’t necessarily overwhelming. He had held back, that much was obvious. But for someone that young, it was impressive. She would even call it prodigious.

Being able to form a domain like that meant he was no less powerful than a seventh-tier. If that were the case, then he probably wasn’t as young as he appeared. Since aura was essentially life force, having stronger mastery over it meant retaining a more youthful appearance. While he looked to be in his late twenties, there was a chance he was actually older. Possibly in his late thirties.

A few moments later, a couple of city guards stopped by the guild. The man explained what had happened, and the guards promptly picked up the unconscious brute and dragged him out.

Everyone in the guild resumed whatever they had been doing, though a few eyes still lingered on Veronica and the armored man.

He walked over to her. “Are you uninjured?”

Veronica straightened herself. A few sore muscles, but she was mostly fine thanks to the overlapping barriers. “I’m alright,” she replied. “But who are you?”
