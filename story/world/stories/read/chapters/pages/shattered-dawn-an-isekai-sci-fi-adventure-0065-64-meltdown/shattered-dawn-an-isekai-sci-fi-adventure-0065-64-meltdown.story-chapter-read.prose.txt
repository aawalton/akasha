The next morning Elion was the first one into the dining room. He’d noticed that most of the owned sat in the same place at every meal. So he collected his food and slipped into a seat next to where Remy and Hol usually sat.

Keyla soon joined him, but he motioned for her to sit a few seats away from him. It would give them a better chance of ending up seated next to one of the Aterfelians.

The man who normally sat where Elion was entered the room. He gave Elion a weird look, then sat in Ryfer’s seat. The man didn’t press the issue.

Ryfer came in a minute later. He walked to his normal seat, and saw that it was taken. He scratched his head, a look of dumbfounded confusion on his face. He glanced around the room, and saw Elion. Ryfer looked back to the man who had taken his seat, and realization spread across his face.

He scowled at Elion, but found somewhere else to sit.

Elion drummed his fingers on the table, nervous energy forcing him to move. He watched the doorway for Hol or Remy. His food rested untouched on the table in front of him. The hum of soft chatter grew as more owned filled the room.

Boff walked in, and the room grew quieter. I don’t think there’s a rule against talking. But it would be easy for Boff to make one, so I guess nobody wants to be the reason for a new rule.

A nasally man spoke up.

“Boff, someone was talking in the bunks last night,” he said.

Boff scanned the room. He glanced down at his clipboard. “Perhaps a reminder of the rules is in order,” he said. “When I turn out the lights for the night, you are to stay in your bunks until I turn the lights back on. No talking is allowed while the lights are off.”

He turned back to his list, flipping through assignments.

Hol and Remy entered, and took their usual seats. Remy sat next to Elion. She gave him a strange glance. She wore the simple, dull colored work pants and canvas shirt that had been common in Aterfel, though she didn’t look like she’d been able to them in a long time.

Boff started giving out assignments.

“Do you know Selna Aerden?” Elion whispered. He stared at his plate of food, trying not to draw attention to himself. Butterflies turned summersaults in his stomach.

Remy looked at him again. Her brown hair fell in tight ringlets down around her shoulders. She seemed to have found a way to keep her hair cleaner than her clothes.

“Selna Aerden,” Elion said again, a little louder this time.

“Who are you?” Remy asked, her voice barely above a whisper.

“Elion. I came from Aterfel, looking for Selna. I came with Keyla.“

He nodded down the table in Keyla’s direction. A large man sat between Keyla and Hol. Remy leaned forward, looking where Elion indicated.

Elion watched the back of Remy’s head, then turned back to his food.

Remy looked at him, eyes wide. “Selna’s daughter,” she said. “You’re here?”

“Yes,” Elion said. “Do you know what happened to Selna?”

Remy shook her head. “She was running the refinery, and then she just disappeared.” She watched Elion closely, dark brown eyes absorbing his reaction.

“How long ago?” Elion asked. “What happened here?”

Remy glanced around the room. Nobody seemed to be paying any attention to their conversation. She leaned her head in, conspiratorially.

“Prator tricked us all,” she said. “He pretended that we were coming here to rebuild New Kairn Tol. People were already here, trying to get the refinery working again, but a lot of stuff had been stollen or damaged over the years, so we got to work helping.”

She knuckled her forehead. “We were so stupid not to realize sooner. After a few weeks it became obvious that Prator had been living here in New Kairn Tol for years, trying to increase Thrandicite production. But one by one he and Bortim were binding people, so we couldn’t do anything.”

“What happened to Selna?” Elion asked.

“I think she was the last one to realize. She was married to Prator after all. She just disappeared one day. Rumor has it she tried to kill Prator, and he just chopped her up with his sword.”

Elion shook his head. “She’s not dead. Or, at least, she wasn’t dead, about a week ago.”

Remy shrugged. “Does it matter?”

Their conversation was cut short by Boff, as he called out their assignments.

Elion and Keyla were again assigned to lidding duty, and as they walked down to their station Elion told her what he learned.

“She didn’t know much. Just said that they were tricked by Prator, and he bound them all. Selna was running the refinery until she disappeared.”

“But she didn’t know what happened after that?”

“No,” Elion said. “Just rumors. A rumor that she tried to kill Prator, but he killed her first.”

“Well, that’s not true,” Keyla said. “Because we saw her. She’s alive.”

“I know.”

They walked in silence, clanking down the metal grate stairway toward the bottom of the pit. Elion’s thoughts turned to Boff’s rules.

“He didn’t tell us we couldn’t talk,” Elion said. “We were able to talk last night because he forgot to tell us that rule.”

“I thought it was weird how quiet everyone got when the lights went out,” Keyla said. “I didn’t feel anything about talking though.”

“This is amazing,” Elion said. “I hadn’t thought about it before, but this could be our chance!”

“What do you mean?” Keyla asked. “We’re not going to be able to talk after lights out anymore.”

“No, listen. Boff has us on a pretty strict rotation. We wake up, we eat, we go to our work area. Boff gives us a specific task or quantity to complete, with orders to come back as soon as we’re done. Then he gives us our next orders. If somehow it were possible to be so late that he doesn’t give us orders, we’d be home free!”

“I don’t think it’ll be that easy,” Keyla said. “He’s going to notice.”

As Keyla and Elion worked lidding barrels, they watched the other workers in Boff’s crew. This area of the acid pits was theirs, but many other crews had claims on other portions of the pits, and worked these diligently.

The offer of additional pay must have gotten Boff’s creative mind running, because he’d assigned some of his owned to wheel out an old pump and mount it next to the current one. He ran around supervising the installation.

“He’s got to pump up the production,” Elion said. “I heard them talking about it last night. They’re paying him in cends. A lot of them.”

“They are?” Keyla seemed surprised. “Why?”

“You said they were valuable,” Elion said. “I assumed that people trade them.”

“They do, but I mean why do they want to pump up production? And what would Boff do with them out here? They’ve become less valuable if you can’t sell them to Ascended, and there aren’t many of those here.”

“Maybe he’s just saving them up, hoping for a big payday,” Elion mused.

Keyla gasped, holding her hand over her mouth. “That’s not going to work,” she said, looking at the new pump installation. Boff was having some of his owned connect the two pumps together, so that the existing one could help power the decrepit one.

Elion looked, but didn’t have the mechanical prowess to understand or see what she saw.

“Should I say something about it?” she said. “They’re jury-rigging it together. It might work for a bit, but someone’s going to get hurt like that.”

They kept lidding barrels.

Because Boff had pulled a few of the owned off of working to help install an additional pump, barrel output was lower that morning. Elion and Keyla caught up with the the flow of barrels, finishing lidding all the ones in the staging area.

By that point they had entirely shut down the pump.

Elion and Keyla stood awkwardly in the empty staging area, trying to figure out what to do.

“He said to stay here until we met our quota, then head back up for lunch,” Elion said, rehearsing his current order. “We haven’t met the quota yet, so as long as we stay here, we can do whatever we want.”

“I’m going to take a break,” Keyla said, and went over to sit in a shaded area. Elion went to go sit beside her. They watched the others work on the pump installation.

“Part of me really wants to go over there and help them,” Keyla said.

“Naw, stay here and rest. We’re prisoners. Slaves. We only do what they tell us to do.”

“I’m just worried someone’s going to get hurt,” Keyla said. She walked over to where a few owned were working on the pump, and called out to them. “Hey!” she said. “You can’t connect them like that. You’re going to have an issue with the axle warping.”

One of the silver-suited owned turned to her and shrugged. “We have to,” he said. “It’s what Boff told us to do.”

“Hey, Boff,” Keyla shouted.

“Stop,” the man by her said. “Don’t bug him.”

“Boff!” Keyla called.

Boff turned. Elion knew it was him, because he was the shortest silver-suit in the area.

“What are you doing?” Boff demanded. “Stop chatting and get back to work!”

Compelled by the order, Keyla threw her arms up in the air and started back to the lidding line in a huff. “He’s making a mistake,” she said.

Elion doubted it. “He won’t listen to you.”

“He will if he knows I’m an Artificer,” she said.

“You can’t reveal that! We need to lay low, keep quiet.”

“If he knows I’m an Artificer, maybe he’ll let me fix things. Then I won’t have to work on these stupid barrels all day long. It’s not like we have another plan.”

Elion looked up at the ridge line, toward the warlock’s camp. “I’ve been formulating a plan,” he said. “If we could both get up there, you could glitch out their machine, and then I could get my knife back. Then we’d slip into Boff’s room at night, and I’d take all the Bondholds from him. I’d give them all back to everyone and we’d be free.”

“You may as well steal all his cends too,” Keyla said. “He must have a stash of them somewhere.”

“Good idea,” Elion said. “Then we’d find Kendry and get our guns back.”

“Great plan,” Keyla said. “Let me know when you’re ready to try it.”

“I’m being serious,” Elion said, “I think it could work.”

“You’re just missing the most important piece,” Keyla said. “Permission from Boff to go attack the warlocks. Why don’t you go ask him?”

Over by the pumps, Boff and the other owned backed up, inspecting their handiwork. Apparently satisfied by the slapdash job, Boff shouted for the pumps to be started up. Engines began whirring, and barrels were moved into place to capture the acid.

“Well we’re going to have to get back to it,” Elion said.

“That sounds terrible,” Keyla said, shaking her head. “There’s no way that—”

The old pump exploded, bursting open and sending globules of acid flying everywhere.
