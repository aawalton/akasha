Ashton filled the world fragment he was holding with his mana, and began to try and manipulate its form the same way that he had taught Millie to.

After all, at the end of the day, this was still a crystal structure made up entirely of magic in one form or another. It wasn't 'normal' magic; it was far more complex than just to say that it was made of mana, but at the foundation, it also wasn't far off.

Ashton wasn't sure if he could shape this crystal structure, but well, why shouldn't he try?

But as he tried to move it, it was like the difference between water and barely-molten asphalt. It was such a solid mass that Ashton felt it might be impossible at first, but it wasn't quite that.

Rather, it looked like he would need some assistance.

Ashton got out his pen and quickly started to write an enchanting array onto a sheet of paper. It wasn't anything all too complex; just something that boosted the efficacy of mana-shaping. It came to be because people thought that it would help young apprentices learn mana-manipulation more easily, though it backfired. Efficiently using an array was much harder than simple mana-shaping, even if that efficiency rose sharply after a point.

Ashton placed the world fragment onto the array and began to try and manipulate it.

"Alright... somewhat hotter molten asphalt, at least," Ashton whispered. If he had proper enchanting tools this would be much easier, but he couldn't make them himself. A smithing spirit was needed to make the base, and depending on the tool, enhancing it, like with the help of a fairy, was also a necessity.

But for now, at least, it seemed as though Ashton would be able to shape it at least a little. He carefully moved the mana through the object, and carefully plucked away a small piece. Like a single droplet being pulled from water, Ashton removed a piece the size of a fingernail.

However, as he did, Ashton felt an interesting property. Although the two parts were now physically disconnected, they still seemed to be part of the same 'fragment'.

It was similar to how Ashton was able to instantly transfer his magic through to the Helping Hand. When he put it into the small piece of the fragment, he could still access the power of the entire piece. However, it was much harder to let enough mana flow through the small piece, like there was simply a smaller 'entrance', and it wasn't easily possible to activate the effects.

With those limitations, Ashton fused the small piece back into the main crystal and then pulled off another piece roughly the size of his thumb. This was large enough to let Ashton comfortably view the veil and interfere with it more easily, but not to move himself over into Astral projection. For that, he still needed the larger piece.

Now, with a bit more shaping, Ashton could turn the smaller fragment into a good centrepiece for what would later become his magical 'focus' to help with his spellcasting. Using powerful magic items wasn't a bad idea, especially if they were aligned with things that were good for his magic anyway.

There was a reason why he, as a summoner, got the 'Veil Mastery' skill. Summoners and the veil were deeply connected, and having the opportunity to connect his magic more closely to that plane would never be something bad.

But in that case, Ashton really already had all of the things that he needed to make the focus, so he might as well just do it now. Millie was still working on her assignment as well, and he had some free time until he went off with Clementine later.

But for now, there was something else he had to figure out.

"Millie, what colour is this stone?" he asked, holding it toward the girl. She tilted her head and looked at it, shrugging.

"It's white, but a little see-through? Like it's a bit foggy," she explained, and Ashton nodded in understanding.

"So it's a milky white. I can work with that," Ashton said with a satisfied smile, but Millie quickly shook her head.

"No, no, it's literally foggy. Like there's fog inside, moving around," Millie explained, and Ashton focused back down on the piece. He could only really see the raw shape that it had, but not what was going on inside of it like that.

"That's even better. Thanks."

With that, Ashton quickly got started. His magical focus was going to be his necktie. Since mana flow was often concentrated in the heart, it was the best spot to efficiently move it out of his body and transform it into an energy that could be used for spellcasting or other magic.

For now, Ashton grabbed his fabrics, and quickly cut it into shape. A necktie was fairly simple to make, so that was the quickest part. However, the aspect that took a little longer was what followed.

Ashton started stitching intricate patterns into the necktie using magic stone threads. Enchantments to help with the flow of mana, mostly, as well as a few patterns that he found in one of the books that were supposed to be useful for a magical focus specifically.

And in some of the important parts of the focus-tie, Ashton put small parts of the world fragment. He had split the thumb-sized piece up even more into smaller shards, which were connected to each other through the magic stone thread. Since they were connected like that, Ashton was able to access each of the small shards at the same time, still letting them come together to use its effects.

It took Ashton a while, but once his fingers started moving, they didn't stop, so he was able to finish it quite fast.

Though, he did have to think... if he made the right offers, would he be able to convince the Spawn of Akrazoth to sew things for him? Not a huge difference between stitching together flesh and fabric, right? The demon should be able to work on leather armour at the very least, right?

Well, that might be something he could figure out at some point. He didn't want to push it. The Spawn didn't exactly seem thrilled about the changed contract and would need to get used to it first before Ashton made suggestions like that.

He put on the necktie. Now, the next step would take a while. The most important part of making a magical focus was to simply fill it with your magic for a long period of time.

After a while, the object's natural pattern would become overlapped in one's own. That was why even Ashton couldn't speed up the process by manipulating the pattern directly. The object's natural pattern was important, and it had to be tied to one's own, but shouldn't change into it. You basically wanted it to be enveloped and mixed with your own mana in a way that wasn't easy to achieve otherwise.

Basically, your own mana and the object's mana had to somehow form a link.

It was possible to speed the process up, but mostly by simply keeping more mana within the object.

And then, after a while, the object would become a focus that was connected to the person that created it.

It was a simple process, so it made sense why it was one of the first things that fledgling spellcasters were taught to do.

Once she got her class and increased her spirit stat a bit more, Ashton should have Millie make her own focus as well.

Since he was already at it, Ashton figured he might as well continue working on some stitched enchantments on the rest of his clothes. It was just work that took a while, rather than being complicated, so he could do it while helping Millie with her assignment.

Then again, she was already doing shockingly well. The shape of the magic stone she was holding had changed a little, most likely unintentionally, but for the most part she did a great job focusing on the actual frequency of the mana within the stone.

Ashton wasn't sure if she was getting it in the right direction, since what she was sensing didn't seem to exactly be the mana pattern. So while that pattern was changing, that wasn't all, apparently. She hadn't been asking Ashton to reset the stone even once, though, nor did she seem frustrated, so it seemed to be going alright.

"How's it going?" Ashton asked, turning to the girl. She looked up at him with a frustrated grimace.

"I don't know... I don't think it's going well..." she grumbled, annoyed. "I can't get it right..."

"Oh? But you're getting closer?" Ashton asked, and Millie slowly, and hesitantly, nodded.

"I think so? The magic stone feels more like you, but it's not... you," Millie said annoyed, but Ashton just laughed and shook his head.

"It was never the goal to make it exactly like me. When you paint someone, it's not like that them, either, right? It's just a snapshot of a single moment, as you are seen through the artist's eyes. Just make it feel like me, in any way, and if you think that it's 'close enough', then you're done."

Millie looked up at Ashton and then back down at the stone in her hand. "Oh... uhm, in that case... I think I've been there for a while."

Ashton focused on the stone. Honestly, he thought the same. He couldn't sense what Millie did, but everyone did have their own unique presences that were fairly recognisable and unique. This one did feel a lot like Ashton, if only at a glance.

"Alright. Well, in that case, it's time for your next task," Ashton said. "This time, try to pull your mana somewhere. Preferably to your palm or your fingertips, but if somewhere else is easier, that's okay, too."

Millie nodded. "Okay... and then?"

"You cluster that mana and keep it there, and then change that to feel like me, or like what you changed the mana in the magic stone to. You alter your own mana this time. But only in that small cluster," Ashton explained.

This was actually one of the last steps that he figured he would need to let Millie become a witch; the specific type of witch that he knew she was perfect to be. Well, even then, she would only be at the beginning of her path as a witch, but even so, she was getting closer and closer to getting a magic class.

And with her talent, Ashton was sure that she would get there in no time.
