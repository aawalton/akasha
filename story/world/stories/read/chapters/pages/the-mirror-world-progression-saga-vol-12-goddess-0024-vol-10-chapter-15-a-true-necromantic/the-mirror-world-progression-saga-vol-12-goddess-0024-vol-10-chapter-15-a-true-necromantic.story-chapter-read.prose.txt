“Clinging, hugging, sharing the same space?” Azure retorted mentally, making it into a purr. “If it weren’t in that accursed place we were doing this, it would be quite dangerous before a big fight.”

Samantha giggled and rolled her eyes at the same time. “Come on, this is serious! You’re doing your buffs?”

“Just a few more moments. Strategy, oh Conqueress of Cute?”

“If there are minions — and I’m sure there will be — deal with them while I deal with the lich. Contain them. And preferably sling out Deathsting. We need as many combatants as possible, considering how limited we are on that front.”

“Well, I am a [Dark Mage], after all. I can summon minions, but depending on the crowd, I might not need them.”

“And why is that?”

“Why spoil the surprise? Besides, I’m ready. Standby!”

Momentarily, Azure appeared, in her full crystalline form, for the realities of the Bridge of Edges. As Azure’s body bloomed out of hers, Samantha didn’t so much as twitch, and they remained in intimate contact, bodies pressed together.

“Well, hello there, sexy,” Azure said, and planted a kiss on Samantha’s cheek.

“Fancy meeting you here,” Samantha replied with a grin. “Ready to get outta here and vanquish some undead?”

“I must be smitten, if that line works for me.”

Chuckling, Samantha shook her head and muttered, “I’ll take that as a yes. Okay, here we go!”

Keeping hold of Azure, Samantha moved toward the well, only one big step away. A vortex pulled them in immediately, and they fell toward what started as a distant window, but very quickly swallowed them and became reality. Primarily, Samantha saw a dark chamber and a sarcophagus of heavy stone.

They were deposited into a large square chamber with one open hallway out. The only light was a reddish glow that made it hard, but just barely enough, to see naturally for her eyes. The entirety of the room seemed to be carved with warped murals, the twisted shapes marred by simplistic runes scoring over every inch, giving all the energy of a madhouse. The sarcophagus was on an upraised dais, and the top was shifted aside, making it clear at a glance that the former occupant was not resting within.

The feel of the place was something alien and familiar at once. That it was kin to the Sage’s network was blatant, like there was a monstrous claw hovering over it, gripping it and sapping its necrotic aura and energy. There was a discordant, frenetic, angry feel in the air — stirred up old animosity and spite for the living. Artificial. It was an external pressure exerted to facilitate the drawing of energy and make it active. Something to drain only over aeons, such was its potency, and the way that the system squeezed it for many multiples more than it should.

It was as if the Sage had found a kind of ‘radioactive decay’ residing in the ancient dead. He sapped something intrinsic, perhaps a Domain itself interlaced within them. Likely, something even deeper and poorly understood. It was the weakening and unmaking of a foundation no one else could detect, much less understand or control.

It was so, so bad. He was making all of reality worse. Perhaps it would take a timeframe she could not properly conceive, but it could entirely break the System one day. Too many errors to continue, and become unable to properly reboot. She had no idea what happened then.

‘You couldn’t ever help yourself,’ the voice within charged, disgusted and angry, ‘and you’ll claim it unintentional, won’t you? What you did in your sleep was anything but. Your will to destroy propagated; your spite for the rest of us dragged everything toward oblivion.’

Further pondering wasn’t feasible, as there were scores of ghoulish undead in every direction, wandering restlessly without any actual direction. That was until there were living targets suddenly within grasp.

“Hrrraugh!” came a cry from many decrepit throats, as the more immediate denizens pounced with sudden, supernatural speed.

“Cover me!” Samantha faded and slid away from them, now floating just above the floor. She dodged clawed fingertips as they sliced the air in front of her. She slammed into another ghoul that wasn’t paying attention behind her, sending it sprawling to the floor with an offended cry of protest and a great burst of dust, both from the floor and the body of the undead.

By now, more and more were noticing her. As yet, there was no sign of the lich, just a sea of undead bodies. That was — hopefully — kind of a good thing.

More prep time!

Samantha decided to do something that was often ‘wasteful’ in immediate, deadly combat with its action economy, when she could do so many things at once. This required a full action, leaving her only with her extra Haste effect. But in this situation, she kinda needed some stable help.

She threw out her hand in a grand gesture and began a summoning…

Meanwhile, Azure flew into action. She immediately tossed her dagger, and the wyvern, Deathsting, appeared in a flash, clawing and biting, ripping apart the undead by the multiples with each strike. They were cut up, dropped, and pushed back, creating space as the wyvern quite deliberately drove deep into the loose ranks of them in the room.

“Die again, worthless constructs!” the black wyvern cried viciously, his spite for them at least equal to their own.

Azure was already casting something else as this went on. She whispered an incantation and made a sign with a hand, as a mass of ghostly blades appeared in a circle around them. They weren’t swords, but like big obsidian shards, sharp both ways, dark substance on the inside, appearing like a silhouette with the ghostly aura enshrouding them. Already moving in a rotation, the blades themselves also began spinning on their axes like a helicopter wing. There was no uniformity to it all, though. The blades did not maintain a perfect circle, striking out at oncoming ghouls and cutting them down. They maintained their own little spherical zone, but otherwise seemed to function independently.

This AoE made short work of advancing ghouls, even as it drew their attention like moths to a flame. They snarled and cried out and stormed toward them to their gruesome ends, decapitated, de-limbed, and/or cut in half as soon as they got there.

On the downside, it did sling gore everywhere, and the smell was especially unsavory. Fortunately, the diced-off body parts did not move on their own. Once lopped off, they stopped moving for the most part.

“Whoa!” Samantha called as she flicked an unknown shriveled-up, undead organ that had splatted onto her armored arm and stuck. Possibly a liver. Gross. She kept her voice steady, however. “That’s cool! On one hand classic, on the other, so uniquely you!”

Azure smiled proudly and nodded. And then she grimaced, as she’d stepped on a bit of intestines that oozed unmentionable black… ooze that positively fumed upward with foul vapors.

Azure probably didn’t say anything because she had opted to stop breathing temporarily.

How unnecromantic of her, tehehe…

Samantha’s summon materialized in the center of the safe zone — a huge, golden-armored Angel spread her wings, brandishing her sword and shield in typical dramatic fashion. Gabriel. “I have come, as called, Your Majesty!” Immediately, she flew over the blades and landed ahead, putting herself slightly clear of Deathsting and swinging her blade, cutting through several ghouls at once, and using her shield to block a greater press of bodies, then shoving them hard with a cry, bowling almost a dozen of them over.

Still with a Haste action, Samantha saw little reason to rush out more AoE herself. Instead, she dropped a buff on Gabriel, adding more defense and some wound negations. Tank survival was kind of important, after all.

Amidst the tumult of undead cries of hatred, hunger, and sheer offense at their continual denial of revenge upon the intrusion, a more impressive undead being came charging in, snarling loudly. It was over seven feet tall, and must’ve been some sort of lizardman. Decaying armor covered its body — magical, which certainly said something about its age and/or the power of the decay — and it wielded a huge poleaxe that was perhaps the only shiny thing native to the room. Its head was uncovered, and both its eye sockets were empty, yet they glowed with a baleful red. Most of its mouth had seemed to wither away, leaving an evil, unnatural grin to its countenance.

The lizard giant charged through the rest with supreme speed and violence, crossing the distance easily, knocking his lesser ‘brethren’ aside with disdain if they were too slow to give way. Gabriel was there to intercept, however. The giant adjusted quickly to attack her, dropping his poleaxe down with a crash onto the shield, perhaps quite logically expecting it to be shattered or split by his mighty blow.

If so, he was disappointed, as the Heaven Wall Shield absorbed the blow with a great resounding clang. Gabriel’s strength and mass were also up to the task of not getting bowled over, as she took the hit with a grunt and merely slid backwards slightly on the stone floor, her wings also arcing back and buffeting in a backward motion that made a gust in the room, in a practiced tactic for a little more absorption of such charges. It also slapped one brave ghoul, who had gotten too close, right in the face, sending it flying.

Though the lizard giant was no doubt an unfeeling undead beast, Samantha thought she detected a look of consternation on its face.

Not for the first time, Samantha felt tremendous glee at how powerful her top Servitors had become during the war. Double-digit warriors were no joke.

Before anyone else could react, Samantha struck. Deciding she’d save her mana, she decided to go with good, old-fashioned laser blasts to the face. The giant was unlikely to dodge, and his skull in general was highly exposed.

Zap, zap! She pointed her fingers and used [Light Manipulation] for precision beams. Both hit and seared the ghoulified beast’s scaly flesh.

It recoiled and screamed, with a shrill sound remarkably like someone dealt pain, but somehow, the note was entirely one of outrage and fury. It flung its smoking head and fell back several steps, obviously disoriented.

Samantha was a bit put out, in truth. She almost certainly outleveled his resistance. She knew it must’ve been [Knight] levels saving its ass. Well, not for long, it wouldn’t!

Even as the others rushed in to take advantage, Samantha felt a sudden gravity in the room, like a sucking black hole had formed. Her eyes shot over to the sarcophagus as she felt and saw a crackle of dreadful magic form.
