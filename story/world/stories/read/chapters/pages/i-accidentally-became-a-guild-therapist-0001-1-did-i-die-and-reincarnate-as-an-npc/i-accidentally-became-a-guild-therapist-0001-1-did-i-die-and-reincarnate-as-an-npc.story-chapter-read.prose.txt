Chapter 1: Did I Die and Reincarnate… as an NPC?

ARC I: THE ACADEMIC EXILE

SYNOPSIS: Livia Quinn’s death catapulted her into a chaotic isekai world. However, instead of becoming a sword-wielding Hero or a fire-slinging mage, she awakens as the most pathetic non-combat support unit imaginable: a Mental Supporter Lv. 1—an “Emotional Buffer” for Adventurers. Amidst the pandemonium of a war camp, Livia must confront the reality that she was reincarnated to be nothing more than emotional furniture.

Death arrived with a screech and the aroma of stale croissants flung into the air.

A humiliating finale to an already pathetic life.

Livia Marcelline Quinn, failed Psychology Major, opened her eyes not in heaven or a hospital, but in a waiting room radiating bad neon light. The walls pulsed with a sickly pink glow—the kind you’d find in a dentist’s office designed by someone who had never visited one. The air hummed like a glitching server on the verge of collapse. Somewhere distant, a notification chimed.

“What the hell?” she whispered, her head throbbing.

She sat up slowly. The chair beneath her was not quite solid—more like the suggestion of a chair, rendered in low resolution. The floor had no seams. The ceiling had no height she could determine. It was the kind of place designed to be forgotten the moment you left it.

There were others here, she realized. Faint silhouettes seated in rows, staring at nothing. Some flickered. One was loading.

Livia looked down at her own hands. No flicker. No loading bar. She was just—there, already fully rendered, like she’d skipped a step everyone else in the room was still stuck on. She didn’t know if that was good or bad. She filed it away, underneath the growing panic, and kept sitting there.

This is not heaven. Livia decided, with great certainty. Heaven would have better lighting.

A robotic, cold, and disjointed voice sliced through the silence: "Respawning into host world… Emotional Buffer 2.0, designation: Mental Supporter Lv. 1, generated. System integrity: 87%."

Buffer. Like a cushion.

Like a listening couch.

A transparent interface flared in front of her face—a video game HUD with a personal vendetta. The stats listed were the epitome of uselessness:

Strength: 0. Agility: 0. Magic: 0.

Emotional Resilience: Error: Pending Review

Substrate: — [Field unavailable]

Class: Mental Supporter Lv. 1

Inventory: Itchy gray robe. Worn sandals. Therapist’s Log (font: Comic Sans).

One field refused to populate, no matter how many times she tapped it. It didn’t throw an error. It didn’t apologize. It just stayed blank—patient, almost, in a way that felt less like a glitch and more like a choice.

“What the hell is this?” Livia swiped at the screen, trying to find a Fire Mage or Swordmaster option. The screen flashed red: Class selection locked. Combat protocols: null. Proceed to emotional support subroutine.

Isekai’d into a customer service simulation.

Her reflection in the wall showed the same Livia: shoulder-length black hair, soft features, and a gaze that plainly said, “I did not sign up for this.”

[Achievement Unlocked: Accidentally Empathetic. Passive Skill Learned: Suppress Panic Lv. 0.1]

“Great,” she hissed, clutching her robe. “The game’s already making fun of me.”

This wasn’t reincarnation.

It was a cosmic joke.

Sliding doors opened with a whoosh, and Livia was greeted by synchronized chaos.

The base camp sprawled like a fever dream assembled by a game developer with too much caffeine and not enough QA testing. Tents in six different architectural styles crowded each other for space—canvas war pavilions next to glowing arcane domes next to what appeared to be a repurposed tavern that someone had simply dragged here and left. Banners hung from crooked poles, advertising guilds with names like “Void Reckoning” and “Absolute Carnage” and, bafflingly, “We Try Our Best.”

Players were everywhere. Actual humans—or at least, things shaped like humans—wearing gear that made no ergonomic sense. A man in full plate armor sprinted past her carrying a tray of potions. Two women argued at a bulletin board, both gesturing at a quest sheet that was actively on fire. A child-sized figure in a giant bear costume sat on a crate and wept quietly into a bowl of stew.

Nobody helped the bear child. They were all too busy.

Between the players were the NPCs—and this was where it got unsettling. They moved on loops. A merchant three tents down repeated his sales pitch every forty seconds, his smile locked at exactly the same angle each time. A guard patrolled a nine-step circuit, pivoting at precise intervals, his eyes focused on a point slightly above reality. Two NPCs walked toward each other, stopped, said their scripted lines, and separated—then did it again. And again.

I am one of them now, Livia thought. I am background.

The stench of sweat, steel, and burnt mana assaulted her introverted senses. She stepped forward, clutching her notepad like a lifeline from her old existence.

She put a hand out to steady herself against the nearest tent pole and stopped.

It was real. Not low-resolution-real, not suggestion-of-a-chair real. The wood was rough under her palm, warm where the sun hit it and cool in the shade, with a splinter she didn’t quite dodge in time. Her whole arm flinched before her brain caught up.

The waiting room had been a sketch of a place. This was not a sketch of anything.

She pulled her hand back and examined the small red mark rising on her palm. Games weren’t supposed to do splinters. Games did visual effects and cooldown timers and health bars. They did not do the specific, stupid, undeniable sting of a piece of wood breaking skin.

She filed that away too. On top of the blank field. On top of the silhouettes that didn’t flicker.

The pile was getting tall.

She decided this was a problem for later. Later already had a very long queue.

This had to be a coma dream. This had to be dissociation.

Or… hell disguised as fantasy fiction.

A nearby player screamed at an NPC about a “trash quest,” while another bragged about soloing a dungeon boss. Livia’s psychology instincts twitched, uninvited.

Poor coping mechanisms. Needs external validation.

[Achievement Unlocked: Overthinking Simulator. +10 Anxiety Points]

“Shut up!” she muttered, waving at the air.

An NPC approached. His tunic read "Quest Support Unit 4.7." His smile was unnerving—not threatening exactly, but calibrated. The precise smile of someone who had been told what smiling was and had approximated it from a diagram. His eyes glitched between blue and static every three seconds, regular as a clock.

“Greetings, Emotional Buffer 2.0!” he droned. “Subroutine: Mental Supporter. Directive: Remain in camp, process adventurer emotional data, log output, avoid quest interference. Combat module: deprecated. Query: ready for initialization?”

“You’re calling me an Emotional Buffer? I’m a therapist? I failed half my classes!” Livia threw her hands up. “I’m incompetent!”

“Directive: process emotional data,” the NPC repeated, handing her a scroll written in Comic Sans. “System appreciates compliance!” He glitched away, already droning at another NPC.

Task: Listen to Adventurer grievances. Offer comfort. Log sessions. Avoid combat.

“This is insane,” Livia muttered, pacing. “I’m trapped in a video game, and my job is to be a feelings dumpster? What kind of isekai hero gets this?” She watched players brandishing flaming swords and summoning dragons.

They were saving the world.

I’m… emotional furniture.

She slumped onto a rickety bench. The camp’s chaos swirled around her, but she felt detached, like a glitch in someone else’s story.

Am I dead?

The motorcycle. The scattered croissants.

Or am I just… this?

She was never enough in her old life—not as a student, not as a daughter, not even as a person. Her parents’ sighs, her friends’ pity, the mirror showing someone always lacking—it all crashed down on her.

She had spent three years studying the architecture of other people’s minds, learning how they broke and how they healed, becoming an expert in a subject she could not apply to herself. Irony of the highest order. A locksmith who couldn’t open her own front door.

Maybe this was punishment. An eternity as a useless NPC.

She laughed bitterly, her signature dry humor surfacing. “Livia Quinn: patron saint of emotional furniture.” She doodled a sad stick figure in her notepad.

[Achievement Unlocked: Self-Deprecation Master. +5 Sarcasm Points]

This game truly sucked.

The camp continued to roar. She watched it from her bench with the detached eye of someone who had spent a lot of time being an observer rather than a participant.

A pair of players nearby were having what appeared to be a genuine argument about loot distribution, voices rising in pitch, gestures escalating. Livia found herself automatically cataloguing it: avoidant communication style, deflection via aggression, underlying fear of being undervalued. She closed her notepad firmly over the observation.

Stop. You’re not their therapist.

You’re not anyone’s therapist.

You failed practicum.

But the instinct remained, quiet and persistent, the way a song stays in your head long after you’ve stopped listening.

Livia’s introverted instincts screamed at her to remain unseen.

Blend into the background. Ride out this nightmare.

However, as a warhorn blared, signaling the arrival of the infamous guild Bloodbath & Beyond, Livia’s stomach clenched.

Her time in hiding was about to end.

And perhaps, her role as an “Emotional Buffer” was about to be tested by sword-wielding psychopaths.
