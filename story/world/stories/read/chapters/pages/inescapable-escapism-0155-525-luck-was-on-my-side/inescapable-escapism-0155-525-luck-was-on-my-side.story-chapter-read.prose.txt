I slipped my backpack onto my back before smoothing out my dress with trembling hands. I was ready to go. My bag was packed with everything I might need if I were to actually go to the gym, and my room was tidy again. There was nothing else I needed to do before leaving.

Grabbing my phone from the bed where I’d left it, I took a step toward my door before freezing and turning around to stare at my reflection in the mirror. My eyes darted back and forth as I scrutinised my appearance, checking to make sure I looked okay and hadn’t somehow managed to mess up my dress or makeup in the few minutes since I’d last checked.

I looked okay, though. Tired, as always, but okay. There was nothing I could do about that, anyway. I’d already tried to hide the bags under my eyes, and I couldn’t add any more. It wouldn’t help, and I knew that from experience.

Tearing my gaze away from the mirror, I glanced around my room again. I wasn’t sure what I was looking for exactly. I think I was just trying to make sure I hadn’t forgotten to do something or that I’d left something out that would make it clear to my mom that I wasn’t truly at the gym.

But I couldn’t see anything that looked out of place. The stack of books on the floor next to my bed could have been tidier; I could have moved some stuff around and put them on my bookshelf, but I hadn’t gotten around to it yet.

Instinctively, I started to move towards the pile before stopping. It didn’t need to be done now, I told myself. It would take a bit of time to do because I had to make room for some of them, and that meant completely rearranging a couple of the shelves, but I could do that when I got back or in the morning.

Still, part of me was tempted to do it immediately. The urge to pull all of my books out and carefully replace each one, making sure to keep them in alphabetical order, burned in my chest, and it was difficult to turn away from the books and face the door again.

I knew why I was struggling so much. I was scared. It was stupid, and I was fully aware of that too, but I was still terrified. I had no reason to be. Not really. I was just going to see Duncan, and nothing bad would happen. We’d probably just hang out and have a nice time, nothing more. Everything would be fine. I would have a good time. All I needed to do was get out of the house without Mom working out that I was lying about where I was going, and then it would all be okay.

A sharp exhale hissed through my lips as I forced myself to move towards the door, trying to ignore how tight my entire body felt. Every single muscle seemed to be tensing, like I was preparing to go to battle, not to a friend’s house, but I couldn’t make them stop.

I needed to. Once I left my room, I could run into my mom at any moment, and I needed to appear relaxed. I had to look as though nothing was going on. Otherwise, she’d suspect something was up, and I couldn’t have that. It would make it so much harder to convince her that I was just going to the gym.

That was all I needed to do, I told myself. I just needed to say that I was going to the gym to do a few classes, and I’d be back later. I wasn’t sure what time I’d be back; it depended on how tired I was after the self-defence class. If it wasn’t too intense, I planned on going swimming or into the main part of the gym to use the treadmill or elliptical machine or something.

Mom would probably believe that. After all, I did go to the gym sometimes, and she’d been the one to tell me I should do some of the classes, so I could just point that out. Then, she wouldn’t be able to get annoyed at all.

Or maybe I shouldn’t tell her that, I realised. It might make her think I was being overly defensive, and she didn’t like that. Before, when I’d tried to explain myself, it had made her suspect that I was hiding something, and I hadn’t been that time.

But it was fine. I could handle Mom’s questions. I’d done it so many times before, and I was probably just overthinking it. It was just as likely that Mom would not care at all about where I was going. She probably wouldn’t even look away from her phone or book when I told her I was going out, and that would be better. Then, I wouldn’t need to explain why I was wearing a dress either.

My fingers touched the door handle, and I took a deep breath. My heart hammered in my chest, begging me with each beat to turn back, but I ignored it and pulled my door open, stepping out into the hallway outside before freezing again.

I paused, straining my ears to pick up footsteps or any sound that would indicate where my mom was. That was what I normally did when I left my room. I wasn’t sure when I’d started doing it or if it was just something I always did, but knowing where in the house Mom was made me feel a bit more prepared. It meant I knew when I’d have to speak with her, and sometimes, I’d catch something that helped me work out what kind of mood she was in.

But I didn’t hear anything. The house was still, and I waited for a few more seconds before starting to make my way along the hall. My steps were silent. Even I could barely hear the soft brush of my own tight-covered feet on the carpet as I reached the top of the stairs and came to a stop again, listening out for my mom.

Mom was probably in the garden. That must have been why no noise came from downstairs. It was where she spent most of her time during the summer, so I had nothing to worry about. The chances of her waiting for me at the bottom of the stairs were low. She’d done it a couple of times when she suspected I was up to something, but she didn’t usually bother. Still, I made sure to keep my expression as neutral and relaxed as possible, just in case.

Anxiety still raced through me as I reached the bottom of the stairs and turned into my lounge. There was another door in the kitchen, through the utility room, but Mom normally just used the French windows in my lounge to get to the garden, and I wasn’t really sure why.

My eyes found the latch on the tall glass doors as I approached, and my steps slowed slightly. The door was unlocked, and that meant Mom would probably be out there. I took a deep breath, steeling myself for the conversation I was about to have, as the lies I was going to tell my mom ran through my mind.

The desire to run away pulled at me. I longed to turn and just leave the house without seeing her. That would be safest, in a way. There would be no chance Mom would know I wasn’t in the gym if I just left, but once she worked out I was gone and hadn’t told her or left a note, she’d be furious. That would be even worse, and it wasn’t worth it. It wasn’t necessary.

Plastering a fake smile onto my face, I pulled the door open and leaned out into the garden. My eyes immediately went to the deck chair Mom was usually stationed in, but it was empty. She hadn’t even left a book out there.

Confusion and worry simmered in the pit of my stomach as I scanned the garden, searching for my mom, but she wasn’t there. There was absolutely no sign of her presence, and I wasn’t sure what to make of that.

She’d left the French windows unlocked, though. That must have meant she’d gone outside at some point during the day, and perhaps she’d just left it unlocked when she decided to come back inside. Either that or she hadn’t locked it since she went outside the day before. She did that sometimes.

Or something could have happened to her, a cruel voice in the back of my head whispered. She could have been kidnapped or dragged away by some wild animal. Perhaps she was already dead in a ditch somewhere.

I shook my head, trying to push that thought from my mind, but it simply tightened its grip on me. Images flashed behind my eyes. I saw blood splattering the grass, a gift left behind from the untamed moose-like creature that had knocked down our fence in search of a meal. And it had found one. My mom had been asleep at the time. She hadn’t woken up until its teeth had closed around her leg.

My stomach turned, and bile burned the back of my throat. That wasn’t the worst image. There was another that turned my blood to ice. I could almost feel my dad’s arm around me, his body heaving with each sob, as we stared at the screen in the police station. My mom, beaten and bruised, was surrounded by masked criminals. They stared into the camera as she begged us to give them what they wanted, so they’d let her go.

Me.

That was all they needed. If I were handed over to them, they had promised to free her, and wouldn’t that be better? I was young. I didn’t contribute to the world, according to my mom. It would be sad to lose me, obviously, but they could probably have another child.

Part of me agreed with her words. That was the problem. Perhaps it would have been better for them to hand me over to whoever it was that had taken my mom from the garden. Then, Mom would be back, and my dad wouldn’t have had to live with the choice he’d made. It was destroying him. I could tell.

I didn’t know why they wanted me, though. The police didn’t seem to know either. At first, they treated the whole thing like it was a joke. They thought Mom and Dad had a fight, and she’d left him, but that wasn’t what happened. They hadn’t fought about anything, and even when she did leave, she always took her phone. She was never away from it for long.

Then the random letters started arriving. I remembered how terrified I was the first time a note had been shoved through the door, speckled with Mom’s blood. That made the police take it a bit more seriously, even though they claimed it was probably just blood from an animal or something at first.

Dizziness crowded my mind, trying to reel me in, but I caught myself before it was too late. Fear gripped me as I swiped at the other worlds, trying to keep them away. I didn’t want to go to that world where the final deadline was approaching. We had two more days. Two days to decide what to do.

It wasn’t real, I told myself, trying to sound firm, but even my thought was shaky. That hadn’t happened in my world. It might have happened in another one, but it wasn’t real. The police would never be so uncaring about a missing person. They’d do something. We would find a way to get Mom back.

She’d probably just gone out for the day and forgotten to lock the door. That happened sometimes. She liked to get out of the house every now and again, and I never had any idea where she went or what she did. She didn’t really have any friends, as far as I was aware, so it seemed unlikely that she was actually seeing anyone. She was probably just out shopping or something?

A realisation dawned on me, and I leaned back, sliding the glass window shut and locking it. I could check whether her car was still in the drive, I realised. If it was gone, she was definitely just out for the day, and if not, then… I’d go from there.

I turned, moving towards the hallway again, before a noise stopped me in my tracks. It was deep and snuffling. There was something distinctly animalistic about it, and a memory slammed into me.

Another version of me was far too familiar with that sound. Wild boars, twisted by dark magic, haunted the outskirts of our village in some other world. They waited, biding their time, until someone strayed a little too far out. Perhaps it was a mistake or overconfidence. They hadn’t realised they’d crossed the threshold, or maybe they mistakenly believed the bloodthirsty animals wouldn’t hurt them. Whatever the reason, they were wrong.

And one had gotten into the house.

I wasn’t in that world, and I never had been, but the memories gripped me so tightly that I felt like I was. Terror coursed through me, and I was convinced I’d see one of those creatures gnawing at my mother’s lifeless corpse in the lounge, the walls that Dad and I spent so long painting the day before dashed with her blood.

It had happened before, after all.

My knees were weak. I gripped my phone as if it were a weapon, wishing that I had something more. A spear or a sword would have been better, but I was woefully unprepared. I thought I was safe. I didn’t think they’d ever be able to cross the barriers constructed long ago, and that was stupid. Nowhere was safe, and I should have known better.

The noise sounded again, deeper and more violent than before. My body trembled, and instinctively, I reached out towards the dizzinesses in my mind, searching for one that felt like home.

Something must have happened, I realised. I must have slipped into another world, one that was more dangerous, without even noticing. I wasn’t sure when it could have happened, but it would have made sense. Things were going too well for me. I’d had too nice a time with my dad the day before, and everything with Duncan was too perfect. There was no way that was reality.

The other worlds lashed out at me as I moved amongst them, and finally, I spotted it. One reality shone out in the darkness, and even just the sight of it made my heart feel lighter. Relief slammed into me as I rushed towards it, but it was short-lived.

I stumbled to a halt, my mind reeling as it fought not to be sucked in. That world wasn’t right; I didn’t even need to visit the world to know that. The sensation that emanated from the reality felt familiar. I knew that I felt safe there, but it wasn’t the place I was looking for.

A scent washed over me, and I couldn’t help but shut my eyes as I took it in. An emotion close to homesickness gripped me, causing my heart to ache, and tears burned behind my eyes as I realised what I was smelling. The sea.

It was Mitch’s world. I didn’t need to go there to know that, and I forced myself to push it away. I couldn’t go back there. I couldn’t face him again. It wasn’t what I was looking for, and I had things to deal with in reality, I told myself softly.

But the heartbreak remained as I opened my eyes again and looked around at the room. It was my lounge, and I was pretty sure I was in my reality. As far as I was aware, there were no wild boards in the UK, and even if there were, there was no magic, so they wouldn’t be worked into a frenzy by it. Everything would be fine. Mom had probably just turned on the television or something.

I needed to be sure, though. That meant I had to go and check, even though I didn’t really want to. I couldn’t just stay standing in the middle of the room forever. I’d have to move at some point, and I was meant to be going to Duncan’s house, so I needed to do something.

Slowly, I edged closer to the door. Despite my attempts to reassure myself, fear still thrashed within me, and my steps were even more cautious than before. I found myself holding my breath as I moved out into the hall and peered around the doorway into my parents’ lounge.

Relief tore through me, the emotion so strong that a hysterical giggle almost escaped my mouth. I had to press a hand to my lips to hold it in as another snore came from my mom’s sleeping form.

The adrenaline drained out of me, and I sagged against the door frame, taking a moment to gather myself once more. I had no idea a human could make that sound. It was closer to an animal or a lawnmower than anything I’d ever heard before, and it was kind of impressive.

My eyes found the mostly empty bottle of wine on the coffee table, and everything suddenly made more sense to me. Mom tended to start drinking in the afternoon, but she must have begun earlier than usual, and that was probably why the French windows were unlocked too. She’d probably been sitting outside and completely forgotten to shut them when she’d come in.

That made things easier for me, though, I realised as I turned away from the lounge and padded towards the kitchen. I couldn’t help but smile to myself as I grabbed the notepad that I usually used to leave Mom a note if she was already unconscious by the time I went out. I wasn’t sure if she ever read them, but she’d gotten annoyed at me before for not leaving one.

I gripped the pencil tightly as I wrote, choosing my words carefully.

I’m just heading out to the gym. There are a couple of classes on today that I’m going to do, and I might go swimming after, but I’ll be back after. Love you!

That sounded alright, I told myself. It was vague, but purposefully so. I’d intentionally not mentioned which classes I was doing or what time they were because then, Mom would have no idea what time I went out or when to expect me back. If she slept for ages, like she usually did, she might assume I’d only just gone out when she woke up, and there was no way I’d tell her the truth.

I read the note again, making sure I didn’t need to change anything, before placing it in the middle of the table. Excitement danced in my chest as I crept out of the kitchen again and walked towards the front door, pausing as I passed the lounge.

Mom was still asleep, though. She’d moved slightly. I was almost sure her head was at a different angle than it was before, but she’d probably just shifted in her sleep. Still, I felt the need to watch her for a few more seconds, making sure she was truly asleep.

It wouldn’t have surprised me if Mom was faking it to try to catch me doing something I shouldn’t have been doing, but I was pretty certain she wouldn’t have gone to the effort of snoring. I’d heard her yelling at my dad for snoring before, so I doubted she would ever believe she snored as well.

She had to be asleep, I told myself as I continued moving. I bent over, holding my backpack carefully so it wouldn’t make any noise. My hand froze as I grabbed my trainers, and I glanced at the pair of heels beside them. It was so tempting to wear them instead. I wanted to look nice, to look special, and that was the kind of thing people wore on dates. I assumed it was, anyway.

But it would have been foolish. I might have been able to convince my mom I’d spent all day at the gym, despite the dress I was wearing, but if I was also in heels, she’d get suspicious. She wore them almost all the time, but if I did it, she would start asking questions.

Reluctantly, I slipped my feet into my trainers and straightened up again. Moving slowly, I slipped my backpack from my shoulders and pulled out my keys before reaching towards the front door.

The lock was noisy sometimes, and my hands were sweaty as I twisted it, but luckily, I’d had a lot of practice sneaking out of the house without waking up my mom. It was a necessary skill to develop; I’d learnt that young. If I woke Mom up when she was asleep or passed out, she always got furious.

A smile pulled at my lips as the door swung open without making a sound. I darted through it and slid my key into the lock before twisting. That was the hardest part. I had to keep it pulled all the way to the right as I shut the door to ensure it didn’t catch, and then I could slowly release it. If I went too fast, it made a thunk that could wake her up, but if I went too slow, sometimes it squealed.

But luck was on my side. Despite my slick fingers, I managed to do it perfectly. I held my breath as I hurried to the window and peeked inside, checking to make sure Mom was still fast asleep.

She hadn’t even stirred, though. She was still in the same position as she had been before, and my heart pounded with triumph. I turned away from the window, keeping to the edge of the driveway where the gravel was the thinnest and therefore made the least noise, before unlocking my phone and typing a message.

I’m on my way!
