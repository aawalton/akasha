Jianyi slowly and carefully followed the tracks he had found in the underbrush. Before taking a step, he checked for twigs or dry leaves that would give his presence away to any creature with ears.

It wasn’t perfect, but he felt that he had made a lot of progress since that first day in the wilderness.

After 30 excruciating minutes, he heard the soft burbling of a stream in the distance and used that background noise to accelerate his progress slightly.

It took another fifteen minutes until he could see the stream through the trees. It was a small one, barely two paces wide, but deeper than one would expect. It seemed to terminate in a large pond fifty paces to the south of Jianyi’s position.

At the center of the pond was a small island with a pine tree that was significantly wider, not necessarily taller, than all the trees in the area. It stood out not just by its size but also by its type, as there were few pine trees in this part of the forest.

The tracks he had been following seemed to lead directly to the expansive body of water.

Carefully, Jianyi closed in further, then, about twenty paces from the pond, he knelt behind a tree to take everything in.

One thing that had become obvious, now that he was closer, was that the pond was not natural—or rather, it wasn’t the result of the water having carved its way into the rock and earth below. Instead, it was the result of a blockage that allowed just a fraction of the water that streamed into the area to proceed further.

Several trees and bushes had been brought down and held together by vibrant vines, and an impressive dam had been constructed. Now that he knew what to look for, Jianyi saw several tree stumps and even some of the still-standing trees had bite marks on their bark.

Slowly, he became aware of a slight pressure emanating from the tree at the center of the pond. While the qi density in their part of the forest was comparable to the outer sect, what was coming from the pine felt at least on par with the inner sect.

One of the most important points in Minghao’s stories over the last few days had been the need for patience.

A good hunter—and fighter—had the patience to wait for the right moment, for an opportunity to present itself.

Jianyi wasn’t sure that he could show the same kind of patience as a cultivator hundreds of years old, but in general, he could identify with the mindset. Patience, in his mind, was just another form of discipline, or rather an area where he could apply his discipline.

Rushing out to the pond might yield faster results, but there was no reason to do so. Important was the outcome—for the hunt or when it came to defeating the demons—faster was better, but late was better than never. With that in mind, Jianyi settled down to see what kind of beast specifically had made its home here, and whether he would be able to defeat it.

***

Sure, patience was key, but two hours later, Jianyi was running low on it.

There had been a few birds and hares, even a deer drinking at the pond, but Jianyi had ignored them all. The prize—or threat—was a larger one.

He slightly shifted from one foot to another. He was young, fit, and had qi running through his spirit; hovering in the same position for hours was still uncomfortable. Maybe more mentally than physically, but still.

Then, finally, he felt more than saw a slight stirring of the lake, close to the dam.

A few seconds later, dark eyes, framed by dark brown fur, emerged from the pond, followed by the rest of a large beaver.

It easily reached Jianyi’s hips, and the broad tail looked more wooden than leathery.

Finally! This was a spirit beast.

Jianyi tried to get a sense for the creature’s strength while the beaver was lazily swimming in circles around the pond, until finally cresting the small island in the center of its domain and rubbing its dense, oily fur against the bark of the tree.

Looking back to the raid on the estate of demonic cultists—which felt like it had been ages ago—he had made the mistake of thinking that his opponent had been in the Qi Condensation Realm, when in reality he had likely only been a few steps ahead of him in the Qi Gathering Realm.

This time, he didn’t have Elder Sun in the background to save him—getting this right was a question of life or death.

Spirit beasts were usually stronger than cultivators of the same realm in a one-on-one situation. The only thing that made him consider fighting the beast was that Elder Sun had said that his qi density was comparable to that of a cultivator a realm higher, thanks to his Celestial Core Card.

But that still meant that he couldn’t defeat a beast of the Qi Condensation Realm.

The beast being that close to the tree didn’t make the assessment easier. There was no doubt in his mind that the creature was stronger than him. Given that he was only on the first step of the first realm, that was almost a given anyway.

However, his gut told him that the beaver was not leagues stronger than him.

He nodded to himself. If he wanted to have a shot at progressing his body cultivation, he needed to take some risks—and this one seemed manageable.

He needed to be careful, though. Smart. Patient.

Sighing, he looked around, trying to come up with a plan.

Fighting the beaver while in the water did not seem ideal. Jianyi didn’t believe that his Bolt of Fire would kill the creature in one hit, as it had done with the mortal deer a few days earlier. That meant that he needed to engage with the spear, and doing so while wading through the pond and losing all mobility was destined to be a fast way to die.

The tracks that had led Jianyi to the pond were proof, though, that the beast left its pond from time to time. That would have to be the moment to attack.

That meant one thing: Jianyi needed to be patient.

***

The sun was close to sinking below the horizon when the beaver finally decided to leave the island in its pond and not just return to its dam but actually leave the core of its habitat.

Jianyi didn’t know whether the creature was searching for food or what else drove it to walk into the forest, but it was the opportunity he had been waiting for.

He continued to hide behind a tree, slowly moving to the side to make sure he would stay out of sight of the spirit beast. Only after it had made it a hundred paces into the forest did he follow, trying to apply all the lessons from Minghao on how to conceal his own presence.

When the creature was three hundred paces from the pond, Jianyi summoned two cards from his spirit to appear before him. In parallel, he accelerated his pace, trying to close in on the beaver.

Adrenaline started to flow in his veins, and he cursed as Force of the Forest and Verdant Mending appeared. With a slightly exaggerated exhalation, he tried to cycle the cards away, but things didn’t work that way—he had to wait for another moment before he could redraw from his techniques.

The beaver had turned around, clearly sensing him, and now looking at Jianyi with its dark eyes, the hair around his neck starting to bristle.

Bolt of Fire.

He went through the motions and, as he closed in on the beast, shot the qi construct at the beaver.

Time seemed to slow down as he chased his own fire attack, spear in hand—the first time that he would face such a creature on his own.

The beaver tried to dodge, but the bolt landed and burned into the creature’s side.

It bared its long, ivory teeth, whether in pain or anger was unclear, but Jianyi didn’t hesitate and followed his technique with a Piercing Fang form of his spear.

The attack landed, but didn’t penetrate deeply—the combination of fur and hide, coupled with him not being able to bring sufficient force behind his attack, limited its effectiveness.

The beaver went on the counteroffensive, rushing at him, and it took all of Jianyi’s dexterity and footwork to move out of the way. He quickly brought his spear forward, trying to keep the creature at a distance—getting bitten by these teeth would be a fast way to end the fight, and likely his life.

Blood trickled out of the beaver’s side, sliding off its oily fur.

Again, the beaver rushed him, but Jianyi managed to land a Flowing Current horizontal slice, extending the wound caused by his Bolt of Fire. The beaver screeched, but continued undeterred—Jianyi remained focused on the dagger-like teeth, but instead of coming at him directly, the creature rushed by him and lashed out with its long, wooden tail.

Jianyi was caught by surprise, but managed to somehow bring his spear between his chest and the tail, preventing his ribs from getting crushed—unfortunately, two of his fingers were caught between his spear and the tail. Sharp pain pierced through him. He couldn’t say whether the fingers were broken or only bruised, but the hit would definitely impact his grip strength.

He was only distracted for a moment by the hit, but it was enough time for the beaver to… not attack again, but get a head start on its race back towards its pond.

Despite the pain in his hand, Jianyi was unwilling to give up on the fight and ran after the beast. It took him a hundred paces or so to get back into a position from which he could attack—and he did so with a Blazing Arc. The overhead slash landed and managed to leave a significant wound. Again, the beaver screeched in pain, but did not stop its race back into its domain.

Rushing through some underbrush that Jianyi struggled to follow quickly, the beaver was close to its pond before Jianyi could launch another attack. He lunged and went for another Piercing Fang. The tip of his spear pierced the beast's shoulder, but at the same time, its tail started to shine in a green light, and green vines burst out of the pond, pulling both him and the beaver into the ice-cold water.

The cold grasped his heart. Within moments, he started to lose the feeling in his arms, and at the same time, he was surrounded by darkness, and he could feel the vines trying to wrap themselves around him, immobilizing him, trying to drown him.

Panic threatened to take over; for a moment, he flailed with his arms, trying to free himself—in vain.

Yet, he did not let go of his spear.

Yet, he did not lose the will to succeed.

His will.

Another thin crack opened in the granite on the bottom of his spirit lake.

He felt his will assert itself.

This was not a moment to panic.

This was a moment for action.

He summoned his cards.

Force of the Forest activated immediately—it was a swift card after all.

His helpless flailing turned into a burst of strength, and he managed to rip a vine from his left arm, then from his leg. Having reached the ground of the shallow pond, he used his strength to catapult himself up to the surface, out of the pond.

For a moment, a short, single moment, it felt like he was flying, hanging in the air.

The beaver was still close—instead of its usual elegant swimming, it was dragged by one of its vines due to its weakness.

Jianyi stretched himself, stretched the moment, and shot his spear forward.

With his momentum and the Force of the Forest, he pierced deep into the beaver.

A moment later, he felt the vines still moving in the pond, falling to the ground.

The beaver was dead.
