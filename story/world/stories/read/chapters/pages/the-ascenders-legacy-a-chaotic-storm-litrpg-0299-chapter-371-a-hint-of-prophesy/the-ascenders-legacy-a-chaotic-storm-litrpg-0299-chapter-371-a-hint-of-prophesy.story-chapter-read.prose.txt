Aodhán had once dreamt a dream.

Before the war, before all this bloodshed, he had dreamt of killing his friends in battle, of reversing an Origin Breach back on the Raventhorn, and of enemy Calamities descending upon him with murder in their eyes.

Several weeks had passed since that night.

Neither of the first two dreams had come to pass yet.

But as a ripple of space-time tore through the battlefield, Aodhán felt with sudden certainty that the third had arrived.

Cold spread through him, his eyes widening as he looked to Master Gyatso, then to the sky above, where Calamities should have been raging.

But he found it still—the heavens themselves frozen beneath the weight of what Zerathis had done.

It was the Icon War all over again, only this time it was worse, because Zerathis hadn’t just broken a rule.

He had broken a code of conduct.

No Calamity or Mythic could ever interfere in a battle between transmigrants

That was the rule.

That was what had allowed transmigrants to fight in a war this brutal without fear of a Calamity or Mythic simply wiping them out. As long as two transmigrants were fighting, no one was to interfere.

It was a rule that favored both sides, and everyone knew it.

Everyone had honored it… until now.

Until Zerathis Starless had blown the brains of a transmigrant apart for killing his disciple.

Revenge burned hot in the mind of every Calamity present, and for one impossible heartbeat, no one moved, as though waiting for some kind of permission.

The stillness lasted only a moment before Aodhán felt it.

Eyes. Intent. Hatred.

Dozens of them, all suddenly directed at him.

His heart stopped, fear gripping it in a chokehold as the cold spreading through him intensified.

“Aodhán,” Gyatso breathed quietly, the same fear reflected in his voice as he grabbed hold of his hands, clutching them protectively.

Reassuringly.

But that reassurance barely had time to become anything at all before the heavens shattered beneath the force of dozens of Calamities racing for enemy transmigrants to kill them.

Aodhán’s eyes widened, just as they had in that dream. His muscles locked, and his mind froze as terror crashed down on him in overwhelming waves.

He didn’t run. He couldn’t.

Not from so many Calamities. He would never get far.

Gyatso, however, didn’t hesitate to try, even though it was an effort in futility.

He had just found Aodhán again after so long. He wasn’t ready to lose him yet.

And without hesitation, he twisted space-time and pulled Aodhán into it, racing against the hand of fate itself as it loomed terrifyingly above them in judgment.

In another part of the battlefield, Nymera had Alaric Thorneveil by the throat when the silence struck.

Confused, she looked up, Alaric’s limp body dangling from her grasp.

She had no idea what had happened so far from Elias’s death, but the murderous intent gathering above could not be mistaken.

Eyes widening, Nymera descended from her position high in the sky—slowly, subtly—lest she be the first to disturb the battlefield’s oppressive stillness.

But she had barely descended a meter when the heavens shattered, and enemy Calamities descended upon her like vultures to carrion.

Terror blooming within her, she flung Alaric aside, her eyes widening further as her mind raced for a solution.

But there was none.

One Calamity she could potentially evade, but this many?

She could neither run nor hide.

So she did the only thing she could.

She bought time.

Raising her arms to the storm above, she whispered, “Will Embodiment: I am the Eye of the Storm. Nothing Reaches Me.”

And from the heavens themselves, beyond the raging storm above, the Origin Plane of Storm answered. A darker, more nefarious storm bled into existence around her.

It descended in the form of a raging tornado so powerful that it pulled at the fabric of reality itself with its currents.

The Origin Storm stretched for miles in every direction, pitch-black and growing according to Nymera’s desire. At its center, Nymera floated, energy bleeding off her in waves as she basked in the storm’s protection, knowing full well it couldn’t last long against Calamities.

She might be the strongest storm transmigrant in the sect, her abilities growing according to her desire, but even she couldn’t hope to stop a Calamity, let alone dozens.

Sooner or later, something would give.

Her desire for protection or the might of dozens of Calamities.

And Nymera feared it wouldn’t be the latter.

After Seraphine’s arrest, Sophran had returned to the battlefield to aid his allies, throwing himself into battle against the one called Butcher.

Still high on victory, he had hoped to arrest another enemy transmigrant.

But over the last two hours, Butcher had proven far deadlier than Seraphine ever was.

Cultivating the path of the Red Hunt, he was an animal of terrifying persistence, his power rising with every injury Sophran suffered.

Despite cursing him thrice since their battle began, Sophran still found himself on the back foot, wounds blooming across his body with every exchange.

Vitality surged through him, healing his wounds as quickly as they opened, but even that monstrous regeneration was beginning to lag behind the pace of Butcher’s violence.

The man came on without pause, ragged crimson robes snapping behind him as he tore through the air in a blur of red mist.

Sophran unleashed a storm of lightning blades, slicing through the air with insane speed, but Butcher was faster.

He crossed the space between them without injury, and Sophran barely twisted aside before clawed fingers raked across his ribs, carving deep enough to show bone.

Sophran retaliated with a beam of lightning that struck point-blank, blasting Butcher into the earth with the force of a meteor.

Halo rushed forward, paws outstretched to curse him again, but Butcher vanished before it could happen, his body dissolving into bloody mist as he reformed only an inch from Sophran’s face, sharpened teeth snapping for his throat.

Sophran jerked backward, his body exploding into vapor just as those jaws closed on empty air.

He reformed behind Butcher with a crack of lightning already gathered in his palm and drove it forward.

The bolt struck squarely, blasting Butcher through three shattered ridges of earth.

But Butcher was up again in a blink, a massive wave of jagged blood tendrils lashing out toward Sophran.

Sophran created several shield constructs to block, but the blood tendrils simply curved around them, fluid yet razor-sharp.

They struck in an instant, slicing off both of Sophran’s arms in one fell swoop.

Sophran screamed in pain as blood gushed from the wounds—only for it to be turned into blades that surged back into his flesh with the intent of ripping him apart from within.

Unbound Sky saved him for the hundredth time in the last two hours, but it didn’t save him from a spinning wave of blood that tore a hole straight through his vapor form.

Sophran coughed up blood, his entire abdomen ripped apart as he lost control of his vapor form.

Butcher pounced in that moment, teeth bared like a predator sighting wounded prey, but Sophran wasn’t quite finished yet.

A forest of jagged lightning vines burst from the earth beneath him, their edges gleaming as they rushed toward Butcher.

Just as the vines struck, however, the sudden silence of the battlefield forced both of them to falter.

Having drifted so far from the main battlefield, they barely realized what it meant until it was too late.

Until the sky broke and Calamities descended upon them—enemies and allies alike—some rushing to save and others to kill.

There was nowhere to run. Nowhere to hide.

Only fight.

So Sophran tapped into the near-bottomless reserves of his vitality, Living Convergence spreading outward in search of allies even as he screamed to Halo, “Protect me.”

Several lines of text crawled across his vision as Halo blessed him repeatedly, stacking perk upon perk atop one another.

Power flooded him, immense and overwhelming, so vast that his body shone like the sun itself.

But against the might of Calamities, Sophran feared it still wasn’t enough.

And when the first attack ripped his entire body to pieces, he realized just how severe an understatement “not enough” had been.

But while all that happened, one transmigrant was noticeably absent from the battlefield.

Ashscar found himself adrift, suspended above the Storm Sect viewing deck, miles and miles away from the battlefield.

While every other transmigrant ran, hid, or fought for their lives, he had been wrenched out of danger by the storm itself, plucked from the chaos without his consent.

Blinking, disoriented as his consciousness dragged itself out of what seemed to be a trance, he stared up at the roiling clouds, his heart heavy as he waited to hear something—anything at all—from the storm.

Seconds passed in silence, and when it became obvious the storm wasn’t speaking, he asked, “My friends are in danger. I want to protect them.”

For a long moment, he heard nothing. But just as he deigned to ask again, a rumble came—faint, distant.

Troubled.

Ashscar grimaced as fear knotted in his gut. His friends were in danger, and he could do nothing to help them save one.

Lifting his gaze to the storm once more, he asked, “How do you intend to save them?”

The storm rumbled again—stronger, deeper—and this time, Ashscar smiled, his eyes glazing over as words that were not his own appeared upon his tongue.

Power settled over him—not the wild violence of battle, but something older. Calmer. Vast.

His mouth opened.

“When the ascendant of storm gathers, the scattered kingdoms fall—not from his wrath, but from the weight of their own contradictions, finally made one.”

But his words weren’t just prophesy. They were remembrance.

And as they left his lips, they dragged a future that should have remained far beyond the horizon into the present and set it in motion.

A future of pain and death.

Of power and ruin.

A future of Authority.

And far away, racing through the chaotic void itself, Aodhán shuddered as something within him stirred.

Something that should have laid dormant for years yet. Something inherited in blood.

It stirred, tingling beneath the surface of his skin like a rush of adrenaline itself. But with death so close, Aodhán barely noticed.

And so the power waited to be acknowledged. Waited for the moment it would be needed.

It didn’t take long at all.
