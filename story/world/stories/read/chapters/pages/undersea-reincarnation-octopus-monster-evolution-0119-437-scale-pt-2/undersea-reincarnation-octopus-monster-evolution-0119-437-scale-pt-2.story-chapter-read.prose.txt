Like an ever-growing, dark cloud above their heads, the number of ships kept swelling throughout the next week, casting its shadow over any success Henry, Maurice, and Velistraine achieved. In a way, it allowed them free rein to nip at the swarm’s heels, tearing into ships with impunity before teleporting away. But with every raid they scored, the swarm adapted.

The first adaptation was arguably the worst. Whenever an attack happened, it was only a matter of seconds before the war queen herself appeared, and through the week, her response time had grown shorter and shorter. Henry never allowed a clash to take place, opting to destroy anything she was about to reach, but her intervention certainly made things hard.

The second adaptation was that ships were rarely alone. They flew in packs, which made dispatching their content take a bit more time. In small numbers, it was fine as it gave enough meat for all three of them, but they definitely had to pick and choose their targets.

The third adaptation was the trapped ships. Not as in ships that would detonate or anything of the sort, as that couldn’t do much to them at this point. Instead, it was ships filled with elites, and elites only.

Henry and Maurice had leveled, even if it wasn’t Henry’s priority to level. Just by infusing Unfettered Echoes with a continuous stream of harvested power, some of that power inevitably ended up watering its surroundings, as it were, which expressed itself in levels. But even with levels, a couple dozens elites was a pain in the neck to deal with. Especially when their composition often varied. It was generally a good windfall when they managed to break through, but more than once, Henry was forced to retreat as one of his scouts caught the movement of the war queen.

Thankfully, retreating was exceedingly easy for him. With thousands of Avatars around, Henry just needed to have a couple of Avatars on Velistraine and Maurice, then throw them in storage whenever he needed to before detonating everything in range. And while that was a little too reactive to his taste, there really weren’t that many ways to keep track of the queens. Once they disappeared in their big ships, at the heart of their armada, it was a bit difficult to keep track of them. Find the Path seemed to just bounce off of them, and well, there was also a risk of that skill backfiring. They might potentially be able to track him back with it. Not that he had to worry about that too much as he never got much out of the attempts.

Hovering far, far from the armada, Henry, in his smaller kraken form, sent his awareness to the other side of the massive sphere of orbiting ships. They’d just been pushed away from the attacks north of the doorway, so now was the time to send some suicide-bombers from the south.

This way, Henry could keep feeding Unfettered Echoes, while he chipped at the numbers of the invaders. As productive and busy the next week turned out to be, all of their progress was being crushed under the sheer number of ships that kept joining the gateway’s position. If a week ago the number had been impressive, now it was just unnerving. There were tens of thousands of ships orbiting the gateway. Maybe hundreds of thousands. Ships. Each containing at least one A-rank elite that would give any mid-grade A-rank from their world a difficult fight, and there were thousands of these. More unnerving than their number was the complete absence of the architect queen. Something Henry very much did not like.

What was she up to? Was she nearing a breakthrough with the door? Was she attempting another breach through the System’s protections?

The stress was enough to keep him motivated, and with the numbers of ships he was single-handedly destroying and stranding, Henry managed to push Unfettered Echoes three more levels, while also breaking through A-rank with Predation, using the opportunity to upgrade Spiteful Detonation and Toxin Production to A-rank.

Those had been his bread and butter to tackling the armada, and while it felt like a drop in the bucket, he was leveling, and he was keeping the swarm busy. As busy as a predator could be when pestered by an angry fly. For every ship he destroyed, ten more would show up the next day. And there was no stopping the damn things. It seemed that a good portion of them had been stationed closer to the nearby sun, and now they were making their way back here.

Henry had a bad feeling about it. They had to make sure everyone was ready. He really hoped the System had been tucking its high A-ranks and its ascended somewhere, because they really were going to need them. Henry and his companions might eventually out-level the swarm and win, but it would take months, if not years considering the rate at which the damn things kept coming.

If they made no mistake that would damn them all, that was.

Henry turned his attention to the line of Avatars he’d sent far, far from the swarm. Deep into space. He’d been working on the extension for a simple reason a few days ago. Once Unfettered Echoes was powerful enough to send them back, he needed to do it as far from the swarm as possible. Just in case the architect happened to be spying on them or in case it had a way of following them through.

Because Unfettered Echoes was finally ready. And he was confident in its capabilities, as he’d spent the whole week feeling his way back home with every level he’d gained. When the Aspect had hit the fourth level, Henry had sensed the connection to his Avatars strengthen, but he’d also sensed the distinctive, obfuscating veil that he could only attribute to the System’s protection. Henry hadn’t tried to push his way through right then and there, just in case, and he continued farming. On the fifth level, Henry felt that connection strengthen, and he felt like he could almost sense something beyond the veil. At this point, infusing The Trickster’s Dancing Constellation with some of Velistraine’s amplifying Aspect, as well as some of Unfettered Echoes’ own essence gave him back flashes of colors and the scent of vegetation. Henry was almost sure he could make the jump, but he felt it was too soon to test the System’s protection. One more level, and he’d do it.

He’d gotten that level five hours ago, and he was now more confident of their success. Because Henry could sense them. His Avatars. Spread out through the reach. He had some more Avatars throughout the world, of course, but he’d left so many in the Reach that they just shone brighter in his mind, and it was still like looking down a dark, long barrel. But it was there.

With Unfettered Echoes at level 6, Henry was ready to make the jump. Which was why he was using his last throwaway Avatars as bombs, to inflict as much damage as possible while he readied himself to disappear. Already, he’d pulled everything within his Maw, having decided not to leave anything for the bugs to use, and a couple dozen miles away, Maurice and Velistraine were waiting for him, hidden within the SS Henry.

So Henry joined them, sparing the swarm of tens of thousands of ships behind him. They weren’t particularly bright in and of themselves, but the fact that even the stars behind them were obstructed said it all.

Henry, for a moment, recalled seeing those clouds of insects on rivers, or better yet, massive bird flocks as they rippled and swarmed.

It was a bit of the same out here. Just… more world threatening than a bunch of birds.

Henry teleported away. He didn’t swap places with a clone, instead using the teleportation he’d been working on with Maurice. They still lacked a way of seeing the position they were supposed to arrive at, but his Avatars allowed him to bypass that.

Most importantly, he didn’t want to leave a clone behind. Or at least, he wanted to leave as few Avatars as possible, and now that he was in position, he dispelled the Avatars that were still connecting him back to the road and the swarm.

Being stranded in space was a little worrisome, but the thing with a swarm of their size was that they were still visible, even from nearly fifty miles away.

In any case, it was time to go home.

Appearing in the central—and only deck—of the SS Henry, Henry found his two companions pacing worriedly. Well. Velistraine was pacing, while Maurice was rubbing his claws together. They were nervous, but there was an undeniable excitement below it as well. Without a word, both looked up at him, and Henry nodded to them.

“It’s time.”

They’d talked it through plenty of times through the week, and there wasn’t much else to discuss now. Everything was planned out, from success to failure, to what they were to do once they made it back. So wordlessly, the two approached him, their gaze firming, until all three stood in a circle. Without closing his eyes, Henry connected himself to the skill that handled the range and spread of his network of Avatars, and reached out with two arms. Maurice tapped a white, glowing claw on one arm, while Velistraine did the same on his other hand. The essence she channeled to him was a deep, joyous red, worthy of the name of Boundless Spirit of The Adventurer, and its color contrasted strongly with Maurice’s white, ephemeral Arcane Seer of Hidden Horizons that made the air around it ripple and whisper by its presence.

Henry was looking forward to a time where they didn’t need to manage their essences as much so he could experiment with these Aspect flavors, but now was the time to focus.

Pulling the two essences within, Henry tamed the wills within and mingled them with the amplifying force of Unfettered Echoes. The two foreign essences didn’t fight him much, as with both their owners’ attention still on him, the essences were docile, and one drop at a time, Henry channeled the empowering cocktail to the root of his passive skill.

Immediately, his mana rushed through his veins, and the thin, straw-like tunnel he’d been sensing his faraway Avatars widened.

Henry began channeling a swap. Here, he would have loved to teleport and not leave anything behind him, but this was too important to mess up with a half-formed teleportation skill. Henry already had an Octomind on the task of making sure the Avatar that’d take his place would dissolve itself as soon as the transfer was complete.

The connection to his Avatars continued to grow stronger, and the skill nexus filled up with power as Henry focused on his target through the distracting fog of the System. Somewhere along the way, Henry sensed a foreign attention, like a blanket attempting to snuff out his endeavor, and then, just as suddenly as he sensed it, it retracted, and before Henry managed to figure out what that meant, the skill was ready.

With a decisive nod, Henry met his companion’s gaze, who looked slightly surprised before they disappeared within his Maw. Henry tucked in the SS Henry as well, then, with one last mental sweep, he made sure nothing was left behind.

Henry found nothing but the cold darkness of space, and in the distance, the swarm that sought to consume all of them. And once they dealt with it, they’d have access to the gateway and to wherever that led them.

It was an exciting thought.

With a flick of his will, Henry allowed the swap to take place, and in that brief, powerful connection he shared with the Avatar that was to take his place, he gave it its orders before darkness swallowed him.
