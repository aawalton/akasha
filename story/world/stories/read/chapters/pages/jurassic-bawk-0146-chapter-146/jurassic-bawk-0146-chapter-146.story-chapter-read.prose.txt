Charlemagne emerged from the Ark and flapped over to the ship.  The entire vessel was a mess from fighting off the pirates, but he wasted no time in ordering the crew to set sail.  If the captain had any thoughts to the contrary, they remained unvoiced, and the sailors swiftly transitioned from making repairs to getting the ship ready to set sail in under a minute.

“Hey, wait!  Are we leaving already?” Josephine called as Phatagin and Phasima made their way back on board.  After inadvertently triggering the Ark’s door, she had returned to her spot at the base of the island’s one sad little tree and was enjoying a cup of tea that she had produced from her extradimensional storage.  “Seriously?  My stomach is finally settling and…”

“Bawk!” the rooster yelled.

“Rude!” the Attendant hollered back, but she got to her feet anyway.  “I didn’t mean that I wanted to live here forever, you unchivalrous lout!  Just for a few hours, please.  I can’t take another night on that boat…I just can’t.”

Phasima glanced at the sky for a moment before suggesting a compromise.

“Charlemagne, the ship is very badly damaged and probably won’t sail at anywhere near its top speed.  And night is coming soon.  How about we let the sailors fix up the ship, and Josephine can sleep on the island?”

The rooster stopped for a moment to consider the idea.  He was still in a foul mood, but Phasima’s advice was usually sound.  That being said, he didn’t want to wait around, and he was very mad that he couldn’t eat a bunch of legendary weapons, and the boredom of traveling for days over the largely empty ocean was starting to weigh heavily on him.  He longed to sink his talons into dirt or grass, or, even better, a cow.  The rations that the ship was packed with were all way too salty for his taste.

Noticing that Charlemagne was vacillating, Phasima sweetened the deal.

“And, if you’re still not sure if that’s a good idea, I found two barrels full of the good stuff inside the Ark.  Noticed them on the way out…”

“Bawk!” the rooster agreed hastily.

In the aftermath of Charlemagne imbibing two full casks of a drink that the System described as “Fortified Baptismal Spirits”, Phatagin decided to join Josephine and Phasima for a picnic inside the Ark, which was apparently large enough to not rock with the motion of the waves.  The trio made themselves comfortable in a corner of the vessel, chatting as they ate.

“So, what are the odds that Charlemagne will accidentally destroy the ship, and we’ll have to fly to Bluebeard’s cove, or whatever it is that the System said was our final destination?”

“Bluebeard’s Chamber, whatever that means,” the Attendant corrected.

Phasima waved a hand in dismissal.  “Yeah, yeah, Chamber.  It’s basically the same thing as a cove, right?”

“I do not believe they are similar at all,” Phatagin added.  “By the way, this tea is quite good.  What type is it?”

“It’s made from the slime of a small animal that lives in moist, underground hollows, mixed with the excretions of two different rare sea creatures.  In fact, I’m not sure what else is in it; I just happened to have a bunch lying around before…well, you know.  Want me to see if I have the recipe somewhere?”  the former Empress of the Forest offered.

Josephine’s face turned green again as she set down her cup with a loud clink.

Phatagin raised his cup to his elongated snout and took another careful sip.

“Delicious,” he announced with satisfaction.  “Although it appears that there will never be more of this tea again.  You should savor it for as long as possible.”

“Eh, there’s no reason to hoard it,” Phasima responded with a shrug.  “Yeah, it’s one of the last relics of my universe.  But if no one but me ever drank it, then the knowledge of what it tastes like will die with me too.”

“What about your sister,” Josephine asked.  “You could share it with her.”

“She hates the stuff, says that it tastes rancid,” came the reply.  “I might as well enjoy it with you.  Besides, I don’t know when I’ll see her again.”

“So, I think that you said she’s also working for the System, like you and me, right?” Josephine inquired, although she already knew the answer.

The Vessarian nodded.

“Yeah, it’s the safest thing that we can do, you know.  There are plenty of ‘collectors’ out there that want to capture and exhibit rare lifeforms.  I don’t know exactly how many others like me are out there, but there can’t be very many.”

“Any idea what she’s doing?  Are you able to talk often?”

“No and no, you know how expensive those multiversal communication terminals are.  Once I get paid for this gig, I’ll send her a note.  She better not be doing anything too dangerous…”

“Is your sister fond of risking her life?” Phatagin asked.  He had finished the tea and was eyeing the pot.  Phasima poured him another cup before responding.

“Well, she’s a bit of a battle junkie like me.  But, unlike me, she doesn’t seem to understand the difference between simulation fighting and combat where permadeath is an option.”

“Permadeath?” the pangolin queried.

“Yeah, you know, there are places where you can die and the System will make sure you make it out okay, like these Systemic Games.  But in most of the multiverse, that isn’t the case.”

“I tend to spend most of my time in places where the System will protect me,” Josephine added.  “My Skill set is great for diplomacy and politics, but not so much when it comes to fighting.  I’ve worked as an Attendant for a few decades now.  I don’t level up much, but I don’t have to worry about being married off for a political alliance or used as a hostage.”

“You nobility?” Phasima asked.

“Worse, royalty,” Josephine responded.  “I’m twenty-fourth in line to inherit the throne of the planet Altantaria.  Well, at least I was, before the System offered me a job.  My family probably has declared me dead.  Or, if they figured out that I’m alive and kicking, they’ll probably disowned me.”

“Oof, that’s rough,” the Vessarian said.  “Family can be hard.  My sister and I don’t always see eye to eye, but I can count on her to have my back.  And I’d do the same for her, permadeath or no permadeath.”

Josephine smiled.

“Maybe I should send my parents a message when we’re through with this event.  I’ve been thinking hard about contacting them and just letting them know I’m doing well.  Maybe if I tell them I’m working for the System, they won’t be so mad at me.”

“They are going to be mad at you in any event,” Phasima remarked.

“How could that be?” Phatagin demanded.  “Mayhap they will be delighted to receive a message from her?”

“Either they’ll be mad that she took a job with the System without asking for their permission, or they’ll be mad it took her so long to contact them about it,” the Vessarian answered.

“That…that sounds about right,” Josephine said, her burst of enthusiasm largely faded at the thought.  “I think I’m going to try anyways.  I’d like to think that I have a home to return to at some point rather than…oh, Phasima, I’m so sorry.”

“Naww, it’s fine.  It’s been a long time since I came to terms with the fact that I’m unlikely to ever meet another Vessarian besides my sister.”

“Perhaps you could send out a generalized query,” Phatagin suggested.

“Pffft, like I’m made of currency,” Phasima snorted.  “Listen, do you want to talk, or do you want to drink?”

“I beg your pardon,” the pangolin said, blinking with surprise.  “Are we not already drinking?”

“I think she means alcohol,” Josephine replied.

“Yes, I am talking about alcohol.  I didn’t give Charlemagne everything, and my Analyze Skill is telling me that whatever this ‘Fortified Baptismal Wine’ is, it’s potent enough to affect someone with a modified Durability of over 5000.  So we can get drunk!”

“I do not believe that I have ever been ‘drunk’,” Phatagin mused.  “Is it pleasant?”

“It is, right up until it isn’t!” Phasima answered.

“It must be experienced to be understood,” Josephine added, causing Phasima to nod sagely.

“Well then, I suppose that, for the sake of gaining knowledge, I must accept,” the pangolin agreed.

Phatagin woke up the next morning with a splitting headache and a snout that felt like it was lined with sandpaper.

“Wha—why did I do that?” he groaned as he pushed himself onto all fours.  The mild exertion made the ground beneath him spin, and a wave of nausea crashed over the pangolin.

Phasima laughed as she watched Phatagin attempt to regain his balance.

“Can’t hold your liquor, huh?” she laughed.

“Shhhhhh, some of us are trying to die over here,” Josephine hissed from the very corner of the Ark, where she had wedged herself.  A hand towel soaked in water rested on her forehead.  She looked absolutely miserable.

“You know that we have to set sail soon, right?” Phasima joked, prompting both Phatagin and Josephine to groan in anguish.

“Why don’t you sit tight, and I’ll go check to see if Charlemagne’s about ready to head out.  That’s assuming he didn’t just leave us behind…”

Before either of the two could respond, the Vessarian popped out of the Ark to check on the ship, noting that the sun had already been up for some time.  The sailors were still hard at work fixing up the vessel, working tirelessly through the night.  In fact, the repairs appeared to be almost complete, and some of the crew had moved on to other tasks.

Where’s Charlemagne? Phasima wondered to herself as she looked around.  The rooster was nowhere to be found.

As soon as the sun rose, Charlemagne had shaken off the last vestiges of his drunken stupor, feeling thirsty but alert.  His incredible Durability and powerful Physique Skill had simply burned the alcohol away while he slept.  Noting that the others hadn’t returned from the Ark, and that the ship was still being repaired, the rooster made a snap decision: he would simply proceed to the Floor’s exit by air, eliminating the need for the ship at all.  Then, once he’d completed the Floor, the others could catch up.  After all, they had their uses.

Taking to the sky, Charlemagne accelerated to his top cruising speed, leaving just a little in the tank in case he needed to fight when he arrived at Bluebell’s Creamery, or whatever the name of his destination was.  Nimrod’s Unbreakable Cudgel, which was still lodged in his stomach, provided the directions in a manner that was impossible to ignore and yet equally impossible to describe.  Ignoring how the effect worked, the rooster flew onward.

Less than an hour later, Charlemagne spotted his destination: an enormous round tower made of white stone that appeared to rise endlessly out of the water.  Surrounding the tower was a barrier island that almost completely closed off the waters around the massive structure.  Almost, but not quite.  For there was a channel about twice as wide as his ship that cut through the island.  Twin forts guarded each side of the entrance, while almost two dozen of the same type of pirate ship that the rooster had previously fought were stationed in the waters protected by the barrier island.

Charlemagne dove to attack.

He once again slammed right into an impenetrable mana barrier that prevented anyone from accessing the tower without first defeating the System-generated defenses.

The rooster turned around to depart as his shattered bones pulled themselves back together, as his Mach 3 encounter with the barrier had not been gentle.  It was then that he noticed something that brightened his bad mood just a little bit.

The forts were outside the barrier.
