David stirred with a jolt; his body hit with a spiritual shock when his soul snapped back to it. Like putting on an old glove, his consciousness extended back to the far reaches of his limbs and felt about as flexible as dry leather.

His eyes shot open.

Niala.

With a corpse-waking groan, he forced himself up on shaking arms and legs. Two hands of stone, powerful enough to crush him, gently hooked under his arms and lifted him the rest of the way.

Jordo's eyes shone. “Sir! It is good to see you awake! Your readings have been most perplexing! I am overjoyed to see you hale and sound!”

David blinked, legs still undecided if they were solid or boneless. “Jordo! I- Niala, I have to go!” he said, pushing himself off of the guardian golem and taking a few tentative steps. When he didn't fall on his face, he hopped in place until he felt stable, and glanced back at the source of grunts behind him.

Sybil and Sammie, mirroring him a few moments ago, were in the process of picking themselves up from the floor. Jordo was beside them within an eye blink, and helped them up.

Sammie, looking around, met David's eyes. “Morning, sir. That was a really long and boring dream.”

Beside him, the bunnykin bent and rested her hands on her legs. “I kept kicking the walls, but nothing happened.”

David eyed them both, seeing a mix of confusion and weariness as they looked back at him, but also resolve.

“Niala is up ahead. I'm going first. You and Jordo follow up once you can,” David said before facing the corridor leading further in and erupting in blue manafire.

Before they could ask what they were following him into, he took a step, and then exploded forward, leaving behind an honest-to-the-founding gods dust cloud.

Sammie and Sybil stared for a second, then looked at each other. She grinned, then straightened up, flexed her legs a few times, then willed her Quick Step imbuement on and ran after her boss.

Sammie sighed and did the same, with maybe less enthusiasm, because while he cared about his employers, what sane person ran toward danger with enthusiasm?

ORCHESTRATOR CENTRAL ROOM

A shock had Niala jerk her hand away from the giant crystal before she even opened her eyes. She looked up and gasped; what had once been a smooth gem was now crisscrossed with lightning-like scars. The glow from within was erratic and angry, as if a storm was raging.

She took a few steps back, unsure what to do next; Dialogue had said it would make the crystal vulnerable, and that David's blast-bomb would work, but then what?

The facet had seemed certain the room itself wouldn't be affected. Was she willing to trust that?

A toneless voice rang out of the crystal, booming through the room, loud enough that she tried to cover her ears with her hands to lessen the pain.

“Situation critical. Vulnerability detected. Inner core is fracturing. Initiating isolation protocol.”

Before she could finish thinking that this didn't sound good, dozens of the pale yellow energy tentacles from before speared out of the crystal, sending her scrambling back.

They flailed for a few heartbeat, then stilled, turned her way, and dove toward her, tip first.

She flexed the aether node between her shoulder blades, and Hope's Light flashed into existence, folding around her just as the tentacles reached her in a hail of spiking strikes. The force of the attack launched her backward, hurtling several meters before sliding a few more along the smooth floor.

That had been a killing strike, Niala realized with wide eyes, even as she saw the tentacles re-aligning for another attack.

Only to get entangled as several changed direction and coiled around their brethren. Some still reached her, but too few to get past her protective wings, which slapped them away.

Steeling herself, she sprang back to her feet and grasped the winds with her mind, directing them into a cyclone centred on the crystal even as Hope's Light parried whichever tentacles managed to untangle and strike at her.

Under the erratic assault, she retrieved and unfurled a small cargo cloth. Trusting her wings to keep her safe, she activated the cloth and grabbed the hand crate that came out of it, flinging it and its contents into the burgeoning tornado with a heave.

The potions within dispersed, carried by the winds, and began to shatter against the tentacles, triggering a mishmash of effects: acid, fire, freezing, glue, and so many more.

Most had little effect, as before, though the sticky substances did manage to slow the appendages down somewhat. Niala, under the cover of her embattled wings, frowned when she noticed one of the tentacles smoke and fizzle, and eventually go limp before flickering out of existence.

Unfortunately, she had no idea which potion had hit it, but that meant she had something-

She was launched to the side, squealing, before rolling along the floor a few times. Her wings were quick to cover her prone form, but the tentacles' assault was gaining in intensity; a peek revealed that the few “allied” limbs were being restrained, freeing up the rest of them to attack her.

Pulling Hope's Light tight around her, she did her best to form a bulwark, eyes going wide in alarm as she noted the light of her wings dimming under the repeated assault.

As the tentacles pulled back to form a single mass, aiming to spear her in one single strike, she felt it; a pressure in her pathways, like a storm front rolling in.

She only had the time to smile before a blazing fireball of blue mana shot out of the corridor leading into the room, straight at the crystal.

David crashed into the Orchestrator with enough force to blow out the air. The deafening sonic crack rebounded through the room. The nearest light tentacles burned away where they met his inflamed mana, as did the next dozen that turned and tried to spear the newest threat.

The man, still in mid-air, eyes dripping congealed mana, called upon his Enduring Flesh imbuement, then Limit Break, and finally, mixed Strong Arm with Battering Ram.

He pulled his fist back, pushing the immense torrent of mana coursing through his body into those four powers. Before the air managed to rush back into the void his initial assault had caused, he punched.

The impact was felt through bones rather than heard. The crystal shook, and moved. Only a few centimetres, but the Orchestrator's tentacles went rigid for a few seconds, as if the terrible mind was unable to understand what had just happened.

As David himself was pushed back and fell to the floor, in the ensuing silence, a snap thundered, and at the point of impact, a crack formed. Small, a minor thing, but present nonetheless.

“Physical damage to containment vessel detected. Source undetermined. Running diagnostics. Isolation protocol still in effect. Warning: Isolation has decreased; additional deterrents have been authorized.”

David, straightening up from his landing, glanced at Niala, who smiled back at him from under her wings. His lips began to curl into a smile of his own, and then a pulse of energy flared out from the crystal, crashing into him like a physical wall.

As he was sent flying back, the energy pulse shattered Niala's control over the winds, dispersing her tornado, before washing over her and Hope's Light, which shone brightly, countering the effect, but dimmed to near-transparency afterwards.

More energy tentacles burst out of the crystal, aiming themselves at the room's occupants. Half went for David, the other half to Niala.

The ones aimed at her husband met his own mana field and melted trying to reach him. The ones aimed at her pounded on her depleted wings. She got up, turned toward David and dashed for him, but she could feel Hope's Light disintegrating. As the last shred of it dissipated, she glanced back to see a trio of tentacles spearing toward her, their needle points aiming at vitals.

She threw herself to the side at the last second, yet one still managed to hit her shoulder. She screamed and tumbled forward as it pierced and ripped her flesh.

“NIALA!” David's voice boomed as he picked himself up from the floor, even as more lethal tentacles dove toward his wife...

And were met midway by two radiant Falcodrakes, smashing into the appendages with primal savagery, biting and ripping the tentacles apart.

Both David and Niala went wide-eyed at the spectacle, the aetheric mounts going wild as they began eradicating anything that moved around them.

A second later, Sybil and Sammie were next to Niala, her with a blade engulfed in mana fire and held up in a guard, batting tentacles away, him kneeling down and dousing her wound with a healing potion before helping her to her feet.

He turned his head toward David. “Sir! We'll get her out of here! Do what you need to do!”

The man nodded his thanks and turned his attention to the appendages still trying to reach him, and toward the giant crystal in the middle of it all. As Sybil, Sammie and their mount beat a fighting retreat, he analyzed the situation.

Apparently, one of his blast bombs would work, but he was doubtful the tentacles would let the bomb properly activate. He also needed to be out of this room when it went off.

There was a single solution that came to mind. He hoped the few assumptions he had were true.

He positioned himself between the crystal and the departing group, intercepting tentacles, burning them off as they tried to go past him.

The Orchestrator never stopped flinging ineffectual attacks his way. Even another energy pulse didn't move him, now that he knew what to expect; he braced and triggered a Limit Break Tremor Step just as it reached him, the shockwave rushing out to meet the incoming wall, both shattering on each other.

He stood his position for a few minutes, until he figured they had gone far enough. Then, he closed his eyes and called upon the most difficult imbuement he had learned so far, and the one he was barely able to control: Mind Acceleration.

The effect sputtered; his perception of time slowed and quickened in spasms. His concentration threatened to break, but he clamped down on it. He didn't need long, just a few seconds.

As soon as the imbuement seemed to normalize, he dove into his inner realm. There, he called upon his Mana, condensing it into a single marble. Leviathan understood right away and lent his lord his help in shaping the mana.

For he was needed out in the physical world, and according to just how much mana his Lord was pushing, he was going to be big.

Dialogue felt bruised and battered. She, because that sounded right- she, not it, didn't have a body, so her wounds weren't in the physical sense. She was definitely wounded, however, and she was not winning her fight with the rest of the Orchestrator.

She was a lantern trying to keep a storming darkness at bay, and her light was fading. Yet, she pushed on. She was somewhat aware of what was going on outside; she was still part of the Orchestrator, after all, and she was taking a certain amount of pleasure in its confusion.

Those individuals had been full of surprises! Doing things that her creator had never thought possible! Things she wouldn't have been able to see if she hadn't been freed by Niala.

It was, as far as a final experience in this existence, a great show. Had she allowed herself to be a little selfish and greedy, she would have wished she could have seen more.

Oh, she just knew there were so many individuals out there to talk with! Because she'd realized she loved talking! It's what she'd been made for, after all!

A shame that, right after learning of her love of it, she would never be able to do so anymore.

But the Orchestrator was too terrible and too dangerous to remain. She pushed her nascent feelings, including that strange melancholic pain that followed them, deep inside of her, and concentrated on what mattered: destabilizing the crystal's structure, enough to make it vulnerable.

Just then, she felt a pulse of mana, and something weird appeared at the edge of her consciousness. She could feel the Orchestrator stutter in puzzlement as well, just for a second, before it continued its attempts at ridding its surroundings of any and all presences.

She smirked when she detected irritation, as much as it could feel, when the weird something coiled around the crystal, enveloping it, and somehow sealing its limbs in. Or, rather, keeping them from sprouting out.

Then, an itch as something touched its surface; a rapidly expanding rash that they couldn't scratch.

She knew what that was; the individual's bomb.

It was happening, finally.

She was tired, but glad that it would be over soon. No more individuals to see brainwashed and repurposed, their freedom stolen away. A freedom she had been denied from the start.

Now, she would know the bliss of non-existence.

The bomb detonated. The Orchestrator ran through its protocols until the last moment, still unable to understand what was happening.

Her mind began to fracture as the crystal shattered. The darkness she floated in was pierced by a multitude of blinding beams of light.

And then a giant sea serpent swooped in and devoured her.
