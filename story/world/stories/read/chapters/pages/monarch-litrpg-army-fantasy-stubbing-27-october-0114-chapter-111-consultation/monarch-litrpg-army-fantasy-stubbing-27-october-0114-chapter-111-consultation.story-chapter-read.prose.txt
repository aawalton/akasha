Rayne moved through the camp with steady steps, though his mind was anything but that.

It had been like this ever since he had woken up a few days ago and learned about everything that had happened. His body was mostly intact, but his thoughts had not settled in the same way.

They kept circling back.

Rayne had never considered himself an overthinker. He was used to acting, deciding in the moment, adapting to whatever came his way. That was how he had survived this long. But now, with no immediate duties weighing him down and too much time left in his hands, his mind refused to stay quiet.

He kept thinking of the dungeon, the battles, the narrow escapes, Fredrick, killing him, and the consequences that were still waiting for him in the form of the upcoming truthsayer trial.

And then there was John.

That thought came more often than the others.

A part of him did blame himself for what had happened. He knew Bran was right, that there was no point holding onto it, that the dead needed to be let go, but knowing that didn’t make it easier to stop thinking about it.

The apprentice necromancer needed to die, and if he had hesitated there, things might have turned out worse for all of them. That much he was certain of. But even with that, he couldn’t ignore the fact that he could have done things better.

Rayne had always been good at staying alive. He trusted that much about himself. But being a leader was different. It wasn’t just about surviving. It was about making sure others did too, and he wasn’t good enough at that yet.

The thought settled heavily in his chest as he walked past a group of soldiers, barely noticing them as they moved out of his way. He needed to improve, not just for himself, but for anyone who ended up following him again.

If that even happened. He didn’t know what the future held for him anymore. Whether he would remain a party leader, or even a soldier at all, depended on things that were no longer in his control.

The truthsayer trial would decide that.

After the news of him waking up had spread, Commander Evans had come to see him personally. The old man had not stayed long, barely a few minutes inside the tent, but he had asked about Fredrick, and Rayne had told him everything.

In the end, Evans had simply told him that he would be sent to Valers for the trial and that he would make sure that the official reports stated that Rayne had acted in self-defense.

Then he had left without another word.

Rayne knew that even that much support was more than most would get in his position. A commander putting his word behind a soldier was not something to take lightly. But even so, he couldn’t help but wonder how much it would actually matter.

Commander Evans wouldn’t be at the trial. From what Rayne understood, only his warband would be breaking off and heading toward the capital.

The trial would be handled by others, people who didn’t know him, who wouldn’t have seen what had truly happened, and would only see his status and blood.

And no one in the kingdom liked his blood.

The thought lingered for a moment before he let out a quiet sigh, shaking his head slightly as if to push it away. There was no point dwelling on something he couldn’t change. Whatever judgment awaited him would come regardless of what he thought about it.

By then, he had already reached Captain Edran’s tent.

Two guards stood outside as usual, their posture straight, their attention sharp even in the slowed pace of the camp. One of them glanced at Rayne as he approached, then gave a small gesture toward the entrance, silently allowing him through.

Rayne gave a slight nod in thanks before stepping forward and pulling open the flap of the tent.

The moment he stepped inside, he straightened and saluted, his eyes moving across the interior.

There were more people inside than he had expected.

Captain Edran sat behind his desk as usual, his posture composed, though there were lines on his face and he looked fatigued. One look at the stacks of documents placed before him was enough to tell him why, and he wasn't alone.

Several additional tables had been set up around the tent, each one covered in scattered documents, reports, and maps.

A handful of men sat around them, writing, sorting, or quietly discussing things among themselves.

Rayne’s gaze moved briefly across the figures before stopping for a moment on one he recognised—Henry.

The old man looked up just as their eyes met, and a grin spread across his face almost immediately, one that carried no warmth behind it. It was as if Henry knew that there was nothing good in Rayne's future, and he might just be dead soon.

Rayne didn’t linger on him. He shifted his focus away without reacting, letting his attention settle back on Captain Edran instead.

A moment passed before Edran finally spoke.

“Rayne,” he said, his voice calm, though there was a hint of strain beneath it. “I apologise. I should have met you sooner, but things have been busy, as you can see. I never got the time, and I wanted you to get your bearings first. I believe you know everything that’s going on?”

Rayne gave a small nod. “I do, Captain.”

Edran studied him for a brief moment, as if weighing something, before nodding back.

“Good,” he said. “We should be moving out of here and making our way back to civilization in about a week. By then, any monsters or undead that escaped us would be slain."

“Mage Casper is leading a few other squads, and dealing with them, right?” Rayne said, having heard that from Nate.

Actually, their warband had been the one with the least casualties while the other warbands had been nearly decimated. Entire squads have been killed.

As a result of that, Commander Evans had given them the responsibility of hunting out any remaining undead, dungeons, and monsters scattered across the region which was actually an easier task than what it seemed like.

Most of what was left were stragglers, broken remnants that had either escaped or had been too far out to be caught in the initial sweep.

There was nothing left that would give them a major problem.

Captain Edran gave a small nod at Rayne’s words, his fingers resting lightly against the papers on his desk. “Yes, she’s busy with that,” he said. “With the reports I have, it should actually be over soon.”

He paused briefly, a faint exhale leaving him before his tone shifted just slightly.

“I don’t know about you,” he added, “but I desperately miss seeing a city.”

There was a hint of tired honesty in his words, something that didn’t quite fit the usual composed image the captain carried. But it didn’t last long. His expression tightened again, his posture straightening as his gaze settled more firmly on Rayne.

“And you should also know what’s going to happen once we do reach one.”

Rayne didn’t hesitate. “We will go to Valers,” he said, his voice even. “For the truthsayers to interrogate me.”

Edran leaned back slightly in his chair at that, studying him for a moment before giving a small nod.

“Yes,” he said. “House Lanrice is pushing for it after we sent the report about Fredrick’s death. It hasn’t been formally granted yet, but it will be. The higher-ups in the army will listen to them, and in my opinion, it’s better to get it over with.”

His fingers tapped lightly against the edge of the desk as he spoke, a quiet rhythm that filled the brief silence between his words.

“As you know,” he continued, “the army can’t protect you, even if you are a part of it.”

For a moment, Rayne almost frowned.

The reaction came naturally, rising before he pushed it back down. There was no point showing it, and no point denying the truth of it either. No matter how much he did, no matter how many merits he earned, his background would always stand against him.

It was something he had accepted long ago.

“What do you think will happen in the trial, Captain Edran?” he asked instead, keeping his tone steady.

Edran didn’t answer immediately. He leaned forward slightly, his gaze drifting for a brief moment as if sorting through his thoughts.

“It depends,” he said after a while, “on who gets chosen to chair it.”

His eyes returned to Rayne.

“There’s always at least one truthsayer present, sometimes two,” he continued. “But beyond that, there are usually two more involved in the proceedings. One will be a capital noble, someone representing the houses, and the other will be from the army.”

The more Captain Edran spoke, the clearer it became to Rayne that there wouldn’t be a single person in that trial he could expect fairness from.

The truthsayers would only be there to ensure he didn’t lie. They wouldn’t decide in his favor, only confirm whether his words held truth. Beyond them, the ones who actually mattered would be the noble and the army representative, and Rayne trusted neither.

Every noble hated him, and the army couldn't be trusted, except perhaps Captain Edran and Commander Evans, and neither of them would be the ones sitting in judgment.

It didn’t bode well for him.

Rayne looked at Edran, his expression steady even as the thoughts settled heavily in his mind.

“Even if I say that Fredrick attacked me first,” he asked, “will they still put charges on me?”

“Yes,” the captain replied plainly. “The trial will simply shift from you being a murderer to someone who didn’t defend himself properly. It sounds stupid, but they will argue that you should have restrained him instead of killing him. That you escalated the situation.”

His gaze held steady on Rayne.

“As a noble, I’ll tell you this much. The crown and the law place nobles above commoners, even if it isn’t written outright anywhere. And forsakens…” he paused briefly, “they are even below that.”

Rayne almost felt Henry’s smirk from somewhere behind him, but he didn't raise his head to look. There was no point.

In the end, the trial was never going to be about proving himself completely innocent. It would be about how lightly he could come out of it. And Rayne had already thought about that.

“I don’t see a way out of it, Captain,” he said after a moment. “Not unless I have the backing of someone prominent.”

Edran’s expression shifted slightly at that, a flicker of surprise crossing his face before it settled into something more approving.

“You have a good brain on you, Rayne,” he said. “And yes, that’s exactly why I called you here.”

He leaned forward slightly, resting one hand on the desk.

“For you to get away with this with as little punishment as possible, you’ll need someone looking out for you. My house will do what it can, and Commander Evans will as well, but the capital is… different. There’s a lot of politics involved, and things don’t move as simply there.”

After that, he continued, going over a few of the noble houses that might have the chance to oversee the trial, speaking of their tendencies, their alignments, and how gaining even a small measure of favor from the right one could tilt things in Rayne’s direction.

It sounded complicated. More than that, it sounded unlikely.

Rayne already knew how most nobles viewed him, and it wasn’t favorable in the slightest. The chances of one of them stepping in for him were slim at best. Still, having Captain Edran’s support meant he wasn’t walking into it completely blind.

That was something.

“In any case,” Edran said, leaning back again, “we can talk more about this on the way. It won’t be a short journey.”

Rayne gave a small nod, understanding his cue to leave. “Thank you for the help, captain.”

He straightened slightly, offering a salute before lowering his hand again. His gaze moved briefly across the tent, taking in the scattered documents before he turned to leave.

But before he could reach the flap, a thought crossed his mind, and he paused. In the end, Rayne decided it was in his favour to talk about it and turned back.

“By the way, captain,” he said, “I wanted to consult you about something.”

Edran raised an eyebrow at that. “What is it?”

Rayne held his gaze for a moment before answering. “I wanted to talk about my class advancement,” he said, “and the options I’ve been given.”
