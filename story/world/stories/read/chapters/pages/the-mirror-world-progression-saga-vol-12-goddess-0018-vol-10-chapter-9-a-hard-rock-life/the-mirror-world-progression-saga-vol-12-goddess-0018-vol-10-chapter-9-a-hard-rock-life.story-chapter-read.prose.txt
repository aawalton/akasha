Unobus was reading reports at his desk when he was shocked out of his focus by a mental ping from Hivemind Central. Inside the fortress, that required a special relay, but there were always inquisitors inside to arrange the ‘chain.’ Immediately, Unobus put the reports down, leaned back, and accepted the communication.

“Your Ascendancy,” Inquisitor Three greeted him placidly in his mind, modulated and with a feel as if from a distance. “Central has reached concurrence on a preliminary Plan Z. Can we count on your approval?”

Unobus sighed. “That would set us back decades in the minds of soldiers and citizens alike, Inquisitor. I don’t take this enemy lightly, but I’m dubious such an extreme is necessary considering the imperviousness of where I stand.”

“She always has surprises. We need many of our own, and as big as possible.”

“Plan Z as a preliminary measure will be blatant, not a surprise.”

“Would you then approve it as a contingency after engagement? Dependent on results?”

Unobus frowned. This was likely the plan from the first contact; the rest was a bit of social engineering to take the edge off of it. A strategy of persuasion, making the ‘second option’ look more reasonable. He wasn’t amused — he preferred straight talk. But inquisitors were inquisitors. “It depends on how much of my leadership will be preserved.”

“To your jurisdiction as the garrison general, naturally, Your Ascendancy.”

So that was the trap. What could be sounder reason than another card available, to use or perhaps not to use? “I see. In that case, a contingency requiring my final approval before execution is just another strategic option. It would only be wisdom to have it available, hmm?” He tried to keep the sarcasm out of his mental voice.

“We concur. Very well, Your Ascendancy. Ah — one other thing. For the necessity of a figurehead, and considering how diminished he’s become… without his best items, Hestrae Beth as an anchor, and with psychological concerns, we’d like Palthennius to be included, and thus offered no immunity or knowledge of Plan Z. In fact, he’s vital to ensure a much more powerful execution.”

Unobus grimaced foully. That was a tough pill to swallow. But the truths were hard to deny, and if he was claimed vital to an Inquisitor’s plans, he was. “So be it, Inquisitor. Suffice to say, if this happens, it had better work.”

“Indeed, Your Ascendancy. If we obtain full approval, you’ll be contacted about next steps.”

Inquisitor Three disconnected.

Unobus took off his cap, rubbed his bald head, and sighed again, with especial weariness. Likely, to be pushing for such an extreme measure, the Inquisitors suspected things they could not corroborate or prove. Once those things became obvious and provable, they’d push for some form of preliminary to the plan, even if designed upon first engagement to preserve the surprise element.

Part of his role was prediction — in this case, it was a near certainty the Inquisitors would get what they wanted. Fear was ruling everyone however this or that agent framed things. Multiples of the Seven dying, valuable operatives dying, one hero missing and presumed dead, another beaten and effectively hobbled. The war was already a disaster, and their foe was hardly beaten. It was going to get worse. Plan Z being instituted was evidence enough. How many would he be allowed to spare? How did he decide where to cut off the chain? Somewhere high, perhaps, just for security reasons… It couldn’t be allowed to get out. Was it sound to preserve some without warning them in advance?

It was a logistical nightmare.

His mind strayed to Palthennius, and it soured him greatly. Such a terrible loss. The man hardly deserved this for all of his dedication.

Dedication will continue, just… in another form.

Casting it from his mind, Unobus poured himself a strong drink and went back to the reports. Most of it was bad news. The Dominion’s bread-and-butter, lately. But Unobus took solace in the perpetual good news of the fortress he was charged with defending. Considering it was invulnerable, this was technically the easiest job in the world. Any given fool could go through the motions and accomplish the goal.

For Unobus, though, that wasn’t good enough. In honor of his slain friend and general, and for the satisfaction of excellence, he’d fight for every mote of additional victory. He’d strive for the most complete defeat of his enemy possible, to go on the record books as a diligent and sincere leader.

Go ahead and come, Witch Queen, and with all your little pets in tow. Matrazere is the end of your road, as all the other heretics before you with their delusions of grandeur. Here is where all quaint mirages face the bleak, hard rock of the truth.

With his mighty spear, Sir Bedwyr One-handed waded through a veritable horde of cobwebs down in the deep, dark catacombs under the mountains. The corridor was apparently some sort of insect thoroughfare? He felt spiders crawl over his surface by the dozens, but he paid them little mind. He was a golem of wood, after all, and their venom was of no concern.

“You missed a large spot on your right, Sir Bedwyr!” Sir Lamorak called anxiously. “I see them! I see many spiders!”

Sir Bedwyr sighed and paused, shifting to knock away the cobwebs pointed out by his most ancient of compatriots, who sadly had a bit of a psychological weakness. “There, it’s done! You silly big clay baby! As if they’d do anything to you…” He continued grumbling, even incoherent to his own mind, formed as they were in now nonexistent languages.

“Sir Bedwyr, please!” Sir Lamorak replied, anguished. “Don’t tease me in this, I beg you. I cannot help it! I don’t like these creepy, crawling, black and brown monstrous beings! Spiders. Euch!”

“Yes, yes, as if I haven’t heard your whining before! Bah.” Angrily, Bedwyr continued leading the way, trying to be diligent to get all the cobwebs in the corridor, as the huge clay golem body of his friend behind him was much wider than his own narrow form.

“I have been looking carefully for the item of note. I see no crest.”

“Obviously not, no. This is a corridor! It’ll be in a bloody crypt.”

“One never knows…”

Bedwyr sighed long-sufferingly. They sought out a legendary, elusive item made of mythril — the Crest of Hivenda — the likes of which would be used for their mightiest of brother-knights. Deciphering some lost linguistic codes no one else could understand (and that had resisted magical means) had led Bedwyr here, but the catacombs were extensive and apparently 99% useless. Well, 90%. He’d found a little hidey hole with some gemstones, including diamonds. Loot! Loot was always something, but especially if he could win the mistresses their pretties and shinies.

The corridor opened up into a wider area, the cobwebs no longer a concern. It appeared to have collapsed on itself, the outer borders now composed of jagged natural rock, with various rocky rubble blocking the way. All around the greater room, there were also somewhat concerning large holes.

Immediately, Lamorak was especially concerned. “What in blazes made those?!”

“Who cares?” Bedwyr asked as he began prying his spear between two stones, forcing one away from the other with effort. “Make yourself useful, already, you great giant oaf! This is your style of challenge, I’d say. The rocks won’t move themselves…”

“Ah. Right.” Lamorak walked over and picked up a small boulder, moving back and gently setting it down. “Let’s try to be quiet, please?”

“Time and tide wait for no man — much less a humble golem.” Inspecting the area for a spot a one-armed gent could do something with, Bedwyr put his back to a large boulder and used his legs to push away another big rock wedged slightly underneath it. His body strained and creaked with the effort, as the rock mostly refused to budge.

“Ahh, wait a moment, there…” Lamorak called uncertainly.

Ignoring him, Bedwyr — with monumental effort — pushed with all his might, and finally the rock gave way with a snapping, breaking sound as part of it chipped before it finally slipped free. The rock above him gave only the briefest groaning sound in warning-

Bedwyr leapt away for his ‘life’ as it all promptly came crashing down. He barely avoided getting buried.

They both backed away, watching the dust settle as the cacophony rang far and wide, likely resounding through the entire catacombs.

“I don’t like this,” Lamorak called fearfully.

“Pfft!” Bedwyr scoffed, but a moment later, he laughed, looking at the headway he’d made with his quick wit and brawn as it was revealed out of the dust. “Look, it’s settled! Ha! You could’ve done even better, you know. It’s not too late, either. Despite this progress, we’ve got a long way to go yet, so get bloody hopping!” Bedwyr took a step, but then paused as he heard and sensed movement.

They both looked around as it got stronger. A series of skittling sounds. Bedwyr brandished his spear.

Lamorak somehow made a squeaky sound — an impressive feat, for a golem.

From the holes all around the room, giant black shapes poured out, of many hairy legs and mandibles and beady eyes and so forth. Giant spiders!

“Ha!” Bedwyr cried, spinning his spear around in preparation. “A truly classic enemy, isn’t it, Sir L-”

Lamorak screamed in quite a feminine cadence and ran the other direction.

“Damn you, knight, get your clay ass back here!” Bedwyr commanded — to no avail. Cursing, looking at the numbers coming at him, he fell back along with Lamorak, knowing he’d need the narrow corridor if he didn’t have his ally’s support.

Once down the corridor, watching Lamorak keep on running, Bedwyr grimaced and turned around to face the spiders hot on his heels. “Sir Lamorak, if you don’t get back here, our mistresses will hear of your foul cowardice! Is this how you aim to be remembered, if time itself concludes?!”

Bedwyr began stabbing the onslaught of spiders as they came, continually giving ground and struggling. Just as he was about to get bowled over, his back hit a stone wall.

Lamorak, screaming in something between fright and a war cry, brought his massive foot down on a spider, smashing it into paste. Then his fist came flying and smashed another into a wall.

“Die, creepy crawlies!” the great clay monster bellowed in anxious fury. “Die, die, die, die, die! All of you, diiie!” His voice was incredibly shrill.

Bedwyr grinned within himself as he took a slightly trailing position, and used the reach of his spear to protect his brother from another giant specimen coming at them. “Now that’s the spirit, Noble Son o’ the Round!”
