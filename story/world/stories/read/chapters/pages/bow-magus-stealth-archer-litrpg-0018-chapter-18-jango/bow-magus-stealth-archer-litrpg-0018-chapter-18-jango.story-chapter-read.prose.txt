Farmers were already pouring out of the main building before Rex even reached the farmstead. Smoke was billowing out of it, and several others were running toward it with buckets in their hands.

A couple of Jango’s men were scattered around the farm, shouting and directing farmers between the well and the farmstead. And through all the chaos, he spotted Andy and Ernie running as fast as they could away from the farm.

Waving his arms over his head, Rex yelled. “This way! Get out, and into the forest and hide!”

“You’re not coming?” Andy said between panted breaths when he got closer.

“Not yet. I’ve got something I need to do,” Rex said, already running past them.

As stupid as it was, he couldn’t just flee. Not yet, at least. He desperately hoped that Slimer had survived, and not just because he had proven to be a useful ally.

Flames were already tracing the wooden ceiling and walls when he burst inside. But the stairs down into the cellar weren’t overcome by fire. They were stone, and while the ceiling above was timber, and clad thickly in smoke, it was good enough. Rex covered his face and held his breath as he ran through the smoke.

You’ve got this!

The cellar was thick with smoke and hot from the fire above. A quick glance confirmed that Jango’s men were toast. Four of them burned beyond recognition.

“You! You’re alive?” Jango spat.

He was on the ground, covered in burns, his face barely recognizable.

“Better than alive,” Rex said, paying him little attention as he rushed past.

He spotted bits of slime all over the place, and his heart sank. He almost gave up, but then spotted a tiny, palm-sized Slimer slowly wiggling. Rex positively beamed with joy. “I am so, so, so sorry, buddy! I hope you can forgive me,” he said as he collected the slime, not worrying about touching it.

He had no time, but needed somewhere to store Slimer. He emptied one of the coin pouches he had looted from the hunters, not caring to collect the coins as he stowed Slimer away.

“You’ll be okay,” he cooed.

Immediately, Rex swung around and rushed back for the stairs. But as he passed Jango, the man summoned enough strength to lunge at him. Unfortunately for Jango, he didn’t have any strength left. He was too badly wounded and fell short of Rex, groaning in agony as he dropped his swords.

“Nighty, nighty,” Rex said, stepping aside and reaching down. He used [ Corrosive Touch ] to melt through the man’s head in an instant.

He didn’t care about saving Jango the agony of a slow death. But experience was experience.

Unfortunately, Rex didn’t have time to go on a looting spree. The fire was spreading quickly, and there was already so much smoke. But he did have time to quickly grab Jango’s sabres.

As he climbed the stairs, farmers were rushing past, throwing buckets of water at the fire, and immediately turning for more. But it didn’t look like their efforts were working too well, and they were far too distracted to care about Rex.

However, before fleeing, he decided to quickly slip into Ulysses's armory. And thankfully, the man wasn’t there, probably having escaped to somewhere safer. He grabbed the first bow he spotted. He needed arrows, but a gust of burning smoke turned him away. And he was forced to escape as he coughed wildly.

Lowering his head below the smoke, Rex struggled to get some air in. He was spattering and wobbling as he got outside. But there were still too many of Jango’s men around, and he did his best to force his feet forward.

If not for the chaos that surrounded him, he no doubt would have been caught and killed right there. But there were dozens of farmhands rushing all around to fight the fire.

Rex focused on keeping his aura down and skirted around the farmers, using them as cover as he made his way back into the forest.

As much as he wanted his gear back, now wasn’t the best time to try to confront Freyja.

“Hey, Rex! Over here!”

“There you are,” Rex coughed.

“You okay?” Andy asked.

“Save it! We gotta go!” Ernie said.

“I’m fine. Let’s move.”

Only a second later, Rex remembered his new boots.

“This way!”

“Where are you taking us?” Andy said. “Shouldn’t we go that way? You know, away from the farm.”

“Yep. We should. Just one second.”

He pushed through some bushes and spotted his prize.

“There you are! Gotcha!” Rex said, grabbing his new pair of boots.

“Can we go now?”

“Yeah, I think it’s about time.”

Rex led them away from the farm. He had to be careful, though. Even with Jango dead, there were more of his followers about. Not to mention Mulvaney and his hunters, or Freyja for that matter.

The hunters had obviously gone looking for him. He had seen them camping in the woods. But they had likely been called back by now, and he certainly didn’t want to bump into them during their escape.

They ran through the forest for several minutes before the System acknowledged that they were no longer in combat, and Rex got his much-awaited notifications.

Ding!

Additional experience rewarded for killing people of a higher rank than yourself.

Borshan, the God of Murder, smiles upon you!

Class: [ Rune Warden ] has leveled up!

Rune Warden is now [ Level 6 ]

Attribute Points allocated!

LEVEL 6? Woohoo!

Rex started laughing out of nowhere as they ran.

“Is something wrong?

Would you look at that? I’m a freaking Freedman! But Borshan again? Yeah, that's not ideal.

“Rex? Why are you laughing?”

“I’ll tell you later.”

As they fled, he could feel the strength and agility that came with his extra Attributes. Running through the forest felt immediately easier. He was lighter, his steps more dexterous, and his vision clearer.

Not only that, but he was a Freedman now. He could enter Tartarus. He could find and join a real party. He could become a real adventurer and complete real contracts. And he could earn some real coin.

All of that would have to wait until they could secure a means of getting off whatever world they were on, though.

They got deep into the forest before slowing for a rest. Ernie was really struggling, panting loudly.

“Catch your breath, and we keep moving,” Andy said.

Ernie just nodded, wheezing as he tried to gather himself.

Rex took the moment to look at his new Status Page.

His new Attributes looked impressive, and they felt more so. When hitting Attribute thresholds, Primary Attributes doubled, and secondaries increased by half, rounding up if uneven.

He noted his Strength Attribute. 3 was the Attribute’s base, and he had +1 conditional Strength because of his Archery Proficiency, granting him 4 Strength when wielding a bow.

No wonder I’m feeling so much stronger now.

At that moment, he remembered Slimer and quickly checked on it.

“Whoa! Careful there, little buddy,” he said.

Slimer had dissolved his way through the coin pouch, which he had tied to his belt. And when he looked down, it was dangling precariously from the cloth. The moment he had touched them, he had realized that only one of the sabres was an artifact, and used the mundane one to pick Slimer up, who immediately started eating through it.

“Is that the thing I threw the axe at?” Andy asked.

“Oh, this? Yeah. Meet Slimer. He’s kinda my partner, or pet, or something. I’m not sure exactly; he doesn’t talk.”

“Neither do dogs,” Ernie added.

“Good point! I guess he’s a pet then.”

Slimer was already eating through the sabre, and Rex decided to examine the sword in his other hand.

Stun Sabre [ Rare ]

This sword stuns targets on hit for 2 seconds, but can be resisted with the Strength Attribute.

Attributes bonuses when wielding: +1 Agility

Rex was a little disappointed. Considering he was the boss of their little operation, Jango’s sword wasn’t overly special. A stun effect was good, but at low levels, it didn’t take too much to kill somebody, anyway. And if it could be resisted with high Strength, then he wasn’t sure it would be useful at high levels.

“Here, Andy, you have this,” Rex said, handing the other sabre over.

Boots of Subtlety [ Uncommon ]

These boots soften the footfalls of the user, making sneaking easier.

Attributes bonuses when wielding: +1 Agility

Only Uncommon, but I can work with that. Sneaking is going to be helpful, especially in my current situation. And besides, I certainly can’t complain about another point of Agility.

But before they could even get moving again, Slimer had eaten his way through the mundane sword and grown ever so slightly. And while Rex had already held him, the little slime had proven to be a little too disobedient to trust with his hand.

This has turned out to be fruitful. Not to mention, I still have the emerald.

The first time he attacked Slimer was kind of understandable. But the second, he had known exactly what would happen. He had blown Slimer up on purpose, and while it had survived, saving them in the process, he felt like a dick for doing it.
