The first expedition to the far side of the world had left days ago, without me and to much fanfare. It wasn't like I was jealous, no, not at all. In fact I was quite happy with it, the team had been picked by Chien and I, all pretty skilled elves, though young ones, no Elder wanted to go, not that there were that many of us.

Myself, I found putting out fires here and there, and on this particular day found myself back in the north. I flew, zipping across the cold plains, not trusting myself to teleport where I was going. This was a place large enough, big enough important enough for me to handle though, so handle it I would.

Pale ground flowed below me, and while I'd not been up this far north in years I knew the way now, the signs I should be seeing, the signs I wasn't. Icehome loomed nearby, with its people, and its frozen walls. It had joined us years ago, though always kept mostly to itself, but they'd called for aid now, and I was going myself.

It had been decades since I'd seen this place, the one I was approaching now, but it looked unfamiliar. As I got there there were almost no guards, and the doors, the doors looked to be melded in place, blocked by snow and ice, with only a small path to a smaller door carved into one of them.

“You called for aid,” I said to one of the guards as I landed, he looked bad, thin, tired.

“You... came yourself?” he said, clearly recognizing me.

“Of course, you're my people, and this is a hard journey for most of us, let's go inside and your leaders can explain in detail.”

When I'd first come here the entry hall had been a much grander place, and much more active, but as we entered Icehome itself I saw less than ten people walking around. Nor did any of them seem to be going to the upper sections, those that used to be the housing for the elders and the Ancient who lived here.

“Where is everyone?” I asked.

“Downstairs,” the guard informed me.

I knew the halls as we walked, for these were not new ones, but old ones. Nor were they all the halls I'd seen before, for many of them were sealed, doorways bricked up and covered with some mix of straw and mud, clearly unused. For a moment I was so, so nervous, but then as we got lower I began to see a few more here and there still lived in, not as many, but a few, seemingly nicer ones.

Though it wasn't as warm either, and as we got down, deep into the actual heating room I saw the difference. Used to be there were pools here, where people lounged as they fed mana into the massive crystal above, given snacks and food for their contributions, but now... now the pools were drained, and people were working on crafts even as they gave mana to the massive red stone,

The crystal was still warm, still giving off quite a bit of heat, but not like it was before, not the thundering blaze it had once been. More the people were so different, so few. Many seemed hollowed out a bit, hungry in the eyes, not starving, for we'd never let out people starve, but not well fed either.

Elder Liet found us not long afterwards, a man with only a few silver hairs, younger than me, though not massively so.

“Your Majesty, we weren't expecting you to come yourself,” he said.

“The message I got indicated there was urgent need, what is it?” I asked.

“Please, come, we'll speak in my rooms, it is better there,” he said.

As we walked I tried to place the man, and with some trepidation managed it.

“You're one o f those from the hunting party that found Chien and I all those years ago,” I said, and his posture stiffened like a board.

“Yes...”“Thank you for that, it was a bad time.”“Indeed your Majesty, it was.”

Surely for he and his it must have been. Their Ancient was gone, their Elders decimated to few, and then half their people leaving. Yet he led on, on into a room not far from the heating room, larger, more spacious, and with one of my magical items in it, one that had been sent to the leaders of all sizable settlements. He touched it and activated the sound baffling.

“We have to abandon Icehome,” he said, not pulling punches.

“Now?” I asked, genuinely surprised.

“After the last winter, and how harsh it was, many of our people left for the south. We've been bleeding people since Neera's death, but that winter... it took enough of them that we can't stand. Another in the next ten years will be the death of all of us. We simply don't have the hunters, don't have the food production, don't have the people to keep it warm enough. There were times I worried over the last one, worried that we wouldn't make it.” His face was broken, like he wished to weep at that admission.“I understand,” I said, said gently, seeing his stress.

“No... do you know how close we came to losing the infants, few as they were? It got too cold, and with everything... We couldn't even call for help, Nobody believed they could make it through the storms to the south to call for aid, and with so few of us left we didn't have enough skilled men or risk ourselves further.”“Be calm my friend,” I told him, trying to push him towards a waiting chair. “You're my people, I won't abandon you. It will be a bit rough, but we've plenty of food now that the worst of winter has fallen away, the farms are going strong. We'll have to find somewhere for everyone, but don't worry, we'll make it work.”

“I was worried you would let us die you know. After everything that's happened I thought you might just leave us to our fate. It's clear you dislike this place, though I don't know why.”“I dislike people in general,” I told him. “But not yours any more than I dislike most. There's no hate from me for your and yours.”“Then why did you do it?” he asked. “Why did you kill the Matriarch?”Now it was my turn to freeze, to lock eyes on him and activate every item I held. Perhaps I could fight myself from this place if I must, but it would be bloody.

“What did you say?” I asked.

“I... I was part of the hunting group that found you, you remember? When we got back I headed back out on my own, to look for any survivors. I found her there, you left her on the ice. I buried her myself.”“Do the others know?”

“No,” he replied. “I never told them, what would be the point? What would it change except to make an enemy we didn't need, but why? Why did you kill her?”

“Almost nobody knows,” I began, taking a seat with him. “Only a few people, even now. I didn't hate her either, not really. We even wanted to help her once we she caught up with us, some of those wounds on her weren't fresh, though I'm sure you realized. She was hurt, badly hurt, that's the only reason we managed it.”“But...”“But it didn't matter, she threatened us. She threatened me, she threatened Chien, and my family too. She was planning on ruling over all of us by force, and with her age, nobody would ever have managed to stand against her.”

“Like you rule over us now,” he said, sounding sullen.

“I rule by consent of my people. I didn't force any group to join me, and never set out to take over these lands. You and yours did that of your own accord, agreed to join my kingdom. We didn't invade, didn't destroy and kill to make it happen, it wasn't the same.”“Suppose that's true. Not that I really liked it when we did, could have told the others, maybe should have, but... you know a lot of our people don't even know what she was like now, other than the stories.”“Good ones?” I asked.

“Ha, some, most even, but not all. Some of the Elders who knew her years ago didn't much like her. Most of them have gone south now though. Kids still hear the rumors from some of their children and grandchildren that stuck around. She was still our protector though, our leader for so long.”

“Now you're the leader of these people.”“Still feel like a foolish child some days,” he admitted.

“Don't I know that one! It doesn't go away as far as I can tell, we always seem to feel like we're not the right one, but it means something. It means you're trying, it means you're not so absorbed in yourself that you worry. Now though, now we can worry together, so let's get our people somewhere safe shall we?”

I extended a hand for him as I rose, offering to help him to his feet. He looked at me in awe, this man, who'd been on the verge of weeping moments before looked like he'd seen something wonderful. It was the look I loved most in my people.

“Yes your Majesty, let us,” he replied, taking my hand and rising.

There was hope for us all yet.
