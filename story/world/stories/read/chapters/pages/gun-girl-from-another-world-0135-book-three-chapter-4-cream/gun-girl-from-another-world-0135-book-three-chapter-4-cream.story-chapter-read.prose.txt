Chapter 4

Cream

The stable and grazing field were the last things I built before we left on our whirlwind tour of the western provinces. Well, I built the stable. One does not so much build a field as they clear it and build the fence around it, but details.

Since it was likely to be a long-term structure, I went to the trouble of doing it right. Well, as right as I could. The fencing’s quality, and low and tight enough to keep the chickens from wandering off, but the floor of the barn-like stable is done in sealed mud brick. I would have preferred concrete, but this is what was available unless I wanted to track down what was necessary to make it, myself. I wouldn’t have minded doing so, and I’ll definitely insist on it for the house, and probably the workshop, but there just wasn’t the time before we would be leaving.

The stable building, itself, is made of wooden cross-beams and frames, covered over with planks and a roof of bamboo shingles. I won’t lie, even at the time, I was thinking of it as practice for the house. I knew the gist of framing in building construction, but I’d never done it with my own two hands before. The stable taught me a great deal and helped me refine my ideas immensely.

Now, it smells like hay and dairy. Mostly because that’s what it contains.

“I admit, Hero Lee, I thought you were crazy when you said you wanted the stalls cleaned regularly,” Elara is saying as we step into the cool interior, mostly illuminated by the open windows. Rather, the windows can’t be anything but open, as they have no panes or shutters, just wooden cruciform cross bars to impede access. Flameless lanterns hang at the entrance for use after dark. Since they’re magical, they’re much more expensive than simple enclosed lanterns, but far safer in the event they land on hay or some other flammable material.

I look around at Elara’s words, though. “It’s for the animals’ sake. We don’t want the experiment ruined by a preventable infection. But it looks like you’ve been keeping up with it well.”

“Yes, actually,” she confirms, “that’s what I was going to say. I’ve never seen a stable so easy to clean as the one you’ve built here. I put the animals out in the field, I do a little shoveling into your wheelbarrow, and then I turn on the water and it all runs out through the gaps at the base of the walls. The brick rinses right off and air dries by the time I bring the animals back in.”

I grin at her praise. “My people are fundamentally lazy. If there’s a way to build that means less work going forward, it’s worth putting in a little extra initial labor to save time later.”

“Most would not consider that reasoning for innovation praiseworthy, Remmi,” Yorin calmly informs me from behind.

I shrug it off, though. “Then call it optimization. The less time we have to spend on otherwise labor-intensive drudgery just to maintain what we already have, the more time we can dedicate to building new things!”

“Which you absolutely won’t also optimize to need as little from you as possible,” Ayre joins in on the criticism, too. “Do your people just live to see how much they can cram in a single day?”

“Yes,” I answer with a sharp nod and absolutely no hesitation. Then I grin with a wink as I spin one finger in the air. “Sometimes, we even consider sleep worth cutting into so we can fit in more things!”

“That doesn’t sound healthy,” Korrigan disapproves. She’s pulling up the rear while holding on tightly to Kyuuga, who, for a change, seems perfectly content to be carried around like a plushie. Maybe it’s because she’s a kid. Maybe he just knows I’ll notice.

“It’s a lot easier when you’ve got easy access to inexpensive stimulants,” I boast. “Then we tend to crash and recuperate on the weekend.”

“The child is correct,” Yorin agrees, “that is not the path to a healthy, balanced lifestyle.”

Ayre taps his chin with an index finger, eyes rolling up somewhere toward the loft in thought. “It does explain why you think it’s normal to run around like a distracted squirrel, though.”

I let my friends’ teasing roll off of my back and turn my attention to the stable’s first residents. A space the size of two stalls serves as the chickens’ coup, complete with beds to nest in, seed feeders, a bird-sized door into the field and plenty of egg shells for them to peck at.

“There’s more of them than when I left,” I observe.

Elara leans in eagerly beside me. “Is there anything else you notice about them?”

I take a closer look, narrowing my eyes like I’m staring at a Spot the Differences puzzle as I glance from fowl to fowl, each clucking happily to each other as they go about their happy chicken life.

I kneel down next to the gate and one of them looks at me, then hurries over. Good, Elara has been handling them like I instructed so they’re comfortable with people.

I scoop the fluffy dinosaur descendant up and hold it close to my chest as I stand back up again. It coos as I stroke its feathers. Its glimmering, immaculate feathers.

“Wait, are my chickens shiny?” I wheel on Elara with a suddenness that startles her. “Do I have shiny chickens?!”

The matron gives a nervous chuckle at my energy. “We started letting them hatch when we confirmed the new eggs had become Essence Eggs.”

With new attention to detail, I lift the shimmering chicken up to eye level with me. Its glimmering feathers sparkle in the sunlight as it tilts its cute little head to look back at me.

I feel like I should be shoving this thing in a poke ball.

NAME: Chicken

RACE: Chicken (Essence)

AGE: 0

LEVEL: 5

CLASS: Chicken

STATUS: Friendly

“Holy shit!” I immediately, reflexively slap the hand that had been making a monocle over my mouth and shoot a glance at Korrigan, but she and everyone else are just looking at me in confusion.

So I turn back to the window, and to that level in particular. This fluffy little darling is level five?! Doesn’t that mean it could kick the tar out of a horned rabbit?!

With that thought, Kyuuga is the next one my eyes fall on. He very clearly is not privy to my inner thoughts, but that’s probably for the best. I don’t actually want him deciding he needs to train my chickens in kickboxing. Besides, I meant a normal horned rabbit, anyway.

Wait, do I want him to train them in kickboxing? It’d neatly immediately solve some of those security provisions Yorin and Elara were talking about us needing. But no, I want them sweet and docile. Especially if being an Essence creature gives them a huge level bump, I don’t want it going even higher with actual combat skills to back it up. That would be a massive risk to the hired help we were also talking about needing.

“Oh, but they still need to stay on the Essence feed,” Elara is saying. “It’s not self-sustaining. They’ll stay Essence Chickens, but their eggs will revert to normal eggs.”

I hug the warm, soft sweetheart back to my chest and turn to Yorin. “Well, I guess that addresses our worries we were having about it becoming an environmental hazard. Even if some of these little ones get away, they’ll just make regular chickens.”

The priestess nods back to me. “The discovery was a weight off of my mind, as well. If they had remained altered, there was a very real chance I would have to order their euthanasia.”

“HERESY, we do not murder the chicken!” I snuggle the birdie closer to my heart. “Don't worry … the mean elf won't hurt you …”

The mean elf looks entirely unimpressed as she crosses her arms. “And what do you intend to do with them when they have lived out their time and can no longer produce eggs?”

I don’t hesitate to answer, though I do jut my lower lip out and to the side as I do so. “Thank them for being their adorable selves and eat them, but that's different from suggesting cutie genocide!”

“I see,” she replies levelly. “Remmi, is the concept of hypocrisy foreign to your people?”

I frown and turn sharply away on my heel with my chin pointed upward, a gesture my bird mimics. “I have no idea what you’re talking about,” I declare as I start goosestep marching further into the depths of the barn. “Elara! Onward to the milk!”

Behind me, Ayre stops next to Yorin like he’s considering patting her on the shoulder, but the priestess shakes her head.

“This is simply Remmi,” she explains to the archer. “I cannot say I blame her; they are precious little creatures. It would be terribly easy to grow attached to them. Truthfully, I am glad the euthanasia proved unnecessary, as well.”

We make our way onward to the cow’s stall. Unlike the chickens, we only have the one. It’s a simple matter of output. A single chicken produces about five eggs per week. A single cow will produce over fifty gallons of milk in the same time. Even with my dimensional storage, we can only use so much, and unlike eggs, refrigerated milk spoils in a week here because it's not homogenized or pasteurized.

… Yeah, that’s another thing I’m going to have to build something for now. If Essence Milk is going to be useful to others without needing rare and expensive magical storage, I can’t tolerate such a uselessly tiny shelf life. Pasteurization will be easy; I just need to get a double boiler and teach Elara how to do it. The trick’s going to be the other half of the equation. While pasteurization will increase the shelf life of the milk tremendously on its own, it won’t be by as much, and the cream will still separate over time. Not only will that negatively affect the milk’s quality, but when the cream mixes with the air, it changes the proteins, kind of like rust, which causes it to sour. Nobody wants milk rust.

Unfortunately, to homogenize the milk, I’m basically going to have to come up with a high-pressure pump to force the milk through a small nozzle. I’m not really looking forward to explaining to everyone why I want to repeatedly run every liter of super-milk through a glorified, food-grade, heated pressure washer, but the alternative is so inferior that I don’t think I’ll be able to tolerate it.

Of course, if we want to make butter and certain cheeses, and we absolutely do, that cream would have to be removed before homogenization, but that’s simple enough to do. Even in an entire gallon of whole milk, there’s only about a heaping half-cup of cream left in. Of course, having that much to spare would require our cow’s milk to be particularly high in milkfat, and I don’t know if it is or not.

Even if it isn’t, though, we can still use the low-fat milk for low-grease mozzarella, or sell it to Zeiya at a discount. After all, low-fat milk is better for steaming and frothing, so she can use it in her lattes and cappuccinos, while lowering the price relative to using whole milk.

Sadly, the cow doesn’t glimmer like my sweet chicken, but she doesn’t seem to mind. She’s just chilling out as she munches away on her feed. I can tell at a glance that Elara has been sticking to my recipe, though I was pleased to find out that a balanced cattle diet was something Imperial natives were familiar with. Even though we’re using Essence crops, I only had to tweak it a little bit.

The main body of the feed, native grasses and clover, required me to plant crops that humans can’t actually get any use out of, but it was all in the greater cause of that precious fluid. It’s then got a flavorful mix of corn, rice and soybean meal. Normally, bone meal would be used as a calcium supplement, but we already were going to have plenty of eggshells, and in the event the experiment succeeded, I wanted us to be in the habit of saving and processing the eggshells to make that component an Essence component, too. After that, the whole thing is deliciously seasoned with garlic, rosemary, citrus peel, caraway and oregano.

The end result smells good enough to eat, and increases milk production and nutritional value while, well, to put it simply, reducing how much the old girl farts. She may not care, but we sure appreciate it.

I thought Elara would go through the process of milking the cow right in front of us, but she and Yorin have proven well-prepared. Rather than a milk bucket, she goes to another, empty stall and comes back with a pitcher and five cups. I would be worried about exposure, but I recognize the enclosed metal container as my own design. Trying to follow my success with the hotplate teapot, I tried to use an ice stone to make a pitcher that would keep its contents chilled without needing ice. The wooden handle proved necessary when too high a setting turned the whole thing frosty.

She pours silky, ivory milk into each cup, and then passes them out to each of us, but before I drink it, I Identify this, too.

ESSENCE MILK (RAW)

----------------------

Fresh milk, straight from the HeavenlyTM tap! Already a superfood, this cool, creamy beverage has been further fortified by vitamins and minerals that don’t even exist! All of the calcium you know and love, now with a supernatural supercharge!

Essence Milk: It does a body grrrreat!

Consuming this item grants the following benefits:

50% Increased Strength for one hour

50% Increased Toughness for one hour

Increased Stamina Regeneration for one hour

2% Smoother, Clearer Skin for twenty-four hours

0.02cm Growth

0.02cm Bust Growth (Preexisting Tissue Required)

Okay, I really hope that last bit is just the marketing department being funny again. I don’t want to end up six feet tall with unibags in a few years from this stuff. Or find out what it would mean for Ayre. Really, I’m crossing my metaphorical fingers that it’s just a snipe at Yorin.

I’m pretty sure it’s not real. Mostly. It’s probably not, right?

Everyone is staring at me, so I shove my vain insecurities aside and take a pull. Before I realize it, the cup is empty, even though I didn’t deliberately drink the whole thing. I lick the residue off of my lips with a smack. Creamy, cold and softly sweet.

“Yep, that’s milk, alright,” I conclude confidently. “Wow, that’s good.”

The others drink, as well, and Ayre and Korrigan give confirming sounds of agreement.

“That’s the best milk I’ve ever had,” the archer declares. “Is it really Essence Milk, though?”

“Sure is,” I reply. “Check your status and effects for yourself.”

There’s a moment of silence as both of them do so. I’m sure Elara and Yorin have long since seen it for themselves.

“Oh, wow,” Korrigan proclaims. “All that for a cup of milk?! I could actually get Grandpa to drink it if the milk back home did this!”

“I’m eager to see how it changes with processing,” I put in.

But at that, Ayre moves to stand directly in front of me, the most serious expression I have ever seen on his face. In a completely, and somewhat intimidating, deadpan, he states but a single word.

“Custard.”

I give a nervous grin. “... Custard?”

“You promised Essence Custard from Essence Milk, Essence Eggs and Essence Sugar, with Essence Caramel Sauce if this all worked. You told me to think of how much better it would taste.”

“... Been a bit on your mind, huh?”

“You promised.”

“Okay, okay, you’ll get it,” I assure my scary best friend. “It’ll be ready for dessert tonight, alright? Please stop staring at me like you’re about to stab me!”

It’s Elara that speaks tentatively into the following silence. “Actually, Hero Lee, if you’re expanding the farm, anyway, have you considered horned rabbits?”

I lean around Ayre’s intense form with relief. “Rabbits? Elara, everyone is raising rabbits. It’s hardly a product in demand.”

She clasps her hands together. “Yes, but think of what they could be like as Essence Rabbits! The soft fur, the glistening horns … the succulent meat …”

Oh, gosh, she’s actually starting to drool …

I’m saved from having to answer by Kyuuga. Having been put down so Korrigan could drink her milk, the mention of horned rabbits immediately has his attention. And as the house matron starts fantasizing about their meat, he hops over with all of the casual attitude of crossing the room.

Her yelp splits the stable air as he punts her knee out from under her, and she goes down on her butt.

“Yeah, that’s why I’m not doing it, Elara,” I conclude with a factual air. “Contrary to popular belief, I don’t actually want the fluff kicked out of me.”
