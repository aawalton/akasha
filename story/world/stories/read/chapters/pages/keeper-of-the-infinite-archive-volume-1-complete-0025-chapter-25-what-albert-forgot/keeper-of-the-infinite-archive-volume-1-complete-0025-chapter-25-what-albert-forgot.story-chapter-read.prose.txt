The plain white bathroom door opened with a whoosh and a familiar gurgle.

Bart and Gregor stepped out of the golden portal and onto the cracked wooden floorboards of the Infinite Archive. Gregor immediately dropped to his knees, frantically patting down his pockets and checking the contents of his leather pouch to ensure no other baked goods had been vaporised by overzealous dwarven safety protocols.

Bart however did not pause to rest. He set his satchel gently on his desk, removed the heavy padded crate, and carried it directly towards the basement stairs. The lower level of the Archive was a vast, dusty expanse of empty stone floors and a few scattered, unstable shelves. Bart followed the six anchor points marked by Restricted Collection Planning, taking the six tiles of Veyric Memory Porcelain from the crate.

He knelt and pressed tile A-1 into the cracked stone floor. It sank perfectly flush with the masonry. He walked the perimeter, placing the remaining five tiles at equidistant points around the designated Restricted Section. As the sixth tile clicked into place, the basement held its breath.

A soft, pale blue light flared from the porcelain squares. Thin lines of magical energy darted between them, connecting the tiles in a geometric web pattern. The light rose upwards, weaving a containment field around the basement shelves before becoming invisible. The air inside the perimeter instantly felt heavier, stiller, and profoundly secure.

A System screen flared to life in front of Bart.

RESTRICTED COLLECTION PLANNING: COMPLETE

Containment Field: Active

Veyric Perimeter Integrity: 100%

Archive Health Status Updated: 0.000005%

Bart adjusted his glasses. A bump from 0.0000005% to 0.000005% (10x) was still a very small margin of survival, but to a librarian, any positive statistical trend was a victory.

A second screen materialised, glowing with authority of the Keeper.

NEW KEEPER PROCEDURE UNLOCKED: RESTRICTED INSPECTION Function: Permits the Keeper to safely examine the metadata, curse structure, and containment status of dangerous or sealed texts without initiating a physical opening.

Before Bart could close the notification, a sharp clatter echoed from the top of the stairs. Albert the skeleton had dropped his broom. The Chief Custodian stood frozen on the top step. His jaw hung slack. Deep within his empty eye sockets, a thin, piercing line of blue light ignited.

MEMORY AUTHORITY CONNECTION DETECTED

Local anchor: Restricted Section

Associated institutional record: Albert, Chief Custodian

Fragment recovery initiated

Bart took a step forward. "Albert?"

The skeleton did not move, but the blue light flared. A shockwave of air rolled down the stairs, and suddenly, Bart was no longer looking at the dusty basement. He was pulled into the memory.

The memory was astonishingly clear, far sharper than the damaged diagnostic one in Dhoravel. This was not a recording. It was a vital, locked fragment of Albert’s own mind from the final 72 hours before the Ruin, forcibly restored to his consciousness.

In the memory, the Archive was not broken. The walls were pristine white marble, though there seemed to be something off, Albert was alive and human…stood in the deep shadows of the East Wing, wearing a crisp Custodian’s uniform. Beside him stood Keeper #4,091 atleast thats what it seemed to Bart.

The previous Keeper looked nothing like a powerful or authoritative wizard. He looked haggard and exhausted. His grey robes were torn at places, his eyes were bloodshot, and his hands trembled violently as he leaned against the heavy, sealed metal door of the East Wing vault. Keeper #4,091 stared at the reinforced metal, tracing the jagged blue seal glowing on its surface. "Five authorities," the old Keeper murmured, his voice cracking with exhaustion and terror. "Memory. Record. Citation. Closure. Succession."

In the memory, Albert stepped forward, his expression tight with concern. "Why divide them, Keeper? It leaves the Archive vulnerable."

Keeper #4,091 turned his head slowly. The look in his eyes was one of dread.

"Because no Keeper should ever be able to open that door alone."

The memory then cut out violently.

The dusty basement rushed back into Bart’s vision. Albert stumbled backwards on the stairs, his skeletal hands gripping the wooden railing for support. The bright light in his eye sockets faded back to a soft blue. Albert still could not remember what terrifying truth lay locked inside the vault. The memory of the Ruin itself was still missing. But the procedural purpose of the fragmentation was finally clear.

Bart stared into the dark corner of the basement. He finally had a concrete path forward. He did not just need to find lost books; he needed to rebuild the Archive's network and re-establish the conditions under which all five Keeper Authorities could be validated.

Memory had responded through Albert. Closure remained connected to the Curator. Record, Citation, and Succession were still beyond his reach. He had to restore them, not just to run the library, but to ensure whatever Keeper #4,091 had locked away, stayed buried….or, if necessary, to finally understand what it was.

Albert bent slowly and picked up his broom. His fingers closed around the handle, but the wood rattled faintly against his bones. Bart climbed the stairs and gently took the broom from him.

"Take a break, Albert," Bart said quietly. The skeleton looked towards the ruined East Wing. After several seconds, he gave a small nod.

Nearly half an hour later, the Archive had settled into an uneasy quiet. With the Restricted Section’s containment perimeter stabilised and the magical network holding, Bart sat down behind his solid oak desk. He exhaled a long, slow breath. He reached into his satchel and carefully unpacked his supplies, lining them up on the polished wood.

He took out the gleaming Veyric Pressure Brewer and the small velvet bag of premium, enchanted coffee beans. Bart placed one hand on the heavy brass Alchemy Grinder. The single drop of Golden Troll Fat had already restored its gears days ago, but the machine that had received its first perfect grounds had not survived the experience. The melted remains of the old plastic coffee maker still sat in a box beneath his desk.

This time, however, Bart was prepared.

He poured a handful of the premium enchanted beans into the hopper and slowly turned the iron crank. The restored gears moved with a smooth, silent glide, crushing the beans into a flawless, deeply aromatic powder that smelled like dark chocolate and roasted hazelnut.

Bart carefully spooned the powder into the glass brewing chamber of the Veyric Pressure Brewer. He poured in a measure of filtered water, clicked the copper dial into place, and pressed the activation button.

The dwarven machine hummed quietly. Where a lesser brewer would have melted under the intense alchemical friction of the enchanted beans, the temperature memory porcelain effortlessly absorbed and contained the immense heat, regulating it to the exact decimal point required for a perfect extraction. A dark, rich stream of coffee dripped steadily into Bart’s faded Beaumont Public Library mug.

When it was finished, Bart picked up the mug. The ceramic was perfectly warm against his palms. He closed his eyes and took a sip. It was perfect. It did not grant him magical powers or did not unlock new System procedures. It was simply the greatest and most perfectly balanced cup of coffee a human being could possibly brew. As the warmth spread through his chest, Bart unexpectedly felt his throat tighten. His eyes welled with tears, for just a few seconds, the taste instantly transported him back to his old life. He was no longer the Keeper of a ruined, cosmic multiverse facility. He was just Bart, sitting behind the circulation desk at the Beaumont Public Library on a quiet, rainy Tuesday morning, listening to the soft hum of the radiators while listening to the outside sounds of the city. For a brief moment, Bart found a slice of home in his broken new world.

He took another slow sip, letting the silence of the Archive wash over him. The quiet moment was broken by the sound of a polite, hollow cough. Bart opened his eyes. Floating three feet above the floorboards on the other side of the desk was Morzath the Undying.

The 7 foot tall lich wore heavy, spiked iron shoulder plates and a tattered crimson cape that swayed in a non existent wind. His eye sockets burned with an intense purple colour.

"Keeper," Morzath said, his voice echoing like grinding tombstones. "I have returned for my scheduled supervised reading. I am prepared to conquer the laminated dough for my croissants."

Bart placed his mug down gently. He was a true professional.

"Welcome back, Morzath," Bart said.

He walked over to the basement stairs, quickly retrieving Master Gurrum Goldbelly’s interlibrary-loan copy of The Master’s Guide to Advanced Pastry Baking by Chef Rickard Astel from the newly secured Restricted Section.

Returning to the desk, Bart activated his new ability before opening the cover.

Restricted Inspection.

A blue overlay appeared over the book. Bart could see the metadata floating above the leather. The chapters on brioche, choux pastry, and croissants were highlighted in green and cleared for public viewing.

But the final chapter was locked behind a heavy red digital chain.

METADATA LOCK DETECTED

Authority: Closure (Keeper #4,091)

Status: Sealed.

Legal owner: Master Gurrum Goldbelly

Loan status: Active

Authorised consultant: Morzath the Undying

Use restriction: Supervised consultation inside the Infinite Archive

"I must inform you that the final chapter of this text remains restricted under historical sealing orders," Bart told the lich. "However, the croissant chapters are fully available for your consultation."

Morzath’s purple eye-flames flickered with momentary disappointment. The air in the room briefly dropped frost forming on the edges of the desk. But Morzath was, at his core, a dedicated hobbyist.

"Acceptable," the terrifying undead warlord rumbled. "One must master the fundamentals of butter lamination before attempting the dark arts of natural fermentation."

"An excellent philosophy," Bart agreed.

He slid the book across the desk. Morzath hovered over it, his skeletal fingers gently turning the pages to avoid tearing the paper with his spiked gauntlets.

From the back room, Albert emerged carrying a silver tray. Albert had apparently interpreted "take a break" as "prepare refreshments for a catastrophe-class lich."

Trying to be an accommodating host to a visiting patron, the skeleton had prepared a fresh cup of the Break Room’s terrible green tea. He held the tray up to the floating lich.

Morzath paused his reading. "Ah. Hospitality. You honour me, skeletal servant."

Morzath took the delicate porcelain teacup in his massive, armoured hand. He raised it to his skeletal mouth and tipped it between his teeth, letting the tea vanish into the purple fire within him.

Morzath froze. The purple flames in his eyes flared violently. The temperature in the Archive plummeted, and the windows rattled in their frames. "What," Morzath demanded, his voice dropping into an octave that vibrated Bart’s ribcage, "is this foul concoction?"

"It is the standard staff green tea," Bart offered diplomatically.

Morzath slammed the teacup back onto the silver tray.

"It tastes like boiled lawn!" the lich roared. "I have razed kingdoms. I have salted the earth of empires. But offering a guest a brew this fundamentally offensive is not hospitality. It is akin to declaration of war."

Albert slowly lowered the tray, looking mildly offended.

Bart sighed, reaching for his own mug of perfect coffee to help defuse the escalating tea crisis. "Morzath, please lower your voice, the library has a noise policy…."

Thud…Thud.

Bart stopped speaking.

Morzath’s purple flames abruptly shrank back into his eye sockets. The sound had not come from the lich. It had not been a portal opening or from the system but from beneath the rubble of the East Wing.

A thin line of cyan light travelled through the floorboards, crossed the ruined archway, and vanished beneath the sealed metal vault.

Thud.

One of the five jagged seals carved into the door flickered blue.

Albert’s eye sockets flared in answer.

Bart slowly rose from his chair.

The vault had not opened.

But for the first time in six centuries, it had responded.
