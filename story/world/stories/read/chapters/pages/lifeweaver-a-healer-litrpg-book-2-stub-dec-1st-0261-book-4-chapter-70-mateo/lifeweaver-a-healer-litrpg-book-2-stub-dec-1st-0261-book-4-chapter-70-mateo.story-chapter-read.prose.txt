The horned lady seemed to have an animal for almost every occasion, and the knight without memories danced in the light with lethal precision while the girl healer moved health around like a conductor ordering notes and chords, with the flick of her wrist, despite her heart-wrenching loss. Priyah, the girl with an indomitable will, who reminded Mateo of his daughter, and the untouchable blade shared something with them all, as did Mateo himself. Even together, they all fell short.

Physical skill mattered little in this particular dungeon. It was fraught with monsters from beyond, aliens who had come to take everything from Earth. It was not the first time, of course, and Mateo's friends had defeated both demons and Singers, but this was different. A nightmare come to life, and what a nightmare it was. Mateo's spear could not pierce that impenetrable darkness. The weapon he had devoted his new life to was insufficient, and he found himself lacking.

Already, they had lost two friends. Two precious lives gone, taken too soon. Thinking of them drew thoughts to his family, stuck in some sort of limbo without their consent. Tears of frustration and rage threatened to fall from the corners of his eyes, so he blinked and focused outwardly instead. Even in his own weakness, he could help scout for threats, and there would be many in a place like this.

The dungeon had gone dark. In that moment, Mateo thought they might have lost, and that the void had risen up to consume them all, but light returned, and he found himself in a city, a dead city. White buildings stood tall on either side of a wide, long, and straight street continuing on in the distance. It was empty except for himself and Penny, who was taking a brief rest. Of the others, there were no signs, and the link between them and the others had broken down. The city was immense and looked somehow both futuristic and ancient. Buildings were crafted with a material unknown to him, and the ground was the same. While it must have looked full of splendor at some point, now it was dead. Not a single soul stirred in that wide expanse, and the white was yellowing all over, and crumbling in places. Mateo didn't hear a single sound except that of his own breathing and footsteps as he took a few long strides to the right to peer down a side street. Walking up to it and leaving the wide main thoroughfare, he slammed his face into an invisible wall.

"Ow," he muttered as he stepped back.

Penny looked over. "What's up?"

He put a hand out and found a smooth surface. Pushing on it, there was no give, and he found no gap. "It appears we cannot leave the street."

"Huh," she said, looking up at the gray sky. "What about if we go over?"

"Flight is not in my skill set," Mateo said as he concentrated for a brief moment. A spear appeared in his hand. It was an exact replica of his actual spear, but it would dissolve a few seconds after leaving his hand. Throwing it upward, he found another invisible surface not far above.

"Guess that's a no to flying," she muttered.

A few more thrown spears showed one possible direction. Forward.

"Guess the dungeon wants us to go toward that big building over there," Penny said, getting to her feet with a sigh.

The one she indicated loomed over the rest of the city, a building of spires and slanted roofs, almost like a cathedral. Continuing forward, they would arrive at its main doors.

"What is wrong, mi hij- erm, hermana?" Mateo asked, seeing the expression on Penny's face. Sometimes you forgot how young she was, due to her expertise and iron will, but she lacked the experience only years of living could grant.

"Except for being in a dungeon with a void creature trying to eat all of Earth, you mean? Oh, you know, stressed about exams."

"Exams?" Mateo asked. "School has resumed?"

She laughed, but it was a strained sound as she looked up at the glassless windows of the surrounding buildings. "I'm just messing with you, Mateo." Shaking her head, she continued. "It's still here. Everywhere. Falzyxi put us here, and I don't know why. Don't think we're going to like the answer. Just wish we were with the others."

"We will find them soon, Penny. The strength of my arm might be lacking, but my trust in our friends does not falter."

Penny walked up and wrapped her thin, pale arm around his waist. "There is nothing wrong with how strong you are. You'll figure out all the system stuff soon, I promise. Want to talk it through again while we walk?"

"I understand what you have told me," Mateo said, looking down at her arm. "But the concepts do not come naturally to me. Also, you need to eat more, my friend. You are too thin. My girls eat more than even I!"

She chuckled as they disentangled and started walking. "No time to eat when you're saving the world, but I'll come over and let you feed me with your family once we're done with all this." Considering, she kept going about Mateo's lack of strength. "It's about belief, I think. You need to know that the system is in there, behind everything. You kind of have to force your brain to see it. Maybe try that?"

"Yes, of course," Mateo said, looking forward. "I will try this."

The anxious look on Penny's face made him want to wrap her in a great bear hug and tell her everything was going to be well, but that he could not do. For one, she was far too independent to take that sort of thing well. Also, in his current state, Mateo was not able to make such a promise. So, rather than employing an empty gesture, he focused.

The attributes, Focus and Willpower, were not ones he had spent many points improving, but that was neither here nor there. A person's will was his own, and the way he wielded it was a result of lived experience and trained fortitude. Neither of those was in short supply in his case. That fortitude and experience told him there were no such things as a void or even a system, but a man must be able to alter his opinion when presented with new proof. A man must be able to admit when he is wrong, or he is still a boy.

With that idea in mind, Mateo continued forward while making himself know there was something beyond reality that he could touch, if he was only able to see it. It was there. He knew it was there. Penny said so, and he knew she was a truthful, honorable young woman. Pushing himself, absorbing the knowledge that there was something there he wasn't seeing, Mateo felt something. A shift, perhaps, but it was interrupted by a figure stepping out onto the street right in front of them.

It was a man, sort of. He was perhaps half the size of a normal adult male. Not a little person, either, or a child. Just small. There was something about him that made the hairs on Mateo's arms stand on end, but before he had a chance to even open his mouth, Penny blasted the new arrival with lightning. The crack was deafening, and the boom of thunder was instant. Their invisible cage stopped sound as well, it turned out, because sound bounced back against it, creating an effect that was rather unpleasant.

Ears still ringing, Mateo turned from the wet spot that was all that remained of the man to look at Penny. "You didn't use your words."

She raised an eyebrow. "It was a void thing. You think I should have talked to it?"

"No, no," he said, jiggling his earlobes in an attempt to recover more of his hearing and end the high-pitched whine that threatened to drown out all other sounds. "Your chant, I mean."

"Oh," she said with a chuckle. "Thought you were lecturing me on being diplomatic or something," Penny gave a small shrug. "I don't have to do it anymore. The chanting helps with the more advanced stuff, and the spells are stronger if I give them some linguistic encouragement, but sometimes it's nice to just," she pointed her index finger at the street, "zap-zap."

"Fair," Mateo said as two more small men stepped onto the street. They were identical to the first one, and the invisible box around the area didn't even hinder them. Considering them, Mateo narrowed his eyes. "These are not like that other one. Are they?"

Penny's eyes widened. "You're starting to get it! No, those two little guys are normal. Well, not normal, I guess, but you get my meaning."

"Hello," Mateo said.

The two men spoke as one. Their shrill voices were so high in pitch that Mateo's shoulders bunched up by his ears. "Greetings, Integrated! Welcome to The Greater System. We are in dire need of your aid!"

"Hold on," Penny said, stepping forward. "Welcome to what now?"

Something blurred forward from the left, and another something from the right. Two more small men, except these were out for blood. The first one latched onto Mateo's neck, biting down with surprising jaw strength and teeth that felt like they were made for rending flesh. The second missed Penny's throat, but got her in the side. Its mouth was large, far too large, and she cried out in pain as more and more small humanoids appeared. What seemed like hundreds of them spilled onto the street. The two who had spoken fled screaming up the street, heading for the cathedral-like building in the far distance.
