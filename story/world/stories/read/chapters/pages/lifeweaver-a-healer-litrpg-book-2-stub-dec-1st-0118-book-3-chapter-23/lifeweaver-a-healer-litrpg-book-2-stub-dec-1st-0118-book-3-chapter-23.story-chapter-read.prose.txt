"What is it?" Luke asked.

Nick held his finger in front of his mouth, shushing them. When he replied, it was in a whisper. "Undead. Lots and lots of undead. It's a throne room, I think. Kind of like a church with benches, and then there's a throne at the other end. There's someone sitting in it."

The corner of Clementine's mouth rose to show her disgust, and before anyone realized what she was doing, she had thrown the doors open. "All abominations shall burn in the fires of hell!"

Nick groaned, and Ariel leaned in to whisper in his ear. "I told you letting the nun join was a mistake."

He shook his head without looking at her. Instead, his eyes fixed on the many, many monsters inside the chamber beyond. "She's saved all our lives enough times to get some leeway, but I'll consider her status after this crap. With Vicky joining, maybe her healing will be enough."

Luke ignored their chatter to look into the massive chamber. This was not the only entrance, but it looked like the main one, with the others perhaps being servant entrances or the like, because the doors were smaller. All doors were closed but unguarded. Light shone in from slots in the walls, which was a little strange considering the room should be in the middle of the keep. Chandeliers hung from the ceiling, with hundreds of lit candles in each one to give even more light. Once rich tapestries hung from the walls. Now, they looked ragged and on the brink of falling apart. Everything in the room looked worn, even the wooden benches. No one had seen to the upkeep in a good long while, with one exception. The throne.

There was indeed a throne at the far end of the chamber, but it was too far away to inspect whoever was sitting in it, one leg slung over an armrest, leaning on their elbow. It was a human, as far as he could tell, clad in white. Was this The Conqueror? There was just the one problem for them to overcome before they'd get an answer to that question. A sea of monsters took up the entire chamber, like a sea keeping them away from the other side.

Skeleton. Level 22.

Skeleton Archer. Level 24.

Skeleton Rogue. Level 25.

Skeleton Soldier. Level 22.

On and on. Hundreds of them. Not just skeletons either. Zombies represented a good third of the group, and several other kinds of undead mingled with the grunts as well, like tall, pale-faced liches in dark robes and red-gleaming eyes, and what appeared to be some sort of dog-adjacent creatures that breathed fire, judging by the flames coming out between their teeth as they growled. With those levels, these wouldn't be pushovers either. So far, though, they all just stood there.

"Uh, what do we do?" Hannah asked.

George took one step to the side, so he wasn't visible through the wall. Staying out of trouble seemed to be his answer to most situations.

"We'll need to get to whoever is on that throne," Luke said.

Curtis formed his sword out of darkness. "I’ve regenerated my stamina. Let's go through the skellies."

"You sound like Ray," Hannah admonished.

"Wait," Luke said, "I have a better idea." With that, he pulled up the hood of his robe, activating its effects.

"My eyes hurt," Ariel complained.

Luke took a step forward and turned his back on the monsters. "Undead fear the robe. Stay close to me, and we should be fine."

"Or they'll crush us once we’re in the middle of that horde and they stop being scared," Nick said, shaking his head. "I don't like it."

Leminal: "My underlings will not attack you, humans. You may approach."

"I'm guessing all of you got that message," Luke said after looking around at a lot of stunned faces. They nodded.

Clementine narrowed her eyes. "Do not believe the lies of those who serve under the fallen angels."

"Enough with that shit, Clementine," Ariel said, raising her arms in exasperation. "All this God and devil talk is getting old."

"Don't turn away from the Lord," Clementine whispered. A moment later, she nodded. "I will pray for you."

Ariel turned her glare from the nun to their leader, and Nick raised his hands in surrender. "Let's just get through this dungeon, and then we'll talk."

"I think we should go," Luke said.

Curtis stepped through the door and into the chamber. "Let's just get this over with. Ray and I have a Scrubs marathon to get to."

Luke and Hannah followed.

Luke: "Who are you?"

Leminal: "I am conversing with your leader, the Knight."

"Nick?" Luke asked. "What's Leminal saying?"

Nick shrugged, looking dejected. "That she doesn't like talking through the communication gems. She wants us to come to her."

"Maybe she could come to us?" George said.

"Suggested that already," Nick said. "She wasn't very receptive."

"Ask her if she's The Conqueror," Ariel said.

"She won't say."

Luke was fed up. Being in a large group helped a lot during fights, even if they grew more complex and unpredictable, but in situations like this, where someone had to make a call, more people just meant more confused voices added to the conversation.

"I'm going," Luke said, stepping up to Curtis and then past the Reaver, who turned to follow, along with Hannah.

The trio kept going without looking back, and Hannah leaned in with a shit-eating grin on her face. "Dissing the most famous guild on the East Coast?"

"They're just a bunch of idiots led by an even bigger idiot," Curtis grunted. "Grinding is what they're good at, and they're really good at that, but they've lost three members at this point because they won't slow down."

A door at the right side of the room creaked open, but no one entered. "Speaking of idiots," Luke said. "Ray better not jump in and kill Leminal while we're talking."

As they reached the first couple of undead, a cluster of skeletons wielding one-handed maces, the sound of feet on stone reached them from behind. Phoenix Phalanx had made the decision to follow. Good for them. It was a little unnerving, having so many pairs of dead eyes watching while you walked past them. The first thing they'd done upon entering the keep had been to kill a large number of their brethren, after all. Did they mind? If they did, none of them showed it.

The undead parted for them, but they did not give a lot of room. Staring through empty sockets or eyes devoid of life, they regarded the group as they made their way through the throng with Luke at their head. Nick made several attempts to get past, but found himself unable to do so without touching the skeletons, and he decided to just let it go.

Closer to the front of the room, the monsters' levels increased, and more liches appeared.

Lich. Level 27.

Lich Seer. Level 29.

Lich Apprentice. Level 20.

That wasn't all. More mage-type skeletons appeared as well, denoted by their respective elemental affinities.

Skeletal Fire Mage. Level 23.

Skeletal Ice Mage. Level 24.

Skeletal Thunder Mage. Level 25.

Ashen-skinned humans appeared as well. They were dead, that much was obvious, but they weren't decomposed like the zombies, though about as much life remained behind their eyes. None. Luke wondered at the assembled army. No one gathered a force like this without having a planned use for them, did they?

Leminal remained in her relaxed and somewhat off-kilter sitting position when Luke and the others made their way to the throne. The area right in front of it was free of monsters, giving the group a little room to spread out. She was not human. Up close, that much was clear.

"She's one of them," Curtis muttered out of the side of his mouth.

Luke understood what he was referring to. The height and proportions were human-like, but this Leminal person was of the same species as the ones they'd fought in the goblin dungeon. She kept her almost-white hair long. That, along with her almost timeless face, made him think of elves.

Integrated Leminal. Bonesinger. Level 36.

"Leminal," Luke said, giving her a nod. "I've met your people before."

His comment made her sit up and lean forward. "Oh? Such luck for you to have made friends with the Singers not once, but twice. Truly fortuitous."

Luke cleared his throat. "Uh, yeah."

"I have come into contact with humans on two occasions myself. Each time ended in battle."

"Sounds about right," Curtis scoffed.

Nick glared at his older brother, then turned to address the Singer, as she'd named her kind. His tone of voice was serious and formal, as if trying to match the woman's strange cadence. "May I ask what you're doing in this dungeon?"

She turned her face to him and gave a languid smile. "Why, I'm stuck, of course."

"You came here through a portal, and it closed," Luke said. It wasn't a question.

"So it was, yes. When the world-rending explosion took the dragon we had been sent to destroy, many of my kind succumbed to its fiery destruction." Her eyes glittered. "Such a weapon. Even after all these years, I remember it like it was yesterday."

Oh. Crap.
