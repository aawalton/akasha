Ethan followed Allyara to the central fortress, which had the air of a government office, despite looking like a medieval castle. Selina and Cara walked confidently, both having spent some time in the city before Ethan arrived. They seemed uninterested in the many Nator working to keep the city fed, and organized, but it fascinated Ethan. An ancient, buried alien city seemed so exotic, but in practice it was a logistical nightmare.

Food was the largest problem, although they seemed to have no problem with hunting and eating the animalistic demons from the forest above. Selina had told him that there were also mushroom farms in caverns on the far side of the underground chamber, which grew rapidly and provided most of their nutritional needs. That was impressive, given there were some thousands of Nator down here.

Allyara took them past the larger offices where Vetta and Oreth worked. They were the local leaders of the city, something like paired mayors, but neither one had expressed much interest in Ethan and his grand claims of saving the world. They’d been more interested in Tomo, watching him with unreadable expressions.

But their attitudes were hardly unique. Most of the city was lost to a perpetual melancholy. They were a defeated people, and Ethan couldn’t blame them for that. They’d lost nearly everything that made life worth living, holding on almost out of habit and stubbornness more than anything else. When every child was fated to be twisted and broken, or lost to rage, every generation fell a little further.

Only the few like the mayors avoided those dark fates, and only because visiting Champions had provided what Bonds they could. The power of their Familiars helped them maintain balance, but sober sanity was a poor gift when it merely left you clear-headed enough to watch your people suffer around you.

Ethan could only add Bastion to the list of reasons why he so desperately needed to complete the Union.

Eventually Allyara turned down a broad hallway, and up a flight of stairs. She led them to a large, well-lit chamber, and the source of the light made Ethan do a double-take. There were lightbulbs. Not magical glowglobes, or torches, but something oddly similar to what he might have seen in a supermarket back home.

More than that, the walls were lined with screens, although most were black. Large, clunky machinery was everywhere, covered in switches and dials, and looking strangely familiar to Ethan, though he couldn’t think of exactly why.

“Welcome to Steelbinder’s laboratory,” Allyara said, moving to the center of the room, and leaning against a rectangular object, looking a bit like an old-fashioned iron lung.

“Where’s Steelbinder herself?” Ethan asked.

Allyara sighed softly, then rapped her knuckles against the metal object. Ethan moved forward, eyebrows drawn down. He found something like a window on the side, but looking in revealed nothing but lights and vague shapes.

“What am I looking at?” he asked.

“A Champion closer to death than most would imagine possible,” the dark haired woman replied. “Anyone else likely would be dead, but Treia has several Techwork ascendancies that provide…unusual advantages. What’s left of her is in there, but it isn’t much more than a few chunks of flesh, and a spirit being held together only by her Familiars.”

Ethan looked down again in wonder, runed-hand reaching out automatically. He withdrew it again with a shake of his head. He couldn’t even feel a presence inside, let alone something he could heal.

“You said we were to meet her. What does that entail, exactly?”

In answer to his question, lights began flickering on all over the room, and previously unseen devices lifted off from several directions. They looked like drones, although he couldn’t see what mechanism allowed them to fly. They whizzed around the group, and monitors flashed to life. Some proved the drones to have camera feeds, while others displayed interfaces he recognized.

“This is like the control room of Corvale,” Selina said, beating him to the revelation.

A feminine voice echoed from one of the drones. “You have seen my greatest creation then, young Nator.”

“Steelbinder?” Ethan asked, looking at the nearest drone.

“Welcome, Chosen, I am glad that Allyara was able to rescue you from the Follower army. Your plan was bold, but less successful than we might have hoped.”

“I am uncomfortably aware of that,” Ethan said flatly.

“Plans fail, Chosen, then we plan again,” the drone replied. “We should be grateful that my satellites were able to find you, and even more grateful that Allyara managed to arrive in time.”

Ethan sighed. “I am grateful, but I hope you’ll forgive my frustrations. Wait, did you say satellites?”

The drone bobbed up and down, as if nodding. “At one point I could view most of Potentia. But my power has grown unstable. I calculate that my recovery will take between seven and eight years, without accounting for how I might need to spend my resources in the meantime.”

“That’s quite some time,” Ethan said. “But surveillance of any scale can be valuable.”

“Unfortunately, I require frequent rest, during which time my drones must shut down. I am pushing myself now, as I understand the importance of your quest, but I will make an unreliable ally.”

Ethan nodded. He was getting used to disappointing news. “I’ll take what I can get. I have many questions, but I’ll start with the pressing one. I wanted to warn my army to expect an attack, but Allyara said the Followers were heading north. I take it we have you to thank for the intel?”

A screen lit up, and everyone turned toward it. It was clearly a satellite image, though clearer than Ethan expected. He watched as it zoomed in, passing through thick cloud covering with what Ethan could only imagine was magical enhancements, to finally reveal what must have been the battlefield he’d been rescued from. He recognized the general layout of the valley, and he thought he could spot the path that the Basilisk had carved into the ground as it slithered through the army.

The army itself, however, was nowhere to be seen.

The image then panned out, and shifted rapidly to what seemed to be the north-east, where it eventually slowed over a large dust cloud. Ethan could just barely make out the dark shapes of massive demons moving rapidly, though he couldn’t make out any particular one.

“I can’t be sure where they’re going,” Steelbinder said, “but I have my suspicions.”

“The Throne,” Ethan whispered. “They’re going directly for it. But they can’t have gathered enough corruption! They couldn’t have had enough before the Basilisk destroyed half their army. Why would they be going there now?”

“I cannot answer that,” Steelbinder said. “We have heard of ‘The Throne’, but haven’t been able to study it thoroughly. As you said, interacting with it takes a concentrated, and vast quantity of Dusk’s power. No Champion has yet managed to discover a method of harnessing that power, it’s been all we could do to keep the demons away.”

“They still come for it, usually once every month or two,” Allyara said. “If we have to hold out much longer, one of them will manage to reach it. It’s happened before, though it was long before the Union began. The thing that touched that power…it was a monstrosity beyond anything I’ve ever witnessed.”

Ethan shivered. He’d seen that creature, in Tomo’s ancient memories. Memories of one of his many lives, where he’d actually grown powerful enough to contend for The Throne himself. What he’d faced had been a horror of tentacles and mouths…and power. It had destroyed Tomo easily.

“But it’s gone, right?” Ethan asked, fearing the answer.

Allyara nodded. “It took many, many Champions working together to take it down. This was centuries ago, but we still bear the scars. After that, we knew we had to take precautions.”

“What kind of precautions?” Selina asked, and Ethan heard something strange in her voice, like she had a theory she didn’t want to confirm.

It was Steelbinder who answered, her own lecturing tone surprisingly similar to Selina’s. “While we couldn’t pinpoint The Throne directly, there have been more than enough clues to make the general location obvious. I believe you’ve seen the rift, Chosen?”

Ethan nodded. “On my first trip to Potentia. And my second, I suppose. I think it was left by Noctem, as the way for Dusk to leave the planet when the Union was complete. I saw Dawn use something similar when she left Nexum.”

There was a long pause, and Ethan realized Allyara’s eyes were wide, and she glanced at the nearest drone as if looking for confirmation.

“What?” Ethan asked, shrugging at Cara.

“You’re name-dropping,” the Nator said. “Most of us haven’t spent quite so much time around gods.”

“Oh, right. Well, it hasn’t been pleasant, if that helps.”

Allyara shook her head, and he thought he saw her mouth “Noctem” to herself. He’d forgotten that most people didn’t even know there was a fourth god.

Steelbinder continued after another pause. “As you say, it is likely tied to the gods. We assumed it was Dusk himself who created it…but that’s a matter for another day. As I was saying, there were clues as to the location of The Throne. The rift is one, and that terrible Champion, another.”

Allyara nodded. “Knowing the general location, we decided that we needed something permanent to help protect the area. We could never be sure how many Champions–if any–would be able to watch over it. Of course, building anything on Potentia comes with countless complications.”

Ethan felt his stomach drop, and looked over at Selina, who was shuffling her feet anxiously. Steelbinder didn’t seem to notice, and kept explaining.

“The decision was made to build a contingency on Nexum, where we had access to far greater resources, and the comparative danger was much lower. We needed something of remarkable power, that we could use a staging ground to mount offensives, or take defensive action if someone ever came for The Throne.”

Allyara smiled, the first time Ethan had seen that, which only made him feel worse. “So there is some good news,” she said brightly. “We may be low on resources, but we do still have our fallback plan. The rituals are already in place to bring the defensive platform from Nexum to Potentia, and it should give the Followers an unwelcome surprise.”

Ethan forced himself to speak. “You’re talking about Corvale,” he said weakly.

“Of course,” Steelbinder replied. “You mentioned you’d seen it, so no doubt you have some idea of its power. The city has been patrolling around the Rift for centuries, waiting for this moment of great need. We’ll have to make arrangements for anyone living in the city, of course, but we’ll have a weapon at our disposal worth any inconvenience.”

Ethan cleared his throat. “About that… You’re aware of the Dimensional Bleeds?” Allyara and the drone nodded, sharing a look. “Right. Well, you see, there was a big one, and Corvale isn’t exactly on Nexum anymore.”

Allyara’s face looked concerned, but Steelbinder’s voice sounded confident. “That’s unfortunate, but not an unsolvable problem. It will take some effort, but I think we can adjust the ritual. It targets Corvale itself, so they should still be connected. We’ll just need to make sure it comes here from Earth, instead of Nexum.”

Ethan squirmed. “Right, that’s great, but… Unfortunately, there’s another problem. We sort of, used the city already. Thoroughly.”

Ethan didn’t know how, but the drone seemed to be glaring at him. “Please clarify. What does ‘used’ mean, Chosen?”

“Well, we needed to fight Twilight. There weren’t a lot of ways to do that…so we had to use Corvale.”

The silence was deafening, but finally the drone spoke again. “You fought a god…with my city. How did it do?”

Ethan and Selina nodded rapidly together, trying to look positive. “It did great!” Selina said proudly. “You could say it saved the day!”

“Defnitely,” Ethan agreed. “Day saved. By Corvale. Totally did what it was built for. Absolutely worth it.”

“And what condition is it in now?” Allyara asked flatly.

“Well, it’s still a city,” Ethan said. Then shrugged slightly. “It’s just not a walking, magic-blasting city anymore.” Finally he sighed. “It’s basically in ruins,” he admitted.

All the drones shut down at once, and the room went dark.
