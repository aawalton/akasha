The well-dressed man, looking like any of the visiting lords or nobles that Toar had ever seen around his family in his youth, strikingly similar, cleared his throat.

The murmurs stopped. Everyone looked towards the man as he clasped his hand together, looking over each of them with a passive expression.

He was young, maybe in his mid-twenties. But looks could be deceptive at higher Tiers, and most nobles—even minor nobles—were at least Tier 3.

“You are all here to answer for the crime that took place in this excavation zone over a week prior.” The man turned to one of the guards, who whispered in his ear.

“A doctor of great repute, a man who devoted his life to curing the sick and aiding the crippled, has died in this place, murdered by one of your lot.”

It was clear from listening to him speak that this man either barely knew Mansol or didn’t whatsoever. It was as if he was just being filled in on the going-ons now. Nonetheless, he spoke with indignance, as if Toar and his fellow workers were ants who had dared to irritate a great beast.

“From what I have heard, not one of you has come forth and admitted to this crime,” he continued. “Apparently, a couple of you saw fit to blame one another, but investigation has proven those accusations to be baseless.”

He sneered. “You vermin have murdered one of your leaders, and when questioned, either lie and obscure the truth or attempt to use our loss to further your own interests. You’re less than human. And you will pay for daring to encroach upon our dominion.”

There was a noise behind him, a trap door being opened and closed, tested. The creak sounded between his words, the only other sound a faint sobbing to Toar’s left.

“When we gave you this opportunity,” the noble continued, seeming to enjoy this speech more by the second, his voice raising dramatically. “You were allowed freedoms. Self-governance. The ability to work together and thrive in this place, to take whatever you wished to strive for, with only one exception.”

His voice hardened. “We rescued you from depraved lives, and gave you everything. You have been treated fairly. Your only expectation was a simple one. Do not bite your masters. Do not rob from us. Do not attack us. Certainly do not kill us. For many of you, we are the parents you never had. We have provided far more for you. What kind of sick, twisted animal attacks his own mother and father?”

He sniffled, as if he were actually affected by his sick speech. “Dogs, all of you. And if you’re going to act like mongrels, then we will tighten the leash.” He spread his arms. “Look past me. This is what your disobedience has earned you.”

The rope had finally been put up. The stage set. For it was a stage, really. To the man before them, all of this was a performance.

“We didn’t do it!” someone from another group shouted. A girl. Toar didn’t know who.

“Silence,” the noble shouted. “The next of you to interrupt me will lose their tongue. Do I make myself clear?”

Deafeningly. Toar squirmed as he watched on. Maisie clutched his hand. She was shaking.

“Truthfully, you have failed on both fronts,” the noble continued, pacing across the stage as he spoke. “Of the sixteen adolescent rifts across the country, this one is the twelfth in terms of productivity.” His eyes narrowed. “Now, we usually don’t instill punitive measures in rifts that don’t need them, but it’s clear that it’s not just a matter of laziness here, it’s a matter of contempt. Of insolence.”

“Lord Overseer!”

“What?” the noble turned, looking at the hangman.

“The mechanism for the trap door is sticking. I’m going to need to oil it. Can we postpone things for just ten minutes while I do so, my lord?”

The noble peered at his subordinate.

“You could see I was talking, couldn’t you?”

His voice was low, almost a whisper. If the surrounding room wasn’t so silent, Toar might not have heard it.

“Y-yes, my lord, but I thought I should make you aware—”

What happened next was terrifying. The man was hoisted in the air, his legs dangling, choking on a spectral blue hand of magic. A moment later, his tongue was being pulled from his mouth by an invisible force.

A swift slice echoed through the air, a gust of wind almost invisible to the eye. The man’s tongue fell to the floor of the scaffold, limp, as blood began to pool in his mouth.

“I told you, the next person to interrupt me loses their TONGUE.” He dropped the man to the floor, wordlessly screaming, tears welling in his eyes. “Are you so stupid as to think that does not apply to you? Could you not have waited until I was finished to tell me something so insignificant?”

He rolled around on the ground, his scream piercing. Multiple workers screamed in turn. The sobbing became louder.

Toar wasn’t even sure what level of magic that was. Tier 3? 4? Even higher? It’d happened so quickly. So brutally.

“Grr… quiet.”

Everyone obeyed. Even the sobs became silent.

“You,” he pointed to a stationary guard. “Take this oaf away. You,” he pointed to a second, “oil the… whatever this one said.”

The noble tutted at the tongueless man was dragged away. He kicked the man’s loosed tongue off the platform, where it splatted on the floor.

“I’ve lost my whole rhythm, now,” he complained. “That’s really taken the impact from all of this. Apologies for the interruption.”

With a humourless shrug, he continued. “I’ll put it simply. You have killed one of us, who is worth an infinite number of you. We have given the culprit ample time to come forth, and they have declined.

“We are going to kill two of you in turn. We think this is generous.”

No one spoke. Toar’s nose twitched with the scent of sweat. None of the guards seemed to overtly react, but some seemed uncomfortable. Would they really allow something like this to happen?

“Wait.”

Toar spoke before realising he’d opened his mouth. He felt Maisie grip his hand tighter, so much it hurt.

Still he barely noticed. His eyes locked with the noble as he turned to face him. At first, he looked as if he were going to loudly demand Toar’s tongue be cut out, like the last interruption.

Instead, he smiled.

“A Halfshade!” He looked to Toar with warmth in his eyes. “I’d been told that there was one of you working in this place, but we never did meet. If you’re worried for your life, young lord, don’t be. We wouldn’t waste your blood.”

Toar blinked at that. It was a literal free pass.

It didn’t matter. He didn’t care.

“I was the one who killed him,” Toar continued, ignoring the gasps and whispers around him. “I killed Mansol.”

The noble, in stark disregard to their setting, laughed.

“You?” he chuckled, before full on laughing. He clutched at his belly. After composing himself with a gasp, he narrowed his eyes, which turned a glassy silver.

“You’re a fresh Tier 2. Class, Scout. Still recovering from multiple wounds inflicted over a month prior.” The noble tilted his head as he stared at Toar. “Okay, I’ll go along with this. You killed the doctor. You and who else?”

Toar’s breath caught in his throat. “Me alone,” he forced out. “I found out he’d hurt another worker, and I attacked him.”

Toar was speaking about Adam. There was no way he was going to mention Jackal or Finn. Not a single chance.

“You attacked him alone?” the noble asked, the mirth gone from his tone. “And you… killed him.”

“Yes.”

Toar said only that. He was scared his words might fail him. Even now, his throat was clamming up. He was struggling to speak.

He was going to die.

But if it was to prevent anyone else from dying for his mistake, it was good that he died here.

Right?

The noble squinted at him a little longer. He bit his lip. No one said a word as they waited to see what the man might come out with next, each moment of inaction like the coiling of a snake, slow and suffocating.

Eventually, he inhaled.

“I don’t believe you,” he said. “You don’t possess nearly enough power, even with the element of surprise.” He hummed for a moment. “If you wish to tell me the full story now, I will punish those responsible. Otherwise, you may consider your claim withdrawn.”

Toar panicked as he spoke.

“No. It was me! Just me!”

“I apologise, young lord, but this matter is now closed to discussion,” the noble said, giving him a sad smile.

“Just listen to m—”

Toar’s throat ran dry. He tried to speak, but no more sound came.

Was this magic? Had the noble muted him with a spell?

“New rule!” the noble said, clapping his hands once. “The next person who speaks out of turn, I will personally kill. Isn’t that fun? No more confessions. We will achieve justice by our own hand.”

Toar attempted to push forward, to continue to interrupt, but he felt himself being grabbed from behind.

Marcois' hand was on his shoulder. His expression was grim. He shook his head.

Toar understood what he was trying to say. It didn’t matter what he did. Any way he tried to derail things now would just result in more death. He had to sit through this.

But people were going to die because of what he did. How could he?

“We’ll be drawing straws for the first culprit,” the Overseer announced. “Each worker whose name is called, step up to the podium and retrieve a straw from this gentleman on my left.” He pointed to a guard stood below.

“Then, present what you’ve pulled and find out if you’ve won.”

The speech ended there. The circle of miners erupted into chaos and noise.

No one attempted to silence them this time. Toar found himself able to speak. He looked over to the noble, who’d had a chair brought to him and was now sitting and watching everyone below panic, like he was in a gallery examining living art.

“What do we do?” Toar asked as his group huddled closer to him, unmasked fear in his voice. “How do we stop this?”

“You could’ve handed us in,” Finn said. “You’d have probably gotten away with it. Maybe you should have told him everything.”

“Fuck that!” Jackal shouted. “I don’t want anyone to die, but I did jack shit wrong! I didn’t ask for that maniac to attack me!”

“So what are we gonna do about it?” Toar repeated, his voice louder this time.

“We should attack them,” Jackal whispered. “There’s way more of us. Like a hundred versus twenty. If we all went at once, we could definitely overpower them. There’s no way they’d be able to handle it.”

“I’m with Jackal,” Ceri nodded.

“It’s a bad idea,” Marcois weighed in. “Maybe some of us can fight, but most don’t have weapons, and those guards are trained. Trained and strong. You saw what one of them did to Jackal a minute ago. If we wanted a chance, we’d have to be far more prepared than this.”

He was right. That was without even considering what that overseer might be able to do. The noble had commanded powerful magic with ease. Who knew how powerful he was?

“Wait, so you just want to do nothing?”

More names kept being called. Esther, Canco, Pel’di… Marcois.

The whole group stirred at the shout of his name. They turned to him, expecting an answer.

“You can’t be fine with this,” Ceri whispered.

“This isn’t fair!” a shout came from further afield.

“If we fight now, all of us will die,” Marcois said, his voice hard, unwavering. “If this is what we have to do to stay alive, we do it.”

He looked between the rest of his group one more time. “I have to go. Don’t do anything foolish. I’m begging you.”

That was all he said. Marcois marched off without another word. Before long, he was standing in a line of unhopefuls, waiting to be given a chance to take a straw.

Toar didn’t look at the others lined up. He felt nauseous. Maisie was crying beside him. Jackal was, too. Finn was biting at his own knuckles, hard enough he broke the skin. Ceri looked on with steel in her eyes.

A silence descended over the crowd as each of us waited to discover who would have to die. It was like all of them had forgotten how to breathe. As if inside their little ring, time itself had stopped.

The silence built to a crescendo. It was broken by a stream of purple noise.

“Winner!” the noble sung, his voice cresting high above waves of despair.

The crowd focussed on the boy that stood before him. It took Toar a moment to recognise him.

It was Selsor.

Of course, he protested. Screamed that something was wrong, that he had been groomed for a leadership position by powerful people, that his stick wasn’t the shortest.

The Overseer cut all of that off.

“Selsor Maryx,” he spoke. “I remember your name coming across my desk years ago.” He smiled. “You’re right, you were chosen for a leadership position. If I recall, you told your interviewer you let grown men beat you for coppers, just so your sister could eat. You showed resilience.”

Selsor said nothing. His greasy black hair stuck to his face.

“Your Actor class, combined with your advanced Pain Tolerance, made you an interesting asset to speculate on.” His voice turned cold. “I can see now what you wound up being worth to us.”

He clapped his hands twice. Selsor was taken away, kicking and screaming. One of them struck him in the chest, and the gangly boy became limp.

He was dragged to the gallow, his head threaded through the noose. He feebly clutched at the rope as it was fastened. His hands slipped repeatedly as he attempted to pull death from his neck.

He died struggling. The floor fell away, and Selsor died gasping and choking, dreaming of a future that never would be.

“Next!”

They weren’t allowed time to catch their breaths. To think. To process. Toar had hated Selsor. He’d wanted to kill the boy himself on multiple occasions for all the scummy things he’d seen him do. He’d never once considered that he might have family he cared for, or any reason behind his actions, but none of that mattered to him. None of it excused who Selsor had chosen to become, an abuser and a tyrant.

But it didn’t give Toar any pleasure to see him die like that. Everyone hated Selsor, he was sure that he hadn’t had a single friend. When he’d been deposed from his group in disgrace, he’d struggled to find anyone who would take him for simple mine work. He’d been barely scraping by.

And now he was dead. And while there were no tears shed for him, there were no cheers either. The boot that pressed down against Selsor still weighed heavy on them all, and while one person had died now, the axe falling swiftly, there was still another who had yet to be claimed.

Would it be another evil person? Another who deserved their fate? What were the chances of that?

“Okay, children! Line up! Let’s see which of you is lucky enough to be chosen next!”

“I volunteer,” a deep voice boomed, though Toar couldn’t determine the origin.

“You can’t volunteer,” the noble sneered. “You must be selected, and what did I say about interrupting!” He turned about suddenly, marching ahead. “Okay. If you wish to die by my hand, then so be it.”

“You think you can kill me alone?”

The challenge was unprecedented, insane. Toar had a suspicion of who it might be, and it was only confirmed when he pushed past the crowd around him to the front of the line.

Vulrak stood ahead of the others, mountainous in his height and width, his long, golden mane flowing down his back. Compared to him, the other miners, the noble, and even some of the guards looked small. He was strength personified. The strongest group leader, and a man most every worker respected.

Him and Toar had only spoken twice, Toar being summoned both times. In each instance, he’d felt both well-respected and scared for his life, but Vulrak, despite his immense aura, had never hurt him. In fact, when Toar had killed Farro, he’d expressed sympathy.

“Such a brazen child…” The Overseer’s eyes turned silver, once again assessing, from what Toar could tell.

He stopped advancing as his eyes shifted back to blue. He looked up at Vulrak, a touch of the confidence drained from his face.

“Tier 3… a Warlord class. You’re Vulrak.”

“I am,” the lion breathed.

“Tsh, not that it matters,” the noble continued, his breath seemingly caught. “I could kill you in an instant if I saw fit.”

“If you saw fit?” Vulrak asked, his voice slow and deliberate. “You said the next person who spoke, you would kill yourself.

“Are you a man of your word?”

“You are not a person,” the Overseer countered. “You, as per our purchase of you, are property.”

“You’re playing word games now?” Vulrak asked. “Tell me, is the grand Overseer too afraid to face me?”

The noble laughed; he cackled. His face turned red.

“You’re… you’re what passes for royalty amongst these peasants, aren’t you?” He wiped a tear from his eye. “My. It must be rather difficult for you to be reminded of the true power structure here. To be reminded that you, Vulrak, are in fact a slave.

“You have no power here,” the noble continued. “I do not have to grace your wishes with a response. If you continue to be difficult, then you will be subdued.”

“Then subdue me.”

The noble snapped his fingers.

He didn’t dare engage Vulrak himself. A guard approached, a smile upon his face, pulling a spear from his back.

He spun the weapon twice and then thrusted it at Vulrak’s leg. The lion dodged before jabbing the guard directly in the face.

He fell back, clutching his nose.

Toar watched in shock as three more guards quickly surrounded Vulrak, weapon drawn. He took down one with a sweep of the legs, then punched another in the chest so hard his ribs cracked. The lion turned to face the third, claws extended, his breaths a low simmering roar.

That guard backed up, giving ground despite having a weapon over the beast.

Someone cheered. A boy in the distance drew a dagger from his belt.

Vulrak’s defiance was making others confident. He’d just one guard out completely, who was still on the floor clutching at his ribs, and the two others he’d knocked down looked sluggish. If he got ahold of a weapon, he might be able to kill them.

Maybe they could fight back. Maybe they had a chance.

“Maxwell!”

That sentiment came to a swift end as the noble called the guard’s name. Toar watched as the guard rushed Vulrak from behind, embedding his sword in the lion’s shoulder.

Vulrak roared, defiantly swiping at Maxwell, who ducked under his attack and withdrew his weapon. Vulrak deflected two more attacks from the guards still hovering around him, slashing at one’s face and tearing off a part of his nose. As that scream filled the air, Maxwell advanced once more, another swift stab embedding itself in Vulrak’s side.

The lion still refused to drop to his knees. He panted as he fought, but his attacks were all missing now. Before long, the other guards were able to land stabs of their own.

The lion, the sole defender of these trapped souls, fell to his knees.

The fight in him still remained. A guard moved in to restrain him and was thrown to the ground.

The tension broke in a war cry. Multiple miners rushed toward the guards to defend Vulrak, Toar included. It didn’t matter if it was possible to win anymore, only that they didn’t watch this man die alone.

The rebellion was short-lived. Most fell from a punch, a kick, a backhand. The high-tiered guards were opponents unlike anything the weaker miners could face, and they weren’t able to even hurt them without weapons. Each was as powerful as Mansol. Some more so.

By the time Toar had eaten dirt, he’d at least managed to land a couple of substantial blows to the guard he’d tackled, the one who’d been attempting to hold Vulrak down. The man looked dazed, his lip split, but Toar had been pulled away before he could do real damage.

All of the would-be attackers—about ten altogether—were thrown back into the crowd.

Toar wished he’d acted sooner. That he hadn’t had to see Vulrak’s group rush to his defense to gain a measure of courage.

“Now, I do hope your little tantrum is over,” the Overseer said, looking over the gathered group before approaching Vulrak, who was currently being held down by four men.

He was bleeding, but it seemed to be slowing by the second. Vulrak was inhumanly durable, even for a lion beastkin. He might’ve been overpowered, even exhausted, but he was still alive. Whether he’d be killed now or not was…

The noble and Vulrak simply stared at one another for a time. Vulrak’s gaze was unwavering. He didn’t blink. He was a bastion of fairness and order in this place, and while his authority had been challenged, while the odds were unfairly stacked against him, he hadn’t lost his conviction.

Even if it was ultimately pointless.

Marcois had been right.

“Well, we can’t have you doing that anymore…” the noble snort-laughed. “You’re dangerous! You could’ve killed someone!”

He bit his lip for a moment, as if he were in thought. Humming, he pulled a strange-looking, thick metal band out of thin air. An [Inventory], Toar knew.

“Remember what this does, Vulrak? I’m told you used to wear one.”

If the metal device meant anything to the lion, he didn’t show it. He didn’t even flinch.

“Make sure he’s still,” the noble told the guards holding him. “I don’t want my fingers being torn off.”

They held Vulrak in place. Meanwhile, the noble unfastened the metal ring and then placed it over Vulrak’s neck, the material tight and unyielding.

It clamped shut, clearly a touch too small for his broad neck, but holding all the same.

“I doubt I have to tell you what that does. I’m sure you remember.”

Vulrak said nothing. He didn’t move or respond. Even when the guards relinquished their hold on him, he stayed on his knees, impassive.

“Really?” The noble leaned forward. “I expected some choice words.”

He walked a couple of steps closer, until he was only ten feet away from Vulrak.

“Or for you to try and attack me.”

Vulrak didn’t move. Just continued staring.

“Still nothing? Well, I don’t like the look on your face. So let’s give a demonstration.”

The Overseer pointed a finger. An electric current pulsed through Vulrak’s body, visible even from the outside.

At first, he didn’t react. Even as electricity rippled across his body, Vulrak didn’t move. But after only three seconds, his lips began to snarl. In five, he was roaring in pain.

He couldn’t resist it. Vulrak lunged forward, his head touching the dirt, his elbows pressed down, clutching at the collar in agony. In moments, he’d been reduced to something beyond a roar, a scream.

The Overseer let the charge run through him for another five, agonising, writhing seconds before finally abating. When he did, Vulrak laid in a pool of his own sweat, his body still twitching. He attempted to push himself back to his knees, but slipped.

“Fascinating little devices. Work on almost all creatures Tier 4 and below…” He walked over to the now docile Vulrak and tapped on a little crystal at the front of his collar.

“This is connected to a similar crystal that I hold within this ring,” he showed his hand to the group, which contained multiple rings, but one diamond was bigger and brighter than the others.

“All I have to do is push a little mana into it, and the receiver crystal activates all of the mana in the subject’s body. Like so.”

He shocked him again. Vulrak convulsed for a second and was still.

“Honestly, I’d happily just fit you all with these, but they are rather expensive, so other deterrants are necessary. Speaking of which!

“Line up.”

Names were once again called. No one from Toar’s group this time. The pit in his stomach was so large that it was beginning to truly hurt. This was torture. This was hell.

A short straw was drawn. Vulrak was still unconscious. No one dared move against the guards again, not after their strongest had been felled.

Toar kept wondering if there was something he could’ve done better. If there was a way he could’ve prevented all of this. He kept thinking and thinking. It was all he could do.

It was another older boy that was chosen. Seventeen or eighteen, a member of Vulrak’s group who aided in the fifteen second rebellion. He wore robes, rather than a jumpsuit. Toar thought he might remember him being a healer.

There were some tears, but the people in Vulrak’s group were shockingly able to keep it together. The boy told them that it was okay, and that they needed to look out for each other and keep safe.

Toar realised that above anything, Vulrak’s group were a unit. They knew what the mines were, and accepted the situation they were in. Vulrak had attempted to sacrifice himself in place of someone else, and when that had been rejected, he’d not backed down. He likely hadn’t believed he could kill the Overseer, but when challenged, he hadn’t capitulated.

Vulrak had showed strength in the face of tyranny, rather than bowing and begging for his life. Why? Because he wanted the others to see their own strength? Or was it simply pride?

The robed boy walked toward the gallow. The noble raised his hand.

“Hmm. No. I will spare you. Get back in line.”

The boy’s face twisted. He’d looked more than ready to die. He didn’t speak a word.

“Frankly, I feel as if this show of force hasn’t had its desired effect,” he sighed. “You’re all especially brave. It’s almost commendable.”

A smile slithered up his face. “But I don’t want you brave. I want you broken.”

He began pointing to different miners one after the other. Back and forth. Humming to himself. After twenty seconds of deliberation, his finger stilled.

“That one.”

Everyone looked, following his eyeline to see who he’d chosen to die.

“No. Not my sister!”

Toar’s eyes settled upon the Overseer’s victim.

A girl about Adam’s age. She only looked thirteen or fourteen. Barely beyond a child. She was all of five feet, with strawberry blonde hair resting around her shoulders and dark green eyes. Beside her, clutching to her like a blanket, was a boy about her age with the exact same hair and eye colour.

“Oh. Are you twins?”

“Not my sister. Not my fucking sister!”

A guard separated the two. The girl was dragged away. She barely fought back. She just kept staring at her brother and telling him not to cry.

A boy held the brother back, a group leader Toar recognised. Garrett. As far as Toar knew, he was a peak Tier 1, and yet he still appeared to struggle to keep the boy in check as he desperately clawed and screamed for his sister to come back.

The faces either side of Toar were gaunt. Marcois had pinned Ceri down. Tears streamed down Maisie’s face. Jackal had buried his face in his hands.

More sobs broke as she raised to the platform. She walked, rather than be dragged. There was no fight in her body, no pointless struggle.

More sobs broke. The boy’s screams became piercing. Again, she spoke to him, but her words couldn’t be made out over the chaos.

“This is the feeling I wanted to evoke,” the noble announced, a splitting grin upon his face. “This sight, this sound… this is what I want each and every one of you to REMEMBER when you next think of defying your masters. Burn it into your soul. Shudder when you hear the name Draegar.

“For I will be watching.”

He gave a bow. As he did, the lever was pulled and the floor falled away from the girl.

Her struggle began soon after. She didn’t go gently. She fought and pulled against the rope until her eyes turned red, until her skin turned purple.

Toar did as he was bidden. He burned it into his soul. Even when his sight was blocked by tears, he continued to commit the scene to memory.

Not just his hate. His inadequacy. His desire to change. To be better. To prevent something like this from ever happening again.

The Overseer, Lord Draegar, a name Toar had never heard, left following the girl’s execution. He came to learn she was called Ellis. Garrett had covered her brother’s eyes as she hanged.

The conversation after was muted. Before the groups were permitted to leave, a guard came to the podium and announced that each group would be monitored more closely from now on to ensure they were being productive. That there would be punishments if they couldn’t bring their standard closer to the top.

It was becoming increasingly clear to Toar that he couldn’t leave this place. Not when he had friends who were stuck here. His contract might expire, and he might be free of all this control, but where would that leave the others? Plus anyone new that was convinced to come here? People like Ellis and her brother?

Toar increasingly found himself asking what Adam would do. A young boy faced against an older, more experienced, and more powerful opponent, and he’d still managed to take Toar down. He was clever. He was a schemer. And, more than anything else?

He was committed.

“Wee!”

I screamed in joy as me and Daryn rode the Roller Blade for the third time. There’d been a couple of new attractions at Baytan’s theme park that Daryn had convinced me to try first, but after experiencing the Roller Blade for the first time, I found myself completely agreeing with Daryn’s earlier assessment.

Fourteen more loop-de-loops, a hundred-foot drop, and we were done. Panting, gasping for air, I jumped out of my seat and made to queue up again.

“Whoa! Hold on there, buddy,” Daryn poked him on the shoulder. “Let’s get something to eat before we go on more rides.”

Wow, I completely forgot food existed. When we’d first gotten to the theme park, all I’d wanted to do was eat. Going on the rides had seemed scary, and my instincts had told me to steer well clear.

Now, three hours later, I’d become completely enmeshed in the feeling of being on rides. It was just as thrilling as combat, just without all of the worrying for my life that was usually involved. On the first ride, Daryn had told me to relax and only hold on to the handrail if I felt I needed to.

I’d quickly adjusted, and soon realised the earlier rides I’d gone on had been relatively tame compared to the Roller Derby, even having young Drassian kids on them.

I almost wondered if Daryn had been building me up to something bigger. I was glad I hadn’t immediately gone on the biggest ride. But now I felt fearless. Like I wanted to tackle everything the park had.

That said, as soon as I got some food in me, my body started to crash. I ate a big slab of meat covered in bread on either side called a ‘burger’, and for dessert, Daryn brought me to a stand where they made a fluffy pink concoction that tasted like sugar and joy.

I was still munching on my candy floss as we left the theme park. Daryn told me we could come back tomorrow if we liked, but he’d better show me to my ‘hotel’ now.

It was something like a huge inn, with multiple bars and restaurants downstairs, but it also had tons of other facilities. Baths, a training area called a gym, an indoor swimming pool, and an even bigger swimming pool outside. Daryn told me I could explore the facilities as much as I liked, and then showed me to my room.

It was like an entire floor of the orphanage. I had to resist the urge to run around and exclaim at the sight of such a huge room. When he told me that this room had been rented for me for as long as I wished to stay, assuming I didn’t file for citizenship, I almost had a heart attack. I had to sit down for a moment just to take all of it in.

“There’s drinks in the fridge, they’re charged to the room. So you can basically consider them free. Oh! And if you pick up this phone and press the button, you’ll be put through to reception, and they’ll bring you anything you want, even breakfast in bed.”

I squinted at him. “What’s a phone?”

Daryn explained to me how a phone worked. Further than that, he gave me one.

It didn’t look anything like the phone next to the massive, super-comfy bed. It was small, sliding, and hand-held. He told me it was in something called ‘grandma mode’ to make it easier to use, and explained to me how to call him using it if I ever needed anything.

I thanked Daryn. He said he’d be around in the morning tomorrow, just before lunch, and that I could freely explore the hotel in the meantime.

He left, and I sat and thought about my day.

This might’ve been the first time in months that I hadn’t spent a day either mining, fighting for my life, trying to develop my skills, or working in service to a mysterious god.

I’d had a lot of fun today. I liked it a lot.

Drassia was pretty awesome.

When I curled up in bed, the softest bed I’d ever laid in, I found myself crying for a moment. I didn’t really know why. I wiped myself off with a nearby piece of soft paper and relaxed my posture. I took a deep breath.

I didn’t have to stay here. I could leave whenever I wanted.

Tomorrow I’d find something productive to do. Some way to grow stronger, or grow my wealth. Something.

I had so many goals—I hadn’t lost sight of them.

I wouldn’t betray myself.
