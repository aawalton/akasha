Hours had passed since Aodhán went completely still, and everyone was beginning to worry.

"What do you think he's meditating on?" Crixis asked anxiously.

"I cannot say," Sophran muttered, glancing at Seamus and Mirelle, who were still deep in meditation. "Nor can I say for the others either."

Darien looked at them and shook his head in amazement. "How are they still meditating? We used only a sliver of energy for them compared to what I used for Aodhán."

"Which means Aodhán will probably remain under longer," Sophran surmised, exhaling deeply. "Oh, what I would give to be in their position right now. A replicable treasure that incites outstanding focus and understanding?"

"A hyper-state of genius," Darien nodded with a small smile. "This is more than just revolutionary. Its implications for the sect are incalculable. If the raindrop I create is this powerful, then how much more potent will those of Mythics be? Of Calamities?"

Crixis shuddered at the thought. "Damn, he's amazing."

"That he is," Sophran said with a smile. "First the interface, and now this? He might have incited a war, but he's certainly providing us with enough power to withstand it."

Silence settled over them as the three boys watched their friends meditate with poorly concealed jealousy. That jealousy didn't dull their vigilance, however. They remained alert in case someone gained a seal and fell prey to the twisted effect in this cramped space.

Fortunately, the sect had taken extensive measures to manage such situations.

And they also had Varéc.

Crixis glanced at the Fury curled up in one corner of the training room, slitted irises sharp with quiet alertness. As relaxed as he appeared, Crixis could tell that Varéc was more than ready to leap to Aodhán's defense at a moment's notice.

Perhaps even defend Aodhán from them.

Their gazes met, and Varéc bared his fangs in what Aodhán had once called a happy smile. Crixis gave a shaky smile in return and quickly looked away, his heart hammering in his chest.

His racing pulse gradually slowed, and after a few more seconds of silence, he spoke again.

"Are all transmigrants this incredible?" He glanced at Sophran. "Were you… like this?"

"Oh no," Sophran snorted. "I had my own impact on the sect, but never like this. Nymera, maybe. She's a beast. But Dravion Ashscar and I were nothing like this. His expression sobered. "Me especially. I'm pretty ordinary."

Darien snorted. "Don't mind him. He's being humble. Sophran here was the second transmigrant to join the sect. His presence alone sparked a drastic increase in the sect's population. You know how it works."

Crixis nodded, and Sophran shrugged. "It's still nothing nearly as impressive as the interface or this." He smiled faintly. "I fear that when it all comes down to it, my impact on the sect will be considered negligible."

"Not to us," Darien said softly, his expression tinged with concern. "Your impact on my life—on our lives—has been tremendous. You care, Sophran, and that matters more than raw power. Where are Nymera and Dravion now? You could have gone with them, but you stayed for us."

Sophran chuckled. "I didn't want you guys ruining the sect while I was away."

"See?" Darien laughed. "That's your impact. You hold us together. Even Dravion and Nymera, for all their power, listen to you because they know you care. So what if your presence doesn't spark a continent-wide war or reinvent the sect's hierarchy? You're making an impact in your own way, and that's more than impressive."

Sophran studied Darien for a moment, then nodded. "Yeah, you're right. I am impressive. I did manage to hold all of you off in the high-speed battle without breaking a sweat."

Darien chuckled. "Well, there goes the sappy moment."

They all laughed, but that laughter stuttered to an immediate end when Seamus suddenly gasped, inhaling deeply as if he was tasting air for the first time.

"Are you alright?" Sophran asked as they all rushed toward him, eyes scanning his bare chest for any sign of a new seal. They found none and exhaled deeply.

"Well, that's a relief," Sophran muttered, and Seamus turned to glare at him.

"No, Sophran, it's not a relief. It's a disaster. I was so close to an inspiration before the hyper-focus just fizzled out." He looked up at Sophran as if about to ask for another raindrop, but his shoulders sagged and he shook his head. "Oh, who am I deceiving? It was enough. My insight and understanding of my path were what were lacking."

He smiled and began explaining. "It was like every single thing I've ever heard, seen, or thought pertaining to my path was laid out before me. Things I don't even remember seeing or hearing. Like a puzzle scattered into fragments. With all the cards on the table, building them into an inspiration was a breeze."

"But you didn't have all the pieces," Darien surmised, and Seamus nodded.

"No, I didn't. So I couldn't complete the inspiration."

The boys exchanged glances, and Crixis frowned. "So all the treasure does is put all your cards on the table? All the fragments of insights you've gathered?"

"And the ones your subconscious has registered," Darien whispered, his eyes bright. "Everything you've ever learned concerning a topic is brought to bear, hundreds of days of meditation condensed into hours."

"Exactly," Seamus nodded. "I need to do this again, but I need more insight concerning my skills, ideals, path, and everything." He looked up at them and pushed himself to his feet. "I need knowledge."

They all stared at him for a moment, and then Sophran said, "Well, thank the storm we have a library just down the road."

Crixis chuckled and glanced back at Aodhán. He understood now why Aodhán had asked him to wait. He would gain a lot from just absorbing the treasures, but he would gain so much more if he took the time to gain some knowledge about his path and abilities.

He smiled and glanced back at Seamus just as Darien asked, "Did you gain anything at all, though?"

Seamus smirked. "I certainly did. I've built an inspiration halfway, got an idea for a new skill, and gained clarity on the direction of my path."

Sophran smiled. "That's good. Having direction is far more important than many people realize. I'm getting more jealous by the minute."

They all laughed and settled down to watch Mirelle and Aodhán, with Varéc growling quietly in the background. However, they'd barely sat down when another gasp erupted, this time from Mirelle.

They glanced at her immediately, and their eyes widened at the glow of golden light shining from beneath her robe.

A seal.

"Oh fuck," Sophran cursed as power rippled out of her core in a burst of energy so dense runes lit up. Energy raced across her skin as she ascended to the next tier, lightning sparking and arcing around in agitation.

She opened her eyes a second later, irises gleaming with clarity and lucidity. But they all knew that lucidity wouldn't last.

They acted immediately. Sophran was a blur of lightning as he clasped Mirelle's arms behind her and bound them in chain constructs. Seamus handled her legs, both boys moving in sync as if they'd done this a dozen times before.

And they had.

The twisted effect was no joke, and in a place as crowded as the sect, with thousands of individuals living in proximity, handling a person suffering from the twisted effect was a lesson everyone was mandated to learn.

But this time, those lessons weren't enough.

Perhaps it was because of the lingering hyper-focus of the miracle rain, or perhaps it was the combination of that alongside the rage and enhancements of the twisted effect. But whatever it was made Mirelle very strong, and she—only at the 55th tier—broke through the restraints Sophran and Seamus had placed on her without strain.

Chaos boiled within her spirit, tilting that lucidity into a negative spectrum and heightening it. Rage erupted within her, lightning churned, and with a cry of utter fury, she charged at the weakest person in the room: Crixis.

She never reached him, though, before she was captured again, Sophran, Seamus, and Darien working together to hold her down. Even Varéc had stood up, wings flared and fangs bared in preparation to attack.

"Lead us to the seal room," Darien shouted as a surge of electricity from Mirelle threatened to rip the training room apart.

Crixis didn't hesitate. He raced out of the training room immediately, with the specials hot on his heels. It only took them a few seconds to reach the cultivator chamber where the seal room was located, and as he pushed the door open, the boys burst in, a bubble of willpower keeping Mirelle's attack contained.

It was flashier than anything Crixis had ever witnessed.

So much wasted willpower, but with the near-infinite pool of double inheritors and transmigrants, it was negligible. With grunts and explosions of willpower, the three boys tried their hardest to push Mirelle into the seal room as carefully as they could.

They managed it a few minutes later, and when the glass door shut, they all staggered back, panting.

"Well, that's one side effect of the miracle rain," Sophran muttered and shook his head. "Perhaps it's a terrible idea to use it in a war."

Darien nodded, but Seamus shook his head. "It all depends on the concentration of energy within each drop, I think. With so many disciples in a war, the concentration would be too low to give anyone a seal."

"Unless it was created by a Calamity," Crixis said.

They all glanced at him, the implication of his words settling, but before any of them could respond, a massive explosion of energy and willpower rocked the entire house to its core.

Their gazes widened, and without hesitation, they raced back to the training room, jaws slacking as they found Aodhán floating in the air, dense amounts of iridescent energy rippling out of his core in waves. The energy pressed down on them like a mountain—so heavy it forced Crixis to his knees and caused the others, save for Sophran, to grit their teeth in strain.

But while the others felt the rippling energy like a physical, suffocating weight, Sophran felt it as authority and ruin. Aodhán hadn't just stepped into a new realm of power—he was becoming that realm of power, like a prince at a coronation.

He was becoming a force to be reckoned with, not just wielding one.

The rippling aura called to him. However, the moment he stepped into the room, a guttural growl echoed in warning. It was Varéc, and he turned to see the familiar standing at its full height, fangs bared and wings flared in preparation. Lightning arced across its form so dense it was like a cloak, and its eyes, once golden slits, had turned crimson.

The warning was clear. He couldn't step in.

Defeating the familiar would be a breeze, but that was neither a battle he wanted to have nor one he should be having with Aodhán at such a critical point in his advancement.

And so he settled to watch, eyes narrowing in fascination as he watched Aodhán become something else. There weren't any major changes, but everything was changing in that subtle way that suggested the embodiment of a path.

The density of his aura heightened with each passing second, and when the second explosion of energy hit, Sophran was prepared for it. What he wasn't prepared for, however, was what happened next.

An iridescent seal burst to life on his chest, larger than the other three and far brighter.

Seamus cursed. "Okay, now we're fucked. We need to get him out of here before he turns feral."

"Varéc is in the way," Sophran gritted out.

He'd barely finished speaking when another iridescent seal appeared, matching the former in size and intensity. Power exploded from him in a crushing wave, and they all stumbled back in shock.

"Two seals," Darien gasped. "This is a disaster. We have to get him out of here."

Shoving the others aside, he rushed into the training room, energy bursting out of his core in concentric circles. Sophran did the same, energy billowing around him as he rushed to save Aodhán and prevent a disaster, while Seamus remained with Crixis.

That was their first mistake.

Wings flaring in agitation, Varéc attacked, a blistering beam of electricity sweeping through the room to blow them back. But Darien barely spared the attack a glance as he rushed toward Aodhán, leaving Sophran to handle Varéc.

Another explosion rocked the house an instant later as Sophran and Varéc clashed, but Darien didn't even look back, multiple constructs manifesting around him to chain Aodhán down like a criminal.

What mattered was getting him to the seal chambers. Not how he got there.

He reached Aodhán an instant later, a blur of motion as he grabbed his legs and pulled them down, lightning chains wrapping around them to lock them in place. He moved to bind Aodhán's arms next. However, the moment he grasped both arms, Aodhán opened his eyes—energy and power burning within them like suns.

Darien froze, chains still held in hand.

In less than a second, Aodhán's gaze flicked to the chains, to the fight between Sophran and Varéc, to Crixis shuddering on his knees beside Seamus, and the trust he'd only begun to develop in the sect melted away like smoke in the wind.

***

Betrayal and rage overtook his expression in an instant, and before Darien could tighten the bind, he snarled, "DISSOLVE."

The binds wrapped around his arms and legs dissolved immediately, and Aodhán scowled. "I've been waiting for the other shoe to drop. I'd hoped to be wrong, but I'd been waiting. I knew all the friendliness was too good to be true. But I never thought it would be you, Darien."

Darien stumbled back immediately, a primal fear bubbling suddenly within him. He opened his mouth to speak, but before he could, Aodhán's gaze hardened, lightning flashed around him, and his lips pulled into a snarl so hateful it was cutting. "I knew I shouldn't have trusted you all, but I guess I'm still so foolish."

Darien's eyes widened, and he barely managed to put up a shield before a beam of energy so dense and powerful it should have been impossible to control shot toward him in an instant.

His shield shattered—an impossibility considering the difference in their tier—but Darien didn't get the chance to be shocked before the beam slammed into his chest. A massive wave of willpower saved him from losing his life in that moment, and he quickly reacted with an attack of his own. {Lightning Void}.

The skill functioned by grabbing hold of every iota of energy within a room and sucking it off into a void. It was one of his strongest skills. However, the moment he grabbed hold of Aodhán's attack, he felt an aversion so complete it scalded.

Lightning resisted him. Lightning resisted—

Shocked, he glanced up at Aodhán, who glared down at him as if he were a god of judgment.

"You have no Authority here," he pronounced, just before all that energy slammed down on Darien once more.

The last thing Darien realized before lightning struck was that Aodhán wasn't under the effect of the twisted effect at all.

He had gained two seals and was perfectly lucid.
