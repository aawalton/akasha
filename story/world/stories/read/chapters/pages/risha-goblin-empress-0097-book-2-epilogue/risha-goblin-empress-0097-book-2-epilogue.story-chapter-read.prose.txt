Lolo watched the small flame as it burned in its bowl. The lock of Talo’s hair burned slowly. They had faced so much together, and now, only one of them stood to see their war won.

A hand found his, holding it, and he looked over to the Under Goblin who held it. Mara met his eyes with a small smile, and his grip tightened on hers as he turned back to the fire.

“He died with a smile.” Lolo’s voice was soft, tired from crying. “He saw the fire of our Mother in his final moments.”

The other Goblin didn’t say anything as she hugged him. Lolo let out a breath, then turned to her to hug her back.

Soon, the flame burned out, leaving the tent in darkness.

Outside, the world still moved. Orders were shouted, and Builders heaved to fulfill the Mother’s desires.

The last of the sunlight was fading, allowing Lolo to leave the small tent where he and his lover mourned the fallen. Lolo pushed the flap aside, letting Mara through to pursue her own tasks.

The sky shone with dark blue fading light, an amazing sight to Lolo, even after so long. And below that sky, stretched an endless expanse of more blue, waves rocking the shore. The air smelled heavy with salt, but Lolo’s nose was slowly adjusting.

Glo, the battlescarred goblin, stood on the edge of a nearby cliff, looking over the monsters as they gathered wood from the forest. An orc Deathbringer stood next to him, one of the few familiar with constructing the river-faring boats.

The ocean was a whole new beast, and even the bravest of souls felt fear staring out at its vastness.

Lolo walked to the general, and stood next to him, his head bowed and his hand on his sword.

Glo’s sharp eyes quickly found the Under Goblin. “Lolo, good. Scouts spotted another tribe near sunrise beach. Can you leave with the Elder to meet them?”

Lolo bowed lower, then turned around. Slowly, the warriors under him gathered, falling into step behind him. They were mostly Under Goblins, but a few High Goblins and a pair of orc sisters had found their way into his small warband.

They were all quiet as they followed him to the edge of their makeshift camp. There, Elder Roa and her Snow Spider companion joined them.

The older Elder greeted Lolo with a nod of her head, and he bowed low to her. “I am Under Goblin Lolo. General Glo has asked me to accompany you.”

The Elder smiled, the heightened fangs of the Spider-Tamers showing themselves, “I’ve heard good things, Lolo. They know we’re coming, so do not underestimate them. Even the smallest of goblins has strength.”

“Lolo knows, Elder.”

The Elder swung herself onto the back of her spider, and led the way down the beach. Lolo and his warriors spread out to escort her. Two scouts ran ahead, marking the path.

Soon enough, the beach gave away to rocky terrain and high cliffs.

Scouts returned, letting Lolo know that they approached the tribe’s territories. Their people watched Lolo’s warriors as they made their way through the rocks.

Finally, they turned a corner, where a single goblin stood on top of his spider. The goblin’s skin was blue, and he had strange lines on his neck. He was unevolved as far as Lolo could tell, with the small stature of a normal goblin. The spider under him was even stranger. Brown, with a black velvety underbelly, the spider had eight eyes, all of which focused on Roa and her spider.

“Elder Awo say hello.”

“Hello, Elder Awo.” Elder Roa dismounted from her spider, then walked towards the two. “I am Elder Roa. I speak for the Goblin Mother.”
