Chapter 75 – We Move as One

Ash clung to the air, thick and suffocating.

Ezra stumbled through it, half-blind, the scorched world around him a shifting blur of black and red. His hands were blistered, his ribs ached with every jagged breath, and his skin peeled back to reveal his raw flesh where the fire had burnt him. Still, he staggered forward, drawn by a single, terrible certainty.

Something was wrong.

"Marcel are you there?” Ezra's voice echoed through their shared mind in an attempt to wake Marcel.

No answer.

Only the faint hiss of water evaporating from the ground and the hollow crunch of his own steps sounded.

He reached the point at which he had left Marcel only a few hours prior, a blackened surface, the singed roots of the late oak sitting cosy in small dimples in the ground.

The only other things present...

Were bones.

Ghost-white. Bleached from the heat, half-buried in the ash of both Marcel and the brush which had covered him. They were all that remained.

Ezra collapsed to his knees, the world tilting violently around him. His stomach twisted, and he retched.

"No, no, no, no—"

Fingers shaking, he reached out, stopping just short of touching the bones. They didn’t look real. They looked staged, plastic, like props in some nightmare play he couldn’t wake from.

But they were real. And worse, they were his fault.

Inside his mind, the pressure built, like a kettle screaming.

And then it exploded.

"EZRA!"

The voice wasn’t his own.

It tore through his mind like a blade, wild and panicked, just like his had been only moments before.

"Marcel?" Ezra gasped.

"I'm here, shit how’d this happen!?" Marcel’s words stumbled over themselves, like someone scrambling up a cliff face, losing ground with every grab. "Where am I!? This is where we put my body, right?"

Ezra reeled back, hands clutching at his head as Marcel’s thoughts battered his own.

"Yes. I swear it was just here." Ezra choked out, horror dawning. "These bones are...” He swallowed, prepping himself to answer, “They’re yours."

"WHAT?!"

Marcel’s panic slammed into him, raw and unfiltered. It made Ezra’s head pound, made his heartbeat stutter.

"How am I gonna get out? I can’t just hop into someone else's body, it has to be mine!" Marcel was practically screaming, a dissonant echo ricocheting inside Ezra’s skull. "I can’t. I can’t be trapped like this!"

Ezra staggered to his feet, clutching at his temples as if he could pry Marcel out by sheer force. But it didn’t work. Marcel wasn’t some parasite he could tear loose, he was fused to him now, wrapped around his mind like an extra skin he couldn’t shed.

"I—I don't know!” Ezra shouted back.

He stumbled toward the bones, nearly falling to his knees again. The sight of them, the bones, made bile rise in his throat. There was no salvaging that. No healing magic strong enough to stitch him back together.

Marcel’s body was gone.

There was nowhere else for him to go.

"This wasn’t supposed to happen," Marcel said, his voice shaking between fury and terror. "The Meld. Ezra, the Meld’s not supposed toleave me here! I’m meant to have a way out... Ezra let me out!"

"Your body was your way out..." Ezra said hoarsely.

"Being dead would be better." Marcel roared inside him. "Not this! Not... being caged, rotting away in someone else's skin!"

Ezra flinched. The raw horror pouring off Marcel was unbearable. It mixed with his own panic until he could hardly tell which fear was whose.

And underneath it all, something colder gnawed at him.

He didn’t know what this would do to them. They were the pioneers of melding. No one else had ever had such success. No one had written about or done experiments on the outcomes of scenarios such as this.

For all they knew Marcel might disappear in the next hour.

How long could they survive like this? Would Marcel stay Marcel? Would Ezra stay Ezra? Might they evolve into something else entirely?

He didn’t know.

Neither of them did.

"I can’t—" Marcel’s voice wavered, suddenly smaller. "I can’t even move. I’m never gonna be able to control a body myself again. I’ll just be sitting in the corner, living your life.”

Ezra swallowed hard.

He could feel Marcel clinging to him, like a second heartbeat threading through his own. The ghost of his soul. A dying star, orbiting too close.

He closed his eyes, forcing himself to breathe, even though the air burned his lungs.

"You're not dead," he said, his voice rough but steady. "You're not gone. If your mind is still here, I promise I’ll find a way to help you out. For now you’re just..."

"Broken," Marcel finished, bitterly. "I’m broken, Ezra. A shell of my former self"

Ezra shook his head. Even if it was true, he refused to say it out loud. If he admitted it, it would be real.

And he couldn't lose Marcel. Not again. Not like this.

"We’ll figure it out," he said instead.

He didn’t know how. He didn’t even know where to start.

But the alternative, leaving Marcel to go crazy alone inside his own mind, was unthinkable.

Carefully, reverently, Ezra gathered the bones into the shredded remains of his cloak. His hands moved automatically, gently, as if Marcel could still feel the touch. Part of him wondered if maybe he could.

He stood up again, staggering under the weight of exhaustion and grief. The bones cradled in his arms. Marcel’s presence a frantic, shivering thing pressed against his soul.

"Where are we going?" Marcel asked, hollow.

"Back to the academy." Ezra said, setting his jaw. "Maybe we'll be able to find some leads there. If not, we’ll say our goodbyes. There’s gotta be an answer somewhere out there."

He didn’t know where that was.

Maybe nowhere.

But he would find it.

Because failure meant something worse than death. Even if Ezra might not feel it, Marcel was hurting. A prisoner inside his best friend’s broken down body.

Ash swirled around them like mourning veils as Ezra set off, each step carving a promise into the ruined ground.

You’re not alone.

You’re not gone.

I’ll save you.

No matter the cost.

And far above them, the dying sun flickered red behind the smoke, as if the heavens themselves were shying away, not bearing to look at this creation.

The world bloomed into colour and motion.

High above the sea, the view soared, weightless and fast, cutting through the clouds like a hawk on the hunt. Far below, waves churned against jagged rocks, pale foam leaving veins of white across the sapphire waters. Then, the view tilted, turning inland as the clouds parted, revealing an island.

A crescent of land, green and gold in the morning light.

Mountains coiled down its spine, half-hidden beneath the mists. Valleys bloomed with untouched forest, thick and wild, while rivers shimmered like veins of glass. Nestled at the island’s heart was a city, vast, overgrown, and strange.

It rose from the jungle like something half-remembered from a myth. Golden roofs jutted through massive trees. Stone spires leaned under the weight of ivy. Bridges arched across deep gorges carved by time and water. Nothing looked new, but nothing looked broken.

The vision descended, sweeping down through the trees into the dense shadow of a canyon. The walls narrowed into a rocky gorge, and beyond that, a yawning cave swallowed the light.

It slipped into darkness.

A massive chamber revealed itself in the gloom. Faint moss glowed green along the walls. Pools of water lay like black mirrors across the cavern floor. By the side, half-lit by unseen light, stood one of six pillars

Old and well worn. Weathered away by the water dripping down its side.

Carved into it was a symbol, the ouroboros, a serpent devouring its own tail. The carving shimmered faintly in the vision. Highlighting its presence abnormally.

To the left of the symbol, roughly etched and newer by centuries, was a rune. Clean but frantic, like many others on the other pillars. A bent Y with its base cut short, a piece on one side, the same on all the other pillars.

Then, without warning...

Darkness.

Ezra’s eyes snapped open.

He sat upright with a sharp breath, his hands fisting in the fabric of his coat. Dust filtered through the cracks in the ceiling above, catching on the morning sun in gold shafts. His heart thundered in his ribs like a trapped thing, every breath shaking.

“Ezra?”

Marcel’s voice slid through his skull like an echo underwater.

Ezra rubbed his forehead, eyes still wide. “I saw it.”

“Saw what, another vision?”

He nodded slowly. “It was... different this time. It was taking me somewhere. From high above. I saw the island. The sea. The jungle. A city I’ve never seen before. It looked like it was doing pretty well for itself, not unlike our own capitol here.”

“Do you know where it was?"

Ezra swallowed hard, still staring ahead. “I thought I didn’t. When the vision ended ‘The War-torn Nation’ popped into my head. It’s gotta be that.”

The silence from Marcel was immediate and tense.

“You’re sure?”

“I can’t see what else it might be. I saw it from above, and nothing too distinctive.”

Marcel’s voice dropped low. “But no one knows where it is. It's been at least a few centuries since the army withdrew and they never released any info on it.”

Ezra stood and crossed to the broken window, staring out across the towers of the old academy ruins.

“I was chatting with one of the knight commanders once. It wasn’t that long ago. He told me about it. Said some sort of curse had been put on it. That the people sealed themselves away cause they were losing the war”

“Sealed?”

“With a Time Lock. A barrier that disintegrates anyone who tries to pass through. Even flying over it is instant death. They say that’s why the armies retreated. They couldn’t break it. Couldn’t enter it again. The only drawback for them is that time moves much slower in there.”

“So what, you want to go there now?”

Ezra didn’t answer immediately. The wind stirred his hair as he watched a bird glide across the ruined courtyard below.

“I think I can make it through,” he said finally. “The vision... it can't have been a coincidence. It must’ve been guiding me. The spirits wouldn’t have shown me that place if I wasn’t meant to reach it.”

“Ezra, I get that your affinity with Time is stronger than most, but a Time Lock? That’s beyond a threat. That’s suicide. Not even you could break through it. If you could, others could have as well. And by now we might have taken over that little island.”

“I’ve seen glimpses before,” Ezra said softly. “The spirits show me things, to guide me. They were the reason I got into this school in the first place. I cheated in the entrance exam. They showed me where all the objectives were, I just had to follow it blindly.”

Marcel was quiet.

But Ezra could feel the unease in their shared mind. A tight coil of worry. A silent scream muffled beneath thought.

“And you think that ruin... what you saw, might help us?”

Ezra nodded. “The stone pillar. There was an ouroboros carved into it. It has gotta mean something. I trust them.”

“I dunno. I don’t think I do. It all sounds sketchy. What if a mind mage was just messing with you or something.”

“It is a bit sketchy,” Ezra admitted. “But it’s the only lead we have. If that island has any kind of idea on how we might help you, then I have to go.”

Another pause.

Then Marcel sighed in his head. “Alright, I cave. Let’s do it.”
