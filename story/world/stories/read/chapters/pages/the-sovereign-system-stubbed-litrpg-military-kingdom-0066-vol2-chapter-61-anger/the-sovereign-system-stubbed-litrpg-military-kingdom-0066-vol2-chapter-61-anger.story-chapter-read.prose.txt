Luke burst out of the room, desperate to escape the unstable man consumed by rage. He collided with a sturdy figure almost instantly, the impact nearly knocking him off his feet.

“Come quickly!” a strong hand gripped his own, dragging him toward the stairs.

It wasn’t until he caught sight of the high ponytail and blonde hair that Luke realized it was Hilda. There was no time to ask how she’d known where to find him—nor did he care. All that mattered was getting away.

As they raced down the stairs, the upbeat rhythm of the ongoing performance echoed around them. The crowd below remained oblivious to the chaos above, drinking and cheering at the stage without a care in the world.

Luke’s eyes darted frantically across the room.

Where is he!?

But the crowd was too thick, the movement too chaotic. Worse still, the iron grip on his arm refused to yield. He yanked with all his might, but Hilda didn’t budge an inch.

“Wait, I have a friend!” he called out.

His voice vanished into the swell of music.

Luke cursed inwardly, weighing his options—until Hilda suddenly stopped, turned, and did something completely unexpected.

Without a word, she bent low and hoisted him over her shoulder like a sack of potatoes, carrying him in a fireman’s lift. The ease with which she lifted him made him feel helpless—like a misbehaving child being collected by a parent.

Before he could fully process what had happened, Hilda broke into a sprint.

“W-what the fuck!?”

Even as he shouted, Luke spotted something—or someone—flying backwards down the stairs. The man slammed into the wooden railing of the stage, which shattered on impact, sending splinters and dust into the air.

The figure lay crumpled on the ground, unmoving. Blood spilled from his mouth in a sharp cough—and then, silence.

“MOTHERFUCKER!”

The roar tore through the hall like a cannon blast, freezing everyone in place.

But only for a moment.

Panic erupted. Screams filled the air as the crowd surged toward the exits in a frenzy of bodies and overturned tables.

From his awkward vantage point, Luke saw Ming Hu descending the stairs. His once-pristine white robes were now soaked in crimson. He looked less like a man and more like a maddened beast let loose in a pen of sheep.

His eyes locked onto the nearest person. Then he charged.

This guy’s a fucking psychopath!

A sudden realization struck Luke like a slap.

Prince Lyon is still in here.

He twisted atop Hilda’s shoulder, scanning the sea of people in a panic. If Lyon got caught in the chaos—if Ming reached him first—it wouldn’t just be a tragedy.

It’d be a cruel, cosmic joke.

But with bodies rushing in every direction, cries of terror ringing in his ears, and dust clouding the air, spotting anyone was nearly impossible.

Moments later, they burst through the doors, the chill of the night air flooding Luke’s lungs. The crowd fleeing the Jade Silk Pavilion was already a blur in the distance, barely visible under the faint glow of the few lanterns lining the street.

“Hilda, put me down! My friend is missing!”

Thankfully, she came to a stop—but didn’t lower him.

“You can’t be serious, Luke. Why didn’t you tell me earlier?” she barked.

Luke bit back the curse lingering on his tongue and raised a hand. “Just stay still for a second.”

He closed his eyes, letting his consciousness ascend.

Huh?

What just happened?

A bird’s-eye view of the street filled his vision, but something was different. As usual, he could see the layout, the people... yet now, several glowing dots stood out against the scenery.

A green dot and a blue dot overlapped in the middle of the street, while another green dot hovered farther down. Luke frowned and focused on the overlapping pair.

Upon closer inspection, he saw himself and Hilda.

What the hell? Is this... a new feature?

“Hurry up, Luke! He’s coming!”

Right on cue, a red mark appeared at the Pavilion’s entrance. A guttural roar followed, shaking him to his core.

Shit, shit, shit—where is he!?

Luke’s gaze darted to the remaining green dot. Relief washed over him as the figure came into focus.

“There! Across the street! Go, go!”

Without hesitation, Hilda charged forward like a bull through a wheat field, plowing through the scattering crowd. Screams echoed around them as people dove out of her way, lest they be trampled by the juggernaut of a woman.

Luke clung to her shoulder, white-knuckled, as they closed in.

“That one!” he shouted, pointing at Prince Lyon, who stood frozen, eyes wide, face painted with disbelief.

“Brace yourself, boy!” Hilda roared.

She dropped her stance, bent low, and in one seamless motion, scooped Lyon onto her free shoulder. Her stride never slowed.

“Oof!” the prince gasped, air forced from his lungs. His muffled cry barely rose above the chaos.

Now a human chariot carrying two grown men, Hilda tore through the streets like a runaway train. Her long legs pumped with furious rhythm, boots slapping against stone.

Luke winced at the jostling but reached across to Lyon, offering a reassuring pat.

“It’s okay, man—it’s me,” he said, his voice oddly calm amid the madness.

Lyon turned his head just enough to shoot him a bewildered look that clearly screamed: What in the actual fuck is happening?

Luke just shook his head, gesturing for him to save the questions for later. Despite their current speed, they weren’t riding a horse, and they definitely weren’t safe yet.

Not while Ming Hu was still on the hunt.

Their journey continued as Hilda barreled through a series of alleys, barely slowing as she turned corner after corner. A few unfortunate souls were caught in her wake—shoved aside or clipped by her passing form—but none seemed seriously hurt. Just some bruised pride and scraped elbows.

Only after a full five minutes did she finally slow down. Without ceremony, she dumped both men onto the dirt ground, the impact sending a jolt of pain through Luke’s spine.

Hilda doubled over, her breath ragged from the exertion. But something told Luke it wasn’t the weight that had worn her down—it was the sprint.

“Luke, what the hell was that about?” Lyon snapped, propping himself up. “What did you do to piss off Ming Hu?”

Luke blinked, torn between laughter and frustration. “This has nothing to do with me…” he said, keeping the rest of the sentence to himself.

It’s because of you…

Well— he sighed internally, —I guess I’m the one who reported him dead, so… technically, it is a little my fault.

Beside them, Hilda straightened up, her composure returning.

“Who’s your friend?” she asked, eyeing Lyon with veiled suspicion. “He looks familiar…”

Before Lyon could respond, Luke raised a hand.

“Don’t ask, Hilda. For your own safety, it’s better if you don’t know.”

She stared at him for a moment, eyes narrowed in contemplation. Then she sighed—but there was a subtle sparkle in her gaze as she turned back to Lyon. For a second, Luke thought she might rip the bronze helm clean off his head, but—thankfully—she held back.

“What have you gotten yourself into?” she muttered, her voice low with concern. “I’ve never seen Ming Hu like that. He had a lot riding on the Second Prince during the succession conflict. With his death… everything in Ralis is going to change.”

Her eyes lingered pointedly on Lyon.

“It’s only going to get worse,” Luke added, giving her a sharp look. He was fairly sure she’d already figured it out—she knew that he’d gone on campaign with the second prince after all.

“I don’t think you should go back to the Pavilion,” he warned. “Ming Hu’s unstable. You might be able to hold your own, but who’s to say he won’t try to kill you while you sleep?”

“I can’t leave, Luke,” she said softly. “Little Qin is probably terrified. She needs me.”

Her expression hardened with resolve, the kind that couldn’t be reasoned with. A fierce will blazed behind her eyes.

Luke sighed.

“Just… be careful,” he said. “Things are going to spiral in the coming days. If you need to disappear, find me. We’ll figure something out—someplace safe for both of you.”

She studied him quietly, then gave a single nod. “Let’s get you two to your new residence. It’s close.”

Turning on her heel, she continued down the alley. Her broad frame filled the narrow path, making it nearly impossible to walk side by side.

Luke glanced at Lyon and placed a hand on his shoulder. “Come on,” he said quietly, guiding him after the woman.

Lyon looked rattled. Luke could see it in his eyes—the slow, dawning realization of what their plan truly meant. The gravity of faking his own death. The chaos that would follow.

“Are you okay?” Luke asked, his voice low.

“I’m fine… I just didn’t think Ming Hu would react like that,” Lyon muttered, still clearly shaken. “Do you think Fairy Qin will be okay?” he added, hope and longing flickering behind his eyes.

Luke resisted the urge to curse. They were neck-deep in one of the most dangerous gambits of the century, and this prince was worried about her? The same girl who had been impetuous and downright rude to him from the start?

I guess the old saying really is true… beautiful women bring the ruin of empires, he thought grimly, offering Lyon a half-hearted pat on the shoulder.

“I saw the way Ming Hu looked at his daughter. There’s no way he’d hurt her,” Luke said.

The relief that washed over Lyon’s face said it all. He really cared for her.

The sight gave Luke a headache.

This is going to come back to bite us… I just know it.

“What we’re doing…” Luke began, hesitating for a moment. It was hard to say aloud. “She might end up hating you once this is all over.”

Lyon nodded slowly, his expression tight with pain. “I know. But if she’s safe… I can live with that.”

Luke felt a flicker of relief—but also a tinge of sadness. The prince’s resolve was real. But after meeting Ming Hu, after witnessing the man’s twisted fury, Luke’s conviction had only grown stronger.

That man needs to be removed.

For the sake of the kingdom—for the thousands of innocents suffering under noble indifference and criminal rule—a tyrant like Ming Hu had no place on the board.

They walked in silence through the winding alleys. Around them, the city revealed a darker side—one cloaked in filth and misery. Dirty figures huddled in shadows. Some sat quietly, others warmed their hands over small fires. The flames offered meager light but only made the stench of rot and waste more pungent.

Women, mostly. A few children. All thin. All hollow-eyed. Haunted.

Luke’s jaw tightened. These were likely widows—wives of soldiers who’d once served the kingdom faithfully. Now discarded, forgotten, and left to rot where no noble eye would see them.

And winter was coming.

Most of them wouldn’t survive.

The thought made his stomach twist—but it also hardened his heart.

This isn’t a game.These are real lives—real people who are dying while you bastards sit in palaces and throw feasts.You don’t deserve your thrones.I’ll take them from your cold, dead hands… and breathe life back into this broken kingdom.

His gaze swept the street. Every starving child felt like a dagger to the chest.

He couldn’t save them. Not yet.

And if their plan failed—if Ralis fell before they moved?

They’d all die.

I won’t let that happen, he thought, his grip tightening into a fist.

I can’t.
