Up ahead, Gwen stood in the center of the building with collapsed walls, atop a pile of large rubble. In front of her was a statue of a beautiful woman. The statue was finely chiseled and roughly four meters tall. A draped cloth hung from her back, touching the ground. Her eyes were closed, her head tilted forward, and her hands clasped in prayer.

The most important thing, however, was the condition of the statue. It was completely unblemished. No chips or cracks could be seen along its surface. It was as if it had been sculpted only minutes prior.

“Is that what I think it is?” Veronica asked, climbing and jumping over the large debris.

“Yeah. It’s Selene,” Gwen answered.

She crouched down before it. In front was a small plaque with writing on it. Although it was technically part of the statue, it bore cracks and had layers of dust across its surface. She wiped it off.

“The Goddess of Salvation, Selene. Our savior. May she find what she is searching for.”

“Searching for?” Veronica repeated. “So this Goddess was searching for something?”

Gwen stood up. “I think so. Don’t know why they’d write it otherwise.”

“It’s odd that the statue is still standing. The entire village has been wrecked, and yet it isn’t damaged at all,” Nasha said, standing off to the side.

Veronica hummed. “Actually, I found something else like that.”

She pulled out the doll from her waist pocket.

They both came over and looked at it.

Nasha took it into her hands. “A doll? It’s cute.”

“I found it under a large, collapsed wall. Not a single scratch or speck of dust. Sage also says it has some kind of strange energy around it. Similar to Dusk’s,” Veronica said.

Gwen crossed her arms. “Why is everything here connected to Dusk all of a sudden? He doesn’t seem like a bad kid. He’s… a bit odd, I guess. But I think that’s because he was raised in such a creepy settlement. I mean, seriously, who lives outside a cursed valley?”

“Regardless, that just means he’s connected to this far more deeply than we thought. He could be working with the demons, or he might have had something to do with why everyone here died,” Nasha said, handing the doll back to Veronica.

“I don’t believe it. Dusk can’t be that evil.”

“Are you saying that just because he’s a kid?” Nasha asked, raising a brow. “Because I’ve seen children do terrible things that an assassin would be proud of.”

Gwen sighed. “Well, yes, but he’s also just… I don’t know. Stupid isn’t the word for it, but…”

“Naive? Innocent?” Nasha said.

She nodded. “Something like that, yeah. I just don’t think he’s malicious. Maybe he just… came out here and got cursed, too? Maybe he prayed to the statue and was given some type of divine blessing, and that’s why it feels strange around him?”

“Maybe,” Veronica said.

She looked down at the doll. “But it’s also true that he’s hiding something from us. I mean, he’s somehow the only person who has traveled this far out? We passed hundreds of monsters on the way here. And if he received some type of blessing from this place, surely he would have told us?”

Gwen just pursed her lips and shrugged her shoulders. “Don’t know.”

“Well, regardless, it won’t matter to us much longer. We should only be a couple days away from the Sanctuary. We still haven’t found the demon that cursed the people in the settlement, so I suspect we might run into it soon.”

Gwen threw a fist into her palm, standing straight. “Demon, schlemon. With my new purify spell, I’ll handle its curses.”

Nasha chuckled.

Veronica sighed.

Gwen looked between the two of them. “What?”

Veronica waved a hand at her, turning around. “Nothing. Forget it. Let’s just search around a bit more, then head back.”

After a few minutes of searching, the three of them regrouped back at the wagon. Dusk was still there in the coach, waiting.

Well, at least he didn’t ditch us, Veronica thought.

“Dusk. Do you happen to recognize this?” Veronica said, holding out the doll she had found.

He looked at her hand and frowned. “That’s… a doll.”

Veronica raised a brow. “Yes. Yes, it is. But do you happen to recognize it at all?”

He went silent for a few moments, staring at it, before finally answering. “It’s something the Goddess Selene made for the children in the village. It was… a good luck charm for them.”

“Good luck, huh?” Gwen repeated. “Well, it definitely won’t get dirty or destroyed, that’s for sure.”

“Then does the settlement also have things like this?” Veronica asked.

He shook his head. “No. The settlement came much later than the village here.”

Dusk’s gaze remained fixed on the doll for another second before he finally looked away. “We should keep moving,” he said quietly. “The monsters from here on are much more dangerous. Especially at night.”

Veronica frowned for a moment before turning to face the others. “Did you guys find anything else?”

Nasha crossed her arms. “Mostly collapsed homes and rubble. But there was one larger building near the north we came across. Looked like some sort of gathering hall.”

“There were bodies inside,” Gwen added, her voice quieter now. “A lot of them.”

Now, Veronica really frowned. “How many?”

“I don’t know. Twenty? Maybe more.” Gwen shook her head. “But… although I say bodies, it’s probably more accurate to say that I found…”

“The remnants,” Nasha chimed in, cutting her off. “Plenty of bodies, all dead, but they had practically fused into the ground. Like they were hit with some powerful blast of energy.”

“Energy? Like a spell?” Veronica asked.

Nasha shrugged. “Hard to say. But whatever it was, it was strong. I think the entire settlement was probably hit by the same blast. Most of the rubble and debris were angled in one direction, if you looked closely.”

“Well… considering what happened later, then a blast might be plausible. Especially if it was from here,” Veronica said quietly.

She tried to be discreet since Dusk was with them—referring to the large explosion that would later occur in the future from the man who had taken the Tears of Selene.

Veronica glanced back toward the ruined village one last time. The Valley of Silence was truly beginning to feel less like a cursed region and more like the remains of something forgotten.

“Let’s go,” Veronica said at last.

A couple more days passed as their journey continued. As Dusk had previously mentioned, the monsters had become much stronger, but also stranger. The ground changed from an ashen gray to a more… strangely vibrant, earthy purple. It was an overgrowth of thick, pulsing veins that weaved through the surface. There was no doubt they had entered somewhere toxic.

There, of course, were still carrion beasts—even ones that now flew in the air. But they also had to worry about poisonous plants attacking them: ones that shot acid or released poisonous air.

The wagon they traveled in was one built for wealthier merchants. Not only did it have a function to drive autonomously on mana, but it also had a shielding artifact. Pour enough mana into it, and it was possible to create a mana shield that would repel weaker animals or monsters. Luckily, it also seemed to protect against any miasma that flowed toward them. And just in case, Veronica assisted, spreading her own thin mana shield around them.

After defeating the latest wave of flying monsters, they all took a break inside the wagon’s cabin.

“I really don’t think I’ll be of much use in any upcoming fights,” Nasha said. “I’ve only learned aura up to the second tier. I can fight the weaker ones, but I can’t even hurt the bigger carrion monsters. Veronica handled most of them.”

“I’m… in the same boat,” Gwen chimed in. “I’m not that good at magic yet, so I’ve just been relying on casting a lot of fireballs. I’ve even using the staff, but most of the monsters won’t even catch fire anymore because of how tough their skin is. That, and the ambient mana in this place is growing extremely weak. It’s almost gone.”

Veronica had her arms crossed. “According to Dusk, we shouldn’t be too far from the sanctuary. I think we’ll still be able to handle things as long as we don’t run into anything unexpected. We’ve gone too far to turn around now. I’ll focus on killing the monsters if you guys can stall or distract them.”

Nasha got up from her seat and came over, sitting beside Veronica and resting her head on her shoulder. “Hey, mind teaching me magic, ol’ Great Exalted Mage?”

Gwen slumped. “Can you guys act all lovey-dovey when I’m not around?” she said, before straightening. “But Nasha’s got a point. How about you teach her magic, too? There’s the Path of Ascension, right? Might synergize well with aura. Or maybe the Path of Veils?”

Veronica sighed, unbothered by Nasha’s actions. “I can’t teach her magic.”

“Why not?” Nasha asked, raising her head.

“Magic and aura are incompatible,” Veronica said plainly. “They are two different systems of power. A person can’t use both.”

“But… I thought I heard about people being able to use them? Isn’t there someone like that in Annesheim?” Gwen asked.

The edges of Veronica’s lips creased. “There is… but that’s more of an exception. Of the people who can use both mana and aura, there are probably fewer than fifty in all of Vitian. But even then, they’re all weak. The most famous one is probably Moze, who works at Valeborn—but he’s old, and only Tier-4 in both aura and magic. ”

“So I’m out of luck?” Nasha asked.

Veronica pursed her lips and shrugged. “Sorry.”

Nasha leaned her head back against the seat. “No. I’m sorry. I thought I was going to be more useful than this.”

Veronica grabbed onto her hand. “There will be ways for you to get stronger. Just… not here. I can try and find an aura instructor for you. I do know of a few who are in seclusion.”

Nasha looked at her and smiled, gripping her hand just as tightly. “Thanks.”

Gwen, meanwhile, groaned from the other side. “Can we add some handsome, dashing knight to our hero’s party next?”

Nasha and Veronica looked at each other.

Then they laughed.

Dead trees rose all around them, their trunks twisted and hollow. Some had split open down the middle, revealing dark purple growths that pulsed faintly inside. Vines hung from their branches and crawled across the ground, some as thin as rope, others thicker than a horse’s leg.

The wagon rolled carefully between them.

Gwen leaned slightly out from the cabin, eyes scanning the area with clear discomfort. “This place somehow got worse.”

“More alive, though,” Nasha said from beside her.

“Feels more… undead to me.”

Time had flown by, and they were now just minutes away from reaching the sanctuary. With all the vines and roots blocking their path, Veronica stayed in front on the coach, unleashing torrents of fire to clear the way. Every plant Veronica saw twitch was immediately blasted with a ball of fire or a beam of light.

The closer they came to the sanctuary, the more it felt like the Valley was waking up. Veronica kept her eyes narrowed, watching for movement while also searching the path ahead. It was already strange that the ambient mana in the area was nearly gone. Something nearby was absorbing it all.

At last, the trees ahead began to part.

Dusk pulled on the reins connected to the wagon, slowing them down. “We’re here.”

All of them looked up, completely shocked.

“This… is the sanctuary?” Veronica asked slowly.

“This was not what I was expecting,” Gwen chimed in.

Calling it a sanctuary almost felt wrong. It was not some small, hidden temple like a church, nor some random shrine dedicated to a lesser god.

No, it was a spectacle.

In front of them was a massive stone structure built into the land itself, rising upward in wide, tiered steps like a pyramid. Hundreds of stairs led toward the top. However, instead of narrowing into a pointed tip, it flattened into some kind of platform above.

Near the base, covering roughly two dozen of the first steps, were large plants and roots that had begun wrapping themselves around the structure. It was as if the forest wanted to consume it, and maybe after a few hundred more years, it would.

“At the top is where people went for prayer,” Dusk said, eyes gazing upward. “It is the most holy site.”

“We can’t take the wagon up those steps, so it looks like we’ll have to climb the rest of the way,” Veronica said.

After a massive display of fire magic, Veronica burned the roots near the base. She then carried Dusk using Ascended Body, while Nasha carried Gwen with her aura enhancements. A couple of minutes later of leaping and jumping up the large steps—the four of them finally reached the top.

“Wow, this place is really… pretty,” Gwen said, being set down by Nasha. Everyone walked forward, inspecting the platform around them.

The ground before them was made of ornate, intricately carved stone. Several pillars stood near the edges, though many were cracked or worn by age. Despite the ruin, there was still a strange beauty to it.

The platform was wide and open, large enough to hold a small courtyard. Faded carvings spread across the floor in circular patterns, forming lines that converged near the center. Some resembled rays of light. Others looked like falling tears, etched so finely that even centuries of dust and decay hadn’t fully erased them.

And at the far end of the platform stood the statue of Selene. It was similar to the one they had found in the village. A woman in prayer, draped in flowing cloth, her hands clasped gently before her.

But unlike the statue in the village, this one had been destroyed. The lower half was still intact, but everything above the chest had been broken away. Jagged stone marked where her shoulders and head should have been, and several fragments lay scattered around the base, too worn and shattered to piece together.

Gwen’s expression softened slightly. “That’s… kind of sad.”

Beside them, Dusk simply stared at the statue, almost mesmerized by it. He remained silent, as if deep in thought.

Veronica glanced at him, then looked away. “The tear is probably somewhere here on the platform. Let’s split up and—”

Before she could finish, the entire sanctuary began to shake.

The stone beneath their feet trembled. Dust slipped from the cracks between the carved tiles, and several of the pillars groaned as if the weight of centuries had finally caught up to them.

Gwen stumbled, catching herself on her staff. “What’s happening?!”

Nasha’s hand immediately went to her dagger, her eyes scanning the platform. “Below us.”

Veronica felt it too.

Something was moving beneath the sanctuary.

The center of the platform shifted. At first, it was only a thin crack forming between the ornate carvings. Then the crack widened, tearing through the stone in a jagged line. Carved tiles split apart as the ground heaved, as if some giant hand was pulling the sanctuary open from beneath.

Veronica looked toward the others.

Nasha and Gwen wobbled from the shaking, but both managed to keep their balance. However—

“Dusk!” Veronica shouted.

A violent jolt surged through the temple grounds. The stone beneath them buckled, and a sharp fracture split away from the main crack, racing across the platform in a crooked line.

Straight toward Dusk.

The boy stood frozen as the ground broke apart beneath his feet. He was going to fall. The fissure widened with every passing instant, tiles dropping into the darkness below before anyone could react in time.

Anyone—except for Veronica.
