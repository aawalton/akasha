Within two seconds of Maurice disappearing, Henry was up in the air, wind howling in his ears as he propelled himself with Telekinesis down the new trail his skill had spun up. The new instance of Find the Path was approximately 20 degrees to the left from the one that would lead them to the orb, which would bring him closer to the central territories of the Reach, right where the golden lights of the Pillars dropped down to empower and excite the wilderness of the land. Apparently, the Pillars on land were a little more dangerous than those they’d encountered in the deep. Hundreds of feet of water made it so there was plenty of space for every fish to harness the lights and have their own territory. On land, that wasn’t as simple. Sure, the monsters could dig, and there were plenty that did so. But space was limited. And the competition was fierce.

Henry really hoped Maurice hadn’t landed there. He had done well against an A-rank in its first ten levels. But if he encountered one in its twenties or thirties, he’d be in trouble and frankly?

Henry didn’t know how far he was. He could be seconds away. He could be hours or god forbid, days.

Wait…No. It can’t be days. The Reach shouldn’t be that large, he thought as he tightened the nexus controlling Telekinesis, activating Rupture Casting which immediately had him break the sound barrier.

Now, he had to keep going until he found Maurice. Though just to be safe, every few seconds he dropped off an Avatar and had it immediately hide, on the off-chance that the path drawn by Find the Path overshot his target so he could retrace his route faster.

And naturally, his speed and mana expenditure drew the attention of the local fauna.

Scaly jaws snapped up to catch him. Projectiles whistled behind him. On the second minute of his flight, a soothing call came up from the thick canopy, and Henry, surprised, felt his own grip on his Telekinesis loosen, only for him to clamp down on the influence hard before continuing forward. Henry was even tempted to pull some essence from Unfettered Echoes, but he just couldn’t operate Telekinesis fast enough to make use of the power the concept would give him.

He had to make do, and in most scenarios, he would be fine with that. If it wasn’t for a small detail.

No no no, don’t, he urged, fear worming down his guts as he saw the link toward Maurice flicker in his sight. Something was fighting off his divination, and whatever it was, it was getting better. This couldn’t be Maurice. Why would he try to stop Henry?

Did something find him already?

Henry pushed himself as fast as he could, occasionally Blinking forward to eat into the distance separating them faster while the link trembled and strained and then—

Find the Path leading him to Maurice failed. Henry didn’t stop flying, and as the scenery below changed from forest and mountains to wetlands, he kept casting the skill, trying to find his path to Maurice once more. The first time, he failed, but he couldn’t tell exactly why. He knew it wasn’t for the lack of familiarity. The first time he’d cast the skill, the Path to Maurice shone as easily as if he’d used a divination anchor. The second time, he could tell something was interfering with his attempt. Something was trying to rebuff him. The third time, he used Unfettered Echoes to break through, right as he finished crossing a pretty wide lake and began exiting the wetlands.

The concepts flitted into the skill nexus, empowering the divination attempt but this time, it was as if a part of himself was within the skill, orchestrating the attempt from among the energies. He felt it search for the thread that connected him to Maurice and for a fraction of a second, Henry sensed something unfamiliar in that attempt. As if he’d suddenly seen into the mechanics of the skill, but the gears and their function made no sense to him.

Then he sensed the attempt at stopping the attempt, like a veil that tried to hide his connection to his friend, and the skill completely obliterated it.

The golden thread reappeared, and it pointed behind him then down.

The lake.

Without hesitation, Henry teleported back to the clone he’d left above the body of water, and let gravity carry him down as he took a fraction of a second to examine the location. Now that he had a second to see the area instead of flying over it faster than the speed of sound, he could tell the lake was a lot larger than what it had appeared to be moments earlier. And it was showing some signs of an old civilization. Near the eastern shores, a few shapes broke from the mostly flat land, and while they were mostly moss-covered, he could tell those were the remains of stone buildings. He saw a few pillars—also moss covered, which might have been a bridge once leading in the water, but it was also broken down, and only a few bits of it remained. And that’s when Henry hit the water, though he left up a few avatars to investigate the shore. Just so he wouldn’t miss anything.

And that’s when his enhanced divination to find Maurice failed again. But at least, he now had a place to look into. He just had to dismiss some of the Avatars he’d used to connect to Thalis and deploy them here.

That was cheap enough of a price to pay.

A couple minutes ago…

Maurice couldn’t really tell how different this new attempts at cracking the Find The Path-Blink fusion was, only that it was taking a lot of mana. And even then, it might have been his own frustration that shoved more mana into the skill nexus rather than it requesting. Suffice it to say, it didn’t work. Not like he wanted. He was still shoved through the odd tunnel thingy, and this time, thankfully, he didn’t appear half-buried into the soil. Instead, he appeared near a shore to a lake, mid-air, then dropped down and hit the murky bogs before he could catch himself with Telekinesis.

Water and algae sprayed about, and Maurice just… lay there, thinking as he stared up at the blue sky above. Miffed. But he was starting to suspect what the issue might be. The problem was that even if it was correct, he wasn’t sure how he could fix it.

Pulling up the definition of Blink, Maurice read the text.

Blink (B): Teleport to a nearby location you can perceive.

Back when they’d first gotten the skill, it had the limitation of only teleporting to areas they could see. Now it was perceive. But what Henry and Maurice had been trying was to teleport to a location they could neither see nor perceive. Was that why all the fusion attempts were misfiring? They didn’t know where they were heading?

How the heck are we supposed to—

Maurice froze for the briefest moment, then Blinked up and away from the ground. But it was too late. Right as he sensed the mental signatures surrounding him and right as he tried to slip away, he felt the tunnel that would take him shutting immediately down as someone cut his attempt off.

The next half second was a flurry of movements. The first he tried was to launch himself up with Telekinesis, but Maurice only made it a couple of feet off the pond before something caught him by the back of the head and shoved his face in the mud. The next thing he felt was his hands pulled behind his back and gripped tight, while someone clicked something shut around his wrist and neck before he was pulled up forcefully to his feet.

At this time, Maurice could finally see his ambushers. And aside from their oddly colored skins, which were different shades of blue or green, they were all wearing bone-masks, which were all decorated and painted with different colors. As for how many of them… there were three. Red mask, blue mask, and white mask. Maurice went to Identify the first of them, but the skill nexus broke down before the skill could even trigger, and he could tell why.

The collar, he thought, having sensed the spike of magic that came out of it as he kept eyeing his captors. He hadn’t sensed them. They had somehow not registered on his Telepathic Sense, but that was on him. When he had teleported away, he should have tried to make sure he was safe first. He had just thought he hadn’t landed too far away from Henry but obviously, that wasn’t the case.

Really goofed, here, he thought to himself, but Maurice did not let himself panic. He wasn’t completely defenseless here, but he should wait for his window. Thankfully, the collar wasn’t suppressing his strength too badly. He could tear his real body out of the shapeshifted shell and discard it, getting rid of the restraints, but… he had a feeling these three could still subdue him. Though they also hadn’t killed him, so that should hopefully give Henry some time to find him.

For now though, they were staring down at him. And Maurice was staring up at them. The standoff continued for a few more seconds, then White Mask turned to the other two. “It’s a child.”

His voice was… odd. Somewhat raspy. And Red Mask’s voice was a little deeper, but still had that odd whistle to it. “Children don’t reach this level. Not alone,” he said, shaking his head. Then a moment later, he brandished a bone spear at Maurice, raising the tip to his neck. “Who do you work with? Who sent you? Who—”

“Divination,” Blue Mask suddenly interrupted, looking behind them. Her voice was definitely higher, though she still whistled a little as she talked. “We need to move.”

Immediately, Red Mask’s weapon disappeared, and both he and White Mask grabbed Maurice by the elbows and zipped toward the water. “Take him to Sel’Veth,” Blue Mask urged. “She’ll cut that off. And if his friends follow us, we’ll deal with them. Move.”

The still waters of the lake approached, then they all dove into its depths.
