A single strand of black hair floated down toward the chiseled stone floor, unnoticed.

“I tire of this pretense,” said a rasping voice that swept across the dungeon seal like a fell wind.

The symbols etched into the stone responded, stoking phantasmal flames that crept neatly between the linework. The flames licked at the clothing of the pair of intruders, not doing any real damage but sending a message regardless.

“And yet,” replied a crowned woman. “You pretend.”

The King and Queen of the Ringed City stood firm against the flames of the infamous Lich of the Manse.

Queen Sarissa struck an imposing figure, in full gilded plate-mail with a mountainous greatsword strapped to her back. A cascade of hay-yellow hair was partially braided into her crown, and the light of the afternoon sun beamed from her radiant skin.

Her husband seemed almost meek in comparison to his wife, but only to the untrained eye. King Arawn Goldwatch wore well-made brown craftsman’s leathers, and the thin, golden circlet that signified his rule was almost hidden among his grey and gold hair. He stroked his beard with a gloved hand.

“I wish that our relationship could go back to the way that it used to be. Why must you be so… unyielding?” King Arawn asked.

Baphelus wore only the flames of his soul. The skeletal form of the Saint of Magic exuded a baleful bonfire of teal that bathed the dungeon seal in his power. His eye sockets became twin cursed lighthouse pyres that bathed the royalty in an oppressive blue that threatened to consume their golden facade.

“The way that it used to be?” Baphelus laughed. “You both used to be so cute. I recall an uncertain young man who didn’t think himself worthy to inherit his family's burden. He came to us humble, and left proud.”

“And now, you would shame me for that same pride.” Arawn rebutted.

“To be proud of one’s own accomplishments is one thing. You were an innocent boy and a good student, yes… but as a [King] you fall to the same follies as your father, and your father’s father. Do you want to see the same parlour trick that I used to humble your ancestors?” Baphelus growled.

With a flash of flame, a frightening creature crumpled to the floor.

“Here. This is one of the infinite monsters that spill from the depths of my burden.”

A hulking metal beast, like a hollow suit of armor with six metal arms and a floating book for a face, picked itself up off the floor, brandishing a dizzying array of magic and weaponry. Saint Baphelus conjured a glittering wall of force, partitioning himself from the rest of the room. After hopelessly flinging itself at the lich, the monster’s torso rotated, as the pages of its book-for-a-head shuffled, forming a scowling image etched in ink.

“What is the meaning of this?!” The Queen shouted.

“Oh, it’s just a simple construct. This one is one of the lesser ones, but it has got more tools and tricks than a watchmaker. Go on, Sarissa. Let’s see those famed skills.”

The construct lunged for the King, and the Queen’s greatsword parried a spear of molten purplish metal that screamed with arcane mana. Sparks flew, rebounding into a dozen or so spells that started etching symbols that were offensive to the eye in mid-air, that began building a dreadful charge.

“Oh, none of that. I’ll handicap it for you.” Saint Baphelus said, shooing away the symbols with the wave of a hand.

“This is madness, Saint Baphelus,” the King said, firming his jaw.

“Madness? You think you know madness? No, this is a fraction of what my allies and I have dealt with every damn day, for longer than the first of your lineage even had the faintest inkling that he might be able to lord his power over others.”

The Queen slashed, and an arc of glittering gold buffeted against the construct’s body, causing the metal to heat and begin disintegrating like the wick of a candle. Even still, it pressed the attack, bearing down on her with lashes of lightning and conjuring weapons of a variety of elements. Spears of water and gouts of flame flew from its hands freely.

“Stop this.” The King spoke.

His voice had a soft tone to it, but it stilled the dust in the air. Not quite a command, but a request. The King had voiced a desire, and his [Domain] was listening.

The monster’s movements began to slow, as if it were moving through a thick syrup. The Queen’s strikes rang with renewed energy, supplemented by her husband’s authority. Even Saint Baphelus’s flames seemed to quell.

But light still leaked from the monster. Something was building. A notion of a much larger magic was taking place. Before it could go further, Saint Baphelus screeched. A guttural sound of annoyance as his magic carried him forward through his own flame. He took the monster’s book head in his hands, burning it to ashes as the metal of its body flaked away, leaking ink and smoke.

The flames began to quench as Saint Baphelus summoned flesh on his own bones. A

gossamer cloak formed from his shadow, weaving around his exposed musculature as his translucent conjured veins began to pulse with flaming black blood. A scowling, pale face knit together as the Lich summoned his lantern to his hand. The sclera of his eyes became ink-black, and blue flame and ash trailed his every footstep. Ghastly apparitions walked alongside the Lich of the Manse.

“Do you see?” The lich sneered. “The deal that I generously handed your ancestor - the same deal that you spit upon every time you disrespect me - is supposed to be so simple. You and your pet nobles all get to play politics in the city above. You wear the crown, you tax the land, you get all of the authority and the fame. In return, our forces keep the flood contained. I keep the spirits content. Our school teaches the next generation and keeps the flame of knowledge lit. Why is it so hard for you to be content with what you have?”

“I’ll repeat myself, since you weren’t listening to me. You’re pretending.” Sarissa uttered.

The sweat evaporated from her brow, and she boldly stepped forth to challenge the lich.

“You pretend that we are but mere mortals that sup from your generosity, offering nothing in return. But the truth is that this school would crumble without the support of our logistics network and the manpower that we provide. How convenient for you, that when you need us to track down an expert on ancient puzzles to solve your latest roadblock, we are useful… but when we make any request, we become ungrateful in your immortal eyes.” She said.

“Sarissa… Sarissa, Sarissa, Sarissa…” The lich cooed. “What a great disappointment you were. Tell me, where is the little girl who used to sit on this floor, braiding my daughter’s hair? Where is the plucky teen who so bravely fought her overbearing family? Where is the bold young woman who swore that she would become an [Archmage]? What is this… politician that I see in front of me?”

“She grew up,” The Queen spat. “And realized that the world doesn’t work a certain way just because she wanted it to.”

Baphelus sighed.

“Hmmm. Indeed it does not… indeed, it does not.” The lich said, after a pause.

The King took the opportunity to step forward alongside his wife.

“Why have you invited foreign royalty into my country without my consent?” He demanded.

“Oh, stop it.” Baphelus scoffed. “You don’t care about the Sūryan delegation. You think me an easy mark because my wife is preoccupied, and you gamble that I won’t risk epochs of good reputation to harm you. Say what you really want.”

The Queen spat. “You dare-”

“Yes, Sarissa, I dare.” Baphelus deadpanned. “We received your permission for the enrollment of all sixty students currently enrolled at the academy that could be considered ‘Foreign Dignitaries.’ You didn’t raise a fuss when we had the Caretaker of Gura here to visit his son, and Gura is certainly a more dangerous political threat to your goals than the Sages, is it not?”

“That’s different.” The King argued. “Gura is a technocracy, and Ammar is a friend.”

“Ammar Qareba is an incredibly powerful [Mechanist], who has earned the respect of perhaps the most ornery being that I have ever met. He is a King in all but name, and his people treat him as such.”

“Do you know what it’s like?” Queen Sarissa interjected.

“And here it is.” Saint Baphelus quipped.

“If you had lost your daughter, perhaps you’d understand.” The Queen nearly screamed.

“Do not presume to lecture me on parenting, Sarissa. You have no idea what you’re talking about. I have lost children, too. What happened to Nathan was a tragedy. Even still, he knew what he was signing up for.” Baphelus replied. A twinge of warmth had re-entered his voice, as if he had finally remembered just how young the two standing before him were.

“Enough.” King Arawn commanded. “I demand my firstborn son. I command Arnault to show himself.”

It-That-Which-Is-Gone-In-A-Wink

Coiled within a pocket dimension, which was in turn twirled in a spiral within a single indestructible hair upon the lich’s seal, a great spirit-beast playfully waited for the rewarding finish of his hastily constructed plan.

Mister Winkles, as he preferred his companion to call him, had unfurled one of his larger forms within his discrete demesne. None could detect him here, especially with both of his favorite observant people otherwise occupied with dreaded outsiders. Wink watched in wretched disdain as the lich wasted his words on the golden people. Why didn’t he just kill them? Certainly, the lich could do whatever he wanted. But, Wink had resolved not to waste too much time pontificating on the fallacies of social creatures. He much preferred to simply wait and watch rather than take too much action himself. He was fabulously lazy, after all. Which is why he jumped at the chance to potentially permanently eradicate one of his responsibilities.

Well, calling it a responsibility wouldn’t necessarily be fair. Wink liked the long-haired boy, even if he spent entirely too much time wearing one of Eudoria’s boring masks. Wink would probably have helped Arnault, even if she hadn’t asked… but it was fun when she did.

And Eudoria had practically begged for his aid, plying him with belly rubs and treats… and he had, of course, generously acceded to her demands.

But… with the advent of one of his new interests carrying a certain familiar spark, Wink formulated a fun little idea. If his hunch was right, if he put these two puzzle pieces next to each other, it might have some… beneficial results.

Was there no end to his magnanimity?

Gio

Gio was panicking.

“Wink! Wink! I shouldn’t be here!” Gio scream-whispered.

That’s the King! And the Queen! And they’re all yelling!

Gio had quickly gathered that he was in some kind of stealth-based subspace, or domain, or… cat realm… or something. The way that the field of black fur that he was gently cocooned inside seemed to fold in on itself reminded him of the inside of his furry savior Babka’s bag, when the smuggler had saved him from assassination.

Honestly, the smell is similar, too. I wonder if this is somehow the same skill?

He was grateful that it seemed that the three powerful figures outside of Wink’s domain couldn’t see him. Gio was incredibly uncertain of the appropriateness of his presence for such an uncomfortable and deeply above-his-paygrade kind of conversation.

Sorry… the cat brought me here. What cat? Oh, the cat that can’t be seen by anybody unless the cat really likes them. No, I’m not crazy… please don’t send me away.

At least Saint Baphelus would probably back him up… only, this was the most terrifying that Gio had ever seen his mentor. The fire, the dungeon creature, the flesh-regrowing… Gio knew that the lich had a legendary history and that he was clearly adjusting his presence when he was around students… but it became clearer to Gio as time went on that Saint Baphelus was a Saint of Magic for a reason.

Suddenly, a familiar gold-masked figure appeared in Wink’s domain. The Inquisitor gently patted Wink’s gargantuan head.

The mind-reader.

Gio tensed.

Simultaneously, the Gold-masked inquisitor seemed to realize that Gio was present. They turned to face Wink.

“Wink… why did you bring Giorgio here?” Asked the metallic voice of the Inquisitor.

“Mao,” Replied Wink’s bassy voice. His titanic head turned to Gio, as his tail swished.

“Relax, Gio. They can’t hear us outside Wink’s domain.” The Inquisitor said.

“You…” Gio said. “You’re the one who administers the mental tests.”

The figure sighed. “The complicated feelings that you have about the test are valid. It’s designed to be as unpleasant as possible to vet you for any possible risks for clone-based magic.”

Gio blinked.

“What?” Gio gasped.

“Did Saint Baphelus not end up telling you?”

“Well, I mean… a lot has happened between now and then. In his defense, I probably started cloning myself before he was ready to teach me any cloning magic.” Gio said, somehow feeling the need to defend his terrifying mentor.

“Way, way, way before he was ready, by my understanding of it.” The inquisitor chuckled.

“Then why test me for it? Why put me through torture for no reason?” Gio asked.

“So that he could convince you to pick a different path, probably.” They replied. “How much of that test do you actually remember?”

“Can’t you just… read my mind?” Gio wondered.

“I find that it’s considered rather rude to trifle through someone’s memories without permission,” they replied. “The point I am trying to make, though… is that I think that your reflection’s outside perspective of the test is making it seem worse than it actually was. As I said, your feelings about it are valid, as it is never a fun experience for anyone involved… least of all me, who has to feel not only your experience of the test, but also force myself to continue administering it while feeling every bit of your feelings.”

“Oh… I guess I hadn’t considered that possibility. I guess you know about Rio, huh?” Gio said.

“Unfortunately… yes. I know a great deal about you. This is a bit unprofessional, but since Wink has brought you here… I think it’s only fair that I let you in on some of my secrets.”

The figure unclasped their helmet. Waist-length gold hair spilled out, revealing the handsome features of Arnault Goldwatch, the former crowned prince of the Goldwatch Empire, and thusly, the Ringed City.

Gio blanked.

He looked to the two members of royalty, having a heated discussion with his mentor.

“You… you’re the missing prince. They don’t know that you’re here. Oh, I just realized that a member of the royal family has read all my treasonous thoughts.” Gio balked.

“Firstly, I don’t acknowledge the title of ‘prince’. I am Arnault. You can call me Arnie. Secondly, they know that I’m here… they just can’t do anything about it. They sure would like to, though. And lastly… I haven’t combed through every single one of your thoughts and memories. Mind magic doesn’t work like that. I’ve got the big pieces, like the whole ‘sentient reflection’ thing, but I try, whenever possible, to avoid delving into deeply personal stuff… for like… moral and ethical reasons. Also, because I have almost nobody to talk to about this, I would really, really, really like you to talk to someone about the fact that you briefly fused with a fragment of Chaos itself when you almost died in that dungeon. Those memories did actual damage to me when I tried to look into them. Do you know how scary that is? Please tell a teacher.”

Gio blinked.

“Oh, I almost forgot about that. It kind of gives me a headache to think about that, but yeah. The way chaos described me is that it was more or less kind of a more chaos-flavored version of me, though… right?”

“No,” Arnault replied.

“You’re going to tell someone even if I don’t, aren’t you?” Gio deadpanned.

Arnault cringed. “It’s… not my place. I have some strict guidelines - both guidelines that I have determined, as well as those that have been determined for me- about what I will and will not tell the school about students. Things like that… I would only tell the Headmaster if I felt that you posed a serious threat to the school.”

“Well, I am glad that you don’t consider me a serious threa-”

“Enough.” King Arawn commanded. “I demand my firstborn son. I command Arnault to show himself.”

Arnault dropped to his knees, clutching his head.
