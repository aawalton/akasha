"I would be doing you a great disservice, Haleford, were I to teach you to use a sword." Jacob immediately squashed Clark's hopes. But that he thought it would be a disservice meant he had something even better in mind!

"Do you know what the greatest weapon is?" The veteran of The Binding Wars asked.

"Not the sword?" Clark wanted to make sure.

"It is not the sword," Jacob confirmed.

"Is the greatest weapon friendship? Or love?" Was his second guess, "Because no other weapon is so mighty as to turn a foe into an ally, instead of simply removing them?"

Jacob gave a thin, soldier's smile. "I am not speaking poetically, Haleford. What do you suppose the mightiest weapon, the one most suited to the purpose of being a weapon, is?"

"Magic?"

"You may as well say steel. Magic is not a weapon, it is a form of technology. My [Frostblade] is a magical weapon, yet it is considered a sword first. One last guess."

"Is your [Frostblade] a sword? Can you not make it into all manner of other weapons?"

"It is a blade by name, and a sword by heart. Though you also demonstrate the means by which 'magic' cannot be said to be a weapon." Jacob nodded, "Do you have any further guesses?"

Not a sword, not love or friendship, not magic, then it must be... "A gun?"

"Closer," Veeran relented, "yet not quite. The mightiest weapon is heavy artillery. There exists nothing superior to missiles, cannons, and mortars. Now, other weapons may exist, and may be more suited to a purpose, yet there exists nothing which artillery cannot destroy. A sword may cut down several foes, a spear may put a dragon to rest, yet artillery can demolish a city, should it be mighty enough."

"Is it truly fair to consider artillery a type of weapon, compared to a sword?" Clark asked. A sword was... well, it was a sword! It was the icon of war, of fighting, of knights and battlemages and everything! Swords were the coolest, so they had to be the best. "It is..." Something. He didn't know what.

"There is no fairness in war, only victory," he responded with a hint of a smile. "Though consider, is 'sword' not likewise a large grouping of similar weapons? Artillery is far less limited by the capabilities of the one firing it, and therefore can be exceedingly more potent. The purpose of a weapon is to remove a hostile obstacle. All else is secondary."

"But I wanted to use a sword," Clark said, kind of dejectedly.

"Nobody wishes to use a sword, Haleford." Veeran calmly insisted. "There is no way in which a sword is used which another tool cannot do better."

"But people want to use swords in a battle," Clark pointed out.

"It was rarer than you may think. Particularly on the mundane side of the world, the sword was not the weapon for a battlefield. Spears, polearms, hammers, bows, those were the weapons for the battlefield," Jacob spoke softly, but with far more conviction and focus than Clark had ever seen from the man. "A sword was a side-arm, the tool to be used when an enemy got far too close. If you had cause to use a sword, it was because something went wrong."

"But lots of people used swords, and they trained with them extensively," Clark was certain Jacob was right, but he knew what was expected in this kind of conversation and happily filled the role. It was fun that way! "They fought duels with them, they carried them every single day, all of those people wanted to use a sword."

"Once again, to use a sword is a sign that something has gone wrong," Jacob replied. "To fight in a duel meant honor had been defiled, to draw a sword in daily life meant that danger had come where it should not have."

"Yet people went looking for duels. They wanted to use their swords."

"Such people nonetheless used the wrong tools for the job. If they desired to kill their opponent, a knife in the back is the more appropriate tool, or a gun to their front. If they did not, an unarmed duel would be far more appropriate.

"A sword is a tool for when things have gone wrong, when plans have failed," Jacob continued. "It is a tool meant to be carried throughout the day, ready to be drawn quickly should the need arise. It has no purpose beyond killing, yet it is the least among all tools specialized for such tasks."

"But..." Clark knew he had lost. "But you use a sword?"

"Yes," Jacob drew his [Frostblade], and Clark looked at the weapon and the ways the mana around it flowed and coiled, a mighty and potent symbol and spell, able to fell mighty dragons... perhaps even Tyrants. "I am a master of the blade. For that is my role. It would be best that I never need to act. Yet when spells falter, when ammunition becomes scarce, when chaos erupts in the midst of normality, when words fail and blades are drawn, I am there. When I act, and I never act hastily, I must be certain that I shall not fail. To prepare for failure is to prepare for life. Yet it is not a path which most should walk."

A slight nudge of Jacob's wrist drew Clark's gaze upwards again, where he met Jacob's own steady eyes. "It is why I came armed as a [Frost Knight]. To be a master of the blade is to be the one always prepared to act, to fight, to deal one last blow when everything else has failed and blood flows from broken bodies. The final line of defense, the last hope, the..."

He took a shuddering breath. "And that is why you are not to learn how to use a sword."

"I am a Healer," Clark pushed back. "To live surrounded by blood and broken hope is who I am."

"You are," Jacob confirmed. "And your role, to bring life back to the lifeless, is a far greater duty than to try and bleed the enemy one last time. It would be a disservice to you and all you would fight alongside to equip you for that duty, when your giftings lead you elsewhere. It is your duty to bring things back to the way they ought to be."

"Okay," Clark nodded. "Then what do you think I ought to learn instead?"

"Why Haleford," Jacob smiled. "I would have thought that much was obvious."

It wasn't.

"A sword is the weapon for when all else has failed. The weapon for when things are working as planned is heavy artillery. And as a Healer, you are uniquely equipped to revive a plan." Jacob stood up, and started walking towards Tower Stream and its bridges. "So artillery is what you shall learn."

Clark got up and hurried after the soldier, but a single thought bubbled up and insisted it be asked. "We have artillery?"

BOOM

Clark agreed, heavy artillery truly was the greatest weapon.

The steam cannon detonated, sending a spray of stone fragments into the mob of dragons on the other side of the wards.

The barrel, made of very thick iron and mounted upon a turret, was impossible to move for anyone other than Jacob, but Clark was learning how to move it using motion slides. There was no up-and-down movement, because everything was so close, but the point of this weapon was not to get Clark accustomed to aiming artillery, but instead to help him practice with using his spell to power the cannon.

For inside the cannon, there was a slightly curved disc of bronze, and behind that bronze there was water. Or steam, as it were.

Well, there had been steam within it. Because the cannon had just fired, the bronze disc had ruptured and all of the steam that it had been holding back had escaped, blasting the ammunition Clark had put into the barrel out of it. Were Clark not himself, it would be a most inconvenient weapon. Yet thanks to [Unblemish] and its ⟨Restore⟩ative powers, he could use it again and again!

The first step was to mend the bronze. That itself was a most excellent form of practice, for it was entirely impossible for Clark to see it from where he stood. Working blindly and internally, it might take him several minutes to properly [Unblemish] it to its initial stage. Yet once he had accomplished it, he then needed to load the cannon itself, with whatever ammunition he desired. Stones were the easiest to come by, and did not require casting iron, and was therefore Clark's preference.

Then, with mended bronze and loaded stones, Clark would ⟨Restore⟩ the chamber of 'air' within the base of the cannon. Doing so refilled it with tons of incredibly pressurized steam, which was the pinnacle of its 'charge' and therefore was that which ⟨Restore⟩ sought to create. Of course, the pressure - first created by filling the base with water and then putting the base of the cannon in a really hot fire for a long time - was more than the bronze disc could take, and then... boom.

It was a most ingenious contraption, and he was in awe at Jacob's ability to come up with it. And he was delighted to put it to use, leveraging the incredible power of steam and healing into a truly devastating weapon of war.

It was also so awesome to use!

Clark finished repairing the disc, shoved a few rocks in the front of the cannon, and started to ⟨Restore⟩ it. Power flowed from his hands, built up within the cannon, and then with a screech of metal...

BOOM

Death was unchained and delivered.

"Once you have grown properly comfortable with using your skill in this manner, we shall speak with Commander Inq and Smith," Jacob spoke up, slightly startling Clark who hadn't noticed him approaching, but it was still great to see him! "In so doing, we can attempt to develop suitable emplacements and portable tools for your use."

It was tricky to get rid of an audible blemish, instead of a visual one, but Clark had gotten a lot of practice getting rid of less-obvious damage lately. In fact, the burst bronze disc would probably work a lot like an ear drum, wouldn't it? Not that Clark's ears had burst, loud noises just overwhelmed the little bits of sensory-stuff on the deep inside of the ear, but it did seem like there were some similarities, magically speaking, which Clark was able to work with!
