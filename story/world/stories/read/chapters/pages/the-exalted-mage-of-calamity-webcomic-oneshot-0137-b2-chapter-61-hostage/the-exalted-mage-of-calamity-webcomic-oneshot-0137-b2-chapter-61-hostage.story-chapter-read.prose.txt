Veronica’s spell was already complete. Now that she had reached the fourth tier, she could reliably cast one of her strongest fourth-tier spells without needing a chant. A spell circle still helped, given the complexity of the spell itself, but she could now cast it with minimal delay if needed.

She held her palm toward Phillus. “Last chance!”

“Kill me?” Phillus said, raising his neck higher. The polychromatic light around his body brightened. “Nothing you’ve shown me even comes close to the power the Last Gold displayed.”

Between his antlers, another orb of concentrated light appeared. He was preparing to fire another beam.

While the stag was distracted, Orban hurled his forging hammer toward him. More roots burst from the ground, however, stopping the blow and completely enveloping the weapon.

Orban clicked his tongue and summoned another stone fist from the earth beside him, sending it crashing toward the spring’s guardian. Again, more roots rose to stop the attack. The Great Stag wasn’t even looking at Orban, as if the spring itself were protecting him.

Veronica’s spell circle brightened, shining no less intensely than the light covering Phillus’ own body.

She took aim, angling her spell slightly to the side to make sure it wouldn’t kill the guardian. After all, it didn’t sound as though he had bad intentions. He simply wanted to keep everyone safe.

But they needed the Asvarian’s body, along with whatever knowledge he might have about the Last Gold, so they wouldn’t be charging blindly into danger.

Just as Phillus unleashed another dense beam of polychromatic light, Veronica fired her own spell: a dazzling concentrated beam of light, several meters in diameter.

The First Rays of Ra.

The two attacks collided above the center of the Lightless Spring.

For a brief instant, neither gave way. Their power was almost perfectly matched.

The surface of the spring caved inward beneath the pressure, forming a shallow depression as ripples raced across the water. A powerful gust of wind exploded outward, shaking the nearby trees, tearing leaves from their branches, and sending dust and dirt rolling across the clearing.

Orban had to brace himself against the wind, grounding his stance behind his shield.

Veronica narrowed her eyes as she fed more mana into the spell. Phillus’ attack carried far more raw power than the last, but she could tell it still wasn’t the extent of his strength. It felt too… even. Their attacks were matching almost too perfectly.

The collision only lasted a few seconds before the gathered energy became unstable.

Both attacks ruptured at once, sending a violent flash of light and force across the entire clearing. The water in the spring erupted outward in a massive wave, spraying across the banks and soaking the ground near them.

Veronica raised a barrier to protect herself from the backlash.

Across the spring, Phillus crouched slightly, bracing against the force with his own body.

Smoke and mist drifted between them for several moments as they stared at one another. Neither appeared injured.

Veronica exhaled slowly, feeling slightly hollow after the spell as her mana rings began to settle. Her breathing was heavier than usual, but her reserves were far from depleted.

Phillus, however, looked exactly the same.

“If that was the extent of your power, then you should abandon this foolishness. You are not ready to defeat the Last Gold.”

Veronica raised a brow. She straightened and lifted her chin. Insults and being looked down upon were two things she despised most.

“You believe that was all I had to offer? You were clearly matching my power, but I was barely even trying.”

Within her body, she began accelerating her mana rings. A purple spark flashed through her pupil for a brief moment.

The markings across Phillus’ body began pulsing once more. “I have never heard more arrogant words from an outsider. If you wish to die so badly, then so be it,” the Great Stag said.

Several roots burst from the ground beside him, rising as high as the treeline. Even the earth beneath them began to tremble.

Phillus lowered his head as light gathered between his antlers once more, denser than before and carrying a much darker rainbow hue.

Veronica’s lips curled into a faint smile. This wasn’t exactly a normal battle, nor had it become a fight to the death just yet, but she felt excited regardless. She hadn’t gotten the chance to test her limits at the fourth tier. She also still wanted to see how destructive demonic energy could be once infused into her regular spells.

“Come at me, then. Underestimating me will get you killed,” Veronica said, a grin spreading across her face.

Just as she began channeling mana for another spell, a shout interrupted them both.

“Stop!”

Veronica and Phillus froze.

It was Perri’s voice.

Everyone turned toward the sound. Between the trees, far removed from their fight, Nasha stepped into the open. She was holding Perri in one hand, a knife pressed firmly against the fairy’s throat.

“Let! Me! Go!” Perri shouted.

Phillus went completely still. The light between his antlers vanished, and the markings across his body dimmed.

Everyone stared at them. Veronica blinked in confusion. Gwen’s mouth hung open, while Theo looked around nervously. Orban studied the scene for a moment before straightening and letting out a sigh.

Perri’s face had gone pale. She struggled against Nasha’s grip as she tried to shake her head. “N-Nasha…”

“Don’t move,” Nasha said coldly. “Try anything, and she dies.”

Phillus’ voice dropped low enough that everyone could hear the grit within it. “Release her, outsider.”

Nasha tightened her grip around Perri. “No.”

“Perri has nothing to do with this. Let her go.”

The blade pressed a little closer to the fairy’s throat. “Then surrender. We’re all on the same side here. We don’t want the Last Gold escaping any more than you do. We’re even willing to defeat him for you. You just need to help us.”

Phillus’ eyes narrowed. “You intend to open the very prison keeping him contained.”

“And as the fairy said, you intend to leave everyone inside trapped forever. Friends and family?” Nasha’s expression didn’t change. “Doesn’t seem that noble to me.”

The Great Stag said nothing.

Nasha shifted the knife slightly. “So stop fighting us. There’s no benefit. We’ll just continue to destroy the spring if this keeps going.”

For several seconds, Phillus remained completely motionless.

Then the roots surrounding Orban began to loosen. The ones holding his forging hammer retracted into the ground, dropping the weapon.

The massive roots towering beside the spring slowly sank back into the earth, and the polychromatic markings across Phillus’ body faded until only a faint shimmer remained.

“There. Now release her.”

Nasha looked toward Veronica.

Veronica stared at her for a moment before allowing the mana building inside her to settle. The purple spark in her eyes disappeared.

She nodded. “Let her go.”

Nasha removed the knife from Perri’s throat and released her.

The fairy immediately spun around and glared at her.

Nasha simply gave her a small nod.

For a moment, Perri said nothing.

Then she huffed and flew back toward Phillus.

After the fight ended, it took Phillus several minutes to restore the damage done to the Lightless Spring. Not only could he control the roots beneath the ground, but he seemed to possess some authority over the trees, water, and earth surrounding the spring as well.

Toppled trees suddenly began to regrow. Broken branches shifted back into place, while displaced earth moved and settled as one. Even the water that had spilled from the spring flowed backward across the ground, returning to the massive pool until its surface became calm once more.

Afterward, Phillus led them farther beyond the trees to another clearing covered in vibrant greenery. A short hill rose near its center, with an old pavilion resting at the top.

Everyone settled there to talk.

Phillus remained outside, as the structure was far too small for him. Apparently, it was the place Morgan used to rest whenever she visited the Lightless Spring.

Orban sat on a nearby rock, while Veronica, Gwen, Theo, and Nasha rested inside the pavilion. Perri hovered near Nasha’s shoulder.

The atmosphere was… not exactly heavy, but it wasn’t polite, either.

Phillus was studying one fairy in particular, along with her choice of company. “Perripippa.”

The fairy looked over at him.

“Why are you standing beside that outsider after she tried to kill you?”

Honestly, it was a question everyone had been wondering, even Veronica.

Perri glanced at Nasha. Nasha glanced back. Then both of them chuckled.

Everyone tilted their heads.

Gwen was the first to catch on. “Wait… were you two faking it?”

Perri pointed a finger at her with so much force that she drifted backward for a moment. “Gotcha!” Then she flew circles around the pavilion.

Veronica looked over at Nasha. “Was that all fake? Were you two just pretending?”

Nasha shrugged with a smile. She leaned back against the pavilion seat, resting her arms across the backrest as she crossed one leg over the other. “It was actually Perri’s idea.”

“Perri’s? Not yours?”

She nodded. “I was about to fire another arrow at Phillus when Perri flew in front of me. Almost took the shot for him. She kept pestering me about how we shouldn’t be fighting. That it wasn’t why we came here in the first place. That’s when we came up with a plan to stop the fight.”

Nasha reached down toward one of the knives resting along the belt on her thigh. She drew it, then stabbed it into her other palm.

Gwen lurched forward, about to scream, but froze once she saw what happened.

Veronica stared at it. “A… false dagger?”

The blade had retracted into the handle, her palm pushing it deeper inside.

Nasha nodded. “It’s a performance dagger. For plays. Had Orban make it for me.”

Veronica, Gwen, and Theo all looked over at Orban, who was still seated on a rock outside.

He shrugged. “It was a quick request. She paid, too. Though I ain’t sure how happy I am ta be makin’ such a fake weapon.”

Veronica raised a brow and looked back at Nasha. “Why do you even have that on you?”

Nasha patted her other thigh, where another pair of daggers rested. “My old ones were chipped, so I had Orban make me a new set, along with a false dagger for my new performer job in Maftem. You two were busy for the past few days, but the singing gig I got involved a bit of acting.” She looked down at the false knife before sliding it back into her thigh belt. “Accidentally brought this one with me since it looks the same as the real ones.”

“So you never planned on hurting Perripippa?” Phillus asked from outside. His voice was still deep, but it carried a calmer tone than before.

Nasha shook her head. “No.”

“And if I had refused to surrender?”

Veronica looked over at her.

Nasha was a rogue. Just as many mages pursued professions in alchemy or magitech research, plenty of rogues became assassins or mercenaries. Veronica had little doubt Nasha might have followed through if she truly had no other choice. There was a lot a person could do once they had already crossed that line.

Veronica knew that feeling all too well.

“I believed you would,” Nasha replied. “After all, Perri told me how much you love her. Almost like she was your own daughter.”

Gwen raised a brow and looked over at the Great Stag, who now wore a complicated expression. “Ohhhh? So he’s basically the grumpy uncle who loves his niece.”

“H-hey, I don’t think we should be antagonizing the guardian of the forest,” Theo whispered.

His worries, however, were unfounded.

Phillus sighed, the light trailing across his body dimming until it disappeared entirely.

“That is true,” he said slowly. “I could never risk Perri’s life. She is the daughter of a dear friend. I would never forgive myself if something happened to her.”

Perri flew down and wrapped her arms around one of Phillus’ antlers. “I love you, too.”

Phillus closed his eyes for a moment, then regarded the group again as Perri settled atop his antlers.

“Then tell me,” he said. “What is it that all of you seek, exactly?”
