The silver key worked, but when I radioed Jeren—letting him know I could escape—he just told me to hang tight, and that he had a plan. When he stood at the entrance to the underground arena and proclaimed me a Fiend, I was surprised. It likely had to do with [Untrustworthy], but surely portraying me as something else would've been better. It made for an interesting story, at least. Prophet and Fiend working towards a common goal, maybe Jeren also had a taste for dramatics.

It was tempting to not go along with it, but Jeren was much more experienced in the Dream than I was, and he had already done the Instance once by himself. He would have a good reason when he got a second to explain.

There were nine survivors out of the ten, which was a relief. After the initial conversation, I thought a lot more of them would be lost to the wolves and other lurking monsters.

My face twisted as I considered how in the hell I was supposed to complete this Instance alone. They were all supposed to be completable in some regard, and some arbitrary metric of fair. The Being in the Static would've intervened again if they didn't consider this fair. Was the answer really to just use my one-time Boon, teleport to the surface, and start gathering people at random? Or had Jeren's presence allowed them to change things? Either way, I was going to kill this Instance Holder one day. The System had shown me it could be done when they turned one into a crater, so it was possible.

"You!" a voice screamed, and I was familiar with the sound for so many different reasons. It was the sound of grief turning into anger. I recognized the half-honeyed words, too. I turned and locked eyes with Talin, the woman who saved me from bleeding out when I cut my arm off.

I wanted to smile, to call out a greeting, because despite how we ended our conversation, I still felt warmly towards her, but I couldn't. The look in her eyes made it impossible for me to say anything. Two other Visitors had to hold her back from throwing herself into the arena at me.

"This is your fault!" she was crying now, and I didn't know what to think. "You tricked me! You tricked me into saving you. I won't do it again."

I stared off into the space the woman occupied.

"We can't let him out. He'll ruin everything." She shifted her focus to the Survivors holding her back, and the rest of the group. The words were frantic, almost pleading, if they didn't have a note of command to them.

It was easy enough for me to put two and two together. Back then, when she had healed me after I cut my arm off, I had thought that the Instance Holder wouldn't be happy with her helping me.

She yelled something else, but I couldn't hear it anymore.

This must've been her punishment.

Last time I had met her, she had only been happy, but now I could see the full range on her face, and on everyone else's. No one tried to hide it, and those who did were doing a poor job. It was mostly fear and confusion.

Talin turned to Jeren, "We have to lock him back in here. We can't let him leave. You have to believe me. He'll ruin everything!"

A flair of anger built in my chest, and I wanted to scream back at her to shut up. To stop shouting, and whining and screaming. I never asked for her help. She decided to do that. I didn't make her do shit.

I didn't make the Instance Holder drag me there.

I didn't ask her to stumble onto me.

I didn't expect her to heal me.

I didn't do a Gods damn thing.

But I would. Gods, I would if it would make her be quiet. I just needed a second of quiet.

My limbs felt heavy, and puppeted to climb the edge of the arena. She shrank back as I arrived at the landing, but the Survivors holding her in place seemed just as fearful— except their response was to freeze—and so kept her in place.

I looked at her, not feeling my face or knowing the expression I was making, and she looked back at me with a grim hatred. My throat tightened, and I could bear to look at her no longer. I turned and walked to the metal door the group had come through and stepped to the other side of it.

I turned all my thoughts to the Instance. I considered Jeren's self-proclaimed position as a Prophet and my own as a helpful Fiend. Then, considered [Untrustworthy] and its consequences.

I glanced in the direction of the Survivors with unfocused eyes and hoped my words shoved every thought of resistance out of their minds. "Lock me back up? Hmm. I suppose you might still have a chance."

I thought I saw Jeren batting away hands from other Survivors and comforting a screaming Talin.

I sat, out of sight, on the other side of the door. I covered my ears as she continued wailing. I didn't want to listen. I didn't want to know. I hated that I even knew one of the Survivors' names, and it seemed like it was never going to stop. I wanted someone to just hit her over the head with a rock so I wouldn't have to listen.

A hand brushed against my elbow. Every nerve in my body fired, and my heart rate tripled. Before I could fully gauge the situation, my fist flew out in an arc and hammered into whatever was touching me.

[Fragile] made the impact ache deeply, but even so, I followed the momentum of my punch and reached for the [Communication Stone] by my side. It was raised high in the air, but I found myself unable to bring it down as one of the hands was wrapped around my forearm.

"Dorian. It's just me." The voice came softly, but it sounded like a whisper.

I struggled against the grip, and I was let go easily. It felt like a scramble to get to my feet, and when I looked down, I saw Jeren with a sheepish, apologetic smile. "It's alright," he repeated, "it's just me."

"Don't do that again." My voice was drained, and the words came out almost like clockwork.

"Heard." A simple word, and when he got to his feet, he fixed his posture and turned to me like nothing had happened.

"So. That didn't go exactly how I had hoped," he shook his head. "Honest. Not sure if this helps or hurts. Leaning towards helping though."

"How could this possibly be helpful?" I spat out.

Jeren only sighed and ran his finger through his beard. He looked away from me, and I could tell he was debating whether to hold back, "I don't know if now is a good time."

I spoke through clenched teeth, "It doesn't matter. I still need to know."

Jeren met my eyes and searched for something. I don't know if he found it, but he spoke regardless. "It went real bad. I didn't even make it to hour three."

I grimaced. That was bad news, but expected. [Loopbound Marcher] was for scouting, as long as he got information, it'd be fine. I motioned for him to continue.

"Learned a few of the Instance Holders tricks. They're petty and cruel with [Suffer]. [Stones Throw Away] is worse somehow. Thought it wouldn't be that bad, seemed like it took around 30 minutes for something to travel through their ally. They use these Stone Worms. Their mouth comes through in a couple minutes, and creatures they ate earlier come pouring out. Acts as a gate."

He looked at me as if waiting for a reaction or for me to say anything, but I just continued to stare at him. He continued.

"I only got four of them originally, found two more, but they refused to join. If we were picked off one by one that'd be one thing, but we were just worn down and [Wayfarer] ran out of charge eventually."

I looked back into the arena and saw the group of Survivors sitting in silence, staring off into blank space with half-glazed eyes. "Stone Worms huh? We can't stay here then." There was no way I would stay here. If the worms were as big as I thought, one of them could just block the door while regurgitating monsters.

"We can for a little bit. They're slow, so it'll take a while for them to get here, and we'll know long before they do. Not subtle things. Plus. The Survivors need time to recover".

We stood in silence. I leaned my back against the harsh stone wall, trying to think of anything that would be useful to know.

"What happened to the last Survivor then, and how did you manage to get the ones who didn't come last time here? Looks like we're only missing one."

Jeren's expression twisted in front of my eyes into something far more bitter, "I didn't. There are eight Survivors and one Visitor in that group. I can't tell which is which. The other two are still out there. They'll be fine until hour three at the very least."

"Ah. Damn." I probably should've had a stronger reaction, but at this point, I didn't think the bad news would ever stop. It was just something I'd have to accept and work with.

"My turn to ask a question then. What happened in there?" his voice was gentle, as if talking to a dog, which only caused my lips to twitch into a snarl. I didn't get why he insisted on treating me like a lost child.

"The statues took turns yelling at me with a psychic attack. The duo monster found it could use [No Free Kill] to become strong. I used the last statue to kill it, then healed with the Boon. I burned almost all of my Identity and Presence."

Jeren remained silent for a long moment. He didn't let anything reach his face. "You did good. We're better than on track to beating the Instance. We also don't know if the Visitor is an ally or enemy yet, so it may be even better still." He tried to give the situation an optimistic spin, but I just felt patronized.

"The Visitor should just be one of the ones you didn't see last time. That should narrow it down at least." I offered, but Jeren quickly shook his head.

"Unfortunately not how it works. My [Title] is party specific. The other Visitor isn't in our party so they were also in my loop Instance. They never revealed themselves."

Okay. So they were an enemy. I'd need to find a way to lure them out.

I scratched my head and nodded, my eyes scanning the Survivors when I locked eyes with Talin. "You said there's two more Survivors," I asked. "Tell me where they are and I can see if I can bring them back. From what you described they seem useful. Survived on their own for a while at least."

He considered the option for a moment before nodding. "Past the ravine, in the ruins. They'll fire a warning shot first so just be aware of that. Take Krevith with you. They had his body last time. They must've known him. Positively," he hastily tacked onto the end of his sentence.

"Alright," I agreed easily enough, "tell him to come out here and we'll head out."

Jeren raised his [Communication Stone], "Keep an ear out. We'll be here for at least another thirty minutes. That amount of time should be fine before drawing in the Stone Worms."

That should've been the end of it. He should've walked away, and I should've prepared to trek across the Instance. Thinking about anything else would be a waste of time, yet I still couldn't help from asking a burning question I had.

"Last thing, the System messages. They're different colors sometimes. When I look at my status it's red; yours is blue and the System is black." When I was barely conscious, a message popped up, telling me to use the Boon. It's probably the only reason I survived, in my delirious state, but if I had been barely coherent ... what had that been?

Jeren let out a small laugh, "You can't just drive by ask me a question like that when I'm about to leave." He crossed his arms in thought. "It has to do with your world, kind of. Generally Visitors from the same world have the same color backdrop to personal messages, but even that isn't always true."

"Have the boxes … the ones not from the Dream ever spoken to you?"

He paused, and his brow furrowed. "Once. While I was in the Instance that gave me [Loopbound Marcher]."

I hesitated, but asked anyways, "What did it say?"

"It told me I had thirty more years, and that if I wanted to give up, it was okay. I haven't spoken to it since."

I slid down the wall and sat on the floor. I pressed my hands against the back of my head and pushed it into my knees. "Has the Dream itself ever talked to you?"

Jeren walked over and kneeled beside me, "Can't say it has. What has it said to you?"

Despite myself, I answered. "That it wants me to gain Presence, and it will force me into as many Instances as it needs to until I gain enough."

Jeren sat beside me, taking care to not touch me. "The Dream is confusing. I don't understand it, and honestly, I don't want to. Many have gone mad trying. Many have gone mad regardless."

"Why? Why do you do all of this then?"

Jeren gave me a small smile, "I could tell you about the economic reasons, the land reasons or food reasons that my world needs us Visitors. I could even tell you about the familiar reasons, how my father was a Visitor, and his father before him and how my grand niece is now one as well—and someone should look out for her. Even about how I've never been to an Instance where someone didn't need help. It's not one of those reasons alone though." I didn't look at him. The more I learned about his story, the more shame built within me. If I didn't beat this Instance, Jeren would be consumed, and it would be entirely my fault. "It's all of them, and they mix together to a simple desire of wanting to continue."

Then, softer. "Despite everything, this is what I want to do."

Jeren left after that to get the Survivor.

I thought about his words as the seconds ticked down. They were crazy. They were all fucking crazy. A hero's complex in the Dream? Really. How utterly stupid of him, and how utterly stupid of me for benefiting from such a thing.

I wish he hadn't joined me. Even if that meant that I failed the Instance, because then at least that would have been just between me and the Instance Holder of this place. But he had, and now I was responsible for whatever happened next.

His choice to join did not absolve me of mine to let him.

I didn't have the capacity to care about so many things and people, and still focus on beating the Instance. Caring about Jeren's outcome, I could accept. He wasn't even a Survivor, so he only tangentially factored into the win condition. I also trusted him to try his best when push came to shove. Then there was Talin. I wanted her to live. It was stupid, but I still wanted it.

My mind ran in circles, unable to reach a single consensus except for one.

No matter what, at the end of the day, I only needed four of them to live, so I could sacrifice one or two if it meant the one I knew survived.
