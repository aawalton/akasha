The wind always moved differently on the flat hills east of the estate. Not strong, not weak—just steady, carrying the scent of warm sand and the faint musk of the sheep grazing in their pens below. The sunlight wasn’t cruel yet, and the air shimmered faintly with lazy mana currents, enough to make my skin hum like a tuning fork.

Fallias sat beside me on the packed earth, her Chancellor spinning between us, flickers of orange sparks and blue trails marking each collision. Mine—Lumivis, currently in his Machina form—whirred opposite hers, steel body etched with faint light lines pulsing in rhythm with my mana. The two clashed, collided, rebounded, sparks scattering across the dirt like fragments of stars caught mid-fall.

She laughed as my Chancellor veered off course. “You keep trying to brute-force your mana flow through him. That’s why it destabilizes,” she teased, brushing a strand of hair out of her eyes. Her tone was warm, not mocking, and the sunlight caught her freckles in a way that made it impossible to look away.

“I’m aware,” I muttered, watching the top sputter and then right itself as Lumivis corrected the output. “He’s stubborn.”

From Lumivis’s Machina form, a faint shimmer of light flickered—his whisper brushing against my ear in that crystalline, almost metallic tone of his.

“She’s right, sire. You overthink when she watches.”

I didn’t respond aloud. Fallias glanced at me, curious. “What’s he saying this time?”

“Something about my form being too stiff,” I said, shaking my head slightly.

Her grin widened, mischievous. “He’s not wrong.”

We started another round. The two Chancellors collided mid-spin, generating a burst of red-orange light that shimmered briefly into a ring of fire before fizzling. I felt my mana lines syncing, adjusting to hers—that strange, comfortable resonance we always had when sparring or training. Our flows naturally found each other, circling, aligning, as though they’d known each other long before we had.

“You’re getting better,” she said softly. “Even Lumivis feels smoother.”

“That’s because your pulse rate stabilizes when she speaks,” Lumivis murmured, faint and careful. “Perhaps—”

“Not now,” I whispered, cutting him off.

Fallias arched a brow. “Talking to your Machina again?”

“Trying to keep him from saying something embarrassing,” I admitted.

She laughed, that warm, quicksilver sound that always seemed to land right under my ribs. “I like him. He’s honest.”

We played two more rounds, each of our Chancellors trading sparks in a silent rhythm, before she leaned back on her hands, watching the clouds drift lazily overhead. “You know,” she said after a moment, “it’s been weeks since we’ve done something that wasn’t about Artes or training or mana structure.”

I lay back beside her, letting the ground’s faint heat soak into my shoulders. “You call this not training?”

“This is different,” she said, turning toward me. Her hair caught the sunlight again—shifting auburn-gold depending on the mana in the air. “This feels like… you and me. Not Walker duty. Not lineage. Just us.”

The words settled in quietly. I hadn’t realized how much I’d missed that.

She turned her hand over in the grass until her fingers brushed mine. The contact was light, uncertain at first, then sure. I didn’t pull away.

“Alexander,” she said softly, “you’ve been quieter lately. Even before… the Hydra thing.”

“Hard to explain,” I said. “I feel… split. Like I’m still catching up to myself.”

Her thumb traced idle circles against my knuckles. “You don’t have to keep up with anything right now.”

I turned my head toward her. The world was bright and simple in that moment—the sunlight, the scent of dust and lavender oil, her face close enough that I could see the tiny scar under her lip from when she’d tripped over a training pole. “You always say things like that.”

“Because you never listen the first time.”

We stayed like that—two Chancellors slowly spinning down between us, the hum of mana fading to quiet. Even Lumivis’s presence in the back of my mind softened, content to dim himself out.

“This,” he whispered faintly, “is what peace feels like, sire.”

Fallias shifted closer, propping herself up on an elbow. “Do you ever think about what comes next? After all of this?” Her gaze flicked to the horizon. “After Danatallion’s Halls. After whatever Vanitas and your family have planned.”

I let the question hang. “Sometimes,” I said finally. “But it feels… distant. Like thinking about someone else’s story.”

She studied me for a moment, then smiled. “Then maybe think about this one instead.” She leaned in, and I could feel the warmth of her breath before she spoke again. “Ours.”

For a heartbeat, I didn’t breathe. The world around us blurred—the rustling of sheep in the pens, the whisper of sand shifting, the low pulse of mana under the soil. Everything else faded into stillness.

I swallowed. “You’re serious.”

“Would I joke about something like that?”

“With you, yes.”

She laughed softly, then her expression softened. “You think too much, Alex.”

“I have a lot to think about.”

“Then stop.” She moved closer still. “Just for now.”

My heart was hammering, and I could feel the faint echo of Lumivis’s voice trying to say something, but I shut the connection off. I didn’t want commentary. Not this time.

Her hand came up, brushing my cheek, and for a second I forgot how to speak. Every thought—every plan, every calculation, every voice that ever tried to tell me who I was supposed to be—went quiet.

She whispered, “You don’t have to be the Hydra or the heir or the student right now.”

“Then what am I?”

“Mine,” she said simply.

The space between us closed. The world tilted, colors running soft at the edges, the heat of the sun giving way to something warmer, quieter. My hand found hers again, and neither of us pulled away.

For a moment, all that existed was the sound of our breathing—uneven, real, alive.

We stayed like that for a while, her fingers intertwining with mine, until the sun had moved high enough to cast long shadows across the hills. Ember’s Cup forgotten, the Chancellors still, the faint hum of mana replaced by the simple pulse of our closeness. Every small movement, every shared glance, felt magnified in the quiet space we’d created together.

“You know,” she said, voice low, almost shy, “I’ve wondered what it would be like… if we weren’t limited by time, by rank, by all of it.” She brushed a hand along my arm, tracing the lines of my mana channels faintly through my skin. “What we could be… if it was just us.”

My chest tightened in that good way, that fluttering that made it hard to think clearly. I swallowed, leaning closer. “I don’t think I’ve stopped thinking about it since…” I trailed off, the memory of her smile in Danatallion’s Halls flashing behind my eyes, warm and dangerous all at once.

“Since the Hydra thing?” she asked, teasing.

“Since forever,” I admitted.

Her lips curved into a small, knowing smile, and I felt it like a spark rolling across my nerves. “Alexander,” she murmured, “do you realize how close we are right now?”

I did. I realized. I could feel her heartbeat, quick and steady, pressing against my own chest, our mana lines twining even without the tops spinning. I wanted… everything. To hold her, to keep her, to let this stretch into whatever it wanted to be without the weight of the world pressing down.

“I know,” I said softly, voice low, dangerous in its own way.

Her eyes darkened just slightly, and she tilted her head, letting her forehead rest against mine. The warmth of her breath brushed my lips, soft, teasing. “Then stop thinking,” she whispered, “and just be.”

And I did. I let go of the calculations, the endless cycles of training and status screens, the pressure of being a Hydra, the heir, the Walker, the student. I let myself exist in her presence, in the soft golden light, in the heat of the earth and sand and wind and something electric rolling between us.

She slid a hand into my hair, tugging gently. I responded by pressing closer, our noses brushing, my hand moving up to her waist, feeling the curve beneath the fabric. The world became smaller, tighter, more focused on us alone, until even Lumivis’s quiet presence faded into the background entirely.

The air around us carried the scent of the dry hills, the warm sand, the faint trace of her lavender oil—and it all became a backdrop for something entirely new, entirely ours.

Her lips hovered above mine. I could feel the heat, the anticipation, the quiet question that lingered between us, taut as a drawn bowstring. My fingers trembled just slightly as I cupped her cheek. Her eyes fluttered closed for a brief moment, and I leaned closer, letting the tension stretch just a little further, savoring the moment.

“Alex…” she breathed, her voice both warning and invitation.

I closed the tiny gap between us, pressing my lips against hers, softly at first, testing the weight of the world against us. Her hands tangled in my hair, and I could feel her pulse racing against mine. I wanted more, and I knew she wanted the same—but I also knew the limits, the delicate balance we had between desire and the world outside these hills.

Finally, she pulled back just slightly, resting her forehead against mine, breath mingling with mine. Her hand brushed along my chest, then slid down to hold my hand.

“Let’s go inside,” she whispered, voice thick with promise. “Before someone sees us out here.”

I nodded, heart hammering, mana thrumming lazily, mind entirely consumed by her. She rose gracefully, offering me her hand. I took it, letting her lead me down the path toward the estate, toward my bedroom, where the shadows of the afternoon would stretch long and golden across the floor.

And just before the door closed, she leaned close again, pressing her lips to mine in a long, lingering kiss. It wasn’t an end, and it wasn’t a beginning—it was a promise.

The door clicked shut, and the warm, dusty light of the hills faded behind us.
