Callie couldn't believe it. It wasn't just Wiggles that she could talk to. She could hear the glimmerhare, too. Did this mean that she could hear all the arcane beasts? Or just the ones that felt safe around her?

This was, without a doubt, the same glimmerhare that Callie had saved from the trap. She could see the piece of her tunic still tied to its leg. However, it was much dirtier than before.

Why you cry?

Glimmerhare cocked its—no, her—head to the side in confusion.

Callie wasn't sure how to answer, so she got up and wiped the tears out of her eyes. "I… I was just hoping to find the dragon. To request help from him."

Dwa-gon? The ancient one?

"Ancient one?" Callie repeated the unusual name. Was this how the arcane beasts referred to the dragon? "Do you know where he is?"

The glimmerhare gently shook her head.

Nobody knows. Ancient one walks where right.

"Didn't you lead me to him before?"

Ancient one asked me.

Callie blinked in surprise. The dragon had asked this glimmerhare to lead her to him.

"Why?"

Don't know.

Callie's shoulders sagged. Frankly, the idea seemed silly. A dragon certainly wouldn't have any reason to explain itself to other creatures. It was a dragon after all. But without it, Callie didn't know what to do.

She looked up at the glimmerhare and decided to focus on her instead, namely on her injury.

"Do you mind if I come closer to you?" Callie asked.

Glimmerhare's nose twitched a bit nervously.

You won't hurt me?

"No, of course not! How is your foot? Does it still hurt?" Callie asked as she slowly made her way back out of the cave.

The glimmerhare jumped a few paces back, but didn't run; instead, she kept her eyes on Callie.

A little.

"Should I remove the cloth? It must be uncomfortable," Callie said, crouching low to the ground.

The glimmerhare slowly and wordlessly hopped over to her, presenting the injured leg to Callie. She reached for the cloth slowly, not to scare the arcane beast. A little one's nose was twitching nervously, while Callie worked on untying the tight knot she herself had tied.

Underneath the dirty cloth, Callie saw that a good bit of fur was missing on the glimmerhare's leg, and there was a somewhat raw, pink mark underneath the tunic. It was deeper in the places where the teeth of the trap had dug in. Callie's heart clenched at the sight.

"It needs more time to heal…" Callie noted bitterly. Whoever had laid out that trap was undoubtedly the worst person in the world. She was filled with anger, and she could feel a bit of the tingling energy bubble within her from the rage.

The glimmerhare jumped back.

"What's wrong?" Callie asked.

Your magic. Angry. It burns.

Callie pulled her hands away from glimmerhare, clutching them close to her chest. Had she accidentally hurt it without even noticing?

"I'm sorry! I'm sorry! I didn't mean to hurt you," Callie said, looking over the glimmerhare. She thankfully didn't see any new injuries on it.

The glimmerhare shook its head.

You no hurt me. Your magic angry. It burns.

Callie tried to understand what the glimmerhare was trying to convey.

"Did I hurt you?" She asked, first wanting to make sure that the little critter hadn't been hurt. Who knew what magic could do? Perhaps it could hurt without leaving any traces on the body?

No, no. You no hurt me.

Callie sighed with relief. That was good at least.

"But what do you mean that my magic is angry? That it burns?" Callie asked.

It burns. Too hot.

Callie just felt more confused. It was true that she had burned the plant and that she felt frustrated and angry at the moment.

"How do I not make it burn?" Callie asked, but then realized that this wasn't the time for it. It pained her to drop the topic of magic; clearly, the glimmerhare knew something. But Mom was more important. "Actually, there's no time for that. I need to find a dandilynx flower."

Dan-di?

"It's a yellow flower, but it hasn't bloomed yet. I'm really sorry. I will come later to apply salve and fresh bandages on your injury. But I need to find the flower now, otherwise my Mom will lose an arm," Callie said, getting up to her feet.

She felt deep hopelessness settle within her. The chances of finding a blooming dandilynx today… Callie didn't want to think about it, but… it was just too early. Maybe in some warmer place there could be some, but it would be so far from the village that they couldn't travel there in a day.

Yellow flower… The tasty one?

"I guess it would be tasty for you? I think it's very bitter. We use it as medicine."

What is this… madeiceene?

"Medicine. It's what makes injuries hurt less. Like the matricaria leaves I rubbed on your injury," Callie explained.

The glimmerhare appeared thoughtful.

Yellow flower. Hurt less. It not grow yet.

Callie nodded. "Yes… I-I don't know what to do. I hoped the dragon could make it bloom for me, but he's not here… My mom… hic… she'll lose her arm…"

She crouched down, hiding her head behind her knees. What would crying solve? Nothing. Yet she couldn't help it. Why was it that Callie was always useless when it came to things that mattered?

Something soft touched Callie's arm, and she looked up to see the glimmerhare staring at her. It was so close now that she had the urge to pet its soft fur.

I could help… But no strength left. Need food. Need madeiceene.

"You can help? How?" Callie asked, confused.

Grow flower. But need strength.

"You could… grow it?" Callie repeated in disbelief. But then again… it made sense. Glimmerhare was an arcane beast, and surely it wasn't just because it looked a bit peculiar and could make its horns glow. Wiggles could do various strange things. Glimmerhare being able to make flowers grow wasn't that crazy to imagine.

"I… I'm going to go get you some food! And a salve with fresh bandages. Will you wait for me here?" Callie asked, rising to her feet.

I wait.

"Thank you, thank you! I'll be back real quick!" Callie said, throwing one last glance at the rabbit and rushing away towards the direction of home.

She ran as quickly as she could, occasionally stumbling and falling over a tree root or scratching her arm on a low-hanging branch. Her lungs burned, and her legs ached from exhaustion—their home was quite some distance away from the cave. Callie wasn't used to running for so long and so fast.

But she had to hurry. Mom's arm depended on it.

Once Callie got back home, she rushed inside and grabbed some of the matricaria leaf salve and bandages from the storage. They always kept some on hand for Mom's injuries. It wasn't rare for her to return home from a hunt with bruises or scratches.

She stashed them in her trustworthy pouch and then rushed out into the garden, plucking out several large carrots and a bundle of salad leaves for the glimmerhare to eat. Thinking about it, the poor critter must have been having a hard time getting anything to eat after being caught in that trap.

Moving around must have been difficult. She likely just huddled down in a safe spot to avoid predators. This only rekindled Callie's determination as she ran back towards the dragon's cave. She tried to go fast, but had to slow down to a quick walk at one point. She was just too exhausted to run.

Unfortunately, before Callie could get to the cave, she heard her father's voice. "CALLIE!"

I must have run out of time… But I can't have Dad finding me now. He wouldn't understand this.

"Callie!" Callen also called her name, but less urgently. However, Callen sounded much closer. In fact…

There he was! She could see him among the trees.

Callie decided that she once again needed to rely on her big brother. She crept her way to him, keeping low and hiding behind trees and bushes.

"Callie!" Callen called, looking around.

"Callie!" Dad called somewhere in the distance.

"I'm here," Callie whispered, having crept her way to Callen.

He was startled and looked down at her in shock. "Wh– Callie? Where were you? I told you to be back in an hour. Dad is freaking out! And why are you carrying salad and carrots?"

"Long story! But I can get the flower. I just need Dad not to find me for a little while longer," Callie said.

"What do you want me to do? He's already angry and stressing out about Mom," Callen objected.

"Just tell him I'm going to bring the flower shortly," Callie said. "Lead him in the wrong way until then."

"Argh!" Callen angrily tousled his hair. "Fine! But you'd better bring that flower, and be ready for punishment later."

"As long as Mom gets to keep her arm, I don't care about punishment."

Callen nodded. "You're right. Now go! Hurry!"

Callie nodded and continued creeping through the foliage while Callen went in the other direction, towards Dad. She would trust her brother to keep him busy. In the meantime, she hurried to the glimmerhare.

Her legs were screaming from exhaustion, not to mention the stinging from the many scratches and bruises she managed to get in her hurry. But she didn't stop for a moment, instead keeping her eyes focused ahead. Soon enough, she saw the rocky outcropping with the entrance to the dragon cave.

"Glimmerhare? Glimmerhare?" Callie called, not having a better way to call the little critter. She would have to give her a name later, too. It felt odd calling her glimmerhare.

Is that me?

The white head with antlers popped out from behind a bush. Despite being completely white, somehow the glimmerhare had managed to hide herself in the foliage without a trace. Could the magic be involved here, too? Surely, something so pure white would be easy to spot.

Focus, Callie! Now is not the time for that.

"Yes, I brought you food. Carrots and salad," Callie said and laid them in front of the glimmerhare. "While you eat, let me tend to your leg."

Glimmerhare hopped out of the bush, to the food in front of her, and began nibbling at the salad. Callie, meanwhile, focused on her leg. While it looked much better than before, it would still require a good while to fully heal. There was also the risk of infection.

She wasn't shy with the ointment, taking a generous amount and carefully applying it on the wound. Glimmerhare flinched, pulling her leg away.

It hurts!

"I'm sorry! It is the alcohol. It stings when applied, but it'll clean out your wound and heal you faster, as well as protect you from infection," Callie explained.

I don't understand. En-feicion?

"It's very bad. You could die from infection. This will help you!" Callie said, hoping the glimmerhare would understand.

I… I trust you. You're a kind human.

"Callie. My name is Callie."

Callie. Okay.

"Do you mind if I give you a name?" Callie asked and reached out to smear the salve on her leg. The glimmerhare flinched but this time kept still, instead focusing on the salad.

I do not mind.

Callie thought about it for a moment as she kept rubbing the salve. What would be a good name for a glimmerhare? She was white and soft and had antlers that glowed.

"Snowglow? How does that sound? You're white like snow, and your horns glow," Callie suggested.

Snowglow… Snowglow. Yes. I like.

At that moment, Callie felt warmth spread on her forearm. Rolling up her sleeve, she saw that the mark was glowing and something new was growing. Just like when she had named Wiggles, another petal appeared on her mark.

Warm magic. Kind.

Snowglow seemed to relax from the soft glow, eating salad with renewed vigor, devouring one leaf after the next.

Callie stared at her mark in amazement for a moment. Did naming an arcane beast somehow help the mark grow? Did it also make her magic stronger? Callie didn't know, but she was sure that this was a good sign. With a smile, she returned her attention to Snowglow's foot, lifting it gently and tying the bandage around it.

These orange roots are very tasty! I love them!

"Those are carrots. Eat up and regain your strength so you can grow that flower," Callie said.

No. You grow.

"What?" Callie asked in confusion as she tied up the bandage. "I can't grow flowers. I burned one before."

Snowglow hopped behind Callie, pointing her little nose at a yellow flower. It was a dandilynx. Callie blinked in surprise.

"What? That wasn't there before," she called, staring at the fully blooming flower. But it wasn't just the dandilynx that was blooming. There was a plethora of small flowers blooming in a small circle around Callie.

Gentle magic. Grow flower.

Callie reached out for the dandilynx flower, touching it with her right hand. The mark on her hand was still glowing, and she could feel a pleasant warmth coming from it. None of that hot tingling sensation she felt before. Instead, it felt like a gentle trickle of water was coming from her hand into the flower.

In response, its petals opened even more, and one of the sleeping buds opened, revealing another bright yellow flower. Callie exhaled as she reached down to pluck the flowers.

There was no time to lose. She had to get these back to Granny Ethel before it was too late.
