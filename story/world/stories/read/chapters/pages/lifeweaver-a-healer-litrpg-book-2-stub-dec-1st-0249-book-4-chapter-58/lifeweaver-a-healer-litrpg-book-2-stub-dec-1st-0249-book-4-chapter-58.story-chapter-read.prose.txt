While Dimitrios’s fighting style with up-close and personal attacks did a lot in terms of damage, it left him vulnerable to counter-attacks. The Mythos was quick and seemed to have the reflexes of a mongoose, but that wasn't enough against creatures able to create new tentacles out of nothing. Luke jumped into the fight and used The Healer's Moment to give him time to turn the tide. He extended a thread into one of their opponents, searching for the lines of power within while he used Weaver's Renewal on Dimitrios, relieved to find the skill working. While those bolts had eaten away large chunks of him, even the side of his skull, it wasn't the same as the void infiltrating his body and taking over.

Back to himself and with full vigor restored, Dimitrios threw himself at the monsters, slicing into their bulk. It was difficult to work out how his attacks worked, but Luke sensed how the Mythos infused each thrust and stab with will while using some sort of skill. Their difference in styles of attack made for some interesting combat, and once they put their respective gifts to use, the void creatures didn't stand much of a chance. Luke cut through their spellweaves and used his will to push back whenever the beings tried to hold on. The thread he'd used at the start found its way to thicker, artery-like pathways of power surging through the monsters. It was obvious how much stronger they were compared to their smaller counterparts, but the power was still elusive, and it took some doing to find it. Once he had, though, it was game over. Their resistance against intrusion into their own physical selves was far better than their control outside of it when crafting spellweaves, but it still couldn't stand up to Luke's threads. The fight was over in a moment.

Dimitrios was injury-free, but Luke had been the victim of a lashing tentacle. While it hadn't found anything to strike, exactly, since he wasn't there in his physical form, the attack transferred over to the physical realm in a way he couldn't quite grasp, resulting in a long gash across his chest which required healing.

"That went better than expected," Dimitrios said with a smile, before remembering their fallen comrade. "Except for the philosopher."

"Did you catch his name?" Luke asked.

"No, but I remember his face from the crowd in that office. This one should have taken Popov's guidance, but I don't think he did."

"No," Luke agreed, shuddering. Seeing a person taken over by the void like that awoke a primal fear in him. The idea of being taken over and transformed into something monstrous... Not a fate he would wish on anyone. While he didn't think anything of the Integrated remained afterwards, or at least he hoped it didn't, it was still not the way he wanted to go.

"I'm heading out," Luke said. "Can you deal with the last attack?"

Dimitrios shrugged. "Sure. I'm getting the hang of it and the experience is amazing. I'll let you know if the system needs repair."

"Thanks. Shoot me a message if it turns bad, but as far as I can tell from here, there's just the one monster, and it's small."

"You can tell?" Dimitrios asked.

"Sure."

"How?"

Luke considered the question for a moment and found he wasn't sure. "I just kind of know. Like it's threaded into the weave somehow."

"You are a man of many talents, Luke Quinn."

"You're not so bad yourself."

Perhaps it wasn't such a great idea to bail on the last attack, but Dimitrios soon messaged to let Luke know it had been a walk in the park. The reason Luke wanted to leave was simple: He needed to find a way to move through the weave without jumping from person to person. It was possible, he knew, since he had identified different sorts of threads and learned to use Weavestep to reach both random Integrated and portals, and even specific types of portals. There was a chance that portals experiencing a dungeon break would be different as well, but Luke thought that maybe, just maybe, working his way through different types of threads would turn out to be more limiting than anything. The problem wasn't jumping using a specific thread. It was finding the correct thread for the occasion. He didn't want to Weavestep to a portal of a specific type or a random Integrated. He wanted the ability to go to a specific portal or a person of his choosing. The threads were all there. It shouldn't be impossible to suss out which to reach out and grab the correct one. There were threads for everyone and everything. This meant, in theory, that the entire system should be open for him to travel without limitations.

That, at least, had been his intention when returning to his physical body, but the speck of void mana in his body started acting up when he sustained the wound from the void creature, and Luke soon found why. Another speck hid in his meta-heart. To make matters even worse, it had begun infecting him, screwing with his mana control. How he had missed it, he couldn't tell. Had to be because of his focus on the fight, but if he had continued in the weave rather than coming back, things could have gone bad. Real bad. Now, Luke dealt it a swift blow with his will, forcing it out of his meta-heart, to where it joined the first speck. Two became one, and it grew larger. While still small, he did not like having some foreign, system-dodging piece of the enemy inside him. Upon finding the power - he needed a better word for that - inside the creatures of the void, and the void creeping into that poor Integrated, he had realized what Luke had within him was not, in fact, a strange type of mana. It was the void itself. Like a disease, it had infected him the same way it had the philosopher, albeit in a minor way. Unchecked, it would go berserk and take over, turning Luke into one of those things. Unacceptable, just unacceptable. It was a problem in need of a solution, and soon, but even more important was the other discovery.

Weavestepping would be convenient and make him stronger, but recent discoveries took priority. Not the void stuff, but the power. Luke needed to learn more about that power. If he could wield it himself, that would open interesting doors. But, more than anything, he needed a way to destroy or direct it. That was how they would stand a chance against the coming disaster that was Falzyxi, and that disaster wasn't far off, he knew.

As luck would have it, Luke possessed the things needed to get started experimenting straight away.

"Smudge, get out here!" he yelled, pulling his companion out of the Stable.

The voidling hesitated, as if reading Luke's intention, but relented and appeared right next to the bench. As far as Luke could tell, he was no longer hurt in any way.

Petting what he thought was the head, Luke told his friend what he planned. In situations like these, it was best to be up front with your intentions. It wouldn't hurt, he thought, well, hoped. The need for information was desperate, and this was the only way of learning about what they might do to even the odds against what they were facing. Smudge seemed to accept the explanations, even if the little critter wasn't smart enough to grasp what Luke was telling him. It's difficult to gauge someone's intelligence when they don't have a face.

The first step was simple, and one Smudge partook in with abandon. Luke fed his companion a monster core while keeping a close eye on the power within Smudge's void. As expected, the power within the core leaked out and joined what was in there. Some of it bled away, but most didn't. Ten minutes later, it was still there. Interesting.

That first experiment told him two things. First, the source of the power within void beings was, at least to some extent, gathered and stored. They ate to grow stronger. Second, and this was just a hunch, was that the siphoning of power from these creatures, to build dungeons, for instance, was done by taking this energy from them in some manner. How? Luke sure didn't know, but he wanted to find out.

"Food for you, huh?" he asked Smudge, who wagged his tail, hoping to get more.

A new theory came to him, and Luke was sure he was right. Even so, he needed to test it to make sure. Concentrating, he reached out to the weave and touched threads at random until he found something representing what he hoped was insignificant: the relationship between a small pebble on the bottom of some sea and the surrounding water. Pulling on it, he did something that Guide would scold him for. Luke cut the system thread and pulled it into the physical realm. Pain shot down his spine, and he coughed, blood spattering on the ground.

"Here, boy," he grunted. "Come have a taste."

Smudge whined and backed away.

Gesturing for the voidling to come closer, Luke's eyes began bleeding. "Come on! Just a small taste. Hurry!"

It took some more agonizing convincing, but once Smudge nibbled a little at the thread, he got his confirmation. Watching the system thread turn into that same power as it entered Smudge's system was just what he had expected and feared.

Luke let go of the pebble thread and leaned back, gathering himself from the ordeal while Smudge now whined for a different reason. The voidling wanted more of what he had just been offered.

"No," Luke sighed. "Not that. Not ever."

Smudge seemed to understand, but he didn't like that answer. Luke considered what he had just learned while hoping he hadn't awakened some unquenchable thirst for the weave in Smudge. It was simple. The void beings wanted the weave because eating it gave them more power and dealt with pesky Integrated doing something to drain that power from them and using it to help new Integrated grow stronger from dungeons. A cycle, of sorts.

Having Smudge there to help in figuring this stuff out was such an advantage. Luke couldn't believe his luck. For a brief moment, he wondered if The Fallen Shepherd had engineered him finding Smudge's egg, knowing this would happen. But no, that level of foresight and planning was impossible. No one could handle that many unknown variables over such a long period of time. And what would Relian gain from Luke learning about the void?

While the Smudge experiments helped in learning about the system and the void, Luke had more questions about the Source, which was what he had decided to call the 'power' inside the void creatures, and more experiments were necessary to find answers. What he wanted to know most was what would happen if he took Source into his own meta-heart. It was, if he was correct, the underlying stuff of everything. Source was the system. It was the void, and it was everything and everyone in both. A resource both scarce and not. Surely that would make for a good jolt of energy to put into himself?

That, however, would have to wait a little. The risk was too high. Instead, he wanted to know what happened when you used a monster core to power something. It was the equivalent of a meta-heart, except it could be used as a battery with the right equipment. Searching through the Integrated Shop, Luke found a cheap, core-powered item. A kick-bike, like the one Curtis used to get around. Slotting a core into it, he focused his senses and traced the Source as it bled out from the core at a slow rate. A minuscule amount entered the bike, and the rest evaporated. No, not evaporated. Upon closer inspection, Luke found that it had entered the metaphysical realm and fed itself into the weave.

"Using cores feeds the system," he said, nodding to himself. "Interesting."

Removing the emptied monster core, Luke thought of another experiment to use, and it would perhaps be the most applicable and useful one considering Earth's current situation.

"Smudge! I need you again!"

The voidling raced back from where he had been gnawing on the bike, as if trying to get at whatever was within. Right, the Source inside it. That was exactly what his friend was doing. Well, at least it wasn't system threads he was trying to get at. It took Luke the better part of an hour to complete the next experiment. Most of that time was spent just trying to convince Smudge to stay still and not struggle. Reaching into his friend's void, Luke found a narrow pathway of Source, grabbed it, and pulled until it touched the depleted monster core from the bike. To his amazement, it worked. The core accepted Smudge's source, diverted and siphoned by Luke. This was it. This was how they would defeat Falzyxi!

"You seem to have taken a keen interest in the void."

Smudge, still a little disgruntled at having a small amount of Source taken from him, fled into the Stable. Luke looked up to see Guide standing before him. "Not just the void," Luke said as he straightened. "It's everything, isn't it?"
