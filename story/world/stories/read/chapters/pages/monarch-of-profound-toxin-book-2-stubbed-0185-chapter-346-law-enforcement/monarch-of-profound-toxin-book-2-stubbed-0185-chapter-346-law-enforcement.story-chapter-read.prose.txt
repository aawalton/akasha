“What are we going to do with that guy?” Heath asked.

Yes… Just what the hell were they supposed to do with him now?

It would have been a lie to claim that he did not wish to tear his broken limbs off completely, crush his bones to dust, and rip out his heart while he was still alive for what he had done to Goo and Bin.

But perhaps that was just a tiny bit too… drastic of a reaction to something like this. Make no mistake, an assault on any child should be punished severely, but even Eik didn’t feel like he could justify murder as appropriate retaliation for this.

This was not the battle field. It was not a war zone. This was Gimleh.

But, oh, by the gods, how he wanted to feel every sensation as the life was extinguished from Gurm’s system.

It was one thing to kill in circumstances of kill or be killed. That was something he had gotten used to years ago. He could and had killed countless beasts and people, such as the cultists of Moon Shall Swallow, and the deed barely touched him. He could live with that. It was easy.

But it hadn’t been easy in the beginning. It had filled him with dread and forced him to question himself and his own humanity. And then, as most things did, it got better with time.

Murder was not the only way to avoid any future issues with this man or his friends. That was just a fact. It was a B-ranker against an X-ranked Worldbreaker. The man would most likely never even dare to be on the same continent as Eik again.

If he killed him now, what kind of path would he then be taking the first step down? How would that change him?

The air seemed to scream as he clenched his fists so tightly that it vibrated the air.

Bin’s little hand on his shoulder startled him out of the stupor he had been in and his eyes snapped to her. “Dad…?”

“Yeah, sorry, sweetheart. Daddy was just thinking for a little bit.”

“About what?” The brief glance she sent at the bad man imprisoned in a block of crystal revealed that she had a pretty good idea already.

“Just, you know… things…” Eik tried.

“I don’t want you to kill him.”

Eik inhaled sharply. “Oh, no, that wasn’t—”

“I’m not an idiot, dad,” she said with a frown. She hadn’t touched her colorful sprinkles dessert.

Well, the situation wasn’t exactly perfect for snacking anyway, although Goo didn’t seem to have gotten the memo. He was already pawing at Uncle Heath’s shirt sleeve and begging him to go and get him another plate of food.

Eik pulled her back onto his lap and wrapped his arms around her little body. She sniffled into the creases of his shirt. “All right, cutie. All right, I won’t kill him. But I will plant a Living Manifestation inside his body that will punish him anytime he does something bad to someone weaker than him,” Eik hissed icily.

Bin peeked up at him and smiled. “Of course!”

Eik burst into laughter and pulled out a pen and a slip of paper from his belt pouch of holding. He leaned over the table, his tongue in the corner of his mouth as he scribbled a short message.

Hey,

I’m Eik.

I’ve caught something for you, so please come pick it up. I’m at wherever you’re led to by this guy.

Please make sure to be here before my kids finish their desserts.

Best regards

Eik (Yes, that one)

As another, winged Living Manifestation, big enough for two or three people to ride on its back, appeared at his side, Eik folded up the note and stuck it in its mouth. “Go and find some law enforcement somewhere. Give them that note, and when they get all flustered and start stammering, bring them back here. Got it?”

The Profound Toxic beast nodded dutifully and launched itself skyward with a gust of wind that rattled the tables and blew dust up everywhere. A thin screen of crystal snapped into place over their food and drinks.

“Now,” he said and stood up from his seat. He wandered over to the five bandits. “I’ll have you all stay put here while we wait for someone to show up to take you away.” Hand raised in front of him, he directed a dome of crystal to sprout around the five-person group.

“Hey! We didn’t do anything to you or your kids!” one of the other men yelled, fear and desperation mixing in his voice. “Gurm’s the one who hit your kid and cursed you out! We didn’t do shit! You can’t do this! You can’t keep us here!” With every word, his voice grew shriller and shriller.

“Relax, dude,” Eik said dismissively as the dome closed around them. “I’ll report the events exactly as they happened, and I’ll even get witness corroborations from the other guests. If you guys don’t already have outstanding issues with law enforcement, you will have nothing to worry about.”

Judging by the expressions on their faces in the moments before the crystal swallowed them up completely, they did, in fact, have something to worry about.

Unable to resist the urge, Eik left Gurm surrounded by Panacean Quintessence while about a hundred Living Manifestations crawled around his body on the inside of the crystal prison, unceasingly chewing through his ever-healing flesh.

He wouldn't kill the man, but he would make damn sure he regretted what he had done today. Although, whether he would be capable of thoughts as sophisticated as regret by the time the authorities got him out of his crystal prison was still uncertain.

“All right, great,” he said and clapped his hands together with finality. “And with that goat shaved, we can return to our seat. Sorry for the disturbance, everyone!” he shouted, arms raised high for attention. “I hope you understand. Law enforcement will arrive soon and everything will settle down, but for now, I’ve isolated the menace, so feel free to continue eating.”

Cautiously and slowly, the first few of the onlookers began to approach. Eik heard his name mentioned many times, so it wasn’t like they didn’t know who he was either. “Is it safe?” someone asked.

“Perfectly,” he said with a bright smile. “Sorry for overreacting.”

“Nah, I get it. If I was strong enough, I would do the same thing if someone did that to my kids,” a woman piped in, the first to actually come all the way up to take a closer look at the crystalline dome. “Some people let their ranks get to their heads and don’t hesitate to distort that into ruthless authority to do whatever they want. Luckily not too often, but, you know…”

“It happens,” Eik finished for her, the woman nodding. “I think I might be able to help. At least a little bit.”

“It’s already over, isn’t it?” she asked with puzzlement.

“Well, yes, this particular incident certainly is, but I’m talking about the future.”

“The future?” Her puzzlement only intensified.

Eik scratched the stubbly beard of his chin as he mulled it over for a few moments. “Actually, I have an idea. Would you mind stepping back just a little bit?”

Suddenly nervous, she hurried to put distance between them.

They were more or less in the center of Gimleh city right now, right? Then straight up should probably be just right. Taking aim, he pointed his open palm at the sky as a spinning sphere appeared.

“Fuck!” he heard Heath hiss as he tackled Eik’s kids to the ground and covered them with his body.

“Dude, it’s obviously not a Death Star,” Eik shouted with a roll of his eyes. “I know I’m reckless and act thrice before I think once, but even I’m not that stupid.”

Slowly, Heath got to his feet, sat both kids back in their chairs, wiped his sweaty forehead, and breathed a deep sigh of relief. “No, yeah… No, I knew that…”

“Uh huh,” Eik muttered and launched the sphere. A few kilometers above the rooftops, it burst and released a massive amount of liquid Profound Toxin along with countless small nuggets of crystal. These exploded loudly into glittering motes of blue, drawing the attention of everybody on the ground.

Rapidly, the floating liquid coalesced into gigantic letters, easily legible by anybody below.

X-ranker Eik Magnasen here,

All of my troops hiding in Gimleh will now come out to wave a greeting.

Just as those letters formed, multiple Living Manifestations sprung forth all around. From the shadows under a bench, the gaps between a roof and a wall, or from the crown of a tree, or a sewer grate. Although they had all seen them arrive, people had apparently happily suppressed the memory of them, because there was a cacophony of screams as they appeared.

Eik winced. Hopefully he wouldn’t be responsible for any heart attacks today.

Silently, the Living Manifestation waved or bowed in greeting to any person near them. Some people even returned the gesture with some trepidation.

The writing continued.

In addition to their original task of monitoring any monster activity in Gimleh, these guys will now also step in against any blatant misuse of personal power.

Anyone seen assaulting someone weaker than them will be apprehended and brought to the local authorities.

The summons are smart and can distinguish between a common quarrel and genuinely threatening behavior. They will not step in unless absolutely necessary.

Have a nice day.

—Eik <3

“Sorry, everybody,” he shouted across the plaza. “I’ve disturbed your nice day out. All food and drinks are on me today, all right? Go get another round and a little something extra, if you want.”

People didn’t really know how to react, but a few scattered cheers rang out as Eik returned to his own table.

“You have too much power, dude,” Michael said. “This might be a little too big brotherish, even in the name of public safety.”

Eik sighed as he chewed on his food. “Yeah, I know… I just got a little upset. I’m well aware that I might be overstepping here. I’ll talk to the appropriate authorities over the next month, and if it’s not working, I’ll reverse the order.”

“That actually sounds like a wise thought. For once. Congratulations.”

“Screw you, man,” Eik spat.

Heath eyed him. “Your big thing with the Gohkamorians is tomorrow, right?” he asked.

Eik exhaled deeply while Bin held a steady drum rhythm with her fingers on his forehead. “Yeah. I’m kind of dreading it, if I’m honest.”

“Really? I thought you would be relieved to get it over with. All you have to do after that is wait for their answer and then… whatever happens, happens,” Michael said with a shrug, an uncharacteristic cold entering the tone of his voice.

“No, I am relieved, but isn’t it too much to demand? I’m threatening to K-I-L-L all of them if they don’t do what I tell them to,” he muttered under his breath, a cupped hand around his mouth as if that would stop the kids from listening. “I hate the Gokhamorians as much as the next guy, but a lot of them are just regular people, you know? There are doctors like you, bakers, teachers, mothers, fathers… There are kids there, Mikey.”

Michael was silent, his jaw tense like a knotted rope. Instead, Heath spoke up.

“That is something that is worth considering, but on the other hand, the reality of the atrocities committed against us is undeniable. We were essentially exterminated, Eik. It was by luck that some of us survived. You just… can’t leave this unanswered.”

Blood thirst welled up in Eik at the mere thought of it. Heath continued.

“I’m sure the commoners of Gohkamoria will comply with your demands. While they might not be able to directly hand over some of the strongest people of their civilization, they can accept your limited invasion by not going against you.”

“Yeah, you’re right,” Eik said with a sigh. “I just hope it goes that easily.”
