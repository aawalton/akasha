Chapter V.XXVII (5.37) - Origami

Kizu’s step crushed the origami person underfoot. His monster leg pierced through his soles and tore into the crumpled paper. Blood boiling, Kizu took another step. As he slowly strode forward, he took in the giggling girls on the cushions. They hadn’t yet noticed him. He recognized the two of them as Emilia’s usual lackeys. They picked up one of the Ione paper dolls and removed its legs. He watched as they set it back on the ground and cackled as the thing dragged itself towards a paper treasure chest.

That treasure chest represented the mimic he and Ione had encountered in the World Dungeon. The origami monster, like the two-headed bear, had one of Ione’s bestiary cards weaved into its paper.

The legless origami version of Ione pressed its body against the treasure chest and rubbed up and down. The origami chest opened up, revealing a paper tongue. It struggled to get away, but the origami Ione held on to it. It attempted to crawl into the monster’s open mouth and hump it from within but the chest flailed about.

At the same moment that Kizu had decided to murder everyone in this room, to his right, the door opened on the white stone wall. Out strode Emilia, half a dozen more Ione paper dolls stuffed together in her arms. However, that only caught Kizu’s attention for a mere moment. When he had entered the dormitory common room, the wall had been obscured by palm trees growing to the side. Now though, he saw it more clearly. Or, more notably, Kizu saw the posters on the wall more clearly.

There were three of them. Each was a crudely drawn image of Ione crossed with some sort of animal and making an unflattering facial expression. That, alone, would be offensive enough to Kemon people. Under each, though, were two simple words. “MONKEY SLUT.”

Kizu froze in place. All noise faded behind a thump of blood rushing to his head. He snarled as he met Emilia’s bulging eyes.

“Back!” Something slammed into him and attempted to drag him backwards. Kizu’s monster leg tore a chunk out of the ground and he lit himself aflame as he struggled against his grappler. His eyes remained locked on Emilia as he was dragged through the painting and out of the dormitory.

He fell back into the hallway and thrashed about, struggling for freedom.

“Kizu!” someone yelled at him. Kizu didn’t care. He needed to get back into that painting. He was going to rip Emilia’s neck out. He’d throw her into an active volcano. He’d feed her to Sojan.

“Kizu!” Basil got right in his face, blocking his sight from the painting.

Kizu blinked as he realized he could smell something burning. Only then did he finally process that his blazing body, held in place by Bazil’s grip, was searing his friend’s skin.

The flames spluttered out but the smell of burning flesh remained. It was tangier than cooked human. Normally, Kizu would have hated the fact that he could tell the difference. At the moment though, he wished nothing more than to burn those girls into ashes.

“Kizu. Listen to me. You need to calm down.” Basil repositioned himself, setting both his hands on Kizu’s shoulders.

“Calm. Down,” Kizu repeated, his voice a growl.

“I knew you’d react poorly to seeing what they’d been using the facsimilater for, but this….” Basil shook his head. “I’ve never seen you anywhere near this angry.”

Kizu could recall only one moment himself when he’d felt this quantity of rage. When the aurora had attempted to force its way into his soul as a means to kill Anata. Strangely, that memory helped ground him. If he lost control here, then Anata would also be forced to face consequences. Punishments to him didn’t just hurt him. They had ripples.

“I want them dead,” Kizu said, his voice now level.

“Trust me, I understand the sentiment.” Basil paused and his body shuddered. The burned skin twisted into a fold to make way for a smooth replacement. “We need to be smart.”

“Why?” Kizu spat. “Why would they do this to her?”

Basil’s face went blank. “Come on, you know the answer to that. You don’t need me to explain it to you.”

Taking in a shaky breath, Kizu considered. “Because I broke up with her.”

“She’s jealous. It’s normal.”

“That!” Kizu’s hand shot out and pointed at the desert oasis painting. “That is not normal!”

“Yes. It is,” Basil said flatly. “Just like how Ulric attacking you isn’t that uncommon for jealous teenage boys. This is standard for girls. Abhorrent? Absolutely. But you’ve clearly been too sheltered if you think teenage girls don’t do abhorrent things to one another.”

“Not uncommon?” Kizu scoffed. “Ulric shattered my leg and left me crippled.”

“Fine, that part isn’t normal. I meant the essence of it. We’re teenagers juiced up on powerful spells. It sometimes makes these things more extreme. Ione is far from the first girl to go through stuff like this and she’s handling it far better than most.”

“She knows?” Kizu’s eyes narrowed. “You knew?”

Basil tensed up and looked like a mouse fallen in the den of a fox. He slowly withdrew his hands from their placement on Kizu’s shoulders. “How about we discuss this somewhere else?”

Rather than acknowledge him verbally, Kizu grabbed Basil’s uniform jacket and used Mort as an anchor to jump them to Owl’s Respite.

The monkey, sensing his rage, scampered over to the side as they touched down on the ship. Mort crouched on a crate, tense and bracing for an attack.

A lantern hung from the lowest boom of the mast, illuminating the dark deck.

Kizu released Basil, not gently. “Explain.”

The shapeshifter took a step back and straightened his jacket. He took a deep breath. “Of course we knew Emilia was targeting Ione. She’s been doing it for months now.”

“What? She has? What do you mean ‘targeting?’ What else has she done?”

“Just little things.” Basil waved a hand. “Mostly stirring up outlandish rumors that nobody believes. Today was an escalation. I wouldn’t have thought she’d go as far as to touch Ione’s cards. That’s a dangerous step. It probably stems from Ione’s lack of reaction to the gossip.”

“What sort of outlandish rumors?” Kizu asked the question quietly, his tone icy.

Basil hesitated. He cleared his throat and he scanned the dark lake. “Mostly about her only dating you to get to Mort,” he replied in a low voice.

“Only dating me to….” The board of the deck under his foot snapped, completing Kizu’s thought for him. “You mean… that ‘Monkey Slut’ sign….”

“That’s been going around for nearly a month. Only the third image was new. Again, destruction of property is really the big line she crossed. Everyone knows how much Ione values those cards.” Basil returned his gaze to Kizu and flinched. “Obviously, we haven’t been taking this sitting down. Ione might not care much for the slander and drama, but we do.”

“Who is ‘we?’ Why didn’t you say anything to me? Just now, you tried to keep me from seeing what they were doing! You were willing to let me talk to Emilia civilly!”

“Kizu. Half of Shinzou Academy has been gossiping about this. Aoi, Ione, Faible, myself. We all knew about it.”

“I didn’t!”

“Because you don’t pay attention to anyone other than those you’re actively making an effort to include. You barely even note the existence of people outside of your friends. Unless one of us interrupts, you’re buried in books. You’re oblivious.”

The statement felt like a punch to the gut and it must have shown on his face because Basil quickly followed up his statement.

“Not that that’s necessarily a bad thing. You’re driven and you clearly go out of your way to keep us in your schedule. However, that drive leaves you blind to things like student drama. I don’t blame you for actively avoiding Emilia all semester, but problems don’t usually just fade away quietly.”

“What have you done so far?” Kizu asked, his voice once again quieting. In this conversation he’d bounced between volumes like a frog on a stimulant potion. He felt unhinged. He promised himself that from this point on he’d keep himself under control.

“Done?” Basil asked. “Well, I got you back to the ship without anyone in the academy suffering fatal wounds.”

“Not now. I meant, what have you done in retaliation?” Kizu formed a fist. “If I spent all this time training and studying, then it’s about time I transferred that into practical knowledge.”
