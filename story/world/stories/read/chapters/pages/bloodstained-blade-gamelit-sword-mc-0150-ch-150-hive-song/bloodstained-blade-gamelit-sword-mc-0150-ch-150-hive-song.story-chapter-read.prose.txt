The Ebon Blade’s opponent’s movements had been as disconnected as the footprints they left behind. Sometimes their shifting, invisible afterimage seemed more like the real target than the shards that moved first, but it was only as the blade studied all of those strange things that it noticed just how frayed the threads of the world around it had become.

While the threads that made up the men and monsters of creation were thicker and braided more tightly together, the beings of hell were reasonably well defined, too. The souls of demons were more twisted, sure, and the landscapes could be anomalous, even at the etheric level, but not like this. This felt precarious.

Here, in the heart of this dark jungle, it almost felt like it would fall into the void if a few more strands were severed. The blade stood on solid ground, but it didn’t feel particularly solid to it, not once its opponent darted forward with impossible speed.

One moment, she was on a tree branch forty feet away, and the next, she’d closed more than half the gap in a single stride, and its wielder barely had a chance to parry with its deathly, impenetrable edge. What happened next was painful.

In that moment, the weapon that had been gliding translucently through the darkness moved toward its wielder’s neck, transformed from an amorphous curving blade to the wickedly spined foreleg of a giant spider. Regardless of what it was, it met the attack with a shower of sparks, and then the unexpected happened. It cut the blade, rather than the other way around.
