Ning carefully dropped down from the tree and approached the massive crater-like opening that served as the boar's lair. The ground still trembled faintly in the distance, which meant the enraged beast was still chasing Xiao Fan.

Good. He had time, though probably not much.

Ning didn't approach the entrance immediately. He circled the area first, examining the disturbed soil and tracks around the lair. Most were old, and among the fresh ones, he could only identify the massive hoofprints of the boar that had just left.

Black boars were generally solitary beasts anyway. Their sheer aggression made sharing territory rather difficult.

Still, Ning wasn't going to bet his life on animal behavior alone.

He crouched near the entrance and extended his senses as far as he could. His vague intuition picked up nothing from within. There was no movement, nor could he hear anything from the cave.

Ning waited a little longer before finally inching forward with the same caution one might use after hearing something move on the other side of the room in the dead of night.

The moment he stepped inside, a foul, musky odor slammed into him.

Ning almost stepped right back out.

The lair was dark and cramped, its floor littered with broken branches, mud, shredded bark, and the remains of whatever the boar had dragged home over the years. Ning ignored most of it at first, however, because his eyes immediately went toward the far wall.

There were severed roots, bits of stem, and traces of lingering qi.

Ning approached carefully and crouched beside them.

From the looks of it, there had been several stalks of Dragon Qi Grass growing here. Every mature stalk had already been harvested, and judging by the torn roots and scattered leaves, whoever picked them had been in quite a hurry.

"As expected, Xiao Fan already got the treasure."

Ning wasn't surprised. If anything, he felt strangely relieved.

The protagonist had already taken his lucky opportunity. Ning wasn't stealing anything destined for him.

Which meant everything Xiao Fan had left behind was free real estate.

"Hah... such good material..."

Ning's eyelids twitched as he picked up one of the discarded pieces.

As a spiritual farmer, his heart ached at the sight. The stems had been torn rather than properly cut, several usable roots had been left in the soil, and there were even pieces of perfectly good leaves scattered across the ground.

Someone like Ning would never let even a single usable piece go to waste.

Clearly, what Ning treasured wasn't worth a second glance to Xiao Fan.

Then again, considering what he'd just witnessed, perhaps Xiao Fan simply had different standards for what counted as valuable.

Three people had come to kill him, "kindly" delivered all their belongings into his hands, and even told him where to find a rare spiritual herb before dying.

Ning suddenly understood something.

It all makes sense now.

He remembered asking Wei and the others how Xiao Fan had cultivated so quickly despite his low-grade spiritual root. Their assumption had been that Xiao Fan possessed an inheritance left behind by his parents or was secretly receiving resources from the Xiao Clan.

Ning had never found that explanation particularly convincing.

Would someone like Xiao Hong really allow Xiao Fan to peacefully cultivate with a pile of valuable resources? If Xiao Fan had openly possessed anything worth taking, Ning suspected it would've disappeared long ago.

Now he had another possibility.

Maybe Xiao Fan's cultivation had been built on a long chain of coincidences and lucky opportunities lining themselves up in front of him.

A treasure here, an inheritance there, a few enemies graciously donating their storage pouches along the way...

No wonder cultivators placed opportunity alongside talent and resources when discussing what determined someone's future.

Ning couldn't help feeling a little envious, but he wasn't going to complain too much.

After all, this time he was benefiting from Xiao Fan's luck.

This was probably the first genuine windfall Ning had received without putting in much work himself. Perhaps there really was some truth to "fortune favors the bold."

Though judging by Xiao Fan sprinting through the forest with a thousand-pound boar trying to turn him into paste, fortune apparently expected the bold to have very quick feet.

Ning preferred his current arrangement.

Let Xiao Fan handle the bold part. He'd handle the leftovers.

His thoughts wandered, but his hands remained methodical. He carefully collected the usable pieces of Dragon Qi Grass and sealed them inside a container, then dug around the remaining roots before scooping several handfuls of the surrounding spiritual soil into his sack.

Once he started, however, his inner loot goblin quickly came out in full force.

Broken beast bones? Into the sack.

Fragments of weapons that probably belonged to unfortunate hunters? Into the sack.

A few pieces of metal whose purpose he couldn't immediately identify?

Into the sack.

Anything that looked remotely useful and wasn't firmly attached to the ground soon found itself inside Ning's possession.

Fortunately, Ning understood his own tendencies well enough to prepare in advance. He had brought a large sack with him.

He even had a second one for emergencies.

As Ning moved deeper into the lair, checking through the remains scattered along the walls, something in the innermost corner caught his attention.

A demon core.

Ning's eyes lit up as he carefully approached it.

Demon cores were condensed masses of qi occasionally left behind by powerful spiritual beasts. Most ordinary beasts couldn't form one at all; generally, it required either an exceptional bloodline or cultivation reaching the later stages of Qi Condensation.

This one was only the size of a large marble, and the qi inside had already become extremely sparse.

Ning examined the surrounding marks before quickly understanding what had happened.

The black boar hadn't produced this core. It must have obtained it from another beast at some point and brought it back to its lair. Judging by how little qi remained, the boar had probably been slowly absorbing the core to aid its own cultivation, perhaps even using it to reach the late stage.

That explained one thing.

It didn't explain why the demon core was so disgustingly wet.

Ning stared at the sticky surface for several seconds before an unpleasant possibility occurred to him.

The boar had probably been keeping it in its mouth while absorbing the qi.

"...Ugh."

Without inspecting it any further, Ning swiftly dropped the core into a separate box and sealed it shut.

The thought alone made his skin crawl.

Shaking off his disgust, Ning made one final sweep through the lair before retreating toward the entrance. His haul was surprisingly impressive for something he'd stumbled into by accident.

Before leaving, he took out a small packet of scent-masking powder and scattered it over the areas where he'd spent the most time. It wouldn't erase every trace he'd left behind, but it should at least make it harder for the boar to distinguish his scent from the mess already filling the cave.

Ning had no idea how good a late-stage black boar's nose was, and he had absolutely no desire to find out by having one track him back to the city.

Better safe than sorry.

When Ning finally stepped out of the lair, only a few minutes had passed. The distant tremors had grown faint enough that Xiao Fan and the boar were probably already far away.

That was Ning's cue to leave.

He activated Shadow Steps and ran straight toward Blackthorn City, two sacks bouncing behind him as he left all the trouble behind.

...

Inside Blackthorn City, Ning headed straight for the marketplace and soon arrived at his usual stop, Xi's Butchery.

He dropped two heavy sacks onto the counter with a thud.

Xi Shu looked up from behind the counter. As if determined to fulfill every stereotype associated with his profession, the butcher was a massive, broad-shouldered man who towered over most people. He pulled the first sack toward himself and began examining its contents with practiced hands.

"A black boar, huh? Damage is minimal, but the poison residue in the flesh knocks the value down a bit," Xi Shu grunted.

"I know. Just give me the usual rate."

Ning had already expected the poison to reduce the price by at least one spirit stone. Normally, that would've hurt a little, but considering what else he'd brought back today, he didn't particularly mind.

"Alright. This one comes to four spirit stones." Xi Shu glanced at the sack, then gave Ning a measuring look. "Not much of a haul today. You usually bring back at least two."

"That's because I found something else this time."

Ning slid the second sack across the counter.

Xi Shu opened it and paused. Instead of another boar, the sack contained an assortment of bones from different beasts, along with a few other materials Ning had picked out from the lair.

"Did you raid a nest or something?" Xi Shu raised a brow as he pulled out one of the bones. "These came from all sorts of beasts."

"Let's just say I got lucky."

Ning didn't bother making up some elaborate story. Xi's Butchery had a good reputation, and Old Zhou had personally introduced him to the place. His status as an outer-sect disciple wasn't enough to make anyone here bow and scrape, but it was enough that an established business wouldn't casually try to cheat him.

Blackthorn City was directly controlled by the Pure Qi Sect, after all, and the status of a sect disciple carried considerable weight within its walls. Even many of the guards patrolling the streets were disciples themselves.

It was somewhat ironic.

Outside the city, an outer-sect robe could make Ning look like a fat sheep to unscrupulous merchants and prime prey to robbers.

Inside Blackthorn City, the same robe was practically a protective talisman.

Xi Shu spent a little longer sorting through the bones before clicking his tongue. "Most of these are chewed up, and whoever left them behind didn't exactly care about preserving them. For the whole lot... twelve spirit stones."

"Alright." Ning nodded. The price was close enough to what he'd expected. "Throw in cutting off the boar's tusks for me, and we have a deal."

Xi Shu stared at him.

Ning smiled.

"Sheesh... fine." The butcher shook his head. "You always have to squeeze something extra out of me."

Xi Shu had dealt with plenty of sect disciples before. Most tried to appear lofty and unconcerned with mundane things like a spirit stone here or there.

Ning asked for discounts.

"Every spirit stone saved is another spirit stone I can spend on cultivation," Ning said with a grin.

Xi Shu snorted. "With that attitude, maybe you will make it past the mid-stage of Qi Condensation."

…

After finishing at the shop, Ning wandered toward the open marketplace to set up a stall.

Most people he passed were early-stage Qi cultivators, with a few reaching the mid-stage. Late-stage cultivators were rare in this part of the city.

Independent cultivators had it rough. Without being chosen by a sect, their talent was usually mediocre, and they had to lease spiritual dwellings from sect-appointed managers. Even cultivating required paying rent.

This was actually one of the sect's main sources of income. Clearly, they understood that being a landlord was the most profitable profession.

While it sometimes seemed like the sect exploited its own disciples, independent cultivators had it far worse. At least disciples had a clear path to rise, along with access to techniques, missions, and sect facilities. Independent cultivators had to obtain everything themselves while walking on eggshells around anyone stronger than them, and even their meager savings would eventually be siphoned away by one expense or another.

The romantic image of carefree "immortals" from legends was a distant fantasy for most of them.

Ning's thoughts drifted as he found an empty spot and set up his stall. Thanks to his outer-sect robe, he didn't even have to pay any "offerings" to the local managers to display his goods.

He arranged three fragmented weapons, two slightly chipped swords, and finally the highlight of his haul, the demon core.

Time passed as Ning practiced Turtle Breathing behind the stall. Occasionally, someone would stop, ask for a price, and buy something. Most people were interested in the demon core, but their offers were mediocre. Seeing Ning's outer-sect robe, they didn't try to haggle too aggressively and simply left when he refused.

Eventually, all the scrap metal was sold for ten spirit stones. Only the demon core remained.

Ning wasn't worried. Demon cores had countless uses across the various professions of the immortal path. Someone would eventually want it.

Just as the monotony began to settle in, someone approached his stall, causing Ning's eyebrow to twitch.

It was Xiao Fan.

A normal Fourth Stage cultivator being chased by a very angry late-stage black boar would probably have perished, but clearly that didn't apply to Xiao Fan. Of course, he hadn't escaped completely unharmed. His clothes were tattered, and his face was somewhat pale, but he didn't seem particularly bothered by either.

"How much for this?" Xiao Fan asked, pointing at the demon core.

Ning remained calm. He didn't act shocked or suddenly call out Xiao Fan's name like some cliché fool. He had long prepared himself for the possibility of meeting him again; after all, they belonged to the same sect.

"Well, I want to trade this demon core for something that can improve cultivation. If you have something suitable, I'd gladly trade."

While spirit stones were nice, goodies from the protagonist were nicer.

"Something to improve cultivation..." Xiao Fan muttered thoughtfully before taking something out of his storage pouch. "How about this?"

Ning's eyes narrowed when he saw the familiar stalk of Dragon Qi Grass in Xiao Fan's hand. It was undoubtedly one of the stalks he'd taken from the black boar's lair.

He had actually hoped Xiao Fan might offer one.

As a spiritual farmer, Ning wasn't interested only in consuming the herb. He wanted to see if he could plant it, and for that, he first needed a proper sample.

"A Dragon Qi Grass?" Ning pretended to be surprised. "That's rare. I'm interested."

"If you're okay with it, let's trade." Xiao Fan picked up the demon core and examined it more closely. "But this demon core seems to have been used before."

Although he pointed out the problem, Ning could still see the eagerness in his expression. Apparently, whatever Xiao Fan wanted from the demon core wasn't simply the remaining qi.

"Hmm..." Ning considered the difference in value. "How about I add ten spirit stones on top?"

"That simple?"

"Yeah," Ning replied blankly.

"No, no problem. It's just..." Xiao Fan looked at him strangely. "The price is fair."

Ning tilted his head slightly, wondering why that seemed to surprise him so much.

For Xiao Fan, however, this might genuinely have been the first time he'd managed to complete a transaction at a fair price. Usually, the moment someone realized he actually needed something, the price mysteriously doubled, some ridiculous condition appeared, or the other party simply decided they didn't like his face.

Was this the day he finally completed a transaction without being scammed? Xiao Fan almost shed tears of gratitude.

"Of course. I pride myself on fairness," Ning said, thumping his chest, utterly confused about why the protagonist suddenly looked so emotional.

"You really should." Xiao Fan nodded seriously. "If you get more demon cores, come find me to trade."

Ning's interest was immediately piqued, but he simply nodded. "Sure."

After completing the trade, Xiao Fan soon disappeared into the marketplace.

Watching his retreating figure, Ning couldn't help but wonder about something. He had picked up the demon core from the black boar's lair, and judging by Xiao Fan's reaction, demon cores seemed quite useful to him. Yet because of the misinformation about the boar's cultivation, Xiao Fan had been chased away and missed it.

Ning had found it instead, brought it all the way back to Blackthorn City, and somehow, it still ended up in Xiao Fan's hands.

Was this Ning's luck?

Or Xiao Fan's?

If this were a novel, Ning could easily imagine a scenario where he foolishly offended Xiao Fan while selling the core, overcharging him, and ending up brutally killed, while the core still fell into Xiao Fan’s hands anyway.

Hmm… food for thought. I suppose.
