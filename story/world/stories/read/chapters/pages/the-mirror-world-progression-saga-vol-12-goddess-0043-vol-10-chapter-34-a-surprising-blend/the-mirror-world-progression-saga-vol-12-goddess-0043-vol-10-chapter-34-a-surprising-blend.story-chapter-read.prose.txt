Along the advance to Fieldcarn, Samantha’s efforts, combined with her side projects elsewhere when not as needed, gained her the last bits of experience necessary to level up [Magineer].

[Magineer 14] gained. +50 Maximum MP added. Bonus upgrade from [Magineer] (Tweak Spell) already present in an evolved state. You may upgrade a [Magineer] Class Skill instead.

After perusing the options, Samantha saw [Sympathetic Targeting] was available, which she already had, and, as she knew from experience and research, this was something quite painfully hard to upgrade by practice. Not to mention, the odds of her having time to pursue that were pretty much zero. She selected that to upgrade it to Expert, which made it a Class Skill ‘officially.’

Sympathetic Targeting (Expert)

Instead of normal targeting for single-target spells and abilities, you may operate by Sympathy, i.e. having some other link to the target, whether by having an aura-retaining personal item, by them having your personal aura-retaining item, a body sample (blood, hair), a physical, artistic representation, having access to an inherent mystical link to the target (portal, detected point of clairvoyance), an analyzed aura, or the like.

The penalty varies, but starts high for minor links (-5 Effective Levels penalty); multiple links reduce the penalty. At this level of Sympathetic Targeting, the maximum Effective Level is up +0 after any other bonuses.

…

This may also be used for a bonus with normal/default targeting, for [Curses] and Will vs. Will contests where such a connection is logically beneficial. At this level of Sympathetic Targeting, up to 2 Effective Levels may be gained, by either a major link or two minor links.

Beyond this, she gained access to the glorious level 14 spells she’d been waiting on, through [Allspell] access. Word of Death that could kill instantly. Immune to Element, which did pretty much exactly as suggested against one elemental damage type, but couldn’t be stacked. Gravity Reversal, which was an AoE hellscape for a large number of non-flying foes, and was very viable in war scenarios, though it wasn’t selective targeting except to exempt the caster. The glorious Summon Dragon, which was vulnerable to de-summoning but could dish out a lot of pain if it wasn’t dealt with quickly, including basically unleashing a destruction spell with its breath attack if given half the chance. Case in point of vulnerabilities, there was Banishing Burst, which dismissed summons, Servitors, most spirits under control, and dealt damage in an AoE to boot.

Many required material components by now, but she’d already prepared for that eventuality and had it all in stock. On the other hand, the power of Level 14 often seemed to take more time than she’d want in ongoing combat. Summon Dragon required literally burning ‘draconic flesh’ in a mini-ritual. Fortunately, this was a rare component and treasure item actually stocked by wizards around the world for aeons. Hideously expensive, but available. It did make you think twice about using it in scenarios you expected it to get de-summoned in before it got anything done.

It seemed like high-level spellcasters were meant to do one measly thing every five to ten seconds and rely on contingencies for defense. Well, she wasn’t going to surrender her speed advantage, stacking lots of near-instant actions on top of each other in that same timeframe… and what she already had was unlikely to be exceeded any time soon in sheer destructive capacity.

Hmm. I wonder if a ‘de-summoning protection’ spell could be made? I’ve never heard of it, so it certainly isn’t standard, but it should be possible. Not sure if it’s worth it or not, but it’s a thought. It’s even kinda in my wheelhouse. Protection and Conjuration.

When her armies finally besieged the fortress of Fieldcarn (and the abandoned town connected to it) in an all-out, from-every-direction blitzkrieg, they found it much less defended than previously reported. They more or less were able to burst through magical protections and carpet-bomb it with destruction magic. Samantha took part, though entirely from long-range.

She also took the convenient opportunity to summon a freakin’ dragon for the finale — once she was relatively certain there wasn’t anyone holding on to any sudden summon dismissals. But seriously, how could she resist?!

An extremely generic, badass, gigantic red dragon began scorching the fortress with dreadful firebreath in long sweeps, ignoring the counterattacking arrows and magic item blasts like the efforts of gnats. Even stone caught to dragonfire, and that fire persisted as if napalm had been dropped. It was horrifyingly fascinating to see it in action — the ‘stickiness’ wasn’t physical, it was from mana. What was painted with the fire was basically ‘enchanted’ with a burning effect, fueling itself to the flames.

After the second run of firebreath blasting the fortress walls, the continuous fire morphing and shaping itself inside buildings through the gaps, burning those inside… the scarce remainder of troops swiftly raised the white flag and surrendered. Putting out the dragon fire was a pain, requiring her and her Antimages to swoop in and disenchant the scorched and eaten-through stone.

They sought out traps waiting within, but found nothing. Nonetheless, Dax refused to camp anywhere near the place. Instead, some Servitors, primarily of Redberry and Zadkiel, some long-duration summons, and a small reserve of lesser golems they’d arranged to leave there, would have to suffice for the garrison.

The acting commander was found dead from multiple puncture wounds in his back that he ‘mysteriously acquired’ shortly after the dragonfire damage began. The surviving high-ranking officer was pretty damned low on the totem pole, but had no problem blabbing, begging copiously for them not to ‘feed him to the dragon.’

He claimed everyone ‘up north’ was in a tizzy and there were ‘troubles.’ The dead officers had been disturbed by the news, whatever it was. Samantha didn’t need to know exactly what it was for it to be music to her ears. Troubles for her enemies? Yes, please!

The dragon’s duration was short, but she maintained a perfect visual copy of it through her [Conjure] ability, so as to maintain the illusion that the monstrosity was her pet. The terror of the soldiers at the sight of it hanging out on the walls and seemingly watching them was palpable. They almost seemed relieved to be sent to the dungeons.

Just the sight wasn’t enough… It was the dragonfire first. They must be trained against illusions. They all know I have powers of that nature, and ‘deceptive’ Servitors. My Faerie Dragons can breathe fire, but not like that. That was some special shit. It was obvious to everyone as soon as it was unleashed. A truly rude awakening…

Once again, she was reminded of the ‘witchfire’ she’d seen her realm had developed in various future visions. It hadn’t happened in her current timeline, likely due to so many other things thrown in Tashome’s lap. She’d gotten close to it in many ways, and seemingly had just seen a full demonstration of what it might be like in practice.

Did she already have it, effectively? She wasn’t sure it got more authentic than out of a pet dragon’s mouth. The intimidation factor was another consideration. Suddenly, her idea about a ‘de-summoning protection’ custom spell was getting more attractive.

As her armies picked up and continued their endless, brutal march to Matrazere, she awaited news on these ‘troubles up north.’ Their information sources were rather lacking out of Matrazere, unfortunately.

Palthennius fought alone, against a veritable storm of enemies, martial and magical, fleeing through the streets of The Proper. His own countrymen, now serving those who would betray them, mind, body, and soul. He didn’t bother wasting his breath any further on trying to convince them. It fell on the deaf ears of absolute obedience.

Studiously, he had to focus on countering the sword and spear craft, as he let the spells try their hand at his protections and fail over and over again. Very slowly, the lesser of them whittled it down. It didn’t matter. He knew what was coming, and he had to be prepared for it.

The enemy was hidden somewhere in the preternatural dark above. Despite being daytime, they’d coated the whole city in nigh impenetrable darkness, cowing nearly the entire populace in one fell swoop, as most needed spellcraft to see through it. Fortunately, Palthennius didn’t need to see at all, but distance mattered. His senses worked far better close.

As expected, his attempted retreat down an alleyway was cut off by a mass of more combatants.

He prepared for his apparent last stand. He didn’t hold his blows, either — he killed. His actions had made them all his enemies. He’d led a mutiny. A day deep, at most, working tediously, and they’d uncovered it just like that. Not long enough, by far… a handful of captains convinced and loyal to him. Higher? He didn’t even know. Many troops, of course, but not enough to swing things. His ‘patron’ had not stuck around, apparently happy to cancel his debt via the information and vanish on his own contingency plans. All-in-all, hardly enough for a revolt, much less a revolution.

He’d failed. There was nothing left but for them to retreat now. Whoever could survive had to seek survival, and those would be few indeed.

I’d do it again, though. I’d do it again… because we’re all doomed anyway. How could I stand by? Knowledge corrupts; forces action. Here I am.

Pathennius even managed a surprise kill on an exposed, flying wizard, who didn’t expect a thrown dart to circle around and stab him multiple times in the back before vanishing. The wizard dropped out of the air onto a roof like a sack.

Try as the footsoldiers might, they could not trap him. He made piles and walls of the fallen, and they had to pull them back just to get at him… and then he made more piles. He darted back and forth, weaving between them, slipping free of holds, his armor protecting him. Finally, they went defensive, and let the spells begin their attrition. They kept him trapped.

He shed the magic like water drops falling on a waxy leaf. He waited. Waited. Waited. The protections wore down. Enough. Hopefully enough.

Finally, the hidden Entropic spellcaster struck. A deterioration spell, one he hoped would weaken and wear Palthennius down. Soften him up for the follow-ups. But that was only with a close contest. Palthennius let it through entirely, failing deliberately by a wide margin, allowing the Entropy to annihilate his body. Being Entropic, this resulted in disintegration…

That was exactly what he wanted.

[True Clone] has died. The 72-hour cooldown has begun. Your consciousness has returned. You are in severe sympathetic pain. You have resisted unconsciousness. You are incapacitated for 30 seconds.

Palthennius took a sharp, involuntary inhale as the eyes of his real body awoke, just barely resisting crying out despite the agony. Lying under a bed, he writhed in paralysis, unable to move thanks to the pain wracking him. Otherwise, his senses told him nothing was moving nearby. Silence and stillness.

He’d never used the ability before. It was fairly new. He’d kept it from everyone, even his superiors and allies, perhaps thanks to his growing paranoia. Fortunately, they’d never had any reason to think a max level had advanced. It was exceedingly rare and poorly understood among martial classes. His, [Heavenly Amalgam], a unique one, was even less understood.

So, they didn’t track me instantly. Possibly believe I’m dead. Confusion is reigning, at the very least. Good. A satisfactory distraction.

Not that it mattered. He was doomed, he knew, like the majority of those he’d dragged into this… and all of those he didn’t.

It was just a matter of when his time would come.
