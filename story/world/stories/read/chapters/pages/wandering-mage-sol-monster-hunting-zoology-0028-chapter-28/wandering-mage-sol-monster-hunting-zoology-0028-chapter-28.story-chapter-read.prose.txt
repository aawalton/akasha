The town was alight with lanterns that hung at the side of a sparkling cobblestone walkway that cut between buildings. The architecture was just as amazing. They were built with a type of stone that Tressa and none of them had ever seen. It was a dark grey that glittered almost like marble, but it didn't give that off. Instead of shutters, they had glass windows. The roofs were made of some type of material that sat in layers over each other much like grass huts, but it looked almost like stone. Tressa stared in absolute wonder.

“Glass windows!” Lisha said, her tongue flicking. “And every building has them!”

Tressa’s head turned. Was it that impressive? Though, thinking back, she saw Lisha’s point; none of the other towns they had been through, and even Ligis, had only certain buildings with glass windows, such as the Guild Hall, government buildings, and richer estates. Tressa was just so used to seeing glass everywhere in Falidorn she was surprised they thought this was such a big deal.

“What kind of stone is that?” Briadon asked. “I've never seen such a thing.”

“Welcome to Corrian Island,” said a booming voice.

They turned to see a small man. He had a massive red beard trimmed in the shape of a V, and his mustache curled fancifully. He wore metal armor and had a massive double-bladed axe on his back. Though, to be fair, that axe would be a single-handed weapon in Briadon’s hands.

“Ah! Thank you,” said Mitchel, bowing to the dwarf.

“Glad ye could make it. Please head towards the inn and get some rest. Make sure to keep yer windows locked at night. We tend to have issues with Petracoles.”

“I'm sorry what?” Lisha asked.

“Pentracoles. They are small creatures, about four inches in height, with razor-sharp nails and teeth, pointed ears, and can use fire magic. They like to steal clothes and small valuables like coins and gems. Just make sure everything is secure.”

Tressa’s ears perked under her hood as she gasped, “I've never even heard of such a creature…”

“Aye, never had we dwarves till we moved onto the island. They are native here. We only have this side of the island developed as there are far bigger creatures on the other end that we don't want to stir any trouble with. So be warned, when heading to the other half of the bridge, there are monsters out there that are stronger than us Dwarves. Make sure that everything is secure.”

Tressa already had her sketchbook out and was jotting down notes. “So they have hair?”

“I'm sorry?”

“Hair? What color is their skin? Can they talk? What do they eat? Why do they like to steal clothing and socks and small valuables? Do they move fast or slow? Do they ride birds?” she asked in quick succession, trying to get all her thoughts out into words.

“Whoa, whoa, whoa, lass, calm down,” the dwarf said, holding up his hands.

Lisha smacked Tressa over the back of the head. “Don't go scaring people with your insane antics over monsters.”

“Geh!” she cried out, rubbing her head. “What? I just want to know.”

Briadon sighed. “Sorry about her. She has a few screws loose.”

“Hahaha, not to worry. We were fascinated by them as well when we first arrived. We learned quickly that they are fast little creatures that, if ye make them angry, will set your belongings on fire and steal all your valuables. And to the skin tone, I'm not sure; I haven't actually laid my own eyes on one. But Torthen has. He is the innkeeper. He and his son have had dealings with them, so yer best bet is to speak to them if you wish to know more.”

Tressa nodded.

“Speaking of the inn, where might we find it?” Lisha asked. “We have had a long journey and would like to rest.”

“Ah, ah, yes ye traveled all day across the bridge. Well, it be down that street to your right. Has a massive cauldron for a sign and a stable out back for yer ox.”

“Thank you, good sir,” Luke said as he bowed.

With that, they parted ways, waving goodbye to the bridge guard.

Tressa’s hand subconsciously came up to her hood, checking it. She knew once they were out of the dark night and indoors she’d have to be careful to keep her ears hidden. Dwarves despised elves and especially High Elves even more than most, because of the Mad King, of course, and what he’d done to their cities.

Tressa didn’t even know much else about dwarves other than word of mouth since any records of them had been burned in Falidorn’s library.

“Here, I think that’s the Inn,” Lisha said, pointing to a sign hanging above the door, and they could hear horses whinnying in the back.

A young human boy walked around the building, wiping his hands on a towel, and looked at them. A look of annoyance crossed his face as he held his hand out towards the oxen.

“Great,” he muttered. “Welcome to the Weeping Cauldron,” he said.

“Unusual name,” said Mitchel.

“Well, it's what the master wanted,” he said, his voice full of annoyance. “So are you going to hand over the reins?”

“Oh, um, no. We can put them up. After we need to account for the cargo, and well, Besse here isn't all that keen on others, so worry not, lad; we got them.”

The boy let out a sigh, showing his relief, not even hiding the fact he didn't care about helping them out. “Alright, just head around back, and the last two stalls should be free. You can put them there.”

“Thank you, lad,” Luke said and guided the ox towards the back, and Mitchel followed.

“We'll meet ya inside and get some rooms,” Briadon assured them.

“Sounds good,” said Luke. “Want to join us for some drinks after all that travel? I'd like some ale.”

“Aye!” Briadon said, and they parted ways.

Lisha walked up the steps and pushed open the door, and loud voices could be heard.

“—telling ye, if the goblins are gathering again, it won't stay in the west,” a dwarf near the door was saying.

The human across from him scoffed and lifted his mug. “Long as they don't come asking us to fight another damn Goblin War.”

“Aye, and who's supposed to stop them this time?” another dwarf asked. “The elves?”

That earned a few laughs around the table.

“The Wood Elves, maybe,” the first dwarf said. “High Elves haven't crawled out from behind their walls since that damned king of theirs.”

“Good place for 'em.”

“I'd sooner trust the goblins.”

A man whose face looked half-covered in rough stone leaned back in his chair. “Could always get the Minotaurs from the south involved.”

“Hahaha, as if! Those racist—” the man stopped, seeing Briadon's massive figure looming in the doorway, standing beside Lisha, Tressa between them.

“Mind finishing what you were going to say?” Briadon said. “What about my people?”

“Hehehe, nothing, sir. Nothing at all.”

His nose flared as he snorted. “Whatever, I don't care. Come, let's get something to eat I'm starving.”

He lead the way over to the far end of the room and set his knapsack down. Then he pulled at the cord on his chest, and his club unbuckled from his back; he caught it mid-fall and leaned it up against the wall.

Lisha also undid her halberd from her back and rested it against the wall next to his, then removed her scimitar from her waist and set down her own knapsack.

Tressa slung off her pack and set it next to her as she sat, setting her sketchbook on the table in front of her.

“What can I do for you three?” a female voice asked as she came over.

Tressa turned to look and was instantly frozen. The woman had whiskers, a catlike face, and fur all over her body. It was a beastkind!

“Ah, beastkind, ey?” Briadon asked, staring down at the small female cat who was even slightly shorter than Tressa.

“Ah yes sir,” she said her tail flicking and her ears twitched.

“Haven't seen your kind around much.”

“I could say the same to you, Master Minotaur.”

“Hahaha, fair enough. I'll take the house stew and a pint of ale.”

“Same here,” Lisha said.

“Ah… me as well!” Tressa blurted out, her hand opening her sketchbook but something stopped her. She looked down to see Lisha’s claw sitting on top, and when Tressa looked up, the Statiki was giving her a look that said, ‘Don’t even think about it.’

“Coming right up,” the cat girl said. “Would you all like the bread on the side?”

“H-How much extra?” Tressa asked.

“Three copper for two slices.”

“Make it four slices, and if you happen to have any honey, could I pay for some?”

“Sorry ma'am we haven't been able to get honey. Our supplier said the winters were harsh, so the honey is out this time.”

Tressa sighed. “Do you have any pastries like pie or Dotrin?”

“Yes, we do have some Dotrin made. It will cost a bit as the berries in it.”

“That's fine,” she said.

“Tressa, your sweet tooth is so bad,” Lisha said. “Do you want to ruin your teeth?”

Tressa stuck out her tongue from under her hood.

Lisha laughed. “I'm just saying.”

“You don't have to mother me. I do take care of my teeth.”

Briadon snorted as the beast kind girl came back and set three pints of ale on the table.

“Thank you,” Tressa said.

“Of course and the food will be out soon,” she said with a polite bow and left.

“Ooo, spiced ale. That's different,” Tressa said with surprise after her first sip. It had the standard bitterness, but it had a bit of spice in the background and made her smile with joy. Everywhere they had gone, she had tried different ales and wines, and each had its own unique taste. She took another long sip and leaned back with a sigh. “This stuff is truly wonderful.”

“Ahaha, seems you are already enjoying yourself, Miss Tressa,” said Luke as he and Mitchel approached.

“Ah, welcome back,” she said, sitting up straight as they pulled out their chairs.

“Everything taken care of?” Briadon asked.

“Yes. Had to pay the innkeeper before coming in as to pay for the ox to rest,” he said.

Two more menus were placed on the table, and Tressa hadn’t even seen the small cat girl come over. She looked back and forth over her shoulder, wondering how the cat girl was so fast, almost expecting to see she had a twin.

“Here you are, sirs. A menu, and if you would like, we do have the house ale, or if you want to be fancy, we do also have some spiced wine and Derin berry whiskey.”

“I'll take the whiskey,” said Luke. “Oh, and the house stew would be fine, and three pieces of bread.”

“Just the house ale. And the special.”

“Alright I'll let the master know.”

“You don't seem too fazed by the beast kind,” Briadon commented.

“Not at all. We deal with them a lot in the west,” said Luke.

“Maybe one day I can go with you,” Tressa asked.

“Haha, maybe,” Mitchel answered. “Perhaps you can help us establish trade with Falidorn?”

Luke cleared his throat at that, apparently not as enthusiastic about the idea but not saying anything.

Tressa took another drink. “There is a possibility that I could speak with the council and get you guys a pass to trade. I know some merchant that come to the capital every so often.”

“That would open a lot of new doors for us,” Mitchel said, looking to Luke.

“I suppose it would… I think we’d just have to be cautious about it. No offense, of course, Miss. Tressa.”

“I promise, we High Elves are not what you think,” Tressa said. “The merchants who do come are treated well. I knew one for a long time but… um, well he retired but he was a good friend.”

“Well, we can definetly think about it,” Luke conceded. “But we would also have to discuss with the guild master. It could open new doors for us. But it would also cause some problems.”

“Here you are,” said the cat beastkind. She placed down Tressa's meal, and Lisha's, then hurried back to the kitchen and brought out the plates of bread and Briadon's plate as well.

Tressa was itching to reach for her sketchbook again as she watched her scamper to and from the kitchen. But she understood it’d be rude to start drawing her right here and now without permission. Frankly, if Lisha hadn’t warned her she wouldn’t have thought twice about it.

“Sirs, your meal will be out next!” she said, placing the plate of bread in front of them.

“Thank you miss. What can I call ya?” Mitchel asked.

“Siri,” she said.

“Nice to meet you, Miss Siri.”

She bowed and walked away, her whiskers twitching with joy.

“God, this is so good,” Tressa said. “The broth is savory and delicious. I think once I'm done I'll seek out the inn master and get the information from him on the monster.”

“No,” Lisha said. “We will get the rooms and then go to bed. You are not going to pester him about the monster this late at night.”

“Well, then I’ll just go for a brief walk and—”

“Or the beast kind,” Lisha said, cutting her off.

Tressa pouted but returned her attention to her meal.

“I told ya to fu’k off!” yelled a dwarf from the other side of the room, causing all patrons to pause and turn his way.

“And I told ya to hand over the coin you owe. I won fair and square. Now pay up ye damn fod.”

“Don't you dare call me a fod.”

“Or what? Ye can't even stand let alone fight fod.”

“Hey, hey, hey, break it up, lads,” said another dwarf as he walked over, separating the two.

“No that fod needs to pay up.”

“Enough with the slurs, lad, or you will be flogged in the town square; you know the laws.”

The man paused and looked at the dwarf as he growled. “Then tell the thief to pay up. I won the game of cards. He owes me.”

The man turned on the dwarf. “Pay up, Miltion.”

“What you talkin' abou’? I don't have to pay at all.”

The dwarf looked at the table to see that the Miltion had lost and nodded. “Yer hand sucked. Pay up.”

“I aint got the money.”

“Then be lucky if ye aint arrested for theft. And your gambling habit needs to stop,” said the man. “Oy, Liltin, mind if I throw him out?”

“Go for it,” said a massive round dwarf that stood behind the bar, cleaning a glass.

“Thank you sir,” he said and grabbed Miltion by the collar and dragged the screeching man to the door and threw him out.

“Come back when ye sober,” he said and shut the door on the cursing Miltion. “Sorry about that, please go back to your meals.”

The patrons turned back to their tables and chatter filled the Inn once more.

“Wonder what that was about,” said Luke.

“Sounds like the dwarf lost a game of cards and didn't have the money to pay up.” Briadon said.

Tressa rolled her eyes. “I bet I could win at a game like that.”

“Ha a naive girl like yourself definitely would lose.” Briadon laughed. “You can hardly speak the language, let alone play a game of cards.”

“It can't be that hard.”

“You would be surprised. Games can be difficult,” said Mitchel.

“She might be able to pick it up pretty quick,” said Lisha.

“Thank you!” Tressa said throwing her hands up.

“Hahaha seems you have a reputation of sorts.”

“Not really,” she said, taking another drink, feeling its warmth fill her stomach. “Hey! I’m going to go check on my desert!”

“But it…” Lisha sighed as Tressa stood quickly and headed over to the bar.

The cat beastkind was out serving other patrons, so it gave Tressa a perfect excuse to go up and speak with the innkeeper. Lisha, of course, knew exactly what she was up to.

“Excuse me, but I was supposed to have a piece of the Dotrin,” she said.

“Ah, are you the one who wanted it? It was commin out in just a moment,” said the barrel-shaped dwarf named Liltin.

“Ah yes. Can I ask, are you the Innkeeper?”

“That I am little miss.” He laughed a booming laugh. “So how have you had Dotrin before?”

“Oh I got to try it in Ligis. There was a Dwarf with a shop there that made excellent pie, and he used Dotrin berries and other items.”

“Ah, that makes sense. May have been my apprentice. What was the lads name?” He asked as he moved about and grabbed a piece of Dotrin. The soft pastry was folded and scored to let the berries cool in a fluffy bread. The juices had overflowed and mixed with the bread to make a delicious, crispy taste. He set the pastry in front of her.

“Utor,” she said as she could feel her mouth fill with saliva as the smell hit her.

“Mmmmm, don't know that name. So guess it wasn’t him. Well, there you are.”

Tressa picked up the small wooden plate and grinned. “Thank you so much! Oh, I wanted to ask, have you ever seen a Petracole?”

“Hmmm, I guess the Bridge guard told ya?”

“Yes, sir.”

“Please just call me Liltin.”

“Yes sir—er—Liltin.”

“Hahaha, ye got some nice manners, lass. Must have been raised right, as most of my kind have grown prideful and angry,” he said, then made a face as he cringed. “Ah, though I could probably count myself among the prideful fools. Though I ain't no fool. Anyway, yer wanna know abou’ them pests?”

“Ah yes sir, lil… leelten,” she said, having trouble saying his name. Though she had learned the common tongue well, she wasn't quite good with names, as they often made sounds that were not common in elvish.

“Tell me, lass. Are you part of the hunters that just came in today?”

“Yes sir Leeltin.”

“As I said, just Liltin.”

Tressa nodded. “Yes, and I really want to know about the creatures.”

His face turned a little sour. “Miss, I'm sorry, but I cannot tell you, as I don't wish for the creatures to end up harmed.”

“I wish not to harm them. I just want to know.”

“Why?”

Tressa reached into her bag and pulled out one of her books, opening it to one of the pages of her drawings.

“I prefer to study the monsters and creatures of this world. I love learning and someday… someday maybe I can help them and the travelers. My people don't have studies on this creature, and I would love to bring it home and help with them learning. But I, well…” she trailed off.

“Ahaahahaha,” he laughed as she tilted her head in confusion. “Sorry lass. It's just not often when someone comes asking about them, and most of the time they're tryin’ to get rid of ‘em. So I find it funny that ye wanna learn about ‘em.”

“As you can see I do like to learn. So please I'd like to know.”

“Mind if I…?” He gestured to her book.

“Not at all,” she said and pushed it towards him.

He picked it up and looked at it. “Is this in Elvish?”

Tressa swallowed, realizing her mistake. “Y-Yes, sir.”

He took a closer look at her, and she knew there was no hiding the color of her hair under her hood at this point, nor her emerald green eyes.

But he let out a calm breath and spoke kindly.

“Worry not, lass. I don't mind as I am friends with a monster. I am surprised to see you beyond your borders. Not many would be too keen to be around you.”

Tressa nodded. “I mean yes. I didn't want to stay in Falidorn. I wanted to learn about the world that was beyond my home.”

“Alright, how about this, lass. I share my knowledge, and you share some of yours with me.”

She smiled. “Alright, deal.”

He smirked. “What do you want to know, lass?”

“Tressa.”

“Alright, Tressa. What can I tell ye about dem?”

“How big are they?” she asked, taking her book back and flipping to a blank page and pulling out her quill and ink.

“I'd say about this tall,” he said, holding his hand above the counter about a foot in height.

“So you say about six inches to a foot?”

“Yes. I reckon.”

She nodded, writing it down. “How about what they look like?”

“Hmm…” He crossed his arms and stroked his beard in thought. “I'd say they are of light greyish skin with sharp teeth and claws. Eye color can change on each individual. They tend to run around barefoot and have little claws. Let's see. Hmmm. Oh, and they have a love for fruit and sweet things. Though ye don't wanna give em too much or they become cruel. But anyway, they like to steal small articles of clothing like socks, gloves, laces, hats, and sometimes, on the rare occasion, undergarments,” he said.

Tressa frantically scribbled all of it down as fast as she could. “It would be easier if I could meet one,” she said. “Then I could sketch them out. It's kinda hard to get an idea when all I am given is a somewhat undetailed description.”

“Yer quite interested, aren't ya?”

“Yes Leeltin.”

“Hmmm, if ye can promise ya friends won't harm em, I can ask one to come and talk with ya.”

Tressa's head snapped up at that. “Really!”

“Aye, jus’ so long as they ain't harmed. Though ill warn ya if ye piss em off they tend to set things on fire. And most of the time its the clothing to the person who offended them.”

Tressa flinched at that, imagining being set on fire by a small creature that could use fire magic. She only had a few changes of clothes, only two of which were for travel, so it would be quite a lot of trouble. “I'll be careful.”

“Good. Now tell me, how old are ye?”

“Oh, I'm 195. I'll be 196 next month.”

“Wow, ye're young for your kind.”

“Yes, sir.”

“I figured ye was one based on yer accent. Though it's been almost a hundred years since I last saw one or met one of yer kind. I think it was a high elf. Hmmm, he was tall and very slim. I forget his name, but he was a strange one, much like ye. But I so remember him. He cursed his people’s new government, saying they’re repeating the same mistakes as the past. Said he had left to wander to see what truly this world had to offer.”

Tressa stopped writing and looked up, blinking. “Do you remember his name?”

Liltin tapped his chin. “Hmmm… hard to say. It was just the one day he was here.”

“Could it be Caelen?”

“Hard to say, sorry, lass. I take it you have an idea who it could be?”

“My uncle, perhaps… But I couldn’t say for sure. Honestly, I don’t remember him well, as he left when I was young.”

“Ha! Well, if he ever comes through again, I’ll be sure to remember his name. I never forget a face, after all. Not that I have many high elves coming through in the first place.”

Tressa took a bite of the Dotrin and let out a slight squeal. “Hmmm, this is so good! It's savory, a little salty, followed by the sour of the berries and topped off with the sweetness and tartness of the syrup and berries. The airy pastry makes the flavors pop out. I love the flatyness of the bread.”

Liltin stroked his beard. “Flatyess?”

“Yes the laters of the pastry,” she said pointing out the layers of the pastry.

“Ha you mean the flakyness.”

“Is that not what I said?”

“No, lass. And ye mean layers, not laters.”

Tressa pinched her lips together and let out an annoyed breath. “The common tongue is difficult.”

“When did ye start learning?”

Tressa tilted her head back and counted on her fingers. “I learned little before. I began studying more seriously six mons ago.”

“Mons? Mons? Oh, months!” he said, snapping his fingers.

“Yes.” She blushed. “Most learn it sooner, but um…”

“Skipped your classes, didn’t you?”

She grinned sheepishly. “I was more interested to learn other things.”

“Haha! Well, lass, if ye’d only been learning in earnest for six months, that's mighty impressive. Ye got one good head on yer shoulders.”

Tressa beamed. “Thank you. Briadon says I am still a long ways off whatever that means.”

He laughed at that. “Ah, ye will get there. With ye long life it won't be hard to learn the different expressions. Hmmm actually it might. Ye live long enough ye see lots of change around ye.”

“Liltin how old are you if I may be so bold as to ask?”

“Ah a fine question. I am—”

Tressa let out a yelp as another fight broke out between two drunk dwarves, with one of them punching the other with enough strength to knock him back and into her, which in turn caused her to fall from her chair and crash onto the floor, her Dotrin getting smashed under the dwarf. Her head hit against the wooden floor, and stars danced in her eyes, and she let out a groan of pain as she rubbed it.

“Owwww…” she groaned.

“Oy!” yelled Liltin, who hurried around the counter to her side.

“Ye dam bit’h ye stay out of me way,” the dwarf growled.

“Miss Tressa are ye alright?” Liltin asked, holding out a hand.

Tressa blinked several times until the stars were gone and looked up into the soft eyes of Liltin. His hand outstretched, she took it gratefully. He helped her up into a sitting position and that's when she realized the entire tavern had gone completely quiet.

The dwarf that had knocked her off her stool pulled a knife from his belt and growled. “High Elf!”
