Things were a mess, and that was putting it mildly. The walk through the forest had been uneventful for the most part, and they had made it through to the other side after following a dirt path for about fifteen minutes. On the other hand, though, that was where the problems started. A rebellion of some sort was in full swing, and no one had opted for a peaceful approach. People were dying in droves. A planted field separated the small group from the sloping road leading down to a keep surrounded by a tall stone wall, but even from a distance they had seen the incredible scope of people dying. Not just men either, but women as well. Thankfully, Hannah didn't see any children in the fight.

Waves of poorly armed, dirt-covered peasants threw themselves at the wall. Some carried ladders and erected them to climb up, but most of those had something scalding thrown over them on the way. Few people made it all the way up. Those who did lasted no more than seconds. Even as the group approached while keeping out of sight, they saw the attack break and pull back, then push back in, trampling those who had fallen in the previous wave. When that broke as well, those inside the wall made the genius decision to open the gate and rush out to continue the fighting, leading to even more death on both sides.

Hannah had wanted to stay out of it until the scene calmed down, but Nick thought they needed to get inside to get a better understanding of what was going on. With their abilities, going over the wall hadn't been a problem, and they'd snuck in through the less well-defended back. A guard still spotted them, and when Elijah tried talking him down, things went south fast. The soldier attacked, and the light from Elijah's parry was bright enough for every soldier in the keep to see. It was... a mess.

Once they made it into the keep, things turned from bad to worse. The King, Jarl, whatever, was in the middle of some demonic ritual, because of course he was, and Lemira almost died trying to talk down a distant cousin and convince him to return to whatever they call their world. That didn't go so well. Once the demon was dead, along with a good number of guards, the King tried to flee. Right in the middle of a step, he turned into a void being.

That particular void being was stronger than it had any right to be, and it took some doing putting it down. By the time Hannah returned to herself, the dead demon had turned as well, and Nick got caught in its void before it sprouted wings and flew off to the battlefield, where it started eating the dead.

Hannah shook her head in disbelief as they stood on the wall looking out across the vast sea of corpses in the gloom of twilight. "The others are in some trouble, too. They can't come just yet."

"Nick is in there," Vicky said, her voice now calm after the initial outburst inside the keep. "We have to get him out."

"It is consuming the dead," Lemira said, scratching at one of the horns pointing through her hair. "Disgusting creature."

"I would end its life, but I'm afraid my spears do not hurt it," Mateo said, showing remarkable growth. Not that long ago, he would have charged in no matter what.

"It'll have to be me," Hannah said, taking a step forward. "I'll deal with it."

A low thunder had been rolling in from the direction of the forest, but it was a familiar sound, and Hannah knew it wasn't thunder, nor was it Penny messing around. That was the sound of horse hooves, a lot of hooves. They broke through the last line of trees before she had made it down to the feeding void being and continued their charge toward it without stopping.

"What a mess," she said to herself as she descended the walls on tentacles of shadow. Having made The Deep Dweller her bitch, Hannah had gained far more control over her shadows. It still wasn't quite at the level of command as Curtis and his darkness, but the gap was shrinking. Running across the field of dead, she used her talent to create limbs that pushed off the ground, propelling her forward. If she didn't make it to the creature ahead before the horses, she worried it would just feed on them too and grow stronger. While she was still new to the whole thing of fighting things from the void, there was nothing wrong with her ability to sense Source and the fact that her opponent was in the process of extracting what little remained from the corpses. Each sliver empowered it, and if it got its tentacles on living people, or whatever they were, things would go south, further south.

The chance of Nick being alive in there somehow was slim to say the least, but Hannah kept running. She needed to try. The enemy needed to get dead, and it was Curtis's brother in there. Or maybe his remains, or nothing at all. The void spared nothing and no one.

Since she was closer and could cover distance better than most at that point, Hannah made it to the monster that looked like The Deep Dweller's sibling and launched her shadows at it, careful not to step into the pull of its void. Reaching in, she concentrated, giving it her all will-wise in an attempt to cut its Source with the first slice. A prolonged battle was the last thing she wanted. Reaching through the void, she felt Luke's presence again. She missed that stupid boy and his infuriating ability to throw himself at every problem without asking for help. Most of the time, he pulled through, but Hannah knew she would need to grow stronger if she wanted to keep up.

The void creature reacted the moment her shadows slithered into its void, and the response came long before she was able to reach the Source, that delicious Source. Hannah hadn't tried taking it into herself, of course, not after all the warnings, but it almost seemed to sing to her, as if it wanted to be part of her. Her contact at The Others had pretty much told her how Source was the answer to every problem in existence and that their quest to harness it was more important than even defeating the void, or prolonging its eventual and given win over The Greater System. There was a lot happening behind the curtains out there. Things were not as black and white as The Greater System versus the void, but was it ever? In that moment, she pushed all those sorts of thoughts away. They were not what she needed. Hannah needed focus to wield her will against the enemy. Thinking of war and splinter factions was not in her best interest, nor were her emotions for her dumbass boyfriend.

Hannah: "Luke?"

That didn't work, of course, but it felt like he was close. Closer still, the more she approached the void creature's Source. Was he in there? No, that couldn't be it.

A tentacle reached up and slammed into her from the side. She ignored it, thinking her Shadow Flesh skill would deal with the defense, like it always did. Not this time. The force of the blow was like being hit by a truck, and she crashed through the air and into a rider at the front of the charge, pushing him off the horse and into the blood-soaked mud.

Cursing under her breath over dealing with will-based attacks, Hannah got to her feet only to realize the damn horse rider had put a knife into her belly. This time, her body had turned to shadows to ignore the attack, but the sheer audacity of it pissed her off.

"What's wrong with you?" she shouted at the too-young man with light brown hair and big, scared eyes. Rather than answer, he drew his sword and pointed its point toward her. It trembled even as his allies rode past and continued on to the void creature. Her words of warning did nothing, so she charged after them, ignoring the sword even as it cut into her side.

"Go home!" she shouted at the young man. He dropped the sword and ran back toward the forest, screaming as he went.

The soldiers' weapons did no harm to the void creature whatsoever, but that didn't stop them from trying. This led to a bunch of them being eaten, horse and all, before Hannah got back into the thick of it. During the brief respite from her attacks, the void creature devoured enough that its Source channels thickened by a noticeable degree. More Source meant more strength, but she wouldn't be deterred.

The sense of Luke's presence grew the more she struggled to reach into the creature, but she ignored it this time and used that attention to push the other attackers out of the creature's range. Dividing her attention in that manner made it much more difficult to keep her form intact. She had been doing it for so long now that it had become second nature, but if she let go, she would turn into a writhing mass of shadows resembling the void creatures themselves, the form of The Deep Dweller. Hannah was no longer herself, but she still felt like she was, and maintaining her old appearance kept her grounded and connected to the others. She doubted Luke would want to be close with her if she sprouted tentacles full of eyes and mouths. She doubted anyone would.

A few things became clear in the struggle. One, the inhabitants of this dungeon had no to little survival instinct. No matter how much Hannah tried, they just kept throwing themselves into the void creature, feeding themselves to its growing strength. Two, Nick was not in there. Curtis's brother wasn't hanging on somehow. He was dead. Three, she wanted some of that Source for herself. If and how that was possible, she didn't know, but she had to find out. Being this close to it lit an ache inside her, a need she hadn't known was there. It felt like being in withdrawal, something she knew more of than she wanted to admit, even to herself. Only, she hadn't been taking Source. So, why?

The answer had been staring her in the face this whole time, and she felt stupid for not seeing it earlier. The Deep Dweller. It all came back to that monster. It had been of the void, so of course it had tasted Source. Now, being close to it for the first time, that withering ember reignited something fierce. Hannah was no stranger to the struggle of staying away from things that were no good for you, either, and she wasn't about to grab a taste. Lingering addiction to the stuff or not, Hannah could keep her distance. She knew she could. Though when she reached in and cut at the Source channels, it took everything in her not to have just the tiniest taste for herself.

Hannah: "We're okay here for the moment. I finished the void creature. Nick didn't make it."

Penny: "I'll tell Curtis. Meet back by where the portal was?"

Hannah: "Yeah."

The ground quaked beneath her feet without warning. In the distance, a clutch of birds rose from the trees with cries of indignation. Something about the dungeon shifted, and all light disappeared.

Penny: "So, uh. Did you just go blind too?"

Just as she had figured, they were far from done with this dungeon, or perhaps it was far from finished with them.
