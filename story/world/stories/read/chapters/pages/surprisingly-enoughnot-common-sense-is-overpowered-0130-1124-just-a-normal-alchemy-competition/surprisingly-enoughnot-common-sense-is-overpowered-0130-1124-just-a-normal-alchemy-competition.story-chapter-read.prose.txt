A fraction more heat could alter the rate at which an ingredient released its essence, while introducing the same medicine a breath earlier meant exposing it to an entirely different environment. More. More. More.

Countless repetitions had drilled the necessary movements into his body, while Know Thyself had helped him eliminate the tiny inconsistencies that once plagued his refinements. What had previously required careful thought had gradually become instinct.

Let instinct guide my hands, knowledge guide my judgment, and perception illuminate the path ahead.

One adjustment flowed into the next, each movement following the patterns he'd spent months committing to memory. The competition grounds gradually faded from his awareness, taking the spectators, rankings, and everything else with them.

For the first time, Ning felt as though he was moving alongside the refinement, anticipating its changes instead of constantly struggling to catch up.

"...Fun."

The refinement continued changing beneath his perception, and Ning continued with it. Every adjustment demanded another decision, every decision carried consequences, and the strain on his mind only grew as the Infinite Circle slowly emptied.

Somehow, despite all of it, he was having the time of his life.

Ning had originally begun cultivating because he wanted to survive, which had seemed reason enough at the time.

Somewhere along the way, however, survival had stopped being the whole reason.

When the medicinal structure finally stabilized enough to accept the last ingredient, Ning introduced the Spirit-Locking herb, allowing its essence to spread through the mixture.

There was simply too much in this world that he wanted to experience.

He wanted to know what existed beyond Foundation Building and the realms he'd only heard mentioned in passing. He wanted to see what Pure Eyes would become after another stage, what Fenghuang would look like when the little bastard finally grew too large to sit on his shoulder, and whether the Infinite Circle could someday turn without stopping.

He wanted to take this strange alchemical method he'd pieced together through obsessive repetition and far too many ruined ingredients and see what it might become after another year, another decade, or perhaps even another century.

There were places he wanted to visit, techniques he wanted to learn, and experiments he wanted to try, even if half his brilliant ideas turned out to be stupid and the occasional stupid one turned out to be brilliant.

Ning wanted to see just how far this ridiculous second life could take him.

His first life had taught him something that he hadn't needed cultivation to understand. The end did not always send a warning first. There had been no grand announcement before his first death, no convenient countdown giving him time to finish everything he wanted to do.

Perhaps the same would be true this time.

He might die tomorrow. He might instead cultivate for ten thousand years and become some ancient bastard who thought a century was an annoyingly short amount of time.

Ning strongly preferred the second option.

Unfortunately, longevity wasn't something he could decide simply because he wanted it. For now, all he could do was keep going.

Another tremor passed through the pill, and Ning's fingers shifted as Crimson Flame tightened around the medicinal structure. His breathing had grown heavier now. Fenghuang stood beside him with his wings slightly spread, White Flame flickering weakly between his exhausted feathers, while the brilliant blue radiance filling Ning's eyes had already begun to dim.

The Infinite Circle was nearly empty, and with it went the extraordinary clarity that had carried him this far. The smallest fluctuations in temperature and spiritual density gradually blurred as Pure Eyes returned toward its ordinary limits.

Ning didn't try to cling to the disappearing details. The enhanced perception had served its purpose, and although it was fading, the experience he'd accumulated through countless refinements remained.

The Infinite Circle completed its final rotation, exhausting the accumulated qi within it, while Fenghuang's White Flame flickered once before withdrawing from the furnace.

Ning was left with his own qi, his own hands, and everything he'd learned before today.

It would have to be enough.

The forming pill shuddered as the remaining medicinal essences collapsed inward, threatening to tear apart the structure Ning had struggled so hard to preserve. He tightened Crimson Flame around it, guiding the final stages of condensation while his spiritual sense strained to maintain control.

His face had gone pale, sweat ran down his temples, and his dantian felt painfully empty.

Still, his hands remained steady.

Ning thought back to what he'd told the elder before beginning.

I may fail today.

He'd meant those words, of course, although he'd also been perfectly aware of the hundreds of people listening and had shamelessly harvested whatever social points he could.

But now, with his attention fixed entirely on the furnace, the rest of his declaration returned to him.

I would rather fail while moving forward than stop simply because success isn't guaranteed.

Ning almost laughed. It seemed his social fu had accidentally produced something he genuinely believed.

He couldn't guarantee success, and there might never be another opportunity to try. But he was here now, and he intended to give everything he had left.

The last instability began to settle.

Ning sensed the change through his spiritual sense. The scattered medicinal currents drew inward one after another, the Spirit-Locking herb binding them into the forming pill while Crimson Flame nourished the structure from every direction.

His breathing gradually slowed.

Within the furnace, the final medicinal current settled into place.

Ning's hand descended, completing the final seal.

For a heartbeat, everything went still.

Then a clear note rang from within the furnace.

Ning let out a slow breath.

The bronze lid shuddered as a thread of spiritual light escaped through the seam, followed by another. Heat rolled outward across Station Seventeen, stirring Ning's robes and ruffling Fenghuang's exhausted feathers as the remaining Crimson Flame curled upward.

Ning raised his hand, and the furnace opened.

Fire spilled toward the sky.

From within the fading flames, a single pill rose into the air, its surface catching the light as wisps of medicinal fragrance spread around it.

Ning watched it rise.

His face was pale, his eyes still burned, and there was barely enough qi left in his body for him to complain properly about either.

Ning smiled anyway.

He didn't know whether this life would last another day, another century, or ten thousand years.

That had never truly been his choice.

How he lived it was.

And whenever the end finally came, Ning wanted to meet it the same way he stood before the furnace now—exhausted, perhaps a little battered, having given everything he could...

and still able to smile.
