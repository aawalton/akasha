“Grace!”

Mom’s shout seemed to echo through the house, and for a moment, I debated not answering her. My back pressed further against the wall of my bedroom as my eyes found the window again. The urge to throw myself towards it and just see what happened, see if I could find a way out through it, gripped me, and I almost took a step forward before my gaze flicked towards Rex.

I couldn’t leave him there, but I couldn’t take him with me. I wouldn’t be able to get down to the ground safely, and I couldn’t risk hurting him either. He was still recovering. The fur on his back was still growing back from where it had been shaved after he got caught in the fence.

And even if we did get out, where would we go? My first thought was Duncan’s house. I would go to Phoebe’s, but her house was further away, and my mom was sure to come looking for me there. She didn’t know I was still friends with Duncan, that we’d started dating, so she probably wouldn’t think of it, and if I managed to get into the field behind my house without being seen, I could sneak out into one of the paths further along. There was a good chance I could get all the way to his without being caught.

But they might still come looking for me, I realised. Mom might not have known about Duncan, but that didn’t mean whoever was downstairs didn’t know about him. I’d been sneaking off to his regularly since I got back from Scotland, and someone could have been watching me.

I was pretty sure that they hadn’t been, though. I’d been careful, and I hadn’t seen anyone, but there was no way to know for sure. Or perhaps they’d hacked my phone. Being at the Academy had taught me that it was possible. They could have been reading all of my messages, have spent the last week or so tracking me, and if so, I had no doubt they knew all about Duncan and me. It would probably be one of the first places they looked.

And if I did that, if Rex and I managed to get away somehow, what would happen to my mom? I still didn’t know for certain who the person downstairs was, what they wanted from me, but if they were part of the group who attacked the Academy, they’d kill her. Maybe they wouldn’t even try to use her to tempt me home. Perhaps they would just murder her and make it look like I was the one who did it.

Then no one would take me in. The police would be looking for me. They would do the dirty work and hunt me down, and the group, whoever they were, could just swoop in at the last minute and capture me. Perhaps they’d take me right out of the jail cell and whisk me away to… wherever.

My eyes found Rex again, panic fluttering in my chest, but he just stared back at me. I didn’t know what to do, but I had to answer my mom.

“Yes?” I called back, trying to keep the anxiety out of my voice.

I wasn’t particularly successful. My fear was audible. Even I could hear the tremor in my word.

Even from my room, I could hear the scoff that came from Mom.

“Don’t just screech at me like a banshee,” she snapped. “Come here and talk to me.”

My eyes flitted towards the window again, and I gave it one last longing look before squeezing my hands into fists and peeling myself away from the wall. I caught sight of my reflection as I turned, and I couldn’t help but grimace. The panic in my wide eyes was obvious, and my face was pale. I was always pretty pale, but it looked unnatural. Unhealthy.

It appeared as though all of the blood had drained out of my face, and that was exactly how I felt, but I knew I couldn’t go and talk to my mom like that. She’d take one look at me and know something was wrong.

Swallowing hard to force down the desperation fluttering in my chest and trying to escape, I pinched my cheeks hard, trying to bring some colour back to them. It worked a little. Well enough for me to leave my room, at least, I decided as I stepped towards my door before faltering. I glanced back at Rex, who had pressed close to my legs, hesitation coursing through me as I tried to work out what to do.

“You’re going to have to stay here. Okay, boy?” I whispered, turning back towards him.

Rex stared up at me, his round eyes remaining on my face as I started to shut the door between us, before letting out a whimper. The sound was like a dagger to my heart, and I wished I didn’t have to shut him in my room, but I was too scared.

It wasn’t safe to bring him with. I didn’t know whether my mom just wanted to speak to me or if she was going to make me go down to speak with the woman too, but if she did, I was worried about how Rex would act. He’d barely stopped growling since that woman had knocked on the door, and the fear that he might snap at her or something to defend me had gripped me. If he did that and we survived whatever was about to happen, Mom might decide he was unsafe, that he had to be put down, and I couldn’t bear that.

“I’m sorry,” I told him as I pulled the door closed.

Another whimper came from Rex, that one just as painful as the first, and I felt tears burn behind my eyes. It was for his own good, though. His safety, I told myself, but that didn’t make it any easier.

I forced myself to turn away from the door, to begin walking away, but a weak scratching noise came from my room, and I almost turned back. I could hear Rex pawing at the door, and each time, the ache in my heart grew stronger.

By the time I reached the top of the stairs, my knees were weak. Fear, pain, and anxiety thrummed in my heart with each beat, filling me with frantic energy, and I had to reach for the bannister to stop myself from falling, but to my relief, Mom looked unharmed.

An irritated expression twisted her face as she stood halfway up the stairs, watching me expectantly, and I tried to smile at her, but I’m not sure how convincing it was.

“That’s better, isn’t it?” Mom remarked, her tone snide.

“Sorry,” I muttered.

I couldn’t stop myself from scrutinising her, scanning her body for any sign of injury that I might have missed at first, but I still couldn’t see anything. As far as I could tell, she was completely fine.

“There’s someone here who wants to speak to you,” she announced before lowering her voice and hissing, “This is important, so be polite, and don’t ask any annoying questions.”

Her voice was loud enough that I had no doubt the woman would have still been able to hear her, and I wanted nothing more than to turn and run. I didn’t want to face the woman, regardless of who she was. I just wanted to retreat to my room with Rex, who I could still hear whimpering softly and scratching at the door, but I knew that wasn’t an option. The woman would probably just kill my mom and then kick my door down if I did.

I swallowed and nodded silently, unable to form words. Mom sent me one last warning look before turning and stomping back down the stairs, but I stood there for a moment longer. My legs were refusing to work. My feet felt as if they’d been superglued to the floor, and it took almost all of my energy to rip my foot away and begin to descend.

My hand clung to the bannister as I descended, slowly following my mom. I knew that if I let go, if I loosened my grip, I would go tumbling down. It was the only thing keeping me upright, and I needed it. My knees were not strong enough to hold me.

And I was hyperventilating, I realised. My breaths were little more than shallow gasps, and I tried to slow them. I inhaled as deeply as I could, holding it for a few seconds before letting it out, but my next inhale was just as ragged as before.

The downstairs hall came into view, and I looked around quickly. My gaze darted around the room, searching for anything that seemed obviously out of place or concerning, before finding the front door. I expected to spot the hulking shadow of someone standing on the other side of the frosted glass, ready to catch me should I run, but I saw nothing. The sun shone through uninterrupted, but that did not make me feel any better.

My feet touched the ice-cold floor. I squeezed my clammy hands into fists again and fell still, taking a moment to breathe deeply before walking forward into the lounge.

“Look who’s finally here,” my mom said, her tone sarcastic. “Olivia, this is my daughter Grace.”

The dark-haired woman smiled widely as she stood, and I examined her face. I was trying to work out whether I recognised her, if she had been part of the group who had stormed the Academy, but I wasn’t sure. Most of those people had been wearing masks. I only saw a few of their faces, and I didn’t think I’d seen her there, but I couldn’t say for sure.

Mom looked at me, her expression somewhere between expectant and angry. I could tell she wanted me to speak, to greet the woman, but I was too terrified. My voice had yet to return, and all I could do was stare at her.

“It’s lovely to meet you, Grace. Although, I must confess, I’ve read so much about you that I feel as though I already know you!” she told me, her tone far warmer than I expected. “My name’s Olivia.”

She held her hand out, but the sudden movement just made me flinch. Olivia’s smile slipped slightly, and her outstretched hand faltered.

If she were not with the group who had brutally slaughtered everyone at the Academy, I must look insane, I realised. She just wanted to shake my hand. She wasn’t attacking me, and I’d overreacted. I needed to do something, to fix it.

Forcing myself to step forward, I forced a smile onto my lips and took her hand. It felt cold in my own, and it took me a moment to figure out that probably meant my hand was really warm. It felt clammy too. It must have been horrible to shake, but to Olivia’s credit, she didn’t immediately wipe it on her dark suit trousers.

“It’s nice to meet you,” I told her, my voice sounding weak and high-pitched.

Olivia’s smile returned as she took a seat in my dad’s usual armchair again and gestured towards the sofa where my mom sat. She clearly expected me to join her, and I couldn’t help but hesitate.

I didn’t want to sit down. That felt dangerous. If I did, it would be harder for me to run if I needed to, but I couldn’t just stand there and refuse. That would be weird, and I could already see my mom’s expression growing more and more stormy. If I didn’t join her soon, she’d explode, and I didn’t want that to happen with the woman still there. Regardless of who she was, the thought of her seeing it made my stomach tighten. I didn’t want anyone to witness my mom acting like that.

Edging around the coffee table as quickly as I could, I moved towards the sofa, but I wasn’t paying enough attention. I wasn’t sure how it happened, how I misjudged the distance, but my shin slammed into the corner of the table, and my eyes snapped to the empty wine bottle sitting on top. It wobbled threateningly for a second before falling still, and relief crashed into me as I sank onto the sofa and perched awkwardly beside my mom.

“So clumsy!” she remarked, sending me a furious look before letting out a high-pitched and clearly fake laugh. “You’d never know she’d done years of dance lessons! She can be quite graceful at times!”

That was as close as my mom ever came to a compliment, but I knew it was just for Olivia. Mom was trying to make it seem as though I was not as uncoordinated as I’d just made myself appear, and it wasn’t entirely a lie. I had done some dance lessons as a kid. Mom had put me in ballet and tap classes from the moment I could walk, but that was a long time ago.

“Oh no!” the woman cried. “That sounded painful. Are you okay?”

I looked at her uncertainly.

“Yeah, fine,” I said. “It sounded worse than it felt.”

That wasn’t entirely a lie. With the adrenaline racing through my body, I’d barely felt it. I was too on edge, too panicked, to feel any pain, and for some reason, the way the woman was acting just made me more suspicious.

Her tone had sounded genuine, and her expression was appropriately concerned. Everything about her reaction seemed… normal, but it was a little too much. It was as if she were a robot, giving the most perfect and expected responses, and I didn’t trust her. She seemed fake.

“Okay, good,” she told me with another wide smile. “Well… I guess you’re probably wondering why I’m here?”

Olivia’s eyebrows rose. She was clearly waiting for a response from me, and I hesitated, scanning the room out of the corner of my eyes before speaking again.

“Umm… yeah?”

Actually, I’d been wondering whether she was about to kill my mom and me, but I guess that fell under the umbrella of why is she here.

“Well, I’ve already explained it all to your mother, but…” Olivia paused dramatically, “I’m here to offer you a scholarship to one of the oldest selective schools in the country, the Sir Thomas Dean Grammar School!”

She reached down, grabbing something from the briefcase that was leaning against the armchair before holding it out to me, and I stared at her blankly for a moment. Something pulled at me as I took it from her and looked down at the glossy paper without taking anything in. A sensation, a memory almost, tugged at the back of my mind.

“A scholarship,” I heard myself repeat. “But I didn’t apply for one.”

Deja vu.

I’d been in that situation before, I realised. It felt similar, painfully similar, to when Ms Brice had come to my house and taken me to the Academy in the other world. Olivia even kind of looked like her, but I knew she was not. That woman was not Ms Brice, and I did not trust her.

“Grace!” my mom snapped. “Don’t be so ungrateful!”

But Olivia just laughed.

“Don’t worry! We actually get that a lot,” she told us. “Our scholarships don’t work the same way as most other schools. Normally, students or their parents apply for them, but at Sir Thomas’, all applications must come from a teacher.”

“I see,” Mom said slowly. “And who put Grace forward?”

My eyes darted to Olivia again. I wanted to know the answer to that. If she said the name of a teacher who wasn’t at my school, then I knew it was a trick.

“Grace’s came from… a Mr Anthony Ray.”

I felt my eyebrows draw together as confusion slammed into me. Mr Ray? He was one of my favourite teachers, but could he be part of that? Could he somehow be connected to the woman sitting before me, to the group who’d hunted me down? I couldn’t believe that. There was no way he could. He was too kind, too good.

They probably just went on the school website. I was pretty sure that all of the teachers’ names were on there, and we only had two religious studies teachers. They probably assumed I’d been taught by him at some point, so it was an easy choice.

“Mr Ray,” Mom repeated, looking at me in confusion. “You don’t have a teacher called that, do you?”

“Yeah, the RS teacher,” I told her, but she just stared at me blankly. “You met him at parents’ evening last year. He told you I should do RS at A Levels, but you said it was pointless. Remember?”

There was no spark of recognition in Mom’s slightly unfocused eyes as she continued to look at me for a few more seconds before turning back to Olivia.

“Well, regardless, we’re more than happy and perfectly capable of paying for Grace’s tuition. As much as we appreciate the… offer,” she said, her disgusted tone at odds with her words, “Grace is not a charity case.”

Mom seemed to have forgotten about not being ungrateful, and I could tell she’d taken the scholarship as an insult. She hated the thought of someone thinking we might be struggling, that we may be in need of any kind of support or help.

She always had. I could still remember how furious she’d gotten when someone had offered to pay for her coffee at Starbucks a couple of years ago. She’d called them rude and conceited, said it was disrespectful to even offer, and even when he said he was doing it for loads of people, that it was for a video or something, Mom had still continued to chew him out. It was mortifying, and I hoped it wouldn’t happen again.

“Oh, no, no,” Olivia said quickly, waving a hand reassuringly. “All of our students have scholarships! We only take on pupils who meet certain criteria, and due to our very generous board members and alumni, all tuition fees are entirely covered by the scholarships.”

Out of the corner of my eye, I saw Mom’s body stiffen, and I glanced across at her as her face slowly lit up.

“So it’s a private school?” Mom asked, her excitement barely contained.

“It is, yes.”

My heart sank. Suddenly, I knew my chances of finding a way out of going to the school were practically zero. My mom would force me to go there, and it didn’t matter how suspicious it seemed. Even if it were clearly a front for the people who had hunted me in the other worlds, she would send me there. She had always wanted me to go to a private school. It was prestigious, she’d told me. I’d be able to make good connections, to find a better husband if I went to one. Everything would just be better and easier for me.

She and Dad had fought about that when I entered secondary school. I’d managed to get into the grammar school in town, but Mom still argued that the other school was better. Eventually, Dad managed to convince her that the grammar school would be better for me, and Mom came around to the idea, but I think it was mostly so she could tell her parents that I passed the test and got in, unlike my cousin, who was three marks short.

“And you said there’s an entrance criteria?” Mom asked, her eyes still alight with excitement.

“Yes. They’re extremely high,” Olivia told her, sending me a smile.

Confusion flitted across Mom’s face, and she glanced at me.

“And you think… Grace meets that?”

“Why of course!” Olivia replied with a laugh, as if Mom’s question was nothing more than a joke. “In fact, she actually exceeded all of the requirements. She’s exceptional.”

Mom’s expression just became more confused, and she looked across at me. I could tell she couldn’t believe that anyone might think I was exceptional, and I felt the same way. It was a trap. It had to be a trap. The way the woman was acting was too perfect. Everything about the school sounded too ideal. It was exactly what my mom would have fallen for, and they must have known that. It was deliberate, calculated, and I was scared.

“Grace is?” Mom asked.

“Yes!”

“And you definitely have the right Grace? There’s another in her year,” Mom told Olivia. “This is Grace Barker. B-A-R-K-E-R. Are you sure that’s the Grace you’re looking for?”

Olivia’s gaze found me, and something flitted across her eyes, making my blood turn to ice.

“I can assure you, we definitely have the right person,” she said, holding my gaze for a few more seconds before looking back at my mom. “We’re aware there’s another Grace in her year. Grace Evans, right?”

I swallowed down my rising panic and nodded mutely at the woman. She knew her name. How? Did she know the name of every person in my class? I wasn’t sure, but the idea of it terrified me. It meant they knew Phoebe’s name, and Duncan’s too. I wasn’t sure if they knew we were friends, that I was dating Duncan, but even the thought of Olivia knowing that they existed felt too dangerous.

Looking down quickly to hide the fear that I knew must have appeared on my face, I stared at the brochure. It had been handed to me so long ago, and I hadn’t even looked at it yet. I’d been too absorbed by the conversation, but I scanned the image on the shiny front cover quickly.

Pain speared my heart. The building on the front was familiar, and I couldn’t help but let my eyes trace the red brick as agony radiated through my chest. It was different, I told myself. The shape of the building was different to the one I recalled. It was taller, and there was less space around it, less greenery.

Still, it looked far too similar to the Academy for me to believe it was nothing more than a coincidence.
