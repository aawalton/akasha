Grimfalk laughed as he watched Charlemagne slam into what appeared to be an invisible wall just before his beak drilled into the cowering human dressed as a park ranger.

“What was that?” Longclaw asked, looking up from her phone for the first time in a while.

“Oh, Charlemagne’s just discovered his first safe zone.  He just tried to eat the System-generated guide, but from what I can tell, there’s a warning that attacking peaceful monsters in a safe zone is a really bad idea.  Even Sungay, my Champion in the Pacific, stopped trying to attack after seeing the message, and he’s about as smart as a board.”

Longclaw put her phone down and looked up at the screen showing the young Beninese rooster’s exploits.

“Do you think he’ll eat the guide anyways?  He’s rather…err…human aggressive.  Probably due to the brain damage he received from fighting the army.”

Grimfalk waved one of his claws in a dismissive fashion.

“Naw, he’ll be all right.  He’s been doing his best to be on good behavior.  I think he’s looking for a safe place for all his hens to hang out.  Which is actually the most selfless behavior I’ve yet to see from any of my Champions, to be honest.”

“I think you’re looking at it from the wrong perspective,” the other theropod said as she shook her head.  “It’s one hundred percent selfish.  He’s only protecting them so that he can have more children.  Don’t forget he will gain power from having kids.  I wonder what sort of a boost he’ll get.”

“It was a super expensive ability,” Grimfalk complained, ignoring the first half of what his companion had said.  “The boost had better be good.  How long before his chicks start to hatch?”

Longclaw frowned.

“Well, it’s hard to say.  Chicken eggs hatch at around twenty-one days but remember that he’s no longer an actual chicken.  His species now displays as ‘R. Gallus Baronia’.  I have no idea what the ‘R.’ stands for, but ‘Baronia’ seems like some poorly translated Latin, an old human language that was used extensively in works of fantasy.  Literally it refers to a territory ruled by a noble.  The point here is that I simply don’t know what species his chicks will be and how long they will take to hatch.  The System really did a number on biology.”

“How much would it be to query the System to ask?  I mean, I’m probably not going to pay for that information, but it would be good to know how valuable it is,” Grimfalk replied.

“That’s a good question,” Longclaw nodded.  “If the information is cheap, it likely means that there’s not much variation.  If it’s expensive though…that could mean that the incubation period is very different, or that there is some other requirements for the eggs to hatch.  Like maybe they would need a lower temperature since chickens are naturally super warm, or something.  Hold on…I’ve got it here.”

The theropod whistled as she read the price tag.

“Huh,” Grimfalk said as he leaned back in his seat to continue watching the show.  “If we’re right, twenty-one days is nowhere near the correct amount of time.  Oh, hey, check out Ricardo!”

Charlemagne lunged toward the park ranger, intent on cutting him down quickly so he could look for someone less human to help him.  But just as his beak was about to slam into the man’s unguarded and squishy neck, it was stopped short by an invisible force.  The impact sent stinging vibrations throughout the young rooster’s body, annoying him.  He was so angry that he almost didn’t notice that the squiggles held a new message for him.

Warning!  You have attempted to attack a System-generated guide within a safe zone.  While you are free to do so, please note that the following consequences will be immediate and permanent.  You will be instantly recognized as a hostile creature to all occupants of any safe zone you enter, and occupants of that zone will be able to sense your location.  Any creature who kills you will receive 500% additional combat experience and will not receive any penalties related to the assault, as it will be considered self-defense.  This will be your only warning of this nature.

Note: System-generated guides may be Identified by any creature, regardless of whether they possess the skill.

The rooster was already lining up a second peck when the full impact of the warning became clear to him.  If he killed this fake human, then he would never be able to convince other animals to help him.  He would fail in his mission and have to return to the hens empty-clawed.  That was unacceptable.

The lizard brain portion of the young rooster’s mind nevertheless argued that Charlemagne should attack, and quickly.  Thankfully, the squiggles had helpfully included the fact that the guide was not, in fact, a real human.  This allowed him to convince his more feral instincts to lay low.

There was a long pause as Charlemagne contemplated his next move.  He had clearly frightened the fake human, so should he try to unfrighten it?  Was that possible?

As the rooster wondered how to approach this particularly thorny social dilemma, the problem thankfully resolved itself.  The fake human suddenly stopped its cowering and stood upright, a broad smile on its face.

“Ahoy, there, you must be new to the area.  Wow, I’ve never seen a rooster quite like you before, can you talk?”

Charlemagne took several steps back, confused as to the sudden change in the simulated creature’s behavior.  But the friendly manners of the guide helped him to remember his main task here: the rooster was looking for help.  He opened his beak and then shut it again, trying to come up with a succinct explanation of his needs.

“Bawark,” he finally said.

“Ah, of course, my good creature.  I can help arrange all manner of help for you, as long as you have something to trade, that is.”

“Bawk,” suggested the young rooster.

“Ah, eggs, you say?” the guide nodded encouragingly.  “Yes, yes, that might just do nicely.  Follow me, and I’ll introduce you to someone who might be able to help.”

The fake human turned around and began marching with exaggerated arm movements toward the forest.  As Charlemagne followed, he noticed that the air up ahead was shimmering, like the pavement sometimes did on a particularly hot day.  The effect intensified as they drew closer, causing his head to ache, until suddenly the shimmering colors resolved themselves in an entire city, with most of the buildings on the ground but some built on platforms set high above the ground.  The entire area smelled of tree sap, roasted corn, and animals.  Charlemagne had never smelled so many different kinds of animals all in one place.

“Right this way, I’ll take you to see the mayor,” the guide said, “her name is Sidney from Sydney, now isn’t that a hoot?”

Charlemagne followed through the throng of lizards, pigs, cows, sheep, goats, birds, and other various creatures, ignoring the stares.  Every so often, he felt a tingling sensation, which was usually followed by a gasp.  He ignored those too.

The mayor’s hut was slightly larger than most of the other edifices, which wasn’t really saying much.  On one side of the door stood the largest rat that the young rooster had ever seen, holding a poleaxe.  The other side was occupied by an unassuming goat.  The pair stiffened as the guide approached and knocked on the door.

“Come in!” a voice called.

The fake human pushed the door open before vanishing entirely from sight.  Charlemagne immediately whirled around, expecting to receive a sneak attack from behind, but there was nothing there.  A high-pitched laugh from behind him caused him to turn again.  Sitting inside the hut, behind a tiny desk that looked very out of place in the simple hut, was a bunny rabbit.

“Howdya do?  I’m Sidney.  Did the guide mention that I’m originally from Sydney?  I have been trying to get him to stop doing that, but…well, we work with the tools that Lepo has given us.  Come in, come in!  I’d tell you to take a seat, but I don’t think that chickens sit, right?  That’s all right.  We’ll get you sorted out nonetheless.  You want some tea?”

The rapid-fire speech confused the simple rooster, and he stood in silence, feeling uncharacteristically shy.

“Bawk,” he finally asked.

“Ah, you’ve come the right place, haven’t you?  If you need help or even just advice, I’m your lady.  Helps me to level up, at any rate.  So, whatcha need?”

“Bawak.  Bawk.  Bawwk,” Charlemagne expounded.

“All right, so you’ve got a whole lot of hens that lay eggs, and you’re looking for somewhere they can live and get fed, but they are currently a long way south in another city.  They are living on a farm and will eventually run out of food.  Is that about it?”

“Bawk!” the young rooster confirmed, happy that he was understood.

“How many hens are you talking about?  Is it more than one hundred?”

Charlemagne froze.  He didn’t know how to count that high.

“All right, settle down,” Sidney added after a moment.  “Try to relax and think of all the hens together in a group.  I’ll see if I can’t get an idea from your thoughts.”

The young rooster pictured the enormous factory floor, with its rows upon rows of cackling, cawing hens sitting side by side in their crates.

“I am sorry, really I am.  But I can’t help ya, mate,” the rabbit interrupted Charlemagne’s happy thoughts.  “Couldn’t possibly accommodate that many chickens.  Why, you’ve got five times as many chickens as I have people residing in this town here.  You should be able to keep yourselves safe if you just invest in security a bit.  Is there some group that’s currently providing security for the hens now?”

“Bawk,” the devolved rooster confirmed.

“How many?”

Charlemagne was still processing the rabbit’s rejection, but at least he knew this number.

“Bawwak.”

“Only five combatants among all those hens?  That’s not going to work at all.  Here in my town I’ve asked for a ratio of ten to one…that’s one combat class for every ten non-combat classes.  Your ratio is…is…well it’s a lot lower than that!  Do you understand?”

“Bawk,” came the ready reply.

“That’s fantastic,” Sidney said with what appeared to be genuine enthusiasm.  “Now, the real advice…do you see the cucumber in the cage there?”

Charlemagne stared intently at the indicated cucumber, which was sitting on an intricately carved chest of drawers standing against the back wall of the hut.  Dense mana swirled around and through the delicious-looking vegetable.  It was clearly no ordinary cucumber.

“If you have Identify, use it,” the rabbit urged.

The rooster complied.

Settlement Token: Lesser (Bound)

This item allows its owner to create a System-recognized Settlement when it is placed in a visible location and activated by its owner.  Only those allowed entry by the Token’s owner may access the Settlement without overcoming the owner’s defense of choice.  Additionally, the owner of the token gains access to a Settlement management panel, where they may create a custom set of Settlement rules or select from a premade list.

“You just need to find your own Settlement Token,” Sidney explained.  “I’ve heard that some Special Areas give them out as Quest rewards and you can also occasionally find them out in the open.  I bet you could find one in just a few weeks, if you really try.  Actually, scratch that.  You look really fast…I bet you’d find one in a week flat.  Oh yeah, there’s one other way to get a Token.  The System guide told me that there is a class archetype called ‘Administrator’ that can create their own Settlement through a Special Ability.”

The rooster spent a few moments examining the vegetable before asking another question.

“Bawwk.”

“Well, it can do a lot of other things, too.  With the right materials, I can build lots of different structures, even things like farms.  With just a few farmers, the city can grow enough food for all five thousand inhabitants and still have plenty left over to trade.  In fact, that’s our primary business here: our growing cycle is about two days, so right now we’re opening our markets three times a week and trading for other things that will help the city either grow or defend itself.  Why, just the other day…”

As Sidney explained what the cucumber did, an idea formed in his mind.  He pounced without hesitation, his powerful beak making short work of the unsuspecting rabbit.  The squiggles wrote him an angry message, but he was in too big a hurry to read it.  A second later his mana-reinforced face slammed into the cage that held the tantalizing cucumber, the metal denting just enough for him to get his beak around the vegetable.

“What are you doing!?” a voice from behind him cried.  It was the rat, who was trying to swing his poleaxe in the narrow confines of the hut.  The side of the blade slammed into the devolved rooster’s head, which in turn caused the magical cucumber to slide right down the bird’s throat and into his stomach.
