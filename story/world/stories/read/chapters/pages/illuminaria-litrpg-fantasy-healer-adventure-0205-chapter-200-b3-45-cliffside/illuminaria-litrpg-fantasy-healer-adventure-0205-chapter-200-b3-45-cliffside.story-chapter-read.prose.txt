Chapter 200 / B3-45: Cliffside

They held hands the whole way back to the Abaaka House. Wen had made her goodbyes at the great hall. For once, the pair did not amble their way through the city. They wanted to be alone, sooner rather than later.

Their one stop was at the Catstaff’s apartment to collect Mojo, only to find that they didn’t need to. Ord Catstaff led them quietly to the door of his grandson’s room, where Joe saw the young spiritualist hugging the shug monkey to his chest, both peacefully asleep, curled up together. Knowing Mojo could easily find his way upstairs in the morning, they left the broodling with Mahq and his kindly grandparents.

Inside Joe’s apartment, the pair kissed. Both were torn between savoring this moment and their desire for each other. Tenderness won out, for the moment. Holding hands, they moved toward the couch. Kenda sat and began to uncoil her braids while Joe placed a [Halefire] into the fireplace for atmosphere.

“Here. Let me,” he asked, slipping onto the sofa behind her.

He moved her hands out of the way and began to unloop her long russet tresses. In the process, he brushed the back of his hand across the gorgeous neck that had been captivating him all night. Kenda shuddered at the touch.

It was only then that Joe realized his hands were hands again. No shaggy pelt covered them. No wicked black talons. He had tried to deactivate [Savage Claws] countless times, only to have his fingertips explode with agony at every attempt. When he felt the wildness purr inside his gut, Joe realized that [Skinwalker] was not the only skill in his repertoire that his feral side had more control over than he did.

He sent a thankful thought to the wild id and focused once more on the woman in front of him. Without claws, he didn’t have to worry about scratching her as he unbound the braids. Sliding his fingers across her skin and through her silky mane was unbelievably sensual in a way his clawed digits never could have been.

Even when the last twist was undone, they both found themselves delighting in this moment. Kenda produced a brush from her spatial bracelet and handed it to him. With slow sweeps, Joe ran the bristles through her hair. After the tangles were gone, he moved the auburn locks to one side and kissed her neck.

That gentle touch shattered their simmering suspension. Spinning around, her lips finding his. They drew each other close.

Joe’s couch had not been made with the intention of withstanding the amorous might of two high-strength adventurers. After several loud cracks and a broken-leg lurch, they discovered the bed had been built with significantly sturdier construction.

Morning came far too soon, and no matter how much Joe wanted to halt time, there was no avoiding the day ahead. The sky was just starting to lighten. He watched it paint the woman sleeping on the pillow next to him in soft hues of pink and yellow. In less than an hour, Mercy Suku would start the ritual to fully heal Margen. As much as he wished he could spend the day in bed with Kenda, he knew he couldn’t. Disappointing the tiny saurian Saint of Cliffside was not even a conceivable option.

Joe brushed a stray lock from her face before sliding towards the edge of the bed. He hadn’t made it two inches before her hand flashed out and caught his arm.

“Where are you going? Stay,” she murmured, eyes still closed.

“The ceremony with Murrcee is this morning. I can’t,” Joe bemoaned. Her fingers let go, allowing him to finish his glide out of bed and wrap a throw blanket around his waist.

Kendell’s eyes clicked open. “Oh. You’re right. Do I have time to get ready?”

“You’re coming?”

“Of course I’m coming. Not only is everyone betting on Murrcee dropping another miracle on you, but the guild is responsible for the founder’s return. All of the officers are going to be there. Well, except Septimus. He and the Mercy got into a fairly epic quarrel over the needs of the Cliffside district and have avoided each other ever since. I have to admit it still amazes us that our illustrious leader is the only person aggravating enough to get on the bad side of the most beloved figure in all of Fort Coral.”

Kenda sat up and stretched. As the sheet fell away, she asked, “So, how much time do I have?’ she groaned, twisting to work her muscles loose.

Any and all thoughts Joe might have had vanished. She was stunningly beautiful. Completely and utterly gorgeous. If a hurled pillow hadn’t smacked him in the face, he might have stayed there, looking at her, forever.

“Sorry. Yeah,” he stuttered. “You should have plenty of time. It’s a fifteen-minute jog to Cliffside, and the last bell rang only a few minutes ago. We have at least half an hour to wash up and get food.”

“Too bad you don’t have a shower. That would have been fun,” she smirked at him, lifting the sheet up to her neck, thereby allowing his brain to reboot and focus on the conversation.

“Ok. The Turtleback is definitely going to have showers now.”

“The Turtleback? You decided on a name for the houseboat.”

“It’s a working name for now. Since it's going to be Yuk’s home too, I’m going to let him have input on what we are going to call her.”

Joe decided to wear one of his good new outfits again. The elvish one he had worn at the ceremony and the meeting with Dorian was more elegant, but the dunian garb was more comfortable. Joe went with the latter. He pulled it on, noting it was far from clean. His hyper-acute nose picked up the smells of spilled ale, smoke, and sweat. There were even a few visible stains. He wasn’t worried. That was what cleansing charms were for. In the blink of an eye, the magic from his new ring washed over his body and attire, leaving them both fresh and spotless.

Kendell drew an outfit from her bracelet, one more akin to her traditional guild garb: a comfortable shirt above a reinforced kilt and leather boots. Even though it was not fancy, she still looked amazing.

They grabbed food and sat at his dining table, recounting funny moments from the Jarlscarp ball. Before they finished, Joe heard the door downstairs open and the patter of feet slapping up the stairs. Mojo burst into the room and pounced on Joe. After a speedy scruffing, he launched himself over to Kenda for her bout of affection.

Joe brought over more food for the shug pup and then used his [Casting Claws] to right the semi-crippled couch. A mending charm fixed the broken leg and tightened up any loosened joints.

They had time before they had to head off, and they took it. Joe found it was nice just to spend the morning talking with someone, even if it wasn’t about anything specific. They ranged through a dozen small topics, often stopping to touch or exchange a small kiss. Mojo, unfortunately, thought that every kiss between the two needed to be punctuated by one from him as well, but that turned out to be more funny than irksome.

By the time the sun was halfway split by the horizon, they were ready. Before they left, Joe quaffed the [Level Lock Draught] Kenda had given him to keep himself from gaining experience for the next 24 hours and missing getting his [Anyone] trait.

Serra Lane, the street his home was on, was one of the streets that divided the working/crafter’s district of Kilning from the lower-income quarter of Rockpoint. The Abaaka House stood on the Kilning side of the street, but within a block, the roadways became more rutted and structures less fortified. Kilning was fashioned almost entirely of whitewashed brick and terracotta roofs. Rockpoint was built from weathered wood and reed thatching.

The further east they ran, the more humble the dwellings became. By the time they reached the bridge that spanned the cliffs at the edge of the city, most of the homes were nothing much more than huts.

The Doolaroog River cut a cleft from the southern farms to the sea, marking the east border of Fort Coral. Over the river was a high stone bridge named the Rock Walk, built by the very clan Joe had celebrated with the night before. It was a wide arch, constructed from gray stone. The lines were clearly dwarvish in nature, heavy and formidable, etched with runes. Joe used [Witchsight] to study the runecraft and was amazed at how solidly each formation locked into its neighbor. This was one bridge that even Cauldrakon would have struggled to tear down.

Across the bridge was the Old Fort. This is where the founders first established their foothold on this far shore. It was half in ruins now. Some of the old walls still stood, but the other half had tumbled down. Joe had heard rumors that the place was haunted, which was why it remained forsaken, but since it was supposed to be haunted by the ghost of Margen, Joe took these rumors to be ghost stories used to spook the young.

Past the Old Fort, resided Cliffside. Some debated whether or not this province was actually part of Fort Coral, or a nearby neighbor of the city. It lay outside the city walls and wards and was more of a shanty town than anything else. The worst of the huts of Rockpoint compared to some of the best homes of Cliffside.

The only reason this huddle of fishermen and farmers survived outside the protection of the city was due to one being, Mercy Suku. Regardless of how bedraggled the homes of Cliffside were, its people were flush with an abundance of health. Joe could not detect a single aura of sickness or major injury under the layers of dirt that most of these humble folks wore. He could feel the aura of wellness that infused the air. While these folks likely lived difficult lives, with meager resources, there was none of the sense of despair that would normally be found in a similar such slum.

The main road of Cliffside led them right to their destination. Pretty much in the dead center of the community stood the shrine of Murrcee. It was a simple structure, but it was clearly well-loved. The adobe-style dwelling was the only building anywhere in sight that was made from clay and not driftwood or reeds. Wreaths of flowers dressed the little home, and seashell ornaments clinked in the breezes that climbed over the bayside cliffs.

The other major feature of the shrine was a single wall, about twelve feet high and almost as wide. This thick brick slab was painted with an exquisite mural. The art depicted people of all races caring for and helping one another. The themes of community and support were illustrated in numerous scenes woven together with swirling borders. This had to be the painting that Runkbadok had done for Mercy Suku all those years ago. Even though Joe had visited the Mercy several times already, he stopped and lost himself in the mural once again, finding new images he had missed on his previous viewings.

In front of the wall was an area paved with flagstones. Often, this patio was covered with reed mats so parishioners could sit and pray or contemplate on the goddess of charity and kindness.

There were already many people gathered for the coming ceremony, all standing around the shrine, not in the communal space in front of the wall. Some were locals of Cliffside, dirty but hale. Many more were residents of the city proper, most of them well-dressed. Regardless of station, all stood together in harmony. Not a single person displayed a hint of impatience, knowing they were not on any timetable of their own. All would wait until the Mercy made her appearance.
