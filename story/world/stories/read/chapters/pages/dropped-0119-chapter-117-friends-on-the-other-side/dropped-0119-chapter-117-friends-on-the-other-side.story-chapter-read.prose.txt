Chapter 117: Friends on the Other Side

It was creepy, make no mistake; the dead weren’t supposed to move like that, and even with all that I’d experienced to date, it still made for uncomfortable viewing, especially when Harvey took the offered appendage, and began to whisper quietly to the body. Even worse, it seemed to be responding, though I couldn’t comprehend how any of it was intelligible; a foreign language was one thing, as even though I couldn’t understand the words Harvey was saying, I could still see the patterns of grammar, punctuation and diction. The undead, on the other hand, offered at most a strange series of grunts, groans, and wheezing, more reminiscent of someone’s final days in hospice care than a form of communication.

I could only conclude that Harvey had a Trait that made it work, much like how I now had access to Language (Cat); it made me wish I’d taken the option that provided Omniglot, just for a moment, before reason reasserted itself and reassured me that my decision was correct, and that hindsight was an exercise in navel-gazing and futility. Whatever was said, it must have been enough to come to an accord, because soon after, the corpse began to glow, entering a state of accelerated decay that saw it crumble to dust in real time, leaving only the skeleton behind for a brief moment, before it too vanished into the aether, while a small wisp of light flew into Harvey’s grimoire, completing the contract.

”All done,” Harvey declared, getting back to his feet.

”Great,” Pumpkin sighed. “Any reason why we had to come along for this?”

”It’s easiest to set parameters at the initial contract,” Harvey explained. “Things like the conditions of employment, but also the hierarchy involved. With the two of you here, I was able to set command protocols for all three of us, rather than requiring a secondary ritual later; it’s just more efficient to get it all done at the same time.”

It was hard to argue with that, at least not without coming across as hypocritical; I’d always been a big fan of lean operations, though I took care not to push it to the extremes, like some of my peers in the executive world. Pension funds were still nominally a public service, even if the individual fund’s motivations were almost entirely profit-oriented; there was a greater degree of oversight than purely earnings driven industries, meaning I had to maintain minimum standards on how employees were treated. Granted, that turned out for the best in the long run, as my more experimental compatriots got to bear the brunt of the blame when their policies fell over; apparently, firing ten percent of the workforce every year didn’t actually make things more efficient, it just built resentment, tanked morale, and ended up with an entire staff comprised of people who were great at meeting quotas and KPIs, and very little else.

I’d even managed to poach some great employees, ones who had gotten burned by the quota game, and were willing to take a bit of a pay cut for a stable and sane management experience, which just went to show that no matter what happened, there were always winners and losers in any given situation.

”What now?” Pumpkin asked, before I could do so myself. “We moving into the new place?”

”Not yet,” Harvey refuted. “At least, not all at the same time. This needs to be done delicately, starting with our new shopkeeper stumbling back home, after a drinking night out that led him to miss the incursion. Give it a few days for him to get back up to speed, and the veil of normality to settle, before we all jump aboard. For the time being, do as you feel best, I’ll be in contact once the new office is ready.”

Not the most robust of excuses, by my reckoning, but one that was common enough in an age before the abstinence movement took off, that it would receive little scrutiny, particularly since the man’s grave had been unmarked, meaning that whoever buried him hadn’t been able to identify him; the perfect recipe for identity theft, if I’d ever seen one.

”We should go find more food!” Pumpkin demanded, because there was never enough on offer for the little glutton. “A big fish, like the ones near the temple!”

”Seafood will be pricy, this far from the ocean,” I warned, though not dismissing the idea outright. “That said, it’s not a bad idea, if we can find anything about to spoil. Being able to store it without going bad will give us a hefty premium, once the reality of lockdown sets in and fresh food becomes scarce.”

I couldn’t recall exactly, whether I’d explained my inventory mechanics to Harvey until now, but I figured this wasn’t a big secret, in the grand scheme of things. There were certainly enough magic items around for a similar effect to exist, otherwise I failed to see how trade could work across such scales, barring the use of expensive magic like teleportation.

“I guess we’re going shopping then,” I declared with a grin. “Time to take full advantage of the five finger discount.”

Not waiting around any longer, I turned on my heel and headed the way we came, Pumpkin jumping onto my shoulder as I went. There wasn’t anything more to see, besides Harvey’s summons fastidiously covering the grave again, dumping a few rocks to make up the difference in space, and it was best to minimise our presence here, just in case some mourner showed up and saw us loitering around. I knew enough sob stories to do a decent job, pretending to mourn, but fake tears were annoying to produce, and dancing around outright lies would be annoying, given my covenants. Best to simply be elsewhere before anything kicked off.

That said, we weren’t only going shopping for food, as I had a few ideas of how to break into the arms business. I just had to see which of them were viable in the local environment.
