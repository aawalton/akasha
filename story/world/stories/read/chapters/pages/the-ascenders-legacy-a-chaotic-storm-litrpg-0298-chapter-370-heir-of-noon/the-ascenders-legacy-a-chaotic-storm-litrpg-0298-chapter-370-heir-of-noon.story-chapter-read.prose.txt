Glass was a strange thing.

Born from violence, shaped by fire, and trusted only because it appeared calm, it spent its entire existence balanced between strength and ruin.

It could bear weight for years without complaint, hold back wind and rain, reflect beauty, carry light—and yet all the while, a single flaw might be spreading through it unseen. A fault line too fine for the eye. A weakness too small to matter.

Until the moment it did.

That was the cruelty of glass. It rarely announced its end when it began. The fracture started in silence, hidden beneath the surface, growing with each strain until the whole structure was living on borrowed time. Then, when the final pressure came, it did not gradually surrender.

It shattered all at once.

It was glass, after all. Fragile. Delicate. Breakable.

But Seraphine, despite embodying glass, had been different.

She had not been perfect, but she had been ordered—her path structured intentionally from the beginning, her skills meticulously calculated, her spirit seamless in its existence.

She had possessed no fault line, no crack in her cultivation, until a part of her had been brutally taken away, a building block removed from her carefully constructed spirit. The deactivation of Reflect had not been a small flaw. It had been a gaping fracture that injected instability into her entire build.

And Sophran had exploited it mercilessly.

But one instability had not been enough for him.

Over the last five hours, he had layered five more curses upon her, deactivating every skill that posed even the smallest measure of defiance. First, he deactivated Glass World, then Body of Glass, then Glass Forge, then Glass Clones, and finally, as though he had not stripped enough already, Glass Manipulation itself—reducing her to nothing more than a grunt in the mundane class.

Still, she had fought, defying his power with the few skills she had left. But it had been futile.

She could not escape his attacks, could not dodge, could not create a single reflection or withstand his blows. She could not even manipulate glass.

All she could do was fall once more into the gaping earth, her body bleeding and bones broken.

She struck the cratered ground hard enough to widen it, bedrock exploding outward beneath her. Bones shattered further, flesh split in jagged lines, and for the first time, her crystal-blue gaze stopped calculating.

Instead, it flashed with agony. And this time, she did not rise again.

She could not.

Sophran descended and came to stand above her, vitality roaring through his body in great waves—vitality she no longer had any means of reaching.

He raised a hand, and a jagged blade of verdant lightning formed within his palm. Lifting it above her head, he looked down at her ruined form.

"This is your end," he said. "Any last words?"

Seraphine coughed, blood spilling from her lips. A long silence followed, filled only by the distant sounds of the battlefield.

"None," she said at last. "Kill me. You have earned it."

But while Sophran had certainly grown teeth in this war, he still walked the Path of the Living Storm. His storm was not ruin. It was not cataclysm.

It was renewal. It was endurance. It was life.

And despite how much he believed she deserved death, he could not bring himself to kill Seraphine in that moment.

He stared at her for a long while. Then he gave a slow nod. "I am taking you prisoner."

Seraphine closed her eyes, unable to fully hide the relief that moved through her at his words.

A line of text appeared across her vision in that moment.

Congratulations! Reflect skill has been reactivated.

Her eyes opened.

But it was too late, and she was too weak.

Even so, it was hope. With the last of her strength, she stretched her arms toward him and spoke.

"Arrest me, then. You have earned it."

But while Seraphine had escaped death by a hair's breadth, Ariana stood upon a far crueler edge—where even her next breath was uncertain.

Ariana Solis had always been a happy child.

Even back on Helios, her home world, she had been energetic to the point of exhaustion, friendly to the point of recklessness, and so relentlessly hyperactive that her father had named her Sunny long before titles and power ever found her.

So when she stumbled upon the legacy of her patron, she had not seen it as chance.

She had seen it as fate.

And Ariana, being Ariana, had embraced that fate with every ounce of enthusiasm she possessed—which, in all honesty, had been a great deal.

From the moment she entered the Solar Sect, she had thrown herself into training without restraint, weaving her beliefs of the sun god into every skill she learned. She cultivated with the hunger of dawn chasing noon, refining herself again and again until she rose above every other special in the sect, eclipsing seniors who had once seemed untouchable.

She had been hailed as the Sun's Heir, a cultivator capable of catastrophic destruction should she ever cast aside her restraint. No one had been able to defeat her within the advanced tier bracket. None had endured the heat she wielded—not even fellow Solar cultivators.

Ariana Solis had been unstoppable.

Until now. Until this… opponent.

Groaning, she tore herself free from a mound of debris and broken earth, blood running from a dozen injuries as she glared at the stern monk who had pushed her to the brink of death more times than she cared to count.

Solar Regeneration had saved her each time, but with every recovery, the taste of death lingered longer, hanging over her like a storm cloud that crept closer whenever she blinked. Ariana feared that the next time her eyes closed, it would already be waiting when they opened again.

But she could not yield. She could not retreat.

She was the Sun's Heir, and she would be damned before she let some grisly old space cultivator end her winning streak.

With a cry of fury, she rose upon Phoenix Wings and shot into the sky like a falling star reversed, a vast aura of solar energy vitrifying the earth behind her. Even space itself seemed to burn in the wake of her ascent, parting around her like fabric pressed too close to a flame.

It was rapturous.

She surged past the space cultivator with a snarl curling her lip, a look of surprise flashing in his eyes. But he was not her target.

Not this time.

Her target was the giant ball of light blazing in the heavens, so distant it lay beyond her normal reach. Yet with every mile she climbed toward it, her power swelled in answer, flooding her channels in savage waves until she could scarcely contain it—until her entire form blazed as bright as the sun itself, burning away reason until only destruction remained.

Then she turned.

Far below, Gyatso floated just above the broken battlefield, small and motionless against a world of ruin.

Puny.

Her entire body was cloaked in incandescent light now, every inch of her radiating power enough to blind lesser cultivators. The authority of her path descended upon her like a mountain, heavier than ever in the presence of the source itself.

Of her god.

The pressure was crushing, but Ariana smiled through it, arms rising as she activated her most powerful skill.

"Heir of Noon." She whispered. "Corona Lance."

And in an instant, the world went dark.

Light fled from every surface at once. The battlefield dimmed as every wandering ray of sunlight bent from sky, stone, and shattered steel, abandoning the land in a single impossible rush.

They raced upward.

Toward her.

An ocean of golden torrents that crashed into Ariana's waiting palms and spiraled around her in blazing rivers as she seized the stolen day and forced it inward. Radiance screamed under compression, folding tighter and tighter until the heavens themselves trembled beneath the strain.

A spear began to form—longer than towers. Brighter than dawn.

Its edges were so sharp they carved glowing scars through the air itself.

Below, shadows swallowed the battlefield whole.

Above, Ariana Solis burned like a second sun.

Laughing now, unable to contain the ecstasy roaring through her veins, she leveled the lance at the monk beneath her and gave a single command.

"Burn."

The lance struck like judgment itself, crossing the distance between heaven and earth in a line of absolute radiance—too fast for sound, too fast for thought, too fast for anything mortal to answer.

One instant it was in her grasp, blazing above the darkened world.

The next, it arrived.

But where it should have found Gyatso, it found only empty space.

And beneath that space, ranks of Blood and Serenity cultivators looked up just in time to understand nothing at all.

The sun touched the earth.

And the world vanished in a devastating wave of white-gold brilliance.

The impact did not explode outward at first; it imploded, dragging stone, steel, bodies, and breath into a single impossible point before reality lost the argument and burst apart, ripping devastation across miles of battlefield. Formations shattered as thousands of cultivators—enemy and ally alike—were erased so completely that not even ash remained where many had stood. The earth itself was vitrified into blood-streaked glass, entire companies of Mythics reduced to silhouettes branded into stone.

It was destruction unseen since the Icon battle.

And for a long moment, the war itself came to a standstill, countless heads turning toward the blazing star in the sky.

Ariana barely noticed any of it. Hatred rose within her as Gyatso reappeared above the battlefield untouched. Whole.

The only sign he had experienced her attack at all was the thin wave of steam rising from his body as sweat poured from him.

"You cowardly swine!" she snarled, energy darkening with hatred. "Why won't you just face me head-on?"

"Because I am not an idiot," Gyatso said, his earlier sympathy completely replaced with horror.

He looked down at the devastation below, jaw tightening. "So many dead." His gaze returned to her. "Your own people among them… and you feel nothing?"

"Spare me your judgment, monk!" Ariana snarled, her power rising as she climbed higher into the sky. "I will kill you. If it is the last thing I do, I will kill you."

"You will do no such thing."

The voice struck like thunder, and they both turned to see Aodhán suspended in the air above them, an expression of abject fury on his face. His frost-white hair was matted to his head with blood, and his entire right arm had been burned to the bone.

"You will do no such thing," he repeated, his voice rumbling with agonized rage. "You have done enough already. This ends now."

Ariana snarled at him, fingers curling in preparation for a surprise attack. The space cultivator might have been too evasive for her techniques, but judging by Aodhán's injuries, he most certainly was not.

And at this distance, one strike would erase him.

But she couldn't have been more wrong.

Her fingers twitched, her form blazed, but before she could so much as blink, Aodhán activated Synaptic Ruin and squeezed—disrupting both her neural and magical pathways in an instant.

Ariana convulsed, eyes widening in horror as the power gathering within her spirit unraveled violently. Her muscles locked. Her thoughts stuttered, and the blazing aura around her collapsed into sparks as her body went rigid midair, every limb frozen in treacherous paralysis.

She could feel everything.

And command nothing.

Panic tore through her eyes as Aodhán crossed the distance between them in a blur, his ruined arm already knitting itself whole beneath layers of crackling lightning. With an expression of cold judgment, he seized her by the face and whispered.

"Leech."

The word landed like a sentence.

Power ripped from Ariana in a screaming torrent—every iota of energy and electrical impulse drained out of her at once. It surged from her core, her pathways, her body, her hair, her eyes, her toes—solar energy, willpower, vitality—all of it stripped into Aodhán until her once-radiant form dimmed to ash, cracks spreading through channels that had never run dry until that moment.

She tried to resist, but her body did not answer.

She tried to scream, but her lungs would not move.

Within moments, the Sun's Heir hung limp in his grip, hollowed and fading—reduced from a blazing star to a dying ember.

Aodhán regarded her for a single heartbeat, his expression blank. Then, in one clean motion, he sliced her throat open.

Blood scattered through the air like shattered rubies as her body fell from the sky in silence.

He turned to Master Gyatso then, a strange heaviness settling in his eyes.

"I'm sorry, Master," he said quietly. "I am no longer the peaceful boy you raised me to be."

Gyatso stared at him for a moment—then slowly raised his own hands, now wielding jagged swords of space.

"Me neither."

Aodhán held the monk's gaze for a long moment before looking down once more.

Ariana Solis was dead.

But it wasn't over yet.

Miles away from her broken body, Meredith was about to learn a brutal lesson—or perhaps, unlearn one.

Stumbling from a pile of rubble, Elias Hale screamed as spatial magic ripped his domain and body to pieces.

He raised boulders and constructs to block the blistering beams of chaotic void, but the cannons simply blew them apart, destroying his defenses as though they were made of paper.

He snarled, teeth gritted in pain as he dodged dozens of beams at a time, his eyes searching for an opening—because as powerful as these cannons were, Meredith had forgotten one crucial detail.

Her constructs were outside his domain, but she wasn't.

She was still in his domain. Still under his rule. And the moment he found an opening, he intended to exploit it to the fullest.

Dodging another beam of blistering void, he spoke. "Shield me."

The earth responded with a roar, a towering dome of rock rising up to cover him—but the dome had barely formed before half a dozen cannons blew it apart. The explosion blasted Elias backward, slamming him roughly against the earth.

Another dome rose just in time, and Elias gritted his teeth. "Yield to me."

Immediately, the earth parted and he sank in, moving through it to reappear miles away. He surfaced just above the sea of bloody glass Ariana's skill had created and turned to face his opponent.

She hadn't found him yet, but Elias knew it was only a matter of time—time he had no intention of giving her.

Eyes narrowing in regal tyranny, he raised a hand and commanded.

"Kneel."

Gravity gathered above her immediately—its invisible presence so vast the air thickened beneath it, so heavy the earth itself groaned in warning.

Meredith found him then, her eyes wide, but it was too late.

Gravity slammed down on her in an instant, more powerful than it had ever been before. Meredith screamed as her body jerked midair as if seized by the fist of a god.

A wet crack rang across his domain. Then another.

Then many, as ribs folded inward beneath impossible pressure. Shoulders dislocated, and bones compressed one after the other in a sickening chain. Blood burst from her mouth as organs strained and ruptured inside the cage of her collapsing body.

For one suspended heartbeat, she hung there—pinned between heaven and earth as every bone protested his decree.

Then she dropped, striking the earth with so much force that it exploded outward in a ring of shattered rock and pulverized dirt.

The earth didn't welcome her. It erupted in a forest of jagged spikes that ripped what remained of her body to pieces, eviscerating her completely.

Space churned, her constructs dissolving as life bled from her.

Meredith held on to it until she couldn't anymore, and then she let go—crimson blooming across her vision as her heart stilled.

It had happened too fast. Too quickly.

One moment she'd been dominating, and the next, she was gone.

Phasing through the earth, Elias appeared before her mutilated body and sighed.

"Maybe I am a god after all."

He turned then, a small smile of victory on his face—

A smile that ignited a cold, blistering rage within a certain icon-happy Calamity.

High in the sky, Zerathis Starless stared at the boy who had ripped his disciple to pieces, a bubbling hatred rising within him. The hatred grew, warring with reason for a moment.

Hatred won.

Without hesitation, he stretched a hand toward the boy.

"Zerathis!" someone shouted from a distance, but Zerathis didn't listen.

He was about to break another rule—one that would no doubt rip this war to pieces again—but he didn't care.

Space churned. Reality shattered.

And an instant later, Elias's brain splattered against the earth in a wave of gray and crimson.
