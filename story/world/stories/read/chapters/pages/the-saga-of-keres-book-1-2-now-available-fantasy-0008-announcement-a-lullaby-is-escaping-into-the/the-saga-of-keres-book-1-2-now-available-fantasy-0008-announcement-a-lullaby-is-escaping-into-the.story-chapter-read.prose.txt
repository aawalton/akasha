HELLO!!!!

Before anything else, I should address the biggest change around here: Both Of Sand and Silver and The Saga of Keres have now been stubbed on Royal Road.

For anyone unfamiliar with the term, stubbing means most of the previously published chapters have been removed from Royal Road as the stories move toward their published editions. The Royal Road pages are staying up, along with a few chapters and updates I can keep here, but the complete books will no longer remain available as a web serials.

That feels strange to write.

These stories lived here for a long time, and Royal Road is where many of you first found my work while I was still figuring out what these worlds would become. Stubbing them was a necessary part of moving toward publication, but that did not make hitting those buttons any less weird.

For Of Sand and Silver, that starts very soon with the publication of Book 1, A Lullaby of Thieves and Tea, on November 10, 2026.

And a quick note for those of you reading this announcement from The Saga of Keres: the rest of this particular update focuses mainly on A Lullaby of Thieves and Tea and the launch of Of Sand and Silver. I am sharing it here too because both stories have gone through the stubbing process together, many of you follow both projects, and I wanted everyone who has supported my Royal Road work to see what has been happening behind the scenes.

A lot has happened. I have now reached that strange stage where I search for my own book online and keep discovering it in stores I did not personally put it in.

Which, for once, is a good thing.

So... here's the giant A Lullaby of Thieves and Tea publishing update.

I knew self-publishing would involve lots of work. I did not know it would turn into about seventeen different jobs.

The last few weeks have meant ISBNs, metadata, manuscript uploads, cover files, retailer dashboards, proofs, pricing, ARC emails, website updates, distribution problems, social media, BookTok videos, Instagram posts, YouTube content, newsletters, retailer research, and an unreasonable number of browser tabs.

There have also been several all-nighters.

The dangerous sentence has become: "I'll fix one more thing before bed."

Then I look outside, and the sun has come up... and then suddenly I have to go to work.

On top of publishing the book itself, I have been building the whole content-creation side around it: writing advice, social media posts and short-form videos, ARC outreach, commissioned artwork, website material, and everything else that comes with trying to launch a book without a traditional publisher handling those pieces.

It has been exhausting. It has also been fun in that strange I have made terrible decisions and would make them again sort of way.

And somehow... A Lullaby of Thieves and Tea is now appearing in bookstores around the world.

The book releases on November 10, 2026.

Royal Road gave this story its first home, so I wanted you to know where things stand.

WHY DO THE PRICES VARY SO MUCH?

This has become one of the strangest lessons from self-publishing.

I can choose my list price, but I cannot choose every retailer's final shelf price.

A retailer may add costs connected to printing, wholesale discounts, shipping, imports, taxes, currency conversion, or its own margin.

That means one store can show the paperback at a reasonable price while another store looks like it expects the book to arrive bound in dragon leather. And yes, some of the current print prices are higher than I want.

Over the next week or so, I plan to adjust the print pricing, with the paperback as my main priority.

So why haven't I done that yet? Because I have been waiting for the final commissioned artwork. I want to include that art inside the finished editions.

Now that my artist sent it, which he literally sent just today, I need to update the manuscript/interior files, upload the revised editions to IngramSpark, Amazon, and Barnes & Noble, then order new physical samples so I can check the final product.

I did not want to change the files, order samples, then receive the artwork and repeat the entire process days later... because, well, that costs money.

Once I finish that, I should have a much better picture of how low I can push the paperback price while keeping distribution practical.

I am also exploring whether I can sell physical copies through my own website. That could give me more control over pricing than some third-party retailer listings. I am also looking into Amazon promotions, coupons, and other options.

So if you see a print price that makes you wince, you may want to wait a little.

I am working on it. And if one retailer charges far more than another, check around first. The official book page will, probably, remain the easiest place to keep track of purchasing options:
