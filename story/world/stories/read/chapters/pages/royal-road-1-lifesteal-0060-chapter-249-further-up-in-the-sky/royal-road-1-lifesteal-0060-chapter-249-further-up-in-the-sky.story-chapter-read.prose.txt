Freddy burst out of the meaty pod, the fluid sploshing all across the floor and seeping down the drain. He gasped and then released a relieved laugh. “Holy fuck that was close!”

He had gone into this performance with a plan. That quickly went to shit.

Originally, he was going to land a group of ten middling performers, kill two at the junction, fall into the trap, behead whoever peeked into that hole, and then, once they were down to the last five in the “boss room”, he had this whole labyrinth he was going to chase them through until the finale where he would hold this spooky lunatic monologue with the last person before killing them in spectacular fashion.

The only thing that actually played out as imagined was one of them peeking into the spooky red hole and getting their head torn off, and even that had been a close thing given how stealthy that guy was. If it hadn’t been for Freddy’s insanity sense, he would have never noticed that someone was approaching.

The first thing that went to hell was the cast.

That dumbass Leopold pretty much never ran public matches, but for some stupid reason, today was an exception. If that weren’t enough, Shane joined too, and then a full cast of star performers decided that they just couldn’t miss this one little fight.

The star performers were bad enough since they held a considerable amount of power, but at the very least, they knew not to interfere with the performance until things got interesting.

Shane, however, was going to get his ass kicked the next time Freddy saw him.

The fight against Shane was the goofiest thing he’d ever participated in. Freddy was crazy strong, but not nearly enough to beat a two-star super elite while hiding most of his abilities. Thankfully, Cecile, the other performer who went there with them, was an actual pro at this, so she had stepped up to ensure that Shane wasn’t at too much of an advantage.

Basically, she had read Freddy like a book and guessed exactly what would happen. Then, she suddenly freaked out about “something she spotted in the shadows” in the middle of that hallway and threw every single buff in her arsenal…

At Freddy.

The look Shane shot her when she did that was pretty hilarious.

But even with several pretty OP buffs under his belt, Shane was not an easy man to put down.

The son of Overlord Lawman was a kind of berserker. He had the blood affinity with the Rich Blood path, the fire affinity, and the lightning affinity. His talent conjured this massive hammer that gave him a barrier depending on the amount of damage he did. It was an effect similar to lifesteal, if arguably better since he could barely even be injured in the first place.

But to be injured, he first had to be hit. With Blood Rage, Ignite Muscle, and Internal Storm running at the same fucking time, hitting him was easier said than done.

Freddy had to admit it—he got his ass handed to him.

The only reason he even managed to win that fight was because he sacrificed an arm to land an opening for a Tsunami Strike. Even then, Shane definitely had at least one spirit ability he could have used to turn the fight, but didn’t, likely because he was too proud to do so.

Cecile gave him a wink and shot him both thumbs up once Shane went down, and he promised to buy her drinks some day. You know, before beheading her.

Heavily injured and without time to heal, he borrowed Cecile’s “something in the dark” excuse and then played mad to get things back on track. Thankfully, the tunnels he’d commissioned had these “monsters” all over the place. Really, they were just inert masses of meat he could hit to quickly heal up.

It was a bit cheesy, but it definitely served the persona of a terrible evil who could somehow return unblemished no matter how many injuries he took.

With Shane down, that still left Leopold. And that boy was no slouch, either.

Originally, Freddy was going to whittle the group down to five people by playing this scurrying creeper, and just as they expected him to keep doing that, he would burst through a nearby wall and swap to slowly chasing them down while tanking every attack. Said wall was the path to the labyrinth that was supposed to be the next stage of the fight.

But with Leopold there, he simply couldn’t afford to tank… much of anything, really. Leopold’s attacks could slice right through his limbs like a hot knife through butter. Actually beating the guy was a serious challenge. Thankfully, the whole horror scenario had obviously rattled him, even if he put on a brave face, giving Freddy a shot at taking him down.

The plan had been simple—use Twisted Pendulum to catch the little prick off guard with a sudden burst of speed. This plan most definitely did not involve Freddy getting chopped into bits. By a miraculous stroke of luck, Freddy had unhinged his jaw—a cheap trick to try and rattle the kid further before taking him down—which gave Freddy the admittedly yucky option of biting half his head off to finish the fight.

It had been far too close for comfort, but really, it had turned out great! Better than he expected, really!

With some relief, he made his way over to a nearby seat to review the fight and see the people’s reaction.

He pulled up a recording of the match on his tablet.

The POV was set to never follow Freddy if possible, meaning that the fight played out entirely from the perspective of the others. At first, he was giddy. Seeing himself as this suspicious, sinister figure was kind of hilarious.

And then, it wasn’t.

The recording reached the scene where he suddenly freaked out before falling into the trap. He vividly remembered being worried his acting would be awkward, but he’d been worried for no reason.

He could play a raving lunatic just fine.

Once he was out of sight, he finally got a better look at the way the performers reacted to his shenanigans. The way they flinched at every shadow, the pallor in their faces as they reluctantly progressed forward. Then, the way they screamed in genuine horror as he dragged them away.

He watched without expression as he witnessed himself biting into a person’s head. He still remembered the taste.

When the recording was over, he found himself staring at the blank screen, a numbness coursing through his limbs. At some point, the door to the room opened, and Madame stormed into the room. “Well… Congratulations are due, I suppose. That performance of yours just scored the seventh-highest ratings of any arena match since it opened.”

“Mmh. Cool.”

She looked at him for a long moment. He could see that she was angry. The telltale signs that she had come there to chew him out still lingered in her expression. But rather than say anything, she sighed, moved over to the couch, and sat beside him.

They were like that for a while, until Madame suddenly spoke up. “I’ll admit that I didn’t expect much from your performance. I thought you’d flounder with whatever you came up with, and that I’d have to help you put a proper act together.”

He smirked. “So how’d I do?”

She snorted. “You’re lucky that it went well and you know it.”

Freddy laughed. “True that.”

“You’re a fantastic piano player. I thought you focused primarily on the violin?”

“I do. I’m just a bit multitalented, I suppose.”

“You continue to astound me. What did you have to reveal to take Shane down?”

He shrugged. “Cecile picked up on what I was trying to do and buffed me up. So, nothing.”

She nodded. “That girl has always been a particularly dedicated one. You owe her a big favor for this. Do you want me to pass something along to her in your stead?”

“I promised I’d treat her to some drinks.”

“After you reveal your identity?”

“Can I hide it now even if I wanted to?”

“It is highly unlikely. The physique, music skills, rapid healing—you’ve shown enough.”

“Well there you go then. Once it’s fully public, round of drinks it is.”

“Did you watch the recording?”

“Sure did.”

“So what do you think?”

He froze, the words he was going to summon nowhere to be found.

Madame stared at him, her eyes seeing straight to his soul, those same hints of anger resurfacing. “Did you enjoy it?”

He looked away.

She took a deep breath. “I still remember the first time I saw a recording of myself in action.” She chuckled. “I had just reached the third star. Back then, that had meant something. Nobody knew what they were doing, so the raw power of the third star made you virtually invincible. And I was no ordinary three-star. But not everyone understood that. An enemy faction that hated my guts sent about fifty men to take me down.”

“I’m guessing that went swell for them.”

“Anyway,” she continued, ignoring him, “after I was done, I pried a recording crystal from the charred, stiff hand of one of the people I’d killed so effortlessly. That was the first time I’d seen one. They were still new technology back then. Old, to me. I had been eleven when the Rift struck, so I knew of screens, of videos, and the internet. I still remember, vividly, what it meant to hold such a thing in my hand…

“It was like a piece of the past, born anew. For a brief moment, I had wondered, then, what the future would hold. Whether I’d wake up one day and find myself in a world just like that little chunk of magical rock—different, but recognizable. Perhaps civilized, yet again.

“And then I watched the video.” Her expression turned stormy, haunted. “Maybe I wasn’t wrong to have had that thought. In many ways, that is the exact kind of world I live in today. But I had changed. I was no longer a little girl. I was…” A touch of grief befel her. “I wasn’t a fool; I knew that others saw me as a freak; but I had known nothing, not before seeing the world through their eyes.

“Fire, flesh, and something in between. The way I made meat dance, fire shine, and people burn. It was horrible. And yet, intoxicating.” Her expression turned wild with well-worn excitement. “Oh, how intoxicating it was to know I could make people so afraid.” The excitement faded as she looked down. “Seeing that video was the worst thing to ever happen to me.

“That was, in many ways, the exact moment I became the thing others already thought I was. A monster.” She went quiet, slowly shaking her head. “By the time I regretted what I was doing, it was too late to fix my mistake. For a hundred years, I had toiled to rid myself of the stink of that reputation. Of the Scorched Fleshmancer. Of the demon people saw me as. To this very day, I see that same fear in people’s eyes. Of those who know what I’m capable of. And it will probably never go away.”

Freddy felt sick at her words.

She whirled on him, more impassioned than he’d ever seen her before. “What were you thinking?”

“I… I don’t…”

“I had thought you had a semblance of reason somewhere under all that bluster, but I was wrong to have let you run rampant.”

“Madame, I…”

“When you said you wanted to make people afraid, I had thought you had wanted others to look over their shoulder before speaking badly of you in public, not… wake up in cold sweat because they imagined you were standing in the corner of the room!”

“It was just an act.”

She angrily extracted a tablet from her storage ring and shoved it into his hands.

It showed an article, one titled Gladiators and Psychopaths. It had released not ten minutes after his match wrapped up.

He read through it, the sickening feeling in his stomach growing deeper. It was an article about the massacres performed by the anonymous category performers. It talked at length about how the arena was a breeding grounds for violent offenders, a place that not only gave them a way to practice using suicidal spirit abilities, but also drove people insane through repeated experience of death.

Right at the end of the article, there was a small passage that made his blood run cold.

This article was supposed to release tomorrow, but I thought there was no better time to set it free but now. Mere minutes ago, I, as well as many of you, I’m sure, witnessed the performance of one “Inquisitor.” It was a match that had surely satisfied the appetite for violence of even the most hopeless of arena addicts.

A masterclass in horror, brutality, and unreasonable power, all wielded by someone best referred to as a “something”—something wicked, inhuman, and vile. Something that mutilates itself, drags men to their deaths, and bites heads off. Something that clearly has a vendetta against society, and more than enough might to execute it, however brief and bloody it might end up being.

I ask you, dear readers, how much further must this go? When will we realize that things such as this must not be enabled? Tell me—do you feel safe roaming the same streets as something like that? Something anonymous, wearing the skin of someone, all while harboring the potential to do that, not as a performance for blood-thirsty masses, but as something real, with consequences that won’t simply vanish.

Today, this Inquisitor is a two-star performer.

And what will he be tomorrow?

Yet another tragedy on the streets? Or a terror in the shadows? Worse?

We do not know who he is. But we’ve seen, clear as day, what he could be.

I implore you, make your voices heard. The rules of the arena must be changed. Violence cannot be allowed to fester in anonymity.

The views on the article were skyrocketing. There were thousands of comments. To Freddy’s surprise, most of them were lashing out against the author of the article, calling them a collection of highly creative insults, and rushing to Freddy’s defense. And yet, there were few who were…

He turned the screen off and handed the tablet back to Madame.

She sighed. “You have revealed enough for people to piece it together. If not today, soon, the world will connect the dots. Even then, you should deny it.”

“And what good will that do?”

“It will muddy the waters. Even if only fools believe you, it will help shield you from the worst of it.”

“The worst of what?”

“What do you think, Freddy, of what? Fear is a net cast wide. You wanted to scare those you dislike. Those who you find distasteful. Perhaps even those you see as enemies. But I promise you, said terror will reach far further than those you aim it at. And it's often those most powerless who will be the most afraid. Trust me. I speak from experience.”

He looked away in shame.

“I know you care for the less fortunate, Freddy. But today, you were thoughtless. You had lashed out, and you will suffer for it. People will never forget this. Not when you become a Lord, or when you become something greater. You will feel the sting of your mistake for a long time to come. I promise you that.

“Deny it and hope it creates just enough doubt to turn it from fact to conspiracy. And do better than this.” She got up, turning to leave. “There is a fine line between act and truth. I pray you rid yourself of these demons before that article turns to prophecy.” She walked out of the room. “Get rid of it before it consumes you…” then, quieter, “the same way it consumed me.”

Freddy stayed in that room for what felt like an entire day, but he had at no point bothered to check the time. The same thoughts kept passing through his head, and the shame continued to grow.

Eventually, he went home, trying and failing to take his mind off everything.

He had been thoughtless. So unbelievably thoughtless. In his anger, he had lashed out… And worst, it wasn’t the first time he’d done something like this. He kept imagining he was still a mortal, so powerless and afraid of anyone who had an aura. What would that Freddy have thought if he watched that same video? The pain of realization stung something fierce.

For the longest time, he had seen himself as a champion of the weak. Hell, he had a badge that proved he had literally sacrificed his life to protect others. And he’d taken that for granted. He’d turned it into an excuse to do nothing, to stop caring about what was right, and just indulge his worst desires.

The face of Sophia flashed through his mind. He wondered where she was and what she was doing. If she were helping people in need, using her healing to change lives for the better. How he could have been there with her, doing the same thing. But instead, he was here putting the fear of crazy elites into the hearts of the masses. “I’m a fucking idiot,” he chided himself.

One by one, the days ticked down. Nobody yet figured out he was Inquisitor, but some forums floated the possibility. There was an image of him walking out of the alchemy lab with all his new muscle, but funnily enough, most believed it was altered. People who had seen him leaving swore up and down that it was real, but they were accused of making shit up.

It was a small comfort. Each day, more people talked about it, and if this was what was being said publicly, he could only imagine how much was being said in private.

There were only a few days left until he headed to the Tower of York.

One evening, he decided to try and take his mind off the whole thing. He prepared himself a hot bath, with bubbles, fragrances, and candles. Once he left, he sat before the window overlooking the city with a fine wine in his hands, lounging in silken bath robes.

The bath didn’t really do much of anything for him. It was probably because of his talent. For others, he was sure a nice warm bath would have some health benefits, but what was it supposed to do for him? His health was already flawless. But still, going through the motions of “something relaxing” didn’t hurt. It gave him just a touch of perspective.

He had thought he had known. That he had accepted it a long time ago.

But as he sat there in his wildly pricey enchanted bath robes, sipping a wine that replenished essence he didn’t even need, in his eye-wateringly expensive luxury apartment, watching over one of the wealthiest cities in existence, he realized he had been kidding himself.

Freddy Stern was an elite among elites, regardless of where he’d started. But even if all the powerlessness had long abandoned him, the mentality hadn’t. He still thought himself immune to being a shitty rich guy. That was a sin he would never commit—could never commit. After all, he was just a little guy at the bottom of society.

His eyes drifted up to the properties floating above the city, their shimmering lights all too familiar. He’d grown always looking up to where his superiors were literally floating above him, the rainbow shadows of their parties taunting him while he suffered below.

He had enough money to buy a property up there. If he so wanted to, he could join, take a closer look at the wealth he’d been obsessed with all his life. But he hadn’t. Because he wanted—needed someone above him. He needed there to be shitty elites he could feel angry about, otherwise…

Who even was he?

On that day, perhaps the most painful realization he ever had dawned upon him, his soft hold on his drink going numb as he sank into himself, eyes drifting below to the underbelly of the city.

He was already there, shining upon those below.

And holy fucking hell had he been shitty.

If he had any soul damage left, he was sure it would have healed at that moment. The weight of that realization couldn’t possibly hurt any harder. There were many people below him now, and many feared him. Hated him. Saw him as yet another rotten psycho with too much power for the good of those who didn’t have it. And perhaps they weren’t wrong.

And surely there were people above him, on the lowest of the floating islands, who just like he, kept telling themselves sweet lies of how oppressed they were by those even further up in the sky.

“No,” he said, getting off his seat and throwing the glass of wine. “Fuck that shit.”

He had suffered too much to let himself succumb to that kind of rot.

Suddenly, he was realizing just how much he was doing wrong.

In a few days, he would be escorted to the Tower of York. On the way, he would be attacked. He knew that without a single shred of doubt. His twisted fate wouldn’t let him reach a reward without putting an obstacle in his path. To this end, he had hired protection, but he now realized just how thoughtless of a move that really was.

If the convoy got attacked—and it definitely would be—people would get hurt. People would get killed. Sure, he had paid these mercenaries their due, and they definitely knew the risk they were taking, but was that really enough to justify it? Was treating human lives like an expendable resource truly acceptable just because everyone involved agreed that it was fine?

More than ever before in his life, at that moment, he knew just how good someone could be at lying to themselves. Mercenary-shmercenary, when it came to a fight and they were about to die, nobody would go “Yeah I knew the risks coming in, so this is fine.” Fuck no. They’d scream in terror all the same, and leave behind loved ones, and their dreams and aspirations would perish with them.

All for the purpose of making one elite that much more powerful.

Nobody would blame him even if that happened. But the standards of a thoroughly rotten society were no measure of ethical conduct, and he’d be damned to let himself keep getting sucked further and further into this mindset.

He had become an archhuman to change things. To build a world slightly closer to the one that would be most fair to everyone. And he wanted, more than anything else, to stop forgetting that fact.

After quickly getting dressed, he made his way over to the arena, and then, promptly requested to meet up with Madame.

There was a way he could be more responsible. A way to prevent unnecessary loss of life. It was a bit out there, and Madame was likely one of the only people in the empire who could help him do this. Sophia could, as well, but… Yeah.

He had to wait for a while since it wasn’t an emergency, but eventually, he got into her office.

“Freddy. To what do I owe the pleasure?”

He gulped. In order to get her help, he needed to explain a few things he would have preferred to keep a secret. “Madame…” He twisted the details a bit. He told her how he was headed to the Tower of York in a few days, and how he “suspected” he’d likely be able to ascend after completing his trial.

“I already knew you were headed to Nova York with that convoy, but I have to admit, this is a bit of a surprise.” She squinted at him. “Ascend… That is in fact a possibility. But what’s the rush? Are you sure you’re as ready as you can be?”

He was as close to that as he could possibly be, but he carefully picked what to say. “My progress has stalled a long while ago,” he admitted. “And to be perfectly honest, I am afraid of being targeted. Even if the Umbras can protect me from most of it, I don’t know enough about their internal politics to feel safe from them.”

She slowly nodded. “A reasonable thing to feel. So, what do you need from me?”

“I… I fear I’m going to be attacked on my way there.”

Madame nodded again at that. “Another reasonable assumption.”

Freddy tensed up.

She got up and paced a bit. “I give it a solid seventy percent chance that you will be attacked. But I feel that with the veritable army you’ve hired and your personal power, the odds of actually dying aren’t particularly high. Remember, the most anyone can send at you is going to be a couple of peak three-stars. A Lord simply can’t afford to make that kind of move. And whoever comes after you will certainly underestimate your power. So, what is it that you need?”

He told her his idea.

She sighed after hearing him out. “You seriously are genuinely insane, aren’t you? But well… If you want to get out of a fight with minimal bloodshed, that’s one way to go, I guess.”

Suddenly, he felt an intense pang of regret. It took him a moment to even process it, and Madame quirked an eyebrow. “You okay?”

“Oh… Yeah… Sorry, I just felt a bit overwhelmed for some reason…” He scowled. “I definitely… do want to avoid unnecessary bloodshed.”

She squinted at him. “Uh-huh… Anyway, are you certain that girl will agree to that? It would be a bit of a hit to her reputation.”

He slowly nodded. “I’m sure I’ll find a way to convince her.”

“Very well then.” She approached him, placing a hand on the top of his head. “Let me in. It will only take a few minutes.”

With some apprehension, he allowed her essence to penetrate his body.

And then, everything went dark.
