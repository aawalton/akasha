Sophia Walker hadn’t ever recovered from the trapped portal where she’d lost Angie and Carlos.

Then there’d been the mess in the twilit Eldritch City where she’d failed to save Dan and could only watch as April’s body twisted and boiled, an infestation filling her nerves to bursting. She still had no idea if the striker had recovered—or if she ever would. They’d made sure Sophia never had contact with her. They didn’t want her to know.

But she knew.

She’d failed April—and Angie and Calos, and Dan. Mess after mess. All of them her fault. And this one would be worse than any of those—she could feel it eating at her like the Eldritch City’s mana-devourers, ripping away at her resolve until—

Sarah Cullman’s eyes met hers over the body they were both working on. They pierced her, and she couldn’t look away as the Spark of Life glared at her. “You’re doing it again. Fresh air. Now. You’re done here.”

“But—“

“You’re going to need your head soon. Can’t be losing it here. And this woman’s past the critical stage. I’ve got her.”

Sophia sighed. Then she stood, used a burst of Mana to clean her hands of the blood and gore they’d been covered with in her desperate attempt to save…God, she didn’t even know the strange-looking woman’s name, and fled from the Spark of Life before the S-Rank healer killed her.

It was only once she stood on the ramparts high on the Portal Tyrant’s keep, looking down at Phoenix sprawling out to the south, that she realized how stupid that feeling had been. Sarah Cullman wouldn’t hurt her. Ridiculous. The pressure came back instantly. The pressure to go back in there and offer her Mana to whoever the Spark of Life couldn’t heal. Her therapists back at the GC Tower told her every healer felt that pressure. It was central to their cores, and to their beings. Without it, they wouldn’t be healers.

Phoenix looked…different. Still, but abuzz.

It took Sophia a moment before she realized what it was. Looking down the long road that had once been I-17, there was no northbound traffic. Every car, bus, and truck on the road traveled south, then turned off to the west.

Sophia was C-Rank. She didn’t want more than that. But even at C-Rank, her senses were beyond the range of normal humanity, and as she focused on the traffic, it became more and more clear that the traffic on every street she could see was moving in two directions.

Away from the Portal Tyrant’s keep, and away from downtown Phoenix.

It wasn’t quite an evacuation, but it was close.

Sophia Walker took a deep, shuddering breath. She hadn’t been part of Kade Noelstra’s core group in a long time. Instead, she’d been working with Jeff on training up the low-rankers. But she knew that whatever Kade touched turned to chaos and insanity. Still, she couldn’t help but be behind him. He’d come such a long way from the stupid support who thought he was a striker, who’d almost gotten himself killed back in that trap portal until she’d healed him.

Whatever was coming, he’d be ready to handle it.

And she’d do her best to take care of the consequences.

Carter Richards read the message again.

He was a high B-Rank. He’d stalled there, and he knew why. It was his merged Unique. Without the intact, original version, he’d never reach A-Rank, let alone S.

But he had a squad again. People who he was responsible for. An ad-hoc team of C and B-Rankers Jessie—no, Guildmaster Gerald—had thrown together. She’d sent a message right after the GC alert went out. Hers was simpler, though.

Guildmaster Gerald: Delver Richards, I’ve attached dossiers for five other delvers. They’ll meet you at the Fallen Delvers Memorial. Your mission is simple. During this fight, there is a VIP you need to protect. I can’t trust the rest of the guild. It has to be this team. He won’t be targeted, but he’s an unawakened human, and he won’t evacuate his home. Keep him alive.

Carter rolled his eyes. Then he cleared his throat. “How are we doing, team? We ready?”

A messy chorus of nods, affirmations, and curses met him as his five teammates confirmed, in varying language, that they were ready. He had something approaching his ideal delving team. A second archer. A mage. A support—a healer would have been nice, but beggars couldn’t be choosers—and two fighters. It was the kind of team he could grind with. That could stretch his natural talents and complement them.

But more than that, it was the kind of team he was used to. Not one of these men and women was a good person. They couldn’t be. If they were, Guildmaster Gerald would’ve had them fighting the incoming Portal Blitz. Instead, they were all delvers like him—people who wanted power and prestige, and would work with anyone to get it.

Carter nodded. “Alright. Let’s fucking move.”

The van started up and took off, heading not west, toward the evacuation zone, but southeast, to the place where Phoenix’s territory and Tucson’s bordered. There, according to the guild leader, he’d find a house with a walled garden.

And inside, he’d find the man he was supposed to protect.

Carter had no idea why he was heading toward the Traynor mansion, but Guildmaster Gerald had a job that needed doing, and he’d do it to the best of his ability.

Ophelia St. Vrain didn’t understand people.

The message had gone out three hours ago. Evacuations should have been moving along. But the lines of traffic traveling through Phoenix had only grown longer. It would have been far simpler to follow instructions, have your documents ready, and enter the parking structures that filled the area around Sky Harbor. Instead, every driver seemed determined to ask a dozen questions. Never mind that every one of those questions would be answered after they parked.

People were so complicated. They did so much that made no sense. If they simply did what they were supposed to do, what they’d committed to doing, things would be easier. But they insisted they were special. They needed special treatment. And that made things hard.

Still, Ophelia had a job to do, a band on her arm with the Desert Wind’s circle of blown sand on it, and a skill set to do that job. As the cars slid by, the A-Rank mage focused on what she’d have to accomplish. She had no team out here. Not a single delver in all of Phoenix was within a mile of Sky Harbor.

It’d be better this way.

Ophelia wasn’t being sidelined. She simply had a task—one she was well-suited for. And she wasn’t worried about it. Her new employer understood what she was good at, and understood how to honor her commitments to her delvers. Guildmaster Gerald had set all of this up quickly, but this part of her plan, at least, was solid.

As for the rest of it? The only part of the plan that mattered to her was Sky Harbor. Everything else was someone else’s problem.

Bob Traynor didn’t ignore the GC’s public message. He also didn’t ignore the private one with instructions to his favorite daughter, the corporation that bore his last name.

He sent out his own, though, to the crews that lined the 303 Wall. It was a simple message, but one that had been a long time coming. The Traynor Cannons would be aimed inward, just as requested. But they’d be targeting his targets, not that pre-teen’s.

Once this was over and he’d replaced Phoenix’s delving guilds with Traynor Corporation mining companies, he’d expand his smelting and forging operations from cannons to tools. Portal metal was a new frontier. The new frontier.
