Zaxius was angry. He was also afraid. And that combination... Well, he couldn't remember when he'd last felt so alive.

He knew how to fight Ascendants. There were two strategies: either overwhelming surprise or patient attrition. It all depended on what the specific individuals' strengths and weaknesses were. With the overconfident phase-user he'd faced back at Drakonias, catching him off guard and hitting hard before he could gather himself was the answer. With a prepared ambush trio, ready and able to support one another to hold him at bay? Patience.

After all, what would be less characteristic of a dragon? Were they not known for their fury and recklessness? So Zax played the part. He did not check his anger as he would have preferred, nor did he engage in conversation for either informative or banter purposes. He clawed and bit and lashed his tail, he sparked his storm and roared in proper time.

And underneath it all, he held his fear and his anger and his need to protect, fused into a scalpel. He chipped away at the central lock that held his power in check, gently enough it didn't react, steadily enough that it would inevitably succeed.

This was a method he'd considered using many times, but until now, had never seen a reason to undertake. The backlash of this particular release method would be painful and, at least temporarily, crippling. But there was no time. He'd been found sooner than he expected, and his understudies were not ready to survive this level of opposition.

If he failed to survive, Noah and Aurelia would die.

If he failed to hold the ascendants' attention, Noah and Aurelia would die.

If he failed to break free before he was worn down, Noah and Aurelia would die.

And... it turned out he quite liked his little human companions. Cared for them to the extent that he could readily bear the indignity and discomfort of playing the mindless beast while slowly carving his soul into something that the chains could no longer contain.

And all the while, the same two questions played over and over and over through his head.

Will we be on time? and... how far are we willing to go?

Zaxius had sworn off the path of Ascension long ago. He chose to stay bound to the world, to the people and places he loved—though that had ended up being a little more literal than he'd intended. And, despite the pain and restrictions and heartache, he had yet to regret the decision. He’d chosen to love, he’d chosen to grieve, and now he was choosing to live again.

But three Ascendants were no easy matter, even for a dragon of his strength. One on one, he could have taken any of them. A dragon’s power was not limited in the same ways lesser races were. If they were on the same level as him, they could never have hoped to defeat him.

Ascendants, though, had the tricky habit of not dying. So while the average draconic lifespan outlasted the average human lifespan a dozen times over, Ascendant humans could catch up and even surpass dragons if they weren’t recklessly overconfident.

He hadn’t met these particular Ascendants before, so he didn’t know much about them. The longer their fight went on, even with his attention divided, the more he began to discern about his adversaries.

The Huntress was impatient on the surface, riding her emotions in an undulating wave, but beneath it she was intensely pragmatic and incredibly focused. Every time Zax tested the bounds of her constrictions, she reacted with immediate intensification.

She was a slow hunter, enjoying the game while her prey inevitably wore itself out against her many entrapments.

The first binding was easy enough to understand, a simple Domain Imposition. He’d seen it a thousand times, a way to isolate your enemy and force them to fight you alone. Her aura had solidified into a dome around the two of them, invisible to all but themselves, but all but impermeable.

As far as abilities went, unthreatening. He could break it later.

The second and third bindings were where things started to get uncomfortable. Her mana-binding seal turned his own attacks against him, and the void-siphon replaced his expended mana with emptiness. Not only would he be unable to recover any mana for the duration of the ability, but if he let his mana get too low, the inner void might just consume him wholly.

Her other main abilities were just as hungry, nibbling at the edges of his script.

Void Annihilation, thanks to Noah, was easy enough to counteract, guiding it with his own lightning to safely detonate around him instead of inside him. Her suppression and oppression debuffs were irritating, but Zax would be astonished by any fight between Ascendant-level powers that didn’t include some level of equalization.

That was why he’d long ago stopped relying on external augments.

The Astral Script could only carry you so far. Either you learned its lessons and carried on once you reached the end of its path, or you accepted the end you were handed and settled down to rest at the peak of mediocrity.

Zax understood this in a way few children of this era would ever discover. It was a lesson he was doing his best to pass on to his students, so Noah and Bun Bun could avoid the traps he’d watched so many fall into.

People like this Void Huntress. He could tell, ten minutes into their engagement, that she was chasing numbers rather than mastery. She cycled her abilities like she was performing strength exercises, pushing them for the sake of using them rather than paying attention to the actual ebb and flow of the fight.

Her application was textbook, her combinations refined, and her execution lifeless in that particular way that came from someone too confident and complacent in their patterns to even notice their way wasn’t the only way, let alone the best way. She was, in a word, inexperienced. She would be the sort who thought because she had reached a higher number that she was superior to those beneath her.

Zax was about to disprove that worldview. And, unfortunately for her, given how many permanent bindings she’d placed on him, she wouldn’t be surviving the lesson.

The second fighter was immediately more interesting to Zax. Many people were willing to augment themselves for power, but few were eager to wholly transform to serve their ideology. The Crimson Cataclysm had taken the concepts of immutability and growth and combined them into his core identity to such an extent that his Celestian heritage was almost unnoticeable. He’d rejected his metamorphosis to traditional adulthood, instead crystalizing his chrysalis to become one eternally on the edge of an advancement he would never undertake.

Zax could have read the man’s entire life from that fact alone. Poised on a precipice, eternally seeking what would never be claimed. The Cataclysm would fight and grow without ever settling into the secure complacency of his companion.

If they’d met under other circumstances, Zax expected he and the Cataclysm would get along very well. He had the right sort of commitment, his strikes were measured and unpretentious, and his supplementary abilities were smoothly integrated into his combat cycles in a way that most people never attained regardless of how long they lived.

The way his armored skin shifted to absorb the elements it was exposed to, the way he controlled the battlefield with rifts and patches of hungry Rot, the way he targeted Zax’s weakest points with flawless perfection and retreated into an immobile unbreakable shell the moment the dragon’s attention turned to him, it all spoke to centuries of honing his skill without ever being satisfied. The elegance of mastery with the focus of perpetual growth.

In another thousand years, this man would be truly a force to be reckoned with. Especially if he found a group to coordinate with whose values better aligned with his own.

The current trio was unbalanced and skewed way too heavily toward control and survival. The Huntress’s drains were irritating, but Zax could have continued fighting in his current state for weeks without being at risk for more than a couple decades’ worth of casual meditation. They had no one to strike properly. Almost as if they were more afraid of him than committed to destroying him.

Which was the wrong tactic to take. Zax’s raw power would always be lower by a substantial margin, so he could see why they’d assume a focus on protection and prediction would be sufficient. Ascension represented a qualitative shift which he’d never undergone himself. But power wasn’t everything. Zax’s strength had never been in brute force, but in the application of knowledge. He thrived in preparation, in the slow dismantling of things everyone said would be eternal.

Abilities, spells, skills, perks… the Astral Script gave them all their own categories and rules and limitations and bonuses, but at their core, they were the same. Imposing the will of the soul on the reality of the world.

And Zax had a very strong soul.

The biggest problem would be not shredding his body in the process. Regrowing that would be a tedious process, and with the number of hooks the Huntress had in him, it would be very easy for him to push too hard and end up destroying his current form in the process. That wouldn’t be helpful to anyone.

The seer was a non-combattant, which made her harder to read. Any time he attacked in her direction, she reacted with plenty of time to spare, but she kept lingering within the range of the Huntress’s domain instead of waiting outside it where Zax couldn’t reach her. He couldn’t decide if this was an intentional trap to lull him into false security, ignoring her nearby presence until she sprung some sort of trap, or if it was a distraction to keep his attention split between three adversaries instead of focusing on taking down any one of them.

Her protections against elemental ranged attacks were equally solid to the Cataclym’s, absorbing his thrown lightning at a distance without reaction. Despite many attacks and feints in her direction, he failed to discern anything further about her. She showed nothing, only occasionally spoke, and either blocked or evaded his every move.

And the whole time one part of his mind was tracking and calculating and analyzing all of this about his enemies while he executed a high-intensity non-repeating attack sequence to keep them from having time to plan anything of their own, the rest of him was squeezing his soul into a new shape, forcing his true binding to bend just that tiny amount that would allow him to slip free.

It was a rush job, for sure. He’d probably be losing a couple thousand attribute points in the transition, but that was a minor sacrifice.

After all, the fact that they were locked in a stalemate meant these three were content to wait him out, to drag this fight on as long as it took to wear him down. And while Zax wanted to stall as long as possible so Noah and the others could escape, he couldn’t escape the urgent certainty that it would be playing into his enemies’ hands if he wasted a single moment more than necessary.

Forty seven minutes into the protracted conflict, practically a blink on the scale of immortals, the strain within his soul finally snapped.

Zax took a breath, and for the first time in a long time, the power flowed through his body and into his soul, then cycled back out and through him in proper form. None of it was diverted into holding him, none of it lost to suppression or dispersal.

For a long, silent moment, he simply stood with his eyes closed, wings raised half-open behind him, enjoying the sensation of true, absolute freedom.

Then a crystalline fist slammed into his ribs, and he shook his head and blinked down at the Crimson Cataclysm. The man had already pulled back to swing again.

Zax caught the arm in one claw, holding the man in place while he stretched out his neck and rolled his shoulders. His scales rippled, shifting from their dull hue to brilliant silver.

The claw he was restraining the enemy with started to tingle, so he pulled it back. The man came with it. In the moments he’d been distracted, the man’s crystal shell had already started to grow over Zax’s finger.

“Interesting. We knew you would be the fun one.” He flexed his claws, shattering the man’s arm and breaking free. The man ran at him with his other fist swinging, and Zax huffed out a breath of storm-infused wind to shove the man back. “We’ll deal with you in a moment. We need to see what we have left to work with.”

As he’d expected, the rush job of forcing his way free of the binding had left deep scars on his soul, whole decades of growth sacrificed to haste, but what were decades to someone of his age? He could blink and another human lifetime would have passed.

But for now, he had his power and mind freed of their limitations and a whole set of shiny new Void Huntress abilities to dissect. And if he broke her soul a little in the process, it would be no great loss.

He shifted down into his battle form, exulting in the feeling of shaking off the lingering restrictions that had so deeply limited his metamorphosis. A fraction the size of his war form, which was itself a fraction of the size of his True form, Battle Form was lithe and streamlined, a spearhead golden body with six legs and four wings, perfect for dealing with small groups of enemies while presenting a smaller target. Only about three times the size of Noah’s draconic form, Zax felt about four hundred years younger as he darted around the interior of the dome binding in a quick survey of the first barrier.

The freedom to leave would be his first priority. He could come back and deal with these people later if needed, even if it meant half his abilities were locked and his mana and attributes steadily drained, that was only a minor inconvenience. Being locked down in a duel circle, though… that could be deadly to more than just himself.

Zax found the flaw in the domain almost immediately. It wasn’t even trying to hide itself. It bound a minimum of two beings, one of whom must be the owner of the domain, until such time as the number of beings alive within it had decreased by one. A classic duel setup, viable for either personal conflict or arbitrating third-party matches.

But the whole thing hinged on the domain’s understanding of ‘alive’.

Zax veiled himself from perception. As far as the rest of the world was concerned, he did not exist. Those looking at him physically could tell he was present, but a creature relying on manasight or lifesense would bump right into him without seeing anything but a lump of dragon-shaped stone.

The domain seal collapsed, its conditions fulfilled.

Zax hissed in triumph and took off into the sky. Let them try to restrict him now.

His four wings beat in a blur as he shot across the valley like a priority courier of the Second Era, laughing as three more attempts to re-establish the duel binding slid off his scales without touching him.

All he had to do now was—

Something slammed down on him from above, tearing through two of his wings and leaving a gaping hole where his two left-front legs had been, Void energy burning at the edges as it tried to assert dominion over what was left of his body.

Zax tumbled from the air, falling far too quickly. Gravity slammed him to the ground at ten times the normal impact weight before he could reorient himself, let alone shift into something better for the situation. Within half a second, crystals were growing up from the ground, fully encapsulating his rear legs while he struggled to move.

“We were in the middle of something,” the Void Huntress said coldly, striding over to where he’d landed.

Zax growled softly. “It seems we may have underestimated you, young one. Concealing your strongest attack all this time?” Her other bindings were more difficult to evade, true, but given enough time he could escape them. Whatever divine smiting she’d just invoked upon him, however, that was a lot harder to outrun.

She raised her chin. “I’m not a child.” She aimed her bow at him, a second arrow forming on its string with a growing black void at its tip. “Go on. Try healing yourself, ‘Old One.’ I’d love to watch you feed your own soul to the Void.”

Zax considered his options at lightning speed, then bowed his head and closed his eyes. “If this is where we end, then so be it.” He gathered his full strength, all the mana remaining to him, all that he could claim from the air around him, then looked up at her with a feral grin. “But if our guess is correct, you can only use that attack once. So let’s see how well you handle complications.”

Zaxius unfurled the full strength of his aura.

Normally, he kept it politely restrained to his immediate vicinity, but that was one of those things about having an ancient and powerful soul. His aura had reach.

Dragon Fear rolled out, flooding the entire valley. The Ascendants before him ignored it, but they weren’t the target.

After all, this was Death Valley. To the creatures here, a dragon saying This is mine? That was just another challenger trying to take what was theirs.

He didn’t even have to wait a full second before the ground started to shake. A moment later, the air started to hum.

“North!” shouted the seer, pointing at the ripple in the stone. “Geron, seal it, now!”

Zax quietly shifted into his human form while they were distracted, only retaining his four silver wings. He’d needed to sever his rear legs to escape the living crystals’ grip, but that still left him plenty of mass to work with.

He threw himself upward, smothered his presence and magnified only his voice. “Beware Death Valley for it is said, here even Ascendants fear to tread.”

With an angry shriek, the Huntress fired off her arrow, but it was without the careful aim or direct targeting of her previous attack. The arrow seared a line of nothingness through the air, leaving a lingering line of nonexistence like a tripwire across the sky, but it was nowhere close to hitting Zax and no risk him flying into it.

Zax perched atop the highest butte within reach, waited just long enough to witness the massive claw tearing itself up from the ground, then collapsed. He was far enough out of reach here to probably survive the coming chaos.

Flying monsters, burrowing monsters, slumbering undead… all set aside their natural differences to come punish the invader who’d dared taunt them. Too bad when they arrived all they’d find were three tasty Ascendants who didn’t even have dragon scales to protect themselves.

He lay on his side, eyes half closed as he traced the spells and abilities he’d been inflicted with, and smiled. Even depleted, drained, exhausted, and having spent almost everything he had on breaking free in the first place, he’d bought himself the time he needed.

The Huntress’s many bindings and drains were still doing their work, and he had no mana to speak of now, only enough to sustain his current form and little else, but they were about to have much bigger problems than hunting him down to finish him off.

Three ascendants vs one weakened dragon, on paper, they might win every time. But knowledge vs brute strength? That was another matter altogether.
