As James and Zhang Chāo walked the room together, James felt the eyes of dozens of other Rulers on them.

There was someone else on the stage now—it seemed as if James’s speech had opened the floodgates for everyone who had been reticent before, and now a line had formed—but more people seemed to be watching the Fisher Emperor and the Heavenly King move through the room than were observing the actual speaker.

This is what happens when all of us get our speaking Skills deactivated, James thought. The people who were strong enough to forge kingdoms and empires can’t even hold the attention of a room full of people who are here to network with them. Whoever did that has a lot to answer for. If this Summit could have prevented a war or something, and it fails just because people weren’t that interested in talking to each other…

But James and Zhang Chāo had no shortage of people approaching them.

“I loved your speech, um, Fisher Emperor,” said one.

“I didn’t agree with all your points, but it was good to see a different point of view than Mr. Shān presented,” said another.

“You must be a very ambitious man, Your Majesty,” commented a third.

Why do you say that? James thought. But he didn’t want to ask that question directly and look foolish. He guessed it was because he’d put himself out there by giving a speech, but he suspected that no one was saying the same to Pú Tiě Shān. Maybe because Shān actually had an agenda when he got up to speak. I was just… mouthing off, honestly. Mainly to get in with this guy.

He eyed Zhang Chāo carefully. The other Ruler was a good wingman so far. Their dynamic was a bit like when Mina had walked James around in lawyer networking events. James thought of himself as slightly extroverted, but as it had turned out, the actual going up and talking to people aspect of interpersonal networking was something that he found draining. He preferred having deep conversations with small groups of people or giving speeches. The banality of small talk was almost physically painful for him.

Skills had compensated for that dearth of energy—reversed it, at times—but now that those Skills were inactive, James found himself slowly losing strength like there was a leech somewhere sapping his vital fluids.

James responded to all of these exchanges as politely as he could. And Zhang Chāo helped. The other man seemed to be comparatively adept at flowery diplomatic exchanges, and he remained as bright and chipper as a pageant contestant no matter how many times they had to answer the same lines from fawning royalty and other types of Ruler.

Still, the first dozen of the approaches that began to drain James took place in a matter of a couple of minutes. The diplomatic overtures only accelerated from there.

Individuals and small groups made their way to him to extend their greetings. It was hard to be certain how much of it was based on the content of James’s speech versus the fact that he seemed to be one of a very small number of human Emperors. Power had its own pull, though James didn’t yet know how much the promotion had increased his actual combat power.

He tried to enjoy the interactions. It was gratifying that so many people from all corners of the world came to essentially pay tribute, along with some nonhumans who had appreciated his apparent openness to them.

But eventually even Zhang Chāo seemed to hit a limit. James was relieved when Zhang Chāo politely yet loudly made an announcement.

“The Fisher Emperor will take a few minutes for a strategy session with some advisors. We must digest all of the proposals and friendly overtures. We will return to circulating the room soon. Until then, His Imperial Majesty would appreciate privacy.”

My style of address keeps getting upgraded, James thought wryly. I wonder what the people here would think if they saw my territory was mostly an undeveloped swamp. I am the ‘Emperor’ of the former Disney World territory… and not much else. Zhang Chāo’s happy to play second fiddle here, but he rules an entire island that has millions of people still living on it. Although… if we count all the nonhumans living in my territory who could go toe to toe with a human in a fight, I definitely have millions of subjects now…

Ever since he had absorbed Orlando, James had an extremely dense population center. He had only taken it recently, but it was his. Irrevocably so until another Emperor showed up or someone killed him.

It was strange to think he had become such a major player so suddenly.

Other Rulers gave them a wide berth as James and Zhang Chāo beelined for an isolated chunk of the room near where the eldritch monstrosity had been hovering a few minutes prior. Even the most formidable nonhumans seemed to be avoiding proximity to the creature. James was less concerned, since it still didn’t hurt his eyes noticeably to look at it for a minute, and since the System had assured them that violence in the location was more or less impossible.

“You seem to have become my handler,” James said with a wry smile.

“You seem to have acquired quite the fanbase,” Zhang Chāo observed.

“Thanks to your suggestion.”

Zhang Chāo shook his head politely. “Thanks to the content of your speech, over which I had little influence.”

“If only they knew I was speaking on your behalf, then you would get all the attention.”

“But I wouldn’t enjoy it nearly as much as you do.”

“I see. You’re a twisted man, mocking my situation.” James shook his head, and they shared a laugh.

After a few minutes of this breather, they returned to circulating the room.

Zhang Chāo introduced James to friends of his, mainly people from geographically near him. He had a few allies from mainland China, he was on excellent terms with every Japanese Ruler, and the Australians seemed to like him too.

It made James slightly nervous to think that Zhang Chāo had so many allies, because it seemed to him to imply that the conflict with Pú Tiě Shān was more serious than mere words. Had James stepped into a burgeoning war on the other side of the world? He might be willing to fight against Pú Tiě Shān himself, if it was merely a matter of two men disagreeing about the right course for the future.

But there were many other people James was responsible for.

Validating his fears, James and Zhang Chāo began to run into Pú Tiě Shān acolytes.

“I would think that one of only a few human Emperors would have a more discerning taste in allies,” said one, bowing politely to James as if he hadn’t just insulted him.

“You and Pú Tiě Shān should rightfully get along, Fisher Emperor,” said another more cordially. “I hope you will go to him and correct this unfortunate misunderstanding.”

“You are a strong Ruler, so I would think you would not be opposed to the imposition of order by force on vulnerable populations,” said a third. “The public only benefits from this. It makes one wonder if the Fisher Emperor is really a paper tiger.”

All of these allies of Pú Tiě Shān’s who confronted James were Chinese.

“I’m sick of this ridiculous wolf warrior diplomacy,” Zhang Chāo said after the last one had walked away. He spoke loudly enough for the departing Ruler to hear. “These toothless wolves ought to know their day has come and gone.”

James knew vaguely what Zhang Chāo was talking about. He chose not to ask questions. There was obviously a history here. Just as obviously, James had stuck his foot in something he did not understand. Rather than getting a version of affairs with Zhang Chāo’s spin, James would hopefully get the complete scoop from his advisors later.

None of them had objected to him speaking, even though Rotter clearly knew a bit about the Taiwanese delegation and their situation. So James tried not to be too nervous.

Along with those trying to cozy up to James and Zhang Chāo and those who seemed almost overtly hostile, they occasionally ran into slightly eccentric characters as well.

“You strike me as a man who’d be a fan of Roman history, Fisher Emperor,” said an Italian Ruler who had been a classics scholar before the System. “What do you think of Sulla’s methods in the late Roman Republic?”

James remembered Sulla vaguely, and he gave a vague answer, deeply fence-sitting. The Italian beamed and tried to start a more detailed conversation about Ancient Rome, before Zhang Chāo thankfully excused them.

“How did I do?” James asked once the two were a little away from the other Ruler.

“I thought you framed your answer well,” Zhang Chāo said. “It sounded nuanced. I’m sure the Dowsing King appreciated your depth of thought.”

“Do you actually know who Sulla was?” James asked, raising an eyebrow.

“Do you know who Qin Shi Huang was?” Zhang Chāo replied without missing a beat.

James waited a moment. “An Emperor of China. I’m almost sure of it.”

They both laughed.

“I will not blame you for not knowing the history of my civilization, as you kindly excuse my ignorance as to the history of yours,” Zhang Chāo said.

“Fair enough.”

The two men had a few more polite interactions before Zhang Chāo excused them from another gaggle of Kings, Queens, and assorted quasi-democratic Ruler types. James thought it was just to get fresh air and recuperate from the constant chatter.

They walked away from their fellow Rulers and found another isolated section of the room for this second break.

Everything seemed the same as before until Zhang Chāo gave him a meaningful look and gestured for James to come even closer.

James stepped into the circle of what the two men had been maintaining as personal space, and Zhang Chāo whispered into his ear: “Listen closely. Do you hear it?”

James closed his eyes and listened, trying to observe everything going on around him.

His senses had expanded. Setting aside his Fisher Emperor powers that gave him impossibly superhuman senses within his own territory, his sheer Perception allowed him, when he focused, to hear sounds that would once have required audio enhancing equipment.

But today, he heard nothing.

At least nothing that was discernible from the background noise of the room. There were many people moving around and conversing, and there was continuously someone on the stage introducing themselves and speaking to the crowd of increasingly disengaged listeners. It made it hard to parse out individual noises.

No, there is a sound that I recognize…

He opened his eyes and turned his head. He saw Mina, Zora, Mitzi, Rotter, and all the others he had come with moving toward himself and Zhang Chāo. Mina’s footsteps were a familiar sound for him.

“Ah, I heard them!” James said. “My advisors are coming our way.”

Zhang Chāo shook his head. “That’s not what I meant. What dialogue did you overhear while you were focusing?”

“I can’t distinguish individual conversations very well in a large, crowded room like this,” James admitted. “Even if I overheard a conversation, it’s all static to me right now.”

At least not without my Skills functioning. Normally, I would be able to selectively listen to some conversations and tune others out to some extent.

“So, my Perception is better than yours,” Zhang Chāo said quietly. He wore a satisfied smile. “Perhaps mine is the highest Perception of all humans in the world, then… Or maybe it was a matter of Will. I need to make better study of which stats affect which capacities. I believe Will increases focus. Maybe that’s the difficulty here rather than mere Perception.”

“Don’t feel too good about yourself until you beat me in an arm-wrestling contest,” James replied.

Zhang Chāo let out an unrestrained laugh, then kept it going for what felt like just a little too long.

Is he… okay? James wondered. It definitely wasn’t that funny.

He was about to say something when Zhang Chāo began slapping his shoulder. It felt like a friendly gesture, or at least James felt it as that for a moment, and it would have looked like that to any outside observer.

But a fraction of a second later, he noticed that Zhang Chāo had slipped something under James’s clothes. It felt like a piece of paper.

Note passing? Are we being watched by someone? And eavesdropped on, too, I imagine…

James laughed along with Zhang Chāo and then gave him a knowing look.

“It wasn’t that funny, man.”

“I’m not used to your American style of humor,” Zhang Chāo replied, grinning and looking pointedly at James’s shoulder, where the note was hidden. “It’s still very novel for me.”

The two resumed a quiet, slow, meandering dialogue, but James’s mind was elsewhere completely. He was waiting for the opportunity to get to that note.

Fortunately, he had the perfect chance right in front of him. Mina and the others had continued to draw nearer, and as they did, James smiled invitingly and nodded at them, signaling for them to finish closing the distance.

As they gathered, their bodies blocked James and Zhang Chāo from almost everyone else’s view for a few precious seconds. It would have to be enough.

Almost too fast for even his own eyes to see, James’s fingers darted up to around his neck, slid sideways through his collar down to his shoulder blade, and quickly snatched out a piece of textured paper. As he glanced at the folded note, he couldn’t help but notice that the material it was made of looked and felt expensive, perhaps handcrafted.

But, of course, what was of interest would be the inside.

James lowered his hand so that his body blocked the paper from view by anyone other than Zhang Chāo. Then he unfolded it and began to read. He tried to keep control of his body language, but he couldn’t help a sharp intake of air as the words hit him.
