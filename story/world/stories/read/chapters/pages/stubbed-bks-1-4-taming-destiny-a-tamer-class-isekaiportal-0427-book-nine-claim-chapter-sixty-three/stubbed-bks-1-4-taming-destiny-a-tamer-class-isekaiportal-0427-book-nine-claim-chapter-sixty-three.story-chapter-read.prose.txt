I join Josiah on the side of the settlement facing the sea. Though darkness has mostly fallen, the last rays of the sun spearing up from behind the mountains to the west are enough to see that the sea has turned a deep purple color. It’s reflecting a darker version of the violet clouds above that are advancing in a distinct front.

The sight makes my stomach sink. Josiah spares me a glance as I join him.

"You see it, don't you?" he asks grimly.

"Is that...?" I trail off.

"I think so," he agrees. "It looks far too similar to last time."

It does. The soothsayer predicted the arrival of a manastorm not long before we arrived at the Lost Continent. And even though we put all our effort into running ahead of it and used our sails to their fullest extent, we were still caught on the edges of the storm. The sky all around us had been an even darker shade of this exact colour, and had been pierced by flickers of myriad rainbow colours when the manastorm was in full flow.

"How long do we have?" I ask Josiah. As the Weather Mage, he's more likely to know than I am.

"Hours," Josiah replies grimly. "I doubt as much as a day. I didn’t even see a sign of it this morning when we fought the water serpent, so it’s clearly coming on fast. It will slow when it hits land, but that will not be of much use to us as we will already be within it."

"And you’re sure it's not a normal storm?" I ask carefully. Normal storms are challenging enough – the speed of the winds, the lashing torrential rain that threatens people and work-sites alike – but without the mutating effects of the Energy bolts that strike from the clouds of a manastorm, they seem far more manageable.

"It's possible," he admits reluctantly, "but unlikely. Normal storms don't generally turn this colour. I suspect we only don't have the iridescent lightning bolts yet because it hasn't completely gathered its strength."

"Perhaps it won't," I suggest hopefully, though I know I'm being overly optimistic.

Josiah sends me a sharp glance. I deflate.

"Thought not," I mutter. Then I sigh. "Will hiding inside the settlement be enough to protect us?"

Josiah hesitates, then slowly nods.

"I believe so. The one time a magical storm hit Glimgrove, we ordered the populace to shelter inside their buildings – or public buildings in the case of many from the slums. It was only those caught out in the storm itself, or those unable to get within stone or thick wooden walls, who were badly affected."

"And when you say badly affected, you mean...?"

"Mutations," he finishes. "Erratic behaviour, in some cases. In some it wore off with time, though the mutations were permanent. In others, the person was forever changed – and in many of those situations, they soon had to be executed because they were a danger to the populace."

The words settle in me like stones. We can't afford to lose anyone from our settlement at this point.

"We'll have to shelter inside the hill for the duration.” But that means no hunting, no fishing, I think with dismay “How long will this last?"

Josiah shrugs, though the tight expression on his face tells me he's not as nonchalant as the gesture might suggest.

"I know not. The storm I experienced in Glimgrove lasted three days. My ancestors' records, however, hold stories of storms that lasted mere hours – and others that lasted almost two tendays."

I listen with dismay. I feared earlier that our rations wouldn’t last more than a week. Twenty days without fresh food will be impossible. If it comes down to it, we may be forced to choose between starving our people and feeding them something that will poison them – and then dealing with the aftermath. I can only hope the manastorm will be on the shorter end of the scale.

And then something else occurs to me. Not everyone can shelter inside the settlement. Artio is fixed in place, trapped by the vines. Should I risk moving him, now that he's in even greater danger? Or can I raise a shelter around him and protect him in place? But if I do that, I won't be available to help people prepare before the storm hits.

Stress makes a knot in my stomach as I'm torn between my commitment to the expedition and my commitment to my own companions. Can I afford to focus my efforts on a single individual? But can I live with myself if I prioritise those of the settlement over my Bonded?

"Markus," I hear. It's only when Josiah speaks and returns my attention to the present that I realise I'm half pulling my hair out, betraying my tension to the lord beside me.

"It's fine, I'm fine," I tell him defensively, then force myself back to calm.

I don't have to choose between responsibilities, I remind myself, logic returning now I’m no longer panicking. That's what delegation is for. Between the two options, I can better delegate people to help prepare the settlement than to protect my companion. That makes it clear what I need to do.

First, communication. I touch the Alliance Bond that connects all of the expedition leaders together.

I have bad news, everyone, I tell them grimly. Josiah is confident that a magical storm is going to hit us, certainly within the next day and probably within the next few hours.

The lords react with just as much dismay as I did. I let Josiah field their questions about the storm itself. After our experience with the previous storm, everyone knows what we're talking about and is aware of the potential consequences – both on us and our surroundings.

Because even if we survive the storm, the aftermath is another matter. We know from experience that after a manastorm the animals seem to go mad for a while, which will be challenging to manage. I also know, from what Josiah said last time the subject came up, that manastorms on land can create changes to the environment and plants, imbuing them with more Energy. On Moriax this was considered something of a boon, but here it's the last thing we need.

There's no point becoming too worried about the consequences now, however. We just need to get through it.

We need to make sure all of our people are within the settlement by the time the storm hits. If you know anyone is still outside, please recall them, I urge the lords. The only good thing about this situation is that it’s happening at the end of the day. If it was the middle of the day, it would be more difficult to get everyone inside.

All of mine are present or have returned and are eating dinner, Elarion reports, relieving me – at least the farmers and Plant Mages tend to keep proper hours.

As are mine, Josiah confirms. It's only the skeleton crew remaining on the ships.

Shouldn’t they also take shelter in the settlement? I ask with some concern.

If they remain below decks with the hatches firmly bolted, they should be fine, Josiah replies. There are enough enchantments on the wooden planks of the ships to ward off the worst effects of the manastorm. I’ll urge those on my ship to move to another, however – after the attack, I’m not certain the integrity of my ship is sufficient.

Do we need to get any resources off it first? I check – I hate the idea that we might lose the ship entirely, but I’d hate it more if we lost valuable resources too.

I’ll direct the crew to move them, Josiah responds after a moment of thought.

The question is, Layton asks tensely, do we have enough food to see us through?

Since that's one of my own worries, I find myself unable to respond immediately. By the way Elarion also stays silent, I guess he’s feeling the same.

We brought the fish we were able to catch after the sea serpent attack, Josiah offers, glancing at me apologetically. It's not much – the serpent scared away most of the shoals nearby – but it's something to supplement what we already have.

I find my voice.

We're working on a treatment for the plants post-harvest, to try to make them edible. We already technically have a method – the downside is that it makes the end product very acidic and unpleasant. However, it is technically edible if there's nothing else to eat. The alchemists and cooks are continuing to refine it.

With as much time as they'll have cloistered inside, they should manage to make some breakthroughs, Elarion suggests, though I sense the concern in his voice.

That's the hope, I agree, unwilling to promise more. However, we will need to ration what we have in preparation for this possibly lasting up to almost a month.

Silence greets my words, all those within the Alliance Bond aware of the grim reality of what that would mean.

Valence, are all of your patrols back in? I ask, changing the subject back to the earlier topic.

No, he replies tensely, though I have sent out the emergency signal for them to return. Unless they are attacked, they should all be back within the next few hours.

I glance at the storm front, which has already grown more threatening in the few minutes I've been standing here, and fear that won't be enough time.

Do you know where they are? I question Valence. I can ask Bastet, Ivor, Noir, and Sirocco to go and find them – to bring them back, or help if they're in a fight.

I have a map of where they were expected to go, but I only have mirror communication with the biggest groups while they're out, Valence replies with a touch of worry. They may have taken a different route if something happened.

Come to the main gate, then, I instruct, and I'll call my companions to join us. Being caught in a forest full of beasts when a manastorm hits isn't something I'd wish on my worst enemy, let alone those working to keep our settlement safe. Layton, I assume all of yours are back?

They are, he agrees. Should we continue guarding the entrance once the manastorm has hit?

I hesitate and consider it. The wooden walls are just as enchanted as the ships. The only issue is that they don't reach the top of the cavern – we didn't want to close it off completely for the sake of air and light, but in this case, we may need to.

Layton and Elarion, can you organise the Earth and Plant Mages to close off the cavern, reaching the top of the walls, so that we're protected from the exterior?

I can, Layton agrees, with a hint of surprise and Elarion sends a similar affirmation a moment later. But is that not more your area of expertise, Markus?

It is, I agree, but I have something else I must accomplish before the storm hits.

The mages should be able to close off the cavern without me, whereas without me, Artio will be left completely vulnerable. I'm hoping to convince the deris to come back with me, but it's possible they'll choose to stay with Artio – in which case it's not just one companion at risk, but three.

Josiah, I continue, before anyone can ask what I'm intending – I don’t want to test if they agree with my priority order, can you organise the Air Mages? We need to make sure the air inside the cavern remains breathable once we're shut in. And Layton, to answer your earlier question, we should still prepare to guard the inside of the settlement. We never know if something might become enraged enough to burst through our walls. Hopefully it will prove unnecessary, but better to guard for no reason than to be caught unprotected.

We continue the impromptu council a little longer with questions and general approaches to the crisis, then I meet up with Valence and my companions at the gates. Bastet, Ivor, Sirocco, and Noir are all willing to go and seek out the patrols still out in the forest, and I'm hopeful all of them will make it back before the storm hits. Since Sirocco is the only one who wouldn’t be able to carry anyone on her back if necessary, I send her to find all of the patrols to make sure we haven’t lost anyone.

Fortunately, although my companions don’t use them often, after seeing how easy they made communication on the journey, almost all of my Tier two beast Bonded asked Master Mirena for the same tattoos that allow the samurans to project their thoughts verbally. If Ivor or Noir have difficulty communicating, Sirocco will be there to help.

As soon as I'm confident the settlement is well on its way to being prepared, I hurry back into the forest, my hair already starting to stand on end as the oppression of the approaching storm presses on us. I don’t have time to check out the well so I’ll have to hope that Thessalanor has it handled.

Lathani catches up with me halfway down the slope, Ninja with her and Fenrir lagging behind as he runs as fast as he is capable.

You didn't think you were going alone, did you? she scolds me.

I give her a sheepish smile.

"Honestly, I wasn't thinking about it."

That earns me an even more pointed look.

You can't help our companions if you're attacked because you're alone, she points out.

"You're right," I admit, sending warm feelings of appreciation down the link to her and Ninja. "Good thing I've got you two to take care of me."

Three, Fenrir adds pointedly as he catches up, his sides heaving.

“Three,” I concede apologetically, sending him the same feelings of appreciation.

We hurry down the hill and into the forest. I've already spoken to Artemis and Orion, so there's no surprise when I burst onto the scene.

"Can I convince you to come to the settlement?" I urge Artemis, knowing she's the one making the decisions here.

No, she replies decisively. We will protect our packmate.

"What if your protections fail or a beast breaks through them?” I point out. “That's why I want you back with me – if the protections fail, you'll both be exposed to the storm."

And Artio will be dead, Artemis argues firmly. If we are alive after the storm, you can help us. You saved me from death once before. You will do it again.

Frustration rises within me. Yes, I did pull her back from the brink when she almost died along with the rest of her pack in the samuran world. But this is different.

"If you're mutated, there's no guarantee I'll be able to undo it," I argue.

It doesn't seem to change her mind.

I stay with my packmate, she tells me firmly.

And I stay with my mate, Orion agrees, pressing himself close to Artemis.

I eye them, then make a disgusted noise as I feel their commitment in the Bond. The only way I’m getting them away from here is by using the Bond to force them – and I refuse to do that.

Sighing, I gaze at Artio again, considering once more forcibly lifting him away.

The next moment I shake my head. Moving his body when his mind is lost in the network connected to the vines holding him might easily break the bond between them. Healing a mana mutation would probably be easier than reconnecting mind and body once the link between them is severed.

"Too loyal for your own good, that's what you are," I grouse at Artemis and Orion, though I know from the Bond that they're aware I'm not truly serious – or at least, that while I'm genuinely upset about them being in danger, I wouldn't change a thing about their loyalty.

"Alright then," I sigh. "Let's see what we can do to make sure all three of you are as protected as possible. But Artemis," I fix her with a serious look, "if the protection fails, you must tell me immediately, and I will do what I can. Worst comes to worst, I can pull all three of you to me with Bond Teleport.” I hate to think what that might do to Artio in his current state, but if it comes down to a question of life or death, I'll have to take the risk – he can't defend himself like this and I won’t lose all three of them.

Artemis meets my seriousness with her own.

We hear, packleader. If all seems lost, we will call for you.

I suppose I'll have to be satisfied with that.

It takes me some time to raise the shelter around them and ensure they have both food and water – hopefully enough for the duration. By the time we head back towards the settlement, the violet clouds are starting to crawl across the sky directly above us.
