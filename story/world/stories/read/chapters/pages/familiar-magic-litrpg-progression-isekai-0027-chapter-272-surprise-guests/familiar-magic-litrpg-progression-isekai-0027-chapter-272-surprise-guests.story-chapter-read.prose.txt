Selina struggled to stay upright as Walter thrashed in pain and confusion. Pepper let out a series of angry whines as he was tossed about the bunker, and Selina only avoided the same fate by hastily buckling herself into the single chair.

“We’re under attack!” she called into the communicator. Apollo couldn’t help directly–they had plans for that, not yet complete–but he could unleash Walter to do as demons always did. Thankfully, Apollo didn’t pause to ask questions.

Walter roared, the sound coming from its crustacean throat sounding like an orchestra from Earth, tuning its instruments. Then the Twilight demon attacked. Attacked everything, it seemed.

Selina’s monitors became a blur of flailing pincers, and the enormous crab-like creature knocked down every tree in sight. Selina was confused by his tactics until she noticed the vines. It looked as if hundreds of rope-thick yellow vines had reached out from the forest to bind Walter’s limbs, and his reaction was to destroy anything that looked similar.

In under a minute, the dense forest had become a clearing covered in shattered trees, yet Selina still couldn’t stop their attackers. She felt another impact as something struck them from behind, and another after that, from a different direction. Was it the trees? They’d never attacked something Twilight rank before, but who could know for sure?

“Selina! Give me an update!” Apollo demanded.

“I can’t see what’s attacking,” she said, trying to keep the panic from her voice. “Something is out there, and it’s trying to bring down Walter, but it’s staying in the trees.”

“Walter is angry, but not overly harmed,” Apollo reported. “I’m calling you back.”

“Wait!” Selina insisted. “This has to be what we’re looking for. We weren’t attacked until the moment Walter turned around–we must have been lured here. This is a trap!”

“Why do you sound excited about that?” Apollo demanded.

“It’s just…interesting,” Selina admitted. “Regardless, I don’t think we’re dealing with the undead here. I’ve never seen them use tactics this sophisticated and I think whatever is attacking us used nature magic.”

“That’s a rare affinity for demons,” Apollo said, sounding curious. “But look, we can come back. The army will be in that area in a few hours, and we can send a force into the woods that’s better suited for this job. Walter is a walking tank, he’s not exactly subtle.”

“Wait!” Selina said. “I saw something! Standby.”

She brought up the interface for the only thing closed to a weapon that had been included with the bunker. Fable had to provide the power source for the construct, and couldn’t make anything significant that would last. But Walter was a tank, like Apollo had said, so there were other options.

The ballista was mounted directly above her, on the roof of the bunker. Selina could select a monitor, then move a targeting reticule over what she wanted shot with a tracking arrow. She aimed directly where she’d seen something disappear into the woods, and a hollow sound from above heralded the missile flying out through the trees.

Remarkably, Selina heard a pained sound through the monitor’s speakers. Now that whatever demon that waited for her had been struck, she had no more role to play. Walter bellowed his challenge, then charged into the woods, thick, sharp-edged shell knocking over trees as his massive crab legs carried him after the retreating target.

“Selina, I don’t like this,” Apollo said.

“I’m not a huge fan myself,” Selina admitted. “But Walter seems okay, and whatever attacked us is running. We might never find it again if we let it go.”

“That doesn’t matter!” Apollo insisted. “We know there’s something ambushing demons in those woods, so we’ll just avoid the damned woods! You don’t need to risk yourself like this.”

“For all we know, it’s riskier to retreat! This thing could fight us all the way back to the army. Better not to let it think it’s won.”

“I think you’re just bored,” Apollo accused.

Selina grumbled. So what if she was? Hunting a mystery demon through the woods on top of a Twilight crab-beetle under her control was a nice break from sitting on a train, feeling useless. “We’ll turn around if we get any sense it’s another trap,” she conceded.

Apollo groaned loudly through the speakers. “Ten minutes. Walter turns around in ten minutes, whether you find something or not.”

Selina grinned. That was fair. She reached down to pat Walter’s shell–which was her floor–affectionately. “Good hunting, big guy,” she said, then leaned back in her chair.

Her eyes were so focused on searching for movement in the trees that she didn’t understand what she was seeing until the monitors were almost completely blocked. She signaled for Walter to halt, then looked in confusion at the forest around her. It was like they’d wandered into a sandstorm.

“Come to think of it,” she said while tapping her lip, “have the tree sounds been getting…strange?” She hadn’t considered it, but she’d been listening to the sounds of Walter ploughing through thick forest for hours, and the snapping of tree-trunks had become familiar. She commanded the demon to move again, and in a few moments the sound of more trees falling echoed around her.

Except…they weren’t falling. They were…shattering?

Suddenly she thought she knew what she was seeing outside, but sent an arrow into a nearby tree to test her hypothesis. A massive pincer swung out, crashing through the selected tree. It exploded into dust.

“Stone,” Selina said, disbelieving.

“What?” Apollo asked.

“This part of the forest has been turned to stone.”

***

Ethan stumbled through the rift he’d created–barely. The ritual had worked, and taken him right back to the receiving room he’d created inside the Fable Train. He moved through the hall into the larger section of the main car, ignoring the blood trail he was leaving.

“Selina!” he called. It was Fable who found him first, appearing in a whirlwind of metal shards.

“Doctor Bishop,” she said, her voice concerned. “You are not well. Would you like a place to lie down?”

“Selina!” he repeated. “Need her now,” he managed, before collapsing to his knees.

“Understood, Doctor Bishop.”

A hole opened up in the floor, and Ethan dropped his bloody burden on the floor as he watched it in confusion. Then he heard confused yelling, which grew louder until Selina rose up through the hole.

“Fable!” she said angrily. “What in the name of the Goddess–” she froze, upon seeing Ethan. Her eyes widened, and her hands began to glow in almost the same heartbeat. “Ethan, your arm!” she cried.

Ethan blacked out.

***

He didn’t know how long it had been when he regained consciousness, but he’d been moved to the small infirmary in the belly of the train. Apollo stood on his left, looking grim, while on his right, Selina cast spell after spell.

“How bad is it?” he asked.

Selina slumped against the bed, and Ethan’s instinctive reaction to catch her caused a flare of enormous pain. Selina tried to push him back down on the bed. “Don’t move!” she commanded. “The arm is barely attached as is.”

Ethan finally let himself look down at his right arm, which had been burned and flayed above the wrist. The fist-sized gap between shoulder and bicep now had bone and muscle, but Selina still had work to do.

“What happened?” Apollo asked.

“Followers,” Ethan said, spitting the word. “They’re here, and it’s worse than we feared. Far worse.”

“Hold still,” Selina said. “This is hard enough with you being Twilight, but your healing rune is somehow part of your arm. I have to regenerate it to regenerate you, and it’s a bottomless pit for mana.”

“Sorry,” Ethan forced out through the pain. “They say doctors are the worst patients.”

“Followers,” Apollo prompted. “What did you see? You only had two days left before things were going to get tricky.”

“I found them to the north-west. They’re traveling up the coast, and they’re raising an army of their own.”

Apollo swore. “That Familiar of theirs?” he guessed.

“Exactly right,” Ethan said. “The whole army was covered in that dark ink. We have to assume that they’re after the same thing we are.”

“But how could they know about it?” Apollo asked.

“No idea, but it’s how we have to operate. We can’t let them take the Throne.”

“I don’t disagree,” Apollo said. “So who did this to you? Did you try to fight the damned army?”

“No,” Ethan said grimly. “I took out a couple of their Twilights. I don’t think they have many, but there was someone else there. Someone Master rank. I didn’t realize it until it was too late, and he did this.”

“Bad luck,” Apollo said, shaking his head. “The other arm, and you could have handled it yourself.”

“Maybe,” Ethan said. “Healing myself is difficult, especially concentrating through the pain.”

Apollo sighed, and Ethan raised an eyebrow. “Why aren’t you healing me?” he asked.

Apollo laughed. “I already did. Selina’s not kidding, your fancy rune keeps drinking most of what we pump into your arm. She’s just taking a turn while I process some more mana–the army gives me what I need, but my own body has limits.”

Ethan nodded, letting his head fall back. “Their army was bigger than ours. Nothing but Twilights. Lots of them.”

“Damn,” Apollo said. “So we need to speed up recruitment? We have some ideas, but they’re incremental gains.”

“We need to speed up, or slow them down,” Ethan said. “That’s why I risked attacking their number. Their own recruitment should slow a bit.”

“Okay,” Apollo said. “Well, we have a new goal. We can brainstorm when you officially have two arms again.”

Ethan chuckled, the movement hurting. “So, how was your month?” he asked.

Apollo made a so-so gesture. “We’ve made some strides. Added to the army. Selina has a pet crab. Good things, but nothing revolutionary.”

“Any sign of the undead?” he asked.

“We don’t think so,” Apollo said. “We were losing some demons in a forest to the west, but we’re steering the army around it.”

“But you confirmed it wasn’t undead?” Ethan asked. “I don’t want to be surprised again.”

“We don’t think so,” Apollo said. “But it was strange. There was some kind of attack we couldn’t see, and when Selina gave chase, she found a stone forest. We figured it was best to just get away from there.”

Ethan had to stop himself from sitting up suddenly. “A forest…made of stone?”

“That’s what she said.” He looked at Selina, whose eyes were scrunched shut in concentration. “Anyway, she kept crashing through them, and the demon she went out on came back covered in dust.”

Tomo appeared at Ethan’s call. “Demons that can turn things to stone?” Ethan asked without preamble.

“I know of nothing capable of such from my world, Ethan Bishop,” Tomo said. “But we do know of something from your world.”
