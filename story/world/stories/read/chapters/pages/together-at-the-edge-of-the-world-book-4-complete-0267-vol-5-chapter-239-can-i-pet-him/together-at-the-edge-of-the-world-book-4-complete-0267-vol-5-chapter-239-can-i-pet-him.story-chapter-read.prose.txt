RIVERWALL

“What do you think they want?” Sybil asked, arms behind her head as she bounced ahead of Sammie, heading toward their employer, lord, and friends David and Niala as per their request.

“Probably to sack you,” he fired back.

She stopped cold. “What?! Why would they do that? I'm super useful!”

“Also super exhausting.”

She glared at him. “Just eat better and sleep more!”

“Anna won't let me do the second one.” He said, walking past her with his hands in his pockets. He smirked at her blush and hanging mouth.

His smirk didn't leave his face even when he was picked up from the ground and carried like a sack of worthless potatoes.

Niala stood in her backyard, flanked by David, looking at the two Scion Guards. Sybil looked upset, while Sammie's uniform was ruffled. She decided to ignore it and stick to what they had been called for.

“Sybil, Sammie, first, we wanted to thank you both for all your help. You could say it's just your job, but I know it means more than that to you! And you've proven it time and again in your dedication and support.”

Sybil's eyes lit up even as a light blush coloured her cheeks. Sammie tried to look nonchalant, but David smirked when he noticed the man stand a little bit straighter.

Niala bowed slightly before continuing. “You have also earned our trust, so we wanted to offer you something special.”

David continued. “That said, this is a gift you must never reveal where it came from. Niala created them, but because of their nature, we decided to only do so again if absolutely necessary. Most likely, they will remain unique creations.”

His wife nodded. “You'll understand when we show you and explain a bit more about it, but if you would rather not have this burden, tell us now; we won't hold it against you.”

They stood and looked at the two guards.

Sammie scratched the back of his head. “Could we ha-”

“We'll take it!” Sybil shouted, lifting a hand up.

The Allwardens looked from Sybil to Sammie; the latter sighed and weakly nodded. With a shrug, Niala retrieved the two starry mana cores from her satchel and held them up. “You'll have to choose. One of them is called Buddy, the other, Valeros.”

Sybil tilted her head. “The mana cores have names?”

The catkin smiled and mentally called forth the aetherial creatures, lobbing the cores ahead of her.

The guards' jaw dropped as indigo energy enveloped the magical orbs, expanding into an amorphous blob before taking the shape of a legendary creature: Falcodrakes.

But magical, indigo, shimmering ones.

Once fully formed, they looked at the two strangers present before turning their attention back to Niala. Her ears wiggled as she answered their silent question. “They're the two we talked to you about. If they accept you, we'd like you to work with them.”

The two creations looked back at Sybil and Sammie, both just about coming to grips with what they had witnessed, were witnessing.

Sammie was the first to find his words. “They're just mindless summons, right? They move as if they were alive, but that's not possible.”

Niala frowned at him. “Sammie! Apologize to Buddy and Valeros! They're not mindless at all!” She admonished, before walking up to the two falcodrakes and patting their plumed manes. “It's alright, he's a good person, he just didn't know.”

The guardsman blinked. “For real? Living, thinking spells? Founding gods, you two just don't care, do you? Hum, I mean, my lords.”

Sybil's ears were spearing the air, her eyes wide and glinting. “Which one's buddy? Can I pet him?”

Niala smiled and pointed her chin at the falcodrake in front of the bunnykin. “Go ahead. Buddy will let you know where he prefers being scratched.”

With a smile splitting her face and a little squeal, Sybil bounded up to the magical creature that towered over her, drawing her hand close to its neck and waiting. Buddy stared down at her, his eyes hard and impassive.

Fear of rejection flashed over Sybil's features, only to be replaced with mirth as the falcodrake lowered his head, gently pressing the part directly behind his eyes into her palm. She began softly scratching him, only for Buddy to press harder. Before long she was ruffling its feathers all over his head, and the aetheric lifeform gave up all pretense of decorum, trilling in delight at the head scratches.

Sammie and Valeros watched the display before looking at each other. The guardsman swallowed. “Ok, so, I'm sorry I said you were mindless. I didn't think my employers were capable of creating magical life. Can we work together?”

Valeros huffed, then walked up to the guardsman, not stopping when Sammie expected him to, and bodily shoved him back. The guardsman fell on his backside, yelping in surprise, before looking up in alarm, only to stop as he saw the Falcrodake's smug expression, its beak split open in a simile of a smirk.

Sammie narrowed his eyes at Valeros. “Oh, so you're like that, huh? A prankster.”

The creature ignored his remark and showed his head down at him, pushing intently against the man until he got the message and began giving scratches of his own. Unlike the effervescent Buddy who was wagging its feathered tail in joy, Valeros only grunted in satisfaction.

Niala looked at David with a pleased smile on her face. He sighed in return.

They had taken a bet on how long it would take for the pair to accept each other. He had lost.

One day he'd learn not to bet against his wife when it came to friendships.

They gave the new partners some time to get to know each other, and taught the Scion guards how to recall and summon their mounts. The process mesmerized Sybil, who repeated it until Buddy lost patience and pecked the top of her head.

Sammie found that very funny, which earned him a glare from the bunnykin.

Niala then asked the guards to recall the falcodrakes, in a way that made it clear she had something else to say.

The pair did as asked, and gave her their full attention.

She smiled. “Thank you. Before anything else, I'll just say that gifting you Buddy and Valeros doesn't have any strings attached. If you ever leave our employ, they're still yours. We'll only take them back if you use them for nefarious means.”

Sybil looked appalled. “I would never! I am a warrior for justice!”

“Yeah, what she said,” Sammie confirmed with a sigh.

Shaking her head, Niala continued. “I never expected you to, but I had to say it. The second thing I wanted to talk about is our excursion back into the hummers' territory. All preparations are done now, so we're ready to leave. We already asked you before, but we're asking again; will you come with us?”

Sybil pumped her fist. “Of course we'll come! It's to make sure nobody is turned into a white freak ever again, right?!”

David took a step forward. “In this case, Sybil, I want Sammie to answer for himself. We don't know how dangerous this will be, but you've heard about what happened when we found the One. You know it wasn't an easy fight,” he said, turning his eyes toward the guardsman.

Sammie looked surprised. “Oh... huh. I'm not used to that. Sybil usually just drags me along.”

The bunnykin stared at him. “You never said anything!”

“I... kind of always say something, but you ignore it.”

She tilted her head one side, then the other. “Can't remember. Are you sure?”

He sighed. “Yes, I'm sure, Sybil,” he said, looking back at David and Niala. “Either way, if Sybil goes, I need to go.”

Niala bit her lips. “You don't need to go, Sammie. We can ask other people, or we might be alright with just Sybil.”

He shook his head. “No. If something happened to Sybil and I wasn't there, I wouldn't forgive myself.”

Looking from Sybil to Sammie, Niala nodded. “Alright. If you're both decided. We're going to go test the blast grenade later today, and we're leaving two days from now. From what Anaakendi told us, it should take four to five days to get there if I can keep giving us a tailwind.”

She scrunched her mouth. “I've never kept it up for that long. Hopefully there's no issue, but, just in case, we'll plan for at least a full seasonal. Do you have any questions?”

Sybil lifted her hand. “What's a blast grenade?”

Sammie didn't say anything, but his curiosity was evident as well.

David and Niala shared a look. He looked at the two guards. “Let's go find out.”

The enchanters had warned that they didn't know what the safe distance was exactly. They had tested it on a very small scale, so they knew it worked, but the full-sized device had yet to be used.

David figured that a kilometre should be plenty, since the amount of pyressence held in the blast grenade should equate to about a hundred-meter conflagration.

Niala, David and Jordo flew out on their usual summoned bird, while Sybil and Sammie followed, riding their falcodrakes on their maiden flight.

Sybil hollered and whooped as Buddy took her into flips and loops at her insistence, while Sammie kept telling Valeros that a steady, level flight was perfectly acceptable.

His voice might have had a faint quaver as he repeated the line to his flying mount.

They reached an uninhabited region to the north, around the same place where the falcodrakes came to be, and took a holding pattern high up in the air. Nodding to Niala, David took out one of the blast grenades and let it fall.

A few moments later, it impacted the ground. They were too high to hear it, but the visual signs couldn't be missed; a small splash of the orange pyressence, followed by an ever-widening runic circle whose symbols repeated themselves over and over until they covered an area the size of a large house.

At the centre of the formation, the mana core visibly glowed as its energy was siphoned out without a care for its survival. The rune formation had a single goal: saturate the pyressence.

The substance began glowing within a second, as mana was forcibly injected into it, even while the orange splash grew to cover the whole of the rune circle.

The mana core's reserves bottomed out, and the pyressence, now spread very thinly over a large area, lost the force keeping it stable.

The observers up high in the sky had to shield their eyes. The first explosion was everything they expected; a large fireball roared upward, its blast wave devastating the area surrounding it.

The second, entirely unpredicted explosion, came up a second after the first. A miniature volcano erupted, shooting up red fiery slag several hundred meters up. A wall of hot air reached the group, right at the cusp of being painful.

Below, a wall of angry-looking smoke obscured their view, though they could see the surrounding vegetation had caught fire in a ring around the devastation.

Niala frowned and bent the winds to starve the flames and clear the smoke. The sight that waited had them gasp.

A deep crater, surrounded by jagged, broken terrain, occupied an area at least two hundred metres wide. Around this focal point, the ground was cracked and blackened, with the smouldering remnant of burned plants further out.

David and Niala looked at each other, then at the other two blast grenades in David's satchel.

Sybil's yowl broke them out of their trance, her voice reaching across the winds. “That was amazing!”

Following a second demonstration, neither the enchanters nor Marshalla and her colleagues could explain exactly why the explosion was that strong. Theories were thrown around, with the most accepted one being a resonance between the pyressence and the rune circle. Whatever the true reason, the end result was a devastating weapon which all parties agreed, though with some mourning from the scholars, should not be replicated.

The stock already created for David would be the total production run, and the data for its creation was committed to a mana-locked book, which was then entrusted to Cookie, with the instruction to never reveal its existence to anyone but David or Niala.

With that specific genie hopefully bottled up, there was nothing left to do but wait for the day of departure.

Niala let herself fall onto the couch, leaning back with her arms limp and closing her eyes. “Sometimes, I don't recognize my life.”

David sat down beside her, placing an arm around her shoulders. “How so?” He asked as she leaned into him.

“I ran away from home to set up a quiet alchemy shop away from all the hubbub. Now I'm married to the stormborn and the princess of a new nation.”

“You also have an alchemy shop.”

She cracked her eyes open and walked two fingers over his chest. “An alchemy shop that's not quiet at all, without talking about the Old Woman, all the enterprises we're running or funding, the thousands of people we're responsible for...”

He grasped her fingers and brought them to his lips, kissing them, before staring down into her eyes. “Do you wish things were different?”

“Hmm, some, maybe? Not you. You, I would have wanted no matter what. All the added fame, I could do without.” She said, her ears drooping.

A soft smile drew across his face. “I think you secretly like the fame, and you're just scared you'll let people down.”

Holding her gaze on him for a moment, she turned her head and burrowed her face into his chest, muffling her voice. “I'm just not sure if I'm the right person for all this. My sister would be so much better suited.”

He rested his head against hers, stroking her hair. “You're the perfect person, Kitten, because you care about everyone you affect. Angeline is efficient, but maybe a bit ruthless. She doesn't mind roughing up people along the way, as long as the goal benefits everyone.”

She sighed, speaking with a whine. “I knoooow! But it's stressful!” Pulling back her head, she looked him in the eyes. “What if we make a wrong decision and people get hurt?! Or even die!”

“Kitten, Niala, my lovely little angel. You can't control the world. We've done our best, and we'll keep going our best, and it's enough. We've already given back a people their future, and we're getting ready to face a millennia-old evil on the off chance we can save the souls of dead people.”

He shifted, turning her way, framing her face with his hands. “I know you'll keep doing your best to make the world a better place, as you've done, and every step of the way, I will love you, and I will support you. You and I, together. Use my shoulders to carry some of that weight.”

She stared back at him, eyes glistening, ears folded down.

He leaned forward, locking their lips together.

She melted into his arms.
