Above the street, above the torchlight, above the broken silhouettes of the slums, Viktor hovered.

Down there, the Iskora men continued their lazy patrol through the narrow street. Their torches trembled with their every idle shift, casting shaky light across fractured stone and moss-darkened walls.

There were five of them. Light armor. Dull-edged weapons. Clearly, they did not expect any trouble. They were confident their presence alone was enough to deter anything that might occur.

One of them spat into the dirt as they passed a collapsed building. Another adjusted his torch with a curse when a few stray embers drifted onto his hand. A man peered down a side alley and muttered something about “keeping the scum in line.”

None of them looked up. There was no reason to.

They remained oblivious to the fact that someone was right above their heads, watching them, weighing his options.

Actually, no.

There were no options to weigh.

Viktor had already decided his course of action.

There were days when he made careful plans, considering every scenario before he acted, days when he refused to do anything reckless, addressing every potential risk and taking every possible outcome into his calculations. But it was not today.

There were days when he was perfectly calm and not easily provoked, when he would never stoop so low as to take revenge on some petty thug over a cheap insult. But it was not this day.

Today, he dropped from the sky.

In an instant, he was before them.

The group froze. Their minds must have scrambled to bridge the gap between an empty street and a shadowy creature suddenly hovering in front of them, and come up with nothing that made sense.

Viktor let his gaze pass over their shocked faces with mild boredom, until it settled on the man in the centre—the one with the scar.

He raised both hands, and air surged at his command. Two concussive blasts of wind exploded outward, one for the two on the left, one for the two on the right. And while those men were still coming to terms with their newfound airborne regret, he was already moving. A glide forward, and he was on the scarred one. He took him by the collar, lifted him off his feet, and dragged him up to the sky.

The bastard screamed.

A raw, loud, and ugly sound that might have woken up half the slums already.

Yes, this was reckless. The Iskora adventurers would be alarmed by this. More guards would come. Mages, too. Maybe even someone like Zubayr or Spiky Head. He could end up in a completely unnecessary fight, and without any prior preparation. Tomorrow, he might regret what he was doing at the moment.

But tonight, he just couldn’t care less.

The screaming pig dangled helplessly in his grip as he ascended higher and higher. Then, once he had reached a high enough altitude, he turned and flew west. Daelin shrank behind him, slowly fading into a distant glow on the horizon, and he found himself being surrounded by a black, endless sea of woods.

“Do try to be quiet,” he told his captive. “Or I’ll throw you down.”

He didn’t bother using a Thauma to alter his voice. After all, what was the point? Why hide his identity from someone who was already a dead man walking—no, dead man hanging in midair?

The thug swallowed hard. And, sensibly, he shut up.

“I have a few questions for you. Speak the truth, for your own sake.”

“I... I’ll tell you everything... Anything you want to know... Just, please, let me go after—”

“Silence, please. Don’t say anything that is not related to what I ask. Do you understand?”

“...Yes.”

“Good. Now, first question. You know what happened on the west side this afternoon, right? The assault on an employee of the Adventurer’s Guild, in broad daylight, in the middle of the street?”

“Yes... yes... All of us have heard. And... we were quite surprised when learning about—”

“Who did it?”

“Xander,” the man said at once. “It was Xander.”

Viktor frowned. Spiky Head?

“You mean Xander the Mithril-ranked adventurer?”

“Yes, it’s him.”

“Why? Why did he do it? Why did he assault her?”

Was it because that man had somehow figured out his true identity and come for his supposed older sister? No, that didn’t make sense. If he had been exposed, they should have attacked him directly. The correct course of action should have been setting up an ambush with both Mithril and the other masked man. Besides, Jeanne had said the bastard wanted to take her away, and Claire was only attacked because she tried to protect her friend.

“I... I don’t know,” the scarred man replied. “But... he’s always been a violent man. We’re all afraid of him. We’re all keeping our distance. Because we’ve heard rumors. Stories about people who were beaten to death over nothing at all. So maybe she said something, or did something, that pissed him off.”

Viktor nodded. That much aligned with what he already knew of the brute.

“She and her friend were walking down the street, minding their own business,” he said. “It was he who came to stop them.”

“Then... I don’t know. Maybe... maybe he took a liking to their looks, so he wanted to try something?”

Ugh.

Well, the bastard did attempt to take Jeanne away. Still, was that really the reason, or was there anything else?

She was a pyromancer, after all, so she was one of their targets. But wasn’t abducting the fire mages supposed to be the masked men’s job? And, if that had been the intent, shouldn’t he have been more discreet? In the end, he made a mess of it, and failed to capture her anyway.

“Where is Xander now?”

“I don’t know. I think he’s already left Daelin. Maybe even gone back to Iskora.”

That, unfortunately, was plausible. With the Reliquaries that man possessed, distance wasn’t much of a problem. He could be anywhere in the world at the moment. Trying to pursue him would be a fool’s errand. A better plan would be to find a way to lure him back here. Well, the “dark spirit” suddenly appearing in town and attacking a patrol would certainly attract a lot of attention.

“Have you... have you finished with your questions?” the scarred man asked. “Can you let me go? You’ll let me go, right?”

“I did say I would not throw you down,” Viktor said.

So, true to his word, he threw him upward instead. Sure, the man would still come down in the end, but that was just the law of nature. If he had any grievance with that particular outcome, he should file a complaint with whichever god had written that law. Viktor had nothing to do with it.

And so he turned away from the collapsing arc of the scarred man’s descent, and drifted back toward Daelin at an unhurried pace. The wind took him east, carrying the echo of the scream behind him like an afterthought.

[Are you going to kill Xander, Master?]

“Yes,” Viktor said. “He was already a dangerous variable even before this incident, and his affiliation with the Children of the Abyss means he is our enemy by default. And now, he’s proven himself to be an unstable and unpredictable factor that could disrupt even the most carefully constructed plan. He is a mad dog, plain and simple. And a mad dog needs to be put down. Therefore, eliminating him will be our top priority before we proceed with anything else.”

[Is that... the reason, Master?]

Viktor frowned. “Of course that’s the reason. What else could it be?”

[...Very well. I will do my best to assist you in this matter.]

“Good. But we will not act rashly. He’s still a powerful opponent, and he’s not alone. He has a large, ancient organization to back him up. I’m not going to challenge him without proper preparation,” Viktor said.

What he had done with the scarred man, what he had just allowed himself to do, was undoubtedly a mistake, one that could lead to many consequences he couldn’t foresee. Well, what was done was done. There was no point in dwelling on it. Still, it did not mean he should do it again.

Come to think of it, this wasn’t the first time he had let emotion cloud his judgment. For example, when he ordered the entire royal family of Lyndor impaled in public. That one decision had so many lasting consequences that plagued his reign for years to come. Of course, he didn’t regret it. If he were given the chance, he would happily do the same thing again. Nevertheless, he wished he had taken more time to consider what would follow, and to properly prepare for it before the act. If he had done that, much of the later trouble could have been avoided.

So, yes. Planning was important.

Also, he shouldn’t keep everything to himself. He needed to talk it through with Celeste, with his Guardians, and perhaps even with his new “friend,” the Eternal Flame, before finally making his move.

“Do you have anything to add regarding the current situation?” Viktor asked.

[Well, yes. There is one thing that I found a bit strange about the account of what happened today.]

“What is it?”

[How, exactly, did Jeanne manage to escape?]

Viktor stopped.

That was... a very good question.

The story, as it had been told to him, began with Xander stopping Claire and Jeanne in the street, then escalated into the brute assaulting Claire when she stepped in for her friend. And it ended with Jeanne carrying the unconscious Claire to the Sanctuary to seek help.

But then, what happened in the middle?

In a situation like that, a clean disengagement was simply impossible. There was just no way Xander would have allowed Jeanne to leave. He would not have stopped on his own. And he was a Mithril, not some low-ranking adventurer she could realistically force to back down.

So... how did she manage to get away?

And, more importantly, why didn’t he realize at once such an obvious contradiction the moment he heard it?

He had done so many things that were uncharacteristic of him today. And he didn’t like that.

Viktor sighed.

“Let’s go back to the Sanctuary,” he said. “We’ll see if she’s ready to tell us the full story now.”
