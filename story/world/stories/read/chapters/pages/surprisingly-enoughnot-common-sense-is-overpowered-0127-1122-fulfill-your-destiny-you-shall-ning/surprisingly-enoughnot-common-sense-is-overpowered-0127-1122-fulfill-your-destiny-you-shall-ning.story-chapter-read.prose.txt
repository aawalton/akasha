Ning stared at the materials now sitting in front of him as though they were evidence from a crime scene, which, considering what had happened to some of them, might have been the more accurate description.

He knew what they were supposed to be. He knew what they had looked like half an hour ago. What he didn't know was what sequence of decisions had connected those two points.

People often said that creativity was one of humanity's greatest virtues.

Ning was beginning to think they should have added some conditions to that statement.

And that wasn't even mentioning the other ingredients. What the hell had she done to the Violet Dusk Flower?

His attention settled on the flower, or what he assumed was the Violet Dusk Flower. Ning stared at its strange discoloration for several breaths, trying to understand how it had possibly ended up in its current condition, before slowly turning toward Station Sixteen.

Feng Yue was standing in front of his materials.

She had already inspected the neatly prepared Crimson Vein Root paste, the separated Ironheart Seed oil, the cleaned Silvercap Mushroom, and the other preparations Ning had spent the first half of the stage carefully completing. Whatever tension had appeared on her face when Fu Xuan announced the exchange was completely gone.

She even looked pleased.

Ning couldn't help himself. "Senior Sister Feng, what in the heavens did you do to these? Especially the Violet Dusk Flower. It looks creepy."

Feng Yue visibly stiffened at the sound of his voice before quickly straightening herself. "Ahaha, Junior Brother! How are you?"

"I could be better." Ning held up the unfortunate flower.

Feng Yue's eyes followed it, and for perhaps the first time since Ning had met her, some of that effortless confidence began to crack.

"T-this sort of preparation work is usually handled by other people," she explained, though she recovered quickly enough to lift her chin. "I naturally know how to do it, but I don't practice the basic parts very often, so my material processing may be... slightly worse than the rest of my alchemy."

Ning looked back at the table.

Slightly?

If this was slightly worse, he was almost afraid to ask what she considered bad.

This Violet Dusk Flower had seen things that no flower should witness.

He picked it up carefully, stared at the strange discoloration running through two of its petals, then put it back down before whatever Feng Yue had done somehow became contagious.

To be fair, Ning had tried very hard to metagame this competition.

He hadn't spent the last three months blindly refining pills and hoping that whatever he happened to practice would appear on the day of the competition.

Fu Xuan had already given them plenty of clues: material identification and preparation, flame control, refinement, adaptability, medicinal understanding, and the final product. The exact tests were unknown, but there was more than enough information for Ning to make some reasonable predictions.

A direct pill-refining test was almost guaranteed, and flame control was obvious enough that he'd practiced both extensively.

Adaptability and medicinal understanding were harder to predict. They could be tested through incomplete formulas, substitutions, unfamiliar materials, unexpected problems during refinement, or dozens of other methods, so Ning had simply prepared as broadly as he reasonably could.

With only three months, there was no way he could catch up to people who had been studying alchemy for years by simply practicing harder. Material processing had seemed particularly worth focusing on because every pill started with ingredients, so instead of memorizing another hundred specific preparation methods, Ning had spent part of his time studying the common principles behind them.

Roots, leaves, flowers, seeds, fruits, bark, fungi, and vines all tended to present different problems. Some medicinal properties concentrated in particular structures, some materials needed their moisture preserved while others needed it removed, and sometimes the correct answer was simply to cut away a damaged portion instead of wasting time trying to save every scrap.

It wouldn't make him an expert on every ingredient he encountered, but it usually gave him somewhere to start.

It also helped that this wasn't his first time processing medicinal materials. He had done plenty of it for Ling Xin, and even before becoming her disciple, he'd handled ingredients while working with Su Fan.

That preparation had paid off.

The first half of the stage had gone almost exactly as Ning hoped. He had understood what the test was asking, his preparation covered most of what he encountered, and for a brief, beautiful period, he had even thought he'd read the intentions of whoever designed the stage rather well.

Then Fu Xuan had told everyone to swap tables.

Ning looked down at Feng Yue's work again.

"I really need to improve my ability to expect the unexpected."

In the end, even with all that preparation, he'd still come up short.

He had prepared for unfamiliar materials, damaged ingredients, different processing methods, and several other possibilities. What he had not prepared for was another alchemist getting there first and committing six separate crimes against medicinal plants.

"Senior Sister Feng, you really got me this time."

Feng Yue gave an awkward chuckle from the neighboring station. "D-don't worry. This is a test, isn't it? If you think about it, receiving more difficult materials gives you an even better opportunity to demonstrate your ability."

Ning stared at her.

Feng Yue's smile stiffened slightly under his gaze, but instead of retreating, she seemed increasingly convinced by her own argument. "Exactly. Someone receiving perfectly prepared materials has nothing to demonstrate, but you, on the other hand, can show the judges just how talented you are at adapting to unexpected circumstances. Really, this might even be better for you."

"Senior Sister-"

"I should focus on my own materials now. The competition is still ongoing." With that, Feng Yue walked away.

Ning stared at her back.

She had actually handed him a table full of medicinal casualties, called it an opportunity to demonstrate his talent, and dipped.

Ning looked toward her station and watched Feng Yue pick up his perfectly prepared Green Tendril Vine. She inspected it from both sides, gave a small, dignified nod as though confirming that his work met her standards, then placed it back down.

"...Shameless."

Ning wasn't quite sure how to feel about that. As a fellow follower of the same Dao, he could almost respect the audacity, but being on the receiving end of it gave him decidedly mixed feelings.

With a sigh, Ning returned his attention to the disaster in front of him. Complaining wasn't going to earn him any points, and irritatingly enough, Feng Yue wasn't entirely wrong. If the purpose of this half was really to test adaptability, then being handed a worse situation also gave him more room to show what he could do with it.

His expression gradually settled. There was no point wishing he'd received better materials when these were the ones in front of him, the clock was still running, and whatever score he earned from here depended entirely on what he did next.

Ning drew a slow breath as luminous blue light appeared in his eyes, Pure Eyes revealing the damaged materials before him in far greater detail, then rolled up his sleeves.

All right. Let's do my best.

...

Up in the viewing stands, Ling Xin stared at Station Seventeen for several seconds before letting out a sympathetic noise. "Ugh. Ning really drew a bad hand."

That was putting it mildly.

The exchange had transformed the competition grounds almost instantly. Some contestants had received materials barely worse than their own, while others now stood before preparations handled according to methods completely different from what they would have chosen.

A few had clearly benefited from the exchange, while several looked as though they had just been betrayed by the very heavens.

Ning's situation was among the worst of them.

Cai Yun took one look at the blackened Silvercap Mushroom and couldn't suppress a laugh. "That seems troublesome."

Ling Xin immediately glared at her senior sister. "You don't have to sound so happy about it."

"Tsk, tsk. I'm merely appreciating the difference in fortune." Cai Yun's lips curved upward. "Maybe being such a good spiritual farmer wasn't a blessing after all."

Her attention shifted toward Yue Chan, who had received a poorly processed set of materials herself. Several of them would need correcting, but none appeared completely ruined.

Yue Chan's exchanged Crimson Vein Root had been processed too coarsely, the Ironheart Seed oil contained visible impurities, and whoever had handled the Green Tendril Vine had stripped away too much of the softer outer fiber. None of it was ideal, but Yue Chan had already begun calmly sorting through the problems.

Su Fan's exchange wasn't much better. He had inherited a Moondew preparation whose frost-veins had been only partially removed and a Violet Dusk Flower that had been heated too unevenly, though the materials were still recognizable enough that Tang Feiyu didn't appear much concerned.

Cai Yun looked from the others back to Station Seventeen. "So yes, I'm quite happy."

A few strands of Ling Xin's hair began turning red.

"Aunt, calm down. Please." Ling Yinran quickly caught her by the arm.

Cai Yun's smile stiffened. She quietly took several steps back. As much as she enjoyed annoying her junior sister, she wasn't foolish enough to keep pushing once Ling Xin's hair started changing color.

Tang Feiyu, reassured that Su Fan was handling his own exchange well, finally decided to intervene. "Cai Yun, stop antagonizing Ling Xin. We both know how that usually ends."

"Hehe. That's right." Ling Xin immediately brightened. "Listen to your senior, Senior Sister Cai Yun."

Ling Yinran gave Tang Feiyu a grateful look before gently pulling her aunt back into her seat. "Aunt, we're here to cheer for Junior Brother. If you make a scene, you might distract him. You know how perceptive he is."

They both knew Ning couldn't actually hear them from this distance, but Ling Yinran wasn't about to take the chance.

"Yes, listen to your niece, Junior Sister," Cai Yun said, only to pause when something occurred to her. She turned toward Tang Feiyu. "Also, Senior Sister, what exactly did you mean by how that usually ends? I'm pretty strong too, you know."

Tang Feiyu regarded her for a moment, then gave a slow, reassuring nod. "Of course. We all know you're strong."

Cai Yun stared at her. "That somehow sounds even more pitying when you say it like that."

Ling Xin naturally took the opportunity to laugh, earning another groan from her senior sister.

Sometimes, Tang Feiyu wondered how both of her junior sisters had ended up with such troublesome personalities. The two were like fire and water, always finding some reason to compete with each other.

Well, mostly it was Cai Yun trying to compete with Ling Xin. But that was beside the point.

Still, something about the exchanges bothered her.

"Do you really think these pairings were random?"

The other three looked at her.

Tang Feiyu set her cup down. "Master said the stations were assigned randomly at the beginning. She never said the exchanges would be as well."

Ling Xin paused.

Cai Yun's attention immediately returned to the competition grounds.

Now that Tang Feiyu had mentioned it, there was a pattern. Several contestants who had performed well during the first half appeared to have received noticeably worse materials, while some of the weaker performers had suddenly inherited preparations in much better condition than what they had produced themselves.

Not every pairing made it obvious, but there were enough examples that it was becoming difficult to dismiss this as mere coincidence.

Tang Feiyu's gaze settled on Ning. "I think Master paired the stronger first-half performances with the weaker ones."

Ling Xin's face twisted. "...That sounds exactly like Master."

Cai Yun considered it for a moment before slowly nodding. "It would also explain why Yue Chan received one of the poorer sets."

"And Su Fan," Tang Feiyu added. "If the second half is supposed to test adaptability, giving everyone an equally difficult problem wouldn't necessarily tell the judges much. Someone who demonstrated better processing in the first half can reasonably be given more difficult mistakes to deal with."

Ling Xin leaned back in her seat with a sigh. "Master has always been like that. The moment you show her you're good at something, she makes it harder."

There was no real complaint in her voice. Fu Xuan had trained all three of them that way. Her reasoning had always been simple: if a task was already comfortably within their abilities, then repeating it would teach them far less than attempting something just beyond them.

Cai Yun's lips curved upward as she watched Yue Chan begin repairing her inherited materials. "In that case, the first-half results should be interesting. It seems Chan'er may have been among the highest."

Tang Feiyu glanced at her. "Junior Sister Cai, perhaps wait for the scores before you start gloating. There are quite a few promising seedlings in this batch."

"Exactly. Don't count my disciple out yet," Ling Xin added.

Cai Yun ignored both of them and looked toward Ning again.

He still hadn't started working. Instead, he was inspecting each damaged preparation in turn, occasionally checking the jade plaque beside them.

"If his first-half performance was good enough to earn that set, then I suppose he's already done well. But this part may simply be beyond him." Cai Yun indicated the table with her chin. "Unless he's learned a high-level restoration or essence-separation technique, some of those materials cannot be returned to their proper state within the remaining time."

"Who said he's going to return them to their proper state?"

Ling Xin's response came so easily that Cai Yun looked over.

Her junior sister was smiling now.

Tang Feiyu noticed it too. "You think he has another method?"

"No idea."

Cai Yun sometimes wondered whether Ling Xin was entirely right in the head. After knowing her for this long and witnessing things like this, she already knew the answer to that.

Ling Xin shrugged. "I didn't teach him some secret technique for repairing massacred medicinal herbs, if that's what you're asking. But you two don't know Ning as well as I do. When he runs into a wall, he has a bad habit of asking whether he actually needs to climb it in the first place."

That habit had occasionally been irritating during lessons. Tell Ning that a certain step was necessary and there was a decent chance his next question would be why. Once he understood the reason, he was perfectly willing to follow it, but if the answer turned out to be tradition, convenience, or simply because most alchemists lacked another option, his mind immediately started wandering elsewhere.

Over time, Ling Xin had learned to enjoy that part of teaching him.

Her disciple was strange, but that strangeness was also what made teaching him fun.

So, while she had no idea what Ning would do, she trusted him to come up with something.

Almost as though he'd been waiting for her to think that, Ning finally started to move.

The Crimson Vein Root paste went to one side. The crushed Ironheart Seeds joined it. After examining the Silvercap Mushroom, Ning placed it farther back, then moved the Moondew Leaves and their cloudy liquid into another group. The tangled Green Tendril Vine followed after a brief inspection.

The Violet Dusk Flower took him the longest.

Pure Eyes glimmered faintly as Ning turned it beneath the light, and a moment later, the subtle aura of Season-Reading Eye joined it. He inspected the petals, checked the remaining time, then placed the flower by itself.

Tang Feiyu understood in an instant. "Smart."

Cai Yun narrowed her eyes. "He's sorting them by condition?"

"Not only condition." Tang Feiyu watched Ning pick up the jade plaque again before rearranging two of the materials. "He's deciding which ones are worth saving in the first place, especially with the time limit."

Most contestants had immediately begun processing or salvaging whatever material looked worst. Yue Chan had started with the most time-sensitive preparation and was methodically restoring what she could, while Su Fan had already discarded a damaged portion of one ingredient before continuing with the rest.

Simply put, Ning was taking a broader view.

After examining them together, he seemed to be deciding how much time each one deserved, which portions could still be saved, and which losses were better accepted.

"That might be the most sensible choice he could have made," Tang Feiyu said as she watched him rearrange the materials. "He's not trying to restore everything. With the condition some of those are in, he'd waste the rest of the stage before he finished."

Ling Xin immediately looked pleased, though Cai Yun caught the expression and shook her head. "Don't start looking smug yet. He's only figured out how he wants to approach it."

"I didn't say anything, Senior Sister."

Ling Xin settled back into her seat with an innocent expression, though the slight lift of her chin said more than enough.

Below, Ning began with the crushed Ironheart Seeds. They looked terrible, but the requirement was to separate the medicinal oil rather than preserve the seeds themselves, so he simply accepted Feng Yue's mistake as his new starting point. He recovered what oil he could from the broken shells and fibers, then stopped once further purification threatened to cost more time than it was worth.

The same principle carried through the rest of the table.

He discarded the ruined portion of the Crimson Vein Root instead of wasting time trying to recover every scrap, cut away the blackened section of the Silvercap Mushroom and let Fenghuang clean what remained, then sacrificed the worst portions of the tangled Green Tendril Vine until he had enough intact fibers to produce a shorter but usable medicinal thread.

"He's discarding quite a lot. That's going to cost him some points," Cai Yun observed as another damaged portion landed in the waste tray.

Tang Feiyu nodded. "There wasn't much he could do with those unless he'd learned some advanced processing techniques."

The Moondew Leaves took somewhat longer, but Ning approached them in much the same way, recovering what usable medicinal liquid he could from the cloudy mixture before moving on. At this point, he had given up on chasing the last traces of purity.

Only the Violet Dusk Flower made him hesitate, and even from the viewing platform, it was obvious why. Whatever Feng Yue had attempted had damaged several petals and accelerated the flower's decline badly enough that there was no clean way to restore it.

Ning studied it for several breaths before gathering Hidden Ice Mist around the damaged petals, slowing their deterioration as he carefully began extracting what little medicinal essence remained.

It was easier said than done.

Even with his Pure Eyes guiding him, the slightest mistake caused more of the essence to dissipate, forcing him to repeatedly adjust his qi as the petals continued to wither.

By the time he finished, the flower was practically a dried husk.

And all he had managed to extract was a single drop.

Ning stared at the single violet droplet for several seconds before letting out a long, weary sigh. His forehead was covered in sweat, and even his hands trembled slightly as he carefully set the droplet aside.

All that effort, and this was everything he had managed to salvage.

Tang Feiyu gave a small nod when Ning finally set it aside. "Not bad. He kept his head and got something usable from all six."

"Of course he did." Ling Xin crossed her arms.

Cai Yun glanced at her junior sister and clicked her tongue. "He certainly has a cooler head than you. If someone had handed you that table at his age, I'm fairly certain half the courtyard would already be on fire."

Ling Xin opened her mouth to object, but after looking down at Ning's salvaged materials, she paused and actually seemed to consider it.

"You know what, Senior Sister? I won't argue with you this time. I probably would've looked at all that damage, decided restoring everything was too troublesome, and thrown whatever was still usable into the furnace to see if I could refine something new out of it."

Tang Feiyu stared at her for a moment before sighing. "The task was to prepare six separate medicinal materials, Ling Xin. Turning them into one mystery pill would defeat the purpose."

"I know that now," Ling Xin replied, sounding entirely unrepentant. "But you asked what I would've done when I was younger. Younger me would've thought that if everything came out as a usable pill in the end, then technically I'd completed the important part."

Cai Yun gave her a long look before shaking her head. "And you used to wonder why younger disciples were reluctant to apprentice under you."

"Senior Sister, my excellent teaching reputation has nothing to do with this."

Ling Yinran, who had remained wisely silent beside her aunt, almost couldn't help but laugh at that.

Who didn't know the reputation of the Spiritual Flower Peak?

At this point, Ling Yinran only hoped that her junior brother could salvage that as well.

...

"That's all. Everyone, hand over the materials you've processed." Fu Xuan's voice carried across the court as the attendants began moving between the stations.

The contestants stepped back while their preparations were collected and brought to the judges. It took some time to inspect everything and compare the results with the records stored in the jade plaques, but eventually the enormous jade screen behind the judges lit up with the rankings.

Ning's attention immediately went to the top.

First — Shen Lu.

Ning recognized the name. Among the gathered participants, he was one of the older ones, and his foundation was said to be very solid.

Then Ning saw the next name.

Second — Ji Ning.

"Being a little performative was worth it."

Ning smiled.

He had realized halfway through that there was no saving Feng Yue's materials properly. Once that became clear, he'd stopped trying to force the impossible and focused on getting the best usable result from what remained.

More importantly, if the materials couldn't be fully recovered, then at the very least, he could make sure the judges saw that he understood what had gone wrong and how they should have been handled.

Thankfully, he'd read the test correctly.

Ning let out a quiet sigh of relief.

Thank heavens.

This would certainly help his final ranking, especially since this was probably the stage where he held one of his biggest advantages over the others.

Third — Yue Chan.

Yue Chan was just plain good, that's all.

Luo Xiulan took fourth, while Su Fan came fifth. When Ning glanced his way, Su Fan was still studying the rankings, but after noticing Ning looking, he simply gave him a small nod.

This was only the beginning. The next two stages would probably involve an actual furnace.

Some distance away, Feng Yue eventually found her own name much farther down the ranking. She stared at it for several breaths, frowned thoughtfully, then slowly nodded to herself as though the result had confirmed some private theory.

Ning decided that whatever conclusion she'd reached was probably safer left unexplored.

Once everyone had seen the rankings, Fu Xuan rose from the judges' platform and waited for the remaining conversations to settle. "The first stage tested your understanding of medicinal materials before they enter the furnace. Many of you performed well while following familiar methods, only to struggle once those materials were no longer in the condition you expected. Remember that an alchemist will rarely work under perfect circumstances. Sometimes the correct choice is not to repair what has already been damaged, but to understand what remains and make proper use of it."

As she spoke, attendants entered the court carrying large wooden racks. They arranged them in the open space at the center of the competition grounds, filling the shelves with roots, flowers, fungi, seeds and other medicinal materials until a temporary treasury had taken shape before the contestants.

There was an obvious problem with it.

There were forty-two contestants, but many of the trays contained nowhere near forty-two portions.

Ning's attention sharpened.

Another group of attendants moved between the stations and placed an identical jade box beside every furnace. Ning opened his and found a plump silver-grey mushroom resting inside, faint veins running across its cap like threads of metal.

Silver Vein Mushroom.

It was one of the rarer First-Tier ingredients. Its value came from how readily its essence merged with other medicinal properties and helped carry them through the body, which made it useful as the principal medicine in a surprisingly wide variety of First-Tier pills.

Fu Xuan gestured toward the boxes. "For the second stage, each of you has received an identical portion of Silver Vein Mushroom. Using it as the principal medicine, you will formulate and refine a First-Tier pill with a clear medicinal effect. There is no prescribed formula. What you make is entirely up to you."

That immediately drew more interest from the contestants, though most eyes quickly shifted toward the temporary treasury in the center of the court.

"The supplementary materials before you are all that will be provided, and there is deliberately not enough for everyone to choose the same ingredients. You will have one incense stick to gather what you need." Fu Xuan's gaze swept across them. "However, attacks intended to cause serious injury are prohibited. Once a medicinal material has been brought back to your station, it is considered secured and may no longer be contested."

Ning looked back toward the treasury and couldn't help but muse.

Well, who would've thought there'd be fighting in a xianxia competition?

The test itself was simple.

They had to decide what they wanted to make, obtain the necessary materials before someone else did, and change their plans if those materials became unavailable. More importantly, rushing toward the most valuable ingredients wasn't necessarily the best choice.

Several obvious combinations stood out immediately, and apparently everyone else saw them too. Clearheart Grass, Stillwater Moss and Silvermind Root had already attracted enough attention that half a dozen contestants were subtly shifting their positions toward them before the stage had even begun.

Yue Chan was watching the same area, although her attention moved between several shelves rather than fixing on a single ingredient. Su Fan seemed more interested in a cluster of pale-golden flowers farther toward the center, while Shen Lu was studying several trays of seeds that almost nobody else appeared to care about.

Ning had no intention of fighting twenty people for Clearheart Grass.

The Silver Vein Mushroom gave him plenty of room to work with.

His attention eventually settled on several less popular materials. Brightmind Flower reduced mental sluggishness and stimulated mental activity, while Drifting Cloud Pollen had the unusual property of making thoughts less rigid, allowing associations to form more readily between ideas that normally remained separate.

Neither was particularly valuable, but Ning had experimented with both during his preparation and knew they could be balanced with Whispering Reed, whose medicinal nature helped prevent an active mind from becoming too scattered.

With Clearwater Lotus Pith to settle the entire mixture, he could probably refine something useful for long periods of study or experimentation, keeping the mind active and flexible without sacrificing too much concentration.

It wasn't the strongest formula available, but that was precisely why Ning liked his chances of actually obtaining everything he needed.

Fu Xuan raised her hand, and the court fell silent for the brief moment before it dropped.

"Begin."

The center of the shared treasury immediately descended into chaos.

Ning activated Shadow Steps and moved with the crowd, though unlike most of the contestants around him, he had very little interest in fighting anyone if he could avoid it. Phantom Veil spread over him at full strength at the same time, suppressing his presence and subtly distorting the impression of his movements.

He wasn't invisible. Anyone who actually looked at him could still see him clearly enough, but in the middle of dozens of cultivators rushing in different directions, Ning became strangely difficult to keep track of. His presence seemed almost phantom-like, there one moment and slipping somewhere else the next, while anyone trying to follow his movements found their attention naturally drifting toward the far louder and more aggressive contestants around him.

Combined with Shadow Steps, it was perfect for exactly what Ning intended to do.

Avoid everyone else's problems.

He had barely taken three steps before someone shouted, "Iron Mountain Fist!"

A fist covered in dull yellow qi slammed into another contestant's crossed arms, sending him stumbling backward and clearing a path toward a tray of Clearheart Grass.

"You bastard! I saw it first!"

"Then you should've gotten there first!"

The second disciple recovered and charged straight back in, apparently deciding that the Clearheart Grass was worth settling the matter with violence.

Ning quietly went around them.

A burst of heat erupted somewhere to his right as another contestant swept both hands forward. "Blazing Palm!"

"Are you insane?!"

The target threw himself sideways, and the palm missed him by inches, tearing through the space he'd occupied a moment earlier. Before either contestant could recover, three others immediately took advantage of the opening and dove for the same tray.

"Get away! That's mine!"

"Dream on!"

"Stone Skin!"

Someone's arm turned grey just before an elbow smashed into it. Another disciple vaulted over both of them, only to have his ankle caught halfway down.

"You! Let go!"

"Not until you drop the Moonshade Fruit!"

Ning slipped past while they argued.

This was going wonderfully.

For him, at least.

Brightmind Flower was tucked near the outer edge of the treasury, and apparently nobody considered it valuable enough to use a martial technique over. Ning reached the tray without trouble, took one portion, and immediately moved on.

Ahead, two contestants were exchanging blows over a bundle of Stillwater Moss. One ducked beneath a sweeping leg and drove a palm toward the other's shoulder, only for the second to twist aside and catch his wrist.

Neither of them seemed interested in the tray of Drifting Cloud Pollen sitting nearby.

So, Ning yoinked a portion and jingled away.

"Coiling Serpent Hand!"

"Don't you dare-"

The first contestant abruptly left the ground and came flying directly toward Ning.

Ning shifted aside almost without thinking, and the unfortunate disciple sailed past him before hitting the ground, rolling twice, and coming to a stop against the bottom of a medicinal rack.

Already injured from the fight, the man struggled to raise his head. His gaze swept past Ning once before abruptly snapping back.

For a moment, he simply stared at the faint, almost ghostlike figure standing right in front of him.

Then his gaze dropped.

Brightmind Flower in one hand. Drifting Cloud Pollen in the other.

His expression slowly twisted.

"Y-you've just been collecting ingredients while we're fighting?"

Ning glanced down at his full hands.

"Um. Yes." He gave the man a pleasant smile. "Please continue with whatever you were doing. I have places to be."

Then he stepped around him and continued on his way.

The disciple stared after him, his face twitching. Perhaps it was his injuries, perhaps it was the sheer injustice of what he'd just witnessed, but he suddenly spat out a mouthful of blood and fainted.

Across the treasury, Yue Chan had chosen a different approach. She moved directly through the center, avoiding confrontations when possible but refusing to yield whenever someone actually blocked her path.

A young man reached for the same medicinal root she did, and the two exchanged several rapid palm strikes before Yue Chan abruptly changed rhythm, caught his wrist, and redirected his momentum just far enough to reach the tray first.

The root disappeared into her hand.

Not far away, Su Fan was having much less peaceful negotiations.

"Put that down!"

"No."

A palm came toward his chest, but Su Fan blocked it with his forearm, stepped inside the attack, and drove his shoulder into the other disciple hard enough to send him stumbling several paces backward. He snatched the pale-golden flower from the tray and immediately retreated before the man could recover.

"Come back!"

"I already have what I wanted. Why would I do that?"

It was a fair point.

Ning spotted the Whispering Reed farther ahead, but this time two other contestants were already closing in on the same tray.

He glanced upward.

Fenghuang, circling above the treasury, immediately noticed.

Ning gave the slightest tilt of his head toward the two contestants. The raven understood as he suddenly dropped between them.

"What the-"

Fenghuang spread his wings directly in front of the first contestant's face, forcing him to stumble back, then swept past the second close enough for his feathers to brush the man's hair.

"Whose bird is this?!"

"How would I know?!"

"That wasn't a real question!"

Both contestants turned toward Fenghuang as the raven landed on a nearby rack and stared back at them with complete innocence.

Neither noticed Ning slip between them.

By the time the first disciple remembered the Whispering Reed, Ning had already taken the bundle from the tray and was running away.

Fenghuang quickly rejoined him with a pleased caw.

"Good work."

That made three. Ning tucked the Whispering Reed away and quickly went over what he had collected.

The idea he'd started with was actually related to the Wakeful Spirit Pills he occasionally used, or rather, one of their improved recipes.

The Clear Wake Pill.

A small amount of Brightmind Flower would help keep the user alert without leaving the mind dull or sluggish, while Drifting Cloud Pollen loosened thoughts that had become stuck on the same thing for too long. Whispering Reed would keep the increased mental activity from becoming scattered, and the Silver Vein Mushroom waiting at his station could serve as the principal medicine that brought the different effects together.

What he still needed was Clearwater Lotus Pith. Its gentler medicinal nature would soften the more stimulating ingredients and keep the user's mind calm and clear.

If everything went properly, the result wouldn't do anything impressive. It was simply a better version of the Wakeful Spirit Pill, allowing someone to remain awake and focused for longer without leaving their thoughts agitated afterward.

There were one or two supplementary ingredients he could add if he happened to find them, but Clearwater Lotus Pith was the only thing he actually needed.

Fortunately, he spotted the pale-white pith among several trays deeper in the treasury. Once again, nobody seemed particularly interested in the Clearwater Lotus Pith itself, though the same couldn't be said for everything around it.

Perfect.

Ning started making his way toward it, slipping around a pair of contestants wrestling over a bundle of medicinal grass.

"Heaven-Splitting-"

Ning ducked beneath a stray arm and continued toward the center of the treasury. Unfortunately, the Clearwater Lotus Pith was right in the middle of the most heavily contested area, where several long tables packed with medicinal ingredients had attracted nearly half the remaining contestants.

Cultivators crowded between the tables, grabbing whatever they could while exchanging palm strikes, movement techniques, and bursts of qi whenever two people reached for the same ingredient.

Ning slipped through the confusion with Shadow Steps, using the fights as a cover. When one contestant was knocked away from a table, he crossed through the opening; when two others collided over a bundle of herbs, he simply went around them.

Eventually, the tray holding the Clearwater Lotus Pith came within reach.

Ning immediately moved toward it, only to hear a familiar shout somewhere to his left.

"Ha! Got it!"

Feng Yue was several tables away, locked in a struggle with another contestant over a bundle of Silvermind Root. Both had wrapped their qi around it and were pulling from opposite directions, jostling the surrounding tables badly enough that several trays were already beginning to shift.

Ning took one look before returning his attention to the Clearwater Lotus Pith. Whatever Feng Yue was doing was someone else's problem, and he was finally close enough that only a handful of fighting contestants remained between him and the ingredient he needed.

Then Feng Yue's opponent abruptly let go.

The Silvermind Root shot backward, and Feng Yue stumbled with it, crashing into the crowded table behind her. Her qi swept across several trays at once, knocking roots, flowers, seeds, and small containers into one another until a handful of red pods spilled loose among the mess.

Someone shouted, "Firecracker Pods!"

Ning saw the red glow from the corner of his eye.

The Clearwater Lotus Pith was three steps away.

He made his decision immediately and lunged for it.

BOOM!

The protective formations flared around the contestants as fire swept across the central tables. Trays overturned, bottles shattered, and medicinal ingredients scattered in every direction as everyone nearby scrambled away from the blast.

When the smoke cleared, most of the fighting had stopped. A substantial portion of the ingredients everyone had spent the last several minutes fighting over was either scattered across the floor or thoroughly ruined.

Ning remained where he was with one hand still extended toward a blackened tray.

The Clearwater Lotus Pith had been on that tray.

Across the wreckage, Feng Yue slowly emerged from the smoke with soot across her face, her hair in complete disarray, and the Silvermind Root held triumphantly in both hands.

"I got it!"

The contestants whose ingredients had been caught in the explosion were far less impressed.

"What did you do, Feng Yue? Half the ingredients here are ruined!"

"I needed those!"

Several more complaints immediately followed as people inspected the scorched tables. Feng Yue's triumphant expression slowly stiffened. She looked around at the wreckage, then down at the perfectly intact Silvermind Root in her hands.

"H-hahaha..." She straightened and raised her chin. "Naturally, this was all part of my plan."

The complaints stopped for a moment.

Feng Yue seemed to gain confidence from the silence. "Think about it. Now all of you have fewer ingredients to choose from, while I already have what I need." She gave the Silvermind Root a small wave and laughed with much more confidence than before. "My genius frightens even me sometimes."

"You were screaming!"

"Just like I said, all according to my plan."

The surrounding contestants immediately started shouting again.

Ning watched from a short distance away, genuinely unsure what to make of this senior sister anymore. Every time Feng Yue did something like this, he found himself wondering whether she was secretly playing the pig to eat the tiger, or whether she really was just an incredibly confident failure.

There was also a third possibility.

She might simply be his natural nemesis.

"Enough."

Fu Xuan's voice carried across the treasury, immediately quieting most of the complaints.

"Accidents are a natural part of alchemy, and dealing with unexpected circumstances is part of this stage. The destroyed materials will not be replaced." Her gaze swept over the contestants before settling briefly on Feng Yue, who suddenly found the Silvermind Root in her hands very interesting. "Use what remains and do what you can with it."

At almost the same moment, another section of the spiritual incense burned away.

That was enough to remind everyone of the much more immediate problem.

The treasury erupted into motion again as contestants rushed to salvage what remained or find replacements for the ingredients they'd lost.

Ning joined them, searching through the damaged tables and overlooked materials for anything that might still be useful. There was no longer enough time to rebuild his original plan, so he took what possibilities he could find before the last of the incense disappeared.

By the time Ning returned to his station, the incense stick had nearly burned to its end.

He looked over the ingredients he'd managed to gather, then at the Silver Vein Mushroom waiting beside the furnace.

"Well, the Clear Wake Pill is out."

Fortunately, three months of practicing different pills had left Ning with more than a few alternatives, including several experimental recipes of his own. His gaze passed over the Red Mallow Seeds before an old experiment came to mind.

It wasn't something he'd planned on making again, but at this point, drastic situations required drastic solutions.

Ning turned toward Fenghuang. "I'm going to need your full-power purification flame this time."

The raven's head snapped toward him.

Caw

"Yes. Full power."

...

By the time the judges reached Ning's station, three pale-golden pills were resting inside a jade bottle.

The older of the two judges picked up the bottle and removed the stopper, allowing a faint sweet aroma to escape. Ning recognized him as Elder Mo Jian, one of the senior members of the Alchemy Hall.

"Silver Vein Mushroom, Brightmind Flower, Drifting Cloud Pollen... Red Mallow Seed." Mo Jian's eyebrows rose slightly. "An unusual combination."

"Yes, Elder. The accident destroyed one of the ingredients for the pill I'd originally planned," Ning explained. "So I changed to an experimental formula I'd tried before."

Over the past three months, Ning had refined a lot of pills. Most were established recipes meant to build his experience with different ingredients and medicinal properties, but he'd naturally experimented whenever an interesting idea occurred to him. Some of those experiments had been useful, while others had mostly been made for fun.

This was one of the latter.

Mo Jian turned the pill between his fingers. "And what does it do?"

"Well..." Ning hesitated for a moment.

Before he could figure out how to phrase it, Mo Jian seemed to reach a decision of his own.

"I'll judge it directly. My Hundred-Flavors Refinement should be more than enough for a First-Tier pill."

The second judge's expression immediately changed. "Senior Brother Mo, are you sure?"

"Of course."

"He's Ling Xin's disciple."

Mo Jian's fingers stopped.

Ning's mouth twitched.

So this is the downside of being Master's disciple.

For a moment, the elder seemed to reconsider. Then something almost competitive appeared in his eyes.

"All the more reason."

The second judge stared at him. "Senior Brother, surely that's a reason not to test it."

Mo Jian ignored him and looked at Ning instead. "Twenty-three years ago, your master gave me something she claimed would improve the sensitivity of my palate. At the time, my Hundred-Flavors Refinement wasn't advanced enough to completely separate the medicinal effect before it reached my stomach."

"I remember," the second judge said, his expression softening into something disturbingly close to sympathy. "I was there."

Ning very much wanted to know what had happened. He also very much did not want to know what had happened.

Mo Jian raised the pill slightly. "My Hundred-Flavors Refinement is no longer what it was twenty-three years ago. I can separate and absorb a minute portion of a pill's medicinal essence through taste alone, experience its primary effects, and disperse the residue before it properly enters my circulation."

The second judge stared at him for several breaths before slowly nodding. "To advance your art for more than twenty years and willingly face this again..." His expression became solemn. "Senior Brother, you truly are an alchemist."

Mo Jian nodded gravely. "There is always a price to pursuing the Dao of Alchemy."

Ning looked between the two elders, then down at the tiny First-Tier pill in Mo Jian's hand.

Why are they being so melodramatic?

They were acting as though Mo Jian were about to enter some forbidden ruin from which countless cultivators had never returned. Whatever his master had done had left a lasting impression on this man.

Before he could finally explain what the pill actually did, Mo Jian scraped an extremely small amount from its surface and examined the powder with his spiritual sense.

"Don't worry. Whatever your master may have taught you, this much cannot overcome my Hundred-Flavors Refinement."

That wasn't what Ning was worried about, but Mo Jian had already placed the powder on his tongue.

For several breaths, nothing happened. Then the elder blinked a few times, and the stern expression on his face gradually relaxed into a strangely contented smile.

"Hah..."

The second judge immediately frowned. "Senior Brother?"

"I'm fine." Mo Jian waved him away, his words coming out slightly slower than before. His gaze wandered upward, and for several seconds he simply stared at the sky. "Very blue today."

"...The sky?"

"Yes." Mo Jian smiled. "Very blue."

He continued staring upward with far more appreciation than the perfectly ordinary sky deserved before his brow suddenly furrowed.

"Why is it blue?"

The second judge opened his mouth, but Mo Jian had already lost interest in the question. His attention dropped to the jade bottle in his hand, and another quiet laugh escaped him as he rolled it between his fingers.

"Why are pill bottles round?"

"What?"

"They roll." Mo Jian gave the bottle another little turn and chuckled. "We put valuable pills inside little containers that can run away from us. That's ridiculous."

Ning said nothing, for he had no words.

"Senior Brother, perhaps you should sit down."

"No, no. I'm perfectly... perfectly fine." Mo Jian swayed slightly, caught himself, then looked back at the bottle as if he'd just discovered something profound. "Square bottles wouldn't roll. But corners might interfere with the formations. Hexagons, then? Hexagons fit together nicely."

His eyes suddenly widened. "And pills are round too."

Ning watched the sudden excitement spread across the elder's face.

Oh no. Was the ratio a little too high?

"Why are pills round?" Mo Jian continued, his words coming faster now. "Rotational condensation, yes, but they don't have to stay round afterward. More surface area means faster dissolution. Different shapes, different absorption rates..."

He paused, then brightened as another idea struck him.

"Layers! Different medicinal layers could dissolve at different times. Outer medicine first, inner medicine later. Could you make three? Five? If the release could be controlled, then perhaps incompatible medicinal properties wouldn't even need to activate at the same-"

One thought was already spilling into the next, and Mo Jian seemed delighted every time he discovered another connection. Some of the ideas almost made sense. Others had apparently started somewhere sensible before wandering off and getting lost.

Then Mo Jian suddenly fell silent, his gaze drifting toward the sky.

"Wait... What if we're all just stardust? Isn't the sun essentially an enormous refinement furnace?"

"...Senior Brother?"

"And cultivation!" His eyes widened. "What if cultivation is simply refining ourselves, like medicinal pills?"

He looked down at the pill in his hand.

"Pills consuming pills... Heavens, what a profound Dao!"

Ning looked at the man.

Yeah, he's completely gone.

Fortunately, Mo Jian seemed to realize something was wrong after another few breaths. He closed his eyes and circulated the Hundred-Flavors Refinement, quickly dispersing what remained of the medicinal effect. When he opened them again, the slight sway in his posture was gone, and most of his usual composure had returned.

He remained quiet for a moment, apparently reviewing what had just happened, before looking at Ning's pill with renewed interest.

"Interesting. There was a mild feeling of euphoria. Everything seemed more interesting than it actually was; even completely ordinary things became inexplicably amusing, and my mood was unusually elevated."

Ning's lips twitched.

"But at the same time, my mind felt remarkably free. Thoughts I would normally dismiss were allowed to continue, one idea connected to another with almost no resistance, and inspiration came remarkably easily." Mo Jian looked at Ning as though he'd already reached his conclusion. "That is the intended effect of the pill, isn't it?"

Ning paused.

"Um... yes, Elder. That is exactly the intended effect."

Yup. There were absolutely no other effects. None at all.
