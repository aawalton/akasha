The moment Djarn’s head was separated from his shoulders, the din of the battle behind me changed. A sudden shift where, before, there had been an almost organized focus to the sound of blade on blade, and flesh against steel.

Now, it sounded something more familiar. Far more like the previous skirmish, when my battle group had encountered the Korvoks within the city.

In other words, absolute mad screaming anarchy on their behalf. Followed, shortly, by an uptick in the number of those screams being cut off in dying gurgles. In all earnestness, I didn’t even need to look over my shoulder to see how things had changed, with the broader conflict. I already knew what I would find.

But I did it anyway, and I was satisfied.

The Korvoks had reverted to their mad, flailing, bestial state we had encountered in other parts of Karag Varla. Their coordination had fallen apart, and now they were just throwing themselves blindly into the blades of mine and Olag’s soldiers. They had already been routed, and they just didn’t know it yet. But instead of retreating, as any normal fighting force would, they instead chose to die. I’m…not even sure chose was the right word, though.

It was more like an instinctual longing for death. Somewhat disquieting to see, frankly.

Still, the result was the same. They were being mopped up even as I watched. I had all confidence that Olag and the rest would wrap this up in short order.

In the meantime, I had other things to occupy my attention. At first, I was tempted to start my investigation on just what the Hell Djarn the Karvokar had been, but then I noticed something on my blood sense.

One of the frantically beating hearts buried deeper within the Hagia Dynamis had started to move. Specifically, somewhere closer to the front of the building and above me. Presumably, even in the depths of this solid stone near-bunker, the people inside must have noticed that not only had the sounds of battle stopped, but Djarn’s pounding on their door had as well.

Almost absentmindedly, I released my combat Skills, since I doubted I would need them anytime soon. It’s not like any of the maddened Korvoks was going to get anywhere near me, while my soldiers finished them off. I shrank back down to my…relatively normal, humanish state just in time to watch as above me, one of the huge, heavy stone blocks that denoted a window slowly started to grind open.

I had expected a lot of things about whoever was behind that ‘window’. Presumably, they would be a Dwarf, preferably one of the few surviving Varlayans here in Karag Varla. Like most Hold Dwarves, they probably would have a full beard, too, since for male Dwarves, it was the law that they had to have a beard, in total inverse of Velancia.

I was right, too. The Dwarf that poked his head out of the stone window of the Hagia Dynamis did have a beard. A perfectly straight blonde one, with an equally long head of hair on his head.

Only this wasn’t a Varlayan. More than that, this was a Dwarf I knew.

I could only gape in complete and total astonishment as Vandimar, the old butcher who had been my friend back in Addersfield, poked his head through the gap.

He was…different. Even that brief glimpse of him was enough to tell me that. Vandimar, Van, had always struck me as a somewhat…effeminate person. There was nothing wrong with that, per se, but at the time, the Dwarf hadn’t exactly possessed a core of steel. There had been a softness to him, in frame, in face, and in spirit. He’d been pampered, sent to Addersfield at the behest of his petit bourgeois Vittolian family so that he could learn ruthlessness from his uncle, the Seneschal, Orinbar. Part of that had been said Uncle saddling him with a butcher's shop, managing several slaves.

One of whom had turned out to be Bleddyn. The other was the woman currently acting as a nanny to my daughter back in Blutstein, Rachel.

I…had gotten to know Van pretty well during my months in Addersfield. Frankly, almost as well as I had Bleddyn himself. Other than Azarus’s house, that butcher’s shop was the primary place I had spent my spent my time there.

I hadn’t seen him in nearly two years now. I’d asked Bleddyn where Van had gone when we had a moment alone together, and all he’d been able to do was shrug his shoulders with a downcast expression. Bleddyn had told me that, these days, Van was the apprentice of Gren, a traveling merchant who had been one of the founding members of the Unchained. They had helped the freed slaves as a Dwarven face for their mercantile endeavors, as they tried to sell the stockpile beneath Addersfield. As a result, they were rarely, if ever, to be found in the actual settlement beneath the former slave plantation, only staying long enough to pick up more goods, drop off funds, and then skedaddle.

That had been before the Skyfall. They had been out at the time on a long journey of their own, and nobody in the Unchained’d had any idea what happened to either of them. It was an unfortunately common story these days. Like most, we could only hope they had been under wards when the corruption came.

But I sure as Hell hadn’t expected those wards to be the wards of Karag Varla of all places.

Now, though, he’d not only slimmed down, but he looked harder.

But as his eyes roamed the street outside of the Hagia Dynamis, perfectly plucked eyebrows raising high at my combined forces standing among a field of slaughtered Korvoks, I couldn’t help but feel…apprehensive.

I had changed, too. Not just in spirit, but there was my curse from Rhazal to consider. I barely even looked Human, even outside of my transformation. Would he even recognize me, gaping up at him in shock?

Turns out…

I needn’t have worried.

His gaze inevitably turned toward the door where a monster had been pounding only minutes before, and found me staring up at him in shock. He blinked at me. Once, twice…

And then his own mouth fell open in disbelief.

An almost involuntary smile crossed my lips then, and I almost started laughing right then and there, among the slaughter. “…Van?” I called up hopefully.

“Nathan?!” Van called down to me, the sound of his voice, even steeped in disbelief as it was, filling me with an almost sense of nostalgia. “Nathan Hart?! That couldn’t possibly be you!”

I laughed out loud, then, sheathing Terractus as I did. “It is! So this is where you went! Bleddyn didn’t know!”

If anything, that only caused Van to goggle down at me harder. “Bleddyn?! Is he here with you?!”

“Why don’t you come down here and open the door, Van, and we can talk about it!”

It was good to see that, even now, after all this time, Vandimar was still just a bit airheaded.

The Dwarf up in the window nodded his head rapidly, before disappearing from view, dragging the stone shutter closed with him as he did. I felt it as his heartbeat rapidly moved further into the building, presumably to tell the rest of the hearts I could feel inside.

Because all of them started moving then, this time toward the door. At the same time, I could tell that my forces had finished the cleanup from the battle that had raged while I was dealing with Djarn, and I felt the commanders of my group approaching to join me in staring at the door. My little exchange with Van hadn’t exactly gone unnoticed.

My attention was stolen when I saw the massive slab of stone these Varlayans called a door start to grindingly creep open in front of me.

To my side, Fade suddenly perked up and started staring intently at the door. His tail began to wag, and an almost doggy grin crossed his lips. I couldn’t help but get my hopes up. He only really reacted in such a canine manner when he sensed the presence of people that he knew, and more than that, actually liked.

Did that mean…?

The door had only opened a crack before I saw something that caused my heart to leap into my throat, and a smile to appear on my lips.

A flash of red fur, as a feline head shoved itself through the gap, followed by the rest of the owner. In seconds, I beheld someone that I hadn’t seen since nearly a month before the actual coming of the Skyfall.

Sena, eldest daughter of Shurenga, herself the only child of the Great Spirit Tarus, Lord of the Sun. Like her mother, Sena was a crimson-furred and black-striped example of a great cat, of a kind that I could only equate to the long extinct saber-toothed tigers from Terra’s past. I knew that one of the racial abilities of this particular kind of Spirit Beast was that they could change their size at will. Right now, she was only a little bit bigger than Fade himself, but I knew she could grow to a size larger than that of a war-horse if she needed.

She was also Azarus’s Familiar in the same way that Fade was mine.

Something…something was wrong, though. While I may not have been as well-acquainted with her as I wanted to be, we were still friends. I used to spend a lot of time around Azarus, before his disappearance, so we had grown comfortable with her. More than that, she was friends herself with Fade, which explained his reaction to her.

But because of my familiarity with her, I could tell she wasn’t happy right now. Oh, she looked a bit relieved, to be sure…but mostly the tiger just seemed stressed and worried to me.

I soon found out why.

“They have him, Nathan,” Sena said bluntly, flexing her claws. “They have him.”

Slowly, the smile on my lips started to die, as her words sank in. As they did, a sense of despair threatened to roll over me as I began to comprehend the reality of the situation.

After all this time…after hoping for so long that I would find him…was this…

Was this really how it was going to end?

I was vaguely aware of Olag as he stepped forward. He seemed nonplussed by the sight of a talking cat, because while Fade was known to be my Familiar, it’s not like he could speak a hominid tongue. “Have who? What is going on?”

“…Azarus,” I said hoarsely. “The Korvoks have Azarus.”

Olag jerked in place, turning first to stare at me in astonishment, and then Sena. The tiger barely paid any attention to him, though, as the doors of the Hagia Dynamis fully opened, revealing the interior. There were dozens and dozens of Varlayan Dwarves on the other side of the door, the first living examples I had found in this Hold. I could tell that there were still hundreds further in, and it was possible that there were more. But my blood sense didn’t have the range to tell.

Most of them, with Van in the front, looked both baffled at the sight of so many Humans, but also hopeful. I could easily understand why. Anyone or anything was better than more Korvoks.

But…

I was mostly occupied with a growing sense of anger and determination.

“Olag of House Florens,” I said forcefully, causing said Dwarves to twitch back my way, at the formal address. I couldn’t help but notice that many of the other Dwarves inside the Hagia Dynamis reacted with shock at the name, Van included.

For good reason, too. All of Dwarven kind knew the names of the Great Houses of Velancia.

“This is Sena, Familiar of Azarus of No House.”

The two stared at each other in sudden understanding for a moment, as they took in each other’s presence. Eventually, Sena looked back at me. “He…doesn’t call himself that anymore. Grandfather allowed him to introduce him as part of ‘House Tarus’.”

That was…interesting, but it didn’t matter much right now.

What mattered was getting Azarus back. I would not leave my best friend in the monstrous clutches of a bunch of flesh-maddened cannibals and flensers.

I shifted my gaze over to the surprisingly grim eyes of Van. He had known Azarus very well himself. “Can I come in?”

“I’m going to need to know what happened…if we’re going to rescue Azarus.”
