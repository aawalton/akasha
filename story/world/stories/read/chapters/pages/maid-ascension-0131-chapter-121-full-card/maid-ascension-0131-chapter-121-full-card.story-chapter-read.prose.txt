Lira watched from the shadow of the doorway as Luxia and Aric turned through the last figure of the dance, the only couple on the floor wearing blue.

It wasn't intentional on his part. He didn't check what Luxia was wearing before he dressed, Lira was fairly certain of that. But intentional or not, the effect was undeniable. Every other girl on the floor wore some shade of plum, violet, or the deep purple that had been sweeping the season, and there, at the center of it, stood a prince in sapphire silk with a baroness's daughter in a gown the color of a clear evening sky.

Lira let herself look at Aric for a moment. His color had come back fully, with no trace of the hollow-cheeked exhaustion she had seen a week ago. Whatever that spell had taken out of him, his body had apparently been strong enough to recover. Lira was glad. She didn't want anything ill to happen to a man who would risk his own life to save a child.

Her eyes moved to Luxia next. She glowed with the light only a girl in love could have, not the practiced glow most debutantes wore after a week of etiquette drilling. Tonight she seemed to have convinced herself that the story she had dreamed about might actually be true. She laughed at something Aric said, too loud for what would be proper in a ballroom, and didn't seem to notice or care.

Careful, Luxia. He's only supposed to be playing a part. Don't fall for the lies you wrote yourself. Your job is to besot him while you have the chance, not to be besotted yourself.

Lira's gaze drifted from the dancing couple and roamed over the ballroom itself. The place was called Blue Peak Hall.

That afternoon, Lira had traveled here in the same carriage as Luxia, Baroness Dalia, and Abigail Talia. She was wedged between hatboxes and suitcases that took up more room than she did, listening to the baroness recite exactly which suitors Luxia was and was not permitted to speak to without an introduction.

The hall had earned its reputation, and its price. A domed ceiling rose three stories up, painted with some pale, cloud-choked rendition of the heavens that candlelight turned faintly gold. Twin staircases curved down from a mezzanine on either side of the entrance, so every arriving family got two full flights of descent to present themselves. The floor itself was pale marble veined in blue, polished to a shine that doubled the chandeliers overhead until the whole room seemed to be trapped between two skies instead of one.

The architect who designed the place had thought of everything, because tucked along the far wall, behind a stretch of paneling made to look like part of the hall's decoration, sat this room, the one Lira currently stood in. The servants' room was a staging area where household staff could wait, unseen, until their families called for them, with the doorway being a wide entrance covered with a dark, sheer muslin curtain.

Lira had understood the trick of it within a minute of being shown inside. The servants' room sat dimmer than the ballroom, and that difference in light created an illusionary effect. From the ballroom side, in all that gold and marble brightness, the curtain read as a wall. From the servants' side, in the dimness, the thin weave turned nearly see-through, so a person standing near it could watch the floor clearly enough. A one-way window built out of nothing but the difference between two lights.

Lira filed the idea away. It could be useful someday.

The music slowed, then stopped, and the dance broke apart into bows and curtsies, the dancers shuffling as the room reset between sets. Lira didn't wait for Luxia to find her. She stepped out from behind the curtain the moment the applause started and met Luxia halfway across the floor, close enough to the dressing room doors that no one would question her presence.

Once inside, she took Luxia's chin gently, tilted her face toward the light, and began dabbing the sweat from her hairline and temples with a folded cloth. Luxia tried to hold still, though she was far too excited for that.

"He signed his name for the last dance too," Luxia said, practically vibrating. "The last one too, Lira, do you know what that means?"

"I know exactly what that means." Lira worked quickly as she started on the powder. She wasn't truly trained at this, though she had done similar work back in her early days at the palace, filling in wherever an extra pair of hands was needed. She found it easy enough. Her scribe's fingers knew how to move steadily, even if the tool had changed from a quill to a powder brush. Her strokes came out even, making sure the blush sat exactly where it belonged.

"My whole card is full." Luxia thrust the little ivory card at her.

"Can you believe it? I didn't think, I mean, I hoped, but I didn't think."

"Of course it's full. This is your debut." Lira's eyes moved to the card as she worked, scanning it, half out of genuine happiness for the girl and half out of an old childhood habit of reading anything that crossed her path.

Lord Duval Sollaris Junior was next on the list. He had apparently turned up outside Salvek's door the same morning as Deren. His sister Paige had debuted three days ago and was among tonight's guests as well, one of the girls dressed in violet, if Lira wasn't mistaken, which meant the Sollaris family was doing rather better than the Valis. it made sense, since one house held an earldom, while the other only held a barony.

Lord James Evergreen came third. His sister Kiressa's debut had been the talk of the palace all week, ever since Kavard had claimed her final dance.

A scatter of other names followed, some she recognized, some she didn't. Sir Liam, one of the knights who liked to spar against the guards in the military keep. Lira had seen his face many times during the long week she had spent watching Thadwick, back before she was deciding how he would die.

She pushed the thought away. She shouldn't be thinking of those memories while helping a girl look pretty.

"There," she said, closing the powder tin and tucking the cloth away. "You should be good for a few more sets."

Luxia beamed at her, snatched the dance card back, and was already craning her neck out of the dressing room, trying to find Aric again over the heads of the crowd.

"Go," Lira said, giving her a small push toward the floor. "Your next dance partner isn't the prince."

"Boo," said Luxia. "I should have had him fill my whole card."

The music started up again, and Lira pushed her a second time, slightly harder. "Go, you're going to miss your dance, silly girl."

Luxia ran out laughing, happy and carefree, and completely unaware that Prince Kavard was watching her from the top of the mezzanine.

Lira's heart sank when she spotted him.

Why was he here? And since when? Why was he focused on Luxia?
