I reappeared back on the arena floor of the colosseum.

Alive.

My clothes were back. My scythe. Interface. All of it.

My breath came in heaves. I seriously thought I was going to die when the floor opened up.

Really? That's the method of transportation back to Penumbra you chose in that particular situation? Damn gods… They were probably laughing right now. They—

A giant disc of ice whirred over my head. I ducked way later than would've saved me, but luckily it'd been a foot too high to decapitate me.

What the fuck?

That's when I realized I stood in the center of the colosseum, the exact same location I'd been before, but the situation here had vastly changed. Unchained had flooded the arena. They tore at one another, claws, teeth, and skills flying in every direction. I stayed low, mostly unnoticed amid the throng of violence.

The battle was far from over.

How long had I been gone? It only felt like a few minutes… But this scene suggests otherwise.

Off in a corner, it seemed the Reapers that remained here had gathered to defend a smaller area with walls to their backs. I couldn't tell who was present besides Lancelot who occasionally flew a few feet up, then dove back down. I prayed Jericho and Calliope were still there—or somewhere safer. After giving oaths to Valiferous, they were as vulnerable as me now. They would not return Vitria if something happened to them.

And what about any Penumbrans who were here, like Eugene? I didn't know where all the residents of the city had gone after coming here for safety. I hadn't seen them before.

I didn't have time to consider it.

I Aero Shifted out of the way of a massive metal-bladed tail that whipped around from a nearby Unchained. It sliced into an elephant hybrid instead of cutting me in half. The beast dropped, front legs removed at the knee. It fell toward me, shadow looming.

I Aero Shifted again to avoid being crushed, only to find myself face-to-face with a Sapphire snake-bird hybrid monstrosity. It bore its fangs and snapped. My scythe was in my hand, then through its throat in an instant. I didn't realize how quick and smooth my draw had become. Maybe that improved with rank?

Or maybe I'd just been killing. A lot.

Belatedly, I noticed my Volition gauge. It was low. Not dangerously so. But enough that I wouldn't be able to make much of a difference here without replenishing it. Using Threads of Fate to sever Pirhani's thread had cost almost everything I had.

Wills from defeated Sins sat in the stands like dormant spectators—some down in the arena as well. But I already had one. Pirhani's Will still resided within my scythe, glowing dimly gold. And there was a Pillar where I could relinquish it close by in the lobby. Close enough, though?

I glanced back at the cluster of Reapers fighting back the onslaught of Unchained. They'd have to hold on.

I set my sight on the door out of the arena. I had enough Volition to make it there. Probably.

Aero Shift rocketed me through the battlefield and into the lobby. I dropped out of the shift amid several Unchained Sins lallygagging here. One even lounged in the destroyed rubble of the registrar's desk where Sibyl typically sat.

"Hello there," I said with a grin, doing my best Obi-Wan impression.

I thought the Unchained Sins would be fighting one another like when they attacked Allium and me at the lighthouse, or even in the stands right after I'd defeated Pirhani. But they seemed more passive now. Even in the arena moments before. I hadn't thought about it until now, but the Sins on the outskirts of the battle hadn't been in combat until I arrived.

That changed the moment they noticed me.

The Unchained in the lobby instantaneously transformed into a froth of swiping limbs and activated skills.

I ducked beneath the claw from a beaver-like Sin, unsure why they suddenly exploded into aggression. My scythe sliced through its shin as I sped forward. Was it my presence? A potential Will to consume that drove them into a frenzy?

I didn't look back to see how badly I hurt the Sin. I wasn't here to kill. Not yet. Speed was the priority. I had to get back to the others as quick as possible.

Darting between the Sins here, I dodged strikes, incapacitated, and made my way toward the room with the pillar. I burst through the door, happy to find it empty and undisturbed.

Not for long though.

I slammed the door behind me and scrambled to latch it shut only to find there was no latch. The bench nearby could work, but…

Something crashed into the door on the opposite side, knocking me away briefly. I held it closed again, leaning my entire body weight against it. The pillar was a good ten feet behind me. How the fuck am I going to—

The door exploded into pieces.

I flew backwards, landing in the cold of the pillar's aura. Small splinters stuck into my forearms and face. Nothing to do but ignore it for now.

I got up to a knee, drawing my scythe.

A pair of Sins fought for entrance in the doorway. An Emerald raccoon-something hybrid won out and charged into the room, only to face plant into an invisible wall.

I stared blankly, not sure what had happened.

The Sin tried again, but was denied a second time. It pressed its hands against the invisible wall as a second and third followed behind it. None of them could get past the invisible barrier.

I looked down at their feet and realized the barrier that held them at bay was the exact location where the frost from the pillar ended.

Sins can't enter the pillar circles. I almost laughed. Was that well known? If so, wouldn't it have been better to keep everyone inside here for protection? It was small, so only a handful of people would fit, but it was better than nothing.

I rubbed the goosebumps already forming on my arms and knew why that wasn't necessarily the best idea. Face the Sins head on or fight hypothermia? Maybe as a staging area though? Or brief respite from the battle?

It didn't matter right now. I had a job to do. But it was a convenient trick to remember.

I walked to the pillar as the Unchained Sins roared and scraped at the barrier behind me. My feet crunched on the frosty stone floor and my breath fogged as I neared the center.

I held up my scythe, dimly glowing with the golden Will of a god.

Then I slid it into the pillar.

The blade yanked forward, locking into place, pulling my hand so hard I almost stumbled into the pillar myself. The veins of the stone pulsed like rays of sunlight, brighter than I'd ever seen before. My heartbeat aligned.

[Will Relinquish Commencing.

Rank: God.

Affinity: None.]

My scythe vibrated violently, heating up to a scalding temperature like a pan that's been in the oven for hours. Feeling like it might explode. The carapace on my hand gripping the weapon peeled and melted. A flood of emotions drowned my mind. Hatred. Anger. Despair.

I hadn't experienced any relinquishing like this before.

I'd never had a god level Will before.

Of course it would be different. Of course it—

My spine flicked straight as a board. Vertebrae cracked. The relinquishing was so violence my Health was visibly dropping. My neck snapped to the side as if someone had kicked my head. I could've sworn my neck broke in that moment.

Then everything was black and I was floating among the stars.
