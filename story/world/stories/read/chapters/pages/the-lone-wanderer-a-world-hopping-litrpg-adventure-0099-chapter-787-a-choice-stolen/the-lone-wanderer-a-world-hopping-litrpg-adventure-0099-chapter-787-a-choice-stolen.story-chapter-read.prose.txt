When Percy came to, he found himself resting on the familiar mattress of his bedroom in the settlement. The splitting headache he had suffered under Baphomet’s torturous possession had mostly receded. Percy’s enhanced regeneration must have mended the damage, though a dull ache persisted.

Opening his eyes and allowing his sensory domain to spread to the rest of his house, he realized that he was alone. The last thing he remembered was collapsing face-first atop the cap of the giant mushroom and splashing in a warm pool of his own blood.

Judging by his current location and the fact that neither his beard nor his hair were sticky, he guessed that his friends had carried him home, washed him, and left him to recover by himself after making sure that he hadn’t sustained any serious injuries.

He was a little surprised to find nobody by his side, though perhaps he shouldn’t be. Everyone knew how resilient he was nowadays, and they were all busy with other matters, since they were still technically in the middle of a war. Besides, Percy could easily reach out to Micky or Nephthys after waking up. They’d also left Zoris with him.

‘I must be the only person with the Insomnia trait who keeps inventing new ways to knock myself out,’ he thought with a bitter smile.

At least his cores hadn’t degraded. He had thankfully managed to keep circulating his mana both under Baphomet’s influence and after fainting, though Micky would have presumably stepped in to help him had his boosting art come undone.

Percy tried to sit up, thinking that it would be a struggle due to how weak he felt.

He was wrong.

He managed to not only sit, but even stand without much difficulty, despite his fatigue. His weakness reminded him of the core demotions he had experienced before the ritual bath, though this was slightly different. His physical strength had not been affected at all, but he felt like somebody had scooped his organs out with a giant spoon and left nothing but an empty husk behind.

‘This must be the side-effect of activating the Decree. Eighty percent of my lifespan is gone,’ he concluded with a sigh.

The new core would eventually make up for some of that, but its influence would be a mere drop in the bucket until it caught up to the other two – a process that required a decade of constant cleansing and another artificial promotion.

Alas, his personal losses were the least of his problems.

Percy was fully aware of how dangerous his brush with the mind deity had been. After exhausting every other option and realizing that Baphomet had inadvertently linked him to dozens of mind users, Percy had activated the Void Decree on a whim, hoping that it would get him out of the mess he had created.

A million things could have gone wrong. His new core could have easily failed to absorb the mortals’ cores, the mind deity could have managed to interrupt the process, and slaughtering the god’s minions had not been guaranteed to free Percy from his restraints.

Hell, if his Status hadn’t jumped in at the last second to help him drag the last handful of offerings into the forming organ, the Catharian deity would have definitely overpowered Percy and his friends.

Part of him wanted to blame Phoebe for not jumping to his rescue – since any mind affinity goddess should have been able to easily protect him from Baphomet’s spell – but Percy was the one who’d asked the titaness to stop stalking him.

Fortunately, the stars had aligned in his favour in the end, though that did very little to alleviate his guilt.

This was all his fault. He’d been advised against selling the secret of the cyan powder in the auction, yet he’d still foolishly gone through with it – blinded by greed and arrogance. In many ways, he was no better than Machaon, as his whole planet was now in danger because of him.

‘Zoris… please tell me the truth. How bad is it?’ he asked the ring on his finger.

‘Not great, but better than it could have been,’ the disembodied deity replied.

‘How so?’ Percy asked.

‘Leaving aside the fact that you killed more than half of Baphomet’s minions, your little stunt seems to have injured him pretty badly right before the connection was severed,’ Zoris explained.

Percy nodded, recalling the terrible ripping sound he had heard as the Void Decree clamped down on the final sacrifice and the bastard’s internal world. It reminded him of the time Ishkuria had torn out a piece of her own internal world, and left herself severely weakened as a result.

‘Won’t that piss him off even more, though?’ he asked.

‘Definitely,’ Zoris confirmed. ‘I’ve never met Baphomet personally, but he’s known to be as petty as they come. Even if you hadn’t injured him, there is no way he would have left you alone after learning about me and your other secrets. Unfortunately for him, he won’t be able to come after you for quite a while.’

‘Even if he can’t attack Remior personally, his faction is more than powerful enough to crush us,’ Percy protested.

‘It is, but you’ve got leverage,’ the sentient ring explained.

Percy raised an eyebrow at first, though it didn’t take him long to understand what Zoris meant.

Baphomet knew many of his secrets, but it wasn’t like he could tell anyone, since he would probably want to seize everything for himself. Ratting Percy out would be the same as giving away a mountain of treasure to another faction.

At the same time, Percy also knew Baphomet’s dirty little secret: the fact that he could no longer use his most powerful and infamous spell. The old goat wouldn’t want Percy to go around spilling the beans at the next auction now, would he?

‘This is just a temporary deadlock. He’ll heal, sooner or later,’ Percy pointed out.

‘Indeed,’ Zoris agreed. ‘I’ve no idea what’s going on in that madman’s head, but I can tell you what a reasonable person would do in his shoes. They’d bide their time until their internal world recovered and they could use their spell again before attacking you.’

Percy considered snitching on the bastard first, to hopefully get another faction to eliminate him before that happened. However, that wasn’t a great idea either, because Baphomet would be more likely to retaliate out of spite if Percy were to drive him into a corner.

‘There’s another thing you should know,’ Zoris added. ‘Catharis has never involved themselves much with other factions directly, so I doubt that it was Baphomet or one of his people who bought the secret from you at the auction. It’s probably a third party that tried to target you and ended up approaching Baphomet because they couldn’t.’

‘How does that change the situation?’ Percy asked.

‘Hard to say. If you’re lucky, they’ve already turned on one another and will keep themselves busy for the foreseeable future. The best-case scenario is them killing Baphomet before he tells anyone else about you. But you should prepare yourself for the possibility that Catharis wins and eventually invades your planet after Baphomet recovers.’

‘How long do you think I have?’

‘Depends on the extent of the damage,’ Zoris replied with a mental shrug. ‘I’d say anywhere between four and ten thousand years.’

Percy exhaled in relief. The situation was still quite bad, yet he had plenty of time to prepare.

While using their Extreme spell, he and Micky had been almost as powerful as a demigod over a year ago. His upgraded mutation, new mindset, most recent batch of registered spells, and third core would tip him over the edge soon if they hadn’t already.

And he should reach Violet with all three of his cores in less than a thousand years at his current cleansing speed. If he managed to get his hand on a Focus, there was a good chance he’d be able to compete with some of the weakest gods by then, even without taking any other spells or upgrades into account. Of course, an Eleventh Circle mage was still an ant compared to somebody like Baphomet, but a lot of other things could change after another three thousand years.

In fact, of Percy’s current enemies, Baphomet was the one he would have to deal with the furthest down the line – killing Machaon, rescuing Marnok from the Cardinal Devil, placating a possibly-furious Metatron, and even reclaiming Huehue from Rhaziel took precedence.

There was no sense in panicking over this newest problem, nor would he have to deal with it alone. Even if Percy and the Divine Order failed to grow strong enough to resist Catharis before Baphomet healed, they could look for allies or other ways to prevent the goat-people from invading them.

‘Let’s make the new core and whatever time I have left count,’ he decided.

Percy’s fingers subconsciously drifted to his forehead, gently caressing the spot above the bridge of his nose. There was no bump, nor any visible change on the outside, though he could easily locate the strawberry-sized cavity inside his skull with his Internal Sight. His third core was currently empty, waiting to be filled up for the first time.

The place his new organ had chosen to take root in unsettled him somewhat, though he guessed that the Void Decree’s activation wouldn’t have been nearly as dangerous if not for Baphomet’s meddling. He’d probably only survived the ordeal due to his upgraded physique making his body sturdier.

The last time he’d calculated his lifespan, he’d estimated that he had around ten thousand years left with just two Green cores, but more than enough to attain divinity after all the promotions. Now, he was down to less than two thousand, and no more than forty after getting all three of his cores to White.

It wouldn’t be enough to reach the Clear grade.

All the other problems aside, his mental battle with Baphomet had cost him his shot at divinity, on paper at least. Percy hadn’t wanted to activate the Void Decree before finding another way to extend his lifespan, but the old goat had unfortunately stolen the choice from his hands.

On the flipside, there were some upsides to Baphomet forcing things.

He had generously provided Percy with the necessary sacrifices, saving him half a decade of granting new cores to the Green wasps. A mind affinity also happened to have been among Percy’s top choices – he could finally become his own mindset specialist, after receiving some training from Nephthys.

Then, there was the blessing.

Percy had probably acquired it because of the piece of Baphomet’s internal world that the Void Decree had absorbed. Even the leaders of the Void Hand might not be aware that it was possible to obtain a blessing in this manner.

Sacrificing a few titans to create the collaborative Decree was one thing, but injuring a god every single time a mortal received it was another. Even a genius like Zurvanai or Remlat wasn’t guaranteed to attain divinity, and deities with rare affinities were a hundred times more precious than gods with common elements.

Even if the alliance was willing to experiment with their most precious assets like that, it might not always be possible to grant someone a blessing in this manner. For all Percy knew, the intimate connection that Baphomet shared with his minions through his unique spell might have made all the difference.

This one even happened to be called “Blessing of Connection”, providing yet another clue as to how Percy might have accidentally stolen it. He’d have to ask Nephthys about it later.

Regardless, there were only ninety-nine Rings of Sacrilege out there, and Percy had no idea how many of them contained auxiliary concepts of rare affinities. It was also a little late for him to be born to a mind deity, so this might have been his only chance to acquire a blessing for his third core.

He would cherish it. And he’d pay Baphomet back for his “kindness” one day.

There were many things that Percy wanted to test, and many plans that would have to change if he hoped to defeat the ever-growing legion of enemies thirsting for his blood and rebuild the broken path to divinity.

Before diving into all of that, he pulled up his Status, deciding to give himself a moment to appreciate the few good things that had come out of the unpleasant encounter.
