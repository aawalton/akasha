Hathaway stepped back and surveyed her financial ruin.

The sub-basement alchemy laboratory had been fully assembled. Star-silver compounding crucibles sat beneath specialized exhaust hoods. Reinforced crystalline glassware, medium-capacity biomatter incubators, tiered formula racks. Rows of climate-controlled material bins lined the far wall, each sealed behind containment wards. Above the entryway, an array of heavily ionized sterile air jets blasted periodic gusts at carefully timed intervals, keeping the atmospheric particulate count at something approaching absolute zero.

And positioned in the far corner, with an unobstructed sightline to the primary workbench, sat an absurdly expensive, magically articulated, heavily cushioned leather massage chair.

The foundational design philosophy of the Curie School, her developer brain compiled, identifying the underlying message with clinical accuracy. We acknowledge that this discipline is going to cause you immense, prolonged suffering. Therefore, we guarantee that at three in the morning, when you are weeping over a collapsed reaction matrix, your lumbar spine will at least be ergonomically supported.

She turned her attention to the wall above the central workbench.

Pinned to the reinforced stone was a large, meticulously printed diagram. It depicted a complex nonagram, a nine-pointed star, intersected by a dizzying web of geometric lines. Scattered across this web were thousands of individual nodes, and beside every node was the name of an alchemical material paired with a small numerical coefficient.

Some values were listed as 1. Others were 0.0001. But alchemy did not permit ambiguity; even the integers were rigidly formatted to enforce exactness. The number one was not written as a solitary digit. It was inscribed as 1.00000. Every single coefficient on the chart was locked to the fifth decimal place.

Learning alchemy meant committing this chart to memory. You did not touch a crucible until you had memorized the board.

Hathaway looked at the nonagram.

This is a lookup table, she realized, a wave of strange, nostalgic familiarity washing over her. It is a hash map with approximately one thousand keys. Every value is a float, locked to five mandatory decimal places.

If she were sitting at a terminal in her previous life, she would simply import the dictionary and call the variable. In this life, she had to manually encode the entire hash map directly into her biological long-term memory.

Sharon Curie's Volume One sat open, but it was currently buried under a tower of supplementary reading: Common Mineral Traits, In-Depth Mineral Analysis, Alchemy Basics II, Potion Material Decomposition, and Common Alchemy Runes. Volume One outlined the execution techniques. To understand what she was actually executing, she had to read everything under it first.

This was, without hyperbole, pure academic agony.

Alchemical formulas were parameterized: the ambient mana surged and receded daily, and the same measurements that produced a perfect result on Tuesday could detonate the lab on Wednesday.

The formulas are environment variables, Hathaway's logic engine processed, staring at a densely packed paragraph on tidal compensation adjustments. Every morning, before lighting the crucible, I have to read the runtime environment. Check the ambient mana differential. Recalibrate the coefficients. There is no compile-once, run-anywhere in alchemy. It is a permanently dynamic system.

She dragged a chair to the desk, sat down, and opened Common Mineral Traits.

She flooded the upper atmosphere with raw, brute-force mana and physically seized the invisible cold currents, wrestling them into a reverse spin through sheer, overwhelming administrative pressure.

Next came the precipitation cycle.
