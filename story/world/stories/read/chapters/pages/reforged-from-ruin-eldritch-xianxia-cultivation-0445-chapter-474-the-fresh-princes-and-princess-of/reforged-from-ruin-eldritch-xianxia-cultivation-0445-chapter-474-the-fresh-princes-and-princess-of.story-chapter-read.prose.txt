“Wow this place is really small but also kind of big, why are there so many rooms? Do we all have one? Oh there’s FOOD there–”

With an effort born of long practice, transplanted in from her other selves, Raika nabs Chen Tai by the scruff of her pelts, hoisting her about an inch off the ground and interrupting the process of the half-feral woman throwing herself mouth-first into the pantry.

“Yes, Chen Tai, there’s food. That food is for sharing. Your food is upstairs.”

Wide eyes, black almost to the their edges, turn to stare at her in wonder. “Just for me?”

“Just for you. Anyone that takes any will have to respond to me.”

The younger woman squirms, then nods enthusiastically. “Ok ok ok ok. Can I please go eat my food?”

“Yes, Chen Tai.”

The moment the beastblooded woman’s feet touch back to the ground, she’s off, a thrum of Qi and distinctly non-human muscle groups letting her sprint up the stairs towards the smell of food. Raika made sure not to leave any edible goods in the other rooms, so hopefully that’ll be easy enough for her.

“Right then,” she says, taking a seat on one of the cushions dotted about the living room area. “Please take a seat. I understand that the trip took longer than expected, but I wanted to make sure everyone is safe. Now that you’re all here, I think it’s a good idea to establish some ground rules and clear up some misconceptions you all might have.”

Win Zei, Geng He, and Jin all take a seat somewhere about the room, with Geng He stepping forward to sit directly in front of her. Wei Zin sits to one side, rather demurely, while Jin takes a much more casual seat, though he remains with his legs crossed and back straight.

Damnit, she really is acting like a mom. Checking her son’s posture, at this stage of things, feels ridiculous.

But also kind of nice.

“Whatever you ask of this one, master, I will work to provide,” Geng He volunteers, bowing a bit forward.

“Yeah, first thing’s first, stop that. I chose you in large part because you didn’t talk like that, and acting like this just to try and make me like you more is only going to do the opposite.”

He blinks at that, but does sit back a little bit rather than keep bowing. “I just meant to show my respect to your true body, master, now that you’ve connected to your construct-spirit. I won’t be ungrateful to someone who has so drastically strengthened me, not when I have the opportunity not to be.”

“And it’s very nice that you’re thinking of other people now, but also, don’t do that. You have a question, ask. A statement? Go for it. I’m not the type of person that enjoys it when someone bows to me. Everybody got that?”

Jin’s had that for years. Her gaze focuses between Wei Zin and Geng He, tracking their responses to it.

Geng He, to his discredit, salutes her instead of bowing, with his hand holding a fist. Wei Zin, to her approval, just nods.

“Good. Then let’s move on. There are three things I want to cover with you all. The first is simple; we are near a village of Qi-Gathering Realm individuals, alongside many mortal children. If any one of you flares your cultivation, you’ll hurt them. Your mere presence is likely to cause changes and problems, but if you fail to show the restraint I demand of you, it will lead to death. Everyone here is powerful enough to wipe out this entire lake, nevermind the towns around it, and not one of you will act in such a manner while here. Am I understood?”

Everyone nods, though Wei Zin hesitates a bit. His robes sway a bit as he shifts his weight, long, flowing wings of grass sleeves making a pleasant swishing sound on the floor.

“It might be best for our fellow disciple to hear these words first and foremost,” he says.

“She and I will have a conversation on the matter once she’s settled in. As you all are aware, Chen Tai comes from a very different background than any of you, and the work of helping her understand where she is and what needs doing falls to me. So long as the three of you understand, here, then don’t worry about her.”

Mollified, the tribal prince nods, shifting gently back to a different position with his arms. She’ll have to review all that courtly-intrigue style of presence he has; it’s been a few years alongside him in some way or another, but his culture’s norms are intricate, and he doesn’t share them easily.

“The second thing you must understand is that you are not safe here. Not entirely. I am strong, and getting stronger all the time, but we are in enemy territory, and should the Fengs, the Divisions, or any higher Imperial authority detect us here, your first and only orders from your master are to take the villagers and evacuate. We are shielded, warded, partially shifted out of space, partially hidden inside some very convoluted conceptual techniques that I’m not sure I could explain if I wanted to, and if danger does come, I am very accustomed to reacting to it with overwhelming force. But you have to understand, deep down, that you are somewhere unfamiliar, and that there is still danger. Wander too far, make too much noise, try and alter some layer of reality without letting me know beforehand, and things can and may absolutely get messy. You are as safe here as I can possibly make you, and I mean that entirely genuinely, but be aware, and be careful.”

That one’s easier. A series of nods all around. Geng He, in particular, seems to almost instinctively want to start making some kind of plans to get stronger, his hands clenching and unclenching in what Raika thinks is unconscious behavior, but he nods as well.

He’s an idiot, yes. He is also someone smart enough to know that when Raika, of all people, says to be very fucking careful, he should probably be very. Fucking. Careful.

“Good. Hopefully, this fact will be entirely irrelevant for your stay here, and everything will go perfectly. I want all of you to feel safe and secure. I also want you all to remember that there is still danger to being in the world, and that should that danger come to pass, your role is to save as many lives as possible and leave.

“As for the third thing, it will likely be the most difficult part of this place, for all of you.”

All three of them perk up in their own ways. Even Chen Tai, for all that she’s out of the room and making messy eating noises upstairs, hears her, because it’s hard not to hear everything for miles on miles when you’re as powerful as she is, as they all are. Geng He leans forward, tempted by the thought of a challenge. Wei Zin braces himself, his heartbeat stilling towards peace as he prepares for more responsibilities.

Rai Jin, already aware of what she’s about to say, stops himself from rolling his eyes, but Raika catches the intent of the action anyways, and gives him a glare.

He holds up his hands in mock surrender, sighing from where he sits.

“The most challenging part of this time, at its core, is that none of you can use this time to try to get stronger.”

Jin knew what she was going to say, but he’s the only one. Wei Zin blinks, confused, but Geng He practically rocks back, looking almost insulted.

“The last few years have been a trial by fire in every possible meaning of the term. All of you are young, and to be shaped exclusively by strife is not a good thing. Each of you is my responsibility, and I will not have you tear yourselves apart senselessly. This time is for rest. Relaxation. Meditation. You want to practice sword forms, or exercise, you’re free to do so. If you want to study, then please, do. If you want to explore the lake and its people, you can, though I insist that you all be on your best and kindest possible behavior if you do. But nothing you do, not even rest, can be done with the intent to become stronger.”

She leans forward, meeting each of their eyes.

“You are strong. All of you. Abnormally strong for your ages, and I neither can nor will take all of the credit for that. But as your master, it is my responsibility to do more than just strengthen you. You all deserve to be well. You deserve time to act like people, rather than weapons, or political instruments, or predator and prey. So each of you will use the time that you are here to completely and entirely pursue recreation, relaxation, and some form of hobby or something, or you will accept that you are no longer my students and lose all protections therein.”

That last part is more for Wei Zin and Chen Tai than anyone else. Geng He is visibly upset, but she knows him well enough to know that it’s more because of the thought of slowing down or losing access to her teachings and resources. Wei Zin, on the other hand, is the sort of person who can always find something to do. Raika’s pretty sure it’s a trauma thing, him trying to get everything around him as perfect as he can make it so as not to lose it. As for Chen Tai… well, safety is not something she’s particularly familiar with, and there are things she fears aplenty out in the wilds waiting for a chance to get her.

Raika won’t let them. Obviously. But some extra motivation not to spend all her time trying desperately to get stronger won’t hurt the girl.

As for Jin, well.

That’s her kid. He ain’t losing shit, and the little brat knows it.

“Are there any questions?”

Geng He immediately raises his hand.

“Yes, Geng He?”

“What… sorts of hobbies are we allowed to pursue?”

“You, in particular, have to do at least two things that have nothing at all to do with fighting. Even if you’re just going out on walks sometimes.”

“But… I’m still allowed to practice sword forms?”

“You are allowed to meditate using the practice of physical movement. That doesn’t explode anything. Or use your cultivation. Or kill anything.”

His face falls incrementally as she keeps checking things off, though it’s only because she’s able to read his microexpressions that he comes across as anything other than stoic and angry.

Wei Zin is next.

“Go ahead.”

“Are we supposed to enact some sort of change or specific mission in regards to the population of this part of the Empire?”

“No. In fact, feel free to ignore them entirely. If you harass or hurt anyone, you will answer to me, but you don’t have to spend time around anyone you don’t want to, or try to make friends, or participate in any events. I recommend it, but only if you truly want to, and are willing to actually treat them like people.”

She does not call out the undercurrent of rot and sharp-edged wind that rolls through Wei Zin’s scent as they talk about Imperial citizens. She lets him keep his silence just as much as she allows Geng He the illusion that she doesn’t see just how frustrated he is.

“Jin? Any questions?”

He sighs, long and slow. “Master’s purpose and intentions are too vast for her disciple to understand, but I hear and obey. However, this lowly disciple asks master for an exchange.”

She rolls her eyes. It’s getting a little hard to tell what’s teenage rebellion and what’s playful banter with him, but she doesn’t really mind it either way. “Go ahead, kid.”

“For the burden of such restrictions, this student humbly requests that the teacher cook at least one meal a week.”

The other two turn to stare at him in surprise at that, or perhaps at the boldness of the question, but she just laughs.

“Yes, Jin, I will be cooking. More than once a week, most likely. I have a bunch of recipes that would kill these poor fishermen, and four willing taste-testers have arrived at my doorstep. Who wouldn’t take advantage of such an opportunity?”

She gives them a little time to get situated after their meeting. Wei Zin spent a while asking about every bit of minutiae he could think to ask about relating to the lake and the towns surrounding it, but Geng He took the first opportunity to storm off. Politely. Sort of. He’s learned to manage his emotions (or hide them, depending on the moment) enough that he doesn’t literally stomp his way up the stairs.

And then, when Wei Zin has spent his questions and found a politically relevant reason to be elsewhere, he leaves her with her son.

“You doing well?” she asks.

“Yes, mother, I am. You literally saw me before I left, and technically kept seeing me the whole way through the tunnel.”

She sighs, long and slow, and he watches her gently as she does.

“That… is a fair point. I just… I guess it’s different when one has the opportunity to ask.”

Her son shakes his head, meeting her eyes with that kind of cold, gentle sincerity she’s grown used to and also hasn’t seen in years.

“I understand. I might not know or feel the details, but I’m familiar with your perceptions. It must be… strange, to see yourself through eyes that are and also are not you.”

“Oh? And you can tell that so easily?”

He shrugs. “We’ve spoken about it. Me and you, Li Shu and I. Apparently, lots of cultivators and other beings that can split their minds can sometimes struggle with it. I’ve been reading into it. But also, you’re not very hard to read.”

“Maybe not to you, kid. I’ll have you know I am an expert liar.”

“Sure, but you don’t really lie to me.”

Damn the kid. He’s really getting better at getting her on her back foot.

“That’s… true. As true as I can make it.”

“I know.”

A silence falls between them. It is neither cold nor distant, neither comfortable nor alien. They have known each other for just almost half and a fifth of their lives, respectively, and also, in so many ways, are meeting for the first time.

There is something comforting in that. On both sides of it.

Rai Jin breaks the silence first. He stands from where he was sitting, turning to her, and bows, as deeply as he can without returning to the floor.

“It is an honor and a joy to get the chance to spend time with you, master. I look forward to the both of us becoming more familiar with the idea.”

She almost snorts at that. Damn brat. So fucking mature for his age.

He really deserves a chance to be a kid.

“Welcome home, kid. It’s yours, for as long as you want it. Now go get some rest. It’s good to get back in the habit of sleeping again, no matter how much your Dantian’s swollen since you were last behind the Wall.”

He nods, and smiles at her, softly. He does everything softly.

He goes to his room, following the trail of lightest cold she’s laid out to it.

All four of her disciples are home. Under one roof. Ready to experience what she has made, and willing to bow to her tyranny, which demands of them rest.

She’s pretty intimately familiar with the concept of how fucking necessary it is, long term. Of how much can go wrong when one pushes themselves forward, blindly and without regard for anything along the path.

She sighs.

It is going to be a difficult few months for them. Probably even more difficult for her.

But worth it.

She blows out the single candle illuminating the living room, letting the light of day be the only source of illumination in the living room for a while.

Then she grunts, levering herself back to her feet. She grabs her staff, braces her shoulders, and starts making her way back to her cabin.

She’s got to lead by example, after all. No matter how much she aches and writhes with the sensations of her wider being, spread across the world, stepping towards imminent war.
