Sally sat cross-legged in the dirt, playing with the detailing of her Plaguesow crossbow while the dwarven king held her tome. The small regiment of armed skeletons stood at attention, empty eye sockets looking past the forest at the bottom of the mountain, toward where the last dregs Radochs were coming from.

King Gralp clicked his teeth together. “A dwarf doesn't sign something easily, lass. Even if you're the prophesied one. I've read this thrice now; are you sure about the details?”

“Nope.” Sally grinned and put her crossbow away. “Worst-case scenario, nothing happens and you get a warrior’s death here against the bugs.”

“But, best-case scenario...” He scratched the side of his skull with a bony finger. “By the mountain, alright. I'll put my faith in you. How do I sign?”

“Oh.” Sally spun Skeleton Key in her right and cut the back of her left hand. She tilted the book towards her so that the crimson could splatter down onto the page.

The king signed his name with her blood, before she snapped the tome shut. Smiling, she allowed it to hover in the air again as she took a couple of steps back.

With his arms crossed, Archie gave her a quizzical nod. “What is this you’re attempting?”

“If you were paying attention, it's already been foreshadowed a couple of times.” Her hand stretched out. “Let me know when you're ready, Your Majesty.”

Glarp straightened himself up and gave her a nod. “Death or glory.”

Sally waved her hand, and with a gust of air, the dwarves vanished.

“I assume they were actually hoping for the glory part,” Archie said.

“Don't get smart with me, or I'll start calling you a catboy.” Sally shot him a brief scowl. “That was just Endless Sleep to pocket them away. Nothing new, even if the ability is meant to be used on zombies. The hard part comes next.”

Deathforged hovered in front of her, splitting open to the relevant page. A green glow surrounded the tome as she clenched her hand closed. Within the space where the group of dwarves once stood, thick mist rose from the rocky ground. Sally’s hand shook as the growing fog obscured the area. Her teeth clenched, and she growled.

With a deep, groaning noise, skeletal bodies emerged from the dense mist. At the head of the regiment was King Glarp.

Sally gasped and hunched over to catch her breath. The necromantic tome snapped shut. After a moment, she righted herself again and wiped the blood from her eyes. “A few more seconds, and I’d have been able to eat my own brains.”

The king held out his hands, inspecting them. Puffing out his rib cage, he took the crown from his head and kneeled down. “You have granted us true immortality. We are forever in your service.”

“Arrange your troops on the rocks with crossbows. We’ll want to hit them from range for as long as possible. Getting into direct combat when we’re so tired will not work out well for us.”

“As you command.” The skeletal dwarf turned in place and started barking commands at the rest of the gathered troops.

Archie tilted his head, a coy smile on his lips. “That’s rather astounding, Sally.”

“To force the System to grant me a new Ultimate?” She returned a grim expression. “Almost killed me. A Score to Settle, as dwarves like grudges and there’s probably around twenty of them. I didn’t count. It’s very unlikely I’ll be able to bring them back to this world if they die, with how exhausted I am.”

“Not quite an Ultimate, but let’s leave the nuance for a different day. We’ll have much to discuss once we’re back home.” The wizard stood up straighter, his eyes moving to the tunnel into the keep. “I will have to meditate to find us an option so that we can return to Sanctuary.”

“I’ll try not to make too much noise.”

He grinned and waved her off. “Time to Think. It requires concentration, but it doubles the speed at which I think. Or slows the entire world around me, who can tell?”

“Ohh, like the hyperbolic time chamber, but for your brain.” Sally nodded sagely.

“Well… I’ll just nod and pretend I understand that.” Archie gave her a shrug as he walked away.

It was difficult to imagine he was the same person as the cat she had known for so many years. There were certainly some mannerisms that felt familiar, but that could just be bias, as she knew who he was already.

Bully stepped over, watching the wizard leave before turning to Sally. “What would you like me to do?”

“Got any potions in that pile that could be useful?” She pointed at the heap of discarded items.

“Maybe a couple. Unfortunately I haven’t been able to get many materials during my time here.” He kneeled down to look through the bottles. “I had imagined this adventure to be more something more exploratory. A detective case where we’d occasionally skirmish with some bad guys.”

“It’s never that simple.” Sally gave him a soft smile. “For what it’s worth, you’ve been really helpful here. Theres no shame in wanting to take a more passive role in the future. Many Outsiders have.”

“Granted, it’s going to take a lot of work to outfit an entire world with as many flask buffs and restorative potions as possible. We’ve been stockpiling for months, but now that I truly know the enemy…” He paused to hand her two bottles. “I suppose that the information alone was worth the hardship.”

“I have a feeling things will be changing for the Outsiders in the future.” Sally held up the first potion to inspect it, but her eyes moved to the shadowed location where the wizard was meditating. “If so, we’re going to need a strong supply line to keep us sharp.”

[Find Bearings Potion][When thrown, spawns in 200-350 metal ball bearings]

[Static Flask][Causes an area of static energy for 5 minutes]

In return, she handed him her crossbow. “Go help the dwarves. I’ll be done with your Inventory soon, and then you can have everything back.”

Sally spent the next ten minutes going through her abilities, selecting the next three she should put in her necromancy tome. After some deliberation—and plenty of macabre arts and crafts—she finalized her selection and put them in the book.

[Deathforged 5/5][Mortis Bomb][A Score to Settle][Endless Death/Endless Sleep][Living Dead][Detect Death]

A decent starting selection. Living Dead was the healing and buffing spell that worked on her zombie pals. While she had a natural aptitude for sensing her summons, Detect Death gave her a more accurate reading on the number and status of any minions within a certain distance.

Content enough, she let the tome hover in the air to circle behind her. Two lines of white thread spun around her, tying Deathforged to her back and looping like a backpack. After checking it was nice and secure, she left Bully to collect his items and stepped over to Hunter.

The shadowy man had been sitting on a rock, watching the cloud of ash in silence. Half of the world was in a state of faux night from the airborne debris, and a gray haze was working its way to the mountains. He gave the zombie a brief glance as she stopped beside him, before returning to staring out at the world.

“So,” Sally began, “you agreed to stay behind to try to destroy the planet.”

Hunter was silent for a moment. “You were listening in, then.”

“I might come off as a goofball, but I didn’t make it this far by being complacent. Are you sure you’ll find your way back to us?”

He nodded. “Pretty sure. I know what to look for now. How about you? Confident you’ll make your way back home?”

“I trust Archie will find a way.” She glanced back at the fort tunnel entrance. The area was still covered in the corpses of the Radochs who had attacked them before. “Even if we can’t pop back on the Omen, we could probably go back the old-fashioned way.”

“You said your world has no death.”

“It has death; people can just come back from it. Things change, but our souls are recreated when needed… rather than being deleted, I guess.”

Hunter grunted, but had nothing else to add.

With a yawn, the zombie deflated. “I’m no stranger to tiring fights, but this System swap has really taken it out of me. Having the dwarves, floating tome, and thread backpack all grind away at my energy as well.”

“I was wondering why you weren’t bringing out any of your pals.”

She held out her hand before lowering it. “Normally I’d be spamming summons, but that’s all I’ve been doing for the last two days. A Zombie Apocalypse or two would really tie up the bugs while we hit them with ranged attacks.”

“I’d offer to possess you, but that’s not likely to go well.” Hunter tilted his head. “Or if the wizard finds a way for you to leave soon, you can let me deal with the cockroaches.”

Sally grinned. “Nah. I can’t let you have all the fun.”

“This sounds rather miserable.” Ren leaned against the large bear with her arms crossed. “You shouldn’t be encouraging this, Max.”

The purple-clad magician gave her a sheepish shrug. “The logic is sound, even if it is risky.”

“If he dies, we might not get off this planet.”

Over by the edge of the void, Theo smiled to himself. “Technically, as long as this world remains stable, we’d be able to send a second rescue party. It just can never be me again.”

“That doesn’t exactly fill me with confidence.” The elf narrowed her eyes back at Max. “And you shouldn’t be encouraging this.”

“We’ve been through worse, endured the worst risks.” He tilted his head. “This is just a little disappearing act, with an emergency exit should we need it.”

Theo took a long breath while the pair sorted themselves out. It was a simple plan in theory. One of his Aspect powers was a limited teleport. Once he located and switched over to wherever the control room was, the two magicians could use a party teleport scroll to join him - assuming it was safe.

Then, if they did come up against any trouble, they’d teleport scroll back out to Wolf. The bear seemed fine with acting as their anchor, even if he had grumbled at the start.

The vampire turned to look at the sky. Doing this at night would be better, but the quicker this was over, the sooner he could be back home. With a fanged smile, he turned to the magicians.

“As discussed, I’ll need that catalyst to work my ability.”

Max gave him a dry smile in return and avoided Ren’s scowl. With the flick of his hand, he removed his top hat and made it vanish into thin air. “Ready when you are.”

The air around the vampire shimmered. In a split second, he snapped from his position and appeared behind the magician. Fangs snapped down on Max’s neck and blood burst from the wound. Bright crimson soaked through his suit as Theo drank deeply from the man.

With an almost feral grunt, he threw the drained body to the ground. As he wiped the blood from his mouth, the real Max appeared out of invisibility to the side.

“Human, Human, Demon,” the vampire said. “Exceptionally sweet, with a little spice to it. Powerful blood.”

“Good enough, I hope.”

“Perfect.” Theo turned his burning red eyes to the void before closing them. He held a finger up in front of his face and exhaled. Reaper’s Arrival required blood to work. Especially at this distance. For such a remote and dangerous destination.

For a moment of extended silence, everyone remained in place with bated breath. The empty nothingness was just too vast and featureless.

Then, there it was.

Theo vanished from his position, waves of cold air rushing over his body before he came to a sudden stop.

Darkness. A small chamber with reflective monitor screens barely picking up a small amount of light, not unlike the control room back in Sanctuary. As the vampire went to move, a buzzing noise crackled through the room. Floursescent lights flickered on, painting the gray room in focus.

Theo turned his eyes slowly across the screens as data ran across them until he noticed the chair in front of the monitors.

A body lay splayed out on it, long dead.

Dessicated, the corpse of a Radoch stared back at him with empty eyes.
