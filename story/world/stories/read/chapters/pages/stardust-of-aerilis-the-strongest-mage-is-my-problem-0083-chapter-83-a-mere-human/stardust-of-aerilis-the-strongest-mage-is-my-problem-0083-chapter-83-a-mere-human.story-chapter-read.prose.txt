Aiven stood in front of Virelle, who was keeping her smile on.

The orb pulsed, slow and even, at her side. The carpet ran red between them. The pale light from the ceiling did not flicker.

"What's wrong, Aiven?"

Her voice was almost the same. Almost. She floated upward a fraction, the layered hem of her skirt rippling. The cadence was hers. The lilt was hers. The face was hers.

He took a step backwards before he knew he was doing it.

"Didn't you want to see me?" she said. She drifted forward, closing the distance he had just opened. "I've been waiting for you."

He made his feet stop.

"What did they do to you?"

She giggled; it was a small, light sound, and it was the most wrong thing in the room.

"What they did? You make it sound as if something terrible happened, Aiven. Don't be dramatic."

He clenched his fist at his side. The Armvil hummed faintly under his sleeve. The frustration that rose in his chest was, strangely, not directed at her, and not at Lucien either. It was aimed somewhere closer in. At himself. At the fact that he was standing here at all. At the fact that he had been too slow to get here, and that he had not been there when she was captured.

"Master simply made me return to my true self," she said. "That is all."

Then he felt it.

The air around her thickened. The pale ceiling overhead grew points of light — small and bright at first, then quickly multiplying. Mana spears, dozens of them, forming spear-tip-down along the vault.

They fell.

Aiven brought both hands up and shaped the dome around himself in a single breath. The lavender curved over his head and locked at his boots, and the spears hit it in a rolling crash of impacts that should have shattered it. They did not.

They struck the dome and stopped. They held against its outer surface for the briefest moment, then dissolved into drifting motes. The dome held.

Across the room, Virelle's eyes widened.

"Interesting," she said. She tilted her head a fraction, the way a child examines a beetle that has unexpectedly walked sideways. "It would seem the similarities in our mana signatures have made your spellwork rather sturdier against mine."

Aiven's brain caught on the word.

Similarities.

Aiven did not know what she had meant.

Virelle snapped her fingers, and a lavender dome bloomed around him.

Tight. Curved. Sealed. His heartbeat slammed against his ribs.

He remembered the Sunken Fane. The mythic-class beast. The three-headed lion. The way Virelle had encased it in a clean dome and then, without flourish, closed her hand, and what had been inside had been only red mist and the wet weight of what was left.

She was going to do it to him.

He was going to die in this room minutes after seeing her again. He was going to die compressed into a wet handful of nothing on a red carpet while she watched.

He drove the Armvil into the inside of the dome. The cyan etchings flared. The obsidian fist landed and the dome did not even shudder.

Virelle smiled.

She raised her palm. She closed her fingers.

Aiven shut his eyes.

Nothing happened.

He opened his eyes.

The dome was still around him. It had not contracted. It had not crushed. It was simply there, a tight lavender cage, and across the room Virelle's eyes had widened a second time.

He moved before he thought.

He fired a mana blast point-blank at the inside of the dome, and where the beam struck, the lavender cracked. He drove the Armvil through the fracture and the entire structure shattered around him in a cascade of dust.

He landed on the red carpet, breathing hard.

Virelle was looking at him with something like surprise. It was wiped from her face almost immediately.

"That," she said, "was not what I expected from a mere human."

He swallowed.

"What do you mean?"

"Never mind, Aiven." She waved one hand. "Your intelligence is too low to grasp it anyway."

She fired without warning.

The mana blast came across the room with the speed of a thrown spear, and Aiven had no time to dodge. It hit him square in the chest.

It did not punch through him.

It hit him like a pole, like a length of rolled steel slamming into his ribs, and the breath left his lungs in a single hard exhale, and he tumbled backwards across the carpet until his shoulder caught a column and stopped him.

He gasped.

Across the room, Virelle's brow drew together. The puzzled, considering look returned to her face.

Then she fired again.

Aiven rolled. The second blast carved across the floor where he had been a heartbeat ago. He scrambled up — and a lightning bolt was already snapping at him from her other hand. He twisted. The bolt missed his chest by a finger's-breadth, but it caught his side, and he felt his coat tear and the skin beneath it sear in a long burning line.

He bit down on the sound.

Virelle smiled.

It was a small, dawning smile, the kind a child gives when an experiment has just produced an unexpected result.

She had figured something out.

Aiven gritted his teeth. His mind was running. Lucien had absorbed his pure-mana attacks but not the lightning, not the earth, not the fist. This was...different. The mana blast had hit him, but not the way it should have. The lightning had cut him cleanly.

Pure mana was blunted against him. Elemental was not.

Whatever the similar signature meant, it meant his own kind of magic refused to bite him at its full weight. And she had now noticed.

She would adjust.

"Virelle!" His voice cracked across the room. "Please, snap out of it!"

"Snap out of what?"

She drifted a half-pace closer, her head tilting again, indulgent. "I have already snapped out of it, Aiven. The moment Master helped me remember who I truly am."

Master.

She said it the way she had once said it to him.

Something behind his sternum gave a small, sharp twist that did not belong on any battlefield. He almost staggered from it, and he had not been struck.

"I cannot believe," she went on, the tone going soft and theatrical now, "how many moons I wasted on a mere human like you. Swooning. Defending you from things you could not handle. Telling you that you were important." She laughed; a short, almost private laugh. "Important. To me. The strongest mage in existence."

"Virelle, no." His throat hurt. "This isn't you."

"Oh, Aiven."

She drifted another half-pace closer. The carpet did not whisper beneath her. Her feet were not touching it.

"This is me. This has always been me. The other one, the one who blushed at your dreadful little gestures, who hovered at your shoulder, who let you call her by her name, she was the fake. I am simply out of it now."

Aiven could not meet her eyes.

He looked down. The red of the carpet swam.

Virelle noticed.

"Oh?" Her voice brightened, cruelly. "What is this? Are you about to cry? Then go on. Bawl. Wail. It is an act befitting a pathetic creature like you. I shall enjoy it."

The room filled with light.

Magic circles — dozens of them, fire-red, bloomed in the air around her in a tight blooming corona.

"Goodbye, Aiven," she said. "It was not fun being with you."

She brought her arms down.

Aiven moved.

He clenched his fist and rushed forward into the firestorm, because there was nowhere else to go. The fireballs poured down on him in a slow heavy rain; he ducked the first, smashed the second with the Armvil, threw up a half-dome against the third and fourth. A spark caught the edge of his coat and he slapped it out as he ran. Another caught his sleeve. He kept moving.

Eight paces. Six. Four.

He drove forward, the obsidian arm leading, aiming to tackle her, to take her down to the carpet, to put his hand on her shoulder, anything that might —

She fired a mana blast at point-blank range.

It hit him in the sternum and slammed him backward across the room. He skidded along the carpet and came up on one knee, gasping. The lightning burn at his side throbbed. The patches of small fire on his coat had eaten into the leather. He could taste copper.

"Did you," he said, "forget?"

His voice did not sound like his own.

She tilted her head. "Forget? About what?"

"About the moments we — the ones —" His voice broke. He remembered the deck of the Cinder-Fox. The dark above and below them. The way she had bared herself to him, finally, the part of her she had been afraid to show.

She finished it for him.

"The love?" Her voice was light. Mocking. "The tears? The little confessions on the deck of a ship?"

He could not speak.

"I remember them."

The breath caught in his throat.

"And I was revolted by them."

He flinched as if she had struck him.

"Me," she went on, the disgust shaping every syllable now, "the strongest mage in existence, trembling over a parasite. A small frightened man who could not even break the seal that has been throttling his power. A leech who would have died in a ditch within a season if I had not deigned to coddle him." She wrinkled her nose. "Disgraceful. I shall need centuries to wash the taste of that affection out of my mouth."

Aiven could not breathe.

"I have babysat you for far too long. I am sick of it."

She drifted, lazy and slow, the orb keeping perfect pace beside her. She turned her face upward toward the ceiling, and her expression changed.

It softened.

Her eyes half-closed. A small, reverent smile touched her mouth, and it was the worst thing yet, because he had only ever seen that expression on her when she looked at him.

It was the look of someone gazing at a thing she loved, and it was not aimed at him.

"Master, on the other hand," she said, the reverence soft and full in her voice, "is superior in every way. He has conquered me. He has reminded me of the glorious purpose I am meant for. The work he and I will do together —"

Aiven was already moving.

He shoved the earth beneath his boots and let it kick him forward and up. Halfway through the lunge he reached for flight and let the wind carry the rest of him. Virelle was still looking up, still lost in that soft reverent half-smile aimed at someone who was not in this room and possibly not in this world.

He hit her.

The Armvil wrapped around her shoulder, his other arm caught her around the ribs, and they went down together onto the red carpet in a tangle. He pinned her at the shoulders. He did not strike. He did not punch. He simply held her there and looked down into her face.

"Virelle." His voice was wrecked. "Please."

He searched her eyes.

He looked for familiarity they shared. He looked for the small, fragile, thread of a voice she had used. He looked for any of it. Anything. The smallest scrap.

Virelle's expression darkened.

WHOOSH.

A blast of wind exploded between them. Aiven was thrown clean off her, the wind catching his chest and hurling him back across the room. He twisted in flight and landed on his feet, boots sliding several inches along the floor before catching.

Virelle stood.

She brushed one shoulder. Then the other. With the careful, fastidious motion of a woman wiping invisible filth off her sleeve.

"Do not," she said, "touch me with your filthy hands again. I am allergic to it."

She raised both arms.

Magic circles bloomed across the entire room. Dozens. He lost count before he could try. Fire-red, white-blue, pale green, every element she had ever shown him and a few he had never seen, layered in the air above her like the ceiling of a cathedral.

The barrage came.

Aiven dodged the first wave. He twisted under a wind slash, ducked a fireball, caught a lightning bolt on the dome that he managed to throw up just in time. The dome held against one bolt. It held against two. It cracked on the third and dissolved.

He kept moving. He had to keep moving. He shoved off the carpet and tried to fly low across the room. A wind slash caught him across the back of the knee, and his flight collapsed mid-arc. He hit the ground hard.

Fireballs found him.

One on the shoulder. One on the back. One on the ribs. Two more across his coat. He could smell his own hair burning. He could feel the leather of his coat melting against his back. He rolled, slapped, rolled again. The smaller fires went out.

The pain did not.

He pushed himself up onto one elbow.

He could not understand why he was still alive.

Virelle drifted toward him through the smoke of her own attack. Slow. Unhurried. The orb at her side pulsing softly, indifferent. She stopped over him and raised one hand and pointed her open palm at his head.

He looked up.

His chest was on fire. His back was on fire. His breath came in ragged scrapes.

He thought, distantly: this might be it.

If it was, he was going to die looking at her.

He kept his eyes on her face.

She smiled.

"Now," she said softly. "Die."

A fireball bloomed in her palm at point-blank range.

The world went white.

Then black.
