Nonetheless, he and Neex took the final bites of their burritos, got some to-go plates, and he paid for the meal. I think I might buy that sweet number, under whatever official civilian cover I can take as a permanent ‘normal life’ type. Soon.

Grinning, Jack nodded. “Right. Gotcha.”

She squeezed a bit more and stayed with her head on his shoulder. In the corner of his eye, Jack could see her hair twitch, in what he was sure was the urge of her tentacles to engage in ‘face-time.’ Neex continued in suspiciously casual tones, “Which probably means not dating anyone else while dating each other, you know…”

“Oh, probably so,” Jack muttered back, fighting his amusement.

“I-if that’s your preference, I mean…”

“Mmn. Is it yours?”“Yes!” she said too quickly and breathily. Then she froze with her breath held. “Wait, did y-? I-I mean, ah, if you…” Suddenly, she pulled back and glared right at him with a perturbed look. “Hey, no fair, I asked you first, and you didn’t answer properly!” Then she saw his face and blinked, quickly shifting blank in the realization that he was teasing.

Laughing, Jack raised his brows and nodded. “Yes, it is my preference.”

She squinted her eyes at him and grunted, with a twist of a lip and a pout to go with her glare. “Don’t tease me about this, Jack.”

“Sorry. I couldn’t resist. Forgive me?”

Another grunt. “Mmmph.” Eyes casting aside briefly, she shifted and leaned up to plant a lightning-speed kiss on his cheek, then hopped down, suddenly very bashful. Deftly scooping up her bags, she darted a step toward the elevator as if she’d thieved something, then spun around, grinning his way with her eyes down and her arms behind her back, walking backward. Finally, she met his eyes and waved. “See you soon! Let me go down first! I want to talk to Bo in my head extremely right now!” She turned back around and ran full-speed to the elevator.

Jack waved in response, barely missing it being exclusively at her back. Watching her disappear inside the elevator, he took a deep breath and shifted the waving hand to rub the back of his neck. He didn’t know why she couldn’t talk to Bo in her head while he was in the elevator, but whatever.

Now that everything had happened, he was feeling slightly stunned. Bewildered? Yes, that was the word. A good kind of bewildered. Why only just then? Well, that convo had just happened, but the date itself was ‘clutch time.’ No room for bewilderment while laying down the charm. But now? Oh, it was all hitting him like a speeding truck.

Okay, so… do I have an alien princess girlfriend, now?

He probably had Bo to thank for that, as a co-conspirator laying out the trap. Not that he was anything but happy to jump right into it, but a trap was a trap.

And, now that he thought about it, maybe even Délight was another to thank, as a sudden imagined ‘competitor’ on Neex’s part. Under the surface, he knew she was a bit anxious about the girl and her penchant for seduction, and the same probably went for Bo, in seeing him get friendlier with her. Relationship FOMO?

Well, if true, whatever. The ‘catch’ has been caught. I’m won. Ha!

Much as it might warm the ego, it was downright hilarious to consider he was some kind of hot item like that. He couldn’t wrap his brain around it. He was firmly in the camp that any ‘score’ and especially any girlfriend he’d obtained over the years was obtained by a stroke of luck, regardless of whether he put the effort in. Sometimes he did, sometimes he didn’t.

Fortunately, he knew absolutely that he liked Neex and she liked him. It wasn’t simply the bond causing that, but the bond confirmed and amplified whatever was already there. That’s why, bewildered afterward or not, the date had not surprised him, and even the status of ‘Dating’ — with a capital D — wasn’t out of the blue. She wasn’t merely experimenting with human things. She’d gained enough understanding of human courtship to seek him out for it.

He reminded himself to ask her about the differences between their cultures in this regard and what exactly this meant on her side. Especially considering what she was, status-wise.

I feel a cliché ‘What have I gotten myself into?!’ question coming on.

Shaking it all off, Jack headed for the elevator and Operations to shed his disguise and check with Memoria about exactly where she wanted him for this ominous consultation. Whatever it was, the odds that it was ‘good news’ were pretty shit.

“So you’re not quite as dense as you were showing, huh? That’s good. I thought perhaps you just had a huge, weird romantic blind spot in that strategic mind of yours. Well, you’re damned right you have me to thank — and you’re welcome. We only had to twist your fraggin’ arm around your back to make this happen, Mr. Hard-to-Get.”

Shirtless in the mirror briefly as he changed, Jack patted his abs in thanks as well. They’d come through, clearly! “Hey, we’ve been really busy! And trust me, I have my reasons for keeping to a zero-pressure policy regarding her.”

“Very gentlemanly of you, but the pressure is on, now, understand?”

“Anyway, you apparently did spectacularly. Ira hasn’t stopped gushing, really, I’ll say that much.”

“Awesome.”

“Not that she seems that hard to please. I don’t think she’s been on a date before, or if she has, in a totally different frame than all you offered. I can relate to that. Everything in this big, crowded world was alien to me, too.”

“I’m very glad she has you.”

“Eh, you two have a next-level connection. I feel subtext, ridiculous chemistry, or maybe both, under the surface. Only a statue could look at you two and not feel compelled to push you together. It’s just the pull of the universe.”

“You know, Bo, you are about the most socially-aware and sensitive person I’ve met. Quite the jarring contrast to your affected, front-facing disinterest.”

“Appearances are usually deceiving. Just how I like it.”

Jack’s mind went back to her tattoo. A tattoo over a brand. We gotta do what we gotta do to protect ourselves, I guess. “There’s nothing deceiving at all about Highfive.”

Bo snorted. “Okay, appearances are deceiving when brains are involved.”

As Jack got fully changed and sorted, he chuckled and shook his head at Bo’s acidic quip. “Despite your insults, you’re always a good friend, Bo.”

“Alright, I don’t have to take you and your endless compliments. I’m outta here… Cowboy.” She disconnected.

Jack froze at the mocking, yet self-satisfied tone of the final word, and soon figured out exactly why. He groaned.

Nooo! She watched that damn movie! The Newsies — please no, anything but that nickname! Jack “Cowboy” Kelly, my accursed namesake. And she even said she was going to give me a pet name in retaliation for mine. Great…

Just as Jack exited his room with intentions to exit his quarters shortly thereafter, he saw Memoria sitting in his seat in the living room, in a pinstripe suit and matching pants, waiting patiently with her legs crossed and her hands in her lap.

Jack stopped dead and sighed. “So we’re going to talk here, then. Okay. You want a drink?”

Memoria shook her head and gestured at the couch insistently, meeting his eyes.

Jack took the seat. “So what is this news, then?”

Memoria snapped her fingers, and Jack’s TV turned on. It showed multiple grainy video feeds, four at once, apparently of some sort of large compound or mansion, as taken from long-range photography. Nothing particularly noteworthy. She spoke as it flipped through the scenes. “This is the home and effective operational center of one Zachariah Heavenward, of the independent Heavenward Platform. Once one of the more functional cult of personality style communities on the back of Zachariah’s deceased father, Azarias, it later redrafted its treaty to become a partner with the Farmers’ Alliance and profited a great deal during an unfortunate food shortage.

“We are suspicious of Heavenward as being connected with the Tiamat cult, though without concrete evidence. The platform itself is highly desirable as having a strong, stiffly-worded treaty compared to most Farmers’ Alliance members. It could act as a bridge zone between inaccessible havens unknown to me and the rest of the various networks branching to NB from the Alliance. I will note these video feeds are only in our possession, thanks to actual native cooperation on Heavenward, from a scarce supply of informants getting far too rich off of helping us. Rich enough, and nervous enough, I’m not sure how long they’ll keep risking it as opposed to retiring.”

Jack nodded along slowly as he watched the video feeds show… nothing much, really. People talking outside. Occasional, seemingly unhelpful views through a window. Vessels coming and going constantly on the roof. “Alright. Following so far…”

Back to the cult, eh? Good. I’d like to keep working on the existing task force, if I do anything before the trip.

Memoria glanced at him and then back at the screen. “Here’s where things get a little more interesting — if you’ll forgive word use soon to seem insufficient.”

The split screen shifted just to one, showing a figure heading into a vessel on the roof, moving fast, hands in the pockets of his coat. Just before ducking into the vessel, he paused and looked out, sweeping his gaze. At this moment, the screen froze and then zoomed in on the man’s face. Enhanced.

Jack shot up to his feet without realizing it. “That’s my dad.”

“Yes…”

Jack swallowed as he stared unblinkingly at the screen; his father’s lined, too-aged, too-haunted face seemed to stare back at him. Anxiety began to seep into his gut.

Damn it, Dad. What the hell have you gotten yourself into, now?

“Why is he there?” Jack asked, shaking his head in annoyance. Memoria didn’t answer, so he turned to her.

She was peering at him with concern and sympathy, searching his face, one manicured hand rubbing fingers together, as if in longing for a cigarette. “I’d like to know the answer to that just as much as you do.”

“Oh, I doubt that very much.”

“All the more reason to find out, hmm? I don’t know where he is.”

A leading question. Jack ran a hand through his hair, sat down, and took a deep, calming breath. He looked back at the screen for a long moment, then turned to Memoria. “How can I help?”

“There is potential information on his whereabouts, contacts, old contacts, tendencies, et cetera, out there. You are his son, and thus a key asset to reach him. I know you don’t have a direct method right now, Jack. But you have a better chance than anyone. For starters, you’re a rare, technical citizen of New Eden under our employ, with a powerful existing contact. That’s an independent platform. I need an invitation or a catastrophically strong cause to do anything there. Even asking their leadership sets off alarm bells. What we did there to secure you and Neex, despite saving many of their citizen’s lives, is still frowned upon, perhaps especially for our efforts to keep it all as under wraps as possible.”

Jack also frowned. “You want me to be your undercover agent there.”

Memoria looked down at the floor and didn’t answer.

Everything I was, everything I am and will be, is dedicated to the jacket, now. To her and the org. That’s what I signed up for. But it doesn’t matter, does it? Toss that aside, and I’d still do anything to find my dad and pull him a million miles away from this. Even the slightest chance he’s been influenced by the Dreamer…

Jack set his jaw. “I’m at your service, Mother. Whatever way we can snag my dad out of this, I’m all for it.”

Memoria looked up at him with a note of appreciation on her face. “I know this is hard for you on multiple levels. It’s deeply personal. I’m just sorry you have to be tested in this way so early. Cadet battles, hard training, getting thrown off buildings, terrifying monsters, it all pales in comparison to family trouble.”

“I’m glad you’re taking it well. And, of course, you already have it in your contract, so I shouldn’t have to tell you we’ll spare no effort or expense to help him, once he’s taken in.”

Jack’s mind flashed to Screamer, and he had to suppress a horrified panic at that happening to his dad. No! I have Q-Loth now. He wouldn’t have to go through what she did. But I have to find him. He looked back at the screen. He didn’t look crazy. Did he? “Thank you. And I’ll make damn sure he talks, don’t you worry.”

“Of that I have little doubt, my son.”

“Alright, what now? When do I start, what do I do?”

No rush, but ‘I need to talk to you.’ We understand he doesn’t know anything directly, but he may have mechanisms or direct you to other potential contacts. Wherever your digging takes you, even if you seem to come up empty, it may trigger your father knowing. Perhaps then, he’ll come to you.”

To everyone else, you got into ‘plat-to-plat trade.’ They’ll infer not to press on the details on the assumption of you being anything from a criminal, to an actual intercommunity liaison, to indeed, a good ole government asset. Meanwhile, we’ll get you a pricey apartment to move into. In fact, it’s already waiting.”

Everyone.” She broke the hug and smiled apologetically. “I think I should apologize for possibly spoiling your ceremony tomorrow. I thought you’d want to know as soon as I did, regardless of-”
