Chapter 106

The group came down out of the north woods in the late afternoon with the Hoshin walking between the mounted soldiers, packs across their shoulders, the few pack animals from the fallen Clawborn, strung along behind them under loose leads.

They had moved at the slow pace of a people carrying what was left of their lives on their backs, and Riley had set the pace to what the slowest in line could hold, and the line had held it the whole day. The light had gone from the high white of midday to the flat soft grey of the hour before dusk.

The trees opened.

The cleared ground rolled out in front of them, and beyond it the walls of the settlement rose against the slate sky with the watchtowers picked out at the corners. From three hundred paces out Riley could see the duty sentry on the wall above the gate turn at the column's approach and disappear, and a moment later the horn sounded once, a single low note that carried clean across the cleared ground.

Riley willed open the HUD.

❖ Approach Registered: Friendly Group

She closed it. The system had logged her group at the wall. The gate would open without further word.

The gate began to swing inward as they came on. Two soldiers worked each leaf, and the heavy hinges turned in the slow patient way they were built to turn, and the inner yard appeared in widening slices between the swinging timbers. Riley saw the wagon crew at the lumber yard look up. She saw the smoke from the forge. She saw Nikola standing in the open yard inside the gate with his hands folded behind his back.

Then, she saw Valrik.

He was at Nikola's left shoulder, two paces back. Broad shoulders with his helm under one arm. Posture straight. He was in his armor; not the bedside robes she had last seen him in, the fresh-from-the-forge plate that had been waiting for him in the racks.

Riley smiled before she had decided to.

The smile happened in her chest before her mouth, and her mouth caught up to it, and she let it stay. The group came on through the gate and the smile stayed all the way to the post where she pulled her horse up and swung down.

Nikola stepped forward.

"Warden."

He looked past her to the group coming through the gate. He was running a tally in his head.

"Forty-one," he said. "The bunkhouse is ready."

"Welcome them home."

He nodded once. "Yes, Warden."

She turned her head toward the gate. "Lead them yourself. Get them settled. Whatever they need tonight, you find it."

"I will."

He stepped past her toward the group, and as he passed, he gave the smallest nod to Valrik.

The earlier Hoshin had come out from the houses along the yard. They moved toward the new arrivals in the loose, hurried way of people who had been waiting for hours and had finally been given the signal, and the arrivals slowed as the two groups met in the open ground. Embraces. Names called out.

Nami was at the edge of it.

She stood with her clay-stained apron still on from the kiln line, and she had come down with the others when the horn sounded. Her eyes had been moving across the arrivals the way the other Hoshin's eyes had been moving, looking for the face she had not been certain she would see.

She saw Tama.

Tama walked through the group and connected with an older woman she had not seen in weeks. Nami stopped where she was. She did not call out. She lifted one hand partway and held it there for a moment and then she lowered it, and she gave Tama a small short nod that Tama returned across the open ground, and then Nami turned and walked back the way she had come, toward the kiln line, without speaking to anyone else.

Riley saw it.

She did not say anything.

Thorne came up at her shoulder.

She did not turn to him. She kept her eyes on the gate.

"Help our returning soldiers back into their uniforms and get them deployed," she said. "I want them on the wall and at the inner gate before the moon comes up. Standard rotations, but heavier on the north wall tonight."

"Yes, Warden."

"And the two guests."

"Warden."

"Secured. Guarded. Day and night. No one talks to them except me and you. They eat what we give them, and they get water, and they get nothing else until I say."

Thorne's eyes shifted past her toward the gate.

The two Clawborn were coming through now. Their heads were down. The bruising along the jaw of the larger one had gone from purple to the yellow-green of the second day. They did not look at the yard as they came in.

"Understood," Thorne said.

"Go."

He went.

Riley stood for a moment in the open ground of the inner yard with the column moving past her, the Hoshin going one way under Nikola's lead toward the new bunkhouse at the far side, the two Clawborn going the other way under guard toward the holding cells at the back of the barracks, and Thorne moving among her soldiers in the practiced quiet way he moved when he had orders to execute.

Then she turned.

Valrik had not moved from where he stood.

She walked the six paces to him.

He straightened as she came up, the small reflexive adjustment he made when she addressed him, and his helm shifted slightly under his arm, and his eyes were on hers.

"You're back," she said.

"I am, Warden."

She looked at him properly then. The colour was in his face again. There was none of the slack she had seen at the bedside, none of the careful breathing of a man holding himself together against an injury.

"How do you feel?"

"Better. More like my old self."

"Truly?"

"Truly, Warden. I want to get back to it."

She nodded.

"You were missed."

He did not answer. The corner of his mouth did the smallest thing that was not quite a smile, and he gave her a short nod, and then he looked past her shoulder toward the gate and toward the yard.

"A lot has changed."

"Yes."

"There is a road running south."

"The southern cutting crews. Eight days now."

"Clay pot weapons. There is a work detail."

"Nami runs it. The pots come from the kiln. The wicks are the issue."

"Wildfire production."

"Limited. It is in the side chamber off the main cavern. Landryn placed it where the draw vents outside the walls."

His eyes moved across the yard again. "And a new bunkhouse. Full, by the look of it."

"As of an hour ago."

He let his eyes finish their tour and brought them back to her, and the steady operational look settled in them.

"Actually," she said, "I need you for something specific."

"I am yours to command."

"The clay pots. The wicks and the pots themselves. I need them tested. We have them in storage and we have men learning to throw them, but we have not put them to a real test under conditions that match how they will be used. I need someone who has seen them work."

He had seen them work at the raider compound. He had seen the fire come up the wall on the night that had ended with him on a horse with his eyes closed. The memory was in his face for a fraction of a second and then flashed away.

"I know the weapon. How can I help?"

"A proper test programme. Range. Burn time. Wick reliability. How the pot fails when it fails. What happens when it lands short. Whether a man can carry six on a belt or whether four is the working number. The men on the line know how to make them. They do not know how to use them. You do."

"Leave it to me, Warden."

"Second thing. Thorne has been thinking about the Level Two soldiers. He came at me on the ride to Hoshin with three doctrines and a preference. He has started drilling a fire team to test one of them. I want you on that conversation with him. Tomorrow. Walk the drill ground. Bring your own read."

"The Level Twos?" His eyes narrowed slightly. "When are they coming?"

"Soon."

"That changes training."

"Which is why I want you on it with Thorne."

"Of course, I am happy to help."

The two Clawborn were being walked across the far side of the yard now, the guards moving them at the slow pace ordered, and the path took them in front of the post where she and Valrik were standing. Riley did not turn her head. Valrik did. His eyes followed them across the open ground, the larger one first, then the smaller.

"Those two."

"From Hoshin," she said. "Tribute escort to a tribute collector named Korr-va. Korr-va did not make the ride back."

Something moved at the corner of his mouth.

"And these are alive."

"For now."

He turned his eyes back to her. "What is your special project for them?"

"I need information from them and we may have to break them to get it."

"Information?"

"Anything about the Clawborn army. Others the Clawborn collect tribute from. Trade routes, supply lines."

"And then?"

"They die. We do not pretend otherwise. But we get what we get first."

He held her eyes for a count of two.

"Would you like to be involved with the project?"

Valrik's eyes defocused for a moment as Riley's brow furrowed.

"Valrik?"

"I would make that my top priority, Warden."

"We will start tomorrow, morning. The cells at the back of the barracks. Thorne knows. He will give you what you need."

"Yes, Warden."

She paused.

The yard was quieter now. The Hoshin had moved out of sight toward the bunkhouse. The Clawborn had been locked up.

She put her hand on his arm.

It was the plate of the new armor under her palm, cold and solid, and his arm did not move under it, but his eyes came down to the hand and then back up to her face, and the small almost-smile was still at the corner of his mouth.

"You were missed."

He gave her the short nod he had given her at the bedside. He held it for a beat.

"I missed this place too, Warden."

She let her hand stay where it was for one more breath, then took it away, and Valrik straightened the fraction more that was the part of his posture he reserved for being dismissed.

"Get some food. Then find Thorne before he turns in. I want you both on the same page before the morning."

"Yes, Warden."

Riley turned and started to walk away, then paused.

"Welcome back."

Then she walked toward the bunkhouse to see Tama.
