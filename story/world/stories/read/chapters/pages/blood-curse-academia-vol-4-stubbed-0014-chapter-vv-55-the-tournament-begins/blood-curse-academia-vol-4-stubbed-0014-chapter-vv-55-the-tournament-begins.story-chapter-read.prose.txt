Chapter V.V (5.5) - The Tournament Begins

Kizu stood off to the side of his Combat F class, stretching while the rest of his classmates trickled in. He’d brought Mort with him to class, as Arclight had instructed several times before, and the monkey was currently off by the courtyard’s creek, trying to snatch up small minnows. Each quarter Arclight liked to announce a new surprise on them, a change in how the class operated and its curriculum. He’d like to think he’d wised up to it by now and would be prepared for whatever she sent after them.

He bent his monster leg in a stretch, feeling it bend backwards at the knee and nearly allowing him to completely fold it on top of itself. With a shake, the leg snapped back into place. The longer he used it, the more versatility he found in the limb.

If Arclight decided they needed to fight today, he would be ready for a fight.

The last of the students plopped down on the grass and they all chatted amongst themselves while they waited for the jovial professor.

Nothing.

Kizu glanced at the academy’s clocktower overhead. Arclight was currently ten minutes late. Not once, in all of his previous classes with her, had she ever arrived late. Now it could have something to do with the Dragon attack after midterms. Maybe she was still filling out paperwork and forms. Or helping with security measures. But still…it felt off.

“Mort, scout the area,” he requested to his familiar.

The owl monkey perked up, a slimy minnow slipping through his fingers, then he dashed off to the side. He circled the perimeter looking for any clues to where Arclight might be at. Kizu silently scanned the courtyard with his eyes for anything out of the ordinary.

The stone statues at the corners of the roofs looked a bit off. He was trying to decide if one of them had been replaced by an actual stone creature when someone beside him spoke.

“Kizu, are you fine?”

His attention snapped back down from the gargoyles above. Evie flinched at his sudden movement. Despite the tropical sun overhead, the Kemon girl wore a hooded cloak over her academy uniform, as usual, with her porcupine quills piercing through.

“Evie? Oh, sorry. Yeah, I’m good. Do you know where Arclight is?”

“I don’t know. Some of the other students are thinking about going back to their dorms,” she said. “Maybe she forgot about class today.”

“No.” Kizu shook his head. “Something’s up. What am I missing though? Any sort of event?”

“The Shinzou Academy Tournament?” Evie suggested quietly.

Kizu blinked. “It would make sense for that to involve Combat F’s class. But the tournament doesn’t start until the end of the semester, right?”

“I…don’t know what day it begins. Maybe it’s on our schedules.”

With no other leads available to him, Kizu pulled out his scrying orb. It had been a while since he last used the academy issued orb. He normally handed it off to Anata to assist her with studying, but even then it hadn’t been seeing much use since her school had begun assigning readings.

A few flicks of his hand and verbal demands and his schedule appeared on the orb’s glass surface. But instead of “Combat F” he found a banner that listed “Tournament’s Prelude. Find Arclight.”

Wordlessly, he passed his scrying orb to Evie.

“Don’t say anything,” he said, keeping his voice down. “Tournaments are competitions. And I believe we’re competing against everyone present.”

Evie nodded.

Now they needed to find a way to gather information. Thankfully, Kizu currently held a resource of vast knowledge in his hands.

With a few whispered commands, his scrying orb’s appearance changed. First, Kizu asked his scrying orb to go into detail about the previous year’s tournament. He figured having a base understanding of what the tournament looked like in the past would help set his expectations for the current year.

It broke it down into three very distinct sections. Information gathering. Team work. And finally individual skill. All three sections were taken into account when tallying up the final scores of the year.

Last year the information gathering had been headed by Professor Krimpit and involved hours upon hours of research in the library. But the year before that it had been led by Professor Ignis and the students were required to instead deceive one another and fool their fellows into believing specific lies. That implied that this year’s information gathering section would be something altogether different from those two years.

According to his quick scan of historical records, information gathering always kicked off the year’s competition. And since all of his Combat F peers were first years who appeared oblivious of what Arclight was currently doing, Kizu currently had a leg up in the first step of the contest.

But even if he understood why Arclight was missing, it didn’t actually get him much closer to where she was.

“Do you have any ideas?” he whispered to Evie.

“I—I don’t know,” Evie said. She fidgetted. “Maybe…she might be in the staff room. I’ve seen her there before with Professor Oasaji a lot.”

“You’ve been inside the staff room?” Kizu asked. He shouldn’t honestly be surprised. Evie was Roba’s favorite assistant and sent to fulfill tasks throughout the academy for the old woman.

“Y-yes. But we’re not supposed to go in without permission.”

“Do you have any other leads? Because otherwise we can either wait here and hope Arclight shows up eventually or wander the academy hallways aimlessly looking for more clues.”

“I don’t know. How are we supposed to know? Arclight probably left a hint for us somewhere. Right?”

While Evie sounded completely uncertain, she raised a good point. None of the other students in the class would know how to access the staff room. They likely wouldn’t know that Arclight even frequented the room.

Then Mort finally spotted something unusual. A small archway at the other side of the courtyard, half buried in foliage. Gulp the kokokyu’s entryway into his little home. The assistant to Professor Arclight would undoubtedly have insight into where the professor had disappeared to.

Kizu silently beckoned Mort to return to him then hurriedly scribbled a note on a scrap of spare parchment from his bag. He wanted to appear unassuming so he was leaving the task of contact to Mort, who was often overlooked by the other students. Most people didn’t understand a familiar’s connection or intelligence. And Kizu was more than fine to capitalize on that ignorance.

Handing the note over to the monkey, Kizu watched as Mort scampered off into the foliage then circled back around to Gulp’s home.

Mort, not caring for social norms, barged into the small building. Kizu closed his eyes and watched through his familiar’s eyes.

Inside the home was surprisingly cozy. Gulp had a nice carpeted floor and a warm crackling fireplace. The kokokyu sat at a table with a stack of fliers in front of him. The blue furred creature smiled with its teeth at Mort’s appearance. Then, without even bothering to look at the note Kizu had written it, Gulp thrust one of the fliers forward.

Mort blinked and looked down at the paper. On it was an animated image of Arclight on a background of white. It was silent, but a little speech bubble appeared above her head.

“Well done! You’ve gathered information via Gulp,” Arclight silently said. She walked over to the side and the speech bubble disappeared. The background of the flier morphed and the image of the sea appeared behind Arclight. A newly formed speech bubble replaced the previous one. “You’ve already displayed excellent intuition. Let’s see if you can carry through. Look forward to seeing you soon!” Then she stepped beyond the edge of the flier and out of frame.

The moment before the flier reset, Kizu caught a glimpse of something crisscrossing in the background where Arclight had stood. It only took a moment for him to process it. A fishing net draped over a barrel.

That was enough of a lead for him.

Kizu blinked his attention back to his own body while Mort struggled to escape Gulp’s suddenly overwhelming hospitality.

“I know where to go,” he whispered to Evie.

“The staff room?”

Kizu hesitated. He felt bad, she sounded so hopeful. He thought back to their school assignment in Tross last quarter. At the end of it she’d confessed her uselessness and desire to be less of a burden to those around her. Was there a way he could make her feel like she was contributing? He already knew where to go though, and it wasn’t the staffroom.

Thinking fast, he readily agreed to go to the staff room. They started to meander their way out of the courtyard along with a few of the other students who were bored or who’d decided class must have been canceled for the day.

Silently, he willed Mort to snatch up one of those fliers and bring it with him. The owl monkey got the gist of what he was hoping for and complied. As they exited the courtyard, he saw a flash of movement out of the corner of his eye as Mort dashed into the bushes. Kizu and Evie left the monkey behind to wait there.

The academy corridors were empty, most students busy in their classes. Hundreds of elaborate glass chandeliers hung overhead in the current hallway they walked down. They glittered with light, absolutely bedazzling ceiling overhead. Looking up, he could see tiny reflections of himself and Evie in the shards of glass. Even to this day, nearly a full year into his studies, Kizu kept coming across random quirks of the academy he’d never noticed before.

“How are your studies coming along?” Kizu asked Evie as they walked in an attempt to break up the silence. “Your rankings improving?”

“Yes,” she replied quietly. “I know I messed up the conjuring on my first day, but my ancestors have helped me improve.”

Interesting that conjuring was the first thing that came to mind when he asked her about improvements. Though he supposed it was one of the few spells he’d actually witnessed her cast. Well…fail to cast. But that had been due to a blunder early in her studies. She was still good enough at that branch of magic to not be lumped with him in the Conjuring F class.

“How do they help?” he asked. He wasn’t especially hopeful, but maybe a different sort of insight could be the break he needed to understand that field of magic. Evie had access to spirit magic which let her act as a vessel for her ancestors; they might have some method unknown to his teachers.

“They let me summon creatures and objects I’ve never seen before personally. And they can guide my hand to cast the spells properly without error. With enough practice, I can even perform the summons on my own.”

Kizu blinked. He didn’t think he’d ever been in a conversation with the Kemon girl before where she was this talkative. It was not only a normal ratio of words between them, the ratio actually skewed in her direction.

Hopefully he could help her keep that confidence up.

She guided him up some winding stairs and then they stepped through an archway and Kizu felt his insides twist. Literally.

Gravity spun around, pulling him in random directions.

“What the—” He cut off as he hung, suspended in the center of a circular room. Doors lined the spherical wall around them. Each was labeled in the Universal Script as well as had an image branded onto its surface. He noticed music notes to signify Professor Ignis and a plump bird for Professor Oasaji.

Everything turned slowly, as if orbiting around him.

“These are doors to the professor’s offices,” Evie said. “They have other entrances throughout the academy but here they’re all located in one place.”

“A spatial enchantment?” That got Kizu’s attention. He knew that there were several complex spatial ward schemes embedded into the foundation of Shinzou Academy, but this was one location that linked the far reaches of the academy all into one central heart. He flashed his spellsense, attempting to get a better glimpse of the enchantments. Alas, the brilliance of the enchantments flaring to his senses just echoed in his head, giving him a slight headache.

Distracted, Kizu didn’t even notice as Evie walked forward toward the door at the center of the spherical room.

He bit back a curse and willed Mort forward.

“You can just walk across this space?” he called out after Evie.

He resisted letting out a sigh of relief as she turned to look back at him.

At that moment, Mort jumped into the room. The pop of his appearance was thankfully covered by the ambient noise of the spatially enchanted room. The monkey placed the flier in front of the door Evie had been walking toward and then jumped away. Kizu felt his familiar use the academy beacon as a quick and easy get-a-way.

“Yes,” Evie said, oblivious to Mort’s quick little stop by. Her voice was barely audible over the thrum of the room as it moved. “You just need to focus your mind.”

Kizu did as instructed and felt the world right itself. He managed to steady himself with his hands and feet and slowly started waddling his way forward.

The entrance Evie headed towards was a double door, making it twice the size of all the others in the room that led to offices. It also didn’t have any sort of insignia on it beyond the basic Shinzou Academy symbol. Kizu understood through context that this was clearly the teacher’s lounge.

As she approached, Evie spotted the flier on the ground at the foot of the door. She crouched down and delicately lifted it up. Kizu winced slightly as he spotted little smudged monkey handprints from the courtyard soil on the crumpled piece of paper. Thankfully, Evie appeared to be distracted by Arclight’s message as the little speech bubbles appeared by her head.

“Information via Gulp?” she read, clearly confused.

His heart skipped a beat. “It’s probably a combat fighter slang thing,” he said quickly. “Anyway, mind if I take a look as well?”

He held himself back from tearing the flier from her hands. Once in his grasp, he made a show of studying it for half a minute.

“I think we should go down to the docks,” Kizu said. “You can see fishing nets in the background of this flier just before it resets. And the sea is clearly visible throughout the second half of the instructions.”

“Oh. I think you’re right.” Evie nodded.

As Kizu readjusted his balance, he was about to suggest he jump them both to the academy beacon to get them closer to the academy’s entrance. Then a door over his right shoulder opened. He noticed the symbol on it. A muscled bicep.

Kizu thought he vaguely recognized the student that exited. But it wasn’t until he spotted a remarkably familiar flier in his hand that Kizu placed him. A fellow Combat F classmate who’d slipped out early. And, as the door closed behind him, Kizu caught a glimpse of a room full of the fliers.

Kizu wanted to kick himself. Of course Arclight’s office would also have fliers.
