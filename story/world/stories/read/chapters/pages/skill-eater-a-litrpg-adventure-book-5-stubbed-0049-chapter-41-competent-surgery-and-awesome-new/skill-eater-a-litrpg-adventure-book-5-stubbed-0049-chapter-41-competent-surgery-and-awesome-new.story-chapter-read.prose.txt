The trio took a few minutes to calm down before stepping into the lobby to receive their items.

Edge’s pulse was pounding, but he played it off as excitement, taking consolation from the fact that the Ancient had either claimed the lens and was celebrating its victory, or its sights were centered on whomever had stolen its prize. One way or another, the monster had no reason to focus on hunters who couldn’t afford a Legendary piece of runic gear.

He hadn’t watched many feeds from World’s End prior to coming to Ord, but the inner workings of the auction house would have been censored regardless. He had no idea what was about to happen and was enjoying the thrill of the mystery despite his recent scare. Over the next half hour, attendants dressed in elaborate white robes approached everyone who had placed a winning bid and guided them up an elegant staircase leading to the second floor.

Trapper was one of the first people to leave. Sakura’s turn came a little later, leaving Edge standing in a steadily shrinking crowd as time wore on. No one was interested in small talk, and not long after, a young man wrapped in pristine white cloth stopped in front of him and bowed from the waist.

Anticipation dispelled his lingering unease as he followed the attendant up the stairs and down a long hallway with dozens of branching passageways. The area was blanketed in privacy devices, making it impossible to peer into the adjoining rooms or watch the other winners. Edge’s guide took him down one turn after another. Before long, he had no idea what part of the building they were in, or even if they were on the same level, as portions of the walk had taken them up and down short flights of stairs.

When the attendant stopped in front of a doorway and bowed again, Edge thanked the man for his service and stepped inside. He passed through a privacy bubble and found himself standing in a high-end clinic. Sterile tiles lined the floor, and the walls were plated with surgical steel. Another employee was waiting for him inside. She was a senior member of the staff, judging by the rich scarlet silk comprising her garb and the shallow bow she offered when he stopped in front of her.

“Congratulations on placing the winning bid on the Arcane Jammer. The implant kit can be packaged if you prefer, but I met you here in case you wanted to install it right away. The surgeon is on standby in an adjoining suite. Would you prefer to undergo the procedure now?”

“Thank you. That would be ideal.”

“Excellent. Please remove everything above the waist and take a seat in the operating chair. The surgeon will guide you through the rest of the process.”

She rapped her knuckles against the only other door in the room, then bid Edge farewell. He removed his suit jacket and shirt and hung them on a hook attached to the far wall. By the time his skin met the chill embrace of the metal chair, the door opened, and an elderly gentleman wearing a black lab coat walked through.

“I recognize you from the Duos’ Clash,” the doctor spoke while arranging his tools on a tray. “Since you’re stage three, I assume you’ve been through implant surgeries several times before. I don’t see a single scar anywhere on your body, so I’m guessing you have some sort of healing magic that will render aftercare unnecessary.”

Edge was impressed that the man had deduced so much in such a short span of time. He didn’t see any point in revealing the details of his skillset, so he nodded and said, “No general anesthesia, please, although I’ll take something topical if you have it. I’ll leave my skills off until the procedure is complete, and you give the all-clear.”

“Excellent.” The surgeon nodded in satisfaction while sterilizing his scalpel. “That will save time and simplify the procedure. I have a nice solution that will block the pain of the incision, although the rest will be considerably less pleasant. I know you’re tough, but I’m going to use some straps to limit involuntary movement. Don’t worry, you can snap them with ease if you choose, so you’re not being restrained. Do you have any questions before I begin?”

“Just one. Will this implant be fused to my core?”

“It will indeed. Bonding them helps the device to sense spells that use your core as their focal point.”

That was the extent of their conversation. The doctor applied a salve that smelled a little like aloe, causing numbness to spread across Edge’s chest as the man secured his limbs with straps attached to the chair. Two minutes later, everything was ready, and the doctor made his first and only incision—a four-inch gash below Edge’s sternum.

He couldn’t feel the blade, but there was quite a bit of discomfort when the man positioned the retractor holding the wound open. Next, he inserted the same straw-like device Lilly had used to reach his core, isolating the welding point and suctioning it clean before sterilizing the area. Edge could sense several passive powers in play, which enhanced the surgeon’s Perception and Control.

“Here comes the tricky part. Try not to move.” Without further warning, the doctor positioned a dropper inside the straw. He coated Edge’s core with a binding agent, then pressed the implant kit against the surface using a pair of tiny tweezers.

Next came the welding that made the device a permanent part of the core. As promised, there was a lot of heat and pain, but it wasn’t nearly as bad as when he’d used the engraving kit to install his Spatial Storage Rune. Changes were taking place within his inner world, but they were subtle, and he couldn’t discern the details without stepping inside his central chamber.

Ten minutes later, the surgery was complete. “It’s time to fill the implant’s aether well. For technical reasons I won’t bore you by explaining, adding platinum aether requires a direct connection and the Jammer can only be charged when bonded to a core. It should take quite a while to expend the well, but when you do, it will require a minor surgery to refill it. Be extremely discerning regarding your provider. If any platinum-grade aether escapes confinement and enters your system, all the skills in the world won’t save you.”

That warning got Edge’s attention. He was nervous, since he believed the doctor’s words, but not unduly so. Every move the man made was performed with impeccable precision, revealing an investment in Control that was well into the triple digits. Edge watched in fascination as the man unscrewed a metal cylinder shielded by multiple magitech seals and removed a tiny syringe that was glowing like the sun.

“Not many people have seen platinum aether with their own eyes. They generally only use it for starship fuel or military-grade magitech.”

As the surgeon spoke, the antiseptic reek of purified magicytes filled the air. Edge expected the injection to be intense and was surprised when the doctor emptied the syringe into his core without any sensations at all.

“That was a flawless transfer,” he explained. “100% efficiency with no residual venting. The smell is air from the processing plant that was trapped inside the cylinder.”

With that, the doctor placed his tools in an autoclave, wiped away the blood, and unfastened the restraints. “We’re done here. I’m impressed you bore that so stoically. Get dressed at your leisure, and an attendant will show you out of the building.”

Before he left the room, Edge asked, “Do you happen to know how much Mortium it will cost to take the device to tier two, or what materials are required to upgrade it to tier three?”

“I’ve never worked with one of these before, so I have no idea about the materials. Only a grandmaster could assemble a Legendary-grade upgrade kit, although installing should be simple enough. To answer your first question, implants that are Rare at tier one take one hundred lifecoins to reach tier two, although you can confirm that number yourself if you stop by the exchange.”

“Thanks again. For the information and the masterful instillation.”

The doctor raised a hand and waved by way of reply, then disappeared through the door he’d entered through. Edge got dressed, helped himself to a glass of water sitting on the counter, then willed his consciousness into his inner world. Before he did anything else, he pulled up his Guide to make sure the device was listed on his profile.

With that out of the way, he took a lap around his father’s workshop, coming to a stop when he spotted a new addition. Filling one corner of the enclosure was a computer station that reminded him of the hardware he’d used before coming to Ord.

There was a panel of lights running across the top of the monitor, all of which were green. He spotted an operating manual posted to the wall, which he thought was a nice touch. When he browsed the contents, he learned the lights meant the Jammer hadn’t detected any skills.

On the screen was a figure representing his body, with a three-dimensional grid that extended for several miles. It took him a minute to figure out what he was looking at, but Edge eventually determined the setup was designed to reveal the origin of any tracking or observational magic targeting his person.

Nice. Not only does it show me the caster’s direction; the Jammer can reveal their location if they’re relatively close, although I’m sure it’s possible to neutralize that aspect with the right countermeasure. Regardless, this setup was even better than he’d hoped, which made him wonder how the implant would improve as it climbed the tiers.

There was a bar in the corner of the screen that read 100%, which provided a more accurate measure of the implant’s aether than its listing in his profile. The final component was a red button with a plastic shield. Running along the base of the protective shell was a label with the word, Counterspell.

“Awesome.” When Edge turned around, a crowd had gathered behind his back.

He explained everything to Rue and his avatars, who had sensed the surgery and were curious about what was going on. As always, he wasn’t sure how much his skills understood, although most of them seemed to grasp the general concept. “I’ll be back to play a little later,” he promised before popping back into his body.

The red-robed attendant arrived shortly after, and he followed her out of the building. Edge made his way to the Lost Traveler, immensely relieved to have acquired a vital form of protection. Now it was time to confer with his team and determine their next steps for dealing with the Ancient lurking in World’s End.
