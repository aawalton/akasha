“Here.”

Gin finally nudged himself between me and Fallias, all elbows and impatience, like a man who’d watched enough emotional revelations for one lifetime. He thrust a book into my hands with zero ceremony. It landed heavier than it should have, not in weight exactly, but in presence.

“Read this,” he said. “Really. Read it. It’ll teach you exactly how to gain power now that you’re outside of the Dream.”

I stared down at it, then back up at him. “Is… that what it’s called?”

Gin rolled his eyes, already halfway to a lecture. “Demeterra’s Dream. Yes. Some call it the Islands of Mirage. Others the Earth Beyond and Below. A few particularly pretentious sects call it the Kindly Lie.” He snorted. “But the technique is the same no matter who uses it.”

He gestured vaguely, like he was outlining a diagram only he could see.

“Wrap someone in your illusion. Give them a small token of power inside it. Let them struggle, grow, suffer, triumph. And then you siphon everything they obtained. Skills. Insight. Mana expression. Emotional conditioning.” He smiled thinly. “It’s efficient. Horrifyingly so.”

I looked back down at the book.

Holding it felt… wrong. And old. And not old in the dusty-library sense, but old like something that had watched civilizations blink in and out of relevance. The binding was a careful fusion of ivory and bone, smoothed and polished until I couldn’t tell where one ended and the other began. The pages weren’t paper at all, but thin sheets of some golden substance that caught the ambient light and bent it, not reflecting so much as remembering it.

The book felt like it knew I was holding it.

“What is this?” I asked quietly.

“Power,” Gin said flatly.

I blinked. “That’s not an answer.”

“It’s the only honest one,” he shot back. “Miasma and Mana are the same force. One thing. One substrate. The old name for it is Myth. Capital M.”

He leaned against a floating shelf, arms crossed. “Demeterra splits it inside her Dream. Calls one half Mana. The other Miasma. Gives you one, tells you the other is dangerous, corruptive, forbidden. That way no one ever recombines them and figures out how real power actually works.”

I swallowed, my fingers tightening slightly on the cover.

“So everything I was using,” I said slowly, “was… diluted.”

“Filtered,” Gin corrected. “Sandboxed. You were playing with blunt tools made of foam and belief. Convincing ones. Effective ones. But never sharp enough to cut the hand that held them.”

The room felt quieter after that. Even the whispering pages seemed to hush.

“I just…” I exhaled, scrubbing a hand over my face. “I just feel so confused now. For the entire time I was there, I was ping-ponged around. From crisis to crisis. From test to test. And now…” I looked up at him. “Now is this the reason why?”

Gin didn’t answer immediately. He sighed instead, long and tired, like someone who’d explained this too many times to too many broken prodigies.

“Yes,” he said at last. Then, annoyingly, “No.”

I groaned. “Gin.”

“So above,” he continued calmly, “so below. It isn’t that you were ping-ponged around because you were in her illusory world.” He tapped the side of his head. “You were ping-ponged around because you were inside her inner realm and her illusory world at the same time.”

I frowned. “That sounds… bad.”

“Oh, it was spectacularly bad,” he said cheerfully. “Her inner realm is where the rules are written. Her illusion is where they’re performed. You were caught between authorship and theater.”

He gestured in layered circles. “Each time you thought you’d broken through a layer, there were three more beneath it. Conditioning loops. Narrative reinforcement. Emotional anchoring. Feedback spirals. We had to peel you out like a stuck page from a book that didn’t want to be closed.”

Fallias shifted beside me, her wings rustling softly. She didn’t speak, but I felt her agreement like a hand on my shoulder.

“And now?” I asked. “What happens now?”

Gin nodded toward the book in my hands. “Now you learn how power actually accumulates. Not granted. Not gifted. Not borrowed. But grown.”

He smiled, and this time there was something sharp behind it. Something pleased.

“That book doesn’t teach you spells,” he said. “It teaches you how to metabolize Myth without tearing yourself apart. How to reconcile contradiction instead of letting it fracture you.”

I glanced down at the golden pages again.

“And if I mess it up?” I asked.

Gin shrugged. “Then you explode. Or dissolve. Or rewrite yourself into something unrecognizable.”

He grinned. “But given what you are? I’d say the odds are in your favor.”

The book pulsed once, faintly, in my hands.
