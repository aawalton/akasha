Chapter V.LII (5.52) - Finn’s First Battle

Finn faced down a fifth-year boy with slicked back hair and relaxed posture. While Arclight announced them, the boy’s lip curled into a sneer, eyes focused on Finn.

Deciding he wanted a better look at this fight in particular, Kizu pulled out a vial of his vision enhancement potion and downed it in a single gulp. A few blinks and he could zoom in to better examine the combatants. After explaining what the potion was, he offered a vial to his father, who declined it with a wave of his hand.

Finn’s opponent was tall and lanky. As he examined the boy, Kizu questioned whether or not he was even fully human. His skin coloring looked a tinge purple and he had six fingers on each of his hands.

Kizu wondered if the two of them had any sort of past dispute. Arclight had mentioned that they’d be paired up against other students to best serve their progression, both physical progress and to help settle social grudges. Supposedly, it was viewed as a means of helping students move on, though Kizu doubted the effectiveness of that strategy. Still, everyone knew that Finn did himself no favors in his social life. Kizu imagined his brother likely had no end of people who disliked him at the academy. Plenty of material for the academy to pull from to start up drama.

With the end of Arclight’s announcement, the terrain shifted under their feet. Unlike the last two battlefields, the wooden planks under their feet remained. In front of Finn, a long wooden table sprouted from below, emerging covered in sizzling plated foods. Chairs lined the table and it looked as if the dining room had been abandoned by a full party mid-meal seconds before the fight. Finn’s opponent stood at the very head of the table, at least twenty meters away.

The match started with a ding, like a kitchen bell.

Finn’s opponent launched himself forward, kicking aside the food and cutlery as he dashed across the table. From a flash of Kizu’s spellsense, he didn’t see anything that signaled magical enhancements on the older student. It was just a mundane charge, straight in Finn’s direction. Whatever plan the student had, it didn’t involve enhancements yet.

Finn took several steps backward and Kizu felt his father tense up in the seat next to him. However, Kizu knew his brother better than to believe he’d run from this fight. He watched as the younger student studied his surroundings.

Finn raised his hands and created a blade of ice the length of his forearm. It hovered beside him for a second and Kizu noted his opponent eyeing the blade, though he didn’t slow his charge.

Right as the opponent was within leaping distance of Finn, Kizu’s brother sent the blade out. It sliced through the air in a sideways arc and the other boy raised an arm to protect himself from the sharp edge.

Rather than strike the student directly at the neck or other vital bit of flesh for a finishing blow, Finn aimed it down. Under his feet. The ice blade chopped into the wood off the tableleg, slicing it into two pieces.

As Finn’s opponent attempted to leap forward, arm extended and electricity crackling between his fingertips, the table tilted under his weight. It collapsed to the side, sending the older boy tumbling, completely off-target.

Finn dashed forward, his fist encased in ice, and he punched down at his prone opponent. The boy rolled to the side and took the brunt of the blow to his shoulder rather than the neck Finn had clearly been aiming for.

The ice around his fist shattered as it met the shoulder. Shards of it embedded themselves in Finn’s victim’s flesh.

If Finn had landed that on his neck, it would have been a swift, easy victory. The shoulder’s damage was nasty, but far from a vital wound. Blood oozed out and soaked the embedded ice scarlet.

The student snarled and discharged a wave of static electricity. It crackled and knocked Finn backwards. The metallic plates and cutlery likewise jolted across the room in Finn’s direction, attracted to the discharge.

Collapsed table to one side, Finn snatched something off the ground and chucked it at his opponent. Half a roast ham, sliced into portions, slammed into the electrical student as he fled in an attempt to put distance between them.

Finn’s opponent wiped away the slice of meat sliding down the side of his face. The moment of distraction let Finn close the distance and slam a fist into the other boy’s stomach. Red blood stained the boy’s tournament uniform on contact.

Only as he stumbled away was it revealed that more than a fist had connected to his belly.

Kizu blinked in surprise and leaned in. His brother had managed to form a spike of ice around his hand. Kizu suspected that it had been a simultaneous combination of enhancing his body and elemental magic that delivered the blow with such power. The now bloodstained ice was expertly crafted and formed with Finn’s flesh used as the base. Without enhanced durability, Kizu doubted his brother could have rammed the attack into his target with enough velocity to puncture his core. Not to mention the fact that the ice remained in one piece after the attack showed a level of skill with the element Kizu had only ever seen from Roku back at his dojo in Hon.

Finn’s opponent sent out another crackling shot of electricity. It appeared the gut wound wasn’t enough to count as fatal. While Finn disengaged to avoid the bolt, his opponent snatched up and imbued a fallen metal chalice with electricity. With one hand clutching his stomach to keep his guts in, he hurled the electrical chalice at Finn.

Dodging back behind the table, Finn kept his distance and blocked the incoming electrical attacks with wooden chairs and shields formed of ice. His arm remained coated in the spike of ice, but he acted defensively. Based on that fact, Kizu suspected that dismissing the spell only to need it again later would cost his brother too much blood. It had been quite the impressive combo earlier. Sustaining a spell like that doubtlessly required less resources than recreating it from scratch.

The smart thing to do from this point would be to wait out the battle. It didn’t seem like the other boy was proficient enough in medicine to close the wound on his own, so all Finn needed to do to claim victory was stand back and watch him bleed out.

Kizu could see the other boy open his mouth, lips curled back. Even without hearing the words, he’d seen enough fights to recognize a taunt.

Finn’s body language shifted immediately. He went from cautious and defensive, to aggressive. He dashed forward and lunged across the broken table, swiping his icicle at the boy’s chest. His opponent smiled as he stepped out of reach and reached his palms forward in Finn’s direction. Electricity danced between his spread fingertips. Finn was up close and lined up perfectly for a jolt to the head.

Beside him, Kizu’s father groaned, obviously disappointed by this turn of events. Kizu couldn’t help but also feel crestfallen that Finn fell for such a stupid strategy when he’d been so close to victory.

Then the ice spike shot off Finn’s fist. It slammed into the base of his opponent’s neck, blood and chunks of flesh splattered up in an arc as the boy’s body collapsed.

Before the boy’s body even fell back into the broken table, the entire arena shifted back to the basic wooden platform. The wounds inflicted on Finn’s opponent disappeared alongside the feast.

Grasping at his now healed throat, the boy on the ground stared up at Finn with wide eyes. Gone was the smirk and taunts from earlier, replaced by a gaping mouth.

The crowd roared their excitement. Far more than the previous lackluster matches. Finn’s match seemed to truly kick off the brutal battles to come.

As if to make up for his opponent’s sudden lack of arrogance, Finn smiled down at him with white teeth that glinted in the sunlight. He offered him no hand to assist him to his feet.

The tournament had begun in earnest.
