“Guargh!”

A puny goblin monster ran forward, holding a measly wooden club in its hands.

“That’s right. Come here, you little green idiot.”

Veronica stood completely still. Her hands rested at her sides, palms flat. There were no wings behind her hand, nor any magic circles prepared.

She simply let the goblin charge at her.

The air shuddered in an instant.

Veronica’s eyes flashed a glowing purple, a faint electric spark running wild through her pupils. Her hair lifted at once as the pressure around her body condensed, emitting a crushing weight. Her muscles tensed, and her cores immediately strained to contain the flow of mana within her body.

A moment later, an enormous explosion sounded. Light detonated in the air, flooding the forest with a brief, blinding flash.

Once the rumbling faded and the smoke withered away, the goblin’s body was nowhere to be seen.

Veronica crossed her arms, tilting her head ever so slightly.

“Well, the power output is nice.”

[You have completed a quest: Form third set of Mana Rings][Mana Regeneration has increased from 300 to 540][Reward: Mana Ring Rotational Burst]-

[Your rings can now rotate to their maximum limit much quicker. This allows your mana regeneration to cycle faster, while also allowing you to enter your Exalted Form quickly. This also generates excess force, which must be expelled immediately as a barrier, kinetic burst, or to empower a spell.]

Entering her exalted form quickly was now possible. It usually took time for her mana rings to accelerate to their limit, which could prove fatal if she needed to suddenly go all out against a powerful foe.

Behind her lay more charred corpses, and many, many more craters.

“Should be enough practice for now,” she said lightly.

It was late noon now. An entire day had passed, with Veronica spending the rest of yesterday forming her third set of mana rings. The process took a while; she even overslept.

Veronica stood alone in the forest, far from Ronswick, testing her new ability.

While she could form barriers nearly instantly since it was almost second nature to her, forming a spell to repel someone at her barrier’s edge still required a short moment of focus to cast. Against a swordsman, for instance—they could quickly dodge her spells if they reacted fast enough. Now, at least, she had a method to attack nearly instantaneously without the need of being a higher tier.

Veronica took a deep, slow breath. A moment later, her body returned to normal as her mana rings went back to normal speed.

Now at Tier-3, with all of her rings formed, advancing to further tiers would become more involved.

The kassal oil she had received from the viscount had not been enough for her to absorb and reach Tier-4. Instead, she would have to find another source of concentrated power to assist her.

Advancing tiers did not just involve absorbing mana or power. It required a deeper understanding of one’s path and of one’s own core.

The process was simple to understand, but complex to accomplish. A mage needed to reconstruct the components and internal structure of their core with higher-tier variables until the original core was replaced with one above its current tier, leading to advancement. This process was gradual and required time, understanding, and a little luck.

The source of power mattered less for those at earlier stages, but it became a bottleneck later in ascension as the required power scaled rapidly. Reaching the 9th or 10th tiers demanded mythical materials closely tied to one’s path.

For Veronica, since she followed the Path of Ruin, she simply needed something packed with power or mana. A blooming mage, on the other hand, might need a rare herb filled with vitality or even a dragon’s heart, which possessed incredible regenerative properties.

She was roughly halfway to Tier-4 now. She just needed a little more time for her cores to stabilize, along with a little more power to fuel her core.

Veronica turned and began walking back the way she came, passing by all the craters she had made deep in the forest. There were still a few things to do today before her meeting with Gwen at the crimson court. She’d knock them all out first.

After a bath from her morning expedition, Veronica arrived at the Mage Tower. This time, however, she wasn’t there to meet Mr. Hugh or Tower Master Rarran. Instead, she took a right, walking past the wooden automatons.

She stopped at a small workshop. The door was open, and she peered inside. Everything was a mess. Multiple tables and stands, workbenches and stools, piles of mechanical devices and knickknacks lay scattered throughout the room.

Beyond the clutter, the person Veronica wanted to see stood inside, humming a tune while tinkering with something too small for Veronica to make out.

Veronica knocked lightly on the door to get her attention.

“Hmm… hmm… huh?”

Chelsea turned around and spotted Veronica standing in the doorway.

She quickly recognized her.

“Oh?” she said, standing up. “You’re that girl who was looking for Hugh the other day.”

Chelsea wore a white apron lightly dusted with black soot. She set her tools aside and patted the apron. “Did you need something?”

“Sorry to disturb you, but I was wondering if you could do me a favor. I’ll pay for it too if it’s something you can do,” Veronica said.

Chelsea raised a brow. “Oh? What’s up?”

Veronica stepped inside. She found an open space on a nearby workstation and placed a small metal ring on top. “I had this ring appraised by a merchant. He said it might be some type of key to a communication crystal. He also said someone at the Mage Tower might be able to reverse track where it connects to. So I thought I’d come here.”

Chelsea walked over and grabbed a set of comically large goggles from a nearby stand. She put them on and picked up the ring, turning it between her fingers. “Oh. These things. Old stuff. Almost no one uses them anymore.”

She rotated the ring again while adjusting the lenses on her goggles. “With newer communication crystals, you don’t really need rings like this. They only allow registered users to access them. Of course, unless it’s a black market crystal and someone is trying to keep things completely secret.”

Chelsea lifted her goggles slightly to glance at Veronica. “Uh. Where did you get this?”

“I found it.”

“You…”

“Found it.”

“… alright.”

She lowered her goggles again and walked to a workbench on the far side of the workshop.

Veronica followed, careful not to step on anything scattered across the floor. “So? Is it something you think you can do?”

Chelsea set the ring down and moved a lamp closer, then pulled over a stool. She leaned in, inspecting the ring beneath the light with a thin metal needle.

“Probably. I just need to track down the mana signature it’s addressed to. That’ll turn this thing into a tracker instead of a key.”

“Does that mean it won’t work as a key anymore?”

“Not necessarily,” she replied. Her attention remained fixed on the ring, her back turned to Veronica. “I can see the inscriptions here. I’d just need to carve the same thing onto another ring, or even a piece of metal. Basically, I’d be making a duplicate. That way their resonances don’t interfere with each other.”

Veronica nodded, half-understanding the process. Still, Chelsea seemed confident she could do it.

“Is there anything you’d like for doing this, then? How much would this cost?”

Chelsea raised her goggles and spun around on the stool. She placed a finger beneath her chin. “Hmm… how about two hundred vix? Shouldn’t take me longer than a day.”

“Alright. That sounds fair to me.”

Veronica handed over two hundred vix, which Chelsea promptly took.

“Great. Come back tomorrow morning. I’ll probably have it done by then.”

Veronica nodded. “Alright. Thank you.”

With that, she left the Mage Tower.

The sky had grown dark as night slowly settled. It was 8:45 P.M. now. After asking for directions from people on the street, Veronica found herself standing before the Crimson Court.

It was a large white establishment, one of the biggest buildings she had seen in the city. Gwen had described the place as somewhere people went to perform—singing, dancing, hosting plays. Now that she was here, however, that description felt like an understatement.

She stood outside, watching people arrive and enter. Many were dressed quite nicely. There were even nobles among them, along with those who clearly carried wealth.

Veronica couldn’t help but notice that her own attire was… lacking.

The women wore dresses, while the men wore fine suits or decorative vests. It wasn’t quite the sort of occasion one would dress for like a ball or a wedding, but these people were certainly dressed for the event.

Perhaps she should have gone shopping for a dress herself.

A few minutes later, she finally spotted Gwen approaching.

“Hey! I’m sorry if I made you wait long,” she said.

“No, I just got here,” Veronica replied, shaking her head.

That earlier feeling of inadequacy only grew stronger as she looked at Gwen.

The girl wasn’t wearing her waitress uniform tonight. Instead, she wore a proper red dress. Her hair had been done up neatly, and she even wore a necklace. Nothing that looked expensive, of course, but the effort was clear.

“Ready to head inside?” Gwen asked.

Veronica nodded. “Lead the way. I’ve never been here before.”

They both stepped forward and joined the line.

Gwen chuckled lightly, her cheeks faintly flushed. “Actually… I’ve never been here before either.”

“You haven’t?”

She shook her head. “No. This place is very famous in town. Known for its performers. That’s why you see so many people dressed so nicely. It’s kind of a big attraction. Coming here is something I would never normally be able to afford. It’s kind of amazing we met Nasha when we did.”

Right. Nasha. She was the one who invited them.

“You said she was a performer here, right?” Veronica asked. “Kind of hard to imagine that rogue acting or performing in plays.”

Gwen tilted her head slightly. “Uh, no. While she is a performer, she mainly sings and dances. She doesn’t do plays.”

Veronica raised a brow. “Singing and dancing?”

“One of the most popular performers in town, actually. I’ve heard her shows are really exciting, though I’ve never seen one myself.”

They reached the front of the line. A man in a suit stood there, holding a clipboard. His eyes swept briefly over Gwen without much thought before settling on Veronica. His gaze lingered on her clothes. He didn’t show it openly, but Veronica could almost feel a scowl forming behind his polite expression.

“Names and or reservation?” he asked.

“Um, Nasha told us to come here today at 9 P.M. Our names are Gwen and Veronica,” Gwen said.

The man’s lips thinned as he began sorting through his clipboard, quickly scanning the list of names.

For a moment, Veronica felt a flicker of concern. Were their names enough? What if Nasha had forgotten to include them? Perhaps she should have given them entry cards or—

“Gwen and Veronica. VIP seats,” the man said.

He reached to a nearby tray and pulled out two small black cards with the letters “CC” printed on them.

“You may choose any unoccupied seats in the front row. We have tables for three or four there. Drinks up to three hundred vix will be on the house for this visit.”

Gwen smiled brightly. “Awesome!” She turned to Veronica. “Come on! Let’s go!”

Gwen grabbed Veronica’s arm and led her inside.

Guess I worried for nothing.
