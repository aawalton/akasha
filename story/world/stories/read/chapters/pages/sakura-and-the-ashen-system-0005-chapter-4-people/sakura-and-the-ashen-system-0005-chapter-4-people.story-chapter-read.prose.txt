Sakura got to her feet. She had to leave. If Crowlis’s records were even half true, staying here any longer was far too dangerous. The basement no longer felt like a refuge.

Sakura looked around more carefully, and only then noticed a crumpled piece of black cloth by the wall under one of the skeletons. Not just a rag, but an old long cloak, worn in places but still intact. At the chest she found a deep inner pocket, hidden so neatly it was as if it had been sewn specifically to keep things out of sight. That was too useful to pass up.

She quickly shook the dust off the cloak, sending a grayish cloud into the air, and pulled it on over her clothes. A simple white shirt that had stopped being white a long time ago after the basement, the ash, and the blood, black pants made of some animal’s rough hide, sturdy but uncomfortable, and shoes with soles that were far too thin. Sakura had no idea where these clothes had even come from, when the last thing she remembered was the subway, the crush of bodies, and death, but right now that was not what mattered.

She hid the book in the cloak’s inner pocket, adjusted the fabric so nothing bulged, then ran her fingers through her hair. Black strands had long since fallen loose, tangled, and stuck to her temples and neck. Sakura quickly gathered them and braided them into a rough tight braid, more for order than beauty. After that, she looked a little more put together.

“The less noticeable, the better,” she murmured quietly, casting one last look at the altar. “I just hope I’ll look inconspicuous enough.”

Getting back up was not as hard as she had feared. Sakura was obviously not going to climb through the same hatch. Instead, she went through the passage the obsidian monster had torn open.

The air there was stale and dusty, and she had to move almost by feel, but soon the gray of the daytime sky appeared ahead, and a few minutes later Sakura had already made it outside, into the cold wind and the pale light. She had only just managed to take one full breath when she froze at once, because she heard voices very close by.

Men were speaking. Several of them, maybe five. Sakura dropped down instantly and slipped behind a broken section of wall, pressing her back against it. Her heart started hammering again. She wanted to peek out right away, but forced herself to listen first.

“I’m telling you, the glow came from here. Right from here, from deeper in the courtyard.”

“And what do you think it was? I don’t think Crowlis rose from the dead and went back to his experiments. He did enough of that already.”

“I wouldn’t joke about that. Hey, look at the flowers. They look like they drank blood and came back alive.”

Sakura cautiously shifted closer to a crack in the wall and looked through it. There really were people in the courtyard. Five men, all armed, wearing worn travel clothes, but not poor ones. Two had spears, one had a short sword, another had an axe at his belt, and the fifth held a long iron gaff, which he was using to turn over the obsidian carcass lying on the stones. Not the one she had killed in the basement, but the first one, the one the larger beast had torn apart above.

“Level five,” said the man with the gaff, straightening up. “Look at the chest plate and the joints. The growths are already almost like a mature beast’s.”

“Five?” another one repeated. “On the road by the trade route? Like we needed that.”

“As if we didn’t already have enough trouble, huh? There are monsters everywhere as it is, and now the ruins too... glowing.”

Off to the side stood another man who was clearly not part of their group. He was heavier, wearing a long travel coat, with a strap across his shoulder and a frightened expression. At his feet stood a cart, and behind it, apparently, waited a mule or a horse Sakura could not see behind the rubble.

“I only wanted to cut across the road,” he was explaining nervously. “How was I supposed to know something like this would flare up here? Sirs, I was just passing by, hauling goods.”

“Then you should’ve kept going,” one of the armed men snapped. “And not stuck your nose where no one asked you to.”

“I just need to know whether the road’s safe now or not.”

“Get lost while you still can,” muttered the man with the sword. “And if you’ve got a tongue to spare, you can go blab in a tavern that you saw a red glow over Crowlis’s castle. Then you’ll be the first one dragged off for questioning.”

The merchant went even paler, nodded quickly, and backed away.

“Right, right, I got it. I’m leaving.”

“Then leave.”

He really did hurry off almost at a run, and the men turned back toward the courtyard and the dead monster.

“Something bad happened,” one of them said more quietly. “Nothing like this has happened here since I was born.”

“Didn’t happen in my father’s time either,” another answered. “Fifty years of silence, and now this. It has to be reported. Immediately.”

“To the capital?”

“Where else? If suspicious activity has been spotted at Crowlis’s castle for the first time in fifty years, it’s not our concern anymore. Let the capital deal with it.”

Sakura pressed herself a little harder against the wall. Crowlis’s castle. So this place was well known. People remembered it. She looked at the men again. Even from here it was obvious they were tense, wary, and nothing like the kind of people who would be happy to see a lone girl suddenly crawl out of cursed ruins after a red glow. She needed help. Very badly. But showing herself to them now would be almost the same as voluntarily putting her head in a noose.

One of the men suddenly crouched and touched a crimson flower growing right out of a crack between the slabs.

“Disgusting just to look at,” he said with revulsion.

“Don’t touch it with your hands.”

“Yeah, I know.”

“We need to check the inner courtyard and the north breach.”

Sakura stopped listening. As long as they were busy with the carcass and the flowers, she still had a chance to slip away unnoticed. Slowly, step by step, she backed away from the wall, then circled the rubble from the other side and, barely breathing, slipped between two fallen arches where thorny gray brush was growing. Only when the ruins were a little farther behind her did she allow herself to straighten up and quicken her pace. A few minutes later the castle disappeared completely behind the trees, and Sakura entered the forest.

It was darker here than it should have been during the day. The treetops closed high overhead, the light filtered through them dimly and unevenly, and the air smelled of damp bark, wet earth, and something bitter. A narrow path stretched between the roots, barely visible, but still a path, which meant people walked here. Sakura followed it, holding the cloak closed at her chest and glancing back now and then, as if expecting someone from the ruins to come after her after all.

Along the way she found herself staring at plants she had never seen before. Tall stems with transparent leaves that seemed to trap gray light inside them. Bushes with black berries like drops of tar. Flowers shaped like bells, but too sharp, with the insides of their petals shining with a metallic blue tint. Everything here looked alien, a little beautiful, and not trustworthy at all.

Sakura looked at it all and kept thinking about something else at the same time. About how those men had known right away the monster had been level five. They looked at the carcass and knew. And her? She had killed the creature in the basement and had not even known what level it was. She did not know how that was determined at all. She did not know whether it could be seen in advance, felt, read from the body, the eyes, the light in the cracks. She knew nothing. Levels, classes, abilities, ruins, the capital, Crowlis’s castle, flowers that came alive... Veyr seemed to speak to everyone around her in a language they understood, while to her it only threw a few separate words now and then, almost out of pity.

“I don’t understand anything at all,” Sakura said quietly, looking down at her feet. “Nothing at all.”

The wind slid between the trees, brushed her cheek, and started whispering again. This time it was not as clear as it had been in the basement. The words were hard to make out, only fragments, quiet and quick, as if someone were hurrying to warn her about something and could not quite manage to say it in time.

“...careful... need to be...”

Sakura sighed without raising her head.

“I know. I already got that. Hard not to.”

The whisper died away, and only then did Sakura realize what was wrong with it. The voices in the wind did not sound like adults. They were thin, high, almost childlike, as if children were whispering somewhere between the trees and trying to warn her in words she could barely catch. The thought sent chills down her spine. Sakura wrapped the cloak more tightly around herself and kept walking.

The path gradually sloped downward. She heard water in the forest long before she saw it. First a distant steady sound, then splashing, then distinct voices. She stopped and listened. People again. This time not wary shouts or military talk, but an ordinary argument.

“How many times have I told you? Watch the net instead of gawking around!”

“I wasn’t gawking, Father!”

“You weren’t gawking, huh. Then who let it float off downstream, by Saint Oulen?”

Sakura cautiously moved closer and saw the river. Not wide, but fast, with dark water and a rocky bank. Two people stood right at the edge. The older man was sturdy and slightly stooped, with a weathered face and a short beard, his pants rolled up and his legs knee-deep in the water as he angrily yanked on a wet rope. A little farther up on the bank stood a lanky blond boy of sixteen or seventeen, clearly his son. Judging by his face, he already wanted the ground to swallow him whole.

“I thought it would catch,” the boy protested.

“He thought it would catch. And the net was just supposed to fish by itself?”

“Well, it was already torn earlier!”

“And if it was torn, who was supposed to tell me?”

Sakura watched them too closely and for too long, at first almost greedily. These people were not from the cursed ruins, not carrying gaffs and spears, not from the capital. Just fishermen by a river. The sight of them pricked something inside her with such strange warmth that it almost hurt. She was already on the verge of wondering whether she should step out first, ask for food, water, at least some human hint of where she was even supposed to go, when the older man suddenly lifted his head.

He saw her. Sakura straightened at once, like a startled animal, and did not even notice how tense her shoulders had become. There was no point hiding anymore, so she carefully stepped out from behind the tree. The fisherman narrowed his eyes.

“Hey. Who are you?”

Sakura stayed silent for too long. She knew it herself. A dozen answers flashed through her mind at once, and all of them seemed bad. She could not tell the truth. She needed to lie quickly, and quick was exactly what she could not manage.

The boy on the bank frowned and glanced at his father.

“Where did you even come from?”

Sakura still did not answer.

Then the man climbed out of the water, straightened up, and asked more roughly:

“What do you want, then?”

The boy snorted, still eyeing her warily.

“Some kind of loon...”
