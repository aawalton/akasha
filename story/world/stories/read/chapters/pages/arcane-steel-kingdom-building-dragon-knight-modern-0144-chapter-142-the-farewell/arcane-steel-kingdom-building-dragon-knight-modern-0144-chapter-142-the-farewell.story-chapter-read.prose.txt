I was currently being squished to death.

My mother dabbed at her eyes with a silk handkerchief. Just when I thought the storm had passed, her breath hitched, and a fresh wave of tears spilled over. She complained about the draft in the corridor, about the weight of my armor, about the dangers of the continent. She found a dozen reasons to keep her arms wrapped around my neck.

She finally let go after a time which felt like hours. She dabbed at her eyes again.

"Alarion..." Sniffle. "My cream pie." She lunged at me, grabbing the edges of my breastplate. "Promise you will be careful. Promise you will come back unharmed." She cupped my face in her hands with an iron grip. "Promise me."

"I promise," I said softly, covering her hands with mine.

My father, seeing my predicament, moved in to try and console my mother. He became her next victim. She latched onto him, her face buried in his tunic, sobbing loudly. "Dear, he is too young to go to war."

My father comforted her, resting his hand on her head, and looked at me. He unlatched the heavy, silver-hilted dragonbone sword from his hip. Our family’s signature blade. He held it out to me.

"Take this. It will keep you safe. It is time I stop hanging onto this. You are its rightful heir."

I pushed it back toward him. "I have not yet earned the right to wield this. It is better served in your care."

My father stared at the blade, then at me. He was thinking. The silence stretched, heavy and profound in the cold corridor of the ship.

"I always dreamt of a day when I would ride into battle with my son," he said, his voice dropping to a low, rough rumble. "To be there if you stumble. To catch you. I am told you have gone to war numerous times. I am truly sorry I was not there for you, and that you had to face all that alone. But now I am here."

He moved forward, my mother letting him go. He engulfed me in a warm, crushing hug. The embrace felt alien. It was the first time I had ever been held like this by a man, by a father. But there was no doubt in my mind. I felt safe.

"Now that I am here, you can stumble and fall all you want," he whispered fiercely into my ear. "No matter which end of the globe you are in, I will come to aid you. The only reason I am not riding into battle with you right now is at your insistence, to keep my survival a secret. I understand you need to prove yourself. Do what you must. But if things ever get out of hand just call... Cygnus and I will be ready to join the battle at a moment's notice."

The anxiety knotting my stomach seemed to melt away. In my past life, I didn't know this feeling. I had always operated without a net. Here, there was a cushion to land on and a shield to protect me. This is what it means to have a father. The only man who genuinely wants you to be better and more successful than him. What it feels like to have someone on my side no matter how tight things get, a person I can rely on without worry.

My father was widely recognized as the greatest warrior of his generation, I thought, pulling back to meet his steady gaze. All the kingdoms feared the day the Wight Lion would descend on the battlefield. His might is what kept the Seven Kingdoms in check and forced them to resort to assassination tactics in the dark. They knew winning in a head-on battle against him was an impossibility. Even at eighty percent of his full strength, having him as a reserve force is a terrifying tactical advantage.

"I will," I replied, my voice thick.

He stepped back and gave me a gentle smile.

Next, it was time to say a short farewell to my sisters. They were currently clutching Kaelus on each side, treating the Dragon Prince like an oversized, scaly stress ball. They turned to me.

I knelt down, the armor groaning slightly. "Come give me a hug."

Both of them rushed forward, hugging me from each side. They didn't fully understand the wider implications of the war, and they had been normal at the beginning of this ordeal, but my mother's outburst had made them emotional. They let go of the hug, and each brought out a piece of paper. Lyra pushed Ela to go first.

Ela handed me her drawing with trembling hands. It was a crude sketch of a dragon, drawn in vibrant blue wax crayon.

"This will keep you safe," Ela whispered, her sapphire eyes wide and serious. "Along with brother Kaelus."

I took it, "Thank you, Ela."

Lyra was next. She handed me her drawing, shoving it into my chest plate. It depicted a stick figure in red and black armor surrounded by massive, chaotic scribbles of orange and yellow crayon.

"The solution to all problems," she declared triumphantly, pointing at the scribbles, "is an explosion!"

I couldn't help but pull my two cute little sisters into another hug. They were trying to protect me in their own way, arming me with crayon dragons and drawn fire.

I ruffled both of their heads and kissed their foreheads. "Don't worry. I will be back in no time."

I stood up, carefully folding the drawings, placing them in a secure compartment on my belt.

Finally, I stood in front of my wife.

Nyxia stood with her arms crossed, her face a mask of enforced calm.

"Well, I am off—"

She didn't let me finish my sentence. She broke her posture, closing the distance instantly, and hugged me tight. Her grip was surprisingly strong.

"Please let me come with you," she whispered against my collarbone.

This was quite unexpected and completely out of character for the Ice Queen of House Black. I couldn't very well take her to a battlefield where Tier 9 entities were my enemy. I came up with an excuse.

"I need you here," I said, resting my hands on her waist. "To handle the politics and negotiations. The Dwarven treaty needs constant attention."

She looked up at me, her crimson eyes searching my face. "Then you can't get hurt."

With nothing else to do, I returned her embrace, holding her tight until the final call to board echoed through the corridor.

The flight deck of The Aegis was a cacophony of roaring engines and shouting deckhands. I stood fully suited in my Reaper armor, preparing to ride on Kaelus's back to reach the front lines. General Mirelle stood before me, holding a glowing data-slate, giving me a rapid-fire report on the current situation on the ground.

"Sir, it appears the strategy of targeting the forest was effective," Mirelle yelled over the din of a departing C-130. "The Elves have diverted all of their military power to stop the spreading fire. The terrain in Sylvanheim is their strongest advantage. They don't typically build stone fortresses; they rely on the forest as a natural deterrent. Losing the ancient woods is unacceptable to them. This will push back their attack on Khaz'Modan by a good few weeks."

I nodded. Excellent. The ICBM strike had done exactly what it was designed to do.

"In the Cinderfall engagement zone, an MCIC has been properly established," she continued, swiping to a new screen on her slate. "You will have full, effective command capabilities on the ground."

The Mobile Command and Information Center was a specialized, temporary command post. It possessed the same functionality and processing power as the CIC aboard The Aegis, just crammed into a heavily shielded, rapidly deployable bunker complex.

"We have set up your quarters in the MCIC," she added. "The rationing allocations for Lord Kaelus have also been finalized and secured on-site."

"The Director of the Shadow Order has arrived and has begun operating from the MCIC."

I suppressed a sigh of relief. Upon my mother's insistence, Patricia had gone ahead, serving as my personal guard and attendant. Honestly, having her around was a huge help. Without her managing the mundane details, I might just drop dead from forgetting to eat while analyzing tactical feeds.

"The Crawlers will be ready for core implantation within one or two weeks," Mirelle reported, checking a final logistical timetable.

She concluded her briefing. "Commodore Theramore is ready to depart. We are just waiting on you to arrive on the battlefield so there is no gap in our defense."

Theramore needed to leave and organize the naval assets to counter the approaching elven navy.

"Good job," I said, raising my voice to be heard over the wind. "Everything seems to be in order. Continue the operations."

Kaelus interrupted the debriefing. Brother, are you finally done? his mental voice projected, a loud, impatient whine. I am about to fall asleep here.

I rubbed his huge, starlit head and climbed onto his back, locking my boots into the specialized saddles carved into his scales.

Let's go.

News of the Lord Commander arriving on the front lines had lit a new fire among the troops. The forward base was livelier than ever. Every mechanic, every engineer, and every Dark Elf Legionary was giving it their absolute 120%, terrified of disappointing the Warlord in the red armor.

General Valen found himself with absolutely nothing to do.

Everyone was proactively dealing with their assignments, the supply lines running flawlessly, leaving the usually busy commander wandering the camp. He decided to accompany one of the newest officers on his inspection rounds.

He walked beside Sir Dalgor, the peasant-turned-knight heading the loyalist forces.

Their destination was the camps of the rescued and the survivors located just behind the main trench lines. The problem at the moment was a man named Fedric. Even though he was one of the very first Cinderfall soldiers to defect, he refused to exit the battlefield and head to the safe zones in the rear. He was insisting on joining the Legion as an officer.

Fedric had also amassed a small group of people who wished to fight, or even do menial labor here at the front, insisting on repaying the Lord Commander for their salvation.

Instead of going to the Obsidian Dominion and farming the land there, some people were just thick-skinned. They didn't want to accept that farming the land would be of more help to the war effort than joining a levy army.

Plus, the regulations were in place to prevent spies from infiltrating the ranks of the Legion. The regulations strictly stated that only a graduate of the Aegis Academy could join the formal Legion structure. The man, Fedric, had stated he would be happy with the job of an automaton, just carrying crates, if it meant he could serve.

His insistence on joining the army was scary, not budging at any cost. So, the plan formulated by the logistics corps was to have him join Lord Dalgor and the massive volunteer army holding the western flank.

As they walked through the camp, Valen stopped abruptly.

Dalgor halted, his hand dropping to the hilt of his newly forged sword. "Is something the matter, sir?"

"No," Valen muttered, his eyes narrowing as he scanned the crowd of refugees and defectors. "I think I saw someone I know. Must be my imagination."

Valen was intently scrutinizing a gruff-looking figure in tattered Cinderfall armor who was trying very hard to keep his head low while carrying a crate of supplies. It was a common occurrence in the refugee camps—men broken by war trying to disappear into the background.

But still, Valen couldn't understand why the man looked so familiar. He was a highborn Dark Elf raised in exile; he shouldn't have that many human acquaintances. He shook his head, dismissing the thought. He had a stubborn defector to deal with.
