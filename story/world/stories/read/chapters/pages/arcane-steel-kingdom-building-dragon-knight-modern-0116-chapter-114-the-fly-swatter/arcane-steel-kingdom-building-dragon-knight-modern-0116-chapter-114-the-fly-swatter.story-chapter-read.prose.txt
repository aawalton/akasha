Theramore picked up a buttery shortbread biscuit, dunked it into his porcelain teacup, and popped the soggy half into his mouth. He chewed slowly, savoring the vanilla and almond.

"Not bad," the old sailor mumbled, brushing crumbs out of his grey beard. "Though the galley down on deck four tends to overbake them. I need to have a word with the quartermaster."

He was standing on the bridge of The Axiom, a warship packed with enough firepower to level a small country, treating the unfolding naval engagement like a matinee theater showing.

On the central holographic display, a cluster of red icons broke away from the smoking ruin of the Cinderfall fleet. They were ascending rapidly, trying to gain altitude through the thick, oily smoke of burning galleons.

"Griffin Knights," Commander Trisa noted, her fingers tapping across her glass console. "Approximately sixty hostiles. They are forming a wedge and diving toward our vanguard destroyers."

Theramore chuckled, a dry, rasping sound that rattled in his chest. He took another sip of his tea. "Have they learned absolutely nothing? Or is that idiot in charge throwing away lives just to buy time for reinforcements?"

"Protocol dictates we scramble an intercept flight, Commodore," Trisa said, her voice strained with the effort of remaining professional. "Hostile aerial assets are closing on the fleet. We need to put Wyverns in the air before they reach striking distance."

Theramore peered over the rim of his teacup. He looked at Trisa.

"Tell me something, Trisa. What do you think the mess hall is serving for dinner tonight?"

The Dark Elf blinked, her hands freezing over her keyboard. "Sir?"

"It’s a Tuesday," Theramore mused, rubbing his chin. "Usually, that means pasta. Spaghetti and meatballs. I like the meatballs. As Commodore of the Iron Horizon, I feel I should be entitled to an extra portion of meatballs. Don't you agree?"

Trisa took a very deep, very slow breath through her nose. She closed her eyes for exactly two seconds. Lady Mirelle had warned her about the old human’s eccentricities, but dealing with him during an active combat scenario was testing the absolute limits of elven patience.

"Sir," Trisa said, her tone hardening. "They are three miles out. We need to launch fighters."

Theramore looked down at his empty cup, sighing heavily as if profoundly disappointed by the universe.

"Exactly as you said, Commander. We scramble jets to intercept threats. Real threats. If we send the Wyverns out there to deal with sixty oversized pigeons, the most those knights are going to do is annoy our fighters to death."

He set the teacup down on a data terminal with a clatter.

"You don't call in an airstrike on a swarm of flies, Trisa. You use a fly swatter."

Trisa’s brow furrowed. "A fly swatter?"

"Engineering has been nagging my ear off for three weeks about getting live-fire combat data on the new automated defense tracking," Theramore grunted, walking over to the main observation window. He pointed a scarred finger out at the grey decks of the destroyers sailing ahead of them. "Let’s test out the fancy toys. Spin up the C-RAMs. Weapons free."

Trisa stared at him, then let out a breath that was half-sigh, half-groan. She keyed the command into her console, authorizing the automated systems. Then, without another word, she turned away from her station, walked over to the bridge’s small kitchenette, and started boiling water. She was going to need her own pot of tea to get through this shift.

The air tasted of burning pitch and blood.

Commander Vane of the 4th Griffin Squadron spurred his mount forward, his knees locked tight against the beast's feathered flanks. Below him, the ocean was a graveyard of Cinderfall pride. Galleons were split in half, burning fiercely despite the water, while the agonizing screams of drowning sailors drifted up through the thick, black smoke.

Vane kept his eyes locked on the prize.

Through a gap in the smoke layer, he saw them. The grey, flat-topped monsters floating on the sea. They were massive, featureless, and ugly. But more importantly, he saw the black metal birds parked on the flight decks.

His heart leaped into his throat. Those were the assassins. The machines that had butchered the Garuda Wing over the capital. And they were just sitting there. Silent. Unmoving.

They're vulnerable, Vane thought, a wild, desperate hope flaring in his chest. They need time to launch. They're sleeping.

Fate had handed him a miracle. If he could dive fast enough, if his knights could strike the decks before the enemy mages could react, they could cripple the metal birds on the ground. They could avenge their slaughtered brothers and turn the tide of this nightmare.

"Form up! Tighten the wedge!" Vane roared into his comm-stone, leveling his crackling lightning-lance. "Aim for the black birds! Burn them where they rest! For the King! For Cinderfall!"

Sixty griffins shrieked in unison, folding their wings and diving at terminal velocity. The wind roared in Vane's ears. The grey decks rushed up to meet them. They were going to make it. They were actually going to make it.

The grey ships reacted.

White, dome-shaped turrets mounted on the bows of the destroyers suddenly swiveled upward. They moved with a terrifying, jerky mechanical speed, snapping into position faster than a man could blink. Extending from the domes were clusters of long, metallic barrels.

Vane urged his griffin faster.

Then came the sound.

BRRRRRRRRRRRRRRRT.

It sounded like a canvas sail being ripped in half by a giant, amplified a thousand times over. It was a mechanical, grinding belch that vibrated right through Vane's armor and into his teeth.

A solid beam of blinding red light reached up from the ships.

It was tracer fire. The C-RAM was spitting out four thousand five hundred rounds of high-explosive, self-destructing incendiary shells every single minute. It was a weapon designed to track and vaporize incoming mortar rounds and rockets before they could touch the sand.

To the C-RAM's targeting computer, a griffin diving at two hundred miles an hour was practically hovering in place. It was swatting a bug.

The red stream of fire touched the knight flying just to Vane's left.

The man and his mount exploded into a fine, red mist. The sheer volume of kinetic energy shredded flesh, bone, and enchanted armor into confetti in a fraction of a second.

BRRRRRRRRRRRRRRRT.

Another turret opened up. Then another.

The sky lit up with intersecting streams of red fire. Vane's glorious charge turned into a meat grinder. The air was instantly filled with the wet, sickening sounds of griffins being torn to pieces. Blood splashed across Vane's visor, blinding him.

"Pull up! Pull up!" Vane screamed, yanking frantically on his reins.

Flying into a C-RAM barrage was like flying into a solid wall of lead. The knight to his right disintegrated. The knight below him turned into a falling fireball. The formation ceased to exist.

Vane felt a sudden, sharp tug on his griffin's left wing. The beast shrieked, spiraling out of control. He wiped the blood from his visor just in time to see the ocean rushing up to meet him, his mind unable to comprehend the sheer, mechanical brutality of the weapon that had just erased his squadron from the sky.

On the shoreline, the Cinderfall coastal defense lines were in a state of absolute, shrieking panic.

Captain Avlen gripped the parapet of his sand-bagged bunker. He watched the last of the Griffin Knights fall from the sky in pieces, raining down into the ocean like bloody rain.

The grey ships were crushing the burning wreckage of the Cinderfall galleons under their prows, advancing toward the beach with a slow, terrifying inevitability.

"Where is the artillery?!" Avlen screamed at his aides, his voice cracking. "Why aren't the Mage Towers firing?!"

Behind their defensive lines, rising tall and ominous against the sky, stood a massive Black Mage Tower. It was a fortress of dark magic, capable of hurling curses and explosive shadow-bolts miles out to sea. It was their ultimate defense.

And it was doing absolutely nothing.

Avlen didn't know the truth. The royal family in the capital had clamped down on the news of House Black's defection. If the rank-and-file soldiers knew that the Spymaster and his entire network had allied with the enemy, mass desertions would have emptied the trenches in an hour. They had been told House Black was preparing a massive, coordinated counter-attack.

"Signal them again!" Avlen barked, grabbing a terrified junior mage by the collar. "Tell them if they don't fire now, we're going to be overrun by metal monsters!"

A heavy, oppressive magical pressure blanketed the beach. The air grew cold.

Avlen let go of the mage and turned around. A ray of desperate hope flared in his chest.

The runes carving their way up the black stone of the Mage Tower were lighting up. They pulsed with a deep, sickly purple light. At the very apex of the spire, a massive sphere of concentrated, destructive dark matter was gathering, pulling the light out of the sky.

"Yes!" Avlen laughed, pumping his fist in the air. "Finally! Let those iron bastards taste the fury of the Hegemony!"

He turned back to the ocean, gripping his spyglass, eager to watch the grey ships crack and sink under the Archmage's wrath.

He heard the deep, booming release of magical energy behind him.

The blast hit the sandbag bunker seventy feet to Avlen's left. The impact was deafening. Black fire erupted outward, instantly vaporizing a hundred Cinderfall infantrymen and their heavy ballistae.

Avlen froze, his spyglass slipping from his fingers to shatter on the ground.

He slowly turned his head.

The Mage Tower was aiming at the beach. At them.

Another sphere of black light gathered at the top of the spire.

Avlen didn't have time to scream. The dark energy lashed out, striking his position dead center. The world turned blindingly white, and then, mercifully, to nothing at all.

Aboard The Axiom, Theramore was practically bouncing on his heels.

When the Black Mage Tower on the shoreline began to light up, the old commodore felt a thrill of genuine excitement. He had spent his entire career hiding from those towers, fearing the day one would turn its sights on his wooden ships.

"Here we go!" Theramore crowed, slamming a fist into his palm. "Trisa, divert auxiliary reactor power to the forward kinetic barriers! I want full shield strength on the destroyers. Let's see what that overgrown stone chimney can actually do!"

He leaned over the console, eyes wide. "Come on, do it. Hit me. I’ll show you the roar of fifty railguns returning the favor. We’ll turn that tower into gravel!"

He waited for the impact. He waited for the shimmering blue energy shields of his fleet to flare under the magical assault.

The dark magic slammed into the Cinderfall trenches on the beach.

Theramore watched through the viewports as geysers of sand, mud, and Cinderfall armor erupted along the shoreline. The Mage Tower fired again, and again, methodically obliterating the coastal defense garrisons from behind. The beach became a slaughterhouse of friendly fire.

Theramore’s excited grin slowly slid off his face, replaced by a deep, profound pout.

He crossed his arms over his chest and let out a long, dramatic groan.

"Oh, come on!" he complained, throwing his head back to stare at the ceiling. "Are you kidding me? I was just about to have some fun!"

Trisa walked back over, carrying a steaming mug of tea. She looked at the tactical map, watching the red icons on the shoreline rapidly disappearing.

"It appears House Black is making good on their alliance with the Lord Commander," she noted mildly, taking a sip.

"They're stealing my kills, is what they're doing," Theramore grumbled, kicking the base of the holotable like a petulant child. "I had the railguns spun up. I had the firing solutions locked. And these sneaky, robe-wearing shadow-freaks just blast them in the back. House Black always ruins my fun."

Trisa pointed to a wider view on the map. "It is not just here, Commodore. Intelligence feeds indicate this is happening across the entire continent. Any Cinderfall loyalists stationed within range of a House Black asset are being purged."

Theramore sighed, his shoulders slumping. He picked up his cold cup of tea. "Fine. Let the spies have their dirty work. Tell the landing craft to advance. Looks like we're walking onto an empty beach."

High above the chaos, suspended in the silent void of orbit, the data flowed into the command bridge of The Aegis.

I stood at the central table, my hands resting flat against the cool glass. The map of the continent was bleeding.

Across the inland territories, the lands governed by House Black, a sudden and brutal civil war had erupted. Mage Towers rained black lightning down on Cinderfall garrisons. Shadow Operatives stepped out of dark corners in barracks and command tents, slitting throats and vanishing before the bodies hit the floor. The Hegemony troops stationed in the north had been completely blindsided. They died without ever knowing why.

Morpheus Black was a man who knew how to hold a grudge. Four years of bowing to a King he despised, four years of playing the obedient lapdog, had built up a reservoir of malice that was now being unleashed with terrifying efficiency. He was purging the Hegemony from his lands like a virus.

"Effective," Nyxia murmured, standing beside me. Her eyes traced the blinking lights of her family's coordinated strike. She sounded analytical.

"Very," I agreed. "Your father doesn't do things by halves. With this single move, we've secured half the continent without committing a single infantry automaton to a ground war. We have a direct, friendly corridor straight to the capital."

Amidst the slaughter of the soldiers, a different kind of uprising was occurring. The Oracle fed me images from the streets of towns and cities across the Hegemony.

The bread bombs had done their work.

The starving populace, their bellies full for the first time in months, had stopped begging at the palace gates. They were looking at the guards who had hoarded the grain, the tax collectors who had taken their last coins. And they were picking up pitchforks, hammers, and cobblestones.

Riots were breaking out. The people were swarming the diminished Cinderfall patrols. The Hegemony was rotting from the inside, collapsing under the weight of its own tyranny and the sudden, explosive hope delivered by silver packages from the sky.

They were screaming for the Lion. They had picked my side because I had fed them while their King let them starve.

I watched a feed of a crowd tearing down a Cinderfall banner in a town square, hoisting a crude, hastily painted silver lion in its place.

"It will be the same when we reach the old Wight territories," I said quietly. "The people there remember my father. They remember what it was like to be protected, not exploited."

I looked up at the main map. The territory of Aerthos was practically ours again. The capital city of the old Arcane Sovereignty, located deep in House Black's lands, was already secured.

"Aerthos belongs to its people," I said, my voice hardening. "And today, they get it back."

The first move was a checkmate. The Cinderfall Hegemony was pinned on the coast, bleeding from within, and facing an army of steel.

The real war hadn't even begun, and King Theron had already lost.
