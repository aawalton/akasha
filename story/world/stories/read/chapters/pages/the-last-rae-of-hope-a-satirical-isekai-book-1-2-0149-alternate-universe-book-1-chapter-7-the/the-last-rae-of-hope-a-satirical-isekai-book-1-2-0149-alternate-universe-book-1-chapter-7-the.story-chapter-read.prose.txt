How was she going to handle the daily commute at this rate? I was reticent to leave our serene suburbs, but the stark reality of necessary relocation was already rearing its ugly head.

“Ready to go inside?” I asked cautiously.

Nora looked up at the building, then at me, before squinting at the skyscraper once more.

“If I told you that sometimes I get weird vibes before bad things happen…” she muttered, “Would you believe me?”

I nodded. “Premonitions aren’t pretend.”

“My gut is in full revolt with whatever’s happening in there,” she admitted. “I was going to play it cool, but…”

I moved away from the revolving door. “I had a weird dream about this interview, too. In fact, I think I had a fight with myself about it.”

I’ll apologize to him later. I should be kinder to my dream demon king.

“I mean, the commute would be killer,” Nora went on to say. “And there’s no way we’d find a place nearby that we could afford, even if we both got a job!”

I glanced around the busy street. “We could just see a show and head back tomorrow?”

***

I didn’t really understand much of the play, but when you pick up tickets at the last minute, you take what you can get. The costumes were interesting, and it was clear they were reciting poetry, but the jazzy dance numbers seemed wildly out of sync with the overall morose tone.

The hotel we chose was reasonable enough, since it was mid-week. Nora showered, ordered room service, and celebrated the return of her appetite by finishing half a large cheese pizza. Once she was asleep, I lay in the other bed, only pretending to look for other jobs closer to home.

***

Olethros was in the same position as before, sitting on the floor, his back against a bookshelf.

“I took your advice,” I said by way of salutation.

He opened an eye and looked up at me.

“Sorry about yesterday. I didn’t quite understand, but you were right. I mean, who wants to commute to the Big Apple for work?”

He groaned, rubbing his temple. “What is this about giant fruit?”

“Never mind. The point is, I didn’t go.”

He was quiet for a long moment. “Good.”

“That’s it? Good?” I folded my arms.

“What did you want?”

“I don’t know, a little acknowledgment? You were very worked up about it last time.”

He closed his eye again and settled back against the bookshelf.

Still sulking, huh?

I looked around his office. If anything, it was worse than before—a stack of crumpled papers sat directly in front of his left foot, serving as his makeshift footrest, seemingly out of spite.

“You’re still not yourself,” I said. “Or myself.”

“You’ve said that before.”

“Well, then. Even more evidence that it’s the truth.” I sat down across from him and leaned forward. “What’s going on, for real? And don’t say it’s none of my business, because it absolutely is.”

He flickered, turning almost completely translucent before his shoulders sagged.

“I’m trapped,” he finally admitted.

I blinked. “Trapped?”

“Here.” He gestured, his palms reaching up, illuminating the surrounding crimson-stained-glass ceiling. “I cannot leave this space.”

I thought about that for a few long moments. It was my fault, wasn’t it? He was always here, in the back of my mind, and for some reason, I couldn’t ever let him go.

“Alright,” I said. “Let me think about how I can fix this.”

“You cannot fix this.”

“Hmph. You should believe in me! I mean, I believe in you, don’t I?”

He groaned again, but I was gracious enough to ignore it.

“Look. This is my dream, right?”

“It certainly isn’t mine,” he agreed, his tail twitching.

“So why can’t I just—” I stood up and held out my hand to him. “Open the door for you?”

He shook his head. “While I appreciate the gesture—”

“Take my damn hand, Olethros.”

With a defeated sigh, he stood slowly and took it. “Try not to be too disappointed, Miss Rachel.”

“I won't be. When I put my mind to it, I can do almost anything. So why don’t you try to have a little hope for once, huh?”

***

I woke up to Nora screaming.

It was the kind of scream that arrives before the brain has fully loaded—pure, immediate, and loud enough that I was upright before I understood why.

The hotel room was still normal enough. Dull, gray city light leaked through curtains that didn’t quite close all the way. Nora was pressed against her headboard with her knees to her chest, pointing shakily.

I followed her finger.

Olethros, demon horns and all, stood by the tiny vanity desk at the opposite end of the room, his sightless gaze contemplating the coffee maker with an expression of profound uncertainty. He turned slowly, holding his hands behind himself.

“I imagine,” he said to the room at large, “that all three of us have quite a few questions right now.”
