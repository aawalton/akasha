Thursday November, 24 (Thanksgiving)

Scott’s house wasn’t quite what I expected. I think I’d been expecting modern and minimalistic, clean.

Instead, I found a comfortable mess. There wasn’t trash anywhere, so it wasn’t dirty, just lived in.  A small bookshelf overflowing with books sat next to a small couch sagging in the middle with an old worn quilt draped over the back. There were a lot of plants, and none of them looked like my sad excuse for an urban plantscape.

The dust, especially on upper surfaces, had gone unnoticed by the looks of it for quite some time.

“Sorry,” taking my coat, he seemed apologetic and a little uncomfortable having me in his space. “I cleaned but this is kind of as good as it gets.”

“I like it.” I did. It felt genuine and warm. It looked nothing like my apartment which had gone from a magazine backdrop to much more filled with stuff magazine backdrop. It feels like a home.”

“That’s a nice way of saying it’s messy.”

“I wouldn’t say that,” I tried to bluff. “It’s cozy and inviting.”

Jay-C was sitting next to a framed photograph of the kindly looking woman I’d seen on Facebook. They had matching crooked smiles in the photo and she was holding up a piece of cake victoriously.

"That's my mom.” He followed my gaze and nodded in that direction after I glanced back at him. “It was from her retirement party.”

“She looks very nice.”

“She is. All of her students loved her. She would love you.” He caught himself and began to trip over his own words. “I mean… Not that I’m.. I mean not that we’re at the parent meeting stage. Obviously, I meant–”

I elbowed him, “Oh yeah, already planning my flight to Ohio, are we?”

He glared playfully at me but relaxed. “Oh yeah, but I’m making you sit in coach.”

“Well, sure, that’s fair. You also will not be getting any of this.” I gestured to the pie I was still holding.”

“Oh!” He reached for the pie, “I’m sorry I should’ve taken that.”

I handed it off to him along with the bag containing the tub of cool whip. He placed the cool whip in the fridge. I glanced curiously inside and other than a jug of milk and a water filter pitcher on the shelf it looked fairly bare.

Turning around he grinned, “Okay, so Chinese food should be here in about twenty minutes. Do you want anything to drink? I have water, milk, beer and something my sister gave me that may or may not be alcoholic kombucha.”

Trying to decide, I bit my lip. I was not a beer person, it always made my stomach upset.

“Oh!” Opening the freezer, Scott turned revealing a bottle of rose. “I also have some wine I quickly chilled.”

“I’ll take a glass of wine,” I said smiling. “I’m sure you forgot all about it.”

“I wish that I could take credit for messing with you. This time though I ran out to get napkins and grabbed this bottle last minute and I did forget that I put it in there to chill.”

After pouring a glass and handing it over we settled onto the couch. Football was on, which I had absolutely zero interest in watching. I was kind of zoning out and sipping on my wine when Scott spoke up.

“I just remembered! I just got to the part where they introduced the dark and brooding fae, Malikai. Careon does not like him at all. But is it just me or is there some sexual tension between Aurora and Malikai?”

“Why do you say that?” I asked trying to not give away any spoilers.

“Oh I don’t know, maybe the long penetrating stares? Which I need to point out here how many times the author uses the word penetrating prior to there being any…well… penetration.”

I snorted into my wine. “That she does.”

“I think Malikai has more going on than has been revealed yet. But Careon seems like a good dude. Very interested in self-image though.”

“He definitely is. With the fae reputation is very important.”

“Right, speaking of it’s still bothering me that Aurora didn’t think her friend Celeste being a fae in disguise was nearly weird enough.”

“What do you mean?”

“Well, first off she practically told her, you know this impossible to believe thing? Well it’s true and not only that but you have a destiny and I was sent here to keep an eye on you.”

“Okay…”

“She was upset for like five minutes and then was like ‘oh yeah that’s fine. Wooo new realm’”

“They were best friends for years. It takes a lot more than like secret royalty to break them up.”

“If you say so–”

“I do say so.”

“I think she is being willfully ignorant.”

There was a knock at the door and Scott jumped up before I could respond. His words struck something in me. Or maybe my own did. But I was deep in thought about Kate by the time he walked back over.

“Sam?”

“Huh what?”

“I asked if you wanted me to scoop you up a plate or if you wanted to serve yourself. I didn’t realize you were so into football.”

I hadn’t really been looking at the TV at all and had no idea what was even going on, but it must’ve looked like I’d been engrossed.

“I’ll serve myself.”

After dishing up plates we sat back down on the couch. The house he was staying in didn’t seem to have a kitchen table, and he seemed comfortable enough eating on the couch that I imagined this was a pretty typical occurrence, not that I had any room to talk.

I was slurping up some lo mein with chop sticks when Scott began talking.

“I keep thinking about something you said about your friend, Kate. That you did something that she couldn’t forgive.”

My chopsticks stopped halfway to my plate and I intelligently swallowed a not-fully-chewed bite. “Mmhmm?” I managed after a gulp of wine.

“What was it that you did that was so unforgivable?” He held up a hand, “I mean, you don’t have to tell me, but maybe it would make you feel better and then I could help you figure out how to approach her and start to make it right.”

“I lied to her and kept a secret after promising I wouldn’t. When she found out I think it triggered some past trauma with her mom.”

“Why is that?”

“That isn’t my story to tell.”

“What’d you lie about?”

Licking my lips I set my plate down to rest on my knees to hide my shaking legs. “It’s a long story.”

“I’ve got time. For what it’s worth I don’t think you’re a bad person. I think you probably got scared and made a bad choice and don’t know how to fix it.”

I considered before answering.  If I told this man I just met my secret before I told Kate, I would lose her forever. If I lied to him in this moment, or was too evasive, there was a good chance that this relationship would go the way of Kate and mine.

My phone began buzzing. I tried to ignore it but shortly after the buzzing started. A second time. “Sorry, one moment.”

“Oh, it’s okay.”

I pulled my phone out of my pocket. ‘Mom’ I sighed. “It’s just my mom, probably calling to tell me how disappointed she is in my existence.”

“You don’t have to answer it.”

“I definitely wasn’t going to.”

I silenced and set the phone to Do Not Disturb.

The mood shifted and the tension was thick in the air.

“I lied about something pretty personal. The issue wasn’t really what I lied about but more that I had lied, you know?”

He nodded. “I get it. Sounds like you’re just human. We all make mistakes.”

“What if I’m not?” It flew out of my mouth before I could stop it.

“Not what?”

“Not human. I don’t know what I’m saying. I think I didn’t get enough sleep last night.”

“Just because we’ve done something we’re not proud of doesn’t make us monsters. But, you’re right. We can all be a little inhuman sometimes.”

He had no idea.

I smiled and went back to my lo mein and football. We had pie and played a game of rummy. It was actually a pretty relaxing Thanksgiving. No yelling, or fighting, no aunts getting drunk and telling each other off.

At the end of the day I made my way to the door and turned back toward him after stepping out onto the porch. “Thank you, for not letting me wallow today.”

“Thank you for coming. Same time next week?” He leaned against the doorframe. A stray piece of hair dangled down in front of his eye which somehow made that damn smile look even more charming.

“Are you inviting me over again?”

“I’m inviting you to wherever you want.” He paused, “I mean… that came out wrong.”

“I know what you meant.” I stood on my tiptoes and leaned in for a kiss. When he didn’t angle his head toward me I kissed him on the cheek. “Good night Scott.”

His smile was soft and his eyes made me feel like I was going to melt through the porch. He grabbed my hand and kissed it.

“Good night Sam.”

I walked to my car floating six inches off of the ground. Given my propensity for the supernatural you may be wondering if this is literal. Honestly, I don’t think so but it might as well have been.
