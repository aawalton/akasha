Ridley made her way out of the theater without bothering to excuse herself, shouldering past people who stood in her path. She caught a few odd looks when they realized they’d just been pushed aside by a 5’4”, 110-pound girl. By now she was well used to it. She jogged back toward the lobby, easily getting ahead of most of the crowd. Sam and Bru-haha were likely still far behind her.

A few people were already talking heatedly to a harried-looking manager who seemed completely out of his depth. He was probably getting an earful about the wallet thief. The victims were wasting their time; as if a mundane theater employee would have any information on a supernatural pickpocket. He waved his hands back and forth in the universal gesture of “I’m not responsible.”

Ridley had been unable to access any new abilities even through her level up. She didn’t know why, but as she researched, she figured meditation would be a good way to grab onto her self-control with both hands. Waiting around for Brian and his team to take out threats she had found had been annoying. As she meditated, some of her passive skills had leveled up, however, and she developed what she thought of as a primal sense of her surroundings.

As she let her mind go, she felt her eyes and ears sharpen. What was out of place in this room? The boy shooting zombies in the arcade had some bloodlust. The guy taking money at the register seemed shady, likely sliding some money under the register for himself. Where was… guilt.

Across the lobby, Ridley sighted down her target. A girl who looked to be in junior high. Long straight brown hair with a ribbon, white t-shirt under a denim dress. She carried herself with an unmistakable posture of guilt. In her hand was a full plastic grocery bag. Nervously, she approached a bored female employee, tapped her on the shoulder, thrust the bag into her hands, and bolted out the front door.

The employee opened the bag. Her eyes went wide.

“Ridley!” Sam shouted, startling her out of her concentration.

“What!” she yelled back.

“Where are you going?” he asked, confused.

Ridley reached up and pinched his cheek.

“Aww, so cute.” She tugged on it like an annoying aunt.

She glanced back just in time to see the employee handing the bag to her manager. As he looked inside, the people around him visibly relaxed.

“My dear Watson,” Ridley said, turning to Bru-haha and pretending to hold an imaginary pipe. “I have deduced that the old lady’s purse is very likely in that bag.” She pointed the nonexistent pipe grandly at the manager.

“Uh, thanks? I guess I’ll go ask,” Sam replied.

“Excellent, Bru-haha, on me! We have a thief to catch!” Ridley spun and dashed toward the front door.

“I shall bathe in the blood of my enemies!” Bru-haha bellowed.

Maybe that gruesome movie scene earlier had gotten him a little too hyped up on violence.

“No killing! We must bring them in alive and unharmed for interrogation!”

Ridley executed a dramatic jump kick and slammed the door open. An employee yelled at her to be more careful. She ignored him, scanning both directions for the little girl. There—she was skulking away down the sidewalk.

Bru-haha burst through the other door with a thunderous “URAAAAGH!”

Feeling inspired, Ridley pulled her hand over her head. She activated her bracelet.

“Release!” she shouted in a high-pitched, theatrical voice.

Her clothes transformed into a full Cardcaptor Sakura outfit: giant puffy pink hat, short white dress, and enormous bow at the neck.

“That… was… AMAZING!” Bru-haha roared before charging after the culprit. “You must show me that movie in the future.”

Ridley locked eyes with the girl again.

“Stop, agent of darkness!” The child didn’t look particularly concerned at Ridley’s attention—until Bru-haha bellowed and barreled toward her.

An ear-piercing scream split the air. The girl ran as fast as her short legs could carry her—which, to Ridley’s annoyance, was almost as fast as Ridley herself. Damned sealed powers. Bru-haha, however, shook the ground with each stride and quickly overtook both of them. He nearly caught up just after they rounded the corner of the strip mall.

The girl curled into a ball on the pavement and started shouting, “It’s not my fault! It’s not my fault!”

Tears of terror, not guilt, streamed down her face as Bru-haha grabbed the back of her shirt and hoisted her up to eye level.

“Little thief, do you know what we do with your kind?”

Her already puffy, red eyes darted around as she shook her head.

“We turn them in to the proper authorities. You could be looking at juvenile imprisonment.”

“I didn’t take them!” she cried, eyes squeezed shut, legs kicking uselessly in the air. “He makes me help him, but I don’t steal them! I give them back when he’s done! He bullies me if I don’t do what he tells me to do!”

Ridley stepped forward and tapped Bru-haha on the arm.

“Put the girl down,” she commanded.

He complied. Ridley moved in front of the girl and placed both hands gently but firmly on her shoulders.

“What is your name?” she asked in a calm but forceful tone.

“Jada,” the girl answered morosely.

“Jada, you said the real thief bullies you?”

She nodded vigorously, giving the best sad puppy-dog eyes she could manage.

“You don’t want to steal from people?”

“He says I’m too slow, like a donkey. So I’m only good for carrying stuff like a donkey. He makes me sneak out of the house when my parents aren’t looking and I get in so much trouble now!”

“Is he close by? Maybe we can make him stop bullying you.”

“YES! YES! He’s over here!” Jada grabbed Ridley’s arm and pulled. “He steals a bunch of stuff then takes a nap. That’s when I try to return it. He doesn’t actually like to keep it. He just likes to take it! He doesn’t even notice it’s gone when he wakes up!”

They followed Jada to the edge of the parking lot, where the rows of spaces gave way to a small grassy area with trees. She jogged around a small tree and pointed downward.

Ridley rounded the trunk, expecting to see a neighborhood bully or an older brother. Instead she found a small, grey, furry ball curled up asleep.

She glanced back at Jada, who backed away nervously. Ridley squatted down and poked the furball. It rolled onto its back, stretched, and yawned, revealing tiny fangs. Little paws reached toward the sky.

A ferret. It’s a freaking ferret.

Quest Complete! Pick Pocket Predicament: A perpetrator is pilfering the pockets of pedestrians. Objective: Pinpoint the position of the pickpocket perp posthaste! Quest rewards: -400 xp -4 silver

“Are you freaking serious!?” Ridley exclaimed, looking up at the girl.

“AH!” Bru-haha exclaimed. “These types of rodents, yes. They are delicious. We should have a barbecue.”

“No!” Jada shouted, rushing forward protectively.

The noise woke the little guy. He hopped to his feet and bounced around with his mouth open, flashing tiny fangs as though he intended to use them.

“Oh, you made him mad!” Jada said, shrinking back again.

“Ha! It’s so cute—OOF!” Ridley was cut off as something blurred and slammed into her face, knocking her off balance. “OW! What the hell?”

She looked up to see Bru-haha swatting at the air. The ferret corkscrewed through the air, landed on his arm, then launched itself off and head-butted him in the face. Bru-haha’s head snapped back more from surprise than pain. He shook his head in bewilderment. The ferret blurred again and vanished.

“They are not this hard to catch where I come from,” Bru-haha muttered.

“Yeah, they aren’t this agile here either. Where did he go?”

“In the tree!” Jada pointed upward.

The ferret was doing tiny hops left and right along a branch, mouth open. There were several small grunts from the little beast. Ridley couldn’t be sure, but it looked like it was laughing at them.

Jada stepped forward, stared intently at the ferret, then turned to Ridley and Bru-haha. “He says you two seem weak. Become his underlings and he will protect you and provide many shiny things for you. He wants more donkeys to carry his loot.”

The ferocious looks Ridley and Bru-haha gave Jada made the girl back away quickly, pointing at the ferret. “He said it! He did!”

“So you can communicate with it?” Ridley asked, still skeptical.

Alexander could understand Flick; maybe this was a similar connection. Maybe the girl was a Beastmaster that had a hidden animal-control ability.

“Yes! He talks to me! We’re connected now. I didn’t know he was so bad until he got so smart and strong!”

Bru-haha waved Ridley closer and whispered, “Do you think this could be a familiar contract?”

“I doubt it. She seems too weak to have gotten a reward from a quest. She hasn’t done anything particularly spectacular other than supposedly talking to the animal.” Ridley turned back to Jada. “What is your class?”

“Class? I don’t have a class. I do have something though.”

Ridley and Bru-haha exchanged a glance. Bru-haha waved his hand. “Go on.”

“I am… an animal companion.”

Ridley’s jaw dropped. “To the ferret? So the ferret is like your trainer?”

“I dunno! He’s supposed to be my pet! He acts like I’m his pet though!”

Ridley couldn’t help it—she burst out laughing.

“It’s not funny!” Jada whined.

“I would disagree, young Jada,” Bru-haha said with an boisterous laugh. “This situation is very humorous.”

After she caught her breath, Ridley asked, “What is your ferret’s name?”

“Jameson.”

“Oh, that’s an interesting name for a ferret.”

“It’s a name that was written all over Daddy’s drinks. He named it when he got it for me.”

“Ah. Well…” Ridley looked up at the ferret. “If I find any fun super shiny stuff for you to steal, I’ll let you know…” Ridley had a thought. “Invite Jameson.”

A green window popped up.

Warning: You are inviting a co-faction to join your party Invite Jameson to join your party ‘Planeteers’? Yes/No

“Wow, it’s a green screen.” She looked at Bru-haha. “Have you heard of a green screen before?”

“No, little fury, but I have not had the pleasure of seeing the power of the gods before.”

Ridley, deciding it couldn’t hurt, selected yes. Maybe it was just a color for animals. The ferret didn’t visibly react, but the invite was accepted.

Party features were weird and new features had been unlocked recently. A localized chat feature was something she and Alexander had played with. She wished there was a mini-map but no luck so far.

Jameson has requested to be named party leader of ‘Planeteers’. Relinquish party leader status? Yes/No

“Hell NO!” Ridley shouted at the fur ball.

The ferret started jumping in circles, mouth open wide in a fit of anger.

Jameson: Bad Donkey! No shiny for you! Came through the chat window.

“That is just too aggravatingly cute,” she muttered.

“Oh mighty ferret warrior,” Bru-haha chimed in. “Would you like to go on a quest to find treasure with me? I have a method to find many treasures!”

The ferret paused. It stared at Bru-haha and then squeaked at Jada.

“But I can’t! I’ll get in trouble!” Jada whined.

“Young Jada, I know this may seem overwhelming, but you should seek out power so that you make those who oppose you be in trouble. This is a gift! Not a curse.”

The ferret grunted at Jada some more.

“I… I will think about it,” she said.

Bru-haha handed a Congo business card to Jada.

“Let me get your contact information as well. I will contact you if there is a good opportunity for the power of Jameson to be on full display. Do you know the name of his power?”

Jada looked at the ferret and Ridley. “He is a thief.”

Ridley and Bru-haha looked at each other again and burst out laughing.

“Brian will have some competition! He is going to be so pissed!” Ridley said through her bouts of giggles. “Oh yeah. I should see if we can party up, Bru-haha.” Ridley turned to the orc. “Invite Bru-haha.”

Another screen appeared, but this time it glowed red.

Warning: Bru-haha is a player for a different system and so has conflicting goals. Are you certain you want to invite an invader into your party? Yes/No

Ridley paused, then looked up at Bru-haha with suspicion.

“No.”

“It doesn’t work?” Bru-haha asked.

“Nope. I guess not. Maybe we can ask Alexander to look into it.”

“Sure. When he gets back.”

“Gets back?” Ridley asked.

“Ah, well… he went on a trip. For an undisclosed amount of time.”

“What? That doesn’t sound like Alexander.”

Bru-haha just shrugged, causing Ridley to feel a tinge of concern.

“RIDLEY!” Sam’s voice echoed across the parking lot.

“Oh, crap!” Ridley quickly went to switch her clothes back to normal. She realized her ring was out of charges. She was starting to get carried away with her ring.

Dummy! she chided herself. Sighing internally, she knew that either way it would all come out eventually.

The ferret leapt onto Jada’s head and flopped down on its belly, clearly planning to stay there for a while. Ridley was tempted to step in and tell the little tyrant that Jada wasn’t just a donkey, but the sight was honestly too adorable.

“I got my wallet back,” Sam said, holding it up triumphantly before looking at Ridley closer. “Whoa! Where did that come from?” He gestured at the full Sailor Moon treatment.

“Ahh, well.” Ridley looked at Bru-haha, who smiled at her consolingly. “Sam, first meet Jameson the thief and Jada his animal companion.”

Sam looked at the group like someone was pulling a prank on him. “Um, I think I missed something.”

Ridley spent a minute explaining to Jada and Sam what was at this point an open secret. She talked about her class and the fight at Congo. Bru-haha jumped in and described how ruthless Ridley was on the battlefield in order to help impress her crowd. Then he told him how they had found Jada and Jameson.

When she had caught Sam up, she waited for him to respond. His mouth had hung wide open for most of the explanation, with the occasional gasp at the blatantly violent encounters she described.

After a long awkward pause at the end of it all, he finally just let out a single word.

“WOW.”

“Wow? That’s it?” she prompted.

“To be honest I had my suspicions, as everyone did. All of those powflicks were, in hindsight, some kind of precursor to all of this. This is way beyond any reasonable expectations on my part—I’m not ashamed to say. You mean to tell me that you…” He pointed at Ridley, “helped to subjugate him.” He pointed at Bru-haha. “And save our city?”

“Mmm, yep. That sums it up.” Ridley shrugged.

“She was a tiny terror that struck fear into our hearts!” Bru-haha said proudly.

“I didn’t think you could get any cooler,” he said, looking impressed. “Your jokes make so much sense now. You literally could have kicked my ass the whole time… that’s kinda hot.”

“You think like an orc, little man!” Bru-haha laughed, slapping him on the shoulder.

Ridley just blushed, at a loss for words.

So they waited with Jada while she called her dad, and discussed more of Ridley’s encounters. When a car drove up with Jada’s half-drunk father, he was far from amused—especially when Jada tried to blame her ferret for forcing her to sneak out. Apparently this argument had come up a couple of times recently.

Ridley was irritated for Jada’s sake and swore to help her if she got the chance, but she felt now she could only make the situation worse.

Sam sighed. “Well, it kinda sucks our movie got interrupted. They did hand out ticket vouchers, though.” He glanced at Ridley questioningly. “Do you want to try again next week for Howl’s Moving Castle?”

Ridley took a deep breath, thought about it for a second, then smiled.

“It’s a date.”
