The night had thickened by the time Ulric pushed open the tavern’s back door, letting in a breeze that carried the sharp tang of saltwater and oil. Oswald followed without a word. The alley behind the Rat’s Nest was narrow and grimy, lit only by the yellow flicker of a distant streetlamp. Rats scattered ahead of their boots, vanishing beneath crates and piles of netting.

“You ever been to the docks at night?” Ulric asked over his shoulder.

“Few times,” Oswald muttered.

They passed beneath drooping laundry lines and rusted drain pipes that wept onto the stones below. The deeper into the alleys they went, the more the air changed, less smoke, more salt, with the faintest traces of rotting fish and iron.

Eventually, the alleys gave way to the harbor. Masts rocked in the distance, groaning gently as the tide sloshed against barnacle-covered piers.

Ulric led Oswald along the edge of the docks, where the buildings leaned against one another like drunkards. Fishmongers’ stalls were shuttered tight, nets hung drying from hooks, and gulls roosted in quiet clusters above.

Then, without fanfare, Ulric stopped in front of what looked like a fish warehouse. The door stood half open, revealing only darkness inside. A faded sign overhead read Darney & Sons – Salted Goods, though there wasn’t a single crate in sight.

“This the place?” Oswald asked.

Ulric knocked twice, then once more after a pause. The door creaked fully open in response. “Yeah, this is it.”

Looks like a place someone goes to disappear, Oswald thought, ducking under the low archway.

Inside, a narrow staircase led down into the dark, carved right into the stone foundations. Ulric lit a lantern from the wall and handed it to Oswald without comment. The flickering flame danced across the worn steps as they descended.

They emerged into a long corridor with old red bricks and exposed pipes running along the ceiling. Symbols of red horses were stamped into banners lining the hall. One had been slashed and mended, the stitching visible where someone had tried to keep up appearances.

“They really put their mark on this place, huh?” Oswald muttered.

Ulric gave a short grunt. “Red Dragoons built the damn arena. Whole thing’s been run through ‘em since the start. You’ll see.”

At the end of the corridor, they reached a heavy iron door with another horse sigil embossed into the center. This one was flanked by a pair of narrow slots that glowed faintly red from within.

Ulric stepped close, rolled up his sleeve, and pressed a hand to the slot. There was a faint hiss. A moment later, the door unlocked with a loud clunk, and swung inward.

“VIP access,” Ulric said, grinning as he wiggled his fingers.

Oswald followed him into the entry chamber. It was clean, polished in a way the docks never were. The floor was dark stone, scrubbed and slick, and the walls were covered in more of the red horse iconography. Everything about it screamed money, but the kind earned from blood and bets.

Behind a wide desk sat a woman in a crisp, red-trimmed uniform. Her hair was pulled into a tight braid, her collar neatly fastened with a silver pin shaped like, you guessed it, a horse mid-gallop.

She looked up as they approached, her smile professional but hollow. “Ulric, you’re late.”

Ulric jerked a thumb toward Oswald. “Had a visitor, put him on my tab. New fighter.”

The woman’s eyes drifted to Oswald. “Name?”

“Oswald.”

“Alias?”

“Black Eye.”

Her eyebrow lifted slightly, but she didn’t press. Instead, she slid a tag across the desk. The little brass plate had a number etched into it and, of course, a red horse stamped in the corner.

“This gets you through fighter access. Keep it on you at all times. If you lose it, you don’t get in. If you try to fake one, we cut your hands off. Questions?”

“Nope,” Oswald said flatly, pocketing it.

Ulric chuckled. “Love the hospitality here.”

The receptionist ignored him. “Your match slot is pending. Wait in the pit lounge. You’ll be called.”

Oswald turned to Ulric as they walked toward the next set of doors. “What’s the pit lounge?”

Ulric shrugged. “Think… armory mixed with prison yard. You’ll get used to it.”

The metal door let out a sharp hiss as it slid open, releasing a sudden gust of hot, breath-thick air. The scent hit first,sweat baked into stone, iron tang clinging like smoke, and underneath it all, the faint rot of old blood scrubbed but never gone.

Oswald stepped in, he noticed how the floor had been polished over time by the weight of countless footsteps. A dark smudge marked one corner near the threshold, a bootprint, still damp with someone’s blood. Eh, could be worse.

The pit lounge opened up in a broad, curved arc, the walls carved deep into the earth, rough at the top where pickaxes had once dug too hard. Thick beams reinforced the ceiling, iron girders bolted into the stone. Cracked banners bearing the Red Dragoons’ horse insignia were draped over the walls, their edges scorched or torn.

Rows of wooden benches sprawled outward across the chamber, notched with gouges from knives or careless armor. Between them stood tall weapon racks filled with everything from spears and axes to jagged, barbed blades that looked more ceremonial than practical.

People moved among the shadows. Some stripped to the waist, while others wore full gear—mail, leather, enchanted bone. A woman with a shattered pauldron was leaning against the wall, wiping blood from her mouth. Nearby, a man with a missing eye chuckled as he wrapped his wrist in new bandages. Two others traded lazy blows with dulled swords, the clack of metal on metal echoing softly across the chamber.

Some didn’t move at all. They sat alone, eyes locked on the floor, hands resting on their knees like monks awaiting death. Not fear, not boredom. Just... stillness. Those are the dangerous ones, not the loud ones.

The far wall drew his attention. Reinforced glass stretched from floor to ceiling. Behind it lay the viewing corridor, overlooking the arena floor beyond. Harsh, white sand formed the battleground, currently stained dark in places. Above the ring, tiered stands overflowed with spectators. The crowd’s roar beat against the glass, only muffled slightly by enchantments.

“This way,” Ulric muttered beside him, nodding toward a branching hallway on the right.

Oswald followed. They passed more fighters in narrow corridors lit by dull, flickering sconces. One man whistled when he saw Ulric, but said nothing. Another glanced at Oswald and quickly looked away.

Down the hall stood a door reinforced with iron bands and marked by a single red stripe. A small bronze plaque hung next to the frame, though the engraving had worn smooth. Ulric produced a heavy brass key from his coat and turned it in the lock with a loud click.

“Private room,” he said, pushing it open. “Not many get one. But since I’m sponsorin’ you... figure it’s worth the coin.”

The room inside was plain, but functional. Stone walls, freshly swept. A narrow bed pressed against one side, while a small wooden shelf offered space for gear. A hook had been bolted into the wall for cloaks or belts. At the foot of the bed sat a heavy trunk, its lid open to reveal bandages, a clean water flask, and a freshly oiled whetstone.

Oswald stepped inside and ran a hand along the shelf’s edge. Better than most places I’ve stayed.

Ulric leaned in the doorway. “Come on. Let’s watch a round. Might as well know what you’re walkin’ into.”

"Sure, why not." Oswald said.

They returned to the glass overlooking the arena. Oswald leaned against the barrier, eyes narrowing as the current match played out. A man in rust-colored armor charged forward, axe raised high, while his opponent, barefoot and wrapped in chains, twisted to the side and spat a spray of fire from her mouth. The flames licked across the metal with a deafening hiss, and the crowd howled in delight.

Ulric arms crossed as he watched. ““First rule is that there's no limits. Weapons, magic, whatever else ya got, it’s all fair game.”

“Second rule—” Ulric pointed as the fire-breathing woman feinted low and drove a spike through the man’s thigh. “—you win by killin’ your opponent… or convincin’ ‘em to give up. Ain’t no bells or timers. It ends when someone’s dead or too broken to keep goin’.”

The man on the ground screamed. He tried to crawl, blood pooling under him, but the woman showed no hesitation. She knelt, whispered something, and drove the spike through his throat.

Oswald blinked once. Yeesh, brutal show.

He turned to Ulric. “That it? Just kill or quit?”

Ulric nodded. “Simple rules make for a brutal game. Now, about the structure, listen close.”

He tapped a thick finger against the side of his glass. “There’s a prelim tourney. Eight fighters, all tryin’ to make a name. Win that, and you get an invite to the real thing in a few months. Plus a shot at meetin’ Senna herself.”

“Yeah? Just like that?” Oswald asked.

Ulric’s head tilted in a slow nod. “You win the prelims, she’ll show, Senna don’t waste her time on losers. Likes to get a read on the fresh blood up close.”

Oswald lifted an eyebrow. “And what if I don’t feel like goin’ any further after that?”

His voice was calm, but a thought gnawed at the edge of his mind. I''ll play along for a bit, but I ain’t here to climb the damn ladder. Just need to get a look at Senna, and maybe catch wind of Aldric if he’s hangin’ around. This whole tournament mess is just the door I gotta kick in.

If I get a decent lead, I’m out. Don’t care if it’s mid-fight, mid-bracket, or mid-damn-ceremony. I ain’t stayin’ longer than I need to. Besides, Ulric’s just here to save his own ass. Can't count on him when things go sideways.

Ulric’s lips twitched like he was fighting back a laugh. He tipped the glass toward Oswald before downing the rest in one long pull. Then, he set it down with a soft clack. “That’s your mess to deal with. You walk out after the prelims, that’s your call. But you oughta worry less about the after and more about winnin’ the damn thing first.”

Oswald stared out at the blood-stained sand. The arena crews were already scrubbing it clean with enchanted brushes, prepping for the next fight. Overhead, the crowd’s roar dulled for a moment before rising again, hungry and wild. Won't be easy, but I think I can pull it off.

“Fine. I’ll make it.”

“Hope so, kid. 'Cause the folks who lose here don’t get remembered.”

The next fight had just ended in a mess of blood and broken teeth when the heavy lounge doors swung open without warning. The metal hinges groaned, drawing more than a few glances from nearby fighters.

Boots clicked confidently against the stone floor. Oswald turned his head.

A burly man strutted through the entryway, flanked by the scent of perfume. His arms cradled two women, both dressed in silks that left little to the imagination.

Their laughter was high and rehearsed, more decoration than joy. The man’s hair was cropped short and bright green, streaked with darker shades at the roots. Twin swords hung from each hip, blades sheathed in crimson scabbards. His martial tunic clung to a powerful frame, his steps exuding the kind of confidence that demanded attention.

“Ulric!” the man called.

Ulric’s lips twitched into a grin. “Vinn. Nice of you to show up fully clothed this time. Thought you retired those poor girls last week.”

Vinn smirked as the women clung to him tighter, clearly playing their roles. “Retired? Nah. Just gave ‘em a day off. These two are fresh imports.”

With a lazy gesture, he patted their backs and gently nudged them away. They sauntered off, whispering to each other and casting flirtatious glances back.

Ulric crossed his arms. “So, what brings you back? Gonna actually throw someone in the ring instead of running your mouth this year?”

“Thinking about it, but you know how it is. Figured I’d see if you were finally sending someone into the meat grinder who knows how to swing a blade.”

He looked Oswald up and down with a slow, appraising gaze. “And this… is what you brought?”

Oswald’s jaw tensed. Here we fucking go.

“He’s registered as Black Eye, try not to trip over your own ego in front of him.” Ulric said.

Vinn let out a chuckle, shaking his head. “Black Eye, huh? Cute name. You swing as hard as you scowl, kid?”

“I swing harder. You just talk loud enough no one notices you’ve been a loser most of your life.” Oswald spat back.

A few fighters nearby turned their heads, sensing blood in the air that didn’t come from the ring. Vinn’s grin thinned. “Big words for a nobody.”

“Big mouth for a fraud,” Oswald shot back, nodding toward the jagged scar running from the base of Vinn’s neck down to his collarbone. “That why you’re not the champ? Got your ass handed to you and never bounced back?”

Vinn's grin vanished entirely. “Keep talkin’, brat. If we meet in that arena, I’m going to make sure your bones get pulled from the sand one by one.”

“Better hope you don’t. I don’t fight for a crowd, I fight to kill."

The two were nearly nose to nose now. Heat bristled between them like storm-charged air. Behind the glass, another match thundered, but neither of them noticed.

Ulric sighed and stepped between them. “Alright, alright, calm down, boys. You’ll have your chance to flex in front of the crowd soon enough.”

Vinn scowled, but didn’t push the issue further. He gave Oswald one last glare before turning back toward the door. “Hope you last long enough to face me. I’ll be countin’ the minutes.”

Oswald watched him leave. I hope i get to kick his ass in the arena.

Ulric clapped a hand on his shoulder. “That? That was Vinn at his most civil. You just poked the boar, and now it wants to charge.”

Oswald shrugged. “So what? I'll beat his ass."

Ulric chuckled. “You’ve got teeth, I’ll give you that. But you’ve got a few days before your first match. Rest up, keep your head down until you're called."
