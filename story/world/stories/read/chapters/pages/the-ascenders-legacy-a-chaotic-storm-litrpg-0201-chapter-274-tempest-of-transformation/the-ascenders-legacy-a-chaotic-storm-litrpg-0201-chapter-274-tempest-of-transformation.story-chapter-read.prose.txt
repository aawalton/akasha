Time left till deadline: 31 hours 52 minutes.

While Arkhan and Sebastian strategized on how to survive the looming catastrophe, Aodhán and his entourage remained blissfully ignorant.

Or perhaps blissful was the wrong word.

"This place sucks," Baxter muttered as he yanked an abyssal pin from the ground, ignoring the cloud of null essence that rose into the air. "Are we going to see anything other than ice and snow?"

"Hey!" Magdalene shouted playfully. "You found me and Brennon!"

"We don't count, Magda." Brennon snorted. "I agree with Baxter. This place does suck. The previous level was hell, but it was at least aesthetically pleasing. This one is just… meh."

"I don't know…" Destra chuckled. "I think this place is pretty great."

"Of course you would think so," Aodhán snorted from his position up ahead as he began cutting his way to the next abyssal pin.

Nearly four hours had passed since Baxter joined them, and in that time, they'd added two more people to their group.

The first was Magdalene, or Magda as she preferred to be called. She was the enhancement awakened Aodhán had helped back in the last level, and who had helped him in return, running by his side with Seojun until they reached the exit portal. The moment she saw him, she'd thrown herself at his feet, thanking him for saving her life and apparently granting her an inspiration.

"That wasn't my doing," Aodhán had protested, trying to excuse himself from the awkward situation—especially when Destra and Baxter began giving him strange looks. But Magda had insisted, and Aodhán had had no choice in the matter.

Baxter, of course, knew of his miracle rain from what he'd heard about the Steppin' Plains, but apparently, it was still strange to witness the reactions of those who'd experienced it firsthand.

Honestly, Aodhán was surprised the rain had been potent enough to grant anyone an inspiration. Aside from the fact that it had been shoddy and rough—improvised under pressure from the scorching heat and erupting geysers. He'd barely channeled any power into it, exhausted as he'd been from nearly three hours of running and fighting for his life.

Yet it seemed he was far stronger than he gave himself credit for.

He was no longer in the evolved class, but the advanced. If he'd been strong before, he was far stronger now. He needed to remember that.

They'd found Brennon barely an hour after that, and the lanky illusion mage had been quickly integrated into their ranks. He'd entered the exit portal alongside Baxter and was at the 38th tier.

Unlike most other captives in Abyssos, though, Brennon seemed to think he was taking a leisurely tour of cultist territory. Every new discovery excited him, and though he wasn't oblivious to the danger, he didn't seem to be fully grasping it.

That trait showed in his words when they found another abyssal pin a few minutes later.

"These pins should have been red," he muttered as he yanked it from the earth. "I bet they would have screamed danger more than black."

He created an illusion of a red abyssal pin and raised it to the air—not for them to see, but for the cultists to see. He placed the real pin beside the illusion and scrunched his face in disgust. "Do better, cultists. Do better."

"What a character," Destra murmured beside him, then raised her voice so Brennon could hear. "You do know they're trying to kill us, right? I don't think the color of abyssal pins is their main concern."

"Well, it should be," Brennon argued. "Aesthetics are important in all things."

He pointed the red illusion at Aodhán. "Take a look at Aodhán, for instance. He's powerful and deadly, but he's also aesthetically pleasing, which makes him a complete package."

Aodhán shook his head, half-amused by Brennon's logic. Magda, however, wasn't quite so amused.

"I'm not sure you have your priorities straight, Brennon," she replied with a frown. "What difference would it make if the pins were red?"

"The experience and the allure," Brennon replied, floating the illusion pin toward her. "Take a look and tell me this isn't better."

"I'd be more concerned with their functions, Brennon," Baxter interjected. "As of yet, we still have no idea what these pins actually do."

"We have theories," Aodhán corrected with a grimace. "We're just not certain. One thing we can be sure of, though, is that they're both useful and detrimental at the same time."

Brennon swooned. "Oh, the drama. I must give it to whoever designed this. Such dramatic genius. To make the pins a potential solution to the coming threat while making them the source of that threat? Absolutely fantastic!"

Destra and Magda groaned, while Aodhán resigned himself to shaking his head. It was night already, but they still had some light.

"I think we should go one more hour before resting," he suggested, and the others agreed.

"We still have some light left," Baxter reasoned, glaring at the stretch of ice beneath them. "Besides, this isn't exactly shelter-friendly terrain."

Brennon nodded. "Perhaps we can camp in the nearest forest. Light a fire and sing sad songs like weary travelers."

"Sing songs?" Aodhán turned to stare at the man in stunned surprise. He wasn't sure which emotion was stronger—disbelief or confusion. The aesthetic obsession he could understand. The man was an illusion awakened, after all. But to sing songs? Even before his captivity, Aodhán couldn't remember the last time he'd sung anything.

He opened his mouth, shut it, and then opened it again. "You realize we're captives here, right?"

"Of course, I do," Brennon frowned. "I'm not oblivious. But that doesn't mean we have to behave like captives. I've always said it: captivity is a mindset. An illusion. And no illusion is foolproof. I refuse to think like a captive, and maybe you all should start doing the same."

Aodhán paused, considering his words. "Well, maybe there's some sense in that."

"Still," Magda agreed reluctantly, "no singing. Singing is for happy people."

They all laughed and continued moving, Brennon chattering away. Occasionally Aodhán joined the conversation, but he kept most of his attention on core sense—pushing his mind against the realm's interference to search for more pins.

In the thirteen hours since they'd arrived on the Shivering Plains, core sense had been invaluable. Aodhán couldn't imagine navigating this place without it. Likely, he'd be dead by now, having picked a fight with someone whose strength he couldn't properly gauge.

As if to prove the point, he sensed another pin within the hour and led them toward it. Then another. And another. Four more pins fell into their possession as daylight began to fade, and with each discovery, Aodhán's confidence in his senses grew.

When they finally spotted the stretch of blue-leaved trees suitable for shelter, they were exhausted but satisfied. After hours of walking and searching, they were ready to make camp.

However, as they began heading toward the forest, Aodhán paused, his senses detecting something similar to the abyssal pins yet distinctly different.

"What is it?" Destra asked when she noticed he'd stopped.

Aodhán pointed to a distant pile of snow. "I can sense something there. Give me a moment."

He teleported, his body seamlessly transforming into violet lightning before reforming ahead. Baxter and Destra teleported after him, while Magda and Brennon ran to catch up.

"What do you think it is?" Baxter asked as Aodhán leaned closer to the pile, eyes narrowed.

"I don't know," Aodhán replied, moving to dig. Before he could, Destra pulled the snow apart, dispersing it into the wind to reveal…

An abyssal pin. Only far larger than any they'd seen.

"Blessed sands!" Magda swore as she joined them. "Is that an abyssal pin?"

Aodhán didn't respond, his gaze fixed on the strange spike. The pin was roughly three meters long, with red runes rather than silver—each one pulsing to a rhythm only they could hear.

A gasp echoed from behind as Brennon finally caught up. "By Raol, did the cultists implement my idea?"

"This isn't the time, Brennon," Baxter scowled, and then turned to Aodhán. "Did the cultists implement his idea? Because if they are taking suggestions, I'd like to suggest that they let me out of—"

"They are not taking suggestions," Aodhán cut him off with an uneasy frown. "These red runes are blood runes."

Destra let out a sound between a squeak and a gasp, while Magda took several steps backward, whispering a prayer. Brennon, however, took several steps forward to see the pin better. "What do you think they're for? It must be different from the other pins."

Aodhán frowned. The abyssal pins were already dangerous enough. Only Raol knew what touching this crimson-runed variant would cause. Yet how could they discover its purpose without unearthing it?

Baxter apparently shared the same thought. "Are you going to take it?"

Aodhán's frown deepened, his instincts warring with his curiosity. Every rational part of him screamed caution. But after a moment, his expression firmed in determination.

"I will," he responded carefully. "Even though I think it's a terrible idea."

Bracing himself, Aodhán stretched his hand toward the shimmering spike, unease pooling in his gut. The moment his fingers clasped it, a crimson screen appeared.

Congratulations! You have found a Grand Abyssal Pin. 50 points

There are 15 more Grand Abyssal Pins scattered across the realm. Gather all sixteen pins to gain immunity from the looming calamity.

Aodhán raised an eyebrow in surprise, realizing that his instincts were right after all. There was a looming calamity.

Not that he had doubted it before, but to see it so plainly written changed things.

"What's going on?" Destra asked, eyeing him carefully. "Your expression just changed, and it's not inspiring confidence."

But Aodhán's expression only darkened further, and he yanked the pin out of the ground with the intent to hand it over to the others so they could read the message for themselves.

The moment the pin left the ground, the earth shuddered. Not a subtle tremor, but a measured quake that ran through the ice like a heartbeat.

They all tensed, eyes wide with caution, but when nothing else came, Baxter whispered, "That's the fifth quake so far. What is that thing?"

"A grand abyssal pin," Aodhán replied, his eyes widening as he connected the dots. "There are supposed to be sixteen of them scattered across the Shivering Plains. If this is the fifth quake, then this is the fifth one that's been found."

"Does that carry significance?" Brennon asked, eyebrows scrunched in confusion.

"It does," Aodhán replied, handing the pin to Baxter and turning to explain. "Gathering all sixteen pins grants immunity from the coming calamity."

Destra's expression fell in sinking confirmation, and she exhaled heavily. "So it's confirmed. There truly is a looming calamity, and it's meant to last twelve hours? What kind of calamity lasts twelve hours?"

"That's not all," Baxter whispered, handing the pin to Magda. "That quake was stronger than the last. I suspect the calamity will arrive once all sixteen pins are gathered."

"Another realm destruction?" Brennon's breath caught, fear flashing across his eyes as memories of the destruction of the last realm resurfaced.

Aodhán's next words didn't help his panic.

"Or something worse," Aodhán replied, his expression hardening. Then he sighed, suddenly feeling very drained. "We should get some sleep. We still have more than a day left before this supposed calamity strikes." He glanced at Brennon and smiled. "Maybe we can even sing some songs."

But they didn't sing any songs that night.

The moment they reached the forest, Destra cleared the snow to reveal bare ground. They gathered logs and soon had a fire going. They purchased meat from the online shop and cooked it over the flames, the only sounds the crackling fire and sizzling meat.

When the meal was ready, they divided it among themselves and ate in silence. Aodhán provided water, and when they'd finished, as if by silent agreement, each person retreated to a corner of the camp to meditate.

Destra recreated her ice mirror in one corner and began chanting an incantation, willing it to reveal something of the looming calamity. Magda and Brennon also retreated to separate corners—Brennon humming quietly, while Magda whispered something continuously to herself.

Aodhán also retreated to a corner, but Baxter soon joined him, his expression heavy.

"You said you could get us out of here," Baxter whispered. "Can you still do so?"

Aodhán glanced at him and nodded once. Of course, he wouldn't be the one getting them out of this cursed place. He didn't even know if anyone had found the will imprints he'd left in the origin plane. He wished he could explain it all to Baxter and Daruk, but he couldn't.

Or could he?

The thought caught him off guard, and he frowned, considering it for a moment before turning to Baxter. "Tell me, Baxter. How close are you to your next seal?"

Baxter frowned. "I don't know. I had an inspiration during the rush to the exit portal, but I'm still meditating on it. I'm not sure if it's strong enough for a seal. Why do you ask?"

Aodhán replied with a question of his own. "What if I helped you with the miracle rain? Do you think that would help?"

Baxter's frown deepened as he considered it. "I truly don't know. Besides, I'm about fourteen tiers above you. I'm not sure it will work."

"It might," Aodhán shrugged, remembering Az'marthon's words: You're stronger than you know.

The words were a wakeup call, and Aodhán decided. "Meditate on your inspiration. After we rest, I'll help you with the rain. Hopefully it works, and if it does, there's something I need to test." He glanced at Destra before adding, "We can include the others too, though I'm not sure Magda will gain anything so soon after her last inspiration."

Baxter's expression grew contemplative. "What does any of this have to do with escaping though?"

Aodhán smiled. "Everything. It has everything to do with it."

Baxter returned to his own corner, leaving Aodhán alone with his thoughts.

I am stronger than I know.

Yet he couldn't shake the feeling that he was vastly underprepared for what the cultists would throw at him.

He was a special case. Unlike the others, his vendetta against the cultists was personal. He wasn't just a spirit to harvest, but a target. Whatever calamity loomed, it would affect him more than others.

Sighing, he closed his eyes and envisioned a storm, letting the roar of thunder and the crackle of lightning fill his mind as he tamed willpower, regenerating all that he had lost in the previous level.

Dread Tempering made the process twice as fast, but Aodhán doubted it would still be enough. He had burned nearly half his willpower pool in the last level. And if this looming calamity was anything like the last…

No, don't think about that, he whispered to himself. Focus on the storm.

The roar of thunder filled his mind once again, and this time Aodhán basked in it, letting the sound overwhelm his consciousness as if he stood within a true storm. Chaos stirred around him, eager to surge into his spirit should he require it, and Aodhán smiled.

He didn't pull in the chaos, though. He didn't need it yet.

Instead, he let the vision of the storm calm his mind. Thunder boomed, lightning crackled, winds howled, and yet his mind had never felt more still.

With each measured breath, the roar of thunder became more distant, the howls of wind more muted, and the crackle of lightning dimmed, until his consciousness felt like the surface of a still pond—completely undisturbed and unmoving.

That peace, however, only lasted a few minutes.

In the stillness, forgotten things began to surface. Half-baked thoughts. Incomplete ideas. Unfinished skills. All the things he'd pushed to the back of his mind. One by one, they bubbled upward.

The first was the Medusozoa's limbs skill he'd promised himself to create once he obtained phase lightning.

Aodhán visualized the half-finished skill with a light frown, certain it wouldn't take him an hour to complete. But after staring at it for a moment, he realized how useless the skill had become. Anything it would allow, he could easily recreate with a dozen carefully controlled rope constructs.

It pained him to dismiss the idea, but Aodhán couldn't justify wasting energy and willpower on a skill he could replicate with {Lightning Manipulation} alone.

What he needed wasn't more skills. He needed more power.

Another idea surfaced—a task to create a speed skill—and Aodhán paused. Before his evolution to the advanced class, he would have started immediately, but after the evolution of {Elemental Lightning}, his need for speed had dropped drastically.

Teleportation was far faster than running, no matter the speed.

The skill wasn't useless, though. In situations where space was turbulent, it could be invaluable.

Nevertheless, it wasn't a priority at the moment. Aodhán shelved it once again.

A third idea surfaced, and this time Aodhán smiled.

The task was simple: make miracle rain an actual skill.

For a long time now, Aodhán had wanted to do this. But for some reason, he'd kept postponing it. There was no doubt that the process required to create the golden rain was long and extensive. Too time-consuming to be used in a rush, each step needing his input and direction.

Put simply, the process of creation was inefficient.

But having created the miracle rain so many times before, Aodhán knew exactly what he needed to do to transform it into a true skill. The solution was simple—let the system handle the complex energy fusion and channeling that usually drained his focus and time.

He'd have to control the power consumption carefully, though. He couldn't let the skill operate on a standard energy cap. It would only become redundant that way. The skill needed an unlimited energy cap so he could increase its power simply by channeling more energy, quintessence, chaos, and willpower into it.

Already, Aodhán could see how energy-intensive it would be—requiring not just willpower and quintessence, but also his bloodline power and perhaps a spark of lightning to reach full strength.

No wonder the system hadn't simply converted the ability into a skill. It was too complex.

But Aodhán knew he could do it. He had the experience. He simply had to create it, and it shouldn't take more than an hour.

Smiling to himself, Aodhán decided to create the skill immediately.

Aside from energy, there were two skills that contributed greatly to the creation of the miracle rain: {Storm Manipulation} and {Eye of the Storm}. To create this new skill, he would merge their functions—not to eliminate them, but to birth something new.

Something powerful.

Chaos churned around him in response, the force of his spirit causing reality to twist subtly. His mind became his forge as he replayed every process required for the miracle rain. His imagination surged as he implemented safeguards.

Any skill without checks was a bomb waiting to happen, and the system wouldn't approve of it. He remembered Andrew trying to create a rare-ranked plant in the evolved class and the countless battles with the system due to unreasonable restrictions.

Knowing his spirit's power, Aodhán implemented only a few safety checks, letting his imagination run as wild as it wished.

Imagination was the limiter of skill creation, they said. But now Aodhán understood that the true limiter was the spirit. A weak spirit would ultimately birth a weak skill, no matter how wild a person's imagination ran.

But a powerful spirit…

Aodhán smiled. Even he didn't quite know how powerful his spirit was.

Time passed in intense focus—his mind racing and heart thumping with excitement as he slowly completed the skill.

It took him exactly an hour and ten minutes.

The moment a notification pinged in his mind, Aodhán rushed to open it, a smile blooming on his face.

Congratulations! You have created a new skill.

[Tempest of Transformation]: Grants the ability to create rain/droplets infused with transformation upon designated targets. Each droplet carries a concentrated mix of quintessence, chaos, and the mental clarity granted by {Eye of the Storm} to temporarily enhance the perception, speed, and mental clarity of the target or targets.

NB: Skill effectiveness is limited by Perfect Imbuement Strand capacity. Current maximum: 19 strands. Targets exceeding 19 tiers above user's level will remain unaffected by transformation effects.

Aodhán's smile widened into a grin, but before he could celebrate, Brennon jolted awake with a scream.

"What is it?" Aodhán asked, eyes wide as he turned to face the man.

Brennon looked at them all with terrified eyes. "I just had a very terrible dream, and I don't ever want to sleep again."
