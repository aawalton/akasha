Back in Sunderland, I thought that I was used to attention. I mean, I was never the prettiest lad around, but I was pretty jacked, and I made a living getting up in the ring and proving that I knew how to box. And, if I were to say so myself, I could make a left uppercut look damn fine. Hence, I wasn’t unaccustomed to having spectators. On some days, especially those leading up to the local boxing tournaments where I’d be sparring with some of the better amateurs, we might even swing a crowd.

I’d never seen attention like this.

According to Two-Steps-Left, the Telvan Outpost was pretty small. There were slightly less than a thousand Hiscariat here – no children, no elderly, all volunteers who had some sort of talent that’d help the Outpost as a whole, or young offspring from important families who could level and grow into lovely young chthulhoid masters.

They had all turned up for the drama, with the morning sun as lovely backdrop as we entered the multicolored Palace.

Oh, they weren’t all physically present. The Palace had been limited to only, along with Two-Steps-Left, Mind-Aloft and a number of others, mostly guards and one ridiculously tall woman who was responsible for internal security.

But it wasn’t like physical distance stopped the Hiscariat! When we’d first entered the Outpost nearly a day ago, the mental chatter had been a constant susurration, small waves lapping at the inner ear.

Now, we were at the center of the storm. It was hard to make out the mental speech of Drift-Ashore against the backdrop of constant attention, even with my Mental Resistance skill working overtime. I thanked the Lord that we had Steve to count on for buffs, or it would be even harder. Even with Mental Control turned up to the max, Mental Resistance was growing like crazy, though.

Thankfully, the debuff from the Lord O’ Freedom’s drugs had run out by now. It was a nasty thing, too, an eight-hour debuff, that lowered our mental stats by eight. That wasn’t the reason we’d waited until the morning, though. It was because our clever plan needed some extra time to work. Discovering and toughing out the debuff was just a lucky side effect.

“I thank you all for arriving to this session at such short notice. We will not waste any time, and I shall start by laying out what singular persons have been informed of so far. One of our Holders, Fortune-Unfolds, has been accused by an outsider of several crimes. Misrepresenting the Hiscariat. Needlessly endangering the lives of others. Other crimes yet to be articulated.” The Holders all stood in a circle, just like last time. The only difference being, this time, we three humans stood in the center, across from Fortune-Unfolds. Very nice and theatrical. She finished. “Before we unveil the truth, we will hear the exact accusations.”

We let Victoria speak here, mentally for extra oomph. She’d spent all night practicing her Mental Communication for this exact point, and I had to hand it to her. She did impressively. Her voice rang out into the ring of Holders, loud and clear, but not too loud like last time. “When we left the Palace last time, we were approached by Fortune-Unfolds. He proposed that we should cut through all the unnecessary bureaucracy and time-wasting, and face the Ceaseless head on.”

“Hah!” Set-Alight’s barked laughter filled the Palace. “Didn’t think he had that in him! That’s what I keep saying. So, what went wrong?”

We’d discussed how to handle this thing. Obviously, I would’ve preferred to just drag the bastard into the room and force him to own up to being a mind-breaking dick. But apparently, that was… problematic. Not least, because the Hiscariat believed that they were civilized people, hence, public torture was frowned upon. So, we’d be getting creative. We were also going to lie a lot.

Victoria started off with the absolute truth. “Nothing went wrong on our end. He promised us that if we managed to hold off the Ceaseless forces for twelve hours, he, and a group of promised Crafters would be able to erect defenses that should be able to hold them off forever. According to him, the point was that, once we’d succeeded, we’d be able to go back to the rest of the Holders, point to what we’d managed, and then everybody else would fall in line.”

“That is not how our decision process works.” Drift-Ashore intoned. “But I must admit, had you managed to do so, we would have been hard pressed not to listen.”

Victoria nodded, entirely at ease with facing off against the group of powerful people.

I found her attitude damn impressive. I doubted I’d be able to hold up half as well.

She pointed in the direction of the Ceaseless territory. “That was why we agreed. It sounded like a fair agreement. And we did our part. We went out there, we faced off against the Ceaseless, and we beat them back, time and time again.”

So far, so good. Now, was when the script would have to change.

Victoria’s face was a picture of ill-contained wrath as she snarled at Fortune-Unfolds, who was taking in everything with a distant smirk. “We upheld our side of the bargain. But when we returned through the canyon, there were no defenses. No traps. No Crafters or Mages. He didn’t even have the balls to face us ourselves. He’d just slinked back to the Outpost and gone to sleep.”

Care-of-Ways exploded. “Preposterous! There is no way you would have survived twelve hours against the collective power of the Ceaseless. Even if you could, your levels would have increased drastically higher.”

“You calling me a liar?” Victoria lied flawlessly. “Every Scout, every Fighter and Spitter, we took on and flung them back! We risked our lives a dozen times over, and survived. You can go right out there and find the corpses!”

For the first time, Fortune-Unfolds spoke up. His voice appeared in my head, filled with warmth and a side note of ironic humor. He hid any confusion or fear at the fact that we hadn’t mentioned any mental manipulation.

I wanted to sock him so bad.

“Not everything in her tale is a lie, I’ll admit. Yet, this detail is beyond ridiculous. Everyone here knows that, should you try to hold off the Ceaseless for long enough, you will attract their Minds and other specialized types. As Care-of-Ways states, you would not have survived against them.”

A rising wave of chatter in my head confirmed that, indeed, everybody knew and agreed.

I spoke up. “You might know all about the Ceaseless. I sure don’t. But I’ll bet I’ve been more up close and personal with the creepy things than anybody here. There were no Minds yesterday. Also, Steve and I have clashed with them once before, and there weren’t any Minds or other weird types back then either. Perhaps that’s just how they react to you guys, because you’re sworn enemies or something.” I shrugged. “That’s not the point, though. The point is that you fucked us over, and you won’t even own up to it.”

Another wave appeared, more confused. First, a few of the Holders repeated my words mentally, and then came the expected confusion, disbelief, doubt. Could what I said really be true?

Drift-Ashore tried to get the occasion back on track. “Fortune-Unfolds. You have heard the accusations placed against you. Please expand on your own position.”

That was my cue. There were a few possible ways this could go. If we caught him in an outright lie, we could hook him. If somebody else knew what his real deal was and outed him, we’d have everything cut out for us. However, Fortune-Unfolds seemed like the type who knew which risks to take and how to weather a stormy conversation.

Mirror of All was the first talent I picked with my new class. And I’d picked it above the other taunt I’d been offered in my defensive talent tree for a couple of reasons. Just looking at the description of the effect, it wasn’t too impressive. ‘…enhances a single fixed emotion in selected targets around you.’ Of course, the ‘selected’ part was lovely. This wasn’t an AoE talent unless I wanted it to be. It offered me options, gave me the option to, say, throw the healers in a party into a frenzy and leave the fighters untouched. There was also the fact that it allowed me to pit my Fortitude attribute against the Mental Power of a target, making sure it’d almost always work – unless they were weirdly immune, like the Ceaseless. The final boon of the talent, however, was what I’d learned over repeated use. It wasn’t like a light switch. I didn’t have to turn the anger of my target up to a hundred straight away. Rather, I could keep the effect and stamina use low, and still get the desired outcome.

Best of all? If I used it against someone who didn’t know what was happening, they wouldn’t even know it was active. We’d tested that part yesterday. I activated the talent at the minimum cost, trying to fan the flames of whatever anger Fortune-Unfolds was harboring. Then I acted as innocent as I could.

The effect was instant, but he hid it well. A slight flaring of the elongated, thin nostrils. A narrowing of the eyes. “Did I talk to these humans? Of course I did. I wanted to learn more about those the Hiscariat might eventually call allies. Did I mention an idea to them? Yes, I did. But it was to be no more than that. An idea. Something we might explore later. Somebody making hasty decisions like this would not become a Holder.”

“So, you did suggest that plan of action?” Drift-Ashore asked.

“I did. I would have implemented it too, if I had the backing of a few esteemed Holders. Set-Alight, Duty-Abounds and Guide-of-Cold might have provided the proper power to back up this sort of attempt, ensured it was viable. I would never have flung somebody like this up against the Ceaseless Minds without backup – even if they now prove that they deserve worse, with their lies.”

Oh yeah. It was absolutely working. For now, I didn’t turn it up. Just watched the show.

Care-of-Ways added her opinion. “I, for one, am now going to speak against any sort of alliance with the humans. If this is the level of trustworthiness we can count on, I would not want to embroil ourselves in their specific brand of barbarism.”

Drift-Ashore did not like that. A tentacle rose and fell like a gavel. “Care-of-Ways, we are currently debating guilt, and your personal opinions are both premature and unwanted. It is not like you to ignore protocol.”

I frowned and turned to Steve. He raised an eyebrow, frowning. Hoping that his facial expressions hinted at what I figured, I expanded the reach of Mirror of All toward Care-of-Ways. She was acting rather weird. First she exploded without warning. Now she ignored the ‘proper’ way of things?

“I am merely expressing what a lot of us believe.” Care-of-Ways didn’t back down. “But I will acquiesce and wait for the final decision.”

“Did you join their group in the wilds?” Drift-Ashore asked.

I carefully didn’t lean forward. This would be a lovely place for him to lie, because there’d definitely be people who’d watched him coming and going. Just for kicks, I turned Mirror of All up a notch. On Fortune-Unfolds and Care-of-Ways, because fuck ‘em.

“I did. I wanted to show them what we were up against, where we might act.” His tentacles shrugged. “We braved the western canyon, showing them what we were up against. They, admittedly, performed admirably against the regular Fighters and Scouts attacking us at intervals. While they do not seem to be accustomed to working together, their frontliner is durable and holds up well against their direct attacks. As such, I had no issue leaving them to increase their levels and explore the lands, while I returned to the Outpost and my regular duties.”

“That was all?” Drift-Ashore asked. “You did not ask them to attack the Ceaseless, or promise any assistance?”

“Why would I? These humans looked tough, but by no means tough enough to take on larger numbers of the Ceaseless.”

“Why do you think they’re lying?” Care-of-Ways snapped. “What can they possibly gain from this? From lying about somebody as upstanding as you? Why are we even listening to them, when we should consider if we need to go against the humans in the Torn Divide?”

That took me aback. Her affront did seem real, as did her confusion. Obviously, my talent had increased her anger. But egging on a fight against humanity? That didn’t sound like the ‘bleeding-hearted pacifist’ Fortune-Unfolds had called her. Rather… it sounded like a real attempt at warmongering?

“Care-of-Ways. You will comport yourself. We are investigating the facts of the matter against serious allegations. This is unbecoming of somebody of your station.” Drift-Ashore laid down the law.

“While I agree that Care-of-Ways should tone down her rhetoric some, I cannot but agree with her.” Fortune-Unfolds said. “You are taking their entire attack on their word. Yet, you have yet to show that you are taking mine seriously.”

My eyes met with the stoic Hiscariat responsible for the internal security. Her eyes were currently wide in surprise. “All right. Let’s be serious for a second.” I faced him dead on. “You were the one who approached us at our shelter, right?”

He nodded in agreement. “I was. I’m not trying to deny that.”

Besides, he’d probably been seen approaching our shelter by a ton of Hiscariat, and lying about that would backfire spectacularly. “And how’d you reach out to us in the first place? How did you speak to us?”

For a split second, his smile was a grimace. He hid it quickly, though. “I addressed you verbally instead of mentally. I realized that this was what your kind are accustomed to, and wanted to put you all at ease.”

“Uh huh.” I didn’t bother to hide my sarcasm. “It definitely wasn’t because you wanted to hide what you were up to.” Another bump to Mirror of All.

The wave of mental chatter proved that I’d hit upon a weak spot there. The snobbish Hiscariats really didn’t like on of their own lowering themselves to talking with their mouths, like a pleb!

“Liam Berg. We will handle the questioning here.” Drift-Ashore said. She spread her tentacles wide. “We will let the Holders ask questions now.”

Duty-Abounds, the stuffy bloke in charge of training, was the first to speak up. “For as long as our Outpost has existed, you have remained consistently pro-active. You have not pressed, like Set-Alight, for precipitous action, but argued to prepare properly before leaping into action. As such, your stated reasoning for your approaching them is logical. But why would they lie?”

The Hiscariat’s sneer spoke volumes. “I do not know humans, or pretend to understand their logic. Why do any barbarians do what they do? Perhaps they want war between our peoples. Perhaps they did expect to be able to take on the Ceaseless and want to hide their hurt feelings at a humiliating loss.”

“I think it’s my turn.” Set-Alight glared at Fortune-Unfolds with undisguised glee. “Now, I’ve come to you with suggestions for action maybe a dozen times. How many times have you acted on the same day?” Clearly, there was no love lost between the two. Yet another thing he’d lied about.

“I cannot remember-“

“None. Not a single time. You will always refer to protocol, to proper address and preparation. You crave for recognition of your vaunted intelligence crew, even if it slows down any plans by weeks. Yet, now, you suddenly act on the same day? It does not sound like you. What changed?”

I amped up Mirror of All even more. Half strength now. I could see the tendons in his neck stand out, as if he wanted to punch somebody – preferably me. “What changed? You shoot down every reasonable suggestion I’ve come up with for months and disparage any intelligence I have as either insufficient or unnecessary. Then you have the audacity to challenge me, when I try to prove my worth and the worth of my work elsewhere? You should treat me better. It is not worthy of a Holder.”

For a few seconds, it looked like Set-Alight was going to rise to the challenge. Then he snorted. “I might have come on a bit strong.”

I looked to the tall, looming figure in her black robe. Her eyes had narrowed to slits, and she nodded at me.

With a deep breath, I took two steps forward and met Fortune-Unfolds’ eyes. “We lied. We didn’t actually hold out for twelve hours against the Ceaseless.”

His gaze was defensive, now. I could see drops of sweat on his forehead. He looked caught between elation, naked fury, and fear. His mental voice betrayed none of it. “About time you admitted it, you scum!”

One more increase to Mirror of All. “The real truth is that we were told by Fortune-Unfolds to go out into the canyon and defend for twelve hours while he prepared defenses. Only, we didn’t make it further than about three hours – because that is when we managed to defeat the mind control he put on us, which would have been our deaths.”

If I thought the Hiscariat had been noisy earlier, I realized my mistake now. The waves of mental chatter turned into a crescendo of mental shouts, threatening to turn my brain into mush. I could feel as Mental Resistance improved by a point, turning the volume down the tiniest bit.

Fortune-Unfolds was no less noisy. His mental voice rose above them all, as he took a step forward and theatrically raised an accusing tentacle at me. “Liar! You will recant that accusation!”

I probably should. Really, it was not like me to accuse somebody when-

A dark shape stepped unobtrusively up behind him. In one flowing movement the black-robed stern-faced woman raised her tentacles and, with a soft click, snapped the lock of a bronze chain in place around his neck. Her mental voice arose and made the sea of mental chatter die in a second. The sudden cessation of noise made my brain feel like it was floating in a sea of silence. “The accusation is true. Today, I have personally witnessed how, on three separateoccasions, Fortune-Unfolds has used a manipulative talent against people inside the Palace.” Her tentacle rose and fell, like a judge’s gavel, pointing out, in succession, Care-of-Ways, Set-Alight and myself.

My mind was a bed of white noise as her verdict struggled against what I knew deep within to be true. That I should withdraw my accusation and-

The woman’s mental voice was dead cold. “As the prime Hiscariat responsible for the protection of the collective from internal threats, I claim the right to expedite judgment. Any Holders against must speak up right now.”

“This isn’t right!” Care of Ways sobbed. “It can’t be true!”

Set-Alight snarled. “I’ve never liked him. But this is beneath us. You-“

The rest of the circle looked like they were suffering from shock. Duty-Abounds looked like he’d eaten something foul. Drift-Ashore’s motherly façade had cracked, replaced by a pallid, blank expression. One short male actually turned around and vomited.

The tentacles of the black-robed woman slithered around Fortune-Unfolds’ neck. Then they jerked, once. The crack reverberated through the entire room.

My mind became my own again, as the manipulative talent ceased to function. My anger replaced my concern and desire to apologize.

And the entirety of the fucking Hiscariat population went batshit insane.
