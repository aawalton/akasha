Four hooded figures approached the city gate from the alleys. Wearing a hood wasn’t illegal in the Shores of Glory—if anything, it was a regular occurrence.

“Are you sure you’ll be okay?” Chloe asked, downcast.

Bob smiled. “Don’t worry, my friend. These are the vibes. Wherever they lead me, that will be the right place.”

“Thank you, Bob. We really appreciate this,” Asim said. Chloe sighed. Behind them towered Torga Ba, arms crossed lightly in a relaxed but still intimidating pose.

“It’s part of the job,” the cultist said. “Besides, my brothers and sisters have already left. I was the last one in the city. The Academy wasn’t for us—we’ll search our own fate in the wild.”

“Are you joining the invasion?” Chloe asked.

“Perhaps, perhaps not. There are seven days left—so much time. When the event draws near, the Tower will lead us where it must.”

“I appreciate this, Bob. You make my job tremendously easier. If you are ever in need, let me know, and I will come,” Torga said.

Bob smiled. He hugged them all, then raised his hood and made for the gate. Nobody stopped him. He slipped out and disappeared into a fate unknown.

“Do you think he’ll be okay?” Asim asked.

“With all the money he’s carrying, he better be,” Chloe said.

“I have to commend you, Chloe. You found a good partner,” Torga said.

“Say that again,” Asim said bitterly.

“We owe him one. So, that’s our biggest loose end tied up. Most of the buyers only knew Bob, and the inspector will never find him outside the city,” Chloe said.

“Didn’t you sell directly to a few people before Bob joined?” Asim asked.

“Yeah, but they aren’t showing off like the idiots in the tournament. Strength is secondary to wizards, anyway. The inspector can’t find them unless he goes around asking everyone at the Academy. At worst, they’ll ask who was friends with the Cult of the Broken Compass and come to me, but I have my story. I’ll fool them.”

“They won’t ask that. You’re safe. It’s Asim I’m worried about,” Torga said.

“Me?”

“You have rented two private labs at one of the only places on the floor with the equipment to produce Muscle Sap. If the inspector is serious about his job, he’ll eventually ask at the Witch Coven.”

“We’re tight-knit. They won’t rat me out. Besides, I got the second lab in the name of a friend. To all curious eyes, it’s just two labs out of hundreds—why would they look deeper?”

“Because you’re a drafter renting a lab at a hundred aura stones per month,” Torga said.

“It’s not as rare as you imagine.”

“What about that friend who rented the second lab for you?”

“She doesn’t know the details, so she’ll stand up to questioning. She wouldn’t betray me.”

“That’s what they always say.”

Asim frowned. “Nobody will get to her, anyway. What about your people? Your scouts?”

“What about them?”

“You had three scouts watching our surroundings when we met with the brothers. They must know what happened. What if they rat us out?”

Torga’s form suddenly loomed larger. “My men would never rat us out,” he said.

“You’ve known them for a week.”

“We share some experiences. They would never betray me, and I would never betray them. Understood?”

The two men stared at each other for a moment before Chloe stepped in-between. “There are no other loose ends. The only question mark is Gary’s clanspeople, but the inspector must have questioned them already. Since we’re here and not in jail, they were a dead end.”

“Cleaning up was such a mess, but I think we did it,” Asim said, sighing sharply. “So now we’re clear, right?”

Chloe smiled. “Right. We’re clear.”

“They aren’t clear,” Senior Inspector Whitefang muttered.

“Excuse me, sir?”

“I said, they aren’t clear.”

His assistant, a blonde woman with piercing blue eyes, frowned. She studied the board on which they’d pinned pictures of all the people of interest and all pieces of evidence they’d managed to collect so far. Red threads connected the pins, forming a not-so-complex web.

“Is this a test, sir?” she finally asked. “We’ve already questioned everyone, and their stories matched. All evidence points to the four brothers. We just have to find them.”

“That’s exactly the problem, Delphine. Their stories match.”

“Sir?”

“When someone questions you unexpectedly, Delphine, you lie. Most do. Even if you’re innocent, you’ll instinctively try to hide any minor wrong-doings, embarrassing stories, or just unrelated details. But here, I asked three disciples, and none of them lied to me. Not once. They were so forthright it was suspicious.”

“Maybe they were just honest, sir,” the assistant tried.

“Maybe, but my gut says there’s something off about this.”

Her face turned sour. “This sucks, sir. If we could just ask them again, push harder, we’d know for sure.”

“Clan law exists for a reason, Delphine.”

“The reason in this case is to make our job harder. Innocent people have nothing to hide.”

Whitefang shook his head. “Everyone has a right to privacy. Even if someone is innocent, they don’t want to feel like they’re under scrutiny all the time. People need to know they’re allowed to have secrets. It’s crucial to maintain trust and loyalty. Respecting people’s privacy while still catching criminals is the delicate balance we’re called to uphold—even if it means that, sometimes, wrongdoers get away.”

“Yes, sir. You’re right. I apologize.”

“Don’t take it to heart,” he said, blowing a puff of thick white smoke into the air. A cigar rested between his fingers, while his boots lay on the desk of his temporary office. He sat in silence for some time, his thoughts known only to himself, before he turned to his assistant. “What’s our next order of business?” he asked.

She flipped through a notebook. “The Head of this branch’s Enforcement Hall has asked to meet you, sir. His name is Deng Sui, a Branch Elder from the Sui clan. He says he might be able to help.”

“Is he good at his job?”

Delphine flipped through her notebook again. “No.”

Whitefang sighed and took his boots off the desk. “Fine. Let’s go see him.”

***

Elder Sui’s residence was a lavish manor. Statues and paintings decorated the space, while the garden held neatly trimmed bushes. Soft music played everywhere. Senior Inspector Whitefang had to hide his disapproval as thinly-clad servants served him tea on an ornamental silver teapot that probably cost as much as a mortal’s house.

“With all due respect, Senior Inspector, letting him off the hook with a simple informal questioning doesn’t look good,” Elder Sui said, sitting cross-legged on a red velvet pillow. His brown robes, the ones that Elders wore to signify humility, cascaded over his back. Whitefang sipped on his tea to hide his snarl.

“Clan law exists for a reason,” he said, for the second time that day.

“Of course. As enforcer to enforcer, I completely understand and respect the law. However, I believe Gary Steel’s circumstances warrant a deeper investigation. He’s risen far too quickly. It would be unreasonable without tremendous resources.”

“Many talented disciples have secrets, Hall Head. If we questioned them about it, the clan would be left without geniuses.”

“Don’t you think it’s too coincidental? He rises at the same time that a Muscle Sap distribution ring has taken deep roots in our branch. He admits to knowing the masterminds but refuses to share more information. How is this the profile of an innocent person? To get to the bottom of this, Gary Steel must at least be questioned!”

Whitefang put his teapot down. His expression was blank. “We must treat our fellow clan members with respect, Hall Head. Even junior disciples. Gary Steel gave me a tip of his own good will. I would not ask him to betray his friends. I’m only interested in the big fish, anyway—buyers are not my business.”

“They’re my business, Senior Inspector. This is my branch, and minor infractions are my duty to settle. How about I question him? You can just wait somewhere hidden and tell me when he lies. This way, even if he files a complaint later, it won’t contain your name.”

Whitefang’s brows fell further. “This is not a matter of face, Hall Head. It’s about completing the mission entrusted to us by the clan. Forgive me for saying this, but I believe your relationship with Gary Steel clouds your judgement.”

Instead of getting angry, Elder Sui chuckled. “I don’t question the people I dislike, Senior Inspector. I take my position seriously, just like you. I truly believe Gary Steel holds the missing piece to complete this investigation. He’s just too suspicious.”

“I’m of the opposite opinion. Gary Steel is a dead-end. The conditions for interrogating disciples are even stricter for me than they are for you, but I still questioned him once, and he said nothing that would warrant a deeper investigation. He even gave me the impression on honest man. Unless new evidence comes to light, clan law makes him strictly untouchable to both of us.”

As Whitefang departed, Elder Sui stood at the gates of his manor to see him off. He wore a fake smile. Unless new evidence comes to light… Gary Steel is guilty, Whitefang. I don’t know what for, but he’s guilty. I’ll find you your evidence.

“Brochor,” he said. The bushes to his side parted to reveal a slim, stone-faced man with a casually intimidating aura. He hadn’t been hiding, of course, only being discreet.

“Sir,” he said.

“Dig deeper into Gary Steel. Find everything you can about him. His friends, his family, any connections in the tower, any strange experiences. Ask everyone, turn every stone. He has absolutely used illegal Muscle Sap, and if we can prove it or cast reasonable suspicion, Duang won’t even need to fight. He can rest before facing Feng Shen and seize glory for our family.”

The man nodded and slipped away. Elder Sui was left staring down the mountain’s height, at the Shores of Glory in the distance.

You shouldn’t have messed with me, Colorful. I have your opening now. The next move is mine—and even clan dogs don’t get a second chance.
