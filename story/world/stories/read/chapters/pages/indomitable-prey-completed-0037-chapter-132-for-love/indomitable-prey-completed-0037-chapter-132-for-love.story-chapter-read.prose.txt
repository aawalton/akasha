[Fandar POV]

Fandar watched the woman he had loved for a decade face down a cavalry charge of peak 2nd tier mercenaries, led by one of the most dangerous men in the Sunset Kingdoms. Alone.

He clenched his fists, squeezing the hilt of the great bow he had carved from a Corrupted Mangrove with his own hands. The wood creaked, though he knew he was in no danger of breaking it. The magic woven through the weapon was enough to elevate him from a moderately powerful fighter to a high-level threat on any battlefield, and it would not yield to mere mortal strength.

Even Vera would struggle to crush his bow, were she of a kind to use one. She’d always preferred a more direct method of death-dealing, and that was obvious to any who watched her now. She had sprinted from the castle like a galloping horse herself, great legs pumping, great shoulders rising and falling. She sped across the muddy field, aiming for the largest horse; the centre of the line. As was ever her way.

Fandar had joined the rebellion after witnessing an ambush gone awry on the road years back. The rebels had tried to take out a convoy of the duke’s tax collectors, but the Crimson Lions had been lying in wait to counter them. The mercenaries had fallen on the rebels with a mad glee, and Fandar still remembered their grins as they sent arrows winging into the confused crowd. He and his brother had been pulling the carriages—the draft horse the duke had requisitioned from them had sprung a limp in the night, so they’d volunteered for the job—and the red-cloaked bastards had given no care for their lives despite their nominal positions working for the duke at the time.

He remembered when Vera had charged his way. He’d been so scared. Younger then, and not much of a fighter besides, he’d thought she came to cut his head from his shoulders and drink his blood—there were stories back then in the early days, after all, and it was only later he had learned how much of it was the duke’s propaganda. But no, even as his brother was killed by an arrow to the throat, Vera had knocked him aside and shielded his body with her own. Then she’d sprung up and charged right at the largest knot of the mercenaries with her blade held high. A fox among the hens.

So as he watched Vera pick the biggest, meanest son of a bitch she could find and run right at him, Fandar found himself completely unsurprised. He watched as she dipped then leapt into the air, twisting in a surprisingly graceful move through the air to bring her broad blade around to meet the head of the Crimson Company.

Stupid fucking name, that, but everyone in the Sunsets seemed inclined to call their little mercenary band some variation of crimson. The colour of blood, the colour of the sunrise and the sunset. What was the expression? ‘A red sky at night, shepherd’s delight. A red sky at morning, shepherd’s warning.’ Something like that.

Vera landed some twenty feet beyond the line of cavalry and continued her headlong sprint at the armies beyond. The mercenary captain was twisted in his saddle, looking harried and struggling to remain seated, but Fandar couldn’t see any obvious injury, despite his hawk-like vision. One didn’t become an archer without advanced perception, after all.

As the red-jacketed horses thundered towards the castle, Fandar glanced up at the dark sky above. Did it count as night or morning? He snorted. Fanciful bullshit. It wouldn’t help, but he felt so helpless right now. He knew he could fight, had trained many of the Marchers himself, as his rebels had taken to calling themselves. But when he looked down to the field below and saw the bloody path Vera carved through that initial charge, he knew that she and her companions were cut from a different cloth than his rebels.

Just like that enigmatic old man she had returned to the Marchlands with, who even now was kneeling on the ground beyond the broken remains of the gate. Fandar frowned, distracted from his circling thoughts as he watched the old man punch the earth, cracking cobble and carved stone, before fumbling at something around his neck.

The man pulled out a necklace, and then, with a reverent kiss and a murmur that Fandar could not quite catch, he pressed it to the earth, kissing it once more before spreading his arms wide and looking to the heavens with closed eyes. Lilting, lyrical chants rose from his mouth, and Fandar assumed he was casting some sort of spell. It had better be a powerful one, considering the growing speed of the cavalry charge heading their way.

Vera’s other companions—the four warriors that had confronted the duke before Vera had arrived—were still spread below holding the gate, but they hadn’t moved for an eternity.

Vera had told him to keep his rebels alive, and that she and hers would handle the majority of the killing. He hadn’t liked it, but he did see the sense in it.

This night was decisive, but in many ways it was also simply the first of many battles. That had been hard to remember when in the thick of it—he hadn’t expected to face both the Sultanate and the Sunset Court on the field, and a large part of him had despaired when they had emerged from the forest, though the arrival of the old monster, and Vera’s ascension to the 4th tier, had bolstered his courage somewhat.

But someone would need to be here to support the country afterwards. Someone had to patrol the roads, clear the Marshes, protect the mineworkers and enforce order. A country couldn’t survive on the edge of one blade, after all. So Vera had asked him to keep his men safe for the true work that would come after the battle. Let her and her companions sacrifice in the moment so that there would be someone left to continue their shared vision over the years to come.

He had faith in her, as did any that had lived and fought through the early years of the rebellion, and especially so those who had joined towards the end of them as he had. But as he watched the kneeling man thrust his arms out and beseech the heavens, he began to doubt. He looked from the unbroken line of horse flesh and armour thundering towards them over the muddy plain to the unmoving forms of the barbarian warriors at the ruined wall, and he cursed.

He turned and signalled to a dozen of his Marchers to abandon the wall to reinforce the gate. He cringed internally as he sent them to their likely deaths, but there was no point in holding the walls if the enemy breached the courtyard, and he couldn’t risk the entire country on his faith in the woman he loved, much as he might wish to.

If he were wishing for things, Fandar was honest enough to admit that he wished he could be out there with her, watching her back and supporting her in her mad struggle. As much as he wanted to let his heart rule him though, he was a leader first, and a lover a distant second. He had his duty, and it was to his Marchers and the people of the Marchlands entire. He could not afford to throw his life away for love, much as it seemed the only right thing to do at this moment.

He sighed, conjuring an arrow that he poured all of his frustration and longing into. Yearning Through The Heart was a powerful skill, and he sighted down the length of the spectral, translucent arrow towards the mercenary captain still reeling in his saddle and turning to cast a lance towards Vera’s back as she ran past the charging horses.

He breathed slowly, evenly, letting emotion leave his own heart as he steadied his chest. A moment to confirm… and then release. An eternity of longing, a fervent wish in the night, and then his arrow found its mark, sinking up to the fletching in the chest of his foe.

The captain of the Crimson Company died in his saddle.

What Vera had started all those years ago—her and her little band of revolutionaries—Fandar had kept alive. Like the flame of love that burned within his own breast, so too had he nurtured a small band of fighters even after she had left.

Fitting then, that he should be the one to kill the Fractal Edge. What Vera started, Fandar would finish. So may it ever be.
