Asher felt the ground underneath his feet lurch ever so slightly, indicating Sarah had to have gotten her whirlpool defense up and running down beneath Poltar. But even if that protected the center of the city, he had no doubt that the sea serpents would do whatever they could to rush past the warriors in the water and start attacking the city from the outside in. Turning toward Sefta, he shook Eupher’s corpse at her.

“We’re out of time! Hurry!”

“Outside! I can’t shift in here!” she ordered, pointing at the door with her harpoon. Her eager smile was nowhere to be seen, and a foreign look of concern was plastered all over her face in response to the horrifying screeches radiating from the depths of the lake. Asher knew that Sefta and her family cared about the people of their city far more than most nobles did, and she no doubt knew exactly how much damage a few dozen sea serpents attacking the city at the same time could cause.

Not wasting time, Asher threw the door open and ran outside. Already there were people up and down the floating street cracking open windows and poking their heads out of doors, looking around in confusion as they tried to figure out what was going on. The serpents’ screeching had been loud enough to wake the dead, and while one or two people here and there grabbed weapons and leapt into the lake, the majority were content to stand around and wait for someone to explain what was going on.

Most of these people aren’t warriors. If the sea serpents get past Sarah and Samantha…

Asher tried not to imagine the death and destruction that would befall Poltar if the lich got their way. Thankfully, Sefta ran right after him, thrusting her harpoon at the ground.

“Toss it there on the edge and step back!”

“You’re still naked!” Asher shouted even as he followed her directions. He wasn’t a prude by any means and didn’t really care about Sefta’s dress choices, but they were about to go up against a lich from the Council of Death. Armor would probably be a pretty wise thing to put on.

“Not for long!” Sefta shouted back, sucking in a deep breath before shoving her harpoon into the ground and leaping straight into the water. Asher knew from having watched her in the past that her transformation was more than a little disturbing, so he didn’t make any effort to peer over the edge of the city and watch her skill do its thing. Barely a few seconds later, a massive, scaled head rose out of the water, its eyes filled with a cunning intelligence as they stared down at him.

“See? Who needs clothes?” Sefta asked, her deep voice vibrating in his chest.

“Focus!” Asher said, kicking the corpse closer to her. “Where else do you smell blood like this?”

Rolling her giant eyes, which really was a sight to see, the serpent lowered her head toward Eupher’s corpse. A shiver ran all the way down Sefta’s new body, before she turned and shook her head with enough force to crush a building if she got any closer.

“Ugh, that is the most disgusting thing I’ve ever smelled. I can feel my sea serpent instincts trying to get me to swim away from the source of that blood as fast as I can.”

“Well we need to do the opposite,” Asher reminded her before grabbing the pieces of Eupher’s corpse and tossing them into the water. Based on what Sefta had just said, with any luck the horrid blood would make one or two of the serpents think twice about getting near Poltar even if they did make it past the whirlpool Sarah made. “Do you know where it is?”

“Oh yeah, I could probably smell that thing from the next city over now that I got a whiff of it,” Sefta nodded, before shuddering a second time. “Grab my harpoon and hop on. Even if this thing wasn’t attacking my city, I want to kill it just for assaulting my nose so badly.”

Asher hadn’t been expecting the invitation, but he took the offer in stride, leaping off of Poltar and landing on Sefta’s back. Her scales made holding on easy enough, and his assassin class helped him keep his footing when she turned and began swimming away from Poltar at an incredible speed. Sefta seemed competent enough with her new form that she could keep her head and neck relatively stable, while the rest of her body undulated behind her, propelling them through the water and toward another portion of the lake.

“So, anything I should know about the lich?” Sefta asked as the water sprayed up around them. “Beyond the fact that they hate Poltar for some reason?”

“Unfortunately we’re going into this one pretty blind,” he admitted. “We know they can enrage and sort of control monsters somehow, but we have no idea what else they are capable of. Ideally it doesn’t matter and we get the drop on it, but we’re rarely that lucky.”

“No kidding. Sea serpents aren’t exactly known for their stealth either. I don’t think we’re sneaking up on anybody like this. Still, how in the world did you go from hunting assassins to liches? Last we chatted you told me you were going around taking down the Assassin's Guild!”

“It’s a bit of a long story… What about you? We haven’t seen you since that whole ordeal with Audrey and the greater demon. We’ve even been staying at the palace, but we never saw you.”

“I wasn’t at the capital for too long, and I didn’t stay at the palace anyway. My father and uncle were busy with official noble crap, and that place is way too stuffy. I joined up with a couple of caravans and just wandered for a while to try and hide from my mother. Eventually I felt bad and missed the lake, so I came home. Not the most thrilling of stories, I know.”

“I’m just glad to see you’re okay,” Asher admitted. “After Audrey died and you took off…”

Asher got to experience the unique sensation of feeling a sea serpent snort underneath his feet, which nearly sent him tumbling into the water.

“What, you thought I was traumatized or something? Please. It’s not like greater demons are a common thing. I’ll probably go my entire life without seeing another one.”

“I guess that’s a decent way of looking at it,” Asher said, glancing over his shoulder and admiring just how quickly Poltar had faded into the distance. Sefta was moving fast. Almost as fast as Samantha could fly as far as he could tell. Wherever the lich was hiding, it wouldn’t be long now before they reached it.

“By the way, I had a question I’ve been meaning to ask you. Are you The Reaper? Everyone was talking about him in the capital, and when they mentioned his Spatial element, my mind went back to those weird skills of yours you used while we sparred with one another.”

“Yeah, that’s me,” Asher admitted as he pulled out Crescent and Nail. His void dagger was on his hip and ready to go if needed, and he prepared himself for anything. “Sorry for not telling you. It was sort of a secret for a while there.”

“Eh, not like I care about names or anything like that, I just thought it was funny that you became a greater power before I did. That’s what I’ve been shooting for ever since I first learned about them.”

“I’m sure you’ll be right behind me,” he said as he looked down at the giant sea serpent swimming beneath him. Before running into The Giant, Sefta had easily been the largest combatant he’d ever seen. She was still in the Stage of Bleed last they’d spoken, but she was near enough to the Stage of Power that she’d already entered the Crossroads; a sort-of in-between stage where one’s body began being influenced by one of their elements. It was why she had serrated teeth like a sea serpent.

Asher still had no idea what his own trip through the Crossroads was going to look like, and he didn’t get the chance to think too hard on it.

“Head’s up, we’re nearly there,” Sefta warned him, snapping him out of his inner musings.

“I’d love your help in the fight, but I have to get the killing hit on the lich,” Asher warned. “This isn’t a status or pride thing, I have a skill that will let me kill the lich for good.”

“I figured,” Sefta snorted. “Quarter mile ahead, coming up fast.”

Asher spotted the lich a split second later, and he narrowed his eyes as he took in his target. Though it would be more appropriate to say he spotted where the lich was hiding rather than the lich themselves, as there appeared to be a large chunk of water simply missing from the surface of the lake. Like a crater in the earth, a giant hole had been scooped out of the top of the lake, and floating in the direct center of it with a glowing staff held high over her head, was the lich.

The lich wore a billowing, pale-blue dress, and turquoise fires danced within her eye sockets as her entire body turned to look at them. She hung there in the center of the watery crater as if suspended by wires, and her staff continued glowing a dangerous red, pulsing every few seconds.

“The Reaper,” she said, her harsh, rasping voice somehow reaching their ears despite the loud sounds of water crashing from the lake into the crater. “So I drew the short straw, did I?”

“That thing can talk?” Sefta asked. Pulling to a stop on the edge of the watery crater, she frowned down at the lich. “It looks just as dead as that corpse you made me smell! Which I’m still pissed about, for the record!”

“Suck it up, we’ve got more important crap to deal with!” Asher hissed, kicking her massive neck for all the good it would do him. He watched the lich like a hawk, trying not to get frustrated at how Identify didn’t give him anything to work with. The fact that liches were innately immune to that skill annoyed him to no end, and he ground his teeth as he realized he was going to have to fight the lich blind.

While she’d been mildly useful for helping him find the lich, once again, Sefta wasn’t going to be of any help in terms of combat. Just like when she’d abandoned them to take on the greater demon, she would probably turn around and run away now too. Asher’s blood rose at the thought of her fleeing with her tail tucked between her legs again, but before he could yell at her, Sefta shouted first.

“Did you just kick me?!” Sefta growled, rearing her head up out of the water and nearly sending Asher flying. If he hadn't caught himself on one of her scales with a quick use of Distortion, he would have landed in the water. “Buzz off, I’ll finish off the lich myself! This is my city it’s attacking!”

“Well it’s my prey that’s attacking!” Asher shouted back, elongating Crescent and moving to stab the blade into Sefta to shut her up. Right before his strike connected, he paused, some quiet part of him screaming at him in the back of his mind.

Screaming that he’d encountered something like this before.

Asher’s breath caught as he realized what was going on almost too late. Before Sefta could try to buck him off again, he focused on Complete Resilience, switching his current immunity to one he’d encountered in the past while hunting down the Finger who once lorded over Dormaul.

Immunity to Rage.

Instantly, the foreign anger that had washed over him without warning vanished, and Asher let out a sigh of relief when his mind cleared up. Of course the lich had a Rage element. It fit perfectly into how she was throwing the sea serpents into a blood-thirsty frenzy and driving them out of their usual zone to attack Poltar. Now that he was free, he just needed to snap Sefta out of it somehow.

A task easier said than done as Sefta twisted around and lunged at him with her jaws wide open.
