Ryland had to admit that he was surprised that Harker had survived the blast. The rupture from the overwhelming amount of magic he had been ingesting through those mana-draining Inscriptions had been tremendous. Even now, the geyser of mana from that blast was towering into the clouded sky, turning the air into a storm around it.

But the man himself had shot out of that impressive column of power to come shooting straight at Ryland.

“Here I come—”

His own manic words, inflected with that insane grin of his, were interrupted when he reached Ryland even before he had finished speaking.

The impact was strong enough to nearly rival the magical geyser rising atop the old temple. Harker had arrived with his fist wreathed in the kind of destructive mana that only Manifestations could assume, condensed to such a degree that the briefest impact would set off a volcanic detonation. There was something almost familiar about that magic.

Ryland had [Eternal Bastion] active, which meant he wasn’t directly affected, despite the severe volume of mana behind Harker’s blow. The same went for the rest of the mages he had brought along with him from the first blast site.

That didn’t mean they went entirely unaffected, however.

While Ryland wasn’t hurt by Harker’s blow, [Eternal Bastion] didn’t stop everything. For all that the magical output from Harker’s fist didn’t break or even crack Ryland’s barrier, it couldn’t stop space itself from rupturing all around him, nor could it entirely nullify the momentum that blow imparted. Which was why Ryland found his feet dragging backwards for a few handspans, even as he remained unhurt.

Meanwhile, his allies had been sent flying. The Guardian himself let loose an undignified scream as the impact’s shockwave sent him sailing just like everyone else.

“Impressive,” Harker said, the boom of his voice eradicating the dust thrown up by his own punch. “After that stunt you just pulled, it’s no surprise you’ve remained unaffected by that blow. It seems I’ll just have to hit harder.”

He was glowing just as he had been the moment he had exploded. It wasn’t quite clear to Ryland just how he was containing his power now. He seemed to be in control. Almost perfectly.

“If I may ask,” Ryland said. “How did you survive?”

“Oh, right!” Harker’s grin widened. “You thought that little trick would be enough to end me, didn’t you? That you’d put an end to my ambitions by simply overloading my manasoul with your own. Well, you were wrong. I knew the fault with the Inscriptions. They all thought me fools, considered me a pawn, but I knew. I have the books. I have the ancestry.”

He was starting to ramble a bit now, enough to make Ryland wonder if he had made a mistake in asking a question. But the information he parsed from Harker’s tirade was certainly interesting.

“—and I will not be disrespected again!” the Goodman was saying. “The lineage of the Founders will not be devalued anymore!”

The Founders. A name Ryland hadn’t heard in a while, but not one he was unfamiliar with. The very first generation of the Rhellians had called themselves as such, and their descendants had gone on to claim positions of great power and influence. Except, it turned out, for this man. This Goodman of the Recreator’s Cult, Harker.

“Harker…” Ryland said. His eyes widened a little. “Ah, the Harker family. That’s your lineage. How intriguing.”

“Intriguing?” Harker clenched his fists.

“It makes sense you’re involved with the temple now. They were quite close to me.” Ryland frowned. “But they would never have stooped to such a terrible choice, no matter how trying the times became.”

“Don’t stand there and cast your judgment with impunity. You don’t know anything about Rhel! You’ve been gone from our world, from the homes and lives you yourself raised up, for centuries. And now you think to decry the steps we’ve been forced to take to ensure our freedom from tyranny?”

He spat at Ryland’s feet. Even that little globule of spit sizzled where it struck, digging a hole through the earth.

“This isn’t mere judgment,” Ryland said. “You—”

“I’m not interested in your proselytizing! [Hurricane Impact].”

The familiarity came again when Harker unleashed the new spell. Harker. Right. That family had specialized in combat magic, and not just the typical destructive elemental ones hurling fireballs and calling down lightning bolts.

These spells were martial, where the sorcery was used to empower physical violence with the boundless potency that mana could provide.

As such, Harker’s fists really did fly in like a hurricane. A whirlwind of blistering blows pummelled in with relentless ferocity, each strike strong enough to shatter a mountain going by how the entire area around Ryland was shredded to dust. A crater big enough to hold a lake quickly grew from the series of relentless impacts.

All while Ryland himself remained entirely unaffected.

Well, almost entirely. The blows couldn’t get past his barrier yet again, but just like that first meteoric spell, these ones were ever so slightly pushing him back with every hit.

“Enough,” Ryland said. “[Moon Eater].”

If Ryland was being honest, then the revelation that this Harker was one of the Harkers he had known all those centuries back had affected his outlook on the battle. No longer was he standing against a faceless enemy merely bent on achieving their goals regardless of the damage he caused.

Harker was still that to a great extent, of course, but the added context of his lineage put a lot of things in perspective.

His determination to succeed for the sake of his freedom, the way he was determined to uphold the ideals of his lineage, the strength to which he had adapted to the power he had found himself attaining. It all spoke of a driven man.

A misguided man that Ryland would still need to put down, but one whom Ryland could understand.

“This will never hold me,” Harker cried out. “Never!”

And he was right. As always, [Moon Eater] had called out the white tendrils with the black thorns, and though they had sunk into Harker’s flesh, they failed to find purchase. They shouldn’t have. The originator of those tendrils tended to eat away at mana made real, including in the cases of Manifestations.

But in this case, Harker was literally tearing them off, forcing himself free from their grasp. It left gaping wounds on his body that had volatile mana spewing out of him in spades. But it didn’t matter.

Not when that violent eruption of mana far behind them was being drawn back in by the Inscriptions, which sent the captured power straight to their target—Goodman Harker.

With a roar, he resumed his attack.

“[Pugilist Meteor]!” he screamed.

Mana convulsed around his arm as he pulled it back. Then punched forward, thrusting his arm from a distance. Though Harker’s lineage boasted great martial prowess, it didn’t mean they were entirely incapable of ranged combat, though even that took the form of martial arts more often than not.

Such as in the current case, where the punches from a distance were unleashing literal meters of mana coming straight for Ryland.

The world was filled with a riot of burning daylight as the impacts reached Ryland faster than lightning bolts. He bore the brunt of them all, occasionally slapping away ones he started to feel a little too annoyed by.

That occurred when he realized that, once again, he was being pushed back by the sheer space-breaking power of those impacts even if he remained unharmed overall.

Because pushing him back seemed to be Harker’s main goal.

“[Concave Nadir],” Ryland incanted.

The concave shield popping up in front of Ryland drew all those meteoric blasts of power to a singular location, one that began compressing his opponent’s mana to a singular location. It grew so bright that the light was rivalling the sun far, far overhead.

Ryland had thought he had successfully stopped Harker’s plan. The magical meteors were no longer affecting him, and he hadn’t reached the temple yet.

But it turned out he had moved far enough.

The Inscriptions came alive far underground. This time, Ryland could actually feel their forceful tug on his manasoul, dragging his bright and hot mana into the ground.

“Ha!” Harker crowed. “There! I’ve got you now. I’ve captured you. You have nowhere to run.”

He was bulging with growing power now. The beam still shooting into the sky, the power he had already gathered from the other mages, and now, all the more magic he was leeching off Ryland to add to his own were all compressing upon his body.

And somehow, he was managing to control it all.

“If you’re wondering how I’m holding all this incredible energy,” Harker said with prideful glee. “Then the trick turned out to be terribly simple. I just need to spend the mana fast enough before it can overwhelm me. [Infinite]!”

Ryland’s eyebrows rose at the spell. All the wounds on Harker now disappeared at the rapid healing, his form turning brighter than the concentration of his meteoric blasts.

With a shake of his head, Ryland simply unleashed the magic [Concave Nadir] had been holding all this while. The compressed blasts gathered into one spot were now able to unleash their volatile magic, storming out in a beam that ate up space as it shot towards Harker.

“[Burst Deflection]!”

The martial spell had Harker punching the blast straight at Ryland, redirecting the power with ease. And Ryland just took it all.

An apocalyptic explosion later, when it felt as though the world itself was detonating, Ryland found himself floating away from a town-sized void that had appeared. His barrier was once again entirely unaffected. But his intention had been foiled too. While the blast had broken the Inscriptions that had caught him, he had been forced back to the first trapping spell.

Where that tremendous bursting magic was shooting off into the sky.

A thousand hooks sank into Ryland’s manasoul, immediately tugging on the endless mana that he was channelling. Harker screamed again, this time with pure, unadulterated joy as he turned into a living star, outshining everything in existence for a few, incandescent moments.

“[Imprisoned Land],” Ryland incanted.

Harker howled in annoyance as he was dragged to the earth. A rift had opened across his location, bringing up the Realm with the strongest gravitational pull in all of the cosmos.

Ryland wasn’t about to lie. The tug had grown terribly severe. In the blitz of mana all around him, in the chaotic storm of magic, he could no longer keep as secure a control over his manasoul as he had been doing before.

But by pulling his opponent down to the ground, where Harker was now trapped just as Ryland was, things were on an even footing. Somewhat.

“This will not… hold me,” Harker growled.

He wasn’t wrong. Even now, he was forcing himself upright despite the tremendous levels of gravity trying to lock him to the ground. Ryland had no trouble seeing what was going on. Harker was quite literally burning mana like an extra source of energy against the severe gravity.

“[Riptide of End Times]!” he shouted.

Power began flaring off him like a thousand fireworks shooting off in every which direction. Despite appearances, he was actually gathering and condensing even more mana, raising the potency of the draw he had inflicted upon Ryland. Energy gushed from Ryland like river rapids, flooding Harker’s soul and stuffing him with a broken amount of magic.

“Prepare for the end, Marionys Ryland,” he said. “The end of the Recreator is nigh!”

“[Absolute Null],” Ryland incanted.

He was aware it might not be enough. For even as he outright cancelled the magic that Harker was drawing on, more just came out. Ryland’s own magic was endless, after all. From Harker’s perspective, there was an infinite source of magic to tap into.

Things became chaotic. Harker was ready to unleash everything he had gathered, [Absolute Null] having only slowed him down for a moment.

Ryland steadied himself. It was time to end this.

Harker’s sparking bolts of magic now coalesced into an array of spiderlike limbs pulling him forward faster and faster with every heartbeat, until his speed was so great that he had turned into a radiant blur. One that was firing at Ryland like he had been launched from a cannon.

For the first time since this madness had started, Ryland finally unleashed his own manasoul. The restraints were gone. The chains holding him back had all been snapped fully.

The world turned into a field of Conceptual Glyphs, several of them crowning his head and wrists while forming a long cape running off his back. He might have just added an entire planet’s worth of mana now flowing through his opponent, as evidenced by the sudden starlike illumination turning the entire region incandescent, but Ryland wasn’t worried.

He was ending things, once and for all.

“[Endless Evolution],” he incanted. “[Soul Shift].”

Power rippled through Ryland’s form. His manasoul became one with his body. At the moment before impact between him and Harker, Ryland’s arm shifted, turning into a limb of metal fused with flesh and blood, pure magic running through its artificial veins.

Then, as Ryland raised his hand, Harker crashed into his palm.

The impact wiped out everything behind Ryland. He wasn’t quite cognizant of what exactly there was, but as far as he was aware, the temple to the Recreator was located in a rather isolated region on Rhel, where no one really lived for leagues upon leagues all around. A good thing, because the destruction unleashed by that blow was of an inconceivable scale.

When the storm of light and power faded enough to start seeing again, Ryland found himself standing at the very edge of a crater whose depths he couldn’t even see normally. It stretched out for leagues behind him, the far edge disappearing into the distance.

“How?” Harker was still pushing against Ryland’s palm, still burning with enough power to light up a whole city by himself. The air was rupturing around him, the ground slowly melting under the pressure of his presence. “How did you stop something of this magnitude? No, it doesn’t matter. This has gone on long enough.”

“Indeed,” Ryland said, interjecting before Harker could continue his spiel. “It has.”

“What are you—”

“[Everlife Spectre].”

Ryland’s arm changed yet again. This time, spectral bones and glowing, silvery muscles turned it into a limb that could latch onto anything. Including Manifestations like the one he was battling.

“Let go!” the man screamed. “Let go, let go, let go!”

But try as he might, however much he struggled, he couldn’t break free from Ryland’s implacable grip on his very being. A thousand twisted tendrils of coruscating magic leaped off his glimmering form and struck Ryland, but they had no effect whatsoever. Shocking power exploded around Ryland, but they couldn’t dislodge his hold by even a fingerbreadth.

Harker was well and truly captured.

“That Inscription you’ve been using,” Ryland said. “It’s nothing compared to the true drain of magic that you can suffer.”

Harker didn’t appear to hear Ryland. Not that it mattered. The Goodman wouldn’t really understand anything said to him, wouldn’t really believe any of it, until he actually experienced it first-hand.

And Ryland could have shown him the difference in their levels. It would be easy to prove, with overwhelming might, that there was no point in Harker trying to drain Ryland’s power. The gulf between them could never be crossed with such mundane tactics, no matter how potent the leeching Inscription appeared to be.

But there was a different way of proving Ryland’s unreachable superiority.

“[Reality Scar],” he incanted.

Ryland slammed Harker back, which fractured space, sending out shards of reality flying every which way and digging into the Goodman’s Manifestation form. His body, such that it was, had now been forced into the vaguely man-shaped crack in reality, imperceptibly dark nothingness bleeding around the edges.

As soon as he had been stuffed in, unreality started its work

The limitless expanse of nothingness created a negative potential, vacuuming away every iota of magic that Harker possessed. The Goodman screamed, a pull a thousand times worse than that of a black hole now ripping into his manasoul.

“What—what is this?” Harker cried out.

“You can’t simply absorb pure power without knowing how best to use it first,” Ryland said. “Power without control and knowledge is useless. That’s all I’m proving to you.”

“Stop this insanity!”

Harker’s cries took on a pitch that wasn’t even audible anymore in the frequencies that Ryland normally didn’t let himself hear. Darkness and light warred against one another, a furious onslaught that turned the world into a kaleidoscope of horrific levels of mana.

Where Harker had been the one channelling the awesome power before, he had now become part of the conduit, a living, breathing mana channel for unreality itself to slake its thirst.

Ryland didn’t even need to hold the man there any longer, so he stepped back and allowed [Everlife Spectre] to dissipate. This was done. Within moments, Harker would be reduced to something even worse than his original state. Harmless, but not dead. A state from which information could be extracted with—

A rent cracked open nearby. A familiar one. And from within its depths, a jagged, glowing purple hand emerged and shot straight at Harker. It grabbed the captured man and pulled him free from the crack in unreality.

For a second, Ryland blinked. Then, as Harker disappeared into the rent, Ryland decided to follow.
