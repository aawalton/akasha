Nasha made it three steps past the edge of the mist before falling to her knees.

She pitched forward and caught herself on one elbow, barely keeping Dawn from sliding off her shoulder and barely keeping her own face from hitting the ground.

“Nasha!” Gwen rushed to her side, magic already gathering between her trembling fingers. “Lie down. I’ll heal—no, purify first. Or pain? Nasha, can you hear me?”

“I brought her back,” Nasha breathed.

Gwen swallowed hard, then lifted Dawn away and laid her beside them. The girl’s half-ethereal body barely seemed real, flickering faintly against the ruined stone.

Before Gwen could decide which spell to cast, a deep growl rolled through the sanctuary from within the hissing steam.

All of them looked up.

A black speck tore through the haze above the sanctuary.

Hevestra rose into view, barely marked with any injury. Its outline cut against the pale sky, whole and steady. Then it lifted one arm.

Day died, and night rose to take its place.

Darkness swallowed the light from the sanctuary in a single breath. A vast cloud of demonic miasma unfurled overhead, thickening until it covered the entire sanctuary and blocked out the visible sky below.

“I was going to save your bodies to drain,” Hevestra said. “But I’ll just kill all of you besides the boy.”

Hevestra flicked its clawed finger down. The blanket of miasma fell with it, plunging toward them in one crushing sheet, meant to smother the sanctuary in a single strike.

“Oh, that does not look good,” Gwen whispered.

Dusk stepped forward, forcing his weakened spiritual aura above them. Gwen pulled at her cloak, summoning the last remnants of its mana barrier, but both defenses looked painfully thin beneath the descending darkness.

But just as the miasma was about to press down on them, Hevestra sensed a pulse of mana.

A wellspring of power building.

Hevestra turned. “You…”

A massive torrent of wind erupted across the platform, ripping the miasma into a violent funnel and blasting it outward over the edges of the sanctuary. The remaining steam vanished with it, revealing the shattered pillars, broken stone, and the ruin left across Selene’s holy ground.

Hevestra braced itself against the gale, snarling. “You pests just never die.”

Gwen looked toward the collapsed pillars.

“Veronica!”

Veronica stood there, forcing herself upright. Her robes were torn, singed, and corroded at the edges. Blood streaked her chin, and ash clung to her face. Her Exalted Form still burned around her, but it looked far more desperate than before. Her hair still drifted near her head, though small strands hung limp around her face. The violet glow in her eyes was sporadic, flickering once before brightening violently.

It was anything but stable and smooth.

[A critical fracture has been detected in your primary mana core.]

She took several deep breaths, already knowing the risk she had to take. However, she couldn’t die just yet. If things came down to it, she would cripple herself and force her mana out if needed. After all, her knowledge was important, not necessarily her power.

She could always make someone else a 10th-tier.

[[Once you leave your exalted form, mana usage will be blocked. I will also be unable to assist you, and all perks will be disabled as I tend to mana core repair. Continuing to stress your mana cores will increase the repair duration and may induce permanent damage.][Current estimated time for repairs: 13 days.]

“Fuck,” Veronica groaned.

Thirteen days without mana.

That was much longer than she had expected.

Maybe she should have been grateful she was still low-tier. If she had fractured her mana core later, after it had grown stronger and more resilient, repair could have taken years. Decades, maybe.

Her gaze cut back to Nasha and the others.

They had pulled Dawn out of the portal. Somehow. Veronica still didn’t know why they had bothered. The girl was practically dead, with half of her body already missing.

But maybe that was the point.

Maybe Dusk needed answers badly enough to drag a ghost back from the edge.

Veronica couldn’t blame him for that. Perhaps she’d do that too, if she had been searching for someone for hundreds of years.

Her eyes shifted back to Hevestra.

This wasn’t the first time in this fight that she cursed the fact she couldn’t fly yet.

Nevertheless, she was the last thing left to stop the demon.

Veronica formed a 4th-tier spell circle, concentrating her mana.

The moment the demon saw it, it moved to strike.

“Fine! I’ll get rid of you first!” Hevestra shouted.

Across the sanctuary, Nasha and Gwen watched Hevestra crash against Veronica again.

And this time, the difference was obvious.

Veronica’s posture had changed. Her shoulders sagged. Her stance dragged lower than before, as if every breath had weight hooked through her ribs. Even the glow around her looked wrong, less like a powerful archmage, and more like a starved flame.

“Dusk!” Gwen snapped, tearing her gaze away. She looked down at him. “That tear. Give it to us now!”

Dusk sat with Dawn in his arms.

Trembling.

Gwen’s expression tightened, but she put a hand on his shoulder anyway.

“We need your help,” she said. “Maybe we can still find a way to save her after this. There has to be someone in the world who understands spirits. An expert or a healer. There’s always something.”

Her fingers tightened once.

“But we have to survive first.”

Dusk looked down at Dawn’s flickering face.

“…Save her?” he repeated.

“If you can give us the Tear of Selene, we can defeat that demon. We can all leave here together,” Nasha said softly, looking at him.

She wasn’t exactly sure if that was true, but it wouldn’t matter if it wasn’t. They would all die regardless.

Dusk stayed silent for a long moment. Then he placed his hand on top of Dawn’s head.

Light flashed between his palm and her fading body.

Nasha and Gwen froze.

Dawn began to unravel from the bottom up, her half-ethereal form peeling into threads of pale light. The glow climbed toward Dusk’s hand, strand by strand, until there was nothing left of her but the brightness gathered in his palm.

Dusk turned his hand over.

Something blue rested there. A shard, half-broken. Shaped almost like…

“A tear,” Gwen whispered, eyes wide. “Dawn was Selene’s tear?”

Nasha’s brow tightened. “But that’s only half. Unless…”

He nodded.

“Wait,” Gwen said, shaking her head, trying to understand. “If you become the other half, does that mean you’ll…”

There was only silence.

“Dusk. Are you sure about this?” Nasha asked, forcing herself to sit up.

“Hey, be careful,” Gwen said, afraid she would hurt herself more.

“I’m fine,” Nasha replied. Instead, she looked over to Dusk. “Is there a way we can bring you back?”

He shook his head slowly. “Maybe… maybe Selene can. But I’m not sure.”

Both Nasha and Gwen furrowed their brows, looking at him.

Gwen gently grabbed his wrist. “We promise we’ll do our best to find Selene. To save not just your sister, but you too.”

Nasha looked at him. “…Yeah. We’ll do our best.”

Dusk stared down at the shard of Selene’s tear. Dawn had already passed away, but her energy was still enough to form the tear. Perhaps the only way to save her was by putting his trust in humans again.

He gripped the shard tightly as his body began to glow. Starting from his feet, his spirit began to unravel, forming the second half of the shard.

Dusk thought back to the memories he had gathered in the human world. How greedy people could be. How much he had suffered. How long he had waited.

And then he remembered the trip he had taken into the valley.

The beef jerky he’d gotten to eat. The bath he was given. And the warmth he had felt inside the wagon, surrounded by all of them.

“I still don’t trust humans,” Dusk said finally, just as half his body turned to light. “But… I don’t know how else to save Dawn. And I don’t want that demon to keep hurting her. So I’m going to trust you all just one last time. To save her, and to find Selene.”

Gwen and Nasha looked at him and nodded, faces determined.

And at last, Dusk smiled.

Then he used the words he had only learned several days ago.

“Thank you.”

As the words left his lips, his body turned to light, forming the other shattered half with Dawn.

The Tears of Selene had never been multiple. Never been two.

Only one, split into Dawn and Dusk.

Nasha reached out, catching the tear in her palm before it fell to the ground.

“We… we’ll be able to save him, right?” Gwen asked, voice low.

Nasha gripped the tear in her hand. “Yeah. We’ll try our best.”

“Stop getting in my way!” Hevestra shouted.

It tried to blur past Veronica, toward the others. Something had happened, but it was hard for Veronica to tell what exactly.

Nevertheless, she refused to meet its demands.

Twin lightning bolts shot down from the skies, converging into one as they exploded in front of the demon and forced it back.

Veronica grit her teeth, arms trembling from the overexertion of her mana cores.

[Current estimated time for repairs: 19 days.]

She ignored it.

Whatever Nasha and the others had done, it had put fear in the demon. That mattered more than the warning. Maybe they had found a way to kill it. Maybe they had found something worse.

Despite Veronica’s attack, Hevestra burst free from the explosion, still intent on barreling toward them.

“That power doesn’t belong to you!” it shouted.

Power?

The word barely registered before Veronica summoned a single spear of light and launched it forward. It stabbed straight into Hevestra’s side. The demon grunted and snapped its gaze toward her, eyes sharp with hatred, but its attention slid back toward the others almost immediately.

“Shit,” Veronica muttered.

She forced her enhanced body perk to activate, preparing to run and throw herself between Hevestra and the others, when something shot across the sanctuary and struck the demon directly in the face.

The impact smashed Hevestra back down into the cracked stone.

“What?” Veronica’s body jolted as she stopped mid-sprint.

The figure landed lightly on the ground, huffing for breath. That was when Veronica realized who it was.

“Nasha?” she whispered.

She focused harder, getting a clearer look at the rogue.

Nasha was in little more than her cropped undershirt, tattered pants riddled with rips and tears, and a single remaining shinguard. Black burns were etched across her body, from her arms to her midriff and even around her calf. Her breathing was rough, and yet she seemed to be overflowing with energy.

Energy…

The same energy Dusk had been emitting.

Veronica glanced back toward Gwen.

Both Dusk and Dawn were missing. Nowhere to be seen.

Veronica furrowed her brow.

What exactly had happened?

Hevestra rose from the rubble once more, cackling. “Now it all makes sense…” it rasped. “How, even after searching for hundreds of years, I never could find Selene’s tears.”

It stood straight, flexing its clawed fingers while staring down at Nasha. “It turns out to have been those brats. Two halves of a missing whole.” It clenched its fingers. “To think I’ve been siphoning their energy this entire time, not realizing that it would have been better to have them both… how regrettable.”

Veronica’s gaze fixed onto the demon. She could understand the gist of what was going on. Whatever had happened, Nasha had become the turning point in this battle.

Nasha slowly raised her hand.

In her palm, clutched between bloodied fingers, was a tear-shaped crystal. It was small enough to fit in her grip, clear and blue, with two lights twisting inside. One was pale gold. The other was deeper, like twilight caught beneath glass.

Selene’s Tear.

Hevestra blurred toward her.

Nasha reacted immediately. It wasn’t perfect, and it sure as hell wasn’t clean. Her body moved too fast for her to keep up with it, power exploding through her legs as she vanished from where she stood. Hevestra’s claws struck empty air, cleaving through the space where she had been, only for Nasha to appear beside it a heartbeat later and twist into a kick.

The blow slammed into Hevestra’s ribs, searing a wound of spiritual light across the demon’s body.

Hevestra flew sideways, rolling hard before smashing into the broken mass of a collapsed pillar. Nasha landed back down in a crouch, one hand braced against the stone.

Immediately, she coughed.

Blood splattered the ground as she covered her mouth.

“Nasha!” Gwen shouted.

Gwen was still using her magic to keep Nasha moving. Selene’s Tear gave her the burst of power she needed to stand, but it did nothing for the rest of her injuries or the exhaustion dragging through her body.

Nasha only raised a palm toward Gwen, signaling that she was fine.

Gwen clearly didn’t believe her.

Nasha sucked in a sharp breath and tried to relax her body. The Tear’s power was overwhelming. Worse, it seemed to be clashing with the demonic miasma still festering inside her, burning away the corruption little by little. Unfortunately, it didn’t exactly feel like a massage.

Dozens of Hevestras suddenly appeared in the air, all glaring down at her.

“You think holding that will change anything?” they said at once. “It’s only delaying your torture!”

All at once, the Hevestras dove toward Nasha, claws extended, intending to rip her apart.

Nasha didn’t panic. Instead, she dashed past the first wave and aimed for one near the back. She lunged forward, throwing a punch straight toward Hevestra’s face. Her fist collided with the demon’s mana barrier and shattered it completely before striking the real body beneath.

The crack rang across the sanctuary as Hevestra was knocked back down toward the ground, the remaining illusions vanishing around it.

Hevestra struck hard enough to fracture the sanctuary floor beneath him.

Nasha landed several meters away, one hand pressed against her stomach. Her vision swam. For a moment, all she could see was white and black, the pale glow of the tear and the darkness crawling around the edges of her sight.

She hadn’t known which one was real.

The tear had pulled her in a specific direction, and she had simply trusted it. Perhaps it was reacting to the remnants of Dusk and Dawn’s energy still trapped inside the demon.

When Nasha looked down at her hand, she saw that it was trembling. Even the pain of punching through a barrier had faded.

That probably wasn’t a good sign.

“Can’t use this for much longer…” she murmured.

Nasha glanced behind herself and saw Veronica’s spell circle forming in front of her. Although Nasha wasn’t a mage, and spell circles were all foreign to her, this one was familiar. Veronica had shown it off before.

She knew exactly what spell it was.

“I see,” Nasha said quietly.

Hevestra rose from the rubble again, furious. A spiritual scar marked its face in the shape of a human fist.

“Fine…” it said quietly.

Two massive black wings grew from its back.

“If I can’t fight you physically, and that annoying mage in the back keeps blocking my mental corruption, then I’ll just drag you all into the void instead.”

Hevestra clasped its hands together, summoning a massive door behind it. The thing was several meters wide, built from shadows and condensed darkness. As it opened inward, only a deeper black waited inside.

The exact same place where Dawn had been imprisoned.

Large tendrils began to leak from the door like flimsy hands, reaching for anything they could grab.

Nasha’s gaze sharpened as she realized what it was. She couldn’t get caught by that attack. Not again.

Instead…

“This is what you want, right?” Nasha shouted, holding the Tear between her fingers.

She forced more and more spiritual energy out of it, letting the glow swell until it overshadowed everything else in the sanctuary.

Hevestra’s greedy eyes locked onto it immediately.

“Then…” Nasha snuffed the energy and clenched the Tear in her fist. “Go and take it!”

She hurled it across the sanctuary, straight off the platform’s edge.

Hevestra’s eyes widened. It immediately broke away from its gate and blurred through the air, too focused on the tear to see anything else. Its clawed hand snapped around the object, finally closing around Selene’s Tear.

But then it realized what she had thrown.

“You…!” it shouted in fury.

In its clawed hand was not Selene’s Tear. It was a small black button, cracked down the center, still faintly glowing with stolen spiritual residue.

The doll’s eye.

Hevestra turned. “I’m going to—!” Its words were cut off as another wellspring of energy caught its attention.

No more was it the power of Selene’s Tear.

No—it was the power of mana.

Veronica finished her incantation. “The children of Gungnir, their rays are birthed.”

Behind her palm, four brightly lit wings shone. The fifth was dim, but by no means was it dark. By pushing her mana core past its limit, she was able to replicate her fourth armoury spell, achieving a greater effect than what she’d shown in the valley.

It was a spell near eighty percent of its power.

[Current estimated time for repairs: 37 days.][Two mana rings have been damaged and must be reformed.][Mana cores have sustained an injury. Your current tier will be downgraded once your exalted form ends.][Warning. You are reaching your body’s limit. Further exertion will result in permanent injuries or death.]

However… the toll was a heavy one. But if it meant survival—then that’s all that mattered to her. She could regain her power, even if it would be harder.

Suddenly, before she unleashed her spell, a chime echoed in her mind.

[New quest assigned (optional): Defeat the demon Hevestra, but avoid destroying its head.]

She frowned, but adjusted her spell at the last moment. Veronica’s spell circle, nearly two meters in diameter, cracked apart. Each shard became a compressed spear of light.

Ten.

Twenty.

Forty spears of light shot across the sanctuary.

Hevestra’s eyes widened. For the first time, it felt fear.

Whatever attack that mage had just cast, it was powerful. Almost too powerful.

Hevestra formed a mana barrier using all the mana it had managed to gather. Then it reinforced its body with aura. As for its spiritual power—

It didn’t have enough time to reach the last defensive measure.

The first two spears broke against its barrier. The third and fourth shattered the barrier itself. The fifth and sixth pierced straight through Hevestra’s torso. The seventh punched through its left shoulder. The eighth and ninth tore through its shadowed tail.

The tenth pierced its neck, severing the head.

The remaining spears decimated the demon’s body, each one sending a scatter of shadows and demonic energy dispersing.

The thirtieth spear pierced nothing but air and instead detonated where Hevestra had stood.

The rest of the spears followed suit.

It was not enough to destroy the Valley of Silence. Not like Dawn had—not even close.

But the series of explosions that ensued was the second largest the Valley of Silence had ever experienced.
