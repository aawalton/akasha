“So, you are truly free from the government now?” Aiven asked, leaning forward. He was sitting across from Marnie in the lavish lounge area of his suite.

“Wow... this is so soft,” Marnie breathed, completely ignoring him. She wasn't just sitting; she was excitedly pressing her palms into the velvet cushions and bouncing with the energy of a child. “It’s been decades since I last sat on a piece of high-quality—wait, is this cloud-silk? It feels like I’m floating!”

Virelle, who was hovering near the decorative fern with her arms crossed, let out a sharp sigh that vibrated with annoyance. “Answer Master’s question, you soot-creature.”

Aiven winced. “Virelle, that’s a bit harsh,” he muttered. He looked at Marnie, noticing the faint bruises on her wrists where the suppression shackles had been. Considering she had just been liberated from whatever interrogation the Aerilis government considered standard procedure, he felt she deserved a moment of tactile comfort.

Marnie finally stopped bouncing and sat down properly, though she still stroked the velvet armrest affectionately. “Should be free,” she grunted, her brass-rimmed goggles clicking as she adjusted them. “I was locked behind a cold stone cell in the central barracks. Then, this morning, a monkey beastfolk in a suit walked in. He handed the guard a scroll with a Vulpine seal, and the next thing I knew, the guard was stuttering apologies and telling me I was free to go.”

Aiven nodded. That matched the description of the Special Acquisitions Unit. It must have been the same spectacled monkey who had stormed the Prancing Paw alongside Vane and Pelka.

“So what are you doing here instead of going back to your greasy abode?” Virelle asked, drifting closer. She looked at Marnie’s oil-smudged apron with a look of clinical distaste, as if afraid the grease might jump onto her skirts.

Marnie’s expression soured slightly. “The monkey told me to stay here. Said that the moment I stepped outside the Vulpine perimeter, the government would probably find a new reason to question me. Said Cyria can’t guarantee my safety if I’m roaming the Industrial District.” She looked at Aiven, her eyes softening. “Said it was part of a deal she made with you.”

“I’m glad you’re okay, Marnie,” Aiven said, his voice thick with relief. “A lot has happened since they barged into your shop. More than I even know how to summarize.”

Over the next hour, Aiven recapped the dizzying sequence of events: the flight to Fangreach, the meeting with Cyria Amberfang, the harrowing journey into the Sunken Fane, and finally, the encounter with Lucien.

Marnie sat in stunned silence, her hand hovering over a gold-flecked tortoise bun she had forgotten to eat. “By my ancestors’ hammers...” she whispered. “In just a few days, you’ve gone from a fugitive clerk to the guy deciding the fate of the world.”

Virelle tilted her head, her violet-magenta eyes glowing with a smug, possessive brilliance. “It is only to be expected. My Master is amazing, after all. A star hidden in a grey coat.”

Aiven rubbed the back of his neck, his face heating up. “I’m not sure if that’s worthy of a compliment. I just wanted to find a way to honor a dream.” He looked at Marnie, his expression turning serious. “Anyways, there are choices to be made. Hard ones.”

“Have you decided?” Marnie asked, leaning in. “The guy... Lucien, was it? His offer sounded pretty sweet on paper.”

“I’ve decided,” Aiven said firmly. “I won’t join him. I don't want a lavish home if it’s just a cage. And I definitely don’t want to serve someone who would kill me the moment I disagree with him. That’s not freedom.”

Aiven looked at his matte obsidian arm. “But not joining him means there will most likely be a fight. A big one. I need to be ready. That’s why I have to use the Loom-Breaker to unseal my mana before he comes for us. Cyria is appointing someone to assist with the ritual—most likely Pelka, since she knows the artifact’s resonance best.”

Virelle drifted down, landing softly on her feet for once. She placed a hand on Aiven’s shoulder, her touch cool and grounding. “If it is a matter of controlling mana flow and the intricacies of the weave, I can also assist. Pelka knows the machine, but I know the ocean.”

Marnie let out a long, low whistle and stood up, patting Aiven on his right arm. “Sometimes, things happen so quickly you don't even have time to process the scent of the smoke. But I’ve got to hand it to you, kid... you’ve stayed cool-headed through all this. Most people would have cracked or sold out by now. I know you’ll make the right choices.”

“Of course he will,” Virelle added, her arrogance returning in a flourish of her translucent sleeves. “My Master is incapable of error when it truly matters.” She then turned her sharp gaze back to Marnie, a playful but dangerous smirk on her lips. “By the way, soot-creature... now that you are aware of the lengths my Master went to for your sake, perhaps you should kneel down in gratitude? He has quite literally freed you from a life of iron bars.”

Aiven immediately stood up, waving his hands frantically. “N-no! Stop! There’s absolutely no need for that!” He looked at Marnie, his eyes filled with sincere apology. “After all, I was partly responsible for your arrest in the first place. I’m just happy the deal worked.”

Marnie chuckled, grabbing the tortoise bun and taking a massive, satisfied bite. “Don't sweat it, kid. I’ll pay you back with better upgrades than the one they had given you.”

Aiven looked at Marnie, who was currently occupied with inspecting the mana-conduits in the ceiling of the suite. "So, Marnie... did Cyria give you your own living quarters here? I assume they have plenty of room here."

Marnie didn't look down from the panel she was poking. "Quarters? Nah. The suit-monkey who brought me up said I should just sleep here. On that big four-poster bed of yours. Said something about optimizing space or whatever corporate talk they use."

Virelle, who had been gracefully drifting toward the refreshment table, froze mid-air. The mana around her feet flared into a sharp, jagged violet.

"Absolutely not!" Virelle shrieked, her voice echoing off the marble walls with a force that made the tea sets rattle. She spun around, her eyes burning with a possessive fire. "I cannot—I will not—share our private quarters with such a disgusting, soot-encrusted presence! This space was supposed to be sacred! It is a sanctuary exclusively for all sorts of use between me and my Master only!"

Aiven felt the heat explode across his face, his social anxiety hitting critical levels, wondering why she had to phrase it that way.

Marnie let out a bark of a laugh, hopping down from her footstool and slapping her greasy palms against her apron. "Relax, Sparky. It was a joke. I’ve got a room right next door. The fox isn't that cheap."

She gave Aiven a wicked, gold-toothed grin and a suggestive wink. "Though, if you two decide to get intense with your sacred activities, keep in mind these walls aren't entirely soundproof. Voices might leak out, so you’d better be prudent. Some of us actually value a good night’s sleep."

Marnie stomped out of the room, her laughter echoing down the hallway as the door hissed shut.

Aiven slumped onto the edge of the bed, burying his face in his hands. "I swear," he muttered to the floor. "I feel I’m the only sane person in this building."

The luxury of the Vulpine HQ was replaced by the functional, vibrating hull of a Vulpine scout-ship. They weren't on the Cinder-Fox this time; Cyria had provided a smaller, faster vessel, stripped of its lounges and banquet halls to make room for rows of humming, crystalline machinery.

They were three hours into a flight toward a remote, uninhabited islet in the far western sector of the archipelago—a place so isolated it wasn't even featured on the standard trade maps.

Pelka was at the center of the cargo hold, her sheep ears twitching in a high-speed blur as she calibrated several artifacts. Unlike her usual archaeological tools, these looked heavy and restrictive, etched with jagged blue runes that hummed with a cold, suppressive energy.

Aiven walked over, "Pelka," he said, gesturing to the machinery. "Those artifacts... are they countermeasures? In case my mana goes out of control during the unsealing?"

Pelka didn't look up, her hands trembling slightly as she adjusted a mana-valve. "Y-yes, Sir Aiven. The Boss was very specific. We're using the Loom-Breaker to force open a seal that was designed to be permanent. The resulting surge will be... well, Noirelle’s data suggests it could be comparable to a localized core-meltdown."

She swallowed hard, her bangs swaying. "These artifacts... they wouldn't be able to reseal your mana, most likely. But they can create a temporary dampening field. It would at least minimize the potential damage to the surrounding island... or buy us enough time."

Virelle, who had been hovering silently behind Aiven like a silver shadow, drifted forward. Her eyes narrowed, the magenta light within them sharpening into a lethal needle-point. "Buy you enough time for what, little snack?"

Pelka froze, her breath hitching. "I... I mean..."

Virelle didn't let her finish. She floated until her face was mere inches from Pelka’s, her hair fanning out like a pre-storm sky. "Is that it? You are anticipating that my Master will be unable to control the tide? That he will self-destruct and erase everything in the vicinity?"

Her voice dropped into a register of cold, absolute fury. "Is that why we are heading to a random rock in the middle of nowhere? These artifacts aren't here to save him. They are here to buy the rest of you enough time to run while you abandon my Master?"

Pelka stuttered, her face turning a ghostly white as she backed into a cooling unit.

Aiven could feel the air in the hold begin to ionize. Virelle was starting to glow, an incandescent lavender light bleeding from her skin that threatened to blow the pressure seals of the airship. She looked ready to seize the controls and take him away, back to the safety of somewhere the world couldn't touch him.

"Virelle."

Aiven reached out, his human hand catching her wrist. The contact was electric—literally. Her mana was so agitated it stung his skin, but he didn't let go.

"Master, let go," Virelle hissed, her eyes watery with a mix of rage and terror for him. "We are leaving. I will not permit these... these cowards to treat you like a disposable battery."

"It’s okay," Aiven said, his voice quiet but unshakable. He looked her in the eye, pulling her gently back toward the floor. "I’ll do my best to prevent that from happening. And if things go south... if I can't hold the tide... I’m trusting you to do something about it. I know you won't run."

Virelle tried to retort, her lip trembling as she searched for a witty, arrogant argument.

"There’s no other way, Virelle," Aiven interrupted. He looked at his obsidian arm, then at his human hand. "This has to be done. For my sake, and for yours too."

In the back of his mind, he saw it again: the image of Lucien pinning Virelle to the rocks with a flick of his wrist, her knees cracking against the stone.

Aiven Roan was finished with being a bystander. He would either become the ocean he was meant to be, or he would go out in a blaze of starlight. But he would never, ever let them touch her again.

"Pelka," Aiven said, turning back to the analyst. "How much longer?"

Pelka checked her gauge with shaking hands. "Twenty minutes until we reach the drop-zone, sir."

"Good," Aiven replied. "Let's get it over with."
