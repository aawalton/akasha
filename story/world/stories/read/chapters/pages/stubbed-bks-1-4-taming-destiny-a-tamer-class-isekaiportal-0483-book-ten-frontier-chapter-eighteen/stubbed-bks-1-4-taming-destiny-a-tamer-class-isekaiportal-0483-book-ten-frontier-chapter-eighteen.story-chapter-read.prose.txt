The fields are a hive of activity when I arrive on air-wings. I felt regretful dismissing Flame Fenrir, but didn’t want to risk him accidentally burning some irreplaceable supplies here. And just as well – even before I land, I see the small patch of swaying wheat stalks, golden and ready to harvest.

I touch down heavily on the ground in my haste and then stride quickly over to Elarion, my eyes glued on the healthy-looking wheat stalks. I barely even spare a glance at the waist-high stalk near it that I recognise as a cutting from the plant monster we killed to reach Artio, Artemis, and Orion in their protective dome.

“My lord,” the people massed around him greet me. I force myself to nod back at them politely, but my eyes quickly turn to gaze at Elarion eagerly.

“You said you’ve done it. Are those…?” I ask, waving my hand at the wheat.

Satisfaction is written on Elarion’s face as he nods.

“Edible, and receptive to enhancement potions.”

“Then you’ve tested on a volunteer already?”

“Three,” Elarion confirms. Then he hesitates. “They ate it only a mark ago, so long term effects are unknown. However, the healers indicate no evidence of Energy poisoning in the last report, and that’s already far better than any test previously. And the indicators the alchemists developed indicate Energy levels that are more elevated than the Moriaxar average, but not so much as to be dangerous even to non-Classers.”

Which possibly means that Elarion is being a little premature in calling the food issue solved, though the signs are good. I hope to goodness he’s right, though – the food issue has underlain every single other problem we’ve encountered in this world so far.

Pulsing Inspect, I carefully observe the halo around the plants. My Inspect isn’t as helpful as I’d hoped it would be – even when the plants were saturated enough in Energy to poison us, they were still haloed in gold. However, I did notice that the halo was unusually rich in colour for an edible plant. Now, haloed in gold as they are, I notice that the halo has dimmed a bit. Good.

The other information that filters back through the Skill is pretty similar to what I’d seen before. However, the plant definitely feels healthier than previous versions.

“How did you do it?” I ask Elarion. I notice that he’s dismissed the other Plant Mages and farmers who had been around him. The farmers have moved over to the field and are beginning to harvest the wheat. The farmers use tools that are rather familiar – it seems that, even between worlds, harvest is the same for mundane people.

“Watch,” he tells me smugly, nodding over to the Plant Mages who are standing around the empty plot of land next to the wheat, their hands held out over it.

Closing my eyes I turn on my magic sight to watch what they’re doing.

I watch the waves of magic – and waves they are. They seem to ripple towards a centre point and then outwards again, until they make contact with what look like knots of magic, and then back again. The back and forth speeds up until it’s rhythmically mesmerising – almost like the pulse of a heart.

Over several minutes, I start seeing differences in the magic left behind after the waves of magic have passed over. The mana concentration in the area where the mages are working is slowly dropping, but in an even, steady manner. And not all the mana is going – the earth-mana is being left alone; it’s a bright green mana that is mostly being cleared away – like weeds from a field they’re about to plough.

Then, once the mana over the whole area is free of the bright green mana, the Plant Mages start moving around. Drips of powerful murky brown mana fall into the field. The Plant Mages then begin pushing these concentrated drops of mana around, spreading them out so they affect a greater area of land while lessening in concentration. The enhancement potions, I realise.

When the whole section of land is covered, I see the seeds being planted – tiny little dots that glow a soft paisley green in my magic sight. More magic is cast by the Plant Mages, a blanket of yellowish green lying upon the earth and sinking into the seeds. I see them beginning to sprout with impressive alacrity – roots sink into the ground and a sprout heads towards the surface as if days are passing rather than seconds.

Meanwhile, the mages place…something at the edges of the fields, a good few metres away from the growing wheat seeds. I can’t actually see them – they appear like black holes rather than objects to my magical sight. Nor can I quite tell their purpose – they seem to be consuming the mana around them, including the mana that the mages have just cast on the wheat – which continues to grow visibly.

Turning off my magic sight briefly, I open my physical eyes and look at the object closest to where we’re standing. It doesn’t look like much – a clay ball.

“What is that?” I ask Elarion, pointing at it.

The smug smile on his face just grows further.

“An adaptation of the Energy poisoning treatment the kitchens have been using to remove Energy from the vegetation we’ve been supplying to them.”

A frown digs in between my brows for a moment as I try to work out why those might be necessary – the levels of the mana I can see within the field are reasonable, after all.

Then, as I turn on my magic-sight again, I realise what’s happening. Although the clay balls are drawing at the mana within the field, their primary function is to stop that bright green mana from returning. Though there are some places where it seeps in between two balls that are a bit too far apart, most of the field is being kept clear of it.

I now realise why there’s such a gap between the balls and the wheat – allowances need to be made for the area of influence of the Energy absorption balls.

Opening my eyes again, I look at Elarion thoughtfully.

“I have to admit that this isn’t what I expected,” I tell him. “I thought you were going to work with the node network?”

“I misunderstood what was happening with the plants to begin with,” Elarion admits, a displeased twist to his lips. “The problem was that the plants were being immediately accepted into the network – that was why they were being charged with far too much Energy. I tested planting them in the lower Energy spots, but that didn’t work as well as I was hoping – because it’s not actually Energy that’s the issue.”

“It’s the network’s mana,” I murmur in understanding.

“Precisely,” Elarion agreed, looking slightly surprised that I’ve come to this conclusion.

I see,” I muse, the pieces falling into place. “So, instead of working with the node, you’re trying to clear its influence away entirely.” It didn’t just look like it was clearing weeds away from the field – it actually was. Mana-weeds, anyway. “And then, after you’ve obtained a base state, you’re adding more mana in the form of enhancement potions, and then using the Energy poisoning treatment to keep the node’s mana away,” I conclude.

Elarion looks at me for a long moment, the hint of a furrow between his eyebrows.

“...Perceptive,” he murmurs after a moment. “How–” he cuts himself off, eyeing me speculatively. “Markus, can you see magic?”

My eyebrows twitch up slightly.

“...Yes?” I respond slowly. “I thought you already knew that.” I swear I’ve mentioned that ability to my allies. Haven’t I? Actually, thinking about it, maybe I’ve never been that explicit about it.

“I did not,” Elarion admits, sounding disgruntled at having to do so. “I thought that you were using your ridiculous number of Shaping Skills to sense changes. Yet I know you don’t have Plant-Shaping, so it could not be that this time. This explains so much,” he murmurs, perhaps not intending me to hear the last bit.

I shrug, reminded by his mention of Plant-Shaping that that is something I’d like to try to learn – if I get a moment. Actually, I have a strong feeling that the best path forward in that direction would be to spend some time here, watching how the Plant mages prepare the field and then the way the potions affect the plants directly.

I’ll try to find the time in between everything else I need to do. Still, I do have one nagging question about what just happened.

“Why did the mages use pulses of mana to clear away the node’s interference?” Because I can only conclude that that’s the bright green I saw there – the bright green that saturates the ground as far as I can see, even to the coastline. “I assume that they couldn’t just push it away in a single burst.”

“Not without potentially alerting the node controller,” Elarion confirms. I grimace at the thought – none of us know what is the ultimate source of the node, but given the power of the network, we don’t want to upset it.

“Then the pulses–”

“Mimic the movements each node makes and thereby make it seem like we are a part of the network. The nodes yield ground to us relatively easily if we do it like that.”

My eyebrows rise up my forehead as I consider the implications of that.

“Then, over time, we could perhaps clear away influence of the node entirely?”

Elarion gives me a satisfied smile.

“That is the aim.”

“And the network controller won’t notice if it starts losing areas of its network?” I ask with a raised eyebrow.

The other lord hesitates for a moment.

“I cannot say for sure, but given how much land I sensed that the network stretches over, small areas of land are unlikely to alert it. I do not doubt that it happens from time to time for other reasons – a beast rampages through, a forest fire, a manastorm strike that disrupts the local mana….”

I nod slowly. He’s probably right. And that works for now, but the future is another question. If our population grows, we’ll need more land to supply enough food for it. But perhaps I’d better not borrow trouble for now.

“So, in the present, how much food do you think can be produced? And in what time frame?” I nod at the wheat which is still green but looks almost fully grown. “If we can keep growing things at that speed, we won’t need much land.”

Elarion grimaces.

“It’s too early to give proper estimates. We have only just discovered a method that works. There is also the fact that, at present, my mages are required for almost every step of the process which means they cannot be helping elsewhere – like on experimenting with local plants.”

I frown.

“Why? I understand that they are needed to clear the field, but could the farmers not use the potions on the grains of wheat? It might take a little more time for the plants to grow without their aid, but as long as they’ve cleared enough fields to begin with….”

“It’s not that simple,” Elarion replies with the hint of a sigh. “First of all, these potions are strong – made with reagents harvested here. We can only use one on the same soil two times at a maximum before needing to shift to a different plot of land and let that one recover for a time – otherwise they become poison rather than enhancement.”

“That sounds like what happens if we take too many mana potions in too short a time frame,” I remark. Elarion nods.

“It’s much the same. Additionally, the pressure of the network’s mana is constant, and the Energy poisoning treatment balls only work well for a few hours before they need to be changed. It’s almost full time work for my mages between all of that.”

I nod at his words about the pressure of mana – I saw it myself. It also makes sense that the clay balls have limited duration. There has to be a way of turning this to our advantage, I think to myself.

Closing my eyes, I trigger my magic-sight and look at the clay balls. Now I’m focusing on them, I realise that the reason some of them are letting bright green mana leak through isn’t because they have been set too far apart from the others – it’s because their radius is smaller than the others. It’s only a guess, but I feel pretty sure that these balls in particular are older than the others – and have absorbed more mana.

Perhaps that’s the problem – the mana is being absorbed, soaked up. But nothing is being done with it after that. A thought occurs to me – because neither Energy nor mana ever just disappear.

“Elarion,” I start, opening my eyes. “Do you have any of the clay balls that aren’t working any more?”

“Certainly,” he says, pulling one out of his Inventory. I touch it but, as expected, it’s completely inert – any Energy or mana that might have been inside has been stripped away.

“Do you have any that haven’t been in your Inventory?”

This makes him pause for a moment.

“Perhaps.”

He strides away and rummages in a couple of the sacks of supplies that are sitting to the side of the field.

A couple of minutes later, he returns with a clay ball held triumphantly in one hand.

“Thanks,” I tell him absently when he hands it over. My attention is on the clay ball that is practically humming with mana. In my magic-sight it’s even glowing slightly, a dark green rather than the bright green I might have expected.

I try pulling the mana out of it, but it doesn’t respond to me. Not unexpected.

What if…? I check that no one is near, then throw the ball towards a tree about a hundred metres away. Given the amount of Strength I chose to put into it, the ball hits the trunk hard.

Immediately, it bursts apart, with a loud sound and cloud of dust and fragments that shoot in all directions. Elarion jumps next to me and I see several of the farmers and Plant Mages looking in the direction of the explosion, clearly startled. Perhaps I should have warned them.

When the dust cloud vanishes, I see that there’s a large hole in the trunk. Not quite deep enough to make it fall over, but it wouldn’t take much.

I look up at Elarion and see a thoughtful look on his face.

“An impressive weapon,” he comments.

“Potentially useful,” I agree. “We’ll need to test how long the clay balls hold their charge, so please don’t put them into your Inventory for now. Actually, if you could put them in different bags and label them with the time, that would be helpful.” I pause for thought. “We should probably test different materials to form the casing too,” I muse. Maybe I’ll put Keshaka onto that – her work with the shackles that captured the feral humans saved time and prevented lots of injuries.

After a bit more discussion, I leave Elarion to continue his tests. They’ll have to come in sooner rather than later – the sun is far on its journey towards the horizon – but I have a feeling that they have all been motivated by the success and want to squeeze every moment out of the day that they can.

As I walk up the slope, an idea occurs to me. Mana bombs are useful for sure, but what if we could create something else even more useful?

Hunter, are you back from your hunt, yet? I ask, reaching out through the Bond.
