Nigel Acard woke before dawn. The air inside his canvas tent held the biting, metallic chill of the winter morning. He pushed himself off the rigid cot, his joints popping in loud protest. He grabbed a rough towel and walked out to the wash basin near the camp's edge, breaking the thin layer of ice over the water with his bare knuckles.

He scrubbed his face. The freezing water provided a sharp, necessary shock to his system.

Nigel was the patriarch of his family. He was a Grand Duke of the Cinderfall Hegemony. His bloodline traced back to the very roots of the kingdom, hailing directly from the firstborn son of the First King himself.

He was also the biggest joke in the empire.

The story was an open secret, whispered in the gilded halls of the capital to ensure the Acards never forgot their place. The First King’s eldest son, destined to rule all of Cinderfall, harbored a flaw. A curse in the blood. He could not bond with a phoenix. The royal hatchery rejected him; the sacred birds refused to answer his call, treating the heir apparent as if he were made of dead wood.

The line of succession bypassed him, shifting to a younger, magically gifted sibling. The First King granted the disgraced firstborn the name 'Acard'—derived from an ancient word for sword. The Acard family became the hounds of the royal family, tasked with the menial, grinding labor of maintaining the kingdom while the true royals rode the skies in golden glory.

Nigel had tried. As a boy, he had walked into the royal hatchery, his heart pounding with desperate hope, only to stand in humiliating silence for hours while the eggs remained cold and inert. Every Acard for generations had failed the same test. They were the only high noble family in Cinderfall entirely devoid of contracted knights. Even minor, upstart baron families occasionally produced a phoenix rider, instantly elevating their status above the ancient, grounded Acards.

So, the Acards adapted.

While the other nobles preened in their gilded armor, practicing flashy fire magic, the Acards trained. They subjected themselves to grueling, brutal, bone-breaking physical regimens. They mastered the spear, the broadsword, and the shield. In a kingdom obsessed with magical fire, they became the undisputed masters of cold, hard steel. No warrior in Cinderfall could best an Acard in physical, non-magical combat.

In a world governed by magic, physical skill was often viewed as a quaint, useless hobby. What good was a perfect parry if an opponent could melt your sword and armor with a thought?

Nigel tightened the leather straps of his breastplate. It was forged from dark, unpolished iron, scored and pitted from decades of hard use.

He remembered a ball at the capital years ago. A visiting Dragon Knight from Aerthos had challenged the Hegemony’s finest to a sparring match. Nigel stepped into the ring. He lasted exactly two minutes before the sheer, inhuman physical strength and localized draconic magic of the Aerthos knight shattered his guard and sent him skidding across the marble floor.

He was hauled out of the ring by a squadron of smirking Phoenix Knights. The humiliation was absolute. The incident became the favored joke of the season. The gossip grew so vicious that his own engagement to a prominent noblewoman was abruptly broken off. Nobody wanted to marry into a cursed, grounded lineage. The Acard name was likely going to die with him; high nobility forbade marrying commoners, and no highborn lady would accept a flightless husband.

Nigel finished buckling his greaves. He picked up his heavy iron longsword, the leather grip worn smooth by his calloused hands.

He closed his eyes, murmuring the words his grandfather had beaten into him during countless, bruising hours in the training yard.

"Let them rely on the blood of ancestors. We rely on the strength of our own grip. They burn bright, and they turn to ash—but we bleed, we sweat, and we endure.”

He never spoke the words aloud in public. Voicing such pragmatism invited accusations of heresy against the First Flame. He kept his head down. He endured.

The Acards served a different purpose. Because the Phoenix Knights considered basic law enforcement beneath them, the Acard family voluntarily took over the policing of the capital's lower districts. They walked the slums, settling disputes, breaking up bandit rings, and investigating kidnappings—the dirty, thankless work the shining heroes of the sky ignored. Over time, the crown simply made the arrangement official. The Acards were the protectors of the common folk, the only nobles who actually knew the names of the beggars in the streets.

During the recent famine, Nigel quietly emptied his family’s ancient coffers, buying grain from smugglers at exorbitant prices to feed the lower wards. It was a hopeless endeavor. There were too many starving mouths, and the king was hoarding the reserves.

Nigel found a brief spark of hope when the marriage between Crown Prince Ignis and Lady Nyxia Black was expedited. He had served as part of the security detail guarding Lady Nyxia during her tours of the kingdom. He saw her character. He watched her quietly organizing relief efforts, slipping food to the needy behind the Prince's back. She was pragmatic, intelligent, and compassionate. If she became Queen, she could change the royal family from the inside. She could save the people.

Then reality struck. The young Lord of Wight tore the sky open and stole her away.

Nigel hated Alarion Wight for that. He believed the boy selfishly robbed the people of Cinderfall of their only hope for relief.

Then, the sky rained silver packages.

The boy fed the very people his enemy was starving. Nigel’s resentment crumbled, replaced by profound, grudging respect. The hope for the Cinderfall royal family changing died, but perhaps a new kind of ruler was rising in the north.

Nigel stepped out of his tent. The freezing wind bit at his face.

Today, King Theron ordered him to lead the vanguard charge. It was a thinly veiled insult wrapped in a suicide mission. The Phoenix Knights handled the heavy lifting during the initial push; now, the King expected the flightless Acard to march a column of regular cavalry directly into the teeth of the enemy's automated defenses.

Nigel walked toward the staging area. Fifty thousand regular knights and heavy cavalry awaited his command. They were men of iron and horseflesh, marching to prove their worth in a kingdom that viewed them as expendable.

His lieutenants stood waiting by his mount, a massive, muscular warhorse bred for endurance.

"Form the lines," Nigel ordered, his voice a rough bark. "We charge at first light."

The sun began to peek over the eastern ridges, casting long, blood-red shadows across the valley of ash in Aerthos.

Nigel pulled a heavy brass spyglass from his saddlebag. He raised it to his eye, scanning the distant enemy lines to identify the weakest concentration of automated turrets. He needed a gap in the armor to funnel his cavalry through.

He swept the glass slowly from left to right.

He reached the end of the trench line. He frowned, lowering the glass for a second, rubbing his eye.

"That can't be right," he muttered.

He raised the glass again, tracking back across the enemy fortifications.

The gaping, smoking hole the Phoenix Knights had torn through the defenses yesterday evening was gone. The line was pristine. Seamless. The shattered turrets were completely replaced. The melted adamantium barricades were rebuilt.

A cold, heavy pit formed in Nigel's stomach. He dropped the spyglass, letting it hang by its leather strap. He yanked hard on his horse's reins. The beast reared up with a startled whinny.

He spurred the horse in the opposite direction, galloping madly away from the front lines and back toward the command redoubt.

This was a suicide charge. The fifty thousand men waiting for his order were going to ride directly into a fully operational meat grinder.

He reached the brutalist stone structure serving as the King’s headquarters. He threw himself off the horse before it stopped moving. His heavy boots slammed into the mud. He sprinted past the startled guards and burst through the heavy oak doors of the war council chamber.

The high lords and generals gathered around the weirwood table, drinking hot tea and reviewing logistics maps.

One of the lords, a man clad in ostentatious gold armor, looked up and sneered. "What happened, Acard? Why are you back here? Do you need a real knight to hold your hand for the charge?"

Another general chimed in, a mocking smirk on his face. "You have been given the highest honor today. We would all kill for this opportunity. The King ordered you to surge at the first sight of light. What are you doing here?"

Nigel ignored the squawking. He marched straight to the head of the table and dropped to one knee. The iron of his armor clanked loudly against the stone floor.

"I need an audience with the King," Nigel demanded, his voice echoing in the cavernous room.

The chamberlain, a thin, nervous man standing near the inner doors, shook his head rapidly. "The King ordered no one disturb him. He is resting in his isolated quarters. No one knows when he will be out."

Nigel looked around the room. The only hope he had of saving his fifty thousand men rested with these arrogant, preening fools. He swallowed his pride. He pleaded.

"My lords, we need to rethink the attack. The enemy has completely restored their defenses overnight. The breach is gone. This charge is sending the cavalry to their deaths."

A heavy-set general let out a loud, dismissive guffaw. "I will have my scouts sent to the front lines to check your claim, Acard. If this is false, and you are simply too afraid to serve, I will strip this honor from you myself. My regiment will lead the charge instead."

The general spat on the ground and yelled for an attendant, dispatching him to verify the report.

The familiar, tiresome bickering resumed. The lords began arguing amongst themselves over who would get to claim the "honor" of leading the vanguard if Nigel was stripped of command. They were eager for the glory, completely ignoring the tactical warning he had just delivered.

Nigel stood up and waited. He watched the sand fall in a nearby hourglass.

Twenty minutes later, the scout returned. He burst through the doors, out of breath.

"It... it is true, my lords," the scout gasped, bowing hastily. "The enemy defenses are fully restored. The line is unbroken."

The murmurs died instantly. The lords stopped arguing and stared at each other, the reality of the situation finally piercing their arrogance.

The heavy-set general slammed his fist on the table. "The King's orders are absolute! We must attack unless the King rescinds the order himself. And it is impossible to get through to him! No sound enters that room. He cannot be called."

Panic set in. The argument shifted from grasping for glory to desperately dodging responsibility.

"The attack must happen today, or the King will have our heads!" another lord shouted, his face pale.

No one was willing to take the blame for halting a royal decree. King Theron had specifically tasked Nigel with this charge to avoid showing favoritism to any of the major noble houses. It was meant to be an easy victory, a freebie. Assigning it to the neutral, powerless Acard family was an act of charity.

Now, it was a death sentence. The nobles were determined to ensure Nigel was the one to carry it out.

"The King tasked Nigel with this honor," a lord proclaimed loudly, pointing an accusing finger across the table. "Hence, he and his cavalry order should be the ones to execute it!"

Nigel’s jaw clicked shut. The taste of copper flooded the back of his throat.

They were unanimously agreeing to sacrifice him and his men to appease the King.

Nigel stepped forward, his hand dropping to the hilt of his iron sword. He opened his mouth to formally refuse the order, to tell them they could rot in hell before he marched his men to their deaths.

An older, sharp-eyed lord at the end of the table interrupted him. His voice dripped with lethal warning.

"You know what happens to anyone who refuses to follow the King's direct order, Acard," the lord stated flatly. "Do not think for a second your soldiers' lives will be spared if you refuse. And it will not only be their lives you forfeit. The lives of their families back home will also be taken."
