For several seconds after Ater’s words, no one spoke. Sakura stared at the old man, trying to understand whether she had heard him correctly.

"How do you know?" she finally asked.

Ater slowly turned his head toward her.

"Know what exactly?"

"That Nobus died. Why are you so sure?"

The old man sat down again and placed his hands on the edge of the table. He took so long to answer that Sakura began to wonder whether he intended to answer at all. Faerd watched him from the bunk, and Gilbert did not rush him either.

"Because I knew Nobus too."

Sakura was silent for several seconds.

"How well?"

"Well enough. And he died. I’m telling you, that man is dead."

Gilbert raised an eyebrow.

"Did you see the body?"

Ater turned toward him.

"No."

"Then why are you so sure?"

The old man exhaled irritably, but answered anyway.

"It happened after Crowlis. Nobus was already in hiding by then, but he hadn’t abandoned his research. He had a workshop far north of here, built underground in old stone passages. Work chambers, storage rooms, several halls for experiments... My point is, no one would stumble onto that place by accident."

Sakura listened carefully.

"What happened there?"

"I don’t know how it started. I wasn’t involved in his work. That day, I had simply gone to visit an old acquaintance. I remember the floor suddenly shaking, and the walls cracked almost immediately."

Ater fell silent for a moment.

"First one of the passages gave way, then the ceiling of the lower level collapsed. I was closer to the stairs and managed to make it up. Nobus stayed below."

"You were with him?"

"Until the collapse. He was near the entrance to the lower chamber. I shouted at him to run."

"And him?"

Ater’s fingers tightened around the edge of the table.

"He said he wouldn’t make it."

The guardhouse went quiet, and the silence was tense.

"Then the lower level collapsed almost completely. A few minutes later, an underground waterway broke through. The water flooded in faster than we could retreat."

Ater fell silent again.

"We went back later. Cleared some of the passages as far as we could. We found the bodies of the people who had been on the upper levels. We never reached the lower chambers. They were buried under stone and water..."

"And you never found Nobus’s body?" Sakura asked.

"No."

Gilbert had been standing by the wall the entire time, holding his injured shoulder. Now he spoke.

"Then you don’t know that he died. You know that he should have died."

Ater turned his head sharply toward him.

"There was no way to survive down there."

"But you didn’t find his body."

Irritation crossed Ater’s face.

"Are you seriously trying to convince me that someone could survive who knows how long buried under stone and water?"

"I’m not trying to convince you of anything. I’m only saying there’s a difference between ‘there was no way to survive’ and ‘I saw him dead.’"

Ater went silent.

Sakura expected another biting answer, but none came. Instead, the old man got up and paced across the room, moving more abruptly than usual.

"Even if we entertain that nonsense," he said at last, "it still explains nothing. If Nobus somehow got out, why didn’t he come back? Why didn’t he contact anyone? He knew people were looking for him. He knew where to find me."

"Maybe he didn’t want to be found..."

"Nobus?"

Ater gave a short laugh.

"No. That wasn’t like him. Nobus always cared that people knew about his work. Even when he was forced into hiding. He wouldn’t voluntarily disappear for decades."

"People change," Lir said.

"Rarely that much."

Faerd had been listening in silence, leaning against the wall. Now he frowned.

"Decades?"

Everyone looked at him.

"How old was the man you saw?" Ater asked.

Faerd thought about it.

"I’m not sure. Thirty. Maybe a little older. Thirty-five."

Ater did not answer for several seconds.

"No."

Faerd frowned.

"No what?"

"Nobus couldn’t have looked thirty-five," he replied irritably, as if that should have been obvious even to a child.

"I didn’t say he actually was thirty-five. Only that he looked it."

"The last time I saw him, he was already over fifty."

Gilbert looked first at Ater, then at Faerd.

If Nobus had somehow survived the disaster, then after all the decades that had passed, he should have been an old man. Yet the man Faerd had seen looked younger even than the Nobus Ater believed had died.

"Describe him," Sakura said.

Faerd looked at her.

"I already did."

"No. I need his face. Details."

He closed his eyes.

"Thin, narrow face. High cheekbones. Dark hair. Not short, but not down to his shoulders either."

Sakura remembered the long table in the cave and the young Nobus arguing with Crowlis.

"His eyes?"

"Dark. Attentive."

Sakura kept picturing his face. The cheekbones, the gaze, the shape of his mouth when he was irritated. Faerd was not describing an exact copy of the man from the memory, but the resemblance was strong enough.

"He looks like the Nobus I saw," she said quietly.

Gilbert frowned.

"Wonderful."

"I’m glad things are getting clearer too," Sakura muttered.

Ater did not react to her sarcasm. He turned his head toward Faerd again.

"How did he speak?"

"What?"

"Nobus. Did he shout often?"

Faerd shook his head almost immediately.

"No."

"Even when he was angry?"

"Especially when he was angry. The more displeased he was, the quieter he spoke. Usually everyone else went silent after that."

Ater said nothing for several seconds.

"What else?"

Faerd shrugged and immediately winced from the pain.

"He hated waiting. The healers often asked for more time between procedures. Said they needed to repeat a test."

"And?"

"If the same result came back several times, he saw no point in checking again."

Sakura remembered the long stone table and Nobus standing across from Crowlis.

We’ve been waiting for months. Testing the same thing.

Crowlis had told him that the structure still wasn’t stable. Nobus had answered:

We can’t keep testing the same thing forever.

Sakura straightened.

"He talked exactly like that."

Ater turned his head toward her.

"Who?"

"Nobus. In the memory, when he argued with Crowlis before the integration."

Faerd frowned.

"Integration?"

"Later."

"He also thought Crowlis was spending too long checking the results," Sakura continued. "He kept pushing him to move on to the next stage because he believed the structure was stable enough."

Ater lowered his head.

"That sounds like him. Nobus always hated unnecessary testing. Sometimes he got away with it, sometimes he didn’t. It never made him any more cautious."

He paused.

"But a similar way of speaking proves nothing."

"I never said it did."

Ater answered too quickly. Sakura looked at him more carefully.

"You recognize it too, don’t you?"

"I recognize some habits of a man I once knew. That isn’t enough. People can have similar habits. They can pick up someone else’s habits too."

Gilbert spoke quietly.

"Along with the name, the face, and the research?"

Ater turned sharply toward him.

"I don’t know."

The old man rubbed a hand over his face.

"It can’t be the same person."

"Why?" Lir asked.

"Because it can’t."

Lir did not ask again, clearly realizing that this was more about stubbornness than logic.

Ater continued more quietly:

"If Nobus survived, he should have come back. There should have been some trace of him. Records, rumors, people who had seen him. Something. He knew where to find me."

"Maybe there were traces," Gilbert said. "You just didn’t find them."

Ater slowly turned his head toward him but said nothing.

Sakura remembered Nobus at the long table again, and Crowlis’s words.

Nobus’s ambition was always stronger than his fear.

She raised her head sharply.

"There’s something else."

Ater turned toward her.

"Crowlis talked to me about Nobus."

The old man froze. Sakura continued.

"He told me to beware of him."

For several seconds, Ater said nothing.

"What do you mean? When?"

Sakura frowned.

"During the vision you sent us into."
