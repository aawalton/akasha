I could feel the hope slowly draining out of me as I drifted aimlessly through the nothingness. Other worlds buffeted me in an attempt to capture my attention, but I ignored them all. I didn’t even need to reach out towards them to know that they were not my reality. I could still feel that at the back of my mind, and it was the one place that was not trying to coax me closer. In fact, it was doing the opposite. It was trying to keep me away and forcing me to delve into another world.

But I didn’t trust them. I couldn’t trust any of the other realities that were pulling at my mind, and I didn’t want to allow myself to slip into them. Every single world I’d been to recently had been bad. Each experience was horrible, and I was scared of what was happening in those worlds. Anything could be waiting for me in them, and I didn’t want to be chased, attacked, or shot at again.

There was no way to know, though. I couldn’t work out what was waiting for me in any of the worlds until I was there, so there was no way to prepare myself. I couldn’t choose somewhere nice, somewhere safe. I just had to accept that the reality might be awful, that people may want me dead.

However, even if I did go to one of the other worlds and it was bad again, I might be okay, a small voice in the back of my head whispered. No matter what, I’d survive in my reality. I might be hurt or injured in some way. Sometimes that passed through the worlds, but I’d survive, and somehow, Rex had managed to get to me in time in the last world.

I still had no idea how he’d done that, though. I was pretty sure it was him. Part of me, perhaps my heart, had recognised him immediately, but it felt wrong. In my reality, Rex was so young. I didn’t know how old he was, but he was just a puppy. Although he had been giant in that last world and in some of the others, he may have been the same age in them too, and that meant I should have been the one protecting him, not the other way around.

Rex was my responsibility too. He was a dog, not a human, and that meant he didn’t understand. He couldn’t comprehend some of the dangers that the worlds posed, and I should have been shielding him from harm, but I wasn’t. I had just left him in the last world to deal with the people, whoever they were, and that made me feel awful.

Still, I couldn’t help but think that even if I went into another world, somewhere else where people were hunting me down, Rex might find me again. It had happened too many times already, so perhaps it would just keep happening. Maybe he’d be able to reach me wherever—

I threw myself backwards, pressing my weight against the door as I slammed it shut and twisted the lock. My heart hammered in my throat, and I could feel sweat dripping down the side of my face. A stitch burned in my side, the pain so strong it left me dizzy and made it hard to breathe.

It was already a struggle, though. Wherever I was, whichever world I had found myself in, was far too humid. I could almost feel the water droplets hanging heavily in the bright room around me. Each ragged inhale felt like I was trying to breathe through a wet cloth, and it wasn’t enough. I couldn’t get enough oxygen in, and my body needed more. I had to catch my breath so I could continue moving, keep running.

But I couldn’t. I knew that. It was why I’d thrown open the door and locked myself in that small, obnoxiously bright room. It was a dead end, little more than a closet, and my only hope was that they would run straight past without realising I was in there. That wouldn’t save me, obviously. It was too late for that, but it might buy me a little more time. I wasn’t sure what I’d do with that, what the few extra minutes would give me, but I wanted them nonetheless.

With my body still pressed against the door, I tried to hold my breath so I could listen out for footsteps, but it was impossible. Because of how hard I was panting, I couldn’t last more than a second without needing to gasp in more air, and the whistle of my inhales mixed with the endless thumping of my heart, drowning out anything else, but I couldn’t do anything about that. I couldn’t catch my breath, couldn’t regain control. Everything was spiralling away from me, and there was nothing I could do.

A sob welled up within me, but I forced myself to swallow it down as I squeezed my eyes shut, trying to push the barrage of panic and terror away. I had no idea what was happening. I’d only just entered the world, and I had no idea who I was running from, what I’d done to cause it, or what they’d do to me when they caught me. All I knew was that I’d somehow found myself in yet another world where someone was hunting me, and that sent another wave of helplessness through me, mingling with the despair that was already threatening to suffocate me.

Part of me felt like I should have been used to it. After all, it kept happening. That was not the first world I’d been to where I was running away from someone. Far from it, actually. It had happened so frequently that I felt like it shouldn’t have scared me at all, but that didn’t stop the fear and adrenaline that had flooded my body. Despite how often I’d been hunted, I was still shaking, terrified of what would happen when I was inevitably caught, and I couldn’t stop myself from reaching out towards the dizzinesses that waited in my mind.

As soon as I got close to them, they pulled themselves away. The world seemed to sharpen around me, the strip lighting in the ceiling above me grew brighter, and everything felt more solid. It felt more real. The harder I tried to escape, the tighter the world’s grip on me became, and I had to grit my teeth to hold back another sob that threatened to escape.

Sharp pain radiated through my jaw, so strong that tears immediately welled in my eyes, and I blinked quickly to force them back. What had happened to my face, I asked myself, but I couldn’t remember. I lifted a hand, gently touching my jaw, but that just sent another spike of pain through me.

The side of my face felt hot and swollen, but it wasn’t as bad as my hand. I hadn’t noticed until that moment, until I tried to bend my fingers, but they were in agony. I could barely move them. My joints refused to cooperate, and the burning was so bad that I could feel my heart thumping in it. Each beat sent a fresh wave of pain through my hand, and my eyes started to drift towards it before I realised what I was doing and my gaze snapped back to the brushed metal wall.

I didn’t want to look at it. Even just the thought of doing so sent a wave of nausea and fear through me, making my already-weak knees feel even weaker. I was scared of what I might see, of how bad the damage would be, and I wanted to remain in denial. That felt safer, less terrifying.

But it wasn’t just my hand and face that hurt, I realised as the sensations gradually sank in and grew more noticeable. Too much of my body felt like it was on fire. The bottom of my feet ached from running for too long in the thin-soled shoes I’d been issued almost a year ago. They were already starting to fall apart, and I knew they did little to protect my feet from the impact of the solid metal flooring.

My knee hurt too. Both of them, actually, and the stabbing pain in my side had yet to go away. I’d thought it was just a stitch at first, something brought on by the running, but now I realised it was more than that.

A memory pulled at me, the image hazy. I vaguely remembered something hitting me in the side, a foot. I was curled up on the floor, my arms wrapped around me as I tried to shield myself from the blows, and someone had kicked me in the ribs. The impact had forced the air from my lungs, leaving me unable to cry out or even breathe, but somehow, I’d found the strength to get away from them.

My head fell forward as the memory assaulted me, making the pain burn stronger for just a second, and my eyes landed on the drying blood that was smeared across my ripped shirt. For a moment, all I could do was stare at the once-cream fabric that had been almost entirely stained red, but then I noticed that the neckline of my shirt gapes open, revealing my tapestry of dark bruises that were already starting to spread across my skin.

Had someone tried to tear the shirt from my body, I found myself wondering, horrified, or had they just grabbed it when I was trying to get away from them? I wasn’t sure. I had no recollection, but the sight alone sent a shudder through me, and I dropped my head back against the door, trying once more to get my breathing under control.

It felt impossible, though. Panic was still coursing through my body, and if anything, I was pretty sure I was breathing even harder than I had been when I first darted into the room. It did not help that I knew they must be getting closer to me.

I was painfully aware that I was running out of time, and I was out of options. There was no way out, no escape, and they would find me again. I’d fight, obviously. I couldn’t just let myself be murdered, but I was already in so much pain. I’d fought so much, and I was tired. My body felt weak and exhausted, and I just wanted it all to stop, but I knew it wouldn’t. There was nothing else I could do, and no one who would help me.

My head snapped up as a brief, desperate hope trickled into my heart. I wasn’t sure whether he was still dealing with those people in the other world or how long it would take him to find me. Hells, I wasn’t even certain he would be able to find me, but perhaps I was not fully alone. There may be someone who could help me.

“Rex,” I whispered, unable to bring myself to speak any louder than that in case the wrong person heard me.

I waited, straining my ears and breathing as quietly as I could, for some kind of answer. I hoped to hear a distant bark or the click of claws against the mental floor outside the room I was in, but I heard nothing. Nothing other than footsteps.

My breath caught in my chest, and I slapped a hand over my mouth, trying to muffle the sound of my frantic gasps as they drew steadily closer. I sent a prayer to whichever god would listen to me that they would keep walking. I begged them to not make the people not notice me, to just give me a few more minutes to prepare myself for the inevitable end, but the footsteps slowed as they approached.

I bit my bottom lip as tears built behind my eyes. Lab doors were being thrown open around me. I could hear the thud of them slamming forcefully against the wall, and I squeezed my eyes shut, praying again and again to please just miss the room I was in. It was a storage room, and a small one at that. Barely anyone used it, and most people just seemed to forget it existed. I hoped that the people would do the same, that they would just pass me by.

But my hopes were immediately dashed when I heard the door handle rattling behind me. Ripping my hand away from my mouth, I pressed it against the door again, throwing all of my body weight against it in case they tried to force their way into the room.

“Hey, should this one be locked?” I heard someone call from just outside the door.

Tears finally broke free of my eyes, slipping down my cheeks as my gaze frantically scanned the room, searching for another way out. There was nothing, though. There was no other door, hatch, or even a vent that I could slip into. I was trapped, and I should have known. The version of me who lived in that world did. That was why she had hidden in there. She hoped it would be missed, that I would be able to rest for just a moment before having to run again.

“Mmmm, let me check,” someone replied.

I could barely breathe. My heart pounded in my ears, my knees shook, and sweat beaded on my upper lip as I waited for him to continue, sending one final prayer to the gods, but it remained unanswered.

“Huh, I guess not,” the guy said before letting out a soft laugh. “I fully would have missed this door too.”

“Ha! I knew it!” the other person cried. “You owe me fifty chits!”

“Alright, alright. You did earn it,” he sighed. “And what’s this? Do I spy with my little eye some blood on the handle here?”

It felt like my heart had been catapulted into my throat. I knew they were right on the other side of the door, and I couldn’t help but notice how normal they sounded. Despite having chased me for so long, they didn’t sound winded at all, and for some reason, that realisation sent ice down my spine.

The door handle rattled again before a sharp knock came from the door. A strangled gasp escaped my lips before I could catch it, and I longed to press my hand against my mouth again to hold back any other sound, but I couldn’t risk taking it away from the door.

“Grace,” I heard the man call. “We know you’re in there.”

I didn’t reply, and I wasn’t sure if he wanted me to, but it felt like he was waiting for an answer. I refused to give him one, though. Instead, I just pressed my lips together, ignoring the pain in my jaw, and let the silence stretch out between us.

After a few seconds, they knocked again. The sound was louder that time. I felt it reverberate through the door, the vibrations seemingly reaching right into my body and wrapping around my cracked ribs.

“Grace, are you going to open the door so we can have a chat like the nice, civilised people that we are, or are we going to have to break it down?” the man asked, the threat audible in his voice.

I didn’t respond to that either. I knew that was a lie. They didn’t want to just have a chat with me. That was exactly what they said before, and it had ended with them dragging me out of my quarters by my hair. I couldn’t recall what I’d done to deserve it. It felt like that had come out of nowhere, and I had no idea what they wanted from me, but I suspect they intended to kill me.

Or at least, I hoped that was what they planned to do. It felt like a kindness compared to the alternatives that were flitting through my mind.

Another sigh came from the person on the other side of the door, heavier that time, and I could almost feel the feigned disappointment dripping from them.

“Well,” they started. “We tried to do this the nice way, but you refused, so I guess you brought this on yourself.”

I held my breath, unable to do anything else as I listened as hard as I could. Footsteps moved away from the door, but I had no hope that they were leaving me alone. Instead, they stopped after just a couple of steps.

“Darren,” the man said. “Would you do the honours?”

“Oh, it would be my pleasure,” another voice answered, a grim excitement simmering in their words.

My fingers gripped the door, and my muscles tensed in preparation, but I still wasn’t ready for the first kick. The blow winded me, knocking the air out of me, and I tried to suck in another breath before the next one came, but my lungs felt paralysed. Pain gripped my chest, burning through my body, and I couldn’t even inhale before Darren slammed into the door again.

The impact made my feet slip on the metal floor, and I hurried to regain my footing, but the other person recovered more quickly than I did. Before I could fully throw my weight against the door again, another deafening bang came from it as Darren kicked the hard metal once more.

A terrifying noise came from beside me, and my head snapped to the side. Pressing myself against the door, I stared in horror at the frame. The metal surrounding the lock was starting to warp. It was bowing, warping from the repeated impacts, and I wasn’t sure how much longer it would last, but I knew soon, it would not hold them back.

Desperation tore through me, and I looked around, trying to come up with a plan, but I had very little. Another bang came from the door, and pain tightened around my chest like a vice. Sweat dripped from my face. My knees felt weak, and I knew I couldn’t remain upright for much longer, but I waited until another blow came before dropping to the floor. The room was just small enough for me to sit with my back against the door and brace my feet on the wall opposite, and that would help. It had to help. I wasn’t strong enough to stay on my feet, but from the floor, I could hold them off for a little longer. I had to be able to.

My eyes clenched shut, and my hands found my hair. It was tangled, matted from the fight, but I forced my fingers into it, gripping as tightly as I could as I fought to keep the door shut. Tears flowed freely down my face. I sobbed, gasping for air with each inhale, and I knew they must have been able to hear me, but it didn’t stop them.

The next kick came too quickly. I wasn’t as ready as I should have been, and it took me by surprise. My head jerked back, smacking against the door, and dizziness exploded through me. Lights flashed before my eyes, and I tried to blink them away, but they grew brighter, more blinding. I felt my body turn limp as unconsciousness washed over my mind, and the fight drained out of me.

“Oh, Grace,” a voice called in a sing-song tone, drawing me towards it. “Aren’t you ready to just give up and turn yourself in?”

A cruel, mocking laugh, muffled by my hands pressed against my ears, came from the other side of the door before another bang sounded. My muscles tensed, preparing for the pain that it would bring, but no pain came.

Horror crashed into me. Slowly, I lowered my blood-covered hands, letting them fall to my sides before lifting my head and staring at the door on the far side of the room.
