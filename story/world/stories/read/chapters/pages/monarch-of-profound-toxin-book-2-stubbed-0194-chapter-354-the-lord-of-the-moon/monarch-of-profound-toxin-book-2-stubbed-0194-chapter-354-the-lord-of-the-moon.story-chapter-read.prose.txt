Eik’s consciousness snapped into place just in time to catch a glimpse of an enormous palm closing around his face and head.

Unimaginable power slammed into him and carried him backward.

If he hadn’t been thrown back with extreme force, warm, wet liquid would have covered him as his sudden appearance in the space that had been occupied by the tiny Living Manifestation just an instant before caused the skull of the host spawn to burst violently and fatally.

Before he even hit the ground, Eik was already shifting into a less solid state, Toxic Liquefaction allowing him to flow through the monster’s fingers. But a sudden flex of dark aura sent him sprawling to the ground, forcibly returned to human form.

A rod of pure darkness that manifested along the monster’s forearm pierced him through the leg, but with a powerful surge of Movement Boost, he ripped it out with not a moment of hesitation. It disintegrated in his hand before he could do anything about it, so Eik conjured a crystalline javelin of his own and hurled it at the opponent, utilizing the flowing momentum of the same movement.

His projectile was batted out of the air by a lightning quick hand before it could cause any harm, exploding like a tactical nuke upon contact with the ground, but the momentary distraction was all the Elixir King needed to roll backward and leap away to put some distance between them.

The creature did not appear particularly interested in giving chase.

The landscape around their brief but violent exchange had been turned into a ruin of smoking craters and annihilated vegetation in a single second.

For the first time since Eik had laid eyes on it, this pitch black being stood up properly, displaying its full height. From scalp to toe, it measured at least three meters. It was basically a less primitive version of the effigies they had located in the worlds infested by miasma. Or rather, it was probably more accurate to call the effigies figures modeled after this creature.

No, the resemblance was more than obvious.

Sporting an impressive three pairs of arms, the creature towered over Eik, rivaling some of the larger Gohkamorians. Despite the deep darkness of its skin, or carapace, or whatever kind of outer layer it was, its features were clearly distinguishable.

The one feature shared by it and the majority of the spawn, was the hauntingly gaunt and almost fragile slenderness. Despite being able to look down at Eik at almost twice his height, both its legs and its arms were a good bit thinner than his, and he wasn’t even particularly muscular for his size.

But most damning of all was its head. There was nothing ordinary about it. Starting at a pointy chin, the jaw curved gently outward and upward until it reached the temple. With no sign of ears, its skull instead abruptly widened before entering another upward-curving arch, resembling the shape of a drawn bow.

No, not a bow, of course. A crescent moon.

And, presumably, that meant that the creature in front of him was…

“The Lord of the Moon?” Eik asked out loud. All of his personal buffs had already been activated, but he now released a large dose of the S-ranked potion held within his internal storage on top. At this point, it didn’t do that much for him, but it was still better than nothing. “That’s who you are, right? You’re the Lord of the Moon.”

The two milky white dots of its eyes just stared back, its massive body still as if frozen.

All of Eik’s muscles were tense in anticipation of an aggressive lunge that could come at any time. All around them, spawn stood, simply watching from the sidelines. They weren’t making any moves to interfere in the standoff.

When final the beast that could have been made of ink spoke, its voice ground and vibrated. It sounded as if it was speaking into the propeller of a large fan set to the highest speed setting. As was probably the case, the monster didn’t sound like it had really spoken before at all, the words coming out in staccato fragments.

No, in fact, how the hell was it even speaking words that Eik could recognize? After all, he only knew three languages. Danish, English, and Alliance Common.

Okay, sure, he had some knowledge of German from school, but he hadn’t taken that very seriously. And as a Dane, Swedish and Norwegian was pretty easy to understand since they were basically just dialects of each other, although few liked to admit it.

But as far as he understood, the translation spell that was pretty much permanently affecting him only worked for “registered” languages, so to speak. In other words, it didn’t just work on any kind of communication.

It was why Atla had had some trouble adjusting the ability properly when she had first contacted Forest. If the Lord of the Moon was speaking some kind of strange, ancient language, it didn’t really make sense that it was translated to Eik like this.

But this wasn’t the time to think about that.

“I hate… that moniker,” the being muttered. It was difficult to tell with the oddly shaped head, but Eik was pretty sure he saw a frown.

“But you are him?”

To this, there was no response offered, but Eik didn’t need one. Even before they had begun talking, he had been able to feel it. The moment he had manifested into his own body, his X-ranked senses had kicked in and made it obvious.

This was the same signature the effigies had given off, albeit wildly different intensities. Facing the Lord of the Moon in person now also made it clear that the spectral eye that had been watching him back when he had escaped from the Arch Temple in Luna, the capital city of the cult of Moon Shall Swallow, had indeed not been a trick by the cult. Even the cult couldn’t imitate the signature of the Lord of the Moon so perfectly.

Well, not that they had gotten much right about him in the first place.

It would have been nice to know a little more about the enemy they were fighting. If the spectral eye had been real, then that meant that the Lord of the Moon knew plenty about them. He would likely have been observing Moon Shall Swallow and their enemies for centuries.

Speaking of, that could be where he had learned to speak.

“What is your goal?” Eik asked. No need to tip-toe around the subject. The situation could devolve at any moment, so if he wanted to get useful answers out of the resurrected Worldbreaker, time was probably of the essence. “What do you want?”

Again, the giant was reluctant to give an answer, instead staring silently as his spawn shuffled around them, their horrendously ugly, grinning faces giggling relentlessly. Eik could have destroyed them in moments, but any move would open him up to a counter strike by the Lord, and this was one motherfucker he couldn’t afford to take lightly.

Eyes never leaving the white eyes even once, Eik slowly began releasing Profound Toxin into his closed palm, where it formed a concentrated drop of the substance. Without moving, he let it soar upward and into one of the few clouds scattered across the sky.

Momentarily, the Lord’s eyes followed the drop as it raced up and up but he didn’t make any move to intercept or destroy it. Why?

Did he not care? Was he just that confident in his own ability to come out alive on the other side of whatever he was met with? Or did he have a trick up his sleeve? Well, he was a Worldbreaker, so probably.

It was sunny. Considering what was going on down here, it felt like it should have been storming. Not including the area laid in ruins by their brief exchange, the grass was green and the trees greener. Except for the rock formations, which resembled pillars stretching high into the atmosphere, not unlike the topography of the world on which the Championship elimination tournament had taken place, this world looked rather similar to Earth.

But then again, so did a lot of other alien worlds. Similarly to the sentient races, there was a strange tendency for almost identical evolutionary development.

No matter how many times he thought about it, it really was strange. And that sentiment was shared by the other races of the Nidafjeld Alliance as well. But the simple truth was that nobody had ever been able to figure out why it was like this, and while it wasn’t like scholars and historians had given up on trying to solve that particular mystery, it had also long since become just one of those things that were what they were.

“You want to destroy? Heal? Rebuild?”

The Lord of the Moon simply tilted his head down to the side, to the point where the edge of his crescent head rested on his shoulder. His answer was a wide grin that Eik knew all too well at this point. “It’s going to be… glorious.”

Ice ran down Eik’s spine as an Apocalypse Canvas the size of four soccer field suddenly shot out from a central point that correlated with the drop he had sent up earlier. It might not be nearly as large as the one he had dropped in Gohkamoria, but it was far, far denser, and that seriously upped its conjuration speed.

Profound Toxin was going absolutely bananas inside his spirit. If ever there had been a time where the blue bandit wanted to step into Eik’s body and take control, it was now. It was practically using his spiritual stomach as a punching bag to get him to let it out to get a piece of the action.

But he wasn’t going to give in that easily. He had his own chicken to pluck with the creepy piece of shit called the Lord of the Moon.

As a super dense, heavy rod of crystal was launched from the Apocalypse Canvas, Eik appeared behind the Lord of the Moon, a long dagger already plunging into the section of its back between the middle set of shoulders.

But rather than a vigorous spurt of blood, ichor, or whatever disgusting fluid might fuel that creepy body of his, the Lord seemed to vanish around the blade and flow off on the breeze. Indifferent to the lost target, the crystalline, heavenly projectile slammed down and cracked the earth, waves of debris expelling tons of dust high into the air.

Barely had Eik registered the flow of the battle when an all-encompassing, light-swallowing blackness began to rise rapidly on the horizon. He leaped backward but it was growing from every direction.

The Lord of the Moon was nowhere to be seen and the hundreds of spawn seemed entirely unconcerned with the development. They simply stared dumbly, as they had been doing from the beginning.

Employing his signature air travel method—explosions under his feet—he rocketed skyward, but even that was not enough to escape. The blackness continued to close in around him regardless. A feeling he recognized as panic dug its claws deep.

In the moment the last slivers of light from the sky were swallowed up by the encroaching dark, a force easily rivaling that of Chop the Blade’s attacks struck him directly in the face, the nauseating crunch of a nose breaking echoing through his skull.
