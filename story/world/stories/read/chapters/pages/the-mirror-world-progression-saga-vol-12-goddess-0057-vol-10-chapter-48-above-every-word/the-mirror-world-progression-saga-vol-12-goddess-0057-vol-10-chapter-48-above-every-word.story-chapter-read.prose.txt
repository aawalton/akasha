Everyone on the alliance’s side began to sober as they sensed the gravitas, the dread gathering and concentrating somewhere ahead, behind the enemy lines. Above them, the air was dense like a wall with enemy undead and gargoyle flyers, intermixed here and there with hippogriff riders. Seen only in windows through the masses, likely held in reserve until the proper moment, her observers showed her larger things lurking — structures of bones in the shape of a dragon.

We’ll need strength in the air, then.

The heroes gathered, and the common soldiers slowed, stopped, and looked back to their leaders, intrinsically hesitating in their rush to fill that waiting space. Samantha understood — they were crossing over a final border.

Samantha gazed over at Azure and saw her lover’s eyes on the central tower, looming even as it remained in the background, visibly upraised on a rocky hill, together with the entire fortress.

Azure turned to meet her gaze and sent mentally, “We can’t yet, can we? Attacking the rune.”

Samantha shook her head slightly and replied, “Ideally, not until we’ve broken this army and beaten at their walls.”

“Is that possible?”

“I sure hope so.”

“There’s something else. What is it?”

Samantha tried not to sigh. “Look, I’m going to need you in the air again, as I’m sure you saw those-”

“That’s not it.” Her glare was insistent. “Tell me.”

This time, Samantha sighed. “They’re obviously going to come at me eventually. To assassinate. Cut off the head of the snake.”

“You’re just going to wait that out?”

“It can’t be much longer. We’re about to march across the final threshold of their domain.”

“What they have waiting there is intended to drain you, then. You’ll stay away.”

“No. I’ll bait them. What I did before already served a bit of a dual-purpose that way. Thinking I’ve been expended on some level.”

“Ah.” Azure looked back over to the other side and scowled at them. “When they make their move, if I can help, I will.”

Samantha almost went into a tirade, denying her that. She knew that wouldn’t work, though. Azure wasn’t asking, and the ‘if’ was something she considered certain despite the words.

Instead, the goddess resisted denying it and said, “Very well, but only as a last resort. I’ll expect you to pay attention to what that is and act only then, in wisdom rather than emotion.”

Azure’s head whipped back around to her lover, surprised at the admittance. With a moment’s hesitation, she nodded. “Agreed. You can count on me.”

Worrisome and dangerous, but assigned duty — trust and honor — will preserve her more than sudden panic. Enough failures of leadership have taught me that.

Samantha gestured with her staff and called out loudly, “Forward, armies of the alliance!”

With their elites and heroes well poised to face the most obvious and concentrated point of resistance ahead, the army all down the line moved forward. Samantha and the concentrated force of their best flyers hovered directly overhead. The glow of the Second Sun in Leiande’s hand made a definitive sphere in the protected center, as they passed over the open, rough ground, a brown-gray desert devoid of life, littered only with the remains of armor from the vanquished undead.

They crossed the threshold, and the world got bleaker still, the sky’s light nothing more than a dim white, practically gray. Even the brown of the dirt seemed to flee existence, like the world had been washed out of all color. Almost all — the Second Sun still glowed golden, in what felt abominable in that place.

The tower in the background loomed larger, sucking in all attention around it like a black hole, crying to the world it dominated in every direction, ‘I am the end of hope. I am the dead end on the many roads of life. I am the pit at the center, and all must fall to me.’

Samantha regarded it as nothing more than a spiritual mirage; a persuasion she rejected. Others were not so unaffected. Most of the elites below had grim faces if they could be seen in their helms and masks. If not, their walk was grim enough to tell the story.

The soldiers fared much worse. The vanguard at most grunted and growled, but at some point in the lines of men crossing over, pockets in the depth of the mass began to moan and make despairing noises. The lines started to disorganize further away from the auras of the leaders at the core.

Supernatural fear.

Samantha Invoked the many, many things she possessed that helped with leadership — [Goddess], [Slayer: Aura of Conquest], [Ascendant], [Acting (Grandmaster)], etcetera — and made her voice a booming force carrying from one horizon to the next, as she called, “Hold firm, righteous men! March with order; march with courage in this, your finest hour! You are made of finer grit than this foul dirt we trample.” She spun her staff and lifted it high, casting a divine spell that empowered her words to bolster morale and bestow additional resistance to fear. “Believe what resides in your heart of hearts instead of your eyes. This place is a lie, and we are the inquisitors! Judges! Executioners. Mark my words: through you, that tower shall fall!”

Above every invocation, above every word, gesture, and System effect, one thing perhaps trumped the rest in efficacy: she believed every word she said.

The nerves of every soldier were suddenly steeled, and call after call of support came. “The tower shall fall!” “Victory to the alliance!” “Hail the goddess!”

A drum beat began and was repeated down the line, and soon the march went forward in a more perfect, determined step than before.

Forward from the elites in the center, across the other side where the lines of undead soldiers waited, they all parted as the alliance soldiers neared. Their own elites were unveiled: ancient undead in gray plate armor — known by the aura of death around them and the red glow of their eyes. Also scattered among them were the clearer visages of uncovered mummified faces or bare skulls, their forms draped in layers of robes and decked in jewelry.

Liches. Of course, there are liches.

This was not all. One figure still approached from behind these ranks, known simply by feel — for here was where all the dread was focused. It was the wellspring that the entire undead army sprang from. A deeper shadow cast over the whole zone at his approach, before he could even be seen. The Second Sun dimmed and found its radius diminished.

An undead being with a body of voided space finally walked out from the rest and took steps without concern or fear, right out from the line — into the radius of the artifact. Breastplate, groin-and-skirt, gauntlets, boots, and shoulder pieces, with a helm like a demonic vulture’s skull, this was his armor of bone. He wielded a longsword and a nine-tailed whip that ended in smoking voids like little cuts in reality. Quickly, Samantha realized the smoking was due to the Second Sun’s radius. Otherwise, it would just be uniform blackness.

Everything else to the being’s form was similar to the end of the whip tails. He was a wraith without apparent physical substance. Complete blackness, only spoiled by the edges turning to smoke and rising up into the air. Even his eyes held nothing, not even the telltale red glow. Somehow, this was the most eerie thing of all about him.

“Welcome.” His voice was a dreadful, deep croak with another layer underneath that sounded like someone screaming from a distance. The croak carried for leagues. “I hope you’ve found the accommodations of our humble abode unpleasant so far.”

Samantha, not inclined to be outdone, used her deific ‘outside voice’ in reply. “That’s quite fair to say, yes.”

“Excellent. Allow me to deepen your experience; take it to the next level.”

“Downward, you mean?”

“Indeed.”

“Who is that, in there?” came a fainter call, from the unassuming knight, Odysseus. “Are you the replacement general?”

The wraithly figure’s bone skull turned slightly. “And you are?”

“Just a curious soul. Odysseus is the name.”

“An odd one. I’m afraid I must decline returning this favor. It’s more fair to say I have no name yet. After this… perhaps the ‘Great Butcher’ or something like that. Suffice to say, I’m the one who will flay your soul and take the body leftover as a trophy. Possibly an animated one.”

Odysseus sniffed derisively. “You’re nothing more than a shell.”

“Oh, I’d say I’m far more than that!” Like a lightning bolt, he acted, slinging his whip in a chickenshit sucker shot.

What a little bitch move!

Samantha was faster than that, though. He was fast, giving her a very tiny window to react, and she was at a distance, which limited what she could do. She tried to just hit him with a telekinetic slam effect through [Matter, As Spirit], figuring even a slight shift would throw off his attack…

Something within his body seemed to just absorb it. It bore no signs of any ability she was familiar with. It was exceptionally odd and hard to read, but she took a fraction of a second to observe what was happening spiritually.

All she could discern was that it went somewhere and did something.

Regardless, her effect was totally nullified.

Right on her heels, the super-fast Odysseus acted, too, and on the closest thing he could: the whip. His blade came up to slice it — and passed through it like it was a liquid. Nothing!

The whip carried through, and he cracked it, aiming for one thing in particular: the Second Sun.

Before Samantha could throw away one of her limited defensive actions to protect this vital, priority item from the enemy, a minotaur pulled some supernatural ‘position trade’ shit with Leiande, thereby taking the strike on his double-headed axe and hand. Samantha had little choice but to honor the sacrifice, especially since it seemed rather manageable as it was, and her needing to conserve her resources.

Multiple whip cracks hit the minotaur’s axe and his hand. Wound negations shredded, and he let out a pained howl — then it cut off as he disintegrated into nothing right along with his axe.

All hell then broke loose.
