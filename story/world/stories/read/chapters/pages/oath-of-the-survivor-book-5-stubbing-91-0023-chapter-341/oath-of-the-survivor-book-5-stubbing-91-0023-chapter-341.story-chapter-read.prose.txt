The next morning, Kyle was awoken by a notification from the Courier’s Guild on his nav bracelet, letting him know that a visitor was waiting.

[WHO IS IT, DR. MAYHEW?]

“Not sure,” Kyle said, stretching as he rose. He knew Jarberry would have just let himself in, but nobody else bothered to send a message. Given that whoever it was had already received clearance through the guild, Kyle wasn’t too worried, but decided to play it safe. With the C.H.A.D.D. pack slung over his shoulder and bound tight with a quick set of cords, he slowly opened the door.

The moment he did, a focused blast of force knocked him back a step, just as a heavy fist careened straight toward his nose. Bewildered, Kyle allowed himself to fall backward as Synaptic Barbs gave him a better picture of the figure entering his apartment, followed by a trio of drones that Kyle only now realized were projecting a stealth field.

The dark-skinned man missed Kyle’s face, but snagged him by the C.H.A.D.D. pack strap and pulled him up to glower at him. “When the hell were you planning on telling me that you made it back to the Hub?” The man growled.

“It’s good to see you too, Marcus,” Kyle said, unable to suppress the broad smile breaking across his face.

“None of that,” Marcus rebuked him, letting Kyle go and closing the door behind him. “You and I have a problem, and we’re going to have it out right now. Why the hell didn’t you try to contact me when you got back? Do you have any idea how much of a mess you stirred up across the Gray Conglomerate? Hell, kid, I had to take extra precautions just to get here and see you without making things worse.”

Marcus stopped for a moment, looking Kyle up and down before his eyes stopped on his missing left arm. “I saw the video, you know.” His voice was softer, a deep pain somehow conveyed through it. “I thought you were dead. You should be dead.”

Kyle met the older man’s eyes. “I would have been, if it wasn’t for C.H.A.D.D. and the work you did to provide the upgrades.”

[INCIDENTALLY, MR. KULHAVEY, WE WOULD LIKE TO SCHEDULE SOME TIME FOR REPAIRS. I MAY HAVE TAKEN SOME DAMAGE TO HELP DR. MAYHEW RECOVER.]

Marcus frowned, pulling out his tablet as Kyle felt the drones behind him run a quick scan. “You realize those components weren’t meant to be used in a human, don’t you?”

[I NEEDED SOMETHING TO PROVIDE AN ANCHOR FOR THE WORLD TREE SEED, WHILE AIDING DR. MAYHEW IN HIS ABILITY TO INFLUENCE IT. MY OPTIONS WERE LIMITED.]

“Well, it kept the kid alive, so that’s something. Still, the damage is… wait a second…” Marcus trailed off, tapping furiously at the tablet as a few more screens appeared, one after another. Finally, he looked back at Kyle, a severe expression on his face.

“These aren’t the components I made. Not exactly. Tell me, where did you get these?”

Kyle was confused for a moment, until he thought back to his meeting with Administrator Delta. “When you say these aren’t the components you made, what do you mean?” Kyle asked.

“The cybernetics in your body still have some resonance with C.H.A.D.D., but they’re too cleanly aligned with your own mana. I’ve never seen anything like it.”

Kyle’s gut reaction was to try and explain it away, but that only lasted a moment. Marcus had taken risks to get out here to see him, despite the Gray Conglomerate’s attacks through the Tribunal. There weren’t a lot of people that Kyle trusted, but Marcus Kulhavey was one of them. So, gesturing for Marcus to sit, he told the story of his evolution to C Grade, and the meeting with Administrator Delta.

When he was done, Marcus just let out a slow exhale. “You’ve gotten yourself mixed up in some dangerous stuff, Kyle. I’m just glad you’re alive.”

“That makes two of us,” Kyle joked, trying to add some levity back to the room. “Still, I have to ask, why did you come all the way out here? Isn’t the Gray Conglomerate going to cause problems?”

Marcus snorted. “Let ‘em. I’ve got standing offers to move my manufactory to two of the other member civilizations. The Gray Conglomerate just has the most attractive tax treatment. And I came out to make sure that you were actually alive, and to talk about the disaster that happened on Kertan Six. Because you have rights after getting caught up in it, and I don’t want to see those rights get trampled.”

Kyle frowned. “What do you mean?”

“I mean that Corthian Mining and everybody else involved in that mess owes you a hell of a lot more than an ‘I’m sorry’ and a smile. Even before it got out that you somehow survived, Angela Corthus had a lot of criticism for excessive force in purifying the planet. Then there was the issue of different groups trying to get access to the planet prior to the auction, which ended up with an assassin getting sent after you, and from the sound of it, nearly succeeding. You’re entitled to something for all that trouble.”

[WHAT, SPECIFICALLY, IS DR. MAYHEW ENTITLED TO?] C.H.A.D.D. asked, and Kyle felt genuine interest coming through the bond.

“To start, they owe you credits. Lots of them. I’m sure they’re all waiting to see the outcome of your Tribunal before making an offer, but don’t let them get away with a low-ball settlement. They need to prove that they made every reasonable effort to check the planet before and after that bastard Perhades wiped it clean. If they didn’t, they’re liable.” The heat was rising in Marcus’ voice again, but he took a breath before continuing.

“There’s also the matter of restitution for this damnable auction. You were living on that planet for years. Built yourself a home there, from all reports. That means you have rights for being forced to relocate. You’re giving up a home that they forced you to make when they abandoned the planet, which also means that you should get a slice of whatever proceeds come from the planet’s sale. Your share might not be a ton, but don’t let them tell you that you don’t get a seat at the table.”

Marcus continued his rant as Kyle continued listening; an idea forming. Their theory that the Tribunal was being used to buy time for something still held a lot of merit, but he couldn’t help but feel that the missing component was found in some of the rights that Marcus kept explaining. It wasn’t simply credits they were concerned with; it was that Kyle possessed some rights to the planet, and rights for compensation.

“Marcus,” Kyle interrupted, “When I was stranded on Kertan Six, I thought about trying to create a place for the other refugees from Earth, potentially leasing out some space in exchange for supplying labor to the other organizations that set up there. In lieu of credits, do you think that’s something that could be negotiated?”

“Kyle, everything can be negotiated in the Gray Conglomerate. But why are you still so fixed on this? They can find their own way, Kyle. Goodness knows the rest of us had to.”

“If what you said about Corthian Mining is true, I doubt that they’re in a good place right now,” Kyle countered. “I’m just looking to find ways to create opportunities for them. That’s it.”

“You’re trying to be a damn hero again,” Marcus countered with a growl, though his expression softened a bit. “Though you may have a point. Corthian is in a rough spot, and if there was a time to lend a hand, this might be it. Still, you’ll need some deep pockets to compete in the purchase of a planet. How are your funds looking?”

“Pretty light,” Kyle admitted. “I was kind of hoping the labor force and promise to help with the reconstruction would cover most of it.”

“It’s something, but it’s no substitute for credits,” Marcus said, shaking his head as he tapped on his tablet a few more times. “The good news is that there’s still some time before the auction. There aren’t very many who have formally expressed their interest in the purchase, but they’re still going through the diligence process, which takes time. And, after they found you on the planet and it got attacked, the Collective has taken a lot of pains to ensure that future visits are much more supervised. Hell, Cain Jewelers lost their right to participate after it came out that they were circumventing the sealed system requirements.”

“Wait, really? They can’t participate at all?” Kyle pressed.

“Nope. Of course, their affiliate organizations still can, but the Collective has its eye on this now. Irregularities are going to get noticed.” Marcus tapped his finger on the edge of the pad for a moment, clearly lost in thought. Finally, he looked at Kyle again. “I’m guessing there’s no world where you just let this go, and get on with your life?”

“I at least want to try,” Kyle said.

“And then what?” Marcus pressed. “Are you going to live there, try to be some type of hero? Governor? King?”

The answer came to Kyle faster than he thought it would. “No way. I don’t have a desire to lead, or frankly, to be that involved at all. I just think the refugees deserve a shot to have a life where they can make something more of themselves.”

Marcus nodded. “It’s a noble dream, Kyle. Just know that it’s a hell of a lot harder than you’re making it out to be. I can do a little poking around while I draft some schematics to repair our drone friend here. What kind of connections do you have in the Gray Congolmerate that you’d want to work with?”

“There’s not a lot,” Kyle said after a moment of thought. “The biggest would probably be Pragmus Meat Purveyors, though there have been a handful of other small enterprises since joining the Guild.”

He pulled up his nav bracelet, and was about to send the list to Marcus, who gave him a flat look. “What are you doing, kid?”

“I was going to send the names?” Kyle said, confusion turning the statement into a question.

“I came all the way to see you in person for a reason, Kyle. Just tell me, don’t send them. Last thing you want is to have any more accusations of collusion now.”

That was a point that Kyle hadn’t considered. Once they finished reviewing the list, Marcus just whistled. “You were busy back then.”

“I was,” Kyle agreed. “And looking forward to getting back to it.”

“I can tell you that you won’t be doing nearly as many jobs,” Marcus warned. “C Grades don’t get handed simple projects very often.”

Checking his nav bracelet, Kyle just smiled. The details for his next special assignment came through earlier that morning while they were talking, leaving two days to prepare before it began. “No, no they don’t.”

“You already got one lined up? Who with?” Marcus asked with a raised eyebrow.

“I’ll be heading to a world in Pillar territory. They have some data that needs to be collected, and I’m the man for the job.”
