King Rothavaro crashed into the steel street of Atlantis, the drop causing a small cloud of dust to rise up, and leaving a small crater where he landed. Caretakers were already rushing in to repair it as he moved purposefully away. He had to admit that the automatons were slightly less unnerving now that they were repaired, appearing as somewhat generic looking humans, in simple grey uniforms.

He had no time for them, of course, his mind was on the same thing it had been for months: his son. Calevaro was out there somewhere, and he needed to find him. He’d been distracting himself as much as possible, but those diversions were growling less effective, and he could feel himself slipping into more and more dangerous thoughts.

He leapt skyward once more, hopping from building to building, ignoring the stunned faces within as he moved to the western section of the city. Atlantis was truly transformed at this point, and in addition to becoming the new headquarters for the CFC, refugees from numerous countries were now calling it home.

Roth wondered how they could feel safe with the Master’s Tower looming over them, but he supposed that this world didn’t truly understand what that meant. Regardless, he had his own concerns.

A few minute’s travel brought him to a shorter, more sprawling building that Michael Bishop had claimed for his new organization. The man was nothing if not a fastidious worker, and he’d leveraged old connections to recruit hundreds of former military operatives. The as-yet unnamed group were becoming the foremost intelligence operation on the planet, utilizing Awakened technology to monitor a struggling world.

Rothavaro respected his efforts, but those thoughts were buried beneath fury and helplessness in equal measure. As such, he didn’t hesitate before leaping through the uppermost window, shattering it as he landed in Michael’s office.

The iron-nerved Bishop didn’t even look up from his desk as Roth used his gift to restore the glass in an instant.

“King Rothavaro,” Michael said, still typing on his laptop.

The king looked around the large, sparsely decorated room with a dismissive gaze, then moved to stand in front of the other man. “It’s done,” he said.

Michael nodded. “So I’ve heard. The last of the Follower facilities has been destroyed. Well done.”

Roth grunted. “There’s still more of the rats. They scurry and hide throughout this world, and we’ll be hunting them for years–if we have years.”

“Perhaps not,” Michael said, finally closing the laptop and standing up. He moved to a large monitor on the wall, and brought up some images with a gesture. “We’ve spotted a number of them, and their behavior has become quite unusual for Followers.”

Roth watched as video clips and images were overlaid, one after the other. They depicted Followers in small groups, huddling together, hiding, or running. They helped one another, and even looked…afraid.

Michael gave the king a moment to digest what he was seeing, then spoke. “With their leadership and upper ranks all absent, the Followers are beginning to demonstrate a previously unseen level of independence. They’re not attacking anymore. Most are just trying to escape us.”

Roth’s eyebrows went down. “What are you about to suggest, Bishop?”

Michael gave a shrug. “We know that most, if not all of the remaining Followers were converted from other races–including our own. It could be that their former selves are re-emerging. If so, exterminating them becomes…complicated.”

The king grunted again. “There’s a great deal of moral philosophy to consider then, and truthfully, I don’t care. You know what I care about.”

Michael nodded, shutting down the monitor. “Prince Calevaro. He occupies my thoughts a great deal–though obviously not so much as your own.”

Roth’s fist went through Michael’s desk, though the speed of the strike left the rest of the desk intact. Michael didn’t even react. Somehow that made the king feel worse.

“My apologies,” he mumbled, struggling to pull his fist out without causing more damage.

“It’s of no consequence,” Michael replied. “I recognize the strain you’re under. How’s Corvale?”

Roth barked a small laugh. He had no doubt the other man knew the answer to that question with more precision than the king himself. “The repairs are ongoing. The single functioning limb was able to stabilize the city, and the real work has begun. Making it walk again is far beyond us, however.”

“Understandable. I believe they’re trying to get the Caretakers to examine it, but they’ve run into challenges with leaving the city. The automatons simply walk back to Atlantis.”

Roth grunted, already exhausted by the topic. “I need to be doing something, Michael.”

“The Dimensional Bleeds continue,” the other man said with a shrug. “Twilight monsters have appeared, perhaps some worthy of you.”

“Director Newton already contacts me when such are discovered. There aren’t enough to keep me occupied.” He met the shorter man’s dark eyes. “I cannot claim to know your brother as well as some. He was…a challenging man to understand. But I know a schemer when I see one.”

Michael’s lip twitched for a fraction of a second, nearly a smile. “That is a fair, if incomplete assessment of Ethan.”

“There’s more to his plan, isn’t there? More than he told us.”

Michael put his hands behind his back and walked slowly around the room. “Ethan always loved to plan,” he said. “But there was little he could organize beyond what he brought with him to Potentia.”

Rothavaro’s eyes narrowed. “Little, not nothing.”

Michael paused, then turned back to him and nodded. “Come with me,” he said, then moved purposefully out the double doors.

The king followed him out into the hallway, ignoring the wide-eyed looks from numerous people in local business attire. The sounds of a hundred people moving to get a look at the sudden commotion irritated Rothavaro, who was in no mood to be gawked at. Yet at a hand gesture from Michael, the hall was cleared, and doors closed.

The man should be a king.

Rothavaro pondered that for a moment, then decided the thought was dangerous. Michael would be Twilight rank soon, his Follower Familiar growing in power at an alarming rate. Combined with a private army… Roth shook his head. He trusted Michael. Mostly.

“Where are we going?” he asked.

“Not far, just the courtyard outside.”

Roth nodded, following awkwardly in the narrow hallway, his royal cloak brushing against all sorts of Earth technology he hadn’t bothered to learn about. When they finally passed through glass doors and out into a courtyard that had been decorated with local plants, he stood up straighter and stretched. At first he saw no one, but his spiritual senses drew him to a solitary figure gardening in the far corner of the rooftop.

“Amala,” Michael said, “this is King Rothavaro. You lived in his city for a time.”

A smallish woman with darker skin and colorful attire stood up, brushing dirt from her hands. She looked at the king with shock, then stumbled back. “I think I remember a king,” she said. “But we didn’t meet. I was in a walking city. Can you believe it, Michael? It walked.”

“It did indeed,” Michael said, face blank as always. The woman immediately turned back to gardening, as if their interaction were complete.

Roth raised an eyebrow, and spoke while watching the odd woman. “She is the one the Chosen rescued from beneath the ice,” he said. “She watched over the twins for a time. I think…” He paused, lowering his voice. “My impression was that she wasn’t entirely…” He searched for the right word. Nexum didn’t have the same focus on mental wellness that Earth did.

“You’re correct,” Michael said. “My brother saw a victim. Someone who’d been used, locked underground, and taken advantage of for decades. He rescued her, because that’s what he does.”

“That’s what he does,” Rothavaro echoed. Even after training the lad for months, he still had a hard time aligning the man the Chosen had become with who the king had seen him as. Their first meeting had been…unpleasant. “With respect, Bishop, I didn’t think you the type to become a minder for one such as this.”

“Generally speaking, you’re correct. There are specialists I’d have given her to, if not for the other issues.”

“And what issues might those be?” Rothavaro asked.

Michael addressed the woman. “Amala, can you please tell me about Jack Foster?”

“Foster?” the king said. “The traitor who nearly stole Atlantis?”

“Indeed. A former director of the Broken Covenant, and traitor to the world.”

Amala stood up, a contented smile on her face. “Oh Mr Foster. He was such a kind man. I worked with him for many years. He always had questions, and tasks that needed to be done.”

The king frowned. “You remember him fondly, then?” That was a surprise. Foster had been the one to keep her locked in a bunker for half a century or more.

“Oh, yes of course!” Amala said. “Such a kind man.”

Roth shook his head. “This is a bit tragic,” he whispered to Michael, “but I don’t understand what we’re doing here.”

Again Michael spoke directly to the woman. “Amala, what did Foster ask you to do for him? After you left your home, of course.”

The woman frowned, as if in concentration. Then her face brightened. “Oh! I remember. He asked me to talk to him, now and again. It was hard when I was in the walking city. I had to go out to a big bridge in order to hear him, but I did so every week, like he asked.”

Rothavaro felt heat in his face, as rage threatened to take over. “She…she was in contact with him?”

“She was,” Michael confirmed. “She was his spy, though she doesn’t truly understand what that means. That’s why she’s here with me. She is a kind woman, and content to garden the days away. But the more I ask about her work with Foster, the better I understand why he kept her around.”

“What do you mean?” Rothavaro asked. He thought he might have been told something about this, but hadn’t been interested at the time.

“Better to show than tell,” Michael said, then turned back to the woman. “Amala, do you remember my brother? His name is Ethan.”

She thought for a long moment, then smiled and nodded. “Oh of course I do! Doctor Bishop is such a nice boy. He would bring me food sometimes. And he let me look after those two sweet children for a time. Is he coming to visit?”

Michael shook his head. “No, unfortunately my brother is away on business. But I think he needs our help, Amala. Can you tell us how to help him?”

The king’s eyes widened as Amala nodded, and a strange power began to pulse from within her.
