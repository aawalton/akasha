The dizzy mouse pointed a shaky finger in a direction before pulling it back to hold tightly. Alexander accelerated slowly for her sake and raised their elevation high enough to go over the city walls.

Both mice let out squeaks, one with delight and one with terror.

After an hour’s travel through the air, Alexander tired. Min Min had gotten used to flying enough to point out the end of the shifting rock plane, and Alexander descended. Flick decided to take advantage of the height and leapt off Alexander’s shoulder to have a nice glide downward.

He’s getting close, Alexander thought.

The pillars were dense as well, and he could see that if they were too close to a pillar, the radiated energy would strike them even if they hovered over the ground. Alexander stared at the dark pillars the lightning would strike near and concentrated on trying to memorize the timing. He was impressed by Min Min, who had figured out the pattern herself. She was far braver than her initial impression upon him.

He switched on his glasses and watched the magic, and that made things a bit easier. It still wasn’t simple by any means.
