Chapter 9

Help

When faced with the prospect of single-handedly reenacting the Valentine’s Day Massacre at the hands of a full team of adventurers, the feathered girl before us reacts in what might be absolutely the most natural way possible: She screams, she cowers, and she begs for her life.

“Ah! No, wait! Please don’t hurt me! I just wanted to say hello …”

Hunkered down on the ground before me, with her arms raised to protect her head, is a child that I’d guess to be between eight and twelve years old. It’s that weird age bracket where they’re just tickling puberty, so I can tell she’s older than Korrigan, but isn’t really in the thick of it yet, so she’s clearly younger than Ayre or myself.

Her side-swept feather cut hair (at least I think it’s hair and not very fine, downy feathers) is a rusty auburn reminiscent of pine bark, and her big, terrified eyes are the bright green of fresh foliage. The feathers down her arms start out the same reddish-brown as her hair, but rapidly transition to pure white by the time they’re off her shoulders, forming broad, pseudoluminescent wings that are currently draped protectively across her form.

She’s barefoot … or, well, bare-talon, as her lower legs are sheathed in scales up to a little below her knees, giving an impression like boots. As for what she is actually wearing, that’s a China silk shift dress, left sleeveless probably for the sake of her wings. Since the fabric is so light, it’s semi-sheer. I can’t really make out solid details through it, but it would be enough to put her decency in danger if she weren’t wearing a brown, form-fitting underlayer of a high-neck, cropped surplice and side-folded boyshorts. The collar of the top pokes out of the neck of the dress, though it leaves the front of her actual neck open.

I’d be embarrassed to wear it, but it looks both youthful and pure on her. It doesn’t do anything to hide how wide her hips and thighs are, though. Maybe it’s a harpy thing, since they’re bird people, but why do I keep running into people in this world with better thighs than I have?! First boys, now young girls! It’s enough to give a gal a complex!

All that to say that I want to pat her head and feed her snacks. I definitely don’t want to perforate her like a turkey.

My gun barrel is already starting to lower out of readiness all on its own, but while emotional conflict might be on my face, Korrigan’s is etched with confusion.

“What language is she speaking? I can’t understand a word of it!”

And Ayre’s face is tense, his bow still held completely steady. “That’s because she’s not speaking a language at all. That’s a dungeon monster!”

“A what?!” The little oni jerks her attention from the bird girl to stare at the elf in shock. “But she’s so pretty!”

“Come on, Ayre,” I put in with a nervous laugh. “Just because she’s a dungeon monster, you don’t have to be so tense! We’re good friends with a whole ship full of dungeon monsters, after all!”

“Sure, now, we are,” the archer counters. “But first, they tried to kidnap me!”

“That’s just because they thought you’d look cute in a sailor uniform!” I shake a scolding finger at him. “And, for the record, they were right!”

That manages to get him to lower his bow as he focuses on me instead of the girl. And promptly whacks my forehead with the weapon.

“We really need to discuss your poor impulse control, Ayre.”

My nonchalant reaction to his abuse seems to drive his ears into a waggling fury. “It was because they wanted to turn me into another dungeon monster!”

“Because you would look cute in a sailor uniform.”

“Don’t just repeat yourself like you think it proves your case! And the last sapient dungeon monsters we encountered tried to either kill us or trap us in an eternal dream!”

Korrigan nods firmly in support of that argument. “I was there for that one.”

“Besides,” Ayre continues as he sweeps a dramatic arm toward the girl, who flinches away from the limb, “you’re the one who told us to keep our eyes open for anything weird! What’s weirder than a tiny, little backwater dungeon like this having a sapient dungeon monster?!”

“I don't know, maybe if she were playing a banjo and singing Public Domain Joe?” I suggest with a shrug. But then I cross my gun arm under my chest and place the fingers of my other hand against my cheek. “Though, actually, it kind of makes sense. A dungeon as small as the Forest Cavern probably can’t support many true sapients. It must have invested most of its power in creating her. But then the question becomes, why? She’s not a powerful defender. If anything, she gives me mascot vibes.”

The girl is slowly starting to unfold herself as she realizes we’re talking amongst ourselves instead of hurting her. She looks up at me in particular, and then scrambles toward me, gripping the hem of my skirt while she’s still on her knees.

“You! I can understand you,” she proclaims. “Everyone else that comes in here sounds like gibberish, but I can understand you!”

The others tense up at the sudden motion, but I make a deliberate show of openly holstering my pistol for their sakes. Then I take the girl’s hands in mine and kneel down to her level.

“I can understand you, too,” I tell her with a warm smile on my face. “And I’m sorry we pointed our weapons at you. We were just on edge, and you surprised us. We’re not going to hurt you, I promise.”

My words settle her down, but she still meets my eyes. “But why? Why can’t I understand anybody else?”

“You can understand me because I'm a Hero,” I explain. “We aren’t actually speaking the same language at all, but the Essence translates the concepts behind our words into the language the other can understand. It's something only Heroes get because we've got a direct connection to the Essence. In that way, I’m a lot like you, the way you're bound to Arcana. You do know that you're a dungeon monster, don’t you?”

Slowly, she nods. “I understand that I was born of the Forest Cavern and exist to help it fulfill its purpose, but if doing so means I need to communicate with people, why didn’t it make me able to do so?”

I rub the backs of her hands with my thumbs. “I don’t believe it to be a deliberate oversight. In fact, I think it is more likely that the Dungeon Core, itself, doesn’t know the language of the outside world, and can’t give you knowledge it doesn’t have. The good news is that this is a handicap that can be overcome. It used to be that even Heroes couldn’t communicate with dungeon monsters, but I figured out how to do so, and that knowledge now allows the Essence to translate even their speech and writings.”

Her green eyes still peer up at me. “But how does that help me? I can’t do that.”

“You’re a smart girl. You can learn the language, yourself, if you put your mind to it! And then the core will learn from you, and you’ll be able to greet everyone that comes through here!” I gently guide her back to her feet. “But first, we need your help with an even bigger problem. The whole region outside of the Forest Cavern will be in danger if we don’t come up with a solution.”

That idea clearly disturbs her. She gasps, her eyes wide. “Oh no! That sounds horrible! What’s wrong?! What could I possibly do to help?!”

Instead of immediately answering, I rub her fingers again. “I'm Remmi. Do you have a name, hon?”

She opens her mouth, but pauses, then jerks her head back and forth. “I’m sorry!”

I crack a grin, unable to help myself. “Sorry is a terrible name! You can do better.”

The harpy’s face immediately breaks into confusion, and she begins to sputter. “I … What? No, I mean …”

Behind us, Ayre groans, hips tossing to the side opposite his eyes are rolling to. “Oh, come on, Remmi! I can only understand half the conversation, and even I can tell you’re just tormenting her now!”

I remove one hand from hers to wave it back and forth at both of them. “Sorry, sorry! I couldn’t resist!”

The elf crosses his arms across his stomach and juts his hip the other direction. “Region in danger, Remmi!”

“I know, I know! I’ll focus!”

I turn back to the bird girl and take her other hand up again so I’m holding both of them before me. “Alright, then, is it alright if I give you one? How about Angel?”

She wrinkles her nose. “That’s the first word you’ve said that I couldn’t understand.”

I smile gently. “That’s because it’s a word in my native language that you don’t really have a concept for, but with your brilliant, white wings, I can’t help but feel it’s appropriate. You see, if it could be said to mean anything, it would be a being who comes down from the heavens in service of the divine.”

But her face falls at my words. “Oh, that sounds too grand for me.”

I turn my smile up to beaming as I give her arms a swing. “Nonsense! Names are meant to be artistic!” I jerk my head toward my best friend. “Do you really think that elf over there has anything to do with the air?”

“Remmi! That’s not what my name means!”

I ignore him and keep my focus on the harpy. “Besides, if you find it aspirational, doesn’t that just mean it gives you a goal to live up to?”

She thinks it over for a bit, but finally nods. “Okay. That sounds good. And it’s better than not having a name at all, right?”

“Right!” I chirp in agreement. “Welcome to Serazin Province, Angel!”

That finally puts a smile on her face as she laughs and thanks me, but then her mind catches up with the moment. “But what does that have to do with the region being in danger?”

I’m still smiling back. “Nothing! I just needed something to call you!”

“... Oh. Yeah, that makes sense …”

“Remmi …” Ayre mutters again, but I wave him off.

“I’m getting there!”

I turn once more back to Angel, reactivating my Serious Mode. “See, the reason why we’re answering the crisis by coming here, and why we need your help, is that the threat to the region is actually coming from the Forest Cavern.”

The harpy gasps, recoiling from me. “The Forest Cavern is the threat?! No, that’s not possible! It can’t be! It wouldn’t do that!”

That crosses me as a rather odd thing to say, for some reason. We’ve seen our share of dungeons, and even a decent sampling of sapient dungeon monsters, but they’ve never tried to argue that their dungeon isn’t a threat. In fact, most of them seem pretty proud of it.

But Angel acts as if I just claimed the priesthood ate babies.

“Let’s calm down a moment and gather our facts,” I urge her calmly. “Most dungeons seem to be pretty hostile to outsiders. Not all of them, true, but they don’t invalidate the trend, and even they still see outsiders as a resource. But you say that’s not the case here. Alright, I believe you. But why isn’t it the case?”

And with all of the fervor of one defending a beloved master, she answers me. “Because that would go against everything we’re here to do!”

Oh? So there’s a definite goal, then? Interesting. “And what is that, Angel?”

“We want to behave ourselves!”

My blood runs unexpectedly cold at those words. It’s a lousy mission statement, vague and poorly defined, but that’s not what causes my face to pale and my grip on Angel’s hands to loosen.

Ayre immediately takes a concerned step toward me. “... Remmi? What’s wrong?”

I know where those words came from, and I rotate my frozen expression toward the archer.

“... We’ve got a problem …”
