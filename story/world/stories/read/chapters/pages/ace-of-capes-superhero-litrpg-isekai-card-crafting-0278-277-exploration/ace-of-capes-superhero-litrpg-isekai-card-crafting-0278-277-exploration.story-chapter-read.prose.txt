Lexie appeared in Dewie’s room with a knot in her stomach.

Unlike with Xena, she didn't feel as apprehensive about showing up unannounced. She also didn’t want to go through the whole turmoil of knocking on the door and possibly encountering Veronica or Vera first. That would be undoubtedly unpleasant.

Dewie hadn’t seemed upset when she texted him she was coming over, and she didn’t think he would be mad to see her, too.

But there was still a faint voice in her head, telling her that Dewie would turn her away and would refuse to see her, just like Xena did.

She found him on his bed, frowning down at his pad and typing frantically. He was so focused on it that he didn’t even seem to hear her walking up.

Even as her shadow was cast over his screen, he was still typing, with a frustrated look on his face.

“Who are you talking to?”

Dewie yelped and jolted, ultimately falling out of bed.

Lexie giggled as she held out her hand and helped him back to his feet. “You okay?”

“Yeah,” he said, rubbing his spine. “You?”

“Yup. Good as new. Who were you talking to back then? You seemed pissed.”

“I wasn’t talking to anyone, just responding to comments.”

“About what?”

His scowl returned full force. “Nothing. They’re just idiots who don't know what they're talking about.”

The pad was blank, and Lexie couldn’t see what had been on Dewie’s SI, but she could make a lucky guess. Only a few things would make Dewie that mad. “Were they comments about me?”

Dewie pressed his lips together, as though he didn’t want the truth to leak out, even as his face reddened with his irritation.

“Dewie…” Lexie prompted.

“They’re dumb buttheads!” He finally exploded. “I was watching a video of your rescuing all those people in Indigo, and someone who said they know you, said you and the heroes conspired to fake the whole thing just to make you look cool and to make everyone like heroes again."

“How does anyone fake something like that?”

“That’s what I said! That's why they're idiots, and when I wanted to nicely correct them, because they were lying on the NET, they all laughed and called me a fanboy. I’m not just a fanboy! I’m your best friend.”

Lexie smiled. After coming down from the heaviness of the visit with Xena, it was nice knowing that this was what Dewie was dealing with and that he was defending her so vehemently, even over something so silly.

As he lifted the pad again to continue his typing, she took it out of his hands.

“Okay, first of all," She put her hand on his shoulder. “Stop arguing with randos on the NET. They’re just being contrarian because they enjoy the attention.”

“But–”

“But nothing. Were most of the other comments on the video good or bad?”

"They were good,” he admitted.

"And how many likes did this comment get?”

“Like ten thousand.”

"And the top good comment? How many likes did that get?”

"A million.”

“You see? There's a very small percentage of haters, not worth fighting over. These are just trolls, doing what trolls do."

Dewie pouted, eyebrow still ruffled. “It’s just annoying, seeing people talk about you like that when they don’t even know you.”

“Yeah, but I'm famous now. I’m told it comes with the territory. They'll get it wrong more than they get it right."

"Not if I have anything to do with it," Dewie said stubbornly, making Lexie pause.

"Just how many comment sections have you been arguing in, Dewie?”

Deiw avoided her gaze. “Not much.”

“Dewie, I repeat, you don’t need to fight with trolls on the NET for me.”

“I don't need to, but I want to. I can't let username CatfaceJenkins convince people they know you personally and spread all those lies about you. They need to tell the truth.”

Lexie felt obligated to argue, but she doubted she could dissuade Dewie from spending his time this way. After all, he was the same kid who insisted on telling people about his creepy visions even when he got his ass kicked for it. Things like truth seemed to matter to Dewie. It was why it was always easier to be honest with him. He was also non-judgmental, accepting everything and everyone at face value.

“It was really cool what you did, Lexie,” He spoke up as he reached for the pad. “Saving all those people… I was super proud that you were my best friend.”

Lexief felt herself blush. “Well, it wasn’t that hard.”

“It was hard enough that you passed out for two days.”

“Eh. I just hadn’t had a nap in a while.” Lexie played with the hem of her sweatshirt as she considered her next words. “About the other thing that you heard Stella say…”

"About you killing Vulcan."

“Uh, yeah.” She wasn’t expecting him to say it plainly like that, but that was one of the things she liked about Dewie. He just blurted things out and got right to the point. She found that she preferred it. “I did kill him. But I did it because I thought I was protecting everyone. My dad. Me. You. I'm sorry I–”

“I know,” he said. “You were probably right.”

“I–” Lexie's mouth had already formed the rest of the apology when she paused. “I was?”

“Yeah. A few things have changed since last week. My dad was going to lose his job. I mean, he would have been impeached. He was stressed, so I looked and saw a crane biting his ankles, and then a vision of him being dragged out of his office by law enforcement while people yelled at him. And Professor Madswick was going to get seriously hurt. I saw that one by accident and told him. But then he called me the other day to ask me what else I'd seen, and I told him that his problem was gone."

“I see.” How did those people play into Vulcan’s long-term plans? Were they co-conspirators, or were they simply random tools that would have been used here and there to bring about a bigger plan of destruction?

“I had nightmares,” Dewie continued. “Not of Vulcan. I could never see him, but there were a lot of bad things happening. The sky was dark. The ground was destroyed. Everything is ruined. Everyone was gone." He shrugged. “My premonitions aren’t through dreams, so I figured those were just regular nightmares. But it hasn’t happened since you killed him.”

"Huh." So, if Dewie's dreams were a premonition, then Lexie's killing Vulcan might have stopped world destruction.

Lexie felt much better about that.

“Yeah,” she said, “But to be honest, I didn’t do it to save the world. I just didn’t want him to hurt you as he did to Lucy Frank.”

“Yeah, I know.” He accepted it so simply without another word, and that rendered Lexie stunned. She expected more of a moral quandary, or something that showed he saw her monster.

But he simply picked up his pad again, frowning as though he’d got a comment reply.

“So…” Lexie ventured as he typed. “You’re not mad at me? Or…scared of me?”

“No,” Dewie said simply. “I'm mad at CatfaceJenkins. Now he's saying you're a hack and possessed, and a pawn the Shadowsbane are using to reclaim power in District 8. Where is he getting this stuff from?"

Lexie laughed, and Dewie showed her some of what he was replying to. After a cursory read, it was clear to Lexie that they were really just trying to say outlandish stuff to trigger him and all the other 'Deckheads'. There were about twenty Lexie fans in the comment chain arguing with Catface, and no matter how much Lexie tried to get Dewie to stop, he refused to.

Lexie took some time to go through a couple of her fan pages, and also a pretty dedicated hate page on AllForums. It was crazy how much stuff was in there, how much complete strangers had to say about her.

Some of it was true or at least close to the truth, like the fact that her powers seemed Eldritch-based and that she'd been experimenting with card magic since she was young. There was other stuff in there, though, about her cheating at the AFC, and bullying someone at school, and those were way off-base.

Dewie got into more arguments as Lexie finally materialized one of the cards and stared at it, twirling it in her hands.

“What’s that?” Dewie asked while he typed.

“It’s Vulcan’s memories,” she said. “I trapped it in there so that we could investigate his plans.”

That finally distracted Dewie from his comment wars, with him focusing on the card in her hands with a widened gaze.

“Really?”

“Yeah. But…there’s a lot of information in there. He's lived through several possible futures. It’s probably going to be confusing to decipher what’s real and what’s just a timeline that never materialized."

“You should probably start from the beginning then.”

“I should, shouldn’t I?”

To be honest, no part of her was looking forward to this, even though it would help her figure out what happened to her mother, and it would help them put a stop to any plans Vulcan might have set in place. She didn’t want to be in the mind of someone like Vulcan. She felt like it would be sick and twisted. And his past...only something dark and depressing could form a man like Vulcan.

Ultimately, she had to bite the bullet. The sooner the better.

Out of nowhere, she heard quick footsteps coming down the hall.

“Dewie, mom says–” The door was thrust open. Veronica stood in the doorway, expression slackening with disbelief.

She stared at Lexie for almost half a minute as Dewie asked, "Mom said what?"

Veronica ignored him, still staring.

"Hey," Lexie said. That snapped her out of it.

"Hey," Veronica responded. "Do my mom and Dad know you're here?"

"No. Are you going to tell them?"

She pursed her lips. "No. It's not my business anyway."

Huh. Lexie could have sworn the Veronica she knew would have done just about anything to get her in trouble. But Veronica looked different. More mature somehow. More tired, also.

Her hair was now styled in a sleek armpit-length bob that was longer and fuller than when Lexie last saw it.

“You’re growing your hair again?” Lexie asked.

“Yeah.”

“Looks pretty,” she said. "I've always liked it longer."

Veronica frowned, searching for the insult in the statement. When she couldn’t find it, she simply gestured for Dewie.

“Mom wants you to come try on these new shirts she got for you.”

"Okay," Dewie said, and he hopped out of bed and headed to the door.

After he left with Veronica, Lexie sighed and finally activated the ghoul card.

The first thing that happened was that the room seemed to darken, and the eyes of the ghoul appeared in front of her.

As she stared at it, she was plunged into it, the memories sifting fast, confusing her. It felt like drowning, like drinking from a firehose, with all the visions coming at her all at once.

She'd installed a mechanism that gave her control of which visions to see, but she wasn't exactly trained on how to steer it in the right direction.

It was like driving a vehicle when you'd never driven before, and she had to find the right buttons to push to get where she needed to go.

But eventually, she found it.

She steered herself to one of the earlier memories, ignoring everything else until she felt like she'd come to a good starting image.

John Blumenthal was seven years old.

She knew because she was him.

She was in his head. She felt like him, felt his boredom as he sat on his bay window and stared at the neighbor's house across the street.

From this viewpoint, it seemed like an ordinary neighborhood. It was an ordinary neighborhood, where perfectly ordinary things happened.

This didn’t look like Earth 9. This must have been before he died and before he transmigrated. She didn’t sense any magic in the atmosphere. But it wasn't Earth 2 either.

There were two TVs in the room, one mounted and showing a meeting between an alien life form and a human coalition. The other one was turned off, sitting beside the bed and attached to a strange device that looked like a fridge in the corner. Now and then, it would release a spark of electricity or pulse oddly.

A modulator.

Lexie knew what it was, though she'd never seen it before. She also knew it was used to contain the leakage of radioactive waves into the atmosphere.

It was out of place in the otherwise organized room.

Then, the drizzling began.

It ran down the window in crooked lines across the glass.

John shifted from the window to the bed, where a remote control lay in pieces. He picked up the screwdriver to get back to work.

There was a knock on his door. He knew before it even opened that it was his mother.

“John,” she said carefully, “Why is the television remote in surgery?”

“It’s not surgery,” he replied without looking up. “It’s maintenance.”

He lifted the green circuit board to show her. She smiled indulgently.

“You dropped it,” he continued calmly. “The battery contact bent. That’s why it only works if you press hard.”

She blinked. "I didn't drop it."

He didn't dignify that with an answer.

She had dropped it several times, but he knew she would never admit it. She never admitted to any of the things she did wrong.

He ignored her and straightened the metal tab with deliberate concentration, tongue pressed slightly to the corner of his mouth.

“You could have told me,” she said.

He shrugged. “It was faster to fix it.”

His mother didn't leave, just stood there as though she didn't know what to say to him.

The atmosphere was weird, but John always felt weird. Like everything was wrong with him, or was wrong with the world.

Through the walls, he could hear the sound of neighbors arguing in the apartment next door. Something slammed. A child cried.

John paused, assessed.

“Mrs. Calder is going to leave him,” he said quietly.

His mother stiffened. “You shouldn’t eavesdrop.”

“I’m not,” he says. “She’s been moving things into her car all week, and I can hear them arguing all the time. She should do it sooner rather than later. Because if he finds out what she's up to, he's going to kill her.”

"John." His mother gasped in outrage as he slotted the batteries back into place. "Don't say things like that so casually."

"Why not? It's the truth." He clicked the casing shut and tested the buttons.

The second TV flickered on, and he set the remote back on the bed.

Fixed.

Outside, thunder rolled.

His mother studied him in the dim room.

“How did you know the battery contact was bent?” she asked softly.

He glanced at her like it was obvious.

“It stopped working all at once,” he says. “If it were dead batteries, it would fade first.”

The woman was silent. Then, after a beat, she repeated, "I didn't drop the remote."

He didn't respond. He just watched her and thought about the fact that, despite his warning, Mrs. Calder would probably die soon.

Lexie opened her eyes, realizing she'd been lying prone on the bed, with her eyes closed the entire time. She didn't know how much time had passed. As she sat up, the lingering feelings from the vision persisted, and she shook her head to shake it off.

That was weird. Vulcan was a strange kid. Not malicious or bloodthirsty, just kind of...tired?

Bored?

She didn't have a perfect word to fit.

Also, the scene had been strangely mundane. Lexie had expected something darker to justify why Vulcan had become who he was. But he seemed to have a perfectly normal childhood. Though she'd only seen a slice of it. Maybe that change came later on. Maybe there was a traumatic experience that caused it.

But actually, that was none of her business.

The next time, she wouldn't go so far back. There was probably no need to go through his entire childhood to understand a man like Vulcan. She didn't need to understand him anyway. He was power hungry and wanted to be the winning Chosen. He'd failed. All she needed to figure out was what other things he'd had in place that she needed to take care of.

Next time, she would try to start the vision after his death on his old Earth and after he'd appeared on Earth 9.

Actually, this was also an opportunity to learn how the Chosen process worked for other Chosen.

Who was Vulcan's guide? Why was he Chosen? Why were any of them Chosen?

Since Tate couldn't tell her, she could just figure it out. This was great.

Lexie checked her phone and found that she'd been out for about an hour, and Dewie had left her a text, probably after coming back to the room and finding her taking a 'nap'.

Dewie: Mom, Veronica, and I are going to buy new shirts. The ones Mom bought don't fit. We'll be back soon, so stay put! And you can stay for dinner?

The last question mark was hopeful, and Lexie couldn't turn it down. She told him she could.

Tate had also sent her a text that was pretty much in code, telling her that he'd found a few of the Developers she wanted and knew how to reach them.

Tate: Can't send them over the NET. Will tell you once you get home.

She smiled at that. One more thing to look forward to.

She needed to pass the time somehow. She was tempted to go back into Vulcan's mind to figure out the Chosen stuff, but she wanted to rest her brain first.

In the meantime, she'd always wanted to explore Dewie's castle-like home.

Especially, his father's study.

Maybe she could find more information there about Dewie's mother's powers. Or she could find out what happened to her.

Lexie got out of bed and left her room, keeping an ear out for the sound of anyone coming. The old Victorian home was silent but loud in its grandeur.

It shared some features with the Firebringer residence, especially its gothic decor, but unlike Stella's home, everything here felt too practiced, almost cold but also flamboyant in all the wrong ways.

There were also no servants idling about. They were relegated to their quarters, not to be seen or heard, until they were being utilized.

So Lexie drifted freely down the gilded staircase and the halls, and she found a room with a carved door that seemed to be a study.

Jackpot.

Of course, there was a lock on it, but Lexie put her hand on the wall and unlocked it within a few seconds.

While doing that, DECODE also picked up something else.

There was a secret room in the study. She could feel it, bulging at the back, made with Alchemy.

Interesting.

Once in the study, she drifted towards a grand bookshelf, laying her hands on it.

With a few more minutes of DECODE, the bookshelf shifted, and she was given access to a dark hollow hallway that sent chills down her spine.

The second she stepped a foot in, a voice echoed in her mind, saying,

"WELCOME TO THE ANCIENT TEMPLE OF JANUS, GUARDIAN OF THE KEYS."
