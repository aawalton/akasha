Hammond heard the clinks of a pickaxe hitting rock long before they were ever near. In fact, he could pick up on the sound as soon as they left the inner walls of Wolf’s Den and headed deeper down the cave network. Through the dug-out tunnels, past the brood chambers. Progressively, the echoes grew louder as Omar and Hammond made their way past the lake and its floating gardens.

The Warden of Wolf’s Den was easy to find. You just had to follow the rhythmic sound of digging to its source. The people who worked deeper in the cave found it comforting, if occasionally annoying. Most said they couldn’t even hear it after a while. But for those that had to make a report to the city leader?

It was a little unnerving. Especially how it progressively grew louder. Sharper. Not in the sense that one’s life was in danger, but more in the vein of you have just let some of the sheep drown and now you had to tell your father about it.

“It’s not gonna be that bad, rookie,” said Omar, glancing over to him as they followed the main tunnel deeper into the mountain. They were leaving the lake area, continuing down the way the Warden had dug out. With his abilities, their leader could sense caves and resources hidden below, so he was continuously digging to increase what they had access to. And while he didn’t need to remind them, the constant sounds of his pickaxe did that just fine.

Hammond shot the man walking next to him a quick look before he focused back on the steep, dark tunnel they were navigating. He didn’t want to hit his head, and there was no light, so Hammond had to watch where he walked. Because the rocks of these mountains weren’t the mundane kind one could encounter on the surface, and if he were to hit his head, the old, magic-infused rock would cut into his skin. Fast regeneration or not, he’d rather not have to deal with that.

“Then why did Yarrick ask us to go tell the Warden ourselves?” he asked after a moment, to which Omar snorted.

“Because Yarrick’s a lazy sack of shit. Not because Varni’s some crazed, violent maniac. But there’s potential those two could join some other forces, and we’d want our boss to be aware of that. That’s it.”

Hammond couldn’t argue any of that. Yarrick was a notorious layabout who just happened to be so damn efficient the city couldn’t be run without him. As for the Warden, Varni, Hammond didn’t have much to go on. In the year or so he’d spent in this place, he’d only seen the Warden once during the solstice celebration. He’d heard the man could get pissed on occasion, but otherwise, he was often alone, busy tunneling down the mountain, unless some particularly nasty monster required his attention.

So yeah, Hammond and Omar were probably not about to get beaten all the way to hell and back but still. Their report was not about to do them any favors and so, they kept going down the tunnel, the ominous clinking of the pickaxe hitting hard stone echoing in his ears and chest, until eventually, there was a light at the end of the tunnel and before they knew it, the short form of Varni appeared, right as he swung his pickaxe and struck the wall.

The movement was minimal. Fluid. Sparks flew when the pick drove into the stone, and there was a little flare of something as the rock was forced away from the mountain. Some sort of natural enchantment breaking down, Hammond assumed, though he was no expert in the matter.

The Warden was maybe a head shorter than Hammond, but in this tunnel, it didn’t feel like it. Lord Varni wore simple, comfortable beige overalls under a thick, dark-leather apron that covered all of his front. His short-cropped hair was hidden below a loose helmet, and it was caked with dust while the man ran his left fingers on the rocks in front of him, searching for a weakness. The man’s arms were nearly as thick as Hammond’s thighs, and the fresh A-rank could see why the man ahead of him was often needed when a big beasty showed up in an attempt to claim their cave. And that was even before he took into account the dangerously gleaming pickaxe in his hand. Its handle was pale, almost yellow, and like overgrown roots, it wrapped around the pickaxe’s head, which, in the soft light of the Warden’s hung lantern, glowed with a soft orange, nearly pink sheen.

Dragonbone and adamantium, Hammond had heard.

Omar had come to a stop, and so had Hammond. But after the first few quiet seconds, he wondered if he should say something. Though when he glanced to his senior, he saw the man shake his head, then signal to him to wait.

The Warden continued stared at the stone barring his way, occasionally tapping with his fingers like one would to find a false-wall, though if the Lord had picked up anything special about the spots he was tapping, Hammond couldn’t tell, but eventually, he saw a pattern, and it was narrowing down on a spot in particular, until finally, the warden took a step back, then swung, and it was as if the metal was leaving a trail in its wake, as if the air itself was parting ahead of the sharp metallic point. The pickaxe hit the rock, digging into the softer spot and carving out a head-sized piece that was glowing with blue veins, then in one fluid motion, the Warden stowed the rock away and let out a slow breath before he turned and fixed them with an impassive gaze.

“What’s this about, then?”

Here, Omar took over, and Hammond was a little taken aback with how serious the man had gotten. Gone were the easy-going jokes and crappy humor he’d displayed when Yarrick had been the one taking their report. Omar was matter of fact and to the point. He recounted everything, his impressions, how the B-rank’s summon had gotten the drop on him and dispelled his skill. The aura they displayed. Everything. When the Warden asked a question, the responses were brief and direct, and once all the details were told, the Warden asked one final question.

“And you think they might join those that want us harmed?”

Hammond didn’t react there, but he also could feel his heartbeat pick up. The Warden was taking their report seriously, and while Hammond had no clue whether those two had been part of another faction or intended on joining one, the Warden was taking the possibility very seriously. High B-rank that could threaten and down A-ranks didn’t grow on trees, and while Wolf’s Den was on neutral terms with the Colony, they were still dealing with raids from the Drowned City, and having those promising B-ranks fall in the clutch of either was out of the question. The only reason Hammond and Omar hadn’t dragged those two along was that the old man didn’t feel confident they’d win, and Hammond had to back him there.

After all, they just stared down an A-rank boar and some other hidden creature, after downing two A-rank eagles.

“I can’t answer that, Lord Varni,” Omar said after a short, thoughtful moment. “But it felt too risky to not share what we saw.”

The Warden stood motionless for a moment, his eyes fixed on the pickaxe he now held in front of him, and with a thumb, he wiped some of the dust off, then he nodded.

“Fine. Then let’s go have a talk with these newcomers. If I don’t like what I hear, then I’ll deal with them myself. Tell Yarrick to prep a team. We leave in an hour. And tell him to bring along the new recruits.”

Hammond let out a breath he hadn’t known he’d been holding. But even before it was fully out, his throat closed up as the aura roiled out of the shorter man, and it was as if the tunnels had suddenly woken up. The earth rumbled, and the presence of the Warden loomed over them as his voice echoed against the stone.

“But if you are wasting my time, there will be a reckoning. Understood?”

Hammond didn’t dare twitch a muscle. But Omar managed a breathy, trembling “yes, sir.”

And then the presence was gone, and it took all of Hammond’s will to not drop to his knees.

Maybe it was a good thing that he didn’t see the Warden often, he decided, nearly five minutes after they were allowed to leave the monster’s presence.

Sitting on the ground cross-legged, the small frown on Maurice’s brow twitched while the latter focused on the building magic. A beat later, he disappeared, only to reappear a few yards away, shunted out of the teleportation head-first into the ground. Henry watched as Maurice furiously kicked the air as he wiggled his hands to find a perch, voice muffled, before finally getting a hold and uprooting himself out of the ground.

It was still a little funny to see, but Henry had been humbled just as many times ever since they’d started this process a few hours ago. “Need some water?”

Maurice looked up, still annoyed, then he nodded, and Henry let out another saltwater orb which he kept aloft so Maurice could use it, while he eyed the sun above. It was still mid-morning, and while it was still too soon to tell for sure, Henry had a strong feeling that they were missing a key component to make the fusion of Find the Path and Blink really work.

They’d been both working at this for the last six hours or so. Henry had tried hundreds of permutations already through his own efforts and his Octominds, and every “successful” fusion had ended up with them basically recreating a shitty, disorienting Blink that could have them pop-out into the ground or through a tree.

“It’s not working,” Maurice growled, stomping over before he plopped down on a log. Well. It was more of a fallen tree. One that hadn’t survived their encounter with a pissed-off boar, not too unlike the one they had met a day before. But it had been only a high B-rank, so nothing to write home about.

“It’s just like… like the skill is kicking me out. But I can’t tell why. Or what’s missing. Is it just more power? Do they need to be a higher-rank? Is it something else altogether?”

Henry couldn’t tell. He’d been thinking the same thing. While the skill nexuses resulting from their vision of fusing Blink and Find The Path seemed stable and functional at first, the results—or lack thereof—spoke for themselves. It was, in a way, like successfully turning on a car. But when you hit the accelerator, the car would just roll on itself sideways. It made no sense. It should be working. But it wasn’t.

“I guess we really do need to find that turtle,” Henry said and to that, Maurice just pressed his lips together, and without words, he closed his eyes, and threw himself in another attempt. Henry just let him. It wasn’t like he’d stopped trying. One of his Octominds was still attempting the fusion, but Henry had decided his other Octominds were better served by managing his widening network.

Henry was still getting used to the vastly improved flow of information he had access to at any time, and with how much he’d been peering through them, he felt the Aspect was on the cusp of a new level. Especially ever since he’d re-established contact with Thalis. In fact, Henry was about to have a chat with Zerathstra when he sensed something off close-by, and it only took a moment for him to realize the alert had come from his real body.

At some point, he’d closed his eyes, and opening them, he realized that magic was visibly building up around Maurice, much more than any of their attempts. Henry’s eyes widened. Had the crab cracked the issue?

Henry watched with bated breath, waiting, and then, there was a flash of light—

And Maurice’s telepathic presence went outside his range.

Henry shot up to his feet, looking around, waiting for the crab to reappear. But Maurice didn’t. Not after ten seconds. And not after a hundred.

“Maurice?” Henry asked, knowing full well that the crab couldn’t hear him.
