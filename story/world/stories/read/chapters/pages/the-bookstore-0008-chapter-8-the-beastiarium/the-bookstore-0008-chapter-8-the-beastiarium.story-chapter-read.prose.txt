“Anyway, what I wanted to say is that the best result does not always come from the easiest path or biggest resource. It comes from knowing how to use what you have to its fullest potential,” Anne said with quiet triumph.

Done.

The trap had been laid, and she only needed one final step to turn the boy’s wavering hope into something steadier.

“I cannot solve your problems for you, but I can offer two ideas. First, I do not believe the mountains block everything equally, so while exploring you might eventually find easier passages with weaker beasts. Second, you do not need to fight every stronger beast you discover. Give yourself time to grow. Observe them, gather byproducts like eggs, scales, and feathers, and learn what you can. Who knows? As you go farther in, you may even come across weaker beasts worth hunting.”

The advice gave Rick a little more confidence, though none of it was truly new to him. He had already thought along those lines. The difference was that hearing it from someone else, especially someone who sounded so certain, made the ideas feel less like desperation and more like a solid plan.

“It is not much,” Anne continued, “but I do have some books that might help. Lend me a hand and fetch that thin grey one from the second shelf.”

She pointed toward a shelf in the customer area. The books kept there were the ones she could sell freely, while those stored in the rooms behind the counter were reserved for lending, consultation, or more special arrangements.

Rick did not hesitate.

At some point during the conversation, he had started trusting her. Worse, or perhaps better, he had started hoping she might actually change something in his life. So he did as told and soon returned with a low-quality greyish soft-cover book in hand.

Anne looked at the three books now before her and felt quietly satisfied. With them, she could help him and, more importantly, turn him into another regular customer.

She started with the finest of the three, an exquisite volume bound in brown leather.

“This is a Mapping Book,” she said. “Give me your hand.”

Rick obeyed, though with visible hesitation. Anne caught his palm, then flipped the pen and pricked his skin with the blunt metal end before he could react. He only had time to wince as a bead of blood rose.

She drew the drop over the leather cover..

“Apio.”

The blood vanished into the leather as though drunk by it. Anne then opened the book to the first page and turned it toward him.

“This blood mark is your position. Now draw a simple representation of the bookstore. A square will do.”

Rick was a little unsettled by the whole process, but he followed her instructions. After confirming that he could choose both the size and the position, he drew a large rectangle in front of the mark representing himself.

As soon as he finished, the page gave off a faint glow. The rectangle widened, corrected itself, and his blood mark shifted neatly into the newly formed shape.

Rick’s eyes went wide.

At last, he understood what kind of book he was holding.

“Can I draw the mountains on it?” he asked, barely containing himself.

“Yes,” Anne replied. “Each page is independent, and the drawings inside it will be resized and repositioned as you add new elements. If you step outside and draw a roadside stone, the page may not change at all, or only slightly. But if you draw the mountains, the bookstore will become smaller to fit the proper scale. Just remember two things. First, you can only draw what you are currently seeing. Second, the book will drain your energy, so do not try to put too much in at once. You must use it wisely. Also, pay attention to how many pages it has.”

Rick was speechless.

Countless uses rushed through his mind at once. With this, he could mark locations, trace routes, record landmarks, and avoid getting lost in the mountains or the forest. More importantly, what Anne had suggested earlier—finding a better path through the mountains—had suddenly become possible instead of merely hopeful.

Then reality returned. Everything would come at a cost, and with an item like this, the cost would not be small.

Anne saw his expression shift and immediately understood what he was thinking, though she did not voice it directly.

“The book is valuable,” she said, probing carefully. “One of a kind, and among the most expensive things I can sell.”

The answer came plainly enough without him needing to say much.

He could not afford it.

Anne had expected as much, which was why she had prepared a different angle before the problem had even fully emerged.

“What if we make an exchange?” she asked, sounding amiable enough that it almost passed for generosity. “I can give you the book, but after every expedition you must come here and allow me to copy the maps you draw. What do you think?”

Rick forced himself to calm down and think.

The Mapping Book was obviously worth far more than he could pay. Letting her copy his maps would cost him no coin, though it would mean giving up the dream of keeping a secret path for himself. Then again, without the book, how could he ever hope to find such a path in the first place?

“I accept, miss,” he said firmly.

Anne hid her satisfaction well.

Without hesitation, she turned to the familiar.

“Moruh, prepare a binding contract.”

The blue familiar sprang into motion. A silver scroll and matching pen appeared in the air before him as if pulled from an invisible archive, and Rick could only stare, awed and a little frightened, as the contract began writing itself in thin air.

Rick could not read enough to properly verify the contents, though he did not seem especially troubled by that. He had no intention of breaking the agreement anyway, and he doubted the contract truly held any magic power.

Anne, for her part, was pleased enough by how readily he accepted it.

“Now, for the other books,” she said, taking up the thin grey volume. “This one is a Syllable for the Common Tongue. Essential, truly. Study it carefully and you should be reading in no time. You only need to touch the words and you will feel their sound. One silver coin, and it is yours.”

She let him test it.

Bookmancy was a wondrous craft. It could create shortcuts in learning and save people time for what truly mattered. Sadly, that was also why the world had grown so blind to it. The fewer people read, the fewer understood what was being lost.

“And last but not least...” Anne laid a reverent hand on the final volume. “Beastiarium Generalis, translated into the Common Tongue. For hunters, it should be considered a treasure. It contains the records of more than two thousand common beasts, including behavior, habitat, and illustrations.”

Rick had still been thinking about the Mapping Book when he heard that. Now his attention snapped fully to the beastiarium.

He could hardly believe so much value could be contained in a single book. If he had access to something like this, he would no longer be forced to rely on guild scraps, rumors, or dangerous trial and error. He would still need experience, of course, but he would not be walking into the unknown empty-handed.

“This one is too precious to be sold,” Anne said before he could ask. “And it cannot leave the bookstore. You may only study it here. Even then, the cost is a little steep—five silver coins.” She paused just long enough to let the number settle before continuing smoothly, “But do not worry. I am willing to exchange it for your game, and I will include the Syllable as a first-time gift.”

Rick hesitated.

The fox might have earned him three or four silver coins, enough to help him survive the month. That was not a small thing. In truth, the loss stung immediately. But he also knew he was getting the far better side of the exchange, and he was not foolish enough to waste such an opportunity out of short-term pain.

The choice hurt.

Still, there was only one answer.

“Deal.”
