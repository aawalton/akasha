Within the Spire of Defiance, as the people of Alcatraz called it, Vaymoz Alcatraz jerked awake from where he had fallen asleep at his desk, a thin trail of drool staining the polished oak.

He was a lean man in his early forties, with sharp gray eyes and a full head of raven-black hair now threaded with strands of silver.

The silver, however, wasn't from age.

It was from stress.

The stress of ruling a city like Alcatraz, and knowing that each morning he awoke to find it still standing was only because the powerful had chosen to spare it for one more day. The stress of constantly wearing a brave face so his people could rest a little easier. The stress of serving not merely as a ruler, but as a symbol.

But most of all, it was the stress of standing before cultivators powerful enough to smite him with a single breath—and defying them anyway.

Yet despite that burden, despite the ever-present fear that the treaties keeping Alcatraz safe might one day fail, Vaymoz truly believed humanity could stand on its own without bowing to cultivators.

Because though they were no longer mortals, they were not immortal either.

They were not gods.

And Vaymoz refused to cower before another man simply because the heavens had chosen to favor him.

Thunder boomed overhead, cracking across the sky with such force that the entire building trembled around him.

But it wasn't thunder.

No.

It was the voice of a Calamity.

The voice of a man who claimed no divinity, yet wielded power like a god.

Vaymoz grimaced in recognition and immediately began suiting himself up, his expression hardening instinctively into a mask of defiance as he fastened belts and buckles with practiced efficiency.

A soldier burst into the room just as he strapped a runic blade to his hip, and he slowly looked up at the frightened man.

"What is it, Rohir?" he asked evenly, as though he couldn't hear the voice thundering outside or feel the room itself shuddering beneath its force.

The soldier opened his mouth, only to falter, his eyes wide with fear until he saw how calm Vaymoz looked in the face of all of it.

How utterly unshaken he seemed.

The panic in the soldier's face eased then, and he drew himself up, his shoulders squaring as a measure of that same defiance found its way into his own posture.

"Calamities, my lord," he replied, his voice now steady. "Three of them, and half a dozen Mythics. They are requesting an audience with you."

Vaymoz strapped a runic pistol to his shin, then gave a single nod. "Invite them in and lead them to the west hall. I'll meet them there."

"Right." Rohir saluted. "Anything else, sir?"

Vaymoz nodded. "My daughter. She's always wanted to witness one of these. Send someone to bring her to the hall after I arrive."

Rohir hesitated, the fear returning to his eyes. "But, my lord... if anything should happen—"

"Nothing will happen," Vaymoz replied dismissively, despite the staccato rhythm his heart was beating in his chest.

But that was the burden of embodying an ideology such as his.

He was the symbol of defiance for every citizen of Alcatraz. How could they believe him when he claimed they had nothing to fear if he hid his own family at the first sign of danger?

Unsurprisingly, his words rekindled the fire in Rohir's eyes, and he saluted once more—this time with genuine conviction. He would carry that conviction to the other soldiers, and from soldier to soldier, it would spread until their resolve burned bright once more.

The soldier departed a moment later, and as the door shut behind him, Vaymoz straightened to his full height.

He glanced out the window at the storm of roiling clouds gathering above his city—clouds that hadn't existed a minute ago and exhaled deeply.

"They are not gods, Vaymoz," he whispered to himself. "They are men... just like you. Men just like your soldiers."

It was a mantra he had repeated for the last twenty years, ever since he took up the name Alcatraz and became the ruler of this city.

Yet even after all that time, the words still rang hollow in his ears.

Because though these men weren't gods, they still held the power of life and death at their fingertips. And here he was once more, a mere mortal rising to defy them.

He grimaced, strapped the last piece of his armor across his chest, and let out a slow breath before making his way toward the hall. As much as he would have loved to make them wait, he suspected this meeting would be vastly different from the ones they'd had before.

The timing was simply too convenient to be a coincidence.

Only a week after he'd discovered the treasure now in his basement, the Storm Sect had come to collect. No. It wasn't a coincidence at all.

Pity.

He had no intention of handing it over. He was the symbol of defiance, and he would not be cowed by a handful of cultivators. Calamities or not.

Not even today, with his daughter watching.

He was the man who had taught her defiance, and he would not betray it now.

***Several meters below, Aodhán emerged into a wide corridor just above the basement.

The first thing he noticed was the pungent scent of sulfur. Then came a trio of voices further down the hallway.

Guards.

He grimaced and swept his gaze across the corridor. White tiles lined the walls, while fluorescent lights hung from the ceiling, their glow flickering faintly overhead. Then his gaze caught on a tangle of wires running along the ceiling.

He blinked.

Then his eyes widened.

He could feel the energy flowing through the structure, feel the crackling current racing through the wires hidden behind the walls and feeding the lights above.

But it wasn't magic.

There were no runes on the walls, nor were there any formations to provide light.

What he was sensing was electricity.

Actual electricity.

How the people of Alcatraz had created it without a turbine, a lightning formation, or any obvious source of magical energy, Aodhán didn't know. But the mere fact that they had brought a smile to his face.

"Is there a particular reason you're smiling, Special Disciple Aodhán?"

His smile vanished instantly, and he shook his head. "Just admiring their technological advancement."

Calamity Selia didn't respond, but an instant later, a chorus of heavy thumps echoed down the corridor.

The guards had been knocked unconscious.

Aodhán chuckled, then took a deep breath and phased through the floor, descending into the basement below. He wasn't quite sure what he'd expected to find, but it certainly wasn't an empty room.

Several dozen meters of open space stretched in every direction, devoid of furniture, machinery, or storage. The only object in the chamber was a small podium standing at its center.

The podium was crafted from storm-touched glass, and floating above it was a sphere of stormlight no larger than a thumbnail. It floated motionless above the surface, emanating a blinding radiance that bathed the entire chamber in pure white light.

It wasn't the legacy seed.

But it wasn't anything Aodhán had ever seen or heard of before.

Selia, however, wasn't nearly as clueless.

She gasped, her invisibility unraveling as she stepped toward the glowing fragment, her eyes widening.

"What is it?" he asked urgently, feeling a sudden hunger begin to stir within him. Whatever this thing was, it was no doubt important.

Selia's lips parted, only to close again before a single word escaped.

She remained silent for several long seconds, and when she finally spoke, there was something almost reverent in her voice.

"This... is a condensed fragment of Edrick's Path. His understanding. His enlightenment." Her eyes narrowed. "A crystallized piece of comprehension itself, likely left behind as a second inheritance for whoever discovered his legacy."

Aodhán's breath caught, and when he looked back at the sphere, it was with entirely new eyes.

"I... I didn't know Ascendants could do that."

"Neither did I," Selia replied thoughtfully, her runic irises glowing with golden light.

For several moments, neither of them spoke. Then Selia's expression shifted, realization dawning.

"If Edrick left this behind, then..."

"Then he must have left it near the legacy seed itself," Aodhán finished, his eyes widening as he connected the dots. "We are close."

"We are." Selia smiled. "Once we learn where Vaymoz found this, the legacy can't be far away." Her runic eyes brightened. "I have to tell Amoz immediately."

She raised her communication chip to her lips, and Aodhán turned his attention back to the glowing sphere.

Now that he understood what it truly was, the hunger within him intensified, roaring through his very cells and urging him to consume the fragment for himself.

He crushed the impulse immediately, banishing it with a sharp shake of his head. He had already benefited from Edrick Ghaltris's enlightenment during the Ascension.

This wasn't for him. It was for whoever inherited the legacy, and if they were fast enough... then it would be for Synové.

A smile touched his lips, and he glanced up at Selia just as she lowered her communication chip.

"What did he say?"

Selia met his gaze, her runic irises shining with confusion.

"Wait." Her brows furrowed. "He said wait."

Back in the West Hall—a massive structure of steel and reinforced stone that resembled a deathtrap far more than a palace—Calamity Amoz and the rest of the group stood quietly at its center, staring at the empty throne before them.

Hundreds of soldiers filled the hall, each clad head to toe in Mythic-tier armor and armed with powerful weapons.

They surrounded the group in an unmistakable military formation, supported by more than three dozen snipers concealed within the ceiling.

It was a welcome fit only for criminals.

Yet no one spoke. Not the Calamities. Not the Mythics. Not even Sophran or the underlings.

Because beneath all that armor and steel, the fear of the soldiers around them was so palpable it was almost tangible.

This was the only way they could feel even remotely safe in their presence. And it saddened Amoz to see it. To recognize the fear and hatred in the eyes of those they were supposed to protect.

He grimaced, remembering the trauma they had endured fifteen years ago at the hands of fire cultivators.

No wonder they feared and hated them.

Hopefully, however, if they could find this seed without destroying anything, that trauma could begin to fade.

Drawing a deep breath, he turned his gaze away from their insulting reception and fixed his attention on the words Selia had just spoken.

They had identified the treasure, and it was something none of them could have ever guessed.

A fragment of Edrick Ghaltris's Path. Floating hundreds of meters beneath his feet.

How had Vaymoz found such a thing? More importantly... where?

It wasn't the legacy seed, but it was unquestionably part of the inheritance Edrick Ghaltris had left behind. If he could convince Vaymoz to reveal where the fragment had been discovered, he could narrow his search from miles to perhaps only a few dozen meters.

The key word, however, was convince.

Despite having come to respect Vaymoz's defiance, dealing with it could at times become very annoying.

But Amoz was nothing if not capable. And after nearly three centuries of life, he had learned many ways of dealing with people like the ruler of Alcatraz, chief among them the art of benevolent dominance.

Making one's host comfortable was the first step toward a successful negotiation. Making them uncomfortable was often the second.

And Amoz intended to patiently work that technique like a charm.

Unfortunately, not all Calamities shared his endless patience.

"How long are we going to have to wait this time?" Calamity Rezin asked, and though he didn't whisper, it was obvious the soldiers couldn't hear what he was saying.

Amoz glanced at him, then shrugged.

"Vaymoz will come out eventually. If he needs to subject us to some humiliation to feel powerful in his own home, then so be it."

Cephas chuckled alongside a few Mythics, their voices carrying this time, and almost immediately, several soldiers paled, their muscles tensing beneath their armor in fear.

Before a full-blown panic could break out, however, a massive door at the front of the hall opened, and Vaymoz Alcatraz stepped into the hall with only a sword in sight.

Yet the effect his presence had on the soldiers was unmistakable.

They all straightened immediately, the fear wiped from their expressions as though his mere presence assured them of their safety.

And in place of that fear came defiance, a collective resolve that only seemed to thicken as they watched their leader walk calmly to his throne.

Vaymoz sat down slowly, calmly, before looking up at the cultivators gathered before him. A hint of recognition flashed in his eyes when he saw Amoz.

Rather than smile, however, he grimaced.

"Good morning, cultivators," he greeted, his tone steady. "What brings you to my city this beautiful morning?"

Amoz didn't respond immediately. Instead, he took a single step forward, and the feeble defiance many of the soldiers had gathered instantly crumbled.

They tensed, their hands tightening around their weapons.

Only then did Amoz respond, a slow smile spreading across his face.

"Greetings, Vaymoz Alcatraz. I must apologize for the rude awakening. I hope we have not interrupted your beauty sleep?"

"No, Calamity Amoz," Vaymoz replied easily. "Had you done so, I would have asked you to wait till I completed it."

Amoz froze. His jaw ticked at the insult.

Behind him, Sophran and the others grimaced, an expression that caused a drastic spike in the tension within the room. Vaymoz, however, barely blinked, seemingly unfazed by the Calamity staring him down. He leaned back into his throne.

"I will ask again, Calamity Amoz. What brings you to my city?"

Amoz stared at the defiant ruler for a moment before slowly letting his anger go. "I have come on behalf of the Storm Sect to seek a treasure in your domain. I require your permission by the Accords to do so."

This time, it was Vaymoz who tensed, his gaze darting ever so briefly to the floor before rising once again.

Amoz barely hid a smile.

So he does know of the treasure in his basement.

Before Vaymoz could respond, however, the door opened once more, and a girl barely above the age of seventeen stepped into the hall, surrounded on all sides by a troop of soldiers. In like manner to her father, the girl barely spared them a glance before moving to sit by his feet.

When she eventually did look at them, her gaze was far steadier than her father's.

Far more defiant.

Vaymoz smiled, feeling his own confidence rise at the sight of his daughter. He glanced back at Amoz and raised an eyebrow.

"What is this treasure that you seek?"

Amoz regarded him for a moment, then took another step forward, spiking the tension in the room once more. Slowly, he let his gaze dip to the ground, his runic irises flaring for just a moment before he looked back up.

He smiled slowly.

"It is not the treasure beneath us."

Vaymoz's breath hitched immediately, and even his daughter stilled, her gaze narrowing slightly. The soldiers, however, went completely pale.

For a long moment, no one spoke.

Then Vaymoz cleared his throat. "If it's not the treasure in my basement, what then do you seek?"

"A curiosity," Amoz replied. "We promise to cause no harm to your city or people while we search for this… curiosity." He smiled. "All we need is your permission."

Vaymoz held his gaze for a moment—a remarkable feat for a mere mortal—before turning his attention to his daughter, who sat poised by her father's seat, watching them all with the same defiant mask as her father.

Only it wasn't just defiance.

Her heart didn't stutter. Her blood didn't race.

It was hatred too.

"Okay," Vaymoz replied. "You have my permission. But that permission is terminated if any harm comes to my city or my people." His gaze hardened. "Should that happen, then you will all become trespassers in direct violation of the Accords of Protection."

Amoz grimaced, remembering that they weren't the only ones searching for this seed. Should their enemies cause any harm, Vaymoz would no doubt attribute it to them.

But telling Vaymoz that would not help matters at all.

"We accept your conditions," Amoz replied, then turned his gaze—intentionally—to Vaymoz's daughter.

The girl stilled immediately, and though she held his gaze far longer than her father dared, she eventually looked away, a silent fury burning behind her eyes.

Amoz's intentions, however, hadn't been to make the girl uncomfortable.

It was to make her father uncomfortable.

"What else do you need?" Vaymoz blurted out, defiant anger creasing his face. "You've gotten the permission you wanted."

"I have." Amoz smiled. "But I have a question."

His gaze drifted to the ground once more. "This… this treasure beneath us. Where exactly did you find it?"

"Why is that any of your concern, Calamity Amoz?" Vaymoz asked, his defiance now fueled by anger. "Does it have anything to do with the treasure you seek?"

Amoz glanced at him, then slowly shook his head.

"No. This is merely a curiosity. Not knowing is making me very curious." He smirked. "Too curious, perhaps. Would you like to alleviate that curiosity?"

Vaymoz glared at him, anger flashing openly across his features before he forced it back beneath that calm mask. A moment of silence passed before he finally, reluctantly, answered.

"I don't know the exact location. My soldiers found it somewhere in the northern district."

"The northern district, you say?" Amoz smiled widely. "How very convenient."

Vaymoz's glare deepened. "Yes. Now, if that is all, Calamity Amoz, there is much to attend to after the excitement of your arrival this morning."

Amoz nodded. "That is all. We shall take our leave now."

He turned, waving for the others to follow him. The moment his back was turned to Vaymoz, however, he raised his chip to his lips and whispered.

"Steal it."

Hundreds of meters below, Selia's eyes suddenly widened in understanding. She glanced at Aodhán.

"He has responded."

"What did he say?"

Selia smiled. "Tell me, Aodhán. How would you like to perform a heist?"

Aodhán frowned. "You mean a theft."

"No," Selia shook her head, her smile widening. "I meant a heist."
