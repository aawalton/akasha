Elion disarmed before entering the town, his high-vis jacket and hood returning. The gate closed behind them with a clang, and guards rushed to positions on the wall.

Pulling the plugs from his ear, he balled up the putty. Nobody asked for it back, so he slipped it into his pocket.

Prator lead his men into town, carrying the injured newcomer. Eleven arachnatronics followed them as they walked. Elion observed the machines. Eight of them were the cargo-transporting variety, with large, flat platforms between their legs where crates could be stacked. Most of them were partially loaded.

The remaining three were equipped with rocket launchers, turrets, and heavy metal plating. Shields guarding the joints of the legs gave these a spiny, crab-like appearance.

A few people rode on the machines, some on the cargo haulers, some on the battle-spiders. Elion couldn’t tell if they were driving or just being carried. The other arachnatronics he’d seen had seemed largely autonomous.

They reached the front steps of the most ornate building in town, the same place that Elion and Keyla had seen Prator talking to Venya. To Elion, it looked like a mixture between a courthouse and a cathedral. The stone columns and stained glass windows were striking, and Elion wondered if it had been built pre-cataclysm.

Prator yelled a few commands to one of the spider-riders, and the machines gathered off to one side, their riders dismounting. Elion followed Prator and his guards up into the building.

Inside a large entry hall opened up before them. Grand staircases twisted up to the second floor, and rooms opened up on either side of the hall. A shimmering chandelier dangled overhead, shining with multicolored lights. Spotlights in the ceiling illuminated the stained glass.

Bortim met them, twisting a ring anxiously on his finger. “Venya’s coming,” he said. “She wants to know what’s going on out there.

Prator grabbed Elion’s shoulder. “You, in there,” Prator said, pointing to a pair of doors.

Elion obeyed. *Not like I have much of a choice.* He slipped through the double doors and found himself in an ornate office. Dark wood trim stood out against light colored stone. Rich blue curtains hung over windows on one wall. Elion lingered beside the doors, listening.

“He’s injured but stable,” Prator said. “Go get the surgeon to reset his bone.” Someone acknowledged the order. “You go find Velnar and make sure he’s sending me regular reports here. Have him double the wall guard and make sure the perimeter is under constant patrol. We don’t know if they’ll try a counter attack.”

Another acknowledgement.

“Bortim,” Prator said, “Come with me for a moment.”

The two of them stepped into the room where Elion waited. Bortim peered curiously at Elion, recognition spreading across his face.

"We meet again,” Bortim said, grinning broadly. “Nice to see you making friends.”

Elion ignored him.

“He’s the boy Venya is looking for,” Prator said. “Take off your hood, Elion.”

Elion did.

“From the way she described him, I was expecting someone more… imposing.”

“He’s a knight. A new one, though.”

“Why is she after you?” Bortim asked, meeting Elion’s gaze.

“She thinks he’s the lost heir to the Golden Throne,” Prator said. He produced Elion’s Starholder pendant. “From what I can tell, he believes the same.”

Bortim grunted. Prator dangled the pendant in Bortim’s face. “You used *this* as a bondhold, and didn’t think to tell me?”

“I assumed it was stolen,” Bortim protested. “He didn’t seem like the upstanding type.”

“Perhaps,” Prator said. “I’m not sure I believe my story myself.”

Elion scowled. “You forced me to tell you the truth,” he said.

“There are ways to cleverly manipulate the truth,” Prator said. “You might have convinced yourself of the truth. Someone might have lied to you, allowing you to spread that lie as truth to others. Either way, it doesn’t matter.” Prator turned to Bortim. “We need to keep him secret. I’m not ready to turn him over to Venya yet.”

Bortim nodded. “It’s better if she stays here while we’re under attack,” he agreed.

“Elion, you still want to work together? You’ll help me protect the people of New Kairn Tol?”

“I…” Elion hesitated. “I want to help people.”

“We haven’t been certain what the intentions of that army have been until today,” Prator said. “They have killed or captured every envoy or scout who has gotten too close. But now we know that we are their target. They’re closing in, preparing to attack us.”

“Why?” Elion asked.

“Thrandicite production,” Prator said. “It’s the most valuable productive industry for miles. Many of the surrounding towns are dying out. My best guess is that they banded together and intend to seize the acid pit and refinery for themselves.”

Elion thought about the black eyes of the infected man he’d fought at the barricade, and recalled the streaks of light fired from laserarms destroying the arachnatronic. *Had the infected risen up, and taken control of everyone in Aterfel? Destroying the Shard must not have cured the infection. How could they have gotten laserarms without overrunning the tower?*

"I want to help people,” Elion said. “But I think I may have friends among the attacking army.”

Bortim and Prator turned to stare at Elion.

“You know something of them?” Prator demanded. “You told me—”

“I know,” Elion said. “I didn’t know anything about them when you asked. But after that fight…”

Elion gave his explanation about the black eyes of the infected. “They hoped that destroying the Shard would end the infection. But it must not have. I wonder if whatever was controlling the infected has somehow been unleashed, and is taking control of as many people as it can.”

“That’s bad,” Prator said. “But it would explain why they haven’t been willing to even talk to us.”

“Are laserarms common?” Elion asked.

“No.” Prator said. “They used to make them here in New Kairn Tol, but that was a long time ago. They’re valuable.”

“They’ve been making them in Aterfel,” Elion said.

Prator scoffed. “I doubt that. It takes a very skilled artificer to construct them, from what I understand.”

Elion did not reply, letting the issue drop.

“What are you going to do?”

“I have a plan,” Prator said. “But I need your help.”

“How?” Elion asked. “What can I do?”

“We have captured an infected person,” Prator said. “We believe that she might help us. She knows things that we need to know. And you won’t be found by Venya if you’re with her.”

Prator looked at Bortim.

Bortim shrugged. “It’s as good a place as any,” Bortim said. “She doesn’t bother me. Can’t imagine she’d come snooping, unless she got wind of it.”

“Wait,” Elion said. “There’s a problem with that. You can’t hide me. Venya can track my location.”

"She can?" Prator asked, surprised. "How have you managed to evade her for so long?"

Elion smiled wryly. "I'm pretty good at hiding," he said.

Prator laughed charitably at the joke. "I suppose you've been hiding for many years. How is she tracking you?"

“It’s my, uh, sword,” Elion said. “That she captured, with the machine. They can use it to detect my direction, through my bond with the sword. Her warlocks have tried to find me with it, but the machine doesn't seem to be that accurate. They aren’t confident in it. They came very close to finding me, but I was able to evade them because it started glitching out. They're still using it though.”

Prator looked at Bortim, who was scratching his chin.

"Yes," Prator said. "We will have to do something about that, won't we? You think it’s using Desmian power to track you?"

“Interesting,” Bortim said. “I suppose that is possible, in theory. I wonder what Binder they have working with them on that.”

Elion nodded slowly. “I feel a bond to the blade. Something like the Bondhold bond.”

“Bortim?” Prator asked.

“If they come snooping around, I may be able to bend the bond and send them looping around elsewhere.”

“*May* be able to? Or *can*? That’s an important difference.”

“Probably can?” Bortim said with a shrug. “I’ll have to stay near him though, and keep an eye out for warlocks just in case.”

“Well that settles it then. Come on Elion, let’s get you somewhere else before Venya comes here and wants to talk.”

Prator's proposed place for Elion to hide turned out to be Bortim’s prison. Bortim opened the door, admitting them to the crumbling building.

Elion faltered on the threshold, scowling at the building. He did not like to remember how he and Keyla had been forcibly bonded.

"I hope you don't mind hiding out in here,” Prator said, holding the door for Elion. “It isn’t the nicest accommodation, but you will be hidden here."

“You’re going to lock me up?” Elion said.

“It’s the best idea I have right now,” Prator said. “This keeps you close to Bortim and out of view.”

Bortim led Prator and Elion back down the hall toward the room where Elion and Keyla had been before. That door was closed, and Prator did not move to open it. Instead, he stopped at the door where Elion had heard the woman screaming.

For a moment, Prator listened quietly at the door. "She has fallen quiet. Good progress, Bortim!”

The man grinned proudly, like a child receiving praise from his teacher.

“I think I’m working out that strange tangle of bonds,” Bortim said, producing a key. “The darkness grips her, a bond from some strange, unfamiliar space.” He fumbled at the lock.

“What if Bortim can’t divert the warlocks?” Elion asked. “I’ll be trapped here, without anyway to fight back or escape.”

“Bortim will figure it out.”

“Wouldn’t it be better to destroy her machine, and reclaim my sword?” Elion asked.

“Perhaps,” Prator said. “If it can be done in the right way. But not right now.”

“You were in Aterfel,” Prator said, turning to Elion. “You know some of the people that were there?”

“I met some of them, yes,” Elion said, thinking about Tharnen, and how he’d left him lying injured on the bridge. *I hope Kasm was able to help him.*

“Good,” Prator said. “I’ll need your help with something.

“I thought we agreed to work together,” Elion said. “You’re just locking me up.”

"We are working together," Prator said, and he tapped the Starholder pendant around his neck. "Let me remind you that you are already bound, whether in a cell or in a mansion. If Venya was not in town searching for you, I could keep you in a nicer accommodation, but the truth of the matter is, we need to hide you, and this is the best place that I have."

Elion sighed.

“Besides, I need you to do something here,” Prator said, lowering his voice conspiratorially. He held the door closed for a moment. “There’s a woman in this cell. Talk to her. Find out what she knows. She’s part of a scheme to assassinate me. Help me out here, let’s work together.”

“Wait,” Elion said, “You mentioned this plot before. Tell me more about it.”

“There’s a group of people who are tying to subvert my leadership here,” Prator said. “I thought I had them all bonded and under control, but this woman somehow managed to break free from the bond, and Bortim hasn’t been able to reestablish it. I need to know what she knows.”

Bortim had unlocked the door, and held it by the handle, ready to open it.
