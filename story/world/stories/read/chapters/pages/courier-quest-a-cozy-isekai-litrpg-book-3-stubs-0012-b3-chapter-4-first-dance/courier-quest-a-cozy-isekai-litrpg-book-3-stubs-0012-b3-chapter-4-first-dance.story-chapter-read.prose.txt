While the reception was being set up, Trevor and Adabelle spent a quiet moment at the west end of Serna Island. The split river merged before them, continuing its journey forward, and they watched the water. One of their two oathing stones was being held between their hands, and both were basking in the peace of the moment. It didn’t last.

“Dad says he’s got everything ready for you,” Rashie announced as she squeezed herself between the two, moving in front of them before leaning back and looking up. Clay was on her shoulder, nodding as if to confirm that the news was true.

“You did very well up there, shark,” Adabelle praised, resting her hand on the girls’ head.

Rashie grinned, showing off rows of sharp teeth. “Is it bad that I was kinda hoping someone would object so I could chomp them?”

“I think Adabelle would have had things well in hand,” Trevor said. “So, maybe a little.”

“There’s no guarantee I would’ve been able to make it to the objector in time,” Adabelle replied with a smile. “I’m pretty sure Rashie and Griffin would have been the first to move.”

“Yeah, you did have all the Summoned on your side, and you took Wayne from me,” the courier teased.

“Don’t worry, son of Ander,” she replied, rubbing his back with her tail. “Me and my posse, as Wayne would put it, were ready to protect you should anyone try to kidnap you from the altar.”

“I think you’ve got that wrong, lady,” he said, shaking his head. “That’s what the groomsmen are for, not the bridesmaids.”

“No one’s stupid enough to try and steal Addymanatine, Uncle Trevor,” Rashie stated wisely, and Clay nodded again. “If anyone was going to be kidnapped, it’s definitely you.”

“A little hurtful,” he replied, holding a hand over his heart.

“But?” Adabelle prompted, challenging him with great amusement.

“But I’m very glad no one tried to kidnap anyone at our wedding,” he deflected with a smile. “Anyway, we shouldn’t keep them waiting. Rashie already had to shoo Wayne away from your tent once, I’m sure he’s itching for a good turnabout.”

“Yeah, he probably is,” the shark girl said, narrowing her eyes. “Deputy dad’s always coming up with plots and schemes.”

“And schemes and plots,” Trevor added.

“Those, too,” she agreed before looking back up at them. “I’ll go tell them you’re on your way, so don’t make us wait, okay?”

“Go on, shark,” Adabelle said, and Rashie took off towards the crowd. The reception was going to be in the same area as the ceremony, they just had to move the chairs first. Once she was gone, the noblewoman turned to Trevor. “Are you ready?”

“I am,” he confirmed.

Together, they threw the oathing stone into the water. It landed where the rivers merged, quickly sinking to the bottom. Its twin remained inside of Trevor’s inventory. They planned on keeping that one, perhaps displaying it somewhere in the home they would share. With the tradition complete, they headed to the reception.

The band was already playing and Wayne was standing on a wooden dance floor. Trevor wasn’t sure where it had come from. He certainly hadn’t noticed one before, but it appeared to be brand new. The cowboy looked up to see them approaching and grinned.

“Here they are, the newlyweds!” he announced, a hammer in his hand. Leaning down, the cowboy used it to knock on a raised piece of wood. It shifted to become flat without cracking or splintering, and he nodded before slipping the tool into his belt.

The crowd, who had surrounded the dance floor, clapped and cheered and the newlywed’s arrival. Trevor and Adabelle waved, not needing more introduction than that. Wayne gestured them towards him, and they obliged. He was quick to bow, offering them the space as he backed away.

“The first dance is yours,” he declared.

“First dance?” Adabelle asked in a whisper, turning to Trevor. “I knew there was dancing, but there’s a first one?”

Trevor smiled as he squeezed his wife’s hand. While Adabelle's request that Jackson’s excerpt be read during the ceremony was a pleasant surprise, he had one of his own. It had been hard keeping this from her, but it was all going to be worth it.

“Try not to make me look like a novice out here, okay?” he requested in a joking tone.

“What do you…” Adabelle trailed off as her husband broke away from her and took a stance he had seen her take for a dance once before.

With a raised hand, he motioned towards the ten assembled musicians. The reception went silent for a moment, and Trevor offered the demonkin a smile. One that she returned wholeheartedly as she recognized the music. It wasn’t exactly the same, but it was close enough to convey what he had planned.

“I only showed this to you once,” she whispered since he was only a few feet away.

“And I’ve been practicing ever since,” he replied.

Then, the courier began to move. Adabelle started a split second after him, but easily matched Trevor’s pace.

The Spring Blossom’s Union was a couple’s dance meant to be performed by a bride and groom during their wedding ceremony. Its accompanied music was soft and gentle, ebbing and flowing as it seemed to circle around on itself, just like the dance.

Trevor had picked out the spiral nature of the rhythm when he had learned it, and had practiced it tirelessly with Clay. The earth elemental was very good at mimicking others. Together, they had come a long way to making sure it was something worth showing off to Adabelle. If it was going to be a surprise, it needed to be a good one.

While he had practiced almost nightly, he allowed the demonkin to take the lead. It was her world's dance, after all, and she knew the complete version rather than the makeshift one he and his familiar had worked out.

The bride and groom circled each other in time to the music. They spun, slipping in and out of the center of the dance floor. When the two touched, it was back to back, and Adabelle’s tail lingered on him as they separated again. Despite the onlookers, Trevor found himself sucked into the melody and his position relative to his partner’s.

Changing directions, they danced slightly closer as the spiral tightened. The two sped up, rubbing elbows on occasion. They reached out, clasping hands together for brief moments. Stolen touches, taken while they were facing away from each other. Then they would move again, stepping away back into their circle.

Trevor was reminded of what Adabelle had told him about Halvsaran nobles. They had a culture of only being expressive and caring in private, while showing off a strong, stony demeanor in public. It even got to the point where saying you love someone outside of family or close friends was something that was somehow frowned upon.

He had found that both strange and lonely, but he didn’t argue with it. Doing so wouldn’t change anything; they were a whole world away and the most important part of it was already here.

As they reached the final section of the dance, he realized for the first time that this choreography was symbolic of that culture. Two partners moved as one, but on the periphery of each other. The feelings were there, just out of sight and out of mind. They collided on occasion but, other than that, they were separate.

Then, they reached out for each other. The orbit tightened. Their feelings were made clear. Still, they moved away as expected of them.

Trevor and Adabelle reached the point where they faced one another, hand in hand, and he spun her around. Their contact was constant, showing each other off to the crowd around them. He fumbled a little bit at this part, not used to the proximity from his time practicing, but she was graceful enough to cover for him.

They ended chest to chest, embracing each other with big smiles on their face, and Adabelle leaned forward to kiss him. The crowd once again clapped and cheered as the band changed music, signaling that the first dance was done.

“You surprised me, Trevor,” the noblewoman laughed when she finally pulled away. “Who did you practice the Spring Blossom’s Union with?”

“Clay,” he answered easily as he beckoned the familiar to him. The stoat, who had been dancing on their own at Rashie’s feet, ran across the dance floor and jumped up onto Trevor’s hand, then up to his shoulder. “You only showed it to us once, yeah, but we were able to figure it out from there. I’ve been excited for this ever since, because I wanted to turn what it meant to you into more than just a vehicle for your spite against that guy who’s name I forgot on purpose.”

“Well, he wasn’t important, anyway,” Adabelle agreed with a soft smile. She reached up and stroked under Clay’s chin. “Thank you for helping with this surprise. It was delightful.”

The earth elemental preened under her attention. “We’re glad you enjoyed it,” Trevor said, speaking for both himself and his familiar. “I didn’t really nail it, though. Getting one side of two parts and trying to figure it out from there worked a little bit, but not all the way.”

“No, it was perfect,” she replied, the quick shake of her head causing the bell on her horn to jingle. “This was just our version of the dance, and I’m much fonder of this one than the old one.”

“Well, when you put it like that, I can see the appeal,” the courier chuckled as he scratched the back of his head.

Rashie moved in front of them, excitement in her eyes, and grabbed them both by the hand. “Okay, now that the dance is over, it’s food time,” the shark girl said as she pulled them towards a long table. “Your guests and bridesmaid and other guests are hungry, you know, but we can’t eat without you.”

“We can’t have that,” Trevor replied, and the pair allowed themselves to be taken to the table where they would be feasting at.

“That was a very fun looking dance,” Klar said as he joined them. Griffin and Thimbleden were already there, though not seated. Drelik was standing on Trevor’s side of the table and Wayne on Adabelle’s as family members.

“It was fun,” the courier responded. “Very spiraly. I feel like we could have gone in circles forever.”

“Good thing the song’s not that long, otherwise poor Rashie would starve,” Adabelle joked.

“Nah, I’ve got pocket snacks,” the shark girl said proudly.

The group looked at her. There were no pockets on her dress, and she wasn’t carrying any kind of purse or bag, either.

“Okay, I’ll bite,” Thimbleden said curiously. “What pocket?”

“This one,” Rashie responded before snapping her fingers. The wedding party watched the girl to see what she would do, but nothing happened for a few seconds. Then, a ball of bread sailed towards her. Her mouth opened wide and she snatched it whole from the air. She chewed a few times before swallowing. “See? Pocket snacks.”

Trevor turned to see where the bread ball had come from. Rupert was halfway in the crowd, fist pumping in celebration of a successful throw. The moment their eyes locked, however, the halfling boy panicked and disappeared.

“So by pocket, you mean Rupert’s pocket,” he said, turning back to Rashie.

“Well, yeah, I don’t got pockets so I had to borrow someone else’s,” the shark girl replied, patting herself down as if checking to make sure she hadn’t missed them before.

“While I applaud the innovation of having yourself your own personal treats bearer,” Wayne started, resting his hand on his daughter’s head. “Maybe now ain’t the right time to show it off. Especially since you seemed to have roped that boy into it.”

“Nuh uh, I didn’t rope Rupert into anything,” Rashie argued, crossing her arms as she ducked away from the dwarf’s hand. “He kept throwing things at me at school. When I confronted him about it, with my words and not my fish, he said it was fun. So I told him that he could throw food at my face and maybe I won’t catch it, and he’s been feeding me ever since. He thinks I’m going to miss one day, but he’s wrong. I will always catch the food.”

“Does he, now,” Wayne said. Everyone in the wedding party except the shark girl was watching his reaction, and he was looking right at where Rupert had disappeared. “Interesting.”

“I think it’s time to eat,” Griffin announced, as quiet as ever. “Wayne, you can talk with your daughter’s… friend later, can’t you?”

“Yeah. We’re gonna have a real long talk,” he agreed with a dark chuckle.

“Come on, big guy,” Trevor said, putting an arm around the cowboy’s shoulders and turning him towards the table. “Let’s get some food in you. You get a little Rashie when you’re hungry.”

“What’s that supposed to mean?” the shark asked indignantly, but she was also the first to sit down.

“Like father like daughter,” he claimed, sharing a glance with Adabelle, who seemed to have been highly entertained from the conversation. They sat down next to each other, Klar on one side and Griffin on the other, and were soon served a delicious meal.
