Henry knew he’d succeeded as soon as the rich, loamy scent of the forest and the raucous bird calls filled his senses. They were back in the Reach’s territory and already, the sweet, comforting sunlight was warming up his cold, nearly frozen skin.

He’d gotten so damn used to the cold of space that the temptation to just lie down and sunbathe until sundown was overwhelming, but Henry didn’t allow himself to rest just yet. Holding on to the empowered connection to his network of Avatars, Henry chucked his attention back through the link, tuning out all the noise surrounding him. He’d had a brief moment to communicate his orders to the Avatar he’d just sent back in his stead, and by now, it should have destroyed itself. It had been… a second? Maybe two? It should have been plenty for what it needed to do. In that brief moment they passed each other through the connection, he knew his order had fully gone through, but if the missing architect queen was up to something, it might have been this.

She might have been waiting for an opening. But Henry had done his utmost to give her as few crumbs as possible. Part of why he’d used a swap instead of a teleport was to make sure he didn’t risk being rebuffed by the barrier and have to retry. Either of his skills activating might have been enough of a signal, and so Henry needed to succeed on the first time. This way, instead of needing the couple dozen seconds—if not more—to recast a teleport, he would only have a second or two of vulnerability. A much more manageable risk. It was an incredibly small window that he was giving the queen, and frankly, he didn’t see how she could hide herself so thoroughly as to escape the notice of all of the sentries that had been surrounding the SS Henry and still manage to exploit the half-second his Avatar would be vulnerable and alone. But still… it wasn’t utterly impossible, so Henry needed to account for it.

Interestingly enough, looking out of the System’s barrier was a little easier now that he was down here. Then again, he knew that already. That was how he had teleported out while Unfettered Echoes was still at B-rank. Which meant that right now, the complete lack of a signal or a connection told him, without any doubts, that the Avatar that had switched places with him was gone. As it should have. It had dispelled itself, and to Henry’s accounting, it hadn’t been more than three or four seconds since his teleportation had been completed. No way she managed to snag my Avatar in that short of a time frame, he told himself. And while there was still a tiny remote chance that the architect queen had gotten the drop on him, he really didn’t think that was the case. Which didn’t assuage his worries at all. Because then… where was she? What was she up to?

Sending his attention down to the skill again, Henry gave it one more thorough scan, ignoring all the nearby Avatars as they lit up in his consciousness. Some, while not accessible to him right this second, still shone in his mind, like dim lights in the distance. Like the ones he’d left in Thalis, or the ones he’d given the pod of porpoises. But nothing showed from outside the System’s barrier and so, finally, Henry let the skill drop and return to its usual level of activity.

Opening his eyes, Henry let out a slow, tired exhale, taking a beat to spread his arms out and enjoy the sun as he dropped down to the warm, comforting soil. The dried leaves crunched below him as he let go of Flight, and Henry took in a deep inhale as he listened to the birdsongs surrounding him. With great effort, because he just wanted to nap, he summoned Velistraine and Maurice, both of whom wasted no time in joining him down to the ground. Velistraine dropped to her knees, eyes misting as she took in the daylight with a big dumb smile on her face, while Maurice gently tapped the soil with his claws. The crab was lifting and lowering his many legs, as if enjoying a texture that wasn’t thin air or bones for the first time in his life. There were no coherent words out of either of them, just various noises of joy or laughter.

Henry suddenly realized he was laughing along with them and like that, a few minutes went by as the three of them enjoyed having solid ground that wasn’t infested with humanoid mantises that would screech into the void whenever one of them was noticed. Not that they weren’t being noticed, of course. A few curious B-rank had gotten close enough to be within line of sight, attracted by the noise, only to go still and quickly scamper away. Not that everyone approaching was that rational. But it would still be a few minutes before the first individual got close enough, so Henry just relaxed.

That didn’t mean his Octominds were idle, though.

Pretty quickly, a wide, larger-than-ever network was being established. Without having to halve his workforce, Henry could field a lot of Avatars. Most of which he’d already created back in space. So now it was just about spreading his Avatars far and wide, then having them pop an Avatar out of his Maw which would then fly—or swim—a couple dozen miles before doing the same thing again. Right now, Henry was sending his attention in three main directions. The first was the nearest orb. The second was the last orb, somewhere at sea, and finally, he was working on reestablishing the link to Thalis.

Unlike the last time Henry was here, his range was much less restrained. Every level in the A-rank Unfettered Echoes had made the numbers of Avatars he could create explode, and at level 6, Henry had somewhere around eight thousand Avatars deployed. As usual, the Lifeblood weren’t as good with their range, but even those could handle at least nine miles before their connection began to weaken, while his Arcane Avatars could now handle thirty miles each, again, before the connection began to deteriorate.

Suffice it to say, reconnecting to Thalis was going to be trivial. It might even happen before he finished handling the minor headache that was heading his way. As for the swarm outside… well. He wished he didn’t have to worry so much about having one of his Avatars captured. Leading the swarm here had been too dangerous. But once they were ready to face the damn things full on, he was looking forward to using everything.

“You have no idea how much I missed this,” Velistraine managed after a few seconds, and from her, Henry was sensing a whole maelstrom of feelings. There was relief there, palpable, and just a sheer, potent joy at being alive. It was one thing to know she had allies after she’d thrown herself into the jaw of death and managed to escape. It was another to be back home.

Henry was happy for her. And while this world had grown on him a little, and was technically his home at the moment, he didn’t know if he felt as much attachment to it as she did.

“Definitely smells better here than there,” Maurice mused as he eyed a small centipede wiggling about in his claw before he brought it to his mandibles and crunched on it. “Yeah. Much better here. Everything smelled like it had been burnt, back there,” he said, looking up at the clear blue sky for a moment before he glanced back to Henry. “You sure they can’t track us back? And who are we supposed to talk to about this? I’ve been thinking at the System real strong since earlier, and it’s not answering me.”

“I don’t know about the System, but considering that last message it sent me, I don’t think it can communicate as easily as it used to. But I still felt it back there, allowing me through. Hopefully, once we have the set completed, we’ll get some answers. As for telling people, I’m already spreading out my Avatars. The Emerald Isles are already in sight, and I should be in Thalis in a few more minutes. But we’ll get back to that in a second. First, I got a few messages to send,” he added, arms curling in amusement as both the lake of the Merfolks and Wolf’s Den came into view.

It was time to say hi to some folks.

Hidden at the heart of its assigned Sector, the System watched, its figurative hands tied behind its back while the living plague had finally caught its scent.

The Manthari Swarm had finally found it and its charge. To think that once upon a time, they had been considered a small, insignificant nuisance. Not much better than the common goblins or other lower-tier creatures that populated countless other sectors.

It wondered if this Sector ever had goblins. There hadn’t been any mention of them, as far as it knew. Not in memories. Not in stories.

Likely not, the System thought, its perception gliding over the punitive workings that had locked it up since its last message to Henry. It had really said more than it should have. But if that was the only working that restrained it, the System could have done more. Now it had to watch and wait for the threat it knew was coming. Because technically, the Manthari had narrowed down the two planets’ location, but they hadn’t found it exactly. If the force it was observing through the Sector’s framework kept its trajectory, they would miss.

But what were the odds of that, really? At best, this was a coin toss. Not enough for the System to disregard the Founders’ guidelines, and not enough to push against the restrictive workings that tied its arms. Not that the System wanted to push the workings. Frayed and overloaded as they were, any push back from it would see them tear and break down, and then the Manthari wouldn’t have to look for them. They’d be called to them like moths to a light.

It’s a miracle we even held out for so long, it thought to itself, watching the massive ships slowly maneuver themselves in their direction. It was a miracle the protective measures hadn’t already failed and while this scenario wasn’t ideal, the System knew that it had been a matter of months at most until another anomaly or breach took place. A year, at most, or whatever being in its care ascended first. Whichever of those happened first, the balance would be tipped, and the veil would come off.

So it wasn’t that big of a deal that the Manthari Swarm managed to sniff them out when the System had let Henry slip through the barrier. It had reasoned that a clash might weaken the veil earlier than expected, and it had been brief enough that it should have been unnoticed.

But one of the Manthari had quietly set up a detection array, likely by grouping all of their ships and somehow crafting them into a massive, modular receiver, and the System had sensed it when its action had tripped the proverbial wire.

To think they had been thought to be extinguished when their Branch had gone quiet. Only for the damn thing to come out swinging, forcing this Sector and many others to be quarantined, and considering the class and size of this Sector…

Well. The System wasn’t surprised that it had heard nothing for a few hundred years already. This had been a frontier Sector at best, and if the Manthari could lock this Sector down so easily, then it might have been worse up the Branch.

Now they were on the clock, and hopefully, Henry and his little friends would collect the orbs quickly. With the heightened permissions the artifact would allow them, the System could shed its restrictions, and then they’d get to work. It had stockpiled plenty since the quarantine had begun. It just needed the go-ahead to start deploying its weapons.
