Chapter 50: Ransa Joy

Raeleq was particularly pleased with the clothes the moles of Crag had picked out for him. They were made of spider silk, as it turned out all of their clothes were, and were both tight enough to be flattering and stretchy enough to be easy to move in. His were a lighter green that stood out against his skin in a way that pleased him.

Most importantly though, the material was so smooth his hands glided over it without resistance. He’d been fully prepared for something rough and durable, like the molekin themselves wore. Instead, he was pleased to be wrong, and more than happy to come out for the feast.

The residents of Crag had set up their feast in the long, widening corridor in front of the Imperial Safehouse. In the four hours or so since they’d arrived, napped, and bathed, they’d nearly finished setting everything up. It could’ve easily been crowded, but he found it cozy, even with the hundred or so molekin setting up tables and cooking in giant pots.

Savory smells assaulted Raeleq, and his stomach snarled in response. They were all hungry, but men of his considerable size had increased appetites. He had no clue how it tasted, but at the moment, anything that wasn’t rotten would do. Before he knew it, he drifted over to the first long table with food on it.

“Hey there, dearie.” a graying mole lady greeted him. “No need to be shy, help yourself! I’ll be honest, you folks showing up is the excuse we needed for a good ole fashioned shindig. I’m Rosie. What’s your name?”

Raeleq smiled, as he’d learned people preferred when speaking to someone his size. “Raeleq, Ma’am. This smells incredible, but…I’ve never been to this world before. What is it?”

Rosie pointed to the big pot of bubbling brown broth. “That’s my spicy root stew, with some mushrooms and grubs. But we’ve also got some BBQ’d grubs, fried beetle innards, and wyrm skewers. What’s your pleasure, Raeleq?”

He thought about it for a few seconds. Most people would’ve been put off by eating bugs, but once you got used to the texture, he found he quite liked them. The southern plains had a number of large bugs that made for good eating, but it was more of a treat than regular fare. Well, when in a new place…

“I’m going to try everything,” he declared.

Judging by Rosie’s squeal, it was the right thing to say. She and another lady got to work, grabbing him a bowl of stew and a heaping plate full of dead bugs and different earthy vegetables. He happily took a seat.

The first taste of salty, savory stew had him groaning and slapping the table in delight. The strong flavors came together like a twister in his mouth, made more intense by the surprisingly intense heat. When he breathed, it was like exhaling fire.

“I love it!” he boomed, and took one ravenous bite after another until tears streamed down his face.

Rosie and her friend clapped and watched as he did his best to fulfill his promise and literally tried everything they put in front of him. The wyrm skewer was tasty, if chewy, and the grubs were sweet and smokey at once, with an excellent crispness to the crunch. The sauteed vegetables made for an excellent palate cleanser in between bites of some of the best food he’d ever had while traveling.

Even the sensory overload didn’t bother him for a change. Raeleq threw himself into indulging himself while the noise level slowly rose with each new person to make it. Multiple bands started playing music, far enough away from each other to not interfere, but still close enough that he picked it all up, even above the chatter and laughter.

All the while, Rosie and her friend Marge peppered him with questions.

“What’s got you imperial folks traveling through our parts? Are we in any kind of trouble? Do you take requests?”

It would have been bothersome, even without the deception. Raeleq was far from the face of the party. He’d be the first to admit that his greatest strengths were his muscle and his inclination towards creatures and the earth. He made for a good scout and warrior, but social situations were…not annoying, but work.

He answered as vaguely as possible, flat-out admitting he wasn’t allowed to say. “But what I can say,” he added at their crestfallen looks, “is that you’re not in trouble. We’ll be coming back this way on our way out, so we might be able to help out a bit more. It’s our duty to protect you all, right?”

The way their faces froze clued him in that he might’ve said something wrong, but then Rosie laughed. “Sure is! And we do appreciate you fine Hermanic citizens. You’ve definitely made everything better in our lives.”

No, there was a nervousness to her Raeleq knew he wasn’t imagining, but he focused on his meal instead. After fifteen minutes of scarfing relentlessly, he was starting to feel a bit full. He still wanted more, but now he questioned it. The molekin must’ve noticed, because they turned their attention away and chatted while he slowed down.

Just as he was about to get up, Jessica sat down across from him, looking around nervously. “Have you tried any of the food?” she asked.

Raeleq blinked, and then glanced down at his plate, the leftover bones, chitin, and skewers heaped high. He looked back at her with a raised eyebrow.

She groaned and slumped. “Okay, so obviously you have, but I’m a bit nervous. That’s…that’s a lot of bugs. I hate bugs.”

Raeleq shrugged, wracking his brain for something helpful to say. “At least they’re already dead. And pretty tasty, too. If you’re afraid of eating bugs, you could always try the stew. It’s the best thing here!” It occurred to him afterwards that there were grubs in that too. Maybe she wouldn’t notice.

“O-okay, I’ll try it,” she said, taking a deep breath. She got up and Rosie ladled out a small helping in a clay bowl Raeleq now saw had molekin holding hands painted along the side, forming a ring of friends.

Her hands shook as she brought the spoon to her lips. A second later her eyes widened. She coughed and sputtered, the spoon falling into the soup with a splash as she tried to breathe. It was funny, but not as much as her eventually calming down, only to say, “That’s…actually really good.”

Raeleq laughed and grabbed a nearby pitcher of some kind of sweet fruit juice and poured her a cup. She took it gratefully, and then got to eating as well, while he finished off the last few things on his plate and settled in to digest in (relatively) quiet companionship.

Despite how spicy it was, Jessica ended up having a bowl and a half, and when she was finished, she pushed it away with a contented sigh. Her eyes and cheeks were red, and her eyes and nose leaked, but she looked as happy as he’d seen her in days. Ancestors, it had only been about a day and a half, and they’d all aged years in that time. None more so than her.

“Hey,” said Raeleq, brows furrowed. “I’ve been meaning to ask. Are you doing okay? Like, overall. I know things are hard, but it’s gotta be hardest on you.”

Jessica inhaled sharply. She looked like she wanted to cry -- or was already crying, but she swallowed hard and shrugged. “I don’t know. Everything is just so…wrong now. And I keep waiting to wake up back in my room, ready to lose another tournament match. The only thing that’s stayed the same is I know my grandfather is disappointed in me.”

Raeleq surprised himself by laughing. And then, surprisingly, so did Jessica. “It’s not funny,” he said between chortles, “but you’re not wrong. If it’s any help, the rest of us are grateful you and Ambrose didn’t sell us out for your own safety. Professor Vanderborn might be disappointed, but the rest of us are really happy to have you on our side.”

He never thought of himself as someone gifted with words, but every so often, he knew he said exactly the right thing. It mostly happened when he spoke from the heart, without pausing to overthink things. This time, he saw the weight leave her shoulders.

“Thank you,” said Jessica, shaking her head with a rueful smile. She brushed a strand of silky black hair out of her face and looked around. “It did get us somewhere interesting, didn’t it?”

“I think so,” he said, nodding thoughtfully. “And we got to meet interesting people. Like Rosie and Marge!”

The two molekin looked up and flashed him big smiles.

“But…” her voice lowered. “As nice as this is, I’m scared. We need to keep moving if we…well, you know. We can’t linger too long anywhere. Not until we have a better long-term plan.”

Raeleq took a moment to think. “I think it’s not worth worrying about it now. We’ve got good food, company, and a bit of peace. Hold onto it as long as you can. No doubt we’ll have to fight again soon. For now, just breathe and focus on our task. If everything goes well, we’ll be moving in about two days or so, right?”

This calmed her considerably, though it took a few seconds for her. It wasn’t in Jessica’s nature to relax, and he didn’t hold that against her. “I guess you’re right. I’ll give it a couple of days until I panic more. Maybe I’ll try something a bit more adventurous. Any recommendations?”

“The BBQ grubs,” he said at once.

Her face filled with despair, and then wry amusement. “Ambrose is going to starve here, isn’t he?”

They both laughed once more.

*****

Raeva relished the chance to just wander around for a bit, no obligations, no threats, just some free time to enjoy herself. The molekin were a gregarious, open people, and they welcomed her with open arms and enthusiasm. It wasn’t hard to join in, grab a bite to eat, and then go mingle. She knew better than to blindly trust anyone, but there was no ignoring the peace and goodwill in the air.

Bedamin celebrations were usually in a big, open circle, with a massive bonfire in the center. They placed food and drink stalls around the edges of that circle, a place to sit and eat with loved ones, and then a ring of nothing but different kinds of entertainment. Music, magic shows, dumb wagers and feats of physical prowess.

Crag wasn’t too terribly different, all things considered. The oval nature of the town was familiar, if a little more cramped and narrow. Rather than a bonfire, they had the center pillar, where Elder Marphy resided. The food and drink stalls were still on the outermost edge, close to the homes built right into the rock wall. The ring of amusement, as she liked to think of it, alternated between games, music and dancing, and the burly molekin showing off.

Raeva stopped at a dart throwing spot, and promptly showed off why she was one of the deadliest women in the south. Although it took a minute to adjust to the much dimmer lighting than she was used to. It was fun, having the nearby people hoot and holler as she hit target after target either dead-on, or close enough that the difference didn’t matter.

But the fun lasted only a little while before it became too easy, and she lost all interest in it. She bowed graciously for her audience and moved on.

The funny thing about Crag was that people were friendly, but nobody hassled her or expected her to stay for long. They were cordial, but she thought she detected this sense of…Wishing she would leave and bother someone else. It wasn’t unlike how certain people at the Academy treated her, and how the Amaric Forum treated her father.

It wasn’t nearly enough to bring Raeva down. There were people to greet, games to play, and the joy of doing it on her own for a change. She’d meet up with her friends soon enough, but sometimes, you just had to wander alone for a while.

As she moved, the music got louder. Banjos let out a twanging wail with each swift strum of the strings, a tall, skinny mole with a star nose plucked away at an upright bass, and one tiny, three foot tall kid banged her claws furiously against drums set on the ground.

The beat sunk its teeth into her, and Raeva gravitated closer. In front of the band, a couple dozen moles danced, or what passed for dancing for them. They shuffled from side to side on their strong but awkward legs, jiggling their shoulders and swaying their claws in the air. It was a wonder they didn’t hit each other more often, as cramped as it was.

Still, she couldn’t help herself. Raeva drifted closer and swayed to the music. The lead banjo player grinned at her and played a little flourish. She laughed with delight and began moving, faster and faster.

At first, she mimicked the molekin, with their odd but fun shuffle. Side, side, spin, sway. It was simple, but as the band picked up the pace, so did the dancers. Side, side, spin, sway, step. They were constantly in motion, laughing and cheering all the while. Her braids, now such a mess after a couple of rough days, swung around wildly, just barely avoiding the shorter people around her.

The song switched, and so did the dance. This time, Raeva felt inside herself for how she wanted to dance. Unlike the last, fast-paced song, this one was slower, with a deep, deliberate thump to its rhythm every third beat. She liked it and bobbed along, stomping once every measure.

Dozens of beady dark eyes watching her intently, echoing her movements a second or two later. They weren’t built for the frenzied, acrobatic whirls of the Bedamin, but anyone could do a simple children’s dance. Stomp, step, step, sway, stomp. Slow, steady, methodical.

Raeva lost herself to the dance. Some things transcended language, species, and even worlds. For the rest of the feast, strangers or not, these were new friends, bound together by community and mutual joy.

Just as the thought sent a thrill down her spine, a wry voice spoke from behind her. “Going native, Ransa?”

She turned to see Ambrose there, looking infuriatingly good in his new, deep purple spider-silk outfit, and freshly groomed. More or less. Raeva was almost happy to see him.

“Just having a bit of fun. Don’t tell me you’re going to be a party pooper on this world too.” She gave him a pointed look.

He scoffed. “It’s hot, smelly, and all of the food is either bugs or overcooked roots and vegetables. I’m beginning to think that this place is a punishment for all my past transgressions. No good deed goes unpunished, right?”

Nearby molekin shifted uneasily. It would be so easy to get mad at him and tell him to fuck off. She had half a mind to do it, but as irritable as she could get, she liked to think she knew Ambrose. He was uncomfortable here, cranky, and spitting venom to try to keep himself safe. The kind, compassionate thing to do would be to show him compassion.

But that wasn’t as fun as making it worse.

“Right. If this is a punishment, it explains why you’re here,” she said to him. “Because I was having a good time before you came to be pissy. Do you think there’s a world out there where you’re capable of being less of an ass?”

Ambrose’s eyes flared with amusement. “Maybe one day we’ll find out. But if that world exists, it will have better food, less stink, and alcohol won’t be banned.” He paused, looking her up and down with a smirk. “The fashion here isn’t too bad though. That outfit suits you.”

It was as close as he got to backing off, she supposed. She huffed and said, “Look, you’ve got two choices here, Amby-lamb. You can either leave, or you can shut up and dance.”

“Dance?” He blinked in surprise.

“Yes, dance. We’re here, there’s music, and I enjoy your company a lot better when you’re not talking. So either make me happy by participating, or happy by leaving. What’s it going to be?”

“Uh. Dance, I guess?”

“Good.” Raeva grabbed him by the hand and pulled him into the fray.

She quickly learned that as smooth as he could be when flinging insults and insinuations, Ambrose had all the grace of a newborn lamb when it came to dancing. She couldn’t resist the urge to say so.

“Hey,” he replied, spinning in place awkwardly and clapping, “I was trained in Amaric Ballroom dancing, not…whatever this is.”

A heavyset mole bumped into Ambrose and nearly knocked him over.

“Hey,” he said, “that was on purpose.”

“Sure was,” the mole said with a chortle.

Raeva joined in for a second, but pulled Ambrose deeper into the dance floor, where a pocket opened up for them. “If you don’t want angry molemen to body-slam you, maybe don’t insult their aroma and culture. You might consider closing your mouth and opening your mind to the possible fun and pleasure to be found in the most unlikely of places.”

“But I like insulting people,” Ambrose protested. “It’s fun. Not my fault others take me too seriously. If more people treated it like a game, they’d be a lot less upset around me.”

“Or you could…Just shut up and dance,” Raeva said again.

For once, he decided to listen and keep his mouth shut, and the two of them spun together. After a couple of aborted attempts at joining in, Raeva rolled her eyes and said, “Watch and do what I do.”

Eventually, with great patience on her end, he got it. And after a couple of songs, Ambrose even seemed to get into it. He flailed around like a maniac in what was an obvious mockery of the way the molekin moved, but rather than being angry, they cheered him on. Raeva loved them fiercely in that moment.

One song turned into another, and again as they moved and got closer. To her great pleasure, Ambrose did keep his mouth shut while they danced. He even started to smile and laugh along with the party. The kind molekin seemed to generously forgive his insults and enjoy the show as they performed.

For all his petty complaints, he wasn’t wrong about the heat or the smell. The airflow in Crag wasn’t great, and this many thick, furry bodies in one area, cooking up a storm turned the party into something of an oven. By unspoken agreement, they left the dance floor and went to a table full of big mugs of juice. Raeva helped herself, but Ambrose hesitated.

“What’s it taste like?” he asked.

Raeva licked her lips. “Like raspberries, but a bit more sour. Why, Ambrose? You afraid of trying harmless juice? We know there’s no alcohol or narcotics here, apparently.”

“I know, right?” he said, a little too loudly. “I’d kill for a beer.”

Everyone near them turned to face them, horror on their furry faces. “What did you say?” one gasped.

Raeva shot him a dirty look. He scowled, and said, “What’s the big deal, anyway? What do you people have against alcohol?”

“Ambrose, shut up,” Raeva hissed.

“Is…Is this a test?” one man asked, nose wriggling in distress.

“Yes,” said Raeva, “It’s a test. Answer the man’s question.”

“But…Part of our deal with your Empire is giving up our vices, working to repent for our sinful ways. You know, and the calamity it brought down on us?” He looked around, receiving a few sympathetic pats on the back. “We hold to that agreement. ‘Cept miscreants like Mulch.”

Ambrose nodded thoughtfully. “Okay. Good answer. Let’s have some of that juice.” The tension eased. He took a thoughtful sip, making a pleased sound.

“Nice save, jackass,” she said, shaking her head. “Remember who we are. We don’t want to wear out our welcome just because you can’t control your mouth.”

“Duly noted,” he said, drinking more. “I’m trying, Raeva. I really am. This doesn’t come naturally to me. What else should I be doing, other than being myself?” The frustration in his voice led her to believe he wasn’t being a shit. At least, not then.

Raeva thought about it for a second, giving it a fair consideration. “I like you when you’re bold and confident, but not when you’re a jerk to people who don’t deserve it. These are good people, who have been taken advantage of.”

“It’s also not our problem,” he whispered, looking around. “We need to keep moving. We can come back and help later.”

And just like that, the magic was gone, and he was the same old Ambrose as before. Raeva sighed. “We might need to pick our battles, but why not this one?”

It was rhetorical. Logically, she knew they couldn’t save everyone. But they’d been welcomed in, fed and clothed, and they would get an escort to Bedrock Spire. They weren’t just random victims anymore. It was personal. Why couldn’t he see that?

“We can’t fall in love with every three legged puppy we find,” he said. “We need to get stronger first, before we do anything. And apparently this world is garbage for combat cards.”

She stared at him, increasingly annoyed until she wanted to punch him. Raeva drained her drink and slammed the mug down. “I’m going to go have fun and enjoy our time here. If you want to obsess over every last detail, go bother Luthor.”

Before he could protest, she left him behind. As hot as Crag was, her head was hotter, increasingly irritated. There was only one solution for this agitation.

Back to the dance floor, among the people she immediately liked and wanted to protect.
