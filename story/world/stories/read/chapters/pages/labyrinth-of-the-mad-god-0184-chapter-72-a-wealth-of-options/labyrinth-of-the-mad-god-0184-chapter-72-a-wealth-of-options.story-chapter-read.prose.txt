One by one, the party walked up to the glowing orb and pressed their palms against its rune-covered surface.

Nick was last in line. When his hand encountered the crystal’s warmth, a colorful prompt that was a stark contrast to the System’s monochrome appeared before his eyes.

Congratulations. You have been granted a petition to the Hall of Heritages.

Would you like to submit a full copy of your profile for the caretakers’ consideration?

He had assumed this process was automatic, and the question caught him by surprise. When he thought about it, there were a few aspects of his new class that he didn’t want to advertise. Ascendant’s Insight and Eldritch Bane were exclusive to dealing with the threat posed by the Labyrinth of the Mad God, and he couldn’t see how they would have any bearing on the Heritages the hall conferred.

Blightborn and Dreadeater were problematic too. Nick wasn’t sure how other species would feel about him sharing DNA with a parasite that had subsumed a sizable chunk of a galaxy, and he didn’t want to be associated with the Hollow God in any way.

After mastering the interface, he removed those four items from his application then gave his permission to share the rest. With that out of the way, there was nothing to do but settle down and wait. Figuro was still grappling with the fate of their people and needed some time to process their grief, so the party gave the guide some space and found other ways to pass the time.

His team began by sweeping the building, making sure there weren’t any monsters lurking in dark corners while searching for valuables hidden amongst the wreckage and any entrances other than the front door. The hall was built with defense in mind. After finding nothing of note, they took turns standing guard while everyone took advantage of the rare moment of downtime.

Nick performed his sword katas while coming up with an organizational system for the mountain of bloodline gems sitting in his backpack. He began by dividing them into categories, then moved on to speculating which would be useful in various situations. He wrapped things up by outlining his thoughts on potential synergies between the various traits, and which he believed would be of the greatest benefit to his friends and allies, although he knew their classes, abilities, and Concepts would have changed considerably while he was away.

Part of the way through, Lurk’s application came back. The beast had three Heritages to choose from, which Figuro said was an excellent result that spoke well of the dinosaur’s path to power. They helped her think the matter through with Bandit translating her questions, though by this point, Nick could get a sense of what the beast was thinking without the lemur’s assistance.

One legacy focused on lightning magic. Another was a martial Heritage intended for bipedal predators. In the end, she went with the third offer, which wasn’t specialized toward beasts, but fully incorporated her Primal Storm Concept. After Lurk made her selection, she passed through the forcefield protecting the portal, raised a claw in farewell, and vanished in a flash of light.

Kenji and Bandit’s results arrived within minutes of each other. Both had five offers to choose from, but didn’t need help in deciding, as one option spoke to them louder than the rest. Nick found himself alone with Figuro after his friends said their goodbyes. Although they were a bodiless construct, it was clear that the guide was a person in every sense of the word. They were grappling with the tragedy that had befallen their planet, and if they didn’t get switched off when Nick departed, would be sitting amidst the ashes of a lost civilization until the party returned.

He felt bad and spent the next few hours asking questions about the city and listening to stories from before Taltos’s advent, learning what life was like on a world where people had been able to use magic from the start. Nick was starting to get worried when the twelve-hour mark arrived. This was taking a lot longer than Figuro had expected, and he was concerned that hiding part of his profile had inadvertently resulted in him being rejected outright.

To distract himself from the rising tension, he opened his menu to peruse his recent gains.

Evasion has increased from [40] to [41].

Sealth has increased from [18] to [20].

Note: You may not increase your Acrobatics and Vital Energy Manipulation skills beyond 25, or Sword beyond 50 until you complete the trials of mastery.

He hadn’t earned a level despite killing quite a few monsters. Advancing his Legendary class was going to take some work, though the power it offered was more than worth it.

There should be plenty of opportunities to level when the stampede arrives. What matters right now is making sure Earth’s defenders have the resources, opportunities, and infrastructure they need to fight at their full potential.

He considered taking some of his trials but decided it wasn’t smart to rush into them without preparing himself first. He planned to work on them while receiving his Heritage, so waiting a few days to get his head in the game made sense. Instead, he sat down and focused on advancing his abilities, integrating the lessons he had learned in the Blade World with the insights he’d obtained during his showdown with the Death of Dreams.

He wound up taking Piercing Ray and Force Armor to Rank 3 without too much effort. He was starting to grasp the underlying principles of spellcraft, and the concepts were cross-applicable. Odd-numbered small steps didn’t come with any choices, but the efficiency bonus was welcome and the visions always left him with a great deal to consider.

When the orb finally lit up six hours later, he learned that he hadn’t been denied after all. The reason Nick had to wait so long was because he had over one thousand offers to choose from.

“Is there some kind of sorting function? I’m having a hard time deciding which offers are worth pondering at length.”

“Oh, did you receive more than one? Yes, there are several tools you can use to organize the information. Why don’t you start by showing me the full list?”

Nick gave his permission to share the information when a prompt winked into existence.

“By the rancid slime of the fourth abyss.” Figaro’s jaw dropped when they saw the results. “I’ve heard of people getting up to twenty, and myths from long before my time tell of heroes with up to a hundred, but never in my wildest dreams did I imagine an applicant could garner interest from 1,117 mentors. Who in blazes are you, Nick? You must really be something special.”

To say he was overwhelmed by his wealth of options would be an understatement of monumental proportion. Fortunately, the filters Figure showed him made it easy to sort, compare, and contrast the long list of Heritages, but even then, coming to a decision was a daunting task.

He began by removing anything that would take years of training or intensive study to even stand at the starting line, as he had an impending invasion to deal with. After eating a cold dinner, Nick came up with a short list that felt like a good fit for his path to power, prioritizing Heritages that would give him a boost here and now but still had potential for long-term growth. He removed anything that relied on a weapon or frequency of mana he wasn’t familiar with, as it made sense to capitalize on his strengths instead of branching in new directions this late into the game.

Since the Heritages were life-long classes that couldn’t be changed, he didn’t have to worry about trait permanence, which was nice, since he had plenty of stones to fill his empty bloodline slots and receiving a legacy wouldn’t use up any.

When he asked Figuro why there were so few skills and abilities listed for each offering, the guide explained that Heritages had multiple stages. New aspects would be unlocked after the others had been mastered and he returned to the hall for additional training.

On the one hand, it made it harder to choose, as he couldn’t plan out his decision based on the total package of power. On the other, that revelation was intensely exiting, since it meant the Heritages were even stronger than they’d seemed while looking through the list.

Nick’s final criteria had to do with humanity as a whole, along with the intelligent beasts that were their new neighbors. Some of the legacies included skills and spells that were utterly unlike anything he’d heard about before. He might be able to obtain insights and techniques his people couldn’t come across any other way, hastening their magical advancement by centuries or more.

In the end, he went with an offer that checked all his boxes. It would fit well with his path to power and synergize with his insanely high attributes. Better still, the Heritage resonated with the knowledge he’d brought with him from the world that was. He thought it would help him master legacy far faster than would otherwise be possible in addition to letting his old life make a meaningful contribution to his new.

When he shared his thoughts with Figuro, their jaw dropped. “I’ve never heard so much as a whisper regarding this Heritage. It doesn’t appear anywhere in my records, which means the requirements are extremely prohibitive, requiring a specialized background my people didn’t have. I don’t understand half the terms that are included with the listing, but it certainly sounds powerful. If you pick it, I would appreciate if you shared some of the details when you return from your mentorship. Indulging my curiosity sounds a great deal more pleasant than the alternatives.”

“Will do. Thanks for the help.”

With that, Nick approached the orb and placed his palm on the surface for a final time. “I’ve made my decision. I will accept the Heritage listed as Magical Engineering and Automated Spellcraft Apparatuses.”

Acknowledged. Your mentor has been informed that you have accepted their offer. Please step into the portal to begin your first session.

Accompanied by a fierce surge of excitement, he ascended the marble stairs and passed through the portal beyond.

Nick had been shunted through a wide assortment of portals, rifts, and teleportation networks by now, including several that weren’t governed by the System, like the wormholes connecting various points within the Tower of Rizzen.

This journey felt more like the last item on that list. While it didn’t trigger a vision this time around, he could tell that instead of traveling from one location to another, he was sliding into a dimension that lay parallel with his own. An artificially constructed plane of existence that was home to tiny nodes governed by different sets of rules.

After entering the hall’s dimension, he was pulled into one of the nodes in the center of the constellation, which Portal Visionary identified as one of the original Heritages generated during the hall’s inception. Oddly enough, the wormhole felt… rusty was the best metaphor he had do describe it, as if the node hadn’t been visited in millennia, if it ever had at all.

When his senses unscrambled, Nick found himself standing in a vast space that reminded him of a modern laboratory. For the first time since his second life began, he was staring at monitors attached to actual computers, although there was clearly magitech involved in their construction in addition to electronic components. Everything was covered in a thick carpet of dust, cementing his certainty that this Heritage had sat empty for centuries on end.

He didn’t spot anything moving through the facility, living or machine. “Hello?” Nothing answered his query so he decided to take a look around—not touching anything as he made his way through the sprawling structure, which was covered in workstations, machinery, and tools whose purposes he couldn’t even begin to divine.

Just when he was starting to suspect something had gone horribly wrong, a portal opened. Through it emerged a man wearing a while lab coat. He was human as far as Nick could tell, although he definitely wasn’t from Earth. The scientist’s core was emitting mana like the sun was rising inside him, and it was clear at a glance that he was several tiers and evolutionary grades above Nick’s own.

When their gazes met, the man startled visibly, as if he couldn’t believe Nick was actually there.

“I’ll be damned. I received a notice from the System, but I assumed it was some kind of error. After all these eons, I thought I would never find an heir to my legacy, but at long last, someone has arrived at my door. I hope this isn’t a mistake. It would be unbearable to have my hopes raised and then immediately dashed after all this time.”
