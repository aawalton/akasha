Day 373, 1:00 AM

The third floor is also dark, as are all until the seventy-fifth. At the bottom of the stairwell leading to floor seventy-six a light shines. Redo says it will come back online in nine hours and sixteen minutes. We need to slow down, so that I have my skill ready on the final floor.

“I guess the real fight starts here.” I smile at Edna. “You can still turn back, I can handle this alone.”

“Have you noticed how the dungeon didn’t give us any attributes last time, despite us destroying all those hatcheries?”

I guess you believe that ignoring the question is a nicer way of saying ‘no’.

“Yes, I’ve noticed. I’ve been telling you from the start that this game was rigged, that the Guide had an agenda, but you thought I was crazy.”

She cocks an eyebrow. “And you’re not crazy?”

“I am, but that’s not what I’m talking about, and you know it.” It’s strange how dying together draws people closer. Edna suddenly seems like a decent human being. One with a botched sense of humor, but at least moral and upright. I wonder whether I meet the criteria in her eyes.

“Do you have any plans for when we’re done with this?” My question is strange, but I think it helps lift the mood. I’m going to try to destroy the crack and Hadriuse with everything I’ve got, but I’m not entirely optimistic about my chances. Our chances.

“I don’t know.” She shrugs. “I was thinking about fixing what can be fixed in this world. The books in Hadriuse’s library contain a lot of information about how the world was before giant bugs started rampaging across the countryside.”

“I can point you to the right books, if you want.” We reach the bottom of the stairs, and six tentacles ending in stingers slam towards us. Edna waves her hand and a wave of fire consumes them, turning them to ash before they reach us. I follow up with the mother of all lightning bolts, a column of purplish electricity zapping the monster dead, and blasting open a hole in its giant bulk.

“This is more efficient,” I lie to Edna. Undoing the evolution would be more efficient, it was also slower, and with the elder abomination that close, I don’t want to risk our lives over a bit of mana.

I step into the empty chamber, its lights completely out, scanner catching nothing, when I notice a sudden gust at the last possible moment. I wave my hand to the right, sending a burst of disintegrating flames at the attacker, incinerating a chitinous blade. It strikes my flank, but fortunately, the spell has worked its magic, and the serrated limb explodes into dust and ashes.

“They got smarter.” I pat the dust out of my servant attire. “Scanner has become a liability. Use a perception spell, one which helps you see in the dark and makes your hearing keener without altering your body.”

Meanwhile, I mobilize my mana. What I want it to do is emulate danger sense, provide x-ray vision, heat vision, and let me hear outside the normal human range.

“Make sure your eyes and ears aren’t overly sensitive, that might cripple you in battle when loud noises and bright flashes happen.”

I take much longer than needed to check out the rest of the floor and find that the elder abominations have scoured it completely. Twenty minutes later, I confirm what I already knew — not a blade of grass, a sprout of shrubs or trees in sight, and no signs of the hatcheries.

“They ate the hatcheries to evolve.” I state the logical conclusion, looking to delay our descent in a way which the eldritch things wouldn’t find strange. “We can expect two elder abominations per floor from now on, and three starting from the ninetieth floor onwards. You replenish your mana, I’ll pace myself to do all the fighting without spending too much of mine.”

I would have liked to check what the assassin bug looked like, but it crumbled to ashes when it struck me. I didn't even catch a glimpse of it, save for the serrated blade with which it attacked me. The limb could have been that of a praying mantis, or something similar, like a mantidfly.

Either way, we head towards the next floor cautiously, if Hadriuse used the chance to evolve a new elder abomination, he could have created something which can lurk in the stairwells, so I keep my eyes peeled.

So far so good, and we reach the bottom. Just like last time, the stingers stab towards us, and this time I get a good enough look for the BSD to inform me of its properties. Seventy percent green mana, ten percent brown mana, ten percent black mana, five percent orange mana, five percent violet mana, base components - twenty percent umbral scorpion, forty percent jagged octopus, ten percent common grasshopper, thirty percent boxer shrimp.

The abomination bursts into ashes in the wake of my firewave, and again, we don’t get to see much past the stingers. I focus my x-ray vision on the edge of the doorway, but the rock blocks my sight. I approach slowly, when my artificial danger sense sends me a vision of a serrated black blade piercing my heart.

The effect is horrible and disjoins my mind from my body. I lurch back, the claw slashing my chest open, striking and breaking ribs, and I return the greeting with a firewave. The abomination dies, and I’m in agony, but a wisp of mana, and a properly focused intent patches me right up.

No wonder wormlords were immortal, just a slight exertion of my will can almost bring me back from the dead. Maybe it could, if my death wasn’t as absolutely terminal in completely destroying my corpse and everything around me.

I stall on this floor too, and repeat the process, without getting injured three more times. Again, the whole thing feels like a Redo loop, and I wonder whether normal people experience that problem.

“Hey, Edna, do you mind if I ask a crazy question?” She motions me with her chin to continue. “Do you ever have that feeling that the same things are happening over and over again?”

“You mean like how you’re fighting the same fight over and over again floor after floor?”

I nod.

“It’s not a feeling, the traps are identical. Either they are trying to dull our senses or they are unable to think tactically.”

I guess the latter is possible. Outer gods, who address me as puny demigod, don’t really need to plan skirmishes between mortals, but it’s better to suspect they are plotting an ambush.

We descend another two floors facing identical attacks, and we’re on our way to the eighty-third. If things keep going like this, we’ll face Hadriuse again in just under six hours. Right on cue, I burn the stinger tentacles to ash, then disintegrate the assassin bug. I enter the chamber, looking left and right, the floor is empty.

I take another step, and yet another vision hits — a flurry of serrated limbs tears me to pieces. I drop to the ground, emanating a devolve pulse all around me, and two of the assassin bugs fall to the ground, convulsing and flaking as they decompose.

A spiteful blade sinks into my thigh, opening a massive wound, and another slashes at my back, the damn abominations hitting me in their death throes.

I forgot to check the bloody ceiling. Anger surges within me, and Rage activates. The whole world turns black before I manage to patch myself up.

“Wake up,” someone shakes me, and I’m afraid I’m sinking in another death loop, but my panicking brain makes out Edna’s voice.

I open my eyes and look at her. The world is pitch black, but I can make out her form thanks to my Initial Darkness Intuition. That’s the first time the skill does something useful.

“What happened?” I cast my perception spell again, that future vision just saved my life.

“I was about to ask you the same thing. The monsters cut you up, but it wasn’t that bad. Then, instead of healing yourself, you tore your pants, tourniqueted your leg, and went to sleep. What the hell?”

Huh. Rage does whatever it takes to keep me alive in any given situation. Usually it’s fighting and killing those attacking me, but I guess sometimes, it just stops the blood loss and goes to sleep to maximize healing and minimize heart rate.

“Another skill of mine, but it misfired this time.” I look at my leg, it’s perfectly fine.

“Don’t worry. I healed you while you slept.”

I stand and stretch my body, not a hint of the grievous wounds I just suffered. Does my high physique mean I can just ignore wounds until my body patches itself naturally? Now that would be funny. How high does one’s physique need to be to regenerate lost limbs?

A good question, something I should test in a future loop, once I have several classes lined up and their level up conditions mapped out and easy to complete in two weeks.

“Are you ready for the next floor?” Edna asks, obviously worried by my silence.

“Yeah, let’s go, we have an ancient archmage to kill and eldritch horrors to banish.”
