“Too much reliance on distributed vitality.”

That was the first conclusion Seraphine drew as Sophran's fingers closed around her throat.

Oh, she was surprised by the outburst of power from one she'd initially assessed as weak. And maybe there was also a hint of fear there too, but even as the pressure around her neck tightened into a chokehold, those emotions barely weighed enough to impede her thoughts.

In less than a second, her gaze roamed the network of vitality and power Sophran had become, noting spirit nodes, thrumming tendrils, and bulging muscles.

Her opponent was no longer a man.

He was a symbiotic matrix. An ecosystem of strength and vitality centered on a single node.

It was impressive. Eerily powerful. But it was also dangerous.

Because living systems contained so many moving parts, unlike the lattices of perfect glass. Motion meant structural strain. Structural strain meant faultlines.

Seraphine smiled.

The pressure around her neck tightened further, almost to a snap, but before that could happen, Seraphine moved—not her whole body, just her fingers—Resonant Scission activating to slice apart the most obvious schism in the matrix: Sophran's connection to the skill itself.

The filament surged outward, cold metallic light gleaming to slice—

Sophran dodged the attack with barely any effort, and Seraphine's eyes widened in shock as she realized she hadn't accounted for his newly improved speed. His grip on her neck tightened—then shifted. Her neck sna—

Seraphine vanished, her body losing depth as she slid sideways out of the physical plane and into the glass she'd strategically placed beside herself. Like water through clenched fists, she slid away from certain death, fear bubbling slightly to the surface of her mind.

She appeared behind Sophran an instant later, a scission already moving to split another seam apart, but Sophran was too fast.

A fist slammed into her before she could even complete the movement, and once more, Seraphine found herself flying through the air. Her body slammed into the earth an instant later, the impact sending reverberations through her whole body.

Body of Glass activated instantly to keep her bones from shattering, and that was the only thing that saved her from what came next—an eruption of lightning vines that stabbed into her like flaming spears.

Another quick slide through the mirror plane took her high in the air, and with a cold fury, she attacked—not against faultlines this time, but to inflict pain. With a fluid flick of her fingers, a volley of invisible glass spears rained down on Sophran—or more appropriately, his afterimage—as an instant later, pain slammed into her back, lightning shearing through flesh as a crackling blade of verdant electricity nearly cleaved her in two.

She swiveled immediately, her body reforming to glass to stop herself from bleeding even as she fired another volley at Sophran.

None of them hit.

Instead, Seraphine found herself in a chokehold once again. This time there was no tightening. Only snap—

Seraphine vanished into the mirror plane again, Sight of Shattered Axis sharpening her reaction time to match Sophran's constantly growing speed. Glass World activated an instant later, her entire domain solidifying into a circular prism of crystal-clear glass that trapped them both.

The skill was an attrition skill, forcing them to fight in a space of her choosing. No one could go out or come into the domain unless she allowed it or died. And Seraphine had no intention of doing either.

With a cold smile, she placed a hand on the prism world, raised her gaze to her opponent, and commanded, "Sever."

The glass world responded immediately, and every surface of the crystalline prism—floor, walls, ceiling—shattered simultaneously without breaking, each panel splitting into dozens, and dozens into hundreds until the entire interior of the Glass World became a labyrinth of floating crystal sheets suspended at every angle imaginable.

And in each sheet.

A reflection.

Hundreds of them. Each a perfect image of herself—eyes sharp, smile cold, and fingers already positioned for Resonant Scission.

Sever hadn't just duplicated her image.

It had distributed her intent across every reflection. Each one capable of directing a scission. Each one tracking Sophran with the same cold, analytical attention as the original.

The question of which Seraphine was real became, functionally, irrelevant.

They were all real enough to be a threat. Real enough to kill him.

And despite all the power thrumming through him, Sophran felt a small thrum of fear race through his body. Slowly, he turned, taking in the labyrinth of reflections surrounding him on every side, above and below, his verdant-gold eyes moving from panel to panel with an expression that Seraphine—the original, standing perfectly still at the dome's center—catalogued with precise attention.

Fear.

Good, she thought. It's high time the roles were reversed.

Then without further hesitation, she moved—an action hundreds of reflections copied at the same time, their hands flicking out in concert to unleash a barrage of resonant scissions at Sophran.

Sophran moved—fast, faster than he should have been—and though he did his best to dodge, he couldn't dodge them all. Not with the limited space and unlimited attacks.

The first scission carved through his shoulder—not flesh alone, but the seam between vitality and muscle. Lightning sputtered violently from the wound as several pathways were sliced apart, destabilizing the flow of energy within his spirit.

A host of tendrils flickered and died.

Another sliced through his thigh before the first could even heal, slicing apart another pathway.

More tendrils died. Sophran's impressive speed dropped, and for half a heartbeat, he faltered.

Half a heartbeat was all Seraphine needed.

More scissions followed.

Some struck flesh. Blood misted into crystalline air.

Others ignored his body entirely, slipping through skin to bite into spirit, severing pathways and destabilizing the ecosystem he'd become. With each attack, more tendrils flickered and died. With each attack, Sophran felt himself losing power.

Yet more attacks came—each slice cleaner, faster, and more powerful than the last.

Sophran twisted, ducked, blurred into streaks of verdant lightning, all in the hopes of killing the real Seraphine and catching a break. He shattered reflection after reflection with sweeping arcs of lightning beams and crushing fists.

But it didn't matter how much he fought or how many panels he shattered. More reflections simply popped out, attacking him in concert.

It was too much. Even with the Miracle Rain, it was too much.

Before long, Sophran's injuries began to pile up. The pain began to slow him down. The tang of his blood and desperation filled the air.

Sophran's vision blurred—not just from pain, but from the sheer volume of pathways being severed faster than Ancestral Continuance could address them.

He was bleeding power. Bleeding connection. Bleeding everything that had made him untouchable minutes ago.

And sadly, all he could do was channel energy into Ancestral Continuance—more and more until vitality itself bled from his pores. Regenerative Mist activated in stuttering bursts, energy wasting from severed pathways. But Sophran didn't care. All he wanted was to regenerate faster—to heal his injuries faster than Seraphine could inflict them.

He didn't even care to kill her anymore. He simply wanted to survive.

Desperate, he raised constructs of lightning and living storm to shield himself, but Seraphine's attacks simply passed through, slicing into joints and tendons to render him helpless.

Sophran, however, didn't give up. Like life itself, he moved, evading attacks and shattering reflections in a futile attempt to buy himself a break. But the reflections simply reappeared as soon as the panels were repaired, which was instantly.

Sophran couldn't catch a break. His injuries were becoming life-threatening, and his pathways were a severed mess of energy and chaos that made it utterly impossible to activate another skill other than the ones he already had going.

A scission cleaved his back an instant later, slicing through flesh and spirit in a blistering wave of agony. Sophran screamed and turned to shatter the reflection that had done it, regenerative mist spilling out of him in suffocating waves.

But this time, as his fist slammed into the glass panel to shatter it, something unexpected happened.

The panel… fogged.

Condensation spread across the panel surface like steam, and the reflection within suddenly vanished.

His eyes widened.

And without hesitation, he forced every ounce of energy he could gather through his broken pathways into the Regenerative Mist. Pathways burned. Entire energy networks unraveled.

But the skill surged, mist erupting out of him in a storm of roiling energy—not controlled, just outward—in every direction at once, verdant and luminous and carrying so much concentrated vitality that the air itself seemed to glow.

One panel fogged. Then three. Then dozens.

Reflections blurred. Attacks diminished. And finally, Sophran felt his pathways begin to knit back together.

The mist spread fast.

Faster than Seraphine could stop it when she realized what he had done.

Eyes wide, she glanced at Sophran, the only one left in a sea of fogged reflections, and for the first time, she felt the weight of the fear bubbling within her.

Sophran had turned her own world against her. And to make things worse, he was healing. Rapidly.

Wounds closed visibly. Dead tendrils blossomed back to life. And faultlines came together cleanly as if they'd never been fractured.

Her gaze locked against his… and Seraphine ran, racing toward the closest panel to seek refuge in the mirror plane.

She didn't make it in time.

Pain slammed into her abdomen as a crackling fist punched straight through her stomach.

Not from the direction she'd been tracking. But from below, the blow carrying not just the physical force, but a concentrated beam of verdant lightning that detonated on impact.

Seraphine slammed into the top of her own Glass World dome with enough force to crack it from the inside.

The world shattered. And disoriented, she fell.

The ground came up fast.

Body of Glass activated—bones held, barely—but the blistering wound in her belly was beyond what her defensive skill could fully address. She slammed into the charred earth an instant later, body cracking from the extensiveness of her injuries.

Sophran had used her own path against her.

He had found her own faultlines.

But she was still alive. And she was not done yet.

Downing a pile of health potions still in their bottles, Seraphine pushed herself out of harm's way as her shattered world rained down on her.

Her glass body cracked from the strain of movement, and she hastily downed another pile of healing potions, her crystal gaze locking on Sophran, who remained untouched at the center of her shattered world, his verdant-gold irises dim from all the pathways he had burned to shatter her skill.

He was severely injured, both in body and spirit, but Seraphine knew that wouldn't be the case for too long.

Sophran wasn't overwhelmingly strong like some of the other transmigrants Seraphine had faced in her lifetime. Even with the buff he had received, she was still able to evade him. That was a testament to her own strength.

No, the danger with Sophran was his seeming immortality; the persistent healing of body and soul that made it almost impossible to kill him.

Even now, that healing was taking place right before her eyes—bones and tissues mending in seconds while her own injuries dragged like cracks spreading through tempered glas—

Power exploded from him before she could finish the thought, and Seraphine snarled in a mixture of envy and hatred, eyes wide as she watched formerly dim verdant-gold irises burst to life once more, gleaming like small suns.

His healing was complete. All the damage she had caused, all the pathways he had burned—all of it, healed in an instant.

It was insane. Terrifyingly so.

Staggering backward, Seraphine cursed her lack of a healing bloodline as Sophran met her gaze, the hatred in them palpable.

There was no doubt about it this time. No more misplaced empathy in his gaze.

He was going to kill her.

But while Seraphine was weakened—severely—she wasn't finished.

It was time to bring out her own big guns.

After all, in war, as in glass, timing was everything.

And what better time to use her aspect of reflection than now?

Sophran raised his hands. Thunder boomed, and a blistering beam of verdant electricity slammed downward—toward her—so powerful it ripped through the fabric of space itself.

Seraphine watched it descend with a pained smile, analyzing. Always analyzing.

And the moment it reached her, she raised a hand to it and muttered, "Reflect."

Sophran was not prepared for the beam that slammed him into oblivion an instant later.

It seemed, after several hours of warfare, the battle was only just beginning.

Miles away, where the earth itself had become a weapon, the herald of storm cried out declarations the storm couldn't hear.

Against the transmigrant of Silent Earth, Ashscar was dying.

Ashscar's lungs burned.

Not from exertion—though there was plenty of that—but from screaming declarations the storm couldn't hear.

For the past fifteen days, he had heralded destruction upon destruction upon their enemies, each utterance establishing him as the second most powerful storm transmigrant after Nymera. He was the mouth of the storm. The pressure before impact. The fear before annihilation. Every declaration he'd made had come to pass.

But now… nothing.

His voice left his throat. He felt it leave—felt the declaration form in his core, surge through his spirit, exit his mouth with all the force and authority his path had built over two weeks of war.

And then it simply... stopped.

Not deflected. Not countered.

Absorbed.

The earth beneath his feet swallowed it whole—sound, intent, authority—as if he'd shouted into a vacuum and expected an echo.

His opponent, meanwhile, stood thirty meters away. Broad-shouldered and patient, doing absolutely nothing beyond keeping Ashscar within range. Over ten minutes had passed since their first clash, and in all that time, Volgrim Kaizen hadn't moved an inch. Hadn't attacked. Hadn't even raised a hand. Hadn't even blinked.

He just stood there, feet firmly planted on the ground and arms folded behind his back, watching Ashscar as if he were watching a mere child.

Ashscar hated it, and for the umpteenth time, he tried to escape the range of Volgrim's abilities. But it didn't matter how far he teleported—when he looked back, Volgrim was always there, watching him with those flat dark eyes that carried neither cruelty nor satisfaction. Just the particular patience of someone waiting for a child to tire themselves out.

The expression spurred a fury within Ashscar. And with a soundless snarl, he tried again—channeling everything Zarxan's blessing had given him: clarity beyond doubt, speed beyond hesitation, power beyond recklessness—into another declaration.

Wings burst out of his back as he merged with Giselle, his lips forming into a beak as the declaration formed—pure and sonorous—

The sound died as Volgrim's domain of enforced silence buried it, and Ashscar felt himself choke on the rest of the sound. His eyes watered from the pain of the backlash, and when he glanced at Volgrim's impassive expression, a surge of rage erupted within him.

"You!" Ashscar growled soundlessly, his eyes burning with rage. He may have been rendered functionally mute. Useless. But he was still a storm cultivator, and he could still fight without his voice.

Wings flapping as he rose high into the air, he raised a hand to the storm and called down a blistering beam of crimson-gold lightning. He looked down at Volgrim, who had risen steadily with him, the earth beneath his feet now a hill, and snarled.

With raw, desperate violence, he pulled. The beam struck Volgrim with a sound like thunder, and Ashscar finally let out a smile.

He might be a herald without voice. But he certainly still had bite.

His smile, however, was short-lived when the dust of the explosion cleared to reveal Volgrim—unarmored, uninjured, and utterly unaffected by the attack—except for a slight tick of irritation in his jaw.

Ashscar shuddered, reeling in shock.

He realized then the trap he was in.

His entire path required the storm to hear him. Required his declarations to reach their destination. And Volgrim was the antithesis to that—his path canceling out sound entirely.

He was useless. A herald with no voice standing in a war that needed him to scream.

There was no winning this battle. Not with all the blessings and buffs in the world. There was only one solution. Complete retreat.

However, just as he made the decision, Volgrim's patience came to an end.

He shifted slightly—barely a movement, more a settling of weight—and the silence deepened. Not louder. Heavier. The particular quality of earth that had been patient for eons but had finally reached its limit.

Ashscar tried to flee, but before he could cross more than a few meters, a massive hand closed around his torso—not flesh, but hard, unyielding rock.

Ashscar choked, eyes wide as the hand tightened, grinding against flesh like iron against leather.

Ashscar didn't just take it. He summoned lightning, he screamed, he unleashed Giselle—he did all he could, but rocks couldn't feel pain, and an instant later, he felt bone snap.

Pain lanced through him like a spear straight through the heart.

He'd barely recovered from that when another snapped. And then another…

His vision blurred in agony. Another bone snapped—his ribs now, he thought distantly—and darkness began creeping in at the edges. His fingers scraped uselessly against rock. Giselle shrieked soundlessly above them.

It was all for naught.

The storm couldn't hear him.

It was right there, vast and furious, roiling above him, yet deaf to his voice. Deaf to his pleas.

This is how I die, he thought as another bone snapped, puncturing his flesh.

The world dimmed. His body fell limp.

And then—

The air changed.

Not temperature. Not pressure.

Intent.

Ashscar felt it even through the crushing grip and the absolute silence of Volgrim's domain.

Someone had come to his rescue. Someone who had brought the storm's wrath with them.

"That's more than enough," a familiar voice said, their tone cold, their power overwhelming.

Weakly, Ashscar turned his head, his vision swimming and bloodshot.

But the glow of power and wrath surrounding Nymera was impossible to miss. Her entire body was drenched in blood, and she looked as if she had aged several decades but was more than willing to lose a hundred more.

Shuddering in relief, Ashscar looked down at what Nymera held as a weapon and felt his heart jerk back to life.

It was a skeleton with the face still attached—a face he recognized as that of the blood transmigrant.

Nymera had killed her own opponent and had come to save him.

Ashscar didn't feel like dying anymore.
