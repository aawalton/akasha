As I lay there, begging, crying, waiting for death. I couldn’t help but think about my own mental state. I hadn’t frozen exactly; I was moving, squirming like a pathetic little worm. But I didn’t run, even though that was clearly the smart thing to do. Panic? Shock? Probably both. But I also felt... sad. Like I was losing something before I ever really had it.

I know that sounds stupid, considering I was about to get stabbed by a barmaid. But still… this world. It was cool. Harsh maybe, but undeniably cool, and I’d barely gotten a taste of it. Just one tavern. Just one barmaid. One with large tits, yes—but that’s not the point.

Okay, maybe it’s a little bit the point.

I wanted to live in this world. Not just die in it five minutes in. So I had to survive. Which meant I had to act now. And so I fricking did.

I grabbed the chair I had tripped on earlier and used it as a shield, just as her knife came down. It clanged hard against the wood. I used that opening to scramble to my feet and run like fuck toward the door.

Every person in the tavern stopped talking. All eyes were on me. Nobody moved to help, though. Not even the dwarves.Thanks, guys.

But just as I reached the door—bam—it swung open. Two guards walked in, full armor, swords and everything. For the moment, I felt relief.

“Oh thank god,” I babbled. “Please help! She’s trying to kill me!”

I pointed at Hildi. “It was a misunderstanding! I swear! I didn’t…”

But when I looked back at Hildi, she was already on her way back to the counter, calm as a sunset. She’d just… switched back. Like nothing happened. Apparently, seeing the guards made her reconsider murder.

Then both guards unsheathed their massive swords at the same time and turned to look at me.

Oh. Fuck.

Right. Skyrim rules. I was flagged as a criminal. Assaulting a citizen—even by pulling them—triggers the guards. Which made sense. I was the aggressor, technically. But were they here to kill me?

Fast probability check – Odds of escape: Zero.

I raised my hands, murmuring every whimpery apology I could think of. “I’m sorry, I surrender, please,” and such.

“Stop, criminal,” one of the guards ordered, even though I was already frozen stiff.

“You can pay a fine of 867 Sigots for your crime, or come with us to face the Baron’s judgment.”

Relief flooded through me. I wasn’t going to die. Not right now, anyway.

“I don’t have any money,” I said pitifully. “Please. I’m sorry…”

They tied my hands—tight. It hurt. Not just “ow” hurt, but sharp, real pain that dug into my wrists. Then they marched me out of the tavern, out into the town.

The same town that looked completely abandoned earlier now had eyes peeking out of every window. People had clearly heard the commotion. And now I was the entertainment.

Great. I was supposed to be the Chosen Hero or something—being paraded through the streets like a criminal… which, admittedly, I was.Tied up, humiliated, and whimpering.

My last life hadn’t been amazing, sure, but this? This was just sad.

We reached a manor house in the center of town. Despite the late hour, it was alive with activity. Servants and maids moved about quickly—carrying ledgers, trays, scrolls, candles. A dog barked once and got immediately shushed by a kid in noble-looking clothes. One guy was polishing a chandelier.

Why? I don’t know. Nobles are weird.

In the middle of it all sat a fat man in a fancy chair, robes draped over his wide frame like a melted cake. This had to be the Baron. He looked bored. Advisors flanked him on both sides, trying to look important.

Then one of the advisors handed a piece of paper to the Baron and gestured toward me—apparently, it listed my crime.

Hold on. I call bullshit. There’s no way the events at the tavern could’ve been written up and delivered by the time we got here. We were barely ten minutes out, tops. It’s not like there were any witnesses running ahead with a report.

Still, there it was. My crime. Officially documented.

“Well…” started the Baron lazily, “we can’t have every adventurer coming to our quiet little town and harassing our lovely Hildi, now can we? What have you to say for yourself?”

This time, I tried not to whine and beg. Or at least, I tried to whine and beg purposefully.

“Sir, please forgive me, I... I didn’t mean to… attack her.” I tried to sound less stupid. He called me an adventurer, didn’t he? That had to count for something. “I’m an adventurer. I want to help the kingdom...” I managed, barely holding it together.

“Help the kingdom, eh?” The Baron stroked his chin, considering. “Maybe after you learn a bit of manners. A week in the cell—yes, that should be enough. We do need adventurers, after all.”

A week… I thought, as the guards grabbed me and started marching me down the stairs.

That’s… that’s good, isn’t it? Not execution, not torture. Just seven days. Basically nothing.

The guard shoved me into a small, stinking, dripping cell and slammed the iron bars shut.

A week is nothing, really. I’ll get another real chance in this world after that—and next time, I swear I’ll manage not to screw it up.

I lay down on the straw pile in the corner… and cried myself to sleep.
