I could barely move, stuck in place like a statue. It was nothing Pirhani did, standing before me. More an instinctual reaction, my mind praying that if I remained still she wouldn't be able to see me, which obviously wasn't true. It was shock more than anything.

The tension in my body released gradually.

"I'd heard he allowed a new Death affinity Reaper into the Umbral Plane, the fool." Pirhani raised a finger to my face. There was no fingernail, no visible joints or even skin on the white appendage. "Only an Amethyst. What a disappointment." She brushed a hand through the purple fabric on my back. "Are you eager to rid yourself of this hideous mark?" she asked, indicating to the symbol of Valciferous emblazoned there.

I remained silent. There was nothing she could do to me here. That's what Valciferous had said. The gods couldn't directly interfere. Assuming he told the truth about that. I could only pray he did.

"And no shadow, too?" Pirhani giggled, looking at my feet. She slid her finger up the back of my neck into my hair and stroked it menacingly.

I could feel the sharp point scraping against my scalp. My heart beat faster.

"He truly played you, didn't he? What did he promise? Revival of a loved one? Monarchy over a city in one of the inner zones? Freedom from this burden? None of which he can actually grant, mind you."

The promises were all lies? Valciferous hadn't been honest with me before, leaving out the small detail of my detachment from the Vitrial Plane. But no… It didn't matter right now. Pirhani could be lying to me just as easily. And regardless, I was at his mercy without my shadow. I'd made the deal, for better or worse.

"I can get you out of it, you know…" Pirhani cooed. Her hand cupped my cheek. "It's not too late."

And there it was. The thread of temptation I knew was coming.

I pushed her hand away gently. "I don't break my bonds."

She laughed. A cackling thing, like electricity bouncing off a Van de Graaff generator. "Well, aren't you just a righteous little thing? Reminds me of another Death Reaper I once knew."

I froze. She knew the last Death Reaper? Of course she would. These were gods. He hadn't even thought to ask Valciferous about it.

But no.

Stop listening to her! She's toying with you. Trying to distract you.

I brushed past her. My horse still waited by the fence.

Pirhani snatched my wrist. The points of her fingers dug into my skin, drawing blood.

So I wasn't completely immune from her after all… I didn't have time to consider the consequences of that.

"Don't turn away from me, you insect!"

I looked back to her, faceless expression visibly contorted in rage. Her eyes had darkened from yellow to burnt orange. I'd have to test the limits of whatever prevented the gods from harming Reapers eventually. May as well be now.

I began to summon my scythe in my other hand.

Then a little ball of orange fury jumped down from above us and landed on Pirhani's head. Nuzzle slithered down her arm and bit her hand with those newly enlarged teeth.

Pirhani shrieked in pain and released me.

I didn't give it a second thought and launched myself up onto the horse's back, Nuzzle already clutching to my shoulder. My scythe cut through the tie holding it to the fence and I urged the beast into an instant sprint.

Pirhani didn't give chase. She only screamed. "This isn't the last you'll see of me, Zephyr of Death. And when we meet again, I'll be the last thing you see!"

I didn't look back. We exploded out through the gate portal into the Meadow at top speed and headed straight in the direction of the Meridian.

I pushed the horse harder as we aimed for a giant glowing yellow beacon ahead. Trix had given me an item she earned. A compass that projected a waypoint into my vision based on a specific location I thought of as I activated it.

I rode along the edge of a forest. We were close now, the beacon like a wall just around the corner. I reared the horse back as we passed the last tree and the tavern came into view. The Meridian and the adjacent stable were surrounded by a few wagons and several horses.

All were dead and destroyed.

Waiting amid the blood-stained grass like silent guardians were three Reapers and a Sin.

Nuzzle groaned like a teenager who'd been asked to take out the trash.

I cursed under my breath. They'd somehow expected someone might use the taverns to alert higher rank Reapers and request aid? Pirhani and her minions had thought of everything, laying contingencies even this far away from their attack in Meadowvale.

It was more than a little impressive. And more than a little annoying.

I should have expected as much from someone who was considered a god.

They'd seen us the second we came into view. The Reapers were Emerald, Amethyst, and Amber. I couldn't see the backs of their cloaks, but I was certain they'd all sworn oaths to Pirhani like the others.

The Sin was Unchained like all the Sin's the puppeteer had under his strings. Spines covered its entire body like a porcupine, but it had large eyes and limbs like a frog. Despite both its inspirations being pocket-sized, this Sin was about the size of one of the wagons that now lay in shambles nearby. I examined the monstrosity with my Second Sight.

Emerald.

That was the rank above me. I held my breath, hoping to see a little star or skull orbiting the monster's Will. No such luck, just a snowflake. Glacial affinity, like Calliope. Assuming I could even defeat it (and all three Reapers), this Sin wouldn't be my ticket to the next rank.

Of course, that assumption was on the verge of idiotic to begin with.

I looked around as if an alternative solution might present itself.

It didn't.

I didn't know of any other taverns that linked the different zones. I was sure they existed, I just had no way of finding them. The compass I held was single use, so I couldn't reset it to a new location even if I had one in mind.

Maybe I could walk up casually and pretend to be on their side? Or maybe I didn't even need to fight them. I just needed to get into the tavern. If I sprinted through their line… Aero Shift might be able to get me to the door. Maybe I could—

The tavern opened and a pair of Amber Reapers walked out.

They stopped in their tracks when they saw the carnage. They turned to run back inside, but giant hands of viridian shadow erupted from the Amethyst Reaper on guard and grabbed them. The Reapers struggled but were bound tight and plopped onto the ground before the Unchained.

They were distracted. No time to hesitate. I kicked the horse with my heels, urging it into a sprint. Just get to the door.

Just get to the door.

The Unchained wasted little effort on the prey it'd been presented. It spat a volley of meter-long spines from its chest into them both. Blood sprayed as the Reaper's were each skewered in a dozen places.

One swirled into her cloak, Health depleted, and vanished, phasing out to Vitria before she could even cry out in shock.

The second slumped to the ground, eyes wide. Blood dribbled from his open mouth. A spine struck straight out of his chest. A faint Amber mist drifted from where the spine penetrated.

The Unchained leaned forward and inhaled the mist through its nose, pulling more and more out like a ropes of scarves from a magician's sleeve. I couldn't quite tell, but its aura seemed to grow brighter.

I didn't have time to ponder.

But one thing was clear: they were not taking prisoners.

With the unfortunate Reapers disposed of, all attention turned to me.

Too late to change the plan now.

I continued my charge.
