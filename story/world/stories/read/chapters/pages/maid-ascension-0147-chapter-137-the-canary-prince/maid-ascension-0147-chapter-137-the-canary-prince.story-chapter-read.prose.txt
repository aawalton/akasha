Over the last few weeks, between her information-gathering work with the Funnel and Horn and the countermeasure spell for the Truth Stone, Lira had imprinted one more spell with Kavard in mind. She was pretty sure it would work, even though she had never tested it on a person, or an animal for that matter. This lack of thorough testing was what made Lira hesitate. She wasn’t sure if the spell would work on a man at a full gallop, bouncing erratically along the road. When she designed it, her idea had been to cast it on a still target, preferably one that was asleep, so she decided to wait for Kavard to stop before doing anything drastic.

Speaking of drastic, after watching Kavard’s reckless riding through the farm fields, she considered other methods of killing the prince. She could always turn the blot of ink into a Runic Sound Mimicry Enchantment and produce a loud noise to spook his horse.

She shook her head. That was too random for her liking. If he survived the fall, or the horse stayed calm for some reason, her paper would be discovered, and she would have shown her hand for nothing. So she would stick to the first plan and improvise only if her life was in real danger.

She followed his ride through her Remote Eye spell. Kavard’s expression remained dark and tense the whole way. Lira could not help but imagine what would have happened if she were an ordinary maid who happened to strike up a friendship with Aric over some books. Kavard would have come for her all the same, and she would never have seen him coming.

What he had done to Mara, Amelia, and Carry had been a man covering his tracks, but this... this was something else. Pure vindictiveness aimed at hurting his younger brother.

What was he expecting to happen? Aric would definitely learn that Kavard had slipped away from Estophon the moment he returned from his trip, and even if the first prince’s plan was successful, it would only turn his brother and main rival for the throne into his enemy.

There was no reason for any of it, no justification at all, but she suspected that Kavard had long since stopped needing to justify his actions, not even to himself. He did what he wanted, and that was all there was to it.

An hour into the ride, her head began to throb. She did her breathing exercises and found that her mana was running low, which she could more or less tell by now after a month of practice.

Kavard was still four or five hours away, so she made herself lie down on her table on the first floor and take a nap, since she would need to be fully rested by the time he reached the capital.

When she woke, the prince was already on the outskirts of the city. A glance toward the nearest window revealed that the sky was turning grey at the edges, which meant he had been riding without stopping all night. The horse looked worse for wear, its head hanging low and its breath heavy, though Kavard did not seem to care about its health at all.

To keep from draining herself again, she checked on him for a minute every fifteen minutes until he reached the city proper, then for a minute every five minutes.

Once inside the walls, she expected him to head straight for the palace… head straight for her.

She was surprised when he turned off toward the lower district and stopped in front of somewhere familiar to her.

Salvek’s Brothel.

Kavard descended from the horse, leaving it panting in the street as he knocked heavily on the door.

Lira immediately activated the funnel rune as a man opened the door. She shifted her Remote Eye view to his face and recognised Dario, Salvek’s second in the duel. She was surprised he was still manning the building. She had thought Salvek’s relatives, who had been fighting over his inheritance, would have run him off.

“Your Highness, you have made it.”

“Yes, slipping away from Estophon was child’s play, thanks to the pendant you gave me.”

So that was where the pendant had come from. Lira’s eyes narrowed.

“Of course. Salvek’s former investor asked me to tell you that he is counting on you to come out on top in your conflict with Aric,” Dario said, closing the door behind them. “The kingdom would be in great peril if that boy ended up as king instead of you.”

Mr. Investor, again. It was the same code name she had found in Salvek’s ledger, next to a pile of magical gold enchanted in a similar manner to the coins Harwin carried. With Kelroth being named Master of Coin, Lira would be shocked if Mr. Investor turned out to be anyone other than him.

“Oh, I know,” Kavard scoffed. “Killing him alone would not be enough. I need him to lose everything he holds dear first.”

“So what are you planning, Your Highness?”

“Draw me a bath first. I have been riding all night, and I need to refresh myself.”

“Right away, Your Highness,” Dario said.

“And see to the horse outside,” Kavard added as an afterthought.

“Of course, Your Highness.”

Dario led him upstairs to a private room with a tiled floor, where an enchanted tub with an active heating rune stood. Lira watched as the prince set his belt, with his coin pouch still attached to it, on a stool by the wall before he started undressing. She kept her view fixed on the stool and then on the tub, looking no further than necessary.

Lira doubted she would get a better opportunity than this. She would have preferred to do it while he slept, but she guessed a bath would work too.

She waited until Dario left the room and the prince had settled into the water before casting through the link.

“Pika. Asatan. Frushum.”

The paired sheet slid out of the coin pouch and drifted low along the floor, small and quiet enough to escape the prince’s notice. Lira guided it behind the base of the tub, away from the puddles of water that had splashed over the rim, until the paper lay directly under the prince. She then unfolded it completely and cast the ink spell on the blot she had added in the library.

“Asatan. Tir. Kisar.”

The blot shifted into a Riad rune and a Fila rune, encircled by Tarasana and Kurur runes. Once the design was in place, she cast the spell.

“Kisar. Riad. Fila. Tarasana. Kurur.”

Symbol. Wind. Fire. Pocket. Space.

She had gotten the idea from a book about mining that she had read months ago. Apparently, people were so bad at detecting foul air that they took small birds with them, mainly canaries. Once the bird died, they knew they were standing in a pocket of bad air.

After her work on the Improved Breathing spell, Lira understood enough about breathing to know that burning air made it unbreathable, so she had created a spell that turned good air into bad air by burning it inside a pocket of space. The spell didn’t produce an open flame, though the paper yellowed and turned brittle faster than it did with the warmth rune. More importantly, just like her warm pocket spell, the pocket contained the effect, which meant the spoiled air could not drift past its boundary.

When she tested it on a lit candle, the flame died out in the middle of the drafty library as if it had been smothered by a glass cup.

This was the first time she was testing it on a living person. She had made sure the pocket of space was big enough to engulf the tub fully, including Kavard’s head, and she hoped it would work as intended. Otherwise, she would have to resort to a more drastic measure.

She watched him through the Remote Eye spell as he sat in the steaming water, unaware of the changing air quality. He scowled at the ceiling, saying nothing as he soaked in the warm water. After a while, he let out a yawn and slumped a little lower.

Lira’s heart started thumping faster as she watched his head dip even further, until it disappeared completely beneath the water.

Lira started counting. “One, two, three... five... ten... thirty... sixty...”

A minute had passed, and he still hadn’t lifted his head above the water. She waited five more minutes, then fifteen for good measure.

This must be it. Surely he must be dead by now. Her heart started beating even harder.

She wished she had created a Remote Detect Life spell, but sadly, the spell she knew didn’t stack on top of her Remote Eye spell, so she couldn’t tell for sure through magic.

The prince had long since stopped moving, as she couldn’t see a rise and fall of his chest.

Had she really done it? Had she actually killed the first prince of the kingdom?

It looked like a perfectly ordinary death, at least. Nothing led back to her.

He had ridden all night, climbed into a hot bath, and drowned while falling asleep, which was something known to happen to careless men.

She shook her head.

No, no matter how much it looked like an accident, they would investigate the death of a prince thoroughly. There was no avoiding that, and the Detect Hate spell would lead them directly to her.

Kavard had been seen threatening her in front of two guards, so her name being on the list wouldn’t be a surprise.

Still, she needed to cover her tracks thoroughly.

“Asatan. Tir. Kisar.”

The first thing she did was transform the Bad Air rune into a Mana Stabilization rune.

“Kisar. Maja. Tureth. Tarasana. Kurur.”

She swept the space clean, eliminating every disturbance caused by her casting. The heating rune in the tub should have served as cover, but Lira wasn’t taking any chances. Divination worked by extrapolating from clues, and an unknown spell like Mana Stabilization would sow so much confusion that any magical divination should be thwarted by the lack of information, maybe. All that was her paranoia at work; in all her research about how murders were investigated, she had yet to learn about the existence of a Mana divination spell, only Detect Hate and Improved Perception spells. But she still decided to take that extra step just in case.

“Pika. Asatan. Frushum.”

The paper left its place behind the tub and slid out through the window. Once it was outside, drifting in the wind, she cast again.

“Pika. Fila. Curad.”

The sheet burst into flame, and her remote view vanished with it. She burned the twin on her desk for good measure. Then she ran through the library, deactivating every magical effect she had left running, starting with every linked paper she had created before moving on to her enchanted bookmarks. She continued until the only magical things left in the library were the heating plates that Emberborn and his apprentices had enchanted, and one active mana obfuscation bookmarker in her alcove. She carried the primers back to the upper room of the tower, hiding them separately, then returned to her alcove and used her ink spell to wipe the writing off the magical journals where she kept her spell theory. With her Perfect Recall spell and ink spell, she could easily restore them.

She sealed her father’s lexicon inside the false stone of her alcove, not having the heart to destroy it.

When all evidence of her crimes had been disposed of or hidden, and her hands had stopped shaking enough to hold a quill, she added a few last preparations before sitting down at her desk.

She waited for the guilt to hit her and found little of it. Kavard needed to go, and her only regret was that Lyth, who knew nothing about his cruelty, would probably mourn him. Aric would be sad too, she guessed. The prince had always had trouble distinguishing between the brother he grew up with and the monster Kavard had become.

She shook her head once more. This was not the time for that. There was still one spell left, the one she had spent the past few weeks working out: her solution to defeating the Truth Stone.

“Pika. Cognar. Veshar.”

Mind. Knowledge. Concealment.

She felt the spell settle over her thoughts, slowly, like a fog spreading over her mind.

Outside the window, the sun was rising over the palace, and Lira Tenlan was already sitting at her desk, the ever-dutiful maid working early hours to keep a library running that was frankly too big for her to handle alone.
