Sakura spent a little more time alone, listening to the night forest and to herself. After the Scroll of Self-Knowledge, everything inside her felt strangely empty and, at the same time, far too crowded with thoughts. She did not want to discuss what the magic had shown her right away, but she also did not want to shove it too deep. For now, it simply stayed with her. Honest. Uncomfortable. Too personal.

But the world, as usual, had no intention of waiting for her to process any of it.

Sakura got to her feet, brushed dry needles off her clothes, and walked over to Gilbert. He was standing a little apart, as if he had been waiting from the start for her to finally return from her thoughts to more practical matters.

“All right,” she said, stopping beside him. “Now tell me directly. Where and how are we going to get the core of a dead mage?”

Gilbert did not answer right away. First he looked around the forest, as if even now he believed someone might be listening. Then he leaned a little closer and spoke in a lower voice.

“There’s a place. Mages hide there.”

Sakura lowered her voice too without meaning to.

“They hide?”

“Yes. A shelter. Small. There aren’t many of them there. Very few.”

She frowned.

“And we’ll be able to get a dead mage’s core there?”

“Maybe. Or maybe we’ll find a way to reach one through them,” Gilbert said. “Either way, it’s worth going there. It’s east, through the forest, and we won’t stray too far from the road to the Lake of Six Mists.”

Sakura nodded slowly.

“Then let’s go.”

Gilbert lifted a brow.

“Not now.”

She looked at him.

“Why?”

“Because you trained in the swamp today, leveled up, and then went through that scroll. Moving through the forest at night in that state is a bad idea.”

Sakura wanted to argue, but no real objection came to her. As always, Gilbert was right. They needed rest, or they would burn themselves out before they ever reached that shelter.

“A few hours of sleep,” he said. “Then we move before dawn.”

That ended the argument. They found a place a little deeper between the trees, where roots and brush hid them from the path. Sakura settled against Pampli’s warm side. The exhaustion hit almost immediately, the moment she stopped moving. Thoughts of the scroll, the dead mage’s core, Lir, and the strange shelter still clung to her mind, but she no longer had the strength to hold on to them.

Gilbert took the first watch.

“Wake me later,” Sakura muttered.

“I will.”

She wanted to say something else, maybe something a little sharp, but instead she only pressed her shoulder deeper into Pampli and slipped into sleep.

They did not sleep long, taking turns. But even those few hours did their job. When Gilbert woke her closer to that gray hour before dawn, Sakura’s head was clearer and her legs no longer felt like cotton.

They packed up quickly. Veyr was very good at breaking people of the habit of taking too long to get ready. If you had a goal, it was better to keep moving before something got in your way.

Pampli got up from the ground with visible annoyance, making it clear that he no longer cared where they dragged him next as long as he did not have to sit still. Sakura gave his neck an affectionate touch, then settled into the saddle as usual. Gilbert climbed up behind her, and Pampli moved forward, gently but confidently pressing his heavy paws through the forest floor.

At first they rode in silence. The forest before dawn was not the kind of place that made anyone want to chatter. But after a while Sakura could not hold back any longer.

“What kind of place is this, really? That shelter for mages... who lives there?”

“Mages,” Gilbert answered flatly.

“Oh, thank you. Very helpful. And do you trust them?”

“No.”

Sakura glanced back at him over her shoulder.

“Well, I can’t say I’m surprised. I know you by now.”

“But that doesn’t matter very much. They can still be useful.”

That sounded much more like Gilbert. As Sakura had already learned, he was a practical person who sorted people into categories of usefulness.

Sakura sighed.

“And have you been in places like that often?”

“Enough to know that when mages live in hiding, there’s always a mix of fear, paranoia, old grudges, and a very specific kind of mutual support.”

“Specific?”

“Until the first serious problem,” Gilbert explained dryly.

After that Sakura fell quiet for a long time, turning over both his words and everything else in her head. Time stretched slowly. Somewhere far off, beasts called to one another, and Pampli kept moving forward with the steady confidence of a king walking through his own forest.

The rest of the road until dawn, and then the next few days after that, blurred together into a chain of forest, careful halts, and short conversations.

They moved east, just as Gilbert had said. Sometimes they rode, sometimes they went on foot when the terrain grew too rough or too suspicious. More than once they had to make long detours to avoid patrols. One time they sat in a damp ravine for almost an hour while a guard unit passed overhead along an old road. The soldiers talked lazily among themselves, but they watched the surroundings carefully, and the whole time Sakura felt as if it would take only one more minute for them to be found.

They weren’t.

Along the way, she had to fight obsidian creatures more than once. Not things truly terrifying, but the kind Gilbert was clearly choosing for her on purpose. Dangerous enough to teach her something, but not so dangerous that it turned into outright suicide. Sakura fought them differently now. She still wasn’t a real mage by local standards, and definitely not someone who had grown up in a world like this, but she no longer slipped into the same panicked chaos as before. She felt distance better. She understood more clearly when she needed a scroll right away and when her own powers were enough.

Over those few days, she reached level six. No new skills unlocked yet, to her mild disappointment. But even without that, Sakura could feel herself getting tougher. It was as if her body and magic had finally started negotiating with each other instead of living in a state of constant mutual suspicion.

By the evening of the third day, the forest began to change.

The ground under Pampli no longer gave so deeply with moss. The air turned drier and cooler. Gray stone appeared more and more often between the trees, and the slope of the land became smoother, as if the entire forest were gradually draining downward.

And soon Sakura understood where the changing terrain was leading them.

Pampli stepped out from behind another line of low pines, and Sakura stopped breathing for a second.

Before them lay a canyon.

A huge one. So wide that her eyes could not take it all in at once. The cliffs dropped away in ledges and broken shelves of rock, while the bottom vanished somewhere deep in a bluish haze. At first it seemed to Sakura as if she were looking at a crack in the world itself. Everything about it was too massive, too ancient, too overwhelming.

Light mist drifted below, and a thin line of water gleamed in the depths. The walls of the canyon were cut by narrow trails, dark slits, and ledges that looked almost toy-sized from here until you realized that each one was larger than a human house.

“Wow...” Sakura breathed.

Pampli stopped too and drew in the air warily. Even he seemed impressed.

Gilbert slid down from the saddle first.

“We’re going down,” he said.

Sakura turned to him.

“Down?.. Seriously?”

“That’s where the mages’ shelter is.”

She looked at the canyon again. From above it seemed almost endless. The lower part was hard to make out no matter how hard she squinted. Just the thought of having to descend into it made her stomach tighten.

“It’ll take a while,” Gilbert continued. “And some parts will be dangerous. Better to rest properly first.”

Sakura had no intention of arguing with that. After several days of travel and more fighting, her body was perfectly willing to agree to any reasonable chance to rest, even a short one.

They moved away from the edge and went down to where a shallow but surprisingly clear river ran between the rocks. The stones along its banks were smooth and pale, and after everything they had gone through lately, the place felt almost unreal in its peace.

Pampli perked up at once. A beast that usually looked dangerous to anyone unfamiliar with him suddenly turned into something almost absurdly guileless. He waded into the water, snorted loudly, splashed with one paw, then did it again, fooling around.

Sakura smiled without meaning to.

“Sometimes he acts like a puppy and not like a giant obsidian beast.”

“Maybe that’s for the best,” Gilbert said.

He walked to the river, stripped off his upper clothes, and got into the water too without wasting time. Sakura happened to be looking his way and immediately turned her head aside.

Too quickly.

Which, of course, did not escape Gilbert’s attention.

“Well now,” he remarked with lazy amusement. “I didn’t think a human body could embarrass you.”

Sakura flushed harder than she wanted to.

“I’m not embarrassed.”

“Of course. That’s why you turned away like I’d taken a swing at you.”

“I didn’t turn away like that,” Sakura muttered, but the lie sounded weak even to her.

Gilbert let out a quiet sound of amusement and dragged a hand through his wet hair.

“This isn’t even ‘a body.’ It’s only half a body. A very modest display.”

Sakura stared somewhere off to the side, trying not to notice the exposed torso, or the fact that he was completely calm about all of it.

“Can anything actually catch you off guard?”

“Rarely.”

She walked over to the river too, crouched at the edge, and scooped up water in her hands. The cold hit her skin pleasantly. Sakura splashed her face and felt the tiredness start to wash away.

Then, without thinking too much about it, she scooped up more and suddenly flung the water at Gilbert.

For a second he did not even realize what she had done. Then he slowly turned his head toward her.

Sakura was already grinning.

“No need to embarrass me.”

Something almost surprised flickered across Gilbert’s face. Then he smiled at the corner of his mouth too and, without warning, splashed water back at her. Of course, he hit her immediately.

“That was an act of aggression,” Sakura said, spitting river water out of her mouth.

“That was a corrective measure.”

“You’re a terrible person, Gilbert.”

“So I’ve been told.”

After that, everything fully collapsed into some ridiculous, almost childish scuffle, and Sakura found the whole thing strangely sweet. Just a little while ago they had been talking about the Castle of Justice, dead mages, and dangerous plans, and now they were splashing water at each other at the foot of a huge canyon, as if the whole world was not trying to destroy them.

Pampli, seeing that the humans were somehow making noise and having fun, decided to join in at once, and his contribution was by far the most destructive. One joyful splash soaked Sakura and Gilbert more thoroughly than all their attacks on each other combined.

“Traitor,” Sakura said, laughing.

Pampli gave a satisfied snort and immediately shoved his muzzle back into the water.

After that, time blurred a little again. They ate and rested. Stretching her legs out on a warm rock, Sakura did not even notice when she drifted into a doze. Gilbert let her, not disturbing her too soon.

By the time they climbed back to the edge of the canyon, the sky had already changed color. The cliffs had darkened, and the bottom below seemed even farther away.

Gilbert led her to a place where a narrow descent began between the rocks. At first glance it did not look anything like a good road. It was a strip of stone that widened in some places enough for careful footing and narrowed in others to barely a single human step. Uneven patches. Gaps. Rare clumps of dry grass that definitely could not be trusted.

Pampli stopped and looked down with an expression that seemed to say he had finally concluded that people really did not always make good decisions.

“We’ll have to be careful,” Gilbert said, with no trace of humor now. “Very careful. You can’t rush here. Don’t put your foot anywhere you’re not sure of the support, and do not rely on crumbling edges. Hold on to any solid outcrop you can, even if it slows you down.”

Sakura nodded at once, now completely serious.

“And if the stone under my feet starts to give way?”

“Then don’t jerk suddenly. Freeze, shift your weight back, find the nearest support with your hands, and tell me right away. No sudden movements.”

“Very reassuring.”

“I’m not trying to reassure you. I’m trying to make sure you don’t fall.”

That was fair. Sakura walked closer to the start of the path and looked down again. The canyon still stole her breath. Too huge. Too ancient. Too indifferent to whoever tried to cross it. For a moment, it felt less like a road and more like a test.

Gilbert came to stand beside her.

“Ready?”

Sakura let out a breath, looked once more into the abyss, then at the narrow stone descent, and nodded.

“No,” she said honestly. “But let’s go.”
