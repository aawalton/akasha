Interlude: Frustrated Fate

Charles Vanderborn had a lot of regrets in his life, but the past hour was probably among the worst. He was a legend, the most powerful wizard in this world and the nearest dozen neighbors. This was all to be his to rule in Turhan’s name, his own little slice of eternity, a taste of immortality for his success, and then forty years of fighting and scheming would have paid off.

If only he hadn’t underestimated his students.

From the moment he returned to Amaria from the Aetherian Expanse, he’d known that there were people in his quarters. The only surprise was that it took Ambrose Adams this long to make his move.

He thought himself so clever, stealing the card. Charles might not have been able to read minds outside of combat, but it had been so obvious that he didn’t need to. Young men like Ambrose were as ambitious as they were selfish. When pressure was applied, they would always go for their own preservation and self interest first.

Until they surprised you one day.

“Faster!” he shouted, though his summoned dragons didn’t need to hear him to follow his orders. The rain was a constant barrage to his senses, and only his speed and staff kept the rain from pelting his face. He didn’t dare go any slower. Not with his good-for-nothing granddaughter piloting his ship, just fifty yards ahead.

Once, he’d treasured the youngest Vanderborn, daughter of his favorite child Elaine. Once, she’d been nothing but promise and potential: a curious little girl who always pushed herself to her limits. It captured his heart, until that promise was cut short by the girl’s limitations. No matter how hard she tried, it wouldn’t change the fact that, magically, she was the least of his family, doomed to mediocrity.

And that had been before she turned traitor and shot him with a cannon.

The serpentine dragons careened through the air, not only at ease in the rain but drawing power from it. Their flames shot out, coming closer and closer to the Wanderer. Charles didn’t want to damage his pride and joy, but it would be better than being bested by apprentice wizards, no matter how talented they may have been.

He was closer now, almost close enough to hit them with a spell. They were strong enough to survive his thrall strategy, but Charles had dozens of spells at his beck and call, and a large enough hand to be guaranteed at least one or two good options. With any luck, he’d be able to slow the ship, or reverse its movement and board. Just a little closer now, he told himself, pushing his [Shooting Star] flight card as far as it could go.

Then, the worst happened. As connected as he was with the ship, Charles felt the moment Jessica found the Aetherian Drive and fired it up. The fool girl had likely stumbled onto it, but it put him on a short clock.

“Go! Go now!” he bellowed, fear driving him on. It was the first time he could remember feeling the emotion in years, and he didn’t care for it one bit.

His dragons rumbled, and lightning spiraled around their forms, charging up for their alpha strike. No matter how much it may break his heart, he’d shoot the Wanderer down before letting it go.

The air thinned, and Charles found himself dropping from the sky. His dragons lasted longer, but they too dropped. They roared, and fired out twin spiraling blasts at the ship…but it was too late. The Wanderer punched through a weak spot in the world and came out the other side. The lightning discharged into the night sky, a pretty but useless flash of light before realization crushed him.

They’d gotten away.

It had been so close. Charles Vanderborn let himself fall through the sky for a few seconds before he changed directions, back to the school. It was too late to chase them now, but it wasn’t too late to salvage this before…Oh no.

It figured that this would happen at the worst possible time for him. He was this close to that promised immortality, and he’d thrown it away on what should’ve been the easiest, safest gamble of all time. He dismissed the dragons and flew the rest of the way back to the academy alone, lost in thought.

This wasn’t supposed to happen. Ambrose was supposed to take the deal. Luthor Graves and Jessica were unimportant, but the Ransas…It would have been one thing to kill the children of a thorn in his side and guarantee an outright rebellion in the south, but with them out of his hands, it was likely rebellion anyway without the pleasure of keeping them as trophies.

Who were they to say no to him? He’d shaped their education and made them what they were. Although Charles was a ruler first and an educator second, he couldn’t help but feel a small tinge of pride and irritation at creating his own problem.

The third year students weren’t, overall, his problem. Losing the Wanderer was an unbearable blow to his pride as well as his ability to get around and enforce his will. When the Emperor found out, he might lose everything he’d worked towards.

If the Emperor found out.

By the time Charles returned to his tower, he’d managed to calm down. He went inside, changed clothes, and dried off by the fireplace while taking stock of what all Ambrose had messed with. Most of his files had been touched and moved, but the only thing missing were the graduation rewards, as well as the two Wildcards. Between those, the students themselves, and the Wanderer, it was the worst loss Charles had experienced in decades.

But he wouldn’t let it embarrass him. There were a million things to be done, but it all started with a plan, and an understanding of the problem.

Five students left and stole his ship. Which meant that their families would hound him for answers, so he needed a story. Ambrose was a thief, but that wasn’t enough. The obvious choice would be to paint them as rebels. He’d need to have his people move up some timetables and it would force him to deal with King Ransa himself, but it could work.

Charles poured himself a stiff drink and let himself relax. This was a hiccup, but frankly, he deserved it for his hubris. It had been so long since anyone had managed to give him real resistance that he’d apparently forgotten that there were people who could. The loss of the Wanderer would have been devastating, if he hadn’t had a backup.

While most of his love and care went into his ship, he wasn’t foolish enough to not have any other vessels capable of moving between worlds. It wasn’t as fast, well defended, or luxurious, but it would get him there. And so long as Jessica was among them, he could find them anywhere, no matter how well they hid.

It was just a matter of time.

Other details flitted through his mind. He’d need to have someone watch over the school in his absence, and head off King Ransa’s questions for just long enough for Charles to get it done and come home to crush any resistance. His hand was forced, but…Maybe it could be to his advantage.

When he got the Wanderer back, maybe he could use the Ransa children as leverage to quell the rebellions. Jessica would spend the rest of her life making up for this betrayal, Luthor Graves he’d just leave wherever, and Ambrose Adams…

Charles Vanderborn was going to see what kind of card he made, and keep him in a place of honor. It wasn’t often that children were able to genuinely frustrate and impede him, so he’d get special attention.

So much to do before he could leave. He grunted, and drained his whiskey. The burn soothed him and drove him on.

The five wayward students had a head start that was only going to grow as he prepared for his departure, but they had no future. Only a little bit of time before dealt with them.

He hoped they enjoyed themselves. It would make catching up to them all the sweeter.
