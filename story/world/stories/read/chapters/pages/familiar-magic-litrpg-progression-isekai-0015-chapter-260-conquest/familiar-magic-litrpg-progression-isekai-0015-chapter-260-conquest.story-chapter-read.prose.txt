King Rothavaro could barely contain himself as he sat at the round conference table in the highest tower of Atlantis. It was all he could do not to tear this tower down–tear everything down as the Awakened talked and talked. It wasn’t as if they didn’t have things to discuss. Important things, even.

But nothing was as important as Rothavaro’s missing son.

Director Newton seemed utterly unconcerned. “Since the destruction of the enemy ship, every Follower force on the planet has been disrupted. They seem unable to function without their leaders. Even those that still have local Twilight generals seem to be floundering. The conduit in Europe has nearly fallen, and America appears to be entirely nothing but Dusks and Dawns. They’ve largely retreated to their facilities, and have ceased any major offenses.”

“We’re winning!” Director Da Vinci said, laughing over his thick beard. The half-dozen government representatives clapped politely, but this was the third time Da Vinci had said as much, and it was clear their enthusiasm had waned. There were few places on this world that had not suffered terribly at the hands of the invaders, and joy was in short supply.

Rothavaro’s foot was now tapping so violently that the room quivered slightly. Nearly everyone ignored it. Elizabeth Smythe, the representatives of the Vitus Domini–the apprentices, at least–leaned forward. “Have we confirmed that the Follower Emperor is truly dead? We lost every remaining Master to destroy their ship; we long to hear that it was worth that incomparable cost.”

Newton sighed. “We have no way to confirm it, I’m afraid. We’ve seen no evidence of any powerful Followers escaping, but we also have no reliable way to prove it didn’t happen. For now, we should all simply be grateful for what they’ve done. This war is nearly won. As much as I hate to agree with my compatriot,” he indicated Da Vinci. “It appears that it is nearly time for celebration.”

Rothavaro’s hands tightened on the steel table, and it crumpled in his grip. With a furious gesture he sent the entire thing flying to one side. It crashed through an entire wall’s worth of windows, soaring out into the city in a hail of shattered glass. In the same breath, Rothavaro followed the gesture with a blast of flame that rushed after the table, obliterating it, and the thousands of pieces of glass at the same time.

He reformed the windows in the next breath, using Twilight’s gift to combine his fire and sand affinities. Then he turned back to the room. “Where is my son!?” he demanded.

All but one person fell back, exchanging terrified looks. Rothavaro was aware that he’d recently become the most powerful person on the planet once again, and he didn’t intend to become an object of terror. But he was a father before he was a king.

Newton stepped forward, wearing that expression that meant he was about to try to calm the situation down with more words. A hand reached out to hold him back, and Michael Bishop–the only person in here that seemed to care that Cal was missing aside from Roth himself–spoke.

“The king is right. Prince Calevaro’s disappearance is highly concerning. At the very least, he is a foreign dignitary that has gone missing on our soil.” His dark eyes narrowed. “He is also a hero. One of the few who can claim to have helped liberate the city we now stand in. That is after he spent months searching for Twilight, battling demons, and saving lives.”

He looked around the room, eyes landing on each person in turn. “He is also my friend.” Somehow the word seemed menacing coming from Ethan Bishop’s brother. “What efforts have been made to find him?”

Newton cleared his throat. “As you know, he disappeared from within the tunnels beneath the United States.”

“After he destroyed the Follower infrastructure, and crippled their horrific breeding programs,” Bishop clarified.

Newton swallowed. “Indeed. After the very important work was done,” he acknowledged. “We’ve had scouting teams in the tunnels searching for him, and have begun to consider other possibilities. But it hasn’t been a priority given–”

“Make it a priority,” Rothavaro demanded through gritted teeth.

Bishop nodded. “I agree. At the very least, anyone strong enough to challenge the prince is a considerable threat that we must be made aware of. At worst, this could be connected to something more.”

“What do you mean?” Da Vinci asked.

Bishop tilted his head, a hand running back over his short hair as he considered. “I admit to a bad feeling. An instinct, you could call it. Right now my brother fights to finally achieve the Union. I don’t need to tell everyone here that his actions are key to the survival of all our people. Mere days after his departure, we lose track of one of this world’s most powerful defenders. We are also going on hope that the Follower leadership is truly dead. Maybe they’re not connected, but I don’t like loose ends of this magnitude.”

There was silence as the figures in the room exchanged glances, each one appearing to hope another would say something to settle the matter.

Rothavaro settled it for everyone. “Find my son!” he roared.

Emperor Gallow had been made weak. The stone he sat upon felt sharp. The air of Potentia tasted of poison, and left him feeling sick. The sun beating down on him from the red sky burned, yet his body shivered. Even before he’d gained his power and been joined to his ship, The Devorare, he’d never experienced such frailty, such mortality.

Having the connection to the ship severed by its destruction left his soul feeling beaten and battered, and yet the physical pain managed to be worse. That final member of the Vitus Domini had unleashed every last axion of power within his body to attack Gallow, and to the Emperor’s shock, it had nearly killed him.

He looked down at the fist-sized hole in his chest, still not quite believing what had happened to him. His Master rank body didn’t rely on internal organs, so the wound shouldn’t kill him, and yet his Master rank body should also be able to heal the injury, and it wasn’t doing that. Bek had needed to provide medical assistance, which was practically a foreign concept to those as powerful as they.

The plan had definitely started off poorly, and Emperor Gallow could do little more than hope the rest of it would go more smoothly. Otherwise, they’d simply come to this abysmal planet to die.

It had taken an additional two days for Bek to declare their weapon was ready to be removed for stasis, and now all they could do was wait for the scientist to release the former Prince Calevaro, and hope the process had worked. Gallow watched with the other six members of his kind on this world, noticing they were communicating rapidly with twitching fingers.

They believed it would fail. They had lost hope, lost faith even, and Gallow couldn’t blame them. They’d experienced one staggering defeat after another, and this wild gambit was the last chance for their race to thrive. He watched with all the rest as the pod finally opened.

“Come out, weapon,” Bek called. Then he stepped back as a figure rose up.

The man who had been Prince Calevaro didn’t look like one of The People. His transformation was limited, and he was still too short, and oddly proportioned. He lacked the beauty and homogeny of Gallow’s kind, but at least it was largely hidden beneath the inky black flesh of his dark-borne.

The living shadow of a man took several shaky steps, a hand reaching up to clutch at his head. When it lowered, he looked around at the assembled kin.

“Bow to your Emperor,” Bek ordered, and the weapon turned to Gallow.

Then he attacked.

The dark-borne formed into blades in the weapon’s hands, obeying its master over the kin–understandable–then the weapon leaped on the nearest target. Dayven, a being as old as Gallow himself, and one of the last of his kind, fell as his head was severed from his shoulders. That was a tragedy, and Dayven had been no warrior.

The rest of the kin dove on the weapon, excluding Bek, but Gallow had already been warned that the process had pushed their newest soldier to Twilight rank, and these Bonded were startlingly powerful creatures. A limb fell, another of the kin collapsed, holding his stomach.

Gallow had still barely managed to rise–he was so slow, so weak. But he was still a Master, and the weapon was merely Twilight. Monarch, the dark-borne Gallow had joined in his youth, shot forward, knocking the weapon back and subduing its lesser cousin. The man writhed and thrashed on the ground as the remaining kin picked themselves up, already healing.

Gallow limped forward, looking down at the weapon and feeling his will slowly overwhelm him. It was far more difficult than expected, and twice the weapon surged back at the last moment, nearly forcing Monarch away. But injured or not, Gallow was ancient, and his will had driven his people across the galaxy.

Gradually, the weapon settled, then obediently stood up, awaiting commands like any lesser. “I hope you’re worth what you’ve cost us,” Gallow said. He turned to Bek. “The test subject,” he commanded.

Bek’s own dark-borne pulled a small, struggling demon forward. Gallow pointed. “Take it,” he ordered the weapon.

There was a fraction of a second’s hesitation, then the weapon turned, holding out its hand. A beast appeared, as large as a transport shuttle. While Gallow had seen pictures of the polar bears this creature had once resembled, now its white fur was lost beneath the weapon’s dark-borne. Abruptly, an inky tendril shot from the beast down into the tiny demon, smothering it.

“Now we see,” Gallow said. Bek had come forward, and all the kin watched with some degree of desperation as the demon struggled madly. Then the great bear let out a roar that vibrated the ground beneath them, and the demon stilled.

“Yes, yes!” Bek said, fingers snapping with his excitement.

Gallow tried not to let the kin see his relief. It worked. His people had their chance. He looked around at the untamed madness of the red world, now seeing a land waiting to be conquered.

He moved to the weapon. “I name you Conquest,” he declared. “You will take this world for our people.” Conquest nodded.

Gallow turned to his kin, looking down with disappointment at Dayven. “Hunt. Draw in the most powerful demons you can find. We must build our army.” He paused, looking at Vaq. “Your orders are different. We are not alone in this place. Go, find Ethan Bishop. Do not face him, but return with his location. We must gather the power needed before he does.”

They bowed, then shot away into the distance. Each was Twilight, and intelligent enough to care for themselves. Gallow turned back to Conquest, seeing that Bek was fussing over him with his devices.

“Is there a problem?” he asked.

“He will serve,” Bek said, but his fingers told a tale of concern.

“See that he does,” Gallow said. “This world is the final chance for our people, and it will be ours.”
