Day 1 - 3 Inspections

"It is bigger than I thought. Maybe countless worming pieces of itself are enough to fill the interior, but for what need? They seem to be fleshy and purple and... green? An eagle wouldn't be able to define if its a visceral red or an olive green by the time it changes. Well, if it were to see it, then I guess it wouldn't be able to define anything by the end...

Funny how the moon's been getting closer to Earth, otherwise I would've needed a much larger piece of equipment... Although I suppose I will need it either way.

If town folks don't appreciate my research as of now, I can't imagine what my image will be when I show this to the world. Not that I care, of course, but I fear for Raviel. I am not all-knowing and therefore can't provide him with the knowledge he needs.

I've locked my door, but I can still hear his breathing outside of it, worried. No child should ever have to feel like that.

I still wonder myself if it's worth it. To leave away a world where God is no longer our ruler; It is both a blessing and a curse. Maybe we're really starting to get out of the nest, or we just tipped over. Either way, I am afraid of taking the next step.

Day 4 - 8 Inspections

Yes, it has eyes. Although it is one slick bastard, I saw it for the briefest of moments. It was dark, with a grayish pupil that soon turned white. It failed to perceive me, thank God. I should stop saying that; it's ironic to thank the being I am trying to free myself from. His eyes, however, were human enough (if you ignored the quantity, of course). Funny how even in the weird and wicked we can see a part of ourselves. A form of pareidolia, if you will.

My niece is coming over for... something. I didn't really asked her. Not because I don't care, of course, it's just that I don't... care. Its hard to admit, but with the work I'm doing here, it gets hard to focus on anything else. I'm just glad she can be with Raviel. He's been getting impatient lately, and I wonder if its because of me.

Unstable isn't the right word, but something is happening. I knew from the moment I spotted it, that I had found something dreadful. A spawn of the moon itself, a child of space. It looms and watches over us, hoping that its outer most shell can protect it.

I still have headaches from that day, but I record it. This journal is proof of my sanity. It is an evidence against irrational thinking and a testament that if I failed as a father, it wasn't because of my research.

Day 6 - 10 Inspections

Is it weird to name it after my own name? Although Logos suits it.

I am now trying a different approach. If I stare at Logos for too much, it seems to notice my presence, almost as if it were a defensive mechanism. Either way, it can see me. It hasn't tried anything, yet, but I'm sure that can change in a moment's time. Early this morning, when the moon was hiding in the blue of the sky, I lifted a simple drawing as I looked at Logos. As expected, he looked right at me. I put the piece right in front of the lenses.

It didn't pay a whole lot of attention, but I could see Logos narrowing one of his thousand eyes, almost as if It is scanning the drawing. I then repeated the process, but showed one of Raviel's drawings. I know a child's sketch of the world is rarely realistic, but that could work in my favor; maybe I can teach it to imagine. Does it even know concepts like empathy or hatred? Does it feel fear?

No time left, Blanche arrived. I'll start tomorrow again.

Day 7 - 13 Inspections

It is getting harder and harder to look. I had managed to slip in and out of the lenses in time for it to miss me. It was working before, so why isn't it now? It's getting more attentive? How!? It is either a symptom of my incompetence or of its patience, which is clearly running out.

Maybe It wants more pictures... Logos is getting anxious. I should stop before It gets nervous.

More moonrocks are being found all around the continent, and people just don't care. The nobles use it to powder their make up or to make pretty necklaces. Some reform their entire houses out of it and others apply it to the tiles in their mansions in hope of giving them more "texture". Really? That's what we're doing? Pieces of the moon are literally falling and people are buying ornaments? It's almost as if the world has less than a millennia to do something about it.

Then I ask myself, is it worth it? And then I go to the kitchen and I see my boy playing with his wooden toys, and I have my answer. It is doing something to the moon, and it's not a question of if, but when.

I have contacts at the post office; they can deliver it all around the continent in a matter of days.

Let us hope I am finished by then.

Harry put down the diary as his eyelids twitched like a leaf on the rain. The God Emblem... The Astronomer really found it. The young thief was basing his whole trip to the Logos Tower on this God Emblem. And of course he believed the tales, because otherwise how could the Tower be standing. But reading this confirmation shook Harry. I'm almost there, mom. I'm almost there.

"Is that the Astronomer?" Manes asked, his voice so weary it made even a twig seem stronger. "That's, like... the guy?"

A few drawings were scattered on a table near the dark board. It contained the child's drawing of a mountain falling in on itself, like a natural hourglass. Another showed a church with a face on top and another a floating island.

"I'm not even sure what he is anymore." Harry replied, staring at the diary. "I think—"

A powerful thud, strong enough to topple a tree, was heard from above. Goldman instinctively turned around, fists raised.

"I think we'll have to go up." Brun said.

"Of course we will..." Matthews sighed as she crushed a leaf, turning it into a tiny piece of herself.

The group climbed the stairs, each step more crooked than the last. Now a ramp, the staircase twisted as if eager to receive them. Thump! Thump! Harry's heart pounded as his steps grew weaker, along with his whole body. Was it from this floor or something particular to Harry on it? If it was inherent to this floor, everyone would be like this... And the young thief continued climbing, fearing his part in all of this.

Levered and overlapping, the group, led by Goldman, had to dodge obstacles. Wooden planks twisted into an arch shape, as if it were a welcome from the Tower itself.

"Ouch!" Madeline gave a soft cry, making everyone turn around. "Sorry! I just... bumped into it."

Tense as a fly on the edge of a web, they followed Goldman, who had his fist clenched.

Further ahead, the mysterious light illuminating the ascent was extinguished and transformed into darkness. The corridor, following from the end of the stairs, seemed long. It was narrow, like a mine tunnel at the bottom of a mountain.

"I don't feel we're safe." Madeline said, breaking the silence.

Oh, don't you say. Harry almost replied, but remained quiet. Those paintings and drawings had affected him. It's alright. It's always alright. I'm close. Harry closed his eyes and sighed deeply, pausing for a second as they all followed Goldman down the dark corridor.

"We've never been safe," Brun replied dryly to Madeline. "Much less on the last few floors."

"Daniel's gone," Manes added, almost to himself. "I'm sure he would know what to do."

"I do too, little one."

The gentle giant glanced back with a smile, barely noticed by Manes, who wore his dark banded armor. As if merging with the darkness, Manes seemed to reinforce his armor, which tightened around his chest even more.

At the end of the tunnel, a light seemed to emerge. Moving through the dark corridor, they remained alert as soon as they noticed the illumination. Goldman tensed his muscles. His bones creaked as he clenched his hand, Matthews stayed close to Harry.

Approaching the fountain, a large circular hall embraced them with warmth and mystery. Comically tall bookshelves covered some walls, but beyond that, what drew the most attention was the gigantic telescope in the center.

Perpetually scanning the white dots surrounded by the darkness of the night, the colossal object occupied a good portion of the center. Resembling a cannon that Harry had only read about in hyperbolic fiction, the cylindrical observer was angled in such a way that it was impossible to see from its perspective.

A circular slope in the middle of the hall was reserved entirely for the telescope. Its wooden structure, unlike the stairs, appeared to have been used for only a few weeks. Centrally positioned, the telescope stood upright in all its glory, seeming like an object of God.

The telescope then, seemingly on its own, changed angles. The force of the movement had some effect near it. A few, fleeting documents, would slip from the corners of the obscure ornamented corners of the table. As they fell onto the immaculate floor, Harry noticed a few drawings.

"...Again." A voice could be heard from behind the telescope, startling everyone in the group. "Same results."

A man emerged from behind the telescope like a magician stepping from behind a curtain. He wore a suit similar to Harry's, but far more carelessly; his tie was cut in half, and the ends looked like they'd been chewed off. His sleeves? As thick as the lens of his binoculars, which now hung over the least tattered part of his attire.

Lost in useless calculations, the man sported dark circles under his eyes almost as black as Manes' shadows. He carried two books, and as soon as he saw the group, he almost dropped them.

The Astronomer.
