“Miss Pedini, please return to your place in line!” Professor Anaximenes shouted.

Minette Pedini was a shorter girl, with a crop of blonde hair that fell in swooping lines across her face, ending just below her cheekbones. She wore a massive smile on her face as she turned to the professor who had tried to help her through her time at the Crystal Ring Academy up to this point.

“I’m sorry, professor.” Minette bowed, “I can’t do that.”

“Foolish girl!” Professor Cazlitine spat. “This is not a game! You need to-”

The burly professor ripped himself out of his seat behind the table, placing himself in front of her path.

“I just want to say goodbye. You’ll at least let me do that, won’t you?” Minette quietly asked. She still wore a smile… but there was no light behind it. The corners of her eyes seemed to droop, betraying a deep sadness underneath her facade.

“What are you talking about? Whatever dramatic display this is needs to stop now, before the spirits take notice.”

“Again… I’m sorry, professor… but they already have. I just want to say goodbye, before I go.”

Professor Cazlitine recoiled like he’d been physically struck in the stomach, eyes wide.

“Spirits… what is this? WHAT are you doing?!” The man yelled. “Inquisitors!”

In an instant of impossible speed, several figures appeared near Minette. Before the masked inquisitors had a chance to close in on the young girl, Saint Vaught appeared, holding a hand up to stop them.

“We may not impede her.” The saint commanded.

Professor Anaximenes lurched across the room, coming to Minette’s side.

“Minette,” Professor A pleaded. “Please think about what you’re about to do. You’re one of my brightest students, please, I beg of you…”

She turned to him.

“Professor… Your help has been… more than I could have ever asked for,” Minette said. “I know that you tried your hardest… but this is my fault. Not yours.” A bit of genuine appreciation leaked back into her eyes, and brought a tear out alongside it.

Professor A’s glassy red armor melted away, revealing his usual teaching outfit. He walked alongside Minette as she trailed across the room.

“It was… nothing more than I would do for any student. But please, you’re so young. Failure isn’t the end, Minette.” Professor A replied.

“Failure…” Minette repeated after him. She chewed the word as she said it, looking like she’d just tasted something foul.

She came to a stop before the stands, right in front of her friends.

“Vivi… Py… I’m sorry. I’m sorry that I shut you both out. You guys have meant the world to me.” Minette said.

Papyrus rose from his seat, coming down to the barrier that separated the seating area from the rest of the room. Vito, Chandrika, and Gio trailed after him.

“Mini… what is this? What are you doing?” Papyrus asked.

“I can’t do it,” Minette answered. “I… think that my dad is wrong about my domain. I don’t think it was ever possible to cast it away from home… away from Carniola. I’m not gonna be able to keep going to school here.”

“Okay… sure… but then why are the professors all… panicky?” Vito asked.

“Vivi!” “Minette cheered. “You did so good! I know that you’re gonna be able to manage your shadow. I just know it. I want you to have this.”

Minette forced a wooden bracelet into Vito’s hands, closing his fingers around it.

“There should be enough juice in there to keep healing you… If you need to get any more piercings. I have faith that you won’t need it much, though.” Minette chirped.

“Minette,” Papyrus demanded. “Please tell us what is happening. Why does this feel like… goodbye?”

“Because it is,” Minette replied. “I can’t stay.”

Gio made some quick mental calculations. He discreetly cast a spell.

“I absolutely wish that I was not forced to interrupt this moment, but I must inform you that you must make whatever decision that you’re about to make in a timely manner- and I must stress that I cannot influence you one way or the other.” Saint Vaught instructed.

“Looks like time is up.” Minette smiled. “I just know that you guys are going to be great mages. You’re both like brothers to me- real brothers, not the strangers back home. Goodbye, both of you.”

Minette, Vito, and Papyrus were all crying. After exchanging a quick hug with them, Minette walked away from the stands, pivoting towards the door at the end of the arena.

The patterns on the wood grain lit up, depicting images of legendary battles, great beasts, and works of mythical magics of ages long past. Professor Anaximenes stalwartly escorted Minette forward, ushering her through the blockade of inquisitors, who each moved aside as if pushed by an unseen force.

A glittering wall of mirrors appeared around Minette as Gio’s domain erupted out of the ground like a geyser, encasing the girl in a makeshift isolation chamber.

Gio melted out of one of the walls.

“Gigi…” Minette smiled. “I’m so proud of you. I’m sorry that I forgot to say goodbye to you. I’ve had a lot on my mind.”

“No… this isn’t about me, Minette. If you go through that door-”

“I’ll die.” Minette finished, surprising Gio.

“I know.” She continued. “I’m not afraid of death. To be honest… I figured that this was coming.

If I fail the exam, I’ll have to go back to Carniola, where I’ll have to walk into the Vodyanitsa to take a similar ‘rite of challenge’ to this one… It’s old, old magic- the way that people used to work with spirits- the kind of magic that my ancestors have been working for who knows how long.”

“And… you’re comfortable with the idea of dying?” Gio asked.

“I come from a long line of death-rite shamans. Since childhood, my father would take me behind our villa every full moon to watch my mother’s corpse rise from the mud to perform a ceremony of welcome, bidding the spirits of the recently departed to join her in the silt. Though none can claim to know what happens afterward, I am comfortable knowing that I will not be alone in death.” Minette said.

“How… macabre,” Gio answered.

“Meh. Death is a natural part of life for those of us who do not chase immortality.” Minette said. “My father is a firm believer that those who live for multiple lifetimes are selfish… and though I won’t claim that the Saints contribute nothing to society, this city is full of other long-lived people that seem to hoard their resources for years while giving nothing back to the people or the land. I’m not a local, so maybe there’s something I’m not getting, but why are the outer rings so much poorer than the Silver and Gold rings? The Gilded City is paved with gold, and there are people out in the agricultural circumference dealing with food insecurity, even though they live where the food is grown. How does that make sense?”

“That’s a fantastic question that I do not have the answer to, and possess no real power to change at this given moment. Would you like to continue discussing this at another time, perhaps in a safer environment?” Gio said, trying to coax the girl away from the door.

“That’s cute, Gigi… but I’ve made up my mind. I don’t think that the spirits would like it if I had to ask you to let me go… so you should probably get out of the way.”

Even at the merest suggestion of her request, Gio felt a pressure on him that felt inevitable. It was like a kind of directionless gravity, pulling him away from her path with the same irresistible pull that one might feel when holding onto a greased rope while hanging precariously over a gaping chasm. He would move out of the way; it was just a matter of how long he could hold on before that happened.

“But… why?” Gio asked. “Even if you’re not afraid of death… are you really willing to just throw your life away for no reason? I don’t get it. You’re so smart- you’re one of the only people to have ever beaten me on a test. And yes, it is a competition, no matter who, myself included, says it isn’t.”

Minete’s eyes sharpened, and something entirely different entered her smile.

“Oh, I know. I reacted the way that I did on the day that I rubbed salt in your wounds because deep down, I knew that we both shared that competitive impulse… I’m just sorry that I ended up picking at a sore spot for you that day…”

“It’s fine… really. I was just overwhelmed with everything, and self-absorbed with my own issues. I shouldn’t have snapped at you.” Gio said, brushing away the awkward admission.

“Maybe that’s true… but I was hurting too, and I think I kicked you when you were down as a way to make myself feel better,” Minette said.

They stared at each other in a prism of repeating reflections. Gio caught Minete’s eye through the reflection, even as she turned away from him.

“I kind of resented you, you know.” Minette blushed. “You’re so… you. You would have had no way of knowing this… but I was once considered the ‘one to beat’ for my generation, back in my hometown. I was the one that everyone measured their progress by… the girl who could do anything.”

“You still can be!” Gio said. “You’re amazing-”

“Stop doing that.” Minette gently reprimanded. “Enough with the meaningless platitudes.”

Gio recoiled. “I just-”

“It’s okay, Gio. Take it from me- too much optimism is annoying, and has a tendency to be just as depressing as blatant pessimism. Besides- I’m not telling you this to write my own epitaph or anything- I’m telling you this because I’m not planning on dying.”

Minette took out her spellbook, ripping out a page and shoving it to Gio’s chest.

What is…

“I recognize that what I’m doing is… dangerous, at the very least. But… so was what you did. Not to say that what I’m about to do is your fault, or anything… but you did inspire me. While I may not be afraid of death… I am afraid of failure. I don’t want to go home to my family as the girl who couldn’t figure out true magic. I want to emerge from this test triumphant, just like you did when you came back from that dungeon with a flashy new domain.”

Gio momentarily forgot about the paper in his hand, gesturing with it at Minette.

“Don’t put that on me.” Gio rebutted, slightly offended. “I did what I had to do to keep going to school here. Not… throw myself at whatever hides behind that door for no reason. I had a plan… do you?”

“My plan was to figure out a way to channel the spirits of my homeland into a domain designed specifically to be used on Carniolan soil. That didn’t work because I don’t think that could ever work. You’re more than welcome to give it a try and see if I made a mistake somewhere. I certainly won’t have any more use for that now.”

Gio looked down at the page that was quickly wrinkling in his hand. Verdant green illustrated vines intertwined with trickles of soft electric blue, combining into ribbons of nature and life that terminated into brown rot and black death. She gave him her domain. Gio quickly smoothed out the creases, gingerly handling the priceless, international piece of cultural importance and unknown magical significance.

“Minette… you can’t give me this,” Gio said.

“Why not? Like I said, it’s useless to me.” Minette huffed. “I don’t think that you will be able to cast it- ever. If you prove me wrong, you’ll be doing my nation a favor. Carniola is in decline, and we need true magic. Part of my reason for not wanting to fail is my own selfish reasons, yes… but also, we have increasingly limited sway with the spirits of the Vodyanitsa. Though I’d never dream of disrespecting the spirits that have brought my family safety and prosperity for generations… they also treat us like helpless children and refuse to allow us to help them with any issues. Recently, a plague has taken root in the swamp… and the spirits didn’t even let us know about it until we started pulling diseased shellfish from our nets.”

A plague… swamp… wait… I didn’t do that… right?

“Hello? Where did you go?” Minette laughed, waving her hand in front of Gio’s face. “You look like you just saw a ghost, which is weird considering that death is kind of supposed to be my thing.”

“Right… sorry, I was just making some connections based on something you said. You were saying about your country needing true magic?” Gio deflected.

“Oookay, Gigi. You can be kinda weird sometimes, you know? But… yes. My father is… stubborn, just like all the men in my extended family. He thinks that true magic isn’t worth the trouble, and if I return to Carniola as a failure, he would be vindicated in his beliefs. The only reason he even let me come here is that even he cannot refute that we need something to change the status quo. I… can’t fail, Gio.”

Gio looked down at Minette’s domain. A spell that she claimed should be impossible to cast.

“Let’s make a deal, then,” Gio said.

“Gio, I’m not going to stop-”

“I know, Mini. I won’t try to stop you from going… I don’t think I could. But… I can compete with you.”

Minette furrowed her brows. “What? You already cast a domain, and passed the test… there’s no need for you to take the alternate path.”

“No, not that,” Gio answered. “I’m ‘challenging’ you to a sort of competition. You need to come back alive, with the ability to cast a domain… before I figure out how to cast your spell. Winner gets bragging rights.”

Minette looked at Gio like he was insane. And maybe he was. But then… she started laughing- a deep belly laugh that brought a mist to her eyes.

“You’re crazy, Gigi. Deal.” Minette agreed, shaking Gio’s hand. “If you can cast that spell… well, I guess I’d have to defer to you as the better mage once and for all.”

They shook hands.

“Good luck, Minette,” Gio said.

“Thanks. You too.” Minette smiled.

Minette Pedini walked towards the door with renewed determination.

With a heaving breath, she reached out to the intricate handle, pulling the ancient door open to reveal a candle-lit staircase that descended into what appeared to be infinity. On the back of the door, a colony of dinner-plate-sized moths perched.

“Oh, hello!” Minette cooed.

She held out a finger and coaxed a furred body to climb on. The moth had three bulbous eyes that gleamed like lanterns, and wickedly sharp claws that perched delicately on the meat of Minette’s hand without piercing through.

Curiously, Gio felt a strong pull towards the moth. It leaked knowledge mana like a premature sigil, and Gio thought that the way its compound eyes looked at him was eerie, like they were seeing through him.

That’s a lot of knowledge mana… does that thing eat books, or something?

“A good sign, I think,” Minette said.

“If you say so…” Gio muttered.

The moth flew off, and Minette began her descent deeper into the belly of the dungeon.
