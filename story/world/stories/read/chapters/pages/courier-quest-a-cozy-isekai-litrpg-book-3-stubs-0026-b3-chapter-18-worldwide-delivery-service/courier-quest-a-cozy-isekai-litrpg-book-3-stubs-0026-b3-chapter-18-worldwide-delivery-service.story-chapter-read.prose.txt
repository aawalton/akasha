Trevor finished emptying his inventory of everything they had taken from the ghost’s dungeon. The armor was packed away in a series of crates, the ectoplasm was in the tub and another smaller container that was almost full, and the seaweed was hanging over a line in the corner to dry.

Out of everything they had delivered, Surtla was fawning over the hat.

“You must be an exceptional fighter,” the kobold remarked as he flipped it over. “Tanazi doesn’t generally give out his personal items, only his coat, except to Summoned who manage to impress him.”

“That’s all Adabelle. She very well may be the greatest swordswoman on the planet,” Trevor bragged as he nudged his wife’s shoulder with his own.

“I wouldn’t go that far,” she replied humbly, though she had a pleased smile on her face. “My style simply countered Tanazi’s. It could have easily gone the other way if I didn’t have as much experience as I do now.”

“She’s very modest,” he said as he put an arm around her shoulder, pulling her close.

“Well, she shouldn’t be,” Surtla stated before clutching the hat to his chest. “This is a great accomplishment for someone who had only gone into the dungeon once. One of our current Summoned took five attempts before Tanazi recognized them and offered them the boon of his sword.”

“I’m certainly glad he didn’t offer me that. My current blade is more than enough for me,” Adabelle replied, her hand reaching down to her hip where she would usually wear it.

“It’s a part of his sense of humor,” the enchanter said with a short laugh. “The Summoned who gained his sword prefers the spear. Another earned the captain’s boots, but they were a kobold who didn’t wear them. Even far back, there are stories where he gave a minotaur his trousers, knowing full well that they wouldn’t fit. Given your horns, he probably got a kick out of offering his hat.”

The noblewoman smiled at that. “It seems my initial impression that Tanazi is softer than he puts on was correct, then. A crew doesn’t get to act like they do without getting reined in by their captain unless they were in on it the whole time.”

“You are correct, Lady Adabelle,” Surtla said with a bob of his head. “At least, that’s what I’ve been told. Cruon knows I won’t be going in there again.”

“You’ve been?” Trevor asked curiously.

“Just once, many years ago when I was a young lad,” he answered, still fiddling with the hat. “I went in with a group of Summoned to see exactly what they had to go through to procure the ectoplasm-covered armors. Those ghosts consistently poked and prodded at me all the way to the end, harassing me every step of the way.”

“Wow, that sounds terrible. I guess you were pretty lucky to get out in one piece, huh?”

“Not exactly,” Surtla said. “They were very rude, but it seemed as though they were only up for verbally assaulting me. None of the violence involved me whatsoever. The only thing of mine they had hurt was my dignity. But enough about that.”

The kobold moved back over to the table with the boxes of talismans, set the hat down, and began closing them up. Clay tugged on their master’s collar, pointing at the headwear with a sense of yearning. They had clearly grown attached to it.

“Technically, that hat does belong to Adabelle,” Trevor said, hesitating a little as he spoke since he wasn’t sure if it was completely true. “It might have been something we collected, but I’m pretty sure that the Quest Master specified that it would be an extra reward for dueling Tanazi one on one. Plus, he did invite us back so he could try to win it back. I’m not sure when we’ll be able to, but it’s on the list of things we’d like to do eventually.”

Surtla paused, not looking back at them. His gaze was on the hat.

“Is there something wrong?” Adabelle asked, sensing a shift in mood.

They watched as the kobold’s tail swished in agitation before he turned around again. “Items given away by Tanazi directly don’t fall under the purview of things that we collect,” Surtla admitted, though he had to force the words out. “A quick question to the Quest Master would see that to be true. However, it is a more important and powerful material than the others. I am willing to offer you a great deal for it. Talismans by the box. Or perhaps anything from my shop below.”

Adabelle shared a look with Trevor. In all honesty, the captain’s hat would have been nothing more than an interesting trophy to her. If she were out with the girls again like in old times, then she would have sold it without a second thought regardless of whether Tanazi wanted her to come back.

But the hat was a memento from her first dungeon with her husband. Not only that, but Clay had also really come to enjoy it. They had decided to stay in their combat form and walk around before coming to Paladona specifically so they could continue wearing it.

The demonkin smiled and shook her head. “Sorry, Surtla. It’s not for sale.”

“Are you sure? I am willing to pay a premium for it.”

“I’m afraid it’s not a matter of cost.”

“If it’s not material goods you want, then I can offer you two tickets for the boat to see Croal’s Fustavele ritual tomorrow,” he bargained, looking back at the hat. “Front row seats to the show of a lifetime.”

“As tempting as that is, I will still have to decline,” she replied diplomatically. “It’s a souvenir from our honeymoon, and that makes it important.”

“A souvenir?” the kobold scoffed. “I’m offering you the chance to go where outsiders seldom can. Not even Klarkartar himself has received this honor on the final day of Fustavele. The boat only takes around forty people each year, and you’re passing up this chance for mere sentimentality?”

Adabelle’s tone became cold. “I can see that we have a difference of opinion on the things that matter.”

Surtla looked ready to argue with her, but took a deep breath instead. “Very well,” he said, clearly unhappy with this development. “I feel as though you are making a mistake, but clearly your mind is set and you intend to make the worst choice possible. I don’t know what Klarkartar sees in you, but it clearly can’t be much.”

The kobold took the hat in his claws and threw it backwards. Adabelle easily caught it even though it had only been haphazardly tossed in her direction. Trevor, however, frowned. Clay, feeling their master’s emotions, arched their back like Mr. Tiggles did when he was trying to be threatening.

He took a step forward. “I don’t like—”

“Honey,” Adabelle interrupted, taking his hand. “I think it’s best that Mr. Surtla here gives us the quest and we get going.”

Turning, Trevor glanced at the warm look in her eyes as she watched him. It was an expression that was completely at odds with her previous tone. The fire in his stomach wasn’t quenched, but it was smothered to a simmer.

“Alright, yeah,” the courier agreed, squeezing her hand. He didn’t bother looking back at the kobold. “The quest then, Surtla, and we’ll be out of your hair.”

Grumbling, the shopkeeper turned back to the boxes of talismans. He had been careful before, but now he basically slammed the lids on the remaining containers. One took a few tries before it slotted on correctly, which probably messed up the neat stacks within. Surtla moved away and stabbed towards them with a claw the moment he was finished.

“There you go, then. Give them the quests,” he muttered unhappily.

“You’re right, Quest Master, we should be enjoying ourselves out there instead of in here,” Trevor said aloud as he walked to the table and started tapping the boxes. This close, he could see that each one had a number on it, likely corresponding to the quest it belonged to so delivery would be easier.

He frowned when he saw that Surtla’s rough treatment had messed up the talismans inside. Some of them were in enough disarray that his Stacks skill didn’t activate. It only took him a few seconds to fix that mentally before he turned to the kobold.

“We’ll see ourselves out. Good day, Surtla,” he said before heading towards the stairs.

Clay specifically created a golden tongue inside of their mouth so they could stick it out at the kobold from their master’s shoulder. Adabelle reached out and patted them gently on the head as she followed Trevor downstairs. He all but stormed out of the building, ignoring the showroom of cool items, and crossed the road to the beach where there was more room.

“Who does that guy think he is?” the courier asked once he was free from the crowd. He began pacing. “That was incredibly rude of him.”

“Trevor,” Adabelle said.

“Yeah?” he asked, stopping to look at her.

His wife approached him and took both of his hands in hers. “He’s just some guy who’s good at magic, that’s all. It’s not worth getting all worked up about.”

“I disagree, he really shouldn’t talk to you that way,” Trevor said, frowning.

“He shouldn’t, no,” the noblewoman agreed. “But there is no accounting for poor decisions, remember?”

He nodded. “Okay, yeah. But he decided that he was entitled to that hat from the moment he saw it, and was going to take it without saying a thing. If it weren’t for that one little word in the quest, extra, we might have just given it to him without realizing that it wasn’t a part of the deal. He’s a swindler.”

“I agree with you.”

“And he was really, really rude to my wife!” Trevor continued, saying it as if that were the worst part. “So not only was he trying to rob us, first by trying to take the hat and then by lowballing the offer like we would want any of the trash in his showroom, but then he gets upset when you don’t budge on what frankly does sound like a really nice experience?”

“Seeing the festival’s finale from the water have been nice.”

“It could have, but I still can’t get over how entitled he was,” the courier scoffed. Pulling his hands away from Adabelle’s, he started pacing again. “Like, you’re a shopkeeper, you’ve bargained before. Has everyone really been so scared to step on his toes that he doesn’t know how to do that anymore? He has a benefactor like Klar, so now he thinks he’s top dog? Where’s he get off talking to you like that?”

The noblewoman caught him by the arm as he walked by, taking his hands again. “It certainly wasn’t a good look for him.”

“He’s going to end up giving Klar a bad name if that’s how he’s going to act when he doesn't get his way, like a petulant child,” Trevor said. “And he even invoked his name! He didn’t even tell us what he would use it for. Like, does it have special properties? What does more important and powerful even mean? It’s just a hat, dude, it’s a gift from a pirate ghost captain to my wife because she’s awesome.”

“We may never find out.”

“Surtla could have at least told us but, noooo, he had to go from zero to sixty in an instant,” he huffed. The courier attempted to resume pacing, but didn’t get very far since Adabelle didn’t let him go this time. Trevor looked down at his wife’s hands, and then up at her small smile. He grimaced. “I’m kind of off on a tangent, aren’t I?”

“You are,” she confirmed with a giggle. “But, despite him going from zero to sixty as you put it, I’m glad to see that you get so worked up over me.”

Trevor wanted to scratch the back of his head, but couldn’t with her holding him. “Well, yeah. I know you can handle yourself, but I’ll always step up for you. I’m also not a noble, so I don’t have to follow the same social rules you do.”

“I’m not sure if I agree with that one, son of Ander,” she said as she dropped one of his hands. Her tail wrapped around his wrist as she started walking down the beach.

“Not sure you agree with which part?” he asked, confused.

“You are a noble now. By marriage.”

Trevor paused as his mind caught up with that. She was right that he had married a noblewoman.

“Huh, I didn’t think of that,” he said before making a face. “Ugh, I don’t want people calling me Lord Trevor, or Sir Trevor. I get that it’s a title and you deserve every bit of Lady Adabelle you receive, especially the way I do it, but I can’t see myself like that.”

“You don’t have to, then,” Adabelle told him, patting his arm. “There are no rules for it. Real nobility in Lucent follows more guidelines than hard rules. The closer you are to the top, the more you can get away with. I’m at the top of the ladder at Tosa and fairly high in Eskretet, so we can get away with a lot.”

“Okay, good. Can you imagine someone calling me Lord Trevor?” he asked with a laugh. “That’s not me.”

“No?” she asked, leaning close enough to rest her chin on his shoulder. “Are you sure you don’t want that kind of authority, Lord Trevor?”

“Nope, not even from you,” the courier said, shaking his head. “Good effort, but definitely not.”

Adabelle giggled again, covering her mouth with her hand. She watched his face carefully for any sign of the anger he had felt before. It wasn’t an emotion she was used to seeing on him. Even when he was treated poorly, he never let it get to him.

She remembered the first time they spoke in her office. He had proudly declared that he was only mean to the people he liked, and that he met people who treated him poorly with polite patience because he didn’t want to give them the satisfaction of getting mad.

The moment she was the target of someone’s ire, however, he became downright indignant. She found that she didn’t mind that fire.

“How about we put a pin in the nobility discussion and, as tourists, get some of that ice cream they’re selling over there?” Adabelle asked, pointing to a wagon set up on the beach. She could smell the sweet treat as it was carried on a colder breeze than normal.

Trevor glanced at it and she knew he was okay. His anger had been assuaged for now. “Sure thing, lady. After that, we can go and check out more of Paladona.”

“That sounds delightful,” she replied, planting a kiss on his cheek as she led him towards the wagon.
