“So... I know I’ve mentioned in the past that I don’t really want to know any of the wild or insane stuff you’ve been doing… but is there any chance you know what’s going on?” Rosh asked him while stirring a sugar cube into his favorite blend of tea. “Things have been sort of strange lately. The guard presence has nearly doubled across the entire city, which normally only happens when things are about to go down. It’s made work a bit more complicated, and while I love the additional challenge, Draken hasn’t been happy.”

“Have I ever told you the fact that you work for the largest gang in the city is the weirdest thing ever?” Asher replied, raising an eyebrow as he took in Rosh’s current state. Despite having put his life as a professor behind him years ago when his life took a rather harsh turn, Rosh didn’t seem to be able to help himself when it came to his clothing. From the neat dress clothes he was wearing to the fluffy scarf around his neck and the fur-lined gloves to help with the cold, Rosh reminded him of a college professor meeting with one of his students over a cup of coffee. The man couldn’t have looked further from a gang member than if he’d tried, which Asher supposed was the point.

“I’m serious, Asher,” Rosh said. Frowning, he shivered and took another long sip of his steaming tea. Whikoga seemed to be getting colder by the day as winter began to truly settle in, and already Asher could see the occasional flake of snow drift down to land on some poor sod’s head as they walked by. “Don’t forget I know military logistics better than anyone. The only reason for such a sudden increase in guards would be if there was going to be a significant raid somewhere within the city, or in order to prepare for an incoming threat. Regardless of which option it is, neither of them are good for us.”

“The fact that I’m friends with both the High Prince and the most powerful gang leader in one of his own cities is definitely a strange conflict of interests,” Asher admitted, taking a sip from his own tea cup before grimacing and adding another few cubes of sugar himself. Rosh had invited him out to one of his favorite cafes when he’d dropped by to check up on him, but unsurprisingly due to the weather, the indoor seating had already been filled up. “At the very least, you can tell Draken not to worry about the guards. They’re not here for you guys.”

“While that’s certainly a relief, that begs the question of what they are here for,” Rosh commented, frowning at him while nursing the cup between his hands. “I don’t suppose you can share that piece of information with me?”

Asher hesitated, thinking back to what Donvath had decided a few days ago. Once they’d realized there was nothing they could do but wait for the Council to launch their attack on the Noala Kingdom, Donvath had instructed them to keep the impending attack a secret. Creating a kingdom-wide panic wouldn’t do anyone any good, and it was likely that if such news were to spread, the five remaining liches would find some way to use that to their advantage. Instead, Donvath was now working overtime, taking it upon himself to shore up any potential holes in the kingdom’s defenses and do his best to make sure when the liches did make their move, they would be ready for them. That meant increasing the number of guards, calling back slayers venturing outside of the kingdom, and doing everything in his power to ensure the kingdom’s eleven cities were prepared for anything.

While he hadn’t been able to help much with the preparations, Asher had at least done his part when it came to preparing for a rapid response. Beyond his marks on Brian, Samantha, Scritchems, and the palace, Asher’s six remaining marks were spaced out across the kingdom. He could only cover seven of the eleven cities, but that was better than nothing. That gave Donvath four cities to devote the vast majority of his forces to, as Asher could instantly drag squads of powerful warriors to any of the remaining seven the moment they received word something was wrong.

“I can’t tell you what’s going on,” Asher admitted, giving Rosh an apologetic smile as the man huffed. “What I will say is there might be some trouble brewing in the coming days. You still have your shards on you, right?”

“Yes, I still have them,” Rosh grumbled. “All eighteen thousand, three hundred and forty-two of them. Despite the fact that you refuse to tell me what they’re for.”

“I already said, I asked Donvath, but he can’t remember what he saw,” Asher shrugged. “He’s got a lot going on. But even if he can’t remember why he told me to tell you to hold onto them, that doesn’t change their importance. Obviously it has to be either for healing you or to give you strength. I’d give you more if you’d actually take them.”

“The last thing I want is the responsibility of walking around with a million shards in my soul,” Rosh said, shuddering at the thought. “You don’t understand. For me, shards are power. You’ve seen how much strength I can muster even just burning a hundred shards. While the amount doesn’t scale linearly, it does still increase the more I burn. I don’t want to sneeze and destroy a city block. I don’t want that kind of pressure.”

“I know, I know,” Asher sighed, deciding not to reopen that argument. Rosh was not only one of his few good friends he had in this world, the man had been his first friend after Samantha and Brian. Even if Donvath didn’t remember why he’d told Asher to make sure Rosh was carrying those shards, the fact that he’d thought of him at all told Asher something was coming that was going to put Rosh in danger. Naturally, Asher had tried to convince him to hold onto some shards. He figured the man would be all but invincible if he took a few hundred thousand shards into his soul, but Rosh had balked at the idea.

At his very core, Rosh wanted nothing more than to live a calm, normal life. While working for the city’s largest gang probably wasn’t exactly what he’d had in mind, Draken wasn’t like any of the other gang leaders, and the fact of the matter was, like it or not, Rosh did have a forbidden element to his name. His second-tier amulet was powerful enough to keep that hidden even from the piercing gaze of the truthseekers, but even so, it didn’t hurt to keep a low profile.

“Whatever is coming for Whikoga, when it does come, I’ll be hiding out within my room, snuggled up in bed and reading a book,” Rosh stated confidently.

“Or… you could come visit the palace for a few days,” Asher offered. “…Maybe weeks…”

“Is it seriously going to be that bad?” Rosh asked, frowning at the uncertain look on Asher’s face.

“I don’t know. Which is sort of the problem. It’s going to be bad, there’s no question there. But to what scale, I just don’t know.”

“What good is the High Prince’s Time element if he can’t even see what’s going to happen to the kingdom?” Rosh scoffed.

Asher rolled his eyes, glad he’d decided to put up a Spatial Lock to block sound ahead of time. He’d already explained that there were caveat’s to Donvath’s Time element, but he couldn’t say exactly what they were without giving away what was going to happen. If he said wizards were one of the few things that messed with Donvath’s ability to see the future, it wasn’t a huge leap in logic to go from that to liches specifically. If there was one thing Rosh was good at, it was logic and thinking.

“You spent the last few months repeating that you didn’t want to hear any details about what I’ve been getting up to. Yet here we are, with you trying to pry them out of me.”

“Curiosity is the gentleman’s drug,” Rosh stated, earning a snort from Asher. Seeing that he wasn’t buying it, Rosh sighed. “…Alright, Draken asked me to try and get some information out of you. I told him it wouldn’t work and that I can’t lie to save my life, but he said it was fine if I told you. He wouldn’t listen when I suggested he just ask you himself.”

“Draken and I haven’t spoken too much lately,” Asher admitted, wincing a tad as he thought back to the last time he’d visited the gang leader. During one of his last visits, he’d all but thrown a severed, talking head at him and demanded he keep Smiles alive using his Death Aura, promising it was only temporary. Then, only a few days later, he’d returned with what he’d hoped was a solution to the issue of Smiles needing a constant supply of death energy. Yes, the powerful gemstone he and his team had retrieved from the wild realm solved the problem, but it also inadvertently bound itself to Draken without his say so. Not only did that permanently decrease his available artifact slots by one, it functionally bound Smiles to him as well, as the undead wasn’t able to go more than a few hundred feet away from the artifact without perishing near instantly. Seeing as Draken was required by law to remain within his manor at all times…

Draken had no doubt been forced to get very acquainted with the newest member of his gang rather quickly.

“Really? How come?” Rosh asked, frowning at his admittance. “Does this have something to do with Plate?”

“Plate? Who the heck is Plate?”

“Some new elite that joined Draken’s gang recently,” Rosh shrugged. “Can’t say I’ve spoken to the man myself, but he walks around in a suit of full-plate armor and looks menacing as anything. Nobody’s ever heard him speak or even seen him raise his sword, so nobody knows what he’s capable of.”

“Nobody’s heard him speak?” Asher repeated, deciding then and there that either Draken had chopped off Smiles’ tongue, or that this mysterious Plate was someone else entirely.

“Nope. But from how he follows Draken around, it’s clear he’s someone important. Plate has all but taken over guarding the manor, freeing Thunder and Crush up to help elsewhere. Rumor has it he doesn’t even sleep, which is one of the reasons why some people think it’s not a person at all and that Draken somehow got his hands on a magic golem.”

That’s got to be Smiles, Asher thought, shaking his head as he realized he was going to have to pay Draken a visit sooner than later after all. Not just to apologize again, but to satisfy his curiosity at how in the world the gang leader had gotten Smiles to actually shut up. As much as the undead had ended up growing on Asher during their brief time together, it seemed he was physically incapable of going more than ten seconds without speaking. Asher had been forced to use Incorporate to shove memories of squirrels and other such random things into his mind just to buy himself a few minutes of peace and quiet.

At the very least, the news that Smiles was still around was a welcome one. Asher admittedly hadn’t been certain if Draken would choose to keep the undead by his side or not, but seeing as he had, that was one less thing to worry about. Smiles had lost a significant portion of his incredible speed after being decoupled from the King of Death, but he was still incredibly deadly. If the liches were to target Whikoga, it was good to know that Draken had such a powerful warrior on his side.

“Rosh,” Asher said, his tone growing serious. “For the next couple of weeks, see if Draken will let you stay in the manor. I know he’s got guest rooms. He let Samantha, Brian, and I stay in some after Horntho Village burned down. Even if I can’t say what’s coming, Draken’s manor is going to be one of the safest places in the entire city. That or the truthseekers’ place, but I doubt Clare would be willing to share her room. I’d feel a lot better knowing you were tucked in there rather than some random tavern.”

“Alright,” Rosh said, seeming to pick up how serious his request was. “I’ll go ahead and ask.”

“Thank you,” Asher said, taking another sip from his tea as he glanced up at the overcast sky that was steadily growing darker. The snow had yet to start falling in earnest, but based on the clouds gathering overhead, it wouldn’t be much longer.

The storm was coming.
