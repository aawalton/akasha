Cheers rose from the arena to the sky. The walls shook, the stands rattled, the excitement was electric. The arena was split in two now, rather than in four, to accommodate the reduced number of competitors.

“Everyone, welcome to the sixth day of the Heaven Reaching Tournament! I am Deacon Wane, and I will be announcing the match-ups for today!” a deacon said on-stage. The crowd cheered harder.

The deacon raised his voice the crowd’s. “Without further ado, let’s welcome our first fighters. Perona Williams versus Lin Yushuang. Gary Steel versus Lily Haldrin. Disciples, please attend!”

The moment he uttered Gary’s name, the audience erupted anew. “Baldinator! Baldinator!” a section chanted. Two people unrolled a massive scroll which turned out to be a banner spelling Baldinator with a hairless scalp on each side. Gary’s opponent, Lily Haldrin, jumped on stage with clear trepidation. Her reddish hair was flowing carefree.

“You should forfeit! That’s really lovely hair actually!” someone yelled.

“Fight! Let us see the baldinator magic!” a younger spectator cheered.

As a few seconds passed, however, Gary still hadn’t jumped on stage.

“Hmm? Where is the baldinator?” someone asked.

“He’s delaying on purpose to increase our anticipation. What a stage connoisseur!”

“Maybe his previous opponents grouped up and assassinated him.”

In the disciple seats, Cog and Alicia looked around. “That idiot. How could he miss his match?” Alicia said.

“He wasn’t in the cabin in the morning. Nothing could have happened to him, right? Oh! You don’t think anyone abducted him?”

“No way. Knowing him, he probably trained all night and had an epiphany which made him lose track of time.” Alicia gritted her teeth. “Come on, Gary. Don’t waste your matches like this!”

“He can spare one loss. Maybe he sacrificed this match on purpose to save time for training.”

“That’s— You know, that actually sounds like something he’d do. Ugh, he’s so confident sometimes.”

“Gary Steel, please attend!” the deacon repeated. The crowd’s cheers had broken into clamor by now. Hope was crawling back in his opponent’s eyes, like she couldn’t believe her luck.

“Last chance, Gary Steel. If you are present, please attend!”

Nobody jumped on stage.

“Very well. Since disciple Gary Steel is absent, I must declare him—”

“Wait!”

A man was at the audience entrance, dressed in Heaven Reaching robes and folded over panting. He spared a moment from his panting to yell, “I’m here!”

“He’s here!” the audience cheered.

“I knew it was on purpose!” some guy said.

“What’s on purpose? Don’t you see he’s panting?”

“Your mother was panting!”

“What did you say!?”

As people rushed to separate the brawling men, the deacon frowned. “Gary Steel, make your way to the arena immediately. Such tardiness is unbecoming of a disciple.”

“I truly…apologize…” Gary managed to say. He jumped, soaring through the air to land on the sand across his opponent. “I apologize,” he told her, finally catching his breath. “I lost track of time. Thank you for waiting.”

“Don’t mention it,” she replied bitterly.

“I suggest you forfeit. I cannot control my powers, and they will make you lose your hair.”

She leveled a spear at him. “I have one loss. I can’t afford to forfeit. Deacon, hurry up and start the fight, he’s recovering!”

“Fight!” the deacon said. Lily Haldrin charged Gary immediately. Her thrusting spear twirled through the air, the tip moving in gradually wider circles which left behind a red trail. Suddenly, the spear caught onto its own trail as if it was physical, then started spinning it like a ribbon. Before Gary could realize what he was seeing, the ribbon stabbed towards him.

“Thunder Step!” he yelled, dodging aside. The ribbon crashed into the sand, leaving a deep hole.

But the attack wasn’t over. Lily’s spear arrived next, thrusting towards Gary. They exchanged blows in the middle of their stage. Gary found her skilled but weak. Her stats weren’t up to par. He was stronger and faster. Unfortunately, he was also exhausted. He lost his breath only a few dozen exchanges into this relentless assault and had to thunderstep away.

She followed suit. The sand under her feet coalesced into a brown ribbon which carried her forward, letting her attack mid-movement with stable footing. She slashed at Gary, at the same time that the sand grains making up the ribbon launched themselves in all directions to cut off his escape.

Gary grit his teeth. He jumped away, spinning his spear rapidly to send the grains off-course, then thrust at his incoming opponent. She had momentum on her side, which made her stronger but less maneuverable. Gary thrust a little slower than usual. His spear pushed through a thick cloud of aura, gathering it all up, to erupt with the sum of their power.

“First Song: Mud!” Gary roared, his spear whistling forward. As it reached its maximum extension, a brown aura thrust carried on towards Lily, whose eyes widened first in surprise, then resolve.

“Ribbon Snap!” she yelled. A tight ribbon overlapped with her spear, then snapped and shot forward like rubber. Her ultra-fast speartip collided with Gary’s slow strike. For a moment, the two grinded against each other, seeming as if they’d reached an equilibrium. It took a moment for everyone to realize that there was no equilibrium at all. Lily’s strike had come at great speed and been stopped in its tracks. Gary’s had been slow to begin with, and it had slowed down further after the collision, but it wasn’t stopping.

The brown aura pushed away Lily’s spear despite her best efforts. It penetrated her defenses, drilled into her aura field, and exploded just before her chest. She flew back as if catapulted, crashing hard into the energy wall that separated their fight from the other one. Her spear span to the ground as she bounced on the sand, unconscious. Red locks of hair floated.

“Winner, Gary Steel!” the deacon announced. The crowd erupted, while Gary simply stood there, looking at his own hands.

I did that? he wondered. That strike… How could it be so potent? If I hadn’t made it explode early, wouldn’t it have shattered her torso? And that was just the first song…

She was a little weak, sure, but…

He snapped out of it to find the deacon bent over Lily. He rushed over. “Is she okay?” he asked.

The deacon opened his mouth to respond but was beat to it.

“I hate…you…”

Lily Haldrin rose from the sands, holding onto her chest. Her once beautiful hair still cascaded around her. A bald head greeted Gary, without even eyebrows, accentuating her wide, furious eyes.

“How dare you take my hair!” she screamed, trying to attack him with her bare hands. The deacon held her back.

“I warned you…” Gary said. “You should have just forfeited.”

“I’ll turn your intestines into a ribbon and choke you with it!”

The deacon frowned and snapped his fingers beside her ear. She fell immediately unconscious.

“She’ll wake up in a few minutes. Take her away,” he ordered Gary, who had to carry Lily Haldrin to the stands. The crowd still cheered.

“Baldinator! Baldinator!”

“That’s right, take their hair and their pride with it!”

“You’re so awesome, Baldinator! I want to betroth my daughter to you!”

“Dad, ew!”

Gary ignored them. He couldn’t locate any friends of Lily’s, so he placed her in a seat far away from his own and sat next to Alicia and Cog.

“Why were you late?” Cog asked.

“I lost track of time,” Gary replied.

“But where did you spend the night? You weren’t there in the morning.”

“Yeah. I was in town. I had a…job to do.”

Next to them, Alicia’s eyes suddenly widened. She opened her mouth to ask something, then stayed quiet. Neither Cog nor Gary were looking at her.

“Come on! Can’t you tell us what that job was?” Cog asked.

“I’m trying to be discreet, Cog. Maybe I’ll tell you later.”

“Fine. What did you think of the nightlife in the Shores of Glory? Did you like the music?”

“I was indoors the whole time. The only music I heard was the creaking of wood all night long.”

Alicia’s hands covered her mouth. She bolted upright and walked away. “Where are you going?” Cog yelled after her.

“None of your business!” she replied.

“Probably the restroom. I keep telling you, Cog, don’t ask awkward questions,” Gary said.

“But I wanted to know!”

“Sometimes we don’t want to tell.”

“Fine. What were you saying about the creaking of wood?”

“Oh yeah. Some dude had broken the doors of a warehouse and the wind was coming in, rattling the broken crates. It was really annoying.”

The waterfall was as calm as always. For reasons unknown to Gary, Lin Xin had abandoned this as her training spot, leaving it as his sole, serene property. Gary sat on the flat stone in the middle of the pond where he’d once broken through, hearing the gentle rush of the waterfall and the slow dripping of water down the creek.

Surrounded by this calmness of nature, he was messing with his aura like it was play-doh, stretching it, twisting it, changing its vibration, and overall trying to make himself as uncomfortable as possible. This was his least favorite kind of training—even worse than thrusting ten thousand times in a row. Not only was it boring, it was also painful.

Gary pushed on with a steely expression, letting the hours flow. As much as this training sucked, he could see results. Stopping his meditation, he suddenly vibrated his aura at maximum power, exuding an aura field that reached five and a half feet away. Leaping off the stone, he fired off a series of thrusts, marveling at the increased efficiency of his aura compared to a few days ago.

He could use less aura to achieve the same result as before, or the same amount of aura to achieve a greater result. His Thunder Step was faster and reached farther. His thrusts had accelerated. Even Fan the Spear had increased efficacy without increasing the speed at which he span his spear. His regular attacks were stronger and more aura-efficient.

As for the Nine Songs of the Spear…

Gary adopted a thrusting position. He vibrated his body in a specific pattern, the colliding ripples filling part of his aura field with greater aura than usual. His spear pushed through them, gathering all that aura into itself. Gary’s muscles strained. It was like he’d wrapped weights around the head of his spear, but still he pushed, extending the spear to the maximum through the strain, releasing the brown strike that had sent Lily Haldrin flying even when stopped early.

He kept moving. A storm of regular strikes left his spear, with a slower but stronger strike mixed in at random intervals. This was the true point of the Song of Mud—by using it freely, Gary could keep his opponents guessing, giving them many opportunities to make a mistake while simultaneously demanding more of their attention than usual. Once he mastered this completely, his strength would spike.

Gary’s use of the Song of Mud was much better than during his battle against Lily Haldrin, but not because he’d practiced it. In fact, he almost hadn’t used it at all since then. The first reason for that was his increased aura control, which made everything easier. The second reason was what he did practice—a move similar to the First Song, though more advanced.

“Song of Life!” Gary roared.

Gathering his aura in the path of his thrust was what he’d learned for the first song. For the second one, he had to go a step further. The aura gathered as before, but this time, it was more than a cloud of energy. Wrinkles appeared on Gary’s forehead from all the frowning. Slowly, the cloud of aura split itself into rings, through which the spear tip would pass. If done successfully, the rings would accumulate over the spear tip, a collection of aura strikes which moved together to create the illusion of a single attack that persisted even if you managed to stop it.

That was the Song of Life. It also contained a core concept of life, supposedly, but Gary was nowhere near attaining it. His vibrations were much closer to death.

The aura cloud split into rings, each positioned at regular intervals. Just before Gary launched his spear forward, his concentration faltered, the rings collapsed, and the strike ended up carrying a brown-green cloud of aura which was even weaker than a regular Song of Mud.

Gary groaned as he fell to his butt, panting heavily.

The second song is easy to learn once you know the first, Master Colorful had said. Master it quickly so we can advance. Learning the third song before your fight with Sui Duang is key!

“Easy my ass…” Gary muttered. Creating those rings felt like exercising a muscle he never knew he had before. He had a feeling it would take him days, and he had to master this song and the next one before he fought Sui Duang. At best, if he never ran into Sui Duang, he had until the day after the finals. That was just ten days away.

“Goddamnit.”

“You can do it, Gary!” Goldie cheered. “I believe in you, and I’m an ancient and wise spirit, so I know what I’m talking about!”

“Can’t you help me with the aura manipulation?” Gary asked.

“Nope! I suck at that! You bring the skills, I bring the spear!”

“But I’m the one carrying the spear.”

“Then I’ll bring the cheers. Ga-ry Steel! Ga-ry Steel!”

“Please stop,” he begged.

“Oh right, that’s not your cheer. Baldinator! Bald—”

Gary grabbed her little head and stuffed it into his sleeve, then sat cross-legged again. Goldie’s appearance had given him a smile. His head felt about to split apart, and he was hungry, but he needed to train. The task before him was insurmountable. If he failed, he wanted to know he’d at least tried his best.
