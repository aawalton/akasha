“You?” I asked in pure confusion.

“Me,” The giant, root crown snake Spirit confirmed amusedly.

Nehushtan.

I…had barely thought of the enigmatic Spirit, in the months following the events of Elderwyck. ‘Nehushtan’, if that was even its real name, had appeared to me when I was also unconscious and near the point of death, right after Nerexxa had essentially accomplished her goals. She had sacrificed a high-level Classer to awaken Rhazal from his slumber underneath the city. As he had risen, I had fallen, along with those of my companions who had survived. I’d done my best to save Sylvia and failed, only for him to present himself to me in my passed-out state.

He had been informative, cryptic, and kind of an asshole, from what I remembered. Elys had chased him away and woken me up. At the time, I had thought it was interesting that she seemed irritated by his very presence. Not angry, per se. It was almost like she had viewed Nehushtan as a bother, something to be chased away.

Not so with Anima. There was real antipathy in her voice when she spoke of him. Whoever, or whatever this odd snake Spirit was, he wasn’t someone the Great Spirit of Life was all that fond of.

I’d almost say she hated him.

Wait. Wait a second.

“Nehushtan…” I said slowly, eyeing the Spirit as he slithered closer out of the darkness. His coils almost seemed endless as he encircled us, the length of them spiralling out from our position. However, I didn’t feel like he was trying to intimidate us at all.

Rather, I think he was trying to show off. There was a certain cant, a certain angle to his body, as if he were presenting his pitch-black scales, flecked through with the occasional gold.

“Most Spirits are trapped within the Concord…” I mused, paying no attention to his peacocking. “Presumably, those remaining on Vereden are Spirit Beasts instead. And yet…I can tell you’re not one of those. Those are relatively new to Spiritkind, dating back to the War in Heaven, and that’s twice now two separate Great Spirits have implied you’re old. At least as old as them.”

“A mildly impressive leap of logic, failure,” The voice of Nehushtan came to me from behind my back. I twisted from my position on her palm to track him, but he had already slithered away into the mass around us. “Really. So many of your forebears were completely dunces that I’m unused to a Precursor displaying even a modicum of insight.”

“Cease your games, old one,” Anima said coldly, the willow vines of her hair rustling softly. “I called you here for a reason, and it was not to listen to your blathering.”

Nehushtan was silent for a moment. When he next spoke, something about his tone raised warning bells across my Lattices. “Blathering, am I?” He said softly, dangerously. “Well. If I am blathering, I wouldn’t want to bore you now, would I? I’ll take my leave, then, and you can solve this mess without my assistance. Have fun doing so from inside your prison.”

“You’d know all about prisons, wouldn’t you, tool of the-”

“HEY!” I abruptly shouted, cutting through the growing argument. When both of the oversized heads of the present Spirits swiveled my way, I held up my hands to stop them. “Look…I’m getting the impression that you two have a history together-”

Nehushtan snorted, but didn’t interrupt me.

“-and I’m perfectly willing to mediate this, if you have trouble even speaking. I’m more than happy to. There are more important things at stake here than whatever is between you.”

Anima…deflated then, shifting her enormous body to almost curl around me. “I am…sorry, Nathaniel,” She said wearily. “You cannot imagine the weight of grudges that last for an age. For more than an age. I…called him here, and yet I do not think I am capable of civility. Please…speak for me.”

A distinctly serpentine sigh echoed out from somewhere to my left, and I heard Nehushtan’s endless coiling cease. “Thousands of years, and yet you are still unable to forgive,” He said, in a surprisingly regretful voice. “How many times did I explain myself to you and yours, in those early days? You understood at one time, and then…it faded, in the face of demands I could not grant.”

“Some things cannot be forgiven,” Anima whispered, unable to look at him. “No matter the circumstances.”

“…I see.” Nehushtan’s head slithered into view, and I couldn’t help but notice how…tired he looked, all of a sudden. He sighed and then regarded me with much less smug reassurance than before. “You must be curious why I am here, Precursor. I was…aware that something was happening, upon the surface of Vereden. But I was not privy to the details until this one,” He nodded to Anima, who still wouldn’t look at him. “Managed to explain the bones of the situation through grit teeth. I understand that Fynneas’s Mantle has been cast from the Concord, and into the sky of the physical to shed the madness infecting it?”

“That’s…what the person, the group who is controlling him, claims is happening, yes,” I said cautiously.

“It makes sense,” Nehushtan said bluntly. “Plausible, even, if one is entirely uncaring for the consequences. This planet is capable of a great deal more than most expect. The spirit of a world is more than capable of bearing such corruption intact. Those upon it however…less so. And so, you must seek out Fynneas and potentially slay him, fulfilling your actual purpose as a Precursor. But there is a problem. The Smaragd War Wards are active across the whole of the surrounding forest, blocking entrance. I…may be able to assist with this matter.”

My heart leapt in my chest, and an involuntary smile stole across my face. I may be embroiled in the actual battle for Rhoscara right now, but I hadn’t forgotten what came next. We had to find a way through the shield protecting the western half of the Barren Forest, if we were ever going to put a stop to the Skyfall.

Honestly, the best thought I’d had had been about trying to find a Long Road path that might connect to the city. But if Nehushtan had some method of getting through the actual wards…

His next words wiped the smile off my face.

“There is a problem, however,” Nehushtan cocked his enormous triangular head. “You will need to come to me if you want it. And I am currently far to the north from where I understand your position to be, failure.”

My brow furrowed. “Where are you, exactly?”

The snake hissed thoughtfully. “I am…trapped, one could say-”

“Imprisoned is more accurate,” Anima said in a half-heartedly scornful tone. She looked up only briefly before turning her face away from him.

Nehushtan continued as if he had never heard her. “-beneath one of the northern mountains. Once, the mountain above was home to one of the Dwarven Holds. I know not the name that they bestowed up it. They were…tolerable neighbors, for a time. However, in the aftermath of the War in Heaven, they rapidly declined. I believe they might have been some of Yorgun’s fiercest devotees. With his death, however…they turned into cannibals.”

I blinked slowly. “Excuse me?”

“Cannibals,” The pseudo-snake offered helpfully. “They consume their own kin. This Clan is a particularly ferocious bunch, as well. My understanding is that they enjoy skinning their victims alive before consuming them. Something to do with the taste.”

I pushed down my revulsion and turned a disbelieving eye up at Anima. “This is what you want me to do? Storm a Mountain Hold hundreds of miles to the north of us, somehow traversing the distance when I don’t even have a way to get there? Our land-ship was destroyed in the fighting! What, are we supposed to take the Long Road to get there? It’s collapsed, all the way north! It would take us too long just to clear the path! We don’t have that amount of time! Not before the Skyfall advances enough for the cleansing of the Mantle to be done, and then all of the wards will collapse!” I shook my head wildly, heart sinking in my chest. “No…this is impossible. Neither of you can possibly be-”

I was interrupted before I could continue my rant. “Nathaniel,” Anima said in a soft, firm tone. “It’s the only way. I’m sorry, but this…it’s the only method I know that could possibly breach those wards. You cannot know how long and how hard we Great Spirits tried to breach Fynneas’s War Wards in our conflict with him. As I’ve told you, it was only through guile and infiltration that the Ward Stone was broken in those ages past. As you’ve said, we do not have the time to do something similar, and this…Anguis will be on the lookout for it. No, Nehushtan’s method is the only manner in which the Wards can be bypassed.”

I grit my teeth, somehow feeling my blood pressure rise as I did. I didn’t even have blood right now! “And the problem of actually getting there?”

Nehushtan interrupted us, coughing faux politely. “As it is…you aren’t the first ones I’ve contacted about reaching me. Several months ago, I was…speaking with Tarus-”

Anima’s head snapped over to look at the snake accusingly. “You didn’t say a word of this to me!”

“Well, of course I didn’t,” Nehushtan rolled his eyes at her. “You can barely look at me, much less speak. But I’m telling you now, aren’t I? Let me finish. I spoke to Tarus, because I sensed that something was amiss. My sensors-senses-” He hastily corrected himself, wincing for some reason. “Are far more precise than yours. I’ve felt odd stirrings from Smaragd for years and have desired to investigate deeper for just as long. Why else do you think I asked you to come and find me those months ago, failure?”

I opened my mouth to answer, before slowly closing it.

I had nothing I could possibly say to that.

The snake snorted at my expression. “Indeed. This all could have been possibly avoided if some people had been able to put aside their grudges. But alas, at least Tarus was willing to humor me at the last possible moment. My understanding is that he dispatched his new Envoy in my direction-”

I abruptly choked on my own spit, breaking out into a fit of coughs. “Excuse me? Did you say Tarus’s Envoy was going to you?! Do you know what happened to him?!”

Azarus! Azarus was Tarus’s Envoy! That son of bitch, he told me he had been going to Silvercrest! That was a whole other mountain than the Holds!

Nehushtan blinked slowly at my obvious enthusiasm. “I take it you know this person, then? You’re in luck. To the best of my knowledge, this person has solved your main issue. As of now, they’re most of the way to the Holds. They have done the work in clearing a path through the Long Road for you. I…detected them some time ago, tunneling through the underground. I was keeping an eye on that direction, just…in case.” He smirked, a distinctly strange expression on the mouth of a snake. “I wouldn’t want to be considered an ungracious host before they arrived, now would I? I must prepare a suitable welcome.”

I didn’t care a whit about the strange Spirit’s indelicate jokes. I had confirmation.

Azarus was alive. And in a strange twist of fate…

He was nearly at where I apparently had to go next. I wanted to slump in relief knowing that my best friend had survived the Skyfall, after months of uncertainty.

But…I refrained. There was still too much to do before I could set off after both him and whatever assistance Nehushtan was offering.

I had to survive Rhoscara first, after. And…

I still didn’t know how he could help us through the Wards of Smaragd.

And so I asked him.

“What can you actually do for us?” I asked, cautiously hopeful. “How are you going to get my forces through the wards of Smaragd?”

“I can’t tell you,” Was the immediate answer.

I blinked slowly before turning disbelieving eyes up to Anima.

She nodded reluctantly. “He is, quite literally, unable to tell you. As am I.” Anima sighed in frustration. “There are certain…oaths that were taken, in ages past, that prevent it. All that I can tell you…is that this method is related to who the ‘gods’ were, and not what they did.”

I narrowed my eyes, turning to regard the suddenly blank-faced Nehushtan.

So.

This had to do with the Netherim. Nehushtan was involved with them in some manner, and he possessed a means to bypass their wards. An old weapon of theirs, perhaps?

Honestly?

I…didn’t care.

If it could help me save Vereden, I would damn my soul for the opportunity.

In a heartbeat.

I nodded slowly, for the benefit of both Spirits. “I’ll go. I’ll gather up my forces, and we’ll make for the Holds. Once we get closer to you, we’ll…figure out just what I can do, to get access to this way through the Wards. If you’re trapped somewhere, Nehushtan, we’ll get you out. In return, you will help us through. Is that clear?”

Animation came back to the snake Spirit, then, and he chuckled. “Oh, you’ll find that both tasks are one in the same, Precursor,” He said mysteriously. “But we have an accord. Assist me, and I shall assist you.”

I took a deep breath, then, and held it for several seconds…and then let it out all at once. I nodded firmly.

“Agreed. The expedition will go…”

“To the north.”
