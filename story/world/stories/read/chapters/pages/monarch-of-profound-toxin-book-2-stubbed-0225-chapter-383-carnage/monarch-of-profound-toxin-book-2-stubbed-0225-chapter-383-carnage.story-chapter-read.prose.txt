Chapter 383

Allarn blinked.

Surely, he had misheard those words. A quick glance back told him that his wife, Junna, and Jann were still safe and sound, although they looked just as surprised as he felt.

He returned his attention to man who had saved him. “I apologize, sir, but I don’t believe I heard you correctly. It sounded like you said you were Eik Magnasen.”

“Yeah, I mean, I did say a bunch of other stuff before then, but you got the gist of it.”

“Mr. Magnasen!” Allarn exclaimed, flying to his feet in startlement. “I’m so sorry for my rudene—” he began but lost his words as he realized that he had just stood up on legs and hips that should have been completely destroyed just moments earlier. “My legs…” he breathed with wonder.

“I thought I might as well take care of that since I was here anyway.”

“May I ask why you are here? No, I mean, why did you help us specifically? Ah, no, what I want to say is thank you! Thank you for saving my family!”

Eik flashed a smile. “No worries. And I saved you because you happened to be right here,” he said and pointed to the side. A fracture hung along the outer wall of a house, humming and churning.

“O-Oh, I…”

“You guys should get going now,” Eik said in the same moment that a crescent of pure energy cut through the sky a district away. “Ah, and Chop’s already started. Hey, tell anyone you meet to just keep running, all right? Spread the word. I’ll try my best, but consider nowhere safe.”

“Yes… Yes, sir,” Allarn said and urged his family along. “Thank you, again! Thank you so much!”

The number of spawn of the Lord of the Moon seemed to grow with no end. What had been a couple of thousand had now become more than twenty thousand advancing upon Gimleh like a plague.

Well, those were rookie numbers.

Manipulating his aura around him, Eik rose into the air as a vast Extinction Zone settled across the city. The strident grinding of it sounded threatening, but Eik had made sure to remove its toxic properties. Right now, all it was, essentially, was a gigantic summoning circle.

The first wave of Living Manifestations numbered over ten thousand, and that number was doubled again in less that two seconds. And then it was doubled again. In less than ten seconds, Eik’s army of Profound Toxic beasts had grown to five times the size of the swarm belonging to Lord of the Moon.

From the moment they came into existence, the Living Manifestation shot skyward to clash with the spawn. All over the city, citizens were being assaulted and killed.

Actually, that wasn’t exactly right. The force of Profound Toxic beasts that Eik had deployed a while back for this exact scenario had shifted from dormant to active the moment the titanic ogre had broken through the crust of the earth.

With more than a million individuals in the city, they had met the oncoming horde with fangs bared. Eik suspected that his presence emanating from the fracture immediately before his actual arrival had made them focus on other targets to protect than the little family he had saved, thinking that he would take care of things here. That had ended up being not ideal, but at least nobody got hurt.

Okay, someone had gotten kind of hurt, but not for long.

But while the Profound Toxic beasts were certainly many, it was an unfortunate fact that there were spawn that would be capable of tearing through them like paper. In general, the average strength of a Profound Toxic beast seemed significantly lower than that of a spawn of the Lord of the Moon. That meant their numbers would be forcefully reduced quickly, and an additional supply would be crucial.

Well, that, or…

A crystalline javelin, measuring almost thirty meters in length, manifested in Eik’s palm, his fingers closing around the shaft as his muscles tensed up in preparation of an extreme release of power. Flat hooks lined the length of the weapon, bunched together in tight clusters to function as barbs.

Multiple shock waves cracked the air in sequence as the projectile left Eik’s hand, the sheer force of its flight causing a churning vapor trail to form behind it.

It reached the ogre in an instant.

Caught in the side of the chest, the titan was yanked off its feet completely and carried back several kilometers, purely by the kinetic energy of the javelin. The impact was such that despite the cessation of the javelin’s cruise, the energy continued through the ogre’s body and out of its back where it super-heated the air in a cone.

Like thunder, the air shattered in a deafening boom, annihilating several dozen spawn unfortunate enough to be behind the giant at the moment of impact.

Clearing a couple of mountains, the ogre crashed to the ground with enough weight to cause a localized earthquake and alter topography.

Eik looked down at his hand. “Damn. These pills aren’t playing around.”

He hadn’t even put his full strength into it and hadn’t taken any of the common grade 7 potions either. He had several liters ready to go inside the internal storage space he’d acquired along with Perfect Consumption, which had now been elevated to Supreme Vessel, but he would save that until it became necessary.

Chop was still sending devastating blade crescents into the air, his immaculate control ensuring maximum casualty count with each attack. Although, judging by the spectacle, he had also decided to hold off on potions for now. Without the same resistance to toxins as Eik, the old man would be susceptible to elixir toxicity. He had twelve vials placed in his bag of holding, but he wouldn’t be able to just chug them like Eik could.

Since the first moment he stepped through the fracture, Eik had been constantly deploying all of his senses in an effort to locate the Lord of the Moon. If the bastard had shown up for the assault on Yanglith just days earlier, then he would surely show up to assist his army against the much more formidable force in Gimleh.

So where the hell was he?

Pointing to the sky, Eik shot a droplet of hyper concentrated Profound Toxin skyward, where it detonated and scattered across the firmament like an enormous blanket of death.

“Destroy everything in sight,” he ordered quietly, his mental orders reaching each of the more than two million Profound Toxic beasts on the battlefield. “Force him out.”

A katana of crystal appearing in his hand, Eik flitted around from spawn to spawn, deliberately picking out the most powerful of the bunch in order to reduce the pressure on his Living Manifestations.

After a handful of seconds, he felt a few familiar presences and headed there. It was Grandpa Gul leading a small force that included both the shield bearer Kalavax and the healer with whom Eik had teamed up to rescue the oracle of the cult, Sinki.

“What the hell are you guys doing here?” Eik shouted as he landed a few meters from them.

Kalavax looked at him, puzzled. “Uuh, our duty?”

Eik blinked. “Oh. Right. Good work.”

“Yeah.”

“Sorry,” Eik added sheepishly. “I worry.”

“We know,” Sinki said with a grin. The healer had been one of the first people to join up officially with Eik when he had first announced the establishment of Eik’s Great Group of Guys.

“Don’t try to engage the spawn unless absolutely necessary. And ‘absolutely necessary’ includes citizens being attacked and your own group being attacked. Always shout loudly to alert any nearby Living Manifestations, and focus on search and rescue. We want to minimize loss of life as much as we possibly can.”

“Yes, sir!” the men and women shouted.

“And another thing,” he said before leaping off again. “Please. Don’t die.”

Kalavax slapped the surface of his shield with a meaty fist. “Naturally!”

Eik was running into the exact problem he had hoped to avoid.

Fighting in the middle of friendly territory, and with low-ranked friendlies trying to get to safety, was not an easy task.

Under different circumstances, he would have launched a single, high-powered Death Star and dealt with all of the spawn in one, fell swoop, but here he could only go so far.

Precision strikes like the javelin, autonomous Living Manifestations, and the semi-guided projectiles from Apocalypse Canvas were okay, but even toxic mist calibrated to only hurt enemies, while deadly to the spawn, would also obscure the sight of everybody in the city. And there were spawn powerful enough to survive a good number of seconds—more than enough time to wreak havoc while everybody was blinded.

After all, they could swing in any direction and hit a thousand targets.

Gimleh had already taken immense damage and Eik could only imagine the number of innocents who had already lost their lives. Each exchange and each swing leveled entire blocks at once, snuffing out souls mercilessly.

Fortunately, there was one technique that worked perfectly for this, and that was Extinction Zone. While it produced an exceedingly uncomfortable sound, that alone wasn’t going to kill anybody, and its area of effect could be calibrated to affect only the spawn.

He had been running it in a circle around himself since he had first started slaughtering the spawn, and it had devastated their population, but the toxicity of the skill dropped off the further out it got from his body. And the most powerful among the spawn could survive longer than he preferred.

An ear-splitting howl cut through the air. Back on its feet, the ogre glowered and began to move toward the city. Eik had followed the first javelin up with several more, gaping wounds in the ogre’s body a testament to that.

But even as he watched, they were knitting themselves back together. Firmly in X-rank, this ogre must have had many skills and traits related to defense and regeneration to be able to withstand the kind of firepower that had hit it.

Letting a potion of each variety surge through his system, Eik felt his power grow immensely as he prepared to launch himself at the giant.

But before he could make a move, a jolt went through the ogre’s body as it stiffened. Out of nowhere, it shrieked—not in aggression or fury, like before, but in agony. Abruptly, its right eye burst, fluids and tissue gushing out like a waterfall, the shriek cut short.

Pitch black limbs grasped at the ogre’s eyelids as the Lord of the Moon crawled out of the ruined socket. He leapt into the air as the titan collapsed backward, tumbling into the mountain below.

At the sight of him, shivers ran down Eik’s spine. There was fear there, yes, but Profound Toxin’s excitement was infectious.

More potion flooded his system and surging streaks of liquid toxin appeared in his palms.

Hanging freely in the air, the Lord of the Moon howled, pulses of power jetting outward and through Gimleh.

Hunger welled up in Eik as the fear was washed away and replaced by a genuine blood frenzy. “I’ve been waiting,” he hissed, teeth bared.
