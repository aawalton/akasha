Monday October, 31st

The karaoke bar was called “Sing Your Heart Out,” which was exactly the kind of name that usually made me want to turn around and go home. Kate knew this about me and had a grip on my arm that would make a vice jealous. She’d made it very apparent she wouldn’t be letting go.

“It’s going to be fun,” she insisted, as she dragged me toward the entrance. “It’s Halloween. Everyone’s going to be drunk and in costumes. No one will care how badly you sing, trust me.”

“It’s not that I sing badly,” I insisted. “It's that I don’t sing at all.”

“That is going to change tonight.” She flashed our IDs at the bouncer, who was dressed as a very annoyed and tired vampire before pulling me inside after her.

The bar was packed. I expected with Halloween on a Monday that the majority of drinking age adults would not be out celebrating. I was wrong. Work week be damned. The room was a sea of costumes. Sexy cats, sexy nurses, sexy things that shouldn’t be sexy and yet they were–like Mrs.Potato Head. A man in a banana suit was doing shots at the bar. A group of women dressed as the Spice Girls (Cumin, Oregano, Thyme, Paprika and Cinnamon) were enthusiastically attempting to dance while belting out “Wannabe.” Creativity was certainly not the skill they were lacking, restraint however…

Kate and I grabbed a seat in the corner. I had caught our reflection in the mirrored wall behind the bar and barely recognized myself. The Aurora costume had turned out better than I’d ever imagined.The dark fabric almost absorbed the light around it making it look like I was a shadow. Someone Anish Kapoor would consider suing for infringing on his Vantablack pigment.

My make up was dramatic. Dark eyes and blood-red lips stood out on my light complexion. Kate had done something to my usually untameable hair that made it look like it was floating slightly, giving gravity the middle finger.

Next to me Kate was radiant. While I absorbed light, she exuded it. The crystals in her crown caught every light in the room shimmering and reflecting the light back. She looked like a beam of sunshine that had decided to take human form and go clubbing.

We looked, in a word, incredible.

“Drinks first,” Kate declared, steering us toward the bar. “Then karaoke. Then we will find you a man.”

“Kate…”

“Don’t you ‘Kate’ me. You promised we’d have fun. And on Friday we’re doing it again. Remember? The roses?”

“I was joking about the roses.”

“And I wasn’t joking about having fun. I need this. I’ll look for someone for you to talk to and if you see one I’ll like, send him my way.

She caught the bartender’s attention with a wave that seemed to draw him over like a puppet. Or, I mused, it was probably just really good cleavage.

“Two shots of,” she looked at the halloween menu. “Does that say brain hemorrhage? and two witches brews, please.”

“We’re already doing shots?”

“It’s Halloween. Shots are mandatory. Especially themed shots.”

The bartender slid our drinks over, and Kate pressed a shot glass into my hand. “To us. To surviving H&S Design. And here’s to looking so good that everyone in this bar is wondering who we are and if we’re a part of some guerilla marketing campaign.”

“I’ll drink to that.”I wrinkled my nose at the shot that did indeed look like a bleeding brain. We clinked glasses and I threw the shot back. The thick baileys almost didn’t go down and I choked down a gag. As the alcohol warmed in my stomach I felt some of the tension in my shoulders start to release.

“Okay,” Kate said. She grabbed her witches brew, a shimmering purple and smoking beverage and began to scan the crowd. “Let’s find a spot.”

We wove through the crowd, attracting stares and whispers as we went. A guy dressed as a werewolf actually walked into a pillar as he stared at Kate.

“Oof. Found one for you.”

She slapped me on the shoulder playfully.

A woman in a witch costume stopped us to ask us where we’d bought our costumes.

“We made them,”I replied.

She handed me her card and looked so impressed I actually felt bad having used super powers to cheat.

We found a table near the back with a good view of the stage and the bar. Kate settled into her seat as if it were a throne and she was a queen overlooking her kingdom. She sipped at her smoking drink and her crown cast rainbows across the table.

“This is perfect,” she announced. “We can see everything from here.”

“You mean you can scout potential victims.”

“I prefer ‘romantic opportunities,’ but if you prefer victims I can change the criteria for what I’m searching for.” She chuckled while taking a sip from her beverage.

“What’s that?” She asked, pointing at the card.

“Oh that witch over there told me she liked our costumes and handed it to me.” I pointed, but I’d lost the witch in the crowd. “It's pretty compelling. Too bad I have no idea what it means.” I turned the matte black card over in my hands. Embossed silver script said “Keeper”. I tucked it inside my clutch.

“Okay,” Kate said, “Let’s assess the situation. Werewolf had potential but the big red mark on his face kind of ruined it for me. Banana man is way too drunk. The viking over there is hot but he’s wearing a wedding ring. Oh. Oh oh, oh oh. ”

She began tapping my arm with every sequential ‘oh’

“You okay over there? Need a change of clothes?”

She shot me a look. “Sam.” Her voice was so low I had to strain to hear her right nxt to me. “Sam. Don’t look now but there’s a guy over at the bar who just walked in–”

I looked.

“I told you not to look,” She sighed in exasperation.

I swivelled quickly back to her. “Well how am I supposed to see who you’re talking about if I don’t look?”

“You’re supposed to be subtle about it. You know casually scan the room and you just happen to hesitate in that direction.”

I rolled my eyes, “Oh yeah, very subtle.” I did as she instructed and let my gaze drift very casually back toward the bar. Past banana suit man, and the group of zombies and chanting something, past a woman dressed as a Disney Villianess… to oh.

Oh.

Oh wow.

He was standing at the bar waiting for a drink. He was dressed as the Dread Pirate Roberts. A black shirt open just enough at the check to show of a toned and golden chest. His black mask was covering the top of his face and a prop sword on his hip finished out the outfit.

It was nerdy, but in a far more cultured way than say a storm trooper outfit was. It was the kind of thing that someone who had potentially seen the Princess Bride a hundred times or so would instantly get and said I’m comfortable enough in my masculinity to dress as a romantic hero from a 1987 film.

His dark hair was slightly messy and I found myself wanting to run my hands through it. He had a strong jaw, and broad set shoulders that suggested that he had definitely seen the inside of a gym or two.

The costume should have been ridiculous. But it really just made him more attractive.

“Holy shit.” I breathed.

“Now it’s you who needs a change of clothes.” Kate grinned at me like the Cheshire cat. “He’s perfect. He’s not wearing a ring. He came in alone. And he’s definitely scanning the room like he’s looking for someone to fall into his lap.”

As if on cue he turned slightly and I got a better view of his face. Strong features and warm brown eyes. Well maintained scruff, that just added to the entire thing. It was the kind of face that looked like it smiled a lot.

There was something about him, something I couldn’t quite place that kept me gaping. His gaze continued its sweep of the room and landed on the table. Our eyes locked and I pulled my gaze away quickly as heat rushed into my cheeks.

“Kate. Kate. He saw me.”

“Good. That’s the point.”

“That’s not… I mean, I wasn’t–”

“Sam,” Kate reached across the table and grabbed my hand. “You are dressed like a dark fae sex goddess. You look incredible, and a hot guy noticed you. Where is the problem?”

“I’m not, Aurora’s not.. a sex goddess…”

“Mmhmm…” Kate said

I risked another glance toward the bar. He was still there accepting a beer from the bartender. This time when his gaze drifted back over the corner of his mouth quirked up in a small smile.

I looked away so fast that I felt a crick in my neck.

“He’s smiling at you!” Kate hissed. “He’s smiling. Go talk to him!”

“Absolutely not.”

“Sam…”

“Kate, I'm not equipped to walk up to a hot stranger in a bar and just start a conversation. That’s not how I operate. I don’t think anyone operates like that other than you.”

“That’s literally how everyone operates in a bar! That’s what bars are for!”

“I thought bars were for drinking.”

“You can drink at home for ¼ of the cost. Bars are for multitasking.”

“Multitasking?” I asked genuinely unsure where she was going with this.

“Yeah. Flirting and drinking. Sometimes dancing and singing too.” Kate sat back in her chair. “Fine. If you won’t go to him, I will.”

“Don’t you dare.”

“Just watch me. That guy is hot hot. I could use a little of that in my life.” She started to stand up.

I pulled her arm dragging her back down. “Kate, I will murder you. I will use my bare hands and it will be messy and painful and no one will ever find the body because you and I watched literally every documentary on unsolved murders that is available to stream.”

“Wow. Possessive already.” But she was laughing and settled back into her seat. “Fine. I won’t ambush the poor man. But if you won’t go we will go up on that stage and sing.”

“Yeah okay.”

“He will be so impressed by your raw musical talent he won’t be able to help but come over and talk to you.”

“I don’t have raw musical talent.”

“You didn’t know you had raw costuming talent either.”

I groaned and took a long drink of my purple drink. This was going to be a long night.

Two more drinks in and I was feeling considerably less anxious. The karaoke had been going strong for the past hour, a parade of drunk Halloween revelers murdering classic songs with varying degrees of skill.

A guy dressed as Elvis pulled off a fairly impressive Hound Dog. A group of nurses turned “I Will Survive” into something between a religious experience and a heart wrenching promise to each other. Banana man attempted Bohemian Rhapsody and had been politely escorted off stage after the third verse.

Throughout it all I kept stealing glances at the pirate at the bar.

He was still there, nursing what appeared to be his second beer. He’d had several chats with various girls who had come up to sit next to him. Each one more intoxicated than the last.  He always seemed to be friendly and easy going and everyone who walked away after talking to him walked away with a smile on their face.

Every now and then he’d glance my way and I’d pretend to be deeply involved in a conversation with Kate.

“You’re really obvious.” Kate informed me after my last attempt to be nonchalant.

“I don’t know what you’re referring to.”

“Your neck is going to be sore from the sheer number of times you’ve looked over there in the last hour alone.”

“Oh come on.”

“I counted. Do you want to know how many times?” She held up a napkin with tally marks before grinning and nudging my shoulder. “It’s cute. You’re smitten.”

“I’m not smitten. I don’t even know his name.”

“Well, I know the solution to that.”

“Kate–”

“Or…” Her innocent grin turned wicked. “Oh look. It’s my turn for karaoke.”

Before I could stop her, she was out of her seat and heading for the stage. I watched in horror as she grabbed the microphone and gave the DJ a thumbs up.

“Good evening everyone and Happy Halloween!”

The crowd cheered in spite of it being the millionth time someone said it when taking the mic that night. Kate was a natural performer and the crowd was eating it up. She stood in the spotlight like something out of a fantasy, costume radiating and crown glittering.

“I want to dedicate this next song to my best friend Sam,” She pointed directly at me. I felt my face catch fire as dozens of heads turned in my direction. “Sam is a dark fairy princess tonight, and she’s been making eyes at a certain handsome stranger all evening but is too chicken to actually talk to him. So, Sam, this one’s for you. And for any of you Dread Pirates out there, she thinks you’re cute.”

I was going to kill her. I was going to use my newfound reality manipulation powers to erase her from existence. I was going to…

The opening notes of I Put A Spell on You started playing. Kate opened her mouth and began singing. She was good. Annoyingly so. Her voice was rich and playful, and she worked the stage like a professional. She gestured at the crowd during the right parts of the songs. The crowd was eating it up, laughing, cheering and filming on their phones.

I wanted to sink into the floor and disappear.

But I also couldn’t help laughing. Because this was so Kate, over the top, embarrassing, utterly shameless. This was why she was my best friend. This was why, despite everything, I couldn’t imagine my life without her.

When the song ended Kate took a theatrical bow. She blew me a kiss and then pointed at me before beckoning me forward with her finger.

“And now ladies and gentlemen, Sam is going to come up here and sing for her mystery man. Let’s encourage her, everyone with a round of applause!”

The crowd cheered and the DJ queued up the next song. Kate was staring at me with an expression that clearly said, ‘You’re coming up here whether it’s voluntary or if the crowd has to surf you up here.’

I drained the last of my witch's brew and stood up. If I was going to die of embarrassment, I might as well have fun while I did it.
