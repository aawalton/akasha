That evening, I glanced at Hinlaw’s fortress whenever I could. Nothing changed. The light in the tower still blazed, flickering. From our vantage in the inner-city keep, it was barely a pinprick, no larger than a star in the sky.

As we were settling down to sleep, though, Sage tapped my shoulder. She was watching out the arrow slit in our room’s outer wall. “Levi, someone’s coming to the keep. He’s in a hurry.”

I sat straight upright. “Who?”

Anchor leaned over, looking out the arrow slit. “It’s the guard you paid off.”

“Shit.” I had been in the process of taking off my armour, but I stopped, then pulled my scale mail back on over my head. I attached my pauldrons and vambraces as quickly as I could, pulled on a set of gloves, then my helmet next.

“Get yourselves geared up,” I told the others as I grabbed my spear and slung my crossbow over my back. “Just in case.”

I checked myself in the window’s reflection, making sure my armour was on properly, then raced through the hallways of the barracks, brushing past other sapper trainees, then rushed out the keep’s gate onto a quiet street. Most of the other civilians were retiring to their houses for the night, and the streets were clearing out.

“What is it?” I asked the guard.

“Sir, Lord Hinlaw is readying his carriage. I came as quickly as I could. He is trying to escape.”

I tilted my head. “He’s trying to leave the city?”

“It would seem so, sir.”

“How much time do I have?”

“A few minutes, sir.”

I nodded, then retrieved my promised price—two silver sceats—from my kit bag and pressed it into the guard’s hand. As I turned back to the keep, I stopped mid-step. My squad was rushing out, fully geared up. Pride swelled in me for a brief moment. We actually looked like a squad. Helmets with matching plumes, shields with matching crests, cohesive armour. Sure, Sage didn’t fit in, but healers never did.

I blinked those thoughts away. Now wasn’t the time for vanity. “We need to ready the horses. Hinlaw is trying to escape, and we can’t let him.”

“Should I find the other squads?” Anchor asked. “Yesman’s, perhaps?”

I opened my mouth, then shut it again and shook my head. “We don’t know who we can trust. Habit is still missing, and we don’t know if he’s alive or dead, or what he’s told anyone. Today, it’s just us. No orders, nothing. We head out on our own.”

We raced back through the keep. The stable was right beside the main street-level gate, giving the horses easy access in and out. A few local guards stood in front of it—Iron-tier Dupes who would dissuade petty thieves, and nothing more.

As I raced into the stable, the guards said, “Hey! You there! Do you have permission from the Drillmaster to be riding the horses right now!”

I ignored them. They couldn’t do anything about it, even if they wanted to.

“We’re going rogue, Sarge?” Anchor asked.

“Lord Ceonwin will understand when he hears about this,” I said.

“What about the Drillmaster?”

I shrugged. “Soon, I’ll outrank him, and it won’t matter.”

“So you are not finishing your training, Brother?” Brawl asked.

I shook my head. “I fully intend on coming back. There should be more than enough time to finish it up while we wait for the next Ealdorman to be appointed.” I sprinted through the stable, running between the stone stalls, until I found Burr. I opened her stall and swung up onto her saddle. “There’s no time for this. Get your horses, and ride to the fortress. I’ll pull ahead—Burr is faster than your mounts—but you’ll catch up.”

The guards rushed after me, scolding us about regulations, and one tried to jump in front of me and Burr. But he thought better of it as soon as she reared up, legs kicking. He jumped out of the way.

Galloping, Burr and I charged out into the streets of Steelshore. Her hooves clacked on the cobblestone and glided over potholes. I’d never been to a city this large since leaving Earth, nor one so crowded, but at least it wasn’t a peak time in the middle of the day. There was room for the civilians to jump aside, letting Burr and I through.

We swerved down the streets, blasting through intersections and dodging wagons and carriages. Whenever I could, I shouted, “Make way!” It helped, but people were surprisingly unaware of the galloping horse charging toward them. They rolled barrels to the sides of the streets, guided livestock, or swept the road with brooms.

As we rushed through the streets, I didn’t have much time to take my eyes off the road, but I needed one last check-in before I faced Hinlaw. Had I used enough of my Presence to reach Gold yet? If I did need to merge my Art and use the merged version, I needed to be a Gold to get the most I possibly could out of it.

I glanced up and down, eyes flicking between the street ahead and my reading slate. Piece by piece, I took in my new list of stats. It read:

Vitality: 161(+30)

Agility: 187(+20)

Strength: 182(+35)

Perception: 113(+15)

Focus: 201(+45)

Presence: 199

A hundred and ninety-nine, and over eight hundred when it came to the rest of my attributes. That was more than enough for the requirement. I just needed one more Presence.

As I rode, I reached into my kit bag. There were still a few Presences left over from Hyrdford, which I could put to good use. I crushed them in my fingers as we swerved through the streets, carefully watching the stat until it ticked over to two hundred.

That was it. I was a Gold, even without the bonuses from my armour, right?

But the slate listed my tier as: Gold - Unspecified Class Upgrade.

How did I upgrade my class? Moonhand had implied that it would take a moment of serenity and self-understanding to actually get the upgrade.

Right now, I wouldn’t get anything like that. I was supposed to have an extra week.

Well, I’d have to beat Hinlaw without the help of a class upgrade, then. I laughed to myself morbidly. Who didn’t like an extra challenge?

As I neared Hinlaw’s fortress, the buildings grew cleaner and taller, and the streets quieted. The trees growing on the boulevards were budding, and it turned into a green mist in the corners of my vision. I kept my eyes on target.

You couldn’t just ride up to the fortress in the evenings. An outer wall separated it from the rest of the city, and the gate was usually left wide open for administrative workers to come and go as they pleased. Today, however, the gate was almost closed. One of the swinging doors was open a crack, letting guards in and out.

As they saw me charging, they tried to push it shut.

“One warning!” I yelled to them. They were innocent Dupes, and I had no desire to kill them. “Back off and leave your master, and I’ll spare you!”

A few of the guards, Irons and Steels, backed away from the gate, but there were two who tried to slam the doors shut. I crushed them with a well of gravity, knocking them unconscious, then used a second well at an angle, ripping the gates off their hinges.

I rode through the gate in a puff of sawdust. Wooden mist trailed behind me as Burr galloped through. I found myself in a plaza in front of the main structure of overlapping towers. It was almost entirely abandoned, save for a few more guards and a carriage.

And Hinlaw and his son.

Wearing helmets and chainmail coats, they loaded their possessions into a carriage. Its driver sat in the coachman’s seat, shifting anxiously.

A semi-circle of ten guards fanned out in front of me. All low-tier Dupes. They shifted nervously, their basic armour rippling orange in the light of the setting sun. They held shields out and pointed spears.

“My offer still stands,” I said. “Give up, and I won’t hurt you. You don’t have to serve Lord Hinlaw. And you don’t have to die for an Ealdorman who’s abandoning you. Loyalty goes both ways.”

A few of them glanced at each other, and about half of them threw down their spears, then retreated. I crushed two of the ones who remained with an Art, trampled a pair directly in front of me, and launched a crossbow bolt into the last, sending him skidding back across the plaza, grasping at his chest.

With the guards out of the way, I aimed Burr straight at the carriage and charged.

Burr bounded over the carriage’s yoke, and as we passed, I lowered my spear, using its tip to slash the two pulling horses’ reins. They were well trained, but that should stop them from leaving any time soon. But just for good measure, I used another Art to crush the yoke, slamming the carriage to the ground and cracking the wood.

The carriage’s coachman scampered away without being asked, leaving Hinlaw and his last son staring at me.

“Trying to skip town, huh?” I asked.

“So it has come to this, has it?” Hinlaw snarled.

“Well, you could always resign the position now,” I said. I motioned toward his son. “Him specifically. I have nothing against him. As for you, Hinlaw? I don’t trust you to back off and leave me alone. And really, it wouldn’t be enough justice for what you’ve done.”

“What I’ve done—”

“Where are your cultists? It seems they’re not that loyal, after all.”

“The men of this nation used to prize loyalty to their lord above all!” Hinlaw spat. “Now, not even Dupes are good for anything. You were bred to be loyal.”

I sighed, then glanced at Hinlaw’s son. “So, what do you say? You’re gonna give up?”

Silence. A salty breeze blew across the plaza, rolling over the cliff from the sea. Behind me, two more sets of hooves clomped through the gate—the rest of my squad. Anchor rode on his horse, his bow strung and ready. Brawl and Sage rode on the other.

“Now you’re really in trouble,” I said. “Let’s make this easy, and—”

“Hincar, run,” Lord Hinlaw snapped. “Go!”

His son broke off, racing away from the carriage, then swung up onto the back of a carriage horse. I tried to intercept, but he swerved around me. Slamming an Art down on him, I tried to knock him off his horse.

He dispelled my Art with a thrust of his fist. A pulse of raw force rolled off his forearm and shattered the swell of gravity, cancelling out my Art. He raced away, aiming to the gate. As he passed, Anchor fired an arrow at him, but he used yet another of the same Art. It stripped away the arrow’s resonance Skill, and the arrow simply clinked off his armour.

“He’s got some kind of Art that negates ours!” I yelled. I glanced between Hinlaw and my squad, then made a split-second decision. “It has to cost a lot of Presence, and he won’t have too many more pulses like that! Brawl, Anchor, Sage, go after him! Don’t let him get away!”

He may have been a peak Silver, but together, they could handle it.

I dismounted, then turned to Hinlaw.

“You’re not going anywhere,” I said.

He gave me a resigned sigh, then spun and raced back to his fortress.
