My horde swept the great plains outside the Dark Tower in formations as straight as drawn arrows. Fifteen thousand of them, at a glance. A small portion of a far greater whole, drilling beneath a sky that stretched crimson from one horizon to the other.

They ran their drills. I had not asked them to. Epherion had fallen. The kingdoms of men had been brought to heel. The Dwarven tunnel-cities were caved in, every one. The Elves had abandoned their great tree-cities, and even my Generals could no longer find them. The Beastkin had already pledged me their allegiance, for theirs was a race that had always believed in the might of a fist, and no fist was mightier than my own.

My horde ran their formations, and there were no longer any fields left to use them in. It seemed some things never changed, even without the Pantheon to demand them. The sight might have pleased me once. It sat unwanted in me now.

"My Lady," a serpentine voice called from behind me.

I did not turn from the great window. "Did you find what I asked, Seryth?"

"I did," the woman replied. "There is no mention of any woman named Ash Vale." My hands clenched. "However, there was a Hero who bore that name. He died seventy-six years ago. I have located his body."

I turned from the window then and regarded her. Seryth was on one knee, her head bowed low. The obedience was perfect. It should not have been so. I had memories of a different demon, one whose back had never been that straight. One who had looked at me with devotion, but something sharper besides. A hunger. I saw none of that in Seryth now. Strange, that obedience should taste bitter.

Seryth nodded. She reached behind her and drew the wicked curved sword strapped across her back. [The Cutter]. She slashed the air to her right, and a tear opened in the world, one that led only into darkness. And from it slid a body, wreathed in white.

I stepped forward. My gait was steady. I made certain it was. I looked down upon the Hero, and for a moment I did not recognize his face. Then I did. This was the face that had stormed the halls of my fortress. The body had been preserved. By whom, I did not know. I had memories of two worlds. In one of them, an Ash Vale had died long before he ever reached my halls. I had heard the report and I had laughed. "[Dispel]," I whispered, hand outstretched. My mana swirled, and I felt the spell take. Nothing happened. The man was still a man. There was no woman folded beneath. "[Greater Dispel]," I said. Nothing happened.
