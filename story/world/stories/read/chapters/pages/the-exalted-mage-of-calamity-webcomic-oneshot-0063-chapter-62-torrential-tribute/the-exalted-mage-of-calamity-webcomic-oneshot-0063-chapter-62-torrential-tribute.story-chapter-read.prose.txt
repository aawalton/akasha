“Excuse us,” Veronica said, slipping past a pair of people along the sidewalk.

“Hey! What’s the deal!” they complained.

“Sorry!” Gwen called back as she hurried after her, catching up to Veronica.

They had made it back into the main stretch of town, away from the dreary slums. Even so, their pursuers were still on their tail.

“Hey, care to explain what’s happening right now? Why did you blow up the building? There might’ve been other people in those rooms,” Gwen asked.

A few passersby turned to stare as they rushed past, but Veronica ignored them. Gwen, on the other hand, felt every gaze.

“Relax,” Veronica said. “I didn’t sense anyone else in those rooms. The inn was empty, aside from the ones chasing us.”

Up ahead, the western gate checkpoint came into view. She needed to draw the assassins away from the city. A fight here would only complicate things, and she couldn’t afford attention from the guards or the Viscount yet. Even with the communication crystal, there was no solid proof linking Dain to any illegal dealings with Lorence. He could deny everything, especially since the matter with Galland was supposedly handled legally.

She still needed a smoking cannon—written or recorded proof that they were hiding things.

“What do you mean ‘sense’? You know tracking spells too?” Gwen asked, quickening her pace to run beside her.

“Er… it’s a long story. I’ll explain when we have more privacy,” Veronica replied.

Gwen hummed and glanced back. There was still no one visible behind them, only ordinary townspeople moving about.

“Where are we going? Are we still being chased?” she asked.

“Yeah. A couple of Tier-3 aura users,” Veronica said. They stepped off the sidewalk and headed straight for the gate. “They won’t go down that easily. We need to deal with them outside the city, or the guards will get involved.”

“Wait—Tier-3? Shouldn’t we just let the guards handle it? Why are we the ones running if they’re the ones attacking us? This is dangerous.”

“They’ll disappear if we do that,” Veronica said, glancing at her. “You couldn’t even see them on the stairs, right? They’re probably using some kind of invisibility artifact. And they’re trained assassins. If the guards start searching, they won’t be found easily again. If we let them go now, they’ll just come after us later—maybe while we’re sleeping. I’ll probably be fine. But you?”

There was also more to it than that. She couldn’t risk exposing herself before tomorrow. If those men returned to the room, they could piece together that she had been the one speaking to Dain. The timing alone would be too suspicious to ignore.

Veronica and Gwen reached the gate and passed through. Unlike entering, there was little scrutiny when leaving.

Once they left the city, they resumed a steady jog, pushing deeper into the woods for several minutes.

“Why exactly are there assassins coming after us? Are they with Lorence?” Gwen asked. “I heard you speaking with someone. Was that related to this?”

“Partially,” Veronica replied. “They’re actually after me.”

Every so often, she glanced back to check if Dain’s men were still following. She couldn’t see or sense them yet, but that wouldn’t last. They would catch up soon. Assassins didn’t let go of their targets that easily—especially not after getting so close.

“It’s a separate issue I’m investigating. It just so happens Lorence might be tied to it.”

“What are you investigating that has assassins coming after you?”

Veronica pressed her lips together.

Gwen was essentially her student now. There was no way the Mage Association would deny their request to keep Rocky safe. Letting her in on this wouldn’t be too dangerous.

“I’m trying to bring a very skilled merchant under my wing,” she said. “But he’s currently indebted to High Crates.”

“The merchant company?”

“Yeah. His name is Galland,” Veronica said. “He’s highly capable, but for one reason or another, he’s tied to them. A few years ago, a shipment caravan was attacked after he and another man were assigned to choose the route. Bandits ambushed them, killed everyone, and took everything.”

“That’s… horrible,” Gwen said quietly.

“After that, Galland took part of the blame. The other man was imprisoned, and Galland was saddled with a massive debt,” Veronica continued. “But I don’t believe it.”

“What do you mean?”

Veronica slowed to a stop, having put enough distance between them and Ronswick. Gwen halted beside her, far more winded.

“He was set up. The whole thing is a farce. They’re using him as a scapegoat,” she said, glancing back the way they came. “I even heard it through the communication crystal. He’s expendable to them. And judging by how Dain was speaking, there may be others. The fact that he’s been working with Lorence already says enough about the kind of dealings they’re involved in.”

“Who is Dain?”

“A senior merchant at High Crates,” she replied.

“Then…” Gwen’s brows drew together as she thought it through. “High Crates is… working with Lorence? They’re his buyers?”

“It’s very likely. But we’ll need solid evidence before we can shut it down.”

Before Gwen could respond, Veronica raised a hand, stopping her.

“They’re here.”

At the edge of the clearing, two dark figures dropped from the treetops. Both wore fitted leather armor with black cloaks. The man on the left was tall and thin, while the one on the right was broader, his build dense with muscle. His cloak was singed, the edges burned and frayed.

Gwen immediately stepped behind Veronica.

“Hey… I can’t really fight. I’m a healer. W-what should I do?” she whispered.

Veronica kept her gaze locked on the two men, refusing to let them slip from sight.

“When I cast my first spell, run back and take cover behind a tree,” she said quietly. “I’ll teach you offensive healing later. For now, just keep your healing on me from there. I won’t let them reach you.”

“R-right,” Gwen replied, her voice unsteady.

The broader man stepped forward. “Done strategizing?” he said, his voice low and edged with mockery. “That fireball you threw at us was surprising. But if that’s the extent of your power, you might as well give up now. I’d prefer beautiful women to stay beautiful.”

Veronica narrowed her eyes. She took a step forward, arms at her sides, fingers tensed. Mana flowed quietly beneath the surface as she prepared her spells. Gwen remained where she was, waiting for the signal.

“Then how about I’m the last thing you see when you die?” Veronica replied. “You can immortalize my appearance once you cross over.”

“Hah,” he laughed. “Feisty, aren’t you?”

He brought his fists together, cracking his knuckles.

Now that she had a clear look, Veronica could see the metal gauntlets. A close-combat fighter. One of the worst matchups for her.

The taller man said nothing, already moving to the side as he began circling the clearing.

Veronica tracked him, refusing to let him leave her sight.

“You’ve been snooping around a bit too much. We were called to keep an eye on you and take you out when the chance came,” the broader man continued. “Mage tower, entertainment district… I figured the slums would’ve been a fitting place to bury you. Didn’t expect you to make it this far.”

He shifted into a fighting stance, feet planted wide, arms raised, fists tight. “So what is it? Stupid, or confident?”

Called, huh…? Veronica thought. They’re just goons, then. No one important.

“Then come on,” Veronica said, mockery lacing her own words. “Enough talking, muscle-head. Winner gets the right to speak.”

The man smiled.

For several long seconds, no one moved. They stood locked in place, watching, waiting for the first move.

A bird chirped overhead.

Veronica took the initiative, and struck first.

She raised her palm, unleashing a Tier-3 spell. A torrent of wind surged forward, forming a cyclonic tunnel that tore through the clearing. The currents sharpened into slicing blades, carving gouges into the ground and ripping bark from the surrounding trees.

The two assassins braced themselves, arms raised to shield their eyes.

Behind her, Gwen felt only a faint pull. She turned and ran, retreating behind a tree just as instructed.

Veronica followed immediately with a second spell. Her left hand twisted with the motion of the storm, feeding flames into the vortex. Fire surged into the wind, amplifying its force into a raging inferno.

It was the same combination she had used during her guild test.

The only difference was—

She hadn’t held back this time.

The flames whipped through the air, fanning out across the clearing.

Then something sharp rang out to her left.

[Automatic Barrier activated.]

A translucent dome of blue mana shimmered into place around her. Veronica turned, catching sight of a small dagger dropping to the ground, repelled by Sage’s automatic barrier.

She cut off her spell and immediately poured mana into her own barrier, layering it over the first.

A second later, the lanky man rushed in from that same side. A dagger flashed in his hand as he drove it straight into her shield.

The barrier shuddered. A small crack was left in the barrier, but it held.

At the same time, her ears picked up the sound of boots from her right.

Heavier ones.

The vortex in front of her dissipated as the mana supply cut off.

Before the man on her left could pull back and strike again, Veronica clapped her hands together. White wings flared into existence behind her palms. A semi-circular ring of condensed light formed around her, its sharp hum vibrating through the air.

She twisted her hands in opposite directions. The ring brightened instantly, expanding as mana surged outward.

Then it detonated.

The explosion cracked through the clearing, sending a concussive, slicing force ripping outward in all directions in front of her.

Light met steel—and surpassed it. The spell sheared straight through the man’s dagger. He raised his arm to shield himself as the ring struck. Blood sprayed as the edge of light carved through his forearm, then tore across his armor and into his chest.

He was thrown backward, body lifted off his feet as the force carried him through the air.

Ahead and to her right, only dissipating flames remained. The fire had burned the entire space and cleared it, but the bulkier man was still unseen.

That, however, wasn’t the case for long.

His invisibility shattered as the wave struck. His eyes widened as the blade of light closed in at impossible speed. He planted his feet and raised both arms in front of his face to block.

The impact hit like a spiked cannonball.

He was ripped off his feet, light biting deep into his forearms akin to that of an axe hitting a log. The force drove him back, tearing through the ground beneath him as he was sent flying.

The ring continued past them, carving and slicing through the trees all around them, toppling them over in unison.

A thunderous crash echoed through the forest as several trees came down, sending birds scattering into the sky.

For a brief moment, everything stilled. Leaves drifted through the air. Embers smoldered across the scorched ground.

Veronica exhaled, her gaze shifting between both directions.

The man to her left had lost an arm. He lay motionless, crumpled over a fallen tree.

But on her right—

“Hahaha! What powerful magic!” the man shouted.

Veronica focused on him.

He was still standing, arms raised. A deep gash ran across them, blood dripping steadily. Even so, he laughed, unfazed. “That was far stronger than the fire spell. It caught me off guard.”

He lowered his arms and steadied himself. Then his body shifted. The wounds along his arms sealed, the bleeding slowing, then stopping entirely.

He tore off his black cloak. “I was ordered to kill you, but… I’ll spare your life by making you my wife instead! Come—let’s fight to our heart’s content!”

He kicked off the ground and charged, moving faster than she anticipated.

Veronica raised a barrier as the man wound his fist back mid-air.

His fist connected with the barrier and immediately sent a violent shockwave rippling across the barrier’s surface. Cracks spread in jagged lines around the impact.

Veronica furrowed her brows as she began repairing the damage. She tried to prepare another spell—

But he struck again.

The second blow shattered her barrier completely.

Veronica cut off her spell and redirected everything into forming another barrier.

“Haha! Come! Let’s see how long you last!”

He struck again and again, each blow carrying enough force to shatter steel. Every time her barrier broke, Veronica rebuilt it.

“Tch.” She clicked her tongue.

This was the problem with close-quarters combat against mages. Against assassins, barriers were enough. Even against swordsmen, while they used aura, their approach was different. Those fights were still winnable, depending on skill.

But a martial fighter? Someone who focused entirely on reinforcing their body with aura?

It was the worst matchup a mage could face. Their strikes condensed everything into raw, destructive force—far more than most barriers could endure. Even highly destructive spells were less effective against them.

However, that was only the case for regular mages.

“Break!” he shouted, driving another crushing blow forward.

Veronica’s barrier shattered all at once, breaking into fragments of fading mana.

He grinned. “Hah!”

He didn’t slow. Stepping in, he closed the distance, his arm already drawn back for another strike.

Veronica clenched her jaw.

Just before the blow landed, mana converged all around her arm, and she thrust it forward and caught his fist.

The impact shook the air. A sharp crack rang out echoing in their ears.

Pain shot up her arm. Several smaller cracks followed. She couldn’t be sure, but she was confident her bones might have just broken.

[Ascended Body remaining duration reduced to 0 seconds.]

Simply catching his fist had consumed the entire duration of her Ascended Body upgrade in one go.

“What?” the man said. “How did you—”

That’s when his eyes widened.

Veronica’s hair began to lift, a spark of purple lightning traveling across the air as a deep hum reverberated from her body.

Her eyes pierced straight through the man’s soul as she stared back at him, unblinking. Her other palm was raised straight in front of his face.

A vortex of power welled up between them. He pulled his arm free, preparing to dodge. But before he could leap away, the ground beneath him cracked.

A single wing formed behind Veronica’s injured arm. The spell ruptured the earth just enough to throw off his footing.

“Shit—”

A concentrated beam tore forward, piercing straight through him. For a split second, his body resisted, muscles tensing, aura flaring in a last attempt to hold—but it wasn’t enough. The light burned through him, splitting him cleanly apart. His body jerked once, then stilled as the beam consumed him.

The force carried on past, expanding and carving a glowing trench into the earth beyond. Blackened and melted under the intensity, the air itself warped from the heat.

When the light faded, only Veronica remained standing, a heavy exhale leaving her mouth.

Before her, a molten crater smoldered, large enough to sink several carriages. The man’s body had fallen into it—what remained of it, at least. His upper half had been completely erased, cauterized by the blast.

“Aah—!” a scream shouted from behind her.

Veronica turned instantly.

Across from her, the dagger-wielding assassin had snuck up behind Gwen. His left arm was severed, but his right arm wasn’t.

He stood behind her, holding a knife directly below her throat.
