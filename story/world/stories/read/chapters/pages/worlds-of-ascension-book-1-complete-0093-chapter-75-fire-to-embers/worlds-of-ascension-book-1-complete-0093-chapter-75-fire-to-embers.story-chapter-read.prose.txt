Days melted into weeks as Aira, Lila, and Alliot immersed themselves in the fiery embrace of Lamcada. What had begun as a cautious exploration of the Emberwood Grove transformed into a rigorous regimen of attunement—their bodies and minds gradually adapting to the volatile power that pulsed beneath the city’s obsidian streets. The trauma of Ziemrot’s experience was different for each of them. It slowly cooled like lava into stone, hardening their resolve rather than breaking it.

For Aira, these weeks had transformed from mere preparation into a journey of intellectual expansion as she embraced Ronorrh’s mental disciplines—techniques that sharpened her mind like a blade in the forge. Under Ronorrh’s guidance, she learned to synchronize her thoughts with the Emberwood’s rhythm, reaching a state of heightened cognition. She still couldn’t see her base stats, but it felt like they were again improving after the slowdown of the previous weeks.

Lila, meanwhile, found an unexpected solace in Lamcada’s openness. Among enlightened who spoke freely with humans and valued cooperation over isolation, her dual perspective became an asset rather than a burden. Her ranger’s experience combined with her new enlightened senses allowed her to bridge conversations between Ronorrh’s instructors and her companions. Each day, she practiced her Peacemaker abilities on minor disputes among the city’s crafters, slowly reclaiming purpose from the ashes of her former life.

That was also a great opportunity to explore an issue that bothered them since they came to Lamcada. One evening, as they practiced forms in the communal training area, Lila paused mid-movement, amber eyes fixed on a human craftsman and enlightened artisan exchanging detailed notes across a workbench.

“It still catches me torch-blind every time,” she said, nodding toward the pair. “How do they even understand each other? Back in Mountain View, we’d need a slate board and crude drawings just to get the simplest point across.”

Ronorrh appeared beside them, her approach silent despite the obsidian floor. “Curious, isn’t it?” Her eyes danced with mischief. “The great mystery of understanding between species.”

Aira studied the human-enlightened interaction. “There’s more to it than writing, isn’t there? They’re communicating too fluidly.”

Ronorrh’s smile widened. “Sharp as the mountain’s edge, you three have earned a peek behind the curtain.” She motioned them to follow her away from listening ears.

In a secluded alcove heated by a small lava channel, Ronorrh revealed a simple copper amulet from beneath her robes. “Our little secret—magitech, as you Lila called it, at its finest. We call them ‘bridge pendants.’ They don’t grant speech directly, but they create a field where intentions translate across the divide.”

“So not mental communication. Like it normally works for the enlightened people,” Alliot observed, his hands trembling slightly as he examined the device. “But enough for meaningful exchange.”

“Exactly,” Ronorrh nodded. “Perfect translation isn’t necessary for understanding. We’ve found intention carries more truth than words ever could. An enlightened affects the gadget with their magic and it transforms it into speech. And for the humans, it works the other way around.”

“This reminds me of something,” Aira offered through Lila. “That’s very similar to how Lila and I communicated in the very beginning of our journey.”

But Lila’s eyes narrowed. “If this is possible, why don’t all enlightened cities use these? Why maintain the separation?”

Ronorrh’s expression darkened momentarily. “Some would say separation serves those in power. Others might argue tradition protects us all.” The playfulness returned to her eyes. “But I’m just a simple shaman sharing trinkets with friends, not a politician with grand theories.”

However, for one member of the trio, the time spent in Lamcada didn’t lead to balance and consolation. For Alliot, each meditation session became a battlefield where loyalty warred with mounting evidence. His friends he had once dismissed as paranoid dissidents—their whispered concerns about Ainorrh’s true agenda echoing through Wentouk’s living branches—no longer seemed like lunatics spouting conspiracy. Their warnings clicked into place like the tumblers of a lock finally aligning.

Too often, Alliot’s fingers unconsciously traced the hem of his shirt, that nervous tell Aira and Lila had come to recognize, as fragments of overheard conversations and inexplicable orders crystallized into a pattern too deliberate to ignore. Something unnatural twisted through the roots of Wentouk, and the more they learned of Lamcada’s openness, the more Alliot suspected Veskai’s leadership might be entangled in the same shadowy design.

***

After weeks of preparation and discovery, the time had finally come. The morning Aira decided to approach the Emberwood Grove arrived with a certainty that hummed through her bones like the calling of the Shards themselves. Each night for weeks, she had meditated with the Splinter of the Worldroot cradled in her palm, its emerald light casting strange shadows as she learned to coax its earthen energy into harmony with her own.

Now, with the fire Source’s rhythm flowing through her veins after countless hours of attunement, the time had come to claim what she’d journeyed so far to obtain. She traced the Rune inscribed on her wrist, feeling it pulse in anticipation—more alive, more responsive than it had been since her arrival in this world.

During her training, Aira focused on mastering the new powers granted by her Energy Manipulation skill, especially the ability to transform energy into matter. She practiced relentlessly, shaping small bursts of energy into solid forms, feeling the thrill of her growing mastery. Alliot and Lila watched her progress with awe, offering encouragement and support.

“It’s amazing how far you’ve come,” Lila said one evening as they sat by a small fire. “Your control over energy is incredible. I have no doubt you’ll succeed in this ritual.”

Aira smiled, appreciating their confidence in her. “Thank you, both of you really, for being with me all this time. I feel ready. But I want to try something new. I know I’ll need to use this new power of transformation of energy into matter. Better understanding of its abilities may give us an edge in the future.”

“If you say so,” said Alliot. “It’s your ritual, your skill, and your quest anyway.”

“Would you two be interested in coming with me this time?” Aira asked. “I think this time of intense training prepared you well to withstand the powers involved.”

“Are you sure?” asked Lila. “Are you sure we won’t be an obstacle?”

“As Alliot likes to say,” said Aira, smiling, “there’s only one way to find out.”

Ronorrh left them to the Emberwood Grove and stopped at the edge, her fiery eyes filled with encouragement. “This is where your journey continues, Aira. The Emberwood Grove will challenge you, but I have faith in your abilities. Remember what you’ve learned and trust in your power.”

Aira took a deep breath, feeling the weight of the moment. She stepped forward, her Rune glowing softly as she approached the heart of the Grove, where the Emberwood trees burned brightest and the ground itself radiated heat like a living forge. Unlike her desperate assault on Ziemrot’s mountain or her careful navigation of Veskai’s waters, she approached this ritual with the confidence of preparation. She extended her hands, palms up—an offering and invitation rather than a demand.

The Grove responded immediately. Flames leapt higher along the trees’ blackened trunks, their eternal fire brightening to a blue-white intensity that forced Lila to shield her eyes. The air thickened with power, shimmering like desert heat-haze as rivers of magma beneath their feet surged upward through invisible channels, seeking Aira like metal drawn to a lodestone.

Aira closed her eyes, her face serene despite the inferno building around her. The Splinter of the Worldroot emerged from her pocket of its own accord, hovering above her left palm. Its emerald glow seemed to struggle against the overwhelming fire, but rather than being consumed, it established its own rhythm—earth and fire meeting, testing boundaries, negotiating balance.

“She’s channeling both elements simultaneously,” Alliot whispered, his voice barely audible through the party chat. Al chittered nervously from his perch on Lila’s shoulder, where he was placed not to distract Aira, his tiny paws gripping her collar tight.

Aira drew a deep breath and exhaled slowly, the air from her lungs visibly shimmering as it mixed with the Grove’s heat. She raised her hands higher, fingers splayed as if physically grasping the invisible currents of power swirling around her. The Splinter of the Worldroot began to rotate clockwise above her left palm while her right hand gradually curved inward, forming a sphere of emptiness into which the Grove’s fire was drawn.

From Lila’s perspective, it looked as if Aira stood at the center of a whirlwind made of flame. The heat should have been unbearable, yet somehow it stopped just short of her skin, creating a boundary layer of cooler air. “By the twisted branches,” she whispered, her eyes reflecting the spectacle. “She’s literally bending fire to her will.”

Alliot had dropped into a defensive stance, his body angled to shield Lila if necessary. Unlike his collapse in Ziemrot, he remained conscious, though his face had paled to an ashen hue. “The air element within me is fighting this,” he said through gritted teeth. “But it’s not winning.”

Al darted from Lila’s shoulder to the ground, then back again, his tiny body trembling with either with excitement or with indecision. His whiskers stood perfectly straight, as if detecting patterns in the energy that the others couldn’t perceive.

The whirlwind of flame began to compress, drawn into the sphere Aira had shaped with her right hand. As it collapsed inward, the Splinter of the Worldroot shot toward it, its emerald light piercing the fire’s heart. For one breathless moment, earth and fire energies collided—two elements that should have opposed each other violently—but instead, they merged in a flash of light so brilliant it left afterimages burned into their vision.

When they could see again, Aira stood unmoving, a small, obsidian-like object hovering an inch above her cupped hands. It glowed with a steady, internal heat, appearing as a small, cracked piece of volcanic glass. Through its fissures shone a molten orange light, as though the fire within never truly died—her second Shard.

“That was intense,” said Lila and looked at Alliot, who was standing on all fours. “By the twisted branches, Alliot! Are you still rooted, or has the fire blown your senses to ash?”

“Yes…” he gasped, “wait a minute… that was something else. I felt the energy surge through me and it wasn’t like anything I’ve ever experienced in my life. And my journey with you has already brought me many unforgettable moments!”

“Aira!” Alliot’s voice cracked with shock, his eyes fixed on something only he could see. “There’s a—a quest interface. Floating right in front of me.” His hands reached out, grasping at the invisible text, trembling as if touching something sacred. “In all my years... I never thought... What did you do?”

Aira’s expression shifted from exhaustion to cautious triumph. “I had a theory,” she said softly, studying his reactions with intense focus. “Every deep connection with a Source has changed something fundamental about this world, about my connection to the System… to the Nexus. With Lila’s transformation, she gained access to quests and leveling that other enlightened don’t have. I wondered if I could...” she hesitated, “...extend that change to someone else… To you.”

“You altered how I interact with the Nexus?” Alliot’s voice was barely a whisper, torn between awe and violation. “Without asking?”

Aira held his gaze. “Some experiments can’t be theoretical, Alliot. And after everything we’ve learned, I needed to know if my influence here is broader than anyone suspected.” She stepped closer, lowering her voice. “What does it say?”

“Isn’t it… private?” Alliot asked. “That’s the first quest I have got in my life.”

“I think we are past private,” said Aira.
