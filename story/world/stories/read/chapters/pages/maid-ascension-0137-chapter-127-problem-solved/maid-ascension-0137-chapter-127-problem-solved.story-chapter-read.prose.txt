Lira spent most of the next three days spying on the new Lord Steward. She would either watch him from the top floor of the library tower while he sat in the garden, or trail him carefully from a distance whenever he wandered outside her field of view.

It was a distraction from thinking about Aric and his ultimatum.

"Pika. Cipos. Syneth."

Eagle Eyes brought his face into sharp focus. She watched him the same way she had once watched Thadwick, cataloguing his habits, his schedule, his vices, and asking herself the only question that mattered.

Was it worth it to kill him?

Lira didn't want to be someone who killed at the drop of a hat, which was why she carefully considered every target. The first time Taren had come into her crosshairs was when she had watched him through the brothel window. Part of her had wanted to Fireball everyone who frequented that establishment. She hadn't, for two reasons. It would have made her existence public, since word of a serial killer targeting nobles would spread, and every competent investigator in the kingdom would be on the case. That would make it impossible to reach her main target: Kelroth.

There was also a thin moral justification for sparing them: the clients were frequenting a legitimate business with all the relevant paperwork. Under the most charitable view, one could argue the clients hadn't known the girls were trafficked, which put the lion's share of the blame on Salvek.

It was thin at best, but Lira needed something to justify it, so she could avoid taking on the burden of killing hundreds of nobles.

But after what had happened to Tessa, Taren had proven to her that consent was optional to him, which made him a valid target once more. More importantly, she had promised the girl it would never happen again, and Lira intended to keep that promise.

So she watched, plotted, and judged him.

The first thing she confirmed was that Lord Taren was almost always drunk. Not the highly functional kind of drunk she had seen among Pallan's better scammers and gamblers, men who needed to hold their liquor as part of the job.

No, Taren was the pathetic kind of drunk. He would show up in the garden most days around noon, already flushed, and settle onto whichever bench gave him the clearest view of passing skirts.

He was also a coward, or maybe an opportunist was more accurate. Most of the time it was only his eyes that wandered, ogling everyone and anyone, noble or servant. Sometimes he would use his hands, a pinch here, a slap there, whenever a girl looked young enough, or too meek to make a scene. It never went further than that, and he would usually back down if a girl fought back. Dira, who Lira knew had worked in a port city tavern before a favor granted to her father secured her a position at Castle Elthram, once slapped his hand away without saying a word and kept walking, probably out of habit. Taren didn't so much as raise his voice at her. He didn't threaten her with his rank, and he didn't call for the guards. He simply let his hand fall, reached for his flask instead, and watched her walk away.

Most of the maids learned to avoid him fast. He wasn't a stranger to the palace, but before his promotion, he had only shown his face a handful of times a month, mostly to visit the bookkeeping tower. Now he came every day, and he had become everyone's problem, not just Harwin's. He drifted through the laundry court or the stables under the pretense of inspecting the staff's work, though the one time he tried to inspect the kitchen, Old Marga chased him off with a wooden spoon.

Lira was conflicted. On one hand, he could do more harm if he leaned more on his new authority. On the other, no one truly respected that authority in the first place. Not the guards, not the senior staff, not the footmen. All of them checked with Harwin or their own direct superiors whenever he gave an order, and most of the time they were quietly told to ignore him.

On the third day, Lira nearly broke her vigil when she saw Taren cornering Maid Elin near the linen stores. The girl was too shy and too startled to do anything but freeze against the wall while he leaned in close. Lira was already moving to run to the girl's rescue when she spotted Celene arriving first.

"Pika. Cipos. Hishar. Kaldris. Velar."

Focused Hearing snapped into place.

"Back off," Celene said.

"Ah, Celene." Taren waved a hand. "I was just asking her to refill my drink."

"Leave my girls alone."

He bristled at that. "Don't talk to me like this. I am the Lord Steward," he shouted loudly. Behind him, Elin took the chance to slip away.

"You need to obey me, woman! I remember ordering the maid uniforms shortened to the knee for the summer," he shouted. "It is too hot for long skirts, and yet you ignored my orders."

"Harwin is the one truly running this castle," Celene said, her voice flat and even. "You are an old pervert with no sense and no tact, and I will not have you near my girls again."

Thwack. He slapped her, open-handed, across the face.

Celene didn't flinch. She gave him a look cold enough that Lira felt it from three floors away, then turned and walked away toward the Main Hall, where King Argon was holding court.

This was the moment that decided it. Lira watched her go and made up her mind. Taren was going to die.

By late evening Lira already had the shape of a plan. Using what she had learned about the body, she thought she could build a spell that worsened the effects of alcohol on it. She had seen men die of drink in the streets of Halthar before, so she knew it was possible, and more importantly, it would read as a natural death, one unlikely to draw an investigation.

She had four of the five words in mind already.

Yorad. Picos. Kalaba. Maha. Something.

Body. Diminution. Life. Water. Something.

She didn't have a word for alcohol yet, and she spent a while turning over what might combine with Water to symbolize it.

It was getting late. She would work it out tomorrow, she decided, watching Lord Taren stumble from his spot in the garden toward the palace gate. Probably heading to the manor house granted him with his new title, which stood only a five-minute walk from the outer gate, the first building in the noble district past the killing field beyond the palace walls.

It was dark, and Lira could only make out his silhouette under the gibbous moon.

That was when a second shape stepped out of the dark ahead of him, blocking him from reaching the castle main gate. It was massive, close to two meters tall and built like a bulwark. There was only one man it could be: Sir Estophon.

The Captain of the Royal Guard, and Kavard's temporary jailor, took Taren by the shoulder and steered him off the main path, toward one of the smaller guard towers set into the outer wall. Lira followed at a distance, Eagle Eyes still active.

Five minutes later, the two of them stood atop the wall itself, in the same dark stretch Lira had used more than once to slip out of the castle unseen. She considered that she needed to create a dark-vision spell; she could make out only two shapes against the moon, not clearly enough to read lips. She wasn't willing to risk Focused Hearing either. Unlike Eagle Eyes, that spell left a thin thread of disturbance stretching between her and her remote ear, and with the palace this quiet at night, and Estophon standing right there, there was no chance it would go unnoticed.

So she watched and waited as the two men had a short conversation atop the wall.

She guessed the guard captain was warning him off about hitting Celene, who had once been the king's woman. A man like Argon would probably want to protect what he thought was his possession rather than out of any real respect for the woman.

So it came as a shock when Estophon casually pushed Taren off the wall.

It happened almost gently. The drunk man could barely stand on his own feet; a simple shove from a man that size was enough to send him sprawling. Lord Taren Cormen, once Viscount of Cormenfield, now Lord Grand Steward of Castle Elthram, went off the wall without so much as a shout.

Lira's stomach dropped with him. She forced herself to keep watching as the noble landed wrong. She focused her Eagle Eyes on his chest, watching for the rise and fall of his breath.

Nothing.

Lira stepped back from the window, back against the wall, her own heart pounding. She had seen enough, and she wasn't going to risk revealing herself to the Captain of the Royal Guard.

She couldn't help but wonder: why would Estophon kill him?

It wasn't for the slap alone, surely not. She remembered Estophon as a scary man from his conversation with Kavard, but he didn't read like someone who would act on personal offense, or an offense done to a friend. So it was an order from the king, maybe, an old debt to Celene finally called in. Or the whole thing could have been planned from the start, Taren installed only long enough to hand Harwin his seat, then the newly minted noble could hold the interim position for years without anyone complaining about it.

She didn't know enough to tell which, nor could she do anything about it for now.

She supposed that it was somewhat of a relief. She wouldn't need to kill him herself, nor did she need to finish the drink-death spell after all.
