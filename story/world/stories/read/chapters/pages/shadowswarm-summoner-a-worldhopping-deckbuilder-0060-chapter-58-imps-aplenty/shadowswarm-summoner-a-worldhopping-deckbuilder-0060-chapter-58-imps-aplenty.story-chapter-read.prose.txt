Chapter 58: Imps Aplenty

Ambrose felt a sudden drop in his stomach, a cold surge of terror at the casual dismissal. And then it turned to immediate, seething anger. “What do you mean ‘I don’t think so’? We’re paying customers, and we have need of these cards.”

Javon scoffed at them. Guards moved to close in, but he kept them at bay by raising a single hand. “Are you new here? Do you not know how it works?” He was almost amused.

“Yes, and yes,” said Raeva. She was just as frustrated as him, but did a better job hiding it. “We’ve got cards to trade in and pay for what we need. Is this or is this not a store for high end cards?”

The older man leaned back, steepling his fingers. Ambrose had a feeling that the only reason they weren’t being tossed out was to give Javon a chance to demean them for a while. Just until he got bored of it and had the guards drag them out, kicking and screaming.

“This is a high end shop. And as such, we’re not in the business of giving out cards to children. Come back in a few years, when you have realized some of your potential or are working for someone too important for me to ignore.”

“What makes you think we aren’t?” Raeva asked.

Javon scoffed. “If you were, I would recognize you. But all I see are two strange children with only one level 4 Affinity apiece. Whichever master let you off the leash early, it’s not my responsibility to get you up to proper standards.”

As angry as Ambrose was, it did tell him a few things. Javon could see their Levels and possibly cards in their deck. And, unless he was mistaken, the man wanted novelty and excitement. It wasn’t just about buying and selling, it was about entertaining himself and making people want his products more. He almost respected it.

There were a few different tactics he could take, but he didn’t have time to think it over. He could see the light in the man’s eyes dimming, his interest in Ambrose fading almost as soon as it arrived. When he acted, it was by instinct, knowing that he was putting them all at risk. Ambrose wanted those damned cards.

“I have no master, and I have graduated my school at the top of my class. But I thought this was a place of curiosity and intrigue, not dull and apathetic. I’ll take my unique cards elsewhere. I’m sure someone will appreciate a genuine mystery.” He sneered openly at the older man, and motioned with his head for Raeva to follow him out.

She looked at him like he was crazy, but then moved to follow. They made it a three steps when, predictably, Javon said, “Wait. I’ll tell you what, children. I’ll give you one chance to catch my attention. Succeed, and we’ll do business. Fail, and my men will give you a thrashing for the disrespect.”

Ambrose turned around, a wide smile on his face. “Now we’re talking. But let’s up the ante. Make it a private conference, with drinks. If you’re half the man your reputation says you are, you’re going to love this.”

Raeva gave him another nervous look, but this was exactly the kind of thing he was comfortable handling. One didn’t make alliances and deals with other great houses without knowing how to seize someone’s attention and get them to dance to your tune. Gambling was a universal language, especially for the old, wealthy, and bored.

A servant took their pads of paper with their order and ushered them into a comfortable sitting room in the back of the shop with plush armchairs. They were poured wine, and left alone just long enough for Raeva to hiss, “What the hell are you thinking?”

“I’m thinking that this is an opportunity to get information and maybe swindle him out of some cards. Would you just trust me? You’re my arm candy-slash-muscle.”

“You wish,” she said, but the door opened and Javon came in.

He motioned for them to sit and took his seat behind an enormous desk made of smooth, polished wood. It had to have been brought in from off-world. “Alright. My time is precious and your cheek is the only thing giving you a chance. What could you possibly show me that I’ve never seen before?”

Raeva shot him another look, but Ambrose was committed. He smiled and spread his hands and said, “I am in possession of a Wildcard with a unique property to it. A few unique properties to it, with events I can’t explain.”

Javon swirled a goblet of wine around. “Go on.”

Ambrose took a deep breath and manifested his card between his fingers, holding it out to be read.

[Oops All Imps: Legendary Enhancement. Unique. All your spells become imps]

“Huh. Okay, I can honestly say I’ve not seen many Wildcards, let alone something like this.” He chewed over his words carefully. “What the hell does that mean, all your spells become imps?”

“Just what it sounds like,” said Ambrose, taking a sip of his own drink before setting it down. “All my spells become imps that are still spells. The spells go off, sometimes multiple times, but they’re attached to an imp body. But that’s not all.”

“Ambrose,” Raeva said. “You shouldn’t be telling him this. We can’t trust him.”

Javon held up a hand. His eyes glittered with clear interest now. “Whatever your concern, I am above the petty politicking and backstabbing between factions. All I care about is my collection and learning more about cards. You have my attention, for the moment. What else is there?”

He drew another card from his deck, producing it to view but not quite unequipping it. This time he pulled out [Umbral Imp] and held it up.

[Umbral Imp: Common. Summons a hazy, shady imp at your side, capable of causing havoc without ever being seen.

Special: Stealth. This creature starts out stealthed until it attacks or uses an ability.

Special: Once every other round, this creature can create a fifteen foot radius magical cloud of darkness centered around it for fifteen seconds, blocking all sight and muffling sound.

Strength: D. Defense: D. Speed: C. Magic: B. 1 mana]

“This wasn’t originally an imp card. I equipped a spell called [Darkness], and it became this. Not only does my Wildcard affect the cards I have equipped, but it changes new additions to the deck. Among other things. And it presents you with a unique opportunity.”

It was yet another gamble, but Ambrose wanted those cards on the list. He handed over the [Umbral Imp], sliding it across the desk. Javon took it and looked at it.

“Okay? Now what? What is the opportunity?” It didn’t matter. They had him now, hook, line, and sinker.

“Look,” said Ambrose. “And think very carefully about what you see there. That’s a creature with a strong magic effect without an ongoing cost. I can equip cards of up to Level 4. Which means that I can turn your Shadow spells into imps with spell effects on them. You want something unique to offer the world, Mr. Javon? I can give you that. But not for free.”

Finally, Raeva seemed impressed rather than upset. It was a risk, putting all of this out in the open, but they needed to get stronger, and they needed it fast. With any luck, they’d be off the world before the information could be traced back to them. It all hinged on cooperation from a man Ambrose knew he’d impressed. The question was how much the old man wanted to try to hide it.

“Who are you?” Javon finally asked. “You aren’t far enough along to be a full agent, but maybe you aren’t merely an arrogant child.”

“Oh no,” said Raeva, “he is. Very much so.”

“You have room to talk.” Ambrose shot back.

Javon laughed harshly. “Okay. You know what? I don’t care who you are. You’ve offered me an intriguing opportunity. Give me a couple of days, and I’ll mull it over. Where are you staying?”

They traded a shocked, desperate look. “We’re only in town for one day, I’m afraid,” said Ambrose. “It’s now or never, I’m afraid. We need these cards for the mission we’re on. We’d tell you more if we could, but you know how it is among the governors and their agents. Give us these thirteen cards, and I will turn any thirteen Shadow cards I can equip into imps for you.”

“There’s just one problem,” said the old man, after a long several seconds of silence. “They’re just imps. No one who wants power is going to care about a small, annoying creature who gets killed easily. Part of power is prestige and appearance. Imps have neither.”

To his surprise, Raeva spoke up on his behalf. “Maybe, but you know what Shadow wizards care about most of all? Whether or not something works and is efficient. I guarantee you that people will pay far more than the worth of the cards we’re asking for. Besides, don’t you want to see what happens? Get people talking about where you possibly got them?”

He laughed and drained his wine. “If you aren’t here to make more, then that loses some of its luster.” His protests were weak now.

“So you can charge a premium for them, knowing they’re unique. C’mon, Mr. Javon. You know you want this, at least as much as we do.”

The smile on his face said they’d won, but he still fought them for another couple of minutes while they cajoled and flattered him. It was all part of the game, and Javon became incredibly helpful then. His servants fetched the fourteen cards they’d asked for, while he presented a long line of rare and uncommon spells to imp-ify.

At first, there was the real fear that it wouldn’t work right this time. The Wildcard’s effects had been anything but predictable, and there was no guarantee it would work right, just because he really, really wanted it to.

But it did.

And for the next fifteen minutes, Ambrose equipped and unequipped Shadow spells of all varieties. Cards to discard, to kill creatures, to draw more cards at the cost of one’s life, to reanimate other creatures or steal them from another’s discard. Four levels wasn’t quite half the range of an Affinity’s power, but it was close enough.

“I trust these are satisfactory?” Ambrose asked, holding up a card.

[Stinging Imp: Common. An annoying, caustic imp good at picking away at larger foes.

Special: Weakening Bite. All bites contain a venom that lowers the strength of its prey.

Special: Flying.

Strength: C. Defense: C. Speed: B. Magic: B. 2 Mana]

It wasn’t an especially strong creature, but it would continuously weaken whatever it attacked. It was like that with all of the changed cards, with the exception of the cards that he and Raeva had chosen. He had three new cards now, though he hadn’t equipped them yet.

“Yes, yes, I do believe you’ve lived up to your part of the bargain,” Javon said, rubbing his hands together. “And if you should ever find yourself in the area again…?”

“Then we know exactly where to go to find a gentleman with a discerning eye,” said Raeva, laying it on a bit thick, but they were all in a good mood at this point.

“Very true. A pleasure doing business, Mr. and Ms. Smith. The servants will see you out.”

They walked out triumphantly, noting how much the line had grown since they’d caught the proprietor’s attention. Several people shot them dirty looks. Ambrose wiggled his fingers in greeting. He turned away and focused on his haul. Three cards in particular stood out to him. Two he’d chosen, and one Raeva had found for him.

[Spell Snipe: Rare. Take aim and lash out at an enemy’s spirit, taking out one of their spells.

Spell Effect: Go through target’s deck and deactivate a card of your choice. Then deactivate this card. 3 mana]

It was good on its own, but had synergy both with [Thought Tap] and the other card he found.

[Desecrate Memory: Rare. Dig through your foe’s refuse and turn their spells against them.

Spell Effect: Cast a deactivated spell from an opponent’s deck for free, and then deactivate this spell. 3 Mana]

He would need to do them on different turns, but if he could get it going, he’d be able to steal individual spells from their deck, turning their own strengths against one another. Ambrose immediately equipped them both, taking out [Mark of Service] after its strange interaction with his Wildcard. He didn’t want to risk that again.

They became two new cards.

[Spiritsnipe Imp: Rare. A spiteful imp, taking aim at specific cards.

Special: On a successful attack of a wizard, execute this creature to go through their deck and deactivate one of their cards.

Special: Flying.

Strength: C. Defense: C. Speed: B. Magic: A. 3 Mana]

It was about what he expected, honestly, and gave him home for the next one.

[Desecration Imp: Rare. A scavenger imp, violating the minds of those he attacks.

Special: On successful attack of a wizard, cast a deactivated card from their deck for free, once per round. 3 Mana.]

It was even better than he expected, and would be dangerous as hell. His deck now leaned more towards expensive spells, which he’d need to watch out for, but it was a little closer to being something he could be proud of, something he could use comfortably. Forget graduation, this was the single-greatest power up he’d had since getting the Wildcard.

That just left the card Raeva found.

[Devil Form: Rare. Embrace the darkness within and take on the form of a lethal, winged demon.

Special: Avatar. The caster transforms into a powerful war form, at the cost of needing to kill to sustain yourself. Lasts 3 rounds.

Special: Demonic hunger. At the start of each round, if an enemy hasn’t been slain, one of your creatures is killed to feed the Demon.

Special: Persist. This card remains in your hand until used.

Strength: A. Defense: C. Speed: B. Magic: B. 5 Mana]

It was intriguing, and it would cover his weakness in battle. But it was also Affinity Level 5, which meant it was likely not equippable until he spent more time at his current level.. And after accidentally binding Tirgahl, he was terrified of using Avatar cards. But…It looked like it would be perfect for his use. He’d always wondered what it was like to be strong himself, instead of just cunning.

And handsome, and smart, and all the rest.

They left Javon’s Emporium, pockets full of cards and feeling pretty smug. It lasted until they saw Luthor standing nearby, as tense as could be. His normally stoic face looked near a panic.

“We have a problem,” he said.

Ambrose cursed. Of course they did.
