Chapter 22: Essence Crafting

Every breath felt like I was swallowing fire. Each inhalation sent fresh waves of agony through my chest, as if my lungs were being scraped raw with broken glass. Over the years I had come to understand that there were many different flavors of weakness. Some people, for example, would simply give up when presented with such a challenge as I was currently undergoing, if they even made it this far at all.

The pain would be too much for them. They would beg for death, gladly. Curl up in a corner and wait for the burning to stop, one way or another. It was an understandable response. Human, even. Most people weren't built to endure this kind of suffering and keep functioning.

But I also found that people could discover strength they didn't know they had if you pressed them into a corner, gave them no other option but to fight for life, or die. Desperation was a hell of a motivator. When your back was against the wall and there was nowhere left to retreat, you either found something inside yourself you didn't know existed, or you broke completely.

My lungs burned with every breath, and the pain of it caused my eyes to sting with tears as I stared down at the leather-bound book. The words swam on the page, blurring together as my vision wavered. I blinked hard, forcing myself to focus. There was work to do.

There was no other choice for me. Read the book, learn to craft, create the antidote, or die choking on crystallized poison. Strength, or weakness. Death or life. Power or powerlessness. These were the choices I now faced day by day, stripped down to their most basic elements.

Why wouldn't I choose power? Any reasonable person would make the same choice when confronted with such stark alternatives.

Except not really. Most reasonable people would want nothing to do with this. Most people, when confronted with whether or not they should kill a sobbing woman in front of them, wouldn't. They'd find another way, or die trying to find one.

I didn't choose power because the other choices were to be powerless. That was the lie I told myself, the comfortable fiction that let me sleep at night. I did it because I wanted to, because I was finding I was good at this kind of thing. Because there was something in me that responded to violence and suffering with hunger rather than revulsion.

And in that moment, struggling to breathe in a hotel room that shouldn't exist, I wasn't fighting just because I didn't want to die. I wanted to conquer this challenge. To overcome it and prove my strength, to myself and to whatever force had set this trial in motion.

So, I pushed through the pain. Not because it was easy, not because I came to some sudden realization about myself that made everything clear. Not because I embraced the suffering and became one with it like some enlightened master.

No, I pushed through it out of sheer will to survive, doing my best to control my breathing, to order my thoughts despite the fire in my chest. To focus on the task ahead instead of the agony that threatened to overwhelm me.

The Flux helped a bit, that familiar warmth spreading through my body when I pulled on the rage and pain that had become my constant companions. The memory of losing my wife and son, the thought of Elisa waiting for me somewhere in this hostile universe. It all helped channel the power that kept me functional, but it did not take away the burning entirely.

Nothing would, until I fixed this myself.

Essence Crafting was an art in a way, as the book described it. Straightforward in concept, but complicated in its execution. This was how the tome presented the discipline, and I found the description to be accurate as I struggled through the first chapters.

Reading was slow when pain made it hard to focus. Each page was a battle, forcing my eyes to track across text that seemed to shift and blur with every labored breath. But gradually, the principles began to make sense.

I learned that certain materials, ingredients, and even Flux Beasts contained aspects of the Resonance Flux within them. The art was in drawing out those aspects, purifying them, combining them in precise ratios to achieve specific effects. In some cases this involved crushing components, or heating them up, or freezing them, even butchering living creatures, depending on what you were dealing with.

The process wasn't just mechanical, though. A lot of this involved sensing the aspect through the Resonance Flux, feeling the way different materials resonated with different aspects of reality. It was a process that wasn't always easy if you meditated on only a few aspects of the Flux.

A Nyxen, for example, would likely only be able to process primal, void, and shadow aspects effectively. They'd trained their minds to resonate with those particular frequencies of power, but that specialization came at a cost.

Other aspects would feel almost alien to a Nyxen. Like trying to hear music in a language you'd never learned, the patterns would be there but the meaning would remain elusive.

The precision required was daunting. Too much of a certain aspect could change a creation entirely, turning something helpful into something harmful. Health potions, for example, needed a certain amount of vital aspect to promote healing, but use too much and it might become an energy potion instead. Or worse, something that accelerated biological processes until they burned out entirely.

The antidote I needed was fairly simple in theory, according to the book. I needed a void crystal to break down the crystallized poison in my lungs, a vital herb to promote healing of the damaged tissue, and mercury to bind the components together and carry them through my system. The book assured me that ingredients like these would be available in the rooms beyond the hotel.

I looked up from the pages, breathing labored, like I was trying to suck in air through a wet cloth. There were two doors that led out across from me, besides the one I had entered through. One was painted a deep green that reminded me of forest shadows, the other was a stark white that seemed to glow with its own inner light.

However, I had a problem that the book made abundantly clear. I couldn't craft the antidote yet. The recipe required at least apprentice level skill in Essence Crafting, and I was starting from nothing. No experience, no muscle memory, no understanding of how the Flux responded to different materials.

Getting there would take time. Precious time that I didn't have much of. Seven days was the deadline, and I'd already lost hours just reading and understanding the basics. I needed to start practicing immediately, building the skills that would eventually save my life.

I decided to start with something simple. A resonance potion. According to the book, it would increase my Resonance attribute by a single point for a short duration, but more importantly, the recipe used pure Flux aspects that were easier to work with than the more complex combinations required for healing items.

The process was described as ridiculously simple to create. Some Flux-infused water, brought to a boil, add some sage, a pure Flux-aspected herb, and let it sit for a few minutes. The book insisted that even a complete novice should be able to manage such a basic recipe.

I didn't think I could mess it up. Famous last words, probably, but I had to start somewhere.

Moving while pain gnawed at my lungs, burning in my chest like a coal that had taken up permanent residence, only increased the agony. Every step sent jolts through my respiratory system, every movement reminded me of how little time I had left.

But I did it anyway. Movement was life. Staying still was surrender

Through the first door, the green one, I found myself in a dark cave that defied the hotel room's modern aesthetic completely. The transition was jarring, like stepping from one world into another. A thin stream of light came from a source I couldn't see, illuminating the area with a soft, ethereal glow that reflected off the black rock like shining obsidian.

The cave was larger than it had any right to be, given the size of the hotel room I'd left behind. Impossible geometry seemed to be a common theme in this place, as if normal physics were more like suggestions than laws.

A small pond rippled gently in the center of the space, water kissing the stone with soft sounds that echoed off the walls. The surface acted as a shifting mirror, reflecting the strange light in patterns that made my eyes water if I looked too long.

Soil in small alcoves glistened with moisture, and a rich, earthy scent permeated the air. Herbs sprouted up from the dark earth, their leaves catching the light in ways that suggested they were more than ordinary plants. I could feel the Flux energy radiating from them, different aspects calling to different parts of my awareness.

I gathered the sage, identified by the purple leaves that seemed to pulse with their own inner light, and filled several glass vials I had brought from the hotel room with the pond water. The liquid felt alive in my hands, warm despite the cool air of the cave, thrumming with barely contained energy.

The sage felt strange between my fingers, more substantial than normal plants, as if it existed in more dimensions than I could perceive. When I touched it, I could feel the pure Flux aspect it contained, clean and uncomplicated compared to the more complex energies I'd sensed in other materials.

Back in the hotel room, I set down the vials of water and began hunting through the furniture and cabinets for crafting supplies. The room seemed to anticipate my needs, revealing equipment as I searched for it. I found a small cauldron made of some dark metal that felt warm to the touch, and a portable burner that looked like it belonged in a chemistry lab rather than a hotel.

The burner plugged into the wall with a cord that definitely hadn't been there when I'd first entered the room. Another example of this place adapting to my needs, or maybe revealing tools that had always been there but hidden until I was ready for them.

I knew from the book that I would probably need some other way to start fires at some point, proper flame rather than electric heat, but for now I had a way to control temperature with the precise controls on the burner. The dial showed settings for different types of heat, from gentle warming to intense boiling.

It was time to get to work. Time to take my first real step down the path of Essence Crafting, using skills I'd never developed to save a life I was still learning to value properly.

I filled the cauldron with the Flux-infused water, watching as it began to heat. The liquid didn't behave quite like normal water, staying perfectly clear even as bubbles began to form along the bottom. The bubbles themselves glowed faintly, carrying traces of the Flux energy contained within.

As the water reached a rolling boil, I added the sage leaves one by one, watching as they dissolved into the liquid with surprising speed. The water changed color immediately, shifting from clear to a deep purple that swirled with currents of silver light.

The smell that rose from the cauldron was unlike anything I'd experienced before. Clean and sharp, like mountain air mixed with lightning. It made my damaged lungs work a little easier, just breathing in the vapor.

I let it sit for exactly three minutes, as the book specified, then carefully poured the finished potion into one of the empty vials. The liquid continued to glow softly, pulsing in rhythm with my heartbeat.

You have created an initiate level Resonance Potion. This potion gives +1 to your Resonance for 3 minutes.

According to the book, I should drink it immediately while it was still warm. I raised the vial to my lips, hesitating for just a moment. After what had happened with the poison, I was understandably cautious about consuming unknown liquids.

But this was different. This was something I had made, something I understood, at least partially. I drank it down in one gulp.

The effect was immediate. I could feel my connection to the Flux strengthen, the warmth spreading through my body with renewed vigor. My breathing eased slightly, not because the poison was gone but because my enhanced Resonance was helping me cope with its effects.

More importantly, I felt a sense of accomplishment. I had successfully crafted my first Essence item. It was simple, basic, probably something any child could manage with proper instruction. But it was mine.

And it was the first step toward saving my own life.

I opened the book to the next chapter, ready to learn more complex recipes. The poison in my lungs continued to burn, but now it felt less like certain death and more like a challenge to be overcome.

I had work to do. And hope I could save my life before time ran out.
