Risha watched the city with growing dislike as she walked through it.

On the streets, hungry and sick creatures begged for scraps, racial castes slowly becoming clearer to her eyes.

Far more demons were servants, beggars, and laborers than any of the other races. She spotted several strange chairs holding Dark Elves and Dwarves, carried by Demons, and never occupied by a Demon. Humans and shades were not as common as the others, but those she did see wore simple clothing, and the occasional weapon. Merchants and delvers if she were to guess.

Klu and Klaz’zks followed Risha and Siyah, and their strangeness drew plenty of looks. Risha had only seen one other orc in the city, and Klaz’zks looked closest to a human with her legs hidden from sight, but the humans here had a much lighter skin tone.

They walked down the stone street, carriages passing them by, most of them on their way to the large castle in the distance. The merchants here were less pushy, working from specialized shops.

Risha paused as she spotted yet another demon on the ground, holding up a cup for spare coins. He sat on a dirty blanket, and scars covered his arms. His right foot was mangled, black and green from lack of healing. Siyah followed her gaze, and her eyes hardened. But she didn’t move, didn’t do anything.

Risha looked at the demoness, anger stirring in her heart “You want to help him, don’t you?”

“What can I do for them?” The demoness voice broke, and Risha’s anger faded.

Risha grabbed the woman, and forced Siyah’s eyes to meet Risha’s. “Everything.”

Risha let the demoness go, then walked up to the begging demon. She stopped, and the demon looked up to the taller goblin.

“What’s your name?” Risha asked.

The demon lowered its gaze in fear, then paused as its eyes caught Siyah walking up behind Risha.

He looked between the two, then answered, “My father granted me the name of Fohral.”

Risha sat down across from the demon, cross-legged. “I am Risha.”  She looked over to Siyah.

The woman hesitated, then sat down as well. “My mother gifted me the name of Siyah.”

The beggar shifted awkwardly, “The darkness treat you kindly, sister.”

Siyah relaxed at the greeting and responded, “And the light watch over you, Fohral.”

He looked down, his hands shifting with nervousness, “I have nothing to greet you with, or food to gift, or-”

“It’s alright, Fohral.” Siyah said, gently laying her hand on the nervous demon’s arm. He stilled, then looked up at her with watery eyes, “You’ve done out people proud by not forgetting our customs.”

The demon glanced at Risha, then looked back to the more familiar demoness, “Why do you sit with me, sister?”

Siyah looked at Risha, but Risha just stared back, waiting.

Siyah looked down, took a breath, then met the demon beggar’s eyes again. “You haven’t been healed.”

Fohral looked at his mangled foot, “No. No one can.”

“Risha.” Siyah’s voice was hard, an edge coming to it that made Risha smile. “Can you heal him?”

Risha let [Mother’s Care] extend from her, and the Demon gasped.

Poison? Poison. It rots his spirit. Igni spoke with anger as Risha’s magic washed over the injured demon. It stops the healing, it eats him. Mother, do you wish Igni to burn it away?

Risha nodded, and the spirit left her, rushing into the demon.

Siyah jumped as green and black steam burned away from the mangled foot. A power came from the earth, seeking to stop Igni.

“Nyx.” Risha called to her other spirit, and Nyx answered, meeting whatever dared to defy the Mother Of Monsters.

Risha’s spirit drained from her, and her teeth showed for the few seconds of spiritual battle she fought.

Then it was over, and her spirits returned to her. [Mother’s Care] healed the foot, leaving a simple scar where once there’d been nothing but bloat and tissue.

The demon blinked, his hands stilling.

“What was that?” Siyah asked.

Risha frowned, looking at the demon beggar, “Where did you injure your foot?”

The demon backed away from her gaze, and Risha paused.

“Sorry.” Risha closed her eyes, “Risha’s anger isn’t with you.”

“I-it’s okay. Thank you, goblin. A demon gave it to me. One touched by the madness.”

Risha opened her eyes, then looked at Siyah to see if she knew what “the madness” was. Siyah looked just as confused as her.

Sensing their confusion, the demon continued with fervor, “It comes from the depths, starting as a sickness and turning into something that drives them mad.” He looked at the two women, hoping they would be pleased with his knowledge.

Risha had another question to ask the Delver’s guild it seemed.

But for now, Risha looked at Siyah. There was a lesson here that she wanted to teach the young demoness, “What now, Siyah?”

“What now?” The demoness repeated.

“He’s healed. Is that all?”

Siyah looked at the demon, starved and scarred. Her eyes hardened as she stood up. Then she lowered her hand. “We could use some help, Fohran. Do you think you could guide us. We can pay you?” She glanced at Risha for confirmation.

Risha nodded. She could spend a few shinies for this.

The demon hurriedly nodded, then pushed himself up on his healed leg. “Where do you want to go…” He trailed off as he caught sight of Klu and Klaz’zks behind them, then refocused on Siyah.

“The Delver’s guild.” Siyah said.

The demon beggar nodded, then turned to lead them away.

Risha waited until he was out of earshot, then spoke quietly. “You have the power to help them, and if you don’t, you have to do everything to achieve that power. You can’t turn a blind eye to your people.”

Risha didn’t wait for a response as she followed the demon. There were more like him, in the corners of the street, hidden in the shadows. She let [Mother’s Care] flow over them, then paused as she detected more of that strange poison. She couldn’t afford to draw attention by fighting it on so many fronts, so she forced herself to let it go.

But she would be back.

Whatever caused this, was insidious. And she did not tolerate those who caused this kind of pain.

The demon led them through the merchants district to a large tavern-like building with a crossed sword and shield carved into its side.

He paused, then turned to Siyah. “This is it.”

“Do you have some free time?” Siyah asked.

The beggar nodded.

“Then stay with us, we need a guide for the day.”

Fohral straightened, his eyes tearing up. Siyah looked away to not embarrass the creature, but Risha watched the tears fall from the simple words.

Risha turned from the two to push the doors open. Inside, armored and armed creatures of all types sat at tables, drank, laughed, and talked.

A desk was tucked away in the back, where a handsome demoness sat on a stool, turning the pages of a book she was reading.

Voices quieted as their strange party walked into the guild. Even here, it was strange to see a High Goblin and an Orc, not to mention the regal figure of Klaz’zks, and the broken horns of Siyah.

Thankfully, they did not stare long, and returned to their conversations.

Risha walked towards the woman in the back, who still hadn’t looked up from her book.

They paused at the desk and the woman turned the page one last time, before placing a simple feather on her spot and closing it.

Risha caught the faintest glimpse of tight, hand-written notes of a journal of some kind before the book was placed behind the desk.

The demoness looked them over, her gaze settling on Risha for the longest before moving onto Klaz’zks and frowning slightly. Klu made her look puzzled, then finally a brief flash of anger covered her face as she noticed the broken horns of Siyah.

“Welcome to the Delver’s Guild. I am Erin. How can I help you…?” She paused, her gaze looking past them for the briefest second before all expression left her face.

She leapt over the desk, and walked past them as a figure entered the building.

He wore a tattered cloak, two horns poking out from under his hood, and he stood at around the same height as Risha.

Erin stopped several feet away from him. “Dam-”

“You’re making a scene, Erin.” A tired humor was in his voice. “And you ignored your customers, that’s not like you.”

Risha stared at him, a primordial fear going through her as she caught a glimpse of his spirit.

Ancient, and terrifying, born of fire, stone and shadow. It was like the spirit of a Spark had caught fire and burned the world in its fury.

Then it was gone, and a simple young-looking demon stood in its place.

He walked past Erin, and sat at a table nearby. Erin tried to follow him, but he motioned towards Risha’s group. “We’ll talk later. That young goblin feels important, you should probably speak with her.”

Erin glanced over to Risha with a curious look. She approached them quickly as an aura of professionalism settled over her. Her gaze settled on Risha, and she bowed low. “Greetings to the High Goblin and her party. The Mother granted me the name of Erin. How can I help you?”

“I am Risha. I have questions about the Madness that’s been spreading here.”

“Madness?”

Risha jumped, and Klu reached for his sword as the young cloaked demon appeared next to Risha.

“What does she mean by madness, Erin?”

Erin glanced at the demon with annoyance, unsurprised by his sudden appearance.  “That’s part of the reason I called you. Something’s not right in the depths.”

The cloaked demon looked at Erin, then back to Risha, a smile spreading across his face, clear despite the other features of his face being hidden by shadows. “Maybe we can all talk then.”

He motioned for them to follow him back to his table then paused, “Ah, I almost forgot, I’m terrible at all the politeness. What’s your names?”

“Klu.”

“I am Klaz’zks.”

“My mother granted me the name of Siyah.” Siyah’s answer drew Damien’s gaze for a long moment.

Damien hummed, then walked over to table, and sat down. He tilted his chair back as he observed them all. “The First granted me the name of Damien, but you can call me Brother Damien.”
