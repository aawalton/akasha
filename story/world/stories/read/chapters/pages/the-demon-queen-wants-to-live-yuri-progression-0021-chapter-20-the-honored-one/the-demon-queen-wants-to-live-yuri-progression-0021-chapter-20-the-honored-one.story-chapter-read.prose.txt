"So," Ash said, stepping over a root, "what exactly is the plan? I hope you have one."

We had been walking for some time. The village was well past us by now, and with it, the hut and the mother and the boy I had failed to heal. "The barrier around the boy’s mind is the System’s power. Its decision," I said. "My Cradle cannot breach it and I have tried. This power only creates."

"You only used the Cradle," Ash said. She had been watching closely. "Why not try the other one then? The Requiem? That’s why we’re here, aren’t we?"

A fair question, if an obvious one. "If I had tried the Requiem as I am now, it would have killed him."

Ash glanced at me. Perhaps the Hero wondered if I was exaggerating. I was not. The Requiem was not an ending. It was the ending. If I had turned it loose on that barrier, the decay would have spread past the barrier and into the boy’s mind.

"So you held back," Ash said.

An odd way to frame it. "I held back, yes."

Ash nodded slowly. "Should I applaud your restraint?"

"You should need no reason to applaud me," I said. "My existence alone was enough, once."

"Sometimes I wonder how you can even walk straight with a head that big."

"With applause," I said. Ash laughed, and the sound of it carried through the otherwise quiet forest. I found myself smirking, before I forced the treacherous expression away.

"So you’re here to…what? Practice until you can break that barrier? How can you be sure it’s even possible?" Ash asked when she’d settled down. "To use this power on him and not…do what you said it might. It seems like a risk, and a big one at that."

I extended my right hand toward the nearest tree. A flicker of black fire left my fingers, crossed the short distance, and kissed the bark. The wood greyed where it struck, a patch no larger than my palm. The rest of the tree stood untouched. "Because I know what this power’s nature is," I said simply. "In my time, the System would have classified it as a type of [Concept Magic]. That was the highest order of power for my Class. The Requiem does not merely decay matter, though I suppose some other fool with this power would have only used it so."

Ash stared at the grey patch on the tree. "Concept Magic, huh," she repeated.

"Yes. And before you ask why you have not heard of it, it is because you are a Hero. Heroes hit things with swords and aura. Anything else is lost on them. After all, there was no base intelligence requirement for the Class. Perhaps a Sage or a Saint might have known this already."

Ash gave me a look that I had come to recognize. It was the one she used when she wanted to hit me and had decided not to.

We walked more, my eyes scanned the tree line. I had little doubt Ash was doing the same, and could grudgingly accept that the woman’s senses were likely keener than my own. It was some time before Ash spoke again, and when she did, it sounded like a question she had deliberated on for days.

"Answer me this then…you were the strongest Demon Queen we ever faced. I don’t think there’s much use denying that." She chewed on something. "Why?"

That was not the question I had expected. I turned to her and raised an eyebrow. "Is that not obvious?"

"Your magic could destroy just about anything from all accounts," Ash continued. "With it, you killed two of the Gods -Calithra and Calith. None of the Queens before you could have managed anything like that from everything I was taught. Why you and not them?"

I turned the question back to her. "Why do you think?"

Ash considered for a moment. "Because you must have had powers the ones before you didn’t. Maybe you found something or-"

"Wrong. Every Demon Queen shared the same Class," I said. Ash was looking at me now. "The same abilities were available to all of us. The same spells, and the same potential. If I unlocked more of those abilities than my predecessors, that does not mean they could not have. The ceiling and floor were both the same."

Soon after, we reached a clearing. I chose to stop there, and Ash followed a moment later. Ash sighed. "Alright. I don’t know the answer, so just tell me."

She assumed I would then? Well, it wasn’t like the answer mattered now. "There were rules to the powers of our time. A spell's power was affected by several things, but all of them could be measured easily by the System. Mine was different. It responded to intent and will. Two people with the same spell could produce different results based on what they meant to do, and who they were." I paused, if only to make sure she could follow where my words led. Heroes were known to be thickheaded. "The class of magic reads the caster. It always has."

Ash stared at me. "Are you saying what I think you’re saying? That you’re…." She trailed off.

I found myself smirking. "I was not the strongest because I was Lysanthia," I said. "I was Lysanthia because I was the strongest."

There was a beat of silence. Ash closed her eyes. "Your ego grows bigger every day."

"It is not ego if it is simply the truth. I would think a Hero would know that."

"It’s ego."

"Very well. Then my ego is also the truth, which makes it the finest ego in existence."

The Hero sighed and shook her head, and I thought I saw her lips twitch. "What’s the plan then?"

"Simple," I said. "You will catch beasts and I will practice on them."

The Hero’s expression went flat. "You want me to be your huntress."

"I want you to do the thing you are good at so that I may do the thing I am good at. This is called delegation. I did it often, in my time."

Ash stared at me for a long moment. Then she drew her sword and walked deeper into the trees without another word. I sat down, started drawing in all of the ambient mana I could. Experience told me this would be a near thing.

The first creature was one of those long eared vermin I now knew to call a rabbit. Ash brought it back in her hands, holding it by the scruff. It kicked and twisted, but the Hero’s grip was absolute. She set it on the ground and kept one hand on its back. "Whenever you’re ready," she said.

I knelt and extended my right hand. I stared at the creature, and just for a moment, I hesitated. What are you doing, you fool?

I closed my eyes, channeled mana into the Black Line. The Requiem answered me at once and Ruin followed. I reached for restraint -trying not to feed the Requiem too much power. Wisps of black fire shot from my fingers.

The impression came. The thing was afraid. Deeply afraid of what was to come to it. But fear wasn't all the beast had. Some part of it had known this day would always come. That did not make the acceptance any easier. I forced the impression away even as the rabbit turned to dust. As if the rabbit had never been at all.

I suspected if I had used more mana, there would not have been dust left. Ash looked at the dust. She looked at me. She wiped her hand against her leg slowly, as if the dust still clung. Perhaps it did.

"That was too much." I said.

"You think?" Something in her voice was hard.

I did not appreciate her tone. "Bring me another."

Ash sighed and went back into the trees. It was some time before she came back. And when she did, she held a rabbit far larger than the last had been. I suspected that was on purpose. I knelt beside the creature again.

I tried to use the Requiem more sparingly this time. In my mind, I imagined a giant fist closing around the flow of power, letting only a small wisp of it through. It worked. What left my hand was only the barest wisp of black. The decay spread from the point of contact across the rabbit’s flank. It moved slower this time. The fur greyed and withered even as we watched. After half a minute, the rabbit started to convulse. I tried to pull the power back, to tell it where to stop.

I could not control the power. Even as I watched, it kept spreading, turning more and more of the creature grey. Eventually, the rabbit went still altogether. I stared at the body, for it was a body now. Closer. I had been closer. The difference between the first attempt and this one was measurable, even if the result was identical.

"Bring me another," I said.

"Lysanthia."

"Another, Ash."

Ash went. She returned some time later with a third rabbit, and when she set it down, she was more gentle than she had been with the last two.

This time I tried to aim for a single point on the creature, just its left leg. If I could contain the decay to a single limb, then I could contain it to a single structure inside a limb, and from there, to a barrier inside a mind. That was how training these types of powers always worked. Or at least, that was how it had worked a long time ago.

A wisp of Requiem hit the leg and the fur greyed. The skin beneath cracked and withered. I had to contain the power here, and I willed it not to spread. I felt the Requiem fight me, but for a single moment, it obeyed. In the next, the decay spread past the joint, and the rabbit died. I was kneeling in front of another dead thing.

The result was the same, but the path to get there was different. I focused on that. Each of my attempts had brought me closer to the control I required. And I could see the issue clearly. The Requiem was a raging flame that wanted to consume. It was no wonder it resisted the fine control I needed of it.

That was the nature of destruction, and for a very long time, that had been my nature too. There had been one benefit to all of this.

It was getting easier and easier to ignore the impressions that came with this power. One could ignore a thing one had come to so thoroughly expect. An unintended benefit, but I would accept it.

It was some time before Ash brought another creature. What she brought was a beast I had never seen before, one Ash called a thorncat. The beast itself was lean and small, with a ridge made entirely of sharp quills along its spine. It snarled when Ash set it down, and it snarled louder when I knelt beside it. It might have bitten, had the Hero not held it so firm.

A creature with some fight in it. Good. A larger body meant more resistance. Perhaps that would help it against the decay.

This time, I used only a small amount of Ruin, no more than a single thread of it from my index finger. I aimed it at one of the quills on the beast. A moment later, and it greyed. The thorncat flinched and twisted, but Ash held it firm. I watched the grey patch and held my breath.

The decay was exactly where I had aimed it and went no further. The thorncat lived. It snarled and snapped its teeth at me, which I took as confirmation.

"Well…that’s progress," Ash said.

"It is insufficient progress," I corrected. "What I did was simple, since I only touched something on the beast's outside. To touch something inside the beast will be harder, and the barrier will be harder still. The barrier is a concept. I need to reach inside a living thing, and then reach inside the inside itself."

Ash released the thorncat and caught it again when it tried to bolt. She held it steady and looked at me. There was a question in her eyes. "Well? Do it," she said.

I reached again. This time, the Requiem's thread went deeper. I could feel the creature’s life around the line of black. Muscle and sinew, blood and bone. Other things the creature was. Things the creature would be, given time. The Requiem wanted to consume all of it. Every fiber of this power strained outward, reaching for more, as a flame reaches for air.

I closed my mental hand around the flame and directed it. I directed it towards a single bone in the creature's long tail. One I could decay without killing the beast itself. My power obeyed, and the bone cracked. The thorncat let out a yelp and Ash's jaw tightened. I pulled my power back, and I waited.

Ash checked the creature. It snapped at her fingers, though she seemed not to feel it. "The tail is broken, but the break is clean. Everything else is fine, Lysanthia." She looked up at me.

I stared at the thorncat.

The thing was hurt and doubtless quite upset, but it was still alive.

The Cradle pulsed on my left arm. It was a stupid choice, when I needed every drop of mana to spare. I made it anyway and extended my left hand. Wisps of white flame poured from my palm and into the creature. The bone knit, and the beast stopped its thrashing. Then it began to struggle again as the bone healed fully.

Ash released it. The thorncat bolted into the underbrush and was gone in seconds. Ash's shoulders dropped, just slightly. I assumed the creature had been difficult to hold.

I rose. My mana reserves were perhaps half of what they had been when we started, and I had already wasted much in my failed attempt with the boy. I flexed my hand. "I would like to try that once more. Bring me something again," I said.

We continued for a time. Ash brought each creature more quietly than the last. And through our many experiments, I started to understand why. The Hero did not want to be doing this. Did not want to subject more creatures to my power. Still, she did it anyway. Perhaps she wasn’t completely a fool.

The sun moved overhead, and the patches of light on the forest floor shifted from white to gold. My mana drained with each attempt but I was getting closer.

Ash brought one last creature. Where she was finding these different beasts, I did not know. She called this one a bristleback. It was dense, with a ridge of dark spines along its back, and it had bitten Ash twice before she had subdued it. The thing’s fur glistened wetly, and it had a scent to it that was simply…strange.

"This one has spirit," I observed.

"Just do it before it tries to bite me again." Ash said a touch too quickly.

I knelt and reached into the bristleback with my Ruin. It threaded past every organ the beast had. I was looking for something inside the inside. I had done this before, a thousand years ago. Though, never like this. Never with this precision. I almost didn’t find what I sought.

The creature was afraid. I could feel it through the Requiem. Its fear was a sharp thing, sitting somewhere neither mind nor body. This fear was a concept. That the world was dangerous, and that it was small. This was my target, the point of all these tests.

The beast went still as I touched that fear with the Requiem. It looked around and stared blankly at us, as if it too did not know what it had been so upset about. Its fear was gone. I had succeeded.

I had reached inside a living thing and unmade a single idea without touching anything around it.

I followed with the Cradle. A beast that did not know fear was rather unlikely to survive the night. Feeling it with the Cradle was much easier. It seemed this was the point of the impressions my powers gave me. It made it easier to touch far more than just the thing itself. The System had liked hints like that before too.

The creature was brave now, with its fear gone. It saw the world, and thought itself the greatest thing within it. Within that boldness, was a tiny seed of uncertainty. I poured the Cradle into that seed.

The bristleback's eyes sharpened. It tensed and started to thrash. Ash let it go, and it raced for the nearest line of trees. Ash watched it disappear. She watched it longer than she needed to.

"A rather charming creature," I said. This ‘bristleback’ would not have been out of place in the Demon Realm.

It was dark by the time we headed back. I saw the occasional glimmer of stars through the trees. It was strange to see them now as so many points of light. In the Demon Realm, the sky had been a bruise of red and black no matter the time of day.

Ash walked beside me in silence for a time. She straightened and looked at the sky. Then she sighed. "We’re too late," she said quietly. "The Inker returns tomorrow."

I did not break stride. "There is a reason I asked you to catch the creatures rather than doing it myself."

Ash glanced at me. "And what would that be?"

I flexed my right hand. "Do not tell me you think there was no reason. By having you handle the hunting, I conserved precisely enough to heal the boy tonight."

Ash stopped walking entirely, and I continued for two more steps before I turned. She was staring at me. Her eyes searched my face the way they had in the throne room, nine hundred and eighty four years ago. "You calculated that?" she asked. "You planned the entire day around conserving exactly enough mana to-"

I had not. I had hoped. That was all. I was sure I had enough power for what I planned to attempt, but only just enough. These Lines were draining an absurd amount of mana to do anything. It took all of my royal bearing to keep me from swaying on my feet. Ash did not need to know that.

I looked the Hero in the eye. "Yes," I said.

Ash held my gaze for a long moment. "Let’s go heal him, then."
