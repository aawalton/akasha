“General!”

The shout echoed through the quiet, book-lined library, startling Luke. He had been flipping through the worn pages of an old tome, enjoying a rare moment of leisure.

“What is it?” he asked, snapping the book shut with more force than intended.

He tried to mask the edge in his voice, but the sudden cry had caught him off guard.

It’s barely been half an hour... Have negotiations broken down already? he wondered, tension knotting in his chest.

But when Commander Bridges came into view, Luke’s unease began to fade. Despite the man’s disheveled uniform and weary face, a wide smile stretched across his features. His eyes shimmered with disbelief.

“The negotiations were a success!” Bridges announced breathlessly. He must have sprinted the entire way.

“We’ve been granted three hours to evacuate the fortress—but we’re not allowed to take any weapons.”

Luke exhaled softly, the tension finally bleeding from his shoulders. Still, he maintained a composed expression.

“Good,” he said with a measured nod. “I would’ve liked more time for the men to rest, but we’ll take what we can get.”

He rose from his seat.

“Gather your troops and meet inside the gates within the hour,” he instructed. “I want you to personally inspect each man. No hidden blades, no ‘souvenirs.’ If we break the agreement, the Selis General won’t hesitate to attack.”

“Yes, General!”

Bridges cupped his fist quickly and turned on his heel, disappearing down the hall with purpose.

Luke watched him go, then allowed a faint smile to tug at his lips.

A small victory, he mused. Hopefully it’s enough to complete the quest.

Shaking the thought aside, he turned back to the scattered tomes on the desk. His fingers resumed their practiced flipping as he dove once more into the world of ink and parchment.

It had been nearly two weeks since he’d last indulged his Scholarly Pursuit—and every day without consuming knowledge felt like time wasted. So long as there were unread books in this foreign land, he had no intention of stopping.

Unfortunately, most of the fortress’s collection had proven underwhelming. He’d hoped enemy territory might yield fresh insights into the Empire—but the texts merely echoed what he’d already studied back in Clayton.

There were the usual biases, of course—nationalistic slants and patriotic embellishments—but Luke had read enough propaganda to separate truth from bravado.

Still, disappointment gnawed at him.

Then, just as he was beginning to lose hope, his fingers stopped flipping through the pages.

His eyes widened.

Sun Tzu?

The title on the aged cover read The Legendary General—a detailed anthology of a man named Sun Tzu and his campaigns more than five centuries ago.

Luke’s mind whirred as he absorbed the dense, neatly written accounts. He hadn’t known much of Sun Tzu back on Earth—only whispers of the name and a few quotes passed around in war forums.

But here, within these pages, Sun Tzu was no myth. He had been a towering figure of history—a Qin General, no less. The very one credited with transforming the Qin Empire into a continental superpower.

At the height of his campaign, the man had led his armies to claim eighty percent of the continent.

Luke stood motionless, the book heavy in his hands, his thoughts racing.

This wasn’t just a piece of history. It was a revelation.

Luke couldn’t help but feel a surge of shock. But as he sat there, letting the words settle, the pieces of the puzzle began to fall into place.

The Ancient Chinese architecture scattered throughout Marxx Kingdom cities…The Chinese names and surnames peppered among the populace...

If the Qin Empire had once held control over eighty percent of the continent, it would explain why their cultural footprint still lingered so prominently even now.

So, what happened?How did a titan like that shrink into obscurity?

The Empire, once a force of legend, now existed as a hollow echo of its former self.

“Luke, we should leave,” a voice called from the library entrance, breaking his train of thought.

He glanced up and saw Tristan standing in the doorway, a faint smile playing at his lips. Judging by his expression, he had likely been there for some time.

“Mmm,” Luke hummed in reply, looking down at the tome still open in his hands. He hesitated—then gently closed it and slipped it into his bag.

Thanks to his Scholarly Pursuit, he had already committed its contents to memory, but something about the physical copy felt... grounding. Tangible.

I’ll need to look into Sun Tzu more, he thought. Maybe there’s a clue in his legacy… a clue to why I’m here.

As he passed Tristan, he paused and placed a hand on the older man’s shoulder.

“You did well, my friend.”

Tristan chuckled softly. “That was the easy part. We still need to cross miles of deserted land—with no horses, no weapons, and barely any supplies. Don’t get complacent.”

“One step at a time,” Luke muttered, suppressing a curse.

He really wasn’t looking forward to what came next.

They exited the library and were greeted by the chaotic rhythm of retreat. The courtyard buzzed with activity as Marxx soldiers moved with urgency, forming lines and depositing weapons.

Commander Bridges stood at the head of it all, barking orders beside a growing pile of discarded arms—pikes, swords, spears, bows, and arrows.

“Anyone who does not relinquish their weapon will be considered a traitor to the Kingdom!” he shouted, his voice cutting through the din. “Move it!”

It was clear he had taken Luke’s earlier instructions to heart.

The scene was surreal—an army divesting itself of the very tools of war. But Luke knew there had been no alternative. The Selis General had likely only agreed to such favorable terms because he had demanded strict disarmament.

Still, it felt wrong.

“Commander, how long until we can depart?” Luke called out, shifting the weight of his bag.

Bridges turned and cupped his fist respectfully. “General, we’re nearly ready. The men have filled their leather pouches with water, and we’ve distributed what dried rations we could salvage,” he said, his tone respectful but grim. “It’s not much—but it should last two days at most.”

Two days…

Luke clenched his jaw, resisting the urge to sigh aloud.

Yunxi lay at least ten days to the north on foot. Even on horseback, the second prince and the others wouldn’t arrive for another week—at best. Only a single day and night had passed since their groups had separated.

He had originally hoped to reunite with Yunxi’s forces somewhere between the two fortresses… but that plan had crumbled with their early departure.

If only they’d managed to hold out another week.

But their rations had dwindled to almost nothing.

They’d had no choice.

Which meant he needed a new plan.

First things first… I need to see if evacuating Xiandong will clear the current system quest.If it didn’t—

Luke cut the thought short as he spotted Christian approaching, flanked by the three other soldiers he’d chosen that morning.

He gestured them forward, ignoring the tension etched into their faces.

“Christian, Blake, Zion, Jason,” Luke began, watching their eyes flicker with faint surprise at the sound of their names. He nearly chuckled—if not for Scholarly Pursuit, he doubted he’d have remembered them all.

“I see great potential in you four. From this moment on, you are hereby promoted to Lieutenant Commander in the King’s army.”

Their jaws dropped.

The four men stared at him in disbelief, the weight of his words still sinking in.

“Soldiers, thank the General,” Tristan’s deep voice cut in smoothly, startling them into action.

“Thank you, General!” they shouted in unison, snapping upright and cupping their fists in salute.

Luke nodded solemnly, waving a hand in dismissal. “For now, assist in keeping order—especially once we’re on the move. We’ll be exposed crossing the plains between here and Ralis City. Stay vigilant.”

At the mention of the capital, he noticed the hope that flickered behind their eyes. No doubt, at some point during the siege, these men had accepted death as their fate. Even now, despite their promotions, a part of them likely wouldn’t believe they were truly going home until they stood within Ralis’s walls.

“Are we truly going home, General?” Blake asked softly, his voice barely above a whisper.

Before Luke could respond, Christian stepped forward, his tone firm.

“General, with all due respect—what about our brothers in Yunxi? Did His Majesty send another general to relieve them?”

Only now did the others seem to remember they weren’t the only Marxx forces stationed deep in enemy territory.

Luke didn’t take offense. He answered calmly.“The second prince is en route to Yunxi to take command. They’ve been ordered to return to the capital as soon as possible.”

But his response didn’t seem to ease their concern. The mere mention of the second prince made their expressions tighten.

They would’ve reacted differently if I’d said the Crown Prince, Luke noted. There’s little faith in the royal bloodline these days…

“What would you have us do, soldier?” Tristan asked, raising a brow. “We’ve got ten thousand men—no supplies, no weapons. We’re not reinforcements, we’re walking targets.”

“That’s assuming we even make it to Yunxi before the food and water run out,” Luke added, extinguishing what remained of their fleeting hope.

He stepped forward, placing a steady hand on Christian’s shoulder and giving it a reassuring pat.

“Don’t worry,” he said. “I spent the past ten days traveling with the second prince. He’s more capable than he used to be. He’s also accompanied by Richard Boyd and fifty of the King’s Guard.”

The mention of Richard Boyd caused a visible shift in their demeanor.

Christian slowly nodded, some of the tension in his shoulders easing.

“Yes, General.”

It took nearly an hour for the soldiers to surrender their weapons and assemble at the northern gates. By now, word of Luke and Tristan’s identities had spread throughout the ranks—whether through whispers or firsthand accounts.

Luke stood before the towering gates, gazing out across the open plain. The Selis army had shifted their formation, pulling back to one side and leaving a wide corridor for passage.

Under different circumstances, he might have taken this as a trap—an ambush waiting to be sprung.Well, actually, he still thought it might be a trap.

His heart thumped in his chest as he stood at the head of the column, every beat echoing louder than the last.

Luke narrowed his eyes, activating Eagle Eyes to zoom in on the enemy lines. There, standing tall in an emerald-green robe, was a massive man, leisurely stroking his beard as he watched the Marxx army from afar.

That guy’s almost as big as Master Boyd… Luke gulped.He didn’t need Scholarly Pursuit to recognize that this man was a true warrior—a General to be feared.

Closing his eyes, Luke fully activated his Eagle Eye skill. His vision shifted, rising high into the sky like a soaring hawk. He scanned the vast terrain from above: the Selis encampment, the open plains, the tree lines, and every possible hiding spot.

No signs of an ambush. No movement along the flanks. The path is clear.

Still… if they were walking into a trap, they would have no means to fight back.

“General, we are ready to march on your orders,” came a voice behind him.

Luke opened his eyes. The eagle’s view faded. He was back on solid ground.

“Let’s go,” he said simply, steeling his heart.

He took the first step forward.

Tristan, Commander Bridges, and the four newly promoted Lieutenant Commanders followed beside him. Ten thousand unarmed soldiers marched behind them, every step measured, every breath held.

Perhaps this is the first time in this world’s history that an intact army has walked out of a fortress without weapons, Luke mused, releasing a breathless chuckle.What would Sun Tzu say about this?

He could feel thousands of eyes watching him—some curious, others skeptical, many filled with contempt or awe. To some generals, such a retreat might feel humiliating.

But to Luke, pride was a luxury reserved for the dead.

What good was “face” if you didn’t live to keep it?

The soldiers filed out in a slow, steady stream. Step by step, they approached the gap left by the Selis formation. This was it—the point of no return.

They would soon find out if the Selis General intended to keep his word.

The men shuffled uneasily. Many kept their eyes glued to the enemy, watching for the slightest twitch, the smallest signal that an attack was imminent.

Luke did the same.

He walked with as much composure as he could muster, but the tension was suffocating. Every step felt like it shaved a year off his life. His muscles were tight, his breath shallow.

Then—his stride faltered, just for a moment.

[DING]

A familiar chime echoed in his ears.

Luke blinked—then smiled.

A satisfied grin crept onto his face.
