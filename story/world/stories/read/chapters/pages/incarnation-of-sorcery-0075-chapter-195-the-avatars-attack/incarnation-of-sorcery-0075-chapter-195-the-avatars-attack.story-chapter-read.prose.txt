Ophelia really should have been able to relax now that she was in the presence of Ryland, but she couldn’t. Not when the stupid mages were calling out summons from the Calamity-cursed Overrealm of all places.

“Shouldn’t we stop them?” Ophelia said rather loudly.

Devas had come down near her. She was trying not to take it as an initiative from him to protect her, especially since the spell being brought into existence by the mages from Jure was something she might actually need protection from.

“Do we need to?” Devas asked.

Yes, she wanted to say. They definitely needed to stop literal avatars of whatever mad god or goddess the mages were calling forth.

But maybe Devas was right. Maybe Ryland’s direct presence meant they had no reason to be worried. Not for their lives, at least. But if furious, cataclysmic fights between mages had proven anything to Ophelia, it was that everything below them was going to suffer tremendously in the fallout.

Basically, the Inscription Ryland and the rest of them had worked so hard to protect might just get wiped out, much like the planet it was on had been.

Which was probably the Juren mages’ goal anyway. They had to know that they’d be no match for someone like Marionys Ryland. But they didn’t need to be. All they had to do was cause enough chaos to ruin the whole Inscription and whatever magic Ryland was looking to cast through it.

She couldn’t tell if Devas realized that too, because he remained just as casual about things.

“Trust in the Great Master, Mage Knight,” Devas said with great reverence. “Failure isn’t something that exists in his lexicon as an oft-used word.”

Ophelia wanted to ask what exactly he defined losing Acrypha as, but she remained mum as the Juren mages’ spell came alive. Next, she wondered what the Oracle thought about it all. A bunch of mages were calling on the Great Gods’ power, which never came without the gods’ blessing. She had to feel some level of conflicted about it all.

The cracks in reality bleeding golden light now spread out and coalesced into one location, quickly turning solid. Figures emerged from the mess of divine energy compressed into mana. Ophelia recognized capes waving off their backs, spears and swords in their arms, a humanoid form emerging from the storm of power with a few too many arms and eyes.

Holy Avatars.

A spell of that magnitude—Tier [28] at least—wouldn’t be available to regular mages. One of them had to be in communion with the Great Gods.

One of them might just be an Oracle.

With lightning and fire flickering across their auric forms, they assaulted Ryland. From the very first impact, Ophelia figured out quite easily that this was going to be a terrible fight. Not for Ryland, of course. For the Avatars.

His figure went alight with too-bright power. The attacks from the Avatars simply bounced off his form. When the violent storm of energy from the Avatars finally struck Ryland, none of it left a mark on him. His innate magical intensity was simply far superior to whatever his enemies could throw or summon.

Ophelia could do nothing but gawk. The sky was alight with their clash, their impacts letting loose devastating blasts. It felt like stars were birthing merely mountain-heights above her.

“You may be correct,” Devas said suddenly. For the first time, he sounded genuinely concerned, if only a little bit. “The fallout will negatively impact the Inscription at any moment.”

“That’s what I’m saying.” Ophelia was glad she was able to tear her gaze away from the brilliant battle above her and focus on what mattered most. “If this Inscription is really oh so precious, then why are we allowing it to come to harm now that Ryland is involved?”

Devas had no answer to that. Debating the matter was pointless. What they needed to do was act.

“The Avatars,” Ophelia said.

Devas nodded. “Indeed. Avatars need anchors to exist and act away from the Overrealm. Destroy the anchors and they will be destroyed in turn as well.”

In truth, they didn’t need to concern themselves with the Avatars’ literal destruction. Ryland was performing an admirable job of that all by himself. But dispelling the Avatars was probably a different task, one which neither Ophelia nor Devas was sure whether Ryland was working on.

So, they took it upon themselves.

“If you create the opening,” Ophelia said. “Then I can strike them down.”

Just like the first Juren mage she had killed. If one was fallible, so were the rest. They just needed to be decisive about it.

Casting [Divine Avatar] hadn’t suddenly made the mages immobile or anything of the sort. That would have made things too easy. They couldn’t have that, now could they?

So, they were once again re-engaging the Juren mages in battle. Devas took to the air and assaulted the infiltrators with his spatial magic. This time, he was squeezing himself below the enemy mages before aiming his spells. Good thing too. Otherwise, they’d use his own powers to destroy more of the Inscription.

“[Spear of the Eternal],” Devas shouted with something akin to glee, as though he was ecstatic at being able to shoot out his Realm-breaking magic.

An actual spear formed in the air above Devas. Overlapping folds of space itself compressed upon a shaft of reality before tapering to a point, and then it flew at their opponents with the speed and fury of a thousand cannon shots.

The infiltrators defended themselves successfully. Ophelia could do nothing but stare as they cast a spell she couldn’t hear from this distance. It created a golden glow not dissimilar to the precursor of the Avatars, and Devas’s spell struck that amalgamation of light with the force of a dozen lightning bolts. It dissipated harmlessly.

Ophelia cursed. The enemy mages were adapting their tactics actively, as if they had already learned how best to counter her and Devas. This was proven by how they stuck closer together now.

Right. This was going to be a pain.

No sooner had she thought of it and started running towards the idiotic mages than Ryland decided to change the outlook of everything. As Ophelia ran on, her broadsword and shield ready to engage, a furious light grew in the sky.

She didn’t even bother looking up. This was Ryland up to his usual shenanigans. She could stand in awe of his power when the fate of such an important battle wasn’t resting on her.

Fortunately or unfortunately, Ryland decided she did need to pay attention.

The colossal tail that waved through the air made not only Ophelia stop, but she was pretty certain her heart had stopped beating for a few moments too. Calling the bony appendage massive was an understatement. Where Rhel had lost its moon in the battle between Ryland and the Demiurge, it had regained a new orbiting body in that huge tail bursting from that giant rift.

It didn’t actually do anything. The tail didn’t swipe or attack the foes Ophelia was after. Maybe Ryland was aware of potentially destroying his own Inscription, so he had purposefully ensured it wouldn’t assault anything or anyone this close to the ground.

But it did pass through the same space that they all occupied. The Juren mages were forced to scatter, their combined defence dispersing in the face of that gargantuan, spiny tail.

Which meant Devas and Ophelia could now pounce.

The Turnalian wasted no time wreathing himself with shattered space by casting [Broken Realm], shooting at his opponents too quickly for them to recover. A Juren mage screamed before he went down, falling strangely slowly like an airship with a ruptured engine.

Next came Ophelia. She ran, then leaped, her sword raised high. [Stalwart’s Retaliation] had been primed for ages now since she had been defending against their relentless assault for far too long. The mage Ophelia had targeted recovered control over herself long enough to cast a barrier around her body. But it was far from enough.

Her blow cleaved through her barricade, making the spell shatter before her chestplate broke and her torso bled freely. She plopped down along with Ophelia, just dead instead of alive.

Ophelia looked up, her breathing like a gale and her blood dancing in her veins. The tides had turned. Momentum was theirs. It was now or never.

The interruption arrived at the worst time possible.

“Ophelia,” the Oracle said through the Glyph of Communication. “I have need of you.”

Ophelia cursed. The Oracle had given that Glyph of Communication to Ophelia when they had been chasing the first insurgent, the man they had caught together with Ryland. “I’m in the middle of something here.”

Even as she spoke, she was already rushing towards her next adversary. The Juren mages had quickly recovered despite rapidly losing two of their own. Once again, despite their reduced numbers, they were doing well to repel Devas’s spatial magic. Ophelia needed to join the fight in truth and turn the tide for good.

“Now, Ophelia,” the Oracle insisted. Despite the urgency in her voice, she sounded suppressed, as if she was forced to whisper. “I know not what exact trouble you’re facing outside, but I sense the presence of Great Gods in the flesh, and it alarms me for you can only defeat what I think that is by destroying—”

“Their anchors, I know,” Ophelia said. Did the Oracle think she was an idiot? “We’re working on it!”

“If you were, then you’d be here with me.”

“What?”

“The anchors are here. Present with me. And now, with Ryland gone, they appear ready to act. Violently.”

Ophelia’s steps slowed down as the full implication of what the Oracle meant finally settled. They were with her. The anchors she and Devas were supposedly hunting were actually in the presence of the Oracle. It wasn’t these Juren mages, some of whom she had killed, but rather, the ones still with Oracle Vertha.

So many questions popped and burst in Ophelia’s mind that she wasn’t sure where to begin asking them. Weren’t those mages supposed to be dead? She had seen Ryland carrying their literal heads. And if they were dangerous, then why wasn’t an Empyrean-ranked mage like the Oracle wiping them out already?

Whatever games were being played went over Ophelia’s head. It didn’t matter. She had proof of what the Oracle was saying.

Despite the deaths of some of the Juren mages, it didn’t appear any of the Avatars had died or faded yet. They wouldn’t disperse completely until Ryland actually made their forms untenable with his impressive magic, but he must have done so already a few times, yet the battle was still raging. All the Avatars were still active.

Ophelia made a decision. “Devas, please take care of the rest of them.”

He was in the middle of casting more spatial magic, and he unleashed the wave of broken space at the last of the Juren mages to keep them busy, but he twisted his head to look at her as he did so. “Where will you be going, Mage Knight? Tired of the battle already?”

“No. We’ve been fighting the wrong enemy. These ones aren’t the anchors. They’re actually the ones Ryland had trapped earlier.”

Viren found that helping the Rhellians was a more worthwhile endeavour than he had expected it to be at first. It made him recall that there were lots of forms of heroism.

Foolishly and childishly, he had grown a bit obsessed with the dramatic and flashy part of being a hero. It was why he was learning magic. One day, he’d be able to swoop in and save a child from mortal danger.

But that was overly fanciful. In a way, hoping for something like that was downright stupid. Why would anyone want a child to be in that sort of danger in the first place? Wouldn’t a better hero be one who ensured a child’s surroundings never got to a perilous point? The real point was that being a hero didn’t mean one had to be a powerful mage capable of great magic. Just helping people was enough.

Which was what he was trying to do with the Rhellians.

He had helped them build the same kind of rudimentary shelters he was using. Food was somewhat limited, but combining his stash with what the Rhellians had brought would ensure they kept going until Ryland helped them procure more. For all that Rhel had been destroyed, there were still sources of relatively clean water Viren had discovered.

And even when it wasn’t clean, whether it be the food and drink they supped or the materials they used, they always had cleaning and purification spells to ensure they were fine.

“There’s been some disturbance I think,” Viren said after ensuring the Rhellians had all of the basics that they needed. He didn’t want to spook them so he didn’t tell them any of the details he had spotted. “But why don’t you all get some rest while I go check what’s going on?”

The refugees had settled rather far from the Inscription, so if anything actually happened, they were well protected.

Well, not necessarily protected. There hadn’t been enough time for Ryland to leave a fortification spell. But that would come in due time. Viren would give Ryland a reminder. Once this mess was done.

Viren’s heartrate started climbing the closer he drew to the Inscription site. Of course, the whole area was so huge, he didn’t reach the site of the battle as soon as he reached the Inscription location. Especially since he suddenly remembered that there were a couple people much closer at hand.

“Um, excuse me,” Viren said. He wasn’t bashful. It was just that he had to be careful since he was in the presence of another Incarnation. “There’s a bit of trouble going on…”

Xyke, the Incarnation that Ryland had introduced as the Glyphmaster, turned to Viren from her hastily-constructed workbench. She pushed up the Glyphtech goggles she was wearing and was clearly suppressing a frown at her work being interrupted. “Didn’t Ryland say he could take care of any trouble that pops up?”

“Yes, ma’am, but I think he might be busy with something at the moment.”

“What could possibly be keeping him busy from protecting what’s supposed to be the most precious thing of his on Rhel?”

She didn’t wait for an answer and turned back to her work. Said work turned out to be the third Incarnation present on Rhel. Though, he appeared vastly different from the other two, so Viren could be forgiven for not thinking of him as such.

“Don’t worry, boy,” Xyke said. “I’m almost done. You see, the Incarnation of Growth we have here lost a great deal of his constituent Conceptual Glyphs during the debacle we pulled him from, some of which are quite fundamental to sapient life. As such, I’m rather busy restoring them.” Her voice grew hushed and excited. “This might be one of my greatest projects ever…”

“Um, ma’am—”

Viren was forced to stay mum as the sounds of the distant battle reached them with a particularly loud boom, which was then followed up by a great deal of tremors coursing through the room.

“Ugh yes, I know,” Xyke said, waving away the dust that had fallen upon them. “Believe me, I’m actually helping.”

“Right…”

She glanced at him briefly. “You’re quite the bold lad, aren’t you?”

Viren flushed, then immediately cursed himself. Stupid, stupid. Staying with Ryland had loosened his tongue too much. He was supposed to be respecting an Incarnation of all people. What was wrong with him?

“If you promise to stop harassing me until I’m done,” Xyke said while continuing to work on the still but glowing form of the Incarnation of Growth. “I will gift you a Tier [25] Glyph. Is that a suitable bribe for your silence? Will you now let me work in peace while I reach the culmination of my efforts?”

Viren really wasn’t sure whether to say yes or no. The moral thing would have been to say no, but did he want to accidentally upset an Incarnation? No, no he really didn’t.

Another tremor answered for Viren. Even the Incarnation of Growth stirred, though Xyke was quick to keep him still.

“Almost done,” she insisted. “Just be a little patient…”

He was. So was Viren. Even when things shook so much that the ceiling literally cracked and Viren’s every instinct screamed for him to run and find Ryland or do anything else instead of remain here. But then, Xyke finally pulled back.

“All done!” she said triumphantly.

Her announcement was accompanied by a fitful flash rolling across the Incarnation of Growth’s body. Said Incarnation now got to his feet. His figure was a lot more solid now, appearing almost perfectly humanoid.

Xyke laughed. “How are you feeling now, my friend?”

The Incarnation turned his head very slowly. “Like I can fight.”

Viren’s eyes widened hearing those words. They felt like they were coming from a great depth, like he was hearing them from the bottom of a well.

“That’s great,” Xyke said with a clap. “Because wouldn’t you believe it, we’ve got the fight of our lives ready for you.”
