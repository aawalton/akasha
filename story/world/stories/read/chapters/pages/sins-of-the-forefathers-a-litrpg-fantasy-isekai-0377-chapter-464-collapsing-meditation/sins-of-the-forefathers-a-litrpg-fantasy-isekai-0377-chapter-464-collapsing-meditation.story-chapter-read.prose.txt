For one, heart-stopping, dread-inducing moment, I was convinced that Alveron was dead. That the entire reason the Elven encampment was so mournful was because they were in mourning. That after thousands of years of effort, Alveron had finally died and deprived Vereden of its final Paragon.

And then I felt it, very faintly. A familiar sensation to me, now, provided to me by one of my more esoteric senses.

A single, distant thump against my blood sense, originating from within Alveron’s skinny chest.

A heartbeat, but just one.

I breathed in, feeling myself relax. He wasn’t dead, then.

But if that was true…what was going on?

I flicked my gaze over to the massive Elf who had revealed the Elder one, and was a bit irritated by the calculation in his gaze. He knew that I had realized Alveron was alive in some way, and had dramatically revealed him in a manner to lower my guard, if only momentarily. As a test, I could only assume.

I was starting to despise dramatic ‘tests’ like that, but I’d just have to deal with it. In a global culture such as what existed on Vereden, where might and power were very much substantial, and yet there was an outright taboo in measuring it quantitatively in others with Observe…

Little idiosyncrasies like this popped up. People tried to one-up the other, to gauge their overall ability.

Irritating.

I kept this off my face, in the same way I hadn’t managed to quite hide it initially. “What’s wrong with him? Is he in a coma?”

The Chief grunted, lowering the sheet to block Alveron from view once more. “Something like that. Come. We have much to discuss.” He turned to face the watching Elves standing around the table, and barked a few words at them. Without complaint, most of them exited the tent in short order…but not all of them. Curiously, Kierla remained within the tent with her fellow unmasked Elf, glaring defiantly at the giant. He glowered back, but seemed to decide to tolerate her presence. He shook his head, and took his place at the head of the table once more.

Exchanging a glance with an equally unimpressed Bleddyn, we copied him, standing opposite of the table. I took the chance to look over the table, and I wasn’t shocked at all to see maps.

Lots of maps that were very interesting to me.

I hadn’t thought detailed maps of the Barren forest, particularly of the western half where Smaragd resided, actually existed. In retrospect, it made sense. The Elves weren’t complete savages, despite the god that had crouched on their collective soul for generations. They must have had a map-making tradition, and I was glad to see it.

So.

That’s how big Smaragd was. If the scale was right, and I could compare it to every map I had ever seen that depicted Blutstein…

The outer reaches of Smaragd were far larger than my new home city was.

I took a deep breath before looking up to meet the gaze of the Elf on the other side of the table. “It occurs to me we haven’t been introduced.”

“It appears so,” He rumbled in response, and then notably didn’t introduce himself.

Irritating.

I must not be the only one to think so, because Kierla abruptly growled from her place near the flap. She stalked across the room until she stood on the left side of the table, halfway between my side and the Chief’s, before pointing toward him. “This is Chief Ragnvald of the Free. He speaks now for us, with the Elder injured.”

“Kierla!” ‘Ragnvald’ barked, scowling at the girl. He said another word in Elvish before pointing to his side of the table.

Notably, Kierla didn’t do whatever it was she was just commanded. Instead, she just jutted her chin out defiantly at the giant and spoke again. “He is the chosen war successor of the Elder, and he is, to my misfortune, my uncle.”

I blinked slowly at this information, and studied the two of them more closely. I…suppose I could see it. There was a very faint resemblance if you looked. The shape of the eyes, maybe. “I…see. You say the Elder is injured? So, it is a coma?”

Ragnvald grumbled for a moment more before shaking his head and finally addressing me. “No, it is more of a sickness. Something he already had for some time. It has advanced to the degree that he is no longer able to lead us.”

My eyes narrowed, but it was Bleddyn who finally spoke. “He had Core Collapse, didn’t he?” He rubbed his chin while I cocked my head at him in surprise. I wasn’t aware that Bleddyn had known that. It wasn’t like Alveron shouted that out for all the world to know. “Is he in the last stages, then?”

“Yes,” Kierla sighed regretfully. “Whatever it was that the Chainmaker whispered in his ear, it…weakened him. Or his resolve, perhaps. Whichever it was, his symptoms advanced quickly.”

“Too quickly,” Ragnvald scowled. I couldn’t help but notice one of his meaty hands curling around the handle to his greatsword and squeezing. “I am not convinced the serpent did not curse him. The Elder lived with his Core Collapse for over a century with no symptoms, but the moment that creature even spoke to the Great Elder, he nearly succumbs to it. Bah. It does not matter now. He shall die all the same.”

“They both will, now,” Maria said somberly, shaking her head. “How long does the Elder have before he…goes?”

At that, Kierla and Ragnvald exchanged a troubled glance, seemingly forgetting their shared antipathy in a moment of brief solidarity. “We do not know, and the Elder did not either,” Ragnvald said with a frown.

“He said that he felt it coming upon him quickly, and that if he did not fight it at every moment, he would succumb,” Kierla said worriedly. “So he descended into a trance to do so. He is not actually asleep, we believe. Simply so deep into a state of meditation while he fights to hold his Core together that he cannot spare a moment otherwise. In truth…we’re not even sure if he can’t hear every single word spoken in his presence.”

Almost simultaneously, every single head in the tent turned in the direction of the corner where Alveron was hidden from the world behind a curtain.

Nothing. No reaction, no movement, no sound.

Honestly, I don’t know what we were expecting. For him to wake up, pull the curtain back and wink at us before lying back down?

My attention was stolen when Theo, Bleddyn’s apparent second, spoke up for the first time. “I gotta ask…why?” He said, scratching his chin beneath his beard. At the foul looks that both Kierla and Ragnvald shot him, Theo just shrugged. “Ain’t mean no offense, but why is he even botherin’? You can’t fix or cure Core Collapse, to the best of my understandin’. If he’s dyin’ of it, then…he’s just goin’ to die of it eventually.”

I exchanged a glance with Bleddyn.

Theo…wasn’t exactly wrong. Core Collapse wasn’t a condition that could be cured. It wasn’t even really a disease, really. It was the natural conclusion to a vastly extended life.

Most people liked to think that you became essentially ageless, if not immortal, when you progressed up into the upper echelons of the power that the System granted. I’m talking in the nine hundreds to the very peak at one thousand. It wasn’t even hard to understand why. Your lifespan gradually extended the more you grew in strength, at an almost insane amount.

The problem was, of course, that even with that extended life span, a person’s soul simply couldn’t contain all of that power indefinitely. Once you formed a Mantle, something you apparently did at the Third Breakpoint, then the bulk of your power became too great to be contained within it. It essentially transferred over into the Concord, existing alongside the owner like a ghost. You weren’t separated from your strength; you just…existed alongside of it in another realm, in a kind of superposition.

Eventually, though, even that distance wasn’t enough. Your own strength would bear down on your soul, gradually crushing it until the whole of your self just…couldn’t bear it anymore. Instant death, and when that happened, all of your accumulated power flooded out in the material realm.

Thus the term, ‘Core Collapse’. Shacklock, that mad bastard, apparently had it, and was only a few decades away from the final collapse.

Alveron was on the verge of it right now.

Theo was right. This wasn’t something that could be Healed away. The last Paragon of Vereden was going to die of his Core Collapse; it was just a matter of when.

But…

“He wants to use it, doesn’t he?” I asked shrewdly, drawing the gaze of the Elves among us. “That’s what he’s waiting for. He’s buying time until the collapse of his core can be used in the effort against Anguis and Fynneas.”

This time, it was Kierla and Ragnvald who exchanged a glance. I couldn’t help but notice Kierla’s held a note of smug triumph, while the Chief’s was a tad sour. He shook his head and turned to me. “Yes, you are…essentially correct. The great Elder wishes for his death to have meaning. It was his hope that with it, he could breach the wards surrounding the western forest, and allow us entry into the heart of the Progenitor’s realm. Then, you could fulfill your purpose and finally rid us of this curse, Precursor.”

I think both Kierla and Ragnavald were expecting a different reaction than the one they got. Oh sure, his statement was plenty dramatic enough, and there was enough of a possibility he was correct.

But it was only a possibility.

And so, it was my turn to glower at the two of them, while Bleddyn sighed in irritation. “Oh, is that so? That would have been wonderful news you could have shared with us anytime within the last few weeks. Instead of, you know. Closing your damn borders and turning not only me, but everyone else away so we could perhaps learn this idea.” I leaned forward irritably, placing my hands flat on the table. “Why was that, I wonder?”

Ragnvald instinctively leaned back from the force of my ire, blinking a few times before he regained his composure. “We could not allow outsiders within. There were rituals to conduct, meetings to be had, and a tourney to undergo for the selection of a proper war leader for our people. The great Elder did not name a war successor, merely a peacetime leader. Eventually, after great effort, I arose to this position.”

Off to the side, Kierla shifted uncomfortably, but didn’t speak up.

“Why is this an issue, Nathaniel Hart? Surely, we now have a course of action after the loss you incurred at the Dwarven city.”

“The issue,” I growled. “Is that we already have a plan on how to bypass Smaragd’s War Wards. Which is something you would know if someone had just said a damned word to us. And those plans don’t involve using Alveron’s sacrifice as a bomb to bypass them.”

For the next several minutes, I took the time to explain the entire plan to the increasingly incredulous Kierla and Ragnavald.

One of them was hopeful about our plans to venture to the north.

The other needed a great deal more convincing. Especially when I made my request for most of their Chamels to act as our leads for the Final Line.

That didn’t go over well at the start. But I was determined, and eventually, I got the Elve’s new war leader to agree to my demands.

We would get the Elven Chamels. We would even get a contingent of Elves to accompany us, who knew how to care for them, plus a platoon of Elven warriors to act as their guards.

That was all fine with me, and I think it irritated Ragnvald just how easy I agreed to his demands once I had mine met.

Still, once it was all said and done, I had one more request.

With the meeting done, I stood alone in the tent but for one person, everyone else having vacated the premises. I wanted a bit of privacy, after all, for what I was going to do next.

I stared down at the sleeping form of Alveron and wondered.

I think they were right when they speculated the ancient Elf could still hear within his meditation.

“You heard all of that, I’m sure you did,” I said quietly, considering the concentric wrinkles on Alveron’s slack face. “You probably caught that I didn’t rule out you using your Collapse at all. Just…not on the wards. Because I believe it’s your right to want your death to have a deeper, more useful purpose. I wouldn’t take that away from you, Alveron. So. I’ll promise you this. If you can just wait a little bit longer, for us to return from the Holds with whatever method this Nehushtan has for us through the wards…I’ll take you to Smaragd. There, you can use it for a more impactful purpose.

Directly fighting against Fynneas.

Who knows? Maybe you’ll even take out Anguis with it. Wouldn’t that be funny…”

I don’t know what I was expecting…

But it wasn’t for Alveron’s eyes to pop open for a single, lightning-quick moment. They met mine, emerald on emerald, and the supine elf gave me the barest of nods before his eyes slid closed in meditation once more.

Message received.

I wouldn’t let you down, Alveron.
