“You should get some sleep.” Came the ghostly voice that no one else except maybe Darius could hear. Alley, who had been staring at the ground for long enough that snowflakes had settled in his hair, shook his head.

“No sleep till this is finished.” The son of the former All-Isles champion sounded flat and exhausted, but there was a determination in both his voice and his purple eyes.

The Oblivion Maiden glanced over from her position hovering above Alley’s head at the two piles of corpses.

“I guess it's only another hour or two, but this shit is going to give me nightmares.”

As tired as he was, Alley was slow to respond. Still, he eventually tilted his head and looked up at the pale ghost that was his perpetual companion.

“I always thought you slept, but nightmares? From this? For you?”

His incredulous tone only grew more pronounced the more he thought about the suggestion. “I’ve been to your home, and it was easily scarier than this.”

Denderra shook her head and floated down till it appeared like she was standing on the ground next to where Alley sat.

“Nooo way, Castle Lethe might be full of ghosts and skeletons walking around, but other than the majodomo, no one is rotting.”

Alley stared at the ghost incredulously for a moment before slowly climbing to his feet. In the hours previous, he and Kyera had made their way up through the collapsing multi-leveled inn, which Alley had since discovered was called a hotel. While the local militia had been somewhat perplexed by the repeatedly resurrecting zombies attempting to impede their progress, it had been a major relief to discover none of them had actually been killed by the zombies that infested the burning building.

In fact, the creatures were fairly weak outside of their ability to come back; that was a benefit of their undead nature. There were quite a lot of them, though, and to Alley's surprise, they seemed to fall into multiple categories. There was no way to know if the undead monsters had killed any of the few remaining people who had been hiding in their rooms during the mercenary attack, but Alley couldn’t spend the whole night feeling guilty when there were living people in need of assistance.

So, with a little shared language and an awful lot of pantomime, Alley and Kyera had managed to communicate to the militia members that he could Card the defeated zombies and thus prevent them from coming back to life and continuing their assault on the living.

So once the survivors had been evacuated and the fires had been fully extinguished, the city defenders had helped Alley stack the various re-killed corpses in a pile in the square outside the hotel. That alone had taken hours, including a brief stop from the militia members to check why the pair of western princes were standing guard over a chained-up and surprisingly cheerful redheaded young man.

Since there was only one Deck in the city, perhaps the whole continent that made use of Undeath Resonance, Alley alone could Card the zombies. That would be fine except that even the strangely powerful Crown Of The Cursed King could only create a single Card per hour, which was approximately twice as long as it took for an Undead creature to self-resurrect.

Considering that there were eight of the civilians and five of the Goret assassins, this meant that Alley spent the remainder of the night, and much of the post-dawn morning killing zombies after they pulled themselves back together, waiting, killing them again, and then turning them individually into Cards.

Technically, he could have carved up a number of the zombies and Carded them together to create a single horde monster, but that would defeat the whole point of having created the monsters in the first place. While Alley hadn’t really been the one to unleash the necromantic power of the Undeath Resonance, he did have blurred memories of why it had been done.

If Alley could Card five of the same monster he could combine the Cards into an evolved version, and by doing so reach the next rank. It was something he hadn’t expected to be able to achieve here in the Frostlands.

So that was what Alley did; it took until the sun was well past dawn, and even approaching late afternoon, before he was finished. Thankfully, the Skalean militia had already cordoned off the area and were perfectly happy to continue doing so if it meant the permanent destruction of the unnatural monsters.

Reasonably confident that Darius had worked the last of the Undeath Resonance out of his system, the group had untied the hunter and sent him off to find a new inn to stay in, probably one that was much smaller this time. It had taken him a little over an hour, which Alley had to admit was a little demoralising, given how long his own task was taking. But at least he, Kyera, and the younger prince Kaj could go get some rest. As a True-Human, Malchik could go without rest for days without any noticeable ill effects, so the elder of the pair of princes had opted to stay with Alley and help out with keeping the zombies dead.

It was a little awkward to spend hours with a man whom he couldn’t understand and couldn't understand him, though they had managed it whilst Alley slowly worked his way through the two groups of zombies. Once he was down to just a few left Malchikk had gone off to ensure the safety of his younger half-brother, which left Alley alone with several armored militia, one of whom spoke Southern and had been given directions by Darius as to where Alley could find them, and of course the ghostly Oblivion Maiden who had alternated between charming jokes, concern for Ally’s well being and complaining about how boring and gross this whole process was.

As much as he didn’t want to, Alley had to admit it was a boring and gross process, not to mention extremely time-consuming. It was still worth it, more than worth it even. Initially, the Zombies from both the civilians and fiery mercenaries were rather on the weak side, but that wouldn’t matter if they allowed Alley to rank up. The idea excited him more and more, as he fed each new copy to Card he already held.

The civilians that had been slain by the efforts of the Goret Brotherhood were fairly standard zombies that created a Card called Vengeful Dead, while the Undead remnants of the Gorets themselves unsurprisingly kept their affinity for Fire Resonance. The first time Alley had seen one, he’d assumed a normal zombie had simply caught fire, but once he started encountering them more and more during his climb through the hotel, it had become clearer what it was he was dealing with. These produced a Card called Pyrric Pyre Zombie, which was initially stronger than their Vengeful Dead counterparts, but as the Vengeful Dead Card grew closer and closer to evolving, it seemed to thrum with power, and as it did, Alley felt a similar thrum from inside himself that only grew stronger.

After adding the fifth Card to his original copy, Alley witnessed the weak Vengeful Dead Card glow and then explode into a cascade of blinding colored lights. First was the shimmering purple of Undeath, which then faded into the sickly green of Zombie Resonance, and finally the red of dried blood that represented Wrath.

After almost a minute, the shining Resonance colors faded completely, leaving behind a much stronger and potentially more interesting Card.

“Hey Dendi.” Said the tired but elated Alley. “ Care to tell me what this does?”

The ghost who had taken her leave reappeared, leaning over Alley’s shoulder. She stifled a yawn and then reached out to run a finger along the physical Card that represented the now truly lifeless monsters.

“Ayy, I’ve seen a Card like this one before, and it’s not bad.”

​

Alley raised both eyebrows as Denderra explained the effect of the evolved zombie.

“Wow, that is pretty goo-” The words suddenly fled Alley as for the second time in as many days a tidal wave of Resonance exploded through his already burned and bruised Resonance system.
