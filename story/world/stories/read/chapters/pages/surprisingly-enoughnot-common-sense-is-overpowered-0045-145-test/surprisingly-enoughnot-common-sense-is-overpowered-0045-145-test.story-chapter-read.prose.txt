Ning's sudden question caught the elder off guard.

Over the years, he had encountered all kinds of disciples during the Spiritual Plant Hall's assessments. Some froze when they didn't know the answer, some threw out wild guesses, and a few particularly daring ones had even tried to cheat with artifacts, only to be caught and sent straight to the Discipline Committee.

But this was the first time a candidate had simply turned around and asked the examiner for the answer.

"Ahem. If you don't know, you may simply skip this question," the elder said sternly.

He had nearly answered out of instinct. Fortunately, he had stopped himself in time.

"No, I think I recognize it."

Ning carefully felt the plant again while focusing on the faint aura it emitted.

Short and thick. Slightly sticky. Multiple fine veins running along its surface, accompanied by a faint, dark spiritual aura.

These characteristics belonged to...

"Elder, this is a Violetspirit Thistle," Ning finally concluded.

The elder fell silent.

Ning waited.

And waited.

The silence stretched long enough that Ning began to suspect the old man was doing it purely for theatrics. Thinking back to Elder Shen, some elders do be like that.

Finally, the elder glanced between Ning and the plant before a smile appeared on his face.

"Correct!"

Ning breathed a quiet sigh of relief.

If I was right, you could've said it sooner, old man.

He reached up and removed the blindfold.

"I'm surprised you were able to distinguish Violetspirit Thistle from Bluespirit Thistle," the elder said curiously. "The two are remarkably similar, and Bluespirit Thistle is far more common. Without using your eyes, even experienced spiritual farmers can mistake one for the other."

Ning blinked as his eyes adjusted to the sudden light.

If he were being honest, he hadn't been completely certain either.

Based purely on what he could feel, the plant could easily have been a Bluespirit Thistle. The differences between the two were minute enough that identifying them without sight was extremely difficult.

So Ning had taken a gamble.

The final question had seemed suspiciously straightforward. After everything that came before it, would the examiner really finish with something as recognizable as a Bluespirit Thistle?

That had immediately set off alarm bells in Ning's mind.

And if there was one thing his time in this world had taught him, it was that the power of clichés was over nine thousand.

Of course the final question would be the rare plant that was ninety-nine percent identical to the common one.

It would almost be disappointing otherwise.

So Ning had harvested the power of cliché and chosen Violetspirit Thistle.

Fortunately, the harvest had been bountiful.

"I just got lucky." Ning smiled and cupped his fists toward the elder before walking away, blinking a few times as his eyes adjusted after the blindfold.

The elder watched him leave before giving a satisfied nod.

"It seems there are some good seedlings in this year's assessment."

As an elder of the Spiritual Plant Hall, seeing talented young spiritual farmers emerge was naturally something worth being pleased about.

…

The results didn't take long.

Once the last candidate had completed the first stage, the presiding elder returned with a jade slip in hand. The remaining conversations gradually died down as everyone turned toward him.

"Ji Ning, outer sect disciple. Sixty-seven First-Tier plants and ten Second-Tier plants correctly identified. His knowledge was comprehensive, and his explanations were detailed. First place."

Ning quietly let out the breath he had been holding.

Yes. All those late nights spent buried in books hadn't been wasted.

The elder continued.

"Ni Lunxia, outer sect disciple. Seventy First-Tier plants and three Second-Tier plants correctly identified. Second place."

One name after another followed, and the atmosphere among the waiting disciples gradually shifted. Some smiled in relief, while others frowned or lowered their heads as their rankings were announced.

"Xin Fu, outer sect disciple. Sixth place."

Xin Fu's face stiffened.

Sixth.

Only five certificates would be issued.

Although this was merely the first stage and the rankings could still change, seeing himself outside the top five clearly struck a nerve. Then his gaze landed on Ning, sitting quietly in the corner, and the frustration he had been suppressing finally burst out.

"You knew the questions beforehand, didn't you?!"

Ning paused.

He slowly turned his head toward Xin Fu.

This guy really seemed to have developed a grudge against him.

“Senior Brother Xin,” Ning said, “are you saying the assessment was leaked?”

Xin Fu froze.

“I—”

“These questions were chosen by the Spiritual Plant Hall. How exactly would I know which ones the elders were going to ask?”

Xin Fu opened his mouth, only to hesitate.

Ning continued calmly, "Unless you're saying someone from the Hall secretly gave them to me."

"I never said that!"

"Then where was I supposed to get them?"

Xin Fu's face stiffened.

"I meant... you must have found some way—"

"Enough!"

A sharp voice cut through the argument.

The presiding elder's expression had darkened considerably.

"The questions used in the first stage are drawn from basic knowledge available within the Spiritual Plant Hall. There is nothing secret about them. This stage tests how much effort you have put into studying your profession, nothing more."

His gaze settled on Xin Fu.

"If your result is poor, return to the library and study. Do not embarrass yourself by blaming others."

Xin Fu's face flushed red, but this time he wisely kept his mouth shut.

Ning didn't think much of it. Memorizing this sort of material was tedious, but hardly unbearable. Compared to calculus and the mountains of historical information he had once crammed into his head for exams, memorizing the properties of spiritual plants was almost relaxing.

More importantly, he hadn't relied solely on the basic texts available in the Spiritual Plant Hall. The alchemy inheritance he had obtained contained extensive information on spiritual plants as well, with particularly detailed records on Second-Tier medicinal ingredients.

That was the main reason Ning had managed to identify ten Second-Tier plants despite Ni Lunxia recognizing more First-Tier plants overall.

Xin Fu, meanwhile, seemed to have made the opposite mistake. He had plenty of practical experience in the fields, but had apparently assumed that experience alone would carry him through the knowledge assessment. Judging by the way his anger gradually gave way to embarrassment, he seemed to have realized that himself.

The presiding elder waited until the murmuring among the candidates died down before raising his hand.

"Enough. The first stage is over."

Everyone immediately turned toward him.

"The second stage will test your spiritual farming techniques. You will enter an illusion and be required to cultivate three different spiritual plants. Seeds, basic tools, water, and the spiritual field itself will be provided. Anything beyond that must be compensated for through your own techniques and abilities."

With a wave of his sleeve, a bronze-framed mirror appeared before him, its surface shimmering like disturbed water.

"The Hundred Illusion Treasure Mirror," someone murmured.

Ning's attention sharpened as the elder placed one hand against the mirror and poured his qi into it. Azure light spread across its surface before erupting outward and enveloping the gathered outer sect disciples.

His surroundings vanished.

...

When Ning opened his eyes again, he was standing in the middle of a spiritual field.

He flexed his fingers and pressed one foot against the soil, testing the sensation. Old Zhou had mentioned the Hundred Illusion Treasure Mirror before, explaining that it could pull someone's consciousness into an illusion whose contents could then be manipulated by whoever controlled the treasure, but Ning hadn't expected it to feel this real.

Compared to the shaky VR technology from his previous life, the difference was almost ridiculous. The soil compressed beneath his feet, the wind brushed against his skin, and even his qi circulated exactly as he expected. If Ning hadn't already known this was an illusion, he doubted he would have noticed anything wrong at a glance.

That was slightly terrifying when he considered what else something like this could theoretically be used for, so Ning decided not to think about it and turned his attention to the trial instead.

Several bags of seeds rested beside the field, along with a selection of basic spiritual farming artifacts. He could freely choose what he wanted to cultivate, and the field would adjust to the appropriate grade, but there was no fertilizer, medicinal solution, spirit water, or anything else that might make the process easier.

The purpose of the trial was clear enough. Rather than testing their techniques individually, the elders wanted to see how well they could use everything they knew throughout an actual cultivation process. It also avoided the problem of directly comparing disciples who practiced completely different spells; in the end, the quality of what they grew would speak for itself.

Ning looked through the available seeds.

He could have chosen low-grade First-Tier plants and played it safe, but there wasn't much point in taking a certification exam just to demonstrate something he already did every day. After a moment of consideration, he passed over the mid-grade seeds as well and selected three high-grade First-Tier plants.

Frozen Breath was the obvious first choice, followed by Dragon Qi Grass. For the third, Ning picked the Serpent Flower.

His interest in this one wasn't entirely professional. The mature plant was poisonous, and while poisonous lilies had served him well enough when his cultivation was lower, they were becoming increasingly ineffective against mid- and late-stage Qi Condensation cultivators.

Ning had already been looking for something better, and Serpent Flower seemed like a good replacement.

Despite its name, Serpent Flower had no actual relation to serpents; the name came from its long, scaled stems and the way its roots wound through the earth.

Rather than spreading evenly through the surrounding soil, the roots actively sought out concentrations of spiritual qi, extending toward richer areas and draining nutrients along the way. The problem came when two Serpent Flowers encountered each other. Their roots would begin releasing poison into the surrounding soil as they competed for nutrients, and unless they were separated quickly, one would usually die. Sometimes both did.

Growing several together therefore required carefully controlling the distribution of spiritual qi beneath the field so their roots never crossed.

Now that was something Ning wanted to try.

He selected the Earth-Shaking Spiritual Hoe and stepped into the field, but instead of immediately planting the seeds, he began by preparing the soil.

[Soil Refining Technique]

His qi sank into the earth, allowing him to feel the differences within it. He slowly loosened several sections while compressing others, then used the Earth-Shaking Spiritual Hoe to redistribute the spiritual qi beneath the surface.

Uniform soil would actually make things harder in this case, so Ning deliberately created several narrow trails where spiritual qi was slightly denser, winding them through the field without allowing any to intersect.

Only after he was satisfied did he plant the seeds and cast Small Cloud Rain.

A light shower descended across the field, and before long, the first shoots broke through the soil. Thin green stems covered in overlapping scales emerged aboveground while their roots began spreading beneath them, quickly detecting the richer trails Ning had prepared.

Each plant followed a different path. One curved toward the left side of the field while another wound toward the far end, but as the third continued growing, its root gradually began drifting toward the second.

Ning immediately pressed the spiritual hoe into the ground and disturbed the qi ahead of it, weakening the existing trail while strengthening another farther away. The root slowed, wandered for a moment, then turned toward the richer path.

"Well, the easy part is done."

From there, Ning continued adjusting the field as the plants grew. Keeping a single Serpent Flower alive wasn't particularly difficult, but cultivating several beside each other became increasingly troublesome as their root systems expanded and occupied more of the available soil.

That was exactly why he had chosen it. This was a trial, but it was also a rare opportunity to practice without wasting real seeds or materials if he made a mistake.

Time passed rapidly within the illusion. The scaled stems lengthened and curled across the field while their roots followed the invisible paths Ning had prepared, and eventually small blue buds began appearing at their tips.

Those flowers were the only non-poisonous part of the mature plant. In fact, they contained compounds capable of neutralizing the poison produced by the rest of it, making them the natural antidote to Serpent Flower poisoning.

Poison and its antidote from the same crop. Ning liked efficient plants.

Outside the illusion, images from dozens of spiritual fields shifted across the surface of the Hundred Illusion Treasure Mirror as time within them raced forward.

The presiding elder quietly observed each candidate in turn. Xin Fu had chosen Yellow Spirit Grain and was cultivating it steadily with the Earth-Shaking Hoe Technique, relying on the years of practical experience that hadn't helped him much during the knowledge assessment.

Here, however, that experience showed.

After watching for a few moments, the elder moved on until his gaze eventually reached Ning's field. Several mature Serpent Flowers now twisted across the soil, their roots following carefully separated paths of concentrated spiritual qi beneath them.

His gaze lingered briefly before moving to the other candidates.

Most of them had nearly finished cultivating their first plant.

The presiding elder raised his hand.

It was time to increase the difficulty.

…

For Ning, planting Frozen Breath was much easier. Using his golden finger, he dug a hole and buried the Frozen Breath seed. Then he refined the soil and, using the Earth-Shaking Spiritual Hoe, combed the qi through the ground and distributed it evenly.

He moved with precision, every action practiced and efficient. After casting Hidden Ice Mist, the suitable environment for Frozen Breath was established.

Everything was going smoothly when, suddenly, a patch of weeds appeared out of nowhere.

“Hm?” Ning watched the weeds disrupt the flow of qi he had meticulously arranged. “This must be the interference from the elders."

[Scorching Purge]

A flame swept through, burning the weeds without disrupting the planting process. The same thing happened multiple times, and just as Ning got used to the interruptions, another surprise appeared.

Squeak.

A monkey nearly six feet tall dropped into the field. Its qi was equivalent to a fourth-stage cultivator. Its eyes were blood-red, and its mouth was disproportionately large for its head.

A Howling Monkey.

At the same time, an announcement echoed through the illusion:

“A spiritual plant master must not only be able to plant spiritual plants, but they must also be able to defend them. Defeat the beast before it causes a rampage across the field!”

Ning had long suspected the elder’s definition of a “spiritual plant master” was wildly different from the norm. Now he had confirmation.

In the elder’s eyes, a spiritual plant master had to be a hexagonal warrior with every attribute maxed, excellent at planting and excellent at fighting.

Most spiritual farmers Ning knew spent their days tending fields, not wrestling spirit beasts.

Ning sighed, trying to conjure a bow or spear, only for nothing to appear. It seemed conjuring weapons was not allowed.

At least the elder setting this trial had some sense. The beast’s cultivation was deliberately set one stage lower than Ning’s.

Fair enough.

The Howling Monkey reared back, its chest expanding grotesquely.

[Sonic Howl]

A piercing screech tore through the air, rattling Ning’s skull. Sharp pain stabbed through his ears, and his vision wavered for an instant.

But Ning clenched his teeth, covered his ears, and kept moving.

If the goal were simply to kill the beast, he could have played it safe, kept his distance, and worn it down.

But this wasn’t a duel.

This was about protecting the field.

Using Shadow Steps, Ning closed the distance in a blur, ignoring the lingering dizziness. The monkey turned, startled that its howl hadn’t stopped him.

Too late.

No weapons? Fine.

Ning planted his foot, twisted his waist, and drove his fist forward.

He still had these hands.

[Bone Forging Fist: Bone Shatter]

Bone Forging Fist was often overshadowed by the flashier techniques Ning learned later. But it was his first martial art, refined over years of practice, polished into instinct.

At Great Accomplishment, its strongest move was brutal in its simplicity.

His fist struck the monkey square in the ribcage.

A dull, horrifying crack echoed across the field.

The Howling Monkey let out a choked scream as its body was hurled backward, slamming into the ground and skidding to a stop, its qi instantly thrown into chaos.

Ning exhaled slowly, rolling his shoulders as he advanced.

Planting… defending… combat…

“Yeah,” he muttered dryly, “As expected of a cultivation world, even farming requires one to know how to throw hands."
