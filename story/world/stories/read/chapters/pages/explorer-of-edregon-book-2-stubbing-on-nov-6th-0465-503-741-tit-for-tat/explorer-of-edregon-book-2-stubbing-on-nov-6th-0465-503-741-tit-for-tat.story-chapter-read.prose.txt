Just from making his way across town, a few things became readily apparent to Vin. For one, it was clear that the town was a true hodgepodge of different races all living together. Petians, elves, and humans were only the beginning. Vin even spotted a minotaur or two utilizing their muscular physiques to carry things around, as well as a few dwarves working in what looked like the crafter portion of town. Not only that, but he got the vibe that travelers weren’t entirely uncommon from the adjacent fragments. Based on how much of a struggle most people had with communicating with the minotaurs, Vin got the sense that at the very least, they weren’t native to this fragment.

No wonder people weren’t that surprised to see Alka in all her armor. They’ve probably gotten a bit desensitized to strange sights if giant, half-man, half-bull people are coming and going all the time.

Vin ended up having to ask a second guard for directions to help find the location listed on the flier, but it wasn’t long after that he arrived at the site of the lessons; a tall, slightly crooked building stretching three stories into the air. Or more precisely, a portion of the grounds located immediately behind the building.

Curious, Vin pushed open a small gate, walking into the yard before spotting a few people sitting off to the side. An elf, a petian, and a human were all lounging back on a few crates, chatting quietly among themselves. The three of them glanced up when he entered, and the human waved him over.

“Hey! Here for the magic lessons?”

“Yep,” Vin said, walking over and giving them all a warm smile. “I’m Vin.”

“Velma,” the woman said, returning his smile before she turned toward her companions. “The small one is my boyfriend Rick, and this is Chelsea.”

“Your boyfriend?” Vin blinked. Before he could stop himself, his brow furrowing as he looked between the regular-sized human and the half-foot-tall petian.

“Yeah. Got a problem with that?” Rick asked. Crossing his arms, the petian narrowed his eyes and stared up at him.

“No, no problem at all,” Vin said, realizing he was probably coming off as rather rude. “Sorry, I’m new here. I’m from another fragment.”

“Oh, like the bulls!” Chelsea said, fidgeting with one of her pointed ears as she looked at him. Her eyes widened the moment they landed on his arm. “What is that?”

“Prosthetic golem limb. Lost my real arm a while back,” Vin shrugged, eager to turn the conversation away from him. “So… have you three met the magic instructor before?”

“Yeah. He’s my brother,” Velma drawled. “It’s probably the reason he’s making us wait so long. I bet he’s still pissed about me finishing the last of the cheese yesterday. As if heading to the market all on his own would be too much for him.”

“I’m more pissed off at the fact that you keep eating my food without asking!” an angry voice rang out, and Vin turned to spot a man who appeared to be in his mid thirties walking toward them. He wore a simple green robe and had a staff with a white gem inlaid in the top. Standing before them, he shot Velma one more glare before turning to smile at the rest of them. “Welcome everyone who isn’t my brat of a sister. I’m Brian, your potential future magic instructor. How many of you are here because you saw the flier?”

Vin and Chelsea raised their hands, and Brian nodded at them, before letting out a sigh.

“...And how many of you are here for some other reason?”

“I thought it would be funny to watch,” Velma admitted.

“I sort of got dragged along,” Rick said, giving Brian an apologetic wave. “Sorry, Brian.”

“It’s alright, Rick. You’re pretty much the one good thing about even having a sister,” Brian admitted, ignoring Velma’s indignant cry as he turned toward Vin and Chelsea. “Well! We’ll just have to do our best to ignore any random hecklers who are just mad that they’re not willing to put in the time or effort to learn any useful skill to contribute to society. If the two of you work hard and put in the effort, one day, you’ll be able to do things like this!”

Vin’s eyes widened as Brian reached out and jabbed a finger into his sister’s forehead none too gently, causing Velma to swat at him. She opened her mouth to say something, but nothing came out. Narrowing her eyes at him, she stomped her foot and gave him what Vin could only assume was a rude gesture before turning and stomping off.

New spell witnessed! Tier-2 Vibration Spell (Nullify Vibrations)

“Took me a few months to put that one together, but it was more than worth it,” Brian grinned, chuckling to himself as he watched his sister leave. “That’s Nullify Vibrations. All speech really is is the vibration of the vocal cords. You stop those from vibrating, and just like that, no more talking.”

“...Precisely how difficult is learning that one?” Rick asked, the petian keeping his voice low as he leaned toward the older mage.

“You and I can talk later,” Brian promised. “For now, you should probably go after her. The spell will wear off in a few minutes.”

With that, Rick hopped down and ran off after his girlfriend, leaving Vin alone with Brian and Chelsea. The elf was still fidgeting nervously with her ear as she spoke up.

“Do you have any combat spells? Anything one could use against monsters?”

“Chelsea, my answer is the same as the last time you asked me,” Brian answered with a sad smile. “I don’t like that kind of magic. You’d have to talk to one of the Battle Mages.”

“They scare me,” the elf admitted, her eyes lowering to the ground. “...And they don’t offer lessons like you do. They only take dedicated apprentices.”

“The world of combat magic is a serious thing,” Brian shrugged. “Sorry, I can’t teach you what I don’t know.”

With that, Chelsea apologized and followed after Velma and Rick. Suddenly, it was just Vin and Brian. The instructor turned his warm smile onto him, and Vin’s stomach twisted when he realized his little game he was playing just to get a peek at some new magic might be a little cruel to the man who just wanted to share the wonders of being a mage with others.

“Sorry. Before you say anything, I’m not actually a novice,” Vin admitted. “In fact, I’m not from this fragment at all.”

“No offense… but duh?” Brian said. Pointing his staff toward Vin’s arm, he snorted. “I think I would have noticed someone walking around with a magical prosthetic limb at some point over the last few decades. Also, I can feel your mana pool. I know you have even more than I do. But I appreciate the honesty. Where are you from?”

“Three fragments straight south,” Vin said, deciding admitting that was safe enough. The fragment directly south of this one was the sky fragment, so it wasn’t as if anyone would be making a beeline for Terra in that direction anytime soon.

“Oh, you’d be a first from that direction,” Brian said, leaning forward onto his staff. “We get a lot of trade from a few of our surrounding fragments, like the minotaurs and the dwarves, and occasionally this one guy covered in hair. But you’re the first human from so far away.”

“Forpurt? The capillan?” Vin asked, getting a surprised blink from Brian.

“Wow, you really do get around. So what are you, a wandering mage?”

“Something like that. My last class was Magical Explorer.”

“He said, not so subtly hinting at the fact that he’s already entered his second prestige,” Brian smirked, laughing at Vin’s sheepish look. “It’s alright, I get it. I’m in my second prestige as well, but I don’t like to brag about it either. A lot of people have struggled to reach the levels they were once at before the slate was wiped clean. Though that’s a far more depressing topic we don’t need to delve into. So, what’s your poison?”

“My what?” Vin asked, getting a shake of the head from Brian. Straightening up, the mage pointed his staff at him.

“Your affinity, your magic of choice! I’m normally pretty good at picking up another mage’s affinity, but I’m having a tough time with you. If it wasn’t obvious, I’m vibration and water. Well, I guess water wasn’t obvious, but you get the point.”

“I’m actually a generalist,” Vin said, trying not to laugh when Brian’s mouth fell open. “I like to dabble.”

“Dabbling is for people who haven’t picked an affinity yet,” Brian argued, giving him a more piercing stare, as if he were trying to look into Vin’s mana pool. “You seriously don’t have an affinity? How did you even manage that?”

“Edregon has lots of different types of magic to study,” Vin shrugged. “I was able to spread out the spells I learned as I raised my magic attribute.”

“Sure, if you can survive,” Brian snorted. “Edregon is way too dangerous to travel through. I’ve never even left our fragment, but I’ve heard all about the failed scouting parties. The Battle Mages tried to recruit me once or twice, but I prefer spending my nights reading by the fire rather than cleaning off monster blood and guts. What brings you here, anyway? I take it you didn’t come all this way just to see what kind of magic we use.”

“I mean… I sort of did,” Vin admitted. “Wandering Edregon and visiting the different fragments to meet the people who live there and learn from them is kind of what I do. This is my…” he paused, doing a not-so-quick count in his head. “...forty-eighth fragment I’ve discovered. Granted, a few of those I just poked my head in and looked around because they were ridiculously deadly, but you get the idea.”

“Well damn, that’s nuts,” Brian said. Scratching his chin, he looked him over more carefully. “Alright. In that case, want to go by standard mage rules? Spell for a spell? Though I suppose that only works if you have vibration or water spells to trade. If not, maybe we can work something else out. I doubt you have any actual money on you, seeing as you're from another fragment, but earning a little coin isn’t too difficult if you have the right skillset.”

“I can’t promise I have any you don’t know, but I have a couple,” Vin admitted. In fact, he had a total of nine spells that could possibly fit the bill. Brian had to already have the more basic sensing and creation spells, but he was confident he had to have at least one or two he could use to trade. “It sounds like you’ve done this before, but I’ve never had any sort of official spell trade. Usually people just teach me runic formations because they like sharing magic, or as a reward for helping them with something.”

“Unfortunately for you, I’m not nearly so altruistic,” Brian chuckled. “And the only thing I need is for Rick to hurry up and propose so my sister will move out. Seeing as I don’t think you can help with that, standard trade rules it is. We demonstrate different spells and explain what they do, and if either of us have brand-new ones the other isn’t familiar with, it becomes a bargaining chip. Things get a little more tricky when it comes to bargaining between tiers, but the system is pretty straightforward. A single tier-3 spell might cost two or three tier-2 spells, and that sort of thing. You want to go first or should I?”

“You already showed one spell,” Vin shrugged, mentally running through his list before deciding to start with one of his more impressive ones. He figured a water mage had to be pretty good at holding his breath. “You wouldn’t happen to know Aqua Sphere, would you?”
