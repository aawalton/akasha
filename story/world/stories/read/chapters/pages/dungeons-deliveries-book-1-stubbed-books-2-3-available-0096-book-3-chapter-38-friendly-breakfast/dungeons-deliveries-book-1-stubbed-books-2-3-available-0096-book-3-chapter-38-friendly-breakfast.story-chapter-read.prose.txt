Alex woke to tiny chirps and a giant lick against his cheek. He scrambled up to a sit, finding himself in the luxurious bed of Snu’s Lair. He was shirtless, full-bladdered, showered, and face to face with a massive pitbull.

“Tank?” He asked, and before he could wipe the sleep from his eyes, the System hit him with upgrade notifications.

Ding!

Ding!

Ding!

WOOF! Tank barked, slapping him with another lick. The tiny chirping rose, and something leapt from Alex’s head with a howl.

The jumping gashapong, a limbed plastic strawberry, held a grey pebble high above it’s head.

The Tax Guild Stone! Alex thought in a panic as he recognized it.

The baked goods and berry gashapong cheered as the strawberry landed onto Tank, surrounding and ensuring the tiny creature and its pilfered claim was secure. Half a dozen other gashapong yanked Tank’s ears back, hollering as they tried to wrench the pitbull’s head around. It appeared the tiny creatures had taken coopted a ride on Tank’s back, somehow grabbed the Tax Guild Stone from Alex, and were trying to make a getaway.

“Tank!” Alex shouted, throwing himself onto his knees and reaching for the dog. “Come here!”

WOOF!

Tank thought it was a joke, scuttling to the back of the mattress and wagging his tail. A tiny piece of toast hung onto the tail’s tip for dear life as it was flung back and forth.

Alex put on a serious, slow tone, carefully edging forward toward the dog. “Tank, you will come here right now. I’m serious. We’re not playing—”

WOOF! Tank barked as he leapt off the mattress to trot away towards a portal. The gashapong roared their victory as Tank jumped into it as if it was a doggie door.

Shit! Alex thought as he crawled out of Snu’s bed as fast as he could. Thankfully, he had boxers on, and [Phantom Step]ped towards the portal. He still wasn’t sure what he ought to do with Tax Guild Stone, but was quite confident that tax and guild monetary management shouldn’t be the responsibility of a strawberry. Or an unruly bunch of gashapong. He ducked into the portal, hopefully hot on Tank’s tail.

His balance shuddered as he was transferred from the Leather Spires to a bedroom of the Safety Spires.

WOOF! WOOF! Tank barked at the open bedroom door. For some reason, the dog wasn’t sprinting ahead, and the gashapong hurled themselves off Tank’s back, seeming to be aborting mission.

They landed haphazardly onto the wooden floor. The strawberry handed the Tax Guild Stone off to a purple plastic spider, who began to scuttle hastily out the door.

“No you don’t!” A pip of a voice yelled as a fancy sneaker slammed down in front of it. The spider reeled as Tank and the other gashapong shied backwards towards Alex, and as dog and toy retreated, Alex spotted who terrified them.

“Give me that.” Vrshkeuc snarled as she snatched the spider up in her hand. The little toy screamed in horror, but from Alex’s vantage, it didn’t seem like Vrshkeuc was squeezing tight.

“Vrshkeuc?” Alex asked as gashapong moved to hiding behind his ankles, peeking around. The metal pitbull came as well with retracted, scared ears, pressing his big body against Alex.

“Just a moment, lover boy.” Vrshkeuc answered without looking at him. She held the spider up close to her face, who still held the Tax Guild Stone, and glared at it. “Hand it over. Now.”

The spider squeaked back an incomprehensible complaint, then seemed to sigh and release the Tax Guild Stone into Vrshkeuc’s hand.

“Thank you.” Vrshkeuc said as she crouched down and gently released the spider. It hurried back to join it’s kind behind Alex’s ankle.

“We don’t steal from our friends.” Vrshkeuc lectured the gashapong. “We don’t steal from our guests, either. Or our patients. Certainly not from the neighbours. And you do not steal from the underwear store next door. Understand? Now come out and apologize to Alex.”

With sullen body language, the gashapong stepped out from their ankle coverage to form a loose crowd in front of Alex. Those with heads hung them, each attempting to appear properly guilty.

“Say sorry.” Vrshkeuc ordered.

A gaggle of squeaks sounded from the gashapong. There was no chance he understood the language, if it was a language, but he saw Vrshkeuc crack a little smile as she looked up at him with raised eyebrows as if saying, Well? Do you accept their apology?

“Uh…it’s okay. Apology accepted.” Alex said. “Just don’t—uh—steal my stuff? Stealing is bad guys.”

The gashapong hung their heads lower.

“That’s right it’s bad.” Vrshkeuc said. “Now, go back to your…whatever it is you do all day.”

Suddenly the gashapong didn’t look guilty, and they all righted and charged off towards a vent in the floor. A tiny wedge of cheese opened it a crack, and one by one, the tricky gashapong descended into the innards of the Safety Spire’s ventilation, their plastic bodies pinging the metal as they ran off.

“Thanks, and hey, Vrshkeuc. Been a while.” Alex held out a waiting hand, expecting her to hand over the Guild Stone.

She squinted at him, tossing the Tax Guild Stone casually up and down in her palm. Today, the gremlin wore a matching red tracksuit and expensive looking sneakers.

“Hey, mind if I get that back?” Alex asked again, stepping forward.

Vrshkeuc sniffed and spun, walking away with the Stone.

“Walk with me, Alex.” She spoke. “We have some talking to do.”

He thought about [Phantom Step]ping and snatching the Stone from her hand, but Tank made a whining noise as if warning him.

“Uh…okay?” Alex said, somehow needing to speedwalk to catch up to the tiny gremlin. “Can I have like a shirt or something?”

“Talk first. Shirt later. Or maybe not later. Depends.” Vrshkeuc said as she turned a hallway corner.

Alex’s mind still reeled. The last thing he remembered was toasting to Petal and downing Petunia’s tea, remarking how it tasted quite like chocolate milk. Then nothing. Now Vrshkeuc had the Guild Stone and he was in nothing but his boxers.

What the hell happened? Wait—

“Wait. What day is it, Vrshkeuc?” He asked, trailing her around the corner with Tank pressed to his side.

“Tuesday. You were out for two days.”

“Two days?!” He caught up to her right as Vrshkeuc took another winding corner. Alex swore the Safety Spires wasn’t this large before. Or as populated.

Alex passed by an open bedroom. It looked like a simple college room, with a single bed, a dresser, and a desk. Inside was a monster made of swirling smoke sitting at the desk drawing a detailed skyline of Toronto.

“Powerful stuff, that tea.” Vrshkeuc responded before speaking into the room. “Morning, Smoke. Coming down for breakfast?”

“You know I fast till noon! And then me and Stevie are doing hot yoga.” Smoke answered, in what Alex thought was the tone of a grumpy teen. “It’s to help clean out the curse. Gosh, don’t you guys listen to me?”

Alex went to [Investigate] the monster, but Vrshkeuc glanced back at him and shook her head, mouthing, It’s rude.

“Yes, yes, we listen to you.” Vrshkeuc answered, continuing her quick steps. “I’ll keep some aside for you.”

If Smoke had a reaction, Alex didn’t get to see it. Vrshkeuc took a quick left, and they passed more bedrooms. Most were closed, and he heard horrendous snoring coming through one. Some, however, were open. He saw a room that appeared to be fashioned into a forest terrarium, with dirt and trees moistening the air. Another seemed appropriate more appropriate for a young girl rather than a monster. They descended a set of stairs, to what Alex presumed was the second floor, then took a quick left through a swinging door that led to the back stairs of the Safety Spires.

“My patience is tested everyday, Alex.” The gremlin said, stopping and turning to him. “Every damn day. This is the only place for some peace and quiet.”

“It’s busier now. Looks like you got your first guests? Or are they patients?”

“Guests. We call them guests.” Vrshkeuc sighed. “Half crazy, or cursed, or rabbid. Whatever. They’re guests. All in need of help or protection.”

“That’s a lot of responsibility.” Alex said as he eyed the Tax Guild Stone in her hand. “Sure you can handle it?”

“Course I can handle it. Not just me. Snu funds it, and her presence and power soothes them all. Britanni does a lot of the heavy lifting with the mental health side.”

“Oh. Right.” Alex nodded, remembering his girlfriend Snu now employed and housed his ex-girlfriend, Britanni.

“And now she’s making me moderate her online community. Fucking arguing with Bosses all the time and telling them to behave. And you know what dealing with Bosses is like. All nuts.” Vrshkeuc groaned. “Yes, Mistress. Of course, Mistress. Oh, don’t worry, Mistress, I’ll handle it. Some time for myself would be appreciated. For dating? I should really ask for a raise.”

“Have you…talked to her about this?” Alex asked, trying to play nice with the gremlin. “If you’re feeling overworked. You know Snu wants the best for you and the gashapong and her Safety—”

“Oh, shut up, lover boy.” Vrshkeuc marched up to him and Tank. “Just let me complain, damnit. Can’t a gremlin just complain now and then? She’s my Mistress. I chose her. And anything I want I can have. It’s my cousins always giving me hell about it. Say I work too much. Don’t spend enough time with them or whatever.”

Alex hadn’t realized that Vrshkeuc had family. Truthfully, he didn’t know much about her species. “I’m sure gremlin family dynamics are complicated. Sorry you—”

“They’re not even my family family.” Vrshkeuc sighed. “Left those assholes years ago. Did you know I came here from the region of Calgary? That’s where the biggest gremlin population is. Well, least on this continent. Apparently there’s a huge one in the islands out in the pacific.”

“No, I had no idea.” Alex answered as Tank laid down in front of the gremlin and bared his belly. A few minutes ago, Tank was scared of her, but the dog seemed happy as one could be as Vrshkeuc began scratching his belly, his tail smacking hard against the wooden stairs.

Vrshkeuc shrugged. “Eh, the Calgary gremlin leader there is a sexist pig. Wouldn’t let any girls into management positions in the hovel. So, I left and made it out here. Spent a few weeks with distant cousins in their hovel, till I heard of this nice lady running a Dungeon looking for monster employees without turning them into her Dungeon spawn. I owe Snu everything. And life’s great. My cousins are sweet. They’re just constantly up my ass. And their place stinks.”

Alex kneeled down to Vrshkeuc’s level, joining her in scratching Tank’s belly.

“I mean, it’s nice to have family that cares about you. In any sense of the word. Sure, they might be annoying, but not everyone has one. I wish I—”

“Don’t pull that sad orphan shit on me. Didn’t I say I just needed to vent for a sec?” Vrshkeuc snapped her head up to Alex. “You saying you don’t have people that care about you?”

“Well, no, I wasn—”

“Saying your lizard friend and Mary and Emilio don’t have your back?”

“I didn’t mean it like—”

“Or Brody? Huh? You know he went to the Leather Spires multiple times to check on you? Now that he can get here without your help?”

“You know about—”

“Course I know about Brody’s improvements. And Giat has been running him around town showing him everything. She’s the demanding one of the sisters. But he was insistent. Think he doesn’t care about you?”

“Brody is the best. I just wish that I could return the favour. He’s done so—”

“There you go again.” Vrshkeuc interrupted him. “Sad orphan shit. We’ve had six gnomes banging on our doors to get a stupid ‘health report,’. Nina and Nino called ten times. So did Jemin and Mary. Britanni had a two-hour conversation with Francesca about your health. And the gashapong got so annoyed with Mr. Mystical’s constant yammering they started trying to catch him with a bucket.”

Alex gulped, unable to help wondering how that conversation had gone, or if Snu had gotten heated.

Vrshkeuc cackled when she saw his expression. “My Mistress’s jealousy has been tempered, lover boy. Relax. But seriously, quit it with this lonely, sad boy garbage. You have people that care about you.”

Alex hung his head, nodding. “Yeah, I know. Look, it’s just hard to not go down that path. It’s easy and comfortable to wallow.”

Vrshkeuc stood suddenly, so that they were eye-to-eye.

“I’m not doing therapy thing with you. You need to talk to someone? Well, Britanni is off the table. One, she’s too busy helping the monsters here. Two, pretty sure that’s a massive conflict of interest. Ask her for a recommendation.”

“I don’t think there’s therapy for what we’ve all been through.”

“There we go!” Vrshkeuc cackled. “See? You didn’t just immediately refer to your own misery.”

“I’m progressing by leaps and bounds already.” Alex said dryly. “So that’s what you wanted to talk to me about? Can I have a shirt now?”

“Nah, I just needed to vent.” Vrshkeuc sniffed before turning more serious. “And what I really wanted to speak to you about was about how your sad boy act is messing with my Mistress.”

“What?” Alex pulled back. “What are you talking about? What did I do to Snu?”

Vrshkeuc’s expression hardened. “I don’t know what the hell you need to figure out. But your shit is messing with her head. She’s built it up that you’re the key to unlocking her Taking. And I won’t have a boy making her crash. So, figure it out, or we’re going to have a problem. A very serious problem.”

Alex clenched his jaw. Part of the Title of the Heroic Hawk core was delivering that which fills the Taking of the Boss. In his mind, that meant Snu.

“I’m trying, alright?” He grunted.

“Try harder.”

He could argue back, but Alex had to admit, the gremlin had a point. There was trying, and then there was trying your best. Snu was special to him. Incredibly smart, thoughtful, and with a giant heart. She always helped everyone she could. The way she treated her monsters was proof enough and the Safety Spires confirmed it. She was no ordinary woman, and definitely in a class of her own amongst Bosses.

So, rather than argue back and retreat from blame, Alex nodded.

“I’ll figure it out. I promise.”

Vrshkeuc weighed him with her eyes and seemed to come to a decision. The gremlin nodded, then smiled her dangerously sharp teeth at him.

“I guess we’ll see. Here.”

Suddenly the Tax Guild Stone and a simple black t-shirt was thrown at him, fast. Too fast for him to normally catch. Yet, somehow, Alex snatched both out of the air with quick hands.

“Can’t forget the pants.” Vrshkeuc remarked. “Though they’re nice now, nobody wants to stare at your pale legs. Oh, and here’s your phone. Probably think about setting up a better password.”

Similar pants were thrown at him, along with his phone.

“You checked my phone?” He asked as he slipped into the clothes and pocketed the Tax Guild Stone.

She winked at him, ignored his question, and turned, hopping down the stairs. Vrshkeuc made a sound as if she was calling a horse.

“Let’s go, Tank. They’re waiting. You too, lover boy.”

“They?” He asked. “Who’s waiting?”

Tank leapt up and followed her happily. Alex tapped his phone to check it, but it was dead. He sighed and bustled after the gremlin and dog, who banged through the door to the first floor of the Safety Spires. The sound of loud conversation flowed out.

Tilting his head, Alex was curious to know who waited on the other side, and when he pushed it open, he stopped dead in his tracks.

“He’s here! It’s murder’n’pulp’n time.” A chittering voice yelped from the dining table. It was a squirrel with fury in its eyes.

“About time you woke up.” Another commented sarcastically, and upon spotting her, Alex’s jaw unhinged another inch. Mary had her feet up on the table, leaning to brush her hand on the person to her right.

“Dude, I was worried sick.” A slick, relaxed voice added, and Alex spotted the poncho. Jemin lounged casually in his own chair, clawed hang on a stuffed duffel bag.

“Alex! My dear boy!” A small, preserved hand waved, followed by the mechanical whirr gears sending a robot arm waving. “You really should come visit me. I told you that you are always welcome at the Science Center. I was equally worried like your delightful lizard friend.”

“OUR WORRY KNEW NO BOUNDS.” A drone toned, snapping off his hovering lotus sit to land dramatically onto the dining table.

Alex was still stun locked. The group was not what he’d been expecting in the slightest.

Jemin and Mary sat on the far side of the table, grinning at him. Beepy stood, hands on hips and posed thoughtfully on the table. Aria sat next to rabid looking squirrel. They shared the lap of what appeared to be a little human girl with bloodred eyes. His friends were surprising. The monsters made more sense, seeing as he was in the Safety Spires.

He had not been expecting Professor Neuronium, Dungeon Boss of the Science Center, to be sitting there as well.

“What are you all doing here?” Alex finally asked, rushing forward to the table. “Professor Neuronium? Mary? Jemin? Beepy and Aria? Uh…” He stopped, looking at the little girl monster and the squirrel. “Hello, you two. I don’t think we’ve met. I’m Alex.”

“We know who you are. I’m Stevie. The squirrel is Nut.” The little girl said in a voice that sounded ancient.

Professor Neuronium laughed from his jar. “I should get out more often. Seeing your reaction is reason enough.”

Alex turned to the Boss. “It’s great to see you all. But seriously, what are you all doing here?”

Before anyone could answer him, the kitchen door banged open, and a new voice called.

“Breakfast time!” It shouted, and Alex’s eyebrows rose further up his scalp. It was a monster, made mostly of translucent, colourful blue goo. And it was carrying a dozen platters of food on melty appendages.

A rush of squeaking gashapong followed the monster out of the kitchen.

“Can someone answer me!?” Alex voice rose. “What is everyone doing here? And Aria, Beepy, where is Zippy?”

“SOLO MISSION. A QUEST FOR THE STRUCTURE OF JUSTICE IN THIS DARK WORLD.” Beepy answered for the pair.

“Bah!” Aria chimed in. “He’s just running an errand. Chill, Beep.”

“That’s great.” He said, staring down as gashapong climbed up him like little monkeys. “And what is everyone doing here? Someone, please?”

“Isn’t it obvious?” A familiar voice called, sending a momentary chill down Alex’s spine.

He turned toward the source of the speaker, having to remember they weren’t enemies any longer. While they weren’t trying to kill or maim each other these days, the old wounds still surfaced.

That was until he saw her eyes. They were tattooed black throughout, as was much of the rest of her body. Her red hair was clipped to a buzz. For all intents and purposes, she looked terrifying, but her eyes held no malice, and if anything, they sparkled with warmth.

“Britanii. Hi.” Alex said as warmly as he could.

She strode in carrying a platter piled high with pancakes, placing it on the table, and carefully moved gashapong that climbed her like a tree to more comfortable positions.

“Hi.” Britanni smiled earnestly at him. “You look well rested.”

“Can someone please, for the love of the System, tell me why everyone is here waiting for me?”

“ALLOW ME.” Beepy stepped forward off the table, hovering perfectly to not break his step along the air.

The robot walked a slow circle, tilting his chin up to carry his speaker.

“ALEX, YOUR ALLIES AND FRIENDS ALIKE HAVE AIDED YOU IN YOUR TRIALS. A BOSS SEVERED FROM THE SYSTEM. A LICH DESTROYED. NOW, BY MERIT, CHANCE, OR CHOICE, YOU STAND AGAIN AT THE CENTER.”

“Get to the point, Beepy, please.” Aria groaned.

Beepy ignored her heckle, coming to hover stand in front of Alex and clasp his hands behind his back.

“WE OFFER YOU AIDE UNREQUITED. OUR COLLECTIVE SPIRITS AND MINDS UNITED. WE WILL COUNCIL ON YOUR SKILLS, TITLES, AND ANY ADVANTAGE YOU MAY GARNER. TO WHAT END, YOU ASK?”

Beepy took a dramatic pause, letting his question sink in.

“WHY, TO KILL A BOSS, OF COURSE. THE BEAST OF BLIGHTWOOD SHALL DIE. BUT THERE IS MORE.”

“My grandmother.” Alex croaked out. “Fatima.”

“YES.” Beepy continued. “BUT SHE IS MORE THAN JUST A SYSTEM USER.”

Alex tilted his head. “Huh? Then she’s like Nina? Had magic before the System?”

No one answered his question, and they all looked around at one another with his question on his face.

Beepy cleared his throat unnecessarily, but Aria cut in.

“Exactly. We don’t know what she is. But she’s not a monster. Or a Boss. She’s not even a Lich. But she’s something, alright. Something different.”

“REST ASSURED, ANYTHING THAT IS CAN DIE ALL THE SAME.”

“Delightfully dramatic!” Professor Neuronium pipped up, clapping his metal hands together for Beepy’s performance. “Now sit, Alex. I heard you had quite the weekend. Tell us your skills, and if you got any upgrades on waking.”

A chair, carried by a hundred gashapong, was suddenly pressed at his knees, and Alex was brought to the breakfast table with his friends. Everything happening so quickly had his mind in a whirl, but he’d been asleep for two days. Not to mention he’d run the airport, claimed the Tax Guild Stone, barely survived, and ran full tilt across the city while emotional.

I am starving.

“Can we eat first?” He asked, already helping himself to a plate of Britanii’s pancakes. “And where’s Snu? She should be here if we’re going over everything. You guys have been busy planning without me again.”

Vrshkeuc barked a laugh as she dragged her own chair over. “She, Brody, and Giat are busy arguing for your life. Don’t worry, you’ll be fine. Probably.”

For some reason, everyone thought that was hilarious, all laughing together. Alex did not find it funny at all.
