While Maurice worked on vibrating himself out of Henry’s grasp from the sheer excitement at being in literal space and having Velistraine in front of them, the latter went on and recounted the hellish experience she’d been dealing with for, in her words, the last two weeks.

To Henry, that was already a bit of a mind-bender.

“You’re sure it’s been just two weeks and not like… this place messing with your head?” he hedged, but Velistraine vehemently shook her head, and out of her ring, a small contraption that Henry was pretty sure he’d seen once or twice appeared.

With a nudge, the device lit up, and Velistraine raised her arm. “I’ve got my time-keeper, see? And I’ve been counting. It’s been fifteen days and seventeen hours since I’ve gotten here. Give or take a couple hours. Why? How long has it been on your end? What happened after the fight? Did Attal make it?”

Henry stared at the cube-like object, thoughts whirling. Even Maurice seemed a little taken aback as the two shared a look before turning back to Velistraine. “It’s been… something like two months from our side,” Henry finally said as he considered the implication. Was this phenomenon caused by this location, or was this the work of the System? If it was the latter, it might make sense. It’d be another measure to prepare the world for the infestation surrounding it, because as they talked with Velistraine, it really was a fitting term for what was happening.

“They’re everywhere. Things that look like what was in Attal’s basement,” she continued, though she definitely seemed to also be considering the difference in the flow of time. Henry was also still considering the issue, but one thing immediately came to mind, and it was that they’d need to get back home as fast as possible. A quick calculus told him that every hour out here was nearly four hours in the hidden world, and he’d rather not waste too much of it.

Velistraine’s eyes were bloodshot, constantly roving around. “Obviously, they’re not just here on this branch,” she added, nodding toward what Henry saw as the paved road, “but they come here often. When I went into the rift after the big one, it had led us here, which was lucky, otherwise I’d be dead already due to the lack of air. On the other hand, I can’t ever stop watching my surroundings or they’ll get the drop on me. Made that mistake once. Can’t risk it again,” she muttered, and Henry decided to keep that in mind.

Stealth might be important out here. But to make the most of things, they needed to understand where they were. Because this… constantly shifting structure made no sense. There was no planet anywhere near, so how were they breathing? Why weren’t they floating away? Clearly, he wasn’t the only one thinking about this, though not for the exact same reason.

Maurice was just curious.

“What is this place, exactly?” the crab asked, and Henry nodded. He was very curious as well. But Velistraine just shrugged, then transitioned that into a pained neck stretch, grimacing all the meanwhile.

“No clue. It’s—I’ve never seen anything like it. I’ve got some ideas, but I’ve been too busy trying to survive than studying whatever this is, honestly. I had to escape their notice. One at a time, they’re easy enough to handle but…”

Henry dipped his head, eyeing the state she was in. He remembered very well the telepathic attack he’d gotten hit with just by examining the half-corpse of the Void Lord. He’d bet those telepathic abilities were a much bigger problem when they were alive.

“They’re never alone, are they?”

Velistraine shook her head. “Never. And if somehow you manage to get the drop on one, they make this… mental screech that gets a hundred of them on your ass in no time.”

“Is that why you’re so banged up? Was it them that got your arm?” Maurice asked.

Velistraine blinked at him, then let out a tired half-chuckle, half-sigh that made her hunch a little more than she already was. She went to raise her severed arm to look at it, but Henry clicked his beak and pulled it back down as he was busy examining it, which was why he answered before she could. “This doesn’t feel like normal B-rank or even A-rank essence. It was that big one, wasn’t it? Please don’t tell me there’s more than one of those.”

The creature was still haunting his half-awoken dreams, with its mirthful amusement at their terror as it tried to tear a hole into their world.

Velistraine shook her head, biting her lip, then turned her attention back to their surroundings, the lights of thousands of stars reflected in her gaze. “I didn’t see another like it, but that doesn’t mean there aren’t. But yeah, I got beat up when I tried to ambush one of ‘em. Took me days to lose the rest. As for the arm, I lost it when I was trying to get away from the big one. I can definitely tell you that it didn’t like how that scuffle turned out,” Velistraine said, grimacing a little as Henry worked on her stump.

The captain had effectively flushed out the foreign essence, but, in a way, that was akin to extinguishing the fire from a log of wood. Sure, the cause of the damage was dealt with, but the wood itself had been damaged and burnt, and just like that log, there was something off about Velistraine’s injury. It was as if her body and soul had forgotten that it could still extend further down the limb. Now, Life Surge could theoretically heal both physical and soul damage, and Henry knew that he was making progress, one thin layer of tissue at a time. It was just that he had to constantly push against Velistraine’s innate resistance and the injury’s unwillingness to comply. If he could use Shapeshift on her, he’d bet he could fix it faster, but that skill only worked on himself. As it was, he needed to keep channeling his healing magic down the stump, and let it slowly and continuously soak, using both the skill and Lifeweaving to reverse the damage. It was methodical work that took some decent amount of focus, but it was working. Which made it a good task for one of his Octominds. What was harder to fix was the occasional twitching of the woman every so often. Henry had already flooded her body with Life Surge, looking for a cause, but he hadn’t picked up anything, which meant that this might be lack of sleep. Or trauma. Or both.

In short, nothing he could fix right here and right now.

Still, as time went by, Henry could see some definitive improvement to Velistraine’s baseline when compared to how they found her. She took a second to yawn, then got her smile back. “As for that big bastard. I think I figured out something interesting about it. I’d love to show it to you guys, but I’m in no state of going back there, and one of you two is still too green,” she added, giving Maurice a side-eyed smirk.

“Hey!” Maurice protested, swatting at her, but Velistraine just chuckled before she gently punched Henry’s arm. “Congrats, by the way. You made it. How are you liking A-rank so far?”

Henry grinned, which, as a kraken, didn’t look like much, so he absentmindedly summoned his own illusion to smooth out the communication. “Pretty fun. Got to immediately put the new boost in practice because of a couple of Merfolk that just had to keep bothering us, then I got here. All in all? It hasn’t even been twenty minutes since I got the rank. But it feels good!”

Velistraine barked out a laugh. “Of course you did. Which ones? From Serpent’s Pass or the ones in the Reach.”

“The latter.”

Velistraine grunted. “Figures. They’re not very diplomatic when they know they’re lacking leverage, and they’re even worse when they have it.”

“Yeah, pretty much,” Henry nodded. “One of them really wanted me to be part of his menagerie. Some sort of beast master, apparently. He’s dead now.”

Velistraine whistled, then flinched, as if reminded of where she was. Still, she asked, voice low. “That’s bad. Was it one of their A-ranks? They’ll be pretty pissed. Is that why you ran here? They sent one of their Guardians at you?”

Henry looked up, then slowly turned to Maurice to find the crab rubbing his claws together, pride wafting off of him as he met his gaze.

“Uh… the dude we killed was one of their Guardians I think,” Henry began. “The normal A-ranks weren’t much. We ran from their Depth Lord. Actually, we ran because we got pincered between their Depth Lord and another human that managed to fight him back.”

Velistraine’s already gawking jaw hung even lower. “You fought their Depth Lord? Who was the other one?”

“Short man. Bearded. Had a pickaxe.”

Velistraine blinked. “Wolf’s Den’s Warden?”

Henry’s human form snapped its finger. “Yup, that’s it.”

The captain stared at them, leaning back for a moment, only for Maurice to click his pincers at her.

“Who’s green now, huh. We’re catching up to you.”

Velistraine slowly turned to Maurice, then pointed to Henry. “He is. You’re still B-rank.”

Maurice stared at her for a beat, then he chucked an apple at her.

***

For a moment, the trio just sat around and talked, eating, resting, while Henry worked on healing Velistraine’s arm. They spoke of her crew, of their foray into the Reach, of its peoples, and slowly, the conversation turned back to their current predicament. Specifically, that big creature and its kind.

Velistraine glanced behind her, toward the horizon where the road stretched far into the nothingness of space. She was quiet for a moment, then she spoke. “It might be still there. Down the branch. There’s… a massive door that way. If you keep following the path, I mean. I haven’t had the chance to look at it or even cast Identify as I was trying to get the heck away. But I’ve never seen anything like it. It—it’s like…”

Velistraine paused, looking for the right words before she shook her head. “You’ll have to see it. If you told me gods were real and they were somewhere, I’d tell you that door might lead to whoever created those gods. It made me feel tiny. Like a speck. I couldn’t even look at it straight. Had to steal glances. But also, there were a lot of them creatures near it, and it’s obvious that they’re trying to get through that door. The big one is trying to get through, but it can’t.”

Henry and Maurice listened, enraptured, and while Henry looked past her toward the horizon, Maurice asked. “Do you think our world’s behind it?”

Velistraine seemed to consider it for a second, but the confident answer came too fast. She had already thought of it. “I don’t think so. It very well might be, but I’d be surprised. That door felt way more important. It felt like it was beyond this whole place. What I think is this. If the System came from somewhere, and now it’s isolated, it’s because that door over there is shut. If I’m right, then that door leads to other places our System can’t reach. Places the damn insects want to spread out to, but they can’t. They’re trapped here, I think. Along with us all. As for our world… well, I bet our crafty System figured out a way to hide us in all the empty space around. That would explain why the bugs haven’t fallen on us like a damn swarm.”

Henry listened intently. As he did, he began crafting a swarm of his own. One to gorge himself on the invaders with and hopefully, to find the way back when they’re ready.
