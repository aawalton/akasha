Having chosen a career in which the death of companions was treated as nothing more than an inconvenience, Harry did allow himself to grieve. I'm dead. He thought to himself. The tower collapsed and I was either ejected or crushed, either of which would result in my death. Even so, he kept conjecturing the possible endings he might have had. Wait...

Submerged in a dense substance that seemed to stretch infinitely, Harry imagined this was what Manes felt when covered in shadows. It was a cold material, but as it enveloped him, it felt like the flame of a fireplace in the forest. Perhaps that was what death meant; an eternal embrace of winter. But no. Well, maybe it did mean that, but not in that moment. The Locksmith felt something solid beneath the soles of his feet, or what seemed to be his feet.

But what... He looked down, only to see part of his body being materialized out of nothing. Like a supporting character in a dream, he only realized he still existed, in some plane, when he saw the protagonist of the place being materialized; a door.

Each of his steps echoed through generations of thoughts. If he thought of something or someone, he could almost hear or even see the place he imagined. That's it. This is the last call of a dying thief. And even believing the door would lead to the end of who he was, Harry did not seem to retreat. Solid as a spider's web, the black floor seemed to oscillate with the Locksmith's steps as he marched toward the mysterious creation.

The door was like those he conjured with his Emblem. The edges were straight and sharp. Carved into the sides were various generic patterns, as if someone had a very basic understanding of what a door consists of. The doorknob? It had no texture whatsoever. Holding the hard sphere, Harry opened the door.

Inadvertently, the young thief smiled when he saw what was on the other side. The Earth, as if registering his reaction, seemed to smile back with the wide curves of its continents and the sharp tips of its highest mountains. Clouds swirled around it like a mantle made of cosmic foam. The water, in exactly the right shade of blue, surrounded the land. Its size, however, was astronomical. He knew the planet he lived on was certainly not small, but observing it in all its glory was almost... artistic. And terrifying.

He stepped through the door and immediately felt the change in gravity. The whitish-gray ground seemed to repel Harry, given how long it took him to fall back onto it. Full of craters, the fissures near the Locksmith would certainly swallow him whole, which did not make him any less anxious. He almost stumbled again as he found his footing in this strange gravity.

Why am I on the moon? He added inwardly as he frowned and looked around. Directly behind Harry, the door seemed to close on its own, and before he could return to the infinite darkness, he found himself stranded on the lunar surface. Right... He seemed to laugh at his own unpleasant situation and at how his mind had conjured something so fitting.

"It is because this is not your creation." A thorny voice, but with the sound of a talking pillow, rang in his mind. "It is mine."

As if the words had pulled the air from his lungs, Harry went quiet, waiting for the voice to continue. When she did not indulge his wishes, Harry, in a reflexive manner, tidied up his tie and got surprised by the fact he was wearing his same old attire.

"...Where am I?" He asked, looking around carefully. "And who are you?"

"That's a question you have asked a lot of times, isn't it?" The voice seemed almost playful. "Are you fond of riddles?"

"Um..." He paused for a moment. "Yes. Please, do go on."

"I live in the shadows, but am not afraid of the light." The voice began. "I can represent reason, but long yearn for an emotional connection. This rock of yours can be my deathbed, but is not my birth. What am I?"

If it wasn't for his last encounter on the last floor, with Raven, Harry might have lost a few minutes or even hours trying to decipher it. The demeanor, which reminded the young thief of the masked entity, soon became clear.

"Logos." Harry replied, confident in his answer.

"You're correct, man of doors." Logos said, calmly.

"So what do I owe you the pleasure?" Harry gave a smile as he simply stared at Earth.

The entity's laugh was similar to the clashing of strings in an out-of-tune lute. "It really is a pleasure. You're the only thing I could salvage from the Tower. I assume the rest was... forgotten."

So the tower did collapse...

"What happened with Raviel?" Harry asked, eager. "What did he use it for?"

In giving the kid the power of the God Emblem, he had taken a risk. Of course, he could imagine what had happened, but Harry needed confirmation.

"The child shattered the object and attained what you refer to as godhood for a couple of seconds." The entity said, conjuring the image of the object shattering in front of the Locksmith. "He used it to undo the happenings of that fateful day. By not looking at the telescope and summoning me, Cardley Logos never panicked and, therefore, the Logos Tower was never created."

"...Why did you do that?" His relief was soon followed by an unnerving question that had been eating at him from the inside. "Why did you corrupt that poor child with your... your antics? Hadn't he suffered enough?"

"Does a river control where it flows?" Logos asked. "It does not. It is the shape of the land that determines it."

"So you're dodging the blame?" His voice was raspy. "That's easy to do now that everything has been corrected..."

"This..." The entity seemed to sigh. "I cannot apologize for my nature, Locksmith. But I do understand what I have caused, and for those actions, I am in debt to all of humanity."

"...What happened back then? How could it happen?"

The young thief kept looking around, afraid that what happened to Raviel could happen to him. Even hearing the creature's voice could madden a listener. Although Harry assumed it wasn't using its "real" voice.

"When Cardley Logos first discovered me, he was careful. I am actually many, and there are parts of myself that even I cannot fully understand. Even though we are together, loneliness finds a way to strike us. We thought that sleep could help undermine that strange feeling, but it only raised a more important question: what is our purpose? And then you came along. Humans with your technology and cities. We saw the great blocks that rose from the land and the pieces of wood that shattered the unwavering waves. It was a spectacle."

"Until the crack was big enough that one of you found one of... us." Harry concluded.

"Yes..." It replied in an almost melancholic way. "Your moon couldn't withstand all the pressure, at least not until you were advanced enough to leave the planet. And once the Astronomer saw us, we saw him. I for one hadn't interacted with the man as the others had; they were eager to have someone to talk to. But our gaze is... damaging to your species. Cardley showed us pictures from his offspring. So many drawings. They were spacious and unrepresentative of reality. A mountain falling on itself like an hourglass. Waves circling around a floating island. That's what we were missing, this... imagination."

"So Raven wasn't speaking for all of you?" The Locksmith asked.

"He was not." Logos's voice was certain, with a tone that made Harry's whole body shudder. "I, for one, cannot blame him; perhaps I would have reacted the same. He was fed the sensitive emotions of a human child and began to experience time in a way we never did. He is no longer with us. We are afraid he was forgotten even by the universe itself."

That was more than he could have hoped for in a conclusion. Most of his questions had been answered, and the dream of knowing the end of the Logos Tower was his alone to relish. That, however, raised concerns about his friends and family; where were they now? If their lives continued as before, they would die. Emblems, such as they were, had defined the mark of their doom. Okay, don't need to get ahead of myself... Harry thought to himself, figuring out what was left behind in this whole ordeal. What about the Metas...?

"Oh, they are a... funny thing." Logos replied, startling Harry once again as he forgot the entity could likely read his mind.

"Shit!" The Locksmith blurted out as he quickly covered his mouth.

"I do apologize." It continued. "It isn't in my interest to intrude on your personal... thoughts. We have already intervened enough."

"It's, um... it's alright." He scratched the back of his head.

"The Metas, as you call them, are pieces of ourselves, lost in the process. Time has a delicate way with you mortals. It stretches and lingers until your last breath, constantly wavering its existence in your face. We are afraid it doesn't... feel like that to us, however. Describing it to you, Locksmith, would be as effective as teaching a whale how to walk again. What I can say, though, is that the Metas embodied those properties as they clashed with the Tower's nature. Just like us, they evolved in a concise way through the many floors the Logos Tower housed."

Harry, after the revelation, began remembering a couple of drawings he had seen on the Astronomer's table and soon reached a conclusion. The Metas' appearance on each floor and precisely how they evolved had always felt strange to Harry. Their presence on the fourth floor, the floating island, for example, felt lackluster. Merely ornamental, the Metas of that floor represented plankton and bacteria on the raging waters. But all around the Colosseum, they had taken the form of a pirate boat. It was surely a work of cooperation that seemed to have a logic behind it, but still didn't explain why some floors made the creatures intelligent, while on others they barely existed.

Logos, eager to understand Harry's conclusion, restrained itself from breaking his train of thought. Although it was a passive property of knowledge, like understanding a language one is fluent in, it decided not to; the young thief had been through enough.

"...The Metas represent the second part of the drawings..." The Locksmith muttered to himself.

"Care to explain your conclusion, Harry?" The entity replied, trying to sound as calming as a cosmic being could.

"They, just like Raven, were bound by the Tower when the whole Astronomer Incident took place. The Metas evolved, but they took inspiration, perhaps unconsciously, from the drawings. When the Logos Tower was created, and the floors came to life as a reaction to Raven's birth, they also came, and with it, throughout the loops, merged to form the final version of each floor. The Metas in the mountain were hourglass shaped because that was what the drawing resembled. Those on the floating island were so small we couldn't really see their appearance, but I'm sure it had something to do with it. Those in the Colosseum were being transformed into boats and so on... I think even the mask on top of the church was a Meta, now that I think about it..."

"That... makes sense." Logos said. "The creation of the Logos Tower was so far out of proportion that I am afraid even we cannot decipher all of its mysteries. Our power is much more of a cooperative effort than coming from a singular source; we rarely saw it used together in such a way."

"Which brings me to my final question..." The Locksmith gulped. This was it. "What happened to the God Emblem?"

Logos appeared to sigh. "You wish to know why you're here, isn't that right? Understand that what Raven said was true. With such a power, you can only change a singular happening from the past, and that has already been done by Raviel; the Emblem is gone."

"But Raven was also asking me to get him out. Wouldn't that count as two separate events?"

"The consequence of you using the so-called God Emblem was his release. The force would expose him to us, which in turn would end his suffering. The Logos Tower, however, would probably still exist, as the time when your mother died happened after the Tower's creation, so it wouldn't affect the birth of Raven."

This stung Harry's heart, but he raised his head, flattening out his own dread. He could have saved his mother, but that wouldn't have changed the fate of his companions.

"So..." Harry said, confused. "What am I to do?"

"We have agreed on helping you, Locksmith." Logos replied with its unison of voices, a camaraderie Harry would never fully understand. Actually, now he would, just not to such an... existential degree. "Since the Logos Tower never existed, everyone who ever set foot in it, dead or alive, will be returned to their bodies, correctly placed in time. Randomly, of course. For you, though, we can put in a little effort."

Wait, what? Harry's heart felt like a tank engine. Thump! Thump! It roared and beat as though there were no tomorrows.

"But..." He tried not to sound out of breath. "You know I would change her fate, right? Even if it killed me."

"Sorry to disappoint you, Locksmith." Logos began. "But you cannot. Time can heal itself if not directly harmed. In one way or another, the story would end with you and your brother Matthews as orphans. We can allow change to be made when it was directly our doing, as it is with the Emblems, but your mother's death had nothing to do with us. She will die no matter what. Perhaps later with the God Emblem, but we cannot be certain."

"But I could buy some time!" He replied, confident. "I could keep delaying it until—"

And then he saw the problem; he couldn't keep delaying it. In some way he would fail, perhaps even thanks to destiny and time itself. He would feel the pain doubled and the guilt would never go away. What good was there in prolonging her suffering for his sake? Her story was over in this life. She had lived as the woman she was supposed to be, and even with so little time, she had loved Harry and Matthews more than he ever could in a million lifetimes. And that was saying a lot.

"I..." He sighed deeply. "Fuckin' hell."

"Fuck indeed." The phrasing felt strange on Logos's echoed voice, but Harry didn't comment on it.

"...I still have a wish, right...?" The Locksmith asked. "Since, you know, your existence almost provoked humanity's downfall."

"You use manipulation skills on us. It won't work. But yes, we... owe you. Regardless of what you choose to do with it."

"...Will the moon be okay with you in it?"

"Attaining control wasn't easy, but we believe it won't shatter as before. We will maintain it."

"I wish for you to leave the moon." Harry said simply, even though his heart was beating rapidly. In the end, he was essentially asking a superbeing to leave its home.

"...Why?" The curiosity seemed genuine. "With the moon not breaking, humanity would be saved. Without us, Cardley Logos won't find the Emblems. You won't have the powers you love so much. We can sense it in you. The adrenaline you feel."

"I..." This was going to be hard. The Locksmith sighed deeply. "I love it. The wind in my hair and the free-fall make me feel alive and free. But we aren't made for it. Humanity isn't made for it. One rotten apple is sometimes rotten enough to bring the destruction of an entire tree. I can't allow that, not when I can prevent it."

"That is... noble." It said after a few moments. "Which conflicts with your nature as a thief. It troubles us. We will think about it later. We shall also accept your terms. Although it will be difficult to find others like your kind. We would also like to apologize. We didn't see it that way. Emotions are... strange."

"...I don't blame you, you know? It was out of your control."

"We do."

"...I see." Harry replied, then smiled. "Well, I hope you can find another entertaining civilization out in the galaxy, although I find it unlikely. You know, we're pretty weird sometimes."

Logos gave a sincere laugh, this time almost human enough for Harry not to notice the imperfections. "You, specifically, Locksmith, gave us a great deal of entertainment. We appreciate it, lord of thieves."

Although not certain he was a lord of anything, Harry did not correct them. The Locksmith then looked back at the door he had come through, and then back at Earth, a gigantic continent smiling back at him. He would hug his brother until his bones broke. He would help the old librarian who lived in the capital. Harry would live every second he could wring from the world as if he hadn't sacrificed himself for its love. For her love. In a way, all he ever wanted was to see her again, wasn't it? To tell his mother she was special. To ask her if he was worthy of her love.

"Can I ask you for one more thing?" Harry asked, his eyes teary.

"...Yes?"

"Is it hard to bring them their memories as well?"

"...Not particularly, no." Logos said. "But what good would that serve you? Only Madeline and Matthews would be able to contact you."

"This story isn't only about me." The Locksmith replied, smiling.

"I see." The voice said as the door opened, revealing a blinding light on the other side.

Harry's life was out of his control. And he was okay with that. Since he does rather well in it, doesn't he?

"Time!" He exclaimed to himself. "We can never get enough of you, can we?"

The Locksmith entered the door. His door.

(THE END)
