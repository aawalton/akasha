Chapter 63

A Year of Changes

The first months of the war under the global coalition was a routine marked by rapid missions and tests in simulated battlefields. New prototypes and equipment were trialed, and if proven effective, moved on to mass production.

The entire hidden world had lived through the beginning with the weight of a secret they could not share—one that, in the end, the world had to know. The truth had finally been revealed to the world.

The hidden world was no longer hidden.

For many, the announcement was a shocking blow. Disbelief overtook the majority, and some could hardly process it. A large majority of the public chose not to believe it for days or weeks, even going so far as to suggest that it was a ploy to divert public attention from a war in which the Fractus were gaining the upper hand on all fronts, even though they were creatures without human intelligence. For others, especially those who had experienced the climate problems, Dark Events and war over resources in previous years and its strange facets, it was merely a confirmation of what they had already suspected.

From the start of the conflict, stories had circulated in physical and digital trenches about soldiers with unusual abilities—tales of individuals who were not entirely human, whose capacities defied logic. These accounts gained traction on forums, where conspiracy theories and speculations grew like weeds.

During the first months, online discussions teemed with ancient theories, hidden beings, and myths of creatures that had lived among humanity for centuries without anyone noticing. Stories of humans with unexplained gifts—not referring to espers—of warriors operating in the shadows, of beings who had waited centuries, perhaps millennia, for the right moment to reveal themselves.

But now the hidden world was no longer just legends. The truth was on the table.

For Mai, the months following the announcement were not as chaotic as she expected. The news left her worried, but also somewhat relieved. Those tears she had shed on the day of the revelation proved to her that, despite her lack of faith and her worries, she still had faith. Her experiences and the hardships she had endured over the years almost made her think that perhaps it was better for the hidden world to remain as it was—that might have been for the best. But in today’s world, that gap was narrowing more and more, and the first step had been taken. Finally, the secrets that had been guarded for so long were coming to light.

Yet a shadow of concern loomed over her. While the revelation of the feys and magic marked the end of an era of concealment, it also brought a sea of unanswered questions. Mai did not know what impact the revelation would have in the near future. War, with its unpredictable nature, could last far longer than anyone expected. And if that were the case, what would happen when it ended?

The thought of endless conflict troubled her. She knew it from her years in Wishper—not just for the feys, but for all species: humans, espers, magicians, everyone. If the conflict dragged on long enough, what would remain of them? Of all of them? She wrestled with that question repeatedly, watching events unfold before her eyes. Though she had long awaited the revelation, now that it had arrived, something inside her feared the future.

What worried her most was the possibility that, in the end, the differences between peoples, between beings, could destroy them all. Humanity had thousands of years of history marked by wars, divisions, and conflicts. How would it react now upon realizing it was not alone in the world? Not even in the universe, for that matter. That the feys not only existed but lived among them, sharing the same planet? That extraterrestrial species were lurking in invisible dimensions of space?

Worse still, how could she balance her loyalty to her people, her friends, and comrades with the new demands of a humanity that might now become even more fractured? After all, it’s not as if the feys were saints. They too had been part of the problem. She had been part of the problem too.

Mai knew it and she admitted it. More than once, she had been forced to kill feys, espers, or magicians, and of course humans who, even without any gifts, had chosen to walk on the darker side of the hidden world.

Conversations on the island had changed. Now, everyone spoke of the future, of what needed to be done, of how to adapt to the new reality. The feys who had hidden among the crowd for centuries were now known. And with them came questions, fears, and inevitable division.

“What will happen when all this is over?” Mai wondered—the same question that repeated itself in her mind.

The conflict was far from over. Yes, something had taken on a new dimension with the arrival of the Fractus and the revelation of the hidden world. But deep down, she knew the true battle was not only against the fractus or the hidden world. It was also against the perceptions, fears, and prejudices of those who, now confronted with the new crazy development, did not know how to process it. And how someone said, the greatest enemy is the fear of the unknown… or it was that the bigger fear was the fear of the unknown. In any case, it was the same thing.

Mai watched the headlines unfolding across the web with a mixture of disbelief and exhaustion. The revelation of the feys had shaken the foundations of the world, but what came next only served to further destabilize the structures of global power. For weeks, the world had been caught in a whirlwind of doubts, theories, and reactions. Humanity suddenly found itself face-to-face with what it had only considered myths and legends. And as people struggled to process this new reality, something else happened: the organizations that had operated in the shadows, those that had maintained the secret for decades, finally emerged into the light.

The reactions were not unanimous. At first, the world’s most powerful nations tried to contain the wave of information, hoping it wouldn’t spread too widely, but chaos was inevitable. It was better to act quickly, the cat was already out of the bag. One by one, the organizations that had played a crucial role in the protection and study of feys, espers, and magicians began to make their stories public.

The Armitage Foundation, born from the heart of Miskatonic University, was the first to step into the open. The foundation had spent decades conducting research on feys, Dark Events, and other paranormal phenomena. Despite its hidden work, its contributions to understanding the entities that lived at the edges of reality were significant. How many human lives had been saved thanks to their efforts? How many secrets, long unspoken, had remained locked away in their archives? Mai couldn’t help but think of the thousands of classified files, the studies that would still remain under lock and key, the dark days when feys and other beings were little more than whispers in the corridors of universities and laboratories. Though, no matter how much of the hidden world was revealed, it wasn’t as if 99% of people would gain access to classified information.

It was more of a: “Hey, You! Yes! You! This exists. Live with it. If you want to know more, do so through the proper channels.”

Next came the SIGN Group from England, a mystical organization dedicated to studying magic over the last few decades, of which Emmeline was a part. Though apparently tasked with protecting the United Kingdom in magical terms, the institution had been at the forefront of researching supernatural entities, feys, and events that defy logic. They had helped contain magical threats and, on occasion, had worked secretly alongside government agencies. While the English magical community was aware of these secrets, the world at large had no idea such an organization even existed.

Then came Pyrene, more recent—a secret network operating in the Pyrenees, whose reach extended far beyond the borders of Spain and France. Under the leadership of Mari, who had headed the organization for decades, Pyrene had provided refuge and protection to feys, espers, and magicians throughout Europe. Unknown to most, hundreds of thousands of people lived under the network’s guidance, which offered not only safety but also a separate political and social structure, isolated from the outside world. The general population had largely never known of its existence, yet Pyrene had been crucial in maintaining balance between humans and beings from other dimensions.

And then came the revelation most impactful for Mai, and probably for many others: Vería, the Russian organization operating under the same premise. Vería had always prided itself on secrecy dating back to post Second World War times. In recent decades, it had focused on researching dimensional anomalies, and its influence on the Russian government had been critical in protecting the magical border in Northern Europe. That they had concentrated on dimensional anomalies raised some eyebrows given the appearance of the fractus—but they claimed no involvement.

Finally, Longwang, the secret network of China, was uncovered. Its task was to track anomalies and protect feys and other entities across its vast territory. China, with its complex relationship with the occult even during communism, had also been a key ally in keeping these secrets contained.

In the following days, other organizations across different continents also began to emerge into the light.

And amidst all of this were Nevermore and Wishper.

Mai felt a mix of pride and apprehension as the names of these organizations came to light. They had operated under the radar, working in secret to protect feys, espers, and all those beings who otherwise would have been hunted or exterminated while studying the supernatural and Dark Events. But now, like the other organizations, they were forced to emerge, like a rabbit from a hat saying, “You knew I was here.” Nevermore, the organization that had changed so many lives, that had been a refuge, a home for many, could no longer hide the face it had kept from the public eye.

The world was beginning to grasp what had been hidden for so long. Societies, religions, governments—all would have to reconsider their structures in light of the revelation of the feys’ existence. The mix of politics, cultures, and beliefs was on the verge of erupting into a series of philosophical and ethical questions. How would religions—or rather, the believers—handle the fact that beings considered mythological for millennia were now walking among them? How would power-play dynamics between nations shift upon learning that some had been secretly collaborating with forces and entities beyond human comprehension?

Mai couldn’t stop thinking about the weight of what lay ahead.

The future was no longer what she had imagined. The truth had been unleashed, and with it, a new era was beginning to take shape before her eyes. But what she did not know—what no one could know for certain—was whether this new era would bring peace or plunge the world into even deeper chaos.

The global landscape was shifting. The public revelation of the feys had been only the first step, the initial outcry that overflowed the vessel of old truths. In the months following the great disclosure, something even more unexpected occurred: the integration of feys into the armed forces according to the plan that had been devised.

Now, the entire world was forced to accept that these legendary beings not only lived among humans, but would also have to fight alongside them.

Reports that began emerging publicly in March and April indicated that, over the past months, trials had been conducted with mixed teams, combining feys, humans, and aeons on the battlefield. The results were surprising—or rather, perplexing thanks to the Aeon.

Eighty percent of the trials were considered satisfactory. The number was high, a more than considerable success, but what amazed everyone the most was the efficiency with which the feys adapted to the dynamics of modern warfare. It wasn’t just about magic or special abilities. They were adaptable—no longer limited to living as mythological or fantastical creatures from books, but somehow already forming part of the military fabric of the world.

Mai knew that integrating feys into the military would be a monumental task. The fear of change was something nations dreaded, and even more so when that change came from beings who until recently had existed only in myths. But she also knew that in this new scenario, humanity needed every bit of help it could get. The war had no intention of slowing down, and the presence of feys and magic could make a decisive difference.

On the other hand, global acceptance of the feys would not be an easy path. Despite the satisfactory trials and the accomplishments that were shared in the combat, distrust would remain in the civilian. People did not forget the old myths—the legends of fearsome creatures who could not distinguish friend from foe. Yet there was something that might soften that distrust: offering help.

At first, it would be difficult for many to see a fey as anything more than a stranger, a figure that did not belong in their world. But if the feys demonstrated their willingness to help, to integrate with the people, perhaps that fear would diminish.

Although the revelation had brought with it another problem—one perhaps even greater than revealing the existence of the feys.

The disclosure of magic carried consequences that most people still did not understand. It was something few paid much attention to at first, but it marked the beginning of another conflict, one that would now be fought within the hidden world itself. Better that way.

For centuries, knowledge of magic had been deliberately restricted. Not only to preserve the secrecy of certain parts the hidden world, but because magic itself obeyed a rather peculiar principle.

The Law of Homogeneity.

Who had first come up with the name was anyone's guess, but that was what everyone called it.

Unlike electricity or any other form of energy, raw magical energy belonged to no individual. It originated from the planet itself, flowing through invisible currents that countless civilizations had interpreted in different ways throughout history.

Ley lines. Dragon veins. Spirit rivers. Sacred paths… Pizza places, et cetera.

Every culture that had walked through the valley of tears in search of understanding and mastering that force had given it a different name, yet all of them were describing the same phenomenon.

Some of those currents rose from the earth itself, spreading through the atmosphere like invisible rivers of magical energy. Within them, the fundamental elements employed in both theoretical and practical magic intertwined with the ether, producing supernatural phenomena and allowing reality itself to be shaped toward a desired outcome.

Some people were born with their own reserves of mana and could combine that inner power with the world's energy to weave spells through study, talent, or intuition. Others possessed no mana of their own, yet could still learn to use magic through rituals, symbols, artifacts, prayers, or years of disciplined practice, borrowing only a tiny fraction of that planetary energy.

Magic had never belonged exclusively to hereditary bloodline mages or to the great magical families. In fact, many of history's greatest documented magicians had been ritual practitioners with no innate mana reserves whatsoever. The mystery of magic itself appeared to be tied to the earliest stages of human evolution and, in ways scientists still struggled to fully explain, was related to espers—a connection that had already been revealed decades earlier.

And that was precisely why revealing magic to the world had been such a difficult decision.

The fewer people capable of using magic, the greater the amount of the planet's energy each practitioner could draw upon. Throughout history, that imbalance had allowed extraordinary—and sometimes not-so-extraordinary—individuals to accumulate levels of power almost beyond imagination, channeling both chthonic and ethereal forces. Entire magical traditions had been built around that principle, passing their knowledge only to carefully chosen disciples. But over the last few centuries, that same system had created new problems. Certain families had amassed tremendous magical influence by claiming control over powerful ley lines and newly discovered magical veins.

The concentration of so much magical power in the hands of a single individual had been one of the reasons Mai had lost Miho during the disaster of 2006.

Revealing magic to the world meant breaking that balance.

If millions of people someday learned even the most basic principles of magic, the planet's available energy would inevitably be divided among a vastly larger number of practitioners. Individual limits would become lower, and it would become far more difficult for any single mage to accumulate overwhelming power. Eventually, that truth of that law would also have to be revealed, though for now it was safe to assume that the public would take a long time to understand its implications. The Council had already conducted extensive studies on the matter, and the overwhelming majority of the great magical families had ultimately agreed.

In a sense...

It was a sacrifice.

The greatest magicians of the future would probably never again reach the heights attained by those who had lived centuries before.

But perhaps that was exactly the future the world needed.

A world where magic belonged to everyone was also a world where it could no longer belong to only a select few.

Magic itself seemed to obey a kind of thermodynamics. Distributing magic power was healthier than concentrating in terms of geophysics and atmosphere order. One way or another, everything tended toward equilibrium—even magic. A distortion of reality created through magic might endure for a time, but the fabric of reality itself constantly sought to erase such distortions unless they were actively sustained.

Mai was not a mage.

Her echolocation was a natural ability of her fey body rather than a spell, and although she understood the practical foundations of magic, she had rarely used it herself beyond raising spiritual barriers against hostile entities. Even then, such techniques rarely functioned outside their original cultural framework unless one possessed enough experience—and enough magical plasticity—to improvise entirely new systems.

In simple terms, she could barely be considered an apprentice spellcaster.

The ritual dances and practices she had learned from the Japanese tradition, along with parts of the broader Chinese and Korean magical systems, could hardly be considered a complete education, and decades had passed since she had last found herself in a situation where she truly needed to rely on them. Although the truth it was that she still love dance, even if has passed long time without practice it.

Perhaps the real problem for her was that she had never been able to anchor herself to a single system of beliefs capable of establishing a Cardinal Knowledge, as most mages and spellcasters eventually did. It was probably the inevitable consequence of spending so much of her life traveling across the world. Beyond Buddhism and Shinto, she had never approached any magical tradition as a serious field of practical study—only as something to understand in theory.

Even so, she understood perfectly why so many veteran magicians had opposed revealing that secret for generations.

Knowledge granted power.

But sharing that knowledge also prevented that power from becoming concentrated in the hands of only a few.

Perhaps...

That had been the true objective behind revealing magic from the very beginning. At least, that was what Rein and the others had explained to her.

In Nevermore's case, Siren Island itself was an Evanescent Land—a magical vein anchored by the island's core.

Mai hoped that particular secret would remain hidden a little longer.

For now, however, she had far more pressing concerns than magic... and whatever schemes the Council might still be weaving in the shadows.

As plans for integration developed, Mai was busy with her own role in all of this, and now she found herself facing new missions here, a new deployment there. This time, it wasn’t about infiltrating a war zone or espionage. Mai was special, and her ability for echolocation could also make her one of the most valuable assets on the battlefield.

It was a strategic advantage that could not be ignored in the event of encountering enemies who might confuse the tactical gear everyone carried. That’s why Mai was about to embark on her new deployment, in operations where she would have to make full use of her abilities to make the feys an even more integrated, more respected force. Performing well as a deployed representative was also part of the job she had accepted. What could she do…

As Mai and the others prepared for the integration of the Joint Forces, as the global army project had been called, a sense of urgency took hold of her. The world had changed and would continue to change. The war would go on, no matter what the future held for those fighting for it. She knew her fate was now irrevocably tied to the changes of that world, to the future they were building—whether in the darkness of a battlefield or in the blinding light of the unknown.

And so she set out. Ready for whatever was to come.

Throughout the months of 2095, Mai’s missions grew increasingly intense. The war, did not cease its advance, and each day the challenges faced became deadlier. Her first destination, already working in a more specific area, was the front along the China-Russia border, where the threat of the fractus had become even more dangerous, with waves coming from Siberia, the epicenter of the problem, where no one could get close.

The situation was critical, human defense lines had collapsed in several sectors, and the cities along the border were on the brink of chaos. Mai had been sent as part of a mixed team that included both feys and humans, a combination that seemed to work effectively there, but also presented challenges in cooperation and strategy.

With the arrival of more reinforcements, she then had to move westward across the country, and later south, bouncing around like a spinning top along with the rest of the mixed troops.

It was official.

The Great War of planet Earth had begun.

And so, her hair slowly began turning silver as the days went by.
