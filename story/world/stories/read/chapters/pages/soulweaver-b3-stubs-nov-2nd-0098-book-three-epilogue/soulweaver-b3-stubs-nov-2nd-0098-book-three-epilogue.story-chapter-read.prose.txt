One second I was standing on the Skytree Plaza and the next, the ground fell out from under me and I was falling into darkness.

I'd been through this before, though, so I knew what to expect. The pitch-black darkness and the sensation of falling into an abyss was nothing new, but did it really last this long last time?

I had just begun to start shitting my pants when the white room rushed up and I slammed into it hard, knocking the wind out of me. My armor absorbed most of it, but I still felt the impact rattle through my bones.

“Fuck,” I groaned, clenching my abs in preparation for what came next.

Sure enough, my elf crashed down on top of me. She barely weighed anything so that wasn’t a problem.

The phoenix that came right after, however, was.

There may have been a few expletives and undignified squawks.

Aerion and Galia were already up and giving me judging glares, as though them crashing into me was my fault.

Relief flooded through me so hard it almost hurt. She was alive. They’d survived!

I rushed up to her and pinned her in a bear hug.

Richard appeared next, somehow falling on his ass with even less grace than me. He hit the ground cursing.

As I’d hoped, there was no trace of injury on his body. The dungeon had healed him, and another knot in my stomach unwound when Arianna and Yashas joined him right after.

Arianna landed on one knee, her armor ringing like a gong, but she was up and letting Yashas down almost instantly. If Yashas looked embarrassed to be carried by his lover, he certainly didn’t show it.

Everyone was alive. We'd all made it.

Then I got a good look at Yashas's face and my relief flew right out the window.

His left eye was still gone. The dungeon hadn’t healed it.

“Yashas, your eye…” I trailed off, not really sure what to say.

“Is a small price,” he said with a wry smile. “I gained something far more valuable in exchange, after all. You'll see next time we delve together.”

I wanted to ask him for more details, then Eskil smashed into the ground with enough force to cause an earthquake.

As ever, the Viking seemed to be completely healthy and well. The giant looked around the white void with mild confusion, probably wondering if this was some new part of Valhalla he hadn’t yet encountered.

Must be so nice to be delusional.

A figure materialized in the center of the room just then, and I steeled my ears for another appearance by Dominion with his ridiculous warrior avatar, voice thundering for no reason.

Instead, a young woman appeared. Or a projection of one, anyway, and it made sense that she’d appear, considering this dungeon decided to land in her territory.

She looked maybe eighteen at most, with long black hair that fell past her shoulders, fair skin, and bright blue eyes that seemed far too innocent for someone supposed to be the goddess of Wisdom.

She wore a simple white robe that reminded me more of a scholar than a deity.

“Brave Champions,” the woman said, her voice clear and sweet. “You have vanquished the forces of darkness that threatened my land. My people are free once more, though they may never know the depth of your sacrifice. For your valor, you shall all be rewarded handsomely.”

The projection smiled, and it looked genuine. Almost proud.

Then, just like last time, the other Champions disappeared one after another to receive their rewards in private. I guess it was something like being loaded into an instanced server in an MMO.

Eskil went first, vanishing in the middle of saying something insulting about me. Richard gave Aerion and me a quick wave before he blinked out along with everyone else.

Then it was just me, Aerion, and Galia.

The familiar notification appeared.

Do you wish to share Blessing rewards?

Options:

— Retain 100% for self.

— Equal split among party members.

— Donate 100% to party members.

Party members: Aerion, Greg, Galia.

Last time, I'd done an equal split. This time was a bit different.

I'd hit Convergence-MAX during that insane final battle. The notification had appeared right after I'd destroyed my clone. Which meant any experience I gained now would be wasted until I underwent another Ascension Trial to strengthen my soul.

The experience would be completely wasted on me.

Aerion, on the other hand, could definitely use it. And Galia, who otherwise needed soul crystals to grow. She’d benefit the most.

No soul evolution seed this time, which was disappointing but not surprising. I’d have to earn my soul evolution the hard way, it seemed.

If I was brutally honest, the rewards felt underwhelming compared to the difficulty of what we'd just survived. Bone clones that were stronger than their originals, a military compound with tanks and fighter jets, and a swarm of lethal zombies.

Cyrus’ final boss fight almost felt quaint in comparison.

But while that might have been an issue before, it definitely wasn’t now.

If the dungeon's rewards were lacking, I'd just make my own.

And I knew from experience that this place wouldn’t kick us out until I confirmed my selection for distributing the experience.

I pulled my forge from inventory and set it up right there in the white void. Heat radiated from its furnace and it was ready to go.

Then I started pulling out metal. Lots of metal. Exotic metal.

I'd been collecting ever since my inventory lost its weight limit.

Every time we'd passed through a building or facility, I'd carve out samples of exotic alloys. Frostsilver from abandoned cars to Voidsteel and Aurorium from buildings. The stuff was everywhere once you knew where to look.

There were literal fucktons here, and despite the cavernous 10 x 10 x 10 ft space my inventory gave me, I agonized about how little I could take.

And the best part? I didn't even need to Initialize any of it. I just had to reshape the metal into a form the system considered different enough from its original state.

What would have posed a massive problem for an ordinary blacksmith, was checked easily enough by whether the Initialization prompt appeared when I touched the finished product.

I started forging. Just simple shapes since there was so much to get through, and even if Aerion had the patience of an angel, I didn’t want to push it. Or store her in my inventory. She’d been through enough of that lately. She could use the break.

As for Galia, I didn’t even think about keeping her out. Not only was she immune to the inventory’s mind effects, but she’d have distracted me the whole time, incessantly squawking in my ear.

After trying out a few shapes, I decided on ingots with one end hammered into a rough point. Bastard swords, at least according to the system's extremely generous definition. The things were without a doubt the most godawful abominations in existence, despite their material.

But hey, far be it for me to question an exploit.

The combination between my cheat Blessing and the incredible forge made the task trivial and I fell into a rhythm as I heated the ore and hammered the ‘sword’ into shape, checking for the Initialize prompt when I thought I had it. I got better at guessing the more I made, and some hours later, I was now the proud owner of thirty 'bastard swords' of various exotic metals, all of them recognized by the system as valid items for Initialization.

Thirty ingots just waiting to be turned into new suits and new weapons.

And by Cosmo did I need more of those.

I finally selected the donation option for the experience—saying nothing about it to Aerion, of course—and watched the notification confirm my choice.

The white void started to fade.

We materialized back in the field where the lone skyscraper had once stood. Now, there was nothing but a massive crater, and it felt incredibly weird to be surrounded by all this nature after having spent so long in the megalopolis. I instantly felt a pang of homesickness hit me when I realized I’d probably never have that experience again.

Whatever skeletons had been around when the dungeon was destroyed had collapsed along with it, and a field of bones lay strewn about everywhere.

The dungeon was cleared. We’d actually done it.

Normally, that would be cause for celebration. For recuperating and recovering before delving another dungeon weeks or months down the line.

Eskil was already bounding away toward wherever Dominion's Champions gathered, and good riddance. The dunce was probably going to brag about cleaving tanks in half or something.

Richard approached us, and when Yashas noticed, he waved Arianna and Ohana over as well.

Everyone was all smiles. “Well, was that absolutely mental, or what!” he said, clapping me on the back. “Brilliant show, Greg. You really pulled through for us.”

“Nah, man,” I said grinning. “That was a team effort if I ever saw one. If you hadn’t managed to give it to those clones, no way would I have been able to slip away.”

“To say nothing of the battle you fought while I was out,” Aerion said with red ears and flushed cheeks. I genuinely couldn’t see a day when she got over her embarrassment at passing out.

“Your contributions were no less important than any of ours, Lady Aerion,” Ohana said, sounding so polite it made even me self-conscious.

“Well, friends,” Richard said, glancing at the entourage of no less than one hundred of Passion’s followers who waited on the other side of the field. “Looks like the cavalry’s expecting me. I'd love nothing more than to hit the nearest pub and trade war stories, but it seems Passion has other plans. No rest for the wicked, eh?”

“Indeed,” Yashas said, looking at his own entourage, parked next to Richard’s. “It would seem I am in the same position.”

He nodded at Aerion and me. “It was an honor fighting alongside you. All of you.”

“The honor was all ours,” Aerion said immediately. “Thank you, for, er…”

To all our surprise, Yashas laughed. “Do not count me out yet,” he said, pointing to his eye. “I see more than ever now. You'll find out the next time we delve together. Which I hope won't be for long.”

I said nothing to that. I knew what I knew about the gods and their games. I seriously doubted I'd ever willingly delve another dungeon again.

Not until I had answers about why they existed in the first place.

“Till next time then, friends. As the General said, it truly has been an honor,” Richard said, throwing us all a salute before turning on his heel and heading off.

“And I,” Ohana said, bowing respectfully. “It truly has been a pleasure.”

One moment she was there, and the next she was gone.

“Well, that is the Champion of Grace for you,” Arianna said before turning to Richard’s receding back. “And Passion, too I suppose. I will miss Richard.”

Out of all of us, she looked like she’d been through a war the most, with her heavy armor scored with gouges and dents from the battle. “You fought well, Greg. Better than I expected when we first met, I must admit.”

High praise from her, considering her lukewarm reception to my Champion reveal. Which was entirely warranted, honestly.

“I hope our paths cross again.”

“Oh, you can bet on that,” I said. “Just try not to get stabbed by any bone clones until then, alright?”

Was that an almost-smile? I could’ve sworn the edges of her lips crept up just a little, but it was gone in an instant.

“Take care, you two,” I said, smiling over them. “Really hope it works out.”

The general and the rebel locked eyes and smiled. “It will,” Arianna said.

“It will,” Yashas repeated before leaning in for a kiss, which Arianna leaned into.

Which left Aerion and I watching awkwardly on until they finally decided to wrap it up.

“We’ll be off now,” Arianna said, gabbing Yashas by the arm and dragging him away, though I noticed not in the direction of their respective retinues.

Well, geez, talk about a couple of lovebirds. I just hoped their gods didn’t yoink them apart. How well did Cunning and Vigor get along, anyway? I’d never really asked Cosmo about it.

Speaking of, I looked up at the sky, took a deep breath, and called out. “Cosmo! We gotta talk!”

Annnnd… nothing. Nadda. No bar or any sudden appearance of a triceratops with aviators.

“What is it?” Aerion asked. “Are you unable to commune with him?”

“Nope,” I said, letting out a sigh. I felt like I was doing that a lot these days. Couldn’t be healthy.

“I'd expected that, honestly,” I said. “Think we’re on our own for this one.”

“Then, what now?” Aerion asked, looking as unsure as I felt.

“We need to tell the other Champions what's going on here,” I said. “They deserve to know the truth about the dungeons, about the gods, about all of it.”

“They will not believe us without proof,” Aerion pointed out. “We barely believe it ourselves, and we saw the evidence.”

“Yep. Which means we need more evidence. Irrefutable evidence that the dungeons are created by the gods, and more importantly… why.”

I thought about what I knew, what Cosmo had shown me, and everything we'd discovered, and an idea came to me.

“Say, Aer? Axian lore says that when the Cataclysm ends, the gods take the Champions to some place far away, right? In the sea somewhere?”

Aerion nodded slowly. “Yes. The stories say the gods grant the Champions a paradise as reward for saving the world. Islands in the sky where they may live forever.”

Riiight. What a load of bull.

“That's where we’re headed then,” I said. “Those islands might have answers. Maybe we’ll find some clue about what the hell’s really going on.”

“If you plan to fly there,” Aerion said carefully, “we will need transportation. Those islands are supposed to be hundreds of miles from any shore.”

I pulled Galia from my inventory.

The phoenix materialized in front of us, and both Aerion and I just gawked.

She'd doubled in size. Doubled! What had been barely large enough to fit Aerion on her back was now easily large enough to carry us both. Her wings were massive, and I had no doubt talons thick enough to rip a person in half.

And of course, Galia being Galia, saw us gawking at her and promptly burst into flames.

Flames that hit me like a five alarm fire to the face, forcing us both to back away.

That only made the phoenix even more excitable, and she took the sky, screeching.

I doubt there was a single person in that field who wasn’t staring at the burning phoenix as she took a victory lap, buzzing the heads of the Champion entourages.

I groaned…

The dungeon experience I'd donated had worked far better than I could have hoped.

“How are we to control her now?” Aerion asked, blanching. “I told you spoiling her was a terrible idea!”

“Yeah, well,” I said, sheepishly. “Assuming we can, I don’t think she’ll have any trouble carrying us now.”

“Oho?” a voice said, and I turned to find Grace’s Champion standing right beside us. “Carry you to where?”

I stared her in the eye, wondering what I should say. But Yashas trusted her, and she’d saved our asses more than once. I figured I’d play this straight. I didn’t have to tell her everything, after all.

“To the floating islands where the Champions are said to be shepherded to after Cataclysms,” I said, observing her reaction.

I’d been prepared for a litany of questions like Why? Or Are you insane? Or How long will that take?

All I got was an arched eyebrow and a single question that I would have never in a million years expected.

“How fascinating! It is presumptuous of me to ask, but… may I join you?”

I glanced at Aerion, who shrugged.

Hell, we were planning on telling them eventually. Maybe if we did find something there, having another Champion would help us convince the others.

“Sure. Why not!”

In the corner of a nostalgic, yet pristinely kept dive bar, the bar’s proprietor sat on a stool by the bar, his finely polished shoes propped up on the stool next to his.

Gone was the jovial smile he’d shown the world for millennia. Gone was the frivolous attitude of the immortal Fool.

He mused that if his Champion were to see him as he was now, the boy might very well freeze up in terror, and he would have good reason to.

For it was not a face he showed anyone. Not for a long, long time.

The self-proclaimed bartender’s fingers were pressed together and his piercing blue eyes went unblinking for many a minute as he stared off into the distance. Past the walls to some unknowable place.

After a period of time, the bar’s lights began to flicker. The space rumbled as though in the midst of some terrible quake, and the walls cracked and collapsed, revealing an inky black void beyond.

The bartender got to his feet and dusted off his pristine white suit.

“And so it begins,” he whispered, though there was no one to hear it.
