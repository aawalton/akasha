Aaron was surprised by just how industrious the goblins were, as he returned to the lot of them working hard. Or perhaps they just felt bad about what had happened, not that he blamed them for struggling to defend against the undead.

They were busy removing the crude goblin defenses and returning the area to its old rustic charm.

Passing them by, he found Zero outside of Zach and Marko’s workshop testing out a rather elegant-looking rapier. Unusually for a rapier, it matched Zero’s massive size, and was about as long as a zweihänder.

Aaron supposed the weapon was a good match for the evolved wolf. Zero had been a speed-based combatant already, and the light weapon suited him as he darted around, zipping back and forth as he turned to silver light and lunged into stabbing attacks.

“Not bad. The sword suits you.”

Noticing his master, Zero halted and turned to him.

“I’m glad you like my new weapon, master. It should make exterminating our enemies that much easier.”

“Interesting choice of words,” Aaron shook his head. “Alright, well, anyway, I’m heading to the temple. Once I’m done with that, we’ll check in with Julius quickly, and then head out. I plan to take over the remaining obelisks in the area, and then return to get some work in with my Profession.”

“As you wish. I’ll be ready,” Zero said.

Stepping up to the doors of the temple, Aaron took a deep breath. For the most part, he was looking forward to catching up with his patron god, but there was always a little apprehension.

And when he entered the temple's inner chambers, a figure strolled out of an adjoining room.

“Yendal?”

“Yes. Well, my avatar at least,” she said. “My avatar can’t leave this temple, but within its walls, this divine shard provides quite a bit of power,” she added as her hands bundled up into fists.

“Oh, shit. I should have seen this coming.”

Yendal smiled and launched into a blinding, but perfectly precise combination of attacks. But Aaron hadn’t just been sitting about fiddling himself. He had been throwing himself against impossible odds, and constantly improving. And when they met, he phased and dodged through her initial barrage with the skill of a master.

For a moment, it seemed that he had nullified her ability to hit him. Up until a burst of celestial power infused with her fists, allowing them to strike his wraith form.

Aaron staggered back against the attack, but he wasn’t done.

“Not bad,” Aaron grinned and shot into his own offensive.

A barrage of attacks followed, each utilizing fate reading, his accuracy overlay, [ Gorgon’s Soul Vortex ], and even his new ghost wings to push an edge, all of which was powered through vigorous energy pulsing out of his C-grade core.

At that moment, he could see everything. Details invisible to even the keenest of eyes. He spotted reactions well before they happened. And thus, by pushing himself against Yendal, the short duel helped him realize a synergy between his Skills he didn’t even know existed.

Through fate reading, he could see the threads of fate, but there were many. It had always been a drawback of the Trait. But using it alongside all of his Skills at once, he could parse the information, using it all to accurately figure out which one thread was the correct one.

It required calculating dozens of moves and outcomes every second, which was extraordinarily hard, and perhaps a telling shortcoming he still had.

I think I might need more Intelligence.

Nonetheless, it was impressive, and he pushed the avatar back. That said, he doubted Yendal was pushing her avatar to maximum power, and yet she was still faster and stronger than him. Her movements were perfect—never an ounce of wasted energy or movement. She dodged just enough, at just the right moment, taking advantage of momentum, and throwing it back against him.

As strong as he had become, the reality was, Yendal’s style was still absolutely absurd. It was peak tier perfection beyond what she had shown him, even in the trials. But even so, his layered Skills worked perfectly with one another, helping to close the gap between them. And after a lengthy back and forth, and a mind-numbingly long combination—one that resulted in quite a few slaps and trips—he landed clean, throwing her avatar across the room.

Yendal slammed a fist into her palm and bowed. “Impressive. You hit me sooner than I had expected. However, that doesn’t mean I have forgotten anything. I’m disappointed you haven’t been using the ring more, but I do admit, you have done reasonably well in your training.”

“Damn, a compliment already? You’re losing your touch, Yendie.”

“Don’t test me. I can still punish you severely if I choose.”

Aaron raised his hands defensively. “Just kidding. Anyway, it’s good seeing you. It’s been too long.”

“You too,” Yendal said, her thinned lips tugging ever so slightly at their edges.

“Before anything else, I have to ask. What’s up with the Death God? I assume you know that he invaded my dungeon?”

“I do. And we saw to it that his meddling would be limited.”

“We?” Aaron raised a brow. “Don’t tell me—”

“Yes,” Yendal sighed. “I've regrettably had some contact with that glutton. Together, we were able to persuade them to restrict that god’s power. So I guess he isn’t entirely bad, as much as I hate to admit it. As for the Death God, he is a pest. But a powerful one. I can’t guarantee that he won’t show up again somewhere else using different means. Unlike most of us gods who respect the rules, he is constantly trying to bend them.”

“I do not know the precise details. What I can tell you is that they’ve been locked in a war of control for thousands of years. A war that Lenriel has almost always come out on top of. Obviously, the Death God isn’t happy about that. He wants to change the balance of power, and has some plan to use his corruption to make this happen. As for you, I believe it is related to that disciple of his. Whatever he wants his disciple to do, he doesn’t want it interrupted.”

Even if he does find a way to get around restrictions, he won’t show up in his own flesh, and will be restrained.”

Understand that you are still an ant, Aaron. Take this opportunity and use it wisely. Get stronger. Push yourself. And then, when you do leave your world, you will be in a much better position to deal with what you find beyond it.”

“That’s it for now. If you want to find out, get out there and work and impress me.”
