Reading my avatar’s information, I no longer felt frustration over the cost. Platinum Rank? That alone made it worthwhile, considering I needed to spend over 1200 Divinity Points to raise a Gold Rank believer to Platinum.

But why Amon?

The name surprised me slightly. I had assumed I would be given the choice to pick one for him, or at least that it would fit the velmoryn world in some way… whatever, that didn’t matter.

What did matter, however, was his class. Or rather, his classes.

[Miracle Arbiter - Amon believes his purpose is to grow your faith and help your believers. He has the ability to grant wishes to those who live according to the ten commandments and, in exchange, convert their faith and gratitude into divinity.]

But those wishes can’t be free, can they? He will most likely use my divinity for that…

I wasn’t fond of the idea of gaining another subordinate who would leech off my power. However, Amon converting faith and gratitude into divinity meant there was yet another method of harvesting divine energy. I would need to observe how he did it and then attempt to replicate the process myself.

Aside from that, Miracle Arbiter made him sound like a genuinely kind individual. Or at least kind toward my believers. I tried recalling whether I had intended to create an avatar focused on spreading my faith when shaping Amon, but I couldn’t remember anything of the sort.

My only purpose had been to grow stronger and secure victory against the Night God…

Perhaps the system, or my subconscious, believed this was the way. Perhaps I had subconsciously thought that by creating a powerful apostle who would convert more believers to my name, I would grow strong enough.

Still… Amon is far too selfless for my avatar. It’s a bit strange…

But then I realized I had judged him too quickly.

[Twisted Arbiter - Amon despises nothing more than hypocrites and blasphemers. Those who seek to exploit faith are his enemies. He wishes to torment them without granting them death and convert their despair into divinity.]

Now that was a twist. With one hand, he granted mercy to those he favored. With the other, he tormented those he despised.

For reasons I didn’t know, or didn’t want to know, I felt relief.

I never believed in people without flaws. I didn’t believe in pure evil or absolute kindness. In my view, everyone possessed both light and darkness. Even the worst of individuals must have cherished someone deeply.

That didn’t mean the two sides had to be balanced. One could obviously outweigh the other. But if Amon had been portrayed by the system as the embodiment of pure kindness, I would have been convinced something was hidden from me. That doubt would have made it impossible to let him live, or I would have constantly felt threatened.

Was I like this when I was human?

Perhaps because I had been powerless back then, I never felt as though the entire world was trying to get me… Even if I was paranoid now, at least I did trust certain people. I trusted Aurelion. And Tekla as well.

As if reassured by that small ray of light, I returned to my avatar’s status window.

Half-velmoryn… like Avenor.

Could that be a coincidence? Avenor had clearly been created as an elf, yet turned half-velmoryn after I took over. I had shaped this vessel to be human, yet it too had become half-velmoryn once my consciousness settled inside.

That more or less confirmed it. Whenever my consciousness inhabited a humanoid creature, they would partially adopt my race - velmoryn. But, unlike Avenor, Amon displayed no distinct velmoryn traits. No pointed ears. No crimson markings. He wasn’t even tall. Not even by human standards.

What he did share with velmoryns, however, was his high magical affinity. In truth, all of his attributes were astonishingly high - especially willpower and magic control. And he possessed divinity as well, which explained why Amon had sensed two different energies within himself.

[God’s Approval - Determines Amon’s ability to use your divinity without seeking approval first.]

Good. If he begins wasting my divinity, I’ll simply restrict him.

His high devotion also made sense, considering he was centered around faith and aiding my believers. But his skills…

[Crimson Rite (Altered) - As a velmoryn, Amon can extract and convert life force into essences. However, he may only consume them himself.]

[Granting Wishes - Amon may grant wishes to devoted believers. Each successful miracle consumes divinity based on the magnitude of the wish.]

[Twisting Wishes - Amon may grant wishes to those he deems sinners. He will twist their wish so that it becomes torment.]

[Thread of Fate (Passive) - Amon has inherited the new magic created by his god. He understands probability and causality, allowing him to alter the faith of those around him by spending divinity.]

[Arbiter (Passive) - Amon may read the minds and intentions of those who claim to be believers of his god.]

While powerful at first glance, what drew my attention the most was that Amon seemed to lack any truly offensive skills. Even his class appeared unrelated to battle.

Can he even protect himself?

For a brief moment, I considered creating a guardian for him, just as I had created a griffin for Tekla. But I quickly dismissed the idea. I remembered how Avenor had begun his life. He hadn’t known how to fight either, yet quickly became one of the most formidable warriors in the tribe.

Amon might not have awakened something like Sylvan Heritage, but his magical affinity was extraordinary. In time, he would become a powerful mage. All I needed to do was provide him with the magical books I had obtained from V.

In the meantime, he can rely on Thread of Fate…

“Verde…” Amon called, growing restless.

“You can use both my divinity and mana,” I said as I closed his status window and stirred my divinity to create clothing for him. Even if it didn’t bother me much, staring at a naked man was still unpleasant.

[Warning: Creation consumed 0.1 Divinity Points!]

“Thank you,” he muttered, examining the clothes that had formed around him - a simple milky-white tunic with subtle silver embroidery along the collar and cuffs, light trousers of the same shade, and a dark brown cloak resting over his shoulders. “By the way, what name did you choose for me? I hope it’s not something lame…”

“Amon. However, I didn’t choose it. You were born with it,” I cut him off.

“Hmm. Amon. I like it. Maybe I should get a monocle too,” he smirked, but quickly composed himself. “Verde, please make a wand for me.”

“A wand?”

“Yes. A simple artifact I can channel my mana into, and it will activate a spell. Let’s say… a fireball?”

“Don’t you remember what Athena said? That Goddess of Fire might not like a priest of another deity using fire magic.”

“Oh. Well, I doubt she would care, but we shouldn’t antagonize her without reason. By the way, your memories are a bit scrambled in my head, so it’s better if you assume I don’t remember much,” he scratched his head and pushed his hair back. “And the wand… I’ll leave it to your imagination, My Liege,” he chuckled.

“Why a wand though? Wouldn’t a staff be better? You could use it to protect yourself…”

“A staff is too conspicuous. And I want to leave a different impression. If people connect me with magic rather than faith, it will interfere with me becoming a prophet…” he grinned.

When did I order him to act as a prophet?

I briefly considered the idea. Aside from presenting himself as my son or representative, I saw no real downside.

Well, except for other gods disliking a foreign deity spreading influence within their domains.

But…

“You will not act as a prophet of the God of Velmoryn,” I said. “You will spread the faith of the God of Many Faces.”
