Romulus stepped out of the communal hall and took stock of the situation, eyes moving from the corpses already on the ground to the various buildings.

His [Blood Hunter] vision told him that the couple that had been mid-copulation were now cowering in the corner of their room, and there were more people within the chapel. He saw movement there, and logic told him that at least one of those life signs would be the Paladin. That was his priority, and when he looked for Kate, he found her entering the compound with her bow held half-drawn.

“There are two people on the top floor of the houses,” he said to her in a voice that registered as coldly furious to his own ears. “I almost took a dive against a teen with radiant magic. Go handle those two, and I’ll start on the Chapel. Join me when you’re done.”

“Are you okay?” she asked him before immediately replying, her jade eyes peering up into his scarlet ones.

“I’m fine,” he assured her with a forcibly more measured tone. “I’m just annoyed at myself for dropping my guard. It was a stupid mistake, and I made it because I forgot who we are and what we’re doing. It won’t happen again.”

“We learn from our failures, Dee,” she said to him with a hidden but audible smile. He could tell what the slight octave change of her voice indicated, thanks both to how long he’d known her and his heightened Vampiric senses. “What matters is that we don’t repeat them. I’ll handle Romeo and Juliet. Don’t get yourself in too deep in the chapel without me.”

“I’ll be careful,” he agreed more amicably. “But hurry anyway.”

Kate nodded and set off swiftly and silently while Romulus turned back to the chapel. A glance at his combat log showed the [Combat Mode] designator still at play, which meant that the System still considered him engaged. He wouldn’t know what progress he’d made to level 20 until he finished the ‘encounter’, it seemed.

No time like the present.

Lightsbane vibrated in his hand and pulsed with enthusiasm as he strode toward the large wooden chapel doors, gripping the runeblade tightly in his armored fist. His footsteps were loud within the sudden quiet of the compound, steel on stone echoing as he made his way toward the far set of steps leading to the chapel and ascended them swiftly.

When he came to the doors, he raised his left hand and pushed against them, finding no significant resistance to his touch. His Strength Attribute came to the fore, and Romulus shoved the doors inward, pushing them apart to grant access to the chapel interior. White walls greeted him, with stained glass portrayals of what he assumed to be Saints and Apostles flanking the sides in sets of three.

The far end of the chapel had a stained glass depiction of Hyperion, resplendent in white robes with a halo of gold around his blond head.

Romulus’ first act was to summon a [Dark Lance] with a drain of hemoforce, and punch it through the god’s visage.

The shattering glass filled the interior with a resounding echo, and a System alert populated his vision.

SYSTEM MESSAGE

You have incurred the fury of Hyperion by desecrating one of his holy sites!

Your actions have earned the approval of Lilith!

Lilith has awarded you 500 XP for your loyalty!

Romulus smiled grimly at the alert and swiped it away while his eyes scanned the interior, and he eyed the other stained glass windows. His feet carried him further into the building while his left hand rose to begin generating more [Dark Lance]s for the remaining windows

Before he could launch them, a door at the far right end of the chapel burst open to reveal a man adorned in heavy silvery warplate, wielding a warhammer, with a sunburst-on-white tabard adorning his front. Another person, a young woman adorned in flowing white robes, followed him, and Romulus smiled grimly.

“Heretic!” the Paladin spat upon arrival, hefting his massive double-headed warhammer in fury. “How dare you desecrate the Chapel of His Radiance!”

Lightsbane vibrated with bloodlust and pulsed with its eagerness for violence.

Romulus, meanwhile, assessed the man in silence instead of responding immediately.

The paladin was reasonably tall, at perhaps six feet, with cropped brown hair, a neat beard, and holy sigils adorning his warplate. He wore a brown-fur collared cloak that fell just above his silver sabatons, and had a Libram of some sort chained at his right hip. A longsword was visible on his left hip, and Romulus presumed he might have a shield of some sort hidden behind him.

The woman with him, conversely, appeared lighter armed: she was a blonde with her hair tied back in a bun, wielding what appeared to be a staff of some sort, topped by a sunburst symbol. She was attired in holy symbols across her body, though Romulus paid those little heed. What was more important to him was her identity. If this was some stroke of providence…

“Roderick Vayne, I take it?” he asked the man in a calm voice instead. “And would that woman with you happen to be Sara Lightspeaker?”

Both Roderick and his companion paused at his question and glanced at one another in momentary confusion.

“You know who we are, yet invade regardless?” the woman called in a voice that was a mix between musical and confidently hard-edged. “You must be daring indeed, beast of the Forsaken Lady.”

“So you know who I am, then,” he replied while steeling himself. He had expected the Paladin, but he had not expected the Priestess as well. If the two of them worked together, this would be far more difficult than he anticipated. A Paladin alone was one thing, but add a Priestess to the mix, and suddenly things became far more complex.

“Our Radiant Lord has warned us of your existence, Vampire,” the Paladin declared boldly. “We were preparing to bring you to justice with His power ourselves, once the Duke finally agreed, but here you come to deliver yourself to our fury!”

“The Duke still isn’t convinced, huh? Thanks for letting me know.”

The pair stared at him, and then the Paladin scowled.

“Insolent dog. You will learn the measure of Hyperion’s judgement before this night is done!”

“Wait, Roderick,” the woman, Sara, said suddenly while stepping forward.

The Paladin glanced at her, but didn’t argue, and instead glared at Romulus.

Okay, so it looks like the Priestess calls the shots.

“Before we render judgment, you lost soul, I would know why you chose this path.”

Romulus raised his eyebrows at that and looked at the woman carefully. Talking was good, after all. It gave Kate time to handle matters outside and rejoin him—and he felt like her Null abilities were going to be critical in dealing with the Priestess.

“Which path specifically?” he asked with every intention of stalling the fight. “Serving the Dark Lady—” he stressed the word to make his disapproval of ‘Forsaken’ very clear “—and becoming a Sanguine Revenant, or the path where I slaughtered your followers?”

The Paladin growled and bristled at his words, but Sara appeared calm and focused. That was both good and bad: good because he could use Roderick’s anger against him, and bad because Sara was definitely the more intelligent of the two, and that meant she would also be the harder of the pair to deal with.

“Why consign yourself to the Dark? Is a life as a wandering wraith, unable to find rest, truly so appealing? All for fleeting power?”

Lightsbane pulsed its disparagement, and he agreed readily with the sword.

Romulus opened his mouth to retort, and then paused.

Life as a wandering wraith? That isn’t Lilith’s thing at all.

“What are you talking about?” he asked instead, while Lightsbane hummed its bloodlust in his right hand. “She of the End is the Divinarch of Death, Priestess. She enforces the cycle. When I die, she has promised to guide my soul to rest personally.”

Now it was Sara and Roderick’s turn to look momentarily uncertain, and then have two notably different reactions: blatant scorn from Roderick, and a more measured, considering look of assessment from Sara.

“You truly believe this,” the Priestess said as a clear statement. “You genuinely believe the words you speak.”

“Why wouldn’t I?” he said while eyeing the two of them and quietly assessing the combat area of the chapel. Five rows of pews, perhaps three meters long apiece, sat in neat rows on each side of the long center aisle, and the entire space might have been ten meters wide from wall to wall.

“The Forsaken Lady lies like she breathes!” Roderick snarled.

Lightsbane pulsed its fury in Romulus’ grip, and he soothed it with a squeeze.

“The Dark Lady did not lie when I freed her,” Romulus responded coldly to Roderick at the same time. “Not once. She even warned me about the cost of following her. I chose to do so anyway, even when your Gilded Asshole tried to tempt me away.”

Roderick took a furious step forward, and Sara’s staff came across to bar his path.

“Why?” the Priestess asked directly.

“Priestess, this is a pointless—!”

The man fell silent when Sara gave him a cold look, and instead, he reached up to stroke his beard in frustration, while she turned back to Romulus.

“Again,” she said firmly. “Why?”

“Why what?” Romulus asked directly.

“Why did you refuse the God of Light’s offer?”

“Because I won’t be anybody’s plot piece,” Romulus said back immediately, and with sincere fervor. “Aligning with Hyperion would just make me a show pony, rolled out as the ‘denier of Darkness’ when it suited the political needs of Hyperion’s theocratic inclinations. With the Dark Lady, I choose my own destiny—and I forge it through might and power. Where I come from, it’s better to be a hunted freeman than a pampered slave.”

Roderick snorted derisively at his words, but Sara’s eyes conversely narrowed in thought.

“Then you truly are dangerous,” the Priestess said before the Paladin could interject. “Intelligent enough to understand the strictures of service, and strong-willed enough to defy the will of the Radiant One. I see, now, why he holds your destruction as such a high priority.”

The Priestess placed her staff on the ground, and her expression turned cold.

“I wanted to understand you, Heretic, so that I may see whether you were merely led astray, as a Traveler incapable of understanding the truths of our world, or if you were a willful recidivist, intent on upsetting the natural order and balance between the holy Light and forsaken Dark. I see you are the latter, and for that, you must be destroyed.”

“Eliminate what you can’t control, huh?”

“The greater good demands ruthless conviction,” Sara responded steadily.

“I hope that comforts you for what comes next,” Romulus replied with grim resolve.

“We shall see,” Sara concluded simply, and lifted her staff.

A prayer of benediction spilled forth from her lips, and Romulus summoned his hemoforce in a saturation of power. A [Dark Lance] formed above his left hand, and he looked from the Priestess to the Paladin, who hefted his warhammer with a look of righteous satisfaction. Golden light rippled along his body, and he placed his right hand momentarily to his Libram—drawing a surge of radiant lightning from the silver-chained book to infuse his warplate.

“By Light you shall be Censured, abomination!” Roderick declared boldly and promptly charged toward him with both hands on his hammer’s shaft.

Lightsbane ignited in Romulus’ grip when he fed the Runeblade with dark mana, and he charged to meet the paladin head-on. Roderick’s hammer blazed with light when he swung it, and every instinct in Romulus’ enhanced body told him to dodge—so dodge he did.

His Agility was pushed to its maximum, and he narrowly evaded the attack, sidestepping the swing and watching the hammer impact the church floor—and shatter it. The resultant detonation of radiant magic threw him back, and Romulus felt burns erupt across his body even through his warplate.

His armored feet skidded upon the stones, and he stared at Roderick in alarm.

“Okay,” he said grimly. “New plan.”

Without another word, he raced for the exit.

The Paladin followed with a roar of rage.
