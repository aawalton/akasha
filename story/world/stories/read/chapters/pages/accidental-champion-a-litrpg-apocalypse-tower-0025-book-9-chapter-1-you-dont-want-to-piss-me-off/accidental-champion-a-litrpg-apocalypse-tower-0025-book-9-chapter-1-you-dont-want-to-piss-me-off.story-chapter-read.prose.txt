It was completely silent in the vacuum of space. Inside his Force Armour, all Xavier could hear was his own breathing, his heart pumping blood through his body, and the sizzle of his burnt flesh at the end of his elbow.

In his attack against the World Destroyer—a single punch that had split the galaxy-eating interdimensional being apart—he’d vented the combined energy from every citizen in two versions of the Silver River sector from two different universes.

When he’d received the kill notification, he’d instantly cut off the two spells he’d had active—Energy Funnel and Energy Transference—and sent out the signal for those sending energy toward him to stop at once.

Because despite what he’d achieved, despite what he’d done, the thick beams of energy through the two portals behind him had still been flowing.

There had been more energy incoming. More energy than he’d needed.

He raised his gaze from his ruined stump and looked at the two halves of the World Destroyer. They were outside his time dilation field, so the World Destroyer had barely split in two, frozen in time as it was again, but the kill notification had confirmed his enemy’s death.

Xavier released a long, drawn-out breath. The pain of his stump throbbed, flesh still burning from the massive outpouring of energy he’d executed, but it was nothing like the agony he’d just experienced when he’d been funnelling all that power through his body. He rolled his neck around his shoulders, hearing several satisfying cracks. His energy channels felt fried. His body wrung out. Exhausted.

Yet he felt absolutely elated. A grin split his face as he turned to look at the others gathered. Adranial, his found family, Volkarin, Rhaalir, Romalda—they were all there, waiting, watching, their gazes moving between Xavier and the dead World Destroyer, awe thick on their faces.

Xavier Prime: [You… you did it.]

The other version of him was watching from the other side of the white, triangular, universe-spanning portal. He was the first to communicate with Xavier, and though he couldn’t see the other man he could hear the awe in his voice.

Xavier: [And you will do it soon, too.]

Xavier Prime: [It really only took one punch?]

Xavier looked down at his stump again. [I’m as surprised as you are.]

When he’d come up with this plan, developed it, refined it, both he and Xavier Prime had been sure that it would work. Xavier had the honours of doing it first as he was the “original” Xavier—at least between the two of them—not that they talked about that much.

Still, as confident as he’d been in the plan he was still surprised at just how effective it had been. He might have believed that it would work. Believed enough to come out here and try it. But even though in his heart he’d known winning would be inevitable, the ease at which he’d done it still shocked him.

Xavier shook his head, then tilted it to the side, watching with curiosity as the sizzling of his flesh ceased and the pain disappeared, the area becoming numb. Slowly, and yet at the same time incredibly fast, the bone, muscle, and skin of his forearm began to grow back.

Xavier Prime: [Time to initiate the next phase?]

Xavier inclined his head, knowing the other man would be able to see the gesture even from his own universe. The others were still looking at what had happened in awe. He would address them soon.

There was something he needed to do.

Xavier hovered around in the air until he faced the World Destroyer once more, but he didn’t stare at the dead interdimensional being. Instead, he looked beyond it, at the stars of far-off galaxies, imagining those stars were the watchers—the powerhouses and old monsters—from the inner sectors that he knew were still observing him, who’d seen what he could do and would even now be trying to figure out how he’d done it, and making plans about what to do with him.

This was the part of his plan he was least sure about, but a part he knew was just as necessary as everything else he’d just done.

Xavier spoke aloud, inside his Force Armour, knowing whoever was watching would be able to hear him. “I know you’re out there. I know you’re watching. And I know what you’re thinking—but I’m not going to let you use me, and I’m certainly not going to let you kill me.” He raised his chin in defiance. “What I’ve done here is just the beginning.” He looked at his arm, which was halfway to being healed, looked at the dead World Destroyer, then finally back at those far-off stars.

“I’m not your enemy, but if you move against me or those I rule, you’ll become my enemy. If you stand in my way, you’ll become my enemy. I don’t care how old you are. I don’t care how powerful you are. I was integrated into the System not even five years ago and you’ve seen what I’ve already done. You have no idea what I’m capable of—so trust me, you don’t want to piss me off.”

Xavier glanced over at his drones. He had one of the drones manifest a spell pattern inside its body, while a few hundred other drones created portals that they flew through. In less than a second, they would be covering the different edges of the two galaxies he ruled over.

“I know you’ve been watching my little empire grow. I know you’ve been seeing what I teach my people. I know you’ve seen what I’ve done today, and you want it. You want that power.” He shook his head. “That isn’t how this is going to work. Your days of watching are over.”

The drones he’d sent through portals, along with a single drone among the thousands that remained, activated their manifested spell patterns at the same time. More portals were created, these ones far smaller than the ones his drones had gone through. In fact, they were the tiniest portals that he could figure out how to create—portals that wouldn’t be visible to the unenhanced naked eye.

Energy flowed from each of the drones and travelled through those tiny portals. The energy interweaved and connected and flowed from one portal to another until two separate webs were created—one around the Silver River sector and one around the Ventorin sector.

As the two webs of energy were fully realised, anyone able to observe them in their entirety would notice they looked like giant, galaxy-spanning spell patterns in their own right.

The webs of energy were initiated and glowed with a brilliant light. In the same instant, the presences Xavier could sense watching disappeared.

The Silver River and Ventorin sectors went dark. No longer would the old monsters of the inner sectors be able to watch their goings on.

Xavier manifested a spell pattern inside himself, as did everyone else around him. He cast it, and so did the others. Now he became dark to far-off observations as well, even when he ventured out of the sectors he ruled.

He released a long breath and finally let himself feel a hint of fear that had been trying to get out as he’d spoken to the most powerful beings in the Greater Universe. He shut his eyes, but the whole time his spiritual sense was working on overdrive, trying to sense if there were any threats coming his way.

Roln appeared nearby. The dead Wanderer turned mental construct hovered in the vacuum of space with his arms crossed and his eyes narrowed. Only Xavier could see the man—he only existed for Xavier. The mental construct had known this was what he was going to do. He hadn’t warned him off doing it, as Xavier had suspected he would, but there was definitely disapproval in his expression.

You don’t think I should have done that, do you? Xavier communicated to the being telepathically. He didn’t want the others to see him talking—they still didn’t know about the mental construct’s existence.

Neither did the System.

“Do I think you should have threatened the strongest people in your universe?” Roln raised an eyebrow that said, What do you think?

Xavier frowned. I didn’t threaten them. I warned them.

Roln laughed. “You know what you did.” He looked at the remains of the World Destroyer. “You had the power to block their sight all along. You wanted them to see—you wanted to be seen. How do you think they’re going to react to that?”

Xavier blinked. Oh, I think I know exactly how they’ll react. He looked down at his arm once more, watched as the tips of his fingers finished their regrowth, and curled them into a fist that was already fully healed. They’re going to come after me. And when they do, I’ll be more than ready. Because as fast as they can move, I can move faster.

Roln shut his eyes. The dead Wanderer ran a hand through his hair then let out a long sigh. “You know…” He shook his head in sheer disbelief. “I want to say you don’t know what you’re getting into. I want to say you’ve just made the worst mistake of your life and it’s going to get you and everyone you care about killed. You’ve just issued a challenge to people who could single handedly blow up your whole galaxy, and you’re floating there in space with barely a care. I want to call you the most arrogant version of Xavier Collins to have ever existed, but…” Again, the dead Wanderer looked at the dead World Destroyer, then he gazed at the two portals. “The scary thing is… I think they’ve got more to worry about than you do.”

Xavier frowned. He looked at the mental construct. Well, that wasn’t what I was expecting from you.

He’d thought the dead Wanderer would at least be angry with him. Xavier had been called insane enough times to start to wonder if perhaps there was a little something off with his judgements, especially when it came to risk.

And what he’d just done? It was perhaps the riskiest thing he’d ever done, verging on pure recklessness, much like entering the void had been pure recklessness.

The dragonkin rotated in the vacuum of space and looked toward those gathered. Adranial was pursing her lips, lines of worry marring her brow. The others wore similar expressions.

Volkarin, that massive dragon, was actually smiling. With the number of teeth that revealed in his huge maw he looked more than a little unhinged. The dragon, back when he’d been the Spirit of Time, had always enjoyed a little chaos—but he’d also, on occasion, showed caution.

There was none of that caution on display today.

Xavier raised an eyebrow at the dragon. [Why do you look excited while everyone else looks worried?]

Volkarin: [Because every time I think you’re doing something that will get you killed you come out stronger—and it’s always interesting along the way.]

Adranial crossed her arms at her chest and shut her eyes, releasing a long sigh.

Xavier: [I appreciate you all being here to back me up.] He failed to mention that he hadn’t needed them there, that was obvious enough. As afraid as he was for them, he had liked having them at his back, liked them seeing what he was really capable of.

That made him wonder how he might use this memory in the future. He’d used his memories, more than once, to help convince others of something—might he be able to use this to that same end?

Xavier: [You all knew what I would be doing here today, you all knew what I’d be saying to the powerhouses we anticipated would be watching. And you all know what’s going to happen next.]

Howard: [Expansion.]

Xavier inclined his head at the former cop. [We need to continue to increase our empire’s strength, and we need to do it at a rate even faster than before. Those old monsters are used to moving slowly, thinking in terms of thousands and even millions of years. Seeing what I’ve done is going to make them realise that’s about to change—we have to be faster than that realisation.] He looked over at the white, triangular portal. [But first, we have to help Xavier Prime do exactly what we’ve just done.]
