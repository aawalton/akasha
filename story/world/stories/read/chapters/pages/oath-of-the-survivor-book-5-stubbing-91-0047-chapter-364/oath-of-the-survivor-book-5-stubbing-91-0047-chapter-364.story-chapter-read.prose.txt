Kyle and C.H.A.D.D. followed the orc guard retinue to the heart of the crater, where a dome of dark gray rock served as the city’s center. As they descended, Kyle got a better glimpse of the general living conditions of the orcs. Unlike Duroc’s village, these orcs all sheltered within the stony walls of the crater itself, following bridges and pathways to get to the large stone construction in the center. Kyle’s initial impression was that they were only a single large rainstorm away from a real disaster, until he saw the intricate system of rain gutters beneath the bridges.

Noticing Kyle’s attention drawn away, the lead guard snorted in amusement. “You noticed our irrigation system, huh?”

“I did,” Kyle confirmed. “I don’t know that I’ve seen a more extensive one before. It’s really impressive.”

“Stone and water are our heritage,” the guard said, holding up his hand as a small bubble of water materialized around it, drawn from the moisture of the air. “We’re as unyielding as the rock, and as relentless as the waters. You’ll see that when you meet our War Chieftain.”

Given their heavier armor and shields, Kyle had expected this group to be more martially inclined, as opposed to a group of Stone and Water Mages. While there were certainly offensive capabilities within both schools of magic, Kyle couldn’t help but think that the combination lent itself far more to a defensive style. With that being the case, the presence of the variant hodag was certainly a cause for concern. It would have free reign to roam and hunt, while the village would have to remain content defending their positions lest the monster decide to invade.

Entering the towering stone dome, they navigated through a covered bazaar until reaching a tunnel system, which led them up a spiral staircase to a large meeting chamber overlooking the shopping area. From his vantage, Kyle had a bird’s eye view of the tense mercantile district, each of the orcs moving with frenetic purpose and focus. They’re nervous here, too, Kyle thought. Of course, between a variant C Grade hodag and the looming threat of war, he could understand why.

“War Chieftain Svynar will be here soon,” the guard said, inclining his head once again. “Safe journeys to you, Courier.”

Kyle gave a shallow bow. “Thank you for guiding me this far. I hope that you and yours will enjoy a season of peace ahead.”

“Would that we could be so lucky,” the guard replied with a soft smile as he left the room.

Kyle watched the shoppers for a little while, until he felt an unmistakable C Grade aura approaching. Turning, he saw an oversized orc walking into the room. Similar to Duroc, it had thick bristles and pronounced tusks, though this one’s coloration was dark, with a near hint of deep blue coloring the fur. It wore a loose robe that looked to be made of fine silk, and carried a massive axe across its back, with a single edge that seemed to be carved from obsidian.

Kyle bowed respectfully, holding out the sealed scroll. “I greet War Chieftain Svynar.”

“Looks like they couldn’t afford a real C Grade Courier,” Svynar said with a derisive snort, grabbing the scroll container from Kyle’s outstretched hands. “A pity your journey won’t be able to continue farther, however. Too dangerous on the road out of the village right now.”

Kyle straightened, ignoring the insult. “I heard there’s a variant C Grade outside your village along my path. You really think it’s that dangerous?”

“I sure as hell won’t be fighting it if I can help it,” Svynar said with a chuckle. “From all the intel the scouts could provide, it’s not a fight worth picking unless it comes here. Which it seems to have no intention of doing. But if you get caught, you’ll get squished like a bug.”

Kyle risked a quick Identify to confirm what Auric Perception hinted at; quickly concluding that Svynar was a low-tier C Grade. Likely near the bottleneck at the top, to be sure, but low-tier nevertheless. Even though the orc had some levels on Kyle, he suspected the classes were far more basic than his own. Coupled with attributes that lacked great synergy, Kyle could understand why the War Chieftain would be hesitant to try and pick a fight against a powerful C Grade variant.

Kyle didn’t share the chief’s compunctions, though he didn’t stand to gain much by being too cavalier in front of the other C Grade. “Surely clearing the threat away is worth some of your effort.”

“Hardly,” Svynar snorted. “The hodag’s territory is covering an ancestral path, but a rarely-traveled one. We can handle any trade disruptions by rerouting through other villages.”

Kyle frowned at that. “It’s only interrupting the ancestral path leading away from your village?” he asked.

“That’s right. Hardly an issue, at least for now.”

By now, Kyle’s gears were turning. The coincidences were adding up, and they were all pointing to Mangalica’s involvement. The other orc wanted the Tournament of Ascension to be disrupted, and then the hodags appear to accomplish just that. While he couldn’t say with any sort of certainty that the orc was involved with the hodags’ emergence with Pragmus Meat Purveyors, there was undoubtedly a connection.

“What have you and your scouts been able to learn about the creature?” Kyle asked.

“It’s massive, for one. Damn thing could eat an orc whole. Its hide is like a thick armor, and its covered in spikes to boot. Definitely a physically-oriented creature, which would make it even more of a pain for us to hunt.”

“If this creature were to be handled for you, would there be a reward for the person who took it out?” Kyle asked, dropping the subtlety to get a better sense of Svynar’s reaction. If the War Chieftain was involved, there would be no better way to find out than to gauge his feelings on the hodag’s potential demise.

Svynar let out a booming laugh, slapping his oversized belly. “That’s a good one! You wouldn’t stand a chance, Courier. Best to run away.”

“But if I did kill the creature?” Kyle pressed, an eyebrow raised.

“Then I’ll eat my own axe!” Svynar roared, falling into another fit of laughter. After a moment, the gargantuan orc composed himself. “I mean no offense, Courier, but this isn’t a foe you can fight. You might be confident in getting away, and I’ll grant that you probably could. That’s your job, after all. But don’t do anything foolish. I still think it’s a mistake to even continue with your delivery, given the danger.”

“Your concern is appreciated,” Kyle said as he stood up. “With respect, however, I have professional standards to maintain. Will I be able to stay the night here before I embark on the next leg, or will I be sleeping on the road?”

“So serious! Of course you can stay! We will have a room made up for you in the outer district.”

“Thank you for your hospitality,” Kyle said with a nod. “I’ll take my leave, and look forward to seeing you at the Tournament of Ascension.”

“Bah! If you continue on your errand, I doubt that we’ll be seeing one another again. A pity, because I like you, Courier. A shame you’re willing to throw your life away for some credits.”

“Oh, it’s more than a job. Duroc is a friend.” With that, Kyle lightly flexed Aura Force, pushing some of presence out into the room and dispelling the weak impression his aura normally gave off. Svynar’s dark, beady eyes went wide as he felt Kyle’s power wash over the room, one hand instinctively reaching for the handle of his axe.

Then, as quickly as the moment came, it passed. Kyle let his aura fall back to its normal levels, then gave a wry smile to the orc leader. “Once again, War Chieftain, I look forward to seeing you again at the tournament. Be well until then.”

Kyle and C.H.A.D.D. left the central building to head toward the evening’s accommodations. As they got out of earshot, the drone finally spoke up.

[WAS THAT REALLY NECESSARY, DR. MAYHEW?]

“Probably not,” Kyle admitted. “I wanted to get a sense of whether or not Svynar was aligned with Mangalica. It was the best method I could come up with on the fly.”

[I FEEL COMPELLED TO REMIND YOU THAT DUROC DID NOT WANT YOU PUTTING YOURSELF IN UNNECESSARY DANGER DURING THIS JOB, DR. MAYHEW.]

“And we won’t be,” Kyle promised. “There’s more going on here than meets the eye though, C.H.A.D.D. I refuse to believe that the appearance of these C Grade hodags and variants are just a coincidence. Much less when the nests have been appearing along established paths that any orc would know Duroc’s forces would have to travel. Hell, Svynar’s axe is more subtle than this.”

[YOU BELIEVE MANGALICA IS BEHIND THE HODAGS?]

“I believe that there’s a connection, at least. And I’d bet that at the bottom of this whole mess we’ll find that the hodags we fought with Arhades are related, too.”

[AND WHAT WILL YOU DO IF YOU FIND OUT THAT IS THE CASE?]

“Pass it along to Jarberry, and then keep my nose out of it. Unless we learn it’s a C Grade responsible, at least.”

Kyle could feel a sense of approval through Pack Leader’s Instinct. [YOU HAVE PLENTY ON YOUR PLATE AS IT IS, DR. MAYHEW. PARTICULARLY IF YOU ARE SERIOUS ABOUT WANTING TO PURSUE MR. DEROSA WHEN YOU COMPLETE THIS JOB.]

“Plus, we’ll owe Marcus some good hard labor if he’s able to secure Kertan Six. No sense in borrowing too much trouble.”

[OF COURSE, YOU ALSO NEED TO PAY ATTENTION TO THE PRESENT. THIS CREATURE SOUNDS LIKE IT WILL BE QUITE A CHALLENGE. IT IS LIKELY AT LEAST AROUND THE LEVEL OF THE OTHER VARIANT HODAG YOU FOUGHT TOGETHER WITH ARHADES.]

“I won’t underestimate it,” Kyle agreed. “We’ll take the night to keep making repairs on Model: Shield, and depending on what data we can find, we’ll even reserve a use of Model: Cannon for it. But I definitely want to take it down.”

[DO YOU BELIEVE THAT SVYNAR IS WORKING AGAINST DUROC’S INTERESTS?]

“I don’t,” Kyle said after a moment. “I think that Svynar is a lot weaker than he lets on, which means that he’s afraid to take too many risks. In that sense, I would expect that Svynar would be very shy of a confrontation with Mangalica, but also not looking to draw any unnecessary attention. Svynar is best served if Duroc takes the brunt of Mangalica’s pressure, and the Tournament of Ascension doesn’t come to pass. I don’t think that he’s overtly working against us, though.”

[THEN HOW DO YOU THINK HE WILL RESPOND IF YOU SLAY THE HODAG?]

“There’s only one way for us to find that out,” Kyle said as he settled into meditation. There was a lot of work to do before the next leg of the journey, and they had a hodag to hunt.
