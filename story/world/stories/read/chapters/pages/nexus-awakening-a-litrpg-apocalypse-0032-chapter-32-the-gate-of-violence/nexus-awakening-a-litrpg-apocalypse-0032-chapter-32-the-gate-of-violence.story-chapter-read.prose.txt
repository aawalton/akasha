Chapter 32: The Gate of Violence

I didn't consider myself a dramatic person. Lynn and I had attended a few plays once upon a time, back when our biggest worry was finding a babysitter for the evening and whether we could afford decent seats. For the most part I had considered theater overblown, artificial emotion performed by people pretending to be someone else. But I did have a tendency to get lost in my own thoughts, and I think it made me a good worker. I could allow my mind to wander as my hands stayed busy, bringing a kind of meditative focus to whatever task lay before me.

The memory of those simple evenings felt like they belonged to someone else now. The man who had worried about ticket prices and intermission snacks was as dead as the family he'd tried so hard to protect. What remained was something that found its own kind of theater in violence, its own poetry in the language of death.

Regardless of my past opinions on drama, I engaged in a little melodrama when I lifted my hand toward the makeshift gate of Keth's camp. Reaching out with the Flux, I made a fist and utterly crushed the wooden barrier. It had no Containment I could sense, being just ordinary timber, and thus exploded as if I had thrown a grenade directly at it.

Splintered wood shot outward in a thunderous boom that echoed through the dungeon's twisted chambers like a shockwave announcing the arrival of something terrible. Fragments peppered the stone walls behind the gate, some pieces large enough to serve as crude spears. The sound reverberated off the impossible architecture, amplified and distorted until it seemed like the dungeon itself was screaming.

I stepped through the broken opening, my resonance blade humming to life in my hand, white light touched with crimson cutting through the smoke and debris. My mechanical breathing heralded my arrival like some ancient war horn, the respirator's steady rhythm promising death to anyone who stood in my way.

I killed an initiate in the first second of entering the camp. My aura of Command Presence froze him mid-stride as he turned toward the explosion, his face locked in an expression of confusion and dawning terror. My blade found him before he could even process what was happening, splitting his torso like overripe fruit. Blood and viscera coated the ground beneath him, his flesh singed and burning like charred meat where the resonance energy had touched him.

Another initiate engaged me immediately, showing more courage than sense as he brought his own weapon to bear. His blade work was competent, better than most I'd faced, and for a few seconds we danced the deadly ballet of combat. But parrying his blows was trivially easy once I let my bloodline guide my movements, his techniques as predictable as the rising of tides. I cut him down with clinical efficiency, opening his throat in a spray of arterial crimson.

I felt Flux energy closing in around me from a third opponent, some kind of binding or crushing technique meant to immobilize me while others struck. But I consumed the ability before it could take effect, my bloodline hungrily lapping up the stolen aspects like a man dying of thirst finding water. The power flowed into me, feeding something deep and primal that had awakened with my heritage.

Shocked cries followed from the initiate who had tried to trap me, and through the Flux I felt her hold on her power waver as confusion and fear took hold. Her mistake cost her dearly as I reached out and crushed her throat, applying pressure until vertebrae cracked and airways collapsed. She dropped without another sound, hands clawing uselessly at her ruined neck.

That was three down in as many seconds, with two left standing. They had retreated to the center of the camp, putting as much distance as possible between themselves and the thing that had just torn through their companions like tissue paper.

One was Keth'Val himself, the chosen initiate I had come here to kill. He was an alien made of some kind of rope-like material and dark, viscous substance that moved independently of any wind. His face was wrapped in strips of the same material, barely humanoid in appearance, with only the suggestion of features beneath the bindings. He looked like rigging from some nightmare ship, as if someone had taken nautical rope and soaked it in blood until it achieved a kind of terrible life.

His companion was a tall, willowy woman with brown hair pulled back in a practical ponytail and form-fitting robes that marked her as someone who took combat seriously. She had the lean build of a dedicated fighter, the kind of person who had survived this long through skill rather than luck.

"Who are you to attack my camp?" Keth's voice was neither deep nor high, carrying an odd quality that suggested damaged vocal cords or alien anatomy. Though he wasn't visibly wounded, his voice sounded like someone speaking around a stab wound, each word accompanied by a wet, struggling quality that made my skin crawl.

Not that I could really tell what constituted wounded for something that looked like one big mass of bloody rope. His alien physiology was completely beyond my understanding, a reminder that the universe contained forms of life that followed no earthly pattern.

My breathing was my only reply, the mechanical rhythm of the respirator filling the sudden silence. Let them wonder what I was, what I wanted, what had driven me to single-handedly assault their stronghold. Fear was a weapon like any other, and uncertainty sharpened its edge.

Keth made no move to indicate he was preparing to attack, perhaps hoping that diplomacy might succeed where force had failed. But his ally had different ideas. She ignited her resonance blade and launched herself at me with the desperate fury of someone who understood that talking was just another way of dying slowly.

I almost raised an eyebrow at her tactical sense. I couldn't feel her aspects through the Flux, which meant she was masking them somehow, hiding her true capabilities behind mental shields or natural resistance. Her blade wasn't anything unique, similar to mine in many ways but lacking the distinctive crimson core that marked my weapon as something special.

I defended myself, parrying her strikes with the easy confidence of someone who had been forged in a crucible of pain and repetition. Reaching out through the Flux, I tried to crush her weapon as I had done to so many others, but felt her power fight back against mine. She had trained her Containment extensively, built up her defenses until they could resist even someone of my enhanced capabilities.

She had a powerful Containment attribute, maybe not as high as my Resonance, but high enough that I could not simply crush her and be done with it. This would require actual effort, actual skill rather than brute application of superior power.

We moved across the camp in a deadly, humming dance, our blades meeting again and again in showers of sparks and the distinctive chimes of resonance weapons in conflict. She was good, better than most, her footwork precise and her blade work elegant. But she had learned her techniques from instructors and books, while I had learned mine through pain and near-death experience.

At one point our blades locked in a contest of strength, the energy fields crackling against each other as we strained for advantage. My mechanical breathing was audible even over the sound of our weapons, mingling with her labored panting as the fight took its toll on her stamina.

"You're that chosen initiate," she said through gritted teeth, her brown eyes narrowed with effort and recognition. "Vash's chosen. I thought you were hiding like a coward somewhere, waiting for stronger people to kill each other off."

I didn't get mad at the insult. I had enough rage pulsing inside me from the memories that played constantly in my mind, Lynn's dead eyes and Jackson's torn body providing all the fuel I needed. One barb from a dying woman wasn't enough of a spark to add to that inferno.

"I was," I acknowledged without shame or justification. The truth was often more devastating than any elaborate lie.

I pushed her away with enhanced strength before reaching out through the Flux and freezing her with my commanding aura. Her Containment was powerful, impressive even, but not powerful enough to resist the full authority of my awakened bloodline. Her eyes widened as she realized she could no longer move, could only watch as my blade descended toward her neck.

The strike was clean, precise, almost merciful in its efficiency. Her head separated from her shoulders and rolled across the packed earth of the campsite, coming to rest near the smoldering remains of the gate I had destroyed.

Keth eyed her cooling corpse for a long moment before staring directly at me. There was no fear in his alien posture, no desperate pleading or offers of negotiation. Just the calm acceptance of someone who had always known this moment would come eventually.

Two curved resonance blades ignited in his rope-like appendages, weapons that pulsed with colors I had no names for. The light they cast was wrong somehow, unsettling in ways that went deeper than mere aesthetics.

Then he came at me with the fluid grace of something that had never been bound by human limitations, and I realized this fight would be different from all the others.
