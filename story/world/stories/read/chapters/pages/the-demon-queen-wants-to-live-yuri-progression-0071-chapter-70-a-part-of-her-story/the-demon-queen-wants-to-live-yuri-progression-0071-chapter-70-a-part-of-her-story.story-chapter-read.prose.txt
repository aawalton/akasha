The chamber was in ruins. It was somehow more in ruins now than when we had begun. None of the black pillars still stood. Many of them had been severed, large pieces of black lying next to the pillars they had been cut from. Some of them had been burned -either by Ren's power or Solara's occasional outburst.

The ground itself was more craters than any proper floor, and one had to step carefully everywhere to keep from losing their balance.

By now, the mana was not dense at all. The bright light had dimmed until it now looked like something closer to dusk. Perhaps this place required a word greater than 'ruin'. If so, I did not have one.

Ren, Corin and Alisa were standing in front of me in a rough line. All of them bore some of the scars of our training. Ren's uniform had burnt at her shoulders long ago. Corin was still faintly swaying, though he should have been able to walk without help. Alisa's once fine coat now looked like nothing fine.

Ash was by my side. Sol was warm atop my head.

There were words that needed saying to the three before me. I had known this for some time now. That had not made actually saying them any easier. Ash leaned close to me and bumped my shoulder. She was an impatient Hero.

"I am going to commend you all," I said, "for your...assistance in my training. Your contributions were unasked for." I paused. "But perhaps they were adequate."

They watched me for one second. Then Ren shook an angry fist at me. "Adequate? What the hell is that supposed to mean? We've been getting thrown around this damn chamber for two days and all we get is adequate? Why I oughta-"

Corin caught her just as she'd started to move towards me. "Ren. I, um- I think that's supposed to be a compliment."

Ren looked at Corin, then at me, then at Corin again. "Seriously? What the hell kind of compliment is that?"

It was a foolish question. "Any statement from me is a compliment," I said. "To be regarded by me at all is the greatest compliment of all."

Sol let out a mighty chirp from atop my head. It rang off the broken pillars. She was agreeing with me, for unlike Ren, she was wise.

Ren's shoulders slumped. "Half-demons," she muttered, "really don't make any Divine-damned sense."

Alisa just folded her arms and regarded me. "Well? You're not about to disappoint me in the morning, are you?"

"I would not have, regardless of your help."

"Good." She huffed and turned half away, though I still caught her looking at me from the corner of her eye.

True to her word, Alisa had not used her mark once. Not in the sparring, not even when a falling pillar-half had nearly claimed her and Ash had had to save her.

She had still been useful, even in our unarmed sparring. Not that I would tell her that.

"So," Ren said, dragging one hand back through her hair. "You're really just gonna try and outlast her, huh." There was something under the words that I recognized, a hesitance that lived in the space before all battles. Odd, to hear it directed for another. "I mean, it's not a bad plan, what with the crazy shit you can heal, but..."

"It is the only logical option," I said. "The Tenet is a trump card with a rather finite time limit. If this Lena is not as capable as you, as you say, then I will have an easier time of it."

Ren sighed. It was a long sigh. "Well. You better not fucking lose, then."

I had almost gotten used to the girl's crassness over these two days. "I was not planning to."

"Well. Alright then." She looked around the chamber. "Can't do anything more now anyway. Man. Wonder what the old man's gonna say when he takes a look at this place."

I assumed that by old man she meant the Archon. I looked at the chamber and found I could not fault her wondering. Then again, he had said we could use this place.

"The three of you may leave," I said. "We are going to stay here."

The three of them paused, in three different ways.

Alisa recovered first. "Is my rival planning to keep practicing into the dead of night? So that she may arrive at her actual duel too exhausted to lift her arms? I didn't take you for that particular breed of fool."

"That will not be a problem. I will be rested enough. I would stay here." I looked at Ash. "Unless you disagree."

"Fine by me," Ash said.

We looked at each other. It was a long look, one that ended when Ash smiled. Ren's face had gone the color of her hair.

"L-let's leave them!" she said quickly, too quickly. She seized Corin by one arm and Alisa by the other and began hauling them both toward the door.

"Ren, what's the rush-" Corin managed.

"Unhand me!" Alisa did not manage anything further, because by then they were through the threshold, and the great door groaned shut behind them, and the chamber was quiet.

"Huh," I said, into the quiet. "That girl is rather effective, when she is of the mind to be."

I turned as Ash made a strange choking sound. She had the look of something stricken. It passed quickly, so quickly I might have thought I had imagined it.

"What's wrong?" I asked.

"Nothing," Ash said. "Nothing at all."

It tasted like a lie. I did not press.

Ash looked around the chamber, rolling one shoulder. "So…you really don't want to go again?"

I shook my head. "I doubt it would matter." I looked down at my own arms as I said it.

The red and green Lines had started to grow dull two days ago. I had thought that was the final stage. I had been wrong. Sometime in the last few hours, the ends of both Lines had begun to curl. Just the smallest fraction, at the very tips.

That's the real last stage, Ren had told me, when she had seen it. When they start curling, it means the Line wants to branch.

Wants to, but is not. I knew what I lacked. Insight. Two days of battle had carried me to the very edge of the Line, and the last step was not a thing that battle could take.

"So what are we going to do?" Ash asked.

I crossed to one of the shattered pillars -whose fallen half made an adequate seat- and lifted Sol down from between my horns. She chirped once and permitted it. I set her on the ground, retrieved the small stack of tomes we had carried from the Athenaeum, and sat with my back against the pillar's stump. "I am going to read."

Ash nodded. She came and sat down next to me. Then she started to shift and by the third shift her side was pressed against mine from shoulder to hip. She was very warm and very soft, in the places where she was not iron. I opened the book and began to read.

From the corner of my eye, I saw Sol begin her latest patrol. She waddled behind a pillar's base on one side and came out the other. She took three steps, stopped, and then took three steps again. Then she turned around, and for reasons known only to her, she started to…roll. She tucked her wings and rolled the way an orb rolls, right until she bounced against a fallen chunk of rubble. Then Solara righted herself and resumed her patrol.

I stared at her. I reached for the bond and found only contentment. Some mysteries were not meant for mortal understanding, and evidently not for near-immortal understanding either.

I read. I had done plenty of it over the last two days, such that I had little left to read of the tomes we had brought. I picked up the tome on Insights. I had not read much of it in the Athenaeum itself and that had been enough for me to consider the whole useless. I had hoped the rest of it would be different. It was not. I found another verse, three pages on from where last I'd read.

The body is a home I was given before I could refuse it. I wasted my years repairing walls that were never broken, and knocking at doors that were never locked.

I stared at the lines. I read them thrice, and they made no more sense the third time than they had the first. I slowly set down the book. "This book is a waste of the paper used to make it," I announced.

Ash leaned over to read the verse. "I don't know," she said. "They definitely tried."

"They tried poorly. Not one of these fools can properly name and explain the thing they did. They can only couch it in enough poetry that no one notices they have said nothing worth saying." I glared at the page. "If I ever meet the compiler of this volume, I will use the Requiem and erase them."

Ash laughed brightly. Perhaps I would let the compiler off with only a harsh warning.

We were quiet for a time. The thin light of the chamber lay over everything like dusk. The Ember Core ground on beneath my ribs, as it always did.

"Lys."

"Hmm?"

"I want to try something."

I paused, and looked at her. Her jaw was set and her eyes were on my chest. "You want to try what?"

"I want to try severing your pain. From your core, I mean," Ash said. "I think I should be able to now."

I stared at the Hero. So that is what she had been doing for two days. I had seen her swiping her left arm in the air every now and then, seemingly at nothing. I had not understood why then, but I did now. She had been practicing.

I had not asked it of her. I- I was not displeased that she had. "Very well," I said.

Ash blinked. "That's it?"

"If it's you," I said, "then it is worth a try."

Ash's mouth hung open, just slightly. Then she nodded quickly, turning fully towards me. She took a deep breath and raised her left hand over my chest. I could see the arm tremble.

"Follow the line of my power," I said. I drew a thread of the Cradle up through my left hand, pressed it past my skin, until it traced the thing that ground beneath my ribs. It was a strange thing, to search within myself so. "Just as you did with Corin."

Ash licked her lips. Her eyes closed. The trembling stopped. She took a deep breath, and then she swiped. The humming faded.

For one breath there was silence beneath my ribs. It was so very quiet, and it was so very-

The pain that was gone came back, and it came back sharper than it had ever been. I was facedown on the stone and coughing before I had even understood that I had fallen at all. I tasted metal on my lips. There was blood on the broken floor.

"Lys!" Ash's hands were on my back. Her voice was frantic. She helped me sit upright. I kept my eyes closed.

Slowly, painfully slowly, the sharp pain began to fade. The Cradle was pulsing on my left arm. The sharp grinding slowly started to ease back to the steady pain I was used to. The same pain it had been an hour ago, no better than it would ever be. I wiped my mouth with the back of my hand and opened my eyes. A Queen did not bleed.

"I'm sorry," Ash was saying, over and over again. "I'm sorry, I'm sorry, I shouldn't have-"

"What-" I coughed wetly once more. "What are you sorry for, fool? I asked for this."

I straightened fully, and only then did I notice the tiny phoenix. Solara was standing in front of me, directly before my knees, and she was chirping furiously. With enough fury that it shook her entire body. Her wings were flared to their fullest span. All four of them.

I reached for her. She buried herself into my hand, pressed her head hard against my palm, and kept up the furious chirping the entire time. "I am fine," I told her. "Do not worry about me."

Sol pecked my hand. Hard. Any harder and she might have drawn blood. I looked at her. She looked at me. Her golden eyes did not blink. She had never pecked me so hard before. "...Point taken," I said slowly.

I leaned back against the pillar. Sol sat against my side, between Ash and me. Her furious chirps slowly died. "That did not work," I said.

"I'm sorry," Ash said again.

"Ash. If you apologize one more time, I will show you another of the [Hundred Submission Techniques]."

She laughed at that. It was a strained sound, but still unmistakably a laugh. "You're impossible."

"You have said that once before. It is only natural for the Demon Queen to be impossible."

There was silence again, different from before. I did not doubt my Hero's ability. She was new to this aspect of her power, but she had gotten the feel for it, as she had said. And her power had dulled the humming, even if for only a moment. Yet the Ember Core had started its grinding again. Why this was the case, I could not guess.

My hands idly brushed over Sol. Her chirps had faded, and in their place was only the sound of her faint breathing. It seemed her series of chirps had tired her. It was Ash who moved first.

Ash drew her knees up. She rested her arms across them and stared upward. Sol stirred between us but did not wake up.

"There was once a girl," Ash said quietly, "who woke one morning to a System screen telling her she now had a Class that should only ever come to a male. She was only a child at the time and so she didn't really understand any of it. But every adult in her village went as white as a sheet when they found out. It was called the [Chosen] Class. It was rare. You see, it was the first Class on the path to the [Hero] class. The girl had never even heard of it until that morning." Ash's voice was even and composed. I stared at her. She recounted this tale as if it all happened to someone else. "There were expectations after that. The weight of all those expectations was on her shoulders before she could even have breakfast. The priests arrived that afternoon. By the end of the week, she had been taken from her home and placed somewhere else."

I said nothing. I just watched Ash. Her body was very rigid. "The priests did not appreciate it when they found out. The Class was only for men. For it to be given to a girl was…" Ash shook her head. "The priests didn't understand it. They didn't have a word for what had happened, or maybe they had too many. Blasphemy was the one they used most. The System was never wrong, so if a girl had been given a class that should have never gone to her, it could only mean one thing. That she had tricked it and all the Gods, somehow. A child had tricked the System, and they all really believed it." Ash smiled, but it was a flimsy smile. "So, there you had a girl who had blasphemed in their words. The only punishment for something like that was death. Yet now the girl had the Chosen class. A sacred class, one that they couldn't touch. So they taught me how to hide."

Me. The story had become about her somewhere at the end. I did not think that Ash had noticed.

"They gave me many lessons. They taught me how to walk differently, speak differently, and use [Disguise] on my appearance. I learned fast." She said it flatly. "It took a month before I was better than any of the priests. In three months, even they seemed to have forgotten who and what I actually was."

"And you hid yourself," I said. "For your entire life."

Ash nodded, slowly. "I did. I was bound not to tell anyone. Not that it mattered, I suppose. I wouldn't have known how to even if I could."

I was quiet for a time. My hand brushed the line of Sol's back as she slept.

"Why?" I asked at last. "Why bother at all? If becoming a Hero demanded that you erase yourself to do it then why do it at all? Surely you could have escaped. Done something." I did not know if Ash could have done anything at all. I just guessed, and hoped.

"Because of the stories," Ash finally said.

"Because of the…stories," I repeated. "I do not understand."

Ash nodded. She was looking up at the high ceiling, but her eyes were somewhere much farther away. "My mother used to tell me stories, you see. Of the great Heroes and Warriors and their greater deeds. You know the ones. Aldan the Godshield. The Twin Phantom Blades of Ashia. Rhaenia the Giant Tamer. Other ones, more than I can count." I knew these names. I had slain Rhaenia myself. Ash likely knew as well as I. "My mother was... not an expressive woman, you see. She worked, and she prayed, and she worked again, and by the end there was too little left in her to do anything but exist. But when she told those stories-" Ash's voice caught. "Her eyes would sparkle. Like she was somewhere better than our village. Like the world was bigger than it was."

Her arms tightened around her knees. "And I suppose I wanted to be the reason they looked like that. Just once, you know?" She turned her head and looked at me, and her face looked almost torn. "Well, Lys? Get it over with."

"What?"

"You're going to tell me that's a silly reason. Aren't you? You're thinking it, so you can say it. It'll be something like 'these foolish mortals and-'" She trailed off.

I shook my head. I pressed myself closer against her side instead, shoulder to shoulder, until there was no gap left to speak across. "The only one who can judge whether your reasons were worth the life you led is you, Ash. If the reason was enough for you, then so be it. I have heard many reasons. Most reasons were far uglier."

Ash was quiet at that. Then she sighed, and the sigh moved through her whole body and into mine. "You know I've never told anyone this," she said. "I didn't think I ever would. Feels strange to say it all now."

"You didn't even consider it?"

She shook her head. "I did think about it, but never seriously. I always felt guilty. What right did I have? I was both a liar and a Hero at the same time. I was as far from a Hero as anyone could be from the moment I started. Heroes were supposed to be true. That's what the stories said. So I tried to be like the stories. That had to be enough," Ash murmured. "That's how I used to think, anyway."

"And now?" I asked.

"Now…" Ash let out a long breath. "Now I wish I'd told them."

I nodded slowly. I felt Sol stir at my side. The tiny phoenix had woken up. Her golden eyes slowly opened and closed. She let out a small yawn. Then she looked around and saw Ash. Sol slowly waddled from my side to hers. She chirped up at the Hero.

Ash reached down and gathered the phoenix against her knee. "Hello, you. Did you like your nap?"

Sol chirped in some kind of answer. Ash began idly brushing her feathers. I did not mind the sight. "Ash," I said.

"Hmm?"

"Did you see it?" She looked at me. "You said you wished to be the reason your mother's eyes sparkled, as they did for those stories. Did you ever see it?"

Ash smiled, but even I could tell that this expression was a smile in name only. "Once. I saw it just once. The one time I ever went back to my village. There was a harvest festival. A bard was passing through then and he sang some of the new songs of the time. The ones about the new Hero, the youngest Hero in generations. The bard got my name wrong. I wasn't that well-known at the time. My mother listened to every single one of those stories. Her eyes sparkled, Lys. They sparkled for the stories about me."

"She did not see you herself then?" I asked.

Ash shook her head. "No. She never found out, Lys. That her child was the one in the songs. I wanted to try telling her someday, if I ever actually succeeded." She shrugged. I had seen the Hero lift many weights by now, and this might have been the heaviest of them all. "She died the winter before your fortress fell."

I looked at the Hero. Ash was staring down at some fixed point on the ground with far more focus than it warranted. And she was trembling, just faintly. Something inside of me ached to see her like this. I knew guilt now, in the gardens I did not see and Demonbloods who did not stand at their full height. In a thing I had never seen and in something else that was assuredly in part my fault.

It seemed I knew another thing now. Grief. Grief for the years of a girl folded small enough to fit inside the legend of a man. It was doubtless just a small part of Ash's story, but it was enough.

What I had just heard was cruel even for me, and I had authored much cruelty. I should have erased one more nation from this world. The one that had made her something lesser than what she would have been. But they were gone to dust already. And I knew Ash well enough to know that she had not told me this story so that I could burn something.

I gingerly reached out towards Ash. She froze. I put my arms around her and pulled her closer. I did not know what to do with my hands. Where to put them, or how firmly. One went atop her shoulder, the other, I suspected, was too low on her back.

Ash was rigid. Slowly, she leaned in. Her head rested on my shoulder and she stayed there. Sol pressed somewhere between our legs. She let out a single long chirp, but otherwise did not move.

We stayed like that for a time. For perhaps a very long time.

When Ash finally straightened, her eyes were wet. She reached up to push her hair back from her face. I blinked. "Ash."

"Hmm?"

I pointed. At her right arm. At the purple Spark that sat there -that had sat there, was now a thin purple Line that ran all the way down her forearm. Ash stared at it. The Veil had advanced, and neither of us had actually seen it happen. It had happened at some point during our conversation. Only that much was clear.

Neither of us had to say the word Insight. So this is the kind of thing the books had meant. A Veil's insight had to be something to do with…deception, then? Or perhaps it was truthfulness? Perhaps it was both? I still did not know.

Ash looked at me and grinned. "Maybe I should write my own verse about this?"

"Ash," I said. "If you do that, I will relieve you of retainerhood."

But I said it warmly, and Ash must have heard it, for she laughed loudly until it filled the chamber. Sol soon joined in, chirping at a joke she could not possibly have understood and the sound of it was better than any verse in that entire useless book.
