Chapter 212 / B3-57: [Anyone] Up

By the time the next morning rolled around, Joe and Kenda had worked each other up into frenzied levels of curiosity. They knew their speculations as to what trait Dorian was about to offer Joe in a few hours were just blind shots in the dark. Even so, they couldn’t stop wondering.

Mojo, picking up on their heightened anticipation, yet unable to understand the reason for it, was losing his little mind. He was leaping between Kenda and Joe, hooting and ruffing, throwing every toy in his collection into their laps to see if one might be the reason two of his favorite people were so keyed up. Finally, Joe had to head downstairs to borrow Mahq, who had both the magic and the temperament to soothe his companion's jitters.

They all grabbed breakfast together at one of the eateries that Mojo and Mahq both liked, and then took a walk around Crafter’s Market. There, Joe and Kenda each had a temporary tattoo applied by a rather humorous artisan named Ello Thween. Ello kept cracking jokes, then quickly admonishing them not to move, which made no sense; if you want folks to stay still, stop making them laugh.

By the time the paired tattoos were finished, it was getting close to half past 4-Bells-Dawn, or 9:30 AM as Joe still thought of it. With about half an hour to spare before the meeting, they delivered the boys to Ord and Berti, making sure it was okay to leave the pup with the elderly couple, which, of course, it was. They adored the little man as much as he adored them.

As Kenda and Joe headed away from the Abbaka House, the pair went over their plans again. Joe should be careful of any trait that would give him a rank or title with any of the fey courts. Such a trait could give him status, maybe helping him stand on a more even footing with the nobility of the Feylands. It could also come with a whole basketful of provisions and expectations that Joe could easily stumble over.

He should also triple-check for any subtle clauses and conditions the master trader might attach to this gift. While the leprechaun seemed to be operating in both of their best interests, the general consensus around the harbor city was that after shaking on a deal with Dorian Barrbarrow, it was wise to then count your fingers.

The Barrbarrow Trading House was a few blocks off of Founders Plaza, in Seaside. The building was more formal than the man’s home, giving Joe the impression of an old-time city office or maybe a bank. Huge blocks of pale marble that were not quite white covered the structure’s facade. Thick formal columns flanked the doors and front windows. The door itself was massive. It was mostly sealed shut, with the exception of an open sally port, guarded by the first jotun Joe had seen in Fort Coral who was not obviously a sailor or shipwright.

Gathmour Rasnegar: Jotun: Bruiser/Storm-Knuckle 19

Even though the seven-foot-tall giantkin was dressed sharply in a formal white shirt, dark grey trousers, and a vest, he had the mien of a bouncer standing by his doorway. He tipped his head to them as they approached.

“Morning, Miss Bracey,” his deep voice rumbled.

“Hiya, Gath. You look well,” she replied.

The big man simply nodded his head in response before looking to Joe. “Good day to you, as well, Mister Morris. Master Barrbarrow is expecting you. Just check in with Rasha, and she’ll point you in the right direction.”

While the jotun had not been blocking the door, he shifted a bit further, creating a ‘please, enter’ spacing.

“Thanks,” Joe said, letting Kenda enter first. Once they were inside the stately lobby, he asked in a low voice, “You know him?”

“Gath, yeah. He used to be a guilder, but he took a job with Dorian five or six years ago. He’s still technically a reserve member, though he hasn’t run any quests for a long time. Between his job here and his arena career, I don’t think he needs any more money or chances to beat the tar out of anything.”

“Arena? Fort Coral has an arena?”

“This is a city of sailors, Joe. You gotta give them a safe way to blow off steam. There’s not just one arena. There are three major ones and plenty of minor fighting rings as well. Granted, none of them allow death fights or gladiatorial melees. Just pugilism and dueling, with only non-lethal powers allowed,” she explained. “Gath is a crowd favorite. With that reach of his and his [Shocking Fist Strike], he was the Grand Sands champion two years in a row. One of these days, we should go see him fight. He’s really good. You could learn a lot about how to use [Brawler] by watching him.”

Joe digested that fact as they stepped up to the receptionist's marble desk.

Rasha Cassae: Human: Host/Advocate 11

Rasha had the rich brown skin tone of a dunian but lacked the broad shoulders, square eyes, and coily hair associated with that earth heritage. She watched them approach and smiled a warm welcome. “Greetings. You are a bit early for your meeting with Master Barrbarrow, Mister Morris. I can have a drink brought out while you wait. Tea?”

“We just ate, so I’m good, unless you’d like …,” Joe began, looking to Kendell, who politely shook her head.

“Very well. Please feel free to take a seat,” she offered, gesturing to a set of benches a few yards away.

Thanking her, they sat. Joe looked up at the clock on the wall, noting they were almost ten minutes early.

Like most Illuminarian clocks, this one’s basic layout was similar to the classic Earth design, but there were some distinct differences. The timekeeping instrument had a circular border with numbers around the edge, but here the numbers ran from 1 to 6 rather than 1 to 12. Illuminarian days were broken into four blocks of six hours instead of two blocks of twelve. There were no hands either. Instead, the numbered disc spun slowly, indicating the time with a pointer at the top edge of the device. In the center of the clock was a second disc broken into four quadrants: Dawn, Day, Dusk, and Dark. Again, whichever one was facing upward was the relevant day-quarter.

Unfortunately, the hours didn’t quite line up with the chronology Joe knew. 1-Bell-Day was noon. Which meant 2-Bells-Day was 1:00. Joe was starting to get into the habit of subtracting one hour from the Day and Dark. Dawn and Dusk were a bigger pain to calculate. For those two quarters, it was 6:00 that lined up with 1-Bell; so 1-Bell-Dusk was 6:00 PM, and 1-Bell-Dawn was Joe’s 6:00 AM, leaving him adding 5 to get the correct time as he still thought of it.

Thankfully, Joe had a cheat. He was already good with numbers, but if he ever stumbled, he just had to flick on [Crystal Mind], and the conversion came to him effortlessly. If he ever saw Maszy again, he owed the priestess a huge thank you. Never a day went by that Joe was not engaging that cognitive skill for one purpose or another, sharpening his focus and reasoning.

Kenda and Joe whiled away the ten minutes talking until Rasha indicated that the master trader was ready for them. She directed them down the left-hand hallway. Impressive murals lined the walls of the passageway, which ended in a set of dark, elegant double doors trimmed with glittering, bright fey silver. Just before they reached the portal, one of the panels opened, and a selkie woman in an aqua-colored robe exited the room.

Joe had never seen her face before, but he easily recognized her runic turquoise cloak and the ornate coral and seashell-adorned staff. This was the arch-druid Cobhlu Dawnsong, leader of the Storm Wardens and one of the highest-ranking members of the Fort Coral city council. While Joe digested the fact that she was coming to Dorian and not the other way around, Cobhlu nodded to Kendell and intoned in a musical voice, “Good morrow, Miss Bracey.”

“Councilwoman Dawnsong. Nice to see you again.”

“And you,” the blue-green-skinned druidess replied before continuing past them.

“Do you know everyone in this town?” Joe whispered just before they stepped through the door.

“Not even close,” she smirked back at him. “I attend the council meetings, and Gath was a guilder. Okay. Focus. Head in the game.”

Dorian was in the process of shelving a ledger, though the manner in which he did so was completely unexpected. Unless one was Gathmour’s size, the books on the top shelf would have been impossible to reach without a ladder or without something like Joe’s amber paws. For the short leprechaun, even the middle shelves were too high. The master trader's solution was a magical bookshelf.

Joe watched the smartly dressed businessman carelessly slip the journal onto an open space. The ledger then began its journey, sliding through the books around it as it moved upward. This compelled the books along its path to shift and shuffle, sometimes moving down a shelf. The book climbed up four levels, causing every other book on the affected shelves to ripple to a slightly new spot. It was a mesmerizing effect. With [Witchsight], Joe watched the enchantment pass from one bit of the shelf’s silver ornamentation to the next.

His attention was yanked off the display by a polite throat-clearing from their host.

“This is going to be interesting. Excellent idea, bringing the guild’s build-trainer with you. Miss Bracey,” he greeted, tipping his top hat to her, which he wore even indoors. “I have no doubt you will appreciate what you will be offered, Joe, but she will be able to confirm its value and save any unnecessary hesitation. Even though the being we are getting the trait from is virtually immortal, Pyrrhos has surprisingly little patience. If you two would follow me, let us get ourselves somewhere less flammable so we can summon her.”

Following the quick-paced man back to the lobby, they turned left, then shortly took another left to a set of stairs leading up. While the leprechaun’s legs were half the length of Joe’s and Kenda’s, Joe found he had to engage [Hunter’s Haste] to keep up. Kenda tossed Joe a surprised expression at their rapid-paced ascent. Dorian obviously knew both of them had the ability to easily follow him, and it was becoming clear that he was not the type to waste a minute if he didn't have to.

They reached an extensive balcony where a massive fey silver brazier had already been lit. Three attendants were on hand tending to the flames. Joe’s acute sense of smell picked up a plethora of unusual smoky scents emanating from the blaze: aromatic woods, a heady incense, herbs, flowers, and spices. He even noted something that might have been citrus peels among the numerous additives to the burning fire.

Dorian thanked his people, and they withdrew, leaving the three of them alone on the great terrace. The brazier was set up at the farthest end of this ballroom-sized balcony. Whoever this Pyrrhos was, the financier was making sure there would be plenty of space between her and his holdings.

“Alright, here we go,” the short mogul declared, pulling a neatly folded document from the inner pocket of his jacket. “Pyrrhos. Come and fulfill your due,” he stated quietly, before flicking the parchment into the burning brazier. The fire flared brighter, but the folded page floated in the flames, not burning.

Second by second, the fire grew higher and hotter, forcing Joe and Dorian to take a few steps back. Even with the extra distance, Joe could feel the skin on his cheeks and forehead tighten as the moisture evaporated. Kenda, having grown up in a glassworks, was utterly unperturbed by the scorching air. The only reason she eventually stepped away and rejoined the other two was when she realized she was standing out there by herself.

By now, the flames towered at least a dozen feet into the air, and the fire had somehow inverted its shape; it was wider at the top than the bottom. The flames still burned upwards, of course, but the shape was more of an ice cream cone than a pyramid.

In a rippling motion, the towering flames unfurled. Two burning wings swept outward, sending a wave of superheated air searing across them. A shield of energy flared around Dorian, who was scowling in annoyance. Kenda raised her hand to shield her face, while Joe had to heal the blisters forming on his exposed skin. It was getting harder and harder to breathe, and he was beginning to fear his shirt was about to burst into flames, when a crazy idea hit him.

‘Fire with fire.’

[Halefire]. Create a spectral campfire. Any living being within Short range of the manifestation regains 64 points of Health every minute. The fire lasts for one hour. Undead creatures in this area will take twice the amount of damage instead of gaining health. The flames radiate a gentle aura of warmth and comfort, blocking unpleasant environmental conditions, such as harsh winds or rain. They will not burn their surroundings or living creatures. Cost: Modest Mana | Range: Short. {Phoenix}

Adding more fire to the scorching air seemed like a terrible idea, but [Halefire] promised protection from unpleasant environmental conditions. It worked. Within the flames of the [Halefire], not only were Joe’s burns being healed, but the overwhelming heat was being blocked as well.

Just as Joe was finally able to draw a full breath, the creature’s head lifted from its chest. The massive fiery raptor immediately swung its gaze onto him and his spell. Joe felt [Undaunted] activate, tamping down the instinctive terror a mouse feels when a hawk fixes its gaze on it.

Pyrrhos, the Flame Eternal: Level 226: Elemental, Striker, Dexterity

Standing amidst his own feeble phoenix flames, Joe found his eyes locked with those of an actual phoenix.

“Holy crap,” he stuttered.
