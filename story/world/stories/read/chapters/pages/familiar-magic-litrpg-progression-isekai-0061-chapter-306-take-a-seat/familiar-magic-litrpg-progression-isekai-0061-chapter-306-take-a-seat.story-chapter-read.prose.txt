“What, what?” Richard asked.

Ethan smiled. “I need your help. I can take The Throne, but it will overwhelm me on my own. I need you all to act as buffers against the power.”

Rich’s eyes widened. “Ethan, that’s Dusk’s power. A god’s power. How can you possibly expect–”

“If this is what Ethan needs,” Valanor said in his stern voice, “then this is what we’ll give him. We’re saving three worlds here, and it’s worth all our lives and more.”

Dean moved to stand next to Valanor, and the two exchanged respectful nods. That made Ethan smile. The two men had not gotten along when they’d first met, both seeming to vie for the role of Ethan’s older brother.

“We’re with you, Ethan,” Dean said.

Richard hastily came up to stand beside Dean, turning a glare on the shield knight. “Don’t talk to Bishops about dying for a cause, Valery, it’s in our blood.”

Valanor’s face remained blank at the insult, and Michael stepped forward to elbow Rich in the arm, before whispering in his ear.

“The big white demon was his brother? How the hell was I supposed to know that? And how was he a demon? Is Valanor a demon? No one tells me anything.”

Michael just shook his head. “We’re with you, Ethan. To the end.”

“Though more preferable,” Prince Calavero said as he came forward, “would be a long and happy life. Full of adventure, love, and good wine, if possible.” One corner of his mouth quirked up in a smile. “But what will be, will be.”

Ethan smiled back at them. Six men, brothers all, and each ready to die by his side. Though Cal was right, ideally they’d all get through this.

Ethan’s smile faded as he looked out on the battle with the demon army, nearly at its end. “I can’t bring Selina or Cara into this. They’ve both been shaped by Dusk’s corrupted power. They’d be torn apart.”

“Do you think either one of them would be willing to accept that?” Valanor asked.

Ethan chuckled. “Probably not. Couple of damned heroes, those two.” He turned back to the other. “We should get started, before anything else can go wrong.”

Belying his own words, he stepped toward the six men, meeting the eyes of each one. “Thank you,” he said simply.

In truth, he tried to find more words. Individual words for every person staring back at him. He wanted to tell Valanor how much his friendship had meant. How the man had become an anchor for him on another world.

He wanted to tell Calevaro that he’d never stopped being surprised by who the man had become, despite the weight of responsibility, and tragedy. That his honor and integrity had been all that stopped Ethan from giving in to the rage he’d once felt, lost in a kingdom and hated its powerful king.

And his brothers… There simply weren’t enough words for who those three were to him, and what their support meant. That they could look at their little brother–the one who’d yelled in each of their faces at one point or another that he’d never be a soldier–and show such trust.

Ethan tried to say all those things, but his mouth simply opened and shut, and he stood there for a moment, feeling foolish in the silence.

Dean reached out and put a hand on his shoulder, smiling a knowing smile.

“Go on, Chosen. Do what you’ve always wanted to do.”

Ethan smiled, and nodded, though he couldn’t make sense of the words. His mind felt ready to explode with the pressure of what he was about to attempt.

Turning, he began to march up the small incline toward The Throne. Revan appeared first, curling up comfortably as if to nap, yet his burning eyes were locked on Ethan.

Deevee appeared next, shrinking in size so the hydra could land behind The Throne, heads peaking over curiously. Finally, came Tomo, the demon Familiar walking shoulder to shoulder with Ethan as he ascended.

“Fear not, we will shield you from the Corruption. If it worked for Strategus, we can surely do the same. If it changes us–”

Ethan smiled, putting a hand on his friend’s arm to stop him.

“Strategus didn’t have an enormous, self-important Spirit to help him. Mine will cleanse the Corruption as it has been all this time, leaving nothing but pure godly-power.”

Tomo’s smiled his draconic smile. “I had hoped…but I did not wish you to need to ask this of us, were it not so.” He paused. “It is good to know my mind will not again fall to the terrors of this world. But…will we survive this thing?”

Ethan put on a serious face. “Look at me, look at this confidence. What does that tell you?”

Tomo smiled wider. “That you are as good a liar as ever, Ethan Bishop.”

Ethan laughed with his friend, but his seat was waiting. He’d paused right before it. Almost time.

“Apollo,” he said into his communication rune, “are we ready?”

“We’re ready. Fable and I can hold the Leviathan without you for a while. Just be quick about this.”

Ethan rolled his eyes. “Sure, I’ll rush through mastering a god’s power and joining three worlds. No worries, wouldn’t want you to strain yourself up there.”

“Finally, some consideration,” Apollo replied.

Ethan smirked, but it faded quickly. “You and Zakiel…if I don’t make it–”

“We’re good, Ethan. We know why we’re here. Just do your thing. Be the one of us who wins.”

Ethan smiled then turned back to the others. “Form a circle around The Throne, if you would. Not going to lie…this will be very, very unpleasant.”

“Just worry about yourself,” Rich said, as he and the others formed a rough circle around him. “But, uh, do we actually do anything?”

Ethan shrugged. “Think about something beautiful,” he said. “Something worth living for. And hold on.”

The men gave nods, exchanging nervous looks, but Ethan knew they were ready. The waiting was only going to make things worse.

He took just one more moment, letting the events of the last few years flow over him. He thought of the thousand mad chances and wild risks that had brought him here, to this one opportunity, and let the gravity of what he was about to attempt settle inside his soul. It very nearly overwhelmed him, but then he felt a clawed hand on his shoulder.

Tomo looked down at him knowingly.

“Life is either a comedy, or a tragedy,” he said.

Ethan grinned back at him. “Same shit happens either way, the only difference is whether you laugh or cry.”

Ethan laughed at the absurdity of it all, as he sat down on The Throne.

***

There could be no preparing for this.

The power of a god defied description, defied Ethan’s ability to feel. It was like holding a world in his hands. But no, not his hands, it was in his mind, his soul, his body. He was this world. This terrible, broken, brutal world.

Ethan had become Potentia. He could feel it, his consciousness extending across a distance so vast that it seemed to shatter his mind again and again, while the power of Dusk pushed it back together.

Time no longer existed, not in a way he understood, yet Ethan hadn’t gone into this unprepared. He knew he had work to do, and had ordered his thoughts so it would begin immediately.

Even as everything that was Ethan Bishop was torn apart, rebuilt, and torn apart once more, his spirit settled itself in the Astral. It became a barrier between Ethan and the power, acting like a filter for the unimaginable torrent that tried to wash Ethan away.

An instant later, that power was split in six different directions, crashing into the brothers that had agreed to something they could never understand. That no mortal truly could.

Yet their wills were strong, and they weren’t alone. Each one had Familiars they’d Bonded with for years, and trusted as much as any part of themselves. Sixteen more wills were added to the mix, all focused on one singular mission: give the Chosen the time he needed to do the work of a god.

Somewhere beyond time and space, Ethan felt the madness and currents of power subsiding, and knew that he was being given his chance, and he had to take it.

Only, there was no instruction manual to this process. Even with a vaguely functioning mind, Ethan didn’t immediately see what he needed to do.

He could no longer feel his own body, there was nothing but the power, pressing down on him as if he were stranded at the bottom of the ocean. Distantly, he could feel his own Familiars giving him everything they had as well, trying to help him find the clarity to make this mad endeavor possible.

Ethan forced himself to think. He knew he needed to finish the work that he’d already started, and complete the Union. But to get there…he tried to break the problem down into its component parts, but found he didn’t have enough information.

And yet…the power was his now, and somehow he knew the answers were as well. For the few precious moments that he sat on this Throne, he effectively had become Dusk, and the god must have known what to do.

Ethan searched, his consciousness diving into the power, searching for answers. He found memories, and knew they’d have to do.

First, he risked a single command, knowing he couldn’t manage many, but intuitively understanding that this was the first part. He recalled Dusk’s energy from across this world. The Corruption, vile and filthy and twisted, was suddenly pulled back to The Throne, and crashed into Ethan’s spirit with immeasurable force.

But that spirit currently belonged to a god, and blessedly, it held.

While it did its work to cleanse the Corruption, Ethan dove deeper into the memories he’d found, struggling to make sense of them.

***

Dusk’s garden was beautiful, as always. Each world had its own remarkable blossoms, but Dusk tried to top himself with each new attempt. Such was his purpose, the purpose that his maker had bestowed upon him.

This time, his crowning achievement was the Nator. His wonderful, perfect Nator.

They were young yet, but he knew they’d bloom into such wonders, given time. Dusk had to be careful, with time. He’d learned the hard way that giving too much of his power, his potential too quickly, and his blossoms died.

Sometimes in spectacular fashion, it was true, but they died all the same.

He needed to plant seeds, and let them grow, let them change, let them evolve. He’d done so this time on this red world, and knew he’d made something uniquely marvelous.

The Soul Rune might be his greatest creation yet, and he’d already shared it with Dawn–though Twilight was being stubborn, as usual. Couldn’t he see its majesty?

Normally, Dusk would shape all the power himself, then leave the rest to the great Union. But the Soul Rune…it would shape the individual, by the individual. Power made to serve, and unique to the soul of its owner.

It could solve problems Dusk could never imagine. Even problems Twilight, for all his complicated equations couldn’t predict. Every soul would have a chance to shape itself for the challenges it experienced.

It was a work of art.

Now Dusk merely needed to wait. Wait for the Union to begin.

Should it have already begun? Time was so difficult to measure.

But he would wait. Wait. Wait.

Any moment now, it would come.

It had to come. He’d been here a long time.

Too long?

Surely not.

Wait. Wait. Wait.

Dusk felt…cold.

Something was very wrong.
