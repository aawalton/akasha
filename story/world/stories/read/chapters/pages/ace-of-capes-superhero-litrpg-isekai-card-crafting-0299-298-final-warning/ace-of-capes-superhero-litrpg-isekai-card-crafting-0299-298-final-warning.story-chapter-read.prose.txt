“What do you mean?” Lexie asked, taking in the scene. It wasn’t like usual, the normal inky darkness that served as a backdrop for most of their meetings.

This dream world was burning, set ablaze by a black flame that covered everything but them. “Go where? Why am I in danger?”

"We both know I cannot tell you that. I already risk a lot coming here to warn you.” He held out his large, clawed hand.“We must go.”

Lexie didn’t take the hand. “Go where?”

“To the Eldritch realm. You must complete your test.”

Lexie let out a frustrated breath. “I already told you that I can’t do that right now.”

“You must.”

“I can’t, not right now. Earth’s in danger and I have to save it.”

Naem made a sound in his throat that Lexie had never heard him make before. It was like a strained sound, like he wastrying to say words but was choking on them.

She blinked at him. Was he trying to tell her something? Well, he already had, but maybe he was trying to say something else, something the Fae wouldn't want her to know.

“Is this about the Fae?”

"You must complete your Eldritch test.”

“They’re planning something, aren’t they?” She thought back to the Lightlarks and what they said about it being much worse for her if the Fae came. “They’re coming to capture me clandestinely. Is that it?”

His visage didn't change. He wasn't wearing a human mask today; he was in full Eldritch form, taking the appearance of the one she'd signed a contract with. Even though he was still as large as ever, there was something also about him that was different, almost smaller. Less...there. Was he fading away? Were they hurting him?

‘’You must complete your Eldritch test.”

“Are they hurting you?” Lexie asked. “Is that it? Are they making you come get me or else?”

“You must complete your Eldritch test.” The way he said it was like a robot that had been programmed to repeat a single sentence over and over. Was this even the real Naem? It didn’t feel quite like him. The dream wasn’t like his dreams, which were calm and silent.

He typically wasn’t this expressive, especially with the desperation in his voice.

It was like someone knew of Naem and Lexie’s relationship and was pretending to be him to trick her in her dream. Neqal? Or maybe the Fae.

This felt like something the Fae would do, especially as all their other attempts to trap Lexie had failed.

Of course, there was also the possibility that this was the real Naem. That he'd just been altered in some intangible way, but it was still him.

However, the real Naem would not be so insistent on her completing the Fae test. It didn’t align with his long-term goals.

She assumed he, like the other Eldritch or at least like Neqal, wanted freedom from the Fae contract. However, if Lexie became an Eldritch Lord, she would be bound by the same oath that bound them all, and she wouldn’t be able to help them.

So why was he being so insistent on it?

Was there something she was missing? Or was he giving her code for something else?

“Do you really want me to complete the Eldritch test?” Lexie asked. “Or is there something else you’re not telling me?”

Naem's eyes were unblinking.

“You’re at a crossroad,” he told her. “Between who you were and who you must become. You must move forward, Lexie Sparrowfoot, or you will be forced to remain still.”

A proverb. Which meant there was something else he was trying to tell her. This was probably about her being the King of the Eldritch. He might be trying to tell her that if she didn’t gain the title now, she would never be able to gain it again in the future.

Maybe there was a time limit on it or something, or maybe the longer she stayed on Earth, the more human her soul became, dominating the Eldritch, which then meant that she couldn’t be the King of the Eldritch and couldn’t be a Lord either.

Lexie took a second to think about the possibility of never becoming a King.

Truly, she felt a pang of loss, but not as much as she thought she would have felt. That goal didn’t really have much of a true grounding anyway. She’d wanted it because it was a certification of her power, because it proved that she was better and stronger than everything and everyone else, and that appeased her Eldritch light.

However, right now, she had more varied light than just the pure Eldritch drive she’d had in the past. Yes, she was still ambitious, but it was tuned toward something else, making her living cards. Yes, she was still vicious, but it was controlled and only used against those who provoked her.

She felt a connection to the Eldritch world, but overpowering that was a connection to Earth and its people. Mostly, though, she wanted to spend more time with her dad and her loved ones. She didn't want Earth to perish.

Saving it was her priority right now, and so she didn’t see herself going to the Eldritch realm any time soon, even if it was to save them.

“We don’t have time,” Naem said.  “We must leave now.”

“I’m sorry," she said. “I don’t think I can.’

“Lexie…”

“Naem, I’ll help you with whatever contract with the Fae you're stuck in,” she told him. "Just not now."

His eyebrows furrowed.

“This is not just about me,” he said, and he gestured with his hand. “You are in danger. Lexie, soul spawn, come with me.”

She stared at him and at his hand. He kept insisting she was in danger, but he was offering her a solution that would put her in even more danger, and he couldn't explain further.

She didn’t see how joining the Eldritch oath and being controlled by the Fae helped her.

Maybe there was a secret meaning there, something he wasn’t saying about how it would protect her, and maybe she would figure it out if she went with him.

But ultimately, she just didn’t trust that to be the truth. And that was the thing, wasn’t it…she didn’t trust Naem, not after he’d left her in the dungeon.

This was how he'd done it the first time, by appealing to her emotion and the strange trust that she'd had in him.

This could be yet another trap, and she would be once again the fool for taking it.

And even if he was telling the truth, leaving right now would mean abandoning her family. Aiden and Tate. Torin. Stella, who was still fighting for her life. Dewie, Xena, and Uncle Max.

It would mean abandoning all those people who’d come to look up to her and abandoning Earth to the Fae and Saint-0 forever.

She couldn’t do that.

She had to save them even if it ended up hurting her. She took a step back.

“No. I’m not going.”

He didn’t try to argue.

His face shifted in a startling, garish way before it smoothed out again, like it had been snapped into place. He stared at her with that dark, unblinking gaze, the silence between them stretching, the world around them burning.

“Goodbye, my soul spawn,” he said, and for the first time, he disappeared before the dream was over.

Lexie stood there, wondering what had just happened. She was confused even as the scene cleared of Naem’s energy and returned to a dream she didn’t remember having of her mother braiding her hair.

She stared at the scene from the outside looking in. She was sitting between Lara's legs, watching an AFC match on the pad, and while it happened, her mother was predicting each move and who would win.

She was right every time, and Lexie kept asking how she knew so much, but Lara refused to tell.

She only stared down at her daughter fondly, her eyes oozing with affection.

"Mom..."

Lexie finally admitted the true depth of the loss she felt in her heart. She'd been fighting it, trying to remain objective, but how did one remain objective about the fact that their mother was dead?

Or not dead.

Likely trapped with a crazy Eldritch scientist.

Lexie sighed. After they were done dealing with Saint-0, she would dedicate the rest of her time to Vulcan's memories and finding her mother. She would find Neqal and take him up on his offer. She would find her mother, in whatever form she was, and they would bring her back.

That was her promise.

It was some time before the dream dissipated in her mind. She felt the sensation of being shaken awake, and when she stared up at her father’s expression, he gently swiped his thumb underneath her eye.

It came away wet.

“You were crying." His voice was quiet, gentle. “Did you have a bad dream, honeybee?”

She shook her head. "It was a weird dream. I dreamt of mom and then…” She sat up in bed. "Naem was there."

Aiden stiffened. His eyes narrowed. “What did he want?”

“He told me I was in danger. Asked me to go with him and complete the Eldritch trials.” She paused. “Could he have taken me with him through the dream?”

“Possibly, but you would have needed to allow him to temporarily possess your body first.”

“Oh.” So he could have saved her from the dungeon if he wanted to.

“Did he explain what the danger was?”

She shook her head. “I don’t think he could. When he would try to say it, his throat would make a funny sound, and he just kept telling me that I needed to take the Eldritch test, but I don’t see how that would protect me.”

Aiden mused on it and rubbed his chin. “Naem is trapped with the Fae. As the strongest Eldritch, he assumes the title and power of an Eldritch monarch, and while that gives him some freedoms and privileges, it also makes him the Eldritch most attached to the Fae, working closely with the Gloom. I don’t exactly know what that does to him, but it can’t be a pleasant experience.”

“So you think that he wants me to become the strongest Eldritch so I can free him from his contract and take his place with the Gloom?"

“Perhaps.”

“But whatever he said sounded urgent and time sensitive. And he looked…" She was about to say that he looked genuine, but she remembered that was exactly how she’d been tricked the first time.

"He’s Eldritch, Lexie,” Aiden said.

“I know.”

“They’re self-serving by nature."

“I know that too.” She’d experienced firsthand what a single-minded Eldritch drive felt like. Once upon a time, all she'dwanted was power, without care for anything else around her. She didn't think about who it would hurt or how her getting that power would affect those around her.

It wasn't that Naem was trying to hurt her by deceiving her. He just always looks out for his own interests.

Still, she was too human not to see the humanity in other creatures, even if they were as peculiar as the Eldritch. "‘I told him no, that I wouldn't be going with him. And yeah…that’s that.”

Aiden seemed satisfied with that answer. “We should still be on the lookout. If he’s trying to warn you, then it means that the Fae might be on the move. By the way, the Alchemist claims that he has no clue who Mikhail is.”

“Could the information have taken it out of his memory?”

“Perhaps. I asked him about creating the ISTS, and he did admit that he worked with the Engineer and the Architect on it, but he said that the First Mage was not involved.”

“What?” Lexie frowned.’

“That’s what he said.”

That threw some of Lexie’s ideas into whack until she said, “Maybe it’s like I said, and he was brought in to replace Mikhail after he was ousted.”

“No. The Alchemist claims he was there from the beginning, from the conception of the ISTS. It was their brainchild, although he had already left by the time they conceptualized the Chosen program, so he didn’t know what it was, had only heard vague ideas of what it could do. When Vulcan visited him, he finally saw what had become of his old research."

“Hmmm." Lexie brushed the hair out of her face. "You think he’s telling the truth?”

“He has no reason to lie to me, and also, he can’t lie to me technically according to our contract. Whether or not his brain was altered before, to remove information, is another matter. Either way, we should tread lightly.”

She nodded. “I also dreamt about mom.”

Aidne swallowed. “Yes, you mentioned."

“I dream about her more often now, almost every time I sleep. After this is done…I’m going to find her. I think she’s still around, just not in the same way as we remember. Neqal basically confirmed this, and I’m going to look for her, with or without you.”

“I’m glad you’re remembering her again,” he said. “It always made me feel sad to think that you might not remember her at all. She would be so proud to see the person you’ve become.”

They went downstairs, where Tate and Torin were already awake and at the table. Both boys were sitting opposite each other, with Isaac’s device in front of Tate. They were silent, but it was the comfortable sort of silence.

"I'm sorry."

Or a cabin in the woods that five friends would go to and get brutally murdered in a horror film.

It would be fine.
