"Have you heard from Paltra?" Maerin asked after another drink. After the fourth shot, she had opted to mix the lemonade in her drink.

I shook my head. The alcohol had torn through my system, and I was well and truly drunk. It felt fantastic. The stress slid out of my body, and I found it hard to focus on anything. Whenever the seed of something awful was planted, the alcohol poisoned that soil and broke it before the roots could claw at my thoughts. I understood the drugs Rowan took a bit more now. I felt strangely closer to him because of it. He had gone through something similar.

Right, Maerin had asked a question. I shook my head, "Nope. I assumed you'd all be caught in the same place, not acting as body snatchers."

"I resent that," she snapped harshly, "you speak as if I had a say. I did not wish to wake up in your friend's fucking bed."

I glared at her. My mind felt split. The reasonable part of me understood the issue, but that part had drowned itself in alcohol. "Trust me. I don't think any of us are happy with that outcome. You killed someone today, and no one can even remember her name."

"Cry about it," she shot back, and I reeled back. She smiled, "Oh, what did you want me to be the one to do that? No. I'm sorry for the girl I replaced, but she's not around for me to apologize to. You don't deserve an apology."

Oh, she was drunk too. I could feel the inhibitions sloughing off of her, and all the frustration beneath the surface lay bare. My comments and the events of the day had struck a nerve somewhere. She was angry. That was fine. I was angry too.

"Oh fuck you," I spat. "I saved your life. If not for me all of you die in that Instance."

"My hero. I didn't know you cared," she mocked. "Thank you for reaching out your hand covered in thorns while slapping me across the face."

"What's your fucking deal?" I took another sip, a distinct lack of vitriol in my voice. It wasn't that her words didn't irk me, but this was all just a distraction. Treating it with any amount of sincerity while I drank myself into a stupor felt wrong.

"I was perfectly content to drink and try to figure out something, if you stop making snide, brainless remarks, we can go back to that." She scowled.

"Oh, of course, let me pretend everything is fine then," a small amount of derision dripped from my mouth."

"River, take me, are you always this self-centered and self-loathing?" She stood and jutted a finger in my direction. "You're acting like the victim of all of this."

"We are victims here. In case you haven't noticed the Dream is basically a cosmic monster." I shouted back, exhausted.

Maerin basically snarled at me, "That's the fucking point. You're not the only one dealing with this. You killed part of a God, yet you still think things happen to you. You're not some powerless rabbit. You're a Visitor strong enough to manifest the Boons of the Dream in reality. You do not get to pretend you're helpless."

I forced myself to pause and take a deep breath. The more she talked, the more I regretted allowing her to come to my world. This was always my problem. I didn't fucking think. I just acted based on how I was feeling, and lately those feelings had been acting like a pendulum---swinging between uncaring and caring too much in equal measure. I was a wreck. I took another drink. I hated to admit it, but I had no rebuttal to her words. I mainly had hurtful responses, but none could refute her point. I was not helpless here.

"Why couldn't you just go to Jeren's cushy little world? I would have taken him up on the offer if I were you." I waved a wayward hand, winding down from the argument.

"Because I had a choice. Plus, you still owe me," Maerin eased back into the couch, clearly still annoyed with me.

I laughed, a full-throated and slurred thing, "Did you seriously come to this world because of a favor?" I stopped myself from saying more. I didn't want to insult her and cause another fight.

Maerin did not find the same humor in the situation as I did. "It was not the only reason. Even without it I wouldn't have gone to Jeren's world. I refuse to be just another helpless Visitor."

"And the others?" I questioned, "I doubt Krevith and Paltra would have chosen here without you."

"They're adults. Older than either of us. They're perfectly capable of making their own decisions for their own reasons. I forced nothing on them," she said.

I stared at Maerin, and my eyes drifted to the ends of her long hair. No longer gray. She looked healthier now, not as malnourished. I couldn't help but think she would make a better Instance Holder than I.

We said nothing for a while, the silence turning uncomfortable after a point. I offered an olive branch, "You look healthier."

Maerin shifted slightly, running a hand through her hair and then touching the half of her face that used to have a massive burn scar on it. "Yes, your world rebuilt my body, I think." She had gone from sitting to lounging at some point. "I don't know how I feel about the changes."

"What do you mean?" I asked, knowing full well what she was talking about. I had felt something similar after beating the Fields Instance.

"It feels, cheap," Maerin started. "I didn't like them. I'd look in the mirror every day and I hated what I saw, but now that they're gone," she let the words hang, struggling with what she wanted to say. "I'm glad they're gone, but they were mine still. I endured so much, and now the proof is gone too."

I didn't know what to say. We weren't close enough to have more of a heart-to-heart than this. Krevith would pull something profound and helpful out in this moment. Paltra would no doubt just hug her friend. I just nodded, "I'm sorry to hear that." I then shifted the conversation topic as fast as I could. "Before we came here I didn't have a chance to talk to everyone. How's Krevith holding up?" A beat of silence, "I know he didn't exactly want to survive the Instance."

Maerin pursed her lips, "Not good. He hardly said a word before we came here. Just that he would follow me, and nothing else. The Fiend did a number on him, no damage to his soul or body, but a lack of control is not a pleasant thing."

I nodded, remembering how The Being in The Static … Ananke stole my mouth and nose in the Dream and how everyone in the caravan was trapped in the Chimeras. "It seems to be a favorite pasttime of the Dream," I replied bitterly.

"Thank you, for saving him. I owe him a great many debts, and even if it was against his wishes I'm glad he survived. He sees himself as evil, as someone unredeemable through action alone. That's why he wanted to return to the River," she had a complicated, almost solemn expression written on her face.

"There was a lot of talk of the River in the Instance. Something to do with reincarnation. What is it exactly?" I asked.

"It is a sort of reincarnation," Maerin admitted. "When we die, our soul is gathered, separated from the body, and the tar is split from the pure. That tar is molded with others of its type and turned into a new soul and given a new body. These tar souls have two goals. The first is to take on the cruel tasks, executioners, soldiers, laborers. The second is to strive to do good. That way, something pure can be born from all the tar of those who came before."

I frowned at that, "Krevith believes he was born from this tar?"

Maerin nodded, "He was born from it. There is no denying that, but there is no denying he is good either."

I found Maerin's words impossible. Everything Krevith had shown in the Instance was kind and thoughtful. He was like Jeren in many ways, just without the hero complex. I took another drag from the bottle.

"We should try to find the others tonight, in the Dream. I'd rather all the Survivors be in one spot, especially with all the spiders and other shit."

Maerin winced, "Hate that you reminded me. I almost forgot."

"Yeah well if I have to remember so do you," I replied.

"How dreadful," was her reply.

"What are you gonna use your favor on?" I asked suddenly.

She looked at me before shaking her head, "You've asked too many questions and I've got to ask none. My turn. Whose Rowan? He's your brother, also a Visitor?"

My expression soured remembering the VHS tapes in Joey's truck bed. I still loved my brother. No amount of his creating Instances would change that, but my feelings were becoming more complicated. Complicated enough that I was okay with Maerin seeing the tapes. "It might be easier to show you actually."

Joey had a VHS player somewhere in his apartment, I knew that. Ten minutes of stumbling later, I had the journals, tapes, and Joey's old VHS player all set up. I slid one of the recordings in and sat on the couch next to Maerin.

"Before we start. Yes, he is my brother, but I knew nothing about this. I thought I knew him, but now ... I'm not so sure," I said.

Maerin gave me an appraising look, but nodded, as if she understood. "I have a similar relationship with my father and mother."

I hesitated over the play button on the remote. "Are they still?" I tapered off.

"No. They're dead. I haven't seen them since I was ten," Maerin said.

This should be where I gave her a reassuring pat or a hug, but I barely managed that with Joey. I usually didn't mind touch, but ever since the plant Chimeras. I pushed the thought from my head. Maerin didn't even like being touched, so there was no point. "I'm sorry," I decided on.

"Me too," she echoed before sighing, "I'm sure we both have complicated feelings about our respective people. Now show me what's on the magic box."

I was too stunned by the last sentence, "What?"

She gestured to the TV, "The magic box that shows images."

"The TV?" I questioned.

"Is that what you call it?" she asked.

I hesitated, "Do you not have these in your world? Wait, what technology level was your world even? I saw guns and some lights, but also the structures seemed preindustrial."

"We have light bulbs," she pointed at the ceiling, "but only the wealthiest could afford them. We mostly used magic in our everyday, but I seem to have lost the ability now that I'm a Visitor. Now play the fucking TV."

I wanted to ask more questions, but Maerin was clearly done answering. I hit play. Then immediately hit pause.

"Sorry, you had magic?" I asked.

"Hit. Play."
