Chapter V.LXVIII (5.68) - Hydra, Beagle, Doppelgänger

The hydra’s first head darted forward, sparks of electricity flashing as it moved with near untraceable speed. Unfortunately for it, Kizu’s jump defied all physical speed limits.

Rather than go on the offensive, Kizu jumped back into the interrogation room.

As he suspected, a nearby beacon attempted to carry him off the moment he jumped. Likely the very same beacon that first trapped him when he found Anata. However, it only knocked him a meter or so off target, landing him in the middle of one of the chairs. It could have been far worse, as it simply required Kizu to step out of the mangled seat. Removing himself from inside a stone wall would have been a nightmare.

“You’re going the wrong way!” his doppelgänger called after him in a sing-song voice. “Come back and dance with me!”

Kizu was tempted to comply. With the returning part, not the dancing. He still had the bowl of soul destroying invisible liquid suspended beside him. If he managed to aim it properly, he could potentially be done with his doppelgänger once and for all. Extinguishing that wayward soul fragment would resolve so many issues.

Rather than a directly offensive move like hitting his doppelgänger with the potion now, Kizu opted for a different strategy. He fled back further, retreating in the direction he’d come from.

It was a gamble, but he was hoping it might pay off.

As he dashed into the next room, he enhanced his strength and scooped Kumiho off the floor. He needn’t have bothered with the spell, as he realized that, while starved and without her legs, she weighed very little.

Kizu burst through the next door and let out a sigh of relief at the sight. His doppelgänger had been honest while ranting about his friends. In front of him, in cages of their own, were Ione and Basil.

It was a surprisingly spacious room with a high ceiling. A glance around revealed bloodied tables to the side and rows of metal instruments lining the walls. Clumps of red fur were scattered around one of the tables and Kizu thought he spotted a large fox foot before he tore his eyes away. The earlier room had been Otochi’s interrogation chamber. This was the Blood Lord’s torture chamber.

“Oh, is it time for the prisonbreak?” Basil asked, sitting cross-legged in his cage. He cracked his neck to the side and then slid his body forward. His flesh squished together and he slid through the bars, despite them being half a handspan in width. “Ione isn’t awake yet. Was waiting until she got up to break out and look for you.”

“It’s fine,” Kizu said. “We can handle this. Prepare for a fight. My doppelgänger is chasing me and he has a hydra. If I can get the liquid from inside this bowl on him, he’ll be gone for good.”

“Always straight to business. Nobody ever bothers with greetings and pleasantries anymore,” Basil grumbled as he took a fighting stance beside Kizu.

Kizu would have preferred for his girlfriend to be awake for this upcoming battle, but Basil’s assistance should be enough to help keep the summons busy.

He quickly stashed Kumiho in one of the empty cells off to the side just as his doppelgänger stepped up to the room’s threshold. He smiled maniacally at them, excitedly waving hello. As he passed through the door, his hydra blasted through the wall. Kizu blocked the spray of rubble with a barrier and flung it back at the beast, swinging chunks of stone around with telekinesis to maintain the momentum.

The beagle leaped through the air, its massive ears acting wings, and slammed its small body into the rubble, knocking it off course right as the stone nearly collided with one hydra head’s eyes. The rubble instead smashed into the torture chamber’s wall, taking a chunk off it. As the dog tumbled back to the floor, it accidentally tripped on his own massive ears, sending it sprawling in a heap. It otherwise appeared unharmed by the self induced blunt force trauma.

“You know, there’s no exit from here?” his doppelgänger asked with a smarmy smirk. “All you’ve done is skip on over to a dead end.”

“That’s fine. I’ll simply go through you,” Kizu replied.

Dexterously, Kizu separated a marble-size bit of the potion with telekinesis, then stretched the space in front of it. He snapped the space. The invisible liquid orb shot forward, quick as lightning, targeting the doppelgänger .

The hydra, unfortunately, was likewise as fast as lightning. In a more literal sense. With a crack, a jolt of electricity shot from its jaws and blasted the liquid from the air. It vaporized Kizu’s attack long before it struck home. The wisps of steam were visible despite the fact the liquid itself hadn’t been.

“Come now,” his doppelgänger taunted. “You truly believe you’re a match for me? I’m your better half!”

“This is the guy?” Basil lifted his arms up and stretched, his body bending unnaturally. “Heard a lot about evil Kizu. Hard to believe you have an even worse fashion sense. What is that you’re wearing? It looks like rags from a fashion five centuries out of date. I do hope you’re not planning to bring that back. You don’t even have the best part of the ensemble. Where are the chaps? You are missing the chaps!”

“Basil.” The doppelgänger grinned. “I’ve been asked not to kill you. But, as your cousin can vouch for, that limitation does not extend to your good health. And I’ve met enough Oozes now to know you’re quite a… resilient species.”

Basil scoffed. Whatever response he intended to bite back with, it was lost as the hydra lunged at him. He deftly side-stepped and his body rippled, a chunk of his mass morphing out of the way. The teeth still grazed his jacket, ripping a gash in the enchanted fabric.

Kizu stepped in the direction of his friend, with the intention to send blades of force in the direction of the over-extended neck.

“Kizu! Focus!” Basil shouted.

The warning came just in time for Kizu to see a potion bottle lackadaisically hurled in his direction. It lacked the supernatural speed he applied to his own tosses and so Kizu had more than enough time to pluck it from midair with telekinesis and return it to the sender. Right at his doppelgänger’s smug face.

The little beagle once again threw itself up into the air, intersecting the potion and bursting into flame. The noble beast collapsed on the ground. Instead of dissolving, like Kizu would expect from a summon who’d taken that much damage, the flames flickered out and the dog rose back to its feet. It shook itself, ears slapping against the stone as they flapped. Then it sat back on its haunches, panting and wagging its tail.

“Good boy!” His doppelgänger patted the dog on its brow. “Nice catch! Who’s a good dog? You! What a little cutie you are. Able to gobble up a fire blast to the face. The bestest little pooch to ever be summoned!”

Deciding to trust Basil to handle the hydra for now, Kizu turned the entirety of his attention over to his doppelgänger, whose baby talk to the dog had now switched to chanting something incomprehensible in what Kizu understood to be backwards Primordial. If that wasn’t enough of a hint, his fingers waggled in Kizu’s direction, clearly indicating the soul fragment’s intentions.

While invisible to the naked eye, nearly all hexes required the magic to expel from the caster and travel a distance to hit the target. That meant that Kizu could block the spell with an antimagic barrier.

At the same time he constructed the barrier protection, Kizu sent out two blades of force in the direction of his enemy. The grand weakness of all summoners was their physical form. It was common for some of the best combat oriented summoners to dual train in illusions to hide themselves from danger and remove themselves from the board or defensive enhancements to run and protect themselves. Since neither was an option for his broken soul shard, it left him exposed and vulnerable.

Or, so Kizu thought.

The beagle intercepted one of the force blades, the impact topping it to the side but otherwise not harming the dog. Kizu expected as much though, which was why he’d sent the two blades at angles where they couldn’t both be blocked simultaneously.

The second force blade ripped through his doppelgänger’s flesh at the same moment that the hex slammed into Kizu’s barrier. To his dismay, his protective shell actually buckled under the impact and shattered. Only Kizu’s monster leg’s reflexes saved him from tanking the hex to his chest. Instead, he pivoted and the hex shot past him.

He watched in horror as it bounced off the wall and ricochetted in Basil’s direction.

The changeling was preoccupied, one of his arms disconnected, split in two, and looped around two of the hydra’s seven mouths, while the other arm furiously cast elemental explosive spells.

Too far to construct a barrier in time, Kizu jumped in front of the hex. The beacon’s drag still pulled him slightly off target, but he wasn’t trying to go far and he now roughly knew where the beacon was from his other jumps, which assisted his resistance to it. He raised his arm and took the hex on himself.

The skin of his palm blackened and flaked. He clamped down on the curse with a blast of antimagic; however, in that split second it took him to cast his spell, the hex had already taken root in half of his open hand. He immediately diagnosed the most likely curse used and knew he had a potion to cure himself of its effects, but first needed to remove the spatial storage ring from his doppelgänger’s finger.

Lucky for him, at the same time Kizu had been deflecting the hex and protecting Basil, his earlier force blade had ripped through his enemy’s flesh, leaving a significant gash in his shoulder and rendering his entire left arm useless.

“Ow!” His doppelgänger poked at the bloodless wound. The arm twitched, but otherwise remained unresponsive to his prodding. From the outside, his body appeared to be organic flesh, its exposed internals told a different story. The bone was a discolored tanned color and thick ichor bubbled out of the bone in bursts, rather than normal blood. “I’ll have you know, Otochi did design pain receptors in this host! It’s extremely rude for you to hurt me like this!”

This was the first time Kizu had ever seen his doppelgänger wounded. The revelation that he functioned in a golem-like body was fascinating, but not especially helpful at the moment. In fact, it only served to make his life even more difficult, as it meant mere mortal wounds might not be enough to annihilate his enemy.

The bowl of soul dissolving potion remained floating beside Kizu, sloshing as he dodged a stray blast of fire from the hydra, but safely stored behind barriers.

Kizu jumped, getting within striking distance of his foe.

His doppelgänger struck. A flick of his wrist and he held an ice blade which he chopped down with in a wide, arcing strike.

Expecting the attack Kizu enhanced his speed and raised his leg. The monstrous claws caught his doppelgänger’s wrist and clamped down on the artificial flesh with an extremely tight grip. Bones crunched under his grasp. The sword’s hilt, likewise caught in Kizu’s grip, shattered.

Unfortunately, it left the doppelgänger’s other hand free to press the attack. Even as Kizu shoved his foe aside, his enemy slammed a potion into Kizu’s shoulder. This time, it connected.

That entire side of Kizu’s body morphed into flames as the broken ice blade fell from his doppelgänger’s hand.

At first, Kizu was stunned by his flaming body. It took him a precious second to realize there was no pain from the flames. In fact, he wasn’t on fire, his body was the fire.

Likely without comprehending what it was, his doppelgänger had smashed one of Kizu’s experimental living flame potions he’d developed from the Dragon blood. Kizu had shelved the project for another day after he deemed it too dangerous to test without safety precautions. And, since Anata was terrified of fire, Kizu had simply never gotten to it. Thankfully, his theories turned out to bear fruit. If anything, it was actually a lesser effect than what he’d expected, as only half of his body roared to life as fire. He briefly wondered how it might have functioned if consumed orally rather than smashed into him. A thought for another day, though.

His foe’s lips continued to move, silently mouthing pained curses as he hunched over his managed hand, his other arm dangling uselessly.

Darting forward, his step a bit lopsided, Kizu sent out another blade of force and severed his foe’s working arm at the wrist. With his flaming body, he tackled his enemy to the floor, the fire scorching the artificial body. Kizu’s own screams tore out of the doppelgänger’s mouth as he burned. And yet, there was also something being said in those words. A nonsensical script that his foe refused to stop jabbering, even through the horrific pain and screams.

Doing his best to ignore the sound of his screams, Kizu used his remaining physical hand to snatch up the severed hand from the stone floor and pluck his spatial ring from it. He let out a sigh of relief as he felt the familiar weight of all his potions within the storage.

Next, still pinning his foe beneath his fiery body, Kizu sacrificed a moment of his attention to check on Basil. The shapeshifter was losing in the fight against the hydra. It hadn’t even been half a minute and already a third of his mass was missing as he desperately fought to simply remain alive. Gone were the earlier explosive offensive attacks, now he was completely on the backfoot, defensively dodging and narrowly avoiding death by electric blasts.

Kizu repositioned himself in preparation to drop the bowl’s contents on his foe and jump to Basil’s side. It was tough though, as the doppelgänger squirmed under him while continuing to scream nonsense, making the bowl difficult to drop without it missing or splashing on Kizu. It still floated beside him, suspended by telekinesis, ready to be used.

There was a chance that killing the summoner might not actually dispel the beast. Ione had mentioned before that a truly powerful summoner could disconnect their summon from their existence, so long as they pumped enough blood into it in advance. And extinguishing the soul might not even be the same as killing him. If the body continued to produce that ichor, the summon might remain.

Too late, did Kizu process the fact that his doppelgänger’s nonsensical noise while screaming held more power than simple insults. In hindsight, the Primordial script should have been obvious, but Kizu had spent far too little time in combat with hex casters this last year.

The paralysis struck him, locking all his joints. His rigid body collapsed, unable to respond to his commands. The fire half of his body surged out of control, obliterating a chunk of his doppelgänger’s body and turning it to ash.

Kizu tried to carry himself with telekinesis, but lacked the supreme mastery required for that level of spell control. All he managed was a tug to the side. Still, that sudden jerk proved vital to his survival as the suspended bowl above him fell.

It splashed to the ground, spilling the invisible contents everywhere. Kizu felt horrific pain as a bit of it etched itself into his soul. His doppelgänger proved even less fortunate, the majority of the contents sloshing onto his flaming leg. It hissed and some evaporated in a cloud of steam, but a great deal of it still penetrated the doppelgänger’s blazing clothes and touched skin.

Kizu thought that was the end of his foe, that finally he’d stumbled on a release from this constant threat on his life.

Then the doppelgänger’s leg popped off.

At the hip, the limb released itself and shriveled under the liquid’s exposure.

Kizu was already in so much pain. His blazing body was fading, returning to flesh and the mortal pains that accompanied it. And yet, he needed to come up with a solution. Something to counter the soul attack he knew was coming. If he didn’t, then his doppelgänger would assume control over his body.

His wayward soul fragment’s body was a mess. Broken, charred, and split into pieces. His foe no longer had anywhere decent to return to. He was desperate and would doubtlessly spend everything if it meant seizing Kizu’s form for himself. As a result of Kizu’s brief exposure to the soul searing potion, it had access to a foothold.

“It’s time for us to part ways,” Zayne said in his mind. “My time in this world is up. My immortality comes to a conclusion. It’s been… so long. Keep my memory safe, Kizu. You hold so many precious moments. They are my gift. My greatest treasure.”

“Wha….” Kizu croaked, his lips barely moving as he agonizingly strained to get words out.

As Zayne’s soul left him, something else entered in his absence. The pain from the potion left alongside the soul’s departure, but a fatigue pressed down on him.

“As my final act, I will assist you in purging the corruption. It’s time to return you to your natural state. Goodbye, my friend. I believe in you. Heal this world.”
