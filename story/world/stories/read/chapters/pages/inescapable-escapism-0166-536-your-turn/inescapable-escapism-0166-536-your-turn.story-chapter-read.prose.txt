It took everything in my power not to shovel every last piece of food into my mouth and devour it in seconds. I wanted to list the plate to my lips and scrape it all in, but somehow, I managed to resist the urge. Still, I couldn’t stop myself from eating as fast as possible without appearing rude or greedy. I tried, but it was impossible.

I was ravenous, and the food was just too good. The moment the first forkful touched my tongue, I almost let out a moan. I knew I was hungry before, but I didn’t realise just how bad it was until I started to eat. It felt like there was a bottomless pit in my stomach, and I could have finished every single tray of food that the buffet had, and still, I probably wouldn’t be satisfied.

My hunger should not have been a surprise to me. I barely had time to eat any lunch. I’d been late leaving the lab because I stayed back to ask a question, which meant by the time I got to the canteen, people were already starting to leave. I only had a chance to have a few mouthfuls before I had to join them and get to my next class.

It would have been smart for me to grab a protein bar or something on the way out. My teachers wouldn’t have minded if I ate that in class. Other people did it all the time, but I couldn’t bring myself to do it. I wasn’t used to being at the Academy yet. Even though I knew it was unlikely, I was still scared that the teachers would shout at me, like the ones at my school did if someone was foolish enough to eat in a lesson.

That was a mistake. My stomach was already rumbling before I got to my fitness class, and that only made things worse. It consumed every ounce of my energy, leaving me with none. I could barely pull my uniform on again after the class, and all of my muscles still ached, even a couple of hours later. I was pretty sure they would for days.

It was kind of fun, though. Looking back on it, at least. At the time, I’d been too terrified to have fun, but part of me was excited for the next swimming lesson. I was eager to try again, to see if I could actually beat it. No one else had managed to, and I wanted to be the first. Maybe I could do it. I knew what to expect, and surely, that would make things easier.

The teacher had said it would, anyway. That was the whole point of the exercise.

I assumed I’d just be doing lengths in the pool or something when I got to class. That’s what I did last time, and I enjoyed it. I’d always liked swimming, but the moment I saw the grin on Jenna’s face, I knew something was up. It was bordering on evil, and once she made the announcement, it all made sense.

A new device had been fitted into the pool. It was something one of the research and development trainees had created, and it was meant to be able to simulate currents, just like in the ocean. It made no sense to me when the trainee explained it. It sounded more like witchcraft than science and engineering, but apparently, it worked.

Jenna had told us it was an important thing to experience. People often panicked when they realised they were caught in a current for the first time, and that normally caused them to get dragged out to sea and drown. We’d been reassured that although it would be scary at first, that would diminish over time.

And, there was a knack to dealing with it. That was what we had to learn. Once we did, it would be less scary and easier to deal with, but before, there hadn’t been a reliable way to expose trainees to it. However, because of the research and development trainee, that had changed.

It hadn’t looked that bad from the side of the pool, I mused as I ate. I could only see a few ripples on the surface of the water, and they were tiny. It looked as though there was a slight breeze buffeting the water, nothing more. I truly didn’t think it would be that bad until it was my turn.

Maybe I should have expected it. A few people had gone before me, and they’d all struggled. That had been scary to watch, but for some reason, I assumed I’d be okay. I thought I was stronger. I was used to swimming, and I’d gone so many times over the summer, so I just expected that I’d be able to fight against the currents.

It was terrifying just how quickly and easily the water proved me wrong. Within seconds of getting into the pool, I felt it pulling at my limbs. My hopes of reaching the other end of the pool, where Jenna waited with the control panel in her hand, were whisked away from me, and I had used all of my strength just to stay afloat.

I’d tried so hard to follow the instructions Jenna was shouting to me, but it was so hard to hear her over the splashing. I was so scared, and I tried my best. I fought as hard as I could, but eventually, I was slammed against the wall of the pool.

My chest still hurt whenever I breathed. Each inhale caused a flare of pain to reignite and shoot through my ribs, and I was so glad Jenna had stopped the device and sent the lifeguards in to get me because I knew I’d be in even more pain if she hadn’t.

The impact had dazed me. It had knocked the air from my lungs and made my head spin. I tried to keep kicking, but I couldn’t even tell which way I was facing. I couldn’t work out if I was going towards the wall again or if I was swimming straight for the centre of the pool, but I knew the current had caught me again.

When I felt an arm wrap around me, I almost burst into tears of relief. A few might have leaked from my eyes as we were dragged to the edge of the pool by the underwater scooters the lifeguards had been given to help them cut through the strong tides.

I’d thought I was about to drown, just like the boy who’d gone before me. A shiver slipped down my spine, and I felt goosebumps break out on my arms. He hadn’t actually drowned, but it had felt close. The memory of his thrashing movements growing more and more lethargic had stuck with me. They were embedded in my brain, and I couldn’t help but think about what could have happened if Jenna had waited just a moment longer before sending the lifeguards in.

It had happened so quickly. That was the problem. It looked like he was doing okay until suddenly, he wasn’t. The boy was still sitting on the floor, coughing pool water out of his lungs as the doctor looked on, when I’d been told to get in the water.

“You feeling better now?”

The question made me jump, and my head snapped up to look at Seth. I’d been so caught up in my own thoughts that I had barely spoken to him since I sat down, and I had no idea what he was talking about. He must have noticed my confusion, though, because he nodded towards my already mostly-empty plate.

My eyes darted towards it, and it took me a minute to work out what he meant. I hadn’t been eating much recently. Faint memories floated through my mind of me picking at my food, barely able to stomach more than a couple of mouthfuls of each meal, which was probably contributing to my hunger.

The last time I’d been at the Academy, I’d been so distracted by what was happening in my real world and my upcoming date with Duncan. I was withdrawn and had hardly eaten or spoken to Seth, and he’d noticed. Then, I had to come up with a lie to explain it because there was no way I could tell him the real reason why I was being so quiet.

“Oh, yeah. Loads better,” I said with a smile before taking another bite of the noodles.

They tasted even better than they had smelled. The sauce was perfectly salty, and it hadn’t tasted that spicy at first, but it was starting to build. I could feel my tongue beginning to tingle, but I just wanted more.

My eyes flitted towards the buffet in the distance again. The queue had died down, and I was so tempted to go up for another scoop of the noodles. And maybe a few more things too. The roasted veggie meat, whatever it was meant to be, had been so good, and I wanted more of that too, but something was stopping me.

I still felt weird about it. Even though it was fairly normal for people to have second or even third helpings of the food, it still made me uncomfortable. People didn’t seem to get shamed or mocked for it, though. Not as far as I had seen, anyway.

It was normal. Most of the trainees exercised every single day. They used a lot of energy, and so they had to eat a lot. It was healthy, but I struggled to force myself to do it, even when I was still hungry after I’d finished my plate.

There had only been a couple of occasions where I’d managed to actually go up again, and both times, I’d heard my mom’s voice in the back of my head asking me how I could possibly want anything else after scoffing myself like a pig. I could almost feel her poking me in the stomach and hear her laughter.

“Oh, good,” Seth said. “I was getting worried.”

Guilt immediately filled me, and I looked down, unable to hold his gaze.

“Sorry.”

“Not your fault,” he told me. “You can’t help getting ill.”

“I guess,” I muttered.

Even if I had actually been sick, I think I still would have felt bad, but the fact that I hadn’t actually been unwell made it even worse. I’d caused him to worry for no reason, and I felt awful about that. I should have thought of something else. If I had a better excuse, he wouldn’t have been concerned about me at all. Or I should have just tried harder. If I were better at hiding how distracted I was, Seth wouldn’t have ever known anything was wrong.

“Oh, also,” Seth started. “You had swimming earlier, right?”

“Yeah?”

“Okay, good. I thought you did,” he said. “I heard people talking and wanted to ask you about it.”

Immediately, worry flooded me. Did he mean he heard people talking about what I’d done in swimming class? Had my attempt been so bad that people were gossiping about me? Or had something else happened that I hadn’t even realised at the time? I’d been flailing pretty hard when I was in the pool. Perhaps I’d caused my swimming costume to move, and I’d accidentally flashed the entire class.

“Oh?” I prompted, picking nervously at my food.

“Yeah! Someone said they’ve had this thing fitted in the pool that’s like…” Seth trailed off, seeming to think about it for a moment. “An insane wave machine or something?”

A laugh slipped from my mouth. I couldn’t help it. I was so relieved that it wasn’t anything bad that it had just come out before I could stop it.

“Kind of,” I said. “It doesn’t actually create waves. Or… I guess, maybe it could? I’m not sure. It’s for making currents.”

A confused look appeared on Seth’s face, and he stared at me as I shoved some food into my mouth.

“Currents?”

“Yeah. Like… under the water, you know?”

“Oh,” he said as realisation dawned on his face. “Why? I mean… a wave machine I’d understand. The pool back home had one, and that was awesome, but… the Academy doesn’t seem like the kind of place to have something like that.”

I snorted.

“I know what you mean,” I told him. “It’s for practice. So we’re prepared and don’t drown if we’re ever caught in one in the sea.”

“Huh, that makes sense, I guess. I’ve heard riptides and stuff can be pretty dangerous,” Seth replied. “So… how bad was it?”

A wince flitted across my face as the memory of being slammed against the wall crashed into me again, and pain radiated through my torso.

“Pretty bad. I mean… it was worse than any real currents I’ve ever felt before,” I said honestly.

I didn’t have much experience, to be fair. I’d spent almost all of my life in England, and the sea around there wasn’t the kind people wanted to go swimming in. It was too cold and murky. I’d gone in a few times in Scotland, and other than that, I’d only really been in the sea in Mitch’s world. That didn’t truly count, though.

“Really?” Seth asked with a sympathetic expression.

“Yeah. Like… a few people almost drowned. It was pretty scary.”

“Oh,” was all Seth said.

He looked down at his plate, and I watched as he stabbed a piece of chicken a few times without actually picking it up or eating it. He seemed worried. I could almost feel how quickly his thoughts were racing, and I hesitated before speaking again.

“When do you have swimming next?”

“First thing tomorrow.”

I’d assumed that was what he was thinking about, and his answer confirmed it. I felt bad for making him more worried about it than he needed to be. He’d probably be fine. He was strong, and I wasn’t sure if he had much experience in the sea, but I’d seen him in the pool before. He was good.

“It was actually kind of fun,” I said quickly, wanting to make him feel better. “And as soon as Jenna noticed we were struggling too much, she turned the thing off, so it wasn’t that bad.”

“Oh yeah?” Seth asked, but he still seemed distracted.

“Yeah! Plus, you’ll probably have one of the doctors there in case anyone does get hurt or anything. We did,” I told him.

“That’s good…”

My eyebrows drew together as Seth continued to pick at his food. My attempts at reassuring him didn’t seem to be helping, and I knew I wasn’t doing a very good job, which made me feel guilty. It was my fault he was worried in the first place. I could have lied and said it wasn’t that bad from the beginning, but I hadn’t even thought to, and that was dumb of me.

I needed to try harder. There had to be something else that would help convince him that he’d be okay. Or maybe I could think of something to distract him from his thoughts. Perhaps that would help.

“There were lifeguards too, and they had these really cool things,” I started, my voice sounding far too enthusiastic.

“Oh?” Seth responded, glancing up at me.

“Yeah, they’re these like… motors that strap onto their wrists and around their waists, and Jenna called them underwater scooters. Have you seen them before?”

Seth cocked his head to the side ever so slightly, and I felt a burst of happiness within me. It might be working. He seemed to be actually thinking about what I’d asked, and that was a good sign. It meant he probably wasn’t trapped in his own thoughts anymore.

“Yeah. I think so,” Seth said before adding, “Like, on Instagram or something. I don’t think they were strapped to the person, though. It was more like a little wheel thing with handlebars that they hung onto, and it pulled them along?”

A grin appeared on my lips.

“They have those too! Jenna showed us!” I told him. “They have like a whole collection of them in one of the rooms attached to the pool, but the ones that strap on are apparently better for things like this because you can move about and grab people easier.”

Seth seemed to consider that for a moment, and I took another bite of my food as I waited, watching him closely to see if I’d slipped up and made him more worried again.

“Huh, that makes sense, I guess,” he said slowly. “But wait. How do they control them? Like, how do you turn the motors on and off, because surely, if your wrist one is going, if you move your arm to grab someone, it’ll change your trajectory, right?”

“Yeah… you’d think so. I have no clue, actually. I didn’t really think about it,” I replied honestly. “Jenna didn’t show us how they worked, but she said we’d get to have a play around with them in the next class, so I guess I’ll find out then.”

“Awesome.” Seth grinned. “And they have the other ones too? Like the ones you hang onto?”

“Yeah! They have all sorts. I mean, I didn’t even know they were a thing. I’d never seen them before, but they look so cool, and the lifeguards were going so fast with them!”

“Oh man, that’s cool. I hope we’ll get to use them too.”

“I’m sure you will,” I said. “I mean, if we’re allowed to, it makes sense that everyone else will be able to as well, right?”

Seth laughed softly.

“You’d hope. I guess we’ll find out.”

“Yeah,” I agreed, smiling as he finally ate the piece of chicken he’d been playing with.

It didn’t seem like he was worrying anymore, I thought as I shoved the last piece of pasta into my mouth. Perhaps he was just better at hiding it than I was, but his expression was no longer distant, and he was eating again, which felt like a good sign.

Distracting him had been the right call, and I knew he’d find the scooters cool. In the induction wing, I’d noticed that he liked all of the weird gadgets and stuff they had at the Academy. I was kind of surprised he hadn’t been assigned research and development as his specialism. He’d seemed so fascinated by them.

Maybe that was why he’d been told his specialism could still change, a voice in the back of my head suggested. The others hadn’t been told that, and I assumed it had been said to Seth intentionally. Maybe they were torn between field ops and R&D for him and weren’t sure which he’d be better suited for. We didn’t have the chance to play around with too much tech whilst in the induction wing, so perhaps they wanted to see how things went once he was in the main building and could.

“Mmm, that was good,” Seth said, glancing towards the buffet. “I’m going to grab more. Do you want anything else?”

I looked down at my empty plate, biting my lip as conflicting emotions raced through me. I did want more. I wasn’t satisfied yet, but it was hard to admit that. I couldn’t shake the feeling that it was wrong of me to even think about going up for seconds, much less actually do it.

But I was still hungry. If I didn’t get more food now, I’d need to wait until I got back to my room later to have some of the snacks in there, and I was running low. I’d been meaning to go to the store room and restock for a few days, but I kept putting it off, and I regretted that.

Normally, I liked to wait until I was pretty sure everyone else was asleep before going. I was more comfortable that way because I knew it was less likely there would be anyone around to judge me for what I grabbed, but I wouldn’t be able to do that tonight. I was already tired, and I knew I’d fall asleep early, so it made sense for me to get another serving of dinner.

“I’m actually going to go up too, I think,” I decided.

“Oh, do you want to go first?” Seth asked. “Someone should probably stay at the table so no one else steals it whilst we’re gone.”

I laughed.

“Good call. I’m good, though. You go.”

“You sure?” Seth checked.

“Yeah. But thank you.”

Seth smiled at me before standing and grabbing his tray. I reached for my cup as I watched him walk back towards the buffet, unable to help the smile that pulled at my lips. I was glad I’d come back to the Academy. I had missed it, and I was happy I could actually focus on what was happening there without being constantly distracted by my thoughts or feeling the need to check my phone a hundred times.

I’d missed Seth too. It was weird. I still felt guilty about how I felt about him. It still seemed kind of wrong to me, but it didn’t bother me as much as it had before. I was able to forget about it or push the thought away more easily, and that was a relief to me. I liked spending time with Seth, and I’d missed how good it felt. I didn’t want to feel guilty about it.

“Your turn,” Seth said as he returned.
