Leaving the Singer system, Luke considered what the short stay had taught him. Judging by the cells and technologies there, along with what they'd found in the military compound, the enemy wasn't as far ahead as they feared, at least not technology-wise. Perhaps it was a dangerous assumption considering how little he'd seen of their world, but his gut feeling told Luke the ones who had come to conquer Earth weren't as fearsome as they had first appeared. More than information about them, the most important pieces he brought back with him had more to do with himself than with the Singers. Infernal mana was part of those discoveries. It brought pain and suffering, both to himself and to those who stood in his way. With a formidable weapon like that, Luke felt like he had leveled up power-wise, even if his experience bar hadn't moved much, if at all, during the recent battles.

The other new piece wasn't new per se, but in controlling and compressing the void mana with nothing but his will, Luke had learned just how powerful that aspect of an Integrated could be. He still had a lot to learn on the subject, but it was as if he had now unlocked a vital piece of what made an Integrated strong. Not levels, skills, or mana, but the strength of their character.

Energetic and with a hunger for learning more about his new powers, Luke arrived back at The Band headquarters, right next to Milla. They were a subdued lot. Losing Nash and Mas, along with many other members, during the system event and Singer invasion, had taken a lot out of them. And who could blame them?

Luke shared what he and Ray had been up to, accepted an overly long scolding about staying out of trouble, then learned that Penny was on her way back because Chicago had all but fallen to the invaders. Things were bleak in the US, and the rest of the world wasn't faring much better with so many Singers joining in the invasion. Milla had also learned that their enemy had intensified their efforts over the last couple of days, and now killed Integrated on sight, not allowing anyone who had touched the gray orb to surrender. Non-Integrated still weren't attacked, though, so that was something, and Milla assured him their parents, along with all the other level one Integrated under DIA's protection, had been moved to a safe location in the middle of nowhere. Even with so many of them in one place, portals no longer opened for Integrated at such a low level, which was a relief.

"It's time," Milla said, after giving Luke a meager list of guild members still remaining in The Band. "You should go into a private room for the meeting."

Luke nodded, taking out the small, coin-like item he was supposed to use. "Sure. Let's see what plan these pact-people have cooked up."

When he put the item on the side of his head, Luke had expected a screen to pop up in front of his face, like a video call sort of thing, but this was not that. Not at all. Instead, the room disappeared, and he found himself in a new place. Blinking, he held a hand forward and poked his sister's shoulder. The touch felt real.

“Ow!”

"This is so weird," he said, clapping his hands. “So weird.”

"You'll get used to it," Milla said, her eyes locked on the door in front of them as it slid open. The whole place looked a lot like what he'd seen in some older Asian samurai movies, where the walls were built from thin pieces of wood with paper covering the gaps. It looked flimsy, but it gave the room a certain ambiance, and Luke even got a hint of pleasant smells and heard the pitter-patter of rain on the roof. Following Milla out of their room, they joined a bunch of others sitting on the floor around a large, oval table. One wall stood open to a garden of lush green, giving a peaceful air to the proceedings.

"This is him?" one of the seated fellows asked. He was dressed in one of those Japanese robe-sort of garments, and had a pair of Katanas placed before him on the table. Perhaps this was this man's place, judging by his wardrobe and choice of weapons, if not his accent. He spoke with a thick French accent and was decidedly not Asian.

"Why don't you let us sit down first, Etienne?" Milla asked, waving for Luke to follow her to a free spot at the table, where she sat, leaving him without room. Luke shrugged and sat down behind her, even after seeing how everyone not at the table proper was standing.

Etienne kept his gaze on Luke, who answered with a raised eyebrow. "What's up, Etienne?"

Milla: "Let me do the talking."

She leaned over the table toward the Frenchman and gave a nod. "What's up, Etienne?"

Luke stifled a chuckle, then nodded to Nick, Curtis's brother and the leader of Phoenix Phalanx, who was also at the table. The others were total strangers. Eight of them in total, not counting Milla, Etienne, and Nick, of all shapes, sizes, colors, and ethnicities, this pact seemed to have been pulled together from people from all corners of Earth. Most were old men, giving the proceedings a very boardroom-like air.

Etienne, younger than most and with intense eyes, seemed to be the one in charge because he spoke with an air of authority that the others didn't question. "Luke Quinn. We have found other Lifeweavers, but none have reported abilities like the ones your guildmistress claims you have. Tell us, can you really close a portal?"

"Ah," Luke said, raising a finger. "Now I hear it. You're from New Belgium, aren't you?"

Milla: "Luke..."

He cleared his throat. "Sorry, I mean. Yeah. I can."

"How?" a blond, short, and squat woman in her forties asked. She sat behind a bald, aging man with liver spots all over his face. Her pronunciation was good, but the accent told him she wasn't from an English-speaking country. The Nordics, maybe?"

"Who are you?" Luke asked.

"Stine," the old man said without looking back at her. Rather than her answering the question, he did so in her place. "Stine is a Lifeweaver, like yourself."

"So is Riak," one of the few women around the table said, nodding to the man behind her. She, too, was older, black, and with gray hair peeking out from a scarf wrapped around her head. Judging by her accent, she was from an African country.

"Three Lifeweavers, huh?" Luke asked, nodding to both of them. "I know of another one, but I'm not really sure where she is at the moment." Maybe he ought to have checked that she had made it back to Earth? Oh, well.

"You need to spread your knowledge to Stine and Riak, and this Dorothy your guildmistress spoke of, if we can locate her."

Luke: "That's the plan? To have the three of us tour the world and close portals?"

Milla: "With three of you able to teleport the way you do, it isn't too far-fetched, is it?"

Luke: "Out of all the Lifeweavers I met in that dungeon, there was only one other who could see the weave."

Milla: "Just give it a try, please?"

Luke nodded. "I'll share what I know, but I'd like to make a suggestion."

Etienne looked pissed and was about to speak, but the old man cut in. "Of course, young man."

"We should have a group invade the Singers."

"Luke?" Milla asked. The others just sat there in what he interpreted as stunned silence.

"Hear me out," Luke said, explaining his line of reasoning. With so many Singers on Earth, they were vulnerable back home. A capable strike force should be able to put the fear of Earth into them. If nothing else, it would draw Singers on Earth back to their home world, meaning the fighting should be easier once the portals were closed. A diversion of sorts, while they cut the Singers off from Earth once and for all.

Once he'd explained, a few looked doubtful, but most were nodding. Again, it was the old man from Denmark or wherever who spoke first, but this time Etienne looked all fired-up, like he wanted to jump straight into combat.

"Why stop at one?" the old man asked.

"What do you mean?" Luke asked.

The old man's eyes widened, and a fire burned in them, matching, if not eclipsing, Etienne's. "The portals are well defended, but not so well that a concentrated effort wouldn't be able to break through. We are spread all over Earth, so we should strike all over Earth. Strike at their hearts once and for all!"

"We won't be able to hold the portals," a thin man in his fifties with a Russian accent said at the other end of the table. "Even breaking through their fortifications will prove difficult." While the older man spoke words of caution, the two younger women behind him were anything but. They stood there. Silent, yes, but also with their hands balled into tight fists, their bodies rigid except for small twitches in their shoulders, as if they were already imagining trading blows with the Singers.

"We won't have to hold on for long," Nick said. "One strong group goes through the portal, and another defends the Earth side. Phoenix Phalanx and The Band can work together."

"The original plan must still stand," the Russian argued. "Closing the portals must be our first priority. Even if we all succeed, that will be no more than a blip. There are eighty-three portals out there. How many can we invade?"

"Enough of them to make the Singers bleed!" Etienne shouted. He tried to stand, having forgotten they were seated on the floor, and slammed his knees into the underside of the table, shaking it. Recovering, he added. "New Belgium will contribute with freedom fighters all over Europe!"

"How were you planning on getting us everywhere to close portals?" Luke asked, breaking through the rising din of voices. When they'd joined at the table, it had been a subdued lot, but Luke's simple idea seemed to have stoked some fire in their bellies. It had been there, simmering, and all they had needed was a spark.

"We are mapping all portals as we speak," the woman in front of Riak said. "You can leave transportation to us. We have several Integrated with long-range traveling capabilities."

"So do we!" Etienne barked.

The old Dane slammed his open hand on the table. "Our Rusher can cover a continent in moments!"

Milla: "Looks like you got your wish. Invade the Singers? When were you planning on telling me about that?"

Luke: "Eh, I kind of thought about it just now."

Milla: "Little brother..."
