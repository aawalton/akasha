“I don’t know,” Athena said. “I think I may know it.”

A cold chill crawled up John's spine.

John started walking, boots echoing faintly through the service tunnels under the net parlor. The military droid clanked after him, aerial drone humming overhead.

“What do you mean you know it?” he asked over their link. “Does that have something to do with the memories we saw before?”

“I’m sorry. I don’t know. It felt like I’d seen him before. I can’t remember.”

“You’re starting to worry me—”

A hooded figure stepped out from the direction of a side room.

“You were told not to bring anyone back with you.”

The voice was gravel dragged across glass. Once it might have been beautiful, now it was only rough and vaguely feminine.

No thermal signature. Vampire. What made his pulse kick was the way he was certain she hadn’t walked through the doorway at all. One moment she wasn’t there, the next she was, as if she'd just appeared in it. He lifted his pistol.

“Who the hell are you?”

Her dress, her voice, her pale warped eyes and slightly misshaped body... all of it linked in his head to the vampire he'd met upstairs in the net parlor server room.

Could have been siblings, turned at the same time maybe, John theorized. But it seemed like a stretch of a theory. Could be that there appearance hinted at a different secret, maybe one that was worse and more complicated somehow. There was usually something worse.

“Are they yours?” she asked, gesturing to the droid and the drone.

“Mine now,” John said. “You didn’t answer my question.”

“Put away your gun. I will escort you to the lift.”

“I’ll hold it, if it's all the same to you."

For what it might be worth to her, he did lower his gun so it wasn't directly pointing at the vampire.

She looked at him like she was deciding whether ripping him apart would be excessive or rude. Then she turned, keeping one somewhat empathetic eye on him as she replied. “Come.”

John followed.

“I didn’t register her presence until she spoke,” Athena said in his head.

“Yeah. Pretty sure she’s like the cloaked thing we picked up last time we were here,” he answered over their mental link. His eye swept the tunnel ahead. “Which means there could be more watching us right now if she wasn't that one."

“Do you think she’s a threat?”

That was a bit out of the ordinary: Athena leaning on his read of danger instead of giving him hers. Maybe she was still rattled.“Maybe. If Kaito wanted to backstab us, now would be the time. He’s got the data and might not be happy we know what he and his friends really are.”

“I don’t sense malice from her. Just the same hunger I’ve felt in every vampire’s thread-echo.”

“That’s something.” His mental voice stayed flat. “We still need to talk about the Angel and your… earlier episode.”

“I don’t know more than you do. And do you have to call it a episode?”

“I’m tired,” he admitted, resisting the urge to groan and rub his eyes. “Sorry.”

“You do not require sleep right now. Your need for rest is still optimized by my previous interventions.”

“Maybe. I might have you knock me out after this anyway.”

“I could force you into a unneeded rest cycle, but it would be inefficient and wasteful.”

“Let’s get back on topic. Any idea how we figure out what happened before? And why you might recognize the Angel?”

“I’ve been running internal checks since I recovered. Nothing’s out of place, except the memories we experienced feel personal to me.”

More suspicions rose up inside him.

“Could they have been yours?”

Silence reigned in his head for a breath. “No. I don’t believe so. I am not a person, John. I don’t feel like I was ever a person. Yet the memories make me feel as if I lived.”

He kept his eyes on the tunnel. “Alright. Could just be something like the Grin head-fuckery magic. Maybe think about it. Or don’t if you need space from it. But let me know if anything changes?”

“Of course. I don’t want to concern you.”

“Oh, I’m concerned. Not about you. For you. And me.”

The vampire stopped at the lift doors and stepped aside. Her gaze slid to the military droid’s cracked, glowing chestplate.

“Would you leave that one behind? I would pay you.”

John blinked, then remembered the vampire upstairs was a tech junkie. Not far-fetched this one was too.

Tech-obsessed vampires. Hell of a world.

“Why?”

“He appears interesting. Would six-thousand credits be enough?”

The droid looked between them, then raised its hands and shook its head side-to-side in a quick, pleading gesture.

“I don’t think he’s for sale.”

She glanced at the droid again. “He,” she repeated. “Interesting personality protocol. If you change your mind, you may contact me through the cafe attendant.”

“Sure. We good here?”

She gestured at the lift. “By all means.”

John stepped inside the lift. The droid squeezed in beside him, keeping as much space as possible between itself and the vampire. The drone floated in above his shoulder emotionlessly.

“She scare you that much?” he asked the droid as the lift cut the vampire in question from view. “That glad I didn’t sell you?”

The droid shook its head again, as if denying any fear, spine straightening.

“Sure,” John said.

The droid’s body language almost read as embarrassed.

“Just make me not regret it. She offered good creds for you."

The droid tilted its head in a questioning way. John read the gesture as the robot asking why he'd decided not to take the money.

“If she’s offering that much, you’re probably worth more. Or she’s got too much money and you’re interesting to her for some reason. Probably something to do with what you've got glowing in your chestplate. Either way, we’ll see if you live up to anything after we fix you up.”

Its optic brightened and refocused, like a blink of surprise, then it pressed its palms together in a silent 'please'.

John huffed out something like a laugh. A threadnet ping lit in his HUD.
