Kalia pursed her lips. “I can’t even imagine how strong he is with a normal spirit.”

“Oh yeah?” Photel asked. “Well, I can’t imagine how strong he’ll be in five minutes.”

“Oh yeah?” Fringe mocked. “Well I can’t imagine spending another week with the guy, let alone a month.”

His words elicited a few chuckles.

Photel’s face then darkened. “Hey, I got a real question. And don’t take this as mutiny or anything, but… do you think he’s planning to sacrifice us?”

Their faces darkened. That was indeed a valid question.

“I mean, the more I’m with him, the more I realize that he’s gotta plan,” Photel said. “But he’s hiding it at all costs. That’s not… normal, is it? I mean, I get his ‘orders are absolute’ mindset, but… you get what I’m saying, right?”

“Yeah,” Kalia said darkly. “That said, I don’t think he’ll sacrifice us.”

“Why?” Tibbi asked.

“Because he gave the Sickles a ‘warning.’ I didn’t understand it at first, but after he told us he had a concrete plan, it made me realize he was warning them of something tangible. And why would he warn them and not us? You know? That just doesn’t make sense to me.” Kalia watched Hayden butcher a hind quarter of one of the cats. “I don’t know. He’s clearly cold, but he strikes me as someone who strives for the best output. And while I get people not feeling the same… I trust him.”

Those words seemed to build a bit of trust, even with Fringe.

Hayden returned with the hindquarter on his shoulder and nudged his head uphill. “Let’s go.”

Kalia and the others reluctantly moved on. She believed he was doing the right thing, but Photel’s question made her waver. She desperately wanted to ask, confident that Hayden would answer, but the group had squandered their opportunity to question him already. If they kept doing it, they would be seen as naggers—and that would be poisonous. So they all continued hiking east, growing ever closer to Violet Mountain, where the most infamous Sickle would be waiting.

It took six hours for Calid, Mackerel, and Ella to reach Plum Ridge. The team had run, taking advantage of the level terrain and their overwhelming power to get there before Hayden’s team made a break for it. It was only once they arrived that they saw something that was far more aggressive in person than it had been in their scout's report. The western area Hayden’s team was coming up to was on a five-hundred-foot cliff that didn’t look that significant when pitted against the eye-catching purple mountain that towered to the east of it. If someone couldn’t fly or use airwalk, they wouldn’t be able to reach them.

Calid studied it. Is he trying to separate us? It wasn’t clear what Hayden was trying to do—or why he was walking toward Plum Ridge. Calid turned back to the vibrant purple mountain before him. He was at the very edge, a space where there were thirty feet of green foliage before it became a sickly mess of purple hues.

Mackerel walked up and stood beside him.

“What do you think?” Calid asked.

Mackerel looked sky-bound, watching men and women with wings flying above the mountain—using seeing spirits to spy on Hayden. “I don’t know. If he wanted to separate us, he already succeeded. So it’d make sense for him to go north, but… he’s still on his way here. If I had to guess, I’d guess he’s hoping we’ll clear the mountain for him.”

“That, or this place might also be plain dangerous, and he’s hoping we’ll kill ourselves,” Calid offered.

“Yeah…” Mackerel smiled wryly after. “That’s also like. But…”

Calid turned to him. “But what?”

“It’s just… whether we capture him or not, we’re running up that mountain. It’s not like we’re going to trust him if he says nothing’s up there.”

“Exactly,” Ella “Frost Witch” Henni interjected, walking up. “It doesn’t matter what Hayden says—we’re going up that mountain. And that mountain, and that one and the dark section of the forest and everywhere else.” She pointed her finger around sarcastically. “You know why? Because we’re treasure hunters. That’s what we do.”

Treasure hunting meant searching for spirit relics, catalysts, and alien artifacts. Rifters were treasure hunters by nature. It was the means by which they made a living. If the Sickles entered after the entrance ceremony like they expected, they would be scouring every inch of the rift to find treasures.

Calid turned to her cooly. “Yes, but our job’s to secure Hayden Reskco.”

“Yeah, that’s the easy part. Or do you think that’s going to be difficult?”

Calid wiped the sweat off his brow. His clothing was damp and sticky. He couldn’t wait to leave. “What are you proposing?”

“I propose we hunt the mountain. The second their wings sees us doing it, we'll know how they'll react. And when they get here, we'll know more about the mountain, its secrets, its traps. Just sitting around here keeps the ball in his court.”

Calid glanced at Mackerel. “What do you think?”

“I think it's bad no matter what, so it's not a bad option. If a team gets wiped now, we still have time to get reinforcements. So if Ella wants to go treasure hunting, I'm supportive. That's said…”

“Not your team,” Calid said.

“Not my team,” Mackerel confirmed.

Ella mumbled something in her native language as she walked away.

“What was that?” Calid asked.

“I said I'm doing it!”

“Okay…” Calid didn't sass her. He just watched the mountain, reflecting on tragic situations he had experienced in rifts.

“Do you think she'll be alright?” Mackerel asked.

Calid chuckled grimly. “Oh, she’ll be alright. I’m worried about her team.”

Ella’s treasure hunter, Link, didn’t share Ella’s mindset. So when she said, “We’re going in, so start scanning,” he wanted to tell her to find a boyfriend and get dumped. Unfortunately, he had signed up for the mission, so he had to follow her instructions.

He sat at the base of the mountain. “Did Carian’s team discover any hints about what he’s after?” Link asked.

“No,” Ella said. “That said, he doesn’t have his main gate open. My guess is that he’s looking for a main gate catalyst, or artifact to improve the opening. That said, it doesn’t matter. I want you to find everything. I don’t care if it’s a catalyst or a relic or an ingredient—I want to know about it. If we destroy what he’s looking for, I swear to your wife’s God, Link, I’m going to freeze your veins into a solid object and tip you over.”

Link rubbed the goosebumps off his arms. “Got it.”

He closed his eyes and activated Shiner, his Golden Eyes spirit. It was a conduit-type spirit that passed through the ground, connecting into root and water stream networks to analyze coran patterns. If there were a plant or a mineral that contained spiritual properties, it would flash like a sparking wire. The spirit would then tag it, allowing him to analyze it.

Golden Eyes was an efficient spirit, so it passed through a quarter-mile network, covering 160,000 square meters. Coran signatures popped up in a landslide reaction as usual, but there was something very strange.

“What’s wrong?” Ella asked when she saw his face.

“Those vines… they’re… unnatural…” Link stared at the network of purple vines that were strung between trees. “Like, they’re not plants.”

“Then what are they?”

“I don’t know… they’re… dead. But they’re also alive.”

“That makes no sense.”

Link clicked his tongue. “I don’t know. It’s like… they aren’t alive, but the thing that’s making them are. Plus… this whole mountain’s lighting up. It’s filled with crazy tunnels and drainage pipes. Ella, this is seriously unnatural. I highly recommend that we don’t—”

“We’re going up it no matter what,” Ella said. “So be useful. Are there any catalysts? Relics? Anything?”

He stared at the massive living bulbs under the ground and the sinkholes. They looked like—

“No,” Link said. “Just a super messed up mountain.”

“The weirder the better,” Ella said as she stepped to the base of the mountain. She took a deep breath through her nose—and then thrust out her hand.

A thick billowing fog spread from her hand and into the violet wonderscape. It was a gorgeous sight, an ominous juxtaposition between milky white and bright purple before all was swallowed by white.

It was an awe-inspiring sight to see a true A-rank flex their power. The fog spread an eighth of a mile before dissipating, leaving a crystalline landscape of frozen trees and biota.

Link picked up a rock out of interest and threw it at a leafy plant not far from him. The plant exploded like a dropped mirror when the rock hit it.

Sub zero.

Ella grinned at his reaction and then put her fingers on the ground. A pulse of electricity followed, spreading through the mountain. All the ice on the ground shattered in a cataclysmic chain reaction, leaving a bed of plant fragments for them to talk on. Only the trees and the dead vines remained.

Ella turned to her team with a damning sneer. “It’s like you’ve forgotten who we are.” She turned to the mountain and started climbing.

Link hesitantly followed behind her, crunching over the icy ground on the way up the mountain.

They had twenty minutes to live.
