[Time]: Year 1981[Location]: The Umbral Cathedral

By 1981, Irene Habsburg was one of two people in the known world who could hold a conversation with a Lantern Cat. A number of witches had watched Ovelia do it over the years. Ovelia had taught exactly one of them how.

The immediate consequence was the complete, irreversible demolition of every illusion she had previously held about the ambient soundscape of any room a Lantern Cat had ever entered.

For instance, during a high-stakes financial negotiation, a Lantern Cat might drift through the room and press its glowing cheek affectionately against her negotiating partner's leg, releasing a heart-melting cascade of soft trills. The opposing Witch would invariably soften: posture easing, expression opening, privately honored by what she interpreted as a divine creature's spontaneous blessing.

Irene would hear the verbatim translation: Cat observes that your tailoring is atrocious, your mana circulation is sluggish, and you smell of cheap bureaucratic anxiety. Hand over the dried fish immediately, for it is Cat's divine and unchallengeable right.

It had forced her to permanently dedicate a cognitive thread during every serious conversation solely to maintaining her flawless aristocratic composure, fighting a losing war against the urge to dissolve into inappropriate laughter across the negotiating table.

They were, without exception, ruthless gossips, relentless critics, and creatures possessed of a self-entitlement so absolute it had achieved the legal status of a physical constant.

They also operated under the absolute conviction that no bipedal creature possessed the intellectual capacity to understand them, which meant they leaked information like a breached dam.

This was how Irene had learned the foundational truth of the Witch of Eternal Slumber. Ovelia had not descended from the cosmos in a blaze of divine glory. She had been hatched from an egg deemed dead and abandoned in the wilderness, scooped up by a group of passing Lantern Cats who thought it looked interesting. Irene had even met the adoptive mothers. They drifted through the Cathedral halls demanding premium snacks and treating the Lord of the Umbral Cathedral with the affectionate condescension reserved for an unusually tall, comparatively furless kitten that still hadn't quite figured out how to groom its own ears.

No wonder, Irene had thought. She structurally believes herself to be a cat.

Irene kept a number of things she preferred to leave off the household's daily circular, and she had spent over a month wearing down Ovelia's patience for a way to shut a cat's mouth. On the afternoon she finally snapped, Ovelia had handed over the administrative override out of exhausted impatience.

The [Lantern Cat Oath]: a binding vow of pure linguistic magic. Force a Lantern Cat to swear in its native dialect, and it physically could not violate the terms. The syntax was ruthlessly inflexible. The phrase must begin immediately with the vow itself. "Cat swears..." and nothing else before it.

Corner a Lantern Cat, compel it to swear treason against the Queen of the Lantern Cats, and the words would hold.

Irene never used it. It was enough that the woman who ruled the world had taken Irene's hand, laid it against her own throat, folded the fingers shut around the hilt, and gone back to sleep.

Irene lay on the Cathedral's dedicated cat sofa: built to seat a fully manifested Greater Lantern Cat, lined inside with dragon hide padding. One lazy roll of her shoulders and she would land directly in Ovelia's arms.

Ovelia was currently losing a war with her own biology.

Lantern Cats were mandated by natural law to sleep approximately sixteen hours out of every twenty-four. The Lord of the Umbral Cathedral had adopted this schedule with a thoroughness that suggested it was not a preference but a religious conviction. She was sprawled across the cushions, starry black eyes half-closed, already lengthening out toward the sixteen.

Irene lightly dragged her fingernails along the soft curve of Ovelia's chin. Ovelia's eyes cracked open. The abyss-deep irises, scattered with the cold light of distant galaxies, narrowed into slits.

"If the stupid girl disturbs Cat's sweet dreams for no reason again," Ovelia murmured, her voice vibrating with the low, unhurried menace of a creature who had not yet decided whether to bother being threatening, "Cat will use Cat's paws to shatter your skull into fine powder."

Irene scratched a little higher along the jawline. Ovelia was the most terrifying entity in recorded history, and she had apparently never once followed through on a personal threat.

"Am I your only disciple?" Irene asked softly.

Ovelia's eyes snapped shut. You woke Cat for this? radiated from every line of her posture.

Irene let the silence stretch. She watched the steady rise and fall of Ovelia's chest. Once it was clear Ovelia had no intention of answering, Irene lowered her hand and applied a deliberate, scratching pressure directly to Ovelia's stomach.

"Meow-OW!"

Ovelia woke in a single blurring frame. Her starry, pitch-black eyes blew wide open, pupils dilating to their absolute maximum aperture. Her cat ears pinned back so sharply they nearly severed the atmospheric pressure in the room. Her body coiled instantly forward, weight dropping low, every line of her shifting into the taut, predatory crouch of a panther preparing to disembowel a threat.

"Stupid, foolish, idiotic girl!" Ovelia hissed. "Question mark!"

Irene did not retreat. She looked directly up into the furious, fathomless depths of Ovelia's eyes. "Answer me," she coaxed, her voice dropping into a register of weaponized gentleness. She reached up and brushed a strand of dark hair from Ovelia's cheek. "You are the best cat in the world."

The predatory tension evaporated. Ovelia let out a theatrical, deeply put-upon sigh and dropped back against the cushions. She raised one hand and examined her immaculate fingernails with a slow, maliciously amused expression.

"Who else would there be?" Ovelia drawled.

"Ash," Irene said quietly. "And Tasia."

Ovelia didn't pause. "A melodramatic megalomaniac. And a toy."

Both came out already polished, as if they had been sitting ready on a shelf for years waiting for someone to ask, and Ovelia's eyes went to the ceiling before the second had finished.

"That is not an assessment," Irene said. "That is a sulk."

Ovelia's tail, which had been lying slack across the cushions, gave one sharp flick against the leather and went still.

"Cat never forbade her from coming home." It was out before the tail had settled, and only then did Ovelia turn her head and look Irene full in the eye, with the steady, unblinking stare of a cat that has just missed a jump in front of witnesses.

"You have let the whole Inner Sea call you her jailer," Irene said.

"Let them." The tail shifted across the cushions, restless. "She cannot put anything down. Hand her a thing that is finished and she will hold it up by the scruff and swear it is still breathing." Ovelia's lip curled. "And the overload burns less out there. There is always another league of Void to spend it on, so she never has to find out what stopping would cost her."

The overload was an older tenant than the Wall. Ash had carried it through every year of her girlhood in the Palace of Rex Mundi and never once asked to be sent anywhere for it. Whatever kept her out there, it was not the fire.

"Then what is it she cannot put down?" Irene asked.

Ovelia did not answer. Her eyes had gone very wide and fixed on nothing, the look of a child who has woken in the dark and not yet worked out which room she is in.

Then she pushed her face toward the hollow of Irene's shoulder. Counting the ears she stood the taller of the two, and she burrowed in anyway like something half her size.

Irene's arms were already open before Ovelia had finished, to take the whole weight of her, one arm round Ovelia's shoulders and the other hand deep in the dark hair at the back of her head, and Irene held on as though everything she owned in the world had just been put into her arms. Against Irene's collar Ovelia's breathing slowed and evened out, and Irene's thumb moved slow along the nape of Ovelia's neck. A cat does not hate anyone for nothing.

She put her mouth to Ovelia's ear. "Melodramatic megalomaniac," she murmured, in perfect agreement.

"Mrrp," said Ovelia into her collar, small and vindicated, and the sound went straight through Irene's ribs and set up house there.

It took Irene a moment to remember there had been a second name. Ash's sister, and the cat's toy; the toy was the safer of the two.

"Tell me about your toy, then," she said into Ovelia's hair.

One ear came up first, and then the face, by degrees, with the air of a creature that had been offered a dangling string and was weighing up whether its dignity could afford to go after it.

"She is not Cat's toy," Ovelia said, with great precision. "Cat said she was suited to be played with. Those are different things."

"Then why haven't you?"

Ovelia's chin settled back onto Irene's shoulder, and she was quiet for a moment before she answered. "She gives the thing away before you have reached for it. She had sent her mothers off in her own heart before Cat ever said the word, and she had buried her sister before Ash was through the gate. Do what you like to her and she will let you, because she has already decided it cannot be helped. That is what makes her suited." The ears tipped back, faintly disdainful. "But Cat likes a thing that runs."

Two names had bought Irene more of the cat than she had come asking for, and she wanted the rest. Ovelia's most loyal hound and her most loyal cat, then; surely nothing there could spoil the mood.

"Famia Schüder?" Irene asked. "And Akkukataya?"

On the second name, Ovelia's tail swept out and coiled firmly around Irene's waist, as though there were still some gap between them left to close.

"Stupid girl," Ovelia muttered. "You have no idea what you keep asking Cat."

Irene loosened her arms and leaned back, just far enough to make the tail work to keep her, and the tail worked. Recaptured, she let her head rest against Ovelia's arm and tilted her chin up until Ovelia had to actually look at her.

"They say you dote on Akkukataya the most," she said, with the easy insolence of somebody who knew exactly how much she was allowed.

Ovelia let out a long, heavy exhale. Her eyes tracked the ceiling with the expression of a cat reviewing a catastrophically corrupted server log that she had personally, inexplicably, been responsible for compiling.

"Cat does not know," Ovelia said, her voice carrying a genuine, bone-deep grievance, "what Cat did to deserve this level of karmic retribution."

"What did she do?" Irene asked.

Ovelia hesitated. Her jaw tightened. It was clearly a memory she had attempted to permanently archive in a sealed vault she intended never to open again. But the biological, inescapable imperative of feline gossip proved stronger than her dignity. She turned her mouth to Irene's ear and dropped her voice into the hushed, traumatized cadence of someone recounting an incident that had permanently altered their relationship with reality.

"The very first time we met," Ovelia breathed, "she walked into my study. She stopped in front of me. She looked at me, and she lay down flat on the floor.

"She then formally, deliberately, with apparent full premeditation, requested that Cat remove my boots. And then use my bare feet to kick her. Her designated target was her own face."

Irene went very still.

"Cat very nearly erased her," Ovelia went on, wounded. "One more breath and there would have been nothing left of her on the rug."

Irene bit the inside of her cheek. "And why didn't you?"

"She was beautiful." Ovelia said it the way one reports the weather. "And she was the most gifted young Cat Witch Cat had ever met. So Cat let her live." Her voice sank lower still. "And then she looked up at me from the floor, and she smiled."

Cat had stood at the center of reality-shattering battlefield collisions. Cat's sanity had never buckled.

Cat had left the study very quickly.

"Beautiful," Irene said. "And gifted." Something in her voice had cooled by several degrees. "Then I am afraid the fault is entirely yours."

The tail let go of her waist all at once, so that Ovelia could rear back and stare at her in naked outrage. "Cat said one sentence! And Cat was handed a logistical plan for facial trauma! If she had lost her mind in Cat's presence, where did she find the presence of mind to specify the boots? And if she had not lost it—" A shudder went down the whole length of her. "Cat has run it every way there is to run it. There is only one answer."

The tail had left Irene's waist on Akkukataya's account, and Irene would remember it. "Which is?" she asked, in the voice of a judge who has already signed the verdict.

"She is a pervert," Ovelia whispered. "An incurable one. Who could possibly have known? Now she is the most useful creature Cat owns, and every single day Cat looks at her and remembers the floor."

Irene's mouth went flat at most useful. She reached up anyway and stroked Ovelia's hair. "She truly is terrible," she said. "You have suffered greatly."

Mollified, Ovelia settled back against the cushions and shut her eyes, and the tail found its way round Irene's waist again as if it had never left.

Every name so far had gone off in Irene's hand. Better to let the cat choose.

"Then tell me," Irene said, as lightly as a woman laying out a snare. "Is there anybody you actually admire?"

Ovelia's eyes stayed shut. "Josephine Durant," she said. "Don Gil Ross. And Famia."

Three names, given flat, in the voice she kept for things that were simply true.

Irene's house had opened the gates two of them defended, and had spent forty years arranging its prosperity around not mentioning them. What the three had in common was the one quantity she had never been able to counterfeit: plain nobility, standing untouched in its alcove.

It tasted like ash on the back of her tongue.

A pervert was safe. A melodramatic megalomaniac sent the cat burrowing into Irene's arms for shelter. Admiration was another currency entirely, and it meant there were corners of Ovelia's mind where Irene was neither needed nor missed.

Her fingers went to the dark fur coiled at her waist, found the curve where it bound her, and began working it loose. If Ovelia wanted clean and noble, she could go and sleep on an altar.

She did not get through the first loop.

The tail snapped tight, pinned her hands flat against her own ribs, and hauled her back flush. Ovelia dropped her chin onto the crown of Irene's head, ears flattened in rebuke, and let out a low, disgruntled rumble into her hair that Irene felt in her teeth.

"What do you think you are doing with your hands."

Irene said nothing.

"Greedy girl," Ovelia mumbled, already going under, the grievance muffled into Irene's hair. "Always looking at what other people have. Be good, and stay where Cat put you."

Within seconds the breathing under Irene's cheek had slowed and gone deep, into the rhythm of a creature designed to sleep through the majority of its existence, and the tail eased by degrees into a loose, warm coil that let her hands go and kept the rest of her.

Irene remained exactly where she was, wrapped in the coil of the dark tail, in the starlit warmth of the afternoon, listening to the steady heartbeat beneath her cheek, entirely content to wait out the sixteen hours.
