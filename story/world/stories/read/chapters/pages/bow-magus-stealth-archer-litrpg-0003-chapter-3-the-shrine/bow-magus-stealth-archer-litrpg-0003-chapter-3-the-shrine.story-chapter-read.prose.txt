Rex didn't like coming to town. It was too busy, the streets always filled with dirty crowds. And worst of all, the Gates of Dorog Targ always brought obnoxious Classed people into town. But he had little choice. His plan required a sword, and it was the only place he was going to find one.

What annoyed Rex most about the Classed who found themselves in Dorog Targ was that they were weak. Or perhaps more importantly, lacking ambition. They were low-level men and women who didn’t want to challenge themselves against deadlier contracts, let alone combat new Gates. And worst of all, in a place like Dorog, they could prance around like there was something special about them.

What now?

The bustling crowd had come to a complete halt, stopping Rex in his path. He could hear whispers and bustling overhead, and loud angry voices carrying over them.

“Get out of our way, you scrubs!"

“Yeah, make room! And don’t make me tell you twice!”

The crowd parted, and two men dressed in chain metal and sporting weapons dangling from their belts came pushing through.

“Outta the way!” One of them barked and pushed a merchant over.

“Hey, leave him alone!” A kid shouted from the crowd.

“You got a problem, runt?”

“Alright now, there’s enough path for everyone,” A seasoned guard with gray streaks running through his hair said as he made his way toward the two Classed men. “Let’s all calm down.”

The guard bowed his head to the two armored men and started to usher the crowd along. It seemed like he wanted to help the two Classed men, keeping the dirty crowds of Dorog to the side.

“That little brain of yours got a problem?” One of the Classed men said, stomping up to the guard. “We said, move it!”

“One moment, I’ll clear the way for you. Just let me get these people–”

“You’re in the way!” Roared the Classed man, and he grabbed the guard by the collar. With ease, he lifted the guard several inches off the ground and threw him into a nearby merchant cart

“HAHA, you got him, bro! Look at that weakling! He’ll think twice before getting in our way next time!”

The display of raw strength sent the crowd reeling back, making plenty of room for the two Classed men to pass through. And several guards nearby who were stationed around the marketplace watched the whole thing, but did nothing.

Chuckling as they walked by, the Classed men mocked the locals as they headed for the tavern.

How pathetic. All they can do is pick on people without Classes. They’re nothing like the ones who saved us.

The two men could have crushed Rex between their fingers if they wanted to. But he didn’t feel fear when he looked at them. He almost pitied them.

The moment they were gone, the guards around the marketplace came to their companions' aid, helping him to his feet. But they pretended as if nothing had happened. They understood that the two Classed men could cut down a dozen guards without breaking a sweat, even if they were obviously low-level.

And no punishment for their deeds would be dealt out, either. The Lords of Dorog controlled everything, and Classed men like the two who had just passed were what kept resources and gold flowing through the Gates. And because of that, infractions were generally ignored as long as they weren’t too egregious.

They didn’t even have a single artifact, Rex mused. All of their gear had been mundane. It was easy to tell, as there were no magical runes in sight. And while Artifacts were valuable, they were not that rare. The books told of cities filled with Classed people adorned with artifacts they had won battling monsters.

Sure, having a Class meant that the two men had challenged a Shrine and survived. Which was an achievement in and of itself. But it also meant that they had power, and they were too cowardly to use it against anything that could fight back.

It was precisely those kinds of men who made Rex hate coming to Dorog Targ. It would be different if they were respectable. Like those who had saved him as a child.

The kind of Classed man he wanted to be.

Letting the thought go, he rounded a corner and came face-to-face with the merchant stall he had traveled for.

Damo was arguing with a few miners. They’d brought back some gem dust they’d found amongst the coldrock, and apparently wanted too much for it.

“Alright, then get going! Because I’m not paying a copper more!”

“Fine, you tight ass! Your loss.”

Rex waited for the miners to clear out and walked up to the merchant stall.

“Hey! How you doing? So, Rex, how’d things work out between you and the mana crystal?”

“Not so good,” Rex slumped. “But that’s not what I’m here for. I’m looking for something else. I wanna rent a sword.”

“Rent a sword? Have you gone mad, boy? That coldrock really is messing with your head. Who rents swords? Wait… does that mean? So, it failed then?" Damo sighed and shook his head. “Sorry to hear that. But I already sold most of your junk.”

“I’m not here for my stuff. It is what it is,” Rex shrugged. “But I haven’t given up. I’m leaving this place, Damo. And there’s not a damn thing that is going to get in my way. Now, what about that sword?”

"Sword? So, you're going for Vigor Class, then? About time you saw reason. But why rent? If you get a sword-related Class, you're going to need a sword. Besides, I ain’t seen you ever hold one. You’re going to need to train for a while if you want the Shrine to recognize you.”

“Well, I suppose I’m happy to buy one if you're willing to part with it!" Rex grinned and placed a half dozen misshapen copper coins on the counter.

"For that? Not a chance. I might have an old shiv I can sell. This one came out of somebody’s you know what. Never lost the smell.”

“I don’t want your ass shiv, Damo. I want a sword. And if you're not going to sell me one for what I’ve got, why lecture me? This is everything. Please, I want a sword.”

"Then wait a while. Save up some coin, and come back. The Gates aren't going anywhere."

“No, no way. I'm not going back to the mines. Besides, once I'm Classed, I'll be able to earn more in a day than I can earn in a month working the mines. There will be plenty of ways for me to earn coin for my own sword, and it'll be better than any of these rusty old things. So, are you going to rent me a sword or not?”

Damo sighed. Rex wasn't wrong. Classed people could earn small fortunes finishing contracts. And not just because they were strong, but also because they could travel through the Gates.

“Have you even swung a sword before?”

"That's not your problem.”

“Rex, I like you, kid. I don’t want you doing something stupid.”

“Don’t worry about me, Damo. I got a plan.” Rex tapped his forehead. “I figured out how to get my hands on a mana crystal, didn’t I?”

“You sold all your junk and don’t have a damn thing to show for it.  And this is—”

“Damo! Please. I know what I’m doing!”

“I’m sure you do. Everyone does when they challenge the Shrine. Most don’t come out, though.”

“Fine. Whatever. Someone else can take my coin.”

“Wait, wait. Fine. Take it. Who am I to prevent a dumb kid from wasting their own coin? But don't come crying to me when the Shrine doesn't acknowledge you."

“Good doing business with you!” Rex grabbed the sword and rushed off.

“And I know where you live, Rex! If you don’t have my sword back to me at the same time tomorrow, I'll be charging double!” Damo shouted as Rex dashed away from the stand.

“See you tomorrow!” Rex called back, already running off with his sword.

It didn’t take him too long to cross town, but as usual, there was a line at the Shrine when he reached it.

While only a relatively small percentage of the population was willing to risk their lives by challenging a Shrine, that still amounted to hundreds of attempts every day on a world like Dorog. And since there were only two Shrines on the entire world, some traveled considerable distances to take their shot.

Rex already knew what he was getting into. It was a fate only for the brave. But being there in person was different. Watching one person after another enter the Shrine and never exit hit differently, and a ball formed in his stomach.

Nahh, it’ll be fine. I’m not like those suckers. That won't be my fate. I’ve got Gates to conquer and a world to escape.

He briefly thought about the elven mage. This wasn’t how he had planned for things to go. Giving up on magic was one thing, but challenging the Shrine and its guardian without good combat skills was suicide… for most.

Shines, Gate, whatever. It doesn’t matter. I’m not going out like Ma did. I’m not just going to wait to get sick.

Hours passed as the winding line made its way up to the hilltop toward the Shrine. Rex’s focus drifted in and out as he struggled with boredom. And it wasn’t until he heard cheering that he snapped out of it.

Blinking, he focused his gaze ahead and saw what the commotion was about. It was Lavrian. He had just exited the Shrine. There was a crowd around him, and it was going wild.

Of course it’s Lavrian. My luck just never ends, does it?

Rex lowered his head, trying his best to be small and unseen. There was no doubt Lavrian would come and gloat if he spotted him.

Luckily, Lavrian’s cheerleaders didn’t stick around for long, carrying him off with loud cheers. They were no doubt taking him back to the neighbourhood to start the proper celebrations.

Well, at least if I die, I won’t have to endure that.

They would party loud and late into the night, and probably well into the morning.

He was still a while away from the front of the line, and boredom quickly returned. And finally, after another two hours, he reached the front of it.

Stepping up to the Shrine, he made his way up the stone stairs and came face to face with a massive, six-armed, winged, and blindfolded goddess statue. The statue was of Effyana, one of the immortal eleven. And spiralling staircases on either side of the statue led up to a marble podium behind it, which was about the size of a boxing ring.

Rex steadied himself and marched straight up the stairs. This was his moment, and he gripped the hilt of his rented sword tightly as he drew it from his cloth belt.

This is it. My last day on Dorog. Time to give it my all!

The moment he stood on the podium, the statue’s head turned around to face him, stone grinding against stone.

Welcome, Rex of Dorog. Prove your skills to me, and be acknowledged.

“Dorog?” Rex said. He was a poor miner without a family name, but he hated the fact that she called him that. Once he was gone, Dorog would be nothing but a memory of the past. “Just Rex. No Dorog.”

Welcome, Just Rex—

“No! I didn’t mean it like that! Just don’t add Dorog to my name. Anything but that!”

Welcome, Rex of the Waste. Prove your skills to me, and be acknowledged.

“You know what, I like that. Rex of the Waste,” Rex said, nodding to himself as much as the goddess statue. “Huh. Now, time to prove myself.”

It was now or never, and he swung his sword with all his might. Back and forth, swishing through the air. Of course, he had absolutely no idea what he was doing. He was just swinging it around, hoping for the best.

The Shrine didn’t speak again. Beneath the blindfold, Effyana’s eyes faintly glowed for a second and then dulled again.

Then nothing.

“Ookay. That’s fine. I wasn’t expecting too much, anyway.”

Effyana hadn’t acknowledged him, but the fact that her statue’s eyes had glowed, even if only for a second, meant that it did account for his rather lackluster display of swordsmanship, and that was enough for him.

Okay, you’ve got this. All going to plan so far. My sword skills might have sucked, but as long as the eyes glow, there’s a chance the skill will be used to craft my Class. And that means I can still get a Vigor Class.

Taking a deep breath, Rex pulled out his bow. Hopefully, this would be it. He would display his bow skills, and Effyana would acknowledge him.

He then walked over to the edge of the podium, placed down a sack, and then walked back to the other side of the podium.

This is it. Worst-case scenario, I become a Veil archer. Better than bluelung.

He aimed his dodgy, makeshift bow and fired. The arrow flew true to its aim, although not far, fast, or with any power, so to speak.

Bullseye.

His dodgy arrow was poking out of the sack, barely piercing it. He was lucky that gerbils were easy to kill.

Rex turned triumphantly to the Shrine and placed his hands on his hips. “Not bad, huh?”

The eyes glowed faintly, then faded.

“What? Is that it? Seriously? I hit a perfect bullseye! What the actual fuck?”

Huffing, Rex opened his bag of bits and ends. He hadn’t seriously considered this possibility. He was decent with a bow, and he figured it’d be more than enough.

It’s fine. Everything is still perfectly peachy! This is why I brought the traps along, after all.

Of course, every skill he used was making the guardian waiting to spawn get stronger. Not to mention that using traps and arrows was definitely going to increase his chance of getting a Veil Class. But Rex was past caring.

It’s now or never!

In the middle of the podium, he began building and setting up some simple traps.

“There. Perfect gerbil catching traps,” Rex grinned as he dusted off his hands.

Once more, the eyes glowed for a second and dulled.

“What? Are you kidding me? Seriously? That was two damn decent skills I showed you, and you’re still not satisfied? What’s it going to take?”

Perhaps Rex had underestimated the Shrine. He was only sixteen, after all. Most would spend many more years honing themselves before attempting it. And his traps were just simple little things designed for gerbil catching. They hardly made him a master trapper.

Rex groaned and rocked his head back and forth. He had really screwed up. He’d given his last few coppers to Damo for the stupid, rusty sword, and he’d have to return it tomorrow.

To make matters worse, the Shrine could only be attempted once every twenty-four hours.

“I’m… going to be homeless, aren’t I?”

Rex sighed. He’d been impatient and screwed up. Though, in truth, the thought of going back to the mines hurt most.

“No… I can’t. I can’t go back to that.”

He remembered his sickly mother in her final days. He wanted anything but that.

Desperate, he delved into his pockets. He needed something, anything. He was past caring about what kind of Class the Shrine might gift him, or how powerful the guardian might be. He just wanted this to be over with.

Then, as he dug deep, he felt it. The chalk he’d drawn the spellcircle with.

“Drawing? Is there even such a thing as artist Classes? How the fuck is an artist supposed to beat the Shrine?”

There was, in all likelihood. Crafting Classes were rare and valuable. But they were usually sponsored by lords, who could help them devise a plan to beat the guardian without combat skills.

But Rex had no choice. It was the only thing he could think of. And honestly, he’d rather die quickly to the guardian than have to go through what his mother did. And so he did the only thing he knew how to do with the chalk: he drew a spell circle.

The moment he was done, he swung back around to stare at Effyana’s blindfolded gaze.

“Come on! Please! That has to be enough! I wasn’t joking about the sacrifices!”

That moment of silence must have been one of the longest he had ever felt. But when the Shrine’s eyes finally lit up, they did so with the brightest light he had ever seen.

“Wait… I did it? I really did it?”

You have been acknowledged by Effyana’s celestial Shrine of the Moon, Dorog.

A Class will be formed for you based on the talents you performed. Please be aware that each of the immortal eleven has their own considerations when crafting Classes.

In her infinite wisdom, Effyana has bestowed upon you the Versatile Class of [ Rune Warden ]

“Rune Warden? Versatile? Huh? What does that mean?”

He was confused; this wasn’t what he expected. But he did remember that Wardens were normally Classes from the Strider Archetype, alongside Classes like Ranger or Hunter.

You have gained the Class [ Rune Warden ], and as such, you have been blessed with the following starting Attributes: [ 2 ] Vitality, [ 4 ] Mana, [ 2 ] Strength, [ 4 ] Perception, [ 3 ] Agility, and [ 3 ] Memory

Feat gained: Rune Scribe

By touching an object and using a small amount of mana, the Rune Warden can inscribe it with a spellcircle from their known spells.

Feat gained: Mana Thread

When touching a spellcircle or an object inscribed with one, the Rune Warden can link themselves to it with a thread of mana. Once bound to the spellcircle, the Rune Warden can activate the spell at any moment with a simple command.

Please select two spells from the following options!

[ Harden ] Make what is soft, hard. When cast upon an inanimate object, [Harden] can turn simple cloth into durable iron. Spell requires channeling, and objects will return to their previous state when channeling is broken.

[ Light ] Brighten your path with the light spell. When cast, [ Light ] provides a shining beacon that can brighten even the darkest of passages and spell effects.

[ Corrosive Touch ] Whether metal or flesh, under your touch, it all melts the same. When cast, [ Corrosive Touch ] coats the user’s hand with a corrosive film that can melt through simple metals and flesh without harming the user.

[ Extra Step ] Move faster with another step. When cast, [ Extra Step ] provides the user with an additional step of an ethereal nature at a minor cost of mana.

[ Fireburst ] Burn, burn it all down. When cast, [ Fireburst ] shoots out a stream of searing hot flames at the target, burning everything in its path and setting alight flammable objects.

[ Shield ] Powerful, yet fleeting. When cast, [ Shield ] creates an incredibly powerful shimmering shield that can block most attacks against the user for a second. Mana cost relative to the power absorbed.

Rex blinked. He didn’t understand what was going on. He’d never heard of Versatile Classes, although there were a lot of things he hadn’t heard about. Stranger, though, was the fact that he could cast spells with it, and the only Classes he knew of that could cast spells were Arcane, Ritual, and Astral.

“Okay, that’s… different. But not unwelcome. I’ve got spells. That’s the main thing. Suck it, Lavrian. You lanky turd.”

He also felt great. Stronger than ever. His vision was improved. He could see fine details much further away than he previously could. And even just taking a few steps around the podium, he felt like his movements were more precise and responsive.

This feels amazing!

However, he immediately noticed one big problem. There was no spellcasting Feat. All spellcasting Classes had a spellcasting Feat. The Feat had one purpose: it granted the recipient the ability to cast spells provided by the Shrines. And without one…

“Hey, hang on… What’s Rune Scribe?”

Rex had never heard of the Feat. But if he had to guess, it was his Class’ equivalent to the spellcasting Feat.

Enabling Feats were important. For example, even if Lavrian could use Arcane magic innately, he would still need the Feat “Arcane Spellcasting” to cast spells provided by his Class.

With that thought in mind, he carefully read over the Feat again. “Does this… does this work how I think it does?” Rex had a mini lightbulb moment. If he understood correctly how his two Feats worked, then things were about to get interesting.

“Okay, that might just be pretty damn cool.”

Rex rubbed his hands together as he went over his spell options again. If instead of casting spells normally, he could engrave them onto items, and could then activate them whenever he wanted… well, then, his entire way of using spells was flipped on its head.

Please select two spells!

“Yeah, yeah. I’m getting to it. You know, you’re kind of impatient for an eternal goddess.”

Having two starter spells to pick from was fairly standard for casting Classes, although the absolute peak Classes sometimes got three.

“It says Versatile Class, though? But it works like one of the spellcasting Archetypes. Interesting.”

Rex knew what he was supposed to do. Pretty much every book explained that a wise caster should pick one offensive and one defensive spell to begin with.

He quickly went down the list. [ Light ] was a utility spell, so he quickly knocked it off his list. Even if it did sound useful. He had read about creatures that could summon clouds of impenetrable darkness, and having a counter for such things would be nice. But it was too situational.

[ Harden ] sounded like the kind of spell that would be useful if everything had already gone to shit. But he’d much rather just buy a suit of armor and hope it didn’t get to that, instead of turning his shirt into iron, or whatever.

Even at a glance, there was no doubt that [Shield] was the best pure defensive spell on offer. But he didn’t like the last bit. What good was a powerful shield if he didn’t have enough mana to block the powerful attack coming his way?

[ Extra Step ], on the other hand, sounded rather mundane at first. But the best defense was not getting hit at all. Speed was a good way to avoid getting hit, and his Perception, alongside his mana, were his highest Attributes.

“That’s not a bad combo. Not at all.”

Furthermore, an additional ethereal leg sounded like it might just have additional uses.

“Okay, that’s defense done. Now two offense spells to choose from…”

Rex immediately knew which spell most books would advise the user to take. It was [ Fireburst ] without a doubt. Every book recommended against spellcasters taking “touch” spells.

While it wasn’t unheard of for spellcasters to use “touch” spells, most spellcasters were generally best suited to remaining at a party’s rear. Which made the whole “touch” aspect rather difficult to utilize.

However, if Rex was able to inscribe his arrows with a spellcircles, then would he need to touch his targets?”

“If this doesn’t work the way I think it does, this is going to be a major pain in the ass.”

He was imagining himself as a spellcasting archer who had to push his way to the heart of the melee to use his “touch” spells. It was a truly absurd thought, but [ Corrosive Touch ] undoubtedly sounded like the better spell if he didn’t have to touch his enemy.

Fire was cool and all, but a spell that could melt through metal? Armored enemies would be Swiss cheese before his arrows… assuming his theory was correct.

“Wait, if it does work like that, then…”

Suddenly, Rex had an urge to look back over the defensive spells. He had felt certain about [ Extra Step ], but actually, it might have been the worst option for him.

After all, what good would he get out of making an ethereal leg appear from objects?

“Hehe, that might actually be pretty funny. But what about [ Harden ]?”

A simple gerbil snare might not have impressed the Shrine of Effyana, but what if he had been able to suddenly harden it to iron? He imagined string tightening around someone’s ankle and turning to iron. And that was only the start of it.

“So many possibilities…”

In truth, despite all the creative ways he could use such a spell, it still wouldn’t be the best option for a purely defensive spell. But what did the books know? Besides, those were caster builds. Was he even a proper spellcaster?

“I’m going to regret this, aren’t I?”

The problem was that Rex’s imagination had already run off, and he was somewhat impulsive. His heart had already decided what spells he wanted, ancient scholars be damned.

Spell selection confirmed, Rex of the Waste.

Summoning the Guardian in 10…

9…

8…

“Well, here we go,” Rex murmured to himself as he took an arrow and, for the first time, activated a spell as he focused on his Feat, just as the training manuals instructed.

Before his very eyes, a pristine spellcircle appeared on the arrow shaft, and he grinned widely.

“Would you look at that.”
