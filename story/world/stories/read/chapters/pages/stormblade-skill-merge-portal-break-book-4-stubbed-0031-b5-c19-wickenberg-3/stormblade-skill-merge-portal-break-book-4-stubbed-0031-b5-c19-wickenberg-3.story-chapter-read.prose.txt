The Paragon’s presence hung in the humid, stinking air.

I could almost smell it. But not quite—the monster, whatever it was, had concealed itself well. The only reason I knew it was here was a distinct mix of energy—a combination of emotionless depth mixed with just the barest hint of the storm’s fury. I recognized that fury.

“The God of Thunder knows this Paragon,” I said.

Ellen looked to either side of the churned-earth dike, staring into the water. “One of his servants? An ambush?”

I thought about it for a moment. Then I shook my head. It couldn’t be. The God of Thunder had given me time, and I trusted that I still had a little of that left. “No. This is something different. One of the Placid King’s minions. We need to kill it. Let’s keep moving.”

The plan had been to hunt monsters until Ellen was at the cusp of S-Rank, then to disappear into the portal’s depths and wait until the Guardians reacted. They’d show up. They’d have to; if we closed the portal, it’d be a disaster, and they’d take the lion’s share of the blame for starting the war in the first place. But now…now, plans had changed. This Paragon might only be A-Rank—it certainly wasn’t at the same level as Quellat—but killing it would set the Placid King back.

Ellen fell in behind me as we pushed down the narrow causeway, ignoring the few remaining monsters. In the distance, a massive, wood-looking island broke the otherwise flat horizon. We sprinted toward it as fast as Ellen could go, and the closer we got, the more intense the Paragon’s aura got.

The island grew clearer, too. It wasn’t an island at all, so much as it was a tangle of driftwood from the corpses of hundreds of the treants we’d been killing. The mound shifted slightly with the faint currents, right where the freshwater and saltwater halves of the dungeon met. A narrow path led up one side. I took one look at it and shook my head. “That’s the way to the boss. Has to be.”

“So it’s not here?”

“Oh, it’s here. But it’s under the water. There’s gotta be a space under that island. Let’s go poke around and see what we can find.”

For an unawakened human, diving into murky, mud-choked water in battle robes and armor was asking to die. For an A-Ranker and an S-Ranker, the water-logged clothes and lack of visibility weren’t even speed bumps. We dove deeper and deeper until, at last, Ellen spotted the narrow entrance to an underwater cavern. I kicked off a twisted log and pushed up toward what looked like a faint light. She followed.

We emerged in a low-roofed hollow in the driftwood. The water shifted from fresh to salt and back again. I pulled myself up onto the flat floor of compressed logs and sticks, finding my feet and readying my sword as Ellen joined me.

Then we waited for the Paragon to reveal itself.

Magda had lost track of the intruders.

The Placid King’s frustration would know no bounds if she let them escape—or worse, kill Xanavos Kankar. He might even voice his mild irritation, and that would be a death sentence for her. But they were doing a brilliant job of staying out of the water, offering her only glimpses of the S-Rank lightning swordsman’s presence.

She’d retreated to her lair in an attempt to come up with a new plan when, miracle of miracles, she sensed both of their presences in the water again—and both were close. No, not just close. Both were entering her lair. Her sense of danger warred with her need to kill. The compulsion was there, and every portal monster like her had to manage it. But even more than that, both her favorite lover and her sworn master would want her to kill these intruders, and there wasn’t a better place for it than her own lair.

She dove deep, sliding through the water and becoming part of it. Her Path was one of sinuous, almost sensual smoothness, and the sensation of water moving across her slick body felt natural as she became one with the mix of ocean and lake she’d made her home in.

Then she waited as both intruders pulled themselves out of the water in her under-wood lair.

“Where is it?” the female asked.

“No idea. Stay on your toes,” the male said. He had the lightning sword. He was the bigger threat. Therefore, Magda thought, she’d have to kill him first and fastest.

She reformed, inches below the water, her eel-shaped body coiled and ready to spring, her pharyngeal jaw ready to pull the swordsman into her gullet and tear him apart. She locked eyes on the back of his head, focused entirely on him.

Jump, bite, end the fight. Easy. Simple. And she’d be rewarded by both her master and core-bond partner. Ready…and…

G—

Before she could go, a wave of fear hit Magda. It was overwhelming. More terror than she’d ever felt in her life. Her body wouldn’t uncoil. She couldn’t lunge. All she could do was watch as the swordsman turned, eyes locked on hers.

And that fear made the aura from the female mage pale in comparison.

This close, the feeling of familiarity was even more overwhelming.

Magda, Paragon of the Sliding Surge: A-Rank

The eel-like monster sat, paralyzed, in the water. Ellen’s aura of fear had locked her down instantly—locked down an A-Rank Paragon. That result was so far beyond what I’d hoped for that, for a moment, I had no idea what to do.

Then I took stock of her. She was long—probably a good fifteen to twenty feet. Skin that looked almost slimy, but in a beautiful shade of purplish-green rather than a filthy brown. Eyes the size of truck tires, black and white with no hint of color. No limbs, but a trio of fins along her back. She was big, but far from the biggest monster we’d encountered. The God of Thunder out-volumed her by quite a bit, even if she probably weighed more; he wasn’t exactly a solid being.

Her jaw opened, revealing a second jaw inside it. She gulped like a fish out of water, massive eyes flickering toward the depths below.

“That won’t work,” Ellen said from behind her. “We’ve got you cornered.”

I braced myself for a fight. It’d be quick; Ellen didn’t need the skill levels. I could probably kill Magda in less than a minute. And she wouldn’t get away.

Instead, she cleared her throat, a horrific hacking sound that brought my sword up in Thunderlord stance. Before I could launch into an attack, she lowered her head. “Thunder God’s servant, I want to bargain.”

“I’m not his servant,” I said.

“You reek of his aura.”

“So do you.”

“Touche,” she said. She took a deep, rattling breath. “The God of Thunder is my favorite core-bond partner. My name is Magda. I am the Paragon of the Sliding Surge Path, and you are currently in the portal world I inhabit. Specifically, in my boudoir.”

“Kade Noelstra,” I said.

“Should we be telling her—“ Ellen started.

I nodded. “She won’t be able to tell him anything he doesn’t already know. Besides, the God of Thunder’s not her master, is he?”

The gigantic eel stiffened. Then her head dropped. “No. I serve the Placid King.”

“Thought so. I could sense his aura on you as well. You’re doubly our enemy, Magda. Both of those Paragons have declared war on me,” I said. “And I know this isn’t your world. You’re not the portal boss, so killing you won’t be a problem for our city.”

Ellen nodded. “True. You’re just another portal monster to us.”

Magda’s half-submerged eyes blinked, sending a faint ripple through the incredibly still water in the wooden hollow, and she shifted to take in Ellen. After staring briefly, she nodded with a slight splash. “I can offer you two a deal. Information for information. You can learn exactly what I’ve been up to here. In exchange, I want to know why you’re at war with my master and lover both. First, full honesty. I was going to kill you both, but I no longer think I’m capable of that.”

“Fine. Talk,” I said. I doubted she’d be able to tell me anything interesting, but—

“I’ve been corrupting the delvers that enter this portal for the better part of twenty years.”

Well, that was certainly interesting enough. I waved with the Storm’s Blade.

“It’s simple. This portal is a permanent fixture of your world. I’ve heard how it’s used, and without it, your city would die. The humans called the Guardians entered it shortly after it opened, and I was here, waiting for them. I can be fairly…persuasive…when I have to be.” Magda wriggled, lifting herself out of the water, and as she did, her body changed from that of a purple-and-green moray eel to that of a similarly-colored, completely nude woman with an eel’s tail in place of legs.

“So, I entered into an agreement with the leader of your Guardians. He would not destroy this portal world, nor would he send his teams too far in. In return, I would keep it culled, while allowing only the bare minimum of monsters to leak out into his fields. The negotiations were…energetic, but we found common ground eventually.” She shrugged, tail waving back and forth in the water. “We both want this portal to survive, after all.”

“And the corruption?” Ellen asked. I nodded, pointedly not looking at Magda, and Ellen rolled her eyes. “Kade, you don’t have to do the ‘modest’ routine. She’s a monster, not competition.”

“Correct. I couldn’t interfere with your Dual Skill bond even if I wanted to,” Magda said, laughing lightly—and obviously falsely. Tension dripped from her, even as she put on a show of being comfortable and vulnerable. “The corruption, then. There is a Guardian who is extremely loyal to her guild master. The second in command. She was my point of influence. The Placid King put me to work on her, and I have bored into her mind for the last several years.”

“Why?”

“Because, Kade Noelstra, Paragon of the Stormblade Path and the Unbroken Storm, and Eleanor Traynor, the Unpathed Shadow, the Placid King has had an eye on this world for a long time, and the Craftsman’s siege of your city was supposed to be his moment of triumph. Instead, it falls on me to fix the Craftsman’s mistakes. If I don’t, the Placid King will be…mildly irritated.”

“That’s it?” Ellen asked, snorting. “He’ll be mildly irritated? Kade’s driven by fury. Mild irritation doesn’t scare us.”

“It should. The Placid King never shows emotion. His very aura is one of absolute emotional stillness.” Magda’s eyes shifted to me, running up and down my body and resting on the Storm’s Blade for a moment before locking with my eyes. “Now, information for information. Why are you at war with the Placid King and my core-bond lover?”

“Because they’re planning on using my core to fuel their ascension to a new realm, and I can’t let that happen.”

“Oh, you know about the ascension? How interesting!” Magda leaned forward. “Yes, the Placid King wants to ascend. So what? You’re on the path toward that too, you know? Both of you might be. Why not embrace it? In a short few thousand years, either or both of you could find yourselves in a better reality. I’m horribly jealous.”

Her slippery body inched closer to mine, and a scent filled the room. It wasn’t anything like the stinking odor of the morass-like world around us. No. This was cloying…almost sensual. Behind me, Ellen cleared her throat. “Kade, be careful!”

“Got you!”

Magda’s body shifted back to her eel-like form, and she lunged toward me. I’d let her drift too close. The Storm’s Blade went up. Ellen’s aura crashed outward.

And the hollow erupted into chaos.
