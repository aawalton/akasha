The Mayor of Copperpine stepped back, his duty done, his face a mask of profound relief. Ysara gave him a curt, dismissive nod, then turned to face the shimmering tear in reality. Without a word, she gathered her cloak and stepped through. Yudursh followed a heartbeat later, his hands resting on the familiar hilts of his twin swords. Arthur, his face a perfect copy of the quiet rogue Kaelin, was the last to enter the vortex.

The world dissolved into a nauseating, silent pull. It was not like moving through space, but like space itself was being pulled through him. A disorienting pressure built in his ears, and the air grew thin and cold. For a moment that felt like an eternity, there was nothing but a crushing, formless void. Then, with a lurch that settled deep in his stomach, reality snapped back into place.

They stood on a raised platform of polished white stone, surrounded by a dozen Royal Guards in gleaming, silver-inlaid plate armor. The guards, who had been standing at ease, jolted to attention at their sudden appearance, hands flying to their weapons. High stone ramparts blocked the view on all sides, but looking up, Arthur could see the uppermost spires of Valoria's tallest towers piercing the clouds, their golden domes glinting in the sun. The air here was different, clean and sharp, carrying the distant scent of salt from a sea he couldn't see. The sky above was a brighter, clearer blue than the one over Copperpine.

This was the heart of the kingdom. The center of the cage.

A man with the insignia of a captain pushed through the line of guards, his expression a mixture of shock and suspicion. He was older, his face a testament to a life of conflict, with a jagged scar that cut through one eyebrow and disappeared into his hairline. He stopped, his eyes widening in recognition as he saw Ysara. He immediately dropped to one knee.

"Princess!" he exclaimed, his voice a low gravel. "Your Highness, we had no word of your arrival." He rose, his professional demeanor snapping back into place as he saluted crisply. "Captain Corbin, at your service. Allow me to escort you to the palace at once."

His gaze swept over her two guards, taking in their disheveled state with a single, practiced glance that held both recognition and concern. Ysara gave a slight, weary nod of assent. "Thank you, Captain. We would be grateful for your escort."

They were led from the portal platform. It was not in a simple plaza, but at the center of a dedicated fortress. High ramparts bristled with ballistae, and the courtyard was filled with a small army of soldiers, a permanent defense against any threat that might emerge from the portal. From this military outpost, they were escorted into a covered, horse-drawn carriage. The interior was plush velvet, a stark contrast to the filth of the boarding house.

As the carriage began its slow, deliberate journey, Arthur looked out the small, barred window. They traveled along a wide, immaculate avenue paved with the same white stone as the platform. The buildings here were not just houses, but grand estates and soaring towers of marble and gold, their architecture a graceful display of arches and intricate carvings. Liveried servants with polished collars moved with a silent, practiced efficiency, their faces devoid of the weary desperation he had seen in Copperpine. The ride was taking longer than necessary, and Arthur realized it was intentional. Corbin was giving the palace a head start, a chance to prepare for the princess's unexpected return.

After a few minutes, the palace itself came into view. It was not a single building, but a sprawling complex of white marble and shining gold that seemed to climb the very cliffs it was built on, a monument to wealth and power designed to awe and intimidate.

‘So much effort to look powerful,’ Drogan’s voice echoed in his mind. ‘In my day, we just piled the skulls of our enemies high. Cheaper, and it sent a clearer message.’

The carriage rolled to a stop in a pristine courtyard where they were met by a tall, thin man in severe black robes. His face was a permanent mask of disapproval, as if he were perpetually smelling something unpleasant. This was the Chamberlain.

He bowed stiffly, his movements precise and economical. "Your Highness," he said, his voice dry as dust. "It is a relief to see you have returned. The King has been most concerned for your safety. He requests your presence for dinner this evening to discuss the events of the Great Hunt."

"Thank you, Chamberlain," Ysara replied, her tone cool and formal. "I will be there." She dismissed him with a nod, then turned to her two guards. "You are dismissed as well. Refresh yourselves. Be ready to accompany me in an hour."

Having given her orders, she swept past, heading towards her personal wing of the palace. Arthur watched her go, his mind a cold, calculating machine. She separates from us at the first opportunity. A princess retiring to her chambers, or a target isolating her pawns before the trap is sprung? The soul pact prevented her from directly harming him, but it was a new magic, its loopholes untested. Trust was a luxury he couldn't afford.

Yudursh caught Arthur's eye and gave a subtle jerk of his head. "Follow me," he said, his voice low. He led Arthur away from the main halls and into a private wing adjacent to the princess's own. It was more like a small, self-contained house than a barracks, reserved exclusively for her personal guard. They entered a shared common room furnished with dark wood tables and comfortable leather chairs. Four doors led off to private chambers.

"The second room is Kaelin's," Yudursh stated, his voice devoid of emotion. "Refresh yourself. We're to be on station outside the princess's wing in an hour to stand guard until she leaves for dinner." He turned and left without another word.

Arthur entered the room, his senses on high alert. The luxury was a distraction, a potential cage of comfort. The floors were polished hardwood, partially covered by a thick, woven rug. A large bed with a soft feather mattress and clean linen sheets stood against one wall. He immediately noted the set of double doors that opened onto a small stone balcony overlooking a manicured garden. An exit. It was his first and most important observation.

Satisfied with his primary assessment of the room's layout, he turned his attention to the attached bathroom. It was just as luxurious. A deep tub and a wide basin, both carved from matching pieces of white marble, stood on a floor of dark, polished stone. Instead of crude handles, there were two glowing runes etched into the stone above the basin. He touched one, and hot, steaming water began to flow from a silver spout, a simple act of magic that felt like the height of luxury.

The sight of the steaming water was a comfort he hadn't realized he craved. He stripped off the travel-worn clothes, dropping the filthy garments in a heap on the polished stone floor. He sank into the deep tub, letting the heat seep into muscles that had been coiled with tension for weeks. He scrubbed away the grime from the city's alleyways and the lingering dust of travel, feeling the last layers of his ordeal wash away. For a few precious minutes, he allowed himself to simply exist in the warmth, a moment of quiet respite before the next phase of his infiltration began. Feeling almost human again, he finally stepped out, wrapping himself in a thick, soft towel.

He opened the wardrobe and found it filled with clothes. On one side hung the practical, dark leather attire of a scout. On the other, fine linen shirts and tailored trousers for formal occasions. He dressed in a simple, clean set of regular clothes. For the first time since his rebirth, he was experiencing the luxury afforded by this world. He was clean, he was in a soft bed, and he was, for all intents and purposes, back in civilization. He felt a flicker of contentment, a feeling he immediately recognized as a dangerous weakness.
