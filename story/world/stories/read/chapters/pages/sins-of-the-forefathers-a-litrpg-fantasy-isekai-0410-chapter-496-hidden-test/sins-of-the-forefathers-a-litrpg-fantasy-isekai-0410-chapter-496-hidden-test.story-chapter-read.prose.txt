“And this old relic denies us access to that dream,” Ul-Korveth’s tone dripped with mingled frustration and contempt. “As the controlling mind behind the Vereden, Nehushtan is the chooser of the inheritance. And we are not worthy enough to him. Thus, our main goal for many, many centuries now, has been the slow weakening of this stubborn fool. We cannot strike him down directly…”

Here, Ul-Korveth stepped forward and disdainfully flicked a finger against the nearly invisible shield surrounding both the throne and the staff Nehushtan was curled around. There was an audible hiss as the superheated Aether attempted to sear through the hardened Korvokian shell of his finger. But my sharp eyes noticed that there was only the barest indication of a burn on that maroon flesh.

Hmm…

Ul-Korveth shook his head in visible disgust. “Impenetrable. Neither mortal hands nor blades are enough to pierce the shield upon the very Throne of War, ruling seat of Mighty Yorgun. We have tried.”

“Ha,” Nehushtan laughed mockingly. “Fond memories. I do so wish you modern degenerates had the courage to throw yourself upon the shield and fry yourselves like your ancestors did. Good fun.”

His response was merely an upturned lip. “It would be a waste of useful flesh. But our long term plan had been working well.” Ul-Korveth shook his head. “Attack the connection between war form and command form for long enough, and this obstacle will be drained in time. It was working just fine. A few more decades, even, and it was likely he would have fallen.”

“I’m sensing a ‘but’,” I said evenly, meeting Ul-Korveth’s eyes.

“Correct.” He nodded approvingly. “I suspect you can even divine what it was. After all, there are few things that have disrupted the order of Vereden more.”

I withheld a sigh. I did, in fact. It was the entire reason we had even left Herztal in the first place.

I was…starting to understand what might drive the Korvoks to desire the Vereden so deeply.

“The Skyfall,” Walter answered for me, coming to join the growing group opposing the Korvokhan.

Ul-Korveth tilted his head thoughtfully. “Is that what you’ve termed the phenomenon? A bit…obtuse. The Church of the Red Truth has chosen to simply call it what it is.”

“The End Times.”

Well. Accurate enough, I suppose.

“And you,” Meia said quietly, as the last of my officers to come to my side. “Want to side-step that end entirely, by fleeing the very planet.”

“How interesting,” Ul-Korveth’s eyes lit up as he studied Meia. “An Orc. Surely, you of all people understand. After all, this isn’t even your planet. If you had the opportunity, surely you would choose to flee back to the safety of Indiqua. There, you could survive the coming storm that shall devastate this doomed world.”

Tellingly, Meia was silent. Her head lilted somewhat, hiding her expression from us under hood.

I think she was shocked when I laid a single hand on her shoulder, judging by how quickly her head snapped over to stare at me. I merely smiled a much more sincere smile at her in response. “If it was an option, I would have done the same,” I said wryly. “You have nothing to be ashamed about.”

A long, slow sigh escaped the Half-Orc Sorceress then, and she nodded at me thankfully. I released my hand from her shoulder, the smile on my face falling off as I turned away from her. My eyes were cold as I returned them to Ul-Korveth. “Speak plainly. What is it you’re proposing?”

“Very well then,” He said crisply, standing up straighter and sliding his hands into the sleeves of his robes before him. “It is thus. As a Precursor, you are the last remaining person capable of passing the test that bars access to the Guardian. If you do, you shall be granted full command of both this wretched facsimile of a Spirit, as well as the Vereden. With that done, you shall surrender control of both to me, and I give you my word.” Ul-Korveth withdrew his right hand, and placed it over heart in an attempt to show his sincerity. “You, your men, and whoever else you wish to be granted asylum aboard the Vereden will be allowed to flee this dying world with what remains of Clan Korvok. The ship is more than large enough to fit the entirety of the Korvokian society that matters and your people.”

I stared at him for a long, silent moment. I wasn’t the only one, either. More than just my circle of Captains looked to be visibly considering the matter. I could almost see the thoughts swirling through their minds.

If we did as Ul-Korveth bid, we would have a guaranteed method of surviving the Skyfall. Not only would we no longer need to even fight the Mad God, Anguis, and all of their combined forces, we would be safe. We could simply pick up all of our family’s from back in Herztal in our shiny new starship, and we could leave. All of the problems of Vereden would become a thing of the past for us. Our future would be found among the endless expanse of the stars. We would be explorers upon that impossibly endless frontier.

It was tempting, of course. Anyone who said it wasn’t was either a fool or a liar.

One problem, though.

“Why, exactly,” I said mildly. “Would I simply hand control over to you, after I pass this ‘test’?”

I suspected I already knew the answer, of course. But I wanted to hear him say it. I think Ul-Korveth even realized that, judging by the delicate snort he allowed to escape the holes where his nose should be.

Still, he chose to answer.

“Why, because of our captives, of course,” Ul-Korveth chuckled. “We have quite a few of them, after all. I didn’t have time to extract much more than names from their leaders, but those I managed to, ah…acquire. One Maria Rellani, and Olag of House Florens? Accompanied, of course, by nearly a hundred different soldiers. Which is, of course, not even mentioning the considerable number of people we’ve taken from Karag Varla…and beyond.”

That was a telling word.

‘Beyond’.

“You…” I started incredulously, and moved into flat out disbelief. “You invaded more than just Varla?”

“Why, of course,” Ul-Korveth’s smile shifted into a smirk. “We shall need more sacrifices than simply our misguided cousins next door if we are to breach this barrier. I suspect you know as well as I do, Nathaniel, how little time we have left before the, ah, ‘Skyfall’ progresses past it’s zenith. It was, of course, our…original plan before you so graciously presented yourself as a second option. Only through a significant amount of bloodletting shall we break this barrier.”

More than one sword rattled in its sheath, then. My people were, rightfully, not very pleased to hear that news.

So.

That’s what they had needed all of their captives for. I had heard, earlier, that they were meant to be sacrifices, but I hadn’t had a clue what they were being sacrificed for.

It turns out that all of those hundreds, perhaps thousands of captives were to be used as fodder to break the shield around Nehushtan. The last remaining barrier preventing them from seizing control of the Vereden.

I narrowed my eyes at the monster across from me, standing so confidently alone, surrounded by literally hundreds of people with a vested interest in preventing that mass slaughter.

I wanted to kill him, but…something about how he didn’t seem concerned at all about us…

It made me wary.

“Go on, worm,” I heard Nehushtan taunt the Korvokhan. Flicking my eyes over to the ‘control spirit’, I saw that he had a serpentine sneer upon his brass lips as he stared down the target of his disdain. “Tell him the rest. Tell him how you would have never been capable of such a working on your own. You primitive degenerates have never and will never possess the knowledge of such things. Tell him about your backer.”

Ul-Korveth was entirely unmoved by Nehushtan’s taunting. He merely smiled at in the face of it. “Tell?” He chuckled. “Why tell, when it would be far more effective to show our guest, Great Guardian. Come, Nathaniel. Please, follow me, and I would be more than happy to lead you to the sacrifices you’re so obviously concerned for.”

I did not care for the sinister edge that crept into his smirk, then.

“It will be an excellent lesson on why you should cease your fruitless resistance.”

……………………………….

I left half of our forces to guard the throne chamber where Nehushtan resided, as Ul-Korveth guided us deeper into the Inner Heart. I wanted us to have an entrenched position to fall back to, in case the Korvokhan was leading us into a trap of some kind. I left them with orders to kill anyone or anything that stepped into throne room that wasn’t a member of the expeditionary forces.

What else was I to do, but to follow after this madman? There was an implicit threat within the air that if I did not do as he bid, then something dire would soon happen to all of the captives he and his crazed society of cannibals and torturers had stolen. I had to bide my time for the right moment if I hoped to spare them from that fate.

Still, I wouldn’t forget how he was holding this over our heads. Ul-Korveth would pay for all he had done.

I made a promise to myself that he would.

Before we left, Nehushtan seemed pleased at the possibility Korvok blood being shed, but Ul-Korveth seemed entirely unconcerned. However…

Nehushtan did leave me with a few parting words, as we stepped beyond the throne for the dark tunnel I had spotted on the other side of it, stretching off into the distance.

“No doubt your ears will soon be filled with all manner of lies about me…Precursor,” He said, sounding oddly subdued. He hesitated for a moment, before continuing. “Understand, that anything I did in long lost ages, I did…because I could not say no.”

With those oddly melancholy words, we passed beyond both the ‘Great Guardian’ and the light of the throne…

And into the deep darkness of the tunnel beyond.

In an odd mirror of the stairs we had descended beneath the Heartspire, there was no illumination in here. It was pitch black but for the faint and receding light of Yorgun’s throne in the distance. Everyone but Ul-Korveth himself had to break out light Skills and Spells once more, because this tunnel wasn’t just leading deeper into the Inner Heart. It was going deeper into Vereden as well.

This almost seemed like a ramp, more than a tunnel.

However, thankfully, it didn’t continue on for more than ten minutes of cautiously following the Korvokhan down into the depths. Similarly to the throat beneath the Heartspire, this tunnel also ended in another vault door deliberately created to mirror the stylings of the Netherim. There…were a few differences, however.

For one, this door was actually guarded. Over a dozen different Korvoks, Immaculata if I was going to guess, stood before gate. They were different from their indolent cousins, however. They were all much more disciplined, and clad from head to toe in what looked like a form of blackened, hardened leather armor. It clung to their forms like the skin that they no longer possessed, displaying a musculature that wasn’t frankly impossible for most mortals. Not an inch of the horrifically beautiful forms I knew that lurked beneath were visible, from the tips of their boots to the crown of their masked helmets. They stood tall, still, and proud before the gate, with a blackened, wickedly barbed halberd taller than they were in one hand, and a round shield wider than their torso in the other.

I didn’t like the sight of them, obviously…but it was the gate itself that caused a shudder down my spine.

Every mystical sense I had cultivated since I had landed on Vereden told me there was true evil on the other side of that door. The metal seemed to have been blackened in some way, but that was the least of the problems with it. Two things seemed to come from the seams and divots in the metal.

The first was a startlingly tainted Aether that the metal was incapable of holding back. It was thick that I nearly gagged at the sensation of it. I could almost see it with my physical eyes, but with those beyond the material realm…

It was nearly black.

However…there was something else that stained the surface of the door.

Blood. Blackened blood was, even now, oozing from every possible seam on the door. Blood was something I had come to expect from the Korvoks, but this was different. Somehow, a howling voice deep inside of me said this was not more of the oceans of vital liquid stolen from Nehushtan’s war form. This was something far more malignant, something almost rotten. It was like the gate was there not just to prevent people from venturing inside…

But to block something from escaping.

The armed and armored Immaculata bowed at the coming of their leader, and Ul-Korveth chuckled and patted more than one set of shoulders as he approached the door. Once he had reached it, he turned to face me and mine.

“Do try and keep your stomach,” The monster said in an almost kindly voice. “This is always a bit of a fraught sight, for the uninitiated.”

Ul-Korveth turned away from us, entirely unconcerned about showing his unprotected back, and placed a single palm on the surface of the corrupted metal. He had to actually push through a layer of the blackened blood to do so, and I nearly shuddered at even the possibility of touching that vile liquid.

The door shifted, almost as if it were alive, and gradually rolled away as if guided by an invisible hand.

Upon the sight of what lay inside…

Even after all we had seen in Karag Korvok…

More than one of my people either immediately turned away or vomited onto the stone below. I was nearly one of them, in truth. It was only thanks to my bodily control that I held it at bay.

I didn’t blame them.

This was, by far, the greatest defilement I had ever laid eyes upon.
