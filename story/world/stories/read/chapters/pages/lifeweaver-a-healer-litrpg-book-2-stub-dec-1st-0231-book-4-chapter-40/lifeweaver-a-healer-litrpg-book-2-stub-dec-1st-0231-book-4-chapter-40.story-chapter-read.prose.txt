Seeing an item cast a spell was a strange experience. While it was no different from a person doing it, a thing performing such an action sent a wave of revulsion through Luke as he raced to reach the portal before the massive Singer weapon fired its blast and launched enough energy through to break Earth itself, or at least bring about damage on par with a third world war, if his gut feeling about the attack proved correct. Soulless destruction. That was the problem. Yes, he'd faced plenty of monsters with spell-casting capabilities, but while they weren't all sapient, he didn't consider them empty husks either. No, a machine that could open portals was wrong and shouldn't exist. Neither should the weapon about to unleash hell through it, but that one was at least created by an Integrated. That made it easier to stomach, somehow.

Upon reaching the portal, Luke put his plan into place. His initial plan had been to bring in Integrated from Earth, Hannah, Curtis, Elijah, and others to break the weapon and save everyone back home. But recent revelations had convinced him that it wasn't the safest way out of this, and it didn't have the largest chance of success. The most effective way to ensure Earth was safe was not to deal with the weapon, but with the portal. At first, he decided to close it, but when he met the Intruders, that plan shifted ever so slightly.

Luke: "Jump through the portal now, Ray!"

Ray: "I've got the Lifeweaver! Don't make me leave him before he coughs up my stuff!"

Luke: "Bring that asshole then, just go through now! NOW!"

With all the barriers in place around the weapon, Ray would maybe be fine staying, but there was no reason to risk it, not for a dagger and a robe. Those were just items. Nothing that couldn't be replaced, given time. Sensing Ray had moved through the portal and over to Earth, Luke got started. Separating the thread from the portal wouldn't be enough. Yes, it would render the weapon blast useless, since the portal didn't go anywhere, but this was an excellent opportunity to give the Singers a taste of their own medicine. They'd destroyed a whole city on Earth, leveling New York, and this coming attack would be so much worse. He hoped it would be some tough medicine for them to swallow.

Luke found the echo thread he was after and affixed it to the portal just in time. The blast from the weapon didn't make a sound, but the yellow, almost solid bar of energy shooting from the front made the hairs on his neck and forearms stand. It was over in a second, and the female Singer operating the thing slumped to the ground, burned out from the impressive mana use. At the same time, her weapon fell to pieces, most of them so hot that the metal glowed orange. Luke breathed a sigh of relief as he turned to face the small crowd of Singers, who all looked pleased as hell with themselves. He was about to give some cool one-liner about 'doctor's orders' or 'best laid plans', but the ground rumbled under their feet as something shifted.

At first, he figured it was an earthquake. That initial impression wasn't wrong, but it wasn't the whole picture. The weave, he realized. It had frayed.

"Whoa," he said as many pairs of fearful eyes turned his way.

"What have you done?" one of those operating the portal machine asked.

Eyes widening, Luke ignored the question as he expanded his senses, traversing the weave. It wasn't just fraying. It was tearing itself apart. Their damn weapon had blown a hole not just through physical space, but through the metaphysical one as well.

Luke eyed the pieces of the machine, now cooled enough to have gone back to grays and blacks, shaking his head. This had not been a weapon that belonged in the hands of a normal Integrated. The female Singer was still on the ground. Dead, he realized. Where did they get it? How the hell did they use it? And the most important question: did they have more than one?

He had hoped to deal a devastating blow to the Singers by redirecting the blast to their own planet, targeting the Intruders he'd just escaped, but this was more than that. So much more. This was genocide.

"...Damn."

Attacks from the Singers followed as their world stood on its head, but they were weak and inconsequential, almost like children targeting him. They were losing their Integrated strengths, Luke realized. He was seeing them being stripped of everything they had earned in real-time as their system broke, the void tearing through the massive hole in the weave to begin its feast.

"This is what you wanted for Earth?" Luke asked them, shaking his head. It was too big for him to even summon a speck of rage. "This is what 'destroy' means to the Singers?"

Disgusted, he grabbed hold of an echo thread and weavestepped back to Earth, leaving their dying world for the last time. He'd signed the death warrant for an entire system, but he had done so with his own home's safety in mind. Not to destroy, but to preserve. This time, destruction followed, as it so often did, but Luke didn't allow himself to feel their deaths echoing throughout The Greater System.

"Us or them," he mumbled, falling into Hannah's arms. "I'm not a monster."

"What happened?" she murmured.

Before answering, he needed to know.

Luke: "Milla, did everyone make it out?"

His sister gave him a long look from across the room of The Band's headquarters, then nodded with a weak smile.

Milla: "Everyone in the EDP returned in time. I'm getting word that portals have closed everywhere. The Singers still on Earth are being pushed out of cities as we speak. A lot of Integrated on our side are dying, but we're winning. What happened?"

Relief flooded through him, and he whispered in Hannah's ear as he withdrew. "I'm fine."

Everyone in the guild had gathered, and Luke's gaze swept over each of their faces. He drew in a deep breath and was about to explain when he realized he'd forgotten someone.

"Shit," he cursed. "Be right back."

The following Weavestep took a lot out of him after all that had happened, despite his mana levels still being comfortable enough. Any interaction with the weave took a toll, and not all of it was reflected in the physical strain or the mana requirements. Exhausted, he landed in a city packed with people. Sound, smell, and color threatened to overpower his senses, and he was forced to rein Weaver's Perception in or risk overwhelm. Judging by his surroundings, he'd arrived in a major Asian city. China, if the flags were anything to go by. Ray was on the ground, straddling Cage the Singer Lifeweaver, a dagger pressed against his throat, while the sea of curious people gave them a wide berth.

"He won't give up the stuff, Luke!" Ray shouted, his voice almost unable to cut through the babble of shouts and the incessant beeping from scooters on the road.

Luke got down on one knee right in front of Cage's face, then leaned in to speak near his face. "Your world is gone, Singer. The void took it."

"Liar!" Cage spat.

"You have Weaver's Echo, don't you?" Luke asked, keeping his voice calm and dangerous-sounding.

Cage gave him a look, then a curt nod.

"You should be able to sense it then."

It took the Singer a long while to confirm, but when he did, tears ran down the sides of his face. "My family."

"Do you want to live?"

Cage's face twisted in rage. "I'll kill you!"

"No, you won't," Ray said, his tone of voice pleasant, almost jolly.

"You are not the last Singer alive. There were some on Earth when it happened. Return our items, and we'll accept your surrender."

"And if I don't?"

"We already told you," Ray said. This time, the mirth was gone, replaced by steel.

Cage wet his lips and looked from Luke to Ray, then to the mass of people still keeping their distance. "A prison cell, then?"

Luke shrugged. "Who knows what'll happen once we reach The Greater System?"

It didn't take the Singer long to decide, and Ray let out an excited whoop when he got his hand on the soul-filled weapon again. Luke was happy to get the robe back, along with the mana-channeling staff and the Mana Orb. He'd missed the items more than he realized, and having them back in his inventory was reassuring. The robe he put on, of course.

Once they returned to guild headquarters with their prisoner in tow, Luke explained everything that had happened. He got a lot of confused looks, with expressions of sadness and righteousness mixed in. Even with everyone's mental block having been wiped away by the Singers, the first question anyone asked him was still.

"So, how many levels did you get?"
