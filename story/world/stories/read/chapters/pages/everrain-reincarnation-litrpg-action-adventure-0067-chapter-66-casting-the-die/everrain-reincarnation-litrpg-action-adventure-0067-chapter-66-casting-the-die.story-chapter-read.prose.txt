Day 155, 06:00 AM

Gila is a genius. Somehow, even twenty days later, that’s the first thought that hits me when I open my eyes in the morning.

Mages never needed to mend their clothes manually by sewing. Even in the unlikely case they mended their robes or whatever, it was done by magic, not by needle and thread. Thus, it stood to reason that mages never needed to develop a spell for threading needles.

I missed out on a level opportunity, possibly multiple, but I won’t miss it when I reset the class and climb through journeyman mage the next time. The secret is to seek out areas and objects mages never touched and develop related spells. Genius.

Unlike Gila, and to a lesser extent Lucy, Millie and Elie are average students, and today should mark the final day of their three hundred hours of lectures. After that, the only obstacle to their path of becoming mages will be the full mastery of their voice and manual motor skills.

I’d tell them to skip it, but Mana Ambivalence is too good to pass up.

“Good morning, my dear students,” I tell the two women as I enter the kitchen where all four of them are gathered, bustling and working while chatting.

Lucy and Gila proved extremely competent and benevolent senior students, helping the two wherever they can.

I acknowledge their greetings and continue with the agenda.

“Today is the final marathon session of me talking about things you probably neither want nor need to know. To reach your next level, after this one, all you have to do is ask me how water vapor rises up even while the rain is falling.”

Gila smiles, and I grin back. “The answer you will receive is, ‘I don’t know,’ and you will get a level for your effort and for my embarrassment. The next level up condition is much tougher…”

I talk until my tongue feels like falling off, and after hours of pointless, self-imposed torture, Millie and Elie finally level up.

Heavens, I just want to stay silent for a week. Maybe do a loop of reading.

Unaware of my disturbing thoughts, my students ask their predefined question, earning themselves another level, before focusing on mastering their fingers, tongues, and vocal cords.

I leave them in the library and sigh in relief as I close the door. Finally, I will have several days of quiet time during which I will take the girls to the dungeon. It’s about time they became full-fledged mages and fought dungeon-spawned bugs on their own.

“Are you girls ready?” I ask them as I enter the kitchen and Gila points at the bundle on the counter.

“Excellent, let’s go.”

We’re dead silent as we pass the mostly clear lawn of Hadriuse’s estate. The thought of meeting Edna dreading the girls, and even I no longer want to see her, fearing what she might have done to herself.

In the back of my mind, I’ve been thinking how I could help cure her condition and restore the grumpy woman I’ve come to know and respect. The answer is probably to become an archmage and revert the damage she had caused herself.

I don’t dare consider the thought as we cross the lawn. She is at the edge of the clearing, but we don’t approach her and head straight for the dungeon instead.

“She scares me,” Lucy admits once we enter the first floor.

“She scares me, too.” I chuckle. “Now, time for you to work on your scanner and offensive spells. We’ll start with the scanner, then I’ll help you become mages, and we’ll try to grab you a couple of levels while we are here, but I seriously doubt you’ll reach level one.” Doubling one’s mana capacity is a lengthy project, and I doubt the girls will achieve it in less than half a year.

Visions of Reality behave oddly down here. Up in the jungle, monsters registered as five to ten percent of their base species, but in the dungeon they are zero percent air, meaning they were conjured, entirely created by magic.

The walls, the trees, everything inside is pure magic with no base components, and while it was obvious, I now wonder to what purpose. Gila and Lucy aren’t absorbing anything as they slay the land lobsters, the amount of mana unleashed is huge, but all of it disperses into the dungeon, soaked up by the walls, so, how does the Guide grant attribute points? The points are undoubtedly real, registered by BSD.

We… I recall how the Guide spoke of itself. A mystery for later, I suppose. First, I will help Edna topple the church and upend the social order. A coup should count as a nation-scale public disturbance. Hopefully, I will advance anarchist once more.

Days pass, the girls advanced to mage class on the first day, and managed the first twenty-seven floors before I had to step in. I forced them to handle the monsters until floor forty, from which I took over, and we cleared the first seventy-five floors in ten days. The return trip took another two days, and we reached the estate with no new problems.

Edna’s tree soldiers line the road. The creatures are indistinguishable from regular flora, and I wouldn’t have noticed them if not for the Visions of Reality. The air feels heavy and oppressive. Lucy and Gila don’t notice it, so I guess it’s me knowing I’m surrounded by monsters, rather than an effect they have on people.

We pass the trees and shrubs to enter the clearing where Edna is holding a different tree, working on expanding her army. She’s basically turned herself into a magic item, spawning soldiers until she can eradicate her enemies, but what then?

We pretend we don’t see each other and lie to the gullible door to get into the mansion.

“We’ll stay here for another day, then we’re heading back to the dungeon. You need more attribute points to invest into physique and more practice with using spells.”

“Griff, how come you’re so good at this, when you started learning magic a handful of days before we did?” Lucy’s question is masked as a compliment, but I understand what she means.

“Edna taught me without sparing any effort, while she mostly left you two to learn on your own. I’ve helped you as much as I could, and you’re not lagging that far behind.” I grace her with a charming smile. “You’re doing excellent. You have become mages in less than half a year. I think Edna took a decade or longer to achieve the same feat. What you have to do now is practice spellcasting as much as possible and perfect the handful of spells you find the most useful.”

The girl blushes in embarrassment, but Gila stands beside her as smug slug pie. She has every reason to feel smug; she outdid me, and I have multiple cheats available.

“I’ll go check on my two other students, and see whether we can upgrade them to journeyman mages.”

I find them in the guest bath, where they are enjoying a swim in a steaming pool. Millie screams and sinks like a stone, while Elie, a sensible woman, just keeps swimming.

“We both reached level eight, and we’re ready to advance our classes. How was the dungeon?”

I like the pragmatic old woman.

“Same as when we visited, but slower.” I shrug, and she nods.

“Gotta give the young ones a chance to learn.”

“What do you mean young ones? How old do I look to you?”

“At least eighty, probably more,” she says as Millie springs up from the water, gasping for breath, her hands covering her bare, rather unimpressive breasts.

“Clever girl,” I say with a smile while Millie screams, “I thought I was gonna drown!”

“Millie,” Elie says, standing up. “The pool is only four feet deep, how could you drown?”

“I’ll be in the library.” I tell them and turn to leave the room.

Millie is stammering something, but I can’t make it out before I close the door. Some pointless nonsense about decency. The poor thing has no idea…

Soon enough, we’re sitting in the library, Millie’s face marbled in red, while Elie and I are talking.

“We’ll start with a simple spell called cleaner. I will demonstrate it slowly twice before casting the spell for real. Watch and learn.”

I teach the spell much like Edna taught me when I notice something. The difference is minor, but I’m certain it’s there. The spell I’m casting with the song and gesturing seems to be using less mana to achieve the same result as the songless and still version.

“You got it?” I ask them, and leave after Elie nods.

Today is day one hundred and sixty-seven since I reincarnated here. Tomorrow morning, early morning on the day one hundred and sixty-eight, I’m gonna tell the girls I’m taking two weeks off before our next dungeon exploration. I have some things to check, and it’s about time I check out what happens with the archmage class.
