Chapter V.LVI (5.56) - Master Brewer’s Combat

Kizu stood across the arena from Ulric. From this angle, he was more aware than ever that only a third of the arena around him was properly filled. Undoubtedly a result of both Hon and Edgeland being cut off from access to the academy. Not to mention the citizens of Tross being too preoccupied with preparations for civil war to worry about children in a tournament.

Though, as Kizu examined the crowds, he realized that last assumption might be wrong. He spotted several very officially uniformed men and women in the front rows with their gazes locked on the two contenders. They seemed to be using this as a recruitment opportunity.

Kizu put that out of his mind and turned his attention back to Ulric. They weren’t about to recruit him to their cause so it hardly mattered how much they stared while he fought.

Unlike Kizu, the older boy never took his eyes off his foe. Even when Arclight stepped up and announced the basic instructions for the match. She rattled off the same instructions Kizu had heard from the previous day’s duels. Don’t leave the arena. Don’t involve the audience. Fight to kill.

When she stepped away, the wood under his feet melded into a new scenery.

Kizu’s boots now rested in a layer of soot, several centimeters deep. Ash fell from the sky in large flakes like snow. A few smoking trees and the skeleton of a burned out shack were all that remained in sight. Other than that, this battlefield was simply himself and Ulric. Which suited Kizu just fine.

With the match commenced, Ulric threw his head back and bellowed a war cry that rattled Kizu to the bone.

Rather than wait around for whatever Ulric planned to throw his way, Kizu jumped forward with the intent to end this quickly. His foot outstretched, he intended to have his heel displace Ulric’s knee and fully sever the limb at the joint.

Unfortunately, things didn’t go quite as smoothly as his last fight in the Tross tundra.

Kizu found himself displaced and stumbling forward. An enhanced two handed fist to his back nearly broke his spine as he toppled over.

Before Kizu hit the soot, he’d already jumped and created a platform for himself a few meters up. He smacked into it, rather than the arena’s floor. Which was great, as from his vantage point he watched Ulric bring a foot down on the place his head would have been. Soot blasted up on impact. With that quantity of force, Kizu had no doubt he very narrowly escaped a swift defeat.

Kizu rolled himself to his feet and dusted off his uniform. His back was bruised, but not broken from Ulric’s earlier strike. Avoiding the brunt of his fall certainly helped minimize that damage.

Ulric clearly had some tattoos applied to him since their last fight. Kizu should have predicted as much, especially since Harvey had gained sleeves of ink since joining up with that friend group. It was a massive mistake to assume this would be the same Ulric he fought back in the Tross tundra.

“Get down here and fight!” Ulric hollered up at him.

Kizu rolled his eyes. Rather than hop down and engage in a fist fight, he stomped his monster foot on his platform and sent a jet of flames at his opponent. He’d carefully crafted a hole in the barrier to funnel and maximize the efficiency of his already powerful flames. He lacked Sene’s supreme control over elemental magic, but combining his two strengths allowed him to simulate the spells of someone like her. The result was a condensed beam that Ulric only barely managed to dodge. Unfortunately for the bigger student, Kizu had the ability to mold his barriers. He shifted the funnel and the jet of flame followed his opponent, blasting soot with fire hot enough to return it aflame.

“Coward!” Ulric shouted as he ran. Kizu’s beam of fire burned his hand the one time he attempted to enhance his resistance, which forced him on the move.

Kizu fully intended to continue standing up above the arena, blasting Ulric until his opponent succumbed to his flames and the match concluded. Sadly, it wasn’t meant to be.

Ulric hurled a potion up at Kizu. Rather than let the potion burst against his hard barrier, Kizu used his newly developed telekinesis to catch the glass vial. Curious, Kizu lifted it up and around to examine the contents. His eyes widened as he processed two things at once. One, the clear liquid inside was definitely not water. And two, the vial was enchanted to detonate.

He hurled it away from himself right as the enchantment triggered. He tried to block it with a barrier, but the liquid proved the antithesis of his shields. It tore through them and splattered on Kizu, which in turn, caused him to lose control of the barrier suspending him.

Antimagic. Somehow Ulric had gotten ahold of liquidized antimagic. Kizu silently cursed to himself as he fell.

His monster leg caught his fall and he pivoted just in time to avoid a blur of a punch from Ulric. The boy moved with a speed that defied nature and Kizu had to jump to the side to avoid a follow-up punch with his left hand.

If Ulric had a strength, it was in his insanely powerful hand-to-hand combat. Kizu was under no illusion that he could match the boy in close quarters.

Ulric reached into a belt pouch and hurled yet another potion in Kizu’s direction. This time, Kizu jumped to the other side of the arena. The bottle curved through the air like it was magically magnetized to Kizu.

Rather than knock it out of the sky or take cover behind one of the burned out husks of obstacles, Kizu took an alternative approach. He jumped directly behind Ulric. In the seconds it took Ulric to locate and face him, the potion bottle shattered against his back.

The potion exploded into a stone tree trunk-like creation that expanded around Ulric and pinned his arms in place with its roots.

If Kizu had known what would happen, he could have capitalized on the grapple. As it was, by the time he fired a spatial blade in Ulric’s direction, his opponent had already flexed and shattered the potion’s grip.

That was a complex brew that had been optimized to have minimal effect if turned against Ulric. This was very clearly not the big student’s usual level of strategy. From everything Kizu knew about Ulric, he was a phenomenal warrior and a subpar tactician. He was basically built for a commander to use. Someone to be led, not someone made to lead.

He also couldn’t enchant or brew even half-decently. That antimagic potion was not crafted by a novice. While Kizu hadn’t ever bothered to brew one like it himself, instead having antimagic barriers take that role for him, he had read about it in one of the advanced textbooks and even researched a few of the ingredients in the past.

So, that raised the question of who, exactly, was behind these attacks? Kizu settled on Emilia. She had the resources to make this happen and had enough of a vendetta against Kizu to make it a reality.

He silently seethed at how this fight was playing out. Without the ability to refresh his blood supply through Anata, every spell cast in this exchange would be a resource drained for his up-coming excursion down into the World Dungeon.

Rather than continue slinging spells at one another, Kizu decided it was his time to pull out some potions of his own. His feet set in the ash and eyes never leaving his opponent, Kizu reached into his spatial storage ring and drew out three vials. He tossed them in the air and they swirled in a circle around him, space juggling them with very little effort on his end.

“I’ve got drinks as well.” Ulric drained yet another potion and Kizu watched his body transform. A shoulder bulged and his back cracked as his form expanded. The academy uniform stretched around him, remaining perfectly fitted despite the metamorphosis. That was great, as it meant the immortality it offered remained active.

Rather than wait the seconds for the transformation to complete, the first of Kizu’s vials shot out at Ulric, the space in front of it snapped forward to offer it extremely accelerated speed. It shattered against Ulric’s chest and burst into flames. The boy’s form was lost in a ball of fire.

Kizu expected that to be the end of it. But he heard nothing from Arclight. The arena remained a shoot covered wasteland.

Then an arm stretched out of the blazing ball of fire. With the speed of lightning, it thumped into Ulric’s chest, the force of the blast blowing not just the fire out, but leaving Ulric on ground untouched by soot.

Kizu was forced to create another force barrier to keep the soot from blasting into his face. He managed to blast the soot down with a wind spell. His vision cleared just in time to see the monstrous form of Ulric pulling something out of his pouch again.

“Slurp on this, dumbass,” Ulric growled in a guttural, inhuman voice as he hurled another vial at Kizu.

The second of Kizu’s three vials shot out and slammed into Ulric’s projectile halfway to him. The glass tinkled as they both shattered. This one of Kizu’s brews was less visually striking than his previous fire potion, though it was significantly more complex as a brew.

Ulric’s face scrunched up in confusion as nothing happened.

It might not be a flashy brew, but this nullification potion required a true master brewer to properly craft. It wasn’t just an antidote, but an instant cancellation of all other brews. He’d wanted it as a precaution for testing out his soul brews and spent far too much time and effort to get it right.

Only then, while Ulric paused, did Kizu finally get a decent look at the older boy after the transformation. It seemed to finally have completed, his growth now stagnated. He now stood three meters tall, over a meter thick, and his skin had turned gray. Kizu recognized this form as similar to that of a troll. He even had tusks like some varieties he’d read about. This polymorph potion very likely required troll blood, something he’d undoubtedly have access to through his friendship with Gob Lucas.

Still, Kizu frowned at the sight. If he’d transformed himself into a troll, then fire should have been extremely effective against him. It was their most notorious weakness and yet he’d brushed it off with ease. And the scrunched up pig-like face was also something he wasn’t familiar with. He was certain Ione would have a ton to say on the species, but all Kizu knew was that whatever Ulric had transformed into, it was a tough beast and needed to be put down.

Ulric howled and charged at him. Every footfall on the ground sent up a cloud of soot and shook the arena.

Rather than slip away, Kizu let his third potion shoot out. The air around him cracked as he snapped space to fire the final vial. It smashed into the side of Ulric’s head, mid-charge and only then did Kizu jump to the side.

Ulric tumbled but righted himself on all fours. The side of his head where the potion had hit bubbled and Kizu smiled as he saw the brew’s effects begin to take effect.

“You know the most dangerous thing about relying on brews for combat?” Kizu called out to his opponent.

If Ulric retained the capability to speak in his current full transformation, he certainly didn’t let it show. He let out a guttural scream and charged again at Kizu.

Simply jumping to the side again. Kizu crossed his arms and leaned up against the burned out tree. This match was already over.

“One of the very first rules brewers learn is to never mix brews when you lack knowledge of the ingredients. I might not know your species, so the exact recipe eludes me. But I spent quite a bit of time researching polymorph potions in our first semester. I know the base ingredients.”

Ulric charged again. He was limping now, half his body rapidly shrinking.

This time Kizu jumped himself so he sat on the horizontal wooden beam of the burned out building. It creaked under his weight, but didn’t give out.

“And, if I know the ingredients, then I know exactly how to twist them for catastrophic results.”

Ulric’s body was somewhere caught between a hulking beast and a mouse. Half of his body was the length of Kizu’s finger while the other half remained the former transformation. A long tail curled out from the older student’s butt and he tripped on it as he scrambled in Kizu’s direction.

“Your potions are top notch quality,” Kizu said from his perch on the burned building. He watched as Ulric dragged himself through the soot. “Whoever brewed them, knew what they were doing. They might even be a master’s work. However, I am the best brewer this academy has to offer. You seriously thought to bring me down with potions? I don’t know if you’re stupid or arrogant. Probably both. Either way, you’re an incompetent brewer who doesn’t understand the art. You’ll never be my equal on this field. Now, do us both a favor and end this farce by dying.”

Kizu chopped an arm through the open air. With the motion, a slice of space cut through the arena’s battlefield.

Ulric’s malformed body fell apart in two chunks, right down the middle. Kizu only had a brief moment to marvel at the effectiveness of his spatial slice and note with no small level of revulsion the insides of the half-formed creature Ulric had become. Then the arena faded and he was greeted by the roar of acceptance from the spectators.
