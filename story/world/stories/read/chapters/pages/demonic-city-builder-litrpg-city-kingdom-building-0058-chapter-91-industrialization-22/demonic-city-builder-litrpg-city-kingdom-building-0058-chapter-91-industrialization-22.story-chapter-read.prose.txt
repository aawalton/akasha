That, like last time, was a lot. More than a lot, really.

Kairon had three new building projects—of which the workshop and whispering tethers seemed of particular interest—that he was more than itching to try out.

Not to mention the whispering hall and smeltery.

However, time was moving and Kairon needed to act. After all, he had an exploit he was set on trying, and it was now or never.

Staring at the blinking notification, he willed his sigil to move.

With a slurp, it slid from within him, spilling onto the ritual and splashing over the menu.

As planned, he changed the single line of text in regards to the ring district density. Specifically, the percentage of forge. The district he felt his city needed the most. To enter a new age. And what better way than for an exploit of demonic forgery to do so?

Hurriedly, Kairon selected “Y”.

The golden sigil solidified, hardening into the menu text itself.

It worked! Thirty-three percent forge had become one hundred thirty-three percent.

Stepping back, Kairon hobbled back towards the tower as quickly as he could manage. In the corner of his vision, that same line of text remained.

As he walked, the city lord kept his head mostly down. It wasn’t until he reached the steps that he saw some oddities with the corner of his eye.

“Wait. Did the tower actually… Grow?”

A quick glance made it seem as if it did. There were several new, smaller towers on either side of the original citadel one. Great windows with a faint purple light were visible, with what appeared to be new rooms.

“Oh. So that’s what you were trying to tell me.”

Kairon wanted to explore just what had changed in the tower, when another notification hit his vision.

He rushed his way up the larger steps and into the tower’s entryway. Now that he came in from the outside, it became clear that even the inside had changed. The interior of the tower’s entryway had become grander, with arching stonework and newly embossed architecture.

Kairon admired the new changes as he made his way inside, finally collapsing on his butt with a sigh of relief. “Whew! Quite the walk! When one leg’s frozen, that’s really a long trek.”

Ranks of lined up warriors, dozens of brood, and several demons all stared at him incredulously.

“Just what were you doing now?” asked Sythara.

“Oh nothing! Just a little experiment.”

“Oh? Is that why this place has changed as it has?” she asked.

“Yeah, I noticed that. The tower’s bigger on the outside, and it looks like it grew some new buildings. We’ll go check everything out here in a minute.”

“It’s grown inside too.” Sythara pointed at freshly made sets of stairs and doorways.

“It—” began Kairon, only to stop speaking as something did finally change with the pact notification. The message began to wobble. “Oh! Yes, here we go! Time to see how this pans out. It’s moving.”

“What do you mean it’s moving, child?”

He waved a claw tiredly. “Not the tower. I mean the pact notification from the plane is moving around. Doing its thing. Just give me a moment.”

Whatever she said back Kairon hardly heard. He was far more interested in the notification that popped into existence.

Kairon pumped his fist in the air. “Success! The draft pool is officially expanding!” His fang tooth grin had grown, that familiar scent of demonic blood in the water growing.

“It’s what?!” Sythara placed her hand on his cheek, the changes in the tower seemingly all but forgotten at his proclamation. “Child. What did you just say?”

“I said it’s expanding the search. I pumped a lot of forge juice into the query, and now it needs to find a target worth the squeeze, so to speak.”

“What do you mean by that? The only way it could expand the draft pool is if it’s trying to find a candidate with more aspect than… No. Child. This… This… Alteration. You used it, didn’t you? The same one we planned for my research comprehension on the tier two brand?”

“Don’t worry—it’s only temporary. Once the ritual completes, I will have my alteration power back. We should have enough of those sigils left to power it for our tier two brand strategy. More once Boney succeeds.”

Assuming he did, that was. But Kairon was confident.

“Child. That is not what worries me. You don’t understand. If this ring ritual of yours works like I believe, and you’ve used the alteration as you’ve described to me…” Suddenly Sythara’s face grew a shade more pale than it’d been before.

“Listen. I understand the concerns, and agree to their validity. But, I spoke with Nod. I ensured all imps were accounted for and prepared far away from the new ring. Which is why we are all here, together!”

The compliance imp was nearby, still monitoring the imps just outside the tower that were collecting from the finished ritual.

“No, not that either. Child. I do not care about the little ones.”

An incredulous yelp of anger came from Nod in the background. Sythara, however, paid it no mind. “Do you understand? Just. What number did you make? And of what aspect?”

“Forge! And I put oh… A hundred and thirty-three percent.”

Sythara audibly groaned.

Vorathrex and Bean had joined them, their expressions comically in contrast. Vorathrex with something between consideration and terror, and Bean with a goofy, childlike grin.

“Did you hear that?” she asked them.

“Yep!” Bean gave his master a cheery smile. “I don’t really know what it means, but it sounds fun, master!”

Vorathrex sighed. “Fledgling. This strategy of yours… I understand the logic. You want a more powerful forge ally to help build weapons for our warriors and tools for…” he waved at Shiny, “the little ones. But if you made an aspect over a hundred percent… What does that mean? I do not know much on this topic.”

Kairon nearly dismissed the notification in the excitement, before catching on the final word.

Entities.

“Huh. That is… interesting.”

It tickled at his brain. Before he could figure out why, Sythara got his attention.

“Child! This worries me. I do not like this feeling I am getting… It is ominous, child. Ominous. The Plane can not be happy about what you are doing.”

“Sythara, I hardly think that the plane is going to cheat us out of giving us a worthy reward for our third ring. Vorathrex came from our first, and you our second. And you’ve both been incredibly helpful to our growth.”

Vorathrex turned, gesturing quickly at Kairon. “I have learned to trust your judgement on these things, fledgling. But I find myself strangely uncomfortable with not knowing your… tactics. Explain. What is going to occur?”

“I have used my alteration to artificially inflate how much forge aspected energy the plane believes is in our newest ring expansion. As a result, I believe the plane is going to give our city a forge candidate of extreme resonance. One that we can use to help jumpstart our smeltery and crafting!”

“Child.” Sythara was practically clawing at her own face as she spoke. “That is all well and good, but do you even really understand what it means if your corruption for any aspect is over a hundred percent?”

“I assume it means The Board, Sythara.”

She barked out a laugh. “So you knew. Gah. Yes of course you did. It’s probably why you did this, you foolish child.”

Vorathrex frowned. “Please. Can one of you explain this to me. I am still lost.”

She sighed. “You see, pit dweller, of all the demons in existence, only those on The Board have reached such a corruption threshold in any single aspect. And the child here is trying to make a pact with one of them!”

Vorathrex gaped.

Kairon, however, just grinned. “Sounds great, doesn’t it! Though I’m expecting something a touch more… restricted. A subpact, according to the compendium.”

“Subpact?” asked Vorathrex, still partially in disbelief.

“Basically a sliver of one of these Board members really,” explained Kairon, rubbing his claws together. “Mind you, a sliver of forge incarnate would still be quite the sliver, don’t you think?”

Right on cue, a new notification appeared in front of him.

“A match! We’ve got him! The Creator he’s called. Sythara, I did it! Oh, this is going to be good…”

“The Creator?” Sythara wrapped her tendrils around her protectively.

Vorathrex made a sound like something between a squeak and a moan.

Kairon’s claws stopped rubbing. That wasn’t something he’d exactly expected. He thought this creator might be like him—itching to find out who would call upon him.

Apparently not.

“Ah. It seems we’ve hit a bit of a snafu.”

Behind him, Vorathrex let out a heavy breath.

“We got lucky, fledgling,” rumbled the giant. “Based on what you have said, if The Creator did not accept this, there is none who can. We may have missed out on a reward for the nest, but I think this is for the best.”

Sythara began to visibly calm, seemingly coming to the same conclusion. “Yes, child. The pit demon is correct.” She actually looked a bit impressed that he’d comprehended the explanation.

“It’s still going.”

“What is?” asked Vorathrex.

“Our pact search.”

“How? If you are right,” began Vorathrex, “and only The Creator has that much forge attunement, then what is The Plane going to do here?”

“Well…” Kairon fished the parchment back out of his cape and roved over the text with his claw, finding the bit he was after. “Hmm. Two things are relevant here. First, the plane has to find us a match. Second, there is only one piece of absolute criteria for the target of our pact.”

“Which is?” asked Vorathrex.

“Which is that a pactee’s primary aspect meets or exceeds the ring ritual threshold. Meaning it has to be over whatever weighted threshold there is. I don’t know if that’s the one hundred thirty-three percent I gave, or some kind of weighted average, but…”

“But the creator already rejected your foolishness!” snapped Sythara. She also gave Vorathrex a dirty look. “And you! What’s gotten into you?! You’re normally the sensible one.”

He cocked an eyebrow at her.

Sythara coughed awkwardly. “For a pit demon, that is,” she rushed to add.

Vorathrex shrugged. “I am just thinking. That is all.”

“Yes. That is all,” said Sythara. The look she gave him made it clear what she thought about his thinking.

“And you!” Sythara spun about, pointing a finger at Kairon. “Child! You have wasted this opportunity for an interesting new subject for my research!”

“Just how have I done that, my demoness? I told you the query is still working.”

“Gah! The only demon that is eligible for this inflated forge aspect has rejected you. So this is all a moot point!”

“The only demon,” Kairon said. Then it hit him.

The only demon.

His eyes went from the parchment in his claw to the line of text still floating in the corner of his vision.

Querying Other Entities. Entities. Not demons.

And the page in front of him said pactee. Not demon. Nowhere—not in the criteria, not in the footnotes, not in the compendium—did it say the pact had to be with a demon.

“Oh… I… I see,” Kairon said slowly, wheels turning in his mind. “Only criteria one is absolute. A pactee’s primary aspect must meet or exceed the threshold. That’s it. That’s the whole rule.” The city lord looked up, a naughty grin on his face. “Sythara. The pact doesn’t have to be with a demon!”

Right as the realization slammed home, the final notifications appeared.

Kairon cocked his head sideways.

“Oh my.” Unlike last time, there was no choice. He thought he might still get other options. Not that he would have chosen them anyway.

It seemed both he and The Plane had the same idea in mind.

That said, Kairon had absolutely no idea what an Infernal Industrializer was. But judging by the sounds of it, and the fact it belonged to The Creator, he surmised it must be quite the tool.

And soon, it would be his tool.

Although there was one clear risk. This tool he got was The Creator’s. The same Board member that had just rejected his subpact offering.

He couldn’t be happy about this.

Then again, he was on another Plane of existence. That had to make any counteractions difficult at best.

Something Kairon would need to think more about.

Meanwhile, the others hadn’t noticed his change in attitude. Sythara, for her part, let out a scoff. “That is fine. Nothing like that is likely to exist. Although…” She paused, considering. “The plane is a big place… I… I don’t know.”

It was at this point that Bean decided he was being ignored too much. “Hey! I don’t really understand all of this, but I did hear mention of this big bad creator. Are you saying we aren’t gonna get to meet him?”

“No. The Creator rejected the child’s gambit,” said Sythara.

Bean deflated at that. “Oh man…”

Their bickering even got the attention of both Shiny and Nod—each of whom had now joined the growing gathering of the city elites.

“Master… Does this creator make more brood?” asked Shiny.

“No. Sound like nightmare… More like litigation creator.” Nod let out a little huff.

Shiny wasn’t so convinced. “If creator big… Maybe big customer too? Master! Get him back! Call him! Tell we give special deal! But…” Shiny hesitated. “Limited time only.”

Sythara looked horrified. “No it doesn’t—gah!” Sythara threw her hands up. “This is pointless. The subpact was rejected. So now, the child has lost the chance to gain a new ally for the third ring.”

“Actually… Not quite.” Kairon gave a tiny, naughty little smile. “It’s a bit more…” He sucked in a breath, smacking his lips. “Complicated.”

“What?” hissed Sythara.

“Fledgling?” Vorathrex was looking much less certain now.

In the back of the tower, a high pitched giggle echoed off the walls. Scampering forward, Hush’s cloaked form climbed atop Vorathrex’s back, pointing one trembling finger in the distance.

“It’s comingggg,” said Hush.

“What? What’s coming? Fledgling?” Vorathrex tried to swat the void imp from his shoulder.

Sythara on the other hand put both her palms on Kairon’s shoulder, squeezing hard. “What does he mean? Child? What does he mean!?”

“It seems that The Creator himself is not the only entity to which enough forge aspected corruption has accumulated. Another was found.”

“Another?” asked Sythara.

“Mhm. Something called The Creator’s Infernal Industrializer.”

Sythara just gaped at him, sapphire eyes going entirely dim for a moment. “What the fuck is that?!”

And, for the third time since coming to this world, a beam of energy erupted from the demonic city itself, stretching from city to sky.

BOOM.

The column was as thick as the citadel itself, an orange line stretching from the forge district and blasting an enormous hole in the clouds above.

Brood imps fell to their knees, wrath imps crouched on instinct to hold their balance, void imps prostrated themselves and revelled in the feeling, and Kairon shifted his view immediately to Sovereign Eye.

Below, he saw his city. The newly made third ring, enormous in size compared to the first two. Each of them holding twelve building slots, for a total of thirty-six new plots.

That was great.

What was of slightly more concern—or excitement from Kairon’s perspective—was the enormous eruption of energy that had swallowed three entire building slots. That space made up an entire quarter of the new forge district. Beneath it, a structure was rising.

And it was taking up all three spaces simultaneously.

Kairon whistled softly.

“In regards to just who, or more accurately what this Infernal Industrializer is… Well, I dare say we’re about to find out.”
