Olethros and I took several slow steps back to consider the situation. The over-armored twins made no move to follow, but the ereborite jewels embedded in their foreheads pulsed slowly.

“Keep going,” Prince Mito ordered, forcing what little he had into the words. “There is no value in freeing me. Go and live on.”

Misty dark animus began to flow around Olethros, the air around us growing cold. “Lucky for you, my partner thrives on effort justification in cases like this.”

Prince Mito recoiled slightly. “Demon!”

Olethros turned toward me, though his sightless gaze lingered a bit on the twins first. “I think you should introduce us. Otherwise, I will be further offended by his mode of address.”

Was now really the time for manners?

“Your Highness,” I called carefully. “I would like you to meet your source material—”

“Captain?!” Mito blurted. “No—merely someone pretending to be you.”

Though it was the standard reaction, I couldn’t blame his suspicions, since he’s dating said someone.

“No reaction even to your voice,” Olethros mused, further ignoring His Highness. “Relias’s puppets don’t seem to have enough strings.”

I huffed. “Criticize him for all his shortcomings later. Right now, we have to figure out a way to save Florian, Mattias, and Prince Mito!”

Olethros straightened, declaring to Prince Mito, “Observe. Only Rachel herself would bother with a triple play in tediousness for your sake.”

Mito stood up slowly, wispy and trembling. “Yet th-those are the names they use when they’re up to no good…”

Right. Florian and Mattias were just supposed to be hired hands on deck under the command of Captain Morin, fake seafaring slayer of the dreaded Karkinanthos.

“Uh… Well, that’s who they told me they were back on the ship,” I answered, hoping to bolster his faith in me. “But I never did ask—”

“Marcus and Fabian,” Olethros whispered.

I leaned over. “Mattias for Marcus and Florian for Fabian?”

“No,” Olethros said louder. “It’s the other way around.”

“That makes absolutely no sense!” I shouted, stamping my foot. “Now I’ll never keep them straight—Mattias or Florian or Marcus or Fabian—”

Both twins twitched, and I immediately cut off my rant. Their heads swiveled back and forth almost quizzically, then fell before slowly returning to a neutral position.

“Shout again,” Olethros urged quietly. “Without the names this time.”

Hmm? Oh!

“Anyway, we’re here to rescue you all,” I yelled. “And I won’t take no for an answer!”

No reaction from the twin guardians.

“Names resonate,” Olethros concluded. “Though we can’t tell them apart. One at a time?”

I nodded, drawing my sword and pointing. “Left one.”

“Allow me.” Olethros translocated, the trailing buzz echoing throughout the chamber.

The left twin’s jewel flared as Olethros appeared beside him, and his sword came up fast. Olethros vanished as the blade cleaved, reappearing farther away from the twin. With a steely shudder, the twin lunged and sliced at the demon king, the greatsword in his hand whistling through empty air. Olethros toyed with him, apparating and disappearing around him swiftly as he drew him away. It was almost a teasing dance, with Olethros acting coy as he darted between pillars, all while the twin chased him, moving faster and faster to slice at his prey.

I summoned my pink aura, waiting for an opening. Will shifted in my grip, becoming lighter and leaner. A faint, smug shimmer ran up his saber blade.

Oh, so you think this is just practice, do you?

He sparkled faintly, settling his curved handle in my palm as if it had always belonged there.

The right twin remained unresponsive, save for a slow but regular pulse from its jewel.

Is it waiting for something?

Olethros’s backwards fades were shallower with each shift, and the left twin committed to a heavy, downward arc with his blade. Switching to a sideways feint, his Majesty conned him into slicing left, leaving him to bury his sword several inches in the cracks of a thick, stone pillar.

I moved in.

The twin wrenched at his sword, but the pillar did not care.

A sharp tap with the saber to Lefty’s shield triggered a flurry of wild bashes. It seemed the twin could only focus on one arm at a time. However, he seemed to instinctively know to protect his forehead jewel, bringing the shield in front of his head every time my saber lashed out to test. It was lighter than I expected—faster as well, the blade flowing rather than fighting the air with each slash.

Will seems to be enjoying this a little too much.

I took over as the twins’ main adversary, forcing him to fight me away with the shield. While he wouldn’t let go of his stuck sword, he did manage to fend me off for a little while. I was, however, just practicing at Will’s insistence. Regardless, Lefty’s shield was taking damage, with deep slices sparkling a faint rose.

Olethros must have decided we had practiced enough. He materialized behind Lefty, his staff already in mid-swing when it suddenly stopped being a staff. His signature scythe-style weapon reformed in a single, fluid motion. The gold crescent buried itself into the back of Lefty’s armor with a screeching hiss. Nora’s blessing flooded blue and bright along the blade, locking Lefty into place. The last thing to move was his shield, frozen in mid-swing.

I released Will as my hand shot for the jewel.

A blizzard filled my mind.

Thoughtless winds howled on endlessly, throwing cold snow in all directions.

“I’m just going to start saying names!” I shouted. “Let me know which one is yours. Fabian? Florian? Marcus. Mattias! Whoever you think you are! Know that you’re not this right now!”

The wind screeched even louder, tossing sleet into my face.

“It’s me! Rachel, Rae, Captain Lightbringer! Whatever you want to call me at this point!”

A white tornado started to bear down on me.

“Wait! Let’s try Ann! It’s Ann! Remember Ann, the merciful mercenary!”

“Ann?” came a ghostly reply, as snowflakes froze in place. “No, never trust Ann… Except…”

It’s working!

I took a deep breath. “I should have let that priest drown back then. I’m sorry now that I didn’t!”

Though knowing Relias, he had probably lingered in the water just to play the pity card.

The voice came back stronger. “You wouldn’t do something like—You! You evaded Naught twice?!”

The last vestiges of the blizzard howled itself out with a heavy sigh, and I found myself watching Mattias’s—er—Fabian’s armor melt off like liquid quicksilver. Will was hovering expectantly near my hand, making it easy to re-arm myself.

Fabian’s skin was a sickly, pale gray; his hair slicked back with dirty sweat. “Always… coming through for me, Ann…” he rasped, trying to catch his breath. He then slumped, dropping hold of his stuck sword and shield before hitting the floor.

“Merely unconscious,” Olethros noted. “I believe a—”

A loud, high-pitched hiss cut him off.

It had started below my threshold of hearing, a vibration that pulsed through the dungeon floor as I became aware of it. Then it rose, the whistling of air mixing with a monstrous scream.

I turned to the source.

The right twin—Marcus—still with weapons in each hand, tried to claw at his sightless visor. The dark jewel within it cracked, dark light leaking as it dissolved.

For an irrational moment, I hoped that whatever connections twins had freed him as well.

I was woefully wrong.

Marcus’s armor began to rattle, his screams louder now as he tried to drop sword and shield.

“Fabian!” he screamed. “Fabian, my brother!”

“He’s alive!” I shouted back. “Fabian’s still alive!”

A whirl of gold ignited around Marcus, but it quickly fluctuated with an erratic darkness.

“Fabian!” he kept screaming.

He wasn't coming back from the final stage of animus overload.

“Mito—” I started.

Olethros was already gone.

He reappeared between Marcus and the chained prince in the same instant the tainted holy knight’s sword blazed to life. Marcus struck at everything within range; a pillar took the first strike, the resulting crack running up the length of it to the ceiling.

Dust rained down in warning as Olethros’s newly formed barrier took the next several hits.

Marcus drew back for another swing, inhuman screams still coming from his visor as his unbalanced aura increased. It was both brighter and darker all at once, incompatible yet building with turmoil.

Will shifted back into a longsword, knowing my budding skill with the saber just wouldn’t cut it.

I raised Will in challenge, knowing I had to draw whatever attention Marcus had left.

“Fight me, Marcus. Avenge thy brother!”
