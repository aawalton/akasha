The amber-like ocean shallowed as Stormfalcon neared Clinmere, when the island screened their horizon and the blushing sky of the dusk, the high tide cheering the onset of the full moons, and the beach embracing the waves breaking on it. Yet the shattered planks rocking on the waters, confronting the advance of the warship, the bloodied feathers drifting beside the mauled corpses, winged and wingless, and the wrecked front-half of a ship jammed in the sands of Clinmere marred the scenic vista.And the Seroyotes met their visit with hostility amidst the aftermath of a carnage.

“Sir Abelard, do you intend to fight us?” Ewan asked, hovering away from the top deck with the four in tow, glancing at the armed men behind Abelard, dried blood smearing their ruffled wings.

Abelard heaved a sigh then waved his men back. “I didn’t know it was you, Sir Ewan, I apologize for the rude welcome,” he said, slouching, rubbing the scabbed gash on his cheek. “As you can see, we’ve had busy few months. We were already low in able numbers, and the raid dwindled us more.”

“What happened here?” Ewan asked, landing on the beach with him.

“Let’s talk inside, your house is well preserved, cleaned regularly,” Abelard said, and gestured politely, matching Ewan’s strides as the protective cover of the island received them.

“Nyte, park Stormfalcon nearby, maintain the shield,” Ewan said in the bracelet and the five walked in with Abelard.

…..

“We just wanted to keep up with others, we just hoped to be not left behind,” Abelard said, when they sat in the small yard, by the pond, as the fishes stirred the water. “Alas, it cost us dearly.” He stared at the glass of water in his hand, the ripples in it mirroring his sigh.

“Sir Abelard, I won't mince my words,” Ewan said. “Those children are hot potatoes; you basically called the raid yourself by leaking such information.”

“We saw the deaths that happened because of them on our way,” Kidd said. “You shouldn’t take this lightly.”

“I know, I wish I knew before though,” Abelard said, chugging a mouthful. “I’ve already extracted all the information from the child and sent him to Morinfair with proper rituals, hopefully, it’ll calm things down.”

“Depends on the intention of who leaked it,” Stefan said. “If he wants to put you down, you’ll have another raid on your doorsteps.”

“About that,” Ewan said, looking at Abelard. “I need some explanation, Sir Abelard. My heart was impaled by Shadowfarer, the one who lives by that hall of history. I barely survived. Would you reckon he was acting alone, or was that the Seroyotes’ intention?” The five stared at Abelard, and the air thickened with malice; a wrong answer could spark the storm. Though the Seroyotes’ had the home advantage, Ewan’s side weighed heavier with the five Step-1 and his Astylinds; even with the addition of Shadowfarer, they could paint the island red if the conflict worsened.

“What?” Abelard said in alarm with a startle, sitting straight, spilling the water on his thigh. “This is a misunderstanding, Sir Ewan. You’re the savior of our species, we could never mean you any harm. There must be something wrong, please believe me.”

“If you really don’t know anything about it, then Shadowfarer is your culprit,” Lance said. “He’s the one who probably leaked your information.”

“When he came at us, his intention was to kill only Ewan,” Stefan said. “He didn’t wish to see your curse broken.”

Abelard widened his eyes, and his chest heaved.  “They want to rebel…,” he said under his breath.

“They? There are more than one Shadowfarer?” Ewan asked.

“Please forgive me, I must go,” Abelard said, thudding the glass down at the edge of the table with a trembling hand, and zoomed away, ripping the shrubs in his trail—Kidd caught the glass as the recoiling wind knocked it off.

“Are we staying?” Nana asked Ewan. “We’ll get involved in their fight.”

“We were already involved when he stabbed me, or maybe since the day I accepted their deal…,” Ewan said, and as the island seethed with rumbling forests and quaking earth, he took a deep breath. “Wait for a chance, and when you see it, kill the Shadowfarer, no matter who gets in our way.”

…..

Yet, they anticipated in vain, and their preparation for the enemy who dwelled in the shadows flopped for the day when Abelard returned with a gloomy frown, the winged men scurrying around the island.

“I deeply apologize for what happened.” Abelard bowed to Ewan. “It was my oversight and my indulgence that caused this. I thought to treat them as equals, I thought to rid Clinmere of its hierarchy…,” he said. “The freedom I gave them was for the prosperity of Clinmere, yet they pushed us to extinction…”

“What happened?” Ewan asked.

“They ran away when you all came, and they…killed the newborns in the cradle,” Abelard said, his eyes bloodshot, and his paled knuckles shivering. “Only a few survived…we are doomed.”

“Do you think they cast the curse?” Stefan asked.

“I was doubting the previous Seigneur of Fallsard at first…but this proved otherwise,” Abelard said.

“Sir Abelard, I presume we’re on the same page on this matter,” Ewan said. “I want them dead, and you want the same. So, I was hoping you could give us some insight on their path, what would hurt them the most.”

Abelard nodded with lost eyes. “The winged royalties carry the light as Lightfarers, while the commons without the feathers live in the depth as Shadowfarers,” he mumbled. “The light of our spells overwhelms the shadow of theirs, it keeps them in check…kept them in check.”

“What if we don’t have light spells?” Lance asked.

“Then fire spell would work the best,” Abelard said.

“Good enough,” Ewan said. “I bought some new ones recently; they can be my test subjects when we meet again.”
