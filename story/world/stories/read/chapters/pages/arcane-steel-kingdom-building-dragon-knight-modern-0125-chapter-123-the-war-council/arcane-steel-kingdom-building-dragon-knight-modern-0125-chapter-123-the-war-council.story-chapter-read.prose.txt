The flight deck of The Aegis was a hive of controlled, mechanical frenzy. A heavily guarded Kantonov K-225 Heavy Lifter shrieked out of the cloud layer, its six turbines howling in protest as it flared for a vertical landing. Flanking it like predatory birds were two Centurion C-130 Gunships, their weapon pods tracking invisible threats, while a modified Revenant tanker drifted above, its optical camouflage shimmering like heat haze. A tight escort of six SF-21 Strike Fighters and four W-29 Wyverns formed a protective cage around the heavy transport.

It was a staggering amount of airpower dedicated to a single cargo plane, but it was carrying two mature Dungeon Cores.

I stood on the deck as the K-225’s massive nose hinged open, the ramp hitting the deck plates with a solid clang. General Goliath walked down the ramp in his standard command armor. Beside him, gliding just above the deck in a shimmering sphere of condensed water, was Aquarius. The Water Dragon looked exhausted but proud, his scaled hide radiating a fierce, damp heat.

Bob stopped a few paces away and snapped off a crisp Legion salute, his fist thudding against his chest plate.

"The extraction was successful, Lord Commander," he reported, his voice carrying the deep, gravelly tone of a man who had spent the last two days fighting through hell. "Both targets secured."

I returned the nod, relief washing over me. "I must thank you for all that you do for me, and for my family, Bob. It truly means a lot that you would travel across an ocean, walk into a horde phase, and drag these out for me."

Bob was taken aback. The stern lines of his face softened into genuine surprise at the informal praise. "As a knight of House Wight, I have taken an oath to serve and protect the bloodline, my Lord. You do not need to thank me. Instead, it is I who should apologize. Again, I could not get there in time. When the alert sounded... I was helpless."

I walked over and patted his giant, armored shoulder. "It’s the thought that counts. And hopefully, soon we won't have to worry about distance. I am working on decoding the Lumina teleportation network. Long-range transit might not be a monopoly for the angels much longer."

A spark of hope lit up Bob's eyes. I knew the guilt he carried. He had been absent when Wighthelm burned, absent when his brother was enslaved, and he had been a continent away when Ela was threatened. The idea of instantaneous travel was a balm to a knight who felt he was never where he was needed most.

As we turned to head inside, our path was blocked by a familiar, chaotic force of nature.

Lyra was marching down the corridor, her silver pigtails bouncing. Behind her, partially hidden by her small frame, was Ela. The contrast between the two girls was still jarring—one vibrating with secure, insulated joy, the other moving with the hesitant, hyper-vigilant caution of a stray cat.

Ela peaked out from behind Lyra, her wide sapphire eyes landing on the towering, armored figure of the General. She shrank back slightly.

"Don't be scared," Lyra coaxed, grabbing Ela's hand and pulling her forward. "Sir Bob is very nice. He just looks gruff. Like a grumpy bear."

I hid a smile. "Bob, this is Ela. My cousin, and the reason for the alert that went out. Our family has adopted her. She is my sister now."

Bob didn't hesitate. He dropped to one knee, the heavy armor protesting with a loud whine of servos. He lowered his head until he was eye-level with the two small girls.

"Your father was one of the finest Dragon Knights House Wight has ever produced," Bob said, his voice dropping to a gentle, rumbling murmur. "It was an honor to ride alongside him into battle."

Ela stared at him. Her eyes widened, realization dawning. He was the other Dragon Knight her new father had spoken of. She stepped out of Lyra's shadow, her fear replaced by a desperate, hopeful curiosity.

"Do you... do you also have a dragon friend?" she asked, her voice a meek, reedy whisper. "Can I meet them?"

Bob's face broke into a rare, gentle smile. "Aquarius would love that," the giant knight rumbled.

I nodded, watching the interaction. I had noticed over the years studying the lore and living with the beasts that Dragon clans had distinct personalities, almost like genetic traits.

The Green Clan, stewards of nature, were largely indifferent, spending decades sleeping in the forests, uncaring of the world's politics. The Gold Clan of the Imperium was notoriously prideful and arrogant, viewing humans as lesser beings. The Red Clan was passionate, aggressive, and prone to violent outbursts.

The Blue Clan—the Azure dragons—were uniquely adaptable, fiercely loyal, and strangely fond of small children. Cygnus’s tolerance of Lyra’s antics was a clan trait. They were the most eccentric of the apex predators.

I looked down at Lyra, the obvious orchestrator of this little interception. She had an agenda.

I ruffled her hair, messing up the neat braids Patricia had likely spent half an hour perfecting. "What do you need, fire-cricket?"

She swatted my hand away, huffing in annoyance as she tried to smooth her hair back down. She placed her hands on her hips and glared up at me. "Brother. It has been three months."

"And?"

She deployed her ultimate weapon: the sad, impossibly cute, weaponized puppy-dog eyes. "Please give me back my crayons. I know enough time has passed. I asked Patricia, and she said my sentence was served."

I blinked. She was right. We were well past her twelve-week grounding for the unauthorized "Big Boom" incident on the flight deck. In the chaos of the espionage wars, the fleet mobilization, and the rescue of our cousin, I had completely forgotten about her art supplies.

"You promise not to cause any trouble?" I asked, raising an eyebrow. "Or any explosions?"

She stuck out her pinky finger, her expression solemn. "I promise not to cause any trouble."

We locked pinkies. It was a binding contract. I reached into my spatial storage ring—a handy little perk of high-level magitech—and pulled out two pristine boxes of colored wax crayons. I handed one to Lyra, and, seeing her watching with quiet longing, I handed the second box to Ela.

Ela looked at the box of colors as if I had just handed her a chest of diamonds.

Lyra squealed with delight, grabbing Ela's hand. They took off down the corridor, an escort of nervous Legionary cadets scrambling to keep up.

"I need to assign a dedicated detail to Ela," I muttered to myself, watching them go. I turned to Bob. "Get some rest, General. Things are about to get hectic. I’m holding a war council in an hour. After you've recovered, you'll be deploying to old Aerthos."

Bob thumped his chest plate. "I am fit to continue, my Lord. There is no need to delay on my account."

The Combat Information Center was a cavern of blue light and tense expectation.

Nyxia, Patricia, and Mirelle were already waiting at the central holographic table. The rest of the High Command joined us via encrypted video feeds, their faces projected onto floating screens around the room.

Valen was currently submerged in the Hydra off the coast. Theramore paced the bridge of The Axiom. Captain Ivela commanded The Vengeance from the air. Ivela was a rising star, a brilliant tactician who had earned enough merits during the coastal bombardment to secure her first star. We were simply waiting for the formal ceremony after the campaign. Right now, she was effectively acting as the General of the Air Force.

"Now that we are all here, let's begin," I said, stepping up to the console. I expanded the holographic display, shifting it from a regional view to a massive overview of the entire active battlefield.

The map was a mess of shifting borders and troop movements.

"We need to move fast to consolidate our gains," I announced. I tapped the console, and two massive, slow-moving icons appeared on the board. "These are the Crawlers. The Colossus-class mobile fortresses."

I pulled up their schematics, showing their thick armor, their articulated spider-like legs, and the massive, empty reactor housings in their centers.

"The two Dungeon Cores General Goliath just recovered will be powering these. It will give us the strategic footprint we need to hold our newly acquired territories. The Mage Towers of House Black can defend the southern inland borders, but the north—the old lands of House Wight—are wide open. We don't have Dragon knights to anchor those lands anymore. We will use steel instead."

"The components for the Crawlers have already been fabricated," I continued, highlighting supply routes crossing the ocean. "They will be transferred from The Aegis and the Obsidian Dominion via heavy lifters and cargo barges. General Mirelle, you will oversee the transit procedure. The 2nd Fleet will provide security for the convoys."

Mirelle nodded sharply. "It will be done, Lord Commander."

I shifted the view back to the mainland. A massive swarm of red icons was gathering in the center of the Hegemony, slowly beginning to march north.

"We have an army of several million Cinderfall infantry and heavy cavalry headed our way," I stated grimly. I adjusted the map to show the locations of the Black Mage Towers, currently raining shadow-fire on localized Cinderfall loyalists. "From the overview, it appears they are deliberately avoiding the Mage Towers, taking a longer, more arduous route through the mountain passes to reach us. They are not the main threat."

I zoomed in on the Cinderfall capital. The projection showed a massive, golden icon resting atop the palace dome. It was static, but it burned with a terrifying intensity.

"The Tier 9 Mother Phoenix," I said, the words heavy in the silent room. "It is awake. It is waiting. In the worst-case scenario, if it launches a direct attack against our forward lines, Bob and I will engage it with covering fire from the fleet. We need a defensive line established before that happens."

I laid out the deployment strategy. "The Hydra and the Kraken will be beached on the northern shores. We will run heavy power cables from their internal reactors to the new Crawler construction sites to help build them. Their onboard forges will be utilized for fabricating localized defenses."

Usually, I preferred to install the Dungeon Core first and have the structure build itself around the power source. Here, under the scrutiny of an advancing army and the threat of a Tier 9 progenitor, it would be safer to build the shell and add the heart last.

"General Valen," I addressed the screen showing the stoic submarine commander. "You are in charge of the build sites. You will act as the final line of defense on the shore."

"Understood, my Lord," Valen replied smoothly.

"General Goliath," I turned to Bob. "You will lead the ground forces and act as the spearhead. You will push inland and establish a perimeter of defensive bunkers, trenches, and automated heavy turrets. We will create a kill-zone here, in the valley."

Bob slammed his fist to his chest. "The line will not break, Lord Commander."

"Commodore Theramore," I looked at the old sailor's eager face on the screen. "You will position the 3rd Fleet near the shore. You are the primary fire support. Your ships will provide overlapping naval bombardment capabilities and deal with any potential flanking attacks from the sea. You will arrange the fleet in an arch, backing the two build sites, sandwiching them between the frontline turrets and your guns."

Theramore grinned, a feral showing of teeth. "They won't get within ten miles of the beach, my Lord."

"Captain Ivela," I addressed the air force commander. "You will take command of the skies. The Vengeance and The Retribution will act as mobile, high-altitude fortresses, ready to intercept any threat from any direction, or to provide close air support to any sector that buckles. You will also order routine aerial patrols and bombing runs on the incoming Cinderfall army. I want air dominance maintained at all times. Do not let a single enemy bird slip past your screen."

Ivela nodded, her eyes fierce. "The sky is ours, Lord Commander."

Finally, I turned to the two women standing beside me in the CIC.

"Director Nyx," I said to Patricia, who was back in her sleek, black Mark VI-S armor, her visor opaque. "You will, as always, provide intelligence and rapid, surgical support. Use your espionage network to disrupt their supply lines. Poison their wells, burn their grain carts, assassinate their quartermasters. Make their march a living hell."

The black helmet dipped in a silent, lethal acknowledgment.

"Nyxia," I said, turning to my wife. "We need to coordinate our ground forces with the rebel factions and the loyalists of House Black. We will be paradropping communication nodes and liaison officers into the contested territories. You will take charge of that front. You know their commanders, you know their tactics. Organize them."

Nyxia straightened her spine, her crimson eyes flashing with analytical focus. "I will need unrestricted access to the Oracle's deep-scan data and a dedicated squadron of Phantoms for drops."

"You have them," I said.

I looked around the room, meeting the eyes of every commander, both physical and projected. We were a young army, a nation forged in a few short years, preparing to meet an empire that had ruled for centuries.

"Is everyone clear on their roles?" I asked.

In perfect unison, the commanders—save for Nyxia, who offered a crisp, formal nod—raised their gauntleted fists and struck their chest plates.

The war council was adjourned.
