Flames devoured the night sky like hungry gods, swallowing stars and spitting embers in their rage. The Bent Shieldmaiden—that unremarkable tavern that had stood for generations—now transformed into a cathedral of fire, its windows bleeding orange light, its timbers cracking like broken bones beneath the weight of its own destruction.

Cade hit the outskirts of town at a dead sprint, the rest of his crew fanning out behind him. The street had become a madhouse of panic—dozens of half-dressed patrons stumbling from the blazing tavern, coughing and retching as smoke filled their lungs. Townsfolk in nightclothes clutched children and valuables, livestock broke free and charged blindly through the chaos, and black-armored Arbiters formed bucket lines while bellowing contradictory orders.

“Gods below,” Rayka breathed beside him, her face a mask of horror painted in firelight.

Through the haze of smoke and panic, bodies continued to pour from the tavern’s main entrance—merchants, farmers, travelers, and locals, all united in terror. Some helped others escape, dragging injured companions clear of the inferno. Many bore burns, their skin blistered and raw. Others lay motionless in the dirt, overwhelmed by smoke or trampled in the chaos.

A thunderous crack split the air as part of the roof collapsed inward, sending a geyser of sparks spiraling heavenward. The crowd surged backward in unified terror, mothers clutching children to their chests, men shouting warnings. The Arbiters maintained their positions, their discipline holding even as the situation deteriorated.

“Look,” Elena hissed, pointing toward a cluster of Arbiters at the edge of the chaos.

A familiar figure in black armor staggered from the tavern’s entrance, his helmet gone, face blackened with soot. One of the drunk Arbiters from earlier—the one who’d hiccupped through his threats. Now he screamed, clawing at his back where his armor glowed white-hot, melted into his flesh. Two fellow Arbiters tackled him to the ground, rolling him in the dirt to extinguish the flames, but Cade knew instantly it was too late. The man’s screams tapered into a gurgling moan, then silence.

“These flames are wrong,” Cade muttered, his silver-ranked senses detecting something unnatural in the way the fire moved—almost predatory, as it sought flesh rather than fuel. “This isn’t an accident.”

As if summoned by his realization, a young man in ragged clothes collapsed to his knees in the center of the square, his face contorted in grief.

“The Destroyer!” he wailed, tearing at his hair. “I saw him! Just before the flames—it’s him! The scourge of Elysia has found us!”

Whispers raced through the gathering, faces turning from the fire to search the darkness between buildings with new terror. Cade’s stomach dropped as he approached the distressed crowd. Their overlapping shouts bombarded him from every direction.

“The Destroyer?”

“Here? Gods save us—”

“My cousin was in Elysia when it fell—”

“They say he controls fire—”

Cade’s jaw tightened. What’s going on?

Something inside the thief rebelled against the spectacle unfolding before him. He didn’t care what they thought. This wasn’t his doing, but damn it all, he wasn’t just going to let this place burn. Putting his hands in his pockets, he called on his magic. As inconspicuously as he could, he reached for the flames that roared in front of him, intending to draw just enough power to dampen the blaze.

The moment his magic touched the inferno, he felt it—another power, feeding this reincarnation of the hells.

“Everyone,” Cade whispered to his crew. “Someone’s making this fire.”

“No shit,” Elena breathed.

“With magic,” Nora corrected, nudging the redheaded thief in her shoulder.

Elena mouthed an ‘oh’ before smiling sheepishly at Cade.

“Find the other caster,” Cade hissed to Orro, sweat beading on his forehead as he tried to counter the foreign magic without revealing his own. “Or this entire settlement is going up.”

Orro’s expression darkened. “Can you stop it?”

“Not without showing my hand,” Cade admitted, the strain evident in his voice. “And whoever’s doing this is good. I—I can feel it fighting me.”

“I’ll see it done,” the assassin answered, unsheathing one of his daggers that seemed to drink in the firelight.

Cade concentrated as best he could, doing his utmost to keep several townsfolk between him and the Arbiters attempting to put the fire out with buckets of water. He saw Orro slink through the crowd, unseen and unheard in all the chaos.

A raw, desperate scream cut through the cacophony—a woman breaking free from restraining hands to surge toward the burning tavern. “My son! Tommen is still inside! Please, someone help him! He’s just a boy!”

Two Arbiters caught her before she could reach the flames, their black armor reflecting the inferno as they struggled to hold her back. Her screams transformed into something primal, a sound Cade felt in his marrow.

“PLEASE! GODS, HELP HIM!” she screamed, the sound louder even than the burning building.

The Son of Ruin gritted his teeth.

I’m not letting you go, you nasty bugger, he thought while attempting to wrangle the flames under his control.

They fought harder. He could feel whoever was behind this horrible spell. They were powerful. Disciplined.

“Upstairs window!” someone shouted. “I saw movement!”

All eyes turned upward. Through the smoke and flames, the silhouette of a small figure appeared at a second-floor window, tiny hands pressed against the glass.

“TOMMEN!” The mother’s voice shredded itself raw.

The Arbiters conferred rapidly, their captain barking orders as three of his men began strapping on what looked like enchanted gauntlets—fire protection, perhaps. But they were moving too slowly, too methodically, as the flames climbed higher, eating through the structure’s integrity with a voracious appetite.

Cade could feel the foreign magic surging, deliberately targeting the floor beneath the child’s window. Whoever controlled these flames wanted the boy to fall, to burn, to become another tragedy blamed on the Destroyer.

“I can’t hold it back much longer,” Cade growled, his concentration wavering as he fought against the unseen caster. “The fire’s going to break through the floor under him any second.”

Movement to his left drew the thief’s attention, and horror filled his mind as he recognized the figure rushing toward the tavern.

“Orro, no!” Rayka reached for him, but Nora held her back.

“Let him go,” the ex-paladin said grimly. “The boy has minutes at most.”

“But they’ll recognize him!” Elena protested.

“Then we’ll deal with that when it happens,” Cade said, unable to stop his friend without releasing his tenuous hold on the flames. “Find me the other caster. Now. Someone’s controlling this fire, and I need to know who.”

Elena nodded, her eyes darting over each face in the crowd.

Meanwhile, Orro cut through the crowd like a blade through silk, discarding his borrowed cloak as he ran. People scattered instinctively before him, perhaps sensing the deadly intent in his graceful movements. The Arbiters preparing to enter the building looked up just as Orro reached them.

“Stop! You can’t—” the captain began.

Orro didn’t slow.

He vaulted over the line of armored men with inhuman agility, drawing his broken orange blade in a single fluid motion. The blade caught the firelight, seeming to drink it in, glowing like a captured sun.

“Get back here, civilian!” an Arbiter bellowed, but Orro was already at the tavern’s front door.

A wall of flame greeted him, the entrance transformed into a curtain of fire. Without hesitation, Orro slashed upward with his exotic sword, and the flames—impossibly—parted before it like a physical thing cut in two. He disappeared into the burning building before the fire could close behind him.

The crowd fell silent, stunned by the display.

“Hang on, Orro,” Cade whispered, focusing all his concentration on dampening the flames around the stairs and second floor. It was like trying to hold back a tidal wave with his bare hands, the foreign magic pushing against his will with spiteful determination.

“Found him,” Elena announced suddenly, her voice low and urgent. “Northwest corner of the square. Tall man in a green cloak. He’s not alone. I think—”

Cade risked a glance in the direction Elena indicated. There, partially hidden in the shadow of an alleyway, stood a familiar figure.

“Shit. Another one?” the thief cursed when he spotted the scarred face partially illuminated by the flames. Rufus. His hands were concealed beneath his cloak, but his eyes gleamed with reflected firelight as he stared not at the burning tavern, but directly at Cade.

“Son of a bitch,” Cade snarled, fury rising like bile in his throat. He understood now. “They’re killing innocents just to frame me.”

“Look,” Elena hissed, pointing toward the young man who had first cried out about the Destroyer.

The accuser was slinking away from the crowd, moving toward Rufus’s position. As they watched, Rufus casually tossed a small pouch that the young man caught with ease before disappearing into the darkness.

“Paid witness,” Nora confirmed, disgust evident in her tone.

“I’ll kill him,” Cade growled, the magic in his core responding to his rage, surging against his control.

“Not now,” Rayka cautioned, her hand on his arm. “Orro first.”

Cade nodded, pulling his hands out of his pockets. He concentrated on the fire. His Destruction magic rolled over it like the smoky cloud billowing over the tavern. Arms outstretched, he uttered a single word as his mind brushed against the will of the fire.

“Kneel.”

The flames resisted, but he forced every ounce of will he could muster into his command. Whatever force animating them buckled. With a titanic surge of his strength, he shoved down. He would not let this wicked inferno devour another inch of that building. Not until—

“Orro,” Rayka whispered as she shuffled from foot to foot in barely concealed panic. “Don’t you dare die on me.”

Cade shoved down harder.

“KNEEL.”

The word echoed out from him like a thunderclap. Several townsfolk glanced back at him, but most gasped as the flames retreated in a single wave of power.

A crash of breaking glass pierced the night as a window on the second floor exploded outward. A dark figure launched through the shower of glittering shards, something small clutched tightly against his chest, his cloak billowing around them both like protective wings.

Orro and the boy plummeted from the second story.

Rufus smiled—Cade could see it even across the square—and with a subtle flick of his wrist, released his hold on the flames. The abrupt withdrawal of the foreign magic left Cade’s own power surging unbalanced, and he staggered, nearly losing control entirely.

With desperate focus, he channeled the excess energy into a cushion of air beneath the falling pair—subtle, invisible to most observers, but enough to prevent broken bones.

The Arbiters moved with impressive coordination, two of them rushing forward to catch the falling figures. They grunted under the impact but managed to remain standing. The black-armored warriors suddenly transformed into unlikely saviors.

The boy—Tommen—was unconscious but breathing, his small face smudged with soot. Orro looked significantly worse, his clothing singed, his exposed skin angry with burns, his lungs clearly struggling as he coughed violently.

“TOMMEN!” The mother’s scream transformed into sobbing relief as she reached for her son. The Arbiters carefully transferred the child to her arms, and she clutched him like she’d never let go again.

For one crystallized moment, the scene took on an almost sacred quality—mother and child reunited, Arbiters standing protective guard, Orro swaying slightly as he fought to remain upright. The charred cinders of the tavern cast them all in the same golden light, uniting them in a tableau of unlikely heroism.

Maybe art wasn’t so dead after all.

The boy’s eyes fluttered open, and he looked up at Orro with innocent gratitude.

“You… saved my boy,” Tommen’s mother whispered, her voice raw from smoke.

Orro simply nodded, unable to speak through his coughing.

“Thank you,” she rasped.

With slow but deliberate movements, Tommen raised a burned hand and placed it on Orro’s head. The crowd stilled, and even Cade recognized the unspoken sacredness of this moment. Orro peered up, meeting the soot-covered child’s weak gaze. The assassin nodded once.

An Arbiter’s voice cut through the moment: “Wait. I know him.”

The change in tone sent ripples of tension through the gathered Arbiters. The mother, sensing the shift, clutched her child closer and took a step back.

“He was at the Tournament,” the Arbiter continued, his hand moving to his sword. “With the Destroyer’s crew. One of the traitors who decimated Elysia.”

The whispers started immediately, racing through the crowd like wildfire. Cade felt each one like a physical blow.

“The Destroyer’s men.”

“Elysia’s butchers.”

“Murderers.”

The mother who had been weeping in gratitude moments before now backed away, horror replacing her relief. She spat at Orro’s feet, her face contorted with sudden hatred.

“My sister was in Elysia, you bastard!” she yelled, tears streaming down her face. She clutched her Tommen closer to her chest as if to protect him from Orro. “She was buried in her shop because of you—because of your master!”

Orro stood motionless, his face an impassive mask that couldn’t quite hide his stunned disbelief. Cade could practically see the thoughts coursing through his best friend’s mind. He’d just risked his life for her child, and now—

“Seize him,” the Arbiter captain ordered, his sword clearing its scabbard with a metallic hiss. “He’s one of them.”

Two Arbiters grabbed Orro’s arms, twisting them behind his back with the enhanced strength offered by their armor. Still weakened from the fire, he couldn’t resist as they forced him to his knees.

Something snapped inside Cade.

The magic he’d been struggling to contain surged forward, responding to his rage, his pain, his outrage at the injustice unfolding before him.

“Enough,” he said, the word carrying power even before he released his magic. “You hypocrites. You claim you uphold honor, and yet can’t see past your own damned pride the moment someone who others have labeled a villain does something heroic right before your eyes. But do you take that into account? No.” Cade’s eyes felt like they were ablaze. For all he knew, they were.

“Who the hell are you? Stay out of official Arbiter business, or we’ll arrest you for contempt.” a nasally Arbiter shouted at him, sword partially drawn out of his scabbard.

Cade stepped forward, out of the shadows, into the firelight. Across the square, he saw Rufus tense, preparing to flee. A part of him wanted to shout and point at the cruel arsonist, but he knew that he would never win over this crowd. Not now.

“Oh, I’m feeling some contempt, alright,” the thief replied, hands splayed out to either side as he continued to stride forward.

The Arbiter unsheathed his sword, its point directed at Cade’s chest. “I’m warning you! This man is one of the Destroyer’s! Stay back!”

“You want the Destroyer?” Cade called out, his voice carrying easily over the roar of flames. “You want someone to blame?”

The Arbiters turned toward him as one, their helmets reflecting the firelight like demonic masks.

“Here I am.”

He released his magic in a controlled explosion—if such contradictory terms could exist together. Wind and flame twisted around him in a spiraling column, his rage given physical form.

The blast radiated outward, knocking the Arbiters off their feet but carefully avoiding civilians. Windows shattered. Carts overturned. Arbiters in full plate armor went flying like abandoned toys.

In the chaos, Cade surged forward, reaching Orro in three long strides. He hauled his friend to his feet, supporting his weight as Nora and Rayka cleared a path through the panicking crowd.

“The Destroyer! It’s him! It’s really him!” The cries followed them, a chorus of terror and hatred that cut deeper than any blade.

Cade couldn’t stop himself from looking back at the woman and her son—the child Orro had just saved at the risk of his own life. She was pointing at them, her face twisted with loathing, screaming words he couldn’t hear over the roar of the crowd but could read clearly on her lips:

Monster. Murderer. Demon.

They ran, a tight unit forged by shared danger, cutting through side streets and alleyways to avoid the Arbiters now shouting and regrouping behind them. Behind them, the tavern continued to burn, the accusations continued to fly, and the true architects of the chaos melted away into the night, their work complete.

When they finally reached the relative safety of the abandoned mill, Cade’s rage had calcified into something colder, sharper. His hands trembled not with exhaustion but with the sheer injustice of it all.

Orro collapsed against a wall, his breathing still labored, burns marking his arms and face. Rayka immediately knelt beside him, murmuring soothing words as she applied salve to the worst injuries.

“You alright?” Cade asked, kneeling beside his oldest friend.

Orro nodded once, his dark eyes meeting Cade’s. “She spat on me,” he said simply, as if still processing the reality of it. “After I saved her son.”

The raw bewilderment in his voice was worse than any display of anger. Orro, who expected the worst from everyone, somehow hadn’t been prepared for that level of ingratitude.

“People believe what they’re told to believe,” Nora said quietly, coming to stand beside them. “And what they’ve been told is that we’re monsters.”

“But he saved her child,” Rayka protested, the unfairness of it clearly cutting her deeply. “She saw it with her own eyes!”

“Doesn’t matter,” Cade replied, the hollow feeling in his chest expanding with each breath. “When the story’s already written, people see what fits the narrative. We’re the villains now. Nothing we do will change that.”

“Even saving a child from burning alive,” Orro added, his voice rough from smoke and something else—a disillusionment Cade had never heard from him before.

“Sometimes,” Nora said gently, resting her hand on Cade’s shoulder, “being right isn’t enough. Not when the lie is louder.”

The weight of her words settled over them like a shroud. Cade had always operated on the margins of society, had always been technically a criminal—but he’d been a criminal with a code, with lines he wouldn’t cross. He’d been the charming rogue, the legendary thief who could make an Arbiter laugh even as he stole their watch.

Now he was something else entirely. Something people feared in the dark. A monster in their nightmares.

“We can’t stay here,” he finally decided, pushing himself to his feet. “They’ll sweep every abandoned building within the hour.”

“The outpost,” Rayka suggested. “It’s the last place they’d expect us to go, especially now.”

“Exactly,” Cade agreed, though the words tasted ashen in his mouth. “We move now, while they’re still disorganized. Straight to the outpost, get the masks, then into the Blightmarshes before dawn.”

No one argued. They gathered their meager possessions with the practiced efficiency of people accustomed to running. Chubbs, mercifully, remained asleep throughout, her small form nestled against Evie’s chest.

Before they left, Nora approached Cade, her storm-gray eyes searching his face. “This isn’t who you are,” she said firmly. “No matter what they say, no matter what they believe—I know who you are, Cade Stormhollow. We all do.”

The simple truth of her words cracked something in his carefully maintained façade. His throat tightened painfully, and he had to look away to keep the sudden burning in his eyes from becoming something more embarrassing.

“Thanks,” he managed, the word inadequate for the lifeline she’d thrown him.

As they slipped into the night like ghosts fleeing the dawn, Cade’s silver-ranked senses picked up the distant cries of the hunt beginning. Arbiters organizing search parties. Townsfolk grabbing torches and pitchforks. All searching for the monster in their midst.

The distant glow of the burning tavern painted the horizon orange, a false dawn birthed in destruction. To everyone in that town, he was responsible for the fire that even now consumed their livelihoods, their memories, their sense of safety. A lie made true by repetition, by belief, by the careful manipulation of those with their own agendas.

Cade had always prided himself on his adaptability, his ability to transform any situation to his advantage. But how did you adapt to this? How did you outthink, outmaneuver, or outcharm a lie that had already taken root in the collective imagination?

For the first time in his life, Cade felt the weight of a reputation he hadn’t earned and couldn’t escape. The hollowness inside him expanded with each step away from the town, with each accusation that echoed in his memory.

The outpost waited ahead, a dark silhouette against the star-scattered sky. Whatever answers lay within, whatever dangers guarded those masks, they seemed almost inconsequential compared to the battle he now fought—not against Arbiters or rival thieves, but against a story in which he’d been cast as the villain without his consent.

A story written in flame and fear, in which he was no longer the author of his own legend.
