The return trip to the Hub was uneventful.  Kyle continued to help with medical treatment over the next few days, continuing to practice with Devourer’s Domain as he hunted down and eliminated small spores embedded in otherwise treated injuries.  The moment they got back into more established Collective space, Kyle received a string of notifications in his nav bracelet.

Excusing himself from the medical bay, Kyle looked at his notifications.  He dismissed many of them outright, mostly being outdated requests for special Courier jobs, but two messages stuck out; one from Suierrillax, and the other from Marcus.

Looking at his Guild Liaison’s first, titled Cool Stuff, he opened it.

Kyle,

First of all, I hope that you aren’t dead.  Otherwise this would be a bummer.

Secondly, we received outreach from one of the Alabaster Court’s Lorekeepers.  It looks like your pal Arhades came through, and secured you an invitation to go study in the Courts.  I don’t think you need me to tell you how rare of an opportunity this is, nor do you need me to explain how much of a fool you’d be to pass it up.

They recognize that there will be a cost to you going, of course, and have generously offered a stipend for your trouble.  I told them that you’re out on a special job and that you’ll need some time to organize the details once you’re back home and settled, but seriously, just say “yes.”  There’s a reason that the Alabster Court produces more A Grades than any major civilization other than the Archdrake Supremacy, and I think you’ll benefit greatly from taking a little vacation.  Goodness knows some time off has done wonders for me.

In any event, let me know when you get this, and as soon as you sign off I’ll get to work coordinating the logistics.  Look forward to catching up again soon.

-Suierrillax

Kyle grinned as he read the message.  He’d been expecting this invite for some time, and it was nice to see it finally come through.  He still had some trepidation, namely due to the Twin Prodigies attempting to sabotage his Practicum, but Arhades had given assurances they wouldn’t be so blatant with a formal invitation on the line.  Their care for reputation was a better aegis than anything else he could have asked for.

Drafting a quick affirmative reply to Suierrillax, he opened the second message, from Marcus.

Kyle,

Just writing to let you know I’ll be out of pocket for a while, working on a large-scale installation for a new client.  Once I get the security systems and everything online, I’ll be back, but expect me to be out for a couple of years.  Of course you can reach me via message, but my assistants are going to be handling most of the day-to-day for a while.  Kertan Six is coming along well, with good folks getting settled in and putting in the work.

I traded some messages with your Liaison, and agree wholeheartedly that you should go to the Courts.  Things are established enough on Kertan Six that they can handle business without us, at least for a while.  Even more so if you can convince Boltsbury to stick around.  In any event, I’ll likely be out by the time you get back, but I look forward to catching up once we’re both back near the Hub.  Take care of yourself, kid.

-Marcus

“He always has big projects,” Kyle muttered to himself, considering the implications.

It was still odd in Kyle’s view to operate on a timetable where a few years was treated like a quick errand.  Then and again, with a lifetime spanning a millennium or two, things tended to get stretched.  That was his life, too, now that he was in C Grade.  Still, with Marcus wrapped up in a project, the timing to go to the Alabaster Courts was good.  With many of the projects on Kertan Six being able to be delegated, they could pick up on expansion once they got back.  Not to mention the benefit of letting people get their feet under them and develop some routine.

[DR. MAYHEW, NOT TO INTERRUPT YOUR THOUGHT PROCESS, BUT THIS APPEARS TO BE A GOOD TIME TO REVIEW YOUR GAINS.]

“I’ll get to it,” Kyle said, mind still thinking about his upcoming trip.

[SOME TIME IN MEDITATION WOULD BE GOOD FOR YOU, DR. MAYHEW.  ONE OF YOUR LONGEST ENEMIES IS GONE.  IF THERE WAS A TIME TO REFLECT ON YOUR PATH, THIS WOULD LIKELY BE IT.]

That comment snapped Kyle’s attention back to the present.  Since getting back to the ship, they’d kept busy treating injuries and filling out reports.  Now, they finally had a chance to breathe.  One Kyle recognized he should take.

“You’re probably right, C.H.A.D.D.”  Kyle sent a quick message to Randolph that he was going to be away for a bit, then made his way back to his quarters.  They were a little cramped compared to what he was used to, but there was still plenty of space for him to set the C.H.A.D.D. pack on the floor before settling in on the bed cross-legged as Kyle started to meditate.

It took him longer than he’d expected to calm his thoughts enough to shift into his Center, but soon Kyle was looking at the familiar sigil of his path superimposed over the gentle spiral that represented his attributes.  He took in the sight for a moment, then focused as words appeared before him.

KYLE MAYHEW.  UNION SYMBIOTE. UNBROKEN SURVIVOR.  LEVEL 64 (C)

SECOND CLASS: WORLD TREE DRUID.  LEVEL 19 (D)

PATH SYNERGY: 93%

DEVOURER’S ANATOMY – ENHANCED CARAPACE (FERRIC AUGMENTATION), AURIC PERCEPTION (SYNAPTIC BARBS), EXOSKELETAL STRENGTH (MUSCULAR COMPRESSION), CEASELESS VITALITY (TENACIOUS VIGOR), INSTINCTIVE INTELLIGENCE (PACK LEADER’S INSTINCT), COVERT DEXTERITY (REFLEXIVE EVASION), UNBREAKABLE WILL (HEART OF CREATION)

VITALITY: 4418

ENDURANCE: 5428

STRENGTH: 1602

DEXTERITY: 2885

WILLPOWER: 7479

INTELLIGENCE: 5370

PERCEPTION: 4208

FREE ATTRIBUTES: 200

With a thought, Kyle allocated his free attributes, putting half into Willpower and splitting the rest between Intelligence, Endurance, and Vitality.  Just one level away from the next skill upgrade, he thought.  The World Tree Druid class had primarily support skills that needed an upgrade, though Kyle was fairly certain he was going to work on Infuse next.  He had no idea what sort of options would be available to him, though he hoped it would maintain its utility both inside and outside of combat.  Once Infuse was upgraded, he’d be able to turn his attention to Cultivate, then finally Graft.

With that all done, Kyle took some time to watch his attributes spin lazily about his Center, reflecting on the symbol of the Unbroken Survivor.  In many ways, Victor had been a wall set before Kyle since his path truly started back on Earth.  The man created adversity for Kyle to overcome; the echoes of his madness impacting Kyle far beyond DeRosa’s direct reach.

And now Valentine is all that’s left, Kyle thought.  The edge of his wound where the World Tree Seed met flesh began to itch when he thought about the injury Valentine inflicted upon him.  That was the last outstanding item of business Kyle had to resolve.  And when the time was right, he planned to do so.

With a sigh, Kyle exited meditation, checking his nav bracelet.  He’d been in the room for a few hours, and needed a walk.  C.H.A.D.D. sent reassurance through their bond, and Kyle slung the pack over his shoulder as he began to wander, stopping in the hangar as an announcement came over the ship’s intercom.

We will be arriving at the Hub in approximately three hours.  Please begin gathering your belongings, and prepare for departure.

By now, a crowd began to gather at the hangar as the Hub came into view.  Even having seen it so many times, Kyle’s breath still caught at the sheer scope of the twisting chain of stars, all surrounded by the thin bands that made up the Hub’s habitations, even as planets gently orbited the myriad stars.

Kyle heard a sigh, even as Synaptic Barbs alerted him to Skippy walking to stand beside him.  The two stood for a moment, looking out the bay window at the approaching superstructure in silence.  Finally, the skrell spoke.  “I can’t believe I’m about to say this, but I think we’ll need to postpone our sparring match.”

Kyle quirked an eyebrow.  “Oh?  Why is that?”

“Full recovery is going to take a bit longer than I expected, and I got some inspiration for concepts to fold into my path.  If I delay too long, I might lose momentum.”

“In that case, I wish you well in your pursuit,” Kyle said.  “We’ll get around to another match one of these days.”

“So long as it happens before B Grade.  No idea where B Grades are supposed to spar,” Skippy mumbled.

If it was anybody else, the ridiculousness of casually discussing reaching B Grade would have made Kyle laugh.  But for Skippy, it did seem like a foregone conclusion.  The little battle maniac was a true prodigy.  Coupled with his nearly reckless drive for improvement, and willingness to push himself to the point of collapse, Kyle had no doubt that he’d make it, assuming he survived that long.

“We’ll do our best to make it happen,” Kyle said, gently rapping his knuckles against the skrell.  He found that he meant it, too.  The opportunity to spar with somebody of Skippy’s caliber when he didn’t have an urgent mission pressing on him would be invaluable.

Kyle’s train of thought was interrupted as he spotted Tillienne in the crowd.  She wore a simple dress with a yellow floral pattern, though it still accentuated her slender form.  His eyes drifted to her injured arm, which was still in a sling.  Despite his efforts, she would need additional healing to clear up the scar tissue that built up after Kyle’s field treatments.  Still, it didn’t seem to bother her one bit as she sauntered up to them.

“What, I wasn’t invited to the party?”  She asked, giving them each a look.  “And here I thought we’d become closer friends than that.”

“Don’t get me wrong, we should spar too,” Skippy said with a grin.  “But I’m afraid you’ll have to wait, too.”

Tillienne gestured at her injured arm.  “I’ve still got one more arm than you.  And two more than Kyle.  No offense.”

He just smiled, causing the vines beneath the bandaged wrap to wriggle and move in an unnatural pattern.  “What, you don’t think this counts?  If you need the advantage, I could always fight without the prosthesis.”

“Tempting, but no,” Tillienne laughed.  “It’s still hard to believe that it’s all over.  If I never fight another mushroom again, it’ll be too soon.”

“You can say that again,” Skippy agreed.  “Once I’m fully recovered, it’ll be time to fight more exciting prey.”

“Does Angela have something set up for you?” Kyle asked, finding it hard to believe that she was planning anything that would push Skippy.

“Well, no.  But I’m sure I’ll find it in due time.”

That made Kyle and Tillienne laugh, and some of the tension Kyle had been carrying since the fight began to melt away.  The shackle that had been DeRosa was finally gone, and the burden was left behind.

As they all talked, Kyle felt the brush of a hand against his, and without as much as a thought he wrapped his around it, Tillienne’s small hand boasting quite the grip.  He met her eyes, and they both smiled.  They had an unspoken understanding that their journeys would take them in separate directions soon, but for now he simply enjoyed the company.  And looked forward to their dinner all the more.
