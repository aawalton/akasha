The tunnel was dark, almost totally devoid of light expect for the torch outside either wall of each of the empty prison cells they passed. The long, eerie path was a straight shot with a slight curve to it so they couldn’t quite see where it led. As far as they could tell, there were no branching pathways nor any doors that led into side rooms. Just a long tunnel, torches, and empty prison cells.

As they walked further and further, Merlin’s swiveling head revealed just how nervous he was. Each time the interior of a new prison cell was visible to them as they passed it, his head shot to inspect it. As soon as he was satisfied it was empty, his gaze alternated between staring further into the tunnel and turning to watch their rear. “It suddenly occurs to me, if the knights catch us, they have plenty of cells for us.”

Arkady sighed. “Yeah, we’d each have our own, with plenty of space in between. Except for Orb of course, they’d just destroy him.”

Orb floated beside Vetica, hoping her quick reflexes and her new, powerful hex would keep him safe. “Thanks man. I wasn’t stressed at all, but now? Now I hate you.”

They walked in silence after that, the steps of their footsteps echoing slightly as they traveled, and shadows danced along the walls as they passed the quivering flames of torches one by one. It wasn’t until they felt it that they paused. It came from further down the tunnel. A feeling of ominous, intense power. One moment they’d been walking, and the next it was like they’d hit an invisible wall of evil. Even Merlin, the only one of them that couldn’t use real magic, felt something off. He shivered, and the sensation on Charlie’s back made him shiver, too.

“We should turn back. That was definitely a ‘we should turn back’ moment, right?” Merlin asked.

Charlie shook his head. Whatever that was, they’d need to be careful, but he wouldn’t stop until he found Mary. “We have to keep moving. Mary’s got to be here!” he said. Though he worried where that feeling was coming from, and he hoped Mary was safe.

Vetica and Arkady nodded at him. They pressed forward, almost faster than before, but now his friends had their weapons at the ready. Charlie was preparing his own assortment of attacks within his dungeon as well. If he had to fight, he would. But he wouldn’t turn back.

Vetica and Arkady took the lead, and Merlin looked behind them now more than ever. Though Charlie suspected he was less worried about being attacked from behind and more wondering how fast they could get out of here if things turned bad. Based on how long the tunnel was, Charlie didn’t think an escape would be fast at all.

But finally, as they turned a rounded corner, they saw the end of the tunnel. A simple stone wall. The prison had only one entrance. One way in, one way out. There were just four cells left, and the ominous power was clearly coming from one of them.

It was Charlie, who called out in a telepathic voice. “Mary? Are you there?”

There was a pause before the response that brought them all to a standstill.

“That is not my name, as far as I recall.” The voice was in their heads. Someone had responded to Charlie telepathically, and all of them had heard it. The voice was deep, grating, and despite the simple response, each word felt laced with dangerous intent. Charlie felt like he’d touched a pool of flowing darkness that he wouldn’t be able to wash out of his soul for weeks.

“This is giving me the ick,” Orb said, slowly descending into Merlin’s pocket. Merlin looked as if he wished he could shrink himself and hide in someone’s pocket as well.

Arkady motioned and crept forward with Vetica just behind him. Merlin moved as if each step was forced, as if Charlie’s will was the only thing tugging him after their companions. He did not want to be here.

When the cell came into view, Charlie knew instantly the man inside was the source of the ominous power he’d felt. A Black man with short hair sat with his legs crossed and eyes closed in the center of the cell. Unlike the other cells, there was no bed, nor any comforts of any kind. Just an empty room sealed by the thick metal bars. At the man’s feet, lying flat in front of him, was a large scythe that stretched nearly from one end of the room to the other.

Charlie wanted so desperately to check the last three cells for Mary, but all three of his companions were rooted in place. He couldn’t blame them. Before the oppressive power emanating from the room, it was hard even for him to move. Each breath felt labored, and his own power felt like a drop in the ocean compared to the power before him.

When the man opened his eyes, the power felt ten times worse. Merlin fell backwards onto his rear, scrambling backwards until his back hit the wall. Arkady and Vetica readied their weapons. The Rawlin assassin looked at the red-haired man. “Arkady?” she asked.

He waved a hand, and a scarlet hex of searching appeared before the cell. Another, slightly smaller one appeared before it, followed by five others, each slightly smaller than the last until the hexes stopped directly in front of the man. It was the most advanced searching hex Charlie had ever seen. An instant after the hexes appeared, they vanished on their own. Vetica readied herself, but the expression on Arkady’s face made her go pale.

“There aren’t any…” Arkady whispered.

Vetica risked a glance at him. “What?” she demanded.

Arkady shook his head and turned to look at her. “I’ve never seen this before. This man…he doesn’t have a weakness. Not one.”

Vetica steeled herself. “Merlin, take Charlie and run. We’ll hold him off.”

The man in the cell still hadn’t moved, but he stared at them, as if quietly contemplating which one of them he’d eat first. Merlin slowly rose, using the wall behind him for support. His legs readied themselves to sprint back to the entrance. But Charlie stopped him. “Who are you?” he asked.

The prisoner smiled. “I have no name.”

Charlie tilted his head. “Doesn’t everyone have a name? What do people call you?”

Orb didn’t dare leave Merlin’s pocket, but shot a telepathic whisper to Charlie. “Hey, bud, let’s maybe not antagonize the nice…well, really evil man sitting before us. This guy looks like he’d eat a bowl of dungeon cores like cereal. I don’t go good with milk, Charlie. Not to mention I’m lactose intolerant,” the dungeon core said.

“Some call me the reaper,” the man said.

“Well, that’s nice and ominous,” Orb said.

The scythe in front of the man glowed, and a dark aura surrounded it.

Arkady shook his head. “The Reaper…Cendra’s assassin? There’s no way the knights captured him. Not even Brandt could…if he’s here…” Arkady didn’t dare pull his eyes off the man. “It has to be for Charlie.”

Vetica looked back at Merlin this time, and her face said it all. She was done waiting for the Reaper to make the first move. “Merlin, go!” she shouted, before turning to face the cell. A black hex surrounded her dagger, and she moved through the cell bars as if she’d stepped through space itself. The Reaper didn’t even look at her. His eyes were focused on Charlie. In the moment before Vetica struck him, everything went black.

Charlie was alone now. His friends were gone, and though he felt cold stone underneath his hands and feet, he couldn’t see it. Instead, he was in a void like space that radiated the same purple, deadly aura that the Reapers’ scythe had. The prison bars were still there, coated in purple. But they slowly started melting, a few drops at first, and then more rapidly. After only a few moments, they’d become nothing more than a puddle on the ground, and there was nothing separating Charlie from the Reaper. The man still sitting cross-legged in front of him. “I sensed your arrival. I’ve been wondering if we’d get the chance to speak.”

For some reason, Charlie didn’t think he meant the arrival in the prison. It felt more like he meant when Charlie arrived in the fort. How much did he know?

“Oh no, this sounds so bad,” Orb said. Charlie turned in surprise to see the dungeon core float out from behind him. “I hid in a pocket specifically to avoid all of this and he just yanked me out like a coin. That’s all I am, Charlie, a little coin to be spent. Maybe he’ll spend me on a frozen treat before pulling the vendor’s heart out and eating that, too.”

Charlie knew he needed to focus on the threat before him, but he still hadn’t found Mary yet. So he did what came naturally. He asked a question. “Where’s Mary? Is there anyone else here? In the cells past this one?

The Reaper stared at him for a moment, perhaps deciding how to respond. An amused smile crept onto his face. “No. I’m the only prisoner within this prison. I would sense anyone else.”

Charlie nodded. The man didn’t seem like he was lying. But still, the information made his heart sink. This was the prison. Mary should be here. But right now, he had to focus on getting out of this situation. He couldn’t help her if he didn’t escape. “Where are my other friends?” he asked.

“They’re fine. I pushed them out of this space so we could talk. I was curious about you. I don’t have many things to be curious about lately,” the Reaper said.

Charlie had never heard of this man before, nor his title. Until Arkady had said he was some assassin of Cendra, Charlie hadn’t even realized there was a connection to the man the cult of Cendra worshipped. There were so many questions on his mind, so rather than debate over what to ask, he settled on the first question that had popped into his mind. “Why do you work for Cendra?”

The Reaper furrowed a brow. He looked down and thought about Charlie’s question for a moment. “I was curious,” he said simply.

That wasn’t much of an answer. “Curious about what?” Charlie asked, unsure of how far he could push the man.

The Reaper waved a hand, and reality distorted. He stood in plain back robes in the front of a magnificent castle. The Reaper in the vision didn’t speak, but the real Reaper did. “The Kingdom of Aysela is so powerful. I wondered if a man like me could bring it crumbling down, and Cendra gave me the chance. After his defeat, I stood there, at the gate of the castle and I wondered if I could bring it down myself.”

The Reaper in the vision disappeared, and the scene changed. Now they were in someone’s bedroom. A man and his wife lay in bed sleeping. The Reaper held an arm out and stretched out his scythe. It bit gently into the man’s neck, leaving a tiny scratch. The vision faded and Charlie watched the scene over and over. Every noble in the kingdom had been gathered for a meeting with the king. In the morning, they all woke with the same cut on their throat. When they gathered and realized, the Reaper arrived in the center of the meeting space. A fancy room where the king sat on a throne and the others stood, having hurried, whispered discussions.

“I am the Reaper. I could have slit the Kingdom of Aysela’s throat last night, but I did not. Now, we both know I can.” With that, the vision ended. Charlie sat alone in front of the Reaper once again.

Charlie thought about what he’d seen for a while. “Why did you spare them?” he asked out of genuine curiosity.

The Reaper shrugged. “Knowing I could take their lives was enough for me. I have no master to give me orders now. After that night, the hex knights took over palace security. I’ve often wondered if it would make any difference, but I know it would not.”

Orb finally found the bravery to ask a question of his own. “So how did the knights capture you then?” he asked. Apparently, curiosity overpowered his fear. When the Reaper spoke again, Orb jumped back in the air.

That didn’t last long.

Another vision. The Reaper wandered through a small village. He had an apple with a single bite in it in his hand. He chewed on the piece in his mouth slowly, as if in thought. Children played in the background. He watched them. He smiled. They reminded him of someone. Charlie felt a subtle sense of loss within the vision.

Then there were knights. Gregory, Thespin, Calda, among others. They surrounded him. With a weary sigh, the Reaper raised his scythe. He’d wanted to relax, to wander off and die. This was an annoyance. In a single sweep, he would take their heads. Most of them. Gregory and two others would likely survive the first blow.

They would not survive the second.

A kid shrieked with laughter; they hadn’t noticed the adults growing serious. They hadn’t noticed the scene unfolding before them. It was then, with a sigh, that his weapon disappeared into a void. Children should not see violence so young. He’d always had a soft spot for the innocence of youth. There was no mission. He had no objective or curiosity. For the first time in his life, he didn’t wonder if there was something he could accomplish. He’d wondered if he could crumple an empire, and he’d left it beaten and battered. The Reaper had finally satisfied his curiosity.

The vision faded. This one was more personal, as if Charlie could feel exactly what the Reaper was thinking. “And now I am here. Not because the knights can keep me here, but because I agreed to come. I am here by mutual agreement. I will rest here until my power fades, and then I will pass on. It will take time. The power I’ve accumulated is vast. But in the meantime, I will rest and contemplate.”

The Reaper was interesting. If not for his worries about Mary, if not for wanting to make sure he was telling the truth and his friends really were safe, Charlie might’ve asked him more. But instead, he didn’t complain when the veil finally dropped and Vetica leaped towards him, scooping him off the ground. In the same motion, she kicked Orb towards Merlin, who caught him in both hands, ready to run.

But the powerful aura was gone, and the Reaper had closed his eyes again. The bars to the prison cell were back in place. Charlie looked around, realizing his friends were safe, and smiled. Arkady nodded toward the last cells. “She’s not here,” he said grimly, warily looking back towards the Reaper.

“Then let’s get the heck out of here before Mr. Reaper attacks,” Merlin said.

Charlie smiled. “It’s okay, he doesn’t hurt kids.”

A smile crept on the Reaper’s face. For a moment, he looked more than just slightly amused. The others were ready to retreat and discuss what to do now, but Charlie held up a hand before they could.

“Reaper, I have a question.”

He opened a single eye and stared at Charlie. That was consent enough.

“If you’ve already answered the question you had, the one about if you could destroy an empire, why not answer a new one?”

This got both eyes to open. “A new question?” he asked, suddenly interested.

“Charlie, maybe we should get out of here while we can,” Merlin whispered.

“Why not see if you can save it, instead? Why not see if you can stand up to protect people, instead of hurt them?” Charlie asked.

“There are some men so evil, they cannot turn back to the path of good,” the Reaper said dismissively.

Charlie thought about that for a moment, and honestly, as much as he hated to admit it, he couldn’t refute the point entirely. But he had one last thing to say before he allowed Merlin to run off with him in tow.

“Maybe, but I don’t think you’re one of them. And there are a lot of kids in Aysela. I’ve met some of them, and they became my friends. If I hadn’t helped them, they could’ve got hurt, or worse, and a lot of that was because of Cendra. So, just ask yourself if you can help them out. Just think about it,” Charlie said.

The Reaper stared at him for a moment. A deep, thoughtful expression crossed his face, and then he returned to his meditation, closing his eyes. “You never asked why I was so far within the prison, why they placed me here. But I’ll tell you, anyway, it’s because I love to hear the music. You should come and listen too.”

“What music? What do you mean?” Charlie asked.

The Reaper did not respond, and it quickly became clear he would speak no more.

With that finished, they quietly left the prison. He wondered what the Reaper had meant by his final words. What music? And another unspoken question lingered over all of their minds.

Where was Mary?
