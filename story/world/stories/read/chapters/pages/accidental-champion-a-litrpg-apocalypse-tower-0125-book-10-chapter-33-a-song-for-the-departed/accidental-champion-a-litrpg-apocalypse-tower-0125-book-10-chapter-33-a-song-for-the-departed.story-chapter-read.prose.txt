Xavier knelt on the ground of the Dark Tunnel inside the Void Dungeon, a slackened smile on his face, his body devoid of will, his mind lulled. He couldn’t make his muscles move.

And he didn’t want to.

He’d read and listened to the description of A Song for the Departed intently. It was one of the Ragatni’s most powerful spells—a spell that entered not just through the ear, but through the very soul. While Xavier had tilted his head with curiosity at its function, he knew that both his soul and his mind were powerful.

It would take a hell of a lot to break through his mental barriers, and some Class C7 Void Dungeon-created beast wasn’t going to be the first one to do it. But even as he remembered having that thought he’d wondered if it was right. Wondered if something strange might be going on. A voice inside his mind had told him to be cautious. It wasn’t Roln’s—he couldn’t hear Roln anymore. It was his own voice.

And he’d ignored it. Ignored it with ease.

He’d come into this fight with nothing but confidence and disdain for the Ragatni’s abilities. That voice that had been silenced spoke now—it was the only thing in Xavier’s mind still making any noise other than that sweet song.

It was the only thing in his mind that was still his.

As the Ragatni started to glow red once more, the ice that Xavier had engulfed it with for a second time steaming and melting, his own voice spoke to him, so tiny and weak. That part of his mind that controlled the voice worked, going through his memories of what had just happened, and noticed something… sinister.

The sweet sound—it had come before. It had come before and he hadn’t even noticed it. The sound had been smaller, more insidious. It had gained entry to not only his mind but his soul, and in doing so it had lulled him into a false sense of security. A false sense of confidence.

For Xavier, this was an easy enough thing to do, considering he was prone to arrogance. There were a dozen things he could have done differently in that fight—things he should have done differently.

Yet he hadn’t.

Now, as he knelt there, unable to move, the small voice being attacked by that sweet sound, the better part of him felt no worry or fear. He listened not only to the music coming into his ears, but the music coming into his mind.

The departed—spirits from all over—sang to him, sometimes in voices he knew, for everyone he had ever known had died trillions and trillions of times over somewhere in the multiverse. Howard. Justin. Siobhan. The people he’d saved and trained. Those he’d met on the different floors.

Even the voice of the son he’d met from the otherworld sung to him.

And the sweetest voice of all—the voice of Adranial—sung to him, telling him it was time to rest. Time to end this madness. He would never be able to defeat the Interdimensional Dragon and save the universe, and why would he need to? The Interdimensional Dragon had not come for the afterlife.

They could live happily, eternally, in the ever after.

There was no need for all this fighting. No need for one battle after another. No need for any of it.

No need for life at all.

Xavier couldn’t help but be drawn toward this, all his defences weakened, his mind and soul pierced by the siren song of death.

It would be so easy to give up, to give in. After all, what was he really fighting for? Adranial was right—he would never win.

How could he?

The Ragatni shifted. The ice slid off its massive body, falling to the ground. It stamped forward, hooves splashing in the puddles, making them steam as it still glowed a fierce red. Xavier couldn’t hear any of this, but he could see it happening before his eyes. See the beast making its way forward. It didn’t run. It walked casually, as though it were on odd-looking cow off to graze another patch of grass.

And I’m the patch of grass.

It was the tiny voice that had spoken. The tiny voice that was still working to regain control. To turn this around. Right now, it was wheedling its way toward switching off Xavier’s hearing, but the closer it got to doing so the more it became susceptible to the sound of the song, and that song weakened it just as it had weakened every other part of Xavier.

The Ragatni walked forward. Smoke puffed from its wide nostrils. Its entire body was no longer engulfed in ice but in flame. The ice all around the tunnel melted, making more water form along the ground as it stomped through it—as though walking through a sewer.

And all the while the little voice continued to fight.

Flames licked out of the Class C7 beast’s maw, its breath stank, like all the rotten things underground, things that should never rise. Though it didn’t move swiftly, with each step it closed the gap. With each step it came closer to ending Xavier. And Xavier didn’t care.

Worse—he welcomed it.

He welcomed the sweet embrace of death. To finally be done with all the fighting. With everything. With the weight of the world, the universe, the whole god damned multiverse on his shoulders.

It’ll all be over soon. Oh, so very soon. Don’t you worry, Xavier. I’ll be here. We’ll all be here, and you’ll never be alone. You’ll never have to fight again.

It was Adranial’s sweet voice that sang those words, and with that small part of him he could hear that they didn’t ring true. That small part that fought heard those words and sensed a tension—a very minute tension—something he might not have sensed had he not been working on expanding his senses.

The spirit of Adranial who sang those words didn’t believe them. The spirit was compelled, made to sing those words by the spell—by the Ragatni.

Spirit… she’s a spirit… she isn’t my Adranial… my Adranial still lives, as I still live.

Even so, whoever this Adranial was, she was fighting the hold that was over her, just as he was fighting the hold over him. But it was that seed of doubt, the tension in her words, that made him fight all the harder. The tiny voice inside his mind grew stronger. That small part of his mind became larger, more influential. The tension he’d heard in that voice now started to enter his mind, his muscles, his soul—his entire being.

This is wrong, the tiny voice said. You must fight it. This isn’t what you want. Death is not what you want.

His head, which had been hanging down, began to slowly rise. All the while he’d been staring at the approaching Class C7 Ragatni, its six meaty legs making a steady yet sedate progress along the stone floor of the Dark Tunnel, stomping in the water that had formed on the ground from the melted ice. The Ragatni’s eyes were locked on Xavier just as they’d been from the start. Something shifted in those eyes as his head rose.

The Void Dungeon-created beast’s widened, showing a hint of surprise. And something else, perhaps—he caught only a hint at first, until he breathed deeply through his nose and recognised it instantly.

Fear.

More of his mind lit up as the smell of fear activated something deep inside him. Something ancient and primal, from the time when dragonkin were nothing more than another predator on their origin planet, a predator who struck fear in every other being down the food chain.

Another deep breath ignited his mind and body, making his heart pump faster, making adrenaline coarse through his veins like a raging river. His head rose higher, then, with as much force as he could muster, he managed to bring one of his legs up, planting his foot on the ground. He pushed, gaining the other foot on the ground, then stood up to his full height.

Still, he fought the sweet music. The music that entered not just his ear but his soul.

But his mind was working just as hard as his body, inching toward the mechanism that would… there.

Switch.

His hearing disappeared, a sudden deafness overtaking him. But he still heard the sweet sound of Adranial’s voice calling to him. The power in it hadn’t diminished overly much, but the tension was clearer to him.

Switch.

Xavier managed to shut off his spiritual sense. The silence that followed was acute, but it wasn’t overwhelming. It was something he’d become used to during his time training and expanding all his senses. He could still perceive the soundwaves coming from the Ragatni’s mouth, and the hint of otherworldly energy coming from the afterlife, the ghostly plane, or wherever these spirits’ voices had been harnessed from.

But it didn’t affect him in the same way.

Lacking his spiritual sense and his hearing didn’t bring disorientation—it brought clarity.

His body, mind, and soul were once again his own. He clenched his fists. Gritted his teeth. The relaxation he’d been feeling, the willingness to give up, to finally surrender to death, had utterly disappeared, replaced with…

Rage.

But it wasn’t a burning rage like he’d been overwhelmed with in the past. This wasn’t a rage that took him over and made him act without thinking. This was a cold, calculating rage. A rage that drove his entire being forward in unison toward a singular goal: Destroy the Ragatni.

And there was another goal whispered in there with the first, a goal he found it hard to ignore: Make it suffer.

The Class C7 Void Dungeon-created beast’s eyes widened, and it thoroughly stank of fear. Its sedate walk had already hastened, and now it was trampling through the Dark Tunnel faster than Xavier had imagined a thing of that size and bulk could move in such a small space. It was hitting the walls and the ceiling as it ran, the place simply too small for it to move freely.

Xavier leant down and grasped the shaft of bone war hammer he’d created.

He didn’t flow ice down the Dark Tunnel for a third time, though he was sure it would have slowed the beast down enough. He wanted something else. Something that would heighten this thing’s fear. Something that would make it dread his power.

A spell pattern manifested inside him with barely a thought. He cast the spell and watched in mute satisfaction as several things burst through the stone ground beneath the Ragatni’s six meaty legs.

Hands.

Grasping hands that looked as though they’d just broken from a grave. Covered in dirt with jagged fingernails and bloody fingertips. The hands weren’t the same size as the ones the Ragatni had summoned—they were larger.

There were six hands in all, and the Ragatni simply couldn’t dodge to the left or right, nor leap out of the way. There was nowhere for it to move as each hand wrapped its undead fingers around each of the Ragatni’s legs, holding them firm, stopping its trampling gallop with an abrupt swiftness that made several of the bones in its legs creak and crack.

A grin slipped onto Xavier’s lips as he watched the beast struggle. It was rare he took pleasure from the pain and death he caused. In fact, it was something he was wary of. He intended for his power to be absolute, but the last thing he wished was for that power to corrupt him.

In that moment it didn’t seem as though any of that mattered. In that moment the only thing that mattered was that he make this thing feel more fear than it could handle before it died. That he make the very Void Dungeon that had created it and was connected to it shudder at Xavier Collins being there inside it, walking its halls, slaying its beasts.

Heading toward its heart.

And I will make you shudder.
