I kept to the coast, traveling further south. No sense being seen above the dunes by anyone on the Meadow. Nuzzle trotted along beside me, kicking at the sand like a toddler.

A gentle breeze carried the salty air to us and up the dunes to our right. The water sparkled to my left, still without a single wave the entire time I’d been here. The scene belied any evidence that our vicious battle had recently been nearby.

Above, the clouds had dissipated, but the golden eagle I noticed earlier still followed us.

At first, I thought it was a wild spirit animal like the other birds I'd seen around Penumbra, but its path was far too intentional. Someone was observing us. I didn't like the idea of that.

It'd be impossible to lose the tail by foot, especially on the beach with no cover whatsoever. I really only had one option.

The Notice of Resignation item I had turned out to be far less useful than I hoped since it couldn't be used during battle, but it was perfect for a situation like this. At this point, there was no reason I needed to be by the ocean outside of sentimentality.

And now's as good a time as any.

The paper oozed out of my inventory and into my hand, tickling the skin beneath my armored arm and it liquified and reorganized itself. Nuzzle took the hint and climbed onto my shoulder. I winced as his claws dug into my back while he climbed.

The wax seal on the notice resisted the tug of my fingernail briefly, before popping off and falling into the sand. I unrolled the paper. It glowed briefly and unceremoniously disintegrated as text appeared in my vision.

[Notice of Resignation Activated.

You will be teleported to the nearest lantern in three seconds.]

[3…

2…

1…]

My amethyst cloak twirled around on its own accord and swallowed me like a whirlpool.

I stumbled back into existence instantaneously.

The beach was gone. The sea, gone. My nose wrinkled at the sudden change from salt air to a marshy woodland scent. I stood among a forest of red-trunked trees. Golden leaves fell gently around me, glittering in the sunlight that broke though the canopy. Insects chirped and bird sang to one another other among the foliage.

Nuzzle jumped from my shoulder and danced around in the ferns and falling leaves. He ate one as it fell, then spit it out in a crumpled ball, recoiling at the taste. That didn't stop him from pouncing on several more, enjoying the chase more than the reward.

Several large figures loomed in the distance, nothing but shadows through the city of trunks. They were all unmarked, these Sins, which was a shame because I needed to continue enhancing my skills and stats if I wanted to catch up with the other Reapers who shared my new rank.

Why can't we just kill unmarked Sins anyway? If the whole purpose here was to eliminate sin from the world, why not just take them all down instead of waiting for them to be Marked? I knew that killing an unmarked Sin—or Reaper for that matter—would result in me being Marked. But what did that mean? Would I be chained down and hunted by other Reapers like a Sin? Would I eventually become Unchained and go on a rampage?

It certainly wasn't worth the risk to find out.

I hiked through the forest, making my way toward the closest Sin. They were supposed to be harmless when unmarked, so it couldn't hurt to get a closer look. Nuzzle chirped a few times as we neared, but not out of anxiety. More like he was trying to say 'hello.'

This one was part giraffe, strolling gingerly among the trees. Its long neck stretched up to an anatomically correct head, except for the ears and long black human hair. The lips actually appeared human too, leaving behind smudges of what appeared to be pink lipstick as they nibbled on leaves from the lower branches. Most disturbingly, the legs were all human, with hands instead of feet.

I could barely look at the abomination. Not only because it was hideous… but for what it represented. A manifestation of human sin. It just strode along as if it was causing no pain. No suffering in Vitria.

My carapace arm tingled, the scythe within begging to come out. To strike down this Sin where it stood. Would that make someone's life better in Vitria? Save someone from suffering or from falling to the temptation of making a terrible mistake?

Would it prevent another accident like I'd witnessed in that vision during my advancement to Amethyst?

I took a deep breath, grappling emotions back under my control.

Nuzzle chirped again and licked one of the hand-feet-things. The giraffe gazed down at him. There was an unnerving soullessness to those eyes.

I left the monster, continuing in the same direction I'd been heading. Eventually we did come across a Marked Sin. It was a deer hybrid, Amber of course—that's all there was supposed to be in the Meadow.

I didn't realize how much stronger I'd become from the simple transformation to Amethyst stage until I battled this creature. It was like in cartoons when an adult holds back a kid by the forehead and the kid just continuously swings their arms and legs making no contact. I killed the Sin in seconds. Earned its Will and another item within a minute. A simple unadorned necklace this time.

Within the forest I had no sight of the black light beacons that identified pillar locations to relinquish the Will. So, against my better judgement, I started climbing a tree that seemed to have a decent path of branches to reach the top. The climb wasn't as easy as Nuzzle made it appear, latching onto the bark and skittering up like a squirrel trying to hide its nuts.

The rough bark dug into my left hand and bare feet as I ascended the tree, but my armored hand bore the brunt of the effort, able to pull me up large junks at a time. It didn't take long to reach the top.

From above the canopy I stared out over the horizon. To the east the sea wasn't too far away. There were two pillars nearby that direction, probably including the one I'd just fought Zeus at. Several more north and—

Black smoke rose into the sky from the west. Nearby too. Not campfire smoke. This was thick and dark. Even from here I could smell the ash. Something was burning. A lot of somethings… And the beacon of a pillar was there too, penetrating the smoke like a spear.

Guess that's where we're headed, eh Nuzzle?

The lizard licked my cheek.

I approached the source of the smoke with caution. We were close, but the crest of a large hill blocked our view. There was no telling who or what caused it and if they might still be around.

I smelled the devastation before I saw it. Charred wood and grass. Cooked flesh…

Finally, atop the hill I looked down on a small village. Maybe twenty or so buildings, once surrounded by a wooden wall.

Everything was gone.

Not just smoldering ruins, but homes torn asunder, pieces of wall thrown a hundred feet from where they started like a tornado had run through. Bodies, both burned and otherwise littered the scene. Some looked far too reminiscent of Allium's remains.

Nuzzle sniffed the air and stuck out his tongue in disgust, then sniffed again, catching some scent, and skittered down toward what was left of the village.

I followed, scythe out and ready.

Nuzzle dug through burnt rubble, unhindered by the heat.

"Please don't eat any humans…" I mumbled.

I hadn't even really considered the fact that these were actual human bodies. They appeared to be Penumbrans from what I could tell. No cloak or scythes about in the carnage. Which meant they had no Will. Did that make one less of a person?

I'd spoken with Judith at the Meridian… And Eugene in the colosseum. They both felt like real people. So what did having no Will actually mean then?

Amid Nuzzle's scrambling I heard a cough.

Someone's alive.

I helped Nuzzle dig, using my armored arm to move seared wooden beams and cutting with my scythe where necessary.

"Hang in there!" I shouted into the rubble. "We'll get you out."

Another cough.

It didn't take long to find a crumbling stone well beneath the fallen house. Inside, the hole down into the water. A person sat at the bottom, soaked and curled into a ball.

"Are you alright?" I called down.

"Alive…" the person said, barely loud enough to hear.

"I'll find something to help you up."

There was nothing stable enough in the immediate vicinity, but I found what appeared to be a rope for a clothesline near a part of the village that had avoided the flames. I tied it onto the foundation of a nearby building and lowered the end into the well.

"Can you grab on?" I called down.

No response.

"Shit…" I gripped the rope and lowered myself over the edge. "I'm coming down!"

The walls of the well were slick against the soles of my bare feet, providing little traction. My hands bore the brunt of my body weight, rope burning against the skin of my left hand. Luckily my armored hand provided relief for the descent.

It wasn't a particularly deep well and I soon found myself splashing into the shallow water beside the person. Faint light filtered in from above revealing the bruised and ash covered face of a young boy in an Amber cloak. I couldn't tell if his hair was white and covered in soot or naturally gray.

"Can you stand?" I asked.

He didn't reply.

I tapped his face gently with a wet hand. He moved his head groggily, rousing. I repeated the question

The boy shook his head, barely moving.

I needed to get him talking, wake him. The water was cold, despite the fire that had raged above.

"What's your name?"

"Silas," he choked out.

"How much Health do you have left? Volition?"

"Not… enough."

I rested a hand on his head. It was soot-covered after all. "I'm gonna get you out of here, Silas"

I tied a rudimentary harness around his waist and under his shoulder. Definitely not the proper way to do it, but it was the best I could think of.

"I'm going to climb up, then pull you out."

The boy's head lulled in a nod.

The way up, I found a few little crevices in the walls to stick my toes into, which made the climb slightly less unbearable. But still… my biceps would be sore tomorrow.

I pulled Silas up, Nuzzle chomping onto the rope behind me to help. It took longer than I'd have liked to admit and I almost dropped him once before wrapping the rope around my carapace arm to avoid any further slipping. Once up, I carried him away from the rubble and leaned him against a unburnt wooden beam.

Kneeling next to him, I said, "I'm gonna find a Will for you to harvest. I won't be long."

There had to be another Marked Sin around here.

Silas grabbed my arm before I could stand. "Is everyone… dead?"

I nodded, not sure I could say the words.

"I saw them…" Silas coughed. "I tried to warn them. I wasn't fast enough… I saw them."

"What did you see?"

"Unchained. Amethyst. Sapphire. Amber. They came so fast. I didn't… I couldn't save them."

I rested a hand on his shoulder. Nuzzle licked his hand. "You did the best you could, Silas. This isn't your fault." I paused. I didn't want to make him relive any more of the memory than he had to, but I had to know… "The Sapphire. Was it a gorilla?"

His eyes were swollen from the smoke, but they widened all the same. He nodded.

"You won't have to worry about them any more," I said.
