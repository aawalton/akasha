“Wow, this place has a lot of security.”

Gwen was the first to enter the room. Veronica and Nasha followed shortly after.

The fifth and final floor had golems guarding the stairway entrance, just like all the other floors. However, unlike the stone golems from the first floor or the steel golems guarding the fourth, the fifth floor had a full spectrum of elementals. All of them—summoned constructs—formed from various elements. Fire, lightning, water, wind, and more, stationed in rows along the entrance.

They seemed relatively harmless at a glance, but each one could fight with power on par with a seventh-tier combat mage. Together, they could take on an eighth-tier, or perhaps even stall an archmage for a short time.

“You are sure that map to Avalon is in here, right?” Nasha asked.

“It should be, if my memory is correct,” Veronica replied. “I was still studying at Valeborn at the time, but I heard plenty about the expedition. Rumors said the map had originally been found here in the Starfault Archives.”

Gwen turned, holding a stack of books in her arms. “Well, even if we don’t find it, we still found a few books that made the trip worthwhile, right?” She looked down at the top cover of her stack. “We did manage to find a spellbook written by Fia. Although it’s mainly for the Path of Blooming and Splitting.”

“The theory and application of the spells are what we need,” Veronica said. “I’ll just have to transcribe them over to the Path of Longevity so they better fit you.”

“Is it that easy to convert a spell from one path to another?”

“No,” Veronica said plainly. “But don’t underestimate me.”

“Cool line.”

Veronica rolled her eyes. “Come on. Let’s start searching. It has to be on this floor.”

The three of them moved into one aisle and began searching through the shelves from different ends.

Each floor was separated by the age of the books and records it contained. The first floor held works created within the past fifteen years, while every floor above it contained older materials. The fifth floor held everything that was two hundred years old or more.

Because of that ancient history, none of the works could be taken out of the fifth floor. They were too valuable and too fragile.

“Hey, mind telling me what you did down there?” Gwen asked. “With the whole lightning-fire thing?”

Her eyes moved along the top shelf, scanning each author’s name. For the books that didn’t have one, she reached up and checked them manually.

“It’s called the Whitebolt Conflux Array,” Veronica said. “An array that uses lightning to elevate the heat of a flame. Of course, there are some additional properties beyond heat. The resulting flame is comparable to an alchemist’s balefire.”

“Were those Tier-3 spells? Or Tier-4?” Gwen asked. “I saw those mages freak out when they looked at your spell. The barrier was even rumbling. It scared some of the people watching.”

Veronica placed a blank book back on the shelf. “They were seventh-tier spells. The fire was just a simple flame.”

Gwen’s hand froze on a book spine. “Wait, really?”

“Not at their full power, of course,” Veronica said. “I cast a much weaker, compressed version. Although it looked strong, the attack power was probably similar to one of my second or third-tier spells. The only difference was that the barrier reacted to the fact that the spell base itself was seventh-tier.”

“Ah, I see.”

As Veronica moved to the next shelf, a thought came to mind. She willed her status screen to appear and checked her quests.

Quest: Sit near extreme heat while circulating your mana.Quest: Achieve Tier-4.Quest: Form fourth set of Mana Rings.Quest: Defeat Hevestra (Complete) (Calculating reward…)

Sage, would the heat from the Whitebolt Conflux Array be enough to count for the extreme heat condition of your quest?

[No, that will not work. Synthetic heat from your own spells will not satisfy the conditions.]

Then what will? Lava? Dragon fire?

[Dragon fire would work. Some alternatives include waiting inside a volcano, or standing near an authentic dwarven furnace.]

A dwarven furnace…

There were a few blacksmiths in Maftem. Surely one of them had to be dwarven. Maybe she could visit one and ask permission to use their furnace.

As for the other quests, she needed some type of catalyst to reach the fourth tier. She could probably find something at the auction that would satisfy the condition. If not, then she would have to go to the Mage Association and purchase one at a premium price.

She’d still have to check her standing in the association. It had slipped her mind last time when she went in for the Blue Dryad’s Potion.

“Hey,” Nasha called out.

Both Veronica and Gwen looked at each other before moving to the next aisle where Nasha stood.

“I think I found it. The Isles of Avalon.”

In her hands was an old brown book.

Veronica walked over, and Nasha handed it to her.

The cover was quite simple, with a painted image of a beautiful island. The words Isles of Avalon marked the front in thin, italic letters. On the spine, the author’s name was simply Morgan.

She opened the book.

The pages were old, but still well-preserved. Each one carried the faint smell of dried ink and aged parchment. The writing was elegant, definitely handwritten, with letters too smooth and curved to be ordinary mass-printed script.

She turned the page.

Inside, there wasn’t a map. Instead, it read more like a research journal. A diary, perhaps. A recounting of events and the author’s thoughts on them.

Nasha looked over Veronica’s shoulder. “This doesn’t look like a normal travel guide.”

Veronica’s eyes moved across the page. She read it aloud for them.

Avalon does not sit upon the sea, though the sea surrounds it.

Avalon does not rest beneath the sky, though stars shine above it.

Those who search westward will find only waves.

Those who search with hunger will find only teeth.

To some, it exists as a place of respite, despite the graveyard of burial rites.

“A poem. Neat,” Gwen commented.

Veronica continued.

At the center of the still ocean lies Avalon, the garden of abundance, the grave of the crownless, nameless king, and home to the forge of the blade that embodied chivalry.

There came to me a warrior without equal, clad in broken armor and carrying the sword that had vanquished all evil. Great was his name, and greater still were the shadows that clawed at his feet. He came not to conquer, but to seek refuge.

The blade heard his command and led him to its home.

And there I was, there to receive him, there to take pity on his soul.

Beneath the white trees, I nourished him. I fed him from the silver spring, cleaned his armor, and saw his sword reborn. I stayed and guarded. I slept beside him as he wept, that poor soul burdened by countless tribulations. A shoulder was all I could lend.

Avalon was peaceful. There, he could rest.

But peace did not last long.

Forth came the harbingers of gold.

Their greedy knowledge. Their insatiable lust for power. They wanted everything. The garden, the trees, the forge, and the treasures. Most of all, they wanted him.

“That… doesn’t sound good,” Gwen said.

“Looks like some group raided the island in the past,” Nasha added.

Veronica nodded.

There was something that made her uneasy. Those raiders. Ones who came in gold. That sounded far too similar to the Asvarians. And from the way Morgan described them, greed was definitely a trait they were known for.

She continued reading.

The harbingers knew of the warrior’s location. But before they arrived, he had already left. Fully healed and renewed, with repaired armor and blade, he departed the realm.

Yet the harbingers came anyway.

I could not defeat them. Instead, I too fled from Avalon. But not before sealing it away, deep within the forgotten isles. Those harbingers would rot there for their sin.

To hunger with greed… I showed them the ultimate price of their actions.

If only I could have stayed to hear their torment.

“Wow, that took a turn,” Gwen commented.

Veronica turned the page, and there it was.

Exactly what she was looking for.

A map of the island of Avalon.

A rough illustration had been drawn across the center, spanning both pages. It depicted the entire island surrounded by ocean. The white trees, towers, the supposed garden of abundance, and even a castle within its center. Along the edges of the page were smaller drawings of armored figures arriving on ships, all wearing strange golden armor.

Veronica stared at it for a moment.

She had called it right. Morgan did not seem to know exactly who they were, but the description she gave was enough. “It seems the Asvarians were the ones who raided the island,” Veronica said.

“The golden mages?” Gwen asked, eyes widening.

“Don’t forget golden warriors,” Nasha added. “They were known for seeking magic, but they also had plenty of warriors and studied combat techniques.”

Of course, it wasn’t too surprising for regular mages to know who the Asvarians were. They were ancient, revered figures. The progenitors of magic.

Veronica knew that they had warriors, but she hadn’t realized that those with aura, like Nasha, would also be aware of them.

She turned the page and found the requirements to enter Avalon.

“In the future, I hope someone may go to Avalon and care for the island once more. I do not know what havoc those harbingers have wrought upon it. But all life needs a home. And with my departure, many beings have lost theirs,” Veronica read.

She continued down the page. “Requirements… lunar salt, dragon essence, a pearl, a plant with spirit power, and… elven, demonic, or fae magic.”

“Are those hard to get?” Nasha asked, still looking over Veronica’s shoulder.

Veronica didn’t know when it had happened, but Nasha had gotten quite comfortable leaning against her. Her neck rested lightly on Veronica’s shoulder, close enough that Veronica could feel her breath when she spoke.

“Hm… maybe. Lunar salt is easy. Dragon essence might cost quite a bit. But the fact that it says essence means we can use anything from a dragon, so even a claw clipping might work if it is large enough. A pearl is whatever. As for the last two… a plant with spirit power, and one of three alternate magics.”

“Can we just pick up a rose and shove some of Selene’s Tear into it?” Gwen asked.

“Maybe. We’ll have to test whether that works.”

“You’ve got the elven magic handled, then, right?” Nasha asked, looking down at the book. “Seems like there are more conditions for making the portal too. Setting up a ritual site, someone reciting the incantation, a full moon overhead—”

“I don’t have elven magic,” Veronica said, cutting her off.

“What?” Gwen asked, tilting her head. “What do you mean you don’t?”

Veronica flipped to the next page. Most of the text was further instruction, along with a bestiary of the living creatures on the island.

Apparently, unicorns roamed there?

“I learned Vitian magic. Not elven. I don’t know any elven magic.”

“Why didn’t you learn?” Nasha asked, finally lifting her head from Veronica’s shoulder.

“Debilitating curse, remember?” Veronica said, closing the book. “When I was sent to Annesheim, I only learned Vitian-styled magic. I had no time to learn elven magic, especially since I was alone. There was no one to teach me.”

“Then do we need to recruit another temporary elf for this?” Gwen asked.

“Probably. Though… elves don’t exactly like dark elves. We’ll have to trouble a dark elf for this matter.”

“Then what now?” Nasha asked. “What’s the plan? Focus on gathering all these materials and go to Avalon?”

“For the immediate moment, let’s all sit down. I’ll need to scour this book a bit more and have Sage record all the information, since we can’t check out books from this floor. They’re too ancient. Gwen can look through her spellbooks in the meantime.”

“Sounds good to me,” Gwen said, already moving past them, heading toward the study section.

“What about me?” Nasha asked.

Veronica shrugged. “Maybe find a cultivation manual or martial technique? There are probably some left behind by past masters here.”

Nasha crossed her arms, tilting her head slightly as if weighing the options.

“Sorry,” Veronica added. “I only really know magic. I’m a bit lost when it comes to helping with aura-related matters. There are a few people I know who could teach you and help you get stronger, but they’re far out of reach at the moment. There are a few things I can actually help you out with in the future—just not now.”

Nasha smiled at the sincerity. “It’s fine. I knew I’d become deadweight eventually if I joined an archmage. I’ll make sure to train so I stay on par with the two of you.”

She turned and began walking down the aisles, searching the shelves for suitable books.
