Rowler’s team was dead.

Arthur felt a flicker of grim satisfaction. Not triumph. Not remorse. Just the cold, clean feeling of taking out the trash. A box checked on a list that only ever grew longer.

The princess and her guard, Ysara and Yudursh, were a contained problem. Tucked away in the silence of the spatial ring. His mind, a machine honed by a lifetime of such moments, had already moved on.

Next objective: Copperpine.

Ysara had called it the nearest bastion of civilization. To Arthur, that meant it was a new cage with more sophisticated bars. Trust was a currency he’d gone bankrupt on long ago. Her words, even bound by a soul pact, had to be audited.

He erased his presence from the clearing. Footprints were scuffed into ambiguity, the faint scent of blood masked with crushed leaves. He set a new course based on the princess’s intelligence and melted back into the wilderness.

“So that’s your grand strategy?” Drogan’s voice dripped with a new, more profound level of disgust. “You didn’t even have the decency to kill them yourself. You just let your pets out of their cage and went for a walk. I’ve seen more honor in a junkyard dogfight. My legacy is now tied to a glorified zookeeper.”

Arthur didn't bother to reply, internally or otherwise. Honor was a luxury for men who didn’t have to worry about where their next meal was coming from. It was a pretty word for fighting stupid. His method had been clean, efficient, and left him untouched. That wasn't cowardice. It was victory. Let the Skullbreaker whine about his legacy; Arthur was busy building his own.

He continued tracing the spine of a low ridge when he saw it. A faint, unnatural line on the horizon. A grey slash against the endless green. An hour later, the line resolved itself into a colossal structure of stone.

The walls of Copperpine.

He slid to a halt at the edge of the forest, shielded by the trunk of a massive tree. A single, hard thump of satisfaction echoed in his chest. Ysara hadn't lied. The city was real.

Relief was a potent wave, so intense it was almost sickening. He crushed it before it could take root.

The first truth is often the bait for the biggest lie. A voice from his past life. His own.

The walls were magnificent. They dwarfed the crude fortifications of Darvaza, rising a hundred feet into the air. Crafted from immense blocks of grey stone, they stretched left and right until they disappeared into the curve of the earth. Along the top, at regular intervals, were the unmistakable silhouettes of Guardians. Ants on a mountain. Their presence was a clear declaration: this was a place of order, of power.

A place that did not suffer the uninvited.

For the rest of the day, he moved like a phantom along the forest's edge. A shadow flitting between ancient trees. He used Mana Sight intermittently, the world dissolving into a vibrant tapestry of energy signatures. The walls were inert. The Guardians were steady, disciplined flames of mana, their patrol routes precise. He mapped their positions, their rotation times. Searched for a blind spot. A weakness.

Found none.

This was a professional force.

His scouting eventually led him to the city's main gate, a monstrous affair of reinforced ironwood and steel. Arthur found a perch high in the branches of a gnarled oak. Concealed. A clear, if distant, view.

Not long after he settled in, the gates groaned open. He caught his first glimpse. Breathtaking. Curved domes, tiled archways. Medieval Persia, maybe. The city stretched on forever, a sea of intricate design.

The sight sparked something. Yearning. A hot bath. A real meal. A bed that wasn’t dirt. It was a beautiful, dangerous temptation.

A weakness.

He crushed it, locking it away in the same mental vault where he kept his grief. His survival depended not on satisfying his wants, but on controlling them.

His thoughts drifted to the crest Ysara had given him, tucked away in his ring. The simplest solution. A princess's seal would surely grant him entry. But the risks were astronomical.

Arthur ignored Drogan. His mind was a maelstrom of calculation and memory. The airstrip in the Alps. The falling snow. The look in Rachel’s eyes just before she pulled the trigger. That final agony was the lens through which he viewed everything now. Trusting Ysara’s crest would be like accepting a key from the person who helped build your prison.

A key might open a door. It could also lead to a more secure cell.

For all he knew, the moment he presented it, an alarm would sound. He’d be surrounded before he could blink. The soul pact prevented her from directly harming him, but how could he trust a royal who saw slavery as a natural part of her world? Could she have passed it on with a coded warning? Detain on sight. For his own protection.

He wouldn't bet his freedom on the fine print of a magical contract. That path was the absolute last resort. A key to be used only when the house was burning down around him.

No. He would find another way. A better way.

The way of the spy.

He would be patient. He would be observant. He would find a crack in their perfect system.

And so he began his vigil.

Over the next three days, he watched and learned the rhythm of the gate. The traffic was sparse and strictly controlled. Large caravans, pulled by hulking, six-legged bison, moved raw timber in and finished goods out, but there were no individual travelers. The only other movement came from hunting parties, who moved with the easy confidence of those who owned the land they walked on. Upon their return, each party was met by a Guardian with a ledger who collected a "Hunter's Tithe", a portion of their kills in cores, hides, and fangs.

The implications were clear. He couldn't just pose as a hunter. They would expect a history. A registration. An identity. An outsider would be spotted instantly.

As the sun set on the third day, bathing the colossal walls in hues of orange and purple, Arthur settled deeper into the shadows, his mind a silent storm of plots. The city was a puzzle.

And he had all the time in the world to solve it.
