The legion forward scouts had arrived and found their column. The group that had found them was led by Magnus, and Grisim—it seemed—was not a fan of his fellow scout and former captive.

“What’s happening here?” Mettius said, taking command of the situation.

Grisim had his blade—a mundane one, not the magical one which had been left in the camp as he’d gone out scouting—drawn and Magnus stood ten feet back with his own out.

“This bastard left us for dead!” Grisim said, pointing with his sword. “He killed the guard and then just left us there.”

Mettius and Magnus shared a knowing look.

“Put the sword down Gris,” Caleb said, sensing there was more to this all. “He’s the only reason we were able to find you.”

“I wouldn’t have needed you to find me if he’d let me escape!”

I thought you were about to escape on your own and we ruined it,” Kelia pointed out.

The banter began to diffuse the situation as Grisim’s bravado warred with his anger.

“Let’s have this talk less publicly,” Mettius said.

Mettius quickly filled the scouts in on the situation in the village, and they relayed the information back using a magical scrying communication device. Caleb heard Canus’ voice coming through from the other end, and the Centurion didn’t sound pleased with the news. Caleb suspected them abandoning the camp in the middle of the night hadn’t gone over so well and was against some legion regulation.

Once that was done, they continued back toward the approaching legion, the three equites, Magnus and Grisim walking far ahead to discuss matters.

“Magnus was following his standing orders,” Mettius said as if that was enough to end the discussion.

“Which are what? Be a coward?” Grisim asked, some of his fire returning.

“Private,” Mettius said, “But he did what he had to to get the news back to the legion which was all of your orders. You and all of these villagers were expendable if it meant getting news back to the legion.”

Grisim stared hard and Magnus, who looked back unflappably.

“Why’d they just send you three anyway?” Grisim asked, seemingly only realizing then that the legion was missing.

Before they could answer, Magnus spoke,

“Officially, Centurion Canus sent them out as an advance force to clear the path of these error monsters. Unfortunately events outside their control lead for them to advance on the enemy.”

“You guys snuck out?” Grisim asked, for once putting two and two together to get four.

All of them shot him a glare.

Canus, was waiting for the five of them in his tent when they arrived. They reached the legion as it was  making its castra for the night, and all around legionnaires were at work using their system enhanced strength.

“Out,” Canus said, firmly to everyone else in the tent as they entered.

The tribunes, decurions and other aids all filed out at his command.

Caleb was about to begin speaking to apologize but Canus cut him off.

“I’ll be speaking,” Canus said. “You will listen. Though you don’t seem to be very good at that. If it were up to me and this situation less complicated, I’d have you three disciplined. But, that is not the world we live in. Since I can’t discipline you, I will have to recover this situation to not make it known you disobeyed orders. So, officially, you were sent out as an advance scouting group to clear the path for the army as Magnus informed you already. You were unavoidably put into conflict with the enemy and had to push the attack lest the legion lose the element of surprise.”

Everyone was quiet, waiting for permission to speak.

“Understood?” he asked, receiving nods, but Caleb had a puzzled look.

“What is it?”

He hesitated before asking, “Why is it complicated?”

“Because I killed my sister,” Mettius said.

“Right,” Canus said.

“Why does that get us off the hook?” Caleb asked.

“Because Centurion Canus doesn’t want to involve himself in the chaos that is the succession. He could reprimand us, but that could be seen as him trying to stack the cards against me. This happens whenever an heir is killed. Everyone tangentially involved does whatever they can to stay clear of it.”

Canus nodded with a grimace.

“Well craters,” Caleb cursed, putting more pieces together. “If being tangentially involved is so bad, how bad is cutting her in half?”

Mettius looked sheepish.

“It’s… going to be a problem. Thankfully you didn’t get the achievement—some of my siblings can detect those—but we are going to want to minimize your involvement in all of this.”

“What do we do now?” Caleb asked.

“What you should have done the whole time,” Canus said. “Obey orders. I’ve sent word to the Legate. He’s on his way here himself with two more centuries of the Legion. He may take a different view on recent actions.”

The group then gave a more formal debrief to Canus, outlining everything they saw. Aides were allowed back in to take notes.

“So you believe the Benefactor to be gone?” Canus asked the group.

They all nodded—the aids made notes of the nods.

“If I could make initialized, I’d be on the move constantly making more,” Caleb said, then quickly added. “If I were crazy and wanted to overthrow the empire—which I don’t.”

Caleb wasn’t the biggest fan of the empire—and was significantly less so now the more he learned about the succession—but he’d never harbored thoughts of becoming a rebel.

“It seems likely the rest of the villages up this valley are compromised,” Canus said, agreeing with the Caleb’s assessment. “We will advance forward through the valley and clear them of the rebels.”

Caleb feared this would be the plan and his heart dropped, though he didn’t see any alternative. If the other villages were like Frost Valley, then there were captives waiting to be rescued.

Canus dismissed them all and on the way out Caleb pulled Mettius aside.

“I wanted to thank you,” Caleb said.

“What for?”

“For coming with. You didn’t have to, and we wouldn’t have gotten out without you.”

“You don’t owe me anything. I was using you as much as helping. I knew Cassandra was out here somewhere and I thought the darkness magic Magnus reported was her.”

Caleb really wanted to know why he felt so compelled to kill her, but didn’t dare ask.

“Why was she out here?” Caleb asked instead.

“She was sent to deal with the rebels, but the Benefactor obviously won her to his side with the promise of power,” Mettius explained. “Standard procedure is to send out a group of initialized to quell rebellions quietly. The Legion gets sent in only if they fail.”

“’Standard procedure?’ How often are there rebellions?”

“Too often.”

The next morning, the legion broke camp and the refugees joined in with the camp followers in the train as they marched to Frost Valley. Caleb had been seen to by the legion’s initialized Surgeon who, while unable to magical heal wounds could do the next best thing, making him able to walk with minimal pain his ingot enhanced constitution could handle until his body accelerated the healing. They found the abandoned and stripped of everything of value to a war effort that could be carried away in a night. They got to work improving the fortifications of the village in the event that this was a ruse, and the mine was sealed shut to guard against an ambush from below. The plan was to push through the valley. The dark archers might have been formidable against a legion of initialized, but the hundred Legionaries and their ten Decurions would make quick work of them.

I made quick work of them, Caleb reflected.

Killing those archers had been too easy once he could find them. He found he had no remorse for what he did to Cassandra and her team, but the corrupted villagers had been hardly an obstacle. They were initialized—even if they’d become so through strange means—and they’d been like children to his strength.

He gained a new appreciation for holding the equites back in battle. He could carve through an army of the uninitialized with ease. He’d practiced manipulating metal on one of the auxiliary soldiers and found hardly any resistance at all to his willpower. If he was faced against a mundane army—which were the norm, the Thousand Blade Legion being the glaring exception—he’d be able to wipe them out alone.

Caleb tried to distract himself from the coming battle with work. He commandeered one of the abandoned smithies, Flavius taking another of the forges within and lost himself in the mundane work of a smith. That was until he’d completed all of Flavius’ work and was left with nothing.

Bog, content as ever to peruse a new stock of metallic treats, lounged curled around the forge, luxuriating in its heat as he chewed on pick axe heads. These were not the earth aspected ones of their cavern forge, but cold aspected ones from the local ore.

“I guess I should probably fix you,” Caleb said, looking at his blade.

The edge had rolled badly where it had struck Cassandra’s plate.

He went over to the grind stone he’d used to sharpen the mundane auxiliary blades and immediately had a problem. The sword skipped off the stone, cutting a chip off of it.

“You’re going to need one of the special grind stones for that,” Drusus said.

“Special grind stones?” Caleb echoed.

“Metal aspected stones that are magically hardened—somehow,” he explained. “The quarter master has them. They use them on the thousand blades. They rarely need to get sharpened but when they do, nothing else can do it.”

Caleb considered this, wondering how he’d sharpened the edge to begin with. Master Dimel must have had special grind stones in his personal forge, but not made a point of sharing the details with Caleb.

I wonder… Caleb thought, recalling his thoughts on his new Sunder skill.

He moved over to the forge and threw the blade in, recruiting Bog to stoke the flame for him as he focused on the metal.

He held the blade and sent his senses through it, and activated the skill Sunder, focusing on the heat of the forge and willing it to concentrate on the rolled edge. The edge grew red how immediately, the rest of the blade sending all the heat to follow his will.

He pulled the blade out, with the edge glowing red hot as the rest of the blade was only a dull glow.

Drusus let out sounds of surprise at the speed at which the blade had heated—and this surprise was calibrated to the already high esteem he held Caleb’s magic in. He was further surprised to see the glow concentrated on the edge.

Caleb brought the blade to his anvil, and brought his hammer down on it, once more using Sunder to focus his blow. He directed all of the force to push the rolled edge away from the blade, flatting it back out instead of rolling it back in on itself.

He kept at it, heating and hammering, manually replicating his sunder with his Imperium as his vortex kept the air separated to keep the corrosive oxygen away from the blade.

Sunder has increased to level 2.

Caleb smiled at the increase and continued his work. As he hammered, his increased perception revealed more mana within the blade he’d not detected nor hammered out during its forging, and he pushed it out now, making the aspect ever so purer.

And then, he was done, and the blade was ready for quenching. He pull out his quenching steel and immediately realized the problem.

It no longer glowed. Whatever magic had given it an insatiable hunger for heat was not gone, and he was left with a useless steel powder.
