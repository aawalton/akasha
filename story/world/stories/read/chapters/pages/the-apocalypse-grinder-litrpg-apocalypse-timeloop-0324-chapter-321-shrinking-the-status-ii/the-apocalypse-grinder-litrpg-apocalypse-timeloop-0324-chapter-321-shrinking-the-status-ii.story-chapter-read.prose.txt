In the end, it had taken sacrificing almost all of the remaining ‘fluff’ skills he’d acquired to push Surging Strikes to the Celestial rarity, but it was definitely worth the high cost. The skill now had a ridiculous damage multiplier on top of a heavily discounted activation cost, meaning that any opponents besides bosses on the level of the kaiju types he’d fought in past iterations would be erased without struggle.

Even at level 0 he had enough stats to rival an early boss like Magriz’al the Crazed, thanks to the improved base stats combined with improvements like Resourceful. The only problem is that I still can’t imagine myself even leaving a single scratch on that strange man. It was a problem to be solved another time. He was fairly sure that as long as he avoided Serenaeth he wouldn’t summon judgment from another dimension. It was annoying, as he was sure there were benefits to be had from a relationship with the sentient tree, but for the time being Ronan wasn’t strong enough to shoulder the consequences that accompanied them.

The other gains were the enchanting skill that allowed him to apply pretty much any enchantment or combination of enchantments to his weapons and attacks, as well as Dimensional Strike, which allowed Ronan to hit enemies with melee attacks from long distance as though he were standing right in striking range.

Ronan’s gaze drifted to the Temporal Obelisk. It was far larger now than it had been the first time he entered the Chronos Clan’s Ancestral Grounds. Attempting to get a handle on distance and size in that place was nigh impossible, but that much he could tell, if only because it now towered over the Chronos Forge by two or three times what it used to. He hadn’t obtained a breathtaking amount of merits during the last hundred or so iterations, mostly because his goal had been to simply accumulate skills and masteries rather than push his limits, but he might be able to afford a decent upgrade. He made his way over and opened up the obelisk menu to see what would be feasible, if anything.

Ronan looked through the list and thought about his options. He could afford multiple tiers for a few of the cheaper enhancements, but they wouldn’t make him that much stronger. His best bet was to either continue saving for a third sub-class—one of the largest power-ups he could obtain—or to grab a third tier in Resourceful and then save for the sub-class. After some brief thinking he decided to go with the second route. Going from the second to the third tier of Resourceful was a huge increase to his health, stamina, and mana that was well worth the 10 merit cost. After that, saving for another sub-class was just the logical thing to do.

You have purchased the permanent enhancement [Resourceful] for 10 chronos merits! Resourceful has increased from Tier II to Tier III

A quick check let Ronan know that the fourth tier of Resourceful would cost him 15 chronos merits. That wasn’t a prohibitive cost given the benefits, but merits were a lot harder to earn at that point than they had in his earlier iterations, not to mention that it would make saving for another sub-class a longer endeavour.

Once he had made his purchase he exited the obelisk menu and took in the scenery one final time. His cousin—it felt weird to call her that given they’d only met for about ten seconds—assured him that it would be fine once she’d dealt with the strange man, but he was still nervous to begin the next iteration. If another powerful enemy arrived, he would be powerless to stop them from doing whatever they wanted to him. Nothing ventured, nothing gained, I suppose. Time to try and conquer the nightmare difficulty tutorial.
