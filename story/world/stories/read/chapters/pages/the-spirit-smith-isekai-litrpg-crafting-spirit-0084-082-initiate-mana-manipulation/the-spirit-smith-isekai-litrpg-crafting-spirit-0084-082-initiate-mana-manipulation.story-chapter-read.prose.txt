For the first time in a while, I sink deeply into meditation. This sort of meditation is different from the normal way that I use to regenerate resources. I go deeper, letting the world around me fade into vague nothingness with only Luma to stand watch for any threats. Though I also have Survival Instincts now, which should work despite my meditative state.

I do not stop there though, and go even deeper. The world fades completely away, and I enter a space like I have been in only once before. It was back when I was imprisoned by a man. I can’t seem to remember his name for some reason. But that doesn’t matter. The space is tied to my Spiritual Resonance skill, I believe. When I first did it, I was basically brand new to this world. But now, I have come to know a few more things.

For one, I now recognize this place as my spirit. To put it simply, I have somehow figured out how to transport my mind inside my spirit. Which is quite strange to think about, but I am no philosopher. This space is filled with nothing but empty darkness, but I can sense that I have some presence here.

If my mind is in here, then I should be able to do things, right? One of those things is to tap into my spirit, and that is likely how I need to develop and use spirit magic. But if I can use spirit magic in here, why can’t I use mana-based magic? Furthermore, as this place is sort of inside me, would it not make it far easier to get whatever truths of magic I need to understand? Or so my theory goes.

Spirit magic definitely needs to wait until I am more knowledgeable about magic in general. I wouldn’t want to fuck up my spirit somehow. But I fail to see how a bit of normal magic can damage my spirit. Thus, I turn my attention to where it all began, Water Ball.

The spell is as simple as it gets. Summon some water in a roughly ball shape. It doesn’t even launch it or do anything else fancy like that. Though there is a version of the spell that does that, I saw a scroll for it once. Despite it being such a simple spell, I cannot understand a single bit of the incantation.

When I trigger the spell through the system, it simply makes me say the words somehow. They are definitely words though, there is that distinct sense of language underneath them. Yet it is like each spell sounds completely different, especially across affinities and even when other people cast the same spell.

Valdir did mention that understanding of this magical language was unique to every person. Which honestly just sounds unnecessarily complicated. Couldn’t the goddess have just made it nice and simple? Oddly enough, as I think these things, I can feel something inside me. Or around me more like. I am going to take that to mean I am heading in some direction that is hopefully the right one. Because honestly, I have no fucking clue what im doing.

My old world had magic, but I never trained to be a mage. I doubt that would help much here either, as this world’s magic is different in almost every way. The only similarity is mana. That seems to be something though, no matter the spell, it is simply some sort of method to shape mana to do something. Well, spirit spells obviously use spirit instead, but I think spirit is technically just replacing mana in that sort of situation. For instance, if I really wanted to, I could probably cast Spirit Link with mana instead.

That is a whole other side tangent though. Mana is perhaps not the answer, but it is certainly one of them. I focus for a moment, and gather up some mana into my hand. Then I move that mana down to my feet, and then put it back into my corestone. It is a very strange sort of thing, unlike stamina, it is like the mana doesn’t exist. There is no real force hindering me as I try to move it around my channels. On one hand, this makes it really easy to give spells lots of mana really quickly. But on the other hand, it makes it a lot harder to get a grasp of what mana actually is.

Obviously mana is important, it is found in literally everything in this world. It is the reason for things being magical in the first place. I guess it would be accurate to say that mana is like the essence of magic. The power source behind all of the strange stuff in this world.

But if that is true, why is it so hard to use? Most people rely on the system to do anything with magic. Be it crafting, or using spells. From what I understand, even higher level magics still rely on the system to do the manipulation of the mana. Now, this is quite a wild theory. But I theorize that the system isn’t actually necessary to do any of this. Valdir has sort of alluded to that fact too.

The system makes things convenient. But one still has to understand how to do it in the first place. It merely takes that understanding and does some magic bullshittery to make it easier to control, and in a weird way, understand.

I think this is why smithing has come to me so quickly in this new world, despite it being completely different. Despite its oddities, the system is merely converting my own personal understanding into whatever effects it thinks suits them best. If I apply this same logic to magic, it all makes sense.

To put it all simply, I am struggling to reach higher tiers of magic because I do not understand it. Magic is a foreign thing to me, something kept at the fringes of society, or only used to make pieces of equipment. In this world, everything is magic, even the youngest child can understand it. Once again, I find myself in a situation where I am weaker than a child.

But that just means it is about damn time I grow up and figure it out. I am certain the key lies in the magical language. The question is, what is the magical language even doing? I say some funny words, pour some mana out of my body, and shit starts to happen. Perhaps the words act as some sort of converter? But that doesn’t really make sense, as isn’t that what runic magic is? From what I can tell, runic magic also has its own sort of language, but it is one clearly more sensible and learnable. Almost like someone is just telling the mana what to do and what to become.

Spells are different, they are stranger and can do absurd things that runic magic never could. Like mass teleporting an entire region, for instance. So maybe instead of telling mana what to do, spells are more like asking? Like saying “mana, please blow this monster up pretty please?” One could get far more varied results that way, but also more powerful ones.

A warmth seems to spread through my body as I think about that last bit. The feeling all around me intensifies too. It is like the air is charged with something, and it feels wonderful. Maybe it is some sort of enlightenment or something. I am inside my spirit, after all, so perhaps I am somehow feeling the effects of my understanding of magic improving?

I sigh, it seems every hint of an answer only begets more and more questions. I am close to something though. Yet, I am missing a key part. Something that should be obvious, that even a child of this world would understand.

For a long while, I just stand there and just let my thoughts do whatever they want. I am able to remember every bit of my journey thanks to my enhanced memory. Though perhaps that is not such a good thing, for it means I also remember every injury, every bit of pain, and bad things too.

As I remember, I truly do realize just how pervasive magic is. Mana touches every aspect of my daily life. From the moment I wake up and eat magical food, to the moment I go to sleep while thinking of how to get stronger. After who knows how long, I realize something.

Not all mana is equal. For instance, light-affinitied mana is not the same as water-affinitied mana. The mana that ends up forming my domain is different too, some sort of Orn-affinitied mana I think. Is there even such a thing as truly neutral mana? My resource pool would be one guess, but when I think about it, that is certainly connected to me in some way. There is a reason why someone can’t just rip the mana out of my corestone and use it, after all.

Perhaps… mana is not a single energy. Rather, it is more like the result of something else. Desire, perhaps, or maybe some sort of fundamental concept. Or in other words, using mana is not transforming it into something else. Rather, it is just realizing the potential that mana already possesses. Spells are just a way to change the potential into something else a bit more useful.

For instance, my Water Ball spell is taking the mana I give it, and altering it into water mana. Then it is releasing whatever that mana is, and creating an effect. I guess it would be similar to igniting a fire. In the end, the fire is a fire, but depending on the fuel used, the properties of the fire itself can be vastly changed. For instance, wood is hard to ignite, but burns for a very long time. Whereas oils are quick to ignite, and burn without as much smoke, but they often don’t last as long.

If I take this idea, and apply it to mana… well it all makes a lot more sense. Mana is merely a fuel, and by changing it around a bit, you get different effects when you ‘activate’ it. Unlike spells, runic magic must do this more mechanically. Rather than changing the quality of the mana, they change its shape, forcing it to fill the shape of runes such that it has whatever desired effect.

In comparison, the magical language that forms spells causes a shift in the mana, changing it to inherently produce whatever effect. This would also explain why it is easier for me to cast light magic compared to water magic. Whatever Orn-mana is, it must be closer to light mana than water mana. I can use both, because I understand both, and thus my mana contains some amount of both. But light magic is both more intuitive, and faster to cast, as my mana has to be shifted less. In contrast, for aspects that I do not understand, there is no trace of it within my mana for a spell to latch onto. Hence why I cannot cast spells revolving around lightning, or wind, or fire, etcetera.

My body moves for the first time in a while as I stand up. The empty blackness of my spirit stretches out all around me. With total focus, I ever so slowly start to incant Water Ball. As I do, I activate Energy Sense and focus on the mana as it moves through my body, and then leaves it as it forms the spell.

I didn't notice anything on the first try. But, I am certain that what I am looking for is real. So I try again, and again, and again. Hundreds of spells later, I finally get a glimpse of it.

The moment my mana leaves my body, it shifts. It is very slight, and the mana is almost instantly converted into the spell. But I am certain that this is what I have been looking for.

Whatever the magical language is, it does not harness mana or anything like that. It is simply a method to change the mana ever so slightly, such that it can be ‘activated’ to produce a certain effect, a spell. Now that I’ve seen it, it is like my entire body lights up with something. Like a fundamental understanding of mana and thus magic itself is taking root within me.

The feeling is euphoric, to say the least. This must be the sort of feeling that people spend millennia searching to find. While I cannot say I fully understand… I think I might just be starting to grasp a part of that.

However, this understanding has only made it all the more clear just how much I still do not know. In typical fashion, I may have found an answer, but it only gives me so many more questions to ask.
