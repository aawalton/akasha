Chapter 92

Riley did not answer Tarin in the yard. She turned to Thorne and told him to take the man directly to the tower meeting room. Thorne nodded and moved. The two soldiers went with him, one on each of Tarin's arms, and the four of them passed through the inner doorway and were inside the tower before any of the Hoshin still being guided toward the bunkhouses had time to register that anything was happening at all. From the outside it would have looked like a small detail of soldiers escorting a man who was tired and possibly ill. Riley wanted it to look like that.

She waited a moment more in the yard before she followed. Long enough for Tarin to be inside and out of sight. Long enough that anyone watching from the gate would have seen her walk away from the new arrivals at her normal pace. She headed towards the tower and where Nikola was waiting for her.

He had a folded paper in one hand and the look of a man who had been standing there longer than he wanted to. He stepped forward as soon as he saw her.

"I know you have something else right now," he said. "This will not take you long."

"Go ahead."

She paused with a hand on the door, half turned towards Nikola.

"The southern cutting crews are nearly a full league out now. The haul teams are burning daylight. We're losing the better part of every afternoon to the round trip and the wood is stacking up in the cut zone instead of coming back to the yard. Landryn and I have talked about it, and he thinks if we pull a detail off the wall and put them on stump removal, then pack the cleared run with the broken stone we have lying behind the quarry, the haul teams could move at twice the speed. The horses can pull on packed ground. They can't pull on the stumps."

"How many men do you need?"

"Forty for the first pass. We should have them trained out of the barracks in a short time. They were earmarked for the Hoshin swap."

"Take them. Coordinate with Landryn on which sections he wants cleared first."

"Do you want all the logistics first, or shall I just begin?"

"Just begin. You have been thinking about it. I trust your read on it."

Nikola paused for a moment, the way a man pauses when he has been given more than he expected, then gave a nod.

"Yes, Warden."

"One more thing, I want you to return in about 10 mins or so. Enter the meeting room and apologise for the interruption. Look to me to give a signal of a head nod, once you see that you tell the room that we are short on all resources and we need to meet right away because production has stopped. Do you understand?"

"Yes Warden, you know as well as I do we need yet another silo, yes?"

"Yes, and I need you to sell this to the room, if needed."

"I understand."

"We are long overdue for a meeting, that has moved to the top of my to do list."

Nikola gave a single nod and he turned and walked briskly across the yard, and Riley turned before he had left her presence and went through the tower door. By the time she reached the door of the meeting room she had put the cutting crews and the haul teams out of her mind entirely.

She pushed the door open and entered the meeting room without looking at anyone. Tarin was already seated at the long side of the table, hands flat on the wood where they could be seen, the two soldiers at the door behind him. He had pushed his hood back. The tunic he had come in on still hung on him, plain and a little wrong on his shoulders, the kind of garment a man wears when he is borrowing the costume of someone he is not. His face was tired, but his eyes were not.

Riley walked the length of the room and pulled out the chair at the head of the table and sat down. Thorne moved from the door with her and took up his position behind her left shoulder. He did not sit or speak, he only watched Tarin like a hawk.

Riley did not greet Tarin. She set her hands on the table on either side of the closed scroll and she looked at him.

"Tea cart in Rivermark, are you here to peddle your latest brew?"

Tarin half smiled. "Hardly."

"When last we met, the silver I paid you for a name…"

"Edrin Kavos," Tarin said.

"Yes, Edrin Kavos. You leaned in close enough that I could see the dust on your collar and you told me he was the only man in the realm who could make a portal, and that if I wanted to know how I had arrived here, he was the man to find."

"I remember it."

"You are a peddler of more than tea; your secondary wares seem to be information. So if it isn't tea then what information are you here to sell me?"

Tarin took a breath through his nose and let it out. He did not look at the soldiers behind him. He did not look at Thorne, he looked only at Riley.

"I have served King Darius Locke of Grey Ridge for years," he said. "I am his man in Rivermark. I have been his man in Rivermark since long before you walked through the town the first time."

Riley did not move. The name Darius Locke was a sound she had not heard before. She turned it over in her head once, then narrowed her eyes. The kingdom she knew about. Zelgra had named it across a tavern table in Rivermark, if not for Grey Ridge holding the boundary the south would already be under their heel, and Garron had named it on the bench of his caravan, that's why I prefer the way Grey Ridge rules, you steal you're punished, simple. Two passing references, a kingdom holding a border. She had not thought about the man who ran it, but she thought about him now.

"Go on."

"My king is the most patient man I have ever served and the most thorough." Tarin paused only long enough to make sure she was still listening. "He has known about you for some time."

"Define some time."

"Long enough."

"Define known."

"He knew your tower had awakened before you reached Rivermark the second time. He knew about you and the Hoshin before you went there the second time. He has known the size of your settlement, the rate of its growth, and the names of every senior person within these walls."

Riley took that in without moving her hands from the table.

"And the silver I gave you."

"Was returned to my king's accounts the next morning."

"And the name you whispered."

"Was an instruction. I was told to put the name of Edrin Kavos into your hands at a moment of your need and to report back to my king what you did with it. You went to the magic shop. You spoke to Alistair Crowe. You did not pursue Kavos further. My king found that interesting. He has been watching you ever since."

Riley let that thought settle for a moment, it had real weight. A king she had never heard of had been measuring her since before she had walls high enough to keep raiders out. The whisper at the tea cart had not been a kindness. It had been a test, and she had been too tired and too new to recognize it as one. Her face did not change. Behind her, she could feel Thorne's stillness. She kept her eyes on Tarin.

"All right," she said. "What does he want me to know."

"Two things." Tarin's hands had not moved on the wood since he sat down. "The first is that the Clawborn have marched their largest army north."

She did not let her face change at that either, but the inside of her chest moved.

"Where?"

"Into the frozen valley above the divide. My king's scouts have confirmed. Empress Octavia has committed her main force to something in that valley. It is unclear as to what it is. Edrin Kavos has been seen in their company and the mage does not move for small matters. Whatever is in the valley, the Empress is either going to take it or going to be taken by it, and she has sent everything she has to settle the question."

"How long?"

"How long, what?"

"How long until they come back?"

"Unclear. A campaign of that size cannot be quick. Weeks at the soonest, possibly longer," Tarin's eyes hardened, "possibly not at all."

Riley let her shoulders drop half an inch, while she looked down at the table for a brief moment, then looked back at Tarin.

"And the second thing?"

"This is also why my king has sent me now. The window the Clawborn have opened by marching north is a window for him too. With Octavia distracted, Grey Ridge can move pieces it could not move before. You are one of those pieces. My king wants to know where your interests lie."
