The next morning, Ferris finally released Jade from the fasting experiment. He tested the slime on the last of his recorded information, and Jade was able to recall everything for the full three days. With a triumphant bounce, Jade was ready to head to breakfast and eat a triple helping of Mother Lily’s cooking.

Ferris stopped them at the door.

“Jade,” he said, “do you want to continue fasting?”

The slime was unhappy with the question, bursting out in bubbles.

“You said it was over today! We are going to breakfast!”

“Yes,” he said, “but you don’t have to eat. At least, we don’t know how much you need. We could continue limiting it. Keep more of your memories day to day.”

The bubbles turned into swirls.

“Why?” Jade asked. “Eating is more fun.”

Ferris sighed, then opened the door. Jade enthusiastically rolled out.

The conversation reminded him of the slime’s lack of aversion to pain or danger. Slimes experienced the world different than him. To Jade, the memory problem wasn’t much of a problem.

It’s hard, he thought, but not eating isn’t a real solution anyway. Jade needs to be able to fight and defend themselves. They like eating. I need to take this knowledge and turn it into a better solution.

At breakfast, Jade was so happy. Ferris couldn’t help feeling sad as he watched the slime enthusiastically consume each bite. His familiar had remembered everything for the last three days, but now it was like watching the decline of a friend.

After they ate, Ferris fed Jade their morning straw. At least they’d have a full Digest River that night. It had been hard to see it empty the last few days.

With that chore finished, Ferris left the city walls to go wait outside near the rift gates for the team to arrive from Bluereef Bastion. Once again, he got to watch the delvers come and go through the gates. There were so many people, with so much variety in arms and armor, that he didn’t even recognize any of the groups from last time.

After about an hour of sitting on the grass, lazily watching delvers, the team from home appeared. Uncle Winton once again noticed Ferris right away and waved with a big grin.

Ferris stood, called Jade up into his arms, and then walked over into Uncle Winton’s giant hug. The man lifted him up off the ground, with his crutch and Jade both taken along for the ride.

“Ferris, good to see you my boy,” Uncle Winton said as he put him back down, “and you as well Jade.”

“Welcome back, Uncle,” Ferris replied after he regained his standing. Jade rippled happily, remembering Uncle Winton positively—although not remembering why.

The four other delvers from home watched the reunion with small smiles, but they didn’t say anything. Ferris knew of them, although he hadn’t spoken to any of them personally. Ferris was pretty sure the thin guy on the right was related to a neighbor, yet he wasn’t sure.

Their group returned to the city, passing through the gate at the walls without any trouble. Once again, the other delvers broke off on their own business, and Uncle Winton took Ferris and Jade to the same tea house. They were given a different room this time, although it was still on the third floor.

After casting the same misty water privacy spell, Uncle Winton turned to Ferris.

“Now,” the man said, pulling a short rectangular box out of his soul storage, “first order of business: I have some presents for Jade.”

The man opened the lid to display twelve more boxes inside. He took the first out and slid away its top to reveal… an eyeball.

“The eye of a yellow-spotted leaper. They are a tier one monster, and their eyes are known to have uses in alchemy. Not an exceedingly rare or hard-to-find component, but still magical.”

Uncle Winton picked up the eyeball by its stalk and tossed it to Jade. The slime ate it, and then burst into a symphony of ripples larger than any Ferris had seen since he fed the slime the magical berries. Perhaps larger.

“Ten out of ten,” Jade immediately sent over the bond, “what is this? Is there more? I want more!”

“It is the eye of a monster from the rifts,” Ferris explained, “my uncle called it a yellow-spotted leaper, but I haven’t heard of them before.”

Jade swirled for a second before responding.

“Eyes are those two things in human heads?” Jade wasn’t great at understanding anatomy that slimes didn’t have, like eyes… or almost anything else. Communication over the bond did come with contextual thoughts, and Ferris was usually able to send an impression of what something was along with the word. “Many eyes around here, can we go get more?” Jade sent back images from the walk-through town of all the… eyeballs… of the people on the street.

“No,” Ferris said, a bit concerned at the thought of Jade rolling around trying to eat people’s eyes. “We can only eat monsters from the rift, never people. These are special, anyway. Not all eyeballs are like this. Uncle Winton brought it just for you to try.”

Jade rippled happily, then rolled over to Ferris’ uncle.

Watching the slime, the man placed a hand near the ground. Jade happily climbed up onto it, and Uncle Winton lifted them into his arms. With one hand, he held the slime, while the other took out another box.

“Not sure if this one has a name,” the man said, pulling a bright red leaf out of the box, “likely it does to someone, but we just collect and sell them based on their magical potential rating.” He fed the leaf to Jade, happily rippling in the man’s arm.

“Leaf seven out of ten.” Jade judged, and Ferris took his journal out of a pocket to begin recording the various scores. He also relayed the score to Uncle Winton, who smiled slightly with a thoughtful look.

They worked their way through the other ten items. Each one had been collected in a tier one rift and would typically be worth selling or using as a minor resource for personal cultivation. Nothing was incredibly valuable, as far as Ferris could tell, but it was all far above what he could typically feed Jade.

The rating scale changed once halfway through, when Jade gave a bright blue claw a score of ten. At least, Ferris thought the scale changed; he didn’t have another eyeball to check against.

When all the boxes were empty, and the boxes were also consumed—Jade had asked to eat them, and Uncle Winton let him—the slime was rolling around in circles.

“Ferris, we must get more!”

“We need to delve first, then we should be able to hunt monsters that all taste like this.”

The slime stopped, apparently lost in thought. Ferris took the opportunity to turn back to his uncle, who was pulling the spiritual device out.

Ferris grabbed Jade and settled down to meditate, placing a hand on the device. He was excited to see his uncle and hear a bit of news from home, and it was harder than normal to clear his mind. Still, the meditation did its job, and he was able to find the emptiness and step into his soulspace.

His goal was to enter the spiritual device and meet his uncle, but Ferris couldn’t resist taking a quick detour to check on the slimespace. Jade had just eaten a dozen bites of magical food, and he wanted to see what that looked like in the Digest River.

The core pedestal in the slimespace was up on the Heartfire tower, so Ferris had to climb down and walk over to the river. He tried to go quickly—his uncle would be waiting for him—but he did carefully check around for any lingering problems. Thankfully, there had been no reappearance of the yellow scarabs. There was still damage to the dome’s walls. Other than that, the slimespace was back to normal.

When he arrived at its edge, Ferris looked down to see that the Digest River was filled with shimmering glitter. The magical berry had made a small cloud of similarly glowing water, but now the same effect covered a much larger area.

A dozen small fish—exactly like the one that had killed Jade’s mental body the day they bonded—swam through the water. Ferris hadn’t seen them since, but now they were here in force, consuming the glitter from the water.

Ferris watched for a moment, but he couldn’t think of anything to do about them. He knew they were hostile, so he didn’t like that they were taking whatever the glitter was, but he also didn’t have a way to collect or use it instead. All the clams were currently full, displaying a qi chunk, and Ferris didn’t even know if the clams cared about the magical dust.

Regrettably, Ferris left the slimespace to join his uncle in the spiritual device. It was good information to know, but there was nothing he could do about it today.

Uncle Winton was once again waiting for him. The cozy room looked the same as last time, with two large chairs in front of a crackling fire, inside a small room with bookshelves. His mentor was sitting in one chair, so Ferris sat down in the other. Jade started exploring the room as usual.

“So,” Uncle Winton said, giving Ferris a large smile, “how have you been? Any better than last time?”

“Better in some ways, worse in others,” Ferris said, looking at Jade. “I had a pretty bad scare where Jade could have died. Besides that, I have been doing pretty well.”

Uncle Winton grimaced a bit, but thankfully didn’t press for more details. Ferris trusted him as much as anyone in life, but the man himself had warned Ferris to keep sensitive cultivation secrets to himself. A potential way to hurt Jade was definitely a secret.

“Anything I can help with?”

Ferris thought about everything he needed. His biggest problems were with the demons. He was able to get the oath statue back, but what if something else happened to it?

“Uncle,” Ferris began, “I had trouble last time with demons stealing my oath statue. Is there anything I can do to protect it while not cultivating?”

The man thought for a moment, then answered. “You mentioned a book called Spiritual Harmony? Have you read it yet?”

“No,” Ferris said, frowning.

“Buy it and read it,” the man said, “it will likely help you with this problem, and more. In the meantime, I can give some advice. Tell me, boy, how would someone normally protect an area?”

“Build a wall around it,” Ferris answered. He had considered it after seeing the giant wall inside the spirit gem refinement device, but constructing a wall large enough to stop the demons was beyond him. Even if he did, they would just climb over when he was gone.

“Think simpler,” Uncle Winton said, “and think about the intent of keeping things out. The act of claiming an area as off-limits.”

“Then,” Ferris replied slowly, “perhaps if you want to show something is off limits … a fence?” People put up fences to keep others out. If the intent was the goal, then the symbology of putting up a fence could make sense.

Uncle Winton simply shrugged, but didn’t say anything more. Ferris hoped that meant he had the correct answer.

Ferris considered the other problems he had two weeks ago. His qi production was now doing well, and he had been able to purge the impurities. However, the conversation with Adept Nyoka had left him with new questions.

“Do you know anything about the term spirit animals?” Ferris asked. It was the biggest source of confusion after talking with the lady with a snake familiar bond.

“Yes,” Uncle Winton said, “they are creatures that can be cultivated in the soul. I don’t have any personal experience with them. Most of what I know suggests not to consider them until the second tier, and so the information about them is targeted at that level. Is it important?”

Ferris nodded.

“I can see if I can learn more.”

“Thank you, uncle,” Ferris said with a slight bow. He still intended to check Hollowfield’s library, as it didn’t hurt to spread out his search.

“I tried the qi rope transfer technique,” Ferris said proudly. “It was a smaller test, but it worked just like you said. I was also able to… purge… the impurities.” He shuddered a little at the memory.

Uncle Winton wrinkled his nose at that, and Ferris now had a personal understanding of why.

“I asked about Clan Hollowfield’s advancement methods,” Ferris said with a shrug, “but I am unsure about them. They all have potential, but none of them stand out as better than the others.”

Uncle Winton nodded. After a moment, when it became clear Ferris didn’t have anything else to say, he moved on to a new topic.

“When Jade gives food a rating,” the man said, looking over to where Jade was roaming around the room, “I have noticed a pattern. Do you know how delvers decide what to bring back and sell from the rifts?”

“No,” Ferris shook his head, “I thought they brought back everything they killed.”

“Yes,” the man nodded again, “monster corpses almost always have something valuable, and processing them is usually easier to do back home. But the rest of the rift will be filled with valuable plants, rocks, and even some special types of water. The red leaf I brought might appear on a tree among a thousand others, yet it is the only one worth collecting.”

“Then,” Ferris said, slowly, “some kind of detection ability?”

“Yes, almost every delver eventually develops one. There are many variations, but a common approach is to detect the amount of magical energy emitted by an object. It appears Jade’s rating,” he gestured at the slime, “matches what my ability detects, at least for the ratios between them.”

Ferris considered it for a moment before responding. “That could be useful, but it doesn’t help if Jade needs to eat it to detect if it is magical.”

“Yes, perhaps you can develop it further. In the short term, I would recommend seeing if you can change Jade’s rating scale to match a more commonly used unit of measurement. Have you checked how precise Jade can get with the rating?”

“No,” Ferris shook his head, “I have been thinking about it as Jade’s preference in taste. If you think it could be useful, I can investigate further.” He wasn’t sure about this. It could be helpful, but Ferris liked the rating game as something that Jade did purely for fun. The slime had also given different scores to mundane food as well. Maybe he could talk to Jade later and separate the concepts of magic and enjoyment, even if they were related.

“Good,” his mentor said, “was there anything else you wanted to ask?”

Ferris shook his head.

“Then,” the man smiled, “It’s time we start planning out how to get you delving.”
