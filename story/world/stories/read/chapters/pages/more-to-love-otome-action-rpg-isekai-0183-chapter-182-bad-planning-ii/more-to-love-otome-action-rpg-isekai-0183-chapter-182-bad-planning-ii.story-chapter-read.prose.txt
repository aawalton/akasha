Aida returned to the Loreh domicile with mixed emotions.

On the one hand, she was relieved that she and Lily would be able to make it to Burston City on time. On the other, she wasn’t looking forward to spending a whole day with Dev—their minor disagreement in the Gullbeaks conference room notwithstanding.

Why tempt the fates?

Unfortunately, her peaceful evening plan to reconcile the disparate emotions in the privacy and tranquility of her own room was disrupted when her mother knocked on her door.

“Aida? Can we talk for a minute?” Meg asked tentatively.

“Sure.” She followed her mother out to their tiny living room, where Ray was standing. He gazed at her apprehensively, making Aida instantly wary.

“Please, sit,” he said, gesturing towards the lumpy sofa. She noticed there was a full pot of tea, freshly brewed, along with three teacups set out on the coffee table.

“What’s going on?” Aida asked, forcing a lighthearted laugh. “Why are you treating me like a stranger?”

When they didn’t join in her laughter, an unpleasant shiver ran down her spine. I shouldn’t have said that.

Meg and Ray knelt in front of the tiny coffee table together so that they were both facing Aida on the couch. They were both acting shifty, glancing at her periodically.

“Will you say something?” Aida asked gently as she spread her mana senses out. It wouldn’t do to be ambushed.

Fortunately, there were no suspicious presences lingering outside.

“Aida, we were thinking—this star cycle is going by so quickly, and it feels like we haven’t really had time to get to know you. The new you,” Ray added quickly.

Her heart jolted. Do they know?

“You won’t understand this until you become a mother yourself,” Meg said wistfully. “But it’s as if we blinked… and you’ve become such a different person now.”

Not trusting herself to respond, Aida just nodded slowly. They seemed to take it as if they hurt her feelings, since they scrambled to clarify their words.

“We’re not saying you’ve become a completely different person,” Ray said, waving his hands in the air. “It’s just that… you’re a lot more confident and proactive now. You’ve been out of the house so often, and not because Suelina came and dragged you out.”

Meg and Ray both chuckled at the shared memory.

Trying to breathe more normally, Aida returned their smiles uncertainly. “I guess this is what happens when we don’t get to see each other every day, huh?”

She noticed a slight wince at her question.

“We just wanted to say that… we know that teenagers change very quickly, but we are glad to see you’re still the same thoughtful and kindhearted daughter we sent off to Maglica,” Ray said. He reached over to grip his wife’s hand, and she squeezed back, gazing at Aida with glimmering eyes. “We’re sorry that we have been so busy working and haven’t been able to spend much time with you during your cycle off.”

“That’s okay. I know you both are really busy. I appreciate your sacrifices.” The two smiled tremulously at Aida’s words. Uncertain if she should probe, yet feeling like she was missing something, she gently asked: “Are you sure you’ll be okay while I’m gone?”

“Of course!” They replied together.

“If you’re sure,” Aida said, reaching forward to pour the tea. She thought back to what her father had said during their lunch together. “And the, um… other family hasn’t been giving you any trouble, right?”

“Of course not!”

Despite their vehemence, Aida was skeptical. People could be petty, and it had only been three days since her father had told the family they had to break the betrothal. Regardless, her parents were either very good actors, or they truly believed they were in the clear and pushing them would do no good.

“All right… I’m glad that worked out.” She took a sip of her tea as she observed her parents. She set her cup back down. “Are you two really fine?”

Meg set her cup down as Ray gently coughed to clear his throat. “You always were very perceptive.”

Aida’s mother fiddled with her fingers for a moment before looking up beseechingly at her. “We just want to know—are you angry at us?”

“No—why would I be?” Aida asked, bewildered.

“We might be overthinking it, but we thought perhaps you were so eager to leave us—leave Buddington, I mean—and go all the way to Burston City for work because you were upset at us,” Meg said bleakly while Ray nodded somberly beside his wife. He seemed especially uncomfortable. “Because we haven’t been able to give you all the things a growing girl needs, we worry… oh, this is so silly.”

Meg swiped fiercely at her watering ocean-blue eyes. “Mother just wants you to know that we will always support you and whatever you decide to do. We didn’t make the best decisions in our lives, so we couldn’t give you the safety net you deserved. We forced you to go to Maglica Academy when you very clearly didn’t want to go. And for that, we apologize.”

After the shocking admission, Meg scooted back from the table and lowered her head to the ground, her shoulders heaving in dry sobs. Ray looked at his wife in shock before turning back to her and nodding somberly in agreement. He also bowed.

Aida stared in disbelief at the two prostrating adults, aware that she was in an extremely precarious position right now. Anything she did or said could easily reveal her lack of these specific memories. She had to be careful.

But… was there a way she could utilize this situation to get more information about her backstory?

“Hey, come on, it’s all okay now,” Aida said, getting up from the couch to lift her parents up. “I mean, look at me… I have a promising job that can help people. I don’t regret anything.”

Her parents refused to meet her gaze, keeping their heads ducked as they straightened with her support.

“And besides, that was all in the past. What’s important is the future. I have a good one, one that I chose. It would be silly of me to hold a grudge over the whole reason I am where I am now. Please, cheer up.”

Despite her best efforts, the Lorehs were still besieged by guilt.

“I suppose the worst of it is that… even though we can see you’re a better person for all your hardships and struggles, we still wonder… if you would have turned out to become such a good person if we had been as involved in your life,” Meg said brokenly.

They need help, Aida realized. Help I’m not qualified to give.

“Shush, dear, you’re rambling now,” Ray said with a muffled voice. He patted his wife’s shoulder as he looked to Aida. “Let’s talk outside.”

Aida followed her father out, leaving Meg behind to sob quietly into her handkerchief. He led her up to the rooftop, where they rested their elbows on the wall and gazed down at the busy street down below.

“I didn’t want to have this conversation in front of your mother,” Ray finally said. His voice was husky with emotion, burdened by guilt from past decisions. He turned to Aida. “Skies know I’ve put her through enough. And I thank you for being considerate of her… but I want you to know, if you have any feelings you want to get off your chest about how we’ve treated you… Take it all out on me.”

Aida looked at the man in front of her. His body, though thin and not as nourished as it could be, was still strong and well-kept from all the manual labor he had to do. There was still a bit of youth left in his face, though there were creases in his forehead and around his mouth from constant worry and stress. In the light of the setting sun, the creases seemed especially deep, as the man braced himself for his daughter to unload years’ worth of frustration against him.

What’s a safe response?

“I’d rather focus on the future,” Aida said quietly, lowering her eyes so he couldn’t see how unsettled she was. “I don’t see the point in focusing on the past.”

A long moment passed between the two of them before Ray exhaled. He reached out and gripped her shoulder.

“I understand,” he said heavily. She noticed his lower lip tremble before he firmed it. “I’m sorry. And thank you for not holding it against us.”

She nodded, unable to discern the correct response.

“I guess I’ll head back inside,” Ray said, releasing Aida’s shoulder and patting it hesitantly.

She nodded again. “I’ll be out here for a bit.”

He patted her back again, lingering on the steps back down to the fourth floor, before he nodded and finally left.

Aida stood in silence for a moment, reflecting on everything that had just happened. It was undoubtedly an important moment for learning about her backstory, but there was no way she was clever enough to capitalize on it.

Finally, she sighed.

“You can come out now.”
