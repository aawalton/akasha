Lyle was surrounded by the same demons that had attacked Eriavel. This time, however, there were twelve of them, with three more lying on the ground in their own blood as a result of deep cuts.

Moonlight scarcely reached the forest floor, and within that limited light, Lyle was hidden in the shadows. But the demons, bathed in silver light, with their ashen skin cracked and a faint scarlet glow leaking from within, looked truly horrifying.

“Your struggle is for naught,” one of the demons barked, a wide grin on her face. She stood behind the rest, leaning on a giant axe embedded in one of the velmoryns’ corpses. “The harder you try, the more painful your death will be.”

But Lyle didn’t respond. She didn’t even acknowledge the demons’ presence.

Her eyes were fixed on one of the Velmoryn bodies scattered across the forest floor, all hacked apart and mutilated. It was a gruesome sight. Guts, blood, and filth smeared across everything in the area.

“Father, your pitiful children have died. Grant me strength to avenge them…”

As her whisper echoed in my mind, I felt a powerful pull coming from Lyle. It was nothing like what I felt when Tekla tried to channel my divinity, not even when Amon used it like an ointment. This time the feeling was much stronger. Stronger and heavier.

A blue window formed in my sight.

[Oath of Just Slaughter]

Your Inquisitor has invoked Oath of Just Slaughter and requests permission to channel your divinity.

Permit the invocation? [ Y / N ]

I didn’t even need to consider it. The moment I saw the mangled bodies of my believers, I felt a burning desire to accept the penalty and kill every one of those demons in the most gruesome way possible.

I wanted them tortured. I wanted to see their despair and hear them beg for death.

But having multiple threads in my mind came with its own advantages, along with the drawbacks. I could isolate any emotion as long as I willed it.

Wasting 500 Divinity Points just to follow my anger would be a terrible decision. Especially since I already knew Lyle would give me that satisfaction.

Her entire figure shimmered red as I allowed my divinity to flood her body.

First, her hair turned scarlet, and a single wing appeared on her back, featherless and grotesque. A crimson aura began to swirl around her body, sinister and overwhelming. The two blades she had already unsheathed shimmered red as Lyle disappeared from her spot.

She appeared right in front of one demon and, with slashes forming an X, split his body into four parts before disappearing again and beheading another.

The demons froze in shock as they realized their opponent had reached a level none of them could hope to match. Fear stunned them. They couldn’t even flee as Lyle cut them down one by one.

She spent no more than a second on each demon. Her figure would barely form before her blades struck, and she was already gone again.

It wasn’t a skill, nor did she use much of my divinity.

This was simply the difference between the mortal realm and the divine. The divinity flowing through her body turned into an unnatural amount of mana, elevating her to something close to a demigod. The excess mana surged outward, creating a suffocating aura that rooted the demons in place.

I didn’t realize she was this powerful.

Even I was surprised as I watched her cut down the eleventh demon. It had only been eleven seconds, and there was just one left.

The one still gripping the axe embedded in a velmoryn’s torso.

“Now I see,” she grinned bitterly, revealing her sharp teeth. “We were meant to die to you, so that He would witness your god’s power.”

Her words didn’t reach Lyle. She passed by the demon, ripping off both of her hands with a single swift slash. Lyle landed behind her and delivered shallow cuts until her entire back was covered in countless wounds.

Seeing the demon was guaranteed to die, Lyle deactivated Oath of Just Slaughter and allowed her to fall to her knees.

[Warning: Passing the Divinity consumed 1.8 Divinity Points!]

The effects of my divinity immediately disappeared. The featherless wing crumbled into dust and the crimson aura dissipated like smoke as she returned to normal.

However, I wasn’t sure if this version was any less terrifying to the demon.

While under the effect of Oath of Just Slaughter, Lyle had been calm and composed. It was likely the influence of my divinity. It must have suppressed her emotions the same way it did mine. But now… now all the rage and hatred surfaced at once.

She charged and cut the demon’s arms up to the shoulders, then thrust both blades into her chest. Their eyes met. Lyle’s were filled with anger. The demon’s… hollow.

“You are welcome to vent your anger, but know that I feel no pain,” the demon hissed, a mocking smile on her face.

As if in response, Lyle twisted the blades until an unnatural cracking sound made her release her grip.

The grin on the demon’s face turned into despair as she suddenly screamed. Her entire body spasmed, and the cracks in her skin began to shimmer and close.

Ashen color faded into light gray as her skin returned to natural velmoryn color, and her larger, bulkier body shrank to normal size.

I felt the connection with her reestablish.

She had turned back into a Velmoryn.

Realizing the woman had returned to her original form, Lyle froze for a moment. But only for a moment.

She charged, her fists tightening until her knuckles whitened, and swung her right hand at the woman, who couldn’t even look at her, her eyes unfocused.

I’m not letting you die so easily.

But before Lyle’s hand could connect, I stirred my divinity and pulled the woman into my dominion. At the same time, I sent a vision to Tekla’s mind, showing her where Lyle was and that she would need help carrying the fallen velmoryns.

The woman, half on death’s door, appeared in my dominion, kneeling before the God of Velmoryn’s manifestation.

“How? Where… God of Velmoryn…” she muttered, her thoughts scattered. It was no surprise, considering her state.

“You will answer my questions,” I said, forcing my influence over her. This was the first time I had used my Authority over the Velmoryns, and only now did I understand how absolute my control was.

Her twisted expression turned normal, and her heaving chest steadied. She straightened, blood pouring down from her hands and chest like a waterfall.

I stirred my divinity and sealed her wounds, though I didn’t heal her. Not yet, at the very least.

“How did you turn back into a Velmoryn?”

“I do not know,” she responded.

Of course. How would she know that…

“Tell me, how were you transformed into a demon?”

“Vael Akrion gave us a potion. When we woke up, we were all demons.”

This is getting nowhere.

“Where are the rest of the Blue Tribe? Where is Akrion?” I asked, noticing black smoke beginning to rise from her body.

“I do not know. We always used portals. But…”

Her words cut off as my crimson energy swirled around her and sealed her away.

Night God’s divinity…

He had played me. Her role wasn’t to force Lyle to reveal she was my inquisitor, but to make me bring her into my dominion.

Now he had gained access to my realm.

My mind spiraled. In that moment of danger, the threads slipped from my control, and my thoughts scattered in too many directions.

But one of those threads reached a conclusion. I had nothing to worry about.

I unsealed the woman and allowed the black smoke to leave her body. There was far more than I expected. My dominion was quickly swallowed by darkness, even affecting my sight. I felt cold, or rather as though something chilling had settled inside my body.

But that was it. The overwhelming amount of hostile divinity, despite how terrifying it looked, failed to influence me at all.

After confirming it had fully left the woman’s body, I moved my own power, and my divinity began to consume it from the edges.

The clash of red and black unfolded before me. My scarlet divinity looked thicker, denser, as it swirled around it violently and pierced the sphere of black whenever it moved.

With each passing moment, more of the black disappeared, until nothing remained.

[Warning: Destruction consumed 10 Divinity Points!][Warning: You’ve gained 200 Divinity Points!]

Pride and confidence bloomed within me as I realized the gap between our divine power. This was the first time I saw it in numbers—even a sliver of my power equaled twenty times more of his.

But the satisfaction didn’t last.

In my haste, I had neglected the woman.

She died. Her charred, drained corpse lay in her own blood, now turned black.

All that remained was a new star in my realm. But unlike the others, it didn’t glow crimson.

It was colorless.
