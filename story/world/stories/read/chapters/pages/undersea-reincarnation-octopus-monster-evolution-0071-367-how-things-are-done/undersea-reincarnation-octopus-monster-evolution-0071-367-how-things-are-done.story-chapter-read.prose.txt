The duel was being held at the beach. Or to be more accurate, Henry should have said duels. Plural. Considering the importance of Ash and the noble scion’s fight, whoever had put this whole thing in place had somehow managed to schedule a whole line-up of spars. To warm the crowds up before the main event and likely extend the spectator’s stay. Such a turnout was a little surprising to Henry, but he guessed it was likely not too out of the ordinary.

A semi-circle of surprisingly sturdy wooden bleachers surrounded a pretty wide stretch of the sand, and from how things were arranged, it seemed that fights could spread out into the water easy enough. As far as Henry could tell, no one was guarding the sea from loose spells. The barely visible panel of glowing light didn’t stretch out toward the ocean.

Henry hummed as he eyed the intricate pedestal placed between the bleachers. It was the source of the defensive magic. Of that, he was sure.

Maybe when this whole thing with the orbs is dealt with, I can look into how those things are made, he mused to himself for a moment before glancing back toward the central structure. Between these, the gear Attal was creating in his lab, and those protective enchantments Henry had seen in Thalis’ arena, there seem to be a whole bunch of other disciplines out there that he didn’t know much about.

Maybe one day he’d look into it.

Turning his attention toward the largest seating structure that faced the sea, Henry decided it couldn’t really be called a bleacher, even if it was playing the same role. The thing oozed luxury. Even if he ignored the central box, the rest looked much more comfortable than what he was sitting on. The seats were spaced out and padded. Waiters were going back and forth, serving drinks and appetizers, while individuals in fancy clothes chatted and laughed, unconcerned with the little bout that was taking place. The whole area was even shaded, as the large, fluffy cloud hovering above was certainly not natural. Which, to Henry, was a little overkill. He didn’t think anyone above D-rank had to worry much about sun damage. As for the box at the top, well, considering the old man lounging right in its center and considering the thin, emerald-encrusted crown sitting on his brow, Henry would guess that was the king. Two glimmering guards stood at his side, each holding a shield and a spear. A quick identify told him they were high B-rank, and he was tempted to test out the king and see if he was also B-rank or reached higher, but he opted not to create a commotion, just in case. Not more than he already did, that was.

Suffice to say, his arrival had well and truly been noticed. Especially because, as it turned out, flying without permit or authorization was frowned upon, especially in events like this one. It would have been worse if he’d landed directly in the venue but thankfully, he’d touched-down a good distance away, and he’d gotten away with just a warning before finding his companions.

***

Maurice had, of course, politely requested a shell with the new skill, and Henry obliged him. The crab had been working on improving his soul as well, so it was no surprise when the young man soon clapped after having activated his skill. But then, he paused, frowning.

“Wait… it’s just D-rank! What the hell?”

“Make sense. I worked for mine. You didn’t,” Henry mused, grinning when Maurice narrowed his eyes at him. As for Velistraine, she just glared at the two of them and muttered something he couldn’t hear over the crowd. But she had been pissed since he’d arrived. “Krakens and their bullshit” had been her succinct response when Henry had explained to the two of them what he’d been up to and what he’d accomplished. Velistraine was annoyed, but she managed a half-hearted congratulation before she began shooting questions about how he’d done while they watched the duels they didn’t care about. Eventually, Henry leaned closer to Velistraine and whispered. “Anything I should know about the duel? Should we be ready if things get… dicey?”

An announcer was still hyping the big match, but it wasn’t scheduled for right this moment. There was another duel they had to sit through. But soon enough, Ash would walk into the ring, and she’d have to fight a noble while the king watched.

That sounded like it could create some issues.

Henry knew the girl and Velistraine had a talk, but just in case things went far with the local powers, he wanted to be ready. He wasn’t keen on getting involved if things got bloody, but he could at least teleport a bunch of them away until things cooled off.

Velistraine seemed to turn over the words a few times as her eyes glanced toward the noble section where Attal was seated, completely ignoring the events as he scribbled on a notebook while some strangers tried—and failed—to strike a conversation with him. “I spoke with her. We’ve got a plan, but you never know. Be ready just in case, I guess,” then glancing at him, she continued through Telepathy. “[I asked her to go easy on him. A draw would be perfect, but a narrow win is fine, too. So long as she doesn’t wipe the floor with him or kill him. The family she’s gunning for doesn’t have direct backers, but the king won’t take kindly to her just waltzing in here and making a mess of his nobility. He might even be pissed enough that he’d side with them just to spite her for the insult. He’s an A-rank himself. An old one, but he’s still got the pride, and he’s closely allied to another. Which means this could make her revenge business very messy. If she wants to be able to eventually retire and live here—or most other civilized cities—she needs to be careful. She needs to do things the right way.]”

Henry raised a brow while a new spar took place. It involved two captains who seemed to have a feud about a docking spot, which got a few laughs from the crowd, and before he could react to Velistraine’s words, a pulse of mana grabbed his attention. Looking to the side, he found Maurice playing with a freshly cast Imploding Bubble, holding it between his hand. The thing was crackling with mana, most likely created through Rupture Casting. That was the only way Henry could explain why the bit of magic was setting his nerves on edge. Without thinking, he set his Octominds to work on a telekinetic cage to contain the spell in case Maurice lost control over. Because already, a few people around them were craning their neck to take a look, or were too busy trying to scoot away.

“[Maurice, cut that out,]” Henry hissed, his eyes never leaving the figurative bomb in the hand of the child-looking crab, and when the sheepish-looking Maurice finally unwound it and dispelled it, Henry finally relaxed enough to turn back to Velistraine. “[What does that mean? Doing things the right way?]”

Henry wasn’t sure what that implied. Was she talking about pursuing the noble family in justice? He had some doubts about that, especially considering nothing had happened when Ash’s family had disappeared. It seemed the local powers were comfortable enough turning their gaze away from theft and murder. As for revenge, was there a “right” way of administering that?

The laws and politics of this world are odd, he thought to himself. While they’d been told to not cause too much trouble, it didn’t seem like there were that many rules for the powerful. Then again, his only reference were Velistraine—who was a bit of a rule-breaker—and Attal—who seemed to despise politics or anything that didn’t have to do with his own work.

“[That means don’t piss in another A-rank’s pool,]” Velistraine simply stated as finally, the amplified voice announced the last fight, and Velistraine’s gaze turned toward her latest recruit. From the sideline, Ash stepped forward into the ring, while the Clydemar scion hopped down from the noble’s section to a flurry of polite claps.

“[Starting feuds with other A-rank is rarely worth it,]” Velistraine explained. “[Especially if you gotta work with them some day or need to take a break in their backyard. This doesn’t mean we don’t get into disagreements of course. But there’s a difference between ignoring a couple of mercantile rules versus decimating a noble family that contribute to running the country. Especially not as a B-rank. But that changes when she hits A-rank. Then she gets a seat at the table, and she can air out her grievance without putting a big, fat bullseye on her back.]”

The duel began with only the barest of fanfares, most of which were used to set the noble scion as some sort of virtuous defender of the law and its agents. The announcer never accused Ash directly, but the subtext was there, and Henry could see the girl’s eyes narrow as she and her opponent circled each other before the Clydemar opened fire, pointing with hand and shooting a volley of fiery projectiles.

“[If she approaches them as an A-rank, then the king and his ally are dealing with an equal,]” Henry summarized as the black, humanoid clones flitted about while the real Ash made a show of how difficult it was to dodge the rain of projectiles the Clydemar was sending. They were showy, and they looked sort-of cool, but to Henry’s mana vision they looked as effective as hand-thrown, ignited sea balls. He was pretty sure she could eat most of them and not even singe a hair on her head. And she did, after a few seconds of ‘frantic’ dodging.

Honestly, the show she was putting up felt more insulting than straight out demolishing the noble.

“[She might not get everything she wants,]” Velistraine winced as Ash caught the noble with a nasty knee to the head, “[but I honestly prefer that. Blind revenge will not end well for her. But exacting justice as an equal, and forcing the king into investigating and extracting the guilty parties would work best. The king wouldn’t want the whole family crippled. And it’s not as if all of ‘em had a hand in what had happened to her.]”

From the looks of it, Ash was following Velistraine’s recommendation, though Henry could see the surface cracking here and there. She’d block or counter the haphazard punches or spells, and it would make the noble look like flailing moron. It was clear as day that this man had never actually needed to fight for his life. Even Tevarius’ son, back in Thalis, would have wiped the floor with him as a C-rank and inevitably, Lucius Clydemar’s movement began to slow, until finally, he hit the sand, after Ash inflicted a particularly vicious cut to back of his knees while two of her shadow had engaged him in a melee. She stood over him, a look of barely concealed disdain on her features as he scrambled to raise himself up an an elbow.

Then the Clydemar said something Henry couldn’t hear over the noise of the crowd, and Ash’s expression went blank.

“I don’t… think things are going well,” Henry muttered as he saw the Clydemar’s bloody lips twist with glee. Ash went still. He could see every inch of her. The trembling hands. The unblinking eyes. The twitching nostrils. He couldn’t hear what the loser had said to her, but Henry wasn’t the only who’d noted the tension. Next to him, Velistraine was leaning forward, ready to pounce. Even the crowd was quieting down while the Clydemar’s shoulders shook as he laughed.

What had he said?
