The strangest thing about hunting through the wild realm was how simple it was thanks to Asher’s skill. With their chunk of safe land that wasn’t at risk of being changed right out from under them, and Kroult’s spell helping to sync their movements, the team did exactly what Asher instructed of them. After closing their eyes and waiting a few seconds, when they opened them once more, the entire world around them past the stable zone would be completely different. Samantha would take a look at their new detection artifact while the rest of them would look around in awe, and once she confirmed it still wasn’t picking up any readings, they would all close their eyes again and repeat the cycle. Using this plan, they were able to go through over a dozen completely unique zones every minute.

However, it didn’t come completely without risk.

“Monster!” Rulfar shouted, and Asher spun around to see what looked like a giant spider with six wings flapping hard on its back zip straight into the area protected from change due to Permanence. The creature was the size of a house and it had far too many eyes, each one of which was trained directly on the five of them. Asher’s stable zone stretched out a quarter mile from him in all directions, but so long as a monster or threat happened to spawn close to that border and cross it before they all could close their eyes again, they were stuck with it.

While Kroult’s flaming eyes flickered in what Asher somehow understood intuitively was fear as the lich took a step back, the rest of them got prepared without having to exchange a word. Instantly, Samantha took to the sky and began firing glowing violet arrows down at the beast, each projectile punching through one of the spider’s many eyes and causing it to release a high-pitched, ear-splitting screech as it changed trajectory and went for her instead. But while it started heading her way, rather than pick up speed, the spider’s mouth opened and it spat a glob of yellow liquid up at her. Samantha managed to block it with her emerald wings, but the glob actually managed to stick to her wings made of pure tritessence, something Asher hadn’t even thought was possible. The added weight to her wing made her lopsided, and Asher’s face paled as he realized she was barely staying airborne.

“Moxy!” Rulfar shouted, already shifting into his battle form as his body grew even larger and he positioned himself carefully.

“Way ahead of you!” Moxy said, busy compressing air into a dense ball in her hands. As soon as she was done, she grinned and took careful aim. “One bear cannon, coming up!”

Asher had already gotten out of the way, dragging a confused Kroult along with him to save the lich from being caught up in the blast. Right before the spider could catch up to Samantha’s struggling form and wrap its long legs around her, Moxy threw her ball of air into the ground directly behind Rulfar the exact moment the bloodhound jumped, causing a powerful explosion of air that sent him shooting upward. With a roar, Rulfar slammed into the side of the spider, cleaving into it with his enchanted battle axe and causing bright-green blood to fly everywhere. Yet even as he and the giant spider crashed to the ground, Asher cursed as he felt a buzzing in his bones and looked up to spot an entire swarm of similar giant spiders quickly flying their way from some large, twisting hive roughly a mile in the distance.

“This was some sort of scout, we need to get out of here!” Asher shouted as Rulfar finished off the spider and Samantha stumbled in an ungraceful landing. “No time to get back into position, everyone close your eyes!”

As what looked easily like a hundred or two of those spiders flew in their direction, Asher clenched his eyes shut and felt the others do the same through Kroult’s link. Right before it could reach the border to his skill, the buzzing vanished, replaced with hard cracking sounds. Opening his eyes just a hair, Asher let out a sigh of relief at the sight of an impossible forest. There were large trees sprouting up all over the place, and every so often one would tilt to the side and fall over. Yet rather than crash into the ground, the tree would somehow roll and flip upside down, slowly coming to a perfect vertical stop upside down where its roots would transform into a new canopy.

“Okay… I think we’re safe,” Asher called out, laughing as a blood-soaked Rulfar dug himself out of the spider’s corpse. “You good, Rulfar?”

“As disgusting as that was, that was the most efficiently we’ve ever pulled off the bear cannon,” Rulfar admitted, giving Kroult an appreciative look. “Your spell is really pulling its weight.”

“It is just a minor spell. But I am glad you find it useful,” Kroult said, shaking slightly as he gripped Scritchems. For his part, the wild beast didn’t look concerned in the slightest, still happily crooning against the lich’s chest.

“Something capable of sticking to my wings is a new experience,” Samantha said, frowning as she dismissed her wings and forced the yellow goop to fall to the ground. “Should we take a sample of this stuff?”

“What do we look like, alchemists?” Moxy snorted. “If I can’t punch it, I’m not interested.”

“I’m not sure how we even would take a sample,” Asher admitted, picking up a stick and poking at the pile of goo. He tried to drag off a tiny piece, but the goo merely stretched, now stuck onto his stick just as firmly as it had been Samantha’s wings. After a moment he had an idea, and he pulled a small box out of his rift. Stretching out a chunk of the goo, he unsheathed the void dagger, grimacing at the sensation of his tritessence being pulled from his body into the eerie blade. He didn’t need it for long, however, and he quickly slashed the blade through the bottom of the goo. Like anything else the dagger touched, the goo it passed through simply ceased to exist, giving Asher a stick with a long, dangly goo tendril hanging from it. All it took was holding that over the box and cutting at the very top for the goo to fall into the container.

“That knife still worries me,” Rulfar muttered as Asher sealed the box up tight and shoved it into his rift, making a mental note to bring it over to Polly at some point to see if she could do anything with it. “Is it destroying stuff, or sending it straight into the void? And if it’s the latter, does that mean Zygrul can see anything you cut with it?”

“The void is a big place,” Asher shrugged, carefully sheathing the void dagger and returning it to his hip. While it was probably safer to keep such a dangerous weapon in his rift, whatever anti-Spatial equipment those assassins had used against him had prevented him from accessing his rift. This way, if worst came to worst and he needed to flee instead of fight, he’d have his void dagger at the ready and could carve his way out. Asher wasn’t a fan of relying on equipment, but when it was down to life or actual death, not just temporary, he’d take whatever advantage he could get. “I doubt the lich is going to notice a tiny sliver of goo popping into the realm somewhere. I mean, isn’t the whole point of the void realm that it consumes everything? Even if we don’t know how Zygrul is surviving in there, anything that ends up there is consumed almost instantly based on everything I’ve read.”

“Regardless of any of that, no death energy source in that weird forest either,” Samantha said, peering curiously at the rolling trees. “Should we keep going?”

“Probably for the best. Just keep your guard up everyone, that spider showed just how easy it is for something to come at us even with the precautions we’re taking.”

With that, they fell back into formation around Kroult and Scritchems, going right back to their cycling of the world around them. Wild terrains and sights that didn’t make any sense flicked past them one after another in the universe’s weirdest slide show as they hunted for something capable of saving Smiles. While they were fast enough to avoid any additional monster attacks, they did end up getting hit with a giant wave that swept over their stable land, and a few flaming meteors crashed into the ground around them as well. After a few hours of cycling through thousands of potential worlds and threats, Samantha shouted.

“We got something!”

Asher had already been closing his eyes out of routine before he jerked them open again, taking a more careful look at their surroundings. Three large moons in the sky illuminated a dark land that had a thin layer of dense fog hovering only a few feet off the ground. There were pillars of earth jutting out of the ground here and there, each one containing what looked like carved runes and mysterious symbols that Asher’s Knowledge element couldn’t make heads or tails of for translating. He didn’t see any immediate threats, but that didn’t really mean much in the wild realm.

“Which direction?” Rulfar asked.

“Directly between where you and Asher are looking,” Samantha said.

“Let’s move before it changes!” Asher instructed, leading the way as they all took off at a light jog. As important as it was for him to get to wherever the source of the death energy was before the wild realm could change again, they weren’t stupid enough to sprint into an unknown area that could contain God only knew what dangers. As if to prove his point, the moment they reached the border between terrain and entered the fog, Asher shivered.

The fog itself was cold unlike anything he’d ever felt before. After only a few seconds he started losing feeling in his legs, and even his upper body began to feel as though it were growing weak with every step he took. He clearly wasn’t the only one suffering as Samantha flapped her wings and shot into the sky, and Moxy began actively using her life element, shivering as her hands glowed purple and she tried to fight back against whatever was happening.

“What is this? Is this the death energy?” she asked, scowling at the slowly shifting fog as it flowed around them.

“No, neither Draken’s aura nor the King of Death’s felt like this,” Asher admitted. Just to confirm his hunch, he switched his energy immunity to Death, and shook his head as nothing changed. “I think it’s a genuine aura of cold somehow.”

“It definitely is. I have an evolved cold resistance, and this doesn’t seem to be affecting me as much as it is the rest of you,” Rulfar pointed out, standing as tall as ever as he looked around. “Whatever we’re up against has to utilize cold and death. Remember, Asher, the rest of us most likely can’t go up against whatever is giving off such intense death energy. I know we’re hoping it's contained, but if it’s a monster with an aura, we’re going to have to seek refuge within your rift while you fight it.”

“I know, don’t worry,” Asher said, triggering his Boots of Levitation and hovering up over the fog to give his regeneration a chance to fight off whatever the cold had done to him as he called up. “Samantha, you see anything from up there?”

“The pillars of earth look like they’re pointing to something!” she said, swooping down and pointing off to the side. “There’s some sort of indent in the ground that the fog is flowing in. But we have to hurry, the world is already changing. Look.”

Sure enough, where three moons had once existed up in the sky, now only two remained, and they were far larger than they’d once been. Even the fog itself outside of Asher’s Permanence looked as though it was taking on a faint reddish color and growing more dense, transforming the land into something that looked out of a nightmare.

“Let’s head to that indent Samantha spotted, that sounds like a good start!” Asher decided.

Asher’s boots of levitation were only capable of bringing him up and down a short ways, and he wasn’t able to move horizontally. Thankfully, Asher discovered that after putting away his Ring of False Death and pulling out his flaming dagger, the warming enchantment combined with his own regeneration was enough to combat the unnatural cold. Samantha remained up in the sky, and in a hilarious solution that Asher wasn’t convinced wasn’t just Moxy messing with him, the warrior rode atop Rulfar’s shoulders, keeping well out of the swirling, freezing mists covering the land. Kroult of course was unfazed by the cold, being an undead and all, though Scritchems elected to stay behind, giving them all one last encouraging coo before flying away from the chilling mists.

With the mists having been bested, they quickly followed Samantha’s directions, bringing stability to the wild realm as they headed for the source of death energy. More and more pillars were passed, and Asher blinked as he eventually realized precisely what the changing runes on them seemed to represent. While he still couldn’t read them, somehow, he managed to get the gist. Almost as if he could sense what it was the people who had created them had done so for. They were a warning.

A warning against whatever it was the five of them were running directly toward.
