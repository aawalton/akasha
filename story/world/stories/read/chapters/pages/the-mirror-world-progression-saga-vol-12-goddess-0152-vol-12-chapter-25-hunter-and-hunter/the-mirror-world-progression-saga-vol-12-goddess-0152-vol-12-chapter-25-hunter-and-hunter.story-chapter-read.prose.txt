Unlike most cities, the dark underground beneath the Capital was entirely controlled by the Inquisitors and their enforcers, to get around the city like its ever-following shadow. They called it simply ‘the labyrinth.’

It was deep within this that the high general of the Dominion armies, Arthen Wybayer, sat in his office chair with a ‘mind amplifier’ device on his head, getting observations continually filtered to him from towers, gargoyle eyes, and the like. It allowed him a three-dimensional inspection of the battle as it played out, not unlike what they knew the heretic general had available.

Things had not gone well in killing her, nor in much else, and it was especially bad after the water-dragon summoning. However, the Sage of Truth had finally unleashed his untasteful little trick. Terrifying, but effective, and he’d been told there would be far more before the trumped-up witch queen could challenge the city.

It didn’t much matter that they’d lost half of their southern-side army of peons. The towers were a greater loss, but they had only ever existed to inevitably fall. The attrition had not been what they’d hoped.

But it hardly mattered. The enemy hadn’t even reached the walls, and he had his own tricks to unveil as the battle became more complicated. That was if whatever the First Sage had planned didn’t end things prematurely.

“General, we have an unusual issue,” said a voice in his head, unrecognized but certainly one of the lesser Inquisitors. “A foreign agent has somehow entered the labyrinth and approaches this operations center.”

Arthen was disbelieving. “How is that even possible? Your kind monitors all the entrances.”

“You well know our numbers are depleted. The exact method is not yet clear, but we cannot possibly maintain the entire labyrinth as we once did, especially having to deal with an army at our walls. In any case, we’re recommending a full enforcer unit to intercept and apprehend.”

“You must have a captured image. Show me.”

Momentarily, a brief image flashed, as a figure was seen skulking through the corridors, fully cloaked. A dagger flashed as the figure threw it unerringly, executing the vision not long after he was detected. He’d probably destroyed an eye sentry.

Nonetheless, Arthen laughed at what he saw — he’d just caught the gleam of cat-like eyes. He knew exactly who this was.

How appropriately cat-and-mouse. Hunter versus hunter here at the end. It’s my turn to become the aggressor again, as suits my station. I wouldn’t have it any other way, especially considering I have all the advantages.

Anticipating his master’s sudden inclinations, Arthen’s warg, Winterseed, rose from his bored, lazing state, head raising, bushy tail wagging slightly, hopefully.

“We will take a full unit, yes,” Arthen told the Inquisitor gestalt, “but I will lead it, and it will be composed of my hand-picked best, with backline support from you lot. The best and most discreet, only, mind.”

“We do not advise-”

“Silence! I’m the commander here. I know this rogue, and he’s a slippery eel that will escape the bumbling lesser goons you’d think to toss in his way. Even if your kind were physically present, you’d never put yourselves in harm’s way. The arts of the witchhunters are needed here.”

With that, Arthen took off the harness-like contraption on his head and set it down on his desk, picking up his wide-brimmed hat to replace it. When he rose, Winterseed rose with him.

“Finally!” the warg exclaimed in his head. “Some authentic, personal killing!”

“Easy, beast,” Arthen communicated back in mild admonishment. “You may bite, and you may taste, but we at least need to make an effort to capture, first. No going straight for the throat.”

The whispers in Crow’s mind were increasingly angry, feverish, and chaotic. He was getting less and less discernible intel as he got closer to the target, and that made him sloppier. After the encounter with the floating eye that he killed, he retreated swiftly from that position, doubling back in the labyrinthine halls he traversed and looking for another safe route. Fortunately, though many ways were dead ends leading often to traps, there were always alternate ways to get around.

At first, the whispers were subdued as he made his way north again, but after a time, they ramped up like madness itself, drowning out his own thoughts. He had to find a hiding spot as he internally tried to get a grapple on them. At a crossroads area, he ducked behind a mass of ancient ceramic vases, untouched perhaps for centuries.

“Stop!” he shouted in his own head. “You’re making this impossible, damn it! Shut the fuck up!”

But they kept on and on, as Crow wondered what to do, unwilling to keep going when the whispers were such a distraction to his focus.

Finally, finally, they just suddenly went quiet, even as Crow felt a tremendous, ominous sense of pushing and straining at the fabric of reality. They were poised and ready, like an avalanche waiting for one last vibrational brushstroke.

It was like the calm of a storm.

Crow heard, saw signs, and otherwise felt presences coming from every direction. More than anything, though, his certainty of being ‘caught’ was felt in the blanketing aura of something quite disruptive to his last avenue of escape: his transportation to his new realm. It had closed off, somehow.

Steeling himself, taking a deep breath, he calmly rose and walked out into the open, into the center of the crossroads.

Internally, he said, “You fuckers had better help me, at this point.”

Shapes could be seen down each pathway as the ways got clogged, all somewhat hidden in deep, cloaking shadows.

Down one corridor, a man laughed. “Hahaha… you just walk out like this? How rich.” His voice was young, but crisp, like that of a natural leader destined for greatness. “Do you plan to give yourself up so easily, then?”

Crow shrugged in the direction of the unseen voice. Behind the veil, he felt the lurking entities that had haunted and helped him practically boiling with aggression. He tried to send them soothing thoughts, preaching patience, as he said, “If you win a one-on-one duel, I will. Otherwise, I win, and go free.”

The laughter came from all directions this time. Finally, the original voice said, “I didn’t expect to be so entertained in this endeavor. I decline your duel, fool. Now, you can either give yourself up, or you will be detained by overwhelming numbers.”

Crow cocked his head and put his hands at his belt. “Show yourself, and I’ll give my answer.”

A scoff came from the dark, as a figure shifted and came into view. “I’m feeling generous, so why not?” A slender yet muscular man was on display, clad in ashy black quality leathers that mixed easily with the shadows, and had on the iconic wide-brimmed hat of the Witchhunters.

His eyes were as sharp and devious as Crow expected, though his face was softer than he’d imagined. Despite the man’s arrogance, his blade was in his hand, ready to be used at a moment’s notice.

“Do you admit to killing the Marchioness Agatha?”

The man smirked at him, still quite amused. “Is that why you came all this way? Not at your precious witch queen’s bidding, but to settle a score? Quite a personal journey to take, but I’m afraid it ends in dissatisfaction. However, I will grant you the boon of answered questions if you surrender. It’ll have to be after the battle, because I have to get back to it soon, but you have my word — the word of Arthen Wybayer — that we’ll have a talk, and you’ll be unharmed until then.”

That actually gave Crow pause for consideration, but within him, the avalanche waiting to fall was getting very close to impossible to hold back.

I guess this isn’t in the cards. Ah well. But I have to get a confession.

Why he felt this, he wasn’t sure, and it wasn’t literally true, but he needed it all the same. “If this is true, give me a taste, then — answer the question. I’ll know I can trust your word if the truth comes from your lips. Otherwise, I have little reason to trust you at all.”

Arthen sniffed and shook his head in disbelief, looking away briefly before regarding Crow again, slowly getting more sober-faced as he studied the other, eyes locked. Finally, he replied, “I can respect that, not to mention respect a man so bold in the face of death. I see that you have the soul of a hunter inside you. So be it, hunter: yes, I arranged her death, as screened through many others in front of me, and covered my tracks well. It’s impressive that you determined this, as it should have been impossible. Just as you being here should be. So there you have it, hunter. The truth is, I’d prefer an exchange of questions between us, as I have my own curiosity to satiate. That’s why I offer the deal.”

Crow took a deep breath, breathing in the satisfaction of the confession, like getting to the end of a long, long road and staring at the reward. In some way, everything locked into place at that moment.

Nodding, Crow replied, “Thanks for that, Arthen. Truly. I would absolutely agree to the deal, though there remains a tender matter of anxiety to resolve on the part of my friends.”

Arthen cocked an eyebrow, both suddenly suspicious and perhaps a little bit doubting Crow’s sanity at that point. “Your what now? Who?”

“Them.” Crow gestured vaguely with his hands, and reached within himself to tear open the very thin veil still between reality and vengeance. The avalanche, the storm, was finally unleashed.

A rush of screaming, frenzied spirits burst out from him in all directions.
