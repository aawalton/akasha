“It’s a Tier four,” Valence announces. It almost feels like the pressure in the room changes as several of us let out the breaths we’re holding.

“It’s odd to consider that that’s a relief,” Josiah remarks, “but such is life here.”

I nod at him as others make noises of agreement.

“It could be worse,” I agree. “At least we’ve already proven we can kill one of them – and in conditions that favoured it at that.”

“This isn’t much better, though,” Josiah points out, his expression faintly pinched. “With as many beasts to get through as there are surrounding it.”

Though Valence’s scouts, with their specialised Skills and instruments, were the ones sent out to identify the definitive Tier of our foe, my Companions have been the ones providing the other details. The flying squad – Sirocco, Bastet, Ivor, and Noir – have been particularly helpful, giving us a literal bird’s eye view of the situation.

We haven’t yet set eyes on the instigator of all of this, but we’ve been able to narrow down where it is most likely to be – the unnatural position of the army of beasts, surrounding a cave system on the other side of the river, is rather indicative.

“At least even the weaker members of our forces can kill Tier ones and twos,” points out Layton calmly. “That’s more than they could do to the roc.”

“That was a completely different style of battle,” Valence argues. “Here we’re fighting the sea – everyone might be able to scoop out a cup of water, but when a wave crashes over us, the ship struggles to remain afloat.”

“It’s just a matter of tactics,” Layton disagrees.

“I’m more concerned about the Tier four potentially escaping if it realises we’ve taken down most of its army,” I put in. “This isn’t an enemy we want to have to fight a second time.”

“Is it likely to try to run?” Valence asks, a slight frown on his face. “It’s a Tier four.”

“An unusual one,” Elarion muses, eyeing the map we’ve been leaning over with consideration. He taps where we’ve placed markers indicating the Tier threes. “I’ve heard of Tier fours commanding beasts – Tier threes too – but I’ve never heard of one having quite such a defensive formation.”

I nod.

“If my guess is right, it has very low Constitution and is therefore fearful about letting enemies close.”

Valence’s frown deepens.

“Not to doubt your expertise, my lord, but…Tier fours are known to be durable. I don’t think I’ve ever read of one that isn’t.”

“And I’m not trying to say that it’s likely to die to a single blow,” I acknowledge. “But in comparison to the roc, or even compared to some Tier threes we’ve faced, I suspect that we’ll find this one is…fragile.”

“What makes you think that?” Valence asks, though he isn’t the only one looking at me in doubt.

I don’t blame them – the idea of Tier fours being durable is the basis of every strategy I’ve read to face them, and our encounter with the wind roc only reinforced that impression.

“I know from experience that beasts have a limited amount of…Evolution energy that they use to make changes. Most choose a roughly balanced build, but not all. Given how unusually powerful its parasites are, it will have had to give up something. I’m making the calculated guess that that is its durability based on the parasites themselves.”

“That is a dangerous assumption to make,” Elarion warns, his expression troubled.

Layton nods.

“Just because its tool is physically weak parasites doesn’t mean that it is a similar creature. Creatures that are not very durable do not generally reach Tier two, let alone any higher. If we base our plans on its physical weakness, the consequences could be disastrous if it turns out not to be true.”

“I take your point,” I accept, “but the truth is that we haven’t seen it yet so any of our plans could go wrong. Even if we assume that the parasites are no indication of what the mastermind is capable of, let’s look at its behaviour. Have you ever read of a physically powerful Tier four that allows its minions to fight all of its battles for it?”

It’s a genuine question – I know that I could be wrong and I certainly haven’t had enough time to study the histories in the way these lords have.

Yet, as they remain silent, I feel more convinced that I’m not.

I nod.

“Heck, I’m not sure that the Tier four has even come close to our settlement. It could have just sent one of its minions to contaminate the wells.”

Thoughtful silence reigns for a moment. I can tell that they’re not entirely convinced, but they’re willing to give me the benefit of the doubt. Ultimately, I’m the closest thing to a beast expert they have – though I don’t have the experience some of the more mundane tamers in the settlement have, my experiential knowledge base is quite different from theirs, and I’ve been able to learn a good deal of accepted Moriaxar knowledge about beasts and how they develop. And make plans for correcting some of the more egregious errors.

“Why is it attacking us anyway?” Valence wonders.

“Does it matter?” Elarion asks with a hint of impatience. “It’s attacking us and we refuse to allow it to destroy our settlement. We need to destroy it before it destroys us.”

“Perhaps we can ask it later,” I offer Valence. “But for now, Elarion has a point.”

“Then what are you suggesting we do?” Josiah asks.

“Well, first, we need to destroy its army. And in the course of that, make sure it doesn’t escape. If it is weak physically, it won’t want to open itself to a confrontation with us.” I consider the question for a moment, then look at Elarion. “Do you think the Plant Mages could guard the exits?”

Elarion taps one finger on the table top thoughtfully.

“Few of them are combat-capable. I don’t want them in danger – we need them too much.”

“What about sending the Earth Mages with them?” Valence suggests.

I nod at him.

“That’s a good idea – they’ll be able to find the exits more easily that way too. We can also send a small group of fighters to help protect them while they work.” I think about a certain owl-bear. “I’ll see whether Artio wants to join one of the groups – he’s pretty combat capable.”

“I doubt he’ll want to go,” Elarion refutes. “He’s more likely to want to be in the middle of the battle.”

I pause a moment, eyeing Elarion thoughtfully. I still haven’t had the time to sit down with either of them and find out exactly what happened. All I know is that Elarion explored the root network, found Artio while doing so, and helped him get out. Enough knowledge for now, but hardly satisfactory.

Now Artio is fine, I’m back to worrying about Kalanthia – the Bond has been all but non-existent in the last few weeks. I know she’s alive, but that’s all. Either she’s behind impressive wards, or she’s gone a long way away.

We could really do with her right now.

“I’m just concerned about putting non-combatants on exits from which a Tier four might come out,” Layton continues. “Weak Constitution or not, I doubt it will be helpless – it has those parasites if nothing else.”

“Then we make sure they have orders to keep their distance and observe, and also give them the means of communicating with us,” Josiah suggests with a note of impatience as he continues surveying the map. “Markus, what exactly is your proposed battle strategy here? There are a lot of beasts. And our people aren’t in the best of conditions right now.”

Valence nods, ticking points off on his fingers.

"Limited potions. Many of our people weakened from days of poor rations. Our healers overwhelmed with the parasitic infection and injuries. And many of our fighters have already been fighting for candlemarks against the beast waves. This Tier four has had considerable time to build its strength while we have only been weakening since we arrived."

"Some of our fighters have levelled up," Layton points out. "It's not entirely accurate to say we've only been weakening. Our Classers have been benefiting from the environment. I've levelled up once myself."

He accepts the gestures of congratulations with a grateful nod.

“It’s a good question, Josiah, though Valence and Layton have good points there too. You’re right that this will strain our settlement’s resources to the limit. However, I’m not sure you’re considering how much of a boon this might be for our people as well – the meat alone from the beasts in this last wave will go a long way towards easing the famine conditions we’re under.”

“Assuming that it’s safe to eat meat infested with those parasites,” Josiah points out.

I nod.

“We will have to verify that, yes. And besides that, as Layton indicated, this battle of attrition will strengthen us – as long as we are able to prevail. But in comparison to fighting the roc, we are far more prepared, and, as we’ve already acknowledged, at least everyone can play a part here.”

“You have a point there, Markus,” Josiah acknowledges. His eyes are shadowed as he remembers the past. “Our biggest issue when facing the roc was just how hard it was to pierce its hide and make any significant injury.”

“Can you Tame the Tier threes?” Valence suddenly blurts out. He lifts his eyes from where they had been studying the map and his cheeks colour slightly as he realises he just cut straight across Josiah. “My apologies, Lord Josiah.”

“No offence taken,” the lord replies, clearly mollified by the quick apology. “It’s an intriguing question – if we could turn the enemy's troops against it, that would certainly save us resources.”

I grimace at the question.

“Holding a Tier three when they don’t want to be Bound is a challenge,” I comment. I don’t tell them that I already feel a little under strain. At least I should be able to release those people I Bound to heal them – neither I nor the healers have seen any signs of complications since I removed the parasites.

I wish I could be in the healing rooms right now – there are still plenty of parasites to deal with and the infestation in the Tier threes just emphasises how important it is to get rid of them while they’re still developing.

At least I was able to take the time to check my companions – and remove growing parasites in the three Tier one raptorcats, two Warrior samurans, and, most worryingly, Bastet. I’m still struggling with the horror that I felt when I discovered that. The baby samurans are next on my list to check, but that requires creating Bonds with them. After recent events, I was planning on doing so anyway, but given the proposed strategy, not until after this Tier four is dealt with.

“Markus?”

I blink, pulled out of my thoughts by Elarion’s impatient question.

“Sorry, my mind went on a tangent there. Anyway, I cannot guarantee that I could turn the enemy’s Tier threes to become our allies.”

Elarion’s brow creases slightly while Valence sighs audibly in disappointment. Layton and Josiah just look a little disheartened.

“However,” I continue thoughtfully. “Even if I can only hold the Bond for long enough to remove the parasite, that at least would remove the beasts from the Tier four’s direct control.” I wouldn’t be able to remove the networks, but I could kill the parasites – without the controller, the network should be inert, though it might make it easier for a reinfection.

“Which would surely mean they would be unlikely to want to continue fighting on its behalf,” Layton remarks. Valence makes a fervent sound of agreement.

“That’s my guess,” I confirm. “Of course, they might continue fighting because they’re angry, or because they see it as an opportunity to advance themselves, but I will do my best to point them at the Tier four and its army.”

“It seems like a sound strategy,” Elarion remarks. “It is easier for you to Bind creatures that are already defeated and confined, is it not?” His tone is light, but I know him well enough by now to hear the edge to it.

“It is,” I agree warily.

“Then perhaps that should be our main strategy – to weaken and distract the beasts until you are able to come and take their blade from the enemy’s hand.”

I eye him for a moment, but there are no clues to his thoughts written on his face. I nod slowly.

“That sounds like a plan. And then when the Tier threes are no longer infected, a party of elites can move onto the Tier four while the others make sure that no infected beasts escape,” I say with grim determination.

Seeing the nods and matching determination on the faces looking at me, I give a small, humourless smile.

“Let’s make sure this is a battle we only have to fight once.”
