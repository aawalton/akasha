“So tell me, why do you want to learn Meditation?” Senior Brother Won poured out two cups of tea as they sat inside the small inn’s quarters.

Aida shuffled uncomfortably. She wouldn’t have felt so uncomfortable lying about her motivation if Brother Won wasn’t looking at her with the kindest, yet most piercing eyes she had come across in recent memory.

She was quite certain she wouldn’t be able to fool him, not with the way he stared unblinkingly into her soul.

Her mana senses told her Brother Won wasn’t some opponent she had to be wary of; he had a little more mana than a typical nonpractitioner, but not enough to suggest he could actually effect any meaningful change with the elements.

What if he’s secretly a Master? Aida remembered what Ezra had said about Masters being able to suppress their mana; and it certainly made sense for an instructor who was preparing to teach a risky technique to be at a Master level.

And she definitely didn’t want him to think she wasn’t serious about learning a dangerous technique; she had to give him as much truth as possible.

“Professor Bruce thought that learning Meditation would be the best way for me to help save a friend,” Aida replied softly. She let the pain of thinking of Ezra show on her face. “I don’t know if it can, but… we can’t think of any other solution, so I’m willing to risk it.

“I also believe the condition my friend is under is linked to the strange… things… we’ve been seeing. The monster attacks, a similarly odd sleep I witnessed in Buddington Town before coming here, the things people have said at the clinic here…” She trailed off before remembering she still hadn’t fully articulated her point. “I think if I can make contact with my friend in the Ascended plane, we may find some answers that can help explain what Wyndia has been seeing.”

“I see.” Brother Won sipped at his tea, his eyes never leaving her face. “And you understand the full risks of what you’re asking to learn?”

“I do.” Aida mirrored him, sipping from her own cup but barely tasting the flavor.

“I’m not convinced you do,” he said, setting his cup down and removing his glasses. He began polishing the lenses with a small cotton cloth he pulled out of his sleeve. “Your desire is noble and to be commended, but you are young, and a powerful practitioner. The biggest failing of youth is that you feel invincible, because your depth of experience is still as of yet shallow. Based on what you’ve experienced, you may feel as if you can give everything up, but the reality is that you can’t yet conceive of what you must give up.”

“Forgive me, Brother Won, but you don’t really think I would let my friend die?” Aida asked rhetorically. “It’s not like I have another option and I refuse to pursue it—we literally have nothing at the moment.”

“I am not saying that I presume to know what you are really thinking, I’m just letting you know that you don’t have to have such lofty goals. It is fine to want to just pursue your own interests, and not risk the very good thing you have going,” the monk said blithely. “Another lesson youth has not learned yet is that life goes on. As time passes, things that seem life-altering are discovered to be quite inconsequential in the long run.”

She knew what this monk was saying was technically correct. She could feasibly move on with her life, and leave Ezra to languish in the school. But there was no denying this “learned” man was also being incredibly dismissive, and that irked her far more than if he had just refused to teach her.

She didn’t want to waste her time groveling to some “mystical man” just to beg for him to teach her a technique that they didn’t know would work or not; it wasn’t as if it was a guaranteed solution, as if she just needed to learn to wield a sword so that she could physically stab the monster in the Lake.

After all, she might actually get enough information to piece together the bigger picture just from working at Relief for Health. Healer Bokar, Charlotte, even the patients coming in for treatment this morning—they had all disclosed novel pieces of information about what they had observed. She just needed time to put it all together.

However, something about the way Brother Won kept watching her made her think he wasn’t truly trying to dissuade her from learning Meditation.

“Are you testing me?” Aida asked slowly. He blinked innocently. “You’re trying to give me an out. Trying to release me from some… perceived obligation?”

They stared at each other for a long moment, Aida refusing to look away from his cutting, obsidian eyes. Finally, Brother Won gave an exaggerated sigh and pushed his gleaming glasses back onto his nose.

“You got me! I just didn’t want to waste my time teaching a youth who will only make a half-hearted effort in learning a very dangerous skill. I’ll be held liable if you mess up, you know.”

He stroked his smooth chin as he considered her. He nodded, as if affirming something to himself, before he leaned forward. “All right. Let’s start with the basics. I want to make sure you are conditioned enough for Meditation training.”

~ * ~ * ~ * ~

Senior Brother Wonathan’s approach to meditation was completely different from Professor Bruce’s, and different from the public meditation class she saw at the temple.

As she suspected, Brother Won’s handling of mana was a lot more advanced than the impression he first gave. He used his bamboo pole to poke the air around Aida while she was Mana Cycling, and though she could ignore the physical disturbance easily enough, she found that he was actually able to disturb her mana in a way she hadn’t experienced before.

“You need to learn how to consolidate your mana,” he told her. “It’s quite the opposite from using your mana to increase your awareness in the physical world. If your mana is too wispy, it won’t be able to pierce the veil between the physical and spiritual realm.”

After several more pokes, each one more uncomfortable than the last, he sent her home on wobbly legs with a cheerful reminder to come back on Sun Day morning.

“See if you can tighten up your mana by half by then,” he called as he waved her off. “I want your mana to be visible!”

“This is impossible,” Aida mumbled to herself as she let herself into her room. As far as she knew, only Masters were able to visibly manifest their mana. That couldn’t possibly be the requirement for entering the spirit realm; Ezra certainly wasn’t at that level, no matter how talented he was.

How long would it take for her to reach Brother Won’s expectations? The world would change far too drastically for any potential success to be meaningful. The only benefit she could see out of continuing with this… spiritual meditation training was that she could improve her mana control.

She rolled onto her back, looking up at the ceiling, reflecting on the trajectory of her life.

She should have asked Brother Won more questions. Been more thorough with their interview. She had been so focused on the goal to “learn” Meditation, she forgot she could—should—have asked Brother Won why he was willing to teach her.

And he’s from the same place as that kid who can’t eat, either…

Aida pushed herself back into sitting position, irritated with herself. She basically knew nothing about her new teacher, and she let a perfectly good opportunity to learn more about what was happening in the Grasslands go. She didn’t have time to grill the patients on specifics of what they’d witnessed while she was working, because the line was neverending and they were more worried about their own health. Brother Won, on the other hand, seemed pretty relaxed when it came to talking about how odd the events that were happening in the world were.

Either nobody in their monastery had experienced anything weird, or… a shiver went down her spine when she remembered how his dark eyes had stared unblinkingly at her.

What if he’s possessed, too?

She knew she needed backup; she wasn’t sure she could handle Brother Won on her own, if he was a nefarious spirit. Especially since he could physically manipulate her own mana. But the Talonwings had already left, and Lily was off in some secluded wood… and she wasn’t the kind of practitioner one would choose to help fight.

But… if he really was malignant, then he could easily have harmed her during the hours they were alone together. What reason would he have to let her walk away?

Suddenly paranoid, she expanded her mana awareness, doing a quick sweep of her room and dormitory building.

Nothing out of the ordinary. No foreign presence.

Calming herself down, Aida forced herself to think rationally.

First of all, there wasn’t anything she could do right now; it was quite late already. It would be stupid if she just rushed out in the middle of the night to confront a would-be dangerous spirit.

Secondly, he let her go; that meant he either was someone who intended to train her, or he believed she was still a fool and not a threat to whatever his plan was. Rushing over now to confront him with no information would just be triggering any traps she couldn’t avoid.

Her best bet was to go see him tomorrow, early in the morning, under the guise of wishing to better get to know him. Play the part of a zealous, naive student, which wouldn’t be difficult to do at all.

Satisfied with having a plan—no matter how foolish it was—Aida fell asleep, praying for a full night’s rest.
