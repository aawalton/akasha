Chapter 19

Decor

We all enjoy the porridge Yorin made, though how she made it so quickly escapes me, then we take our time packing up our camping gear while Angel explores and plays with Korrigan and Kyuuga. By the time we’re about ready to leave, even the driver is up, and he’s shocked to see more girls present than were here when he went to bed, especially with one of them being the Sacred Priestess!

He wisely bows out and goes to tend to the horse, rather than get involved in whatever we’re messing with, probably due to the conversation we had last night. So we bid him farewell, leave some tea on the fire for him (missing out on the porridge is the consequence of sleeping in), and assure him that if he needs to return to town for anything, he doesn’t need to wait on us, and we head down the road for the Forest Cavern.

… Oh, and we absolutely don’t tell him that the cute harpy girl he was spoiling is a dungeon monster. I’m not sure he has the mental fortitude left to handle that one.

We are nearly to the dungeon entrance proper, with Angel admiring the fetching tree line of her own home for the first time, when Yorin adjusts her speed so that she comes alongside me.

“Remmi, it occurs to me that it has been notably beyond an hour since you brought the child out of the dungeon, yet you stated that as the duration. I understand that we are within the dungeon’s domain, but that should yet remain outside of her normal scope of movement.”

“Ah,” I reply as I rub my head with a grin. “That’s the Hedge Witch trait. Variables like duration and power are increased, while costs are decreased. If the effect of a spell says it lasts for an hour, it’s actually an hour and a half. Between that and the mana cost reduction skill you recommended, the cost is a quarter of what it should be, too.”

The priestess nods as she considers my words. “Yes, that trait is similar to Xuhi’s Witchblade Master trait, though Hedge Witch’s effects are weaker. It was originally simply Witchblade, but as she honed her spells and techniques, it transformed into a mastery version of itself. If you continue to expand your capabilities, your trait may do the same. Her Apprentice of the White Witch trait eased the burden of learning those new abilities greatly, but being a Hero effectively places you on the same plane in that regard.”

I diplomatically refrain from bringing up the fact that I’m aware there is an entire System-recognized trait tied entirely to having a connection to Yorin, who I know is said White Witch. It is something she has never brought up or volunteered, so I’ve decided to respect her privacy. Instead, I pivot the topic slightly. “I’ve actually been considering getting another passive skill to boost the performance of my spells, too. If I can get Empower up to a doubled stat boost, Kaido’s God Fist skill can go suck eggs! Not to mention what it would do for Spiral Shot’s penetration or any other combat spells I’m looking at.”

Again, she nods. “That is a reasonable plan. Many overlook the exponential potential of basic mastery in favor of more visually spectacular novelty. The skill that you desire is Magical Potency. It increases a spell’s performance at the drawback of increasing its cost, but you can opt not to use it at any casting if you so desire. However, with your current mana power, I do not doubt that you can afford it.”

I pull it up as we keep walking, as there’s little reason not to go ahead and get it if it’s affordable. There won’t be any need for it in the Forest Cavern, but, hey, procrastination is the enemy of progress, right?

It turns out that it’s actually quite affordable, probably because it’s got a trade-off instead of just being a straight bonus. It applies a 50% potency bonus based on the base value of the augmented spell, but increases the cost the same way. That means it brings Empower up to exactly a 100% increase, or doubled physical stats, but raises my adjusted cost from two and a half mana per second to three and three-quarters per second. That definitely lowers Empower’s operational time, from four solid minutes with my current mana to just two minutes forty.

But then, back when I first got Hedge Witch, I celebrated it bringing my Empower time up to two minutes, and I’m still ahead of that. And if I can’t finish a fight in over two and a half minutes, and can’t find a moment in that time to knock back a vial of my Essence Sweetened Apple Juice for enhanced regeneration, we’ve got way bigger problems that more time probably isn’t going to solve.

This means that, now, when I Empower, my Strength and Toughness will match my Intellect at four hundred points, and my Agility will nearly double it by jumping to seven hundred. I get a giddy rush of dopamine just thinking about it. I push down the desire to put the Forest Cavern off and rush to a Silver dungeon just to punch stuff with it. Meanwhile, Spiral Shot is giving a four-hundred percent increase to penetration and a fifty percent bonus to damage.

When Yorin frowns at noticing my fingers twitching, I beam a grin her way as I fold the offending digits behind my back.

It’s weird how that never seems to make anyone feel better. It just makes frowns deepen and gazes more accusatory.

“Anyroad!” I try to push past any possible lectures about my number-go-up-philia.

When we do reach the doorway to the cavern part of the Forest Cavern, with its towering harpy art, it informs us that its energy level has returned to Moderate, which seems like an indication we’ve already succeeded with our short-term mission. I stop us just inside anyway, though.

“Hold on, everyone,” I say. “Before we go any further, there’s something I need to check.”

Everyone looks at me with confusion, but since even I don’t know what I’m going to see, I opt to ignore them instead of trying to give them an explanation. I place my attention before me, and see if I can call up the technical data that my new trait mentioned.

Sure enough, a system window opens before me, but it’s awash with shifting graphs and numbers. I chew my lip, wanting to dig into it right there, but that would be rude to everyone waiting on me, so I swipe it away instead.

“Everything alright, Remmi?” Ayre asks.

I nod. “Yeah, just checking another thing that new trait gave me, but it’s going to take a bit to sort through. Let’s head on down for now. If I’m right, I’ll have time to look over it before we get into any of the fun stuff.”

That last comment gets Yorin to frowning again, though only a little one this time. “Remmi, your concept of fun has historically borne great risks, not merely to physical wholeness, but to the financial stability of whatever region you are within and the psychological well-being of those around you. If this is to be one such time, tell us now. I beg of you, do not save it for a surprise.”

I wave it off with half a laugh. “No, no, nothing like that. It’s just that, if the plan went through as intended, the dungeon should be separated into two parts: One safe, and one where the action happens. If so, it should be fine to go over the information before we go into the latter without making you all stand around the entrance.”

Her flat look does not show confidence in my claim.

“I’ll tell you the same thing I told Ayre, Yorin,” I say. “Frowning too much causes early wrinkles!”

Unlike Ayre, the priestess remains unflappable. “If you truly held such concern for me, Remmi, you would not present so many causes for me to do so.”

And just like that, she walked right past me and began making her way down the stairwell, leaving the rest of us with nothing to do but follow behind her.

The stairs still open up into that park-like main chamber, but the changes are immediately obvious. Right away, there’s a reception counter ready to greet adventurers coming into the dungeon, and the tables of vegetables have been cleared out of the park area. In their place is a collection of food carts and a long, rectangular pavilion lined with beds that is likely the medical area for treating injuries sustained in the dungeon.

On this side, the reception counter is also revealed to fold around into a second face that looks rather like a storefront, and against what was once a solid wall is a wide, three-floored building that looks every bit like a forest inn built into a cliff face under leaf-dappled sunlight. Near to the park fountain is a raised platform that looks perfect for an improvised performance.

All of it is completely and utterly empty, as if a village full of people just took up everything but the structures and left.

“It is as an eerily well-preserved abandoned settlement, reclaimed by nature,” Yorin summarizes in a way I can only agree with.

I nod heartily as I cross my arms, proud of the result of the core’s hard work. “Well, yeah. Even if we’re going for Friendly Neighborhood Dungeon, rep still demands a certain decor vibe.”

“I was about to start exploring the place before you summoned me,” Angel puts in. “Do we want to do it together now?”

I look around at the rest of the group, most of them looking back to me for the same reason, and I nod. “Yeah, I’d say that’s a good idea. We need a solid image of exactly what kind of facilities we’ve got here, so we need a thorough tour anyway. Why don’t you go ahead and lead the way, Angel?”

She’s a bit insecure about that, but with a little encouragement from everyone, she decides to give it her best shot. Since we’re standing in the park area, she starts with that, clearly making her summaries up on the spot. We do our best to be supportive, including whispering suggestions to her every time she stalls.

Because it’s small, we check out the reception counter first, finding ample space for filing cabinets or inventory in the back. There’s nowhere for the receptionist to take a break, but they’re probably just expected to go a dozen yards over to the food carts and benches.

The medical pavilion isn’t huge either, but it’s big enough to treat half a dozen comfortably, and twice that in an emergency. It can probably be staffed by only two people, and if I were handling it, only one of them would need to be an actual healer, like with magic and such. The other would be more than fine as a nurse or paramedic equivalent, with emergency and field medical training.

The inn is bespoke, though. If anything, I’m worried it will make the place in the village feel inadequate. The first floor is a tavern and restaurant with a massive hearth surrounded by open floor, typical of places that rent that area out as a cheap lodging option. The kitchen is fully stocked and move-in ready, and there’s even an indoor well and booze cellar for it. The bedrooms are on the second and third floors, and come in singles, doubles, triples, and suites.

I won’t lie and say we don’t clown around a bit. We do a bit of pretending to be merchants and customers with the empty food carts, and we each take a turn with some brief singing on the stage under the excuse of testing the acoustics. I belt out a little country tune, Ayre does a piece that reminds me rather distinctly of pop, Korrigan sings a traditional children’s song, and Yorin gives a dramatic performance worthy of an opera that I suspect is of religious origins. Even Kyuuga insists on taking a turn with bunny chirps and a foot-kicking drum solo. Angel, of course, doesn’t know any songs, but she tries some pitch ranges anyway.

It’s after we’ve finished the entire tour and are all taking a break at a picnic table near the water fountain that Korrigan gets my attention. I’m focusing on the dungeon’s data feed and making sure that everything is stable and sustainable when she tugs on my arm.

“Hero Remmi, are we going to check that out next?”

The “that” she points to is a massive gate in a stone wall, about where the path toward the boss had been. It’s open, but even with the park area so well lit that it might as well be actual sunlight, nothing is in the doorway but darkness. The embossed angelic harpies are here too, beckoning the bold through the portal.

“Yeah,” I confirm. “That’s our next stop, alright. The dungeon proper.”
