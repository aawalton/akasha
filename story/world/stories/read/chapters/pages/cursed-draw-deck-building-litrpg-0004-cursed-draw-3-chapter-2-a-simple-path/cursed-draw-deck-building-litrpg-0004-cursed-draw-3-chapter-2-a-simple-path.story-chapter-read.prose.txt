“Oh crap, the zombies!” Alley yelled as recollection settled upon him.

“Zombies?” Came a confused and simultaneous reply from Kyera, Denderrra, and even Darius, who was still wrapped up in Alley’s CoreSteel chain.

The four of them and the pair of princes had made their way out of the still-on-fire lobby not long after the Skalean militia had moved in to both fight the blaze and try to rescue any remaining survivors. Alley had detached his hook and used his nigh-unbreakable chain to help the struggling Prince Malchik to restrain Darius, and then the pair of them had dragged the ranting and raving hunter out into the dark of the late night. For the first time since he had arrived in the Frostlands, the continent had lived up to its name and begun to lightly snow. Alley, who had spent almost his entire life in the Deep River Country, was aware of the weather phenomenon, but had never actually seen it.  Were he not so concerned about his friend’s potential transformation the juxtaposition with the burning building would have been almost beautiful.

Biting the nails of his right hand and watching his friend struggle as he processed the appalling amount of Resonance, Alley was far too stressed to appreciate the flickering orange glow as it reflected off of the falling snowflakes. Still after a few minutes of ranting about slaughter, and his face blurring into one that looked ominously like his shadowy gargoyle form Darius began to not only calm down but weaken dramatically. He also vomited an utterly inhuman amount of black liquid all around himself, on his front and onto Prince Malchik, but after that, he let out a long groan and was clearly himself again.

“I hate you so much, Alley,” he said with an exhausted smile. “I feel like I’m three hangovers deep, and this black stuff smells awful.”

Letting out possibly the biggest sigh of relief he had ever uttered, Alley smiled back at his friend.

“Hey, blame your parents. It wasn’t my idea that they link our Resonance systems together.”

“Well.” Replied the hunter before slumping down into a non-bile covered section of snow. “I’m starting to think it was very irresponsible of them.”

Alley agreed and began untying his friend, until Darius shook his head.

“Leave it for now, you know, just in case.” The redheaded boy had said weakly.

“So,” Said Kyera with a wistful gaze up at the huge building that while still on fire but had been reduced to more of a smoulder than a blaze thanks to the efforts of anti-fire portion of the Skalean militia. “I guess that was more Doom in action, huh? Good thing you both have mystical transformations now…for some reason.”

Both boys let out a soft groan, and Alley began to try to explain exactly what had happened to him.

“I, Look I can’t just do that whenever I want to, It was certainly nice to be the power house for once, but the whole thing has some high costs, and…” That was when his blurred memories of having ‘The Cursed King’ inhabit him began to settle, and Alley started yelling about zombies.

Zombies were one of the weaker kinds of human undead a person might come across; usually needing to attack in swarms or rely on the ability of Undeath Resonance to resurrect them repeatedly until their prey was worn down and exhausted.

Alley had interacted with zombies a few times and read about them even more. In theory a small pack of them should be no real threat to the Frostlander militia members, especially since the amount of Undeath Resonance in the city should be so low that they wouldn’t even be able to resurrect themselves.

There were only two problems with that particular line of thought. One, the militia would not be expecting near mindless undead to attack them as they moved through a burning building and checked for survivors. Two: As much as he hadn’t wanted to, Alley had functionally Manifested a portion of Anaten within himself. Denderra’s brother hadn’t invented necromancy, but he was perhaps the best at it out of anyone who had ever lived. Even a weird amalgamation of him and Alley made from bleeding soul fragments had brought with it an utterly insane amount of Resonance. The same Resonance Alley and Darius had spent approximately ten minutes expelling into the air near and in the building that held those zombies.

Alley was confident that it wasn’t enough to permeate the wood and stone that made up the huge inn on a more permanent basis, but for tonight at least, the zombies that ‘he’ had animated would easily be able to pull themselves back together.

‘Why had The Cursed King done that?’ Alley mused as he turned and ran back into the smouldering building he had fought so hard and almost died to escape. There had to be some reason. Alley didn’t have anywhere near as good a recollection of his time with that being replacing him as he’d like, but he did know that it had been trying its best to keep him alive. He even had hazy memories of it talking to him just before it left, not to mention the sense he’d gotten at the time was far from a feeling of malice or antagonism. If anything, Alley thought that The Cursed King wanted him to…get stronger.

“Oh, you have got to be kidding me!” Alley shouted. That had to be it, the necromantic powerhouse that had briefly inhabited Alley’s body wanted him to grow, to gain ranks. How did one go from rank eleven to rank ten? Every child knew the answer to that, and it was arguably the simplest of all ranks to overcome.

All one had to do was evolve a regular creature Card, not a Unique like the Pirate Karietto had been trying to use to gain his own next rank. Just a regular monster that had the same Resonance flowing through it as whatever the main Resonance as your Deck was.

How did one evolve a regular non-unique creature? Well, that was easy, you just got together five copies of the same creature Card and merged them into one. Anyone could do it; you didn’t even need to slay and Card the creatures yourself, just own the Card somehow.

Alley slipped the Goret Brotherhood mask he had taken from one of the killers over his face as he sprinted up the less damaged of the two flights of stairs. As much as he didn’t want to risk being mistaken for one of the unscrupulous mercenaries by the militia, the one-eyed mask would magically protect him from breathing in too much of the billowing smoke, and even from whatever flames and heat remained in the collapsing building.

Alley’s mind was still racing as his exhausted legs carried him up the flight of stairs and through the first level of the giant inn. As the three Resonances his Deck made use of were Curse, Undeath, and Shadow, with the most major one being Undeath Alley was at something of a disadvantage when it came to evolving a non-unique monster. In particular, it had become almost impossible for him once he had taken the Transiteration gate from Cursed Isle to the Frostlands. Undead were so rare up here in the far north that if it wasn’t for the Cursed Draco-lich currently residing in his Library most people wouldn’t even believe undead existed. There was some possibility of simply buying undead Cards from the various traders that came and went through a city as large as Skalean, but given the rarity of undead up here Alley suspected if he could even find someone carrying those kinds of Cards they would be prohibitively expensive.

Well, The Cursed King had gone out of his way to solve that problem for Alley in one of the fastest and most direct ways possible. The Necromantic being had simply animated the corpses in the building above, so that Alley would have a supply of reasonably weak monsters he could spend twelve or so hours hunting down and turning into Cards. If they were all the same type of zombie, which he was growing increasingly confident they would be, then Alley would have a fairly simple path to gaining his next rank.

At least, it should be a fairly simple path. What may have happened instead is that he had doomed whatever few survivors were still in the building or the heroic militia who had entered the building to find them. Hopefully, that wouldn’t be the case, and he would find a bunch of zombies already dead, and waiting for Alley to start Carding them. The thing was, he couldn’t shake the feeling that if that were the case, then militia members and the civilians they had come to save would already have been making their way out of the building, yet there was no sign of them outside, in the lobby, or here on the first floor.
