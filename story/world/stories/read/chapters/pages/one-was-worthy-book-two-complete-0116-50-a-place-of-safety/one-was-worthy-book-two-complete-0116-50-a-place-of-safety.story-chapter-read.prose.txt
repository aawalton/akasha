Twilight had come as I ran.

Screams grew louder the closer I got to the wall. My lungs were burning with the effort, but I didn’t dare slow down. The number of need bubbles melding into the collective had slowed to a trickle but there were still more than one hundred and fifty people who believed me. People who trusted me enough to listen when I asked.

Even I wasn’t sure what would happen, but I knew that everything I needed was in the barn. There were individual need bubbles I’d made with more requirements than this reached by the time I reached the bridge.

Just a little farther.

I tried to force myself not to look at the wall, but I couldn’t resist. The logs were trembling. People were leaping off the battlements, often carrying those who couldn’t make such a jump, only to leap back to the top in the hopes of saving more people. We were already failing, and Freyna hadn’t even shown her true capabilities yet.

Arrows sailed over the wall, and I could feel a blaze of heat as the enemy army finally got close enough for Tacurian’s normal fire magic to be useful. That terrified me. It meant that Freyna truly had countered my crossbows.

I shook my head and ran faster, turning off the road towards my home. If I’d had any free points, I’d probably have placed them into Running.

I smashed through the door to the barn, knocking it askew on its hinges, before finally laying my eyes on the supplies I needed.

The need in my mind lit up. The need of Pemolar’s Hill itself, or at least a decent percentage of its population.

I activated it. Two Iron ingots. Wood scraps. A bit of clay.

Time…

Slowed.

I gasped, as the world seemed to pulse. Color drained away from my vision for a moment as I gazed around me.

I saw through the flimsy walls of my home. The tiny amount of ingredients lifted into the air as they had always done. Red, black, and dark purple light flared like a sunburst over my head, but I saw none of it. My eyes seemed to zoom out. My viewpoint wasn’t large enough. I needed to be able to see more. The need required it.

I stepped outside, eyes gazing through animals, people, and buildings as I witnessed the land all around Pemolar’s Hill, taking it all in at once as if my eyes could see in all directions. Perhaps viewing it from above like the mini-map in a game. I perceived it all.

The huge undertaking of the wall to the East. Unsteady. The northern Trench, rapidly filling with advancing soldiers. The Soldiers to the west, killing rift monsters by the dozens.

A fine outline.

I lifted my hand, guided by my talent as I always had been. Even in the trance-like state, I gasped as they rose.

It began to the north. My people quavered, but my talent was gentle. Steel formed beneath their feet, before bursting through the bottom of the trench. Tacurian and Eschal alike, screamed in panic as they lost their footing. Guard rails made from steel that only looked like stone blocks, erupted behind them, arresting their falls even as they were lifted into the air.

Like dominos falling in reverse, the wall began at the Denarlan Road, lifting up out of the ground as if it had always been there, waiting just beneath the soil for my call. It spread in both directions to the west and east. Massive steel blocks fit together like puzzle pieces. Over the road, a gate formed, and before it. A drawbridge. What in the world would we need a drawbridge for!?

The question was answered before I’d even finished the thought.

The walls weren’t the only thing that grew. The river, an ever-present part of Pemolar’s Hill cutting straight through the town and flowing out of the lake to the north, expanded. Water flowed in, surrounding the outside of the walls as the trench widened.

Eschal soldiers unfortunate enough to be close to the burgeoning wall panicked as the ground beneath their feet gave way, only to be washed downstream as water poured from the river in an unnatural torrent, flowing in the wrong direction. In moments, that shallow ditch became ten, twelve, or even twenty feet deep.

The wall continued growing, bursting out of the river, a sluice gate and all of the equipment to lift it forming as the battlements stretched like a snake. When it reached the wooden wall to the east, it ceased to sprout from the ground, and instead, morphed the wooden wall into a steel palisade.

Our soldiers were shouting. Eschal soldiers were being pushed off the walls, but both sides were in shock as the crumbling wood transformed into a fortress the likes of which the world had never seen. Not even April’s world had ever witnessed a structure like this.

Boulders that had almost crushed the wooden walls now fell like pebbles against a mountain, barely even causing a tremor.

I turned to the west and saw the new walls reach all the way to the lake as people were carried into the sky, up onto the new ramparts. The walls didn’t stop at the lake, though. Instead, they extended into it, further than any dock on both sizes creating a massive, artificial bay.

Staircases behind the walls formed almost as an afterthought, and four towers rose even higher at the points where the walls needed to turn, their tops capped with square outposts and even higher vantage points.

“By the gods. Oh by the gods,” I heard someone say, and realized mutely that it was me.

I wasn’t idle. I turned away from my home, back to the bar that I loved so dearly.

The walls weren’t enough. Even their mighty strength might crumble. Even their towers filled with arrow slits might not keep everyone safe. The enemy had terrifying wind magic and attacks made of sound. Lightning still struck occasionally, but it had become weaker. Even so, it was proof that these walls alone were not enough to fulfill the need to protect Pemolar’s Hill.

Tears came to my eyes as the light of my talent pulsed. The bar. The place that made me feel the safest, began to glow.

Its walls became mighty steel stones, matching those of the outer defenses but its interior remained warm and inviting. Its old wooden floors stayed as they were, but they expanded, and grew. Staircases multiplied and rose, as the bar was surrounded by an outer wall, yet colossal windows formed, to preserve the open feeling that had always made me feel welcome. Actual bars lined the original room on both sides and tables formed from nothing to fill the void created by all that new space.

The few bedrooms that were barely enough to let the bar qualify as an inn multiplied into a fleet of rooms. The tables which even now held the injured and the dying, became a grand hall that would make any king weep in envy.

The castle spread out from the bar, and homes that would’ve been destroyed were instead pushed, the land temporarily turned to dough, malleable and easily shaped. Siderooms formed. Stables, and kitchens, staff rooms, and suites. An armory formed from the knives and silverware.

All of it paled in comparison to the bar itself.

The symbol of everything safe to me in this world. It collapsed in on itself, folding together as it began to shine an amber color, that reminded me of good ale and better nights. My table, my stage became a dais, and upon it sat a pedestal, holding the new crystal. An amber-hued wooden crystal.

I shuddered as a pulse erupted from the new artifact and washed over me. It hit with a physical force, knocking some people over, and terrifying others as it blasted out past the new walls.

Without warning, all magic directed towards Pemolar’s Hill evaporated. All talents geared towards war sputtered and died, yet its defenders lost nothing. This place was a void. A deadzone. Anyone who meant it harm, would find they lacked the ability to do so.

This place was safe. It always would be.

As abruptly as it began, it ended.

Sound came back, and light returned to normal. Suddenly, I was inside the massive walls of a castle courtyard that dwarfed even Denarla, and a castle that towered over the landscape at least six stories high.

Pemolar’s Hill had become a fortress.

From the collective needs of 153 individuals, you have gained 1,470 free points!

I trembled.
