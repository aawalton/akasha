One of the wanderers, the one who seemed to be in charge, approached Sakura first. His step was so light that Sakura barely noticed how he was already almost breathing in her face. He was tall and lean, angular like a teenager, but still mature enough in appearance. A bright wide cloak hung from his shoulders, embroidered with ribbons and metal plaques, and his long hair was tied back with a colorful cord.

"Marvelous! No, that was simply marvelous!" he exclaimed, throwing up his hands with such passion that Sakura might as well have just stopped the end of the world. "Lady Silence of the Forest, did you see that?" He seemed to be addressing the forest. It was strange. But the man, meanwhile, continued, "We all saw that! It was a miracle! He almost killed us, and you approached him like an old friend!"

Sakura, still standing beside the horse and feeling his warm neck under her palm, was caught off guard.

"I... didn’t do anything special," she said. "I just tried to calm him down."

"Nothing special?" The man pressed a hand to his chest with feeling. "Lady, you single-handedly saved our horse, our merchandise, half our belongings, and possibly a couple of lives as well. Forgive me, but that is very much special!"

Sakura felt uneasy under such stormy admiration. After everything that had happened to her lately, this open, almost childish gratitude felt strange. In the end, she had only calmed a horse. Exactly the kind of horse she had apparently dealt with in her past life. It was a horse. Not a giant glowing slug, not an obsidian monster the size of a tiger, but just a horse.

After everything else, he felt like a real trifle. Like saving the world first, then getting a kitten down from a tree and receiving tearful gratitude for the latter.

Sakura looked away slightly and, as often happened in moments like this, immediately tried to move to something practical.

"I did what I had to do. Better tell me, how is the girl? The one who fell."

The lean wanderer immediately turned halfway around.

"Ria! Soul of mine, are you alive?"

"Unfortunately for some, yes," came from the path.

That same girl was already sitting on a fallen crate, grimacing, but looking lively enough. Someone had helped her up, and now another woman in a bright scarf was fussing near her, offering a vial of potion, but the girl refused. Ria carefully moved her shoulder, then waved her hand to show that no catastrophe had happened.

"I’m fine," she said. "There’ll be a bruise, a reason to whine, and a chance to extort extra food from you, but overall, I’m alive."

"Glory to all mad roads!" the lean man breathed out with feeling and turned back to Sakura. "See? It all worked out! And it worked out, by the way, largely thanks to you." He played with his intonation so brightly and charismatically that it was hard not to be fascinated. Sakura thought he would have made a wonderful actor.

Hoping the man would not notice and suspect anything unnecessary, Sakura decided to carefully “peek” at his name. It probably was not very polite. Sakura still had not fully understood how people in this new world treated that kind of thing, but she was too curious not to use that function again. So she focused, staring at the man’s face, and the System lit up for her:

KREST AVON

Race: Human

The information was almost impossibly simple. Sakura took it into account and, of course, pretended she had not learned anything about this wanderer that he had not said out loud.

Now that the first chaos had passed, she was able to look at them better. These people really were bright, not only in their clothes, but also in their gestures and voices. So confident, optimistic, and relaxed. None of them looked at Sakura with the hostility or suspicion she had already gotten used to. On the contrary. They saw her as their mysterious forest savior. It was even a little disorienting.

The wanderers began fixing the mess their horse had recently made. The lute player was adjusting the shifted harness while quietly soothing the horse, and another woman was gathering the bundles scattered across the road and returning them to the wagon. While the horse had been raging, many things had fallen out of the handcart and the donkey-drawn wagon, and now the group was collecting them and putting them back in their places.

Meanwhile, the lean man bowed again, far too broadly, almost theatrically.

"Allow me at least to learn the name of the savior of our brainless beauty."

Sakura froze, and several questions immediately appeared in her head. First, could he really not learn her name through the System right now? The Gray Veil Salve had long since worn off her skin, and now her name and race could be visible to anyone who wanted to know them.

So it was most likely a matter of politeness. Apparently, in Veyr, proper etiquette meant introducing yourself out loud and not peeking at someone else’s information. Or peeking, but not mentioning it to their face.

Second, she immediately hesitated, wondering whether she should give her real name, the one currently wanted. Or invent another. But if any of the wanderers checked her and Gilbert, they would immediately recognize the lie.

So all that was left was the truth.

"Sakura," she said. "And this is Gilbert."

Gilbert gave a short nod, adding nothing from himself. He was still keeping slightly aside, but he no longer looked as if he was expecting a knife in the back at any second. Rather, he was simply watching too closely.

The lean man lit up as if he had just been told the names of two new stars.

"Krest," he introduced himself, theatrically pressing his palm to his chest and bowing slightly. "At your service, at your roads, at your accidental miracles and sudden catastrophes."

Sakura exhaled almost imperceptibly. These people really did not seem to know who stood in front of them. There was no recognition in their gazes, no tension that should have appeared in people who had heard about a manhunt, a reward, or someone’s loud escape. The wanderers seemed to exist a little to the side of all the horror that had been left behind the walls of Ogrom. And right now, that worked in Sakura and Gilbert’s favor.

But not enough to stay near them for too long.

At that exact moment, something softly bumped against her foot. Sakura looked down. A small dark leather case with a worn inscription on the side had rolled toward her across the ground, bouncing over roots and pebbles.

Sakura automatically bent down and picked it up.

"What is this?"

She ran her finger over the inscription and read aloud:

"Self-Knowledge."

Krest immediately burst into laughter.

"Oh, of course!" he exclaimed, waving his arms wildly. "That scroll is always trying to escape us. Some kind of fate, truly. Apparently, in our company, self-knowledge is considered too dangerous an activity."

One of the wanderers behind him snorted.

"Self-knowledge is dangerous," one of the men said. "What if you find out you’re an idiot, and then you still have to live with yourself for the rest of your life?"

Sakura could not help smiling. Meanwhile, Krest had already jabbed a finger at the case in her hands.

"Take it for yourself. Since it rolled up to you on its own, that means it chose its owner. No one here values it anyway. Not one person has even unrolled it yet! Can you imagine? A complete lack of spiritual discipline in the group."

At first, Sakura wanted to refuse politely. They already had too many strange things on their hands. But then she looked at the case again.

Self-Knowledge.

She had no idea what it meant, but she had long since understood that many coincidences in Veyr were not just coincidences. As if fate itself was leading her by the hand. Or maybe Sakura just badly wanted to believe that.

Besides, if she was being honest, she needed extra knowledge right now like air.

Sakura lifted her gaze to Krest.

"Then... thank you. Really."

"Oh, don’t thank me too early," he said cheerfully. "What if you gain insight after using it, become horrified, and decide you should never have gotten involved with us at all?"

"That’s possible even without the scroll," Gilbert remarked dryly.

Krest immediately turned sharply toward him, but there was no aggression in the movement. On the contrary.

"I like you! I sense in you a refined taste for life’s inconveniences."

"I simply have a great deal of experience."

Sakura put the case away. And almost at the same second, she noticed Gilbert’s gaze change. Only slightly, but she had already learned to catch it. He had switched from polite waiting back into practical mode.

"Since the conversation has turned to scrolls," he said, "what else do you have?"

Krest first froze thoughtfully, then spread into such a happy expression that it seemed he had only been waiting for someone to ask him that question.

"Ah!" he breathed out with feeling. "There it is. Now we are speaking the language of civilized people."

He turned so sharply that his cloak swept up behind him, and with a broad gesture invited them to follow.

"Come, come. Don’t stand there like poor relations at the threshold of fate. Now I will show you a small, modest, entirely unremarkable assortment gathered by our hardworking hands and shameless luck."

He led them to the large donkey-drawn wagon and, with clear ceremony, pulled the thick cloth off it.

Sakura blinked in surprise. She was not sure she had seen this much junk even in Saorex’s cave. This collection was a true festival of chaos.

On shelves, in boxes, in hanging nets, and simply in piles lay all kinds of things. Flasks with cloudy liquid, bundles of dried herbs, strange metal parts, amulets, knives, belts, pouches of powders, scrolls, little boxes with stones, and a great deal more. It would have taken an entire day to inventory all that junk.

Sakura had the feeling that if she dug properly, somewhere deep in this wagon she would find an ancient cursed artifact, and maybe even some kind of mummy.

Meanwhile, Krest had already entered his usual role.

"And so!" he announced solemnly. "Before you is not merely a wagon. Before you is a stronghold of reasonable trade amid the complete madness of the roads. Here you will find useful things, rare things, suspicious things, things about which I ask no unnecessary questions, and things you should not hold in your hands for too long if you want to sleep peacefully."

He proudly slapped his palm against the side of the wagon.

"Welcome to my little shop."

Krest, of course, tried to sell them half the wagon at once, including a vial “against bad dreams,” which, according to Gilbert, smelled suspiciously like cheap cough tincture.

In the end, they took only what could actually be useful: several health potions, a couple of mana potions, three or four basic scrolls of elemental magic, and a set of small items that might prove useful or might turn out to be completely useless to them.

But among that simple set, there was one thing that stood out. It was a pendant with a flat bone button carved with a crooked eye. Krest said it was called an Amulet of Roadside Inattention and assured them that the little thing “makes a person duller than a bad sermon.” Gilbert examined the amulet more closely and did not argue.

"Not invisibility," he said quietly to Sakura. "It just makes you unmemorable and easy to overlook. It works especially well in a crowd. If you don’t do anything strange, passersby will forget you quickly."

"And if I do something strange?"

"Then no amulet will help you."

Sakura looked at the little thing doubtfully.

"Sounds like something that only helps smart people."

"That is why you will not wear it without my instruction," Gilbert said dryly.

Among the other useful small things was a black paste that softened steel. It could help with old locks and bolts if necessary. And that definitely sounded like something that might one day help if they were locked up or shackled somewhere again.

Although Sakura had some money in her Inventory, Gilbert did not allow her to even try to pay. Instead, he led Krest aside, they exchanged a few phrases, and Sakura saw coins appear in Gilbert’s hand. She noticed a copper gleam, but there was silver among it too. So the purchases were not too cheap. Or were they? Sakura had no idea how the currency system in Veyr worked. Somehow, with all the chases and attempts to survive, she had not yet had the chance to buy or sell anything.

Judging by Krest’s sly smile and Gilbert’s cold, impassive face, active bargaining was underway. Then the wanderer’s smile dimmed, and Sakura understood that Krest had failed to get as much as he had counted on. He accepted the coins from Gilbert, but then, despite that, bloomed again with enthusiasm and friendliness.

When Gilbert returned, Sakura quietly asked him, "Do we have what we need now?"

"To get Lir out?" Gilbert clarified. "We have some of it. But it isn’t enough. We’re missing one insanely important ingredient..."

"What kind?"

Gilbert leaned in and whispered directly into her ear, making sure no one else could hear.

"The core of a dead mage."
