It took Aurum a bit of time to come around to the prospect of playing along with the portals. He wasn’t exactly used to their dark, vine-laced maws swallowing us, let alone finding himself suddenly running in a different direction. We took it in small strides, literally and figuratively. Walking first. Portal to 90 degrees to the right. Then, moving a little farther before we turned some more.

Coordinating things like running and slashing with the saber were next, but not done during the same afternoon. We took breaks, pausing to talk with colleagues new and old who would drift in to watch our progress. We’d break for the night when the Bastion said it was time, and at this point, I had to believe it was running smoothly on Universal Time Constant, or UTC.

Oh, that’s a Nora-ism, but perhaps even a Naught-ism. You see, Speranza’s time constant changed. It turns out Chester broke the barrier between worlds when he traveled through Naught first, which affected how time works between them. Subsequent synchronization between realities was required. What had been done could never be undone, to protect all that was and all that shall be, everywhere that Naught is or will exist.

Nora added those last two lines with dramatic effect the first time she explained it to me, then insisted that Naught had said it just like that to her. To this very day, I’m not sure I believe her. The more I questioned the circumstances around their casual conversation—for example, when the hell did they have a chance to talk casually—she’d start to flinch and then say she couldn’t reveal any more. At that point, I gave up, knowing it was either something that happened when she was trying to escape from Relias, and she had some trauma she wasn’t ready to admit, or that she had been trying to pull a quick one over on me and critically failed.

Demons drifted through the training yard at times over the next several days as well, solo or in small groups. Amos and Tarek lingered the longest. I think Galenus tried to shoo them a few times, but their reply of “following orders” seemed to curtail any exodus, making it even clearer that Olethros was up to something and didn’t want me to be distracted by it.

Or maybe he absolutely wanted me to know he was up to something so that whenever it was executed, I wouldn’t be surprised by it.

Honestly, I wouldn’t have tried to change him no matter what the catalyst was. He was being him, and I’m sure it’d pay off when all was said and done.

“Here I am trying to impress our newest guest with your horsemanship, and you’re just jotting in your journal!” Tetora’s voice boomed from behind.

“You’re back!” I set aside my stuff and stood up with a creak, my lower back a bit sorer than I wanted to admit. “How’d it go in Nearhaven?”

Tetora’s arms were folded in triumph, and a large grin stretched to the corners of his face. He gestured to the man next to him. “Look who I found!”

It was not Taika, but a man.

He was, by all accounts, the most forgettable-looking human I had ever laid eyes upon in Speranza. Mud-colored hair of shoulder length. A truly unremarkable, mid-life face. He had a bit of a smile, but nothing seemed to be behind it. Average height, simple robes. No accessories adorned him whatsoever.

“Hello, sir,” I greeted him. “It’s nice to meet you.”

Tetora’s eyes flashed, and his mouth gaped, but the man reached up and patted his shoulders before he could scold me.

“A pleasant greeting is more than I would ever ask for,” the man said with an easy laugh before looking at me with brown eyes. “Though I would gently remind you that we’ve met in the past.”

I scratched my cheek, but despite my efforts, I couldn’t recall him.

Tetora became impatient.

“Councilor Inutilis!” he exploded.

“Wait! The councilman who ran away?!” I replied thoughtlessly.

“That’s the one,” Inutilis said with a full-on grin. “My reputation remains intact.”

“What were you doing in Nearhaven, Your Grace?” I asked, hoping the mode of address would help me avoid having to apologize.

“What you told me to do,” he replied, nodding his head. “It’s not perfect by a long shot, but we’re learning to get along.”

I turned to stare at Tetora, my eyes prodding him for more information.

“Human, hybrid, demon,” he said, tugging at an ear. “It’s unnerving. Against everything we were taught. And maybe even working?”

“And I had something to do with that?” I asked, blinking.

“You helped finance it,” Inutilis affirmed. “Though I would ask that if you come across any more wealth, please consider donating again. As always, we thank you for your financial contributions!”

I frowned a little. “That’s all I did, and nothing else?”

I threw money at a big problem, but that didn’t fix it.

Inutilis shook his head. “The things you didn’t do were just as important.”

“The things I didn’t do?”

Tetora cleared his throat. “Like, tell any other priests about it.”

I wrinkled my nose in thought.

Did I mistrust Relias back then? And on top of that, did I mistrust my mistrust, so that I didn’t act upon it?

Tetora could see the thoughts on my face. “You didn’t tell me either.”

“Ah.”

Trust no one, it was, then.

He pouted, his whiskers drooping. “I’ll forgive you this time…” He trailed off as he put his hands behind his head. “Taika’s enjoying herself there, at least. Lots of squabbles to settle.”

“So she’s alright!”

He nodded again with a small, squinty-eyed smile. “Of course she is. Taika, Inutilis... Morcelos… All of them are leaders there.”

“Morcelos!”

The leech demon of Chairo was there, too?

Inutilis rocked on his feet for a moment, then reached into his sleeve and pulled out a letter. “I am here to extend an invitation to meet regarding Speranza’s ‘Relias Problem’; however, I must request that you only set foot on our island accompanied by Prince Mito, Lady Nora, Aleph the Oxlike, Iron Tiger Tetora, Demon King Olethros, General Ragnerus, and Mistress Aziza. Otherwise…” he paused, depositing the document in my hand. “We aren’t able to achieve quorum.”

Instead of reading it, I stared at Tetora for a long, silent moment.

“Taika counts as your third hybrid for balance,” he said with a shrug, as if it explained everything.

“Tetora!”

He scowled, a bit of sweat running down his cheek. “You’ll get them in line, and we’ll all hang out on the beach for a bit. Show the world that Nearhaven’s the new tourist attraction.”

Inutilis winced uncomfortably. “We agreed to talk publicly about how things work there, but we’re not ready for visitors.” He turned back to me. “We’re an experiment at best. We fight about things great and small. But we have a small council that tries to do better.”

“But that’s exactly what Speranza needs. To see a different perspective!” Nora declared as she walked through a mirrored portal. “Even if it’s part propaganda, we can give the world a pause long enough to disrupt some faith and trust in Relias.”

“It’ll also call his attention right to them,” I warned.

“That’s why we talk about the timing first,” Inutilis replied, glancing about the magical training yard. “Deals and deeds will come later. In the meantime, I came here specifically to request that you go back to ignoring our little home for now, such as it is.”

Before I could reply, Nora gave me a rueful look. “I tried recruiting for our cause there. Can’t blame a girl for trying, can ya?”

“Not the first time,” I agreed before turning to Inutilis. “Going forward, we’ll respect your privacy until a larger, in-person meeting is feasible.” I struck out my hand. “I have to admit that the name Inutilis doesn’t give you credit.”

He shook my hand firmly. “The greatest gift my local priest gave me. I was born in Porta, and my amity skills aren’t much, but they were enough to put me in danger. Luckily, no holy knight wants ‘a useless priest’ on the frontlines with them, so I leaned into it.” He laughed then, a deep, rich sound that made fun of life itself. “I couldn’t fool Vetus. He told me if I wanted to be more believable as an incompetent, I’d have to make more mistakes and accept a few more bribes.”

I scowled slightly. “Bribes?”

Inutilis nodded. “Nothing Speranza-shattering, I assure you. Small bribes to make local officials believe they had my eternal favor. Then collect equal amounts of money from all corrupt parties. Throw up my hands and say they’re tied when something important happens. You know, normal politics.” He paused. “Of course, once I took his advice, Vetus had the audacity to nominate me for the Council when the seat became available. Scoundrel knew he needed a succession plan.”

I found myself sighing wistfully. “I sometimes wish I could thank him for being so duplicitous.”

“He did the right things the way he could,” Inutilis agreed. “Even if it was the wrong way.”

The next morning, I found myself in the parlor with Nora and Olethros, my stomach tight under the pressure to come.

“It’s about damn time,” Bodhil declared as she apparated, thrusting out her hand with clawed fingers. “Our Great General’s eye!”

Wasn’t I the one waiting for her to arrive?

Beigaldi appeared next. “The eye!” he echoed, though he left off calling me a name like usual.

“Remember, no tricks.” I opened the drawstring of the holy pouch slowly, shoving my hand in and gripping the slimy mass within before it could escape. “You two know what you’re doing, right?”

“Never question the expert.” Bodhil snatched the eye out of my hand, only for it to fight her even more. “Beigaldi!”

Instead of offering his power to subdue it, he unhinged his jaw, opening his mouth beyond the dictates of anatomy. With what could have been the start of a fight, Bodhil thrust her hand into his mouth, shoving the eye down his throat even as it shrieked in protest. Beigaldi snapped his jaw shut as Bodhil faded, never bothering to withdraw her hand before reforming next to him.

Nora, I, and even Olethros stared a bit aghast, none of us quite expecting the procedure. Beigaldi’s form expanded strangely in a few places, as if something was wriggling to escape him, but then he shut his eyes and braced himself. Once the wriggling stopped, he opened his eyes, the left one now crimson and no longer with its customary horizontal slit.

“Darkness,” he uttered finally. “Only darkness. No pull from here. I need to be on the surface, not in the Bastion.”

Galenus shimmered into form next to me. “Let’s try Turri first.”

“Turri?” I asked. “Not out somewhere in the Wastes?”

“Merely a hunch,” he replied, his eyes flashing. “Care to place a bet on it, Captain?”

What's gotten into him—oh.

“You’ve been giving me the side-eye a lot lately,” Nora objected before I could say a single word. “But who am I to stop Galenus from becoming a well-rounded and sassy demon?”

“Let’s gather the party and head for Turri,” I said with a sigh. “Once we find the General, there’s no turning back.”
