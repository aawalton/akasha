I sit down at my desk and pull out a leather-bound book and a pen. Opening it to the beginning, I pause, my pen hovering over the blank page. Nicholas gave me the book as one of his parting gifts, urging me to write in it. For future generations, he’d said – this is to be the grimoire I leave for the family when I’m gone.

I’m transfixed, frozen by two simultaneous and equally powerful feelings. A deep sense of honour that Nicholas considers me enough a part of the family that he wants my journey recorded, preserved in a book to be passed down through generations to come. And alongside that, an almost overwhelming sense of fear.

What if I say the wrong thing? What if, because I’m not originally from Moriax, my words mislead rather than guide and end up hurting those who come after me when they try to replicate some of my achievements? What if I simply explain things so poorly that future generations read it and have no idea of what I’m trying to say?

I inhale a quiet and slow breath, then control the exhale too, my emotions settling as I rationalise things.

I don’t have to give this to Nicholas. I can always tell him I forgot or was too busy. And if nothing else, it may serve as a useful record of our journey.

Besides, even on Earth I heard that journaling could be good for the soul.

I never bothered with it then – I was always too busy. I didn’t see enough value in it to take time from everything else I was doing to just sit down and reflect. Actually, if I’m honest with myself, I avoided looking at everything for fear it would destabilise things that finally seemed to be going right for me.

But now, I can’t help but wonder what I might have realised about my life if I had. Perhaps I would have come to some of the conclusions I’ve only reached now – but  before everything went so completely wrong.

Taking another deep breath – deeper, really, than the moment warrants – I set pen to paper and write the date.

It is the second day of Meridia.

I can only hope that the prosperity promised by this half’s patron god will soon reveal itself, because so far, our journey has been plagued by misfortune despite leaving in an ‘auspicious’ month – Navaris, the month of Sailor and Wind. Though, to be fair to the ‘gods’, I’m not sure better winds or sharper sailors would have got us out of the events that troubled us. What events, you might ask? Well, it all started when….

It is the sixteenth day of Meridia.

Future descendants, if you learn nothing else from this journal, learn this: Mages are, without exception, dreadful teachers.

Perhaps that is a little harsh. Perhaps there is the odd rare gem among their ranks who manages to be a half-way decent teacher; unfortunately, I have none of those here.

All I seem to have are those who have wielded magic for so long that they have entirely forgotten what it was like to begin. Is it my fault if I cannot grasp what they are trying to tell me within the first two tries? Especially when they don’t seem to take into account the fact that I have had no formal training and have no idea what they’re on about half the time.

Descendants, perhaps also do not follow my example and allow your frustration to take over to the point where you break a pen by gripping it too hard.

Admittedly, though their words have been largely useless to me, I’ve found some value in having a demonstration of what is possible to achieve. But increasingly, I find myself thinking that I may be better off working things out for myself.

Magic, in my experience, seems deeply tied to perception. Two mages can produce identical effects through entirely different thought processes and movements of their magic.

That, I suspect, is part of the problem: to the mage, their way is obvious. To anyone else, it borders on incomprehensible.

Mage Vellus, for instance, imagines water currents as threads in a cloth. That just doesn’t work for me. In my view, it separates the current from the water surrounding it in a way that feels fundamentally wrong.

Mage Rouness, on the other hand, thinks of currents as pathways. That, I can grasp better, but it still refuses to translate into anything practical when I attempt to use it as an image, especially since she seems to send her magic out like little workers to repave the pathway in a slightly different direction. Perhaps I am too afraid of my magic gaining sapience and rebelling against me if I go down that route.

After six hours of instruction today, I fear I am no closer to controlling sea currents than I was when I began. I don’t feel like it’s been a good use of my time – or that of the Water Mages who have other duties.

Both mages, obviously disgruntled, have assured me that this is to be expected. That controlling the currents of the sea requires a powerful Water Mage, and that I should not be discouraged.

I find this less reassuring than they likely intend. If anything, my experiences with magic so far have taught me the opposite: that it is, in many ways, limitless.

There is always a way. I simply have to find what works for me. And not allow my failures to discourage me too much. That is also something worth practising – for all of us.

21st of Meridia – Rest Day

Despite the period patronised by Prosperity already being over, I’ve found this to have been quite a productive month. We’ve been lucky with the winds and have made good progress, and our fishermen have found the seas particularly fruitful. Our leatherworkers, seamstresses, and enchanters have finally outfitted everyone in armour that suits their fighting style – lighter armour for the mages and archers so their mobility isn’t compromised; heavier armour for the front line fighters. Even all of my companions have armour – apart from Pride. He still refuses to wear anything that risks covering his sail, even though there’s no one around who finds it an attractive feature.

On a more personal front, I’ve been pleased to see advances for several of my companions. Hunter seems to have found her niche in enchanting with large-scale enchantments like those that keep the ship floating instead of the small ones like in the kitchen that keep the stoves burning – Alyna has found far more success with those. This will be useful when we land on the Lost Continent – I’m sure we’ll need plenty of structural reinforcement, offensive, and defensive enchantments.

At the same time, Hunter is starting to experiment with her own rune designs – something that I’m not sure is a good idea. Certainly, Master Lizil Mirena is more than a little concerned about it and she’s the expert. But Hunter is determined to bring some of her heritage into this new world and insists she’s being careful.

Loran is also making progress, though his is a lot less alarming. Since discovering that he actually has an internal matrix, he has made steady progress in growing it – like a beast would. It seems that achieving the first piece of the internal matrix is the most difficult part of the process for humans. I theorise that there is some kind of barrier that, once breached, allows the matrix to begin reacting and developing more naturally. So far, he has shown little sign of Evolving, but I would be very surprised if it happened so quickly.

His internal matrix has taken on something of a disc shape, which is unlike most I have seen, whether in beasts, myself, or Nicholas. It has great width and breadth, but very little depth. The overall shape – something like a spiral galaxy with a black hole at its centre (a disc with lines that spiral away from the centre) – seems as though it could function well when he begins building the pressure required to condense a Core. Time will tell, but I’m cautiously optimistic.

By contrast, Mage Tiria is extremely frustrated. Despite her efforts, she has neither been able to gain Meditation nor locate her internal matrix. I am considering creating copies of my Meditation skill, but I remain undecided. It carries a cost, and we do not have an unlimited supply of Cores – especially not ones powerful enough to replicate a Master-level Skill.

There is also the question of whether I can trust her with it. I like her, but I can’t afford to make decisions purely on emotion. Tiria is not tied to me beyond being part of the expedition, and I would prefer to know her better before deciding whether to share something that has been so fundamental to my own success. Not because I am unwilling, but because I don’t want to give such an advantage to someone who might one day become an enemy.

So far, nothing I have seen of her suggests that this is likely. Still, I have learned to be wary of nobles. Everyone has their own motivations, and Moriaxian nobles can be particularly cutthroat.

That said, Tiria does not seem to conform to that pattern. She remains blunt, direct, and somewhat dubiously principled. But not malicious.

If I’m honest, it’s refreshing to be with someone who forgets my status more often than not, and who treats me as secondary to whatever experiment is on her mind in the moment.

That feels odd to consider – I’ve never enjoyed being ignored. But here, I find that a good portion of the attention I gain is uncomfortable. I suppose because it is more attention for what I am, rather than who. At least Tiria treats everyone with the same kind of absent-mindedness – actually, she’s more likely to notice me than she is most of the other people. And I don’t think it’s just because I’m the expedition leader or the Great House heir…

Could it be that I’m delaying giving Tiria a stone just because I want to spend more time with her? Is my slow drip feed of information less about lack of trust and more because it gives me an excuse to spend time with her?

No.

No. I’m spending the time with her because it would be mutually beneficial if she learns more about the Class system.

Sure, she’s attractive. I’m not blind. And her blunt honesty has a certain charm that is difficult to ignore.

But she’s my subordinate. That alone makes the situation questionable. Especially since I accidentally blurted out that if she wanted me to help her properly, she’d have to accept a Bond from me and she actually seemed to consider it.

Then again, if I continue as Nicholas’s heir after the competition – and I see little reason not to despite all the downsides – then I will hold a position that places me, technically, above almost everyone I meet. If I restrict myself to those of equal rank, my options for a romantic partner become vanishingly small – and mostly male.
