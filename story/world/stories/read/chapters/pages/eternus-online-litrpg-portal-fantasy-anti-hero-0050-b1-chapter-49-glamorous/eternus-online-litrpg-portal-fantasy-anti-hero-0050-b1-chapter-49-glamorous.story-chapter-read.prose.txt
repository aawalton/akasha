Romulus watched the flames spiral around Corva and Mayarella—Cora and Maya, in his head, still—until they collapsed inward, absorbed into the attire and accouterments that the Dark Lady had seen fit to bestow upon his friends.

Maya resolved first, and Nate very nearly dropped his shield when she did.

Their usual healer was attired in crimson robes, paneled by black cloth that bore prayers to Lilith in gothic Eternian Common that trailed along their bodies and shimmered with silver thread when they caught the Lichfire lighting the throne room. An elegant ruby-topped blackwood staff rested in her right hand, and her body was not bereft of protection: small black armor plates adorned her thighs, shins, forearms, and shoulders; built around a laced bodice that completed the central part of her ensemble.

Knee-high sturdy leather boots completed the fit, with thick half-inch heels that hardly seemed to bother her stance. Maya glanced down at herself, stared at the staff, and then squealed before throwing herself into Nathanius’s arms, shouting out “I got Black Celebrant!” as she did.

Romulus found himself laughing with Kate while their attention shifted to Corva instead.

The elven Channeler had not been neglected merely because she had failed to attain a sacred calling. Black robes hugged her body in a way both similar and utterly different from Maya. Where Maya’s robes had a sanctity and measure of ceremonial gravity to their presentation, Cora’s were entirely made for combat.

Instead of adhering to ceremonial austerity, they clung to her more subtle curves with visible design intent; belted at the waist below the tight-fitting bodice by a silver clasp reminiscent of the one Romulus remembered Lilith herself wearing, with dark iron pauldrons, armored gloves attached to dark iron vambraces, cuisses, and sturdy plated gothic ‘stompers’ as Cora would invariably name them.

Violet inscriptions threaded across the hem of the robes, completing the battlemage’s attire.

Her blue-black hair fell in a straight curtain around her Korean features, parted around her point ears, and a slender rapier was sheathed on her left hip, with a large dark iron septagram—a seven-pointed star—hovering just above the palm of her left hand, inlaid with a faintly shimmering pattern of enchantments and glyphs around a small amethyst at its center.

“Wow,” Corva said, breaking the moment as Kate raced toward her and Romulus rose from his throne. “I asked her for something badass, but this is next level…”

“Btich! You look lethal!” Kate shrieked, slamming into Corva and drawing an immediate and delighted laugh from the elven Channeler while they embraced. The septagram hovered unbothered nearby when they did, and Corva carefully kept the rapier from digging into the Nighstalker’s waist while they embraced.

Romulus descended to join them, arriving first at Nate and Maya and clasping hands with Nate before hugging Maya.

“Congratulations on Dark Templar and Black Celebrant,” he said after he released their group’s long-suffering healer. “Now there’s no excuses left for why we can’t conquer the world.”

“I’ll settle for surviving the next week, brother,” Nate said with a laugh while glancing with pleasure at his shield. “The world can wait, for now.”

“Oh my gosh!” Maya put in almost at the same time while her eyes glazed over in a way Romulus recognized as belonging to System access. “I have so many new Skills! [Dark Mana Channeling], [Sanguimancy], [Shadowmancy]—and she even gave me [Alchemy]! There’s so much!”

“Congratulations,” Romulus said with sincere happiness for what she’d gained. “That means you’re a triple threat between the two disciplines and the crafting skill.”

“I already have potion recipes, too...” Maya said while her eyes trailed over her invisible menu.

“Oh my god!” Corva exclaimed, drawing their attention as she kept one arm around Kate, who was clinging to her in glee, while the elven woman’s distant gaze raced across an invisible screen. “[Dark Mana Channeling], [Shadowmancy], [Tenebromancy], and [Entropomancy]!? The Lady fucking rules!”

Romulus and Nate laughed at Cora’s outburst, and Kate immediately detached herself to hug Maya a moment later, gripping her as the two started bouncing in place while Corva approached with her septagram hovering near her shoulder instead.

“This star-thing is cool as fuck, too,” she said when she came over, gesturing at the amethyst-inlaid septagram. “It increases my Spell Potency by 15%, and it reduces my mana costs by 30% globally. That’s so fucking badass.”

“My staff is kind of the same!” Maya said eagerly. “It increases my Spell Potency by the same amount as your focus, but instead of reducing mana costs, it increases my Vitality by five points.”

Both women grinned at each other immediately afterward, and Romulus felt satisfaction ripple through them. Mortarius finally spoke into the brief, content silence that followed.

“Which Spellforms has the Lady granted each of you?” the specter asked while approaching.

“[Dark Aegis] and [Dark Lance] for me,” Nate answered immediately. “Both are Novice 1, as are [Dark Mana Channeling] and [Shadowmancy]. I also have [Shield Bash] and [Stand Your Ground], as well as my passive [Block] ability. Dark Templar also gives me a permanent plus Ten Percent to my Vitality, Willpower, and Endurance. That’s kinda neat.”

“Nightstalker gives me ten percent to my Dexterity, Agility, and Endurance,” Kate supplied. “I also get [Shadowstride], [Null Arrow], and [Void Strike]. Go figure, right?”

“That all makes sense, though!” Maya added. “Black Celebrant gives me that ten percent to Vitality, Willpower, and Agility, for some reason.”

Romulus raised his eyebrows at the news and glanced at Mortarius, who seemed entirely unsurprised.

“Those bonuses are guides,” the Specter stated. “For example: Strength will matter for you greatly, Nathanius, but you should not expect it to cover everything; and for you, Mayarella, your health and mana will be very important as a Sanguimantic healer, but a lack of Agility or Endurance will leave you vulnerable when you should be mobile to avoid being targeted.” The former Autarch looked across them with intent crimson eyes and looked faintly thoughtful. “Have you all been educated in Attribute importance?”

“Kind of,” Corva replied, dismissing her System window. “All my stuff is at Novice 1 as well, for what it’s worth, and I don’t have any bonuses for an archetype, but my Attributes are the same as they were before. I mostly invested in Intelligence and Willpower.”

“Same for me,” Maya agreed with a nod. “I kind of neglected Endurance, though. That may be an issue after what you said, Mortarius.”

“Stamina gained from Endurance helps a lot,” Kate added immediately and with emphatic certainty. “Trust me, it sucks to run out of breath mid-fight. It may be easier as primarily a Channeler, but you shouldn’t ignore the cost of it entirely.”

“It would probably help me keep my shield up longer, too,” Nate said thoughtfully. “Strength is fine for resisting physical damage and helping me be able to hold it, but it won’t be much use if I run out of breath after a couple of blocks.”

“You would also require the health value to withstand the damage the shield and armor cannot entirely stop,” Mortarius pointed out. “Presuming the damage does not kill you instantly.”

“So, Vitality,” Maya said immediately. “Lots of Vitality.”

“Health and regeneration,” Romulus agreed. “It’ll save Nate’s bacon more than just resistance will. Not every attack can be avoided, no matter how good of a tank you are. He needs to be able to shrug off the damage through bodily durability too, not just rely on the Lady’s new armor.”

“Trust me, I’m aware,” Nate answered with a grimace. “The first couple of fights taught me that lesson.”

“That is wisdom,” Mortarius agreed. “Return to the dais before we continue. There are things we should discuss, as well, regarding the future threats you will all face.”

The five of them nodded at the specter’s words and followed him toward the throne at his gesture, with excited glances at System screens still occurring as they made their way across the chamber’s vast platinum-veined black marble and settled down along the dais’ seven rounded steps, while Romulus himself claimed the throne again and Kate plopped herself confidently onto his arm rest, forcing him to steady her with his arm once more, to her evident smugness.

His best friend was incorrigible about forcing him to pick up her slack.

Mortarius faced the newest three once they were assembled—having already aided Romulus and Katherine on their own planned futures—and addressed them calmly.

“There is one important factor you must understand, even if Romulus and Katherine have alerted you. It would be imprudent for me not to cover it: your gifts from the Lady are now irreversible. Dark Mana devours the capability for learning another Affinity. It confers tremendous power, but you must understand that you are now irrevocably tied to the Dark Affinity.”

“We were warned,” Maya said warmly. “Kate and D—Romulus were very specific.”

Romulus smiled at her in thanks for the correction, and Mortarius graced her with a faint smile.

“Good,” the former Autarch said. “The second matter is that of your roles in combat. Romulus has already shown himself to be a very aggressive vanguard, as we would call them in Eternus. Katherine is evidently a flanker. Based on Nathanius’s clear preferences, he would be classified as a bastion, while Maya and Corva would be identified as a battlemender and warchanneler respectively.”

“Battlemender, huh?” Maya murmured and then smiled at Nate. “I like that.”

“Warchanneler works,” Cora agreed with a grin that Kate returned. “Fucking excellent, Mortarius.”

“Flanker suits Kay perfectly, too,” Nate put in after squeezing Maya’s hand where she sat beside him. “She’s always finding places to squirrel into for ambushes, and Romulus being a vanguard is just poetry. He was like that in our last Traveling experience, too.”

“I wasn’t that bad…” Romulus said with wounded dignity.

“You charged into a boss room demanding they repent for their sins, Lulu,” Kate reminded him with a laugh. “Half the raid started calling you ‘Leeroy’ instead of ‘Leon’.”

“I was passionate,” Romulus said with what defense he could muster, while his friends laughed and Mortarius appraised him skeptically. Lightsbane, ever-loyal, pulsed in support of the idea of charging in, though was clearly displeased about the “repent for your sins” portion, being a Revenant Runeblade of the Divinarch of Sin, among other Dominions.

“I got some of those, too,” Corva observed after a moment of scanning her own System interface. “[Dark Lance], [Shadow Binding], [Erosion], [Summon Minor Abyssal], [Void Shield], and [Malediction: Frailty].”

“Malediction?” Romulus asked with immediate interest.

“An offensive curse,” Mortarius explained. “In this case, an Entropomantic one that reduces the target’s strength, vitality, and endurance by 10%, if I recall correctly.”

“Yep!” Corva agreed. “Though it says my Spell Potency has to compete with their Spell Resistance?”

“Correct,” Mortarius said. “That is the nature of exchange in Eternus. That is why both Intelligence and Willpower are imperative for your path, Corva, and Maya’s as well—your Spellforms will directly compete with your target’s Spell Resistance. For every percentage of superior Spell Resistance they have, they have that much more chance to resist the ability.”

“How does that work with stacking debuffs?” Nate asked immediately, unconsciously glancing at his shield. “Could someone just stack those on me until I’m useless?”

“No,” Mortarius replied when all eyes turned to him. “Maledictions are unique to the Dark Faith, though things like [Radiant Censure] or [Burning Castigation] exist for the Light and Fire Affinities, as minor examples. Each Affinity has its own tools and means of debilitating enemies or restoring allies.”

All five of them nodded in understanding without interrupting, and Corva paid particularly close attention while Mortarius continued. “You also cannot ‘stack’, as you put it, negative modifiers that overlap from a single Affinity. Corva could use multiple distinct Maledictions, certainly, but she could not apply [Malediction: Frailty] to someone already suffering from its effects. She could only refresh its duration—and if it failed, accidentally purge it from the person altogether by overriding and erasing the existing Malediction.”

“How does the whole Potency versus Resistance thing work?” Maya asked more intently.

“Simply put, if you have 10% Spell Potency, but someone else has 15% Spell Resistance, they have a positive 5% Spell Resistance against your abilities, increasing their chance to resist them by that five percent. If you possessed 70% Spell Potency, and they possessed 40% Spell Resistance, you would have a total advantage of 30% Spell Potency, increasing the chance of success—or potential damage—against that enemy by 30% in total.”

“But items can also serve as mitigation for that damage or those effects via Enchantments, right?” Romulus asked immediately and with genuine interest.

“Correct,” Mortarius said with approval.

“Damn,” Corva said thoughtfully. “That’s a lot of shit to remember. I’m gonna be nose-deep in my [Codex] for hours at this rate...”

“It grows remarkably more manageable as your Intelligence, and thereby your capacity for learning, memory, and information processing, all increase with it,” Mortarius noted with faint amusement. “Though your [Codex] will be an excellent supplemental tool as well, yes.”

“That’s all very glamorous, Mortarius,” Kate said while the others ruminated on what the specter had revealed, “but I feel like we’re skipping over the important part: what, exactly, is our immediate objective? We’ve got a zealot crusade getting ready to be dropped on our heads, we’ve kicked a hornet’s nest in the city, and the Necropolis has exactly five of us and one very handsome and wise but ultimately combat-ineligible ghost-king. What, precisely, is our next move?”

“Don’t die,” Romulus offered, drawing a laugh from the other four, though Kate gave him a light smack upside the head. Mortarius seemed to approve of her umbrage, and then turned toward Romulus directly.

“What comes next is up to the Revenant-King,” the specter said after the laughter died down, and the other four pairs of eyes drifted back to Romulus. “We have options. The [Mana Nexus] remains largely stocked, and there are capabilities within it that will serve our needs, namely when it comes to immediate defenses.”

Romulus met their gazes and Mortarius’s own and then shifted his glance, bringing up the [Mana Nexus] with a spoken command and browsing through its options in silence, ignoring the [Dark Consecration] and [Guardian Golems] until he settled near the end, over the [Summon Shade] and [Reanimate Dead] options. His eyes narrowed in contemplation, and he compared the costs: 500 Nexus Mana for [Reanimate Dead], and 1,500 Nexus Mana for [Summon Shade]. His eyes flicked between them, and he slowly smiled.

“Yeah,” Romulus said after a moment. “I think I have a pretty good idea of what comes next.”

“That being?” Kate asked immediately, her green eyes intent.

“We’re going to make ourselves a defense force,” Romulus said in reply. “And then we’re going to make this Necropolis a fucking nightmare.”

Mortarius’s approving smile was all the confirmation he needed.
