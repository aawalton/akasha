That meant something of mine had been left behind within them. A mark, or perhaps an imprint, embedded deep within whatever force animated their existence. That was why controlling them now felt far easier than it should have been. Even with just Basic Necromancy, I could influence thousands of undead.

“Interesting. Truly interesting.”

A faint smile formed on my lips as I looked over the silent legion before me. If that was the case, then this wasn’t just a temporary force that would disappear once I canceled the possession. It was something that could persist and grow even afterward. The more undead I raised while possessing Voldrath, the more I would be able to retain control over them once the possession ended.

Clearly, this was far more valuable than I had initially thought, which made the cost of plausibility far more acceptable.

These are signs of divine warriors… The last faction should be located somewhere around here.

I concealed the undead legion and observed the place from a distance, including the divine warriors guarding the perimeter. As I had suspected, everything here felt unnaturally uniform. The movements of the patrolling warriors were too synchronized, their positioning too precise, as if every action had been predetermined.

“Velkhar…” The name surfaced in my mind almost immediately. The God of Deceit. This had to be his faction.

Using Mana Sense, I could detect a faint dissonance between presence and perception. It felt as though what I was seeing wasn’t entirely real, as if layers of false information were constantly being woven over the truth.

Back when I was still a God-King, this kind of divine warrior faction wasn’t uncommon. They were usually ones that would sacrifice everything as long as it led to victory. And that was precisely what made them far more dangerous than the others, which meant direct confrontation would be the worst possible approach.

I lowered my presence even further, pulling my undead forces back into the surrounding ruins to avoid detection. Charging in blindly would only play into their hands. Against an enemy like this, information was far more valuable than brute force. Besides, my objective in this first stage wasn’t to defeat the enemy factions by destroying their statues, but to outlast them.

After all, this was a three-way battle. Rather than expending resources to eliminate one faction while leaving myself exposed to the other, it was far more effective to focus on defense and establish myself as a threat they couldn’t eliminate without suffering severe losses, but not dangerous enough that it would become a threat if left alone. In doing so, I could force the other two factions to clash with each other instead.
