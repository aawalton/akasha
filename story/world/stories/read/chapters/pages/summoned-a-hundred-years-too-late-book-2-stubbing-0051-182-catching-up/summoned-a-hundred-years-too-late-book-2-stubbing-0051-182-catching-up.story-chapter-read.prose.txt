Because Balrum was built directly into the mountainside, it was no surprise that its agricultural tradition involved a significant portion of high-altitude grazing. Bovines, sheep, and goats shared the vast pastures above the tree line, providing meat, milk, and wool to the town.

Alexandra climbed up one of the many paths leading up the mountain and soon located a small farm nestled between a stream and a pasture. Small, hairy cows were munching on grass, watched over by none other than Willow, who sat on a wide, old tree stump.

“You’re back,” she said as Alexandra approached. “They found Zeynep.”

“I know. I looked in the wrong direction.”

Alexandra sat next to her friend.

“What took you so long?” Willow asked.

“Troubles, as always.” She hesitated. “And it's not over.”

Willow rubbed her eyebrows. “Let’s hear it.”

“So I was looking for Zeynep in Oldwater when I heard about the pass,” Alexandra started, explaining her reasoning. “In hindsight, I should have waited and gone with the team Torak was hiring.”

“Yes.”

“But,” Alexandra raised a finger. “I did find something interesting. Well, maybe interesting is not the right word. Let’s say concerning.”

She told her about her meeting with the Paladins and the Architects.

“So the Legate and the Vanguard both know where you are,” Willow said. “That was quicker than we anticipated.”

“Yes, but the good news is that the entire Architect squad was killed.”

“And the Paladins?” Willow asked.

“I’ve hired the survivors for our guild.”

Willow stared at her. “Okay…”

“What?”

“Don’t you think it's a little reckless?”

Alexandra shrugged. “I have you, a prospective Keeper. It’s not that different.”

Willow raised an eyebrow. “Oh? Is it?” she asked, her voice glacial.

“Ugh… well… it’s different because we’re friends. But the Paladins have been helpful, and I can’t reject their help considering what happened next.”

Alexandra went on to explain her encounter with the cult of desire and the information the Paladins gave regarding the cult of rot.

“In that case, it’s good you went there. Even if it wasn’t your intention,” Willow said. “Our focus should be on the cult of desire. We need to do everything we can to protect Nara and Kam from those maniacs.”

“Speaking of Nara, how is she doing?”

Willow’s expression soured. “Not great. She’s trying to hide from me, but I can tell the malediction is taking a toll on her. Kam is trying, but he’s not much older than Nara. I don’t know if he’ll find a cure in time.”

Alexandra put a hand on her shoulder. “If Kam doesn’t make it, I will. We have at least a year. I’ll be quite far along the Silver rank by then.”

“You know,” Willow said as she looked at her. “It’s hard to picture where you’ll be in a year. You’re not the most stable person.”

Alexandra froze. “Ah? Well, it’s true that I’ve moved a lot since I was summoned, but the circumstances…”

“I, Kam, and now those Paladins you told me about decided to follow you. For Kam and me, part of it is because, despite everything, we like you. For the Paladins, they’re only here because of what you represent.” She paused. “Nara hasn’t chosen anything. Stop acting as if she isn’t there.”

“I’m not—” Alexandra started, only for Willow to interrupt her.

“When’s the last time you talked to her?”

“Sometime before I left to look for Zeynep,” Alexandra answered with a shrug.

“When exactly? What did you talk about?”

“I… don’t know. I don’t remember. Nothing special, I guess.” She looked away at the cows grazing on fresh mountain grass. Their small size and long hair made them all fuzzy and cute.

“Well, isn’t that exactly the issue?” Willow asked. “Get yourself together, Alex. I know it's not easy for you, but we need you, and you need this too.”

For a moment, Alexandra didn’t answer. She clutched her fist, the one away from Willow so that she wouldn’t see it.

Don’t lash out. She deserves better.

She took a deep breath, counted to three, and exhaled slowly.

It pained her to admit it, but Willow was right. She had been avoiding Nara. It wasn’t even a conscious decision, just the path of least resistance. It was easier to let Willow handle it, so she did.

It wasn’t fair to Nara. It wasn’t fair to Kam. And, more importantly, it wasn’t fair to Willow.

“I’ll try to be better,” Alexandra said, her voice so soft that she was afraid Willow wouldn’t hear her.

“Good. That’s all I’m asking,” Willow said, standing up. “Now let me show you what I’ve been up to.”

The two of them walked to the farm. The owner wasn’t there, but Willow assured Alexandra that it was fine.

“I’m helping him enchant his storage and fermentation cuves,” she explained. “Letting me eat some cheese is the least he can do.”

Alexandra followed Willow into the farm's cellar, where the scent of aging cheese immediately filled the air. Rows of large wooden fermentation cuves lined the walls, faintly glowing with the intricate enchantments Willow had been infusing into them.

“It’s incredible,” Alexandra admitted, running a finger along the rim of the nearest vat as she felt the ambient mana agree with Willow’s intentions. “I need to practice more.”

Willow picked up a sharp knife from a wooden table. She sliced off a generous wedge of hard, aged cheese and handed it over. “Eat. Then, you go talk to Nara.”

Alexandra grabbed the cheese and stuffed it into her mouth. “Actually,” she said, mouth half-full, “I need to take a beating first.”

Willow sighed. “Do I want to know?”

“It’s not my fault if today’s quest is dumb!”

“I think you’re a bad influence on your journal.”
