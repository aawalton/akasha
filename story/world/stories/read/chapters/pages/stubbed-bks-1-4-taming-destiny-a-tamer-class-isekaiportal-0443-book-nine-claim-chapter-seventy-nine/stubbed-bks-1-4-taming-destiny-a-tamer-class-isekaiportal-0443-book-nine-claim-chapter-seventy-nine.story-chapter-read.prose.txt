Fixing my metaphysical eyes on the spot my magic cannot touch, I dive through the woman's body towards it. As I reach where it was, I realise with a jolt that it's no longer there. I cast around with my mana. It can't simply have disappeared.

It takes me a few moments, but I find it – it's shifted into her spine and pulled some sort of stealth shroud around itself, making it difficult to detect even now that I have such control over her body. This must be why we lost it before. Its stealth is clearly its most powerful asset. Even now, though I can see where it is, I still can't make out any details of it.

Wary of it slipping away again, I move more slowly this time, sending magic to encircle it on both sides so it has nowhere to go. It takes a moment to recognise the trap, and once it does it begins shifting faster and faster in agitation, pressing against the closing walls of my magic with increasing desperation. This time I'm determined not to let it escape.

Then, just as it begins vibrating faster and faster, a bad feeling surges through me. I move to close the trap all at once, but I'm just a fraction of a second too late. The dot explodes violently, its veil of stealth falling away as it dies. I can't pay much attention to that, however, because I'm already fighting to stop my patient from being paralysed for life.

Fortunately, this isn't the first time I've dealt with critical spinal injuries, and I'm far more practised now than I was then. It takes only minutes to heal the damage, though I dimly hear a whimper of pain from my patient as the nerves reconnect. Sending apologetic thoughts her way, I remain in her body and search carefully for any more of the parasites. I can't be certain only one has made its home inside her.

I don't find any more living creatures or eggs, but what I do find is a horrific amount of evidence of how completely the parasite had possessed her. Its network is far more extensive than either Healer Thessalanor or I were able to detect. The roots of its presence extend throughout her entire nervous system, disguised as just another part of it. The seat was clearly in the rear part of the brain, but it's so interwoven that I struggle to believe it’s possible to remove it from someone without a Bond.

I'll need to speak to Healer Thessalanor about whether he has any ideas. Back on Earth, I think this would have been a death sentence – no surgeon could remove it so precisely without risking catastrophic brain damage or permanent paralysis. But this is a world of magic. It might be possible that magical surgeons are capable of such a task.

Finally, I withdraw back into my own body.

"You found the cause?" Healer Thessalanor asks, less of a question than I might have expected. Glancing at the woman, I can see why he would be so confident: her eyes are clear and focused once more, and she's no longer straining at her restraints.

"I have," I confirm, then turn to her. "I'm sorry – I didn't catch your name."

"Milith, master," she replies quietly, her voice hoarse from the growling she's been forced to do.

"Kera Milith. I wish we could have met under better circumstances, however I can tell you with confidence that your actions in the marketplace were not your own, and you will not be held accountable for them."

Some of the rigid tension in her shoulders eases, though a good deal remains.

"Am I... am I healed?" she dares to ask, her eyes fixed on her feet, her hands tightly clenched in the restraints that continue to pin her in place.

"Almost," I tell her. "I just need to remove the remains of the parasite from you."

Perhaps I could have worded that better. A shudder goes through her body, and a ripple of horror passes across our new Bond. I draw a knife from my inventory and watch her flinch violently.

"It's all right," I soothe, sending reassuring feelings along the Bond. "I'm just going to make a small cut. I promise it won't even hurt."

My reassurances don't seem to do much, but she doesn't pull away as I approach slowly. I clench my teeth, not enjoying feeling like the bad guy, but aware I need to get this done, and the sooner the better. I have no intention of leaving the remnants of the parasite inside her – who knows what might happen?

Touching the skin at the side of her neck, I numb the area through the Bond. Then, making the small cut I promised, I close my eyes and focus on drawing out the detritus of the exploded parasite – and its network.

Now that it's dead, it's straightforward enough to infuse my magic into the remains — though there's more resistance than I expected from something that should have no active Energy of its own. Bits of the parasite begin slipping out through the wound like thick clots, though not all of it is red. Some pieces are white and yellow — internal organs, at a guess.

By the time I'm finished I've had to empty my hand three times into a bowl Healer Thessalanor silently provides. It's a surprisingly large amount of biological matter. No wonder Kera Milith looks thin. Probably half the nutrition from her rations has been going towards feeding the parasite's network.

Once I'm sure nothing remains, I heal the wound, release the numbness, and step back. Kera Milith looks at me – not quite meeting my eyes, her gaze settling somewhere around chest height – with an expression of disbelief.

"Is it done, master?" she asks hesitantly.

"It is, however, I would like to continue to take precautions. If you’re willing to keep the Bond in place for a few more days, that would be my preference.” I eye the restraints and hesitate for a moment. “I would also love to remove the restraints but feel that that wouldn’t be wise at this moment.”

“I…I thought I was healed?” she questions with apprehension roiling through the Bond between us. I suspect that if she weren’t so desperate to know the answer, she wouldn’t dare ask the question.

“You are as far as I can tell,” I assure her. “However, this is something new. For your sake – and everyone else’s – I’d rather not find out the hard way that I missed something. By remaining here and restrained, we should notice anything more quickly, and then be able to treat it. Is that alright with you?”

"You may do as you like, master. I would not dream of limiting your actions," she agrees obediently, though the undercurrent of resentment in the Bond shows that she’s far less pliant under the surface than she tries to appear. I keep my sigh inside and my weariness away from the Bond.

“Of course, if you notice anything that feels strange or wrong, make sure you tell us. Now, are you hungry?"

The sudden surge of want through the Bond thoroughly betrays her polite, "If it pleases you, master." I reach into my inventory for some emergency rations and hand her a chunk of meat.

Healer Thessalanor makes a small face as she gnaws at it with the single-mindedness of the truly ravenous, but I don't hold it against her. Healing is demanding on the body at the best of times. Hosting a parasite and surviving its removal must be more demanding still.

"I'll be back shortly," I tell her, then step out of the room, catching Healer Thessalanor’s eye and signalling for him to follow me.

In the corridor, I turn to speak to the healer, then stop as something catches my attention. Through the still-open door, I can see that both men have changed. Where before they seemed indifferent to one another and to Kera Milith, they're now straining toward her with clear aggression, growling through their gags. She's pressing back into her corner, pulling against her own restraints even as she continues gnawing hungrily at the meat.

I might have thought that it’s the food that’s attracted their attention but even when she finishes the last morsels and licks her fingers clean to catch the last little drips of fat, they continue trying to get at her with the same mindless aggression. It’s very different from the last time they were left alone.

I exchange a glance with Healer Thessalanor.

"Interesting," he says quietly. Then, his expression shifting a little, he focuses back on me fully. “What did you find – a parasite, I take it?”

I nod, then launch into a quiet explanation of exactly what happened after the Bond snapped into place. Thessalanor listens quietly, the lines at the corners of his mouth deepening as he presses his lips together in concern.

“I’m not sure anyone here would be capable of such intricate surgery,” he admits after I finish. “I can take a look at the other patients, but what you’ve described here, my lord…” he shakes his head. “I’ve never heard of anything like it.”

“It is odd,” I agree, thinking back to the clear evidence of judgement – the parasite choosing to self-destruct when it realised it had no way out – and limit of its self-preservation instinct – the decision to self-destruct in the first place. “I’m going to treat Ker Bolis. Perhaps see if you can spot the parasite in Ker Himlan now you have more idea of what to look for.”

“Certainly, my lord,” Thessalanor agrees.

We step back in. The men's attention immediately swings to us. I move to stand between them and Kera Milith, and the aggression in their growling shifts accordingly. Whatever drives them, it reacts to proximity – and the lack of another parasite, if I’m reading this right.

“Would you like some more meat?” I ask Kera Milith. She nods with another quiet, “If it pleases you, master,” so I pull out another chunk and hand it over, then shift to crouch beside Ker Bolis, the man on the mattress in the corner, even as Thessalanor sits on the edge of Ker Himlan’s bed. Bolis’ eyes are wild and unfocused, but he stills slightly as I meet his gaze.

"This won't take long," I say, though I suspect he can't understand me, and trigger the Dominate.

The Battle of Wills is much like the one with Kera Milith – a brief, uneven contest. In contrast with Kera Milith, Ker Bolis eagerly agrees to the Bond as soon as he feels the sincerity in my explanation about the parasite, and the Bond forms quickly. I dive into his system.

Just as in Kera Milith, I find the parasite in the rear of his brain, directing everything from the back seat. Alert to its tricks this time, I don't lose track of it as it shifts. Rather than trying to immediately trap it, I consciously allow it to begin moving and herd it – slowly and carefully – all the way down through his spine and into his gut. I want to try to capture it alive if at all possible. And if not possible I'd far rather any self-destruction happened somewhere other than his brain or spinal cord.

Keeping my magic close enough to alarm it, but not so close as to provoke the self-destruct response, I guide it through his gut. Then I give it a moment's respite, withdrawing slightly as though I've lost track of it. Watching intently, I wait until its vibrations reduce and it begins making tentative movements to shift back upward. That’s when I strike, wrapping a stranglehold of magic around it and driving it through his gut as fast as I can, through his rectum and out of his body before it's had time to register what's happened.

I surface from my trance just in time to see a red, slime-like glob ooze through his clothes and land on the sheets — where it vibrates and explodes. I feel several droplets land on my face and grimace. I've avoided injuring my patient, but the parasite is clearly capable of self-destruction even outside a body. And, without any sort of containment, it’s messy.

"Is that what it looks like before it explodes?" Healer Thessalanor asks, though his tone makes it clear the question is rhetorical. There's undisguised disgust on his face as he surveys the ruins of the parasite – the spreading splotch of red now decorating the mattress, the wall, and Ker Bolis's clothes. He's managed to stay outside the blast radius.

"Apparently so," I agree.

He leans forward to peer at the remnants, comparing them to those in the bucket. Using Flesh-Shaping, I start to gather these ones together – who knows if they could contaminate someone somehow if I leave them around.

"Do you think it would be possible to capture one alive?" he asks curiously.

I shrug.

"I tried with this one, with no success, as you saw. Perhaps if I continue to wrap my magic tightly even once it’s left the body, it will prevent the detonation, though it may just contain it.” Or, I think to myself, I may be able to Bind it somehow, though given it has no eyes, that may not be possible.

Healer Thessalanor straightens, his expression sharpening into something more purposeful.

"I’ve examined Ker Himlan, but even knowing what I’m looking for, I’m unable to pin-point it. If I can’t even identify where it is, how can I remove it?” He sucks his teeth thoughtfully. “Something that hides this effectively, that can network itself through an entire nervous system, and that uses self-destruction as a last resort…this is unlike any parasite I've encountered or read about." He shakes his head slowly. "It’s incredible that you’ve managed to find it at all, my lord. I think we need more eyes on this. I'd like to call in some of the other healers to examine what's here, if you are amenable."

"Please do," I agree immediately. "Call whoever you think will be most useful."

He nods, giving the two bowls of parasite remnants a last long look, then straightens and steps towards the door.

"I'll return shortly."

Once he's gone, I crouch beside Ker Bolis. Like Kera Milith was after the death of the parasite, his eyes are clearer now, the wildness ebbing, though confusion and disorientation are rapidly replacing it.

"You're safe," I tell him simply. "I'll explain everything once you've rested." Figuring he’ll also be hungry, I leave a chunk of meat next to his hand – like Kera Milith, feeding himself will be a little awkward, but possible. “Please do eat,” I tell him, responding to his questioning look.

He quickly starts tearing into the meat chunk.

“Thank ye, me lord,” he tells me, real gratitude infusing his voice and drifting through the Bond between us. “Gods, it feels so good te be able te taste meat again. After that bout o’ gods’ punishment I had a couple of tendays back, me sense of smell never quite gone back to normal. ‘Till now, that is.”

“I’m glad you’re enjoying it,” I reply absently, my mind catching onto something in his words, though it takes a few moments for me to work out what. Then it clicks – ‘gods’ punishment’ is what they tend to use to colloquially refer to what I would consider to be the flu. “Wait, you said you had a bout of the gods’ punishment? Was that coughs, sneezes, a fever, joint pain, that sort of thing?”

“Aye, me lord,” Bolis replies, looking at me quizzically for a moment before continuing to tear enthusiastically into his meat. “Couldn’t keep food down neither. Came te the healers, but it didn’t help more’n a little.”

Suddenly, it clicks – I know where his face is familiar from. He’s one of the first eleven cases Thessalanor and I studied when the illness that ripped through the settlement first started taking hold.

And now my brain has made that connection, it makes another. Ker Himlan was also one of those cases, I realise with a dawning sense of horror.

Wheeling towards Kera Milith, I lean forward almost aggressively if the way she flinches is anything to go by. But in this moment, I can't care about that – I’m just desperate to disprove my theory.

“Please tell me you didn’t have similar symptoms a couple of tendays back?”

Kera Milith hesitates a moment, but I feel the truth through the Bond between us.

“You did,” I conclude, my voice dull. “And you didn’t report it, I take it?”

She wordlessly shakes her head.

I curse loudly. Because if my theory is right – and so far, it’s three for three – then we’re not just dealing with a few cases; this is a wide-spread infection.
