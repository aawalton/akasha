The underdog. The being that no one ever had any faith in, but through insurmountable odds, they defy all expectations. There was once a time in Palou’s life where he loved to watch the Exofront and see the most unlikely hero rise to the occasion. Those stories were now dead, lost to the corporate greed that had become the Exofront.

Yet when Palou was faced with an underdog of his own, he realized why the Arbiters of the past despised them.

“He’s ruining everything!” Palou screamed into his monitor. His face was only an inch away from the screen, his nose prickled from the static generated from it. He banged his fists against the table. “How could this happen…” he whispered.

He watched as participant 65 created a trail of fire and chaos wherever he went. Participant 65 was not a being that screamed the makings of an Exoking, a title given to the strongest on The Exofront. Yet he didn’t seem like a complete side character either. His ability to survive was commendable; for all his lack of charisma, he made up for it with wily guts.

Just as he instructed, his star abandoned him, leaving him to be eaten by the Krovalith. By sheer luck, nothing found him in time. When he finally found something, those pesky Xyphons were there. At the time, Palou didn’t care for his existence, his eyes only on his star. He continued to watch him every so often, each time he did he would bite his nails.

The Earthling refused to die. Palou laughed at his attempt to steal from the Xyphons; he thought that he’d be seeing the most entertaining death. Luck struck again. The Xyphons were wiped out by the Fassner brothers.

Palou hated that Exofront politics had been dragged to his small Beta world. House Dorfrasse, a top twenty clan, just had to interfere. Rooting out the Xyphons funded by Clan Kuta. Prejudice wasn’t something Palou was too interested in. Trying to decrease the Xyphon clan’s power out of hatred for their race was ridiculous in his eyes.

He didn’t care for the history surrounding his people and the Xyphons. “They undermined my authority, just like that.” Palou tried to compose himself, but his day continued to get worse. The bonus event spiraled away from him.

Lars of House Dorfrasse died. He’d have a headache soon enough. Worst of all was when participant fifty six refused to kill the buffoon. He handed him a Tekaza, a coin that could barely buy a glass of Juja Juice. Participant fifty-six was easily fooled.

To think, one Tekaza, the minimum amount to send a gift or a message, prolonged his life for just a little longer. Palou checked his audit logs. He laughed, realizing that Clan Kuta had inadvertently saved his life. His laughter grew to anger. “I should be deciding who lives and who dies.”

He set all his monitor screens focused on a single man; a pitiful existence though he may be, he piqued Palou’s interest. Then the Alpha happened. The creature that Palou had saved for his star to defeat. He watched as the man was completely defeated. Palou’s eyes gleamed watching the alpha step towards his body, preparing the final blow.

Yet the oddest thing happened. Palou wouldn’t even call it fortune. Participant Sixty-five’s entire status window changed. He went back to level one, something Palou had never seen before. Even his name had changed. Before Palou could investigate further, he noticed he had a mystery box in his inventory.

He was never gifted another mystery box, Palou raised an eyebrow. He checked his audit logs once again just to confirm. Yeah right here, awarded mystery box after committing the first kill. How did he get another one? He couldn’t help but wonder what the hell was going on.

He didn’t even blink before the Alpha was defeated. He looked towards his monitor with his admin controls. The auto mod gave another mystery box to him. He fell over his chair in frustration. “God dammit. Why did I set up that boss?”

Inadvertently helping the very man he wanted to see dead, oh how he wanted to burn down the entire facility. Participant sixty five had changed after the Alpha. He was self assured. Charming, even. The furthest thing from what he was before.

How could a person change like that in a single moment?

It didn’t matter, Palou shrugged. He had no care in the world, not until he used participant eighty one’s stigmata to track down his star. He first killed the sniper. A completely dispensable participant who valued himself too highly. But what came afterwards was most crucial.

Palou was confident in his star. She could defeat him with her eyes closed, especially with the Exowing suit he had generously gifted her. But Sixty Five was unpredictable. He didn’t attack; he wasn’t conceited defeating the Alpha and Fifty Six.

He waited and watched.

He must’ve made the realization that he was brutally outmatched. So he used the one thing that he knew was her weakness. Number Fifty-Eight. A pitiful woman with no actual skill. Her perception was high and her ability to be strung along fascinated him.

She had once again proven to be a liability to his star. Palou clenched the bottom of his chair as he watched the pair run away. He hoped they would both die somehow.

That day never came. He desperately tried to get his star’s attention. He didn’t bother writing a note this time. Who cared if the higher ups found out? He couldn’t let her die. Not when she had so much more story left to be told. “Oh, Regressor, you can’t walk into the claws of the beast.” He hummed to himself with a smile, sending as many messages as possible to convince her.

Regrettably, he had to use one of the reserve storage cubes that he was meant to keep in case of an emergency or after an event. “It’ll all be worth it,” he muttered to himself. With the bribe set, his angel of light waited for his direction.

Before he set everything up, he looked towards the monitor with the map of the jungle. Red dots pulsated and moved every second on the map. Each dot represented a person. He watched as the dots slowly faded away. They had been killed for failing the bonus event.

Turning that monitor off, he went back to his plan. The trio had been by the cliff side on the cityscape. Before the bonus event, all he had to do was zeta beam his star first and have her wait. Then came teleporting participant sixty five right beside the cliff.

Palou rubbed his hands together, watching the man finally fall to his death. “I love gravity.”

His admin monitor caught his eye once again. Sixty-Five’s stats and name had changed once again. It was more accurate to say it had returned to the original state before he had killed the Alpha.

Palou’s fingertips trailed Sixty Five’s name. “Could he possibly—”

His door suddenly crashed open. He swiveled in his chair to see who entered, hugging his back against his monitors. “What are you doing here,” he hissed.

A Kryk entered through the door, wearing scrubs and a face mask. Her brown chitinous body glimmered under the dim lighting. She held a broom, a mop and pulled a bucket behind her. Her four insect-like arms worked with ease, a dexterity that Palou envied. Even if they didn’t look the most attractive of races.

“I have to clean your office,” she said, her mandibles surrounding her mouth clicked.

Palou stood up from his chair, his head hitting the low ceiling. “Ow! Dammit Shujan! I don’t need someone cleaning my office.”

The Kryk ignored him, using two of her four arms she broomed and mopped the floor simultaneously. “Palou, you haven’t left your office yet. Most of the other Arbiters on this floor barely do anything except goof off. What are you doing on those computers?” asked Shujan, her bulbous eyes peering at him as she worked.

“It’s none of your business.”

His eyes drifting to the floor, Palou noticed the flattened droid he threw. A bead of sweat dripped down his forehead as he watched Shujan clean.

Shujan must have felt his unease. She stopped cleaning and turned to him. “What’s the matter with you, boy?”

“Nothing.”

“Is it about those threats you were getting?” Shujan’s eyes looked at him with pity.

Palou raised an eyebrow. “What threats?” he asked softly.

“Forget I said anything,” she replied, returning to cleaning.

Palou felt a sinking worry from the Kyrks words. He shot towards her, snatching her broom and mop out of her hands. “What did you hear?”

Letting out a sigh, Shujan shrugged. “The Endangered Creatures Division was annoyed to find you were using one of their planets. You apparently reduced the creatures living there to a fraction of their population. I didn’t believe them, of course. You’re a good boy after all.”

“No, no, no, no, no.” Palou walked back and forth, his shoulders hitting the walls of his tiny office. “They should’ve never realized. How did they figure it out?” He was sure he bribed the right people at the ECD, but clearly they were going to use the Krovalith’s for something. It’s fine, it’s fine, he told himself.

“Shujan, they didn’t report me, did they?”

Shujan shook her head. “Not that I know of. I think they’re focused on other matters as of right now.” She looked at him, her eyes full of suspicion. “You didn’t do anything wrong, did you?”

Rubbing his temples, Palou tried to smile. “No, of course not, I’m just stressed is all.” He returned to his chair, looking at his monitors. “Just—just get the cleaning done and leave, please.”

“Sure Palou, you should really go home. The other Arbiters on this floor have already gone.” Shujin said.

“No, I have too much work to do.” Palou bit at his nails. They’re going to find out soon enough. Mr. Steiger isn’t going to be happy. He contemplated what he could do, but he was already too far gone. He needed to end this. His world must come to an end as soon as possible.

A smile grew on his face as he etched closer to his monitors. His eyes only focused on watching his star.

He suddenly remembered contestant sixty five. He clicked a button on his keyboard, and the monitor shifted to one of the droid cameras focused at the bottom of the cliff. Rubble and broken glass filled the ground. But he couldn’t find the Earthling.

Clicking through more cameras, he couldn’t find a trace of him. A sense of discomfort pulsed through his body. He quickly shifted to his other monitor with his Admin controls. He scrolled through all the participants until he found who he was looking for.

Track Participant?

“Let’s see where you’re hiding.”

(Error 404) Participant could not be found

Palou’s heartbeat pounded in his chest. “Where did he go?” he whispered.

Contestant sixty five’s body had disappeared. “Ciro Adams…what the hell are you?”

***

Shujin closed the door to Palou’s office. She couldn’t understand what was going through that young man’s head. He seemed far too enamoured with his job. Then again, a lowly janitor could never understand. She sighed, pulling her cleaning utensils along.

In her free hand, she held a flattened, monitoring droid in her hand. She examined it closely. Why did he destroy it? She thought. Thinking it was faulty perhaps and Palou had yet to call the engineering team, her mandibles clicked in excitement.

Maybe I could receive a bonus if I hand this over.

She juggled everything in her hands. The dexterity of her race was always useful. Pulling out her PDA from her pocket. She looked at the time. It was getting late and her two children were probably worried. “I’ll just save it for tomorrow,” she mumbled.

Heading to the toilets to get changed first. Once out of her janitor scrubs, she navigated to the exit of the Ballack Inc headquarters. Only Palou remained. Besides the endless security, of course.

“I hope he doesn’t work himself to death,” she said, fidgeting with the droid under her sinewy hands.
