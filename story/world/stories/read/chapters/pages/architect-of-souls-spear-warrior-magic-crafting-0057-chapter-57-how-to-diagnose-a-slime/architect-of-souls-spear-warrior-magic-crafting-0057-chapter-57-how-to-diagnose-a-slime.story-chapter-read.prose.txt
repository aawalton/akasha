Ferris spent the next six days focused on work and qi gathering. He used all four of the remaining incense sticks. Each day, he diligently fed Jade enough straw to fill the Digest River with qi.

They reduced the amount of food until they’d found the limit where some of the clams wouldn’t produce qi. It was a bit under six bundles; after that, he just decided to feed Jade all six to make it easier.

Jade hated leaving a meal unfinished, after all.

The work refining spirit gems was tedious, and getting pretty boring—with occasional bouts of excitement. Of the twenty-four gems he’d refined, five of them had lingering monsters to fight. He had to be constantly on guard for more ambushes while repeating a monotonous task. After the red lizards, there had been two more types of lizards, some kind of rat creature, a horrible biting fish, and a flying scarab beetle.

The last two were the worst. The fish were in a blue gem that took forever to slog through alone. Then, the scarab beetles would flutter about above him in a yellow gem filled with sand until he finally went and got his crossbow to shoot them down.

Each day was packed, and there was almost no time left after he’d worked and finished his cultivation chores. This included harvesting qi from the Air Passage twice—once for the normal collection and then again after meditating with a burning incense stick—fishing all the qi from the Digest River, watering the Core Tree and both seeds in their pots, and then finally his newest torture: practicing with the legacy imprint spear.

Ferris tried to spend at least half an hour each night with the horrible thing, although mostly it felt like an exercise in futility. Uncle Winton hadn’t exaggerated when he said it wouldn’t be fun, but he kept at it. He ignored the little voices in his head, wondering why he even kept practicing a style he couldn’t master.

Reading through Bluereef Surging Spear Art was both helpful and frustrating. The first part of the book was direct and understandable, but then it quickly shifted tone. Everything after that was… abstract. It talked a lot about different mental states a warrior must embrace. There were lists of mastery levels, with confusing explanations. For example, to reach the First Mastery of Bluereef Surging Spear Art, one must have ‘conscious control of flow state.’ Looking up flow state, it only said one must ‘awaken the spear.’

He did spend a little time glancing through the first few pages of the Raging Stream Jagged Point Advancement Method book from the legacy chest. Uncle Winton had warned him not to read too far into it, but the man had been vague on how far was ‘too far.’

Ferris believed this was on purpose. A sign his mentor was trusting him, as well as an acknowledgement that Ferris might be alone when he needed help. The box containing information on Bluereef’s water spellcasting ability was locked completely, so Ferris knew it would have been possible to lock this book as well.

Unfortunately, there wasn’t anything directly useful for him. The first part of the book outlined a set of daily exercises aimed at improving control of magical water, and Ferris didn’t feel comfortable reading further yet. Not that he really had time anyway.

The oath statue hadn’t changed, still hanging from its frame at the chain demon’s fort. He’d made several more trips to check on it, but the cracked statue hadn’t repaired itself or gotten any worse.

Ferris tried to begin clearing away the black rock surrounding the Core Tree in the slimespace, but it turned out to be harder than he’d expected. At first, he believed he could break it apart with his axe, but that barely made a dent in the ground. Either the tools in the spirit gem refinement spiritual device were much stronger—likely, as they literally glowed—or the black rock in the slimespace was harder. He considered spending qi to make a pickaxe, but he wasn’t sure that a different form would give a significantly better tool than just his axe. Additionally, he didn’t know what benefits clearing the black rock would give. For now, he decided to wait and focus on his more immediate problems. He already had too many projects to work on.

But all his hard work was showing results. He’d earned six merit points and spent one on food for Jade, putting him at ten of the twelve he needed. The Qi Pool had swelled to seven times its size, finally looking less like a puddle and more like a pond. He’d even needed to use his shovel to deepen it. Not quite the endless ocean he wanted, but definite progress.

He planned to keep working every day until he had enough merit to afford Acid Resistance, and then likely keep working after. The ability would almost certainly require purchasing additional resources from the clan, after all.

The next morning, however, Ferris woke up to an unpleasant surprise. His normal morning routine involved repeating the list of safety instructions to Jade. But, instead of the usual excited response, the slime was acting strangely.

“Jade, are you okay?” Ferris asked over the bond, starting to get concerned.

“Not feel good,” Jade responded, a bit wobbly.

Ferris had never heard of a slime getting sick, and he didn’t know what he could do.

“Do you want some food?” he asked, concerned for the slime.

“Yes!” Jade always wanted food, although the slime appeared to be having trouble moving around.

Ferris carried Jade to breakfast, although he grabbed the plates and returned to his room. He didn’t know if eating would help the slime, but there wasn’t much else he could think of.

Jade ate each piece of food, seeming to enjoy it. The slime gave their usual ratings, but something was definitely off, and it appeared to be getting worse. Each bite was taking longer and longer to dissolve, and Jade’s speech over the bond was becoming delayed.

Finally, Jade stopped talking completely, and Ferris’ worry turned into panic. His familiar didn’t respond at all, as if still asleep. Ferris had seen the slime sleep before, but they had never failed to instantly wake up to any stimulation.

Worriedly, Ferris picked Jade up, but Jade was still unresponsive. Even a mention of the remaining food failed to get any reaction.

Looking at the slime in his hands, Ferris considered what he could do. Jade appeared normal, at least. None of the stories he heard growing up covered this situation; bonded spears didn’t get sick after all. Could slimes get sick? It wasn’t like there was a monster doctor he could take Jade to. He could go talk to the slime ranchers, but they wouldn’t be able to help if this was related to cultivation…

Elder Harper might be able to help, he thought, standing up. I will go to her office.

Walking to the door, he hesitated. Looking down at Jade in his arms, he didn’t know what to do.

Slimes can’t die as long as their cores are intact… right?

Ferris shook his head. Just because he had been told it didn’t make it true.

But what can Elder Harper even do for a sick slime?

She could do more than Ferris.

Is that really true? What can I do that I haven’t tried?

Sitting back down, Ferris watched Jade’s still form. Going to Elder Harper would have… consequences. He wasn’t sure what, but he knew there would be a price. It would be more than worth it if it helped Jade.

Yet… could he solve this on his own? What would be the first thing she would do?

She would examine Jade’s body, then use some kind of visual ability to check on the slime’s spiritual health.

Ferris didn’t have any ability like that, but he could check the slimespace.

His experience investigating Jade’s healing had shown that slimes were very magical in ways that other monsters and humans were mundane. If there was something wrong, it might be represented in the slimespace. The food Jade had been eating had also dissolved more slowly, so it could even be related to the Consume Pool.

Nodding to himself, he lay back down on the bed. If this didn’t work, then he would take Jade to see Elder Harper. It was a risk, but the slime’s core appeared fine.

Closing his eyes, he tried to cultivate. It was harder than normal, as he kept imagining new things that could be wrong. The ever-present feeling of the bond still existed, but was strangely silent.

In the soulspace, Ferris awoke alone. He had half hoped Jade would be waiting for him in his soul, perhaps if the slime had figured out how to cultivate alone. It wouldn’t have explained the lack of response over the bond, but he had still hoped.

Entering the slimespace, Ferris was greeted with absolute chaos. A war was being fought all around him.

Everywhere he looked, swarms of yellow scarab beetles were attacking the grey ants. He instantly recognized the scarabs from the sandy spirit gem Jade and Ferris had refined yesterday.

Ferris had no idea how they had gotten into the slimespace. He’d taken a couple of their corpses to craft with later, but he was sure they were all dead first. Plus, they were all stored next to his Heartfire.

There had to be thousands of them all over inside the dome. Most were on the ground, but every so often their backs would open up to show delicate wings, letting them slowly fly from point to point.

And some are much larger, Ferris thought. The ones he’d fought in the spirit gem had all been smaller than Jade. Somehow, many of them had grown larger than the ants. A few massive ones were even bigger than Ferris.

While the scarabs covered the ground, the grey ants resolutely defended their pillars. Each yellow pillar had a dozen ants fighting to keep the scarabs at bay. A few of the grey birds hovered above, higher than the scarabs wanted to fly. Every so often, one would dive to attack an isolated scarab. The scarabs were slower than the birds, but much greater in numbers. At least the larger ones appeared to have a bit of trouble flying.

He couldn’t see if they had taken over the tallest tower, or if the birds still held it.

The glowing walls of the dome belonged to the invaders. The scarabs covered it, digging into the green translucent material.

Ferris only had a couple of seconds to take everything in before the nearest scarabs noticed him. They immediately rushed over, skittering along the ground towards where he stood. He had his bone spear and dagger with him, but had left the crossbow behind.

It would take a couple of seconds of calm concentration to leave the slimespace, and the invaders didn’t give it to him. The closest one jumped up into the air, wings appearing out of its back to push it higher as it charged.

Ferris jabbed his bone spear through it, cleanly skewing the bug. Thankfully, none of the larger ones were nearby. These were just a bit smaller than Jade, and much smaller than one of the grey ants. Unfortunately, they had an advantage in numbers, and more were quickly arriving.

Abandoning the fight, Ferris needed a better vantage point. He couldn’t make a plan without better understanding the situation. He quickly grabbed the core from its pedestal and ran for the nearest yellow pillar.

Last week, he’d moved his core pedestal from atop the Heartfire tower to down near the Consume Pool. Thankfully, there were a good number of pillars nearby. He had to push his way through several groups of the scarabs, but he was able to get by with just a few cuts. The things had sharp claws and teeth.

Ferris had always had an unspoken truce with the grey ants. They had never attacked him, and he had never given them reason to. They were currently fiercely protecting the entrance to their pillars, and he was afraid they would attack him if he approached. Unfortunately, it was that or fight a hopeless battle surrounded by more enemies than he could defeat.

He slowed as he approached the nearest pillar, turning to use his spear to keep the scarabs back as best he could. He put his back to the ants defending the pillar. If they attacked him, he was probably dead either way. Still, he tried to move closer slowly, watching to see if the ants would be hostile.

Thankfully, the ants seemed to welcome him into their ranks. They moved around him to fight the scarabs, giving Ferris a much easier time. One ant even pulled a scarab that had latched onto Ferris’ leg off him, crunching the thing in its mandibles.

He took the opportunity to secure the core tightly in a small pocket in his belt, then was drawn back into the fight.

Even with the help of the ants, Ferris wasn’t able to rest. The ants were defending their pillars, but primarily at the holes leading underground. The scarabs also had the advantage of flight, so they were darting about through the air at Ferris’ head.

After a few minutes of frantic defense, Ferris saw a pattern. There was an ebb and flow to the fight. First, the ants would rush up out of their pillar to try to push the scarabs back. They would overrun the nearby scarabs, gaining some of the area around the pillar.

Unfortunately, the scarabs would then counterattack from both the ground and the air. The larger scarabs were much harder for the ants to fight. Then, smaller flying scarabs would land on top of them. The ants’ rally would be quickly wiped out. The combination was like a rock the ants couldn’t break.

The larger scarabs were much bigger threats to Ferris than the smaller ones. He made sure to never engage them alone, only poking out with his spear when supported by the ants. Thankfully, the truly massive scarabs appeared to be more around the dome’s wall.

The most dangerous times for Ferris were directly after a push by the ants failed. Even with his back to the pillar, he would often be attacked by several smaller scarabs at once. The only thing keeping him in the fight was the fact that he was so much bigger, and he could take a couple of hits while waiting for the ants to resurge. This came at a cost, though, and he was bleeding from a dozen different bite wounds.

The next time the ants surged back out in force, he stayed back. He lifted himself a bit higher up the pillar. The yellow sandstone material had a lot of small handholds, making it easy for him. Looking around, the scarabs were everywhere. He spotted a few places where the ants still held. Most notably, the two towers that held the Core Tree and Jade’s Heartfire were solidly ant-territory.

With grim determination, Ferris decided to head for the Heartfire tower. It was the most important location in the soulspace. He’d be able to make a better plan from behind friendly lines.

Dropping back to the ground, Ferris watched for his opportunity. The ants held the spires, while swarming groups of yellow scarabs separated them.

After a few centering breaths, Ferris charged out with a battle cry. He passed through the ants’ forward line. Nearby scarabs leapt at him. Keeping his body low, he resolutely ran forward. At first, he tried to defend with his spear. It didn’t work, slowing him. Instead, Ferris focused on continuing forward. Being pulled to the ground would mean death.

The moment he reached the next spire, ants converged on him. Roughly, they ripped any latched-on scarabs off him. The tiny claws and teeth left gashes as they were yanked off.

He took a moment to check himself over. It was painful, but nothing critical. Turning to the next spire, he ran out again.

Three more spires to go. Then two. One left.

Each charge left more wounds. Yet he pushed through.

At the last spire, things got complicated. He was one final push from safety. The ants had a much better defensive line protecting the base of the Heartfire tower. Yet that meant the yellow scarabs were present in much greater numbers. Many more of the larger scarabs skittered around, and even a few massive ones loomed above their brethren.

Ferris spent a few minutes searching for an opportunity, but quickly realized there wouldn’t be one. No matter where he looked, the fighting was fierce. If he wanted a way through, he’d have to make one himself.

He’d gotten a better feel for the battle around him, charging from spire to spire. It was a contest of swarms. The ants were larger than most of the scarabs, but struggled against both the larger enemies and being attacked from the air.

No matter how many of the ants died, more seemed to emerge from the spires. Where did they all come from? How deep had they dug down? In this moment, he hoped they were endless, yet knew better.

At least the spire he was at had more reinforcements than the one down by the Consume Pool. They needed it, as many more larger scarabs were pressing in.

This was where Ferris saw his chance. He started to press forward, darting out with his spear to injure or disable the larger scarabs. Never overcommitting. Never letting himself extend too far. Yet pushing the battle line forward.

The ants could deal with the flying enemies, or they could fight the larger ants. It was when they had to do both that they lost ground. Ferris couldn’t do much about the smaller scarabs in the air; each one he killed was quickly replaced. However, a well-placed spear thrust was worth a lot more against the large scarabs.

With mounting momentum, Ferris pushed the battle line forward. He danced along the bleeding edge, targeting each enemy with ruthless precision. The imprint spear sang in his hands, needing little more than a thought to impose reality.

Ants died. Scarabs died. Both sides threw more bodies to the slaughter.

Ferris lost himself to the fight. Enemies buzzed through the air around him. Scarabs would latch onto him. The ants would tear them off. It was more important to find his next target. Push the line forward.

Closer, and closer.

Then, a shape arose before him. Stopping, Ferris looked up at one of the massive scarabs.

Bleeding and tired, he lifted his spear.
