[Congratulations. Skill proficiency: Nature Synthesis Technique has increased to Level 42.]

The notification sounds rang out in rapid succession. His skill proficiencies had risen another step, especially Peach Blossom Dance. The higher Jinyo’s Plana became, the more skillfully he could use the technique.

What would happen if Sword Mind and Peach Blossom Dance both reached 100? That was very interesting. One thing was certain: even with the same technique, the difference between proficiency level 1 and 50 was like heaven and earth. Between 50 and 100, it would be like hell and paradise.

‘The farmland we need is 15 rai. We need 36,000 hours of crop acceleration tickets. I have to kill 300 more Eltharoks. Without the Otherworldly Heavenly Territory, there’s no way we could do it.’ Jinyo carefully calculated the resources required. Tilling the soil, eliminating threats around the village, removing rocks and tree stumps—those were just side benefits.

This was what Jinyo had been aiming for from the very beginning. He had gathered 15,000 hours so far—almost halfway there. Without Esther, Rina, and the other survivors working together, there was no way Jinyo could have collected so many crop cultivation tickets.

Before five in the morning, Jinyo stuffed the scattered monster corpses into boxes until they were full.

[You have sold 200 kilograms of cursed fruit, Grade G quality.Received: 20,000 spirit coins.]

[You have sold 320 kilograms of Grade G- monster remains.Received: 1,600 spirit coins.]

[You have sold 500 kilograms of Grade G wood.Received: 500 spirit coins.]

A total of 22,100 spirit coins was transferred into his account through the system. The purchase box was like a recycling bin, helping dispose of tree stumps and monster corpses so they would not stink. Even Jinyo had no desire to keep their withered, mummy-like bodies for fertilizer.

‘Just as I thought, cursed fruit really brings in excellent profit. Even if the purchase price isn’t as high as Immortal Rice.’ Jinyo sorted out the cursed fruits with good genes and stored them in his farmer’s bag. The rest he dumped together with the trash. Then he opened the catalog and ordered as many potato seeds as possible, along with the spellbook Iron Fiber Reinforcement, absurdly expensive, but likely very useful.

Still 15,000 hours short. No, actually, it was not necessary to reach the full 36,000 hours. If he wanted to push progress as fast as possible, it could not be enough to have only Rina and Esther luring the monsters. Jinyo had to kill them as quickly as possible himself.

The only way was to refine the techniques he already possessed, such as Peach Blossom Dance and Sword Mind, the most fundamental offensive arts. Jinyo borrowed a phone and had others record videos of him fighting. During breaks, he would replay them and use the Sage’s Eye to search for flaws.

That was why Jinyo improved so quickly. But at the same time, his mind bore an equally crushing burden.

“Big Bro Yo, are you okay? Drink some cold water and freshen up first. You’re going to pass out again at this rate. Acting like some manga protagonist…” Naho ran over carrying a cold bottle of water. His younger sister handed it to him. Jinyo took a sip to quench his thirst. Once water entered his body, his mind relaxed more than he expected.

“How’s the gathering of materials going?”

“No idea. The monsters here hardly drop any cores, and we can’t drain blood from them either.”

“Is that so? Then poor James has been stuck with a troublesome job.”

“You’re the troublesome one. Go rest for a bit. Or let me kill them in your place. Otherwise, fight me instead.” Naho volunteered to take over.

“No. If someone helps, the system splits the rewards with them. Naho, I figured out one thing about how the system’s power works. Normally, if you kill a monster or defeat a boss, you get experience points, right? And that makes you stronger.”

“Yeah. That’s basic knowledge.”

“Then why don’t I get experience points, and get tickets instead? The answer is: the experience doesn’t disappear. It’s converted into another form—tickets used to accelerate plant growth. And I only get the experience once I harvest those plants. I calculated it already. I’ll receive twice the experience I invested.”

“Twice?!” Naho cried out. What was that supposed to mean—double experience?! If that was true, her brother’s level would skyrocket.

“What level are you now?”

“20. Back at the farm, I had to split experience with Pop, so my level only rose this much. But if I killed 1,000 monsters, used the tickets to speed up potato growth, and harvested all of them… don’t you think my level would shoot up?”

“...” Naho was speechless. Her brother’s way of leveling up really was unlike normal people. Others grew stronger the instant they killed a monster—it was the reward for success. But Jinyo had to go through one extra step.

“That’s why I can’t let other people help. If they do, the experience gets divided, and the number of tickets I receive decreases. The monsters in Hollow Gate are limited. I don’t even know if there’ll be enough for me to kill 1,000.”

“Got it. Do your best, Big Bro. And don’t worry—I won’t tell anyone you’re using other people as tools to power-level yourself!” Naho laughed brightly. Then she turned toward a direction where a large group of footsteps could be heard. If it was not Rina, then it had to be Esther dragging another pack of monsters in.

Jinyo exhaled frostily. When people’s lives and deaths depended on his actions, there was no chance for rest. Even while chatting pointlessly with his younger sister, he did not feel rested. His nerves tightened as another horde of monsters approached. Esther sprinted with all his might, charging forward. His clothes were smeared with mud and torn in several places from branches. His handsome face was blackened like ink, as though his heart had been consumed by fury.

“Eat well, you bastard. A hundred monsters. Exactly the order you asked for.”

“Haha… I didn’t think you’d really manage to drag a hundred over. Did you run all the way to the forest on the other side just to bring me monsters? No wonder you took so long.” Jinyo’s right eyelid twitched. The vibration from hundreds of pounding footsteps was genuinely terrifying.

“Don’t go crying for help from anyone. You ordered it yourself, so finish every last one of them, you lousy customer,” Esther snapped, accelerating again and vanishing from perception without the slightest hesitation. The only thing that man wanted was to see Jinyo in a pitiful, miserable state.

“Uh, Naho… I know I said I didn’t want help splitting the experience, but I do want someone to take the kicks fo—” Jinyo turned to look at his younger sister, who had just been eager to help. But there was only empty space. Not even a shadow of his beloved little sister remained.

“Naho… Aaagh!” Jinyo bolted at full speed. What the hell was this? An army of a hundred monsters—and more than half of them were Eltharoks. That Esther was clearly doing this on purpose.

“Sorry, Big Bro Yo. I did want to help, but taking the beatings in your place? That’s a different matter!” Naho pressed her palms together in prayer. That was all the help she could give him. Since staying would be useless, she fled the scene instead. Her brother probably would not want anyone seeing such a pitiful sight. Yes, running away was also her way of helping him.

One day later, the number of survivors had increased exactly as expected. Planting torches made from resin trees had helped guide people together. As long as the rain did not put out the fires, the eerie fog no longer threatened their lives. The grass porridge, full of nourishment, staved off hunger for the whole day, and Rina no longer had to use her sword’s power.

Everyone was happy. It was likely the first day since they had fallen into Hollow Gate that anyone had felt real peace of mind. Shelter, food, safety—these were the things that satisfied human beings. But there was one person who was not particularly happy.

“I’m so tired. I want to die.” Jinyo sat pale beneath a tree, curled up like a boiled shrimp. Gretel picked up a twig nearby and poked him to see whether his father was still alive.

“You’re exhausted from just this much? My mother has done farm work for three days and three nights without sleep.”

“Don’t compare someone living at level 50 to me at barely level 20. Anyone above level 50 isn’t even human anymore,” Jinyo groaned. Every nerve in his body was crying out for rest before they burned through all his glycogen and turned him into a lump of flesh. Sitting in meditation and reading without sleep was possible. But slaughtering packs of a hundred monsters continuously for thirty-six hours—that was too barbaric.

“A level-20 guy who keeps butchering level-20 to 30 monsters like pigs and dogs for nearly two sleepless days isn’t human either, if you ask me. So is it enough now? I’d like to stop running over that mountain. My legs are about to give out.” Esther’s legs trembled violently. Right now he looked like one of those people doing bizarre dances online—his upper body perfectly normal, but his lower half like soft rubber, collapsing and wobbling in a viral internet clip.

“You’re still weak. You’re a man, yet you can’t even match me, a woman. Not only did you lose, you can’t even endure as much as I can,” Rina said in a mocking tone.

“What did you say? Want to try again? This time I’m going to figure out what trick you used.” Esther drew his ether sword from its sheath. Rina sighed and waved him off.

“When we get back to the outside world, I’ll play with you to your heart’s content. But don’t force me to waste my energy now. Using Divine Punishment too much isn’t good.”

“Scared?”

“I don’t know. I’ve never learned how to spell that word in my life. But the fact that you can say it so easily shows what kind of person you are. The sort who blusters to cover up his own weakness.”

“You bitch…”

“You two—why do you keep snapping at each other? You’re only making yourselves more tired. And you too, Rina. You don’t have to play along with him.” Jinyo spoke weakly. He curled up and lay down, truly no longer responding. No matter what anyone said, he was done. Rina and Esther looked at each other. It seemed the brutal monster-dragging would end here, because Jinyo had reached his limit.

The proficiency of both Sword Mind and Peach Blossom Dance had stopped at level 60 and would not rise any further. Jinyo’s mind was too exhausted to learn any more techniques or arts. Esther carried Jinyo on his back and threw him onto a bed in one of the houses. Even when Esther deliberately dropped him hard, Jinyo did not stir awake. His defenses were full of openings. Anyone with a weapon could kill him easily.

“Damn it… what the hell am I even doing?” Esther muttered under his breath. Instead of going outside, he sat in the corner of the room and watched over Jinyo. Everyone else had gone out to do their assigned tasks. Only Esther had received no particular order. It was not that nobody wanted him to do anything, but that no one could order Esther around except Rina and Jinyo.

He did not know why, but ever since staying with Jinyo, Esther felt unable to take his eyes off this little demon.

‘Why does he push himself that far? Other people’s hunger isn’t even his problem.’ If Esther were in his place, he would guard his own bowl of rice to the bitter end. He would never do something as pointless as sharing food with strangers.

‘Does he really think he can grow anything here? And yet somehow I ended up getting swept along with what he’s doing too. It’s completely pointless… so why?’ Esther stared blankly out the window. He did not leave that room for even a minute—not until Jinyo woke up a few hours later.
