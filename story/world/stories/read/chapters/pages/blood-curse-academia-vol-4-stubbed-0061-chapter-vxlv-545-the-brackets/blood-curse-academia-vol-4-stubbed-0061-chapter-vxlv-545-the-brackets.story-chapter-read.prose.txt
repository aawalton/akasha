Chapter V.XLV (5.45) - The Brackets

After examining his schedule for the final weeks of the semester, Kizu found a slot perfect for delving into the World Dungeon. He wasn’t about to just bolt off on a whim like the last time he’d met Otochi. This time, he would be prepared to face the Blood Lord. He’d already discussed it with both Basil and Ione. They’d decided to keep the group tight, just the three of them. Anata, under no circumstances, would not be joining them. They also knew that if Taroe or the headmaster learned about their delve, it would be over before it even began. From an objective perspective, Kumiho’s capture and potential death was no great loss. If anything, the end of a Harbinger at the hands of another Calamity was objectively a good thing. So they deemed keeping those adults out of the loop vital. Just because they’d been sympathetic to their cause in regard to Anata and Basil, didn’t guarantee their support with Kumiho.

Kizu, not wanting to lose precious time in the classroom, scheduled their delve for after the completion of their classes for the semester. While they didn’t yet know the tournament bracket’s matchups, they did know the schedule. And there would be a four-day break at the center of the tournament. It was possible to have up to six days of no matches, so long as none of them ended up in the loser’s bracket; however, without knowledge of who’d they’d be facing, it was best to plan for only the four days.

“With Ione’s mount, we should be able to get down to Otochi’s lair within a day and a half,” Kizu said to the two of them. They were currently hunched over a table on Owl’s Respite. The skeletons, who usually played cards at this table, had been banished to the deck above.

“Your bell would speed things up,” Basil pointed out. “It only took hours when we fled the dungeon.”

Kizu shook his head. “I can’t count on the World Dungeon listening to me. It’s too choosy about when it wants to assist.”

“That’s fine with me,” Ione replied. “I don’t like when we use the bell. It always limits how many monsters we encounter.”

“You’re certain about only us three?” Basil asked, for the third time in this meeting alone. “Aoi could bring a horde of undead, Sene’s powerful, Faible’s knowledgeable about the history down there. A full party of us delving would be more secure.”

“Aoi is a princess of Hon,” Kizu said. “One of the only nobility not sealed inside Hon’s borders. If something happens to her, it could cause cascading political fallout. Neither Faible nor Sene know anything about Anata’s origins. I don’t know how they’d react to that information and would rather never find out. Especially considering Faible’s history with Calamities.”

“Sene would rat us out, too,” Ione helpfully pointed out. “And having Aoi remain here gives us someone we can trust to watch over everything while we’re gone.”

Basil leaned back in his seat, his spine curving around the top of the chair as he stared up at the ceiling. “I suppose. There’s no one else?”

“Stina needs to watch over Mae. I’ve already told her to be vigilant during that time.”

“Your brother?”

Kizu gave the shapeshifter a flat look.

“Fine, fine. What about your buddy, Harvey? I haven’t seen him around for a while.”

“That’s.…” Kizu frowned. In many ways, having Harvey along wouldn’t be a bad idea. He was a competent fighter and not likely to rat them out. However, he was also the reason Kizu had rashly charged down into the World Dungeon the first time in an effort to locate his sister. “Harvey isn’t trustworthy,” Kizu concluded.

“Fine, fine.” Basil waved a hand. “You still have the outfits I made for our adventure last semester? Do you need me to sew something new?”

Kizu exchanged a look with Ione. “I think we’re both fine.”

“You certain? Fads have shifted significantly in the last half a year. I could spin-up something to match the current times. Sasaki is bringing back feathered shoulderpads.”

Ione yawned and stretched. “When exactly do you want to go, again?”

“I already told you.” Kizu sighed. He pulled out his scrying orb to show her the week, but he paused as he noticed a new message. It was from the faculty staff.

Kizu examined it. He read it three times over, just to be certain he understood correctly.

“What’s with the creepy grin?” Basil asked. “You look like you belong dressed in a dark robe and eyeliner. At any moment, I half expect you will smash a cane carved from blackened bone down on the table, declaring your allegiance for a dark entity, forgotten to the world at large.”

“What?” Kizu offered the shapeshifter a quizzical glance and then reread the message on his orb once again.

“I can kind of see it,” Ione said, agreeing with Basil’s weird comment. “Kizu, let me know if you decide to go in that direction. Basil will get us outfitted and I can summon us menacing beast escorts. Altering most summons’ color schemes to be black with red eyes is child’s play. Oh! Do you think I’ll be able to summon the evil creature you’re worshiping? That would align me with you perfectly.”

“I… you guys realize we’re here to discuss world-ending Calamities? Not to brainstorm a strange fiction where I worship the things?”

“Well, it’s your fault. You’re looming so sinisterly,” Basil replied. “Just tell us what you’re reading.”

Kizu finally pulled his gaze from the scrying orb and set it on the table. “The first brackets are all out. We know our first opponents.”

“Really?” Basil lifted an eyebrow inhumanly high. “Who’d I get matched up with?”

“Um.” Kizu flipped through the listings. “A second-year named Agnus?”

“Oh. I slept with her last year. We met at a party that went a bit unorthodox. This Tainted guy, Braxton, smashed through four different doors that night. The wood shrapnel in the third door sprayed all over her and I helped remove and heal the splinters.”

“That’s almost romantic,” Ione said.

“Almost,” Basil agreed cheerily. “Less so when she puked up this orange goo all over the sheets later. Someone spiked the punchbowl with a potion and didn’t get the recipe right. Thankfully, my excellent metabolism kept me in tip-top shape. Everyone else though… well, I think they actually ended up deciding to paint that villa orange to cover up the stains.”

“The potion recipe might have been fine to begin with,” Kizu said. “Once you add alcohol into the mix, it’s going to cause the effects of almost all brews to go wild. You’re all lucky it was just puke.”

“Who’s mine?” Ione asked. “Who am I fighting?”

Kizu scanned the list again. “Someone named Horus? He’s a fourth-year.”

Basil winced. “Oh. Yeah, I slept with him too. Um, word for the wise, Ione, keep your distance. His family is a raccoon breeder. And he smells the part. Get too close and the smell might knock you out cold. He’ll also probably summon some of the critters, so mind their claws.”

“A summoner?” Ione features turned sharp and alert as she considered. “Hm. I think I’ve seen him back when I still competed in the weekend challenges two years ago. He actually has a few magical beasts. He used a tanooki trio to beat a girl using water elemental spells.”

“You seriously remember someone else’s fight from two years back?” Basil asked. “I was chatting with you last week and you revealed you couldn’t even recall Krimpit’s name. You called him the, ‘angry tweed professor.’”

“It was a momentary flub in my memory.” Ione waved her hand, dismissing Basil. “Besides, Krimpit doesn’t summon interesting creatures. It was fascinating to see Horus’s tactics with the tanooki. I learned a lot from the experience.”

“Anyway.” Basil turned his attention back to Kizu. He steepled his hands and leaned in. “Neither of us have particularly interesting opponents. So that leaves you, Kizu. Who are you fighting for the first round of the tournament?”

Kizu read the pairing once again before he spoke the name. It wasn’t someone he’d genuinely given much thought to in these last few months. And yet… he felt his pulse quicken with excitement as he reread the listing.

“Ulric.”
