Prince Arwen, Lord Regent of the Kingdom of Starhaven, sat behind his desk, leaning heavily onto it as he cradled his head. The message-scroll lying in front of him was active, but it only transmitted audio, not visual data. He saw, as much as felt, as the magic held within clicked into place when the field agent holding the pair of scrolls finally activated her own copy.

“My Lord-“ the feminine, slightly nervous voice of one Stephanie Graham sounded from the scroll, heard only by the Prince.

“Is it true?” He asked gruffly, forgoing all propriety and politeness. It usually wasn’t done, even when conversing with field agents who were trained to be blunt and economical with their words, but Arwen didn’t care at the moment. How could he? “That report you sent, is it true? I need to hear it, if you lied just to get my attention … well, you have it now but you won’t like the consequences, Agent.”

“No, Lord Regent,” the woman replied with an admirably level tone, though a bit too hastily. She was well-trained, but nervous still. Why wouldn’t she be? Rank 1 Agents were a dime and a dozen; no King, Regent or even a random Royal would ever personally request to speak with them. But they were living in strange times now, and this woman in particular had given him news that was stranger still. “It is true, and told to be as unbiased and detailed as I could make it to the best of my abilities. He fits the profile perfectly, name, looks and power.”

Gabriel. Arwen thought to himself, closing his eyes for a moment as a tumultuous mix of emotions clashed in his heart. He had so few people he could call friends as a prince, but Gabriel Vexley was … or had been, one of them. What are you thinking? Why? Why leave me? Why? I would have-

Arwen cut that foolish thought off. He knew why. He hated it, but if this agent’s report was true, then he could even … understand it. Sympathise with it, even. He hated it. It felt like betrayal and it stung like nothing else.

The Lord Regent said a swift farewell to the low-ranking agent; the woman had her orders, and there was no need to ratify them. She had been smart enough in her actions so far, so he trusted that she wouldn’t do anything foolish in the future, knowing he would have his eyes on her and every single one of her weekly reports.

Arwen liked to think that he would have let Gabriel go free if he asked. If his old friend laid everything on the table, if he explained everything and asked honestly … Arwen fantasised about it more than once in his idle moments in the last month. Of saying ‘yes’ with a smile, of retaining his friend even if he had to let him go for a bit … but a part of him wasn’t so sure. The truth was, he would never know whether he could have let Gabriel go, let one of the two people he truly trusted just run off when he had the entire Kingdom weighing on his shoulders. He didn’t know; he couldn’t know. Not unless he actually had to make the choice, a choice which Gabriel had taken out of his hands.

And if Arwen couldn’t be certain he would have been strong enough to say ‘yes’, then how could he be truly mad at Gabriel for not trusting him to say ‘yes’? Especially when Gabe had the lives of his loved ones possibly depending on that answer. His old friend sacrificed every relationship he’d built in the last five years for his family, and Arwen hated that he couldn’t find it in himself to be mad at him for it. It was unfair.

He had mentioned a pair of sisters he’d been torn away from, and a friend, and a mother. Once, when he was too drunk and sappy to be his usual brooding self. They were here all along, on Earth. The family he’d been torn away from, the family he couldn’t even be sure still existed.

Arwen’s eyes snapped open, and his gaze slid over to the papers spread out across his desk, reports of people of importance in the city of Graz. He pulled them closer, staring at the enchanted moving images of the four people one of his oldest friends left him for. A Pegasus-blooded woman, his mother, a half-elven girl, his elder sister, a dwarf, his best friend, and finally … the primary reason as to why he dismissed any thought of acting rashly, or with ill intent: the Halvyr. His little sister.

By the Stars, his moronic field agents almost assassinated a Halvyr descended from the Avatar of the Archon. Maybe if the girl was just a System-made Halvyr rejected by her primogenitor, then it would have been fine, but she had a Bonded Spirit. A child of Agreas, no less. That spoke of acceptance, making it evident that her proper integration into the Astral Court was just a matter of time.

And his Shadow Agent almost killed her. Worse, the Sprite knew. Which meant Agreas knew, and if Agreas knew, then so would the entirety of the Astral Court. What a stellar mess this agent had dropped in his lap, as if he didn’t have enough things to stress about.

If they succeeded, the only question would be whether the Astral Court or the Church of the Stars got to me first. I’d be a dead man walking … maybe every member of our government would be, perhaps even all our citizens, if any of the Old Monsters felt especially pissed.

Starhaven might have had a handful of Rank 5 people in its employ, but only two were actual combatants, and that didn’t mean much anyway. Rank 5 went from Level 200 all the way up to Level 500, and the difference between a Low Rank 5 and one nearing the true Level Cap could be night and day. Arwen’s two Rank 5s were below Level 250, and the Great Spirit King Anachreon was said to have been stuck at Level 500 long before the Eternal Empire was first established. Besides, he was the Avatar of the Archon. Arwen knew just enough about what any of those facts might mean that he understood that he could barely fathom just how ridiculously powerful that ancient being truly was. Anything his mind could come up with was likely massively underestimating him.

So Arwen sighed and discarded any thoughts of establishing a line of contact with his old friend, or getting ‘revenge’, as worthless as such a thing would have been. Now that he knew there was an honest-to-the-Stars Halvyr living in Graz, he would have to scrap all his plans in the region. Europe as a whole might have to be written off and ignored … even if it was proving to be the ‘softest’ of the Continents bordering Starhaven.

Afrika was an absolute mess, overrun with monsters and roaming Packs of Beastkin. He’d already lost dozens of agents in South America to accursed gangs — why the hell were there so many of them!? — and North America wasn’t much better with that lunatic Rank 3 Beastmaster defending it. How that cunt got his hands on a Dwarven Sunblaster, he would never know, but that weapon was almost as much of a menace as the madman’s extensive menagerie of overgrown lizards that roamed the south shores in search of would-be infiltrators. Antarctica was still a massive nope and in dire need of being wiped off the map and … that was it. Asia, Indonesia and Australia were possible, though they were further away, but perhaps he’d just have to suck it up and order the scouting missions to be expanded to those continents as well.

Maybe he would just focus on one. If he abandoned further scouting missions and focused solely on a single continent, or maybe even just a single region, then he could easily overwhelm the defenders. He had few agents, but he could mobilise the army. The majority of the enlisted were Rank 2s, with a few officers and veterans at Rank 3, while the younger soldiers started at Rank 1 after graduating from training.

He had some elite forces, much smaller in number, but they were all Rank 3s, with even a single full kill-squad of Rank 4s being at his disposal, but they were useless for so long as that accursed barrier kept them inside Starhaven. It was the lower-ranked, but well-trained, loyal soldiers that mattered now.

He could probably put together a regiment or two of purely Rank 1s, or he could wait another two months and send them out without the veteran Rank 3s.

Even that Beastmaster and its unending horde of Rank 3 pet lizards wouldn’t be insurmountable. It would be costly, but as Regent Lord Arwen could send the Titans into battle … or even authorise the creation of a new one beyond the Barrier. That way, even if he could only send Rank 2s through, they wouldn’t be horribly outmatched. Not that he would make that choice. There was no point in throwing bodies into the meatgrinder when there were continents much less protected. Africa, for example, seemingly had no returned local Rank 3 protector, or if there was, then Arwen’s agents managed to catch them before they crossed through the Barrier.

So do I go with South America, Africa or one of the more distant continents? Maybe Europe? The Dragon Vein runs through the entire continent, so I could just focus on parts away from this ‘Austria’ place.

He just had to make a decision. Yet another decision which could decide whether Starhaven eclipsed any previous golden age under his rule or was left ruined. No pressure. No pressure at all.

Arwen couldn’t help but curse Gabriel again as he slumped back and stared blankly at the ceiling. I really wish you were still here. Laurel’s horrible at listening, and I have no one else I could complain to.

Mia waited with bated breath, awaiting the harsh judgment that was sure to come. The last few days had been trying, but she’d done her best, spending hours long into the night practising just so this cruel judge before her might offer her a single word of praise-

“Passable,” Gabe said reluctantly, and Mia whooped in joy, punching the air with an exaggerated expression of pure glee plastered across her face. Camie and Lina, who’d been watching on as she presented her spellwork to Gabe, giggled while her brother merely rolled his eyes, letting out a huff of amusement. “Oh, sod off, it wasn’t that bad. Just a few days and we now know for sure that you’ll be safe from miasmic Light radiation. It’s better to prevent these sorts of things than go looking for a cure later on, especially when I doubt we’d find all the ingredients for a potion of the calibre we’d require to treat Light-induced super-cancer.”

Okay, maybe Mia’d exaggerated just how much effort — and blood, sweat and tears — the endeavour had required, just a teeny tiny bit. But in her defence, they could have been delving into that stupid Rift days ago had her brother not been such an overbearing worrywart, so he deserved a good amount of ribbing in return. It was a good thing he’d been panicking and overthinking stuff back when she insisted she come along to the meet and greet with Mark’s parents in ‘The Lair’, because Mia was certain he’d have kicked up an even bigger fuss about her reckless risk-taking otherwise.

“You said I was immune to it, since the arcane mana in my veins would negate the danger, and that the others would be safe as well because Rank 1 Light elemental Rifts usually have such weak radiation that it’s more likely that we get cancer while sunbathing on a random beach than while delving that Rift,” Mia said dryly. She would have made an effort to roll her eyes even harder than her brother, but they were likely to roll right out of their sockets if she did that. “But I can now cast a halfway decent Light Ward, so we are good to go, yes? Every other preparation has been completed days ago.”

Apparently, now that the time finally came for Mia and company to delve into yet another Rift — a little more than a week after their visit to that creepy Lair where Mark’s parents now lived — a place where Gabe couldn’t follow them, or protect them, he was … stressing himself out, to say it lightly. Mia understood it; it was hard not to sympathise with him, and he even made halfway reasonable points, but it was clear to everyone that those points were coming from a place of emotion and not logic. So she went along with it, though if he came up with yet another excuse to delay the delve, she was afraid her patience would finally run out after days of indulging him.

“Fine,” Gabe said like he’d just eaten a whole lemon. “Yes, you are as ready as can be. Let’s go get some lunch, fill your spatial bags and then I’ll escort you to the Rift. Sounds good?”

Mia nodded slowly, eyes narrowed in a way that showed she was still mildly suspicious. Gabe huffed, then rose to his feet. “Oh, come on. I’m not unreasonable … this was a lesson. Even if the Light Ward might not be crucial for this delve, I’ve just taught you how you should prepare for Rift delves properly in the future. You learn everything you can about it and prepare preventative measures against every source of danger you can imagine facing inside. It’s called being prepared.”

Mia almost believed him. Almost. But she didn’t say anything, because while she could tell those were justifications he came up with on the spot, that hardly meant there wasn’t a kernel of truth to them. Actually, they sounded perfectly reasonable, and she couldn’t really bring herself to be all that annoyed at Gabe. He was right; it was good to get into the habit of over-preparing for Rift delves while the danger was minimal and mistakes were much less likely to be lethal. Good habits took a long time to build, after all, so the best time to begin forming them was always years ago, with the second best being today.

Mia could already see the towering edifice made of glimmering crystal rise above the tree line in the distance, apparently something constructed by an Elite monster — the agreed-upon nomenclature for ‘Escaped Rift Guardians’ or monsters like them who were disproportionately powerful for their Level — that had been booted from the Rift to protect the Gate. Though it probably had a bunch of little helpers, because even if there were a dozen Marks running around, the horde of dwarves would still find it challenging to raise an edifice half as imposing as that tower. Mia seriously doubted it was the sole work of a single creature.

“ … and as I said, since this Rift is hardly a threat to anyone out here in the boonies, this is not a ‘do or die’ situation,” Gabe said, continuing on with his final speech as he carried the delving party towards the aptly dubbed ‘Crystal Tower Rift’. “You might find yourself slipping back into that mindset, considering all your prior delves were like that, but here retreat is a perfectly acceptable and strategic option. Any monsters you kill before retreating will deplete the Rift’s stores of mana just a bit, hardly as much as a full Clear, but it’s still not to be dismissed. If the Guardian proves to be too much to handle, Rifts can be kept stable just by slaughtering all lesser monsters every so often. Also, many of you are at the cusp of another Level Up, so retreating to consolidate your gains before trying again with much better odds than before is also a perfectly sound strategy. Especially since many of you have that Golden Apple you’re going to eat after the next Level Up, and as I said, such immense Attribute gains may have the unfortunate side-effect of knocking you unconscious for a few hours. I don’t think I need to spell out just how quickly half of your party falling unconscious could result in all of you dying horribly. If you do Level Up, leave the Rift before eating those Golden Apples. The Rift isn’t going anywhere.”

As for why he was talking about clearing the Rift instead of destroying it? Well, the inhabitants of this Rift were some weird crystalline creatures that used the same crystal their bodies were made of to make their buildings. These crystals, while not proper mana crystals or monster cores, weren’t that far off, but more importantly, they were all infused with only Light mana without any additives or other elements. When ground down, these crystals apparently allowed alchemists to brew health potions of a considerably higher purity than before, as well as a higher potency version of them dubbed ‘potions of regeneration’, which could grow back soft tissue and even partially destroyed organs.

So destroying the source of these crystals was out of the question, but letting the Rift grow too powerful to properly ‘farm’ was also something that couldn’t be allowed to happen; luckily though, farming the Rift and Clearing it every so often was exactly what they had to do to keep it from growing any further. It sat at a comfortable Level 16 at the moment, so it was still manageable, but the Mayor wanted it down to an 11 if possible, and kept below Rank 2 by any means necessary. If that failed and it looked like the Rift was about to Rank Up, it was to be destroyed despite how much of an absolute metaphorical goldmine it was for the alchemists of Graz.

Mia shielded her eyes from the sun, trying to make out the details of the still-distant crystalline tower. She and the rest of her delving party were sitting in something vaguely reminiscent of a rollercoaster cart Gabe had made entirely out of solid Arcane mana. It even had those over-the-shoulder restraints the more exciting rollercoaster rides used to secure their passengers and keep them from falling out of their seats.

Gabe only had the Order aspect, his own Arcane Affinity leaning just a tiny bit towards negative energy, just enough to downgrade it to Major Arcane Affinity and prevent him from ever using Chaos-aspected arcane magic. It showed too, though it was hardly entirely negative. His focus was narrower than Mia’s, and he’d spent five years refining it, so he could mould arcane mana like a master potter might work clay. This cart, for example, was created using nothing but his Arcane Mana Manipulation Skill.

The crystalline tower looked tiny, but the fact that even the tallest trees surrounding it barely reached a third of its height meant it had to be positively enormous from up close. Which was rather strange, because the structure itself looked shoddy at best, something which became even more obvious as the details became clear with the growing proximity.

It looked like some titanic child’s attempt at making an old drum-tower from a pile of rocks and wet sand. It was misshapen, rather ugly, and made everyone looking at it mildly uncomfortable in the same way any buildings lingering at the very edge of full structural collapse did. Alas, it stood for now, and perhaps it would remain standing for a long while if Gabe was right and there really were enchantments woven into the structure to prevent its seemingly imminent collapse.

Gabe finally ran out of things to say, having reiterated all of Zeigler’s points while adding some of his own. This was a proper, sanctioned operation after all, and the Mayor insisted on ‘doing it right’, to set a good precedent and all that. All the previous Rift delves had been rather gung-ho, with no one really knowing what they were doing, winging the entire thing with bare minimum planning. Considering they’d just barely skirted past disaster more than a few times, Mia couldn’t really bring herself to object even when the whole thing started feeling much too bureaucratic for her liking.

So yeah. They’d heard all the warnings before, been given a hastily compiled brochure that was the very first iteration of the ‘Delving Rulebook’, and had already done all the preparations described within. After all, to do things 'by the book', there needed to be a book to follow in the first place. The Rift’s surroundings had been scouted, the crystalline monsters spat out by the Rift studied for weaknesses, and a scouting team had even poked their heads into the Rift so the actual delving party would know what to expect on the other side. Using all that, they even had the beginnings of some strategies they could build upon once they were inside and knew more. Mia even had that stupid Light Ward which would protect everyone from the harmful effects of being exposed to thick ambient broken Light mana, while also granting a minor resistance to all attacks powered by Light mana.

They were ready, more than ready. The only problem would have been the lack of a proper frontline ‘tank’ who would stand as the vanguard of their formation. Mark was staying behind, and Brent was not really vanguard-material even if he could act as a halfway decent one if he really had to. The same went for Camie.

So they picked up a new party member at the Mayor’s insistence. Mia’s eyes flickered over to the person in question, finding the brown-haired girl looking bored out of her mind, staring longingly at the rapidly approaching crystal tower. At first glance, she looked like a random college student on her way to a ren faire dressed up as some primitive tribal warrior woman.

The sight might have been amusing, considering Anna — the girl in question — was somehow even shorter than Mia and had those big doe eyes that made her look utterly harmless. Mia was sure more than a handful of monsters or opportunistic assholes found it out the hard way that just because someone was tiny and a girl besides, they might still be fully capable of messing them up. Looks were no longer the premier way of determining whether you can beat someone in a fight, and the minuscule girl that would have easily gotten lost in any crowd more than proved it with her ability to punt men five times her bodyweight through a wall. If it was a drywall, that is. She wasn't quite at the level where she could put people through brick walls, thankfully.

See, Anna was a Bear Shifter, someone who could swap back and forth between Human, Anthropomorphic and Beast Forms at the drop of a hat. Mia still wasn’t quite sure she was comfortable trusting a Beastkin who’d failed to resist the Werewolf King’s Call, but whatever, it wasn’t like her life would be in all that much danger in a Level 16 Rift even if Anna messed up, intentionally or otherwise.

They had met just a few days ago and spent a few hours sparring every day, both against each other and against monsters as a team under Gabe's careful guidance and watchful eyes. It was just barely enough for some semblance of trust to form, though Mia still doubted she would have risked relying on the girl in a place as dangerous as a Rift had Konstantin not vouched for her.

We will just have to be careful. We’ve beaten a damned Raid that was four levels higher than this one. This will be easy if we don’t mess up and play things safe.
