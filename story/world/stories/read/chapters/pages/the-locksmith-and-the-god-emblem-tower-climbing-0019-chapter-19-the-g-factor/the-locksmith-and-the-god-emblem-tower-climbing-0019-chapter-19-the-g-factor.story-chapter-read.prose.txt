They were now both walking under the darkness, each with a candle provided by Vela. Goldman thought about using his Emblem to reach one of the cabins where Dracno had been imprisoned, but he feared for the young thief. Harry kept looking back constantly, and the moment he realized they were alone, he cleared his throat and said without much ceremony.

"Do you think I'm annoying?"

“Do I think you're... annoying?” Goldman repeated. "Where's that coming from?"

"I, um... I don't know." Harry suddenly felt pressured. "It's just... I tried to talk with Daniel earlier, and although we left on good terms, it always felt like I was walking on egg shells."

"Oh, do not worry about that, lad!" The pirate replied with a smile. "Daniel is like an island; hard on the outside but... also hard on the inside. Okay. That wasn't my best analogy, but you get my point."

"Are you trying to say that, like an island, Daniel keeps to himself, not even noticing the ships and folks around him?"

"See! You even complete my analogies. You truly are an indispensable asset to the crew, Harry."

And although the Locksmith sensed a bit of sarcasm coming from Goldman, he inadvertently gave into a warm smile. Being indispensable felt good. Although now that the young thief thought about it, maybe that's how the pirate actually saw him; Goldman wasn't the sarcastic type.

"Why do you call us your crew?" Harry asked.

"I..." Goldman paused for a second. "As you may know, I have always lived relishing the sea breeze. The ocean is to me what the forest is to the deer; I couldn't live without it. The world may be cruel, though, and soon I was pegged as thief and a liar, forever meant to be dragged around by the waves. What kept me together was my crew. They were my anchor, you know?"

"Anchor?" Harry avoided his gaze as he chuckled. "I don't think I've ever had an anchor per se."

"Of course you do. Anchors keeps our morale in check. It can be both a friend and a rival, always leading us to improve ourselves. Maybe you just hadn't figured it out who or what it is, aye?"

"Right..." The Locksmith laughed awkwardly. "Is Daniel your anchor? I imagine you guys met here on the Tower."

"Oh, couldn't be further from the truth, lad." Goldman looked back, his eyes reflecting the giant flame that came from the ground, as if the whole floor was a candle. "We met on our way to the Logos Tower. Back in the old days, our navigator had drowned in a terrible accident. We had at most two weeks left of food but stranded in the middle of the ocean. I, as naive as one could be, created an Emblem with the Compass Symbol; I thought it would show me the directions to safety. Apparently that power is already in someone else's hands, for I gained the power to... well, do what I do best. This, however, led us to being found. I met Daniel when we were in prison, waiting for our sentence."

"Funny how Emblems can somehow fuck you over, right?" And with a nod from Goldman, Harry changed his tone as he looked to the fake stars in the sky. "Do you, um... do you wish you were dead? In your sentence, I mean. Do you think that being thrown into the Tower was the better decision?"

Goldman took the time to answer the Locksmith's question, for it wasn't an easy one. Despite being infected with the disease of optimism, Goldman seemed immune to stupidity.

"You are the universe experiencing yourself." He gave a warm smile.

Harry listened quietly.

"Cherry, my love, told that to me. She was a poet, you see; always dreaming of words. I don't think I can even understand what it could mean, but I'm sure she could. I should've asked her what it meant, what any of this meant." He spread his arms. "But I can't. I can only dream of seeing the world how she saw it. This is both my prison and my blessing. I hope one day I can. So no, Locksmith; I'm glad I get to live another day, another loop, or whatever. What about you? What keeps you going?"

Harry was lost for words. What started as an innocent and insecure questioning soon became a testament to Goldman's virtues as man and as a human. The young thief couldn't begin to imagine the strength needed to equate Goldman's will. In this melancholic sea, Harry remembered his purpose.

As if the world was hearing his thoughts, they soon arrived at the cabin with Dracno inside. "We arrived! Now please, Locksmith, are you sure that you don't want me to come along? What if he tries something?"

"I..." Harry then shook his head and said with a determined expression. "I can deal with it, don't you worry. If I need anything, I'll just knock or scream. I won't take long, okay?"

He waved and went inside the cabin. Amid the penumbra, it was possible to see in the middle a candle on top of a desk. Without the beds, the place was so empty that even Harry’s thoughts seemed louder. The silence of the night was translated to the solitary cabin, now without the ambient sounds of falling leaves or crickets in the bushes; only the sound of wood twisting under the weight of the man in the center.

Dracno, beside the candle, his only source of light, sat on a chair that twisted to bear his size. He was bound by several chains and ropes, making Harry wonder if the man could breathe under all that pressure. All of this was meek compared to the lack of presence he once had. The helm, still preserving his face, was somehow even more lifeless than before.

Without a word, Harry slowly approached the man with the intent of discovering his state. As the group had said, Dracno was either dead or in a catatonic state; he only stared at the candle.

Harry cast a glance at the dancing and constant flame, almost begging for attention. He then took a step forward, scared to death that the man could attack him; even a grip could break his neck.

Although the young thief was scared that Dracno could chase them through future floors, something didn't tell him he would, if he were really alive. The body heals, but not the mind...

“…Which floor did you reach?” Harry said, his eyes narrowed. “You said you've been to the top. What did you see up there?”

Maintaining his inertia, not even the man’s finger moved. It was as if he wasn’t even there. Harry swallowed his questions and tried again, now a more direct approach.

“Dracno. That’s your name, isn’t it?” The man remained still. “I know I hurt you pretty badly, I mean, look at my hand! Don’t think you were the only one who lost something in that punch.”

He felt bad for comparing his amputated hand with the lobotomized state of the giant in front of him. Now that Dracno was calmer, Harry felt a strange sense of ease. Even among his moments of madness, it was almost as if some part of him resurfaced amidst his ravings. He stared with serious eyes and spoke like a human being. Harry couldn’t help but think that he wasn’t always like this.

Seeing few results, he decided to move away from the man and the flame. Wait. Harry remembered the emphasis one of the cultists had put when they mentioned the name Locksmith.

"Locksmith." Harry said. "One of your followers reacted when the name surfaced. What does that mean? Does it have to do with any future floors?"

Like an insect, the man then turned his head toward Harry. Frightened, Harry took a step back as he almost fell. Thump! Thump! But after a few seconds, he gathered his courage and approached the giant.

“Separate and join. That’s why you captured Goldman and Daniel and put them in that cell. It doesn’t make sense to me but you said it was to separate and join, right? What is that? Why separate and join? What does that have to do with the floors above—”

At the same moment, Dracno used his Emblem and a log came from the ground. A small one, minuscule compared to those he used earlier in the fight. What does this mean? Dracno then grunted something and fell silent again.

“…Do you want to say something?” Harry guessed. “If it’s true, use your Emblem once, if it’s a lie, use it twice. Your name is Dracno?”

The former warlord, after a few moments, created a log from the ground that promptly gave into the earth once again. Okay, it works.

“You imprisoned Goldman, Daniel, and Vela on the same day?” He wanted to test how far he remembered.

In response, he conjured a log once, and after a few seconds did it again. It was a lie. Okay, he remembers. However, I don't think this will be very useful if I don't know the right questions.

“We’re going to do it another way now." Harry approached the man. "I’ll say various letters and you tell me when to stop.”

The golem conjured his log once. Harry didn’t have enough information to ask questions about future floors, and answers like true or false only made sense if he needed confirmation, not knowledge.

“A, B, C…” And then when he said the letter G, Conrad created his stone armor.

Harry continued, and this time stopped at the letter O. He looked around the cabin, feeling almost as if he was being watched. Suddenly, Dracno got up from his chair, that creaked desperately. Harry, in response, fell backwards, ignoring the pain in his leg as he saw the giant man almost hit his head on the ceiling.

“Locksmith?” Goldman shouted through the door. “Are you almost done?”

And Harry only replied with a generic shout. He was too scared to properly invent an excuse.

"The Lock is God." Dracno said as he loomed over a wounded Harry.

"The God Emblem?" Harry asked, his voice trembling as if someone had replaced his vocal cords with cheap nylon ones.

"Two in the same."

Goldman then broke down the door, running to Harry who was on the ground.

“What happened, lad!?” He asked while Dracno was already sitting and watching the dancing flame of the lonely candle.

Startled, Goldman helped him to his feet. Harry stared fixedly at Dracno and then turned to Goldman. He said nothing, picked up his crutch from the ground and signaled to the man that he wanted to leave. The mask on top of the church, as Harry got up, blinked unbeknownst to the young thief.

“I thought he had said something.” He spoke expressionlessly. “But it was nothing. I’m sorry for having scared you… It wasn’t my intention.”

And at the end of his sentence he seemed to have regained a bit of color in his face. A part of him knew that it was just a madman babbling, the words of a lunatic, insane. Another wondered if it was true. What could it all mean?

Harry then remembered the incident where he first tried to use an Emblem. How could he forget the day when he tried to use the Lock Emblem?
