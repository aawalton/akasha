I don’t know how long I ran for. I didn’t even keep track of the corners I blindly sprinted around, but I kept going for as long as I could. Eventually, the pain burning in my side forced me to slow, and I peered over my shoulder and up at the screen before looking down at myself. I had to check that I hadn’t somehow been shot without noticing.

The stitch was so bad that I half-expected to see blood soaking into my white jumpsuit, but it was unstained. There wasn’t even any mud along the bottom of my trouser legs, and I had no idea how they could possibly still be so clean. I remembered seeing them trailing in the mud, but it was spotless.

Pushing that confusion aside, I pressed my empty hand against my chest and tried to get my breathing under control. Each ragged gasp hurt. It sounded harsh and tore at my lungs, but I forced myself to inhale as slowly and evenly as I could before looking around again.

I had been too distracted by running before. I’d barely been able to actually examine the maze around me until that moment, so I wasn’t sure how far I’d gone or if I was in a new section. It felt like I’d travelled halfway across it at least, but for all I knew, I could have just been going in circles.

Very little had changed about my surroundings, I realised. The walls had reverted back to the usual light stone, and the ground was nothing but dry dirt. I was grateful for that, though. It was so much easier to run on the solid, compacted ground than it was on mud. That had been almost impossible.

But I still hadn’t seen whoever it was that had cast the spell. Even when I was running, I didn’t spot them, and that worried me. I wasn’t foolish enough to believe it was because I’d actually managed to evade them. They were better than that, so it was more likely that they were just tailing me from a distance.

With a pained sigh, I forced myself to continue walking. Every muscle in my body objected. They screamed at me to stop and collapse onto the ground, just lie there for a while, but I didn’t have time for that, and it would be stupid. If I stopped, they would catch up with me or do something to make me run again. Watching someone taking a break wasn’t very entertaining. No one would want to see that.

But then endlessly watching someone run wasn’t that interesting either, so they must have had some other goal, a small voice in the back of my head whispered. They wouldn’t just force me to flee through the maze until the timer ran out, would they?

Perhaps they were trying to herd me towards a trap. Or maybe there was a dead end somewhere ahead that they wanted me to reach. Then, they could corner me. I’d be stuck, forced to face them, and the mere thought of that sent a wave of terror through me. It was so strong that it almost pushed me out of the world. I could feel my grip loosening, the dizziness returning, and I had to fight not to drift away.

If that was their plan, I thought, I didn’t know what to do. I couldn’t recall who was chasing me. My memories provided nothing more than shadowy silhouettes, but they struck fear into my heart. Somehow, despite knowing nothing about them, I was certain they were stronger and better trained than I was.

It was more than that, though. It wasn’t just that they were better trained, I corrected myself as I broke into a run, the pain in my side immediately returning. It was that they were trained, and I was not. I’d just been thrust into that place, and somewhere along the way, I’d picked up a weapon. I couldn’t remember when or where I’d found it, and I didn’t know how to use a staff.

But some version of me had used one before. I’d been trained with it, I recalled as a slight smile pulled at my lips and was inevitably picked up by the unseen camera floating overhead. The weaponry tutor at the Academy, whose name had escaped me, had instructed me on how to use one.

We hadn’t had many classes, and I wasn’t sure how much of that would translate over to the new world I was in, but I hoped it would help if I came face to face with someone. Otherwise, I’d just need to point the thing at them and hope for the best, but I didn’t know how well that would go. I didn’t know any spells, obviously. That knowledge was kept to the chosen, the worthy, but if I survived until the end of my timer, I would join their ranks. I would have proven myself, and nothing would be hidden from me.

I stumbled, almost falling, as my mind caught up with what I’d been thinking, and shock shot through me. Spells. I had just thought that I didn’t know any spells, I realised as I glanced down at the unassuming wooden pole now clutched gingerly in my hands.

My steps slowed again, and I couldn’t rip my eyes away from the staff. What did I mean by that, though, I continued to spiral. If it was exactly what it sounded like, then that meant the weapon I’d been holding pretty much since I entered the world was… magic.

It didn’t look like a magic wand, though. It was too long, but it wasn’t big enough to be a proper staff, like the one I’d seen in fantasy movies. It was just… somewhere in the middle, just a little longer than my forearm and about as thick, and that didn’t seem right.

But it had been warm when I’d picked it up before, and it shouldn’t have been. There was a heat coming from it, as if power or life pulsed in the centre of the stick, so perhaps that explained why, but could I even use it? I wasn’t a magician. I was certain of that, so maybe if I pointed the magical staff at someone, nothing would happen. They’d just laugh at me and then kill me, and that would be such an embarrassing way to go. Not that it would matter. I’d be dead.

I needed to test it. I had to check whether I could use magic whilst I was still relatively safe, before they caught up with me. Then, if it didn’t work, I’d know not to even try it when it mattered. I could just focus on fleeing or fighting them normally, but if I could…

Excitement and fear rose within me, and I couldn’t finish that thought. My mind ground to a halt, and my thoughts stopped suddenly. I didn’t want to let myself hope, to believe that I might be capable of that, but I had to try.

Coming to a stop, I lifted the staff before me, mimicking the stance I’d seen other magicians use in movies before, but then I froze. I didn’t know what to do next. Obviously, I’d never been taught how to use magic before, and I wasn’t sure I’d even seen anyone do it. I didn’t know where to start, and I racked my brain, thinking back to my other worlds, but I couldn’t remember wielding it anywhere else either.

I just had to try something, though. If I didn’t try, then there was no chance that anything would happen. But, if I did, perhaps I’d get lucky.

“F-fireball!” I muttered as loudly as I dared, choosing the first and most obvious spell I could think of.

A gasp was ripped from my mouth as an almost electric shock jolted down my arms, singeing my nerves. I barely managed to cling to the staff as it lurched violently, nearly tearing itself from my grasp, and a splutter of flames and sparks shot from the tip before dying out.

Shock slammed into me, and for a few seconds, I could do nothing more than stare at the faint soot stain on the floor. As soon as I recovered, I poked it with my foot to check that it was real, and the black mark smeared across the dry dirt.

My brain felt like it was short-circuiting, and I wasn’t sure whether it was just the lingering stinging sensation in my arm or the shock that was causing it, but I couldn’t stop myself from lifting the staff to my face. I peered at the flat tip, trying to work out whether there was any opening or anything where the fire could have come out of, but there was nothing.

Quickly, I jerked the staff back as I realised how stupid I was being. That thing was just spewing flames, and yet I decided to hold it up to my eyes and stare directly at it. If it had decided to spit out fire again, I could have died, and it would not have been a pleasant or painless death.

But it didn’t do that. It had only reacted when I told it to. I was the one who caused it. I’d used magic, and that knowledge sent a spike of awe through me. Despite my fear of the people hunting me down, a delighted giggle almost escaped my lips.

I had just wielded magic.

It was something that so few people, both in my world and any other, got to do, and I knew it was only temporary. Somehow, I was aware that I had only been granted that ability because of the staff, and I had to be holding it to cast any spells, but that didn’t matter. I wasn’t sure I’d ever let go of it again.

Excitement tingled within me, and I grinned down at the staff. My arm still hurt. Pain continued to shoot up and down my bicep, but I didn’t care. I needed to try another spell, and any amount of pain was worth being able to actually shout something out and have the magic respond.

“Water… gun!” I said, starting strong but quickly losing my confidence.

I was pretty sure that wasn’t an actual spell, but a move from a video game in my reality, and I worried that it would not translate well. The world I was in might not have any guns. They felt unnecessary if people could use magic, so my spell might not have made any sense, and I opened my mouth, intending to say something else. I wanted to try a different spell, but pain had already begun travelling down my arm like a tidal wave crashing into a cliff.

A spurt of water, hesitant and weak but definitely real, flowed out of the end of the staff, and I had to bite my lip to hold back more giddy laughter. I couldn’t stop myself from lifting it to my mouth and taking a gulp of the clear, sparkling water. It was so pure that it tasted of nothing, and I hadn’t realised how parched I was until that moment, but I hadn’t drunk anything since I’d entered the maze. I vaguely remembered finding a fountain that had been built into the wall at some point, but I’d ignored it. There was no way to check whether the water had been tainted, so that felt like the safest thing to do, but there was nothing wrong with my water. I had just conjured it, and I was certain that it was perfect.

Pulling the staff away from my face, I caught sight of myself on the screen above the maze. I was beaming, and I lifted the still-spluttering staff aloft in celebration of what I had done as a realisation washed over me. If I had done that, if I could use magic, maybe I truly did stand a chance at surviving until the end of the timer.

But too soon, my hope began to fade, and I found myself thinking that if I could do that without any training, surely the others would be able to do the same. They’d be able to do much, much more, and that prompted me to begin jogging once more.

There was no way that I’d be able to win in a fight against them, both with or without magic. They were physically stronger than me, and they knew actual spells, too. I didn’t, and I had no idea why they even let me have the staff in the first place. I doubted it would be much use if I were to get caught, but was that the point?

Perhaps whoever had orchestrated the whole thing was aware of that too. Maybe they had just scattered the weapons throughout the maze to make things more fun. The spectators, the ones watching the giant screen, might have enjoyed seeing someone like me attempt magic. It could make the final fight to the death even more entertaining, even though my defeat was inevitable.

Plus, it would give the others hope. They’d see someone who came from the slums like them wielding magic, and they’d believe that they could do the same one day. Perhaps they’d watch my death and think that if they were in my situation, they’d do things differently. They’d make different decisions and be able to prove themselves as worthy, even if it was impossible. It was a false hope, an illusion, but that mattered. Hope was powerful. It kept people from questioning the unfairness of their situation because maybe one day, it would get better.

The sound of a pebble hitting stone came from somewhere in front of me, shattering my thoughts, and I bolted. Spinning around, I sprinted in the opposite direction, running along the corridor I’d just come down, but it didn’t matter that I was backtracking. I just needed to get away from them.

Footsteps chased me as I barrelled through the maze, and I had no idea whether the person following me was someone who came to the maze with me or one of the people hunting me, but I wasn’t willing to slow down for long enough to check. I didn’t even want to risk looking away from the path in front of me to glance up at the screen, and I prayed to whoever would listen that I was not being led back towards the overgrown passageway I’d barely escaped before.

Luckily, I turned corner after corner without seeing any hint of green, until suddenly, I passed a turning that took me by surprise. My brain didn’t have enough time to process it until I had already moved beyond it, and I barely hesitated before making my decision.

Stones and dust scattered around me as I came to a stop, my momentum propelling me onwards for a moment, before I threw myself back towards the junction that had stolen my attention. My gaze flitted towards the path ahead of me, spotting the people chasing me in the distance, but it was impossible to make them out with the sweat dripping into my eyes. They just looked like hulking shadows, but I forced myself to ignore them as I threw myself around the corner.

My eyes turned wide as I staggered to a halt and looked around, trying to understand what I was seeing. The small clearing that surrounded me was nothing more than a square patch, only a couple of metres across. White and pale blue wildflowers poked up amongst the grass, but it quickly gave way to nothing. Beyond it, there was only sky.

I had reached the edge of the maze. I had found one of the exits, and the sight had pushed all other thoughts, all other worries, from my mind. All I could do was stare down at the city beneath me. My city.

The maze must have been floating, I realised, but the thought was distant. It was eclipsed by wonder as my eyes scanned the tall spiked towers that rose up from the buildings below. My heart pounded in my ears as my gaze roamed the buildings, the universities and labs, and longing filled me, but I was too high up to make out the people wandering below. They were nothing more than tiny specks of colour.

But I could see the flowerbeds in the courtyards and the ones that lined the wide, open streets. The colours were so bright that they were visible, even from up there, and my gaze lingered on them for a moment before venturing further out, seeking out my home. I wasn’t sure what it would look like from above. I’d seen it from the rooftops, but that was different.

My heart sank as I took in the fabric that covered some of the narrow streets at the edge of the city, quickly realising that it all looked the same. The fabric that had been strung up to shield the people living below from the harsh sun turned pale quickly, and that made it hard to work out which part of the slums I was looking at. Even the buildings all looked similar. I couldn’t even find the big factory my sister worked at.

Panic began to rise within me again as the footsteps behind me grew closer. I was trapped, I had no escape, and I still couldn’t see my home. My search grew more desperate, and I knew there should have been something I could see. My sister had hung something. A scrap of dark fabric, as big as she could afford, should be swinging from the top of the building we lived in. She said she wanted me to be able to see home from the towers of the university, so I could come home and visit her one day.

She was entirely convinced that I’d make it there, and I’d tried to tell her not to, to save her money, but as my eyes found the tiny triangle of already-fading purple material, I couldn’t help but be glad she’d done it. I might not have proven myself, but at least I got to see my home one last time.

“Oh, that’s a shame,” a voice said. “She stopped running.”

The footsteps chasing me came to a stop, but strangely, I wasn’t scared. Instead, I just felt at peace as I continued to stare at the sign of my sister’s love.

“Uh oh. It’s a dead end!” another person added. “Looks like you’re trapped!”

Cruel, jeering laughter rang out behind me, but I didn’t react. I wasn’t sure how many people were there; I had yet to turn towards them, and I could see the screen drifting closer, but I didn’t want to look at it. I’d have to take my eyes away from the flag to do that, and I couldn’t.

“You should have taken a different turn,” the first guy continued, his tone almost sympathetic. “You know, if you’d got right instead of left at the end there, you might have been able to get away from us.”

The second guy laughed again.

“If you got lucky,” he said, the smirk audible in his voice.

“But hey, we’ve still got a while left until this whole thing is done,” the first guy started. “Maybe if you ask us really nicely, we’ll let you go again. Give you a head start before we start chasing you. What do you think?”

I remained silent. I knew his words were a trap anyway. He had no intention of letting me go. He just wanted to see me beg, and I refused to give that to him. My last moments would not be on anyone else’s terms. I would not be forced to plead for my life with anyone. I begged that my sister would not be watching, but if she was, I couldn’t let her witness that.

“Pff, we’ll see,” one of the boys snorted.

“Alec, what the hell, man. Can you stop?” the first boy snapped.

A slight shuffle came from behind, and the temptation to glance at the screen now floating in line with the maze, pulled at me faintly.

“What?” the second boy said, a slight whine entering his tone. “She’s not even listening to you. Do you know if she can even understand what you’re saying? These people are so stupid and uneducated. I bet she doesn’t even know English!”

“She does,” the other guy replied firmly before a couple of footsteps came from behind me. “Hey, I’m talking to you, Grace.”

That part was directed at me, and I couldn’t help but let my eyes snap to the screen. I saw the man closing the gap between us, his hand reaching out towards me, and I wasn’t sure what he was going to do, but it didn’t matter. I let my arms rise slightly and stepped forward.
