Barry woke up with the crystal humming right at his face, its irritating vibration waking him up. He knew his morning peace was no more. Maybe it was his blood pressure, or the lame people who gave themselves the title of gods and still could not do what he said.

Which was to leave him the fuck alone. The prayer crystal was still humming, which was not a good indication at all. It was not loud enough to kill anyone yet, but loud enough to keep him from ignoring it, which was even worse for Barry. He sat up on the stone bench, rubbed his face, and looked at the three people still in the hall.

Barry saw Thor, who was sitting in the same corner he had pointed him to the night before. He had not moved much. He looked utterly lost, scratching his back with his hammer. Barry would ask what happened to the fellow, but with the mood he was in now, he couldn't even bother.

The tall frost giantess, who still did not have a name, sat near the water barrels, already awake. He knew why. His goggles still indicated that she was suppressing her magic, her divinity.

Thud!

Bela entered, cautiously as always. She must have come back from her usual meetings. The only useful individual in the room.

Barry pointed at Thor.

"You. Stay there. Do not go out and inspire anyone. Do not flex your muscles. Do not look heroic. You are a battery today. Your job is to leak all the faith at a steady rate so this crystal does not eat itself again."

Thor's jaw tightened. He nodded once. "I understand, but Barry, like I told you, we can leave any moment."

Barry did not say anything. Barry then looked at the giantess.

"You..."

"Yes, me," she replied, standing ready, looking down on Barry, not in an insulting sense but literally, as she was three times taller.

Barry's goggles already scanned everything that needed to be scanned about her: the many bindings of magic around her body, charms, enchantments. She was even using curses to her advantage. Clever. She could be useful if she listened.

"Keep making more crystals," he simply said.

She inclined her head. "As you say."

Bela stepped closer. "The people are already gathering outside. They saw the light last night. They think the crystal is angry. They think the gods are angry."

Barry stood and stretched until his back cracked.

"Ha! Tell them the crystal is fine. Tell them the human is fine. Tell them if they keep staring I am going to start charging rent. Go."

Bela left without arguing.

Barry put on his gloves, made from wyvern's hide and crystals, and placed both hands on the crystal. The feedback crawled up his gloves and from there to his goggles. He muttered under his breath the whole time.

"Stupid tree. Stupid faith economy. Stupid gods who can't sit still for five minutes without breaking the furniture."

Thor watched him work. He wanted to ask if the load was still dangerous. He did not. Barry had already told him once. Asking again would only get him another insult. So he did what he does best: staying quiet and trying to keep his presence small.

But it was still better than going to Asgard and getting yelled at by his father. Better than searching for his sister, who seemed to have run away again.

He checked his wrist. The blue tattoo blinked. A call from his mother. Which he ignored.

The giantess did her job, creating more crystals, mashing her divinity so hard it formed the faith crystals, which she only knew how to make after Barry told her.

She could not help but think of the possibilities if he were her devotee, following her orders. The whole universe and all of its realms would tremble before her if that ever happened.

She moved around, carrying the crystals and placing them near him, and walked around like she belonged there. Every time she passed near Thor, she felt the same private amusement. He still had no idea.

By mid-morning the hall filled with ordinary noise.

Giants brought cracked tools. Children stood in the doorway and stared. One old male put a piece of dried meat on the floor near Barry's feet as an offering. Barry kicked it back without looking up.

"No. Keep your food, dammit. When did I become a shrine?"

The old giant looked confused while Bela translated. The giant took the meat and left, muttering something that Barry heard and ignored at the same time.

Thor shifted in his corner. He too heard what was said, and he did not like it, not one bit.

"They only know one way to show respect," Thor said.

Barry did not turn.

"Then they can learn a second way. Respect is not putting dried meat on my floor."

The giantess set down another bucket and spoke in the local tongue with Bela. She understood that to grow close, she needed to do more than cause chaos, so she focused on helping create crystals and assisting where needed. The two of them now sorted a line of milder cases: coughs, joint pain, children with low fever. She worked without complaint, but inside she was mapping everything: how Barry answered questions, how Thor stayed silent when insulted, how Bela kept glancing between them like she was waiting for a fight that never came.

A child giant who was already the same height as Barry peeked from the edge of the door.

"W-why do you not accept our worship?"

Barry answered while recalibrating a secondary node.

"Because worship is a feedback loop that turns people into batteries and gods into parasites. I am already stuck cleaning up one of those systems. I am not starting another."

The child heard it, somewhat comprehending it, and looked back at Bela. Bela translated it into a PG-13 short version. The child still simply smiled.

"I see. Human is not hungry..."

Thor looked at the floor, shaking his head.

Around midday they ate.

Cloned meat and thin broth. Same for everyone. Barry passed the bowl, and Thor accepted it without comment, eating slower and slower. He had stopped trying to make conversation after the third attempt got shut down.

In Asgard silence was rare. Here it was constant. A constant Barry liked, but Thor kept glancing at Barry's hands while the man ate. The knuckles were still scarred from earlier work. Thor wanted to say something about it. He did not.

The giantess sat across from them and ate slowly. She asked Bela ordinary questions about the day's schedule. Barry could not help but look at Thor, maintaining his silence.

"What is really up with you? I said do not speak with me, not to anyone else."

Thor still kept his silence, biting off the meat when the same child came again, his nose sniffing at the meat with obvious drool at the edge of his mouth.

Thor showed his meat, and the child nodded aggressively, coming closer. He smiled, giving a bowl and a hefty leg piece to the child. The child bit into it, taking a large chunk in an instant.

He could not help but smile further. In all his glorious moments, pillaging his enemies, saving billions of lives, this one moment gave him more joy.

He patted the boy's head. "Good boy, now head back to your grandfather. It is already late."

The child nodded, taking the meat with him.

And after some minutes, the crystal spiked again, humming vigorously once more.

Barry was on his feet again, leaving his food and checking what happened. His calculations were right. He knew it.

He sighed, knowing the reason. All the hard work for the day was melting away before his eyes.

"One kid. One damn kid and the whole faith resonance wants to throw a tantrum."

Thor stood up as well, knowing fully well whose fault it was.

"Barry, I am—"

"Argh! I hate this place. I hate the tree. I hate every idiot who decided prayer was a valid power source."

Thor's face stayed flat, but something in his chest tightened. He had caused the last overload. He knew it. Sitting still while Barry fixed it felt like the only useful thing he could do.

Thor looked down, swinging his hammer to fly away.

"Thor!" Barry called.

He turned. "What? I am leaving like you asked."

"You shit fuck, who told you to leave? If you leave, whose going to be my battery? Come here and help me."

Thor nodded, a smile appearing at the edge of his lips. "Of course. I already know you cannot accomplish anything without me."

"Yeah, yeah, save those words for next time you mess things up."

Loki watched them, seeing the trust Barry had with Thor, and she did not like it. She did not like it at all.
