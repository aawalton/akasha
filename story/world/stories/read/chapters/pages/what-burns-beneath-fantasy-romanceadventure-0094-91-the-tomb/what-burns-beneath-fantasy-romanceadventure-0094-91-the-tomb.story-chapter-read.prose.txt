There was a human hand staked to a door.

I saw it immediately and smelled it a moment later, but the reality still took a while to sink in.

First, I had to be sure the structure before us was, in fact, a door. Doors typically led to buildings, but we stood on the edge of a pine copse and before a massive hill. Sunset bathed the rustling trees and swaying grass in somber blues and purples. Crickets and owls sang quietly all around, and a dozen horses stood calmly tethered nearby.

It would have been beautiful, if not for the hand on the door.

My mind insisted that it couldn’t be a door, no matter what it looked like; a broad, dense wooden rectangle with iron hinges affixed to a tall pine plank embedded straight into the hillside. It was opened at a diagonal, revealing heavy scrapes in the earth and a tunnel leading down. Pungent, yellowish globs of rotbutter were smeared along the frame.

Definitely a door. In a hill.

Then the next realization hit. The swollen, purplish shape staked into the wood. One with a mangled mass of dried blood where the wrist would be and five bulbous, black-tipped fingers dangling down.

A hand. On a door. In a hill.

It almost sounded like the start of some grisly nursery rhyme. I fought down acrid bile as oaths were muttered behind me- alongside the sound of Royce gagging- and an unfamiliar figure approached.

“Identify your- oh, hey, Renner.” The man paused. He was my height, narrow-featured, and wearing the Order’s plate and cloak. One hand rose in a wave.

“Cole.”

Renner stepped past me as I continued to stare at, and reconcile, the horrific display. Dozens of gnats crawled along the swollen flesh and a cold breeze carried the scent of decay.

“We saw the smoke.” He nodded upwards, towards the hill’s crest and the white-plumed fire lit atop. “Should’ve expected a corpse after what happened earlier.”

Cole had thin brows that drew in at a severe angle and a scar running through one corner of his mouth. The effect of each, and the evening shadows, gave his youthful face a particularly grim cast. He grimaced at the dangling flesh. “It’s Derrow. Well, we think it’s Derrow. There’s… more of him inside. Josie’s in there with everyone important trying to figure out what the blazes is going on.”

Renner’s voice was wary. “Does that mean you’re not important? I didn’t think to ask earlier, but then-”

“Jareth finally made good,” the Thalessian actually laughed. “Yeah, he’s been going on for days about how the next time you come north he’s gonna kick your… ah, sorry.” He shot me and Teela an abashed grin.

“Someone should,” Royce muttered behind me.

“They haven’t blessed anyone else early. Special circumstances, I guess.” Cole shrugged. “A dozen of us volunteered, but he’s older and got more experience, so-”

“Yeah.” Renner said flatly. He scowled into the treeline. “Keep an eye on him, would you?”

“Always do.”

“Tell Josie if he starts acting weird. At all.”

“I know, brother. Trust me, everyone’s keeping an eye on him. He’s hardly allowed to go anywhere by himself.”

“Good.” Renner gave a low whistle, examining the hand. “Derrow, huh? Shame.”

“Yeah. Never liked him much, but still.” Cole gazed at the sky. “You going in?”

“Yep.”

“Mind Vhaik. He and Jareth rode up a while ago and he’s in a mood.”

“Sir Vhaik,” Renner sneered as he stepped past, earning a snort and an exaggerated eyeroll from the other man. “And when is he not?”

“Fair point.”

Teela piped up from behind, “Wait, so we’re all just… allowed to go in? You’re not… suspicious of us?”

I goggled at her.

She spread both hands. “What? I just wanna make sure that if all the bad people show up, he’s not gonna let them walk right in.”

Cole waved one hand as another breeze stirred the gnats, flooding our nostrils with rancid air. “Heh, nah. Josie said Renner and his civilians- her words, not mine- might get nosy once the smoke went up. Said you can’t touch anything, but she could always use the extra eyes. You can all go ahead.”

I followed Renner in, holding my breath against the stench of poor Derrow and the smeared rotbutter.

Passing the barrow door felt a bit surreal. One moment the air was cold and the world around me was alive with humming insects and rustling plants. The next, it was… dead. The outside landscape was abruptly muffled by thick earthen walls. The temperature rose almost immediately; not hot, by any means, but with the heavy warmth of unmoving air.

Renner stopped so suddenly that I bumped into him. He was still for a moment- so still that I began to peer around and over his shoulder.

“Why are we stopping?” Teela whispered. Even that soft sound seemed stifled by the earthen walls.

“Renner?” I hissed, tugging at his cloak. “Is everything alright?”

He stood rigid. Then, with a sharp breath and no explanation, he stalked ahead.

The tunnel sloped downwards. It was wide enough that we all could have walked two-by-two and high enough that even Royce didn’t have to hunch. Low voices echoed from ahead alongside flickering torchlight.

The tunnel wasn’t long. We emerged into a vast, circular chamber of root-strewn walls and a floor of dark stone. Deep grooves marred the ground in a pattern of curves, swirls, and lines. A dozen men and women in emblazoned cloaks and heavy-looking armor were scattered throughout. Most of them stood or knelt near what looked and smelled like more pieces of poor Derrow.

Joselia was crouched in the chamber’s center. She rose upon seeing us. One hand held a torch and the firelight made shadows dance at her feet.

“Ah. Figured you’d show up.” She gave Renner a tight smile.

“Yeah,” he bit out, sounding more terse than usual.

I glanced over, wondering if I’d see a glare leveled at Vhaik, who stood watching us with obvious displeasure, or even at Jareth, who was deep in conversation with an unfamiliar paladin near the far wall. Instead I saw stiff shoulders and a jawline of stonelike rigidity. His nostrils flared with fast, shallow breaths.

“We’re letting civilians interfere with investigations?” Vhaik’s clipped voice made the hairs on the back of my neck stand up. Shadows played across the severe planes of his face. “Are we truly so desperate for aid?”

“If a few more pairs of eyes can get to the bottom of this mess, yes.” Joselia snorted.

“I… I don’t know what help we can be.” My voice sounded thin in the fetid air. “I’m so sorry about your fellow. Derrow.”

Joselia grimaced at a nearby lump on the stones. Whatever it was had a strip of dark cloth draped overtop. “He was a good man. He deserved far better than this… display.” She gestured around to a few foul-smelling mounds with blood spattered beneath them.

Royce sagged against the earthen wall. His face was pale and drawn. “Where’s… I mean, isn’t this a tomb? A burial ground? Where’s… er, everyone else?”

I frowned. I hadn’t thought of that, but he made a very good point. Aside from the templar’s remains, the cavern was entirely devoid of bodies.

Which… it shouldn’t have been.

The blonde paladin began pacing. Firelight made her hair shimmer like liquid gold. “That is an excellent question, Royce. This is a sizable tomb, and one of only four such barrows outside the city. All are located in places where the forest grows quite wild and the land is unsuitable for farming. The valleys to the southeast- everything below the lake- are quite fertile. Crops fuel the city’s economy. I’m sure you saw quite a few farm goods in the market, and even some of the city proper is dedicated to gardening. Crop trade runs the city, so the dead are put to rest where it won’t… ah, interfere.”

Joselia recited all this with the air of someone giving a lecture. When she finished she shot us a wry look. “Ah, the city’s baron came to hear our findings earlier. He was quite upset by the attacks, and also quite… verbose. As soon as barrows were mentioned, he gave a most detailed speech about… well, history and geography and cropland and quite a few other things.” She smiled faintly. “I daresay I’ve learned my fair share about the Sedgewick Vale economy today.”

“Breathless?” Renner’s voice was thin. Another glance showed that his eyes were closed. He cleared his throat. “You said… the city’s had some lately.”

Her brows creased. For a moment the brusque, business-like demeanor faltered and she looked concerned.

“Our best guess is they came from here.” A grizzled-looking older man with stark white hair and a face laced with scars approached. His cloak was spattered with dust. “You’re the witness who spoke of this creature’s… warning? Mention of a ritual?”

Different creature. I winced, nodding as my eyes darted to a deep groove in the floor. But I can’t exactly explain how I know that.

“Hm.” The man’s voice was gravelly. “I fear there may be some truth to it. No sign of our other fellows, and these markings on the ground… I’ve not seen the like before, but it’s certainly got my blood up. They’re too straight and deep to be anything but man-made. Or Fae-made, I suppose, though stars’ know what the point of them is.”

“Could you collapse the room? Break the beams outside or smash up the stones or something?” Teela was craning her neck back. Her chime-like voice rang off the stones. “I mean, people can’t do creepy evil stuff in the creepy evil room if the creepy evil room doesn’t exist.”

Silence crawled through the cavern. Quite a few eyes turned to her.

The young woman’s cheeks turned a vivid shade of pink. “What? It’s just an idea.”

Joselia laughed. “And this is why we let civilians interfere, Sir Vhaik. Young lady, you may have a brighter mind than the rest of us put together.”

Her flush deepened. Jareth glanced over with a little grin twisting one side of his mouth.

The grizzled paladin chuckled, too. “I’d love the idea, but only if we had more time. A handful of days and a crew of good stoneworkers… collapsing this place would be first on my list.”

“But not in one day,” Joselia sighed. She flashed Teela an approving look. “It was a good thought, though.”

The Thalessians continued to speak. The central bunch crouched, tracing the grooves and murmuring observations in solemn tones. I listened to their speculations until their voices washed into a muffled hum.

I paced a slow circle through the cavern. Examined the shadowed stones, the root-strewn walls, and the smears of dried blood. It was all grim and ominous, but offered no explanation as to what danger might truly lurk here.

I don’t know what I expected. I suppose a big sign that said ‘here’s our plan, this is how you stop us’ was probably too much to hope for.

After a time I glanced back towards Renner. He stood at the tunnel entrance with one hand braced against the wall. His eyes were closed. His other hand was clenched around his swordhilt.

It’s the smell. The air. It’s… it’s like the cave.

The realization hit me right in the gut, and I felt its truth immediately. The stifling smell, the too-still air thick with earth, smoke, and the smell of rot. The sickening familiarity of it all.

My own breath hitched. My heart sank as I waited for the hard lines of his face to smooth.

They did not.

‘I’m not a good man.’

I crept towards him without a thought. Pictured him standing in the doorway of Agnes Green’s inn, scowling at me while covered in sap and mud. Dropping his weapons as cold steel dug into my neck. On his knees in a cage, putting himself between me and a waking nightmare.

My vision blurred. A moment later, I found myself tracing the knuckles of his swordhand.

Copper eyes snapped into focus. Fixed on me with a nearly-wild fierceness.

I said nothing, just tangled my fingers with his and squeezed.

The world around us somehow grew even more still. The earth stifled every sound and breath, weighing down… until he squeezed my hand back.

I tugged him back towards the tunnel. He let out a heavy breath, following wordlessly.

At the tunnel entrance I turned back to make sure Royce and Teela were coming. Then I froze. From this angle, with the torches scattered all throughout the room, things were a bit… clearer. Broader.

My gaze trailed down, tracing the heavy grooves along the floor. They were deep, almost shockingly so, and carved a pattern from wall to wall.

I’d been staring at patterns like that for the past ten years. Lines and curves that snaked with foreign grace. Symbols that screamed they meant something, though any hope of understanding what lay out of reach.

I knew exactly what was carved into the floor. Not what it meant or what it did, but I knew what it was.

We were looking at a massive rune.
