Curtis didn't need help. That much was clear. With that in mind, Luke turned his focus to the goblin mages once the ones who'd pursued him were dead at his feet, their monster cores in his inventory. It hadn't taken the mages long to start flinging spells his way once they spotted him, and they'd shown decent proficiency in doing so even while holding their portals open. Now that they'd seen what Curtis and Luke were capable of, though, one of them had dropped the spellweave that kept the portal open to focus on the fire-based damaging spells they seemed so fond of. The intensity and strength of the spells grew, but they were still simple enough to tear apart with Destabilize Spellweave. A few exploded out of his reach when they touched goblin fighters, but Luke only saw that as a win.

The little bastards were a ferocious bunch. Even with his reach advantage and much higher level, Luke found it difficult to fend them off and even kill them. Quick on their feet and utterly fearless, even if he struck one, several others took its place, cutting or thrusting. Those he did hit didn't become wet puddles of blood on the ground either. Sure, the attacks did damage, and Luke even killed a few in a single strike when he hit their heads, but it wasn't the overwhelming show of force he'd hoped for, not like Curtis's showing. Perhaps he'd put too much faith in the physical side of things after raising those stats. Come to think of it, Luke hadn't beaten any monster or Integrated with a fighter class using physical force in a long while, if ever. Mages and the like, sure, but the rest he'd dispatched with the skills of a Lifeweaver.

Sighing, he stopped trying to kill goblins with the staff. Instead, he used it to deflect attacks and find an opportune moment to reach out with a thread of mana. Once he’d got hold of one, it was only a matter of time until he'd snaked threads into every single goblin before him. With no sort of protection against the sort of pain Luke brought, they all fell.

On the one hand, this was a more efficient and powerful way to fight. On the other hand, it wasn't the same as smashing a monster to bits using hulking biceps and swelling pecs. Not that he possessed either of those, not even at that point with his improved physical attributes. Either way, the power fantasy of bashing things when he wanted and using his skills to complement the fighting wasn't working out. At least not here against the goblins. Maybe he just needed to accept that he wouldn't turn into a physical powerhouse. That wasn't one of the Lifeweaver's strengths, and Luke didn't want to pour too much into that idea. Risking a subpar future where he tried to push the physical aspect and let the mental fall behind was not something he wanted to risk. As things were heading, there would be no time to correct mistakes.

Luke just hoped Curtis's fears of doing just that were unfounded. Most goblins had streamed toward the Reaver at that point. Once the goblins found their courage again, they had resumed throwing themselves at the unstoppable force of darkness that was Luke's friend. That was fine, in Luke's estimation. It allowed him to focus on the mages.

The goblin mages were getting frantic. Cutting through a complex set of spellweaves, Luke avoided something that looked like it might have turned into some sort of whirlwind of fire as he approached. He wasn't sure when or how, but seeing a lot of spellweaves, a whole lot, he'd started to get a sense of what spells they formed. The patterns repeated. Perhaps they repeated enough that he was beginning to gain the knowledge to read them, or perhaps it was another aspect of Weaver's Perception. Either way, the spell effect didn't matter, since Luke destroyed it before it had a chance to land.

Killing a few more goblins on his path to the mages, Luke couldn't help but stare at the one still holding a portal open. Not the mage, per se, but the spellweave. Intricate, far beyond the other spells, it drew his attention just as well as a person standing there shouting his name would've, like it wanted him to see. There was something in there, something for Luke, in that particular weave. Approaching it, he felt closer to understanding what it was about this specific spellweave that intrigued him so much.

Close enough to almost reach out a hand to touch it, Luke felt as if he was on the brink of some new understanding, even as he cut through spellweave after spellweave, rendering the mages' attacks useless.

The portal spellweave fell apart.

Luke blinked. He hadn't done anything, and it didn't look like the goblin mage had moved. In fact, it wasn't moving at all. Frozen, in fact.

When that realization dawned on Luke, a mess of half-transparent armor and weapons crashed into the grass right on top of a mage, crushing it to death in an instant. Another turned a spellweave on itself, blowing its brains out. The last two goblin mages just stood there, their eyes wide in terror, as color drained from their little green cheeks. Vitality pulled right out of you does that to a person. Or monster, in this particular case.

"Luke!" Nash boomed, jogging up to stand next to Savannah, who was attempting to scrape dead goblin mage off her summoned armor.

"Hey guys," Luke said, shaking off the disappointment of the portal spellweave falling apart before his eyes. "Sorry to barge in."

"The more the merrier!" Nash shouted, despite standing right in front of Luke. A new face appeared next to Sam and Mas, a middle-aged woman wearing a very nineties-looking suit jacket and matching skirt, pearl earrings, and a pearl necklace to go with high-heels, of all things. More than anything, she reminded him of Rory's Grandma on that TV show, Gilmore Girls.

Integrated Lou-Ann. Mendmaiden. Level 21.

"Good day," Lou-Ann said when she noticed him noticing her.

"Ma'am," Luke said, tipping an imaginary hat in greeting.

"Looks like that grumpy friend of yours is just about done," Nash said.

Luke turned to see darkness pulling into Curtis's form from all over the grass, even as he himself shed the blackness, returning to flesh and blood.

"Seems that way," Luke said, turning back. "Let's give him some privacy so he can put himself back together."

"What the hell are you doing, taking our XP?" Savannah barked, emerging from her summoned construct.

"Didn't know this one was taken."

Reward: Title: Goblin Slayer.

Goblin Slayer: Goblins look upon you with fear. They will be hesitant to attack you from now on.

Dungeon Closing in 60 minutes.

"Chill, S," Nash said, shaking his head as he looked down on the now drained and dead pair of goblin mages. "Didn't even see these assholes until the goblins stopped coming. Have you ever seen so many little dicks in the same place? Shit."

Luke wasn't sure what Nash was referring to, since a lot of the goblins did, in fact, not wear underwear, but he chose not to comment. "I'm sure you would've been fine. Curtis wanted to try something, and I just wanted to observe." Turning to Savannah, he added. "Didn't mean to steal anything. Sorry about that." Never hurt to be polite.

Savannah's face reddened, and she looked down at the ground. "It's fine. Stupid."

Nash burst out laughing, prompting her to stomp her way back to Sam, Mas, and Lou-Ann.

"Rowdy bunch you've got now," Luke noted.

Nash put his hands on his hips and nodded as he looked over his group. "Not bad, huh? We make a fine team! You're missed, of course, but LA here knows a thing or two about keeping things grimy!"

"Uh, okay," Luke said, looking around, realizing someone was missing. "Hey, where's Priyah?" Before anyone had time to answer, he blanketed the surrounding area with Weaver's Perception, looking for heartbeats.

Just as he found her, a hand rose from the tall grass. "Here..."

"Hurt?" Luke asked, craning his neck in an attempt to see more of her.

"Nope. Tired."

Nash laughed again. "She did a lot in keeping the buggers off us. Priyah has come a long way!"

He sounded like a proud father, and Luke couldn't help but smile. "It sure looks that way. You've all come a long way. I'm glad you decided to join The Band."

Luke knew they were coming, but he was still surprised at the ferocity with which Nash threw up metal horns. "The Band! It's our guild, isn't it? So much better than Integrated Solutions Group." He leaned over, as if about to throw up, and made a hacking sound. "So bad. So, so bad."

"Hey," Curtis said, coming up behind Luke, his voice subdued. "We should get out of here."

"Right!" Nash shouted in stark contrast. "That business with the Singers. Was that what you called them? Nasty business, that! Don't worry, though. Milla filled us in. As soon as they get here, we'll show them Earth isn't to be messed with."

Curtis cleared his throat. "Yeah."

"Yeah, well," Luke said. "The dungeon is closing. Let's get back."
