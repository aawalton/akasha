The sudden ending of violence was almost more unnerving than the siege itself.

The roaring, chaotic symphony of clashing steel, detonating magical plasma, and shrieking monsters had completely evaporated. In its place, a suffocating silence settled over the timber ramparts of Ashenfall. The pale winter sun finally broke entirely free of the eastern hills, casting a harsh, unforgiving light over the impossible sea of rotting meat that simply stood, motionless, upon the northern plains.

Josh stood near the edge of the parapet, his heavy steel gauntlets resting on the splintered wooden railing. The cold wind whipped at his dark greathelm, carrying the nauseating, oily stench of the smouldering pitch trenches directly below.

He let out a long, shuddering breath, feeling the deep, bone-weary ache in his muscles slowly begin to recede.

A familiar, chiming sound echoed softly in the back of his mind. A translucent blue system notification materialised in his peripheral vision.

[Level Up! You have reached Level 27.]

Josh closed his eyes as a sudden, potent rush of raw vitality flooded his system. The volume of experience points gained from participating in the massive, apocalyptic meat grinder, combined with the destruction of the towering Bone Goliaths, had pushed him across another threshold. The deep, bruising agony in his ribs, a lingering reminder of the Goliath's heavy club, faded into a dull throb. His Adamantite-infused bones felt impossibly dense, yet his body felt lighter, recalibrated by the system to handle the immense physical weight of his class.

He dismissed the notification, turning away from the horrific spectacle of the static horde. He needed to check on his party.

He found Bhel sitting heavily on a wooden supply crate near the inner stairwell. The dwarven fighter was still stripped of his upper armour, his broad chest wrapped entirely in pristine white bandages. He was breathing easily, his dark eyes alert as he methodically ran a whetstone over the curved edge of his iron axe.

"How are the stitches holding up, Bhel?" Josh asked, his heavy boots clanking against the floorboards.

"Carcan's magic is flawless, as always," Bhel grunted, offering a brief, satisfied nod. "The skin is fused perfectly. I feel like I could wrestle a forest troll, though the healer explicitly ordered me to remain seated. I am currently debating the consequences of disobeying her again."

"I strongly advise against it," Josh noted dryly, a faint, weary smile touching his lips. "She looked ready to turn you into a patient again if you crossed her."

Josh looked past the dwarf, spotting Brett lying on a makeshift stretcher a few yards away. His friend was completely unconscious, his pale face relaxed in a deep, restorative sleep. His crimson cloak was draped over him, shimmering faintly as it passively gathered ambient thermal energy from the morning sun to feed his entirely depleted mana core.

With his friends safe and recovering, Josh allowed himself to simply sit down. He dropped heavily onto the wooden deck, resting his back against the solid oak barricade. He had fought the heavy vanguard, he had jumped off a thirty-foot wall, and he had held the line against an ocean of the dead. He felt he had earned an hour of unbothered rest.

However, the uneasy calm on the ramparts did not last long.

A group of guard captains and senior Guild administrators had gathered around Caistina Tharistrar near the watchtower. They were pointing down at the thousands of aimless, milling zombies and engaging in a rapid, highly animated tactical debate.

That can’t be good, he thought to himself.

A few minutes later, the booming voice of Captain Aris echoed across the wooden walkway.

"Listen up!" the captain roared, magically amplifying his voice to ensure it reached the exhausted defenders lining the wall. "The commanding entity controlling this horde has clearly been neutralised or distracted! The monsters outside our walls are currently passive, lacking any driving force or knowledge of what to do! But we cannot simply leave ten thousand rotting corpses on our doorstep!"

A low murmur of uneasy agreement rippled through the gathered militia.

"We have an unprecedented opportunity," Aris continued, pacing along the wooden deck. "The Guild is authorising a cull. We are forming strike forces to push out beyond the gates. We will engage the horde in small, strictly controlled groups. You will pull a handful of zombies at a time, destroy them, and move onto the next group."

The captain stopped, looking directly at the ragged, exhausted ranks of the civilian militia.

"For the lower-level adventurers and the brave citizens who stood with us today," Aris announced, his voice dropping into a harsh, pragmatic tone, "this is free experience. This is a chance to level up your classes, increase your physical stats, and make yourselves stronger for the future, without facing the coordinated threat of a dungeon break. You will be escorted by high-tier veterans to ensure no one is overwhelmed."

The psychological shift on the wall was immediate and entirely surreal.

The terrified bakers, merchants, and farm boys who had been weeping in fear just hours ago suddenly looked at the milling horde with a completely different perspective. They were no longer looking at an apocalypse. They were looking at a massive, unharvested field of experience and levels. In a world governed by stats and levels, the promise of easy experience was an intoxicating, irresistible lure.

A loud, heavy groaning sound echoed from the base of the central gatehouse.

Josh opened his eyes, listening to the heavy thud of timber supports being removed. The city guard was unsealing the main iron portcullis, preparing to let the culling squads out onto the plains.

"Volunteers to the lower staging area!" a sergeant barked, waving his arms. "Form up into groups of ten to twenty! Do not step past the gates until a veteran is assigned to your squad!"

Josh leaned his head back against the wood, closing his eyes again. He had absolutely no intention of participating in a glorified scavenging hunt. He had hit level twenty-seven, his party was alive, and he simply wanted to close his eyes until his muscles stopped burning.

Then, the heavy, rhythmic clanking of boots on the wooden stairs caught his attention.

Josh opened one eye. Marching purposefully past his resting spot, heading directly for the stairs leading down to the gates, was a group of a dozen newly minted volunteers. They carried crude spears, rusted short swords, and heavy wooden clubs.

Leading the pack was Garrik.

The broad-shouldered tavern landlord was still wearing his heavy, dented steel greathelm. He gripped his utilitarian iron broadsword tightly in his right hand. He did not look like a man seeking easy experience points. He looked like a man riddled with guilt and anxiety.

Josh let out a heavy, frustrated sigh. He grabbed his dark steel heater shield, pushing himself painfully up from the floorboards.

"Garrik!" Josh called out, his deep voice cutting through the chatter of the volunteers.

The tavern landlord stopped at the top of the stairs, turning to look back at the towering human.

"What are you doing?" Josh demanded, stepping quickly over to the older man. "You held the line during the breach. You did your part. You have a wife and a business to protect. Leave the clean-up to the young kids looking to level up."

Garrik looked at Josh, the deep lines around his eyes visible through the narrow slit of his visor. The tavern keeper’s expression was weighed down by a profound, suffocating guilt.

"I stood on the wall, Josh," Garrik replied quietly, his voice tight with suppressed emotion. "I stood right next to a young boy who was a stable hand for the local merchant guild. I watched him thrust his spear bravely, and I watched a monster tear his throat out while I was busy defending my own flank. I watched five of my neighbours die on these planks."

Garrik gripped the hilt of his broadsword until his knuckles turned white.

"I did not take a single scratch," Garrik confessed, his voice dropping into a harsh, bitter whisper. "I just stood there and watched kids and old men bleed to protect my tavern. I have not done enough for my home today. I am going out there, and I am going to put a few of those rotting bastards into the dirt permanently."

Josh stared at the tavern keeper. He completely understood the insidious, poisonous nature of survivor's guilt. He had felt it himself when he looked at the fallen men and women around them. He knew there was absolutely nothing he could say to talk the stubborn man out of this crusade.

Josh let out a low, gravelly curse, directed entirely at his own deeply ingrained protective instincts.

He drew his longsword from its scabbard, the heavy steel hissing sharply in the freezing air. He stepped smoothly past the group of volunteers, taking the lead position at the top of the stairs.

"Keep your shield high, Garrik," Josh ordered, his voice returning to its uncompromising, authoritative rumble. "You'd best not die on me, or Barb will kill me."

Garrik offered a slow, deeply grateful nod.

As Josh prepared to lead the group down the stairs, he felt a prickle of awareness on the back of his neck. He looked up towards the upper parapet.

Carcan was standing by the wooden railing, looking directly down at him. The elven healer raised an elegant, perfectly arched eyebrow, clearly questioning his decision to abandon his well-earned rest.

Josh offered her a helpless, apologetic shrug, gesturing vaguely towards the tavern landlord with his sword hand. Carcan let out a highly exaggerated, visible sigh, shaking her head before turning away from the railing.

Josh led the small squad down the steep wooden steps, descending into the dark, cold staging area located directly behind the main iron portcullis.

The space was packed tightly with hundreds of eager civilians and low-tier adventurers. The adrenaline in the air was palpable. They were bouncing on the balls of their feet, gripping their crude weapons with white-knuckled anticipation. It felt entirely wrong to Josh. They were treating an apocalyptic battlefield like a lucrative hunting ground.

Captain Aris stood atop a wooden supply crate, shouting his final briefing over the noise of the crowd.

"The killing fields have been divided into quadrants!" Aris barked, pointing towards the gates. "Do not push further forward than the group next to you! We maintain a solid, unbroken line! You pull one or two monsters at a time, you drop them, and you wait for the next! If you charge blindly into the thick of the horde, you will pull too many of the enemy, and we will close the gates and leave you out there!"

The heavy iron chains of the portcullis groaned loudly. Two massive winches were turned by sweating guardsmen, and the thick, spiked iron gate slowly rose, allowing the pale morning light and the freezing wind to flood into the staging area.

"Move out!" Aris commanded.

The cull began.

The groups poured out of the gateway, fanning out into the freezing, churned mud of the plains. The stench of the smouldering pitch trenches was horrific, forcing many of the civilians to pull rags over their noses and mouths.

Josh led Garrik’s group towards the eastern quadrant, stepping carefully over the ruined, blackened corpses littering the immediate approach.

"Keep your formation tight," Josh warned, keeping his heavy dark shield raised. He did not intend to fight. He was acting solely as a heavily armoured sheepdog, watching the perimeter and ensuring the eager civilians did not do anything foolish.

As they walked past the ruined trenches, a quiet footstep splashed in the mud to Josh's left. He turned his head sharply, ready to bring his sword up.

"Did you honestly believe I was going to let you walk out here alone?" Carcan asked smoothly, stepping into stride right beside him.

The elven healer had her amber-topped staff gripped firmly in her hands. Her pristine pale green robes were now thoroughly ruined at the hem from the black sludge, but her luminous silver eyes were bright and alert.

"I thought you were monitoring Bhel," Josh murmured, a sudden, undeniable warmth blooming in his chest at her arrival.

"Bhel is asleep, and Brett is currently snoring loudly enough to wake the dead," Carcan replied, a faint, teasing smile touching her lips. "Besides, you are escorting a group of completely untrained civilians into a horde of monsters. Someone is going to get injured undoubtedly. I prefer to be close to the bleeding."

They reached their assigned quadrant, roughly fifty yards from the city walls.

The horde was a terrifying sight up close. Thousands of zombies and rotting ghouls simply stood perfectly still in the freezing mist. They were completely devoid of the shrieking frenzy that had characterised the dawn assault. They stared blankly at the frost-covered grass, swaying slightly in the wind, entirely passive.

"Pull a small group," Josh ordered the squad. "Make some noise. Draw them out."

A young baker stepped forward, nervously banging his rusted sword against a wooden buckler. "Hey! Over here, you rotting bastards!"

The noise worked. A group of three bloated, purple-skinned zombies slowly turned their heads. Their milky, lifeless eyes locked onto the baker. They let out a low, gurgling hiss and began to stumble forward, their arms outstretched.

The engagement was entirely anticlimactic.

Without the dark, oppressive magic of the Bone General forcing them into a relentless charge, the zombies were slow, clumsy, and incredibly fragile. The group of civilians swarmed the three monsters, thrusting their spears and swinging their swords with desperate, panicked energy. The zombies were hacked to pieces in a matter of seconds, collapsing into the mud as permanent corpses.

Instantly, a faint, translucent blue glow washed over the baker and a young farmhand.

"I levelled up!" the baker shouted, his voice cracking with sheer, euphoric disbelief. He stared down at his blood-stained hands, flexing his fingers as the system rewarded him with a minor increase to his physical strength. "By the Light, I actually feel stronger!"

The taste of easy experience was a potent drug. The initial terror of the squad completely vanished, rapidly replaced by a reckless, greedy confidence.

They began pulling more groups. Three ghouls. Four zombies. Garrik fought methodically and quietly, using his heavy iron broadsword to cleanly decapitate any monster that stumbled too close to the front line. He was working through his guilt with every swing of his blade, his posture rigid and focused.

Josh watched the skirmishes with a growing sense of unease. It did not feel like warfare. It felt like a slaughterhouse. They were methodically butchering helpless, passive targets. But he understood the necessity. If the commanding intelligence returned, these thousands of bodies would instantly become a deadly threat again. They had to thin the numbers while they had the chance.

"Do not push too far forward!" Josh barked, noticing the eager civilians drifting further away from the safety of the shield wall. "Maintain the line! You are getting too close to a big group!"

His warning came a fraction of a second too late.

A young man, intoxicated by the rush of gaining two levels in quick succession, decided a shambling zombie was no longer a worthy target. He pushed past Garrik, his crude iron spear raised high, and charged directly towards a stationary figure wearing rusted, interlocking chainmail.

It was not a soft, rotting villager. It was an ancient skeletal warrior from the vanguard.

"Boy, stop!" Garrik shouted, stepping forward to intercept.

The young man ignored the warning. He thrust his iron spear clumsily towards the skeleton's exposed ribcage, expecting the creature to simply stand there and take the blow like the zombies had.

The skeletal warrior was not mindless meat. It possessed the deeply ingrained, ancient muscle memory of a trained soldier.

The moment the young man crossed its immediate threat radius, the skeleton reacted with a lethal speed. It did not simply stumble forward. It pivoted smoothly on its rusted heel, sweeping its heavy, jagged broadsword in a precise, fluid parry that knocked the crude spear aside.

The young man stumbled forward, his momentum carrying him into the skeleton's guard, his chest completely exposed.

The skeletal warrior brought its broadsword around in a rapid, brutal counter-attack, thrusting the jagged iron blade directly toward the boy's stomach.

Josh was already moving, his heavy boots churning the mud, but the distance was too great.

The rusted blade pierced the thick boiled leather of the boy's tunic, sinking deeply into his right side. The young man let out a sharp, breathless scream of agony, dropping his spear as the freezing, necrotic metal bit into his flesh.

Before the skeleton could rip the blade free for a lethal second strike, Josh arrived.

The Guardian did not bother swinging his sword. He triggered Juggernaut's Tread, slamming his dark steel shield directly into the skeleton's chest with the force of a battering ram. The impact completely shattered the creature's ancient spine, launching the ruined bones backward into the mist where they instantly dissolved into golden dust.

The young man collapsed into the freezing mud, clutching his bleeding side, his face instantly turning a sickly, pale shade of grey as the necrotic chill entered his veins. The reckless confidence of the squad completely shattered, replaced by the horrific, sudden reality of their mortality.

"Move back!" Josh roared at the stunned civilians, raising his shield to cover the fallen boy.

Carcan was already there.

She dropped gracefully to her knees in the filthy sludge, completely ignoring the chaotic shouting around her. She ripped the boy's ruined leather tunic open, exposing the deep, jagged puncture wound.

"Hold him still," Carcan commanded softly, her melodic voice cutting through the boy's panicked sobbing.

She pressed her elegant, pale hands directly over the bleeding injury. Her amber staff pulsed brightly, and a brilliant, searing wave of golden light flooded from her palms. The holy magic violently burned away the dark, freezing corruption left by the rusted blade, forcing the torn muscle fibres to rapidly knit back together.

The boy gasped, his back arching off the mud as the crippling pain was replaced by a soothing, profound warmth.

"You are incredibly foolish," Carcan scolded gently, keeping her glowing hands firmly on his side until the skin was completely sealed. "This is not a training ground. These monsters have killed veterans with decades of experience. Do not let your greed blind your judgement."

She stood up smoothly, picking up her staff, her pale green robes now thoroughly stained with the boy's blood. She looked over at Josh, her silver eyes carrying a sharp, knowing gleam.

"I suppose my presence here was entirely unnecessary after all," Carcan noted dryly, a faint smirk touching her lips.

Josh let out a heavy breath, shaking his head. He reached down, hauling the pale, trembling boy back to his feet. "Fall back to the rear of the group. If anyone else steps out of line, you won't have to worry about the zombies as I'll be the one smacking you."

The squad settled back into a much tighter, significantly more terrified formation. The grim reality of the cull had been firmly re-established. They continued to methodically pull small groups of zombies, but they did so with extreme, paranoid caution.

Josh stood near Garrik, his storm-grey eyes scanning the vast, silent sea of monsters stretching towards the northern horizon.

They were winning. They were successfully clearing the field and gaining valuable experience. Yet, the heavy, suffocating knot of unease in Josh's stomach refused to dissipate. The silence of the plains felt incredibly fragile, like a thin layer of ice covering a deep, raging river.

He looked towards the dark, corrupted treeline in the far distance, wondering exactly what was happening in the shadows, and praying that Perberos had not missed his mark.
