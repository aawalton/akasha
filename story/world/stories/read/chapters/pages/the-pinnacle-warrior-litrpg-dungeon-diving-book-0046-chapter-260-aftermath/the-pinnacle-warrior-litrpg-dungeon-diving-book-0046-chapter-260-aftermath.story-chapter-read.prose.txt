“The best way to recover is to just leave it alone,” Astrid said, shrugging helplessly.

I could even hold a baby without endangering it. “You said this hammer gives you 300 bonus attribute points?” Rebecca asked, seeming to want to look at the hammer that had just burned her. “That seems unbelievable to me.”

“There’s plenty of reasons for why it gives so many attribute points,” Astrid shrugged with a laugh. “The biggest reason is that it doesn’t have any additional special parts to it. Like, it just hits things good. Then, with it being one of the few pieces of actually Mithril tier equipment that I’ve got, it just turns out that it gives a lot of attributes.”

“The way you say that sounds like you plan on being able to continue to replace your equipment for basically every level you get,” Rebecca said, staring at Astrid. “Isn’t a new piece of equipment something that the entire party saves up for to be able to afford?”

“Yes, but you make a lot of money delving. A good way to make sure that you continue making money and don’t die is to get newer, better equipment when you can. It’s a little bit more complicated than that as to why we have the equipment that we have right now, but there’s no need to get into the specifics of that.”

“If you have multiple pieces of equipment that give you that many attribute points, you must have an awesome Class, right? Like, two rares in a row?” Rebecca asked, continuing to pay attention to Astrid specifically as they both walked between the prisoners and helped attach mana cuffs to the people who were going to be brought back to the Duchess. Apparently, it was a piece of equipment that was frequently used for criminals in the capital? Astrid had no idea about the things’ existence, as they hadn’t ever been used, to her knowledge, in Stahlstadt growing up. That was the only time she had any extended exposure to the guards of a city, and surely her mother would have mentioned something like that around her family, right?

“Something like that,” Astrid nodded as she got close to the next person. This was the Steel tier assassin that had been focused on taking out the Duchess’s army’s supporters when the Wanderers appeared. Muti had done a number on him, dozens of cuts up and down his arms, and then a gnarly bruise across his neck and going down into his armor from where she’d nonlethally but very painfully cut him down.

“Well, I’ll give you some space,” Rebecca said, stepping back. “If you’re willing to talk later, I’d be happy to continue to pick your brain.”

“Come by any time, and if I’m busy, I’ll let you know,” Astrid replied. The excitement Rebbeca’d shown about talking to Astrid had been useful in ingratiating her to the army, and more than that, very flattering, but as Rebecca had continued asking questions and following her, Astrid could see how being a name known throughout the entire country could pretty quickly result in wanting to escape from the public eye.

Are you worried about your friend, though?”

There’s no way the enemies have people who can keep up with her in terms of pure attributes, and her explosive movement abilities are outstanding.”

If there’s anybody stronger than Steel tier moving around, it’s not too hard for a Dungeoneer to feel that somebody’s trying to be sneaky.”

Astrid and Felix nodded their thanks for the answers as the woman continued moving along. She was maybe a sergeant, not in charge of the army as a whole, but a leader of some sort.

“You didn’t get to use your new Skill?” Felix asked as the two of them were left, mostly alone. They continued to look around and try to see if there was anybody or anything that needed their attention, but it seemed like their part in this battle was largely finished.

“You know I didn’t want to accidentally kill anyone with it,” Astrid chuckled. “Not all of us have direct upgrades to passive skills we already had. How was Impenetrable Fortress?”

“Now that it’s Mithril, I think just about every one of those attacks I could have taken without even wearing my armor. We’ll have to test it more against enemies that can actually challenge us at all, but for its maiden voyage? Really good,” Felix said, smiling. “It’s a really impressive Skill.”

“I can’t wait until we can see it like that,” Astrid agreed, smiling at him.

“And when you can actually let loose?” Felix asked, and a knowing look on his face.

“Yes, I’m also looking forward to being able to see what the new Class can do. That’s not a surprise to anybody.”

The conversation died off as one of the enemy, a Steel tiers, probably the leader of the army, woke and started struggling against his chains. Felix rushed over to make sure nobody got hurt, also pushing the man down and forcing him to be quiet as he did so.

Unmoveable Density (Steel): Increases the skill holder’s power and fortitude by 25%. For a minor stamina cost, reduces the skill holder’s alacrity by 20% while generating a graviturgical aura within ten meters that forces everything towards the Skill holder.

Astrid had thought about using the active aspect of the Skill in her subjugation of the enemy soldiers, but given the fact that she was at least a tier above every single one of them, and two tiers over most, to say nothing of the vast gulf in attributes between them, given that she had only ever had extraordinary rarity Classes, she could very easily have crippled or killed people just by moving through their ranks with the Skill active.

“Looks like we have no more cuffs,” Benedict said as he walked nearby. He continued to use both of his primary Skills at the same time, focusing entirely on healing their allies and slowing their enemies with Song of Victory and Voice of Liberation respectively.

“I think we’ll be fine, so long as nothing unexpected happens,” Astrid replied. “And no, that isn’t me wishing complications into existence.”

Benedict jokingly glared at her as she cut off his opportunity to tease her, but he didn’t push the issue. Instead, he asked, “How long do you think it’s going to take for Muti to get back? And do you think it’s going to be her or Skandr that shows up first?”

“I’d guess that Muti will take about two hours total,” Astrid said, looking around and feeling useless. “As for the communications with the town? Aaron didn’t tell me anything and I doubt he would have even if I’d asked for specifics. So no guesses there.”

Benedict made a grunting noise that didn’t quite make a whole lot of sense, but Astrid didn’t care to question him. Ever since they’d gotten back into Humanity’s lands, he’d been off, staring at the terrain around them, going surprisingly and uncharacteristically quiet all the time. Astrid figured she had a pretty good understanding as to why he was reacting that way, but it didn’t make him any easier to deal with.

Instead of getting into some long discussion with him, or trying to poke at him until he would reveal how he was thinking about being back home, but forced to continue fighting, at least for a little while yet, Astrid just looked around. The other soldiers, now that they were saved from certain death or enslavement, worked relatively well together, though it was obvious that they weren’t men and women trained for years in warfare. Instead, they were simply people who had signed up for the war and hoped to be able to protect themselves and their families.

Minutes passed in what was a blissful quiet, and Astrid reveled in it. Or at least, she’d like to think that she did, when in reality, her anxiety continued twisting in her stomach. She was going to run into Greely again; how was that going to be? He’d helped the party immensely with getting them entrance into the Verdant Sanctuary, guiding them in Purcell, and more. He was actively assisting the same side that they were going to support, so he could be counted as an ally.

However, Astrid had lost all of that previous deep-seated respect for authority that she’d always had growing up. Instead of being willing to blindly follow any advice from somebody in a leadership position, the thought of being told what to do had Astrid a little bit twitchy. She couldn’t help but laugh at herself a little bit as she made that realization, though, given that she had very literally just signed up to join in a war. She wasn’t going to be in charge of anything, other than her party, and she knew it.

At least she didn’t need to listen to any commands just yet. Instead, she found somewhere flat she could take a chair out of her spatial pouch, and got comfortable. As the army got more and more control over their enemies, there was less for her to do, and she allowed herself to try to relax past the nerves thrashing in her belly.
