The moment my feet hit the floor, I threw myself away from the ladder and dove towards the door. Seth’s words were still echoing in my head, and I wasn’t sure whether he was talking to himself or if he was somehow speaking to the people in black below, but I didn’t want to know.

Pulling the storeroom door open, I sprinted out before my steps faltered as a strange, creeping sensation hit me. Something was wrong. I knew it instinctively, but it took me a moment for the realisation to set in.

The Academy was silent, and that was not right. I expected it to be loud. It always was during the evening. Voices and laughter should have been drifting out of the boys’ dorm rooms around me. I should have been able to hear the televisions in the common room at the end of the corridor, but there was nothing. It was quiet. Unnaturally silent.

Could the people already be in the main building, I wondered. That didn’t feel right, though. They should have been stopped by then, or we would have noticed. I knew that I hadn’t been paying much attention to anything. I was too distracted by my own thoughts, wallowing in fears and concerns about Katie, but surely, I would not have heard the fight happening below.

I needed to keep moving, I realised, forcing myself to take another step away from the storeroom. I had yet to hear Seth’s shoes on the ladder, but I knew he couldn’t be far behind, and I couldn’t just stand there in the middle of the corridor and wait for him to catch up with me.

My heart hammered in my chest, and I could feel my head spinning as I hurried away as fast as I dared, my steps silent on the wooden floor. I had only just begun to run, but my breath was already far too fast. It was coming in shallow gasps; I was on the verge of hyperventilating, but I could do nothing to stop it, and I still didn’t understand why the Academy was so quiet.

It should have been prepared. We had been taught that an attack was possible. The country had many enemies, and the information we gathered was crucial. We were important and useful, so it made sense that someone would target us. The tutors had never shied away from that, and they even ran drills. I’d yet to be a part of one, but I’d heard stories of them.

Maybe that was what had happened. Perhaps it was just a simulation to make sure we were ready for when it happened. Seth could be in on it. He could be helping Rodgers out, I told myself frantically, but that didn’t make sense. He had only just left the induction wing. He hadn’t taken part in a drill either, so I didn’t understand why they would choose him to play that part.

I glanced over my shoulder, looking in the direction of the storeroom to ensure Seth wasn’t behind me. I was terrified that he’d appear out of nowhere and attack me before I had the chance to defend myself, but the corridor was entirely empty.

A shaky breath escaped my lips, and I allowed myself to slow as I reached a corner. I didn’t know what to do. Even if it were just a simulation, I felt out of my depth and terrified. I hadn’t been at the Academy for long enough. They were waiting until more people were out of the induction wing before running the training on what to do if the building was attacked, so I had no idea what to do. All I knew were the snippets I’d heard older trainees talking about, like the security system and caches hidden around the building.

My eyes widened, and I felt my heart thump. The caches. They contained weapons that we could use in case of an emergency. If I found one, I could use the weapons to defend myself and the Academy. I could help protect anyone who needed it and fight back against the people dressed all in black.

Fear edged into my heart, dimming the hope that had burst to lift there. I was terrified that if I found one of the chests of weapons and used it, I could kill someone. If it were just a test, they may be innocent. I could take a person’s life, a person who didn’t do anything wrong or deserve it, and that was something I wasn’t sure I would ever recover from.

If it were just a simulation, though, I told myself as I peered around the corner before hurrying out, they would have known someone might use those weapons. Even if it were just a precaution, I had no doubt they would have replaced them with fakes. The guns would probably just shoot blanks, or paintballs, or something, so it would be fine. As long as I found the cache, everything would be fine.

But that was the problem. I didn’t know where they were. I could picture it in my head, and I was certain that I’d seen some of the big yellow crates around somewhere, but my mind had turned blank. I couldn’t remember exactly where it had been or even when I’d spotted it.

Tears of fear and frustration prickled behind my eyes, and I gritted my teeth as I racked my brain, trying to remember, but it took me a couple of seconds before I did. The hope that had kindled in my heart moments ago flared brighter as I remembered almost dropping a bottle of shower gel on the box when I’d gone to grab something from the storeroom at the far end of the girls’ corridor.

It was kind of far away, and I didn’t know what stood between me and that room, but I was certain it was there. All I needed to do was get to the storeroom, and then I’d never need to find out what it was Seth, or the rest of whatever organisation he must have been part of, wanted from me.

Anxiety gripped me again as a muffled thump echoed through the corridor. I ducked instinctively, looking around, but I saw nothing. I couldn’t even tell which direction it was coming from. After a moment of hesitation, I continued creeping forward, my steps slower than they had been before.

Why did they want me? That question pulled at me, demanding my attention. I was just a normal kid in that world. Sure, I’d been recruited by the Academy, so there must have been something kind of different about me, but I didn’t know what it was. I had no idea what they saw in me, but I knew I was not special enough to cause whatever was happening.

I reached another corner and checked over my shoulder before coming to a stop. It was starting to worry me that I’d yet to see or hear Seth. He should have been following after me, should have already caught up, but he hadn’t, which felt wrong. I didn’t let myself linger on that for long, though. Instead, I forced myself to look forward again, staring at the slightly greyed wallpaper as I strained my ears, trying to pick up any sound coming from in front of me.

All I could hear was that same muffled thumping that I’d noticed before, but I had no idea what it was. The beat was uneven, the rhythm inconsistent, and I hesitated, trying to figure it out before giving up. It didn’t matter. Not right now. It sounded far away, and that meant I could deal with it later. Once I had weapons, I could hunt it down.

My hands curled into fists as I took a deep breath, my muscles tensing in preparation before I peered around the corner. I’d been expecting to see people, for the hall to be filled with operatives dressed in their black uniforms, but what I saw instead made my heart jump into my throat and nausea slam into me.

A body lay in the middle of the corridor, blood pooling around it. There was a streak leading away from it too. I couldn’t work out whether they’d managed to crawl that far before collapsing or if someone else had been with them, but they’d been dragged away.

I barely noticed that I was moving towards them, my legs numb yet shaking, until I was standing over the body. My eyes scoured them, spotting the exit wounds on their back that matched the holes in the wall behind me, before landing on the blood.

The hope that I’d been clinging to up until that point died just as quickly as the boy before me must have done, and suddenly, I was certain the attack was more than just a training exercise. The body was too still, and the blood was too realistic. I’d seen a lot of blood since I began daydreaming or flitting between worlds. I’d become far too familiar with exactly what it looked like, what it smelt like. There was no mistaking it.

And I recognised the boy. The realisation crashed into me so hard that I almost vomited. I stumbled backwards, pressing a hand to my lips to hold my nausea in, but my eyes remained on his face. I could not look away, and the horrifying awareness that I did not remember his name clawed at me.

I didn’t know him well. I’d seen him around the Academy a couple of times, and we had only spoken once or twice. It was nothing more than a pleasant greeting, but it still felt wrong that I couldn’t recall what his name was. The boy died because of me, and I should have remembered that.

A tremble tore through me. It was my fault, I realised. Seth had said that the people, whoever they were, were there because of me. I was the one they were looking for, the one they needed, so that meant I was the reason the boy had been killed. And if anyone else had died in the attack, that was my fault too.

I couldn’t move. It felt as though my feet had turned to ice and frozen to the floor as the urge to turn myself in gripped me. It was the only way to stop it, to prevent anyone else from getting hurt because of me, and that was so tempting that I almost did it. My mouth had begun to open. I was prepared to call out, to shout to the people who may not have even been in the building, that I was handing myself over, but something stopped me.

Perhaps it was just cowardliness or unearned confidence, but I knew there was no guarantee that it would help. For all I knew, even if I gave myself up, they would continue murdering the trainees. I might be forced to watch as they gunned them all down, my hands bound behind my back and powerless to intervene.

At least if I didn’t surrender, there was a chance that I could help. I wasn’t the best with a gun. I’d had some training; I could hit a target, but I needed more practice. Still, I could still do my part. I might be able to take down some of the attackers, and maybe I’d be able to protect some trainees.

I nodded to myself, taking one last look at the boy before turning. As far as I could tell, he did not have a gun or any kind of weapon. Perhaps he had fallen on it when he’d been shot, but I couldn’t waste time checking, and I wasn’t sure I could bring myself to touch him, so I needed to keep moving so that I could find one.

My legs shook as I walked away. My knees threatened to give way, but somehow, I managed to make it to the end of the corridor and paused for just a moment to listen before dashing forward again.

The scent of blood grew heavier as I moved towards the staircase in the centre of the building. I could almost feel it in the air around me. It felt as though I were swimming through it, fighting to get past, and it should have prepared me. I should have expected it when I spotted an open door, but still, I couldn’t help but glance inside.

Panic thrashed in my chest as my gaze landed on a body face down in the middle of the dorm room before finding another corpse slumped on the bed. I stepped towards them, wanting to help, but the blood soaking into the rug beneath them had already begun to dry. It was too late for me to do anything, and I forced myself to back away.

I had to just keep moving, I told myself as I began to hurry down the corridor again, passing even more open doors. I couldn’t look into any of the rooms, couldn’t let myself get distracted. Unless I found some survivors or someone who was still alive and in need of help, I couldn’t stop. I was so close. One more corner, and then I’d be approaching the top of the stairs.

But my resolve wavered the moment I crept around that turn, and the tidal wave of panic and despair loomed overhead, threatening to crash down on me at any second. Every single door in that stretch of the corridor had been thrown open, but that is not what held my attention. It was the blood coating the floor so thickly that I could not see the wood below, and I didn’t even notice the bodies at first, but once I did, it was impossible to look away.

They had been kicked carelessly to the edges of the hallway, making space for people to walk along the centre, and I had to press my hands to my mouth again as I gagged. Tears stung my eyes as my body heaved, and I couldn’t stop myself from vomiting onto the already wet floor.

When did the attack start? I couldn’t help but wonder as I wiped my mouth with the back of my hand, feeling a storm of determination mixed with hopelessness raging within me. Surely, it must have been going on for hours without me even noticing. The people I’d seen on the front lawn must have been the final wave, the last group of backup or something, but I still didn’t understand how it had happened. We were trained. We should have been prepared for an attack, but they still managed to overpower and kill so many people. So many trainees.

They must have been better. Better trained, more organised, and that terrified me. If Seth were speaking the truth, if he meant it when he said they were looking for me, I wasn’t sure I’d be able to defeat them, but I had to try.

My foot slipped on the blood-slicked floor, and my knee jolted as it snapped forward, straightening too far as I tried to catch myself. I grabbed the wall, biting my lip as pain radiated through my leg, but I managed to stay upright.

I kept moving, my steps a little more cautious than before, as I approached the top of the stairs. I’d still yet to see anyone, and I had almost managed to convince myself that they’d already left, when a soft cry reached my ears. It was coming from below, and I couldn’t help but crouch and peer around the wall, looking down into the foyer.

A trainee knelt on the floor, her hands tied behind her back and blood streaking down her face from a gash on her forehead, but the man standing in front of her seemed unaffected by the sight or her tears. He wore no helmet. His face was utterly emotionless as he lifted a gun.

I opened my mouth, intending to shout, but a quiet bang sounded before any noise came out. Tears streamed from my eyes, and I clutched the wall for strength as the girl fell backwards. The urge to run towards her, to put pressure on the wound on her chest and scream for help gripped me, but I could tell it was too late. Her glassy eyes were staring at the ceiling, and I knew she was already dead.

The world spun around me, and I clung tighter to the wall, trying to stop myself from falling as I continued to cry silently, but I knew I had to keep going. If I stayed where I was, I’d be found, and I had to find the weapons.

Swallowing as hard as I could, I looked around, scanning the people streaming through the foyer below and the corridor behind me. Seth was still nowhere to be seen, and bloody footprints crossed back and forth over the landing, but it was empty. I couldn’t even see anyone in the girls’ corridor on the other side.

I stayed low as I edged forward, moving slowly so as not to draw attention to myself. There were too many gaps in the wooden bannister around the top of the stairs. I was entirely exposed, and if anyone even glanced up, they would see me, but somehow, I managed to make it to the other side without being spotted.

Relief washed over me, but it didn’t last long. Within seconds, I realised there were just as many bodies littered through the corridor. A thick carpet of blood spilt out before me, and the temptation to turn away resurfaced. I didn’t know where any of the other caches were, though. If I abandoned my plan and tried to find another, I could be captured before I made it that far, so I had to keep going.

Nausea, horror, and heartbreak warred within my heart as I straightened up, slowly moving past the corpses, but I couldn’t stop myself from glancing at their faces. A sob bubbled up in my throat as I spotted even more people I recognised. I lived near those girls. I walked past their rooms every single day to my dorm. Although I found them scary so I didn’t say much to them, we had spoken a handful of times. I actually knew them, and the fact that they were dead, that they had been killed because of me caused something within me to shatter.

“Oh,” an almost silent gasp was torn from my throat, and I couldn’t help but come to a stop.

I gazed down at one of the girls, trying to wrap my head around what I was seeing, but I couldn’t. Georgie, the R&D girl who’d always smiled at me whenever she saw me and joked so easily with Rodgers, had fallen against the door to her room. Her glasses were splattered with blood, and she still wore the headphones she always carried.

Bile began to burn the back of my throat again, and I had to look away. If I kept staring at her, I would be sick. The horror of what had happened, the cruelty, would overpower me, and I couldn’t let it. If it swallowed me, I’d turn into a blubbering mess and be useless. I had to push it aside, squash it down and pretend it didn’t exist. It was the only way to keep going.

I straightened my spine and shook my head, trying to push the thoughts away, but they refused to leave. They stayed, lingering at the back of my mind and ready to strike as my hands curled into fists, and I forced myself to start walking again before quickly breaking into a run.

“Hey!” a shout came from directly behind me before a hand closed around my wrist. “Guys! I got her!”
