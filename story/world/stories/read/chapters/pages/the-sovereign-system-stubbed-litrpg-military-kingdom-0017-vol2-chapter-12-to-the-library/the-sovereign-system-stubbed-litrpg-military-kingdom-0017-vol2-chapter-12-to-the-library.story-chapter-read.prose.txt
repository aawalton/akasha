The next morning, Luke opened his bleary eyes, having struggled to sleep for most of the night. The incredibly supple—yet dangerous—figure lying beside him had made rest nearly impossible.

When he woke, however, he found Victoria was no longer there. He exhaled a quiet sigh of relief.

Stretching, Luke winced as a dull ache pulsed through his muscles. The lingering pain from his match against Michael Ring yesterday made itself known with every movement.

He let out a low grumble and pushed himself to his feet.

“Good morning, young master.”

Luke froze. Only now did he notice Victoria standing nearby. She wore a clean white robe, the image of a dutiful servant girl. Yet something about her exotic features—particularly those deep brown eyes that seemed to draw a person in—made her beauty feel almost unnatural. Almost unreasonable.

“Allow me to dress you,” she said.

Without waiting for permission, she stepped forward and slipped his robe over his shoulders. Her movements were smooth, practiced, and efficient.

Yet Luke’s mind was elsewhere.

It hit him then—he truly knew next to nothing about this woman.

He’d first met Victoria in Clayton City while she posed as a maid, and then again in Valand City. Despite his efforts to pry information from her, she remained elusive, always giving just enough to satisfy but never enough to trust.

At one point, Luke had suspected her connection to Hayden, the Blood General’s descendant. She’d been introduced as his subordinate, and for a while, he’d questioned how long she’d played that role.

Her story had eased those doubts... but only slightly.

“I trust you slept well, young master?” Victoria asked, flashing him a smile that didn’t quite reach her eyes.

Luke resisted the urge to hurl the curses already lining up at the edge of his tongue. Instead, he gave a simple nod.

“Is it common for you to sleep in the same bed as your master?” he asked, raising a brow.

Victoria’s expression turned playful. She fluttered her eyelashes, her tone coy. “Whatever could you mean, young master? I would never share a bed with someone who wasn’t the young master.”

Luke stared at her, blinking slowly.

‘Does she think I’m stupid? She’s obviously acting...’

He was about to call her out when her expression shifted. In an instant, the air thickened. Victoria’s eyes hardened as she fixed him with a cold stare.

“Or perhaps you are trying to imply something, young master?”

A cold sweat broke out on Luke’s back. “N-No! Of course not!” he stammered, clearing his throat in an attempt to recover.

“Boy, I sure am hungry… How about we get some breakfast?” he said quickly, making for the door. Internally, he was cursing.

‘Damn it. I will never understand women...’

After experiencing her mood swings more than once, Luke had all but given up trying to make sense of Victoria. For now, he would keep her at arm’s length. Until she truly became his subordinate, he couldn’t afford to trust her completely.

When he arrived at the dining hall, he found Kayson and Sebastian already seated, waiting for him.

“Oho, someone’s looking a little sleep-deprived,” Kayson said, grinning. “Looks like you had fun last night.”

He threw Luke a knowing look, accompanied by several exaggerated winks.

Luke ignored him. It was the only way to stay sane.

Victoria appeared in the dining hall wearing the same coy expression she’d worn earlier. All three men turned to stare as she gracefully took a seat beside Luke, her cheeks tinged with a faint red.

‘This woman… What is she trying to do to my reputation?’

Luke sighed, resisting the urge to drop his face into his hands. At that moment, there was nothing he wanted more than to dig a hole and crawl inside.

It wasn’t until he finished breakfast that he finally began to feel like himself again.

After his duel in the arena yesterday—and the spectacle last night in the dining hall—his name had already spread across the academy estate like wildfire. Wherever he went, students whispered in hushed voices or avoided his gaze entirely.

‘Thank you, Michael,’ Luke mused, a small smile tugging at his lips.

Now that he had defeated the Marquess’s son, no one in the academy was foolish enough to provoke him. For the time being, it seemed things might remain peaceful.

“It’s a bit early for classes. What are you planning to do this morning?” Kayson asked, curiosity lighting his tone.

Luke’s smile grew brighter. “I have some light reading to do in the library.”

“Light reading?” Kayson frowned. “That sounds boring.”

“Knowledge is invaluable, my friend,” Luke said, lifting his chin in mock arrogance. “Only those who dedicate themselves to its pursuit may one day hold the world in their palm.”

“Huh? What kind of crap are you spouting?”

Luke ignored him and turned to Sebastian. “Why don’t you take Kayson through some training this morning while I visit the library?”

“Yes, young master.”

Before Kayson could protest, Sebastian’s hand clamped down on his shoulder. The butler’s polite expression twisted into something far more sinister.

“You heard the young master,” Sebastian said smoothly. “Let’s get started.”

“L-Luke! Save me!” Kayson cried out, desperation in his voice as Sebastian dragged him away.

Luke didn’t spare him a second glance. He’d experienced Sebastian’s training firsthand and knew it was nothing short of torture. But Kayson had the advantage of being enhanced by the system. Only Sebastian could push him to his true limits.

As Luke made his way toward the library, Victoria fell into step behind him, keeping half a body length’s distance.

“Do you have any orders for me, young master?” she asked.

Luke gave a short nod. “I need you to stand watch while I browse. My reading technique is... rather innovative. I’d like to keep it hidden.”

“Understood. I shall kill anyone who approaches.”

“H-Hey, that’s not what I meant…” Luke stammered.

Thankfully, by the time they arrived, he’d managed to explain what he did mean—without any talk of murder.

‘I really need to start being more specific with her,’ Luke sighed inwardly. ‘She takes everything so literally.’

But the moment he stepped into the vast library, his mood soared.

Ten floors stretched above him. The first floor was dedicated to lecture rooms, but the remaining nine were filled with books—thousands upon thousands of them. An endless sea of knowledge.

As they ascended the staircase, Luke’s mind worked quickly.

‘The last time I tested my limits, I could absorb about a thousand books per day. But that was before my intelligence grade increased… Maybe I can manage more now.’

They reached the second floor, and Luke’s pace quickened. The entire space was packed with bookcases, each brimming with books. Near the entrance, a large directory board gleamed, making him smile in satisfaction.

“Can I help you, my lord?”

A male servant stationed on the first floor approached Luke with a respectful bow.

Luke paused for a moment before asking, “Could you give me a brief overview of the library floors?”

“Of course, my lord.” The servant straightened slightly, hands clasped behind his back. “On this floor, we store historical chronicles pertaining to the Marxx Kingdom, as well as public genealogies. The third floor houses philosophy and moral texts, while the fourth is dedicated to poetry and literature.”

As the servant spoke, Luke’s interest in the first four floors quickly faded. While those subjects might be worth browsing at his leisure, he wasn’t sure how long he’d be staying at the Royal Academy. Time was something he couldn’t afford to waste.

“The fifth and sixth floors cover medicine, astronomy, and engineering,” the servant continued. “You’ll also find military strategy and tactical manuals there. Copies of our treaties are available for review as well. As for the seventh floor, it contains texts on law and governance.”

Luke nodded to himself. He’d likely start on the fifth floor and work his way up. Even the seventh floor’s legal texts could be valuable if he ever needed to manage his own territory or command forces in the future.

But that thought he kept to himself.

“The eighth and ninth floors are off-limits to students,” the servant added, bowing his head slightly.

Luke raised an eyebrow. “And the tenth?”

“The tenth floor is the residence of the head librarian,” the man said quietly. “Very few have been invited there in the past fifty years.”

‘Fifty years? What is he, some old hermit?’ Luke mused.

“Thank you for your assistance. You’ve been quite helpful,” he said with a polite smile. “I’d like to head up to the fifth floor, if you don’t mind.”

“By all means, my lord.” The servant bowed again, gesturing for them to proceed.

Luke ascended the stairs, Victoria trailing close behind. They bypassed the next two floors without a second glance and arrived directly at the fifth.

Unlike the second floor, which had been cramped with bookcases, this room was much more open. At a glance, Luke estimated there were only around ten thousand books here.

But this posed another problem. Without shelves to obscure him, he was far more exposed while flipping through the tomes.

At least, for now, they were alone. That wouldn’t last during the day.

‘I’ll need to come early if I want to absorb knowledge from these books without interruptions,’ he thought.

“Victoria,” he said, glancing over his shoulder. “Stay by the entrance. Let me know if anyone comes nearby.”

“Yes, young master,” she replied, her tone calm and efficient as she moved to stand guard.

‘Alright… time to feed my brain.’

Luke strode over to a nearby directory and studied it closely, searching for the best place to begin. As the servant mentioned earlier, this floor contained texts on medicine, astronomy, and engineering.

Eastern medicine in this world might hold some merit, but Luke couldn’t see an immediate use for astronomy—at least not yet. Still, this wasn’t Earth. He couldn’t dismiss the subject entirely without giving it a chance.

‘Maybe I’ll start with engineering,’ he thought. ‘Since I understand the basics of some modern technology, this knowledge could help me with infrastructure in the future.’

Luke gave a firm nod. “I’ll start there,” he muttered, striding toward the section.

Luke made his way over to the designated section and found over two thousand books neatly lined up across several shelves. Letting out a steady breath, he pulled the first tome free and flicked through its pages.

Almost immediately, he noticed a difference.

Whether it was due to his increased intelligence—or something else entirely—the information was being extracted from the book much faster than usual. His heart leapt in excitement.

Placing his hand lightly on the bookshelf, Luke concentrated and tried to recall the contents of the tome he’d just scanned. As he suspected, it was all there. Not a single page missing. Every diagram, every instruction, every note—perfectly imprinted in his mind.

‘I should be able to cut down my reading time even more now.’

But as he stood there, something strange began to happen.

He felt information flowing freely into his mind without any effort on his part. Luke’s eyes widened in shock. Part of him wanted to analyze what was going on, but he was afraid that breaking his concentration would abruptly end the flow.

Irrigation systems. Flood control. Architectural plans for palaces, bridges, and roads. Metallurgy and materials engineering. The knowledge came in waves, filling his mind with complex concepts and practical applications.

It was as if the books were reading themselves to him… or directly downloading their contents into his brain.

He didn’t know how long it lasted, but eventually, a sharp jolt sent him stumbling back from the bookshelf.

Pain exploded behind his eyes. It felt like someone was stabbing his forehead over and over with a knife. His hands shot up to clutch his head as he gritted his teeth against the piercing headache.

“Young master, are you alright?” Victoria’s voice was close. She abandoned her post and ran to his side, catching him as his legs gave out.

At another time, he might have been embarrassed to be so close to her. But right now, he was too weak to care. He leaned into her support as she carefully guided him to a nearby chair and eased him down.

“What happened?” she asked, genuine concern in her voice.

“I… I’m not sure,” Luke managed, cradling his head in his hands. His thoughts were scattered, fragmented. He fought to piece them together.

The last time he felt like this was after absorbing a thousand books in preparation for the Military Academy exam. But this felt different… stronger. More dangerous.

“How long have we been up here?” he asked, his voice hoarse.

Victoria’s brows furrowed. “It’s only been ten minutes.”

‘Ten minutes?’ Luke’s heart skipped a beat. His eyes flew open in disbelief. It felt like he had absorbed the knowledge of thousands of books in that short time.

Still cradling his head with one hand, Luke pushed himself to his feet with the other and slowly made his way back to the bookshelf where it had all begun.

His gaze scanned the spines of the books in front of him.

And then he realized it.

He knew them. Every single one. He knew their titles, their content, every word printed on their pages. Engineering schematics, theoretical designs, material compositions—it was all there in his mind.

‘No way… I absorbed the contents of all these books?’ Luke thought, stunned.
