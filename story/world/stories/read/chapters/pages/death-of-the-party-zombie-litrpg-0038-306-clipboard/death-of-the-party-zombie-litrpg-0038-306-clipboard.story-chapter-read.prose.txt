Several large monitors flickered briefly, various data arrays updating or changing into detailed graphs. Chuck tapped away at a keyboard, his eyes unblinking as he took in the information provided. Fully focused on the pair currently off-world.

He didn’t even flinch when someone knocked at the door.

“Come in,” the Architect called out. He already knew who it was, as he had called them to the tower.

The door opened automatically, and Lucius stepped in. The Shade paused to take in the control room, and rubbed the side of his hooded head with the blunt end of a pen. “This room always stuns me on the rare occasion I see you here.”

“Part of the reason I called you,” Chuck replied. “Apologies. It’s quite late.”

A thumbs-up emoji appeared beside Lucius’ head as he sat down in a nearby chair. He placed a clipboard down carefully, avoiding Dent’s keyboard. “It’s no problem. I don’t sleep much, and my work is important. Especially when it’s you.”

With a dry smile, Chuck finally tore his eyes away from the screen to acknowledge the Shade. “It’s the same as last time. Too much stress and focus and I start feeling less of a person and more of a series of computer functions.”

Lucius nodded. “You always push yourself when the Outsiders are off on a mission. Dent isn’t around?” A question mark appeared in the air briefly.

“I sent him out for an event. Despite it being counterintuitive, sometimes it’s good to act as if things are normal.” He glanced back to the monitor. “Even if they aren’t.”

“Things are bad?”

Chuck sighed, his wireframe form flickering. “Not exactly. Theo is fine. Sally and others are… getting by. I don’t even know why I worry so much. Even after all these years, Sally getting injured grates on my nerves. Not that it bothers her.”

Lucius tilted his head to the side. “It was a simple matter of repeated exposure, wasn’t it?”

“It was. As soon as we gained the ability to respawn, Sally was out in the fields getting herself killed. She never shied away from being part of an event, even when her death was guaranteed. There's nobody in Sanctuary who has died more - or in as many ways - as she has. I have the records to prove it.” He shook his head in disbelief.

“Given their casual attitude toward death, it surprises me they never got bored of it all.”

“Things got weirder once Sally and Theo returned with their Aspect powers.” The Architect tapped his fingers on the desk. “Those two are both the best and worst pair to have control over life and death. I'm glad they are good people... or at least on our side. Good is subjective.”

"They are certainly inventive.”

Chuck wrinkled his face up, frowning as he recalled a memory. “Theo took the time to learn a spell that is especially good against undead. Specifically, skeletons. To the detriment of learning other abilities, he mastered it. A Turn Undead spell that can vaporize animated bones, even though using holy magic burns him. He then combined it with his Aspect of Death power and somehow turned it into a special attack that works on... any skeleton.”

The Shade sat in silence for a moment. “Do you mean skeletons still in use?”

“Correct.” Another silence filled the room. “And do you want to know the first thing he did with this horrific warcrime? Used it on Sally. She volunteered. I would have thrown up if I were capable.”

The pair individually imagined what a suddenly boneless Sally would look like. It was probably not the worst death the zombie had endured, but it was still uncomfortable. Unnatural.

“Limit testing is their thing,” Chuck concluded, breaking the illusion. “Sally wanted to know what sort of damage her Aspect of Life healing could recover. Definitely not her entire skeleton vaporizing in an instant, that is for sure. She can still be killed, but I understand why she received Life instead of Death - and not just because of her zombie raising. Sally is just too stubborn to die. She's always had an annoying doggedness when it came to persisting against the odds. Even in her current situation, a war of attrition is difficult to win against her.”

“They aren’t both so self-destructive, though.”

“Theo is more of a numbers man. Efficiency and the gritty workings of the System drive him.” The Architect glanced at the active monitors. “That said, he doesn't care for the administrative overview. Seeing behind the curtain would ruin his fun. He sees no use in exploiting the nuances of written reality if one knows all the rules. In many ways he is easier to deal with. He can be left to his own devices and other than some extra burden on mu part, he doesn’t get into much trouble. Not like Sally, who strives toward saving things.”

Lucius nodded. “We are talking a lot about them and not yourself, here. We have acknowledged in previous sessions that there is a lot of weight on your shoulders. How do you currently feel about your position?”

“My role is... constant.” The wireframe man sighed, but put on a wry smile. “Rewarding but never ending. A constant state of bringing order to chaos and then seeking out chaos for greater future order.”

“Just as driven as Sally and Theo.”

“Yes, I suppose I'm not unlike the pair. Less inclined to use violence, but we are guided by purpose. We're from the same Earth, after all.” He shrugged. “Perhaps that is part of it.”

The Shade made a few notes on his clipboard. “This role isn’t a continuation of the you left behind?”

“To be honest, my old life feels like a distant dream most days. I've gone from a delinquent diner cook to the overseer of an entire planet. The events of the last few years have certainly shaped me into the man I now am, of course. I couldn’t have done it without Dent or the Outsiders.”

“Is it fair to say that this feels more like your natural home, despite how strange it all is?”

Chuck nodded, and his smile softened into something more genuine. “Sally has been pestering about changing some of the language we use. Things like Unique, Players, or System-created are from a very... human-centric perspective. A callback to when this world seemed like an odd video game made real. Even as I sit here as an intangible being, real is an odd phrase, but feels right. This is now my reality, and I am fully accepting of it.”

The Shade tilted his head, but gestured for Chuck to continue.

“As for the naming conventions, she was keen on Adventurer for those who have traveled from worlds outside this System Universe. For sentient beings native to the Sea, Starborn was her suggestion.”

“Sounds pretty noble.” A sparkling star appeared beside Lucius’ head. “While most of my work is with the Adventurers that live in Sanctuary, I do occasionally see some Uniques—or Starborn—who question their sense of self. A unifying identity may help.”

“We haven't settled on anything yet. It's difficult to find a non-disparaging word that can label such a wide variety of beings. Most System worlds differ in the level of intelligence their created denizens exhibit. Few have those like yourself, or like the other Outsiders.”

“Is there a known reason for the difference?”

“Your guess is as good as mine. The Starborn on Sanctuary were originally thought of as bugged System Monsters. In some way, that is true—no offence—but there is something in your code... your soul, so to speak, that is beyond my reach and understanding. It's what makes you, you.” The Architect lifted his hands up in resigned apology.

There were still things outside of his reach. Something that both frustrated him, but pushed him toward improving. Learning more about the Sea, the System Universe, and all the various planets residing in it. How some creatures were two-dimensional and robotic on one world, but could be fully sentient and no different than a truly living being on another world was a mystery.

This written ‘soul’ was surely the difference. He long suspected that humans taken from Earth and put here were just rebuilt. The original was killed, and consciousness uploaded to a recreated body. Somewhere along the line, some Systems had fabricated the complexity of a living being and had applied it to created Monsters.

He blinked away his errant thoughts, seeing that he had left the patient Shade waiting in silence.

“There’s no idea, I suppose. These worlds are built on Adventurers gaining experience and skills, usually by killing the System-created.”

Lucious tapped his pen on the edge of the clipboard. “I’ve heard that you want to change that? Break the cycle.”

Chuck smiled and looked back at the monitors. “We’ll have to see about that.”

Sally rolled through the churned dirt, slashing her blade through the darkness and into the leg of her opponent. The Radoch shimmered and faded from view. She growled as she righted herself. “I can’t see which is the right bug.”

Humphrey swept his greatsword through two copies of the same large cockroach, and they both vanished as well. “Yes. What an annoying ability.”

The zombie’s eyes burned bright crimson as she glared around the opening. They were meant to catch up to Edward after running through a high-leveled Radoch along the way. It hadn’t been that simple, unfortunately.

Not only was the area thick with the bugs, but their leader had an ability to create holographic clones of himself. They had probably dispatched twenty of the identical roaches and hadn’t landed a hit on the actual one. In between the rest of the surging horde, it was difficult for her to get a thread on the lifeline of the one she really wanted to stab.

The potions were coming from Bully, but by the time they arrived Edward might be back on the Omen. At this crucial stage where they were right below on of the giant alien spaceships, she didn’t want to lose another Outsider. She hadn’t seen Archie or the shadow figure either.

She could feel the demon was still alive. The problem was that there was something else grating at the inside of her skull. Another problem that she didn’t quite understand.

“Let me take over here,” the Death Knight suggested. Dark energy whipped around his body, pushing back several assailants. “You are needed elsewhere.”

“You sure?” she asked, already knowing the answer.

“I just need to get lucky once, and then an imposed duel will end this parlor trick.” The red flames behind his helmet rose as he shot her a skeletal grin.

Sally nodded. “I’ll leave you with a parting gift then, Pops.”

Despite the heat of battle, a chilling breeze swept through the treeline and circled the clearing the pair were fighting in. A thick silence followed it, the pressure of the air dulling the sounds of alien chittering and the clang of weaponry.

Zombie Apocalypse shook the ground as scores of hands burst from the mud, reaching up into the sky. As her zombie army crawled from the earth, Sally hopped around the distracted Radoch forces, cutting down two more images as well as three low-ranked bugs.

Sally slipped into the darkness of night, allowing her zombies and Humphrey to take the attention of the Radochs. Getting away from the fray should be a relief after all the fighting and lack of brain eating she had been doing for hours.

But as she hopped through the shaded terrain and got closer to where Edward was, she couldn’t shift the heavy weight in her stomach.

Something terribly evil was ahead.
