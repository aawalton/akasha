Remember, Xaiver, you’re not alone.

Xavier took his hand off Bones’s hilt. It had been some time since the soul bound weapon had spoken to him without him speaking to it first. It had become more and more silent over the years, a voice in the background, something he barely paid attention to.

Something told him that was his fault.

Standing in the middle of Queen Aecia’s throne room, looking at the projection showing the three-million strong armada, Xavier frowned.

I can’t trust the other Champions.

I’m not talking about the other Champions.

Xavier glanced over at Queen Aecia and her entourage of ten A Grades, then thought of the twenty billion Denizens that were on this world…

Yes, I suppose you’re right.

But how could he best use them to his advantage, especially as he wanted to gain as many Mastery Points as he possibly could from this floor?

That was why he was here, after all—to get to B Grade as fast as possible. Bringing anyone else along would just mean he’d have to share in the Mastery Points.

He shut his eyes. Saw Palini, dead on a stone floor.

And what if he just got them killed?

Still…

Xavier expanded the time dilation field, once more encompassing the queen. “Enlisting the other Champions isn’t going to work.”

Queen Aecia locked eyes with him a long moment. Her gaze looked as though it was trying to pierce straight into his mind. “You are sure of this? You spoke to every one of them?”

“I’m sure enough. Apparently, no Champion ever fights on your side, not after the System gives them the choice.”

The queen cursed, and she didn’t sound quite so queenly as she did.

“Then we are doomed,” said the tall, elven mage. “We are in worse shape than when we summoned the Champions. All this has done is give the enemy more warriors…”

Xavier pursed his lips. He couldn’t help but wonder about the near infinite number of times this had played out in the past. Since the dawn of the Tower of Champions in his own universe, this floor had existed. And every time a Champion had made it all the way to the seven-hundred-and-fiftieth floor, the vast majority of them had chosen to fight for the armada.

Perhaps there had been a brave few who’d chosen to fight for the queen, but if any of them had won, it certainly wasn’t common knowledge.

They always get defeated, don’t they? They’ve probably never survived this war. That armada can’t get through the shield by themselves—the bastard System is the only reason these people end up losing.

And why? Because they were a peaceful society?

“I’m not going to let you lose,” Xavier said.

Perhaps he should have said something less definitive, like, I’ll do what I can to help you win. But that wasn’t the type of person Xavier was.

The queen raised an eyebrow. “Your time dilation field is formidable, but the enemy will have time mages of their own. Once they identify yours, it won’t be long before it’s countered. Besides, we won’t be able to fight inside that field for long. The enemy will have the advantage. Our spells will get stuck in cooldown the moment we use them, yet every new ship worth of troops they teleport down will be come in fresh.”

Xavier smiled.

One low-level A Grade and a hundred-thousand B Grades per ship… you know, that doesn’t sound so bad if I can fight them one ship at a time—especially if you take the other Champions out of the equation.

“Oh, cooldowns won’t be a problem. And we won’t be waiting for them to come down here.”

Xavier manifested eleven Time Pocket Stream spell patterns simultaneously. It was a great deal to maintain all at once, and he felt the strain on every single one of his energy channels, but it was the quickest way to illustrate a point.

“What did you—” the tall elven mage started.

“Cast a spell, any spell, and watch,” Xavier said. “Trust me.”

“Why?” the queen asked.

He shrugged. “What other option do you have?”

Every one of the queen’s entourage looked to her. The queen didn’t take long to make her decision. She nodded slightly. Spells were cast. Eyes widened.

“How did you…”

Xavier raised his chin. “I told you, I’m not exactly an ordinary time mage.” He spread his wings. “In fact, I’m a hell of a lot more than that.” In his mind’s eye, he saw the lead ship of the armada. “I’m going to take the fight to the enemy, before the other Champions fully arrive, before they choose to fight for the Empire of Servitude, before the System allows any of the enemy to teleport down to your world.” He looked at each of them. “Now, would you like to stay here, or come along?”

~

Xavier hovered in space above Galadsea’s force field. Behind him were four elves from the queen’s entourage. The tall elven mage, Brarri. The elven woman in full-plate armour and a long, thin sword in hand, Yalia. A female elven archer in close-fitting leathers with a longbow as large as she was tall by the name of Evali.

And last but not least, Queen Aecia herself.

Many of her entourage had begged her to remain on the planet. The queen had refused.

But she hadn’t taken every one of her number, just in case this “insane plan” didn’t work. Before they’d left, Xavier had brought up evacuation plans—that was what happened on the fifth floor, after all. But Queen Aecia had shaken her head in dismay.

The System had forbidden them from escaping.

Xavier was getting real pissed off at the System’s choices regarding these people. They’d lived in peace for two million years, with no one knowing the location of their planet, with force fields that should have protected them even if they were found, and here the System was.

Interfering.

Messing all that up.

All it wants is conflict, Xavier thought. Well, you’re going to have one with me soon enough.

He put thoughts of the System out of his mind as he stared at the lead Empire of Servitude’s ship. Something was niggling at him. A moral imperative at the back of his mind telling him that the people onboard that ship might be innocent but for their signing of a contract.

Killing them might not be the only solution to this floor.

Freeing them from their contracts, on the other hand…

But they had signed those contracts, and freeing them wouldn’t help him gain Mastery Points, so he shoved that voice back down deep in his mind. This wasn’t his universe.

He couldn’t save everyone.

Xavier and the four elves were connected to the same Communication Stone channel.

Queen Aecia: [So, you’ve taken us this far. What’s the next step?]

Xavier: [I want to see how strong their shielding is.]

Brarri: [You really think you can break through?]

They’d spoken, back in the throne room, about the ships’ defences. The Queendom of Serrilia hadn’t simply waited while the armada approached. An army had already been sent out to greet their enemies long before the ships had reached the planet.

Every single one of them had died, and not one of them had been able to pierce through the shielding around the enemy ships.

Xavier had wondered why they’d sent ships in the first place. Not that he didn’t love the idea of massive spaceships shooting between star systems and galaxies, but with the ability to simply use a portal it made him wonder why it was necessary in the first place.

Queen Aecia had explained that they’d set up a portal lock System for several hundred light years around them. No one could simply portal into their space. The only way to get to their planet was to fly into it.

And the best way to do that was on a ship.

Xavier: [If I can’t, then we’re going to have to fight them back on the planet. Something tells me no one wants it to come to that.]

The four elves looked at him expectantly. He could feel their spiritual senses pushing against him, trying to discern what he was going to do. He wasn’t used to the pressure of an A Grade’s spiritual sense, let alone four, but he wasn’t about to let them know it unnerved him.

Xavier examined the lead ship’s shielding. It covered the entire ship—there wasn’t even a handy vent that he could fly into. His Immortal Soul lens was able to discern the runes that helped power the force field, but they were, of course, inside the field. There was no way for him to edit them.

Even Strike Out couldn’t bypass a shield as powerful as this one, so that plan was thrown out the window.

His Immortal Soul lens wasn’t powerful enough to peer through the force field either. That wasn’t a surprise, even if it was frustrating—he hadn’t been able to peer through the steel walls back in Arrack’s dungeon either, not until he’d edited the runes.

Xavier’s wings flapped lazily, making his body hover up and down as his thoughts swirled.

A smile slipped onto his lips.

What if they didn’t take this into account?

Xavier cast Soul Strike. He couldn’t pinpoint where the enemies on the ship were, but he was sure he would hit at least a few of them. On the last floor he’d completed, he’d gone up against a thousand-strong pack of Blood Wyverns, and so that’s what his soul apparitions appeared as.

The dragon-like beasts flew through space, maws opening wide in silent roars, revealing their many sword-long, needle-sharp teeth.

With The All-Seeing Eye, he observed the others’ reactions to his sudden use of a reaper spell—each of the elves hovered back from him slightly. Except for Queen Aecia. The woman held her ground.

Still, he felt a tinge of fear coming from her.

Most Denizens tended to be squeamish around those who could reap their souls.

Xavier didn’t blame them.

The soul apparitions soared toward the ship. When they hit the force field they didn’t phase through it as they should have—not once had he ever seen a soul apparition affected by the physical world—instead, they dissolved on impact, the soul energy that comprised them scattered until it was so thin it disappeared from even his Immortal Soul lens-empowered spiritual sense.

In order to allow the soul apparitions entry to the ship—what he hoped would be entry—he’d expanded his time dilation field. He’d only done it incrementally, however. Just far enough for them to reach the shielding at first.

Brarri: [I told you that you wouldn’t be able to pierce it. That shielding is almost as strong as our own. Maybe even stronger, as it doesn’t have to protect an entire planet.]

Xavier’s lips formed a thin line. That was only his first test.

He threw powerful spell after powerful spell at the force field, all the while examining it with his Immortal Soul lens, trying to discern if it possessed any weaknesses.

Nothing got through. Nothing even dented it a little bit.

He’d never encountered shielding like this before, but it made him very curious as to how he might create it himself. If he could protect his own planets with shielding like this…

One step at a time.

While none of the spells he manifested ended up being effective against the shielding, Xavier had a lot more tricks up his sleeve.

The elves watching him were starting to glance at each other more frequently. Whatever confidence he’d gained with them seemed to be running out as they watched him fail over and over again.

Xavier simply chalked that up to the fact that they weren’t used to the freedom of being inside a time dilation field. It gave one time to test things. He didn’t have to bring out the big guns first thing, and while he didn’t manage to break through the shield, every spell he cast against it taught him something new.

That knowledge was more than worth the effort.

When he’d paused after sending that last spell, Queen Aecia gave him a piercing look. [It’s time to return to the planet. If you can’t penetrate their shields, we’re going to have to do this a different way.]

Xavier didn’t reply. His focus was on the energy within the force field. The energy was a mix of Intelligence Energy, Toughness Energy, and Spirit Energy. There was even a bit of Willpower Energy thrown into the mix.

Several parts of his mind had been studying the energy the entire time, and what he’d quickly discovered was that it was indeed similar to Galadsea’s shielding. The energy that comprised the shields came from the Denizens on the ships.

Their power strengthened the shielding. He could feel the different, unique energy signatures.

Most of the energy was B Grade energy, of course, as the ship only contained a single A Grade, yet the shielding was more powerful than any A Grade he’d met could create on their own.

Looks like I’m not the only one who has thought about the power of unity after all.

That wasn’t the only thing he’d discovered about the shield as he’d been examining it, of course.

He delved deeper and deeper into the energy that powered the shield, scattering it at a single point. He’d also contracted his time dilation field around this point, stopping any more energy from the rest of the shield from reaching it.

That was something he’d discovered when he threw spells at the shield. While he hadn’t damaged the shield, every single spell he’d thrown at it had killed off some of the energy that powered it—just not enough. Energy from other parts of the shield simply rushed to fill in the gaps.

Not that he’d actually made a gap through the whole shield yet. That was what he was focused on doing here.

He was careful with how much Soul Energy he used, but he’d become a lot more adept at scattering energy since the first time he’d done it against Arrack’s Locked Down spell. He could do it far more efficiently than he’d imagined.

Bit by bit, he dissipated the energy in a single spot, creating a hole in the shield that the rest of its energy wasn’t able to fill with the active time dilation field preventing it from reaching that spot.

The hole wasn’t large enough for a Denizen to fit through, but it didn’t need to be.

Now that there was a gap—even if one so small it could only fit a needle—Xavier could reach through with his mind and manipulate the energy on the other side.

Queen Aecia: [Xavier Collins?]

Brarri: [Do not ignore my queen’s words!]

Yalia: [Why is he so silent? Is there… something wrong with him?]

Xavier threaded his time dilation field through the tiny gap in the shield he’d made.

He stretched the shield until it reached the runes etched into the ship’s metal hull. Tilting his head to the side, he began to make edits to the runes. He didn’t need to cast Strike Out for this, now that he could slip through the shield, he could directly manipulate the energy on the other side—all it cost him was Soul Energy.

And he had more than enough of that for the task at hand.

Xavier couldn’t help but smile. The edits didn’t take near as long as they would have with Strike Out and a stylus. Manipulating runes directly felt so much more natural, as though this was how it was always supposed to work. Instead of forcing the universe to do his will, he was having a conversation with it.

And the universe was listening.

The force field switched off.
