The atmosphere within Aodhán’s training room was tense with anticipation as Crixis, Sophran, and Darien watched Aodhán swallow one treasure after another, cringing with each blessed treasure that was absorbed without inspection.

"Aren't you supposed to at least inspect it?" Sophran asked, flustered.

Aodhán responded by picking up the Tideheart pearl—the treasure Crixis's parents had given him—and swallowing it. "I'll have more than enough time to inspect it with the drop of transformation Darien creates."

The pearl pulsed within his spirit in that moment, releasing a wave of energy that clashed with that of the other treasures. Aodhán grimaced as pain lanced through him and glanced up at Darien. "I'm ready. Do it."

Darien stared at Aodhán's dilated pupils and pale face—the effect of the energies clashing within his spirit—and asked, "Are you sure about this?"

"I am." Aodhán smiled up at him as a chaotic wave of energy rippled out of his body. He grimaced again, the pain tripling, and his gaze on Darien sharpened. "I am sure, Darien. Now do it."

Darien didn't hesitate.

In the little time since Aodhán arrived in the special sect, he had done so much for them—giving them a better chance at gaining familiars, and now this tempest skill. This was the least he could do in return, and he wasn't about to fail his newest comrade.

With a snap of his fingers, he conjured a larger storm cloud into existence and saturated it with energy till it exploded with rain.

"Careful," Sophran muttered beside him, aware of the step he was about to take next, and he nodded.

"Always."

Heat erupted from within him a moment later, and three seals suddenly went dark, their quintessence rushing into his spirit to join the river of chaos pouring through him. Willpower joined the unstable merge an instant later, and Darien shuddered with the strain of keeping it all together.

Willpower and quintessence clashed like shattered mirrors within his spirit, unwilling to merge, pulling away even as chaos pulled them together—the swirling ball of gold and black turning iridescent as he funneled more chaos into it.

With each infusion, the mixture stabilized further until it shone pitch black, flashes of gold and violet gleaming intermittently. But even stabilized, the energy was not easy to manage. It pressed down on his spirit like a block of concrete—viscous, nearly solid—and yet as light as air, threatening to burst apart at the slightest mishap.

Darien couldn't imagine the insanity that had pushed Aodhán to create such a technique, but he couldn't deny its power any more than he could deny his own existence.

Despite his many attempts to do just that.

He locked gazes with Aodhán once more—the boy who had shown up with war and chaos in his wake—and realized in that moment that though they might all be deemed special, this boy was more than special. And his actions right now could serve as a defining moment for his future, for his path…

His ideal sang at the thought of it, the mantra welling up in his spirit like a song.

I AM PRESENT AT BECOMING.

He smiled, and without hesitation, channeled all that energy through {Sanctum}—the cognitive skill he'd gained to escape his father's abuse.

Energy rushed into his mind like a tide, swarming and flooding it in a wave of power so intense the world stilled. Time slowed to a crawl; space froze, and the world itself exploded with clarity. The colors grew brighter. Sounds grew sharper—so sharp he could hear the fizz of electricity on Aodhán's hair. The scent of sweat and musk filled the air, suffocating in its intensity, and even with his mouth closed, he could almost taste the chaotic energy rippling out of Aodhán.

He felt like a god standing before mortals, and his smile widened as reality itself began stretching into infinity, colors bleeding into each other in a mosaic of delirious art.

But that sense of overwhelming power only lasted a few seconds before the strain on his mind set in, like the crushing pressure of the trial mountain itself. He let go of the energy immediately, channeling it all through the storm cloud above and into a single raindrop.

The rain stilled immediately, the cloud settled, and from within it, a single luminescent drop descended, as bright as a sun and as powerful as any blessed treasure Darien had ever seen.

"By the storm," Sophran breathed beside him, eyes wide. "It's like a treasure in itself."

Darien smiled. "It is a treasure, Sophran, but it's not yours." He directed the raindrop to Aodhán's forehead and smiled. "It's for Aodhán."

The drop splashed against Aodhán's forehead, and he shuddered at the impact.

"It's cold—" he began, but never finished the sentence as raw, golden-white lightning suddenly zapped into his mind. His eyes widened, dilated pupils bulging. His body jerked, his muscles spasmed, his breath locked in his chest.

And then he stilled—a stillness so absolute that to all watching, he seemed almost to have died.

But Aodhán wasn't dead.

He was simply in a dream. Or perhaps a vision was more accurate.

A vision he'd seen before.

***

Aodhán floated in the sky dotted with golden clouds, but just like the last time he'd had this vision, the colors were all wrong—bleeding into each other like a bad watercolor painting.

Below him, however, was a grand city of silver and gold he now recognized as Sunstone. Golden spires rose into the sky like needles, tips gleaming in defiance.

But Aodhán knew these were the last rays of sunlight those spires would catch.

True to his thoughts, a man clad in a silver military uniform appeared before him a moment later, white hair billowing in a nonexistent breeze. The man turned to stare at him, runic irises gleaming, but rather than fear as he'd once done, Aodhán smiled.

"What am I to learn here, Az'marthon?"

Az'marthon smiled and simply turned around to begin his destruction on the sector below. Day turned to night as clouds gathered and darkened immediately. Lightning came next, and before long, the screams of thousands of individuals reached his ears.

Massive beams of lightning smashed down, ripping spires and domes asunder in a tempest so powerful reality itself seemed to weep in agony. It was even more brutal than Aodhán remembered, and when it was done, what was left of the once-beautiful city was rubble and scrap metal.

Az'marthon turned to look at him then, his smile replaced with an expression of tempestuous fury as he said, "I am the tempest. I rage. That is my path. And that path is inside you."

The vision ended, and another immediately took its place—this one unfamiliar, or perhaps, forgotten.

He stood by the window of a vaguely familiar house this time, watching the patter of rain against glass alongside a woman whose face he couldn't make out. He couldn't have been more than four years old in the memory, and with each clap of thunder, the terror in his expression was obvious.

"Why is it raining, Mother?" he asked the woman, a discontent expression on his face. "I was just about to go out to play. Wasn't the sun just shining?"

"Well, it certainly was, young one." His mother chuckled. "But storms come out of nowhere these days."

His younger self frowned. "It's playing hide-and-seek. That's me and Father's game."

His mother laughed. "No, young one, the storm isn't playing hide-and-seek. It's just unpredictable."

"Unpedicabu?"

His mother laughed. "Darling. It means you never see it coming."

The vision stilled, and then his younger self stared straight at him, eyes shining with a wisdom transcending that of any child. "I am the storm," he said. "I am unpredictable. That is my path. And that path is inside you."

Just like the first, this vision also vanished, and another took its place.

This time, it was a vision of his battle against Varéc in the Willowood mansion—chaos rippling out of Varéc's sinuous form in a wave so thick it was almost solid. At the end of the vision, Varéc turned to him and growled, "I am the storm. I am chaotic. That is my path. And that path is inside you."

That vision also faded, and then a fourth appeared, but this time it wasn't exactly a vision. Instead, he found himself in a white room, legs crossed in meditation. Before him were three paths—raging tempest, unpredictability, and chaotic storm—highlighted in vivid bright colors.

Aodhán stared at the three paths, only just understanding many of the things that had happened in his life so far. All those times when he'd been battling in uncontrollable, overwhelming rage, he'd apparently just been fighting against the influence of Az'marthon's path. So hell-bent he had been on differentiating himself from the slaughterer that he'd unconsciously rejected the man's path too.

The path of chaotic storm was one he'd also experienced many times—mostly at times when he was fighting in sync with Varéc, like his first battle in Abyssos against the Nightmaw Reavers. All the skills and titles he'd gained from those battles proved it.

Chaos-Clad Butcher? Synchronized Carnage?

Their names alone showed their alignment.

And then there was the path of unpredictability, the path he'd chosen for himself long before he could even spell the word "storm." A path his mother had taught him. A path that had played a predominant role in his approach to the storm.

But "predominant" didn't mean "full," and all along, it seemed his spirit had been straining to follow the truths of three different paths at the same time. He couldn't continue like this. He either had to pick one or—

Aodhán blinked as a fourth path flickered into existence—midnight black against the white room.

The path of authority.

He frowned at the path, eyes twitching in confusion. But it soon became obvious that this wasn't a path his spirit had trod before. It was an option—one his spirit was asking him to explore, and the moment he accepted the idea, another vision appeared, or perhaps a series of visions: glimpses of his life mashed together in a simulation of what would have happened had he been walking the path of Authority.

The first instance was a memory he could never forget. The tribulation he'd experienced in Abyssos. And in this vision, he unfortunately got to relive it.

Lightning struck him down, shredding flesh and smiting bone. Muscles dried up, blood pooled, and bones cracked. The pain was unbearable, just as he remembered. His vision swam, and his mind stuttered. However, just before the world could go black, Aodhán felt a sudden stillness—not raw power, but authority, a control so absolute it forced every facet of the storm itself to obey him.

He was the seat of influence, and even a tribulation feared that influence. It grew tame instantly, seemingly shuddering at the violation of his presence. Several other instances played out after that, visions of the storm destroying or creating according to his will.

It was a powerful path, one that would grant him total control of the storm itself. No longer would he have to react to its unpredictable whims, its chaotic outbursts, or its raging temper. No longer would he have to fear it; rather, the storm would fear him. He would be its ruler, controlling it with an iron fist.

With this path, he could tame the storm.

But Aodhán didn't want to tame the storm.

He wanted to ride it, to fear it, to feel it, to respect it, and to fight alongside it. He didn't want to bend it to his will but work alongside it. He wasn't a tyrant, and he had no aspirations to become one.

Yet there was no denying just how powerful this path was. The control it afforded alone was astounding, and to give it up totally would be foolish. The storm was a difficult element—complex beyond compare. He needed that control.

And yet, he didn't want so much control as to render the storm completely spineless.

Undecided, he glanced at the path of authority and then the others.

The way forward couldn't have been clearer. He needed to pick one.

Yet he hesitated.

He glanced at the four paths again, contemplating, and that was when he realized something odd. The paths weren't parallel to each other. They were slanted, meaning they were on track to intersect.

They were converging.

His ideal sang within him at that, and Aodhán remembered the name of his icon.

The Prime Convergence. It couldn't be a coincidence.

Eyes wide, he jumped to his feet and began running, electricity crackling beneath his feet as he tracked the lines to find the point where all four paths intersected. He ran and ran and ran… and then he found it—an intersection of paths, merging into a single iridescent path so colorful it seemed like a rainbow.

The path of RUINOUS AUTHORITY.

Visions of this iridescent path flashed across his mind—law, dominance, rage, destruction, creation, power, control—and he smiled.

It seemed he had found his path after all.

His own anvil of ruin.
