The first crack of Rufus’s fire whip sent Gavin stumbling backward, tripping over debris in the crumbling cathedral.

He didn’t feel the impact when his back hit the wall. He couldn’t. It was as if his entire body had gone numb with terror.

Rufus stood framed by the cathedral’s broken archway, emerald corruption pulsing beneath his chitinous skin like toxic veins. The crown's influence made his familiar sneer monstrous, eyes flickering between human brown and sickly green.

“Well, look what I found,” Rufus drawled, twirling the molten whip between his carapaced index finger and thumb. “Our little lost pup, all dressed up with nowhere to run.”

Gavin’s heart hammered against his ribs. His silk ascot suddenly felt like a noose, choking off both air and courage. Despite the chill of the ancient stone room, sweat plastered his expensive shirt to his skin.

“I... I’m not...” The words died in his throat.

It was happening again—just like every time he’d faced Rufus since Thornbridge. His muscles locked. His thoughts scattered. His body betrayed him completely.

Rufus’s laugh echoed against the cathedral walls. “Not what? Not afraid? Not running?” He sauntered closer, emerald light casting grotesque shadows across his face. “Save the lies, pup. I can smell your fear from here.”

He wasn’t wrong.

Shame washed over Gavin as he recognized his own scent—bitter with terror and soured with his own pitiful submission. The beast inside him, normally so eager to emerge under stress, had retreated to the darkest corner of his being. At least it was honest about the cowardice that plagued him.

“The others,” Gavin managed to choke out. “I need to help them. Please, just let me go.”

“Do you want to help because you care, or because you’re trying to overwrite your mistakes?” Rufus asked, ignoring his plea. “Do you really think it works like that? That you can ignore the blood debt you owe me and the Stone Britches?”

The questions hit like battling rams against his chest.

Memories flooded back—moonlight streaming through broken windows, blood-slick cobblestones, screams that ended too abruptly. The sickening copper smell of death coating his tongue and lips. The horror on Bernard’s face when he found Gavin, naked and human again, surrounded by carnage he couldn’t remember causing.

“I… I didn’t mean to,” Gavin whispered, the words as hollow now as they’d been then.

“Oh, so it was just an accident? Alright, then. All is forgiven. Let me just go ahead and tell that to the crew.” Rufus spat on the floor. “Three of our best, pup. You tore them apart like fucking paper dolls, and you call it an accident?” His eyes narrowed. “Does Cade really know? Does that pretty little siren who makes eyes at you know what happens when you lose control?”

Gavin felt his knees grow weak, his shoulders hunching as if to make himself smaller and less of a target.

“No answer?” Rufus smirked. “I didn’t think so. You’re a coward, Gavin. Always have been. You’re hiding behind those fancy clothes and that fancy accent, but anyone who knows you can see the truth.”

The fire whip lashed out without warning. Gavin tried to dodge, but his leaden limbs refused to respond quickly enough. Flame scored across his chest, burning through expensive fabric to sear the flesh beneath. He staggered with a cry, the pain white-hot and familiar.

“That’s for Thornbridge, ya mutt,” Rufus growled.

Another lash, this one catching Gavin’s right shoulder, spinning him halfway around.

“That’s for betraying Bernard.”

A third, wrapping around his left forearm before he could pull away, the enchanted flame burning through fabric to scorch skin and muscle beneath. The smell of his own cooking flesh filled his nostrils.

“And that’s just because I never liked you, you pretentious little shit.”

Gavin collapsed to his knees, trembling. Across the cathedral, the sounds of battle continued—Cade fighting Bernard near the ritual circle, Nora forcing back shadows from some god of the abyss, and Elena and Evie battling corrupted minions. Through tear-blurred vision, he glimpsed Chubbs straining against her magical bonds, golden light pulsing around her small form.

His friends were fighting—fighting and possibly dying—while he cowered before his tormentor.

Rufus circled him slowly, savoring his victory. “Bernard told me to bring you back alive, but he didn’t specify what condition.” His voice dripped with a cruel sort of anticipation. “I’m thinking mostly alive should do it.”

Gavin’s gaze drifted to the shredded remains of his left sleeve. The burn underneath was already blistering, weeping clear fluid that mingled with blood where the whip had cut deepest. The pain should have been overwhelming, but somehow it felt distant, unimportant.

“Nothing to say?” Rufus prodded, giving Gavin a contemptuous kick. “Where’s that smart mouth now, pup? The one that had all those clever remarks about my intelligence? About my breeding?”

“Please,” Gavin whispered, hating himself for the plea. “Just let me go.”

“Oh, if only you could ask the dead you’ve left behind if they’d let you go. Go own, ask them.” Rufus placed a muddy boot against Gavin’s Rufus face and smashed it into the floor. “Ask ‘em, you furry fuck. Ask Morgan. Ask his sister who had to identify what was left of his face.” Rufus’s voice hardened. “Ask his kids who don’t have a father anymore because you couldn’t control your fucking nature.”

Each word landed with the weight of a mountain. Gavin’s shoulders hunched further, his gaze dropping to the stone floor. The guilt was suffocating, a weight he’d carried for months without respite.

“You know what the worst part is?” Rufus continued, crouching to bring his face level with Gavin’s. “Bernard gave you a chance. He took you in, taught you, even after he knew what you were. And how did you repay him? By turning on your own crew.”

“It was the full moon,” Gavin mumbled weakly. “I told him... I warned him I couldn’t—”

The back of Rufus’s hand cracked across his face, the emerald energy leaving a searing brand on his cheek.

“Excuses!” Rufus snarled. “Always fucking excuses with you.” He grabbed Gavin’s ascot, yanking him forward until their faces were inches apart. “You think these fancy clothes make you something? You think running with Stormhollow’s crew changes what you are?”

Across the cathedral, a cry of pain cut through the din—Evie’s voice, high and clear despite the chaos. Gavin’s head snapped up, eyes searching desperately until he found her. The siren was cornered by two of Bernard’s corrupted minions, her staff creating a barrier that was rapidly failing as they pounded against it with emerald-laced weapons.

“Evie,” he whispered, something stirring deep within his chest.

“Oh, is that her name?” Rufus’s lips curved in a malicious smile. “The little songbird? Maybe after I’m done with you, I’ll pay her a visit. Bernard might even let me keep her, once this is all over.”

The words hung in the air between them, vile and explicit. Instead of crushing Gavin further, they ignited something—a spark of rage buried beneath the fear and guilt.

Another scream echoed through the cathedral, this one unmistakably Cade’s—a sound of such raw anguish it cut through everything else. Gavin’s gaze snapped toward the ritual circle just in time to see Bernard drive an emerald lance through Cade’s chest.

“NO!” The word tore from Gavin’s throat. It was a primal, unhinged, sound.

Rufus followed his gaze, and satisfaction spread across his corrupted features. “Looks like your new boss is about to meet Morgan and the others. Shame. I was looking forward to making him suffer properly.”

Time seemed to slow. Gavin watched Cade collapse, blood pooling beneath him on the ancient stone. He saw Chubbs struggling against her bindings, her small face contorted with rage and terror. He saw his friends—his family—fighting and falling while he knelt in submission before a man he’d feared for too long.

Something deep within Gavin’s core shifted. Not the beast—not yet—but something more fundamental. A realization, crystallizing with sudden, perfect clarity.

Rufus had been right about one thing.

Clothes didn’t make the man beneath. But neither did mistakes. Neither did debt. Neither did the darkness lurking inside, waiting for the full moon to emerge.

What made a man was his choices.

“The stars don’t care what you’ve done,” Evie had told him once, her voice gentle yet firm as they sat by a campfire. “They only care what you choose to be.”

What did he choose to be?

The cathedral trembled as Bernard’s ritual reached some critical juncture. Stone cracked. Dust rained from damaged arches. At the center of it all, an otherworldly tree began pushing through a dimensional tear, its branches glowing with emerald light.

Rufus glanced nervously toward the phenomenon, momentarily distracted from his prey. “Looks like my fun is over. Bernard will want to take you—”

His words cut off as Gavin’s hand shot up, catching the debt collector’s wrist in a grip suddenly strong enough to make bone creak.

“What the—” Rufus began, surprise replacing cruelty.

“I’m not going anywhere with you,” Gavin said in an eerily calm voice that didn’t quite feel his own. “Not today. Not ever again.”

The change began subtly—fingernails lengthening into claws, canines extending past his lower lip, muscles tightening beneath his burned skin. Not the full shift—not yet—but enough that his burns began to knit closed, enough that his senses sharpened beyond human limits.

“The fuck you think you’re doing?” Rufus snarled, trying to pull away, genuine alarm replacing his previous confidence.

Gavin rose slowly to his feet, still gripping Rufus’s wrist. Bones cracked as they rearranged themselves beneath his skin, and his entire frame expanded with new strength.

“I’m making a choice,” he said simply.

The whip lashed out again, but this time Gavin saw it coming. His free hand shot up, catching the blazing cord mid-strike. Fire seared his palm, but the accelerated healing of his partial shift countered the damage nearly as fast as it formed.

With a savage yank, he pulled Rufus off-balance, sending him staggering forward. In the same motion, Gavin’s fist connected with the debt collector’s jaw. His enhanced strength drove the man back several steps. But more than that, the impact sent shivers down his spine and a wild grin carved itself into his lupine features.

Rufus recovered quickly. With a flick of his wrist, emerald energy flared around his hands. When the flames condensed, he was holding a pair of fiery daggers.

“So the pup found his teeth,” he sneered, though the bravado couldn’t mask the uncertainty Gavin could now see in his eyes. “You forget who taught you to fight?”

“Bernard taught me the basics,” Gavin acknowledged, circling now, his movements fluid and predatory. “But life taught me the rest.”

The debt collector lunged, daggers slashing in a deadly arc. Gavin sidestepped, and he allowed the blades to pass inches from his face. The movement felt natural, instinctive—as if his body had finally remembered how to move without his mind getting in the way.

This is who I am, the telepath thought as he moved.

“You think this changes anything?” Rufus spat, blood trickling from a split lip. “You think one lucky punch pays your debt? You still killed our crew. You still betrayed Bernard.”

The flashes of memory cascaded once more through his mind. And with them came clarity. Painful, alarming, clarity. He remembered that night. He could still taste the blood coating his teeth. He knew what their flesh had felt like to rip apart.

But once he pushed past the wall of guilt clouding his thoughts, he could see clearly for what felt like the first time in months. How had he let things go on like this? Why hadn’t he fought back?

Why hadn’t he used the truth?

“Is that what he told you?” Gavin asked, and that strange calm settled over him again despite the ongoing battle. “That I betrayed you all?”

“I was there!” Rufus snarled, attacking again with wild, rage-fueled strikes that Gavin easily evaded. “I saw what you did to them!”

“Did you?” Gavin pressed, catching Rufus’s wrist mid-strike and twisting until the dagger clattered to the stone floor. It exploded into cinders. “Or did you just see the aftermath?”

Something flickered across Rufus’s face—uncertainty, perhaps, or the ghost of a memory long suppressed.

“You weren’t in the room when it happened,” Gavin continued, and while he did, he reached out with his mind. Normally, he’d need a focal stone to connect to another mind, but that was just to simulate what he already had with Rufus: familiarity.

Gods above and below, did he know the distinct aura of his debt collector. He could practically dream of it thanks to how much the man had haunted his shadow. But with that excruciating familiarity came a door Gav had never considered before. All it would take—

There.

He slipped into the man’s surface thoughts without any trouble. Rufus’s mind was a mural of shattered glass. It throbbed and pulsed with emerald energy, but the slimy darkness had no other source than Rufus himself.

“You heard the screams. You saw the bodies. But you didn’t see what triggered it,” Gav pressed.

“Shut up!” Rufus stepped back quickly, creating distance between them. “It doesn’t matter what triggered it. You were the monster who tore them apart!”

“The job was infiltration,” Gavin said quietly, images unfolding in his mind as he finally allowed himself to remember what had been locked away for months. “We were supposed to observe only—Bernard’s direct orders. But Morgan had other ideas.”

Rufus’s attacks grew more frantic, less controlled. Emerald flames erupted from his hands in wild bursts that scorched the cathedral walls but missed their target as Gavin continued to move with newfound grace.

“Morgan wanted to impress Bernard,” Gavin continued, the truth settling into place like long missing puzzle pieces. “He wanted to show initiative. So he ordered the others to join him in confronting the cultists directly. Against the plan. Against Bernard’s explicit instructions.”

“LIAR!” Rufus hurled a concentrated ball of emerald flame that would have incinerated a normal man.

But Gavin wasn’t normal. Not anymore.

He dodged with preternatural speed, the fireball exploding against a stone pillar behind him. Dust and debris rained down, but Gavin didn’t flinch, nor did he take his eyes off his opponent.

“And when it all went wrong,” he pressed, advancing steadily, “when the cultists called for reinforcements and the Lifekeepers responded, who gave the order to abandon Morgan while he was still breathing? Who decided three lives were an acceptable loss?”

The color drained from Rufus’s face beneath the emerald corruption.

“Bernard,” Gavin said, the name falling like a stone between them. “Bernard left them to die. Not me.”

“You don’t know what you’re talking about,” Rufus insisted, but the conviction had drained from his voice. “You weren’t there when—”

“I was there in the only way that matters.” Gavin tapped his temple. “I can see it all, Rufus. Every sordid detail. Every calculation Bernard made about ‘acceptable losses.’ Every lie he told to keep the crew loyal afterward.”

His telepathy pushed deeper, past Rufus’s surface thoughts and to the memories beneath. He yanked on them without concern for what they’d do to the man’s mind. He pulled forth dozens of memories of Bernard’s cold assessment in the aftermath, of calculated decisions about which version of events would best serve his purposes. Rufus knew the truth. But like him, he just needed to be shown it.

“Bernard needed a scapegoat,” Gavin said, understanding blooming cold and clear in both their minds. “He needed someone to blame for his own poor leadership. And I was perfect—the outsider, the freak, the one nobody would defend.”

“Stop it!” Rufus clutched his head, as if he could physically block Gavin’s mental intrusion. “Get out of my head!”

“And you,” Gavin continued relentlessly, “you knew. You knew and you let him use me anyway. Because it was easier than admitting Bernard abandoned your friends to die.”

Rufus’s composure shattered completely. With a wordless howl of rage, he charged, abandoning skill for brute force. Emerald energy cloaked him like armor as he barreled forward, intent on ending the telepathic invasion by any means necessary. Gavin met him head-on, for he was no longer afraid.

For what cause does a wolf have to fear a rat?

Claws extended from his fingertips as he caught Rufus’s arms, stopping the charge through sheer physical power. They locked together, grappling at the edge of the dimensional tear, emerald light casting both their faces in sickly relief.

“I’ve carried guilt for months,” Gavin growled, his voice deepening as the change progressed further. “Guilt for deaths I caused but didn’t intend. What have you carried, Rufus? What burden have you shouldered for the people you willingly left behind?”

The question struck home. Rufus’s struggles grew desperate, panicked. “Let me go!” he snarled, emerald flames erupting from his skin in a last-ditch effort to break free.

But Gav’s hold on his arms was the least crushing grip he held on Rufus. For his telepathic fist was squeezing the Stone Britches’ crew member with all of his might.

“LET ME GO!” Rufus screamed.

“Not until you face the truth,” Gavin insisted, ignoring the flames that licked across his partially transformed arms. “Not until you admit what really happened.”

Their mental connection deepened—not by Gavin’s design, but by Rufus’s unraveling control. Images flooded between them: Bernard ordering the retreat while Morgan still called for help; Rufus hesitating at the doorway, looking back at his wounded comrade; Bernard’s hand on his shoulder, pulling him away; the cold calculation in Bernard’s eyes as he decided which version of events would be most useful.

“You left them,” Gavin said softly. “And you’ve been punishing me for your guilt ever since.”

Something broke in Rufus then—not just his resistance, but something deeper, more fundamental. The emerald energy surrounding him flickered and dimmed, revealing the human beneath the corruption.

“What choice did I have?” he whispered, the fight draining from him suddenly and completely. “Bernard was—is—the leader. You don’t disobey Bernard.”

Gavin’s grip lessened, though he didn’t release the debt collector. “There’s always a choice, Rufus. Always.”

Around them, the cathedral continued to tremble as the ritual neared completion. The dimensional tree had pushed further through the tear, its roots spreading beneath the ancient stones in a web of glowing emerald energy. The air itself felt heavy, charged with enough magic to press against Gavin’s enhanced senses like a physical weight.

“There’s always a choice, Rufus,” he repeated, watching realization dawn across the debt collector’s face. “And I’m giving you one now.”

Rufus stared back, the emerald corruption flickering beneath his skin as if uncertain whether to consume him entirely or retreat. “What choice?”

“Run,” Gavin said simply, releasing his grip. “Run from Bernard. Run from all of this. Or stay and face what’s coming.” He gestured toward the spreading roots, the crumbling cathedral, and the battles still raging across the chamber. “But whatever you choose, it won’t be as my jailor anymore.”

For a long moment, Rufus stood frozen, caught between anger and fear as the world quite literally crumbled around them. Then his gaze shifted to Bernard near the central dais, and to the crown upon his head with its swiveling emerald eyes.

“He’ll kill me if I run,” he whispered, real terror bleeding through the bravado.

“Maybe,” Gavin acknowledged. “But he’ll kill us all if he succeeds here.”

A section of the ceiling collapsed nearby, sending stone cascading to the floor in a deafening crash. The tree pulsed brighter, branches unfurling toward the ruined ceiling as if reaching for the sky above.

Rufus looked from Bernard to Gavin, then to the exit still visible through settling dust and debris. His next words emerged hoarse, nearly lost amid the chaos. “What about my debt? The one I owe Bernard? Would you have me be an oathbreaker too, Gav?”

Gavin’s partially transformed face broke into a grim smile. So far as he could remember, this was the first time Rufus had used his real name.

“Some debts aren’t worth dying to pay.”

Understanding flashed across Rufus’s features—not gratitude, but recognition. With a jerky nod, he staggered backward, turned, and fled toward the cathedral entrance, abandoning his post and mission in a final act of self-preservation.

The moment stretched for Gavin as he watched his tormentor vanish through the broken archway. The fear that had ruled him for so long, the debt that had shadowed his every step since Thornbridge—gone, though not in the way he’d imagined.

Not through payment. Not through violence. But through truth.

“What a strange day this is scraping up to be,” Gav muttered.

A terrible scream jerked him back to the present. He whirled to face it. Golden light erupted from the central dais, momentarily blinding even his enhanced vision. And in the middle of it, Chubbs lay there, spine arched as she screamed. The light intensified, and he had to shield his face.

When he could see again, horror replaced triumph. Cade stood there as Bernard loomed triumphant, emerald energy gathering for a killing blow. Chubbs strained against her bonds, and golden light flared from her eyes and fingertips as the ritual drew from her divine essence.

No time for reflection now. No time for the luxury of savoring his newfound freedom.

Gavin sprinted across the cathedral floor, pushing his transformation further with each step. Fur sprouted along his arms and neck. His jaw elongated, teeth sharpening into deadly points. Muscles bulged and rearranged themselves beneath his expensive clothes, now little more than tattered remnants.

He wasn’t fully wolf, but neither was he fully human. Gavin O’Rourke became something in between, more powerful than either alone.

A flash of golden light drew his gaze upward. Through the cathedral’s crumbling roof, two figures descended—Jer moving with impossible speed thanks to his enchanted boots, and Orro following close behind, his orange blade glowing with fierce intensity.

Understanding flashed between them without words. Three synchronous attacks, three targets. Jer for the crown. Orro for Chubbs’s shackles. Gavin for whatever stood in their way.

Bernard’s corrupted minions surged forward, forming a wall of emerald-infused flesh between Gavin and the ritual dais. He didn’t slow, didn’t hesitate. With a roar that was more beast than man, he crashed into them, claws slashing, partially transformed body moving with a grace and power he’d never before achieved even down in the crypt.

Bodies fell before him, corrupted flesh splitting beneath his claws as he carved a path toward his friends. Emerald blades bit into his sides, his back, his legs—but the transformation kept him moving, kept him fighting as wounds closed almost as quickly as they formed. Silvery energy spun and wove around him in tight ribbons of moonlight. For the second time that day, Gav abandoned himself to the freedom he felt.

He fought not with the desperate frenzy of Thornbridge, but with a controlled fury. Each movement was precise, each strike calculated. This was his choice, his power, his nature. Gav would embrace it rather than fear it.

Through the chaos, he caught glimpses of the others, and the sight of them redoubled his pace. He glimpsed Jer snatching the crown from Bernard’s head in a feat of acrobatic brilliance. Nearby, Orro’s orange blade sliced through Chubbs’s magical shackles. And off to the side, Nora forced the demon she fought back through the dimensional tear through sheer determination and skill.

They were winning. Against all odds, against enemies that should have crushed them utterly, they were winning.

A rumble shook the earth, and Gav stumbled to the side, nearly falling onto a pile of smoldering bones. The cathedral floor buckled violently beneath them, the stone cracking in elaborate patterns that mirrored the ritual circle. The tree pushed through completely now, massive roots spreading beneath the ancient stones in a network that encompassed the entire cathedral.

“Cade!” Gavin shouted, forcing his way through the heaving floor toward his unsteady friend. “Hold on!”

He reached the dais just as Orro lifted Chubbs into his arms, the assassin’s face unusually gentle as he cradled the child-goddess. But where the girl had been, a young woman of perhaps ten years now stood, her golden light fading as the ritual’s drain took its toll.

“Help me,” Orro gasped, his strength visibly failing as the ritual pulled at something deep within the orange blade he carried. “The blade—it’s connected somehow. Can’t hold her much longer.”

Gavin didn’t hesitate. Drawing deeper into his transformation, he allowed the change to progress further into a true hybrid form. With it, he would be powerful beyond human limitations yet still governed by human thought.

Fur covered his body now, muscles bulging with inhuman strength as his frame expanded to accommodate the change. His clothes tore away completely, irrelevant now compared to the needs of his friends. Besides, they could always lend him a towel once he transformed back.

With a single motion, he swept both Orro and Chubbs into his arms, their weight barely registering against his enhanced strength. Behind them, Cade stood to his full height. Blood continued to drip down the thief’s arms, but his voice was remarkably steady as he addressed Gav.

“Go,” Cade said, gesturing toward the exit. “Get them out.”

Bernard rose from the rubble. Emerald energy gathered around him once more despite the crown’s removal. The ritual’s completion seemed to have granted him power beyond his human limitations.

“I’ve won,” Bernard declared, blood-flecked spittle flying from his lips as his gaze locked on Cade. “For all your clever alterations, for all your friends’ heroic efforts—I’ve won.”

Gavin hesitated, torn between the need to flee with his precious burdens and the desire to stand with Cade against this final threat.

“GO!” Cade shouted, with more strength than should have been possible given his wounds.

The cathedral trembled more violently, whole sections of ceiling now collapsing as the tree’s power continued to grow. Through gaps in the ancient stone, Gavin could see storm clouds gathering above, unnaturally fast and tinged with the same emerald light that infused the tree.

Decision made, he turned and sprinted for the exit, Orro and Chubbs clutched securely against his transformed chest. Behind him, he sensed rather than saw Bernard advancing on Cade, emerald light dancing across his skin like lightning.
