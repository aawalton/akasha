Chubbs didn’t like flying. Not without Bunny. Not without being asked if she wanted to. And most definitely not with the scary man who smelled like wet dog and rotten eggs.

The cathedral ceiling raced past in a blur of cracked stone and broken beams as Bernard—except not Bernard, not anymore—carried her up the spinning dais. The whooshing air made her ears pop, and her stomach lurched as they finally stopped at the very top. Ancient dust spiraled around them in the cavernous space, dancing in shafts of sickly green light that streamed through holes in the decrepit roof.

“Put! Me! DOWN!” Chubbs kicked her tiny legs against his arm, each punctuated word accompanied by a determined thump of her heels against his ribs. “Cade’s gonna be so mad at you!”

The thing-that-used-to-be-Bernard barely seemed to notice her blows. His eyes—once brown and tired-looking but now gleaming an unnatural emerald—gazed out at the decrepit cathedral with something like reverence. The crown on his head pulsed with sickening light, veins of green spreading across his temples and down his neck like poison ivy.

“Beautiful, isn’t it?” His voice had changed too—layered now, as if someone much older and much angrier was speaking through him. “All these eons, and my work remains.”

Chubbs stopped kicking long enough to glance around. The cathedral didn’t look beautiful to her. It looked broken and scary and very, very old. But something about it did feel... familiar. Like a dream she’d had once but couldn’t quite remember.

Bernard’s corrupted crew spread out across the cathedral floor below them. They moved differently now—jerky and wrong, like puppets with tangled strings. Their eyes glowed with the same eerie light as their master’s, but dimmer, emptier. One of them—a woman with half her face covered in those strange green veins—tilted her head at an impossible angle while awaiting orders.

“Guard the eastern seal,” Bernard commanded, his voice booming through the cathedral. “Nothing interrupts the ritual.”

The corrupted woman bowed—a sharp, unnatural movement—before loping off toward a crumbling archway.

“You’re being very rude,” Chubbs informed him, crossing her arms. “I don’t want to be here. Take me back to Cade and Bunny right now.”

Bernard’s gaze finally fell on her, and something ancient and terrible looked out through his eyes.

“You don’t recognize any of this, do you?” He sounded almost disappointed. “The great Creation, reduced to this... babbling child.”

Chubbs felt her lower lip tremble. “I’m not Creation. I’m Chubbs. And I’m not a baby. I’m almost five!”

That strange feeling was back—the one she’d been having more and more lately. Like someone was knocking on a door inside her head, trying to get in. Or maybe trying to get out.

With a dismissive snort, Bernard carried her to the center of the dais. He touched his palm to a faded symbol carved into the stone. Instantly, a ripple of energy pulsed outward, like a stone thrown into a pond.

And then, magic happened.

All across the cathedral, ancient runes ignited—first in the walls, then the floor, then spreading outward through shattered windows and crumbling doorways. The light raced across the marshy landscape beyond, illuminating a massive pattern that had been invisible moments before. A web of interconnected symbols, circles within circles, stretching as far as Chubbs could see.

Despite her fear, Chubbs couldn’t help but gasp. It was beautiful—and terrifying. The geometric precision, the way each line connected to form perfect symmetry across miles of terrain... it spoke to something deep inside her. Not the scared little girl part, but something older. Something that understood what she was seeing.

“You made this?” she asked, her voice suddenly small.

Bernard laughed—a sound like grinding metal. “I did. Before you and your sister ruined everything.” He waved his hand, and more runes lit up across the dais where they stood. “This was to be my greatest achievement—the final solution to the chaos of existence.”

Chubbs frowned. “That doesn’t sound very nice.”

“Nice?” Bernard’s face contorted, the crown pulsing brighter. “You speak of ‘nice’ when you were the architect of untold suffering? You, who gave mortals the power to shape reality while denying them the wisdom to use it properly?”

The knocking in Chubbs’ head grew louder. Images flashed before her eyes—hands (her hands?) crafting something beautiful and terrible. A tree. A throne. Power beyond measure.

“Stop it,” she whimpered, pressing her hands against her temples. “I’m not listening to you.”

“You will listen,” Bernard snarled, his patience clearly thinning. “You will remember who you are, Creation, and you will bear witness as I undo your greatest folly.”

He strode to the edge of the dais, dragging her along. Below, his corrupted followers had taken positions at key points around the cathedral, their movements twitchy and unnatural as they activated smaller ritual circles.

“You’re a meanie,” Chubbs declared, trying to wriggle free of his grasp. “And your crown is ugly. You look like a dumb king of nothing.”

Something dangerous flashed in Bernard’s emerald eyes, but instead of striking her, he smiled—a terrible expression on his transformed face.

“Such childish insults from one who once crafted worlds,” he mused. “I wonder... how much of you is still trapped in there, Creation? Can you feel your true self, scratching at the prison of this infantile mind?”

The knocking in Chubbs’ head became a pounding. The voice that sometimes whispered to her was getting louder, more insistent. Words in languages she shouldn’t understand echoed through her consciousness.

Remember... remember who you are...

“I’m Chubbs,” she insisted, but her voice wavered. “Cade says so.”

“Ah yes, the thief.” Bernard’s smile grew colder. “Your ‘protector,’ who keeps you ignorant of your true nature. Who lies to you with every breath. Who plans to sacrifice you to that sister of yours.”

Chubbs’ eyes widened. “Cade wouldn’t do that. He’s my friend.”

Bernard’s laugh rang through the cathedral, a terrible sound that made the activated runes pulse brighter. “Friend? Is that what he told you? Then why does he never answer your questions about your powers? Why does he silence anyone who speaks of your true nature?”

The memory of Cade’s face flashed in Chubbs’ mind—that look he got whenever she did something “special.” Not surprise. Something else. Something like... guilt.

“He’s taking care of me until my parents come back,” she said, but the words felt hollow now. Had Cade ever actually said that? Or had she just assumed?

“Your parents?” Bernard’s voice dripped with mockery. “Gods don’t have parents, little Creation. They simply are.”

Chubbs stomped her foot, sending a tiny shockwave through the dais that made the nearest runes flicker. “Stop calling me that! I’m not a god! I’m just Chubbs!”

Bernard regarded her with something almost like pity. “Your denial changes nothing. Even now, your power leaks through that childish vessel.” He gestured to the runes that had flickered at her outburst. “You feel it, don’t you? The connection to everything around you. The urge to shape, to create.”

Chubbs bit her lip. She did feel it—had been feeling it more and more. The way things sometimes just... changed when she wanted them to. The way she’d fixed those masks without really knowing how. The way sometimes she dreamed of stars being born.

“I want to go home,” she whispered, a tear sliding down her cheek.

“Home?” Bernard laughed again. “Your home is nowhere, Creation. You abandoned your domain long ago, when you and Life decided to play your games with the mortal world.”

He turned, dragging her toward the center of the dais where a circular depression had begun to fill with swirling, luminous energy.

“But soon,” he continued, “none of that will matter. Your essence will fuel the completion of what I began eons ago. The Oakthorn will rise once more, and this time, there will be no one to stop me.”

Panic flared in Chubbs’ chest. “What are you going to do to me?”

“Nothing you didn’t once attempt to do to me,” Bernard replied, his voice suddenly cold. “Imprisonment. Dissolution. The recycling of divine essence.”

Below, one of the corrupted crew members—the one Cade had called Rufus—activated another ritual circle. A pillar of sickly green light shot upward, connecting with the swirling energy on the dais. The crown on Bernard’s head pulsed in response, drinking in the power.

“The mortals believe ancient rituals require blood sacrifice,” Bernard explained, almost conversational now as he worked. “Such primitive understanding. What rituals like these truly require is essence—the fundamental spark that makes a being what it is.”

He traced complex patterns in the air, each gesture leaving glowing trails that solidified into intricate, three-dimensional runes. “Your essence, Creation, is particularly potent. Even fragmented and diminished as it is.”

The knocking in Chubbs’ head had become a thundering crescendo. Images were flooding her mind—visions of cosmic forces, of divine wars, of a tree that held reality in its branches. And through it all, a desperate voice calling her true name.

Fight him! Remember what he tried to do! Remember the war!

She shook her head violently, trying to clear it. “Stop it stop it stop it!”

Bernard paused in his working, regarding her with the derision Chubbs reserved for bullies. And annoying goats. “Fascinating. Your true self is trying so hard to break through. But this vessel is too limited, isn’t it? Too human. It must be so difficult to keep someone so powerful contained in such an inconvenient vessel. I can’t even begin to relate to that—Oh wait, I can, you insolent wench!”

Chubbs took a step back, her heart pounding in her chest.

He reached out with one corrupted hand, fingers stopping just short of touching her forehead. “Perhaps I should help you remember. It would be so much more satisfying if you knew exactly what was happening.”

Chubbs jerked away from his touch. “Don’t touch me! You’re gross and you smell bad and my Cadey is going to kick your butt!”

To her surprise, Bernard actually laughed—a sound almost genuine amid the corruption. “Such loyalty to the thief. You truly don’t remember, do you? What he intends for you? What Destruction will do once he delivers you to her?”

“Cade wouldn’t hurt me,” Chubbs insisted, but a seed of doubt had taken root. He had been taking her somewhere. On a journey to meet someone important. Someone who would help her “remember.”

“No?” Bernard raised an eyebrow, the crown pulsing in time with his amusement. “Then why has he concealed your nature from you? Why does he tense whenever you display your power? Why does he exchange those worried glances with his companions when you aren’t looking?”

Each question landed like a physical blow. Because Bernard was right—Cade did all those things. He did get weird whenever she did something special. There were secrets in the looks the grown-ups shared. She hated those looks almost as much as she hated goats.

“You’re lying,” she said, but it sounded weak even to her own ears.

“Am I?” Bernard gestured, and a shimmering image appeared in the air between them—a memory playing out like a dream.

Cade and Nora, speaking in hushed tones while Chubbs slept: “We can’t keep this from her forever,” Nora was saying. “She deserves to know what she is before you take her to Destruction.”

“And tell her what?” Cade hissed back. “Hey, Chubbs, guess what? You’re actually the reincarnation of a goddess that Destruction wants to murder. Sweet dreams!”

The image dissolved, leaving Chubbs trembling. “That’s... that’s not real. You’re making that up.”

“Your denial changes nothing,” Bernard said, almost gently now. “The truth remains. Your precious Cade has been leading you to slaughter since the day he found you.”

Fresh tears welled in Chubbs’ eyes. “He wouldn’t. He... he ruffled my hair. He said I did good when I fixed the masks.”

“Of course he did,” Bernard replied. “One praises the lamb before the sacrifice.”

With a vicious wave of his hand, the central depression in the dais transformed into an intricate cage of shimmering energy. “Enough talk. The stars align, and the ritual must proceed.”

He seized Chubbs by the arm, his grip painfully tight. “Your essence will fuel the return of what was lost. The Oakthorn will rise again, and this time, there will be no divine interference. I’ve made quite sure of that.” He gave her a wicked grin.

“Let me GO!” Chubbs shrieked, her voice carrying a hint of something ancient and powerful. Several runes near her feet shattered, their energies dissipating in wisps of emerald smoke.

Bernard’s eyes widened slightly, then narrowed in calculation. “Still some fight in you. Good. That will make your essence all the richer.”

With a casual strength, he lifted her and tossed her into the energy cage. Chubbs landed in an undignified heap, quickly scrambling to her feet as the bars of light solidified around her.

“The ritual begins at moonrise,” Bernard informed her, turning to make further adjustments to the dais. “In the meantime, consider this: if your precious ‘Cade’ truly cared for you, why did he lie about what you are?”

He left her there, moving to the edge of the dais to direct his corrupted minions in their preparations. Chubbs pressed her hands against the bars of light, only to recoil as they burned her fingers. She was trapped.

Trembling, she sank to her knees in the center of the cage. Was it true? Had Cade been lying to her all this time? Was she really some kind of... goddess?

The voice in her head had gone quiet, but the images it had shown her lingered. Worlds being born. Powers beyond imagination. And a terrible war between siblings that had shattered reality itself.

She hugged her knees to her chest, making herself as small as possible. “Cadey,” she whispered. “Please come find me.”

Bernard’s voice carried across the dais, cold and confident: “This time, I won’t fail. It’s poetic, isn’t it? That the one who started this all would be tinder used to bring it all down?”

Moonlight began to stream through the broken ceiling, touching the first of the ritual circles with pale silver light. The countdown had begun.
