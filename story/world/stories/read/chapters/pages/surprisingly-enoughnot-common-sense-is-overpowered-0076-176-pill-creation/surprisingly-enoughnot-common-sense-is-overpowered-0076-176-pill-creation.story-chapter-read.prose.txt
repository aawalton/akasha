As usual, Ling Yinran found herself in the alchemy hall, idly watching Ning practice alchemy.

It reminded her faintly of her younger days, when she used to watch her aunt refine pills, except this time, there were far fewer accidents and, thankfully, no explosions. That alone already made this a far more pleasant experience.

Still, after standing there for a while, she could not help but speak.

“Junior Brother, aren’t you tired of doing so much alchemy?”

From her perspective, Ning was either refining pills or on his way to refining pills. Ever since he had successfully learned the basics, he had been doing it nonstop for the past five months. His work ethic, if nothing else, was terrifyingly consistent.

Ning lifted the lid of the cauldron and glanced inside.

Another low-grade Qi Gathering Pill sat there.

Its shape was uneven, its qi unstable, and it seemed ready to crumble at any moment.

He stared at it for a moment, sighed, and waved it aside.

“Senior Sister,” he said calmly, “I’ve realized that alchemy is all about repetition, perfecting already precise movements, and controlling the rhythm of the process. All of that requires relentless practice.”

He paused, then added with a small smile, “Besides, it’s fun to grind a skill to its highest potential.”

Ling Yinran blinked.

“…Grinding skills,” she repeated slowly.

That was another thing she had noticed. Ning had a habit of using very strange lingo: grind, optimize, debug, status. He might not have noticed it himself, considering how casually he used such words, but as long as he was making progress and not blowing up the alchemy hall, she had decided not to question it too much.

“That sounds reasonable,” she admitted after a moment. “But maybe you should take a step back and process things for a bit, since you’ve been having trouble refining the Qi Gathering Pill for a while now.”

She was not trying to discourage him. It was just that Ning’s current Qi Gathering Pills were sitting at around twenty percent purity, barely maintaining a consistent shape and definitely not safe for consumption.

Ning did not respond immediately.

Instead, he reached for the Image Recording Stone placed nearby. Its edges were worn just enough to show how often he had been using it.

He infused a thin strand of qi into the stone, and a projection flickered into existence above it, showing Ning himself standing before the cauldron earlier and refining the exact same Qi Gathering Pill.

From this perspective, he could see every movement, every pause, and every shift in the flames, including tiny moments of hesitation that had felt insignificant during the refinement but had ultimately contributed to the failure.

Ling Yinran’s brows rose slightly. “Also, I never thought you’d use an Image Recording Stone like this.”

Ning nodded, his eyes already focused on the projection.

“Watching from a third-person perspective helps,” he said. “When you’re inside the process, everything feels smooth. Continuous. You don’t notice where things go wrong. But from outside…”

He gestured lightly toward the image.

“…patterns start to appear.”

The projection replayed, and this time, the mistakes were obvious. At one point, the flame rose just slightly too high, while at another, his timing lagged by less than a breath.

They were not major errors, but they were already quite telling.

Ling Yinran watched as Ning went over his mistakes. “You know,” she said, “I’m definitely stealing this idea.”

This was another reason she liked hanging out with her junior brother. Every so often, he came up with some little idea that surprised or amused her.

In this case, using an Image Recording Stone could potentially be useful when she was configuring her puppets. She could review the entire process afterward and check for imbalances in their movement sequences or activation process from another perspective.

Ning didn’t even look at her as he replied, his attention still fixed on the projection. “It’s not stealing if it’s between fellow disciples. It’s called sharing knowledge to strengthen each other. Besides, with how familiar we are, ideas like this are meant to be shared anyway.”

Ling Yinran snorted lightly. “You always have a way of saying things nicely.”

After a few more moments, Ning dispersed the projection with a flick of qi and turned back to the cauldron.

“Let’s try again.”

Ling Yinran blinked. “That fast?”

Ning had already started moving.

This time, however, he did not reach for the Qi Gathering Pill ingredients. Instead, he picked up something far simpler.

Fasting Pill materials.

Ling Yinran raised an eyebrow. “You’re going backward to the Fasting Pills?”

“I would like it if you call it going back to basics,” Ning retorted.

His hands began moving, and the difference was immediately obvious.

The ingredients were processed smoothly under his control, the grinding even, the mixing precise, and the proportions adjusted instinctively through sheer repetition. His qi flowed steadily throughout the process, and where there had been small stutters in his rhythm while refining the Qi Gathering Pill, here each step flowed seamlessly into the next.

Ling Yinran watched silently as the flame rose and fell at exactly the right moments.

A short while later, Ning lifted the lid of the cauldron, and three pills rose slowly from within, suspended in the air by the residual qi.

They were flawless.

Ling Yinran stepped closer and examined them carefully.

“…Ninety-six percent purity,” she said after a brief pause. “All three.”

She glanced at Ning.

“…You’re getting increasingly better at this pill, however.”

Ning nodded calmly, completely unbothered.

“Yes. After all, I’m the one cooking them.”

Then, Ning picked up one of the pills and rolled it lightly between his fingers.

“The real problem I’m facing,” he said, “is the jump.”

“Jump?” Ling Yinran repeated.

“From Fasting Pills to Qi Gathering Pills,” Ning explained.

He placed the pill back down and gestured toward the ingredients on the table. “The complexity increases drastically. Fasting Pills use simple materials with few interactions and stable reactions, so even if you make small mistakes, the structure usually holds.”

He paused briefly.

“Qi Gathering Pills are the opposite. More ingredients, more volatile interactions, and conflicting properties that need to be balanced in real time. The fire control alone becomes significantly harder.”

Ling Yinran listened quietly as Ning tapped the edge of the cauldron.

“Each ingredient has its own optimal temperature range, and their interactions aren’t linear. If one step is even slightly off, everything can collapse.”

Ling Yinran nodded slowly. “That’s true.”

“So I’m bridging the gap,” Ning said simply. “I’m using Fasting Pills as the base and adding complexity step by step.”

He gestured toward a stack of jade slips. “That’s why I’ve been looking through Master’s recipes. Fortunately, she has quite a collection.”

Ling Yinran snorted. “That’s putting it lightly. Her biggest hobby is collecting strange recipes. If something looks even remotely unusual, she’ll keep it.”

Ning smiled. “That sounds exactly like her.”

He picked up one particular jade slip and quickly looked through its contents.

“Let’s try this one. It fits perfectly with my requirements. It’s a step up from the normal Fasting Pill, but doesn’t reach the complexity of the Qi Gathering Pill.”

After checking the required ingredients, Ning began going through what he already had available.

“I don’t have Blood Qi Grass,” he muttered, “but I do have Dragon Qi Grass, which is a superior version. As for the purification liquid… the cleansing property of Fenghuang’s white flame should be enough.”

Ling Yinran watched as he began adjusting the formula.

Creating a new pill was not something that could be done by simply adding sugar, spice, and everything nice, even though that did create the Powerpuff Girls. Ning certainly wasn’t capable of inventing an entirely new pill on the spot, but making minor modifications to an existing recipe was within his limits, especially when the changes involved ingredients he was already familiar with.

After making the necessary adjustments, Ning began the refinement.

Since it was his first time attempting this particular recipe, he did not expect to succeed on the first try. Apparently, however, he had gotten quite lucky.

When Ning opened the cauldron, a single perfectly formed pill rose into the air.

He paused, both because he had somehow succeeded on his first attempt and because the resulting pill looked somewhat different from what he had expected.

“…Huh?”

Ling Yinran leaned closer. “Junior Brother, this doesn’t look like the pill in the recipe.”

She pointed at it. “It’s… pink. The one you selected had a slight reddish tint at best.”

There was no better word for it.

It was undeniably pink.

Ning stared at the pill as well, frowning slightly as he examined it.

“…And the purity is around sixty-seven percent,” he muttered. “That’s… surprisingly high.”

Ling Yinran folded her arms. “So, what does it do?”

Ning rubbed his chin.

“The original pill was supposed to help replenish blood,” he said slowly. “If I replaced the main ingredient with Dragon Qi Grass, then the effect should be stronger.”

He paused before adding, with considerably less confidence, “Probably.”

Ling Yinran glanced toward the corner of the room.

“…Should I bring the rat?”

Given their master’s track record with “creative” pills, keeping test rats nearby had become standard practice at this point. If there were no willing volunteers, there were always the rats.

“That should be best.” Ning nodded after a brief moment of consideration. “Human experimentation can wait.”

Human experimentation, in this case, simply meant posting a mission in the Mission Hall asking for a disciple willing to test the drug.

Ling Yinran gave him a look. “Don’t say it like that.”

Ning coughed lightly and chose not to respond.

Just as Ling Yinran turned toward the corner to retrieve one of their increasingly overworked test rats, a hand suddenly reached in from the side and picked up the pill.

Neither of them had noticed anyone enter.

They turned and found Ling Xin standing beside them, the newly formed pink pill resting between her fingers.

Ling Xin said nothing at first as her divine sense swept over the pill, examining its structure, internal qi flow, and stability. Then, without any hesitation, she brought it slightly closer and sniffed it.

Her expression shifted just a little.

“…Hmm,” she murmured. “A sliver of Dragon Qi Grass, Cloudwheat, basic spiritual grain… and you added a supplementary Yang-type ingredient as well?”

Her tone was casual, as though identifying the composition of a newly created pill was no more difficult than recognizing vegetables in a soup. Considering Ning had once needed to go through an entire blindfolded ingredient-identification test for his spiritual farming certification, this was probably what a genuine master of alchemy looked like.

Ning blinked.

Ling Yinran, on the other hand, looked entirely unsurprised.

“Aunt, you’re awake?” she asked. “I thought you were cultivating.”

“I was,” Ling Xin replied lazily. “But I felt an interesting pill in my vicinity, which triggered my pill radar, so I am here.”

Ning stared at her. “You can just… sense that?”

Ling Xin glanced at him and lifted her chin slightly. “Most alchemists can’t,” she said, with a faint trace of pride. “But I’ve developed a kind of… sensitivity.”

Ning looked at her for a moment, unable to tell whether she was being serious. Given the world they lived in, however, there was a non-zero chance that she actually meant it, so he decided not to question it.

“Anyway,” Ling Xin continued, as if nothing unusual had been said, “you were discussing the effects of this pill, right?”

Ling Yinran nodded. “We were about to test it.”

“There’s no need,” Ling Xin said.

Before either of them could react, she casually tossed the pill into her mouth and swallowed it.

Both Ning and Ling Yinran froze.

“…Aunt,” Ling Yinran said slowly, her voice rising, “what are you doing?”

Ling Xin waved a hand dismissively. “Relax. The ingredients are too low-level to have any meaningful effect on me. Besides, experiencing it firsthand is the fastest way to understand a pill.”

She glanced at Ning, her gaze a bit pointed.

“The willingness to take the plunge is what separates the mediocre from the exceptional.”

Ning immediately understood what that look meant.

She was absolutely trying to set an example.

More importantly, she was absolutely expecting him to eventually follow that example and eat one of her goddamn pills as well. Ning had somehow survived this long without becoming a test subject for any of her special creations, and he intended to preserve that achievement for as long as possible.

He pretended not to notice.

“Master,” Ning said instead, seizing the safer option, “what are the effects?”

Ling Xin closed her eyes briefly and focused on the changes within her body.

“Hmm,” she began after a moment. “It replenishes qi and blood. The internal flow is smooth, and the effect is—”

She stopped.

A faint blush appeared on her face.

Ling Xin opened her eyes.

“…Oh.”

Ning and Ling Yinran both stared at her.

“Oh?” Ning repeated carefully.

Ling Xin coughed lightly, her composure returning with visible effort. “…It seems your pill also has an aphrodisiac effect.”

Silence filled the room.

“What?” Ning and Ling Yinran said at the same time.

They both looked at her again, and this time there was no room for doubt. Ling Xin’s breathing had become slightly uneven, while the faint flush across her face was unmistakable.

“That’s correct,” she said, maintaining a surprisingly calm tone despite the situation. “The pill stimulates blood flow to enhance recovery. However, the side effects appear to include… heightened stimulation.”

She paused.

“…And not a weak one.”

Even while experiencing the effects herself, Ling Xin continued analyzing the pill as though this were nothing more than an ordinary alchemy lesson.

Ning decided there were some things he was better off not thinking too deeply about and simply began jotting down the effects.

Ling Yinran, however, frowned slightly. “Aunt… shouldn’t you do something about your condition?”

“Oh, right!”

Ling Xin reached into her storage bag and took out a small white pill. Without hesitation, she swallowed it, and within moments, the effects disappeared as though they had never existed.

Her expression returned to its usual calm.

“…Handled,” she said.

Ning looked at the pink pill again.

“…An aphrodisiac, huh?”

He did not know what to feel.

This was supposed to be a stepping stone toward refining Qi Gathering Pills. Instead, he had somehow created something entirely different.

Not that it was useless. Even now, Ning could think of several possible applications, from using it as bait for certain wild beasts to finding some way to disperse its effects as a gas and use it to mess with an enemy.

Ling Xin, on the other hand, looked delighted.

“As expected of my disciple,” she said, nodding approvingly. “The first unique pill you create on your own, and it’s already this interesting.”

Behind him, Ling Yinran muttered under her breath, just loudly enough to be heard.

“…There are two of them now.”

Ning chose to ignore that.

First, he had sold light novels.

Now, he had created an aphrodisiac.

At this rate, he wasn’t sure what path his cultivation was taking anymore.

Would anyone believe me if I said I didn’t choose the gooner life, but the gooner life chose me? Ning pondered.

Ling Xin clapped her hands once, breaking him out of his thoughts.

“Still,” she said, “this deserves recognition.”

Ning looked up.

“For your first successful innovation,” she continued grandly, “I will reward you.”

That got Ning’s attention.

“I’ve recently been studying teaching methods,” Ling Xin continued. “Apparently, there is something called the ‘carrot and stick’ approach. So, tell me any one of your requests. Nothing too big, but something I could help you with.”
