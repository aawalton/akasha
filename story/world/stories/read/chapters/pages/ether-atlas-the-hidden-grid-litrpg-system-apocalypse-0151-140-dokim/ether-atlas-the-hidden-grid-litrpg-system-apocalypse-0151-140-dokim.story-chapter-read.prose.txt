My grandfather would not trust a rope he had not first hung his whole weight from, over a low branch, with a heap of straw laid beneath him. Every rope is honest, he said, and every rope has one place in it where it stops being honest, and only a fool goes looking for that place with a real drop already under his boots. He found his over the straw. He died old, in his bed, of nothing worse than age. — Codex of the Long Watch, A Field Observation, the mountain passes

Arthur set himself between two pillars and activated the network.

"Donna, hit the pillar, let's see what that does."

Donna came over and swung at one of the pillars, then waited for Arthur to comment.

"Again," was all he said, and so she did.

She hit the pillar three times, and by then it was obvious to her that something was happening. The pillar should have chipped, even at less than full force, and instead she could see only a few scuff marks.

"It catches, alright. The force from the pillar is transferring into me, like it did with the strike on the shield. It's not a hundred per cent clean, though. Not all of the force transfers, I think," Arthur said, releasing the field and walking out of the terrace towards a small boulder.

He took a stance and brought the maul down. The stone cracked but did not split. Arthur walked another few paces to a tree, and this time he punched the trunk and let the remaining force go. The blow left a radial indent in the bark, but the tree was otherwise unharmed.

"I can release the force in multiple strikes and through multiple contact points," he said, coming back to the group. "The trick would be understanding how much to let go. Too much and it's overkill and wasted force; too little and whatever we're fighting doesn't go down, and we give it a window."

"You're punching trees now? What is this, Minecraft?" Steve said.

Sarah gave Steve a puzzled look as Arthur barked a laugh.

"Don't. Nerd joke," Donna said to Sarah.

"What I don't get is that earlier, with Contact Reinforcement, the force hitting the ground would damage it. I've cracked enough floors to know that. But this time, the force ran through the floor like water running down it, rather than a heavy load dropping," Arthur said.

“The earlier version of the technique was not grounding the force. It was moving it through your body and dispersing it. Now you are grounding the force, but with a caveat.” Archie replied.

“Grounding requires a continuous path into a mass that is fixed and able to accept the load. The hill will take however much force you give it. The terrace of a building will not. You might still be able to ground the force, but the building will not come out unscathed.”

"So the stone's part of the hill, and the hill isn't going anywhere." Arthur flexed his hand where the lattice was slowly fading. "So it grounds the force right through the hill. If I've got my hand on something properly anchored, I can take a big hit and pour it straight into the ground and be rid of it."

“An acceptable summary. As long as your body is able to carry and move the force. A building collapsing on you will still kill you, since your body right now is incapable of handling that amount of force,” Archie said.

"Right, Guv. I can always count on you to keep me honest."

Donna went next and wanted to move to the open hill to give herself more room.

"Steve, throw something at me. Get a small stone."

"I would like it noted that this is against my instincts," Steve said, and lobbed a fist-sized chunk of marble at her sternum.

Donna neither caught the stone nor made any move to dodge it. Her hand came up, met the stone a metre off her chest, and knocked it straight down at her feet. The movement was sparse and sudden.

"Did anyone see her hand come up?" Arthur asked.

Both Sarah and Steve shook their heads.

"I didn't decide to bring the hand up," Donna said, dropping out of Vector. "It told me where my hand had to be. It was not a maybe. I decided to stop the stone, and it told me when to put the hand up, and how."

"Steve, throw the next stone at least ten metres away, towards open ground," Donna said, activating Vector again.

Steve threw the next piece of stone, high and wide. It sailed over Donna, and she made no move to intercept it.

"How do you reckon I get to a stone twenty metres in the air?" she asked.

"I don't know. Jump, maybe. I thought we were testing variables."

"We are, but logical variables," Donna said, her technique still running. "Again, and this time keep it at a height I can theoretically reach."

"I don't think she understands the word theoretically very well," Steve whispered to Arthur, and lobbed the second stone about ten metres from Donna.

The moment the stone was in the air, Donna shot forward. The group saw something almost like afterimages before they heard a clang as the mace met the stone and sent it flying back over Steve.

"Bloody hell," Steve said, ducking instinctively. "Watch where you aim your cover drive."

Donna dropped out of the Drive and gave Steve a smirk.

"I think she heard your theoretical comment," Arthur said.

"So we did learn two things. One: if I'm trying to intercept a trajectory and it isn't possible, theoretically or otherwise," Donna said, giving Steve a look, "the technique won't even give an intercept solution. I did try to intercept the stone in the air. I got nothing back."

"Okay, last test. Arthur, stand at least ten metres back from Steve and Sarah." Donna looked at the other two. "And you two stand at least three metres apart. On my mark, Arthur will move towards you. Arthur, your task is to touch one of them on the shoulder, but don't make the choice until the very end. Let's try and fool this thing."

The party took their positions, and Donna moved about thirty metres from where Arthur stood. Arthur saw the lattice light back up as Donna said, "Now."

Arthur moved towards the two standing dummies. He was not carrying his shield or maul and moved much quicker than in the last test. About three metres away he moved his hand towards Sarah; at two metres he lunged towards Steve. Right before contact he felt another hand grab his wrist and yank his arm away from Steve. The next second Donna dropped out of Drive beside him.

"It's like the juiced-up version of Google Maps," Donna said, panting. "Constantly recalibrating the path to my target. I could feel the adjustment the technique made when you feinted at Sarah and went for Steve."

"Non-lethal by choice," Steve said, writing. "You could put yourself in front of something instead of through it."

"Or I could pull you out of the way of something," she said, looking at him. "Which, statistically, is what I'll end up doing."

"Rude, but probably true," Steve said.

"Okay, enough. No more Drive for you for at least two hours," Sarah said, pushing Ether into Donna.

Steve went next and took a stance on the open hill.

"I know what concussive does held over a volume. Let's try fire," he said, and raised Harrow.

Arthur watched a column of air some five metres from Steve shimmer, and the next second it caught fire. The air caught fire. Arthur was up and moving towards Steve before he knew what he was doing.

"Bugger me, that's a wall of fire," Arthur said, reaching Steve's side. "That's a 4th level spell."

The column of fire in front of them, roughly two metres by three, was throwing enough heat to make Arthur sweat. He went around the fire and came back to stand next to Steve.

"No real thickness. Maybe a foot," he said.

Donna and Sarah had walked up to the two men as well. "How's the reserve drain?" Donna asked.

"Not bad, but not great either. I can keep this up for maybe five minutes, if I had to guess."

Arthur jogged to the nearby trees and came back with a branch about six inches thick and a metre long. He glanced up at the wall of flames and tossed the branch through it.

The branch passed through the curtain, and as it did there was a crackling noise.

"Yup, it's burning, alright. And since every inch of it crossed the fire, every inch of it burned at almost the same time," Arthur said, coming back to the group.

"It could be because it was too dry. It doesn't take a lot for dry wood to go up," Donna said.

Steve nodded, then without warning turned his arm to face the sky and fired a concussive blast. The blast roared into the sky above them, and simultaneously the curtain of fire in front of them collapsed.

Steve took a breath and accepted some water from Sarah. "It's either-or right now. I can deny an area or actively participate in a fight."

Sarah walked in front of the group, and before she had even taken a stance Arthur felt a familiar jolt of Ether hit his Matrix, which was Sarah's Disrupt trying to shut him down.

"You really need to stop doing that. Donna has both CON and STR above 25; she's a perfectly viable candidate for experimentation," Arthur said, shaking himself.

"She might be a closet sadist," Steve whispered.

"I already know what Restore, Arrest and Disrupt do. The only thing I need to check is how I can run two of them together."

"I need to test mending someone while still being able to throw out a Disrupt if needed. I need an injury," Sarah said, her eyes unfocused as she read their Matrices.

"It seems I've done a very good job. No visible injuries that will give me any real data," Sarah said, frowning.

Donna got up, nonchalantly ran her palm across her axe blade, and a bright red line bloomed in the middle of her palm. She held the palm out to Sarah. "Here."

Arthur was staring at the palm when he heard Steve mutter behind him.

"She is proper mad, she is."

Arthur watched as the gash on Donna's palm started to close in front of them. Sarah's face was a mask of concentration, and when the wound was halfway closed she said, "Arthur, brace yourself."

Before Arthur could say bollocks, a jolt of Ether landed on his Matrix. This one was much stronger than the last, and Arthur could feel the foreign Ether trying to overpower him. He grunted, fighting the intrusion, but despite his best efforts felt his body going rigid for a couple of seconds before his Constitution asserted itself and he regained control.

Donna's wound was only half closed, but the bleeding had stopped completely.

"While I restore using an active channel, the mending goes much quicker, because I'm actively healing. The moment I used Disrupt, I had to concentrate on that instead, and the Restore channel turned passive," Sarah said, moving to Donna and taking her hand. The wound disappeared in less than thirty seconds.

Steve was sitting on a rock, scribbling furiously. "So, either active healing, or active something else and passive healing. Another either-or," he said, nodding.

Sarah moved to Arthur and put a hand on his chest. "You okay?" she asked with a smile.

"Yeah," Arthur said, looking down at her.

"If Petros were here, he would have said, look at them, they are making the duck," Donna said, and chuckled as Sarah reddened

"Afternoon well spent," Donna declared as they gathered up their packs. Arthur, as usual, was stuck carrying the field pack.

They had spent an hour recovering from the drills and tests, sitting on the steps of the temple over a late lunch as the sun went from an incandescent white to a warm orange.

They started down the hill while there was still light enough to see by comfortably.

"Archie, do you AIs have levels?" Donna asked, negotiating a bend.

“No.”

"No more explanation than that?"

“I do not think any more is merited.”

"Still, you must have something. Versions? Is Archie 3.0 smarter and less irritating than Archie 2.0?"

“I optimise my own Matrix. There are no versions." Archie floated calmly down the hill. "Your progression ladder is a local approximation of capabilities I possessed before your ancestors developed speech.”

"You hurt his feelings," Arthur said.

The light was almost gone by the time they came down off the pine slope to the shore. Spyros was where they had left him, but two other men were working beside him. One sanded the new plank while the other held the caulking taut. A second caïque rode just offshore, its nets piled amidships.

"Was he keeping them in the hold?" Arthur said. "Does the boat even have a hold?"

"Re, the wizards return," Spyros said, getting up when he spotted them. "This is Michalis." He motioned towards a heavyset man, who gave the party a quick nod. "And Vangelis," he said, motioning at the second man, who was sanding one of the planks.

Sarah gave the two new men a smile and exchanged Greek with them and Spyros.

"He says the other two were fishing the shallows when they saw his boat and came over to help," she said. Spyros seemed unworried by the company.

"Good men, for Aeginians," Spyros said, nodding.

Arthur ran his eye along the hull. The patch over the shark's damage was sound as far as he could see, though the caulking was only half done.

"Will she float?" he said.

"She floats now. That is not the same as sailing. She is not finished," Spyros said, motioning at the boat. "She wants another day's work before I trust her with four people and my own neck. Siga siga. We sleep here, and we go when she is ready and not before." He said it, and that was the end of it.

As the sun went down, they gathered on the beach to share Eleni's bounty with the two Samaritans. Michalis stopped counting after the fourth jar. Vangelis looked from the field pack to Sarah, decided not to ask how, and tore the bread instead.

"Your mother has an almost preternatural sense of how much food a journey might need. She couldn't have foreseen the delay, or the extra hands," Steve said, popping an olive in his mouth.

"Maybe it's a technique, who knows," Sarah said.

Steve popped another olive. "So. What's the plan for tomorrow?"
