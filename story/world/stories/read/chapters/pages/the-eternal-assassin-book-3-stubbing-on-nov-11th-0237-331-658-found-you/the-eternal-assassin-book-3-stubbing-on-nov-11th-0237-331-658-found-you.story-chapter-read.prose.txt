Carving their way from one room to the next as they made their way through the fort wasn’t exactly easy, but it wasn’t nearly as difficult as Asher would have expected. He’d come to view the liches making up the Council of Death as these masterful wizards with deadly lairs filled with traps and untold horrors that would push them to the brink of death. Eventually, he realized the truth.

Jormon’s base was just as bad as any of the other liches. The poor lich was just hard countered by their preparation and Asher’s skills.

A disease and poison specialist was probably the scariest thing for any regular warrior to go up against, doubly so when he seemed to have an army of insects running around, just waiting to sting or bite an intruder and deliver a fatal payload that would kill a man in seconds. Even as skilled as they were, Asher and Samantha continued to receive various bites and stings as they pushed from one room to the next.

Unfortunately for Jormon, Asher’s Complete Resilience meant he was able to ignore the building of the poison and disease for a bit, and even purge it when the effects got too annoying. And he’d immediately used Permanence’s second-tier effect on Samantha the moment she’d gotten her first sting, which meant the poison-disease combination that desperately wanted to dissolve her body from the inside out wasn’t able to do a damn thing as it remained frozen in time. With their helmets handling whatever airborne diseases the lich had no doubt filled his lair with…

Poor Jormon was probably having a rather rough morning.

“If I were Jormon, I’d probably turn tail and run the moment I realized none of my defenses were working,” Asher said as he slashed through a centipede the length of his arm that tried to wrap itself around his neck. “We need to find him before he gets the chance to do so.”

“You don’t think he can teleport?” Samantha asked. Blocking a spider from landing on her head with one of her wings, she then spun around and shot an arrow at a scorpion that tried scurrying at her from behind, nailing it to the ground with a well-placed arrow. “Richard can teleport. If he can, I figure the liches had to be able to as well.”

“It’s strange, but I don’t think they can. Or at least not as easily,” Asher admitted, kicking out and sending a rolling bug the size of a baseball flying before it could get to them. “Maybe it has something to do with the fact that their bodies aren’t actually vessels for their own souls. I don’t know the reasoning, but if they could teleport away to escape danger, I just don’t see how I would have managed to kill any of them thus far.”

He could only hope that was the case, because while the bugs weren’t slowing them down all that much, Jormon’s fort was still on the larger side. Most of the rooms were devoted either to growing alchemical reagents or breeding grounds for specific insects, neither of which were all that exciting. Asher was planning to at least collect the plants and see if he couldn’t sell them back at the Grand Auction, but he’d been hoping for things like a collection of enchanted weapons or a pile of gemstones sitting out in the open. Sadly, Jormon didn’t seem like the biggest enchanter, and the complete lack of any sort of magical defenses confirmed that theory.

“I’m starting to wonder if Jormon was like the weird, naturalist member of the Council. The guy who just wanted to run away and live in a forest free from society, where he could play with his bugs without people looking at him weird,” Asher said as they finished up the last of the bugs in yet another room. “Maybe that’s why he wiped out his entire country. That’s one way to deal with being the odd man out.”

“It’s certainly an extreme way of doing so,” Samantha said, trying to wipe the sweat from her brow and frowning as her hand smacked against her glass helmet. “I’ll just be happy once we finish up here. Come on, next room.”

It took the two of them almost an hour to clean out the entire fort, moving methodically from one floor to the next. Eventually, they found a small room located near the top of the fort that had a large hole in the wall. There were scratch marks on the ground and a few fallen alchemical reagents here and there, and it didn’t take them long to figure out what had happened.

The large cages filled with horse-sized beetles off to the side certainly didn’t hurt. One of them was open and empty, and the scratch marks on the floor looked identical to the claws on the rest of the beetles.

“The bastard ran away!” Asher said, staring out the open window in shock. “I can’t believe this!”

“I mean, you have been going around killing off one lich after another,” Samantha said, looking more closely at one of the other beetles as she examined it. “He probably couldn’t have gotten too far. And he made the mistake of leaving the other beetles behind. Now that I know what we’re looking for, I can track them with Natural Guide. So long as he hasn’t left the forest, I can find him.”

“Have I ever told you how much I love you?” Asher asked, earning a snort from Samantha.

“You can tell me all about it later. Right now, we have a monster to kill.”

Grabbing his arms, Samantha flared her wings and shot out of the open window as they took off into the sky.

Of course, it was never quite that easy.

“Looks like we were right about the air defenses!” Asher warned, his face paling as an ocean of insects rose up to chase after them. They came from trees, from bushes, from the grass itself. Every insect with wings and some without that were caught up in the storm so dense it was like a physical wave. The ground beneath them was blotted out, transformed into a writhing sea of mandibles and wings as a powerful hum of flying insects shot for them.

“I’m going to need speed and maneuverability,” Samantha said, gritting her teeth as more and more insects flew up out of the forest and started gaining on her. “Wait until I give the signal, then Recall to me!”

“Got it! Good luck!” With that, Asher Recalled back to the palace, blinking as he popped into existence in the quiet, calm of his bedroom. It was beyond jarring to go from such a horrifying situation to the serenity of an empty room. Despite how hard his heart was pounding, he tried his best to take himself out of ‘combat mode’ as he took a seat and waited for the signal. He knew Samantha was more than capable. Not to mention she was thankfully within a forest for a change, which meant her already impressive abilities would only be all the greater.

Unable to help himself, he focused on his Eternal Mark, using its second-tier effect to scry Samantha and watch as she escaped the ocean of bugs. He quickly realized what she meant when she said she needed maneuverability, because moments after he vanished, she blasted a hole for herself through the swarm with her Fountain skill. As soon as she had an opening, she dove after the geyser of water as she shot down into the forest and began flying through a number of large trees to put space between her and the swarm. Asher could only watch in awe as she flew through the dense foliage and thick branches at max speed, Woodland Stride allowing her to ignore the forest’s natural terrain and travel far faster than their team had been. Only now did Asher realize just how terribly they’d all been slowing her down.

Of course, there was a reason they hadn’t sprinted forward at max speed.

Samantha could travel through trees and natural growth without issue, but the same couldn’t be said for the things living within the forest. Her Nature Sense allowed her to tell when there were things like a squirrel living within a tree so she didn’t crush it while using Woodland Stride, but it didn’t offer the same warning when it came to monsters. Asher nearly Recalled to her when a literal spider monkey dropped out of the canopy and tried to grab her with its many arms, but Samantha banked away at the last moment, slicing the monster in half with her wings before continuing onward. He let out a sigh of relief as she kept going, before pausing as he realized she wasn’t just running from the sea of bugs. From the looks of things, she was heading in a very specific direction.

She was still tracking down the lich.

It wasn’t too much longer before she managed to leave the swarm of bugs behind her. So long as she continued flying below the canopy, the insects living within the heart of the forest didn’t seem to mind her being airborne. At the very least, they didn’t fly into another berserker rage and try to drown her in their bodies. Without having to worry about the bugs, Samantha let up on her speed somewhat, but she still didn’t give him the signal, and Asher couldn’t blame her. He knew she wanted to finish off the liches just as badly as he did, and she could track Jormon down far more easily on her own. There was no range to his Permanence skill, which meant she was still all but immune to the lich’s poisons and diseases. Unless Jormon had some final trick stored away, Asher would really only be needed for his Sever the Thread skill to finish the job.

Out of nowhere, Samantha froze, quickly landing atop one of the thicker branches before whipping out an arrow from her quiver and pressing the tip against her upper arm. The pain flashed through his Eternal Mark, and Asher Recalled to her side, nimbly landing next to her in the canopy. Holding a finger to her lips, she pointed down toward the ground, and his eyes widened when he spotted what she was pointing at.

There, barely a few dozen feet below them in the center of a small clearing, was Jormon. The bright, almost neon-green fires burning in his eye sockets were a dead giveaway, even if the ancient and decrepit skin on his frame wasn’t. The large beetle he must have used to fly out here on was munching on the root of a tree off to the side, and Jormon himself was in the middle of setting up what looked like some sort of ritual circle, all the while muttering to himself too quietly for them to hear. As he watched the hunched-over lich work on finishing the circle, Asher gripped Crescent in his hand, realizing he had the perfect opportunity. As much as he loved pushing himself to the limit and engaging in desperate battles, some things were more important than his own wishes.

As if reading his mind, Samantha silently pulled an arrow from her quiver and tilted it toward him, raising an eyebrow in silent communication. Nodding, Asher imbued an Eternal Mark onto it, giving her a thumbs up as he mentally prepared himself. Without saying a word, Samantha silently drew back her arrow, took careful aim, and fired it directly at Jormon’s chest.

“Wha—” was the last sound the lich muttered as the mundane arrow thudded into his chest and he recoiled back, snapped out of his ritual preparations from Samantha’s surprise attack. Before he could even finish the question, Asher was there, appearing before him as though he’d always been there as he slashed Crescent forward and removed Jormon’s head from his body in a brilliant arc of blue. As a fourth lich fell by his hand, Asher let out a tense sigh of relief, grinning at the sight of the flames snuffing out in Jormon’s eye sockets.

“Is he dead?” Samantha asked, landing beside him with a quick flap of her wings. She had a look of astonishment on her face, and she stared at the lich’s fallen corpse as if she couldn’t believe it. “Just like that?”

“Just like that,” Asher confirmed, sheathing his dagger as he looked around at the almost-complete ritual circle. “Bit of a nice change of pace after the intense battles to the death the King of Death and The Lost Lady put us through. I think my hunch may have been right about teleporting. I’d bet this ritual was designed to take him somewhere safe.”

“That alone might be a huge find,” Samantha pointed out, growing excited as she stared down at the careful lines of gemstone dust that had been laid out. “If Donvath’s coalition can figure out where this was supposed to bring him…”

“We might have a starting point to hunt down those liches we don’t know the whereabouts of,” Asher nodded, realizing it was actually happening. With the fall of Jormon, that made four liches that had died to his hand. Zygrul was missing and Kroult was on their side, which meant the Council of Death’s original fourteen was officially down to eight.

It was time to form the coalition.

“I don’t know what a handful of different countries trying to work together for the greater good is going to look like, but I bet it’s going to be hectic,” Asher sighed, wondering if his newfound fame as The Reaper would end up being a boon or a hindrance in the days to come. He hadn’t forgotten about the fact that the Lambit Republic wanted him dead, and he doubted they were the only ones. Sudden shifts to the careful balance of power always made people wary, which meant he was going to have to be on his toes moving forward.

“Whatever happens, we’ll tackle it together,” Samantha promised, taking his hand and giving it a reassuring squeeze as she beamed at him. Asher wasn’t certain if it was the simple confidence with which she held herself here in her natural element, or the glowing, emerald wings stretching out from her back, but he had a hunch it wouldn’t be too much longer before the hunter ended up becoming a greater power in her own right. Doubly so if her training with that strange druid panned out.

“I know,” he said, returning the hand squeeze before placing an Eternal Mark at the unfinished ritual site. As he summoned a Spatial Lock around them and triggered Recall, warping them halfway across the continent and back to their bedroom, he laughed.

“Six liches down… eight more to go. Maybe by that point we’ll have grown strong enough to make Donvath and Gran tell us all about this mysterious pact of theirs.”
