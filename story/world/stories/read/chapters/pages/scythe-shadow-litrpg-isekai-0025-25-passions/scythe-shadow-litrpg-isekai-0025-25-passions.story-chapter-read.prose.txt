After my encounter with Valciferous, I rushed outside the colosseum to the nearest lantern. People called my name as I passed, congratulating me on my victory, asking me about the Death Affinity.

I ignored them all.

My focus driven onto one thing and one thing alone.

Avery.

I tinked the lantern with a fingernail, then fell into my shadow, leaving Penumbra and my unexpected—unwanted—newfound fame.

I woke abruptly in my bed.

Dawn's golden glow was beginning to shine through the curtains, basking the figure beside me in a warm light. She looked like an angel—probably the closest thing that existed to one now that I knew what lay beyond the conscious realm.

I'd become the servant of a god, but I certainly was no angel. Perhaps the farthest thing from. It'll bring me back to her sooner. Forever. That's all I wanted. That's all I needed.

Her.

I pulled a lock of hair from over Avery's face and tucked it behind her ear. It felt like an eternity since I'd last seen her. Each breath sighed against my body. Each heartbeat resonated with my soul.

"Good morning," I whispered.

She groaned and slapped a hand at me.

"Averyyy," I sang into her ear, reaching a hand around her stomach.

She slowly opened her eyes and turned her head toward me. There's nothing more beautiful than seeing your fiancé's face in the morning. I took in every freckle. Examined the nebula of color in her eyes. The arch of her nose. The curve of her cheeks.

I almost lost this. I came so close to never seeing this again.

"Good morning yourself," she said. Her voice hoarse from sleep.

I didn't care. It was the most glorious sound I'd ever heard.

I kissed her hard. Deep.

"Someone's feeling a little better," she giggled, reciprocating playfully with a little tongue.

A fire ignited within me. Not lust. Something more real.

I didn't know love could burn that bright.

I kissed her again, passion swirling between us like a star. A connection beyond physical, beyond emotional.

The angel and the Reaper.

We lay together in the morning glow for a long time after, not speaking, not thinking. Just being.

Our life together would be incredible. Nothing would get in the way of that.

I'll make sure of it.

That night I returned to Penumbra, as I knew I would.

It was dark there, the sky starless and moonless. I stared up at the inky black and took a deep breath.

I have a lot of fucking work to do.

"Uhh, Zephyr?" a voice asked from nearby.

One of the guards who'd guided me up to Valciferous' chamber after my arena match scrambled to his feet. I recognized him as the nervous one who'd asked if I was really a Death Reaper. He'd been leaning against the colosseum wall. It looked like he'd been there for hours, his helmet askew, hair messy beneath, eyes groggy.

"Were you… waiting for me?" I asked.

"Uh. I… Yes. I was instructed to help you claim your winnings from the brawl. Since you left so quickly, you missed out on the standard debriefing with the registrar and we wanted to ensure you didn't miss out on the rewards."

"Well, I appreciate that. What's your name again?"

"Eugene, at your service, sir."

"Great to meet you, Eu—"

I glanced up at my Volition.

Holy shit fuck no you dumb fucking idiot.

I'd been so thrown off after pledging my services to a god damn god and needing to see Avery that I hadn't even considered the status of my Health and Volition. If it was low after the match, it was beyond dangerously low now. Barely a blip of blue.

The memory of that boy's head dropping off his shoulders came unwillingly back.

I felt at my neck. My cloak was tighter… Or was that my imagination?

"Sorry, I need to go harvest a Will. Like now. Somehow."

How could I be so stupid? All that, to accidentally kill myself like this?

"Actually, there's no need," the guard said. "Arena participants are often low on Volition after a brawl, so a free Will is one of the prizes. It won't give you any augmentations since you didn't kill the Sin yourself, but it'll still replenish your Volition. The loser can get a consolatory one for a small fee as well. There's even a pillar room right beside the registrar's desk. I—Hey! Where are you going?"

I started power walking into the colosseum. "It's a bit urgent. I'm gonna need that Will about ten minutes ago."

"Right. Of course!" Eugene hurried ahead, skidding to a stop at a large desk with a metal grate overtop that looked a bit like ticket booth at a concert or sports venue. He spoke rapidly to the Amethyst Reaper girl behind the counter, gesturing at me.

The girl examined me briefly with her dark eye, then handed Eugene a dormant Will.

He jogged over to me, almost dropping the Will in the exchange.

The little line of blue was even smaller now, but I was less worried now that I had a Will in hand.

"You just have these lying around? Shouldn't there be Reapers rioting at the doors to snag these?"

Eugene scratched the back of his neck. "Uhh, well… Running low on Volition isn't really an issue for most experienced Reapers. They defeat a Sin, harvest the Will, refill Volition. Lather, rinse, repeat."

I frowned and summoned my most evil all-powerful genie voice. "Eugene, are you calling the Reaper of Death inexperienced?"

I summoned my scythe, trying to look menacing. Eugene cowered slightly.

Then I stuck the blade into the Will in my hand and harvested it right there in the colosseum lobby. I winked at the guard, who sagged with relief that I wasn't going to smite him, and earned several stares and murmurs from others.

Then, I sprinted to the pillar room Eugene indicated. Other Reapers in line let me pass.

"Is that him?" one asked his companion.

"The Death Reaper…" another said. If I'm gonna get a fancy title, I need something better than that. So dramatic. And dark.

I relinquished the Will with a sigh of relief. It didn't take me to the starry place, but that was perfectly fine by me. I was alive.

God damn, that was way too close.

For a moment, I wondered why the Ruby woman hadn't tried getting the boy to harvest one of these Wills instead of one directly from a Sin. It was an obvious solution. Too obvious… He'd probably refused this too.

I strolled back to the registrar's desk, ignoring the stares.

Eugene waited for me there, deep in conversation with the girl who'd provided the Will. Curly red hair—like Santa's sleigh red, a beach of freckles over her face, and a bright smile. He looked at her the way I looked at Avery.

I slid up beside him, nudging him with an elbow, feeling no guilt at interrupting. The girl's cheeks flushed.

"You made it!" Eugene said.

"Barely an inconvenience. And who is this?"

He cleared his throat. "This… Uh, this is—"

"I can speak for myself, thank you very much," the girl interrupted. "I'm Sibyl. And you're Zephyr. Astral Death Affinity. Amber Reaper. Congratulations on winning your brawl."

“Oh,” I said, not expecting her to know everything about me already. “Did you watch the match?”

“Brawl,” she stated. “And no.”

“Word travels fast around here, huh?”

“It does. But even if it didn't, I have Soul Sight,” she said, as if that put the question to bed.

I returned a questioning look.

“Ugh, noobs…” She rolled her eyes. “You may be a Death Reaper, but you sure are clueless. It’s one of my skills. Gives me the ability to observe the Wills of other Reapers. Unlike you, I can actually read your Will. Feel emotions, collect information on Affinities, all that.”

“Well, that’s a mighty neat trick. No wonder they gave you this job. Guarantees whatever winnings are earned go to the right person."

Her eyes brightened for the first time since she looked at me, and I got the distinct feeling that people didn’t normally care about the person behind the counter. Except Eugene of course.

“I just work here on the side for the extra money since I don’t have any combat skill," Sibyl said. "Soul Sight is a job requirement. Life Affinity, if you were wondering”

"I was, actually!"

She began filing through a box of small notes that rested on the counter behind the grated barrier. Eventually, she plucked one one of the cards out and held it up.

Sibyl’s eyes widened as she examined the card. “Wait, you’re the one who had the scythe pulled on you?”

Ah, so not all the news travelled that fast.

“Yes,” a male voice interrupted. It wasn't Eugene.

A hand wrapped around my shoulder. It tingled like I’d been exposed to a mild electric current.

“Yes, he’s the one. The great, incredible Zephyr. Of course, I knew him before he had a name. The man who won against the delinquent using a scythe, who looked certain doom in the eye and smashed it through a stone wall. The mighty Death Reaper.”

I turned my head to find a face inches from mine. A face I’d hoped never to see again.

Zeus.
