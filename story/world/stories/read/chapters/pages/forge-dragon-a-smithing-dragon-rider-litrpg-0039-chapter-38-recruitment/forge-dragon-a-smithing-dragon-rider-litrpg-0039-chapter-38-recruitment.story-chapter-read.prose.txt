The next morning when the Centurion arrived, Caleb wished that they’d snuck away. The man was out of his armor, wearing the formal tunic of a legion commander in the field. He was in his forties with graying hairs and looked exhausted.

“What am I going to do with you?” he asked again.

“I wasn’t kidding about the triumph,” Grisim said.

Caleb took a deep breath to stop himself from punching his brother.

“A triumph?” The Centurion asked. “No. Laurels are on the table however for your brother here.”

He looked to Caleb and gave a quick bow of respect.

“I am Canus Pontidius Centurius.”

“Hi…” Caleb said uncertainly at the sudden show of respect. “I’m Caleb… Kavilson. This is my brother Grisim… also Kavilson.”

“Oh I am well aware of who you are,” Canus said in a way that suggested he knew more than they expected. “Frost Dragon Hunters it is said, though it’s odd for two dragon hunters to have a tamed iron dragon with them.”

“Yeah…” Caleb began unsure of where he was going.

“I care not for what lies you told your village. I am grateful for your aid and for that I owe you a debt. Without you we would have taken many more losses before we were able to bring down that giant.”

“You’re welcome,” Grisim you said from where he stood outside the conversation.

Canus turn to him with a glare and said, “we will get to you in a minute.”

“I have a problem you see,” he said, turning back to Caleb. “ I came here to recruit and replenish my numbers and now I have less numbers than when I first came. I came here to recruit two uncommon scout type initialized and have now lost three Decurions, dozens of Legionnaires, and hundreds of auxiliaries. I must see that those numbers are made whole and as they were lost in defense of this village, it is in within my rights to conscript to reclaim those numbers.”

Grisim was about to protest, but Caleb shot him a stern look. He sensed the Centurion was looking to help them.

“By my reckoning—which is the only reckoning that matters with the Legate absent—I would need to conscript eerie initialized in your village and then half the villagers. Now, conscripts only count for half, so if we could recruit volunteers, we would only need to steal all your initialized.”

“The villagers would be better off going with you than staying here to be killed by the Frigidi with our initialized gone,” Caleb said as he sensed the Centurion was waiting for a response.

“Exactly. But I have no wish to see this village left defenseless. I did just fight for it after all. Think what you will about the empire—and keep those thoughts as thoughts—but I take my duty to defend its people seriously. So, I need you to work with me.”

“How many people will you need to take if I join?” Caleb asked.

“That depends on how much you share about your abilities,” Canus said. “I have no inquisitor here so it falls to me to establish your value. If you claim to be an uncommon Frost Dragon Hunter, I can’t gainsay you, but that will doom your village. If you can sufficiently convince me of your value, I will do what I can to preserve your village.”

“What about me?” Grisim said, inserting himself once again, “I’m—”

“You,” Canus said, turning to Grisim with all his helpful and friendly tone gone, “Will be oathbound to the empire before we leave this tent be it through a slave collar or an oath. If you are extremely lucky, you will not be executed as soon as the Legate finds out of your existence. If your class had not evolved to a rare one, I would have already been forced to execute you per regulations to reclaim the blade.”

I guess they were listening, Caleb thought trying to think over what else they might have overheard.

A initialized skills were carefully guarded secrets for many reasons. For one, most communities that could create initialized didn’t want the secret of their success to be found out. The details of a class can give clues to unlocking it. A community like Caleb’s value to the empire was the initialized soldiers it could provide for the legions—the other legions that is, the Thousand Blade Legion didn’t recruit initialized as far as Caleb knew.

Self preservation was the other reason for the secrecy. Knowing one’s skills was as good as knowing one’s weaknesses.

Canus turned to Caleb.

“Let me tell you what I know of you—which so far reduces the debt of your village by roughly a quarter. You are a rare ranked dragon rider with an Iron Affinity gained from your iron dragon bond. You are a blacksmith but not a blacksmith. You are married—which does slightly reduce the debt though only by a single uninitialized. You were fairly capable as an uninitialized combatant.”

“You looked into me?” Caleb asked, not sure why he was surprised.

Canus gave Caleb a look which told him that was a stupid question.

Caleb considered what to say. He sensed correcting the misconception about the affinity would be a mistake though he couldn’t say why exactly. It also implied that Bog wasn’t an iron dragon as the Centurion thought which begged the question as to what he was.

Caleb decided to be fairly honest—his village was at stake after all.

“I am Caeli Soul,” Caleb admitted, watching Canus for a reaction.

The officer hid it well, but Caleb’s perception was able to catch the slight tensing of the muscles around his eyes.

“That grants me a greater Wind Affinity and doubles my points spent on mental stats. I have put all my points into my mental stats and have 22 across the board,” Caleb said, and then gave a rundown of the rest of his stats—leaving out the details of his affinity for all metals.

Caleb sensed all the metal on the man before him brass, gold, silver, and more he couldn’t name but no iron. He’d come prepared for what he’d thought was a master of iron. He wouldn’t have taken such precautions if the threat of an Iron Affinity weren’t great. But thinking back to the battle he saw sense in it.

“Well… you are significantly more valuable than I expected,” Canus said, reassessing Caleb.”You have unlocked an abnormally large number of skills for someone so new to all of this.”

His polite show of respect had turned genuine during the rundown.

“Your master spoke very highly of your smithing abilities and said he just raised you to a journeyman, but the journeyman piece wasn’t there for me to inspect.”

“Oh, I left that with my wife, but I have this—” Caleb lifted his hammer from the ground and handed it over. “I made it the next day.”

Canus took the hammer, showing his own enhanced strength off by keeping his arm from sagging at the sudden new weight.

He held it up close to his eye, then called for a knife to be brought to him. An aid brought in an ornately jeweled dagger, and Canus scratched it across the surface.

“Hmmm,” Canus said. “You truly made this?”

Caleb nodded.

“That was a lightly enchanted dagger meant for piercing similarly enchanted armor and your hammer dulled the tip.”

Caleb nodded. He wasn’t sure what to say. Where was the line between skilled blacksmith and Blacksmith that would see him taken away to the empire? He supposed on some level it didn’t matter with his oath already sworn, but he didn’t want to be sent away to the capital away from Grisim lest his brother say something rash and get himself executed for his stupidity.

“You will pay just over half the village’s debt,” Canus answered when Caleb only nodded. “I think I could stretch it that far without raising any eyebrows. You being a smith is truly a boon to you. Getting the Legate to assign a Blacksmith is like getting him to part with one of his own daughters. Even a half decent smith with an Iron Affinity is a great benefit to a legion.”

“What about Bog?” Caleb asked.

“What about him?” Canus asked.

“What is he worth?”

“His value is that of a single uncommon initialized. There’s a document outlining animal companions if you’d like to see it. Iron dragons are very uncommon, but not that uncommon or valuable.”

Caleb was disappointed. He could reveal that he wasn’t an iron dragon, but held it back for now.

“So what now?” Caleb asked.

“Now you swear your oaths, or I fetch a collar.”

***

No collar was fetched, instead the Legion’s Aquilla, a golden standard with an eagle on top, was brought in and they were forced to swear allegiance to the empire. The standard was enchanted and bound them to their paths.

Caleb tried to study the magic within the gold and steel of the device but the amount of details were far too great for him to even begin to comprehend.

“Alright men,” Canus said in a formal tone of command. “We will work on fitting you into the chain of command later. For now, join the rest of the legion in helping with recovery efforts.”

Caleb and Grisim were both relieved they weren’t at once whisked away and gladly went out to aid in the reconstruction of their home. Everywhere they went teams were at work clearing debris, tending to wounded and collecting the dead.

All the villagers stared at Caleb in awe as he passed, looking at Bog with a mix of confusion and fear. The legionnaires in turn looked at Grisim with pitying shakes of the heads and signs of prayer to the Elders in his name—a fist to the chest.

“I think I might have made a mistake,” Grisim said to Caleb after the third such gesture.

“You think so huh?”
