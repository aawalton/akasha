"Wait, so..." The Locksmith rubbed his forehead, "How can your dad be younger than you? How does that make any sense?"

"The Tower picks up folk from all history. What happened was—"

"But who arrived here first?" He continued to bombard her with questions, "I mean, if he's been here longer, surely he'd still be around your age if you arrived—"

"Look, you don't age here." She cut him off, catching his attention, "Dad left when I was nine. When I got here as well, even after what could be years, he was still the same age as when he left."

"That doesn't make a whole lot of sense, unless..." Harry jumped her with his hungry eyes, "How does the loop happen!? I mean, what happens to us?"

"Hey, hey!" Brun backed him off, "Let us do this another time, shall we? We'll fill you up as we go... Now tell me, what can you do besides creating doors?"

"You only care about my body, don't you...?" He snatched a loaf of bread and climbed on the table. "Why do you ask? For what service am I needed?"

The Locksmith asked playfully, grinning as he ignored a suppressed gasp from Brun. The young thief, however, felt no safer than a fly on a swamp. What could these individuals want him for? Yes, to reach the end, he would need help and connections. All of that, though, would come with trust.

He scanned her face, hoping to catch onto any scent of danger in her intentions. What he captured, however, was a strange sense of determination. Glancing, Harry saw the man with the candelabra standing near the entrance. Blocking it...

"Hey! Those are not yours to take!" The old lady warned him, "G-get down!"

"So I can't also do this, then?" Harry fell backwards as a door connected him to the roof. Grabbing onto a faded chandelier, the young thief was hanging as freely as a ballerina in a concert. "Or this?"

Like a maestro, he orchestrated his doors and passages as he confused the whole group. Brun was desperately looking around, trying to find his next move as Prism just stood on the side, attentively staring. Vela, however, had the strangest reaction; the man was... happy? His mind could tell otherwise, but Harry wouldn't be able to know even so. Like a moth to a flame, the man was side stepping to give room to Harry as the young thief appeared to rotate through the large room.

Concluding his performance, the Locksmith was now two steps away from the exit, wondering if he should just bolt. But first, he straightened his tie as his doors appeared to fade out of existence.

"...Impressive." Brun said, narrowing her eyes.

"That's all you have to say?" Harry replied, then sighed deeply as he looked back, the exit door right there. "...Why am I here? Why do—"

"The why does not matter, Locksmith." The old woman replied, her voice suddenly stern and commanding. "It is in the how that your abilities are worth to us. You're here for a reason, and whether it is for foul murder or for infuriating a noble, you can help us get to the top."

"...What do you know about the last floor?" Harry asked, still not budging from his spot. "And the... prize?"

"The God Emblem?" Brun said, chuckling. "That's a tale made for drunkards and greedy thieves. Even if it were real, though, we don't want it."

"We has not been properly defined." Prism shifted uncomfortably in her spot.

"Either way," She dismissed the young woman. "We will reach the last floor, or die trying. You can either join us or be left behind." When the man didn't respond, she sighed and added. "The only way to find out if the treasure is at the end of the rainbow is by chasing it. Is it so hard to help a few colleagues along the way if it means you'll succeed in the end?"

"It's the colleagues part I'm not buying." Harry said, his body still stiff.

"Oh, so you're one of those, aren't you?" The old woman chuckled again. "Tortured soul with no hope left for connection?"

"...I—"

"Give me a break." She cut him off. "We don't seek connection, we seek success. That's what we all do, it just happens that success is a tough thing to achieve."

Maybe Harry was just tired of being alone for so long. Yes, he got used to it, and actually cherished his time in isolation from time to time, but this wasn't about him anymore. This was a new place, and he needed friends. No. The Locksmith needed help, as much as it hurt to admit.

"Fuckin' hell..." He lowered his shoulders and took a deep sigh. Adjusting his glasses, the man grinned with his long dark hair touching his shoulders. He looked at the group. "What can you all do?"

Maybe I shouldn't have asked... Harry thought to himself as he watched Vela's candelabra spew a jet of flames condensed enough that it looked like lava. Swoosh! A gust of heat hit the young thief, promptly forcing him to take a step back. The strange but calm man, Vela, had the Candle Emblem on the Triangle Form. With his candelabra, he could entice the flame of candles, turning his ordinary object into a flamethrower. Well, glad he's on our side.

Before he could turn to Brun, he was momentarily blinded. "Argh!" He grunted. "Stop that!"

When the Locksmith first met Prism, he should've imagined the reason for her name. The young woman, with her Emblem, the Prism Emblem on the Circle format, could manipulate the direction of light; refraction. That, annoyingly, allowed her to blind enemies if a source of light was near and, no less important, could turn herself invisible. Maybe that's why I didn't see her until she grabbed me... Tricky power, hum?

"...It's funny." She replied.

Well, I messed with her with my doors before, so it's only fair. "And what about you?" Harry turned to Brun.

The old woman picked a piece of paper and narrowed her eyes on the white space. Exchanging glances between him and the paper, Brun said nothing. For a few seconds, everything kept quiet, until...

"Harry Bockart." She blurted out. "25 years old. A thief, colloquially known as Locksmith, who possesses the Door Emblem. A suspicious individual who never had a girlfrie—"

"What the fuck!?" Harry grabbed the paper, only to see it was still white. "What was that?"

The old woman gave him a smug smile. "I'm sure you had a rough life."

"That's all you have to say!?" Harry replied, still indignant. "How did you... how did you know all that?"

"The girlfriend part?" Brun said, raising an eyebrow. "I mean, it is quite obvious, you look like a beardless wayfarer who robbed a professor's clothes—"

"No!" He shut her down, once again. "I don't have one because I didn't want to, okay? It's purely by choice. I mean it."

"I like travelers." Vela said with a tranquil smile. "They tend to possess a strange combination of wisdom and meekness which I respect."

"I... Thanks, Vela." Harry replied, sincerely grateful for the defense of the candle man.

"I have the Manuscript Emblem." The old woman replied, her tone more serious. "If I focus long enough on certain objects or individuals, I can see a list of information pertaining to said object or individual. The longer I focus, the deeper it gets. Eventually, I can get someone's weakness and strong points. With objects, they tend to get a bit more generic, seeing how simpler they are regarding the emotions of a human. As with all Emblems, I do have a limit to how much I can see."

"And only you can see the page?" Harry wanted to confirm.

"Yes." She replied, but seeing how uncomfortable he felt, sighed and added. "I apologize. It was not my intention to make you feel out of place. I just needed an example."

"Just don't do it again." Harry replied, then grinned. "Otherwise I might accidentally start creating passages all over the place."

The old woman gave a warm smile in reply. "Understood."

Knowing all of this, now companions, abilities, the Locksmith needed to understand what they were going to do next. Brun had mentioned saving her dad and someone called Goldman. Apparently, they were the leaders of the group. In scouting for the next floor, however, they never returned. After three loops, the group began preparing to go on in search of the two missing members.

"And how do you know they're alive?" The young thief asked the group.

"We don't." Prism was the first to reply. "But we need to. For the sake of morale, you know?"

"And," Brun added. "Because we can't reach the third floor from the first, obviously. We have no idea what the next floor looks like. Actually, apply that to all the subsequent floors. I'm not one keen for believing in tales, but as far as we know, all of them are right about what's coming next. I mean, you've seen the worm outside, right?"

"Yeah, Prism introduced me to it." Harry glanced at her.

"Correction," She replied, slightly twisting her neck. "I saved you from it."

"Either way," The Locksmith dismissed Prism. "How do we get to the second floor, then? With my passages, we can quickly ascend the stairs."

"That won't be needed." Vela said. "I mean, it will be, but not upwards. The hatch to the second floor is on the bottom of here. We've seen Goldman and Daniel climbing down there."

"You mean floating, right?" Prism said, but before Vela could respond, Harry raised a hand.

"Wait, the second floor is down? Is the Logos Tower actually called the Logos Basement?"

"Yes for the first question. The second—" The old woman then, before continuing, looked at her watch and rapidly blinked. "Shit! I forgot! Locksmith, use your C-Coin!"

"I—My what?" Harry took a step back, frightened by the sudden exasperation of Brun. "What are you talking about?"

"You don't have it!? It's supposed to be with you! We all come with one, it's like the Tower's token. Check your coat or something."

And as soon as she finished speaking, Harry found a tiny dark coin with a weird symbol inside; a clock. Is this a C-Coin? And when he looked at Brun, the woman's glare seemed to confirm his answer.

"Throw it like you would a coin!" She said. "Fast!"

And the Locksmith complied, throwing the coin with his thumb. The strange object, which oddly looked like a tiny Emblem, made a tingling noise as it fell down to Harry's palm.

"Nice..." The old woman, now relieved, scratched the back of her head.

"...Did I just sell my soul to the Tower or something?"

"Something like it." Prism chuckled. "Although I believe your contract started the moment you entered the portal."

"She is trying to scare you, Mr. Harry." Vela replied with a smile. "There is no devil, for this place has been forgotten even by God."

That's not better. He added inwardly as the world around him suddenly changed.

"You set a point in time." Brun explained. "Now when the loop restarts, you'll appear here, as if this was where you first arrived. We all have it, and it appears to restarts every loop. We call them C-Coins, but you can call them whatever you want. It is the single most important object for us to continue climbing without worrying about the next loop."

"...So the loop is real?"

"Of course it is. In fact, we're getting near one in... twenty seconds."

"Wait, what!?" Harry widened his eyes. "Are you serious?"

"Yeah. The first time it was a bit overwhelming for me as well. And don't freak out if they don't work out after; we don't know why either."

Looking around, as if he were a convict ready for hanging, Harry didn't understand the woman. "What doesn't?"

But before she could respond, the loop was restarting.
