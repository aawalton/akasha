Once they returned to Lily House, Ferris took Jade downstairs to the basement for the third and final of that day’s feeding of straw. The slime happily ate their way through two and a half bundles of the stuff, then greedily considered the remaining stacks. While Ferris felt the slime was smart enough to understand why he didn’t immediately feed them all of it, Jade wasn’t quite at the point of caring. Long-term planning was a work in progress.

It was still too early for dinner, but Ferris stopped by the common room and asked Mother Lily to drop his evening meal by his door that night. He could have just used the sign on the box outside his door, but he was passing by anyway.

He’d learned a lot over the last two days, but that only seemed to make his task list longer. He expected to be working extra shifts over the next week, so he needed to take as much advantage of today as possible.

Locking himself and Jade in, Ferris took a moment to appreciate the comfort of his own room. So far today, he had meditated on a tile floor at the bathhouse, then on the sandy ground of the training room. He was looking forward to his bed.

Lying down with Jade on top of him, Ferris closed his eyes and sought the emptiness of his mind. He had so many new theories about the creatures in the slimespace, but no real way to prove them right or wrong yet. It was hard not to keep thinking of them, but meditation was all about acknowledging the thoughts… and then letting them go.

Waking up in the soulspace with Jade next to him, Ferris took a look around to decide what to do first. He usually started with gathering qi, but today he was able to cultivate earlier than usual. He wanted to give Jade a chance to digest the food first.

Looking at the Qi Pool, Ferris finally felt that his constant struggle with qi was finally about to end. He still had about half of what he harvested yesterday left over, and tonight it would only grow. He still had lots of things he wanted to spend the qi on, but nothing that he needed. The daily emptying of the pool should be over.

Ferris sat down in front of the Qi Pool, while Jade started systematically exploring and mapping the area.

The first thing Ferris wanted was a journal to store notes in the soulspace. It didn’t sound as vital as some of his other needs, but it was getting harder to keep track of his observations, experiment notes, and future plans. His uncle had also told him to start keeping a record of personal demons, then begin investigating their sources.

He was starting to become accustomed to the casual use of paper that Hollowfield had. It no longer felt as scandalous. Back home, the schoolhouse protected its prized textbooks viciously, and woe to any child who failed to show proper respect.

Since he was most familiar with it, Ferris decided to start by making a chalkboard. Placing his hands in the Qi Pool, he imagined a simple wooden board covered in black paint. He tried to keep the qi used to a minimum.

Pulling his hands out, he now held a simple chalkboard. It was identical to any of the many boards he had used growing up.

Next, he needed something to write with. Placing one hand back in, he imagined a long, slender stick of chalk. The powdery rock was one of a thousand resources that delvers would find in rifts and bring back to support the clan. It was cheap due to the lack of any extra processing it needed to be usable. A monster’s hide could similarly be brought back and processed into velum, or plant matter processed into paper, but the work involved would make it much more expensive.

Withdrawing his hand, he now held a piece of chalk. A few tests on the board showed that it worked as expected. This would be useful for organizing his plans, but for now, he set them aside to work on his next project: a journal.

Ferris remembered the small room his uncle had shown him in the spiritual device. It had two chairs, a comfy fireplace, and most important of all: a bookshelf. It was a message Uncle Winton was sending Ferris, about the direction to take his home base in the soulspace.

Looking around, Ferris saw his Heartfire, the Qi Pool, the core pedestal, and then a whole lot of stuff lying around on the ground. In stark contrast to the cozy room, Ferris had a utilitarian campsite. It wasn’t that he had no organization, but there were no places to put things away. No comforts of home and hearth, or even a simple chair to sit in. Ferris would need to build them, and he wanted to start with a bookshelf filled with journals.

Of course, even a simple bookshelf was likely a luxury Ferris couldn’t afford yet, but he could start with a single book. He once again placed a hand in the Qi Pool and imagined the journal he had recently purchased in the real world. He pictured the texture of the cover and how it felt to flip through the pages. The smell of the glue holding it together.

Pulling his hand out, Ferris was holding something in the form of a book. Unfortunately, the shape was the resemblance ended. When he tried to open it to flip through the pages, it was stuck together as a solid block.

Playing with the failure, Ferris considered what he did wrong. The journal was probably the most complicated thing he had tried to create with qi forming. Everything else was a single material shaped into an imagined form. The most complex thing he had done previously was change the nature of something with his intent, like making a flexible string. The book he had tried to make had many closely touching pages, all bound together in a cover with glue.

Taking a step back in his goals, Ferris placed a hand back into the Qi Pool and imagined a single page of paper. He made it a large, thin rectangular sheet.

Lifting his hand, he was pleased to see a much better result. The paper was just as he had imagined it, and even felt like he remembered from the sheets in his journal in the real world.

The last thing Ferris wanted before giving crafting a break—his poor Qi Pool was shrinking too much—was something to write with. While his experience reading from books had been common, writing on paper had been rare. Before coming to Hollowfield, he had only done it in a special writing class. Ferris was used to using a feather and ink, but in Hollowfield he had purchased a writing pencil along with his journal. The fabulous thing was much more convenient than carrying around a vial of ink; he simply had to sharpen it occasionally.

Still, he wanted to keep things simple. Ferris decided to start with a feather and ink. The writing pencil had several components that Ferris didn’t fully understand, and he wanted to experiment with qi forming a liquid.

He first tried to make a feather, but it was a failure. It looked more like a small stick than anything else. Ferris realized that a feather was actually pretty complicated. Everything from the shaft to the branching vane was all complex and detailed.

Perhaps that was why the design for the bolt from Basic Crossbow Construct Crafting used thin fins extending out, rather than anything like a feather to stabilize its flight.

Trying again, Ferris decided to lean into this ‘stick’ idea. He made it as simple as possible while still having a pointed, hollow tip. This worked much better, although it remained to be seen how well it held ink.

Speaking of which, the ink required a bit of planning. Ferris had made glass vials when testing the acid resistance of various materials and intents, so he was able to easily make a glass well to store the ink in.

The problem with the ink was how to contain it as he created it. If he just stuck his hand in his Qi Pool as usual, it would not end well. The ink would simply pollute his Qi Pool.

Instead, Ferris decided to fill the inkwell with liquid qi, and then place a finger in as well. He focused on the qi at the tip of his finger and imagined forming it into black ink.

It worked well, with the minor inconvenience of dying his finger black. It would hopefully fade back to normal over time, as he didn’t have any way to scrub the ink off.

Finally, with a sheet of paper and ink, Ferris considered what to use it for. The first thing he wanted to record was a description of each type of personal demon, and when they had appeared. He pulled over the chalkboard and started to organize his memories.

Before coming to Hollowfield, Ferris hadn’t been cultivating for long. He had mostly stayed near his Heartfire. The first time he’d explored, he ran into a pack of red imp-like demons that tore him apart. The experience made him cautious.

Next were the blue frogs, which had appeared on the day Ferris arrived in Hollowfield. Just because he had only noticed them on that day didn’t mean they hadn’t existed before, off hiding in the darkness.

The chain demons followed, announcing themselves by stealing his oath statue seven days later. These Ferris felt he understood the best, likely representing his fear of being magically bound to Hollowfield and Jade. Special note was given to the lanky chain demon, which was both smarter and stronger than the rest. He hadn’t seen anything like it since.

Lastly, the cloaked demons with the golden horns started infesting his Air Passage. They’d appeared the day after the chain demons had stolen the statue. Perhaps they were related to his concern over being stolen from, although that didn’t feel quite right.

Happy with his notes, Ferris went to transcribe them to paper. He quickly realized that it was not easy to write without a table, but he managed by using the chalkboard as a lap desk. The stick-feather wrote moderately badly, but it got the job done. Ferris went slowly, making the letter as neat and concise as possible, so there was plenty of room leftover to add more. He spent a few minutes just looking at the finished record, happy with his progress.

While considering what else he wanted to record, Ferris spent the qi to make a couple more large sheets of paper.

On one sheet, he wrote down everything he could remember from the books Elder Harper had shown him while deciding on his bond. He couldn’t share that information due to the oath, but writing it down would help him remember for personal use.

The next sheet got a log entry for each meeting of Elder Harper’s research project. They had met once as a full group and three times with just Serena, Jade, and Ferris. He tried to remember as many details from those first meetings as he could. Today’s list of experiments with Serena was much more recent in his mind, so he was able to add a lot more to the latest entry.

After that, he made a page dedicated to all his observations in the slimespace, particularly about the strange creatures there. This shared some information with the previous notes on the research project, but he figured it was worth two separate records. He also included everything he learned from Adept Nyoka on familiar bonds, and a reminder to investigate spirit animals.

Starting a new page, he made a list of all abilities he knew or suspected slimes in the wild had. The three examples that Uncle Winton had shown him were the start, but he hoped to collect more stories from various delvers and Hollowfield’s library.

When he had time, he wanted to visit the library and transcribe the list of tier one acid abilities with technical manuals available from the clan. It would likely take a while, as he would need to enter and exit meditation a lot to copy it all over. Gilbert had said it was allowed, and even hinted that there were abilities or techniques to help.

Strangely, it would probably be easier to record the blueprints from the jade slip on Basic Crossbow Construct Crafting than the real-life books. Even though it was stored in a jade slip that no mundane person could access, he could just go over and copy them directly.

Looking at the stack of papers, Ferris decided to splurge a little on a box to hold them. It would be a shame to simply lay them on the ground. He probably should have saved the qi, but it just felt better for the records to have a real, protected home. He qi formed a simple wooden box with a lid that sat on top, then placed the records into it.

Looking at it all together, Ferris couldn’t help feeling satisfied. Writing the notes hadn’t given any shocking discoveries or uncovered anything new. But it all felt more organized in his head. More… manageable.

The process had also taught him more about qi forming, and its limits. Such things always came with a cost, but it’d been worth it.

The more he knew, the easier the next challenge would be.
