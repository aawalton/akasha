Gary was span through the familiar expanse of swirling colors and distant stars. When reality clicked into place again, he stood on the fourth floor.

A pleasant breeze welcomed him. The terrain was friendly, rolling hills and fields of grass, with a forest visible in the distance. Birds chirped overhead, chasing each other through the skies.

Gary, who had been ready to pounce, slowly relaxed. “This is a much better welcome than on the third floor,” he muttered.

“Yeah! No floor boss this time,” Goldie replied.

The rest of the clan had appeared around him, minus the Elders, who couldn’t access the floor while the event was ongoing. Most people were junior disciples at the Birth of Aura realm, but there were also dozens of Core Formation experts. Before anyone could organize them, a blur shot out of the forest, raising a cloud of dust as it sprinted at them.

“Careful!” someone shouted. Two hundred spears were drawn at once.

“Don’t attack!” Feng Shen’s voice rose over the clamor.

The sprinting blur slid to a stop before them, showering them with stones and dirt. People coughed. When the dust cleared, they saw a powerfully-built, seven-foot-tall humanoid, naked except for a pair of shorts, a pair of boxing shoes, and two blue boxing gloves which fit snugly over its hands. A proud afro decorated its head, while its skin was dark. Despite its clear eyes, it was a monster.

Pro Boxer, Level 30

Now we’re talking! Who cares about tentacly abominations of horror? I’m more of a fun man myself. This guy’s not your enemy, but anyway, he’s a super-duper-heavyweight who’s practiced since his mother’s womb. When he’s not beating up beef boys—or when he’s not at war—he roams the floor in search of worthy sparring partners.

“Osu!” the monster yelled, spreading its arms low and to the side. “Are you in our corner, or theirs?”

“We’re allies,” Feng Shen said, stepping out of the crowd.

“All of you?”

A screen popped up for Gary.

Invasion Quest

Your courage is recognized. You are now part of the ongoing invasion quest and must select a side to fight for. Will you choose the Gymnasium or the Tentacle Horrors?

The Gymnasium, Gary thought.

Army registered. For the duration of the event and one day more, Gymnasium monsters will not be hostile unless provoked, and Tentacle Horror monsters will be hostile on sight.

The event lasts until either army leader is slain. Until then, leaving this floor is not permitted. Rising above Level 50 is not permitted. Individual rewards will be given out at the end of the war and calculated based on the amount of merit you have claimed. Happy warring!

The pro boxer monster kept its eyes narrowed at the clan as everyone fiddled with screens. Finally, it dropped its shoulders and spoke. “You’re my partners! You really scared me there. Come, come, I’ll take you to the gym!”

It turned and sprinted dead-bolt through the field.

“Run, everyone! It will not wait!” Feng Shen said, and suddenly, there came the glorious sight of two hundred cultivators putting their spears away and running full speed ahead through a grass field. Gary was at the front of the pack, right behind the Core Formation disciples. Everyone behind him was covered in so much dust he doubted they could see.

He made a quick group chat.

Gary: Are you guys okay?

Cog: I can’t see, I’m blind!

Alicia: Gary, where are you?

Gary: Up ahead. Let me know if you lose the group and I’ll come find you.

“Follow the people in front of you, everyone! If anyone gets separated, yell as loudly as you can!” Feng Shen’s voice washed over the crowd.

Cog: How is he the leader, anyway? There are more senior Core Formations. He just became one.

Gary: Maybe it’s potential?

Alicia: It’s due to respect. Everyone acknowledges Feng Shen’s intelligence, kindness, and hard work, even the seniors. A few might be disgruntled, but he’s the only person who could lead the entire group.

Gary nodded. He then sent another quick text.

Gary: Are you okay back there?

Lin Xin: I’m ahead of you, Gary.

He raised his gaze to find Lin Xin’s graceful form sprinting with the Core Formations. He laughed and accelerated, quickly catching up.

The pro boxer’s pace wasn’t too demanding. Even the weakest Birth of Aura cultivators could follow. As time passed, however, many found themselves lagging behind.

Cog: How long will they keep this up?

Alicia: Do you see anything, Gary?

He squinted through the dust raised by the pro boxer and the few disciples ahead of him. All he saw was sky, grass, and flatness.

Gary: Maybe their gym is underground?

Cog: Shit.

Alicia: Shit.

More time passed, and the pro boxer showed no signs of slowing down.

Strength and Speed affected a person’s sprinting speed. Vitality affected their endurance. Gary couldn’t see behind him due to the dust, but if even he was panting, then dozens of cultivators must have already dropped out. They’d have to follow later by themselves.

He wasn’t worried about Cog and Alicia yet. Those two had trained hard and reached the last days of the tournament preliminaries, and also, they were Level 25. They could handle this much. For now.

“You run a lot!” he called out to the pro boxer.

“Sprinting is important! The coach told me you’d show up, so I figured I might as well do my morning run!” the monster replied.

So this is just its morning run, huh… Gary thought, shaking his head with mirth.

A different screen popped up.

Quirk: Sorry for the delay, had to wrestle some naagins. Big day. How’s the fourth floor, bud? Fun?

Gary: I guess.

Quirk: Right! So, new floor, new quest! Are you ready?

Gary: Is it Legendary again?

Quirk: Always.

Gary: Bring it on.

The chat disappeared, giving way to a wider screen.

Quest: Tickle Satan’s Balls

Tickle the balls of Satan Jr. and survive for one minute.

Accept? Y/ N

No, Gary thought.

Quirk: Woah there! I blocked that, but don’t go around refusing my quests! You cannot get another for this floor!

Gary: I’m not tickling anyone’s balls, Quirk.

Quirk: Because it’s gay?

Gary: BECAUSE I’M NOT A CARTOON, QUIRK! Who even is this guy? Doesn’t he sell the Satan Shakes?

Quirk: Come on!

Gary: No. You picked this quest to mess with me. I’m not doing it.

Quirk: The quests are random. I can only pick the difficulty! Come on, bud, they’re all like that. Quests adapt to the floor. It would have asked you to graft a tentacle to your back on the swamp floor, and here it asks you to tickle someone’s balls. It’s thematic!

Gary: It would have asked me to do what?

Quirk: Oh, um, yeah. It would have kickstarted a whole side-plot, too, where the puppeteer whispers in your mind through the tentacle. Anyway, glad you didn’t cancel that Elder slapping quest.

Gary: Remind me to never take a horror floor quest.

Quirk: Sure. So, ball tickles?

Gary closed the chat and sighed deeply.

Gary: Feng Shen has advanced access, too. Did he receive the same quest?

Quirk: I’m not his liaison. I couldn’t tell you even if I knew, but I promise you it’s something equally ridiculous.

Gary pressed his eyes shut. He thought to the crate of Satan Shakes which gave him fifteen stat points in half an hour. To the Starvein manual, its massive promises, and how well it seemed to fit him. He sighed again.

Gary: Fine, Quirk. Ball tickles. Whatever. I accept the quest.

Quirk: Cool! Happy warring!

Gary shook his head. His neck ached from all the times he’d done it in the last minute.

More time passed. Despite his massive physique, the sprinting boxer showed no signs of stopping. His shoes thundered against the grass as he shot into a forest, crossed it entirely, then emerged in another wide plain. A settlement rested in its middle—something between a building and a city. It was still distant, so Gary couldn’t see it well.

Monsters were everywhere around it, though.

Gary: I can see it! Don’t give up!

Cog: If I had…a heart…it would have…given up already.

Alicia: I’m sorry, Gary. I stopped at the edge of the forest. I’ll catch up later.

Gary: Don’t worry about it. I’ll save you a good squat press or something.

As they neared the city, he could finally identify the monsters around it. They were chubby men and women, all wearing the same retro bodybuilding outfit: a sleeveless tank top, very low-cut on the sides—also called a stringer tank—with tight, high-cut athletic shorts underneath. Their entire outfit was striped red and blue, and they had clear muscles under their chubbiness, making them resemble high-weight wrestlers rather than fighters.

No, that wasn’t exactly right—different people had different builds. Only the first group was chubby. Some were hard and stout, with wide arms and boxing gloves. Others were leaner, wearing shin protection alongside their gloves, while yet more were massive, extremely muscular, and with ridiculously low body fat. All wore the same red-and-blue retro bodybuilding outfit.

“Some things are universal, but why is it always the wrong things?” Gary lamented.

Beef Boy, Level 24

In the Gymnasium’s hierarchy, Beef Boys are the lowest and most populous caste. They’re hard-working amateurs just starting on their journey of boxing, wrestling, calisthenics, or body-building, and they’re characterized by low intelligence, unreasonable bravery, and unwavering zeal.

While Beef Boys spring from the grass fully-grown, legend says they come from another place in the Tower, where they can return if they become strong enough…

Gary could see women among them, so he could only assume that boy was some sort of gender-neutral term here.

The beef boys were scattered on the grass around the settlement, either alone or in groups, all engaged in training. Some shadow-boxed, some worked out, others did hand-stands. A few sparred in pairs or had free-for-alls in larger groups, which was mostly people getting senselessly beaten up. A class was held by a massive, sumo-like woman who had her beef boy disciples doing endless push-ups. Side-abs were visible on their ribs. All over the field, grunting was heard, and the grass was wet by sweat.

Gary liked it.

“Osu, senior brother!” the beef boys called as the pro boxer passed by them.

“Osu!” he replied, finally slowing to a jog. As the dust settled, Gary could see that only a quarter of the original group remained—most being Core Formation disciples. A string of panting people lay on the ground all the way to the forest. Cog had also given up, just short of the training field. Even his mechanical constitution couldn’t take this sprinting.

The pro boxer tilted his head up at the sumo trainer before finally entering the settlement. Gary paused at its entrance. The name “Bayonet Boxing Gym” was spelled in a wooden plaque over it, while the sound of thuds echoed from inside. Gary realized this was no settlement at all. It was a massive, open-air gym surrounded by walls.

On the inside, beef boys busied themselves on various gym equipment. Dozens of bench presses stood in a row, all occupied by huffing beef boys. The weight plates ranged from two hundred pounds to several tons, but they were the same size as normal gym plates—just much, much denser. All equipment expected of a normal gym was present, including saunas and massage rooms where beef boys took care of other beef boys. The only instrument missing was the treadmill.

A deodorant stand stood to a side, right next to a Satan Shake stand featuring the oversized mug of Satan Jr. as Gary had seen it on the quest reward crate. A beef boy manned each stand.

Besides gym instruments, there was equipment for various martial arts. Boxing gloves, mits, and helmets were strewn to a side, reeking of sweat so bad Gary could smell it from here. There were also belts and leg protections. Boxing bags hung in a long row, pummeled by a string of beef boys, while two pro boxers sparred in one of the gym’s four rings. To the side stood a wrestling circle, where two massive beef boys had locked heads and arms. Shouting echoed alongside the sound of gloves hitting mits and bags and well-toned bodies.

Gary looked near himself, seeing the robe-dressed, spear-wielding cultivators walk into the gym. They seemed so out of place. The Birth of Aura disciples were looking around in as much surprise as himself, while the Core Formations who’d been here before followed the pro boxer to the center of the gym. Gary hurried to do the same.

By this point, Gary felt exhausted, and even most Core Formation disciples had sweated. The pro boxer was only slightly panting. “I brought the new guys, Coach!” he said.

“Then get back to training!” a bark came in reply. The pro boxer scurried away, quickly tearing into a boxing bag.

Gary had to admit he hadn’t noticed the coach until he spoke. Unlike every other monster around, who seemed like a fit person at their peak, the coach was the only old one. He was tiny, too, in the usual manner of old people, and he supported himself on a cane. Under his short hair, however, he carried a temperament so fierce it even outstroke his sharp eyes.

“Why are you still here? Go train!” he ordered the cultivators, briefly taking his eyes off the pro boxers sparring in the ring.

Coach Bayonet, Level 50 (Field Boss)

Once a world-class boxer, Bayonet is now the owner, manager, and head coach of the Bayonet Boxing Gym. While past his prime, Coach Bayonet remains an experienced fighter. Attacking him incurs the automatic hostility of every monster in and around the Bayonet Boxing Gym.

“Greetings, Coach Bayonet. We are here to assist in the war against the puppeteer,” Feng Shen said, stepping forward and cupping his fists.

“Why didn’t you say so?” the coach asked, giving them a second look. “The board is over there. Outside battle, I want to see you all running thrice per day and training twice. You can’t defeat anyone with those twigs you have for arms.”

“Yes, Coach,” Feng Shen replied, and the coach turned back to the ring, ignoring them. He barked a few commands, and both fighters adjusted themselves.

Gary turned to where the coach had pointed, alongside the rest of the cultivators. A large board was just getting hung on the back wall, a beef boy struggling to hammer the nails while another held the stairs under it. A third monster was watching over them, but this was no beef boy.

A lean build that someone had injected with as much muscle as it could stand without ripping. Thin, golden boxing gloves. High-cut boxing shorts, dark skin, and a clean-shaven head. Just by standing there, this monster exuded intense energy and aura, like a coiled tiger about to pounce.

It turned to look at them. Red eyes swept over the group. Gary felt the pressure.

Boxing Champion Strok, Level 50 (Sector Boss)

Strok used to be a poor, struggling orphan. The turn in his life came when he realized he was good at fucking people up, at which point he decided to do it plenty.

Strok is now the best boxer on the floor. He has been trained by Coach Bayonet to annihilate anyone in the ring and outside of it. If faced in single combat inside the ring, the Boxing Champion adjusts his level to the challenger’s and reduces his rank to Field Boss. Defeating him rewards the Boxing Belt, one of the Gymnasium’s four champion belts. Collect all four belts for a special reward.

An orphan? I thought they spawned from the grass, Gary thought.

“Sup. You must be the new guys,” the champion said. A wide belt sat on the monster’s waist, with the letters GBA on its top.

“Yes, Champion,” Feng Shen said.

“Good. Check out the board and then go train as the coach said. Ignoring him is disrespect, and anyone who disrespects the coach will have to deal with me.”

The champion moved aside, watching them with arms crossed as if actually expecting them to train. Gary ignored the staring monster for now to look at the board. It was wooden and massive, with a long piece of paper occupying most of its surface. While it looked ordinary, the paper was clearly magical, as instructions and names kept appearing and disappearing on its surface. Two lists were on it, the first exceedingly simple.

BEEF FOR HIRE

Beef Boy Mercenary: 1,000 aura stones

Pro Boxer Mercenary: 25,000 aura stones

Boxing Champion Mercenary (1 of 1 remaining): 500,000 aura stones

Gary understood that. The second list, titled Rewards, was far longer, containing hundreds of items. He recognized very few. Most sounded like alchemical materials or items imbued with specific properties. For many, he didn’t have the slightest idea what they were.

The few he did recognize, though, were all impressive. The Flying Fools Card was the same as the third place prize of the tournament, and it was a life-saving treasure. He also spotted a few with promising names. As for the last item on the list…

REWARDS

Satan Shake: 100 merit points

Satan Shake Crate: 666 merit points

Flying Mat: 1,000 merit points

Flying Fools Card: 10,000 merit points

Lifetime VIP Satan Pass: 200,000 merit points

Talent Serum: 500,000 merit points
