Chapter 33: The First Two Transmigrators Part 2 (The American's POV)

I hadn't paid the floating screen much attention, but now felt like a good time.

"Hey. Can you tell me what this is?" I raised the still-smoking leaf up close to the notification hovering in my vision, while keeping it near my nose. Weeds are expensive. No reason to waste a good product just because I was talking to a mysterious screen.

[A weed dosed with spirit energy.]

"I see." I turned the leaf slightly, watching the ember at its edge glow steady red. "What exactly is this spirit energy?"

[Energy that rains down from the heavens itself. The higher you are, the denser it becomes.]

"Does it make the weed taste better? You know. Overall experience?"

[Probably. Absorbing spirit energy tends to be quite addictive.]

That made something click. No wonder Wei had been sitting there without moving for the better part of an hour. From where he was sitting, that probably felt like an endless supply of weed.

I looked down at the plant still rooted in the dirt beside the bush. It was glowing indifferently to my presence, quietly pulling in whatever ambient spirit energy happened to be floating around it. Then I looked up, past the treeline, scanning the horizon until I saw it.

A mountain. Tall enough that its peak disappeared into low clouds.

"If I move this thing up there, would it taste even better?"

[Yes.]

I had questions, but I crouched down anyway. I dug carefully around the base of the plant, working my fingers into the soil until I'd freed the roots without tearing them. Then I cradled the whole thing with a generous fistful of dirt in both hands.

I stood back up. "While we're at it. Is cultivating up there more effective too? For an actual person, I mean."

[Yes. Height matters.]

"Right." I nodded to myself. "I should probably go grab Wei, then."

I turned and headed back the way I'd come, weed cradled carefully in both hands the whole way, careful not to jostle the roots.

***

Back at the crater, Wei hadn't moved an inch. Same cross-legged position, same slow, deliberate breathing, same stillness that made him look less like a person and more like a piece of the landscape that happened to be shaped like a person.

I crouched beside him. "Hey. Let's head up to that mountain over there." I tilted my head toward it.

He ignored me entirely. Didn't so much as twitch.

"Are you asleep?"

"Shut up, Juan," he said, without moving. "Can't you see I'm cultivating?"

"Sure, but it'll be way more effective up there."

"Cultivation doesn't require height." His eyes stayed shut, but his voice had gone sharp. "You think you just walk higher and somehow get closer to heaven? Are you stupid?"

Fair point. But the floating screen had told me otherwise.

I sighed, set the weed down carefully on a flat patch of grass, and dug the berries out of my pocket. I placed them beside him. "Goodbye, Wei. I'll be heading higher, then."

Nothing. Not even a grunt of acknowledgement.

I picked the weed back up, tucked it carefully against my chest, and started walking toward the mountain.

***

The way up was not easy, especially with both hands occupied. The slope sat at a stubborn forty-five degrees, steep enough that I had to lean forward a bit just to keep from tipping backward, weed cradled against my chest the entire climb.

It took an hour, maybe more. Hard to say exactly, since checking my phone required setting the weed down first, which felt like a betrayal after everything we'd already been through together.

I stopped under a tree eventually, set the plant down carefully in a patch of shade, and dug the phone out of my pocket.

Eleven percent battery. 12:26 PM. No wonder my stomach had started staging a protest. I sighed, pocketed the phone, picked the weed back up, and got to my feet.

That's when I saw the kids. Three of them, sprinting up the slope ahead of me, laughing, weaving between rocks like they'd done this exact climb a hundred times before.

Civilization?!

"Hey! Kids!" I broke into a jog after them. "Are you from the top of the mountain, or below it?"

This was important. If there was a settlement lower down, that seemed like the smarter move. Actual food, actual shelter, and actual people. You can't eat spirit energy, whatever it apparently tasted like.

One of them, a boy, turned his head without slowing down. "Are you stupid, old man? Why would we live somewhere low when a higher place is right there?"

The other two laughed.

So the system had been right.

[I'm always right.]

I nodded. "Can I come up with you guys?"

"Sure, old man." He grinned. "If you can keep up, that is."

"I'm twenty for the record." I pushed harder, legs burning, and the distance between us didn't shrink at all. If anything it grew. Three small figures pulling steadily ahead of me up a slope that seemed to get worse the closer I got to the top.

By the time I finally crested the peak, they were long gone, presumably tucked into one of the small wooden houses scattered across the summit. The whole plateau smelled like grilled meat, rich and smoky. My stomach made a decision on my behalf before my brain fully caught up.

I followed the smell. I found the source: a small house near the edge of the settlement, smoke curling from a chimney. I knocked using my foot, since both hands were still full of glowing weed.

The door opened. A young woman stood in the frame, wiping her hands on an apron. "How can I help you?"

From somewhere behind her, at a table, a kid's voice rang out. "Ah! It's the old man!"

"Again. I'm twenty," I corrected.

"Yuto, that's not nice." The woman half-turned toward the table.

Yuto didn't answer. He'd already gone back to focusing on the piece of meat in front of him.

I looked between the two of them. "It must be hard, raising a kid on your own."

"What are you talking about?" She crossed her arms, cheeks puffing out. "I'm his sister. I'm not that old."

I laughed awkwardly. "Sorry." Then my stomach made its presence known, loud and without shame.

Len sighed. She turned. "Come in and join us." She walked back to the table and sat on a chair.

I followed inside. "Thank you—"

"Len," she said.

"Thanks for having me, Len." I turned to the kid. "And Yuto. My name's Juan, by the way."

"No one asked, old man." Yuto shoved another piece of meat into his mouth without looking up.

Len stared at me, or more specifically at the weed I was still clutching against my chest like a small, glowing infant. "You're not putting that on my table. Outside."

"Right." I ducked back to the door, set the plant down carefully on the step where I could still see it through the gap, and came back in. I pulled out one of the empty chairs and sat.

Len set a plate in front of me and piled it with meat.

Looking at the quantity of meat on the table, I couldn't help but ask, "Is meat easy to come by up here?"

"Huh?" Len gave me a flat look. "I got lucky today. Tomorrow it'll be vegetables."

"I see…" I regretted asking the question. I ate gratefully.

"So. Where are you from?" Len asked.

I considered telling her the truth. That I was from a world without floating islands, without spirit energy, without any of this. A world where the only thing that made you high was actually smoking the plant currently sitting on her doorstep. I decided against it. Even I knew how that sentence would sound out loud.

"I came from below," I said instead, which had the added benefit of being technically true.

"Obviously you came from below." Len sighed. "Fine. If you don't want to talk about it, don't." She set her wooden spoon down. "Do you have anywhere to stay?"

"Unfortunately, no."

"You can stay with us. It won't be free, though."

"Of course. What do you need me to do?"

"You'll tend the fields. You look like someone who knows plants." Her eyes flicked once toward the door where the weed sat glowing faintly against the wood. "And you'll clean the house."

"Sure," I said, with considerably more confidence than I actually felt, since I only know how to cultivate weeds. "What about you?"

"I'll hunt. Hopefully I get another one tomorrow."

"Go get 'em, sis!" Yuto cheered, mouth still full.

"Wait." I set my spoon down. "Shouldn't it be the other way around? I hunt, you handle the fields and house?" It felt wrong on some deep, instinctive level: a man sitting comfortably indoors while a woman went out to wrestle dinner out of the wilderness.

"You're tall, but you look like someone who doesn't know how to hunt," Len said. "And you are so white. Do you even do manual labor?"

"My color is inborn," I replied.

"Yeah, he's a slowpoke." Yuto grinned. "The game would be halfway to the next mountain before he even got his bow up."

I had no rebuttal to offer beyond a wounded sense of masculine pride. I sighed. "Fine. I'll be the housewife."

"What are you talking about?" Len stood, already moving toward the door. "Hurry up and clean the dishes. Your work starts now."

"Yes, ma'am." I gathered the plates, stacking them carefully, and looked around the small room. "Where's the sink?"

"What sink?"

"Water? To clean them?"

Len pointed out the door. "Go right. There's a river."

I looked outside. The ground past the doorway sloped sharply downward, disappearing into treeline well below the level of the house. "Don't tell me I have to go down there?"

"Obviously. Gravity pulls water down."

"Right." I picked up the whole stack, balanced it against my head, and headed for the door. I was already dreading the climb back up.
