“What he said is true, Mr. Guard. These weapons could be repaired, but we do not have the necessary ingredients to repair them,” the blacksmith said. James felt relieved when the blacksmith told the guards the truth. Nightmares of his coworkers being framed by their boss for something they hadn’t done during his workdays haunted him.

The blacksmith didn’t lie because he was honest. If a trial were held and he were found lying, the punishment would be more severe. That was why he told them the truth.

“Hm… I see. That changes a lot of things. The case was concluded too hastily. It shall be reopened because of additional evidence,” the guard pondered. “We need to assess the value of the end product, minus the service fee, and minus the ingredients. Do you know how much they cost? How much would the end product be valued?”

“I do not know, sir.”

“You don’t know?” The guard frowned. “In that case, this case shall go to trial before the Census Bureau.”

“I will accept.” The blacksmith nodded.

“What the hell? Couldn’t you just force the blacksmith to return the weapon he stole?”

“Will you return the weapon?” the guard asked. “If you return the weapon, that means no value was lost, and the case would be concluded.”

“No! I don’t want to return it! They are going to destroy it recklessly!”

“Assumptions or subjective bias will not hold in court, Mr. Blacksmith.”

“I will not return it,” the blacksmith said, his voice stern, hugging the rusted weapon as if it were his child.

“Trial it is, then.”

“Fuck it! The weapons are right there!” James cursed. He didn’t expect to be thrown into a legal fight inside a dungeon. He thought this kind of shit was the privilege of the modern world.

“If you take it carelessly and injure him, it would be another crime, and there would need to be reparation. If you broke the weapon, that would also be a crime.” Kaina explained the logic behind why the guards couldn’t simply force the blacksmith to return the weapon.

“Shit!” James cursed.

“Well, luckily, it happened at the end of the month, when jobs were scarce. We do have time, right, James?” Kaina smiled wryly.

“When is the trial?” After taking a few deep breaths to calm himself, James asked. His frown and irritation were clear for everyone to see. Luckily, he was not faced with short-fused, power-tripping police in the USA, but an NPC inside a dungeon. No matter how much he cursed, as long as it was not directed at them, they wouldn’t react.

“I shall go to the Census Bureau. The trial should be held somewhere in four or five hours after I debrief them. Would the concerned parties wish to follow me and wait at the Census Bureau?” the soldier asked.

“I will stay with the weapons!” James announced.

“I will come to the trial,” the blacksmith said.

And so, the three of them went to the trial. They were brought to a waiting room where they had to wait. The entire time they were there, the blacksmith kept hugging the weapons. James pondered the blacksmith’s defense—his motive. Was it simple thievery?

“You said you never even left this city, right? How are you going to use it?” James asked. “Was there even a buyer for it? You said you needed some rare items to repair it.”

“I am not going to use it! I was preventing these treasures from being broken by reckless hunters like you,” the smith said.

“Um, Mr. Smith, are you that enraged that we will use the rusted weapons and break them?” Kaina asked.

“Yes. I have seen this kind of weapon only once in my lifetime! There is rusted armor my grandpa had in the basement. By some chance, he managed to obtain starmetal and liquid metal. The result was a precious artifact that was used by our top soldier. I wanted to prove that I have the same skills as my grandfather! This will be my life’s masterpiece!”

“… I see.” James nodded. He was miffed that the blacksmith was using such an underhanded tactic, but he didn’t hate his motive.

“Kaina! I was searching for you, and here you are, on trial. What problem did you get into with James?” Kouki sprinted toward Kaina. James had left him in the tavern earlier, thinking this business would be brief.

James glanced at the lovebirds while scoffing as Kaina explained the events to Kouki.

“Just so you know, it’s not my fault. This guy wanted to steal my weapons,” James said.

“I understand. You are certainly not at fault. I will be on your side, Mr. James.” Kouki nodded.

“The trial will be held soon. Please come with me,” the soldier said.

Afterward, James was brought into a court. In movies, the main characters were usually the victim or the defendant, but James was somehow the prosecutor, even though he was the victim. Then the blacksmith sat in the defendant’s seat, even though he was the perpetrator. Again, James was reminded that dungeons were basically another world. Nothing made sense.

Wait… who was the defendant in court representing again? Was it the victim or the perpetrator?

James’ thoughts swirled to TV dramas he had watched. He was basically illiterate when it came to legal matters. He had never been in court before.

“Greetings, Mr. James, Ms. Kaina, and Mr. Alubrhan. Let the trial begin. First, let us clarify. The defendant has stolen Mr. James’ weapons. According to the law, the defendant must repay Mr. James and Ms. Kaina with goods of equal value, and you gave them steel weapons. Am I correct?”

“Yes, Judicator,” the smith nodded.

“The case should be over, but Mr. James and Ms. Kaina argue that the value of these rusted weapons is far higher than the steel weapons. Am I right?”

“Yes. These rusted weapons could be repaired. In that case, these weapons would be more powerful than steel or iron weapons,” James nodded.

“Is that true, Mr. Smith?”

“Yes. That is true, Judicator.”

“I shall invite two other smiths as neutral parties to ascertain whether these rusted weapons have the potential to be better than steel swords,” the Judicator said.

James wondered whether these so-called neutral smiths would lie. But they didn’t. While the neutral smiths certainly gave a lower estimation than the thief smith, they all agreed that the rusted weapons could be repaired and that the end result would be better than steel weapons. Just when James thought he had won, the thief smith replied.

“However, Judicator, that was only an estimation. The current value of these weapons is much lower than that of steel weapons. Without the ingredients, I could not repair them. I propose to the Judicator that I keep these rusted weapons until Mr. James or Ms. Kaina obtain the necessary ingredients for me to repair the items. Then, I will give the end product back to them while they pay me the service fee as usual,” the smith said.

“That sounds reasonable. So, Mr. James and Ms. Kaina, you have two choices. You either accept the steel weapons and give the rusted weapons to the smith, or you can let the smith keep the rusted weapons until you find the ingredients to repair them.”

Shit. I have been had!

James finally realized what the smith wanted. The smith had been honest from the beginning. He only wanted to repair the weapons. No matter how this turned out, the smith would win. James was too rash. He should not have let his feelings take hold of him. He didn’t like the way the thief smith handled it, but in the end, it still went the way the smith wanted.

“Fine. Will there be a guarantee that the weapons will be safe?” James sighed. In the first place, he wanted to repair the weapons and give the unnecessary ones to survivors like Andrew or Kouki. He was wrong to raise a fuss. Now the whole town knew they had secret weapons. When James saw Kouki, his face told him everything. Kouki wanted to bargain for James to test out the new swords.

“I will bet my life on it!” the thief smith said confidently.

“Alright. Is there anything else? I am leaving,” James sighed. Even though everything went his way, he couldn’t help but feel wrong—like something had betrayed him.

“The case has been concluded. We thank you for your understanding. May this town be in order and flourish,” the Judicator said.

“James. About the rusted weapons…” Kouki went to him.

“No. I am taking the axe, sword, and bow. Finders keepers, right?” James said. Hearing that, Kouki pouted. “I am giving Kaina an iron spear because I do not use a spear as my main weapon and it was just a normal weapon.”

“…What if I help you find the ingredients? Will you at least let me take a look at the new sword? You use axes, right? Besides, I see that you have a new sword there.”

“…If you are just taking a look, that’s fine. But I need to compare the axe, bow, and sword. I have a very useful sword skill for dueling… If it was just an elemental weapon like my axe, I am fine giving it to you.”

“Will you give Kaina the spear?”

“I think I will give Andrew the spear. Dunno how he is doing there.” James pondered the red moon. The timer said 70 hours. Too little time to do any adventuring, yet too much time to just idle around.

“Damn it. If only we didn’t have the blood moon,” Kouki cursed.

“Let’s visit Andrew and gather our intelligence. Who knows, he might find something useful,” James said.

“Nice idea!” Kouki hopped on board. He and James then rode their mount to the east, where Andrew hunted monsters with Anderson.

The land was desolate and dry; only bones and dead trees remained. Soon enough, these bones would disintegrate as well. Life was fleeting in this land. Nothing lasted more than a month. James sighed at the futility. But deep in his mind, were humans any different? If one stretched this one month into 100 years, humans and animals might as well be the same as these monsters—living and dying in the blink of an eye.

Deep in thought, James arrived in the next town. There, smoke rose from it.
