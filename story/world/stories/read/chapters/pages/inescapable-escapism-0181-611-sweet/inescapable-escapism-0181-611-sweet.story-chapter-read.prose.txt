A telltale crack sounded as I pulled the front door shut, and I couldn’t hold back the smile that crept over my face. I didn’t even try. There was no point; I knew that Mom wouldn’t see it. The sound was reassuring, though. It sent a wave of relief over me as I began to make my way down the drive.

Mom had just opened a bottle of wine, and that meant she was unlikely to follow me. She didn’t like to walk much when she was drinking, and she generally didn’t drive once she’d started either. Obviously, she’d done it a few times, and I was pretty sure that was illegal.

Actually, I was certain it wasn’t legal. I’d mentioned it to her once too, and that was foolish of me. I should have known better, but I didn’t think before I spoke, and it had annoyed Mom. She’d been furious and insisted that she was fine to drive because she’d only had half a bottle, but that was a lie.

I’d been at the dinner too. I saw her finish the bottle, and no one else was drinking white wine. She was the only one, so she must have been over the limit and should not have been driving, but I couldn’t say anything else about it. She was already too angry, and it scared me. She managed to get us back to her parents’ house without crashing, though.

But the fact that Mom had opened a bottle was a good thing. It meant I was safe to go on the walk with Rex and Duncan without having to look over my shoulder every few steps to check whether she was following me. I probably still would, but it was unlikely that she’d be there.

Unless it was intentional. Maybe she knew that I’d assume she wouldn’t come after me if I thought she’d started drinking, and she’d waited until she knew I’d hear it to open the bottle. She might have been trying to lure me into a false sense of security because she’d realised that I was trying to get out of the house without her. Or maybe she’d figured out that I was hiding something from her. That didn’t feel particularly likely, though. I’d acted well, and she hadn’t seemed suspicious, so I was probably fine.

Rex scampered ahead of me as we reached the end of the drive and turned onto the pavement, glancing back at me often. He seemed so happy to be going out for a walk, and that filled me with happiness. However, it was quickly replaced by guilt as I realised I needed to text Duncan to let him know we were on the way. That meant we’d need to stop.

My eyes flicked between Rex and the street around us as hesitation pulled at me. I knew that it would probably be fine for me to text Duncan whilst walking, but I was too worried. I was scared that he’d be startled by something and run off while I was distracted by my phone, and I wouldn’t notice in time, but if we were stopped, it felt less likely to happen for some reason.

I glanced over my shoulder, peering in the direction of my house to check whether there were any signs of Mom following me, before looking back at Rex. Reluctance continued to turn within me, but I forced myself to ignore it and speak.

“Rex,” I said, my voice feeling abnormally loud in the otherwise silent street. “Sit.”

He looked confused, but he did as I instructed, and I reached down to stroke his head.

“Good boy. I just need to send a text, but I’ll be quick,” I told him as I pulled my phone out of my pocket and unlocked it.

Hey, Mom’s not coming, so I’ll see you in a couple of minutes? I wrote before sending it to Duncan.

I began to slip my phone back into my pocket, but it vibrated before I could let go.

Yesssss, was all his reply read.

Smiling, I started walking again. My steps were bouncier than before. I could feel my ponytail swinging back and forth, and I knew that I was grinning. I probably looked weird, but I didn’t care. I was too happy, and nothing could bring me down. Not when I was about to go on a walk with Rex and my…

The smile faltered on my lips. I was wrong before, I realised. I thought it was impossible for anything to bring me down, but that was enough to push the happiness out of my heart and replace it with anxiety. It simmered in the pit of my stomach, filling me with unease and worries.

My boyfriend. That was what I’d almost called Duncan, but that might have been wrong. He may not have been my boyfriend. We’d never actually discussed it, and I didn’t know what to do about that. Should I ask him about it? Ask him what we were, what he wanted us to be?

I knew that was the smartest thing to do. Then I’d know where I stood with Duncan and what was actually going on between us. I wouldn’t need to live with the undercurrent of uncertainty and confusion that I’d been feeling whenever I thought about him, but I wasn’t sure that I’d be able to actually do it.

If I asked him, if I brought it up to Duncan, I could ruin everything. Things could become so much more complicated between us, and I didn’t want to do that. I was enjoying whatever it was that we were too much. It had been so nice and fun, and I didn’t want it to come to an end. Not yet.

But school was starting again soon. I had to know before then. Otherwise, it could be a problem. How was I meant to know how to act around him? Would he want me to pretend nothing had happened, or would he be fine talking to me and holding my hand or hugging me in front of his friends?

A mental image forced itself into my mind with such clarity that it felt like I was there. I saw myself walking across the canteen towards Duncan, a smile on my face. He was still standing as I approached him and threw my arms around him, feeling his body stiffen in shock. Surprised laughter came from his friends, and I felt Duncan finally move. He pushed me away and demanded to know what the hell I was doing, the shame audible in his voice.

Pain tore through my heart. I knew it wasn’t real; I was just imagining how it could go, but it hurt as if I were truly there, and I couldn’t help but wonder if it had happened in another world. It seemed unlikely. Duncan wasn’t that kind of person, and he’d never acted embarrassed to be my friend, but perhaps it would be different if people thought we were dating. Maybe he’d be ashamed of that.

I shook my head, trying to push the mental image and lingering ache in my chest aside. I didn’t need to think about that now. Hopefully, it would never be something I needed to worry about, and Duncan and I would probably talk about it soon. He was bound to bring it up before school started again, and if he didn’t, I’d find a way to do it. It would probably be hard, and I didn’t want to, but if it seemed as though he wasn’t going to mention it, I would.

Taking a deep breath, I glanced down at Rex. He appeared completely unbothered by the storm raging within me as he bounced along the pavement happily, and I longed to be as carefree as he seemed. That would have made my life so much easier.

“This way, Rex,” I muttered, cocking my head towards the overgrown path that led towards Duncan’s house. “Be careful.”

Rex glanced up at me for just a moment before turning onto the trail, ducking under a thorn-covered bramble that I had to skirt around. I followed after him, trying to keep my mind clear as we walked, but it was impossible. No matter how hard I tried, my thoughts kept drifting back towards Duncan and the inevitable conversation that we’d need to have at some point.

Within what felt like seconds, we were nearing the end of the path. I took another deep breath as my eyes found Duncan’s house, and another wave of anxiety slammed into me. The urge to turn away and go home or flee into another world, one where I didn’t ever need to face Duncan or worry about what might happen, pulled at me, but I forced myself to continue walking.

My anxiety grew stronger. For some reason, knocking on his front door always filled me with nerves. It was stupid. I’d been to Duncan’s house so many times, especially over the last couple of weeks. I had no reason to worry or feel scared, but I still did.

It was just so nice. I think that was the problem. I was enjoying spending time with Duncan too much, but it couldn’t last forever. Nice things never did, so it was inevitable that it would end before too long, and I wasn’t ready. I didn’t want it to ever end.

Letting out a slow exhale, I glanced down at Rex. Would he be okay with Duncan? They’d met before, and he’d seemed to like him, but that was brief. They’d only spent a couple of seconds together, and not properly. I was scared that things would be different once we were on the walk or when we got back to Duncan’s house after the walk.

“Sit,” I told Rex, waiting for him to do so before knocking.

Anxiety thrummed within me as I stood there, staring blankly at the door, but it only took a few seconds for Duncan to pull it open, and the grin on his face as he came into view caused butterflies to take flight in my stomach. I couldn’t help but return the smile as he reached out, pulling me into his arms and pressing his lips to mine.

Too soon, I pulled back and looked down at Rex, checking to make sure he didn’t think Duncan was attacking me or something. He didn’t seem too worried, though. He wasn’t even looking at us. Instead, he was gazing into Duncan’s house curiously.

“Hey,” Duncan said.

His soft tone made my heart seem to skip a beat, and I had to swallow before I could reply.

“Hey,” I replied.

“And hello, Rex,” Duncan continued, bending down to stroke him. “How are you doing today? Are you having a nice walk?”

His voice had become more animated. It sounded almost like he was talking to a baby, and I found myself grinning again. The way he spoke to Rex was so adorable, and Rex seemed to like it too. His tongue lolled out as he leaned into Duncan’s hand, silently requesting more attention.

After a few seconds, Duncan straightened up, his eyes finding me again.

“Ready to go?” he asked.

A jolt of excitement, tinged with a little anxiety, shot through me, and I felt my grip tighten around Rex’s lead.

“Let’s go.”

I moved back as Duncan grinned before pulling the door shut behind him and locking it. He pocketed his keys, turned towards us again and looked down at Rex.

“Shall we go for a walk then, Rex?” he asked, slipping into an excited tone.

Rex’s tail began to wag rapidly, and he stared up at me, seemingly waiting for me to confirm that we were about to get back to our walk. My smile widened, and I cocked my head towards the road behind me.

“Go on,” I said to him.

He didn’t hesitate. Within a second, Rex had spun around and started to race away from me, pulling at the lead in his eagerness to continue moving. I laughed as I followed him, and Duncan did the same, falling into step beside me.

“So, how’s your day been so far?” he asked, sending me another smile that caused warmth to spread through me.

My lips twitched. Duncan knew the answer to that question. We’d been texting pretty much constantly all morning, so he already knew how it had been. I didn’t really have anything new to share with him, but I replied nonetheless.

“Good. How about yours?”

“Good,” Duncan said before shooting me a look out of the corner of his eyes. “Better now, though.”

Happiness spread through me, and my cheeks began to heat up. I looked down, trying to hide my reaction because I didn’t want Duncan to know just how sweet I found his words, and my eyes landed on his hand just as it twitched.

My breath caught in my throat. It appeared as though he’d started to reach out towards me, seemingly intending to take my hand, before he stopped himself, and I was torn. I wanted him to. I wasn’t sure that I’d ever actually held hands with someone before, and I liked the idea of it, but I was terrified that my hand would be sweaty.

Plus, we were in public. We were out in the open, and anyone could see us. So many of the kids who lived in our village also went to the same school as us, and if they saw us holding hands, rumours would start to spread. People would think we were together, and Duncan might not want that.

Or Mom could see. If she had followed me, she would have seen us holding hands, and I couldn’t think of any excuses for that. She’d know that something was going on between us, and I didn’t want that. She wouldn’t take it well, so even if I wanted to and Duncan didn’t mind people finding out, I couldn’t risk holding his hand in public.

I hadn’t replied to him, I realised. It had only been a few seconds, but he’d said something sweet, and it felt wrong not to respond at all. I needed to think of something, and I wanted it to be kind of flirty, but my mind was blank. Luckily, my lips moved without conscious thought.

“Oh yeah?” I asked, my tone innocent, as if I didn’t understand what Duncan meant.

Duncan’s smile widened, even as his cheeks turned pink.

“Yeah,” he replied. “I mean, any time I get to see you, it makes my day better.”

I bit my lip. I hadn’t expected him to say something quite so… earnest, though. It caught me off guard, and I felt strangely off-balance. It felt as though all of the oxygen had been sucked out of the atmosphere, and I couldn’t breathe, but I had to. I had to find a way to reply to him.

“You too,” was all I could say.

Did he mean it? Or had he just said it to flirt with me? It sounded genuine, as if he actually meant what he’d said, but I couldn’t quite believe it. No one had ever said anything so sweet to me, and the thought of me brightening someone’s day just by being in it didn’t feel right. I wasn’t that kind of person, so he couldn’t have meant it.

It was cute, though. He was cute. He appeared to always know the right thing to say, and it seemed to come so naturally to him, whereas I struggled. Even though I generally felt calmer around Duncan, I was still distracted by my own thoughts, by my own anxieties, far too often, and that made me feel bad. I wished I were better, that I were less in my own head. It didn’t feel fair to Duncan.

“Which way do you want to go?” Duncan asked, his voice breaking through the whirlwind of thoughts spinning in my head.

I blinked, looking around. I’d barely been paying attention to where we were walking, but luckily, we were somewhere safe for Rex. There were no cars on the street, and he trotted along the empty pavement ahead of us.

“Mmmm, maybe that way? Towards the old train tracks?” I suggested, glancing over my shoulder in search of my mom before nodding at the turning ahead of us.

Duncan glanced down the path I’d gestured to before nodding.

“Sounds good!”

An old railway had cut through our village in the past, but it had been abandoned years ago. Most of it had been paved over, and the area around it had been left to the elements. It was overgrown and wild, but there was a path that led through it, and hardly anyone ever seemed to bother with it.

It was a nice walk, though. Phoebe and I used it sometimes, and we rarely ran into anyone else, so it seemed like the best place to take Rex. I’d taken him to the park with Mom the day before, and I didn’t want to just take him there again. Plus, Duncan and I would probably be alone there, and I liked the idea of that too much.

We walked slowly, our pace leisurely, as we turned onto the trail. A comfortable silence had stretched over us, and I felt myself sigh silently as I looked around. Sun streamed in through the leaves overhead, and the world felt quiet and peaceful. The only sounds, other than our footsteps, were the rustling trees and a soft burbling that came from a nearby stream.

It felt as if Duncan and I were in our own little world, like we’d somehow slipped away from reality, and I never wanted to leave. I wished we could stay right there forever, to build a house on the wooded path, and stay there for the rest of eternity. Then, I’d never have to face Mom again. I wouldn’t have to work out whether Duncan and I were dating or if it wasn’t that serious, and I could just be happy.

“So, what are your plans for the rest of the day?” Duncan asked. “Are you still going to come over later, or…”

He trailed off, but his gaze lingered on my face, and I felt myself grimace as a realisation hit me. I’d never asked him whether it would be okay for me to come over and bring Rex. I’d just assumed it would be fine, but it might not be. He might not be okay with having a dog in his house. His parents might not allow that, and they weren’t home yet, but they could still find out.

“Actually…” I started slowly, unsure how to ask him.

“It’s fine if you have plans or something,” Duncan interjected quickly.

It seemed as if he’d seen my wince and guessed that I’d changed my mind but didn’t want to tell him. That just made me feel worse, though.

“No, no, I don’t! I was just… wondering if you’re okay with us going back to yours after the walk?” I said, cringing as I spoke, before adding, “Me and Rex, that is.”

“Oh.”

Guilt and anxiety roared within me, prompting me to keep talking. It felt like I’d just invited myself over to his house, and I didn’t want him to think that. We’d already discussed it, obviously, but that was just if my mom insisted on coming on the walk with me, and I realised I wasn’t sure whether he still wanted me to go to his if we did manage to go together.

“If you’re busy or were going to do something else, that’s completely fine, though,” I told him. “Or, like, if you don’t want to have Rex in your house.”

Seemingly having heard my words, Rex glanced back at us. I smiled at him, trying to reassure him that everything was alright, despite the anxiety racing through my body.

“I’m not busy at all,” Duncan said with a wide smile. “I want you to come over, and of course I don’t mind Rex being in the house!”

I wanted to return his smile, but something was stopping me. I couldn’t help but hesitate and ask, “Are you sure? Your parents won’t mind him getting fur all over the carpet or anything?”

Duncan laughed, the sound bright and easy.

“Nah, they won’t care,” he assured me. “My uncle comes over all the time, and he always brings his dog. I don’t know what breed he is, but he’s this huge, fluffy thing that sheds like crazy. Like, he’s even fluffier than Rex, so I doubt my parents will even notice any of Rex’s hairs. They wouldn’t mind if they did, though!”

I bit my lip. I wanted to believe his words. I hoped that they were true, but still, I needed to double-check.

“Really?” I asked.

Duncan came to a stop and turned towards me, and I glanced at Rex before doing the same. Rex must have noticed us stopping, but he barely broke his stride as he ran towards another tree and sniffed it.

Anxiety simmered in my stomach as I chewed the inside of my lip, looking around to make sure Mom wasn’t hiding nearby before forcing myself to meet Duncan’s gaze. It was hard. I didn’t know what I’d see in his eyes, but he was smiling at me, the expression warm and gentle.

My heart thumped painfully, and I held my breath as Duncan reached out, taking my free hand. His smile grew slightly wider.

“I’m sure,” he told me before leaning forward and pressing his lips to mine.
