The world was not right.

Sir Daryl Pescal’s life had once been set. He’d made knight at a young age, survived the Hydra attack, and had been on the road to earning a medal of valor for his role in the victory. The rank of Field Marshal had been within reach.

Until Lilliana Silverwater usurped the ducal throne and began her “cleanse.”

Now, instead of holding a cushy post backed by the authority of a high-ranking military official, Daryl found himself and his dwindling troop hiding in a backwater village—unable to sleep even in the dead of night.

The usurper’s knights, calling themselves her Scarlet March, had been hunting them for nearly two months without pause. Somehow, no matter the tactics he used, they never lost the trail. Not even once. Without the artifact his uncle had bestowed upon him at his knighting, Daryl doubted they’d have lasted half as long.

Worse still was the beastwoman leading the hunt. It was the same abomination he'd met when Lilliana was summoned to the Duke’s court, prior to the man’s death.

Daryl exhaled sharply, his nostrils flaring as anger churned in his gut. If anything needed purging, it was the beastkin. Not him. Not his men. They’d served the Kingdom faithfully ever since his uncle had allowed him to form his own unit.

Unable to remain still, Daryl rose from the flimsy village chair he'd snagged from one of the houses they'd pillaged and stalked around the bonfire one of his remaining men had stoked. It crackled like snapping lightning in the dugout pit, doing little to banish the bone-deep cold clinging to him like a damp shroud.

“We should be inside the houses,” Carex muttered from across the fire, brown eyes the only part of him visible under his golden armor. Like the others, Carex refused to remove his golden-steel armor anymore. Not after the first midnight raid, when the Scarlet March slaughtered over half their troop in one strike. “They’d be more fortified.”

“We’ve been over this,” Daryl growled. “It also makes us easier to trap. Out here, we have escape routes.”

“What if they spot the fire?” Carex muttered. “Would be an unworthy death, to die because of a damn bonfire.”

“They haven’t noticed it yet,” Shera replied confidently, strands of blonde hair leaking from under her helm. “And they never will. This flame spell was given to me by a royal of Tenebris. Maybe if that sorcerer who follows the usurper wasn’t in coma, they’d have a chance. But with a beastkin leading them?” She snorted. “Doubtful.”

“Well, they’re finding us somehow,” Jacob, the largest and bulkiest of his men, said, tapping the helm he’d removed. His fat fingers rang dully against the golden steel. All of them stared at the helm, mourning the pride it once inspired. “If it’s not the fire, we’re making another mistake.”

“Maybe it’s the trail of bodies,” Mariah, the other of the female mages, said dryly, gesturing toward the burning pile of corpses feeding the fire. “Or maybe the stench of burning flesh. That tends to draw attention.”

“Your sarcasm is unappreciated,” Shera said with a frown. “And you insult my magic if you think there's any smell.”

“That’s funny,” came the familiar voice of the usurper's pet beastwoman. “Because I can smell it.” Dozens of heart and mana cores suddenly exploded into existence around them, lighting the dead night with the frenzy of magic and energy.

For a single breath, the camp went still.

Then the soldiers moved. Training overtook fear and his remaining knights launched into action.

Shera and Mariah reacted first, magic pulsing from them in a massive torrent as they combined wind and flame into a rampaging tornado of fire that swept over the trees and huts where the voice had come from. Everything it touched was instantly annihilated, sucked into the power of the swirling wind and eviscerated to ash from the burning flames licking the air as if the destruction is wrought sought to taste the flesh of death.

But Daryl didn’t wait to see if it had worked. He was almost certain it hadn’t. They’d used stronger spells on the beastwoman before, and she’d survived with barely a scratch.

“How did they hide so many cores?” Mariah shouted as they sprinted in the opposite direction.

The beastwoman had always been an anomaly—her core had never been detectable, despite her obvious power. But dozens of others? Hiding that many cores wasn’t possible without advanced stealth magic.

The seven of them fled through the village and split into groups of two. Daryl alone veered off on his own.

He tore into the woods, branches lashing his armor. He had less than a fraction of a second to collect his bearings and take in the new forest environment before something scraped against his sixth sense—a flicker of motion. A sliver of air displacement. He threw himself sideways as a bolt of lightning sliced past his head and obliterated a tree behind him.

When he glanced up, he saw the damned beastwoman blurring toward him, sparks of lightning crackling around her like a living storm.

He barely had time to raise his broadsword before claws wreathed in lightning slashed for his throat. He deflected the blow just in time, her talons skimming off his blade instead of tearing open his jugular. But in the darkness of the wilds, he missed her other hand until it shredded through his golden armor with a screech of rending metal that sounded more like cheap chainmail than noble steel. Then her leg flashed and cracked against the side of his skull, sending him and his armor clattering to the ground.

Pain lanced through his leg, sharp and immediate. He winced, but didn’t fall back, struggling desperately to climb back to his feet. He couldn’t run. Not against her. If he tried to retreat, she’d cut him down like a deer in a thicket. He had to press through her attack and strike back. he figured an inferior beastkin couldn’t beat him in a straight fight—not if he stayed focused, fought smart, and held out until his men dealt with their own pursuers. Then they could put this abomination in the ground.

“I hope you remember me,” the beastwoman snarled, flicking her blood-tipped claws toward the ground. Red ichor splattered against a nearby tree, glowing briefly under the arcs of lightning that danced around her. “Because I remember you."

“You’re the usurper’s mutt,” Daryl growled, forcing energy through his bronze core. The bronze energy surged within him, unrefined and raw, but more than he'd ever summoned at once. He’d hit a wall at the peak of bronze core, but there was no way this... this thing had surpassed him.

She tsked her tongue at him. “Well, that was rude.” With an easy shrug of her shoulders, she cleared her throat theatrically. “Sir Daryl Pescal, second son of Elliot Pescal, Viscount of Ellis County, you are hereby charged with three counts of high treason, forty seven counts of murder against Lysorian citizens with particular discrimination against beastkin individuals, five charges of rape, 6 counts of insubordination, and…” the beastwoman’s voice trailed off as she pulled out a small roll of parchment and opened up. After a quick scan, she continued, “Oh, right. And 131 counts of bribery.” She rolled the scroll shut and shoved it into a pocket. “By command of Her Grace, Lilliana Alistar, Duchess of Alistar, and under the war powers granted by King Zer’Nack, you are to be immediately executed for your crimes against the Kingdom of Lysoria.”

“Treason?” Daryl roared, spittle flying. “I have done nothing but serve the Kingdom!”

The beastwoman snickered, silver hair catching the wind. “I wouldn’t call selling secrets to the Pandorian Empire and plotting to overthrow the King 'service.'”

“The King is a puppet!” Daryl spat. “Weak. The Kingdom would be better off without him.”

She tilted her head, golden feline eyes narrowing. “A puppet? Whose puppet?”

Daryl tried to laugh, but it came out a ragged, blood-soaked scoff. “You fools don’t even see it, do you?” His chest heaved, blood soaking through his armor from the gash in his thigh. “The Pandorian Empire isn’t the real threat. It’s the Holy Kingdom. The Church of Life. They’ve got the whole continent crushed between their holy fingers. The Emperor’s the only one trying to save Pularea, you sanctimonious wretches. Duke Alistar could see that, why can't you?"

“Interesting,” the beastwoman said quietly, twirling a strand of silver hair around one clawed finger. She shrugged. “Holy Kingdom, Empire... they all look the same from where I’m standing. But I do agree with you on one thing, Daryl—Lysoria would be better off without the King. And it will be. Eventually. But…” She held out a hand, and a long spear shimmered into existence—summoned from a storage ring, no doubt. Daryl cursed under his breath. “…it’ll be better off without you first.”

“Fuck yo—”

The words never finished. A thin rapier punched through his back, the tip erupting from his chest like some obscene joke.

He staggered, mouth hanging open. He tried to turn, to see who had struck the blow—but the world was already slipping away.

How had someone gotten behind him? Even the beastwoman, with her lack of a core presence, wouldn't have been able to do that.

“You stole my hunt,” the beastwoman growled.

Daryl wanted to snap that he wasn’t anyone’s prey, but all that came out was a bloody, dying gurgle.

A child’s voice answered, soft and high-pitched, though with a strange ethereal feeling as if she were nothing but a ghost. "You were too slow, Lady Nida."

A young girl’s face lowered into his fading vision, and Daryl realized he was sprawled flat in the mud, the cold earth seeping into his armor. From where he lay, all he could see were the girl's solemn expression and the beastwoman’s blood-spattered feet.

Just before the darkness swallowed him whole, his mind was able to register the small girl. Sky-blue eyes and tangled, dirt-streaked blue hair. Hadn't he seen her before? Where...

“Next time, don’t touch my prey, Iketha,” the beastwoman said, her voice cold with irritation. “Grab the body. The Duchess wants them all brought to her.”

The girl gave no reply. Or maybe she did. Maybe he was already too far gone to hear it.
