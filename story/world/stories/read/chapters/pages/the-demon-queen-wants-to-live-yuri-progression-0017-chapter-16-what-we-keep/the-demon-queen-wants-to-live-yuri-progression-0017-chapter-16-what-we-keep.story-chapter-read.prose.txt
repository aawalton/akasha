More than One Thousand Years Ago.

The [Great Abyssal Mother] lay dead at my feet, and I was annoyed.

I held my hand outstretched, fingers still curled from the [Greater Silence] that had ended her.

In front of me was a corpse so large that its tail vanished into the dark at the edge of the cavern. The Hydra had twelve heads, each split and splayed in every direction. The heads were open into snarls. They never would again. The teeth alone were taller than most of my generals.

Around the great beast were other small bodies. The bodies of my horde, that which I had sent into this place. Hellhounds lay in piles all around the cavern, their bodies twisted and mangled over each other.

Imps had been crushed flat and a full regiment of Fiends -two hundred strong- had been reduced to a smear of viscera along the far wall.

It was truly a waste of my time. Only slaying a God would have let me get more experience points now. I lowered my hand and turned, regarding the man behind me.

"Zarvok."

Zarvok stood twenty paces behind me. He was leaning on his warhammer, and even so, he towered. The demon was ten feet of muscle, and was almost but not quite broad enough to block the tunnel entrance behind him.

He leaned on a warhammer taller than he was. A slab of black iron that he dragged whenever he walked. The marks it left behind were a reliable way to track his movements through my fortress. He was also bleeding from his skull, and somewhere beneath his chestplate, if the steady dripping was any indication.

"Your Royal Darkness," he said. His voice was so deep I could feel it in my chest. "My apologies. The beast proved more tenacious than the reports suggested. Taking out any of its heads alone was-"

"Zarvok." My one word silenced him. "I gave you three hundred soldiers and half a day. This creature has been living and nesting in these halls for an entire week. You could not manage this task yourself?

Zarvok looked at me and shifted his feet. He had the good sense to choose his words carefully, which was rare. "The beast's regeneration was -if Your Royal Darkness would permit me the observation- beyond the pale. Each head returned larger than before, as if death itself recoiled and so simply-"

"Zarvok." I had not permitted him his observation.

"Yes, my Queen?"

"Shut up."

I turned back to the corpse and regarded it one final time. I did not have time for this. I had wars against the Elven Dominion to plan, but instead here I was-

There was a sound. It was so small that only my ears might have heard it. The sound was wet and…almost keening. It came from somewhere beneath the giant corpse, from the part of it that curved against the cavern's right wall.

"My Queen?" Zarvok took a step forward.

I raised a hand and stepped forward. I found the source. The Hydra had been nesting in my lower halls, and I saw the nest itself.. It was built from shed scales, pressed against a carved hollow in the wall itself. Inside it, half buried in the remnants of a shattered leathery egg, was a hydra.

The creature was small, no larger than the smallest of my hellhounds. The beast had only three heads as opposed to its mothers twelve. It was still wet from the fluid of its birth. Its six eyes were all open and watching me.

I raised my right hand and gathered mana. The [Balefire] would be simplest. It would leave nothing to clean up later. Hydras were rather cumbersome to deal with if given the chance to grow. Best to vaporize the creature now before it tried to flee. Seconds passed, and the creature did not flee. Its three heads just watched me with an intensity that seemed almost absurd for something smaller than even a hellhound. Its body was trembling, though whether from cold or from the aftershock of its mother's death, I could not say.

"Well?" I said. "Attack me or run, death lies either way." It did not attack, nor did it run. "Your mother tried. She failed, obviously, but she tried. Surely you can manage the same."

The hydra pressed itself against the nest. It just…lay there and did not move.  I held the [Balefire] ready. One thought was all it would take.

"Perhaps the fool child believes you are its mother, my Queen."

I turned and stared at Zarvok. He looked uncertain about having spoken. That was good, at least he had some decency.  "Its mother lies dead behind me." I said flatly. Was the fool blind? Zarvok was a great many things, and intelligent had never been among them.

"Indeed, Your Darkness…and yet the hatchling does not run." He paused, and then he looked thoughtful. "I know something of this from my sister. If she were here, she would say the creature has chosen. They fix upon the first powerful thing they see, and they do not let go."

"Your sister's expertise in rearing beasts is noted and unwelcome."

The hydra still lay there. I looked down at it. It looked back up at me. Its six eyes were a deep and unremarkable black. I considered.

A hydra was no dragon, but it was closer to one than most beasts would ever be. [The Great Abyssal Mother] had destroyed three hundred of my soldiers before I had ended her. That was the bloodline of this thing. This, raised under proper direction, trained from birth - it might grow into something formidable. Trained by me, perhaps it could one day swat the greatest Dragon from the sky.

It was a strategic consideration. That was-

Then the weight of the Class came. It pressed against me from every direction. I felt it pull me towards the correct choice. The one the world had written for me and everything around me. Whatever story Fate had written for this world, this Hydra had no place in it.

I gritted my teeth and pushed back with will as much as I did mana. I clenched my jaw and it ached from the clenching. The [Balefire] guttered and died in my hand.

"My Queen?"

I ignored him and knelt. The beast regarded me, and then it rose. One of its three heads pressed against my knuckles. It was very warm. This creature had no name, not yet. Someday, the [System] would name it, if it became formidable on its own. There was only one other way to give it a proper name.

I opened my mouth and named it. The cavern faded, and the name faded with it.

I woke to warmth. It was a gentle warmth that came from a thing that sat against my chest. And it pulsed in a slow rhythm, one almost akin to my own heartbeat. The pulsing came from the egg. I knew that, somehow. It was the remnant of the beast I had unmade.

My arms were curled around it. My fingers had locked in place, and when I tried to move them now, every joint in my body complained.

And there was another thing besides the warmth. A half remembered memory, or perhaps the remnant of a dream. It had been a long time since I had dreamed of anything at all. When I tried to focus on it, it slipped from my grasp. All I found was an impression of something that had once been important. I opened my eyes.

The light that poured in was almost blinding. I was forced to squint at first. The shutters were open, and the sunlight was almost directly pressed against my face. I squinted until the room took proper shape. I was in Martha's hut, and on my own bed. That had not changed.

I had injuries, impossible not to feel. I started to take stock of them, one by one. The skin along my arms was a tender pink. In other places, the skin was new. I reached for my horns, and was relieved to know they were fine. Next, I reached for my hair. It was perhaps thin in some places, and altogether too short, but I had expected far worse.

I tried to sit up. My ribs informed me this was inadvisable. I sat up anyway and that was when I saw the girl. How the child had managed to bring so much dirt inside was a mystery. Why the child was even here in the first place was a greater mystery by far.

The girl noticed me. Her head snapped up and the stick froze. We stared at each other for a long moment.

And I saw a bowl next to her. One that had stew, from the smell of it. Even from here, I could tell that it had gone cold, and had been cold for some time. The girl must have been here for hours. Sara looked at me, then at the bowl. She cupped it with both hands and held it towards me. The girl said nothing, and so it took me a second to realize what this was.

“I do not need to be nursed, child.” The girl continued to stare, and the bowl stayed exactly where it was. “I am perfectly capable of feeding myself when I choose to.” She still said nothing. “You may leave the bowl and go. That is acceptable.”

The girl either did not understand, or she did not care. My stomach growled, and it was a treacherous, craven sound. I took the bowl. The stew was cold as I had guessed. Nonetheless, I ate.

The girl continued to watch me as I ate. When I finished, she took the bowl from my hands, set it beside the door, and returned to her patch of dirt. She resumed drawing without a word. I did not know what she was drawing. It looked like little more than pointlessly meandering lines.

The girl did not leave after the food was done. I did not ask her to either. The egg pulsed by my side, and I held it closer.

Eventually, there was something other than the pulsing of the egg and the girl still drawing in the dirt. I heard the sound of footsteps outside the door, one that quickly faded as they drew closer.

It opened a second later, and when Ash stepped inside, she was walking on the tips of her toes for some strange reason. She saw me awake. She regarded me for a long time, and then she straightened. Then she noticed the girl, still sitting on the floor.

There were deep shadows under Ash's eyes. But when she turned to the girl, her voice was warm. “Sara, you’ve been here since morning, haven't you?”

The girl just nodded.“Thank you for watching over her and bringing her food. You can go now, I'll take care of things from here.” Ash placed a hand on Sara’s head. The girl beamed up at her. Then she collected her stick and rose. She looked at me at the door's threshold, just for a moment. Then she was gone.

Ash turned to me and our eyes met. “You’re up,” she said. She said nothing more, not yet. I knew what she was going to say.

She was going to lecture me about being reckless, no doubt. Heroes always loved their lectures, even when they were the greatest fools themselves. I waited, but it did not come. There was only silence. I broke it first.

“How long?” I asked.

“It's been three days, or just about.”

Three days? That was too long. I had expected one. Perhaps two, at worst. The Inker had given us seven days, and even before the phoenix we had spent one, and so we had-

“We have three days left before he returns,” Ash said. “The village is fine. Everyone was afraid but that's settled down. Most of the damage was to the square and all the huts closest to it. We've been rebuilding those. Nobody…” She paused. “Nobody died.”

“I did not ask.” Something inside of me unclenched anyway.

Ash nodded. "Well…I'll let you to rest up then. Try and get some sleep, you probably need it. Maybe you can help a bit when you're feeling better." Then she turned and was gone.

She assumed I would help. I had not agreed to do anything of the sort. I had not given her permission to leave either.  Now I was alone in the quiet, with an egg in my arms.

The shell was almost absurdly smooth, and it was pulsing. The pulsing was slow and it did not match my own heartbeat, though the two seemed to drift toward one another when I held still long enough.

Most of the egg was smooth stone, and the rest was strange markings carved along its surface. Most of those markings were in the form of slightly brighter golden lines. Those lines caught the light and glimmered.

Then there were the black lines. Those markings were thin as hair, and covered perhaps a third of the egg. When I ran a thumb along one, my finger felt faintly cold. It was impossible not to notice with how warm the rest of the thing was.

I took a breath and forced a wisp of Cradle towards the egg. The white light met the black veins and…nothing happened. Nothing was healed, and nothing was changed. If this was corruption as I feared, then my Cradle could not reach it. Perhaps it would fade on its own or perhaps it would not.

I did not try to use the Requiem. I had almost had the thought, but I had dismissed it before it had even formed. Only a fool would use the power of decay on a thing they meant to keep alive. Something one intended to keep alive…when had I decided that?

I held the egg up. It was heavy enough to feel substantial in my hands, but not so heavy that holding it was a burden. Whatever creature lay inside the egg was growing, slowly but surely. Perhaps that was what the pulsing was. The beat of the beast within.

It would be a phoenix, presumably, or what remained of one. I had never seen a phoenix egg before. Never seen a phoenix either, until recently. They were creatures spoken of in old texts that even I could not verify. I had assumed they were nothing more than superstition. What hatched from this egg might bear no resemblance to the beast that had attacked the village. Or it might bear every resemblance.

The thought unsettled me, but I refused not to have it. If this egg hatched and the beast that came forth bore four broken wings, then I would deal with it. I had ended the mother and I could certainly end the child. I stared at the egg and felt something inside of me crumble.

I set the egg down on the bed beside me. It was reasonable, and logical. There was no tactical reason to hold this thing while I slept. The egg just sat there. I watched it for a moment, then reached over to pick it up.

There was a reason for it, I saw it now. The bed was slightly uneven, and the egg might roll. After all of the trouble I had gone through, it would be a waste to have the thing fall over.

I held the egg close and closed my eyes. Its soft warmth settled over me, and it pulsed gently against my ribs. I checked on the egg three times before I slept. I did not know what I was checking for.
