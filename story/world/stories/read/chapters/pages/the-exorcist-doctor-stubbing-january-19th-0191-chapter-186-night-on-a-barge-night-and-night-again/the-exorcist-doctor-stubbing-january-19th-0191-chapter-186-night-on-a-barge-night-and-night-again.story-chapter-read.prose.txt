Everyone picked a place around Miss Umbrage’s old fishing barge. Gael claimed the very front of the ship, standing with one boot braced against the low railing and his coat whipping behind him in the wind, while Maeve stood beside him. Liorin and Evelyn climbed onto the roof of the bridge because they wanted to be high up, and Cara and Fergal stood at opposite corners of the stern, each giving the other enough room to cast without entangling their lines. That left Vivi and Jin along the front-side railings, a few paces behind Gael and Maeve.

Vivi would like to be inside the bridge somehow to get away from the cold wind and rain, but that simply wasn’t possible. She wouldn’t be able to cast her lines under a roof, and—perhaps more importantly—she wouldn’t be able to hear Rebus, Bran, and Nixy shouting instructions as they paced along the middle of the barge.

“Don’t cast yer lines yet!” Nixy shouted. “Wait! You too, Mister Raven!”

Gael, who’d already lifted his rod over one shoulder, stopped halfway through the motion. Everyone else froze around the barge as well. Rebus carried a dented bucket full of grey bait dough under one arm, Bran had several coils of spare fishing line looped across his shoulders, while Nixy moved from person to person, checking their hooks, their knots, their hand placements—just how many things did she actually have to check before they were 'ready' to fish?

But judging by the look on his face, Vivi suspected this was the first time Gael had ever been ordered around by a girl who barely reached his ribs.

“... Nah. That’s wrong.” Nixy slapped the lump of bait on Vivi’s hook as she passed by. “It ain’t right.”

Vivi frowned down at the bait in her own hands. “What part of it is wrong?”

“All of it.”

“Oh.”

The bait looked perfectly normal to her, though. It was evenly shaped, properly secured, and only slightly misshapen from where her fingers had slipped on the wet dough. She’d followed the instructions printed on the paper packet the Saint’s Hands had purchased from one of Wraithpier’s fishing shops down to the very letter, but Nixy couldn’t care any less about that, because she plucked the lure off the hook and tossed it overboard.

“Shop bait’s for tourists and dummies. Or dummy tourists.” Nixy took Vivi’s hook between two fingers and held it up to the weak lantern light, showing it to everyone on the barge. “A proper Myrmur lure needs blood! Everythin’ in Wraithpier enjoys blood, but Myrmurs just ain’t shy ‘bout it!”

Then Nixy demonstrated by pricking the tip of her finger against the hook, and a bead of red welled up immediately. She pressed it into a piece of her own bait dough from Rebus’ bucket, then kneaded her blood through until the grey mixture darkened.

“Has t’be fresh!” she explained. “Mix it all through, not just on top! If ye drip it outside, it’ll wash off too quick!”

“Is that sanitary?” Vivi asked.

“We’re tryin’ to attract man-eating Myrmurs! What d’ya think?”

All around the barge, everyone holding a fishing rod began doing the same. They plucked off their bait doughs, pricked their fingers with their hooks, and kneaded their blood into the grey food. Vivi wasn’t too keen to hurt herself, but still she bit her lip and did it—re-hooking her darkened bait dough onto the end of her line after a while.

Once they all secured their blooded lures, Gael raised his rod again.

“Right. And now we—”

“Stop!” Bran shouted. And every rod halted for the second time. The boy marched towards Vivi, having chosen her as his next instructional victim, and took hold of the lower half of her fishing rod while she still held the grip. “The lure casting’s got t’be right as well! Can’t just whip the lure out like that!”

Then he positioned Vivi’s hands farther apart along the grip as he lifted her rod, showing it to everyone else.

“If ye throw the lure too hard, it’ll smack th’water wrong!” he shouted. “Big splash means crooked break, which sends vibrations everywhere! Sensitive fish feel it and scatter, and Myrmurs are definitely sensitive to stuff like that, but if ye drop the lure too soft and it don’t go far enough, Murmurs’ll also feel feet movin’ on th’boards or smell people nearby—so you gotta cast it far, but soft!”

Vivi frowned down at her rod. “But how does one throw something far without using much force?”

Bran considered that.

Then he pointed at Gael.

“Like how he walks,” he said plainly. “Relax everythin’. No anger, no nerves. Go loose. Ye sway around and loosen yer tension, then fall forward like yer drunk and about t’pass out, but then while yer fallin’, just… like… lean all yer weight through and throw a punch as hard as ye can!”

Silence settled over the barge beneath the hiss of the rain, and Vivi tried to picture it—relaxing, falling forward, and then ‘punching’ while falling—but the explanation didn’t make much sense to her. Evidently, it didn’t make much sense to everyone else as well, since they were all staring down at their rods, struggling to comprehend what Bran just said.

But Gael alone grinned, wedged his rod beneath one arm, and reached under his coat for a metal flask.

“I get it,” he said, pulling the cork free with his teeth. “Just gotta do this, right?”

He tilted the flask back and began drinking. As he did, he loosened his shoulders. His knees bent slightly as the barge rolled under him, and the fishing rod began lolling from side to side in his other hand. Now it was like he swayed with the waves, his head drooping, one boot dragging against the deck and the other dragging the opposite way—and then he suddenly staggered forward.

Maeve gasped and reached for his coat as he pitched towards the railing, but the staggering was just a feint, and he didn’t fall overboard. Instead, his entire body flowed into his cast, and… unlike the other times they usually did it, there wasn’t a violent snap from the rod or a hard whip through the air. His bait sailed out into the darkness, much farther than Vivi would’ve believed possible from such a lazy movement.

Several seconds passed before a tiny plop came from the distance, so soft it was almost swallowed by the pitter-patter of the rain.

Gael straightened then, turned around, and grinned at Bran.

“Like that?”

Bran shot him a thumbs-up. So Gael produced half a dozen more flasks from beneath his coat—Vivi had long since stopped questioning how much alcohol he carried on his person—and he tossed them around, making sure there was enough alcohol to make their way around the barge.

For Vivi’s part, she gingerly accepted the flask when it fell into her hand, and took only the smallest of sips from it—still it burned her tongue, throat, chest, and maybe even several organs, making her cough into her sleeve.

Jin looked over immediately. “You all right?”

“Ye… ah,” she managed, though her voice came out scratchier than she intended. “It’s only… rather strong.”

Gael glanced back at her as well. “Wanna know the alcohol percentage?”

“I’d… rather not.”

But now that heat spread rapidly through her face and neck, the rain no longer seemed quite as cold, and the barge’s constant swaying became oddly easier to understand. One by one, everyone cast their lines. Their knees were loose, and their bodies were all swaying to the currents, so their lures flew into the darkness before entering the water with soft little plops—now they all had their lines cast out, and Gael rested his rod against the railing.

“So now we wait,” he said cheerily. “With any luck, we’ll—”

“No!” Rebus interrupted. “There’s more!”

“Saintess, there’s still more?”

“Course there’s more! Ya think Myrmurs would just swim up and eat dead dough?”

“Uhh… yeah?”

Rebus ignored Gael and pointed at everyone’s rods. “Now, you make th’lures dance!”

And before anyone could ask what he meant, Rebus began humming. It was a strange tune that sounded like something fishermen might sing while hauling nets at dawn, and he tapped his boots against the deck, stepping sideways, rolling one shoulder, miming holding a fishing rod with both hands. Bran joined him, stamping softly and dragging one heel across the boards. Nixy followed with smaller steps, her shoulders rocking while she hummed a higher part of the tune.

Together, the three children danced along the center of the barge, and if Vivi weren’t standing in a roaring downpour, she might’ve found the performance rather adorable.

“Don’t just shake the rod!” Rebus explained between steps. “Lures’ gotta move like they’re alive! Make ‘em happy! Hurt! Scared! Playin’! Whatever you can make your lure feel, you gotta transfer that emotion through the rod! That’s how the Fishermen do it so easily!”

The three of them continued humming and dancing, and everyone else, meanwhile, simply watched. Gael and Maeve looked halfway between fascinated and exhausted by all the instructions. Fergal and Cara’s arms were folded, while Liorin, Evelyn, and Jin seemed perplexed by all the new ‘rules’ they apparently had to follow. After all, who knew fishing in Wraithpier could be this complicated?

“... Can it be a pair dance instead?” Vivi asked.

The humming never stopped, but Rebus looked over his shoulder while continuing his little steps.

“Anythin' goes!” he replied. “Long as it's soft an' amusin'! Fish like watchin' lively things!”

Vivi still needed another few moments to gather her courage, but when the barge next rolled beneath another passing wave, she let herself drift backwards with it until her shoulder bumped into Jin's chest.

Before she could lose her nerve, she reached across with her free hand and grabbed his. She gave herself an anxious smile as she took the first step, guiding him into the harbor-step once again—twisting and turning and swaying around their section of the barge. All the while, her other wrist gave her fishing rod the gentlest tugs, making her lure somewhere in the darkness perform the same lively motions as her.

Jin seemed a bit taken aback at first, but then he relaxed his shoulders and simply allowed the movement of the boat and her hand to carry him. He matched her rhythm almost instinctively, and the rest of the barge watched the two of them for several moments, as though unsure what to think of them.

Then Gael grinned beneath his plague mask.

“...Well,” he said, turning dramatically toward Maeve. “If this whole fishing business is gonna be annoyingly complicated anyway, we might as well make it slightly more fun.” He offered her his free hand with an exaggerated little flourish. “May I?”

Maeve looked from his hand to his rod, from his rod to the rain, from the rain to the barge—and then she sighed, slipping her free hand into his.

"... Fine."

Near the stern, Fergal watched them for a moment before awkwardly extending his own hand towards Cara. The man looked infinitely more comfortable threatening gangs than asking someone to dance, but Cara sighed, too, before taking his hand in her own. Meanwhile, Evelyn had absolutely no intention of asking politely. She simply grabbed Liorin's wrist and yanked him towards her.

Before long, every pair had claimed their own little corner of the tiny barge. Gael and Maeve drifted slowly around the bow while their rods rose and fell with each careful step. Cara and Fergal circled one another with slow, measured movements, while Evelyn spent half her time correcting Liorin's footwork and the other half laughing whenever he stepped somewhere unexpected. Rebus, Bran, and Nixy continued weaving between everyone, humming their old fishing melody and occasionally calling out little corrections whenever somebody forgot to move their rod gently enough.

It must be an utterly absurd sight for anyone watching from another boat.

A rotten old fishing barge that looked one strong wave away from sinking, drifting on pitch-black waters filled with man-eating horrors, rain pouring from the heavens—and people dancing in pairs while holding fishing rods.

But Vivi couldn't care less.

She was simply happy to dance the harbor-step with Jin once more, and this time, there weren’t even any claws or blades swiping at their faces.

“I told you,” she said softly, looking up at him with an earnest smile as she twirled him through another turn.”I wanted us to have a proper dance one more time.”

The corner of Jin's eyes lifted ever so slightly.

“... Yeah,” he mumbled. “This is the best dance style after all.”

She chuckled, smiling so much her cheeks almost hurt. Before long, they weren't even talking about fishing anymore. Instead they found themselves quietly comparing dance styles from Vharnveil to those she'd learned since arriving in Bharncair, with Jin insisting that anything requiring memorizing dozens of formal steps sounded like punishment, while Vivi argued that that sort of elegance had its own rewards, even if she didn’t like them either.

She became so absorbed in their carefree conversation that, for several wonderful moments, she almost forgot they were here for a job.

Then—

TWANG.

A sharp sound rang through the night. Every conversation stopped. Even the children’s humming ceased. The vibration travelled down Gael’s fishing rod, through the wet planks beneath his feet, and then back up into everyone's boots.

All of their heads snapped towards the bow where Gael was, and silence settled over the barge.

Then—

TWANG.

His rod bent sharply. Without delay, Gael immediately released Maeve's hand to seize the rod with both of his own, and Rebus' eyes went wide.

"You’ve got one!" the boy shouted, pointing excitedly towards the dark water. "Now yank th' bastard up!"
