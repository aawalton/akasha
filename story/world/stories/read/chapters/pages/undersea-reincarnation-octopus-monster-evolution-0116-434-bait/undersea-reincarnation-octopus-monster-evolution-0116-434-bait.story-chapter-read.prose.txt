In the half second of the queen looking up at his invisible Avatar and it reaching for a connection to pluck, Henry detonated all of his surrounding clones. Almost every Avatar within half a mile of the queen exploded, lighting up the road and the surrounding darkness of space in a series of bright flashes of light and wild mana. From down the road, it looked like blue fireworks had gone off, most of which he couldn’t see as the swarm obstructed the view of the Avatars down the road. But that was fine. This outing had been plenty productive, and now he had a better idea of the threats they were facing.

But Henry wasn’t done, and his mind was still going a million miles an hour. He had considered the risk of his Avatars being tampered with, but he hadn’t really thought it could happen. Certainly not with how the queen attempted to. He had believed his Avatars to be at risk for some potential telepathic tampering or some sort of forceful mutations, but he hadn’t thought it was even possible for the link between him and his Avatars to be affected or even interacted with. Not that it was easy. The queen’s first attempt had been like thrumming a guitar string. It hadn’t managed to sever or take over the link, but it did touch it, and Henry wasn’t about to give it free practice. When it turned out it could sense his hidden Avatars, he destroyed them before it could even make the attempt. But not all of them.

Henry had left the one Arcane Avatar he had sent around the queen and its escort to take a closer look at the door, but considering these new revelations, he made sure to pop a second one out of his Maw right next to it before he heavily cloaked them both with a generous dose of Unfettered Echoes, dividing it equally between Trickster’s Camouflage and Faint Presence. If one of them was discovered or taken over, the second would detonate them both. Now, one of them was looking back, observing the queen and what she would do next, while the second paid close attention to the door.

Both were paying close attention to any further tampering on the link connecting them to Henry, even if he was very much aware it might not be enough. That the queen might be feigning her calm while she studied something about him she’d noticed. Right now, she stood where he had left her, completely still if it wasn’t for the waving and vibrating antennas above her head. With a gesture of its claw, the swarm dispersed in every direction while she tilted her head back and forth, hounding for his scent.

Around Henry’s real body, Velistraine and Maurice were asking what was happening, but Henry couldn’t spare them the attention just yet, though he could empathize. From their point of view, everything was likely happening too fast to make much sense of. One second the queen was in front of them, and the next the projection was cut off as his Avatars all detonated.

Well. Most of them.

Turning his attention back to the massive golden door, Henry refrained from casting Identify, just in case that gave him away. Instead, he just approached with his two remaining Avatars, floating quietly with Fly. Oddly enough, the door did not behave like the road. It was static. Unchanging and uncaring of its surroundings. It must have been at least a hundred feet high, and its width completely sealed the whole road. There were no locks he could see, and no gap anywhere he looked. In fact, this thing looked more like shutters than a door. It was a massive slab of metal, engraved with countless glowing symbols that shifted and changed under his gaze. The sight made Henry’s eyes and brain ache as if he’d been stabbed through the eyes, and it almost distracted him from the queen that was slowly approaching from behind as she looked about, still searching for him.

With one eye still on the queen, Henry thought of Velistraine’s words about this place. “If you told me gods were real and they were somewhere, I’d tell you that door might lead to whoever created those gods,” Velistraine had said. As much as it sounded like an exaggeration, Henry felt the comparison was warranted. Still, his time was ticking down, and the scale of risk to reward ratio was tipping unfavorably. He would have to leave in a couple seconds, and he was paying close attention to the queen’s arms. He didn’t know if she was faking it or if she had somehow sensed him. But this door, this… gateway. It felt important. It felt even familiar, somehow. This was knowledge he could make use of. Maybe not today, but eventually. The System might even know about it. Was this its work? Henry frankly couldn’t tell, but he’d have to ask.

Looking up and down the massive structure, he wondered what was behind it. And if this wasn’t the System’s work, then whose work was it?

As if reacting to his thoughts, an unfamiliar presence intruded on him, and Henry nearly detonated his clones immediately, thinking it was the queen, but it wasn’t.

Now he knew why the gate’s presence felt familiar.

Infestation still detected in this Sector. Quarantine measures are in effect.

Individual recognized as non-native to the Sector.

Individual recognized as not part of the infestation.

Individual may request a partial lift of quarantine measures once—

Henry had gone completely still, reading the text as it appeared in his vision. This looked like a System’s interaction, but it didn’t seem to recognize or care for him. It wrote in a clinical manner, which made it pretty obvious that this was another entity, one whose whole responsibility was keeping this gate closed. But as mind-blowing this revelation was, Henry was still paying attention to his surroundings, so he noticed when the queen had gone still, most likely sensing his surprise at the sudden text, and he detected when something tugged on his connection to his Avatar.

Without any hesitation, Henry detonated both Avatars and lost sight of the queen and the door. And just to be safe, he pulled all of his Avatar away from the site. Better safe than sorry.

For a moment, Henry hovered next to Maurice and Velistraine, dazed from everything he had seen, then he began recounting everything while simultaneously, he worked on improving his network. It would lose in efficiency, but he couldn’t afford carelessness given what the queen had displayed. So, Henry worked on some countermeasures.

The main issue was that the queen might be able to mess with his clones, maybe even take them over and somehow access what a clone might still know. While that was the worst case scenario, so far Henry didn’t know if it was possible. It might be able to sever his connection, or end the skill prematurely, or maybe even trace it back to its source. All of these were dangerous possibilities. And they all meant two things. First, this meant he couldn’t have one of his Avatars fall into the queen’s grasp. That meant a buddy-system was required. This would cause the aforementioned drop in efficiency, but it was worth his peace of mind. Secondly, Henry needed to understand exactly what the queen was capable of, so he needed to run some experiments. Maybe not right this second, but he had to prepare for them. So, Henry decided to make most of his army into Arcane Avatars, and in the stomach of each of them, he created a little pouch that would house a second Arcane Avatar. This way, if the carrier-Avatar happened to be severed or taken over, Henry would have a second on the scene, to both observe what was happening and to get rid of the evidence.

That would be the standard loadout, he decided. Though he’d continue thinking of a better design for another Avatar. One he might present to the queen as bait.

***

The next few days fell into a comfortable, though somewhat tense rhythm. Within the next few hours, a new elite of the swarm had made itself known. It somehow looked a bit like the queen itself, though much smaller, and without the scythe-like arms. It also had longer antennas, and it took one of them barely a moment to detect the hidden alcove within which Velistraine had made camp and where one of Henry’s Avatars had been “hidden”. They had blasted the alarm, and the Queen herself had shown up moments later while Henry “ran away”, using the road before he detonated the Avatar in question on one of the landing ships. Over the next couple of days, Henry made sure that most, if not all of his attacks happened on and around the road, where the three of them ambushed, detonated, or poisoned anything they encountered. This gave Henry a chance to work on Spiteful Detonation and its fusion with his toxin-delivery system, while also implying that he relied on the road for his survival. That didn’t mean the swarm didn’t suspect he could navigate the empty space around them, of course, but through his observation, he realized that most of the swarm, while capable of handling the void of space for a while, could not survive it for too long. They needed air as well, which meant that Henry could also breach their ships and leave them to die alone.

Day after day, the three of them tore into thousands of the invaders. Velistraine burned out the rest of her anxiety and regained most of her confidence, now that she had reliable allies to support her, while Maurice practiced with his freshly empowered abilities and began pushing them. As for Henry, he funneled everything to his freshly upgraded Aspect, and with every elite they downed, he took a step closer to bringing them all home. Unfettered Echoes and his Avatars were getting a lot of practice, and so he wasn’t surprised when the first level turned out to be as easy as funneling a dozen pillbug ships’ worth of cores to it. The second required nearly double that, as well as one of the larger ships and the many elites it contained. But with two extra Aspect levels, Henry believed that he might just have enough to bridge the rest of the gap. With the two levels, the clones still in the Reach didn’t flicker at the edges of his awareness anymore. They were constant. They were still far and out of his reach, and he couldn’t sense anything else than their existence, but they were constantly present.

Though while he might be able to boost the connection enough to slip through, it seemed like it wasn’t worth the risk, especially if the System might block their re-entry, or if any new complication would make itself known. It might be better to wait and gain a few more levels. Because right now, the levels and the enemies were in abundance. In the meanwhile, Henry kept a careful eye on the queen. He hadn’t seen her more than three times in the span of a week, but he believed his bait would work once presented. So far he made sure to run or destroy his Avatars when trapped, so his plan would work once enacted. Clearly enough, the queen really wanted a piece of him, and while she had an amazing ability to detect his Avatars within its immediate area, she seemed to struggle with anything further than a couple hundred feet. Naturally, Henry was wary of a trap, so he was careful. In the meantime, they leveled. All three of them worked on their Aspects and skills, and in fact, on the seventh day, Henry had decided to use his new and improved Spiteful Detonation to invite the queen, by having a squad of three Avatars attack and destroy a pillbug ship posted on the road.

His bait Avatar was ready, one in a squad of three Lifeblood Avatars, and Henry waited and fought while screeches blared and ships flocked to him, which he used to detonate both blank Avatars before letting the third one be “captured”, just in time for the queen to show up with a surprise of her own.

A second queen.

This one had a gunpowder-like chitin, angular and lined with dangerously sharp edges. Its scythe-like arms were curves of complete and absolute darkness, and to Henry’s gaze, it was almost as if just by their passing, a small tear was cut into the world. The War Queen was nearly as tall as the Architect. But while the Architect gave him the impression of a crafter and an inventor, he got nothing but relentless slaughter and the sharp edge of a sword out of it. So its title was not at all surprising when it popped into his vision.

[War Queen of the Swarm (?) - ?]

Henry swallowed, eyeing the two large figures as they confidently approached him. It made sense that the Architect Queen didn’t work alone. With a second in the picture, Henry wouldn’t be surprised if there were a third. And a fourth. Maybe even something else above them.

If the Architect Queen had been alone, the System might not have struggled as much as it did.
