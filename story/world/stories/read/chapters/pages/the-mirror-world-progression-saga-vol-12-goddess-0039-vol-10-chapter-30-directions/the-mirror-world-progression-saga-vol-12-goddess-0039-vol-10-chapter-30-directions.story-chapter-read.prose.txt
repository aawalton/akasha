According to Endless Templar, Samantha needed to investigate and meditate on how she could mar the primary powerful protective rune on the fortress of Matrazere. Firstly, she moved herself to the spirit world proper, down from the extra-planetary Limbo area all the way to the ‘surface.’ Matrazere was not hard to find. It oozed despair and depression from the city extensions nearby it, and the walls of the fortress themselves oozed oppression, along with other things she couldn’t quite put a name to. A mutant, twisted inversion of Entropy, perhaps.

Over a city cast in shadows and decaying like a necropolis, the fortress in the spirit world loomed high and tall, and its rune was obvious and prominent, proudly and dominantly declaring nothing would assail it, much less break it. Ironically, not even Entropy would. The Sage himself — at least hypothetically — couldn’t tear it down. Perhaps he had a hidden method, perhaps not.

She studied and analyzed and looked for any weakness within the work, with her perceptions enhanced to prime with [Deific Pneumamancy]...

She found nothing.

Sighing, Samantha flew herself back up, to be a decent distance from the power of the place, but not so far as to lose all perception of it. She meditated. Calmed herself completely and drained out all her worry and everything demanding her attention. Once all was more or less still and her mind cleared, she turned back to the matter of the rune.

Marring it in the spirit world would involve attacking its meaning somehow, but the energy required was mindboggling, especially to expect it to transport itself effectively into the miniature realm. While Matrazere existed in the mainline Calrenazzod reality, it had carved itself something of its own brand within the greater reality. Things that didn’t ‘line up’ would not have it easy making the transfer.

The perfectly right energy to disrupt, along with a huge amount of it. Anything directly from her didn’t really seem to match. This was a clever, painstaking, unique inversion of Entropy, to forge something anti-destructive.

‘I have the capability,’ Endless Templar says. Or, no, me and my ‘coven’ have the solution. Hmm. Azure? Who else?

Recalling something that had been percolating for a while without a clear outlet or goal — though ostensibly considered for Matrazere many moons ago — Samantha zipped over to the Bridge of Edges and moved to a certain ‘special zone’ she could locate with ease, like she was magnetized to it, because it was both there and not really there.

Underneath a containing pocket utterly sealing it from going anywhere, something terrifying roiled. It had been stewing here, then began boiling, transmuting by unknown means and processes. It sat in its own unique space, covered by a thin, disturbingly flexible barrier into Samantha and Azure’s contrasting space. Everything was made of hard edges in that place, but here this was, like an impregnated organic sac full of acidic energy.

Bast. His persona within, what it swallowed and kept in some hidden space close to us, slowly boiling the destruction it absorbed during the Merrington battle into something worse and worse and worse.

It twitched under her gaze, and Samantha felt a wave of nausea hit her, together with a sudden splitting headache. She backed away.

I’m definitely not attuned to this shit. Ick! We have to get rid of it soon… one way or another.

Staring at it, feeling the raw power of destruction it held, Samantha wondered outloud, “Can this be our weapon, then? Our means to mar the rune? Destroy it in the spirit world?”

She felt something massive ‘beyond’ the Bridge of Edges roil, seemingly adjacent to her. An affirmative?

Going on instinct, Samantha reached out toward it and made a bridge up through the voided space she barely understood, but was called the System Deck, of the now subsumed Fortuneteller.

It worked, and in an instant, she was there.

She was facing the back of a figure surrounded by fog, and immediately around the black silhouette was an orange glow.

The figure was walking away, but stopped and turned around to her. It seemed to have long, wild hair that billowed all around it. Its eyes were hollows in the black silhouette, simply showing the orange glow behind it. It was subtle, but ‘it’ was most definitely a ‘he.’

He stared and seemed to wait.

This is Bast’s inner being, representing The Chariot. The Director’s brother. Er. Sort of.

Samantha felt awkward suddenly. “So, ah… hi. You’re awake!” She walked a bit closer.

The figure held up a hand, which Samantha heeded and stopped approaching, at which point the hand dropped, and he spoke. “More or less.” His voice was smooth and buttery, soft, even as it echoed with ominous, supernatural gravitas.

Samantha cleared her throat and asked, “You heard me, just now? It seemed like you were nodding.”

The figure stared. “A lapse. I was aware of you.”

Samantha raised an eyebrow and gave an impish grin. “Spying, are we?”

There was such a long pause it cast doubt that he’d say anything, but finally he replied, “I am looking for Her, in you. I thought she was being drawn out, but she remains reclused.”

Samantha opened her mouth and closed it twice. “Yeah. Sorry to disappoint.”

“Don’t take it as a slight. You are very small, I am very big. It’s the natural order. Bast is your contemporary, not I, and you’ve both made your choices about your degrees of intimacy.”

Samantha formed a wry smile. “Right, thanks, that clears it up for me.”

The figure nodded and started to turn to go again.

Samantha blurted, “Y-you’re different than I expected!”

The figure turned once more. “Oh? How so?”

“I dunno, ah, polite? Kinda. And rather calm and collected. I was expecting someone more… boisterous. Aggressive. So on.”

A pause as the silhouette figure looked aside for a moment. Then he met her eyes — maybe, sorta — and said, “The calm before the storm.”

Samantha smiled. “Good answer.”

With a momentary hesitation, the figure added, “I’m changed, also. A game has become torture. Prison. Slavery. I chain myself into your hand like a beast of my own volition. You are a fragile thing contorted into a shape you don’t deserve. For these reasons, I won’t treat you poorly, however deep and mixed my feelings exist for my… sibling.”

“I see. I… appreciate that.” She left a brief pause. “So, I had a few questions, if I could? Before you leave.” The figure gave the subtlest acknowledgement, so Samantha continued, “Why did you take control of Bast at Merrington? Why swallow the energy?”

The figure looked down, shook his head, and sighed. “Whys.” It was spoken like a curse. “Those aren’t my specialty in answering. I simply do what is best and most efficient to continue the charge that is called on. I remain in motion.” He shrugged with his hands out. “Except when time is stopped like it is here, which I find aggravating.”

“Sorry about that. I’ll try to be quick! So, you did that to be effective and efficient? It didn’t seem that way on the battlefield. Rather the opposite.”

“Then the seeming is false. Bast survived and preserved more lives than he otherwise would have, through the rampage I facilitated. I saw the path that was needed. Do you truly believe he wanted to remain in control at that time? False again, if so.”

Samantha frowned, but nodded anyway. “Thanks for the explanation, in any case. I guess I just have one more thing. The most pertinent thing. How do we use this brewed-up, awful, destructive energy, acid, energetic-acid-mass — whatever — on the rune?”

“That’s simple: unleash it. It will obliterate whatever it is directed at.”

“I… don’t think it's that simple for us. Can you give us some tips? Like, how do you see the process leading up to the unleashing going?”

A long, thoughtful pause commenced as the entity looked off. Finally, he answered. “Bend the dimensionality of your spatial interstices to the precise point needed — as precise as you can get — with the energy on the other side, like poison ready to flow from a fang or needle. Squeeze with pressure on the sac until it flows, and keep control of your fang to prevent it from bursting. For as long as possible. Inevitably, the needle will become a nozzle, and then a maw before it is expended, but you can mitigate the progress over the release period. Do not attempt to close it off.” Another, briefer pause. “You will need the three involved to coordinate. Our higher-to-lower foci — Bast, Azure, and yourself.”

Samantha’s eyebrows climbed higher and higher as he went, in wonder. Eyes wide, mouth parted by the end, she breathed a sound of relief and exclaimed, “Wow, thank you! That really does help. Now I’m visualizing it… okay, I’m actually getting kinda excited about it!”

“Yes, Child,” the entity said with deliberate patience. “But less so, when you realize this is like regurgitation. Or puking.”

Samantha made a disgusted face and glared at the figure, with her hands going to her hips. “Really? Was that imagery necessary?”

The entity shrugged. She wasn’t sure, but she thought she detected amusement. “Was there anything else?”

“Should I really use this on the rune in the spirit world, as opposed to the fortress in the physical realm? Or even save it for the Sage? If it can obliterate anything.”

“It won’t work on the physical matter protected by the rune because that’s how the magic works. Like the inverse of attacking a creature. Do you attack the concept of a boar, or do you simply shred its flesh and pierce its heart? But this place you target is no boar. It is matter bound and converted to conceptual existence. The wall is spirit, its substance flowing from the rune. The rune is thus your target.”

The figure turned and began walking away through the fog. “As for the Sage, I’m afraid destruction is no good against [Entropy], however it suffices for this strange inversion he conspired. You’ll need other artifice, other weapons, for that daunting, ultimate task…”

“Thank you for everything!” Samantha just managed to toss out before the figure and fog vanished into nothing.
