The next morning, Luke awoke to the sound of wind rushing through the valley, its cold fingers slipping into the tent and making him shiver. Though the sandstone walls shielded most of the camp, the howling gusts still brought with them an eerie sense of dread.

Today was the day he and Tristan would scale the cliff face. Only now did the full weight of that reality settle in—climbing a sheer wall, nearly a thousand feet high, without ropes or harnesses.

Even if he wasn’t the one climbing, he’d still be battling the biting winds at the edge of a precipice. One wrong move could be fatal.

Grimacing, Luke pushed himself upright.

“What did you do to me?” came a voice—sharp, demanding.

Luke flinched, startled to see Tristan’s scowling face just a foot away. He barely resisted the urge to let out a scream of surprise.

He’d come dangerously close to losing all the respect he’d earned as a makeshift general.

“Tristan… how long have you been standing there?” Luke asked, trying to steady his breath.

“Long enough,” the older man replied, his tone clipped.

“Alright, calm down. I’ll explain everything.” Luke gestured for him to sit.

To his relief, Tristan complied, giving Luke a moment to gather his thoughts. There was no point in lying—at least, not completely. He explained the nature of his ability and how, by becoming one of his subordinates, Tristan had received certain benefits.

He deliberately omitted any mention of the Sovereign System, framing it instead as a divine blessing.

Tristan listened without interrupting, though the furrow between his brows deepened with each passing moment. The concept of being “blessed by the gods” wasn’t unheard of, and that familiarity had likely kept him from outright disbelief. Still, the guy would need time to process it.

Good thing Sebastian drilled those local terms into my head, Luke thought, stretching out his sore shoulders.

“So you're saying I'm now blessed by the gods?” Tristan asked, his voice low with disbelief.

“In a way, yes,” Luke answered casually. “Your strength and endurance have improved.”

Of course, he didn’t mention that he could choose which traits to enhance. No need to provoke more questions—or suspicions.

“I see…” Tristan’s voice trailed off. He looked down, his expression contemplative. “Was this your plan all along? To strengthen me before the climb?”

Luke cleared his throat awkwardly, the reaction enough to give him away. “You’re not exactly wrong…”

Tristan narrowed his eyes, clearly preparing to scold him—until a thunderous growl reverberated through the tent, loud and sudden as a beast’s roar.

Both men turned toward the sound.

Tristan clutched his stomach, wincing. “I don’t think I’ve ever been this hungry in my life…”

“It’s completely normal. Don’t worry,” Luke lied, only now recalling that he’d knocked Tristan out before dinner the night before. And with their current rations reduced to one meal a day, the poor man hadn’t eaten in nearly thirty-six hours.

Before Tristan could respond, Luke stood abruptly and cleared his throat.

“Let’s reward the soldiers with some breakfast this morning,” he announced, making his way toward the tent flap.

He couldn’t afford to let Tristan go hungry—not when he would be relying on the older man to carry him up the cliff. One moment of weakness, one small distraction, could spell death for them both—blessed or not.

“Good morning, General!”

As Luke stepped out of his tent, a chorus of voices rang out in greeting. The soldiers, standing tall and alert, saluted him with newfound respect. It seemed his awkward attempt at raising morale the night before had actually worked. The camp felt lighter, the tension eased.

Luke returned their greetings, offering nods and the occasional word in response. When he reached the soldier in charge of rations, he made his request clear.

“We’ll serve breakfast today,” he said.

Cheers erupted from the men, lifting the already soaring morale to new heights.

“Make sure you cook plenty,” Luke added, clapping a hand on Tristan’s shoulder. “The Deputy General needs all the strength he can get.”

Only then did Luke realize just how dense the old man’s body had become. His palm met unmoving muscle—hard as stone. Even with deliberate pressure, there was no give. The transformation was striking.

Some soldiers exchanged curious glances but soon returned to their tasks with amused smiles.

Before long, the campfire crackled to life, and the scent of simmering boar stew filled the air. For a brief moment, the cold wind and imposing cliffs faded into the background. If not for the looming ascent ahead, one might have mistaken the scene for a countryside picnic.

When the stew was ready, Tristan wasted no time. He devoured three full bowls in minutes and returned for a fourth, much to the cook’s shock.

Watching him eat so ravenously filled Luke with a strange sense of reassurance. He had yet to witness Tristan’s strength firsthand, but if appetite was any indication, the old man would endure the climb just fine.

“Are you full yet?” Luke asked, half-joking as he eyed Tristan’s now rounded belly.

“Mmm,” Tristan hummed in satisfaction, a small smile tugging at his lips. “I don’t think I’ve eaten that much in years.”

The sun had now fully crested the horizon, its golden rays spilling over the valley. At that moment, the fabric of the nearby tent—the only other one in the area—shook gently as Prince Lyon emerged, bleary-eyed and disheveled.

His expression shifted instantly as the smell of stew hit him. Gone was the noble heir; in his place stood a man possessed by hunger.

He greeted Luke and the others with a lazy wave, then made a beeline toward the cookpot, bowl in hand.

“I’m sorry, Prince Lyon… we’re out of stew,” the cook said apologetically.

The prince froze, bowl clutched to his chest, looking as though he might cry.

“I saved you some,” Luke said, producing a hidden bowl from behind his back.

He had originally set it aside for Tristan, just in case the climb left him needing a second round. But now, seeing the prince’s pitiful face, he couldn’t bring himself to deny him a warm meal.

Prince Lyon accepted the bowl with trembling hands, clearly touched. He blinked rapidly, fighting back tears as he took his first bite.

Several soldiers looked on, bemused, but they quickly returned to their duties, chuckling amongst themselves.

Tristan, now seated with his hands resting over his full stomach, turned to Luke with a satisfied sigh.

“So? What’s your plan now, young Luke?” he asked. “Will the soldiers stay here while we scale the cliff?”

Luke shook his head, his eyes drifting to the second prince, who was still absorbed in his bowl of stew. He’d been mulling over this issue for days but hadn’t found a satisfying solution—until now.

Watching how much the prince had matured over the past week, Luke began to think he might actually be up to the task.

“The second prince will take the soldiers to Yunxi,” he said calmly.

Though his voice wasn’t raised, the entire camp fell silent. Only the sound of wind cutting through the valley remained.

“Hm?” Luke glanced around, quickly realizing that every eye was fixed on him. Even Prince Lyon had frozen mid-bite, his spoon suspended inches from his gaping mouth.

“Young Luke, this…” Tristan’s face scrunched with hesitation, clearly struggling to find the right words to dissuade him.

“Y-You want me to lead the soldiers!?” Prince Lyon sprang to his feet, the remnants of his stew sloshing onto the ground. He didn’t seem to notice—or care.

Luke’s patience began to fray.

“You plan on becoming king one day, don’t you? If you can’t even lead fifty soldiers, what makes you think you’re fit to lead a whole damn kingdom?” he snapped.

The sharpness in his tone made the prince flinch.

From Luke’s perspective, the request wasn’t unreasonable. Yunxi was a six-day ride—seven if they followed the eastern border. The enemy was currently occupied laying siege to Xiandong Fortress, which meant the route should be safe. Even in the unlikely event they encountered a hostile force, their small group could easily retreat to Ralis City.

Prince Lyon stammered, clearly rattled. His discomfort was written all over his face.

Realizing his blunt approach had only made things worse, Luke suppressed a sigh and rose to his feet. He approached the prince and placed a firm hand on his shoulder.

“This is your chance,” he said, his voice low and steady. “Prove to your brother—and those smug nobles back in the capital—that you’ve got what it takes to lead. It’s in your blood, after all.”

The prince blinked, processing his words. Then, slowly, he tilted his head in thought.

“You’re right!” he said, his voice rising with enthusiasm. “I can already picture their faces when I return triumphantly!”

Luke’s eye twitched. The complete 180 in tone was almost too much to stomach.

He can flip a switch just like that...

“Don’t get overconfident,” Luke warned, his grip on the prince’s shoulder tightening slightly. “A good leader is cautious. He uses every resource available to him.”

“I see...” the prince murmured, more subdued this time.

Of course, Luke didn’t explain that he was using Lyon as one of his resources. The irony didn’t escape him.

He turned his gaze toward Richard, the broad-shouldered knight who stood nearby. Their eyes met, and a silent understanding passed between them.

None of this would be possible without Richard. While the prince had improved, he was still far from ready to command on his own. But with a strong adviser watching over him, Luke could rest a little easier.

“Once you reach Yunxi, I want you to head south along the eastern border,” Luke instructed, speaking slowly and clearly to ensure the prince understood.

“If all goes well, we’ll meet you halfway. But if we’re not there in ten days…” He patted the man’s shoulder again. “Retreat to the capital.”

“Yes, General.”

With his orders given, Luke turned to the rest of the camp to make it official. He addressed the soldiers directly, stating that Prince Lyon’s commands were to be treated as if they came from his own mouth.

The response was firm and unanimous—fifty voices roaring with affirmation.

Satisfied, Luke made his way to his horse and gently stroked its neck. Though it lacked the loyalty and spirit of his mare, the gelding had carried him faithfully through the past week. He murmured a quiet goodbye.

“Young Luke, you seem to have a lot of confidence in not only the prince, but also me,” Tristan said as he approached, his voice tinged with amusement.

“Yes,” Luke replied with a faint grin. “Though for completely different reasons.”

But the older man didn’t laugh. Instead, his expression turned somber.

“Do you really expect me to carry you while climbing that mountain?” he asked, his voice low.

“Yes,” Luke answered simply, without hesitation.

Tristan frowned. “Even if you tie yourself to me, there’s no guarantee I can keep us from falling to our deaths. I’ve lived a long life, Luke—but yours is just beginning.”

“You have the potential to rise all the way to the top,” he added, his tone heavy with sincerity. “Why take such a risk?”

Luke’s smile softened. He could feel the concern behind the older man’s words, and it touched him. But his eyes stayed firm.

“There’s more at stake than just our lives, my friend,” he said, his voice steady. “If I’m not willing to risk my life for my men, what kind of general would that make me?”

Even Luke was surprised by the conviction in his own words.

Months ago—when he had first arrived in this world—he never would’ve agreed to something so reckless. He chuckled inwardly at the memory of his own cowardice. Back then, the idea of danger had paralyzed him.

What changed? he wondered. Was it the system? Or have I just grown used to this world?

His gaze drifted, losing focus for a moment.

He thought of the bloodshed at Xiu Fortress, the brutal half-month siege of Clayton City. He had seen death in every form—slow, sudden, cruel. And with the introduction of gunpowder, many of those deaths had come by his own hand.

That kind of responsibility… it would have crushed the man he used to be.

But somehow, he had carried it.

Maybe I haven’t become fearless, Luke thought. Maybe I’ve just stopped running.

“Well,” Tristan said, breaking the silence with a crooked smile, “it’s hard to argue with that logic.”

He turned toward the distant cliff face.

“We should get going before the sun climbs too high.”
