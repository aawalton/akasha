Soohyun, on the other hand, was helping prepare defenses. She had been working with squads from Dober for a while now, setting up new outposts every time they took down one of Darius’ obelisks, and she had a knack for it.

And of course, Aaron was off fighting against one of the undead commanders. Terry had returned to the settlement and informed everyone what was happening.

Zero had a desire to fight by his master’s side, but if he had called Terry back, then it was probably best he didn’t go. Aaron knew what he was doing, and if he needed help, which seemed unlikely, he would call for it.

Zero flashed across the front lines, dipping behind enemy squads, and even stopping to take out a few of the weaker ones.

Aaron

The fight had gone on far longer than Aaron had planned, or appreciated. Well, to be fair, he enjoyed a good punch-up, and he was learning to face off against someone with completed mana anchors. But there were better times to be doing it than when almost surrounded by armies.

“Take this, you bag of bones!” Aaron shouted as he threw increasingly powerful punches.

But it was becoming painfully clear that overwhelming the Death Reaver with simple attacks simply wasn’t going to work.

He had already been drastically increasing the power of his attacks, but it wasn’t making a damn difference. More mana simply gave the Reaver more to soak up.

But after one particularly long exchange when he let his frustration get to him and went a little too wild, he noticed something. In his fury, Aaron had gotten a little unhinged, throwing stupid power with a whole lot less focus than usual, and the result was a tremendously powerful fist sailing past the reaver harmlessly.

As usual, the reaver sucked up all the mana pouring off his powerful strike; unlike the other attacks he had thrown, it had nothing to heal. In the recklessness of his rampage, it had recovered, and without somewhere to expel the influx of mana, it poured straight back into its core and thudded angrily.

Huh? What was that?

Suddenly, he remembered that the Death Reaver was only D-grade, much like himself. And like himself, it had a true core and completed mana anchors.

Sure, it might have been at a higher level than himself, but that didn’t mean its body was as tempered as his, or that its Classes provided as many Stats as his did. Not to mention his gear that gave a sizable boost in power.

And then there were the anchors themselves. How much additional strain would completed anchors pose on a regular D-grade?

Aaron raised a brow. Even if the soul was some powerful ancient being, that didn’t mean its body was its equal.

And most importantly, he knew better than just about anybody what a fractured soul felt like.

The reaver healed quickly, though. Still, his revelation gave him an idea. The reaver was using the damage against its own body as an outlet for all the energy it was draining from him and cultivating within its core.

But what would happen if it didn’t have an outlet for all of that energy?

Aaron smiled. It’s certainly worth testing out.

The reaver didn’t seem to realize what he was doing at first. Aaron roared and went wild, putting on an act as a madman raging at an enemy who wouldn’t die no matter what he did.

As his rage grew louder, he flung his fists all over the place. Now, the reason this worked was that the Death Reaver had been avoiding his strikes as best it could when enough of its body was formed that it could dodge. After all, it needed to find a balance between draining Aaron’s mana and eating his strikes.

But suddenly, that balance was thrown off. Aaron’s strikes never seemed to land, even when the reaver was fully healed and stood still, waiting to be hit.

He probably looked like an idiot, missing a static target. But he had to sell the rage to test his theory, and hope his opponent believed he was lost in some mindless rampage.

Unfortunately, the reaver didn’t have to take damage. It could also use Skills, and soon it started to fire them at Aaron, who, for someone who seemed to have lost all precision, was remarkably good at dodging.

Aaron was starting to get worried, though. Not just about keeping the ruse going, but if the reaver could keep blasting attacks off, the mana wouldn’t reach a critical point.

It was obvious that the reaver was simply better at controlling its mana than Aaron. It was an unfair contest, since they were a reincarnator who had far more experience. But nonetheless, he had to find a way to overcome this problem.

That said, despite the reaver’s control over its mana, it hadn’t reduced the amount of mana it was siphoning from him. He wasn’t exactly sure why; perhaps it just wanted to get the fight finished as soon as possible, and figured draining all his mana was the best way to do that.

But that was also Aaron’s opportunity. He ate some food as he powered himself, cycling more mana through his own core. There was a benefit to this reaver draining him; he was fairly certain under these conditions he could push himself to the limit without worrying about destroying his soul.

They clashed a few more times when Aaron got a brilliant idea. He waited for the reaver to strike, and threw a strike with [ Equal and Opposite ]. The attack caught the reaver’s attack before it could finish, and it was dismissed just like that.

In that moment, he also realized he could use Yendal’s attack far better than he had been able to in the past. It wasn’t at the level of a Passive Skill yet, as it was designed to be used by divine warriors, but he could use it far more liberally if he kept his core pumping mana at full power.

Eating furiously as he was desperate to generate as much mana as possible, Aaron waited for the reaver to strike again, and once more, he used [ Equal and Opposite ] to cancel the attack.

Timing was everything. Even a fraction of a second would allow the reaver to draw out some mana into a Skill. It was almost like the hand-slapping game, and Aaron was smacking the reaver’s attacks straight out of its hand before it could do anything, over and over again.

As he pushed himself, he concentrated on his mana anchors, infusing them, detaching them, and enhancing them, further pushing the amount of mana out of him and into the Death Reaver.

He could see the undead creature growing increasingly furious as its mana welled up within it, and yet, it didn’t stop siphoning. It just kept drawing more and more mana in, even as Aaron looked like an insane hand-slapping glutton, eating at an exponentially increasing speed, using his spirit limbs to help feed himself faster and faster.

The mana literally rippled around them, distorting the air. The situation was getting critical, and he could feel the Death Reaver’s soul cracking under the increased pressure of unstable mana filling its core with nowhere to go.

And yet, curiously, it didn’t stop.

If it was going to keep siphoning his mana, then Aaron was going to give it mana to siphon, and he kept slapping away its Skills before it could get anything started, and his speed advantage was finally showing its value, as the reaver was unable to do a damn thing about it.

“You greedy bastard!” Aaron said as he swallowed an endless line of food whole, generating more and more energy, sending arcs of mana whipping across the ground, cracking earth and exploding.

And it kept going. Draining more and more mana as Aaron desperately churned out as much as he could, doing everything to make sure he could keep cancelling out Skills, and feeding and feeding it.

Its soul was literally bulging. Cracks spread across every inch of it. And Aaron got the feeling that its inability to stop syphoning was somehow connected to the way its soul had been bound to the corpse it controlled.

And he kept feeding it mana as he fed himself. Draining so much of his food supplies while the battle was still so young was a worry, but he first needed to complete the objective before him.

It kept going, light piercing out from seams all across its soul as more mana flooded into it at an absolutely reckless rate. And never once could it even get the opportunity to start a Skill. Aaron was just too fast for it.

“No! Stop this madness! I yield! I cannot take any more!” The Death Reaver pleaded.

It wasn’t just scared, though. Aaron could feel the immense pain that its soul was bearing. It was far beyond physical pain. Something that dug deep into the recesses of its very being.

Suddenly, its eyes went wide, and an endless agony could be seen within them, and then nothing. Its soul literally burst, and the undead shell fell to the ground limp.
