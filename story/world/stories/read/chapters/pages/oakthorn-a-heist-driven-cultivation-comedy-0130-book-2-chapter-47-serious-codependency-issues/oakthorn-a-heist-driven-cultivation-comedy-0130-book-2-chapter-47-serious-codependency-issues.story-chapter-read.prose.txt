The Oakthorn Tree towered in the center of the cathedral, roots cracking through ancient stone as its branches unfurled toward the crumbling ceiling. Emerald light pulsed through its trunk like a heartbeat, sending waves of raw power rippling across the floor.

Bernard—or what remained of him—crashed through the debris, his gaze locked on Jer and the Soulkeeper’s Crown.

“Give it to me,” he snarled, emerald energy crackling across his skin. His body moved wrong, joints bending in unnatural directions as Mordred’s consciousness fought for control. “The crown. NOW.”

Cade stepped between them, his newly forged chest gleaming in the chaotic light. “Jer, go. Get everyone out.”

The acrobat hesitated, crown clutched tightly against his chest. “But—”

“GO!” Cade roared, eyes never leaving Bernard. “Get Chubbs to safety.”

Just as Jer turned to flee, Bernard launched himself forward with inhuman speed. Cade was faster. Gold-rank strength surged through his limbs as he intercepted Bernard mid-air, catching him squarely in the chest with his shoulder. The impact sent them both crashing into a heap of broken pews, wood splintering beneath their weight.

“I need that crown,” Bernard hissed, his voice fluctuating between his own and the ancient resonance of Mordred. “It’s mine. MINE!”

“Like a starving direwolf needs its last meal,” Cade replied, driving his knee into Bernard’s sternum and following with a sharp elbow to his jaw.

Bernard’s head snapped back, blood spraying from his lips. His hand shot up, fingers wrapping around Cade’s throat with crushing force. “You don’t understand… what you’re interrupting. If you stop me now, we all die.”

Cade twisted sharply, breaking the grip with a practiced movement. He rolled sideways, narrowly avoiding a blast of emerald fire that scorched the stones where he’d been a heartbeat before.

“Pretty sure you’re bluffing,” he grunted, springing to his feet. “Besides, I’m not much of a fan of the whole godhood, revenge, probably some global destruction happening.”

Bernard lunged again, emerald energy coalescing around his fist. Cade ducked under the strike, feeling the heat of it pass inches from his face. He countered with a swift uppercut that connected with Bernard’s chin, following it up with a spinning kick to his ribs.

Gods, this is so much easier as a gold ranker. I bet I could even beat Orro in a fight now…

The corrupted man staggered back, momentarily stunned. “You’re stalling,” he spat, blood trickling from the corner of his mouth. He wiped it with a corroded sleeve. “The Throne manifests even now, Cade Stormhollow. And once Mordred binds himself to it, not even you can stop us.”

Cade’s hand dipped casually into his satchel as he circled his opponent. His fingers closed around one of the orbs they’d collected during their journey. “Binding rituals,” he replied, keeping his expression neutral. “Fickle things, aren’t they?”

Bernard charged again, faster this time. His fist connected with Cade’s jaw, sending him stumbling back. Emerald energy crackled around his other hand, forming a blade of pure magical force.

“Hold still and die with dignity,” Bernard growled, slashing at Cade’s chest.

Cade twisted sideways, the emerald blade slicing through his shirt and grazing his metal-fused skin. He countered with a swift jab to Bernard’s throat, followed by a wind-infused kick that sent the man flying backward into a fallen column.

As Bernard struggled to rise, Cade flicked his wrist, sending the orb from his satchel arcing through the air. It glinted briefly in the emerald light before disappearing into the shadows of the cathedral’s high ceiling. Wind magic guided its trajectory, keeping it aloft and out of sight.

“Getting slower,” Cade taunted, circling Bernard warily. “Crown separation hitting you hard?”

Bernard’s face contorted with rage. “You think this is a game?” He thrust both hands forward, emerald energy erupting in a concentrated stream.

Cade dived sideways, the energy blast obliterating a stone pillar behind him. He rolled to his feet, summoning a shield of destruction magic to deflect a second blast. The energies clashed in a shower of sparks, the impact driving Cade back several steps.

“Not a game,” Cade replied, releasing a controlled burst of flame that forced Bernard to retreat. “More like payback for your horrible decoration choices down in that crypt. That was you, right Mordred? Seriously, hire a professional next time. The whole animated-skeleton thing was totally overdone.”

With a subtle gesture of his left hand, Cade directed the hovering orb toward the towering tree. The artifact descended silently as he engaged Bernard again, keeping his opponent’s attention firmly fixed on their battle.

“Why, you—” Mordred growled through Bernard.

The corrupted Progenitor unleashed a flurry of strikes, each one charged with emerald energy that left scorch marks on the stone floor. Cade parried the first with his bracer, ducked under the second, and caught the third with a cross-block that sent painful vibrations up his arms.

“Stubborn maggot,” Bernard snarled, pressing his advantage with a knee aimed at Cade’s groin.

Cade twisted his hip, taking the blow on his thigh instead. He countered with a palm strike to Bernard’s solar plexus, channeling just enough destruction magic to send the man staggering backward. He had to pace himself, lest he unleash too much at once again.

Just a little more, Cade inwardly coaxed his secondary spell.

Behind Bernard, the orb made contact with the Oakthorn’s bark. A brief crimson flash went unnoticed as the artifact sank into the living wood, disappearing completely.

Bernard doubled over suddenly, clutching his chest with a gasp of pain. “What... what did you...”

“What? Swallow some marshwater? That stuff has got to be bad for you, even if you’re an ancient tyrant hellbent on deicide,” Cade commented innocently, keeping his expression neutral even as he sensed the subtle shift in the cathedral’s energy. “Must be losing your edge.”

Fury replaced confusion as Bernard straightened, emerald light blazing from his eyes. “Enough games!” He slammed his foot down, sending a ripple of emerald energy across the floor that cracked the stone and forced Cade to leap backward.

Cade landed lightly, fingers already closing around a second orb in his satchel. “Only just getting warmed up,” he replied, weaving between eruptions of emerald fire that scorched the walls and ceiling.

Their battle carried them across the cathedral, through shattered pews and fallen masonry. Bernard hurled chunks of debris with inhuman strength, each projectile wreathed in emerald flame. Cade countered with precision blasts of destruction magic, shattering some missiles while dodging others.

Okay, just a few more, the Son of Ruin thought as he engaged another spell.

A stone the size of a wagon wheel caught him in the shoulder, sending him spinning into a half-collapsed pillar. Pain flared through his newly healed chest as Bernard closed the distance, emerald energy gathering between his palms for a killing blow.

“You really got to try another move, man,” Cade grunted at the Progenitor. “You stab a guy one time, and suddenly think he’ll fall for every time.”

“Do you ever shut up?!” Mordred roared.

“Stab me once, I’m the fool…” Cade replied in a sing-song voice. Then he furrowed his brows. “Wait, that can’t be right.”

Cade rolled sideways as the blast reduced the pillar to rubble. He sprang to his feet, conjuring a small whirlwind that sent the debris flying back at Bernard. The distraction bought him precious seconds to slip the next orb from his satchel.

“What does your fighting accomplish? Be honest with yourself. You are wasting your life away at this futile endeavor,” Bernard mocked, emerald shield flaring to deflect the stone fragments. “Accept your fate, thief. Our Throne rises. Surrender now and I will let you die quickly. I give you my word.”

“Never been one for accepting fate,” Cade replied, sending the second orb skimming along the floor beneath the scattered debris. Wind currents guided its path behind Bernard, toward the towering tree.

Bernard charged, emerald blades materializing around his hands. Cade met the attack with a rapid series of counters—ducking under the first slash, deflecting the second with his forearm, and kicking the bastard back a few steps when he tried to vertically bisect him. Each movement flowed seamlessly into the next, and their deadly dance continued.

Behind them, the second orb embedded itself in the Oakthorn’s roots with another crimson flash.

Bernard staggered mid-strike, emerald energy flickering around his form. “What treachery is this?” he gasped, genuine confusion breaking through the rage.

Cade pressed his advantage with a flurry of blows—left hook to the jaw, right jab to the sternum, spinning elbow to the temple. Each strike landed with gold-rank force, driving Bernard back against a half-shattered altar.

“No treachery,” Cade replied, maintaining his assault. “Just evening the odds.”

Bernard recovered with a roar of defiance, emerald energy exploding outward in a desperate surge. The blast caught Cade square in the chest, sending him flying across the cathedral to crash into the far wall.

Pain erupted through his newly forged chest, metal and flesh both protesting the impact. He rolled to his feet just as Bernard materialized before him, emerald fire wreathing his hands.

“You can’t stop this,” Bernard snarled, unleashing a torrent of emerald flames.

Cade countered with a shield of destruction magic, the energies clashing in a blinding display. Heat scorched his face as he struggled to maintain the barrier, Bernard’s power growing more desperate with each passing moment.

The flame died suddenly as Bernard clutched his chest, emerald energy flickering erratically around his form. Something was affecting him, though he seemed unaware of the cause.

Cade seized the opportunity, releasing his barrier to deliver a devastating uppercut that lifted Bernard off his feet. As the corrupted man crashed back to the floor, Cade slipped a third orb from his satchel and flicked it toward the cathedral ceiling.

Bernard rose shakily, blood streaming from a split lip. “Something’s wrong,” he murmured, more to himself than to Cade. “The connection weakens.”

“Finally noticing, are you?” Cade circled warily, tracking the orb’s position from the corner of his eye. “Bit slow on the uptake for a would-be god.”

Fury replaced confusion as Bernard unleashed another barrage of emerald projectiles. Stone and wood ripped free from the cathedral’s structure, hurtling toward Cade with deadly force.

Cade wove between the missiles, destruction magic flaring at his fingertips to deflect those he couldn’t dodge. One stone grazed his shoulder, another scored his thigh, but he maintained his focus on the battle and the orb now descending toward the Oakthorn.

A third crimson flash as the artifact embedded itself in the tree’s bark.

Bernard collapsed to one knee with a cry of pain, emerald energy pouring from his eyes and mouth like blood. “What... what are you doing to me?” he gasped, voice fluctuating between his own and Mordred’s.

“To you?” Cade approached cautiously, fourth orb already in hand. “Nothing at all.”

Bernard’s gaze locked onto the orb, understanding dawning in his corrupted features. “The seals,” he snarled, emerald light blazing from his eyes. “You’re using them against the ritual.”

Before Cade could respond, Bernard surged forward with inhuman speed, tackling him to the ground. They crashed across the cathedral floor, emerald energy burning Cade’s skin wherever it made contact.

“GIVE IT TO ME!” Bernard roared, fingers clawing for the orb clutched in Cade’s hand.

Cade headbutted him, the impact momentarily stunning the corrupted man. He followed with a knee to Bernard’s midsection, creating just enough space to slam both palms against his opponent’s chest. Destruction magic erupted between them, sending Bernard flying backward to crash through a row of broken pews.

As Bernard struggled to rise, Cade hurled the fourth orb toward the Oakthorn. Bernard’s hand shot out, emerald energy lashing like a whip to intercept it. “NO!”

Too late. The artifact embedded itself in the tree’s upper branches with another crimson flash.

Bernard’s body convulsed as if struck by lightning, and emerald energy erupted from his mouth in an agonized scream. His form flickered between solid flesh and translucent energy, the separation becoming visible as Mordred’s essence strained against the binding power of the orbs.

For brief moments, Cade could see them as distinct entities occupying the same space—the broken human and the ancient consciousness that had consumed him. The orbs weren’t just affecting the ritual; they were creating a barrier around the Oakthorn, a barrier that Mordred could not cross.

“You don’t understand what you’re doing,” Bernard gasped, his voice momentarily his own again. “He says... he says you’re sealing him out...”

Cade approached cautiously, fifth orb in hand. “That’s exactly what I’m doing.”

Something in the cathedral shifted as Bernard processed these words. The emerald energy flickered more violently around his form, Mordred’s essence fighting desperately against the growing barrier.

“The crown,” Bernard whispered, eyes widening in horror. “You’re forcing him back into the crown.”

Before Cade could confirm this revelation, Mordred seized control with renewed rage. Bernard’s body rose from the floor, suspended by emerald energy as the cathedral trembled around them. Stones tore free from the walls, orbiting him in a deadly vortex.

“YOU WILL NOT DENY ME!” Mordred’s voice boomed, shaking the foundations of the ancient building. “THE THRONE IS MINE!”

The vortex of debris expanded outward, stone and wood becoming deadly projectiles as they hurtled toward Cade. He countered with a burst of wind magic, creating a protective sphere that deflected the smaller fragments though larger chunks still broke through to batter his already battered body.

A massive stone caught him in the ribs, driving the breath from his lungs. Another grazed his temple, blood flowing freely down his face as he struggled to maintain his barrier.

Through the chaos, he flicked the fifth orb from trembling fingers. Bernard tracked its arc this time, emerald energy lashing out to intercept the artifact mid-flight. The orb shattered into glittering fragments that scattered across the cathedral floor.

“NOT THIS TIME,” Mordred growled, satisfaction evident despite his weakened state.

Cade’s expression remained unchanged as he drew the final orb from his satchel. As he had with all the previous ones, he coated it with a layer of his combined magicks, giving him complete control over its flight as much as how it responded to the strange tree.

“Pity about your counting skills,” he observed calmly. “Would’ve served you well for godhood.”

Bernard’s eyes widened as he realized the trap. “How many—”

The final orb soared across the cathedral, its path guided by currents of air too subtle for Bernard to track. It embedded itself in the Oakthorn with a flash of crimson that momentarily outshone even the emerald light of the ritual.

The effect was catastrophic.

The barrier completed, sealing the Oakthorn completely against Mordred’s attempted binding. Bernard’s body erupted in emerald fire as the ancient consciousness was forcibly rejected, with nowhere to go but back to its original prison.

“NO!” Mordred’s voice transcended physical sound, reverberating directly through Cade’s bones and magical pathways. “THE THRONE! MY ASCENSION!”

Bernard collapsed to the floor, human once more, though his body bore the scars of Mordred’s partial departure. Blood streamed from his eyes, ears, and mouth as he struggled to rise on trembling limbs.

“You... you sealed him out,” he gasped, meeting Cade’s gaze with clear eyes for the first time. “The orbs... they’re keeping him from the tree...”

Cade nodded grimly. “The same artifacts that kept him in the crown can keep him out of the tree.”

“Clever boy,” Bernard whispered, a ghost of a smile touching his bloodied lips. “But he won’t go quietly.”

As if summoned by these words, emerald energy erupted from Bernard’s body once more. Mordred’s essence fought desperately against the barrier, refusing to return to its prison.

“The crown,” Bernard gasped through gritted teeth. “He needs... he needs to be drawn back...”

Cade understood immediately. With the Oakthorn sealed against him, Mordred’s essence needed to be forced back into the crown that had contained him for millennia.

“Hold on,” Cade urged, summoning both magics simultaneously.

Destruction formed a lance of golden fire in his right hand, while Chaos manifested as crimson lightning in his left. The energies should have annihilated each other, yet they coexisted in perfect harmony as Cade brought them together.

Bernard convulsed as emerald energy poured from his form, Mordred’s essence fighting to maintain its grip on the physical realm. “Do it,” he begged, voice barely audible. “Free me from this curse.”

Cade drove his combined magic into Bernard’s chest—the same spot where the Progenitor had impaled him earlier. Golden-crimson energy pierced emerald defenses, burning through corrupted flesh to strike at the essence beneath.

“NO!” Mordred’s voice echoed from everywhere and nowhere. “I WILL NOT RETURN TO THAT PRISON! YOU WILL DOOM US ALL IF YOU DO! SHE COMES! CALAMITY COMES! I’M THE ONLY ONE WHO CAN STOP HER!”

For a heartbeat, time seemed to stop. Bernard’s body hung suspended, impaled on Cade’s magical blade. Then emerald light erupted from the wound, pouring outward as Mordred’s essence was forcibly expelled.

The Progenitor’s spectral form hovered briefly above Bernard’s body—a being of pure emerald energy, ancient and terrible. With the Oakthorn sealed against him and Bernard’s body too damaged to contain him, only one vessel remained.

“You think this ends anything?” the ancient consciousness snarled, voice distorted by rage. “I will return. The crown merely delays the inevitable. Pray that I’m not too late, ruinous child. For if I fail, we’re all doomed.”

“Story of my life,” Cade replied, meeting the Progenitor’s gaze without flinching.

With a final, defiant scream, Mordred’s essence was drawn inexorably toward the Soulkeeper’s Crown still clutched in Jer’s hands as he fled the cathedral. The emerald energy compressed, condensed, and vanished into the ancient artifact with a sound like a thousand souls wailing at once.

Bernard collapsed into Cade’s arms, mortal once more and rapidly fading. Blood bubbled from his lips as he struggled to speak. “You... clever bastard,” he whispered. “Used the prison... to keep him out...”

“The orbs were designed as soul barriers, so far as I could tell,” Cade explained softly, lowering Bernard to the ground. “Figured they might work just as well keeping one from going where it shouldn’t.”

A weak smile flickered across Bernard’s bloodied face. “Always... thinking sideways...” His gaze drifted toward the towering tree in the center of the ritual circle. “The Throne... not what he claimed...”

Cade followed his gaze, studying the massive oak more carefully now that his life wasn’t so directly on the line. Indeed, there was something off about it. It took him several embarrassing seconds to finally put a name to what had been bothering him, and when he had, he groaned in frustration at his oversight.

Despite everything Death had told him, despite its ominous name, the tree lacked the black thorns that should have been its defining feature.

“Well shit,” he murmured, understanding dawning fully. “It’s not an Oakthorn Throne at all.”

“Just... an oak...” Bernard coughed, more blood spattering his chin. “He lied... to all of us... wanted godhood... but the tree rejected him...”

The cathedral trembled violently around them, stones breaking free from the ceiling to crash among the debris. The massive tree’s roots spread wider, cracking the ancient foundations as they sought purchase in the earth below.

“Go,” Bernard whispered, his voice fading. “Save... your friends...”

Cade hesitated, unwilling to leave the dying man despite everything that had happened between them. “Bernard...”

A bloodied hand gripped his wrist with surprising strength. “Thank you,” Bernard breathed, clear-eyed for the first time since before their doomed dive into the crypt. “For freeing me.”

Before Cade could say anything, Death materialized beside them, his form wavering as if reluctant to fully manifest within the cathedral.

“Destroy it, Cade,” the deity commanded tersely, gesturing toward the oak. “Destroy it now, before it’s too late.”

Cade turned toward the ancient being, still cradling Bernard in his arms. “Why? It’s not what you said it was.”

Death’s expression darkened. His form crackled dangerously, and ice crept along the cathedral’s stone floor from where he hovered. “That changes nothing. It must not be allowed to remain.”

“Seems like it changes everything,” Cade replied, gently laying Bernard on the ground. “You told me it was an Oakthorn Throne. You said Bernard would ascend if it manifested. But there are no thorns. So, what’s the threat, really?”

“There are things beyond your mortal understanding,” Death snarled, staying conspicuously outside the ritual circle. “The tree is a nexus of power that should not exist in this realm.”

Cade rose slowly after setting the limp Bernard down. He started to study Death with newfound suspicion.

“Then why won’t you go near it?” he asked, taking a deliberate step toward the oak. “Why stay outside the circle?”

Death’s form flickered again. “Can’t you ever just do what you’re told, boy?”

He’d heard those words before, and he did not like the comparison.

“Or maybe...” Cade took another step toward the tree, feeling its energy washing over him in gentle waves. “Maybe you’re afraid of something else entirely.”

The cathedral shuddered as a massive portion of the ceiling collapsed, narrowly missing the central oak. Through the newly formed opening, Cade could see storm clouds gathering with unnatural speed, lightning flashing between them.

“There’s no time for this,” Death snapped. “Destroy it! Use your chaos magic to unmake it!”

Bernard’s breathing grew more labored, blood pooling beneath his broken form. “Don’t...” he gasped. “He fears... what it truly is…”

Cade looked from Bernard to Death, then to the towering oak at the center of it all. In that moment, he made his decision.

“No,” he said simply.

Death’s face contorted with rage. “You fool!” Ice began to spread rapidly across the edges of the lingering ritual circle, boxing Cade inside. “You have no idea what you’re preserving!”

“Maybe not,” Cade admitted, walking deliberately toward the oak. “But I know that you’re afraid of it, and that’s good enough for me. You really shouldn’t have called me ‘boy.’”

As he approached the massive tree, Cade could feel its energy resonating with his own chaos magic, the crimson threads of power responding to something ancient and familiar. The sensation was not one of draining, as Death had claimed, but of recognition—as if the tree itself acknowledged his presence.

Bernard’s last breath escaped in a rattling sigh behind him. The man who had been both enemy and victim was gone, freed at last from Mordred’s corrupting influence.

Death’s form began to fade, retreating from the cathedral entirely. “You’ve doomed yourself,” the deity warned, voice echoing as his presence diminished. “When you realize what you’ve preserved, you’ll beg for my forgiveness.”

“Not holding my breath,” Cade called after him, never taking his eyes off the oak.

Alone now in the crumbling cathedral, Cade stood before the towering tree. Its branches swayed gently despite the absence of wind, emerald light pulsing through its trunk like a heartbeat. The orbs he had embedded in its wood glowed with crimson energy, forming a protective barrier that sealed the oak from external influence.

Cade reached out his hand and placed his palm against the smooth bark.
