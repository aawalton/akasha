Gwen regarded her Regent.

“It’s true,” Richard nodded. “They’re selling like meat pies fresh out of the oven.”

They sat on the branches, listening to the swaying of the wind and the fluttering of Harpies with their bright plumage overhead, conversing in soft, melodic tones. “Petra's joining us for dinner,” Richard informed her.

Gwen nodded. It would be good to chew the fat with Petra and catch up on the mundane matters of life.

The sun set, and the tree began its nightly bioluminescent glow, joined now by a Goddess who couldn’t tone down the psychic energies leaking from her divine body. Below her feet, a city of millions milled about happily, unburdened by the horrors she had made manifest across the ocean.

In the hours after Teithuacán, with a billion Mer minds coming down the Soul Tap at once and her Astral Body ballooning toward heat death, she had channelled the flood into her World Tree, because it was the only vessel in the world big enough to take it. And then, for however long at Neo Tenochtitlan, she had repurposed Sympathetic Life-Link to pump her surplus into the same place, over and over, at an industrial rate.

“Richard…” Gwen’s voice came as a whisper to her cousin. “Can I tell you something?”

“Sure thing.” Her oldest friend and family sat next to her, his jacket finally off. His eyes were pale blue. Lea was watching as well.

“I am scared shitless,” Gwen said, allowing the oppression to escape her chest. “This… whatever this is, I don’t know what I am doing.”

Richard laughed, and his laughter carried no further than the next branch before it was swallowed by the space.

“Did you ever?”

Gwen kicked the air, feet dangling. “Is that supposed to make me feel better?”

Richard patted the trunk. For a second, the whole tree seemed to shiver in response.

Gwen looked around them in alarm. The sensation, the presence, the playful mischievousness—it was far too familiar.

“Sufina?”

The leaves rustled—there was the trill of a woman’s sharp laughter—then all at once, their world filled with the living flames of amber leaves.
