System teleportation was as uncanny as ever.

One moment, James had just kissed his wife goodbye, finished a conversation he was having, and was in mid-blink—and then he was in another space.

As soon as he set eyes on his new surroundings, James thought it was probably an extremely geographically diverse arena. This was partly because the bit of terrain he landed on was a mountainside, and he could see in two directions for a few miles until the arena ended in what his mind conceived as a sort of wall of darkness.

But the other reason was simple prior experience.

This arena—with a small mountain range where James was, massive lakes and at least one swamp in one area, and an icy tundra in another—appeared to be divided into quadrants just like his Orientation space had been. A little part of James almost felt nostalgic. Almost. He would have been nostalgic if not for all the killing and nearly being killed that he had been a part of back then.

He took a deep breath, smiled—it was good to be back in a setting where he was expected to fight for his life, especially when actual permanent death was borderline impossible by the System’s own rules—and then he clenched his teeth and quietly groaned. He looked down at the left side of his chest, right where his heart was.

There was an arrow of light sticking out of his rib cage.

That was a little too quick, James thought.

As if confirming that the arrow had been too sudden, before the competition even began, a female System representative’s voice rang through the air.

[Welcome to the melee arena, and happy hunting!]

James twisted his lips in a grimace. The arrow didn’t hurt—he no longer felt pain unless he wanted to expose himself to it—but that feeling of being targeted at the very beginning—as if he was somehow a weak link to be quickly disposed of—was genuinely a bit painful. Only to his ego, but his ego was a lot more vulnerable to harm than his physical body by this point.

He scanned the surroundings for thirty seconds—he saw dozens of people, human and nonhuman—before his eyes finally lit on the one with a bow. She was still looking at him, a slightly guilty expression on her face.

The woman the Holy Kings had referred to as the Virgin Queen.

She mouthed the word, “Sorry,” and lifted up the bow as if confused, poking at it and pantomiming as if to say that there must be something wrong with it, or maybe that it was the bow’s idea. The play-acting was a little bit funny even to James.

He waved to her, wiggling his fingers, then casually reached down and plucked the light arrow out of his chest.

All his Skills were functioning again, and his rapid healing regenerated his heart completely and restored it to full functioning almost as soon as the arrow was out of his chest. The wound closed after that, and the light arrow vanished.

The Queen ran off as quickly as she could.

He used Identify to get her name.

Esmeralda Ortega Cortez, huh? I’ll remember it. James didn’t bother chasing her. She had hopefully at least learned a lesson about the limits of what one could do with a bow and arrows, even magical arrows made of light. I have bigger fish to fry.

He pointed his right fist into the air. Meteor Strike! He threw three quick punches surrounded by flames. Three fireballs rocketed off of his fist into the air, exploding in a little fireworks celebration directly overhead. That was to be his signal to tell the others where he was. His location was central enough that he would try to wait for them to come to him.

Wait a second.

Cortez had probably been hired by his real enemy. He looked after her. She was still close enough for him to see her, which meant close enough for him to catch her instantly with Warp Step.

He pictured Cortez aiming that bow at one of his allies and shook his head.

Well, I wanted to let you go…

Warp Step.

James stepped forward and appeared five feet away from Cortez, in a meadow beside a lake. There were monsters and humans fighting and killing each other in every direction around them, but for a moment, James and Cortez only paid attention to each other. She turned her head and took one look at him, and her eyes practically popped out of her head.

“Oh my God!”

Soul-Freezing Gaze.

She had been trying to turn away and keep running, but James caught her in the middle of looking at him and made direct eye contact. His Will steamrolled hers in an instant, and her body stopped in its tracks, completely paralyzed.

An expression of horror spread across her face. She looked like she wanted to say something, but Soul-Freezing Gaze’s power held even her jaws and vocal cords tightly.

“So, I guess you connected with the Holy Kings after all,” James said, shaking his head lightly.

“Mmf.”

“I don’t know whether that’s an admission or a denial, and to tell you the truth, I don’t care. If you’re with the Holy Kings, I recommend you choose better friends next time. They’re not long for this world. You came after me in here, where the stakes are basically nonexistent, so I won’t take it personally. Really, it’s almost a show of respect to come at the king. You recognized that I was the most powerful target you could aim for, and you shot straight at me. I get that. Don’t ever come at me anywhere else, though. I’d rather not have to do this in real life.”

He could practically hear her wondering, Do what?

In answer, he swung his arm to the side faster than her eyes could follow. His nails slashed through her throat—pure physical power, no Skills required—and he could sense that she was nearly dead before she hit the ground. He stepped just outside the radius of the pool of blood that slowly darkened the grass around the body.

Not her, master? Roscuro asked.

Not her, James affirmed. Hopefully she’ll make a better choice in the real world. I’m already going to court enough controversy going after the Holy Kings themselves, let alone people who only spoke to them. She seemed like a nice young woman.

He bent and picked up the bow she had been holding. It was a beautiful piece of craftsmanship. The body was made from a silver-colored metal and set with pearls, while the bowstring looked almost to be made of light itself. It just mysteriously glowed.

Huh. James looked down at his hands. He didn’t feel any pain, but he saw the smoke and smelled the odor of cooking flesh from where his fingers made contact with the material. The metal of the bow actually burned his skin to the touch. That makes it impractical to do a bit of test shooting, I guess. Am I cursed now? Is the weapon holy, and it’s reacting to my body?

There was still so much about the world he didn’t understand. He dropped the weapon beside the woman’s body.

James sensed as a familiar presence entered a ten-foot radius around him.

“Count Aleph,” James said. “I hope you’re not just here for a drink.” He gestured at the dead woman, who was still leaking a steady fountain of blood. “In fact, don’t you have things to do? It would be a terrible shame if we failed in our mission.”

“Just confirming that this is where I should find you,” the Vampire replied.

“I’ll be here,” James said. “Or back a little closer to the mountain, where I set off my fireworks display. Show up in this general area. I’ll have my eyes open looking for you and the others.”

“Or I’ll find you first,” Aleph suggested.

“Doubt it.” James activated Invisibility.

“I can still sense where you are,” the Vampire said. “You have her blood on you. But a very useful Skill.”

I do have a little bit of her under my nails. I can barely feel that amount of blood myself, but he can smell it. Such sharp senses. James was reminded of the fact that he had declined the opportunity to become a Dhampyr, which undoubtedly would have led to a Vampire Evolution by now.

“I just don’t want anyone to spot me and pull me into a pointless fight that doesn’t contribute to my goal,” James said.

“I understand. I’ll find you.” The Vampire hurried off.

James spent the next ten minutes choosing his position in a slightly elevated place—back on the mountainside, but not as high up as before—getting comfortable, and looking around. There was a lot of killing going on. It was a wild contrast with the Summit he had just left.

A giant reptile slashed at a giant fish in the shallows of one of the great lakes before him. A Sasquatch-looking creature fought with a female Vampire James dimly remembered seeing at the Summit.

Closest to James, an African hunter armed with a spear hunted a lion down in the meadow in what must have been an unintended replication of millennia-old tradition—except that the lion had a spear, too, and the African spearman’s weapon blazed with a green mystical fire.

The two of them thrust violently at each other, dodging and wheeling around, trying to catch the other off-guard. Finally, the lion allowed himself to be gored, but in exchange, he stabbed the human in the thigh. As the hunter staggered back, the lion threw himself forward and managed to get off a clean swipe with one of his paws. That tore out the hunter’s throat, and he collapsed.

It’s too bad I’m not trying to win the melee, James thought as he watched the lion tend his wounds. There are a lot of fighters getting pretty banged up that I could just finish off.

The giant reptile, which had defeated the giant fish, was suddenly seized by an explosive flurry of tentacles and dragged deeper into the lake.

Finally, James saw a first sign of hope that his plan might succeed.

Just beyond the swamp, Luna stepped forward. In her jaws, she clutched a heavily bloodied figure James recognized as one of the Holy Kings. The white-gloved man. Even better, Luna wasn’t alone. Beside her, the Panther Queen carried what was left of another Holy King.

James imagined—hoped, really—that the man was still alive despite the fact that he was missing much of his lower body. One leg had been amputated just above the knee, while on the other side, there didn’t seem to be anything left below the hip.

The Fisher Emperor deactivated Invisibility and sent a telepathic message to both of his allies: Can you see me? I’m on the mountainside right now. I can come to you.

You can wait where you are, if you wish, my Emperor, Luna replied.

These two will not be giving us any further trouble, the Panther Queen agreed. Her voice carried a grim satisfaction.

But James felt nervous. The Holy Kings had gotten at least some wind of his intentions, even if the specifics might have been impossible for them to guess. Even now, they might mount some sort of rescue mission to save these two. A bird in the hand was worth a lot just then. He would hopefully gather thirteen birds, but it wouldn’t happen if he didn’t take care to deal with the ones he actually had.

He moved with Warp Step and suddenly appeared before the stunned wolf and panther.

I always forget some of your impressive powers, Luna sent, bowing her heads slightly at his appearance. She did not release her grip on her prisoner.

“Nice,” the Panther Queen said in a much more relaxed tone. She dropped her horribly maimed foe onto the grass at her feet. The unconscious figure didn’t noticeably react to landing on the ground.

“Thank you both,” James said. “You’re the first to make it to me with enemies in tow, so you know. I’ll take it from here.”

Roscuro, he thought, and at that single word, the Soul Eater Orb transformed into the shape of a longsword.

“Fisher Emperor,” said the Holy King who had become Luna’s chew toy. “You think you can intimidate us with this display of power? We already knew you controlled monstrous creatures. We knew you were dangerous. That was never going to deter us—”

“It’s not about intimidation,” James said softly. “It’s about ending the threat.”

Without further preamble, he stabbed Roscuro’s point directly into the white-gloved man’s chest.
