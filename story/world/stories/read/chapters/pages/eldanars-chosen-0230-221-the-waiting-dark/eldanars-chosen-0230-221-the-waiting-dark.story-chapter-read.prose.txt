The transition from night to day brought absolutely no warmth to the high ramparts of Ashenfall. As the bruised purple sky of the eastern horizon slowly bled into a pale, washed-out gold, the freezing winter wind seemed to cut even sharper. It whipped across the open walkway, catching the edges of heavy cloaks and rattling the loose iron rings of chainmail. The light did not reveal a pristine, snow-covered landscape. Instead, the dawn simply illuminated the horrifying, creeping reality of the northern plains. The dark, oily corruption had spread significantly during the night, painting the distant frost-covered grass in sickly shades of black and grey.

Josh stood perfectly still upon the reinforced wooden platform situated directly above the main iron portcullis. He rested his thick, steel-clad forearms against the top of the wooden parapet, his storm-grey eyes locked on the distant, corrupted treeline.

The silence on the wall was profound and heavy. Thousands of conscripted militia members, veteran adventurers, and seasoned city guards stood shoulder to shoulder along the miles of timber defences. Nobody was speaking. The only sounds were the howling of the wind, the rhythmic creaking of the massive wooden support beams bracing the gates below, and the occasional, nervous cough of a terrified baker or blacksmith holding an unsharpened spear.

They were all waiting for the end of the world to simply walk out of the trees.

"The light has fully broken," Brett murmured softly, his voice barely carrying over the wind. The elemental mage was huddled deep within his shimmering crimson cloak, his pale eyes squinting against the glare of the rising sun. "If they maintained the marching speed Finn reported last night, they should be right on top of us by now."

"They are out there," Bhel grunted from Josh's right side. The dwarven fighter rested his good hand on the hilt of a heavy throwing axe, his dark eyes narrowed into fierce, uncompromising slits. "I can feel it in the stone below the timber. The earth is unhappy."

Carcan remained silent. She stood just behind the heavy fighters, her luminous silver eyes fixed on the spot where the old northern trade road disappeared into the dark, rotting canopy of the forest. Her knuckles were stark white as she gripped her amber-topped staff. She was undoubtedly praying for her twin brother, currently lost somewhere in that suffocating darkness.

Suddenly, a sharp, collective gasp rippled down the length of the wooden ramparts.

Josh instantly straightened up, his armour clanking loudly in the quiet morning air. He gripped the leather handle of his dark steel shield, his heart giving a massive, painful thud against his ribs.

Something was moving in the distant treeline.

The dense, dead undergrowth bordering the dirt road began to thrash wildly. The movement was frantic, chaotic, and entirely lacking the slow, mindless shambling of a typical ghoul. All along the wall, the terrifying sound of a thousand weapons being drawn echoed in unison. Heavy steel broadswords cleared their scabbards with sharp, metallic rings. The veteran rangers stationed on the upper parapets instantly nocked their arrows, pulling the heavy bowstrings back to their ears in perfect, mechanical synchronicity. The mages raised their hands, wands, and staffs, all sparking with raw, highly volatile energy.

"Hold your fire!" a hoarse-voiced guard captain roared from a nearby watchtower, waving a bright red pennant frantically. "Identify the target! Do not waste your mana!"

A single, solitary figure burst violently from the dark, rotting pine trees, stumbling out onto the frost-covered dirt road.

It was not a towering Bone Goliath or a scuttling Crypt Weaver. It was a lithe, leather-clad rogue. She was entirely alone, and she was running with a sheer, desperate intensity that suggested her lungs were about to physically burst.

"It is Lyra!" Carcan gasped, leaning over the wooden parapet, her serene composure completely shattering with profound relief. "She was part of Perberos's squad!"

The half-elven rogue looked absolutely terrible. She was missing her heavy travel cloak, her dark leather armour was heavily splattered with foul-smelling black mud, and her eyes were wide with sheer, primal exhaustion. She stumbled constantly as she sprinted across the open plains, her boots slipping on the frozen grass, but she forced herself back up every single time. She did not look behind her. She simply kept her eyes locked on the massive timber walls of the city.

"She is entirely spent," Josh noted grimly, instantly recognising the clumsy, heavy-footed gait of someone running purely on the absolute dregs of their adrenaline.

He leaned far over the edge of the parapet, looking down at the massive, jagged trench filled with highly volatile alchemical pitch separating the plains from the city gates. "She will never make it across the trench, and the main gates are completely barricaded from the inside!"

"Get a rope down there immediately!" Brett shouted, pointing down to a squad of heavily armoured spearmen standing on the lower defensive tier.

The guards did not hesitate. A burly sergeant grabbed a thick, heavy coil of hemp rope from a nearby supply crate. He rapidly tied a large, secure loop at one end and hurled the heavy coil over the wooden parapet. The thick rope unspooled rapidly, dropping past the pitch trench and slapping heavily against the freezing cobblestones just outside the sealed iron portcullis.

Lyra finally reached the edge of the trench. She did not have the energy to attempt a running jump. She simply threw herself forward, her hands grasping weakly at the heavy loop of hemp rope.

"Haul her up!" the sergeant roared, grabbing the rope with both hands and leaning back.

Three other guards rushed to assist him, but the angle was difficult, and the sheer weight of the exhausted rogue was proving entirely too much for them to lift quickly.

"Move," Josh ordered, his deep voice carrying unquestionable authority.

He shoved his way past the straining guardsmen, dropping his heavy shield onto the wooden deck. He grabbed the thick hemp rope with both of his massive, steel-clad gauntlets. He planted his heavy boots firmly against the wooden floorboards, and hauled backward with a massive, explosive heave.

Lyra shot up the side of the towering timber wall with terrifying speed.

Within seconds, she crested the upper parapet. Josh reached out with one hand, grabbing the rogue firmly by the thick leather collar of her armour and hauling her completely over the wooden barricade.

Lyra hit the wooden walkway in a tangled, shivering heap. She rolled onto her back, staring blindly up at the pale morning sky, her chest heaving with massive, ragged gasps that sounded like tearing canvas. She could not speak. She simply lay there, trembling violently from the cold and the horrific physical exertion of her hours-long, unbroken sprint.

"Give her space!" a commanding, melodic voice echoed through the gathered crowd of concerned fighters.

Caistina pushed her way through the ranks of heavily armoured guardsmen. The elven mage looked regal and entirely unshakeable, her sapphire-blue robes immaculate against the grime of the ramparts. She knelt smoothly beside the gasping rogue, her pristine white staff glowing with a faint, calming light.

Carcan was already there. The elven healer unstoppered a small, glowing green glass vial with her teeth. She gently lifted Lyra's mud-caked head, carefully pouring the potent, high-grade stamina potion directly down the rogue's throat.

The magical liquid acted almost instantly. The violent, uncontrollable trembling in Lyra's limbs slowly began to subside. The sickly, grey pallor of her skin was replaced by a faint flush of returning colour, and her breathing gradually slowed from ragged gasps to deep, shuddering breaths.

Lyra blinked, her cynical eyes slowly focusing on the faces of the high-tier adventurers and commanders surrounding her.

"They are coming," Lyra rasped, her voice painfully hoarse, completely lacking its usual sharp edge. She weakly pushed herself up onto her elbows, looking directly at Caistina. "We found the main marching column deep in the northern valley. The timeline is gone. They are coordinated, and they are moving relentlessly."

Rochelle, the stern head clerk of the Guild, stepped forward from behind Caistina, her thick leather ledger clutched tightly to her chest. "What are their numbers, Lyra? We need an accurate count to arrange the heavy infantry."

Lyra let out a short, completely hollow laugh that sounded entirely devoid of humour or hope. She looked at the clerk, her eyes reflecting the absolute, primal horror she had witnessed from the rocky ridge.

"You cannot count them," Lyra whispered, the sheer gravity of her words silencing the entire section of the ramparts. "It is an ocean. There are thousands of heavily armoured skeletal knights in the vanguard. There are endless ranks of ghouls. We counted at least two dozen Bone Goliaths. There are easily ten thousand monsters marching down that road. Perhaps significantly more."

A wave of profound, suffocating terror washed over the listening guardsmen and militia members. Several young farm boys carrying crude spears literally dropped their weapons, their faces draining of all colour. Ten thousand monsters was not a siege force. It was an apocalyptic tide capable of wiping the entire province from the map.

"And where is the rest of your squad?" Caistina demanded softly, keeping her amethyst eyes locked firmly on the rogue, entirely ignoring the rising panic surrounding them. "Where are Ronald and Perberos?"

Carcan held her breath, her slender fingers digging painfully into the wood of her amber staff.

"They did not turn back," Lyra explained, wiping a thick layer of freezing black mud from her forehead. "Ronald deduced that an army of this impossible magnitude requires a centralised, intelligent commanding entity to maintain their formation. If that commander reaches the walls, Ashenfall falls."

"So they are attempting an assassination," Brett concluded, his pale face tightening with severe worry. He pushed his imaginary spectacles up his nose, his brilliant analytical mind instantly calculating the microscopic odds of success. "They are going to try and kill the general in the middle of a marching army of ten thousand undead."

"Perberos intends to put a steel bodkin directly through its skull before it ever sees the lights inside of the city," Lyra confirmed, looking up at Carcan with a mixture of profound sorrow and deep, abiding respect. "He refused to retreat. Ronald took them deep into the eastern forest. They are attempting to flank the horde and track them from the shadows, waiting for a clear shot."

Caistina slowly stood up, smoothing her sapphire-blue robes. She looked out over the dark, corrupted northern horizon, her expression entirely unreadable.

For a long moment, the elven mage trainer said absolutely nothing. Then, a slow, fierce, incredibly proud smile touched the corners of her lips.

"Fools," Caistina breathed softly, her melodic voice filled with a deep, uncompromising reverence for the bravery of her adventurers. "Absolute, magnificent fools."

She turned instantly, her battle persona snapping back into place with terrifying authority. She pointed her pristine white staff at a cluster of young, fast-looking guardsmen standing nearby.

"You three!" Caistina barked, her voice echoing loudly across the wooden walkway. "Runners! Get down to the lower levels immediately. I want this intelligence spread to every single guard commander, the city council, and the Guild administration. Tell them the numbers are overwhelming, and tell them to expect heavy siege rams at the main gates. We are holding the line!"

The runners saluted frantically and sprinted away, their boots pounding loudly against the steep wooden stairs as they descended into the chaotic city below.

Josh stepped away from the huddled group surrounding Lyra. He picked up his heavy steel shield, strapping it securely back onto his left arm. He walked slowly to the edge of the wooden parapet, resting his gauntlets on the top rail, and stared out into the pale morning light.

He expected to see the undead horde cresting the horizon at any moment. He braced his legs, readying his mind for the horrific visual impact of ten thousand monsters. But the horizon remained completely empty. The dark, corrupted plains stretched out before the city walls, entirely devoid of movement.

"Where are they?" Bhel grunted, stepping up to the parapet beside Josh. The dwarf narrowed his dark eyes, scanning the distant treeline. "If she ran all night, and they were already moving, they should be filling that field right now."

"An army of that size moves at the pace of its slowest units," Brett explained, joining them at the wall. "Thousands of shambling ghouls and massive siege engines cannot match the sprinting speed of a high-tier rogue, even if she was exhausted. We still have a window. But it is closing rapidly."

That window of time became the most excruciating psychological torture the defenders of Ashenfall had ever endured.

The frantic energy of Lyra's arrival slowly bled away, replaced by an agonising, stretched-out period of impatient waiting. The commanders used the precious minutes to finalise their last-minute defences.

Behind the heavy wooden parapets, the ramparts were a chaotic hive of desperate logistics. Sweating, exhausted labourers dragged heavy wooden carts filled with shattered masonry, loose cobblestones, and heavy iron anvils up the steep stairs, piling the crude projectiles neatly along the edge of the walkway. Squads of militia members hurriedly checked the tension on the heavy ballistae mounted in the watchtowers, loading massive, iron-tipped bolts the size of small children into the firing grooves.

"Bring up more pitch!" a quartermaster screamed from the lower tier, his voice cracking with stress. "We need the secondary cauldrons boiling before they hit the trench!"

Young apprentices ran frantically back and forth, carrying bundled sheaves of heavy arrows and distributing glowing red health potions to the veteran fighters. The noise of preparation was deafening, a desperate attempt to drown out the suffocating fear of what was coming.

Thirty minutes after Lyra collapsed onto the walkway, the atmosphere on the wall shifted violently.

Josh felt it before he actually heard it. A deep, unsettling vibration travelled through the thick wooden floorboards beneath his steel boots, buzzing faintly against the soles of his feet.

He closed his storm-grey eyes, trying to isolate his hearing from the frantic shouting of the labourers and the howling of the winter wind. He focused entirely on the freezing air rolling off the northern plains.

It was incredibly faint at first, easily mistaken for the rumble of distant thunder.

But it was not thunder. It was a deep, rhythmic, continuous grinding sound. It was the heavy, metallic clanking of thousands of rusted iron plates striking together. It was the deafening, unified thudding of ten thousand pairs of skeletal boots hitting the frozen earth in perfect, unnatural lockstep.

"They are marching," Josh stated quietly, opening his eyes.

The sound slowly rolled over the massive timber walls, washing over the terrified defenders. The frantic shouting of the quartermasters and the hammering of the labourers instantly ceased. A profound, suffocating silence fell over the entire length of the Ashenfall ramparts once more.

Every single pair of eyes locked onto the empty northern horizon. They strained to see through the pale morning light, desperate to catch the first glimpse of the approaching nightmare.

But they saw absolutely nothing.

The rolling hills and the creeping black sludge completely obscured the advancing army. The sound of their marching was a disembodied, terrifying promise of slaughter. It was a psychological weapon designed to completely break the morale of the defenders before a single sword was swung.

The waiting resumed, but it was no longer a frantic period of preparation. It was a slow, agonising descent into pure terror.

The minutes bled into an hour. The sun climbed higher into the pale, cloudless sky, casting stark, bright light over the corrupted plains, yet it offered no warmth to the shivering men and women standing on the walls. The rhythmic, deafening thudding of the approaching horde grew steadily louder, vibrating in their teeth and shaking the loose arrows in their quivers, but the enemy remained entirely hidden.

Nerves began to fray violently.

Further down the line, a young baker carrying a rusted meat cleaver suddenly dropped his weapon. He let out a ragged, hysterical sob, falling to his knees on the wooden walkway and covering his ears with his soot-stained hands, desperately trying to block out the sound of the marching boots.

"Stand up, lad!" a veteran guardsman barked, grabbing the weeping boy by the collar and hauling him roughly back to his feet. "Do not let them break you before they even show their faces! Pick up your steel!"

Josh gripped his shield so tightly his gauntlets groaned in protest. He looked at Carcan. The elven healer was standing perfectly still, her luminous silver eyes fixed on the horizon, her lips moving in a silent, continuous prayer to the Mother Tree. Brett was pushing his imaginary spectacles up his nose every few seconds, his pale face slick with nervous sweat, the bridge of his nose rubbed red. Even Bhel looked tense, his thick fingers constantly adjusting his grip on his axes.

Two more agonising hours dragged by. The psychological weight of the unseen apocalypse was paralysing. The constant, grinding noise of the approaching army filled their entire world, making it impossible to think, to breathe, or to find any shred of courage.

Then, far to the north, where the old dirt trade road crested a high, rolling hill miles away from the city walls, the shadows finally broke.

A dark, solid line appeared against the pale morning sky.

Josh leaned over the wooden parapet, his storm-grey eyes narrowing sharply. The line thickened, bleeding over the crest of the distant hill like a spill of black ink on a pristine canvas. It was not a chaotic swarm. It was a massive, unified block of dark steel and rotting bone, perfectly ordered and terrifyingly vast.

The rhythmic thudding of their boots suddenly matched the visual confirmation of their arrival, cementing the horrific reality in the minds of everyone watching.

"They are here," Josh stated, his deep voice carrying a cold, uncompromising finality.

The siege of Ashenfall had finally arrived.
