Jane cleared a section of her worktable for Bella's use. They worked side by side, each focused on their own projects but sparing enough attention for the other that they could share the space without bashing heads. Occasionally, Jane stole glances at her friend out of the corner of her eye.

"That smells incredible,” Jane said, watching Bella assemble what looked like a simple egg dish with vegetables and cheese. "What are you making?"

"Nothing. It’s not a full dish. I'm testing ratios." Bella expertly cracked another egg into her bowl with one hand. "I want to see if there’s anything I can improve before the restaurateur’s showcase."

Jane nodded and went back to her keln dough, but she found herself watching her friend more and more as the evening went on. Though Bella’s experiments were simple enough, Jane slowly became aware of just how exacting they were. Every batch had some controlled variation, up to and including several different ways of slicing vegetables and meats.

"Why are the cuts all different?” Jane asked.

Bella didn't look up from her work. “Cooking times. I can manage by moving things around the grill, but figuring out the size for each cut makes it a bit easier to get everything perfect at the same time.”

"I never thought about that."

“You wouldn’t need to. It’s the kind of thing that only matters when you do it hundreds of times a week.”

Bella poured the egg mixture over the vegetables and immediately began adjusting the heat. She moved the pan off-center on the flame, then tilted it slightly, then moved it back. Jane thought she understood why, but the sheer scope of how many adjustments Bella was making was mind-boggling.

"That seems like a lot of work for eggs,” she observed.

“Normal eggs, maybe.” Bella was still keeping her eyes on the pan. Jane had never seen her friend so focused. “But not eggs for a king. Those kinds of eggs have to be perfect. More perfect than usual, I mean.”

The completed dish came out of the pan a few minutes later, golden and puffy and basically an omelette, despite Bella claiming it was an experiment. Bella cut it into wedges and handed one to Jane, who dug in without hesitation.

"This is incredible," Jane said around her mouthful. "Bella. I mean it. This is really, really good."

"I know." Bella took a bite of her own wedge and chewed thoughtfully. "Though I think I want a bit more salt. And maybe some fresh herbs? I’d have to add them at the end, though, so they don’t get overcooked… I don’t know. It might be a little much."

"You look like you have it all locked in. Every step. I don't know if I'll ever get there with baking."

"You will." Bella pointed at Jane's worktable with her knife. "You're already better than you think you are. You just need more time. Even now, there are some things you have almost perfect, right?"

Jane looked at her works-in-progress. The keln dough was rising nicely. The meat buns were cooling. A fresh batch of burnt-top bread was ready for the oven.

"Maybe," she said. "I hope so."

They worked for another hour after that, trading bites of their test batches and offering opinions on each other's efforts. Bella's feedback was helpful as Jane began finalizing her plan in her head.

The keln would be her first offering. She had adjusted the recipe slightly, leaning more towards the herbal than she generally did. The result was a flatbread with more bite than usual, something that would hopefully stand out among the other keln presentations she knew she would see.

The buns would be her second item. She had finally gotten the balance right, she thought. Each one was a small package of bursting flavor, juicy and meaty and generally an explosion of taste that hit all at once, from the first bite to the last.

Finally, she would present the burnt-top bread. In some ways, this third item was Jane’s most personal offering. It had been one of her first innovations, a variation on something she had seen in other shops years before she had ever thought of being a baker. The trick was getting the sear across the top just right. It required a perfectly even application of heat that crisped all the herbs and seeds and seasonings she had worked into the crust.

Getting that sear consistent had taken a great deal of practice. Jane had eventually figured out that a tiny touch of magic made all the difference. She could feel when the crust was about to go from perfectly charred to ruined, so she could pull back the heat at exactly the right moment. By now, Jane was good at making burnt-top bread. Maybe even ‘good at it’ in a way non-magical people couldn’t achieve.

And it sells very well, thank you very much.

Jane surveyed her three offerings, nodded slightly, and stepped back from her worktable. "I think I'm done. At least for tonight."

Bella looked up from her station, where she had already started cleaning up the remains of her own efforts. "You figured out what you’re presenting?"

"I think so. Keln, buns, burnt-top bread." Jane wiped her hands on her apron. "It feels right. Like those are the things I should be showing people."

"Good. Then get some sleep. You have a shop to run tomorrow, and then three days to perfect everything. You'll need your energy."

Bella finished wiping down her station with practiced efficiency and moved to the door, stepping out into the cool night air.

"Thank you," Jane called after her. "For everything tonight."

Bella grinned. "Don't thank me yet. Wait until you win a spot at the showcase."

"If I win."

"When you win." Bella waved and started down the street. "Good night, Jane. Don't stay up too late."

Jane waited until the door closed and turned back to her kitchen. Bella was right, of course. Jane had recent experience of how difficult a day in the bakery could be after a night of little sleep. She cleaned up, then did the bare minimum amount of prep for the next day before going to bed.

Yet even as her body sank deeper and deeper into the mattress, her mind wouldn't quiet down. She kept thinking about the showcase, about the other bakers, about the king and queen and prince who would be eating her food if she made it through. It was all a lot, and it had come on suddenly. On some level, she wasn’t even sure she wanted to win, despite the fact that she was putting in all this work.

She drifted in and out of fitful sleep on those worries, like a door rocking on its hinges in the wind. Eventually, exhaustion won out, pulling her into a deep slumber filled with dreams of herb ratios, competitions, and kings.

—

The next morning came too quickly. Jane dragged herself out of bed and into her kitchen, slurping down her morning pick-me-up drink as she started the day's baking. Slowly, her mind caught up with her body. It wasn’t the widest variety or the highest quality baking she had ever done, but it was respectable.

Nobody notices the little fluctuations in stock but me, anyway. It’s all fine.

The shop opened on time, and customers began filtering in almost immediately. Jane sold her regular stock and chatted with the familiar faces. All the while, she kept part of her attention on plans for the showcase. She made tiny imaginary adjustments to her recipes and presentation throughout the morning, jotting them down in her notebook whenever she got a free moment.

Dave showed up right on schedule. He had been coming around noon every day, picking up whatever meat buns Jane had made and running them down to the Underbridge Market. Today, she had made extra, all little experiments to prepare her for the eventual showcase batch.

"These are really good," Dave said, taking one of the sample buns from the cooling rack. "Even better than yesterday."

"I've been practicing." Jane piled the last few buns into the basket and set the whole thing on the counter. "I have a competition coming up. Bakers from all over town are presenting their best work."

Dave's eyes went wide. "For the king's visit? I heard about that. Everyone's talking about it."

"Are they?"

Jane paused. She had been so focused on her baking that she hadn't noticed what the rest of the town was doing, or even paid much attention to the chatter between her customers. The only other person she had heard talk about the king’s visit was Bella, and Bella’s profession was so close to Jane’s that it had hardly registered as a different thing.

"What are they saying?" she asked.

"Just that it's exciting. People are fixing up their shops, painting things, making everything look nice." Dave grabbed the wrapped buns and tucked them under his arm. “The Underbridge Market is buzzing about it, too. They want to make a good impression. The crafters are planning a bunch of showcases.”

“That's good. I wonder if Allen has any part in it.”

“Not sure. Probably.” Giving her a wave with his free hand, Dave headed for the door. “See you tomorrow, Miss Jane.”

Dave left with his delivery, and Jane turned back to her work. She had a scant few days to perfect her recipes for the bakers’ showcase. She intended to use every hour of those days.

The full practice began as soon as the door closed behind the last customer. Once again, Jane worked long into the night. This time, though, she fell asleep almost as soon as her head hit the pillow.

If the competition followed her into her dreams again, she didn't remember it in the morning.

The next day flew by faster than she’d expected. It was the day before a rest day, which meant most customers bought a tad extra to tide them over. Between serving customers and running her regular operations, Jane barely had time to breathe, let alone practice. She squeezed in what she could anyway, making batch after batch of her showcase foods in the gaps between everything else. The day ended with another concentrated round of even more practice before she got into bed.

Tomorrow, she thought vaguely as sleep pulled her under. Tomorrow’s the day.

I hope I’ll be ready.

—

On competition day, Jane awoke with a sense of profound gratitude for Glenfall’s ‘rest day’ system. She still got up and did some baking, focusing on a few batches of the essential basics for rest day meals. This way, anyone who hadn’t managed to visit her the day before could still get what they needed. It was a fairly standard habit for the town’s bakers on rest day.

Jane would do about the same tomorrow morning for Lee-day. It meant she rarely had a day entirely off, but that was fine. Between the two days, she usually got enough rest on a weekly basis.

Not this week, though. This week, Jane’s rest day was all about preparation for the competition that evening.

The burnt-top bread was still coming out perfectly every time. She had made so many batches over the past several months that the process felt automatic, her magic reaching out at just the right moment to control the sear. The keln took more attention, since the herb mixture was not yet quite where she wanted it, but she was getting closer with each attempt.

The buns were the trickiest. The filling needed to be just right, in cooking time and in spice blend, or the whole thing fell apart. But she had made enough of them by now that she was starting to sense when something was off.

Any batch that didn't meet her standards went into Dave's delivery pile. She had encouraged him to stop by at his usual time, rest day or no rest day. The Underbridge Market didn't seem to mind getting her rejects, and it meant nothing went to waste.

When she took her final competition-batch of burnt-top bread out of the oven that evening, her arms ached and her back was sore, but it was worth it. After three days of focused effort, she felt confident that her offerings represented the best baking she could currently achieve. She was ready.

Ready or not, it’s happening.

Hefting her laden baskets, she locked up the bakery and set off.
