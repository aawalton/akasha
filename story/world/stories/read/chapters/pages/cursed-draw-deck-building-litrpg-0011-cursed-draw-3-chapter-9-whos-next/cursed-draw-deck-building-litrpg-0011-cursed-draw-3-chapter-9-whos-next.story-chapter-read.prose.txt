Alley raised an eyebrow and looked to the tournament official, who offered him a helpless shrug in return. The man was an auburn-haired fellow who was a few years older than Alley and quite skinny, especially for a Frostlander.

Raising one of the voice amplification devices to his lips, he called out for the fourth time.

“Trine Blackwinter, your match is beginning. Trine Blackwinter, please make your way to the arena floor.” The man's voice bellowed out across the arena in Frostlander and then in Southern. He waited a few more minutes and then shrugged at Alley once more.

“Looks like you win.”

Alley didn't quite know what to say or think. He was happy to make it through the round without having to spend any Vials or risk injury, but he couldn't help but feel disappointed.

Alley was confident that anyone who had made it this far into the tournament had to be a genuine threat to winning the entire thing. The potential to face that level of opposition in an environment where death was extremely unlikely, even in a loss, was more than a little exciting. So the fact that his opponent just hadn't shown up at all was a major letdown.

With a little sigh just to himself, Alley headed over to a long table with a trio of tournament officials sitting behind it at the side of the arena and confirmed that he had indeed won and qualified for the next round of the Frostwing Trials.

“Well, I guess I’ll go watch some of the other matches.” It was a concept that normally excited Alley, but he couldn’t help feeling listless and dejected about the idea of watching other people play when he didn’t get to. Alley’s heart had sped up, and his hands had gotten clammy with an anxious kind of excitement he was increasingly growing familiar with.

It was similar to the sensation Alley had gotten when his mother had won her third All-Isles Championship and brought home a Deck for him. The mayor of Valeton had insisted Alley do a demonstration with his newly acquired Aegis of The Fisher Lord. It made him shockingly scared, like he would somehow mess up Manifesting his Cards or do something else ridiculous.

Back then, it had been the scariest thing Alley had ever experienced; now though, it was a sensation that filled him with energy and made him feel like his reflexes were at their absolute sharpest. After being in a state like that for almost half an hour with no payoff in the form of a Challenge Alley was tired and unmotivated.

Gathering his shreds of motivation along with his backpack that he'd placed with a dozen or so others next to the officiating table, Alley fished out his journal and moved into the stands in search of a seat. He might not feel like doing it, but there was still a lot to be gained from taking notes while he watched the other competitors.

Two and a half hours later, Alley followed the flow of the crowd out of the arena. It had taken about twenty minutes, but Alley had found his enthusiasm returning as he watched the Challenges take place and bore witness to all manner of unusual strategies and wondrous monsters. Taking notes could never be as fun as playing in a match himself, but as a student of the game, he couldn't remain dejected in the face of so many Decks on display.

The crowds dispersed in all different directions to presumably avail themselves of the various amusements and food options that were covering Skalean city. Alley, on the other hand, still wasn’t quite used to navigating a city, so he opted to wait around outside the arena until most of the people were gone and he could try and get his bearings. He had been in Skalean for weeks at this point, so he didn’t get lost, but making it back to the inn he and his friends were staying at meant following a very specific set of landmarks and not deviating from the path he had already gotten used to at all.

“I miss having a ship,” Alley muttered to himself as he checked and rechecked the streets he walked down. Using his little skiff, Alley had been able to navigate using the stars quite effectively. Here in this city with its population of around a hundred thousand and lights to match, Alley couldn’t even see the stars at night.

Of course, it wouldn’t have helped now, as the sun was only just setting as he arrived at the inn. Alley wasn’t sure what the name of the establishment he had woken up in after finally getting rid of the small zombie horde, as he couldn’t read Frostlander, but the sign featured a painting of a pair of burly men arm wrestling with their right hands, while drinking with their left.

“You guys win?” He called over to Darius and Kyera, who were sitting in a semi-circular booth table against the far wall. The princes Kaj and Malchik were seated with them, while a pair of heavily armed, armored, and absolutely giant Frostlanders stood guard in front of the table. One of the guards glanced at Malchik, who nodded, so they stood aside and let Alley sit down.

“It was kind of a pain in the ass, but yeah, I won.” Replied Darius as the foursome scooched over so Alley could squeeze into the booth as well.

“Oh yeah?” Alley asked as he reached out to pour himself a drink in a clay cup from the communal pitcher of water, and nodded a greeting to the pair of Frostlanders who could only barely understand him.

“Yeah, the guy’s Deck was all Cards like my Harpoon Rain, he spent the whole time running around and throwing copies of his knives at me.” Alley thought he might have liked to have seen that.

“So what did you do?”

“I forced him to jump, then threw my spear at him while he was still mid-air.” Jumping was always tricky like that, unless you had some sort of ability to maneuver in the air like Soren Creed, it meant you were unable to change direction until you landed. While Alley occasionally pulled off some acrobatics in the midst of a Challenge, he tried to avoid jumping in a fight unless he felt there was no possibility of a follow-up to whatever he was avoiding or there simply was no other choice.

“Kyera?” Alley asked.

“I won, too.” She replied before tearing into a cooked leg of some sort of game bird. “The guy I was against focused on playing enchantments on a giant crocodile thing, but he couldn’t get around my Bell of the Burrows.” Alley frowned for a second as he searched his memory for the Card she was talking about.

“Oh, right, the Relic, I know I tease your Cards a lot, but that thing is nasty.”

“Your brother had a match too, right?” Alley went on asking Kaj. The younger prince took a few seconds to reply, and his accent was thick, but Alley was still impressed that such a young boy was even able to speak a second language at all.

“Malchik won pretty easily, and then we went to interview the illusionist and some of the other survivors from the attack the other day.”

Darius barked a laugh and said something to Malchik that only half the table understood but still drew a laugh from the older prince.

“Huh?” Asked Alley, glancing at his hunter friend.

“Malchik didn’t ‘win pretty easy.’” Darius said, still amused. “ He fucking crushed his opponent. I saw the match, and it probably lasted ten seconds tops.” The red-headed man’s eyes narrowed, and his tone became a lot more serious.

“If our princely friend here doesn’t make it all the way to finals, I’ll eat my spear.”

“You think he can win the finals, too?” Asked Kaj, his youthful face full of excitement at the prospect.

“Nah,” Replied Darius with a wink. “'Cause I’ll be waiting for him there.”

The young prince shook his head and made a dismissive noise.

“He already beat you once, so I know he can do it again.”

Darius laughed and reached out to ruffle the younger prince’s hair.

“I guess we will have to wait and see, but right now I’m more interested in why Alley hasn’t mentioned his own match. You didn’t lose, did ya?”

The table turned their eyes on the latest arrival, who frowned and offered a half-shrug.

“I won, but it was weird. My opponent was supposed to be a woman called Trine Blackwinter, but she just never showed up.”

“Trine?” Asked Malchik with a shocked expression before launching into a rapidfire jumble of Frostlander that Darius gave a rough translation of.

“Huh? That is weird. He says that Trine Blackwinter is the name of a noblewoman from up real far north, her family is famous for invading their neighbors…like a lot.”

“I don’t speak Frostlander.” Said Alley with a raised eyebrow. “ But I'm pretty sure whatever he said didn’t involve ‘like’ as a description.” Darius waved him off.

“He said it all formal and princey, but that's the gist of it.”

“Maybe something important for a noble happened, and she had to pull out.” Mused Kyera. “ But for us this is what matters.” As she spoke, the Framer pulled out a parchment from her messenger bag that she had sitting against her hip. Unfolding it on the table to reveal the next day’s matchups.

As the number of competitors dwindled, it was slowly becoming possible to predict who the group was going to play against based on their names.

“Jackson Matsui?” Said Alley as he spotted Kyera’s next foe first. It was easily the weirdest name he had ever heard, but for some reason, it filled him with dread.
