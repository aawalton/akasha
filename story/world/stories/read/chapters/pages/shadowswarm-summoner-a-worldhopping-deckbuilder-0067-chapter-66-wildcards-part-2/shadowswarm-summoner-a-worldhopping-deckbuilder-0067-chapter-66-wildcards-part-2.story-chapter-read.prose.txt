Chapter 66: Wildcards, Part 2

Jessica had never felt so low, not even the day they all went on the run.

That had been terrifying, but also exhilarating in its own way. She’d finally broken away from her grandfather’s control and no longer needed to worry about disappointing him. Her life was over at that point, but she had been, even if only for three days, free.

Now the Bedrock Spire security forces marched her and Raeleq into lockup, high up near the top of the spire. The spire itself belonged to Boss Parl, according to him, and included a dungeon for “the hoity, toity, self-important prisoners”.

She hadn’t said anything then. Neither had Raeleq, but that wasn’t unusual. Jessica mostly spent the walk up crying silently, cursing herself for her weakness, her inability to do more. And Ambrose and the others…

Jessica wanted to scream, fight, maybe lay down and die. The last one clung to her, with the unfairness that she’d live to have to face her grandfather after all, and Raeleq…Poor Raeleq would have to live, knowing they killed his sister and not him.

The only thing to break through the pain and despair was the anger. Dexi’Lynn had betrayed them. Betrayed her. They were supposed to be friends, fellow airship enthusiasts, though she had to admit that her interest was more by necessity than passion. They smoked together, and she was going to show Dexi the Wanderer.

Now, she had one thing on her mind, one promise to herself: before her grandfather came, she was going to kill that treacherous kobold. With her bare hands, preferably. Jessica imagined squeezing the life out of her, and then a flying kick into Boss Parl to knock him into the lava too.

Ambrose may have been an asshole, but he was her asshole, dammit. Her best friend, somehow. He’d want her to get revenge and rub it in the stupid mole’s face when she did.

“Alright kiddos, here we are,” Boss Parl’s infuriating drawl made her look up.

The spire loomed, made of a glossy black material it took her too long to realize was Obsidian. Sturdy metal doors were set into the spire, and it took four guards a few seconds of grunting to open them. Parl motioned, and the guard behind Jessica shoved the back of her head, right as another punched Raeleq in the kidney. It was petty, unnecessary, and a good motivation to do as they said.

The moment they stepped inside, it was like something cold and slimy settled around her mind. Her access to her combat cards was cut off. She could still [Leap] if she wanted or needed to, but it wouldn’t do her much good. It was…

Well, as far as prison cells went, it was actually pretty cozy. It looked like two one-room apartments behind bars. There were books and the chairs looked comfortable enough that Jessica longed to collapse in one. She supposed she would be able to before long.

“This is our prison cell?” she asked, surprised. She and Raeleq stepped inside.

“What, this ain’t good enough for you, Princess?” Boss Parl let out another awful belly laugh.

This made Raeleq look at him, agony in his eyes. “She’s not a princess. My sister is though. You sent her to her death.”

The molekin took up the entire doorway and then some. He pointed a sharp claw at Raeleq and favored him with a nasty grin. “The only reason you aren’t takin’ the same lava dip is because the damned kobold claimed you were necessary to get that wildcard. I’m willin’ to humor that for a little while…But don’t test my patience, boy.”

In front of him, Dexi’Lynn laughed nervously. “He is necessary. You can trust me, Boss! All I want is to make up for my failure and capture, you know?”

“Hrm. Is that so? Well, we’ll see about that. Until then, why don’t you get comfy too?” He grabbed her and flung her into the other cell. She hit a chair hard enough to break it and rolled along the ground.

Jessica almost smiled.

The guards locked the cells and left them alone. The second the doors closed and heavy, loud locking systems fell into place, a buzz went through the cells, making Jessica’s teeth itch. She knew from experience it was magical surveillance. They were being watched, but not listened to. Probably.

Dexi’Lynn groaned and picked herself up off the floor. She reached into her inside pocket and pulled out her pipe, miraculously in one piece. “Oh thank the dark one. I would’ve cried if I couldn’t have a smoke. Are you two okay? You’re welcome, by the way.”

Raeleq stared at her, jaw slack. Jessica joined him in open, scornful shock. There was audacity, and then there was whatever this was. “What the hell is wrong with you?”

“What’s wrong with me? What’s wrong with you? I just saved your lives, and this is how you treat me? Shameful.” The kobold lit up and blew smoke across the room. “I’m sorry about your friend though.”

“Friends,” Raeleq corrected, heat entering his voice. “You condemned my sister to death. If I ever get my hands on you…”

“Ooh, promise?” Dexi made a rudely enthusiastic sound. “Look, he was going to kill us all. I managed to save some of us. And your buddy Mulch might save the others. He’s got a chance though, from what I overheard of the plan. So, I got you here, and Mulch might get them out. It’s the best I could do. And…I’m sorry it wasn’t more.”

Jessica swallowed hard. Her burning, irrational anger told her to scream at the kobold and blame her for everything. Everything was so crushing, so final, and she just wanted relief of some kind. Even the temporary joy of giving in to her rage and pain and emptying it all out in a flurry of curses and threats.

Instead, she sank to the ground, shaking with fear and grief.

“Is this true?” Raeleq asked gently. “Or is this another lie? Something comforting so we don’t kill you the first chance we get.”

Dexi’Lynn shrugged. “I wouldn’t put it past me, but no. I mean it. I kinda like your group so far. But more importantly than that, I picked you, big guy, for a reason. We’re going to break out of here, and you’re a strapping young lad, ain’tcha? My brain, your brawn, and Jessie’s secret ingredient, maybe we can get out of here.”

The back of Jessica’s neck prickled. “What secret ingredient? What are you talking about?”

The kobold puffed away at her pipe. “You going to pretend you aren’t still carrying it? I swear, you’re lucky I like you, with the way you idiots talked in front of me. Think about it. If I knew, then Jack knew too. What do you think that means?”

Oh. Oh no. “They’re going to be coming for it soon,” Jessica realized.

“Ayup. It’s time, babygirl. Whip it out and equip it. Let’s see what happens.” Dexi gripped the bars of her cell.

“What’s she talking about?” Raeleq asked, before he remembered. “Oh. The Wi--”

Jessica jumped to clamp her hand over his mouth for just a second. He got the message and nodded. “So equip it!”

“No!” It was wrong. She didn’t know how to explain it without sounding pathetic. Jessica peeked out between the bars, at the only way out of there. “Raeleq? You take it.”

“What? No, it’s yours. Ambrose said so.” He swallowed hard.

“And Ambrose is dead,” she said, surprising herself with the coldness in her voice. “I am telling you to take it. It’s wasted on me.”

“I’ll take it if you two are gonna be stupid about it,” Dexi’Lynn called helpfully.

Jessica gave in to her need and collapsed in the comfy rocking chair. It was better than it looked. She anxiously rocked back and forth, fighting the impending attack.

“Do you think so?” Raeleq asked, ignoring the kobold. “What do you think I could do with it? Think it’ll give me a big and strong enough creature to bust us out? You’re smarter than me, and it’s supposed to give you what you need. Who else would make better use of it? Take it, now.”

Jessica couldn’t lie, there was always the temptation at getting something good, something powerful. Something that would show everyone that she wasn’t just the mediocre legacy student with stunted mana. The Wildcard would do that. And if by accepting it, she wasted it…Well, better she ruin it for anyone else than let the enemy have the satisfaction of taking it from her.

Ambrose would approve of that, at least.

The doors opened slowly, grinding against the ground as Boss Parl and his entourage came back in, this time with Jack.

“Well, would you look at that? It turns out you were lying to me and had the card the entire time. But not for long. Hand it over.”

Raeleq stood in front of the cell door, standing tall, despite experiencing just how much stronger the mole was than him. “I have it. I already equipped it.” He grinned, putting his hands on the bars.

This was it, do or die. Jessica stood up and pretended to stretch, reaching for a secret leather sleeve she kept the Wildcard in. It had remained protected and secret through the firestorm in the spider cave and the brawling earlier. Her hands shook as she pulled it out.

“Beetleshit! I know the girl has it. Boys, open it up. Girlie, you either give it up or we’ll kill him.”

The guards rushed to do as he said, while Raeleq shouted, “I don’t care, equip it now! Please, Jessica, do it!”

She ejected the useless [Mist Elemental] card she’d once been so proud to have. It seemed good at the time, being able to disorient and confuse her classmates. Now, in the real world, it was almost quaint.

“I mean it, girl. I’ll bite yer damned head off!”

What would Ambrose do?

Jessica flipped off Boss Parl, and equipped the Wildcard.

Leveling an affinity was always a rush, almost pure euphoria for the long, lingering handful of seconds it lasted. Information flooded her brain, too much for her to be able to keep track. Jessica was in a blizzard, buffeted from all sides by sharp, shocking bites she soon realized were cards. Cards of all affinities, swirling around her. They blew by, moving in chaotic patterns that somehow made sense to her.

She reached out and directed the storm, looking for the one card that didn’t fit, the one for her. Each shift in the wind made them all move at the same time. All but one.

Jessica centered herself and breathed. Distantly, she knew this wasn’t real, it was all in her head. But it was important. She paid more attention this time, and waited, biding her time.

There! She struck with a quick snatch and pulled up a blank card with shifting colors. It seemed to taunt her, asking her one last time if she was sure.

She was. For once in her life, she was ready. She had to be.

Lines formed on the card, filling in the blanks at random until three words formed.

[Give and Take]

And then pain struck her, rising like a musical scale until it became deafening, blinding, all consuming. Before she could do more than gasp, darkness took her.
