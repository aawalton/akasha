As they slipped out of the prison, Obie noticed and excused himself. They hurriedly caught up with Charlie and the others once they were out of sight of the guards. “Hey!” he said excitedly. “The guards told me something interesting, Charlie! Do you remember when I told you how strong our leader was? That Brandt guy? Well, turns out he’s coming here! You’ll get to meet him.”

His friends stopped in their tracks. “What?” Arkady asked.

Obie nodded excitedly. “My friends just told me during our game. I won, by the way, all sixteen times. I’m glad you showed up when you did, because I think they were starting to get the hang of it. But don’t worry, I’ve never lost a single match of expert level —”

“Brandt’s coming here?” Merlin asked, cutting Obie off.

Obie nodded. “Yes! The day after the hex storm. Apparently, Gregory sent word for reinforcements, but they were still finishing up that big meeting and are too far away. They’ll get here, but the storm will probably have been dealt with by then!”

“What do we do now?” Merlin asked, a desperate look on his face. After telling Obie they needed to get home and get some rest, they’d returned to their home base.

Arkady was silent for a moment. The longer that silence stretched, the more Charlie dreaded what the red-haired man was about to say. “If Brandt’s really coming…we need to leave. We can’t wait until the storm hits.”

Merlin looked surprised, but Vetica was quiet. She’d come to the same conclusion.

Charlie shook his head. “No! We can’t leave without her.”

Vetica and Arkady exchanged a gentle look. “We don’t even know where she is, or if she’s even still here, Charlie. There’s just no time left…we only have one day before the day of the storm, and there’s a possibility Brandt arrives early.”

Charlie shook his head. “Then all of you can leave! I’ll find her. I’ll find her myself!” He shook his head. “I promised Richard.” He fought back against the tears forming on his face, but it was a losing battle.

Merlin wiped the corner of his own eye. He took a moment, and then composed himself. “Alright, listen up, you bunch of scallywags!” he said, getting everyone’s attention.

Vetica and Arkady both looked up at him in confusion.

Charlie looked at him with hope.

“We have a full day before the day of the storm. The plan was always to break Mary out by then. Sure, we’re getting low on time, but when has this team ever succumbed to something like that? Richard made us promise not to risk Charlie in this, but that doesn’t mean we can’t use every single moment left to save her. One day. Twenty-four hours. Let’s make every moment account. If we can find her, we can still save her during the storm.” Merlin said. “How’s that sound, Charlie?”

Charlie thought for a moment. He didn’t want to leave even then, even if the storm came and passed; he didn’t want to leave without Mary. But he knew that would be risking the lives of his friends. “Alright, we’ll make every moment count. And we won’t have to worry about leaving without Mary, because I know we’ll find her.”

Smiles lit up around the room, and then, they pulled out the map of the fort. It was time to plan.

Though Charlie wished they could go straight back out, they were all exhausted now. So they slept and woke up early, ready to spend the day searching. The next morning, they hit the streets hard. There was plenty of time to look around and they weren’t the only civilians moving through the streets today. It almost seemed something was going on, but Merlin and Charlie, who’d opted to stay together for the search, didn’t waste time asking. Arkady was checking out the other potential sights he’d pointed out on the map. They might not be keeping Mary in the main prison, but she could easily be held somewhere else. One of the houses under lock and key, or in one of the other stone encampments. Some knights slept within the fort’s walls, in the stone barracks. It was possible they’d left Mary in her own room in one of those as well. So Arkady focused his efforts on those two points, and planned to sweep the other encampments if those proved fruitless. Vetica’s hex, and physical power allowed her to check out places the rest of them could not. She took to the rooftops, breaking into homes through windows or back doors whenever she needed to. If anyone could navigate those places without being caught, it was her.

Merlin and Charlie would go around looking in plain sight. There might be something they’d missed. But after walking around the civilian areas of the fort for most of the day. They’d finally came to the conclusion that nothing was sticking out. So, they agreed to take a little more risk. Merlin went to work, being the master of words he was.

“You haven’t seen any kidnapped women around, have you, miss?”

“Excuse me, good sir, I was wondering if you’d spotted a woman being held against her will? No. Alright, sorry to bother you.”

“Ma’am! Ma’am! We’ve been trying to reach you about your car’s extended warranty!” Merlin looked at Orb in his pocket after he’d said it. “I really don’t get the joke, and I regret taking part in it.”

Unfortunately, there weren’t any leads, and Merlin was already get stared at by a few people. Luckily, he could always blame it on the head injury he had coming into town. A fact that, once he realized it, left him with very little concern about looking crazy. Of course, their blind questioning led them nowhere.

It wasn’t long before day turned to night, and a sense of dread was starting to fill them. They were running out of time. They’d searched all day and still they’d found nothing.

“Charlos?” someone called out.

Merlin and Charlie turned to look at the boy waving at him. It was Tomlin. Aveo was behind him with his arms crossed, shaking his head with a smile as the boy ran up to Charlie in Merlin’s arms.

Aveo walked up. “Taking the lad for a walk? You must be Merle? Arkady mentioned you at dinner earlier.”

Merlin raised a brow. “Oh, did he now? Yes, I’m Merle. Just out for a walk. What are the two of you doing out here in the dark?”

Tomlin was uncomfortably close, leaning on his tiptoes to whisper something in Charlie’s ear. He wasn’t tall enough to whisper quietly enough that Merlin couldn’t hear it. So he caught most of the message.

“…you should come to the performance!” he’d said.

Merlin looked up at Aveo. “Is something going on? What performance?” he asked.

Aveo laughed. “Come and see. This isn’t something you’ll want to miss,” he said, clapping Merlin on the back with the force of someone who doesn’t know how to contain his own strength.

Merlin looked at Charlie. “Well? What do you think?”

“I don’t know guys, this sounds like a total waste of time,” Orb said.

Charlie thought about it. “If it’s a performance, maybe a lot of people will be there. We can ask around or see if we spot anything!” he said. Deep down, he hoped Vetica or Arkady had more success with their own searches. Maybe they’d find someone who knew where to look, or maybe it would help them approach the problem with a fresh perspective. Regardless, something drew him to want to go to the performance, and it was the closest thing he’d felt to a lead all night. “Let’s go,” he said to Merlin. Without hesitation, Merlin marched off after Aveo and Tomlin followed, letting out an enthusiastic whoop.

As late as it was, a large crowd had gathered in the streets directly outside the tower in the center of the fort. The tower loomed over them. Two enormous bonfires roared, one on either side of the crowd, and plenty of additional torches lit the gathering so it was easy to see wherever you went. The sound of laughter and people talking filled the air. There were pleasant smelling foods being passed around, and the faintest hint of smoke from all the fires. The temperature was perfect, and the moon above shone brightly on them all. For a moment, Charlie felt totally at peace. He allowed himself just a moment.

Merlin looked around, astonished. “It looks like the whole fort is here…is…is this everyone?” he asked. He wasn’t exaggerating. There were easily hundreds of people here. Charlie spotted hex knights still patrolling the walls and the gate, but there were some he recognized in the crowd as well. Some wore their armor, others were dressed in more casual ways. Normally, having so many knights around would be stressful, but seeing the expressions on their faces put Charlie’s mind at ease. It seemed like everyone was just here to have a good time.

Aveo nodded. “Aye, just about. This started not long after we finished building the fort, a group of musicians starting getting together to play, and a crowd gathered. Word spread, and more and more people came and eventually Gregory made it an official thing. We do it every week, usually a couple days from now, but on account of the hex storm we’re doing it early. We’ll do it again after the storm passes to celebrate. It reminds them,” he said, pointing at the hex knights with his last words, “…what they’re fighting to protect.” Then he gestured around them. “And for the rest of us, it’s a chance to build our community. To ease our minds from the worries of the storm to come. Everyone in this fort works hard all week. We all have our ways of relaxing, but this, this is a place for everyone to unwind together.”

As he finished speaking, as if on cue, an emerald elemental hex appeared in the air before the crowd, and a stage made of rock shot from the ground. It was an earth elemental hex then. Musicians shot out from the door at the bottom of the tower with instruments in tow and took to the stage.

“Who's ready for some music?” A man asked with a thick accent and a thicker mustache.

The crowd went wild. The men wasted no time. They strummed guitars, blew their flutes, beat drums they held in their hands, and struck their violins. They were an odd assortment, and Charlie never would’ve imagined any of them were musicians if he hadn’t seen this for himself. But when he heard their music, when he saw the crowd swaying and people dancing, and the bobbing of heads, Charlie thought he’d never seen such a merry occasion.

“Merlin, I think the world needs more stuff like this,” he said, his eyes full of wonder.

Merlin looked over at Aveo, who was nodding his head, and Tomlin, who spun wildly in circles. “Charlie, I think you’re right.” He smiled. Charlie felt a tap on his shoulder and turned to see Arkady. “Hey kid! Merlin!”

Merlin turned to look at him. “Oh, I wasn’t expecting you here,” he said.

Arkady nodded over his shoulder. There was a pretty woman standing behind him in a nice dress that hung down just past her knees and a thick coat over her shoulders. Her hand was on Arkady’s.

“I uh…got caught snooping around, and apparently coming to this was my punishment,” Arkady said. From the way he said it, the knights hadn’t caught him. Apparently, this woman had been the one to find him. Charlie thought it was weird that this was a punishment, though. Arkady seemed happy!

Orb sighed. “Ah, young love, would that I could meet a pretty dungeon core of my own, but alas…fate is cruel.”

Merlin smiled. “I see. This must be the Orielle I’ve heard so much about.”

Orielle perked up. “Oh, Arkady’s been talking about me, has he? Anything I’d like to know?” she asked.

“Actually, I—” Merlin was interrupted rather suddenly by Arkady’s hand on his mouth.

“Let’s go enjoy the music, maybe a few feet in any direction but here?” Arkady asked playfully. They didn’t actually wander far. Orielle hugged Tomlin, and then, to everyone’s surprise, including Arkady’s, she took his hand and started to dance to the music. Arkady laughed. He laughed and smiled and danced, and Charlie was so happy to see him like that. He wondered what it was he was seeing, that spark in Arkady’s eyes. Charlie wondered what you called that.

A thought occurred to him, and he reached out with his mind so only one person could hear. “Vetica? Can you hear me? If so, come to the tower whenever you can.”

It wasn’t long before she arrived. She wore a serious look on her face. She saw him and let out a breath, hurrying towards them through the crowd, slipping nimbly past people dancing and humming along to the music. “I thought something might be wrong. What is this? I heard the music and planned to come see what was going on, but I didn’t expect all of you to be here.”

“That’s why I called you. We have to keep looking, but…I wanted to share this with you, too. I wanted to you to have a pleasant moment to, Vetica. Because you’re special to me, all of you are special to me.”

Arkady looked up, surprised he’d been included in that. Orielle noticed he stopped dancing and grabbed both of his hands, still facing him and dancing herself. “Come on, keep up, redhead!” She grinned.

Arkady laughed and nodded one last time at Charlie. “You too, bud,” he mouthed.

Merlin took Charlie out of the baby carrier and lifted him up onto his shoulders like old times. Charlie could see even better now, and swayed his head to the music, enjoying it. He noticed Obie in the crowd, standing next to a woman with a baby. He was leaning in really close, asking it questions. When the baby only giggled in response, Obie scratched his head. He looked around and noticed Charlie before hurrying over. “No progress yet. I still can only talk to you, Charlie.”

Thespin showed up and joined them as well, standing next to Aveo, but occasionally stealing glances at Vetica from a distance.

Obie started dancing next to Tomlin. He showed Tomlin the dance move Orb had taught him. He pointed a finger to his left, just past his waist, and then lifted it high into the air on his right and swayed at the same time. It didn’t fit the music, but the two boys loved it. Thespin made small talk with Aveo and eventually asked if anyone wanted something to drink. Only Aveo did, and Thespin left and quickly returned with two mugs. Charlie assumed it was the drink Merlin said was only for grownups. Then, everyone’s attention turned upward, toward the top of the tower. Tomlin clapped his hands excitedly. Obie cupped his hands on either side of his face and cheered.

Aveo nudged Merlin. “Open those ears of yours, lad. This is the best part,” he said. “The songbird.”

The top of the tower, now that Charlie looked closely, had a little balcony at it. From where they were, it was easy to see the figure stride out. The person’s face was covered, and they wore a long, flowing white garment. Charlie wondered what was going on, and he started to ask Obie about it when he heard it.

Singing.

Charlie could already imagine what the woman looked like from her voice alone. She had hair that was the color of pleasant orange autumn leaves, and her smile was as white as fresh falling snow. She was the kind of person who looked like she gave great hugs. Mary had always been the best singer he’d ever heard. And here and now, he realized with a glad heart; she was within his reach. Mary had never been in the prison, she’d been in the tower. They’d be able to save her.

“That’s her…” Charlie told his friends. Vetica’s mouth fell open. Merlin laughed heartily. Arkady looked over in surprise, and then he turned, surprised once again to look at Orielle. She’d wrapped her arm around his and laid her head on his shoulder. He smiled, and he looked back at up at Mary. The songbird. Orb rolled in Merlin’s pocket.

“I freaking told you we should come to this! I told you! I was right, admit it!” Orb said.

And then, as peace settled in Charlie’s heart, he remembered the words of the Reaper. “I’m here because I like the music.” He looked down at the ground below and realized that the prison cell they’d visited earlier must be close. Charlie smiled. It hadn’t been a random comment, it had been a hint. He’d given Charlie a hint about how to find Mary.

Charlie looked up, and spoke only to himself, so as not to distract Mary and ruin her song.

“I’m coming for you, Mary. I’ll get you out of here.”

And with her so close in sight, he finally felt like he could relax and truly enjoy the moment. Tomorrow there would be fighting. He’d have to use his powers to rescue her and to help them escape. But tonight, Charlie’s memories took him back to that little home in a small town called Troa. Where Mary would sing and rock him asleep at night. And though they weren’t there back then, he imagined all of his friends in that house. Merlin, Vetica, Mousifer, Bleedy, Orb. Everyone. Even Obie, Tomlin, Arkady, and Orielle, for Arkady’s sake. Everyone of them. Charlie imagined all of them together, and he listened to the music, and Mary’s song, and he closed his eyes and swayed. He was happy. He was so very happy.
