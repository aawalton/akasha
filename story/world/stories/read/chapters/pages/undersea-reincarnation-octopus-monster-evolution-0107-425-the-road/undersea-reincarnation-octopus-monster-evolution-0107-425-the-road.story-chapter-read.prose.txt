When the skill spat him out again, Henry immediately felt that he had stepped somewhere he shouldn’t. The air around him was foreboding. Stifling. It wasn’t hot or anything, but it was as if some great weight was bearing down on him and on his lungs. Not to mention, what his eyes, skin, and senses were telling him made no sense. Henry felt solid ground under his arms, but the feedback he was getting from it kept shifting. One moment, it felt like asphalt, hard and coarse, and in his sight, an incredibly wide and long road stretched to the horizon, like a highway in a no-man’s-land, only lit by the twinkle of stars. The next moment, it turned into a mighty branch of a gigantic tree that made the trees of Thalis look like twigs. The next moment, it was water, a current of it, like a deep river, and it just kept switching from one perspective to the next, from a road, to the carpeted corridor of a hotel, to a cave tunnel, blasting him with all sorts of sensations that he was struggling to parse, even with the help of his Octominds, making him feel like he was having a fever dream, until he felt a firm hand on his back.

“Pick one, Henry, don’t stare at all of them.”

Henry whirled, already swinging and barely stopped his limb in time to find Velistraine, her eyes bloodshot and her face full of grime. She was almost unrecognizable, but he didn’t dwell on that just yet. The world surrounding him was still maddening, but the interruption helped him take a step back and slowly, as he took Velistraine’s appearance in, he settled on one of the perspectives he was being hammered with. At first, it didn’t do much but progressively, the rate at which the perspectives were shifting slowed, until, for Henry, it settled on a stone-paved path. As for Velistraine… well, he was glad he had come here.

She really wasn’t doing so well.

Gone were the hat and dashing red vest. Her hair was burnt and cut, and what hadn’t been lost was matted to her scalp. Her gear was torn and reduced to rags, to the point that by now, she was barefoot, with pants that had become uneven shorts, and a surprisingly clean shirt that he was pretty sure she’d just put on. She must have caught his eye, because the next moment, she went to brush the shirt, then froze, looking at her grimy hand before bailing out, a grimace marring her harried features.

That was something else. Velistraine was missing her left arm at the elbow. The cut was clean, and from the look of it, it was already healed over. Henry wondered if she’d lost it fighting, or if she’d cut it herself. She had her bone sword in hand, and he worriedly noted that it was chipped in multiple spots.

“Had to put something on, you know,” she said, gesturing to herself with a distracted smile. “I was alone out here, but if I have company then I should put something on and all that. Anyway—”

Without warning, she lunged at him and wrapped him in a big, one-armed hug, tapping him with her right hand a couple of times as if trying to make sure he was real, and that only lasted a couple seconds before she shoved him and pressed her lips together. “I told you not to come, you dummy. It’s way too—”

Velistraine suddenly stopped talking, tilting her head to the side before grabbing him and dragging him away from the road, pulling him right over the edge where they landed on a makeshift scaffolding, and right then and there, Henry nearly lost his lunch.

Below him, the dark, star-glittering emptiness of space stretched infinitely, making all of his arms and muscles clench involuntarily. Actually, the space stretched infinitely in every direction. No matter where he looked, Henry saw… emptiness. There was a sun some distance away, but it could be millions of miles away or maybe billions. Henry was no astronomer. He could just tell that it was far, considering he wasn’t sensing that much of its heat on his skin.

But how was he breathing?

Concerning, but a question for later, he told himself while quietly, he kept still and followed Velistraine’s gaze. She was looking upwards, and it took Henry a moment to see what she was staring at as slowly, a shape detached itself from the sky. For a moment, Henry thought his eyes were playing a trick on him, but as the seconds went by, the dark, gray object continued its descent, and as it approached, more and more details became visible and for a while, Henry wasn’t even sure what he was looking at until he saw the chitinous legs unfold, only moments before it disappeared above them to land.

That had been a ship.

Henry was sorely tempted to send a clone up and observe what would come out, but considering the wariness Velistraine was displaying, he just cloaked himself and kept still while reviewing what he had just seen. It looked… well, the thing almost looked organic. Like some sort of dark, large pillbug with legs that could fly in space. Its carapace was somewhat reflective, making it difficult to make out from a distance, and from the angle it had taken, it must have landed some distance down the ‘road’.

Henry wanted to ask what that ship was, where they were, what happened to Velistraine, but he didn’t dare cast any skill, just in case that triggered something. And so… he let a portion of his attention shift to his notifications. The interface struggled a bit to show up and when it did, there was something a little off about it. It was as if it was everything was a little grayed out. But the items he’d received before teleporting to space still showed up just fine, and after those spicy encounters in the cave, he’d gotten a couple, mostly from having managed to get a couple bites here and there, but one in particular caught his attention.

Unfettered Echoes of the Trickster (B): Level 9 → Level 10

This is it, Henry thought while still keeping a wary eye on his surroundings. Now, all three of his Aspects were at level 10, and he was an A-rank himself. There was nothing stopping him from pushing them forward to the next level. He even had the partial core from Gar’Dak, as well as a nice stockpile of cores to at least push one over the edge. Which would obviously be Unfettered Echoes, as already, Henry was making the worrying discovery that he could sense none of the Avatars he’d left behind on… water-world?

Actually, what’s the name of that place? Henry wondered. Seavaria was the name of the second planet, but what was the name of the world he had just teleported out of?

Henry had no clue, now that he thought of it. But he also couldn’t feel his connection to that place, though it might be too soon to worry. Then again, that might be why Velistraine had told him not to come here.

Velistraine tightened her grip on his arm, and Henry went still, but then she relaxed, and he continued waiting, his thoughts whirling as a couple of realizations quickly crystallized in the back of his head. In a fashion, they were a mix of good news and bad news. The bad news was that maybe, maybe, he was now also subjected to the System’s defense around the world, which might make his return a little more challenging. Henry would likely need to level a bunch, enough that he’d slip under the scrutiny of the System—or trick his way through like the turtle did. He needed raw power. And well, it seemed like he was now sharing space with invasive, insectile creatures, which would be the good news.

Henry had plenty of targets for some good old indiscriminate extermination. Of course, he’d need to be cautious, as whatever was up there seemed to have given Velistraine a challenge, but now that he was cut off from most of his Avatars, he could tell that his capacity had freed up. He could summon hundreds of Avatars and soon, thousands once Unfettered Echoes reached A-rank. He could drown the creatures in his army, and unlike Velistraine, Henry was a dedicated self-healer.

Frankly, the more he thought of this… timeout, the more he found himself salivating at the opportunity, and it must have leaked somewhat through his aura, because Velistraine elbowed him hard and gave him a brief shake of her head, so Henry calmed down and just meditated. Both to keep his greed in check and to recover his mana from the fight and teleportation, and around ten minutes later, Velistraine finally relaxed, and she turned to him, giving him one more tiny hug and smiling at him, teary-eyed.

The two sat down on the scaffolding, hanging off the side of the impossible ‘road’, ready to talk, but before they got started, Henry summoned Maurice out of his Maw, while simultaneously, he wrapped a hand around Velistraine’s severed arm to feel out the extent of the damage.

The crab appeared right next to them, and just in case he was too heavy for the structure, Henry held him aloft, while he kept himself floating with Flight. At first, Maurice visibly cringed, his eye-stalks shaking and flinching at the sights, but quickly enough, he got the perspective trick down, at which point, he raised both claws and catapulted himself at Velistraine.

They were going to have a chat. Soon. But for now, a little celebration was due. Their friend was alive, and they likely had an all-you-can-kill buffet at their disposal. What more could they ask for to prepare for the end of the world?
