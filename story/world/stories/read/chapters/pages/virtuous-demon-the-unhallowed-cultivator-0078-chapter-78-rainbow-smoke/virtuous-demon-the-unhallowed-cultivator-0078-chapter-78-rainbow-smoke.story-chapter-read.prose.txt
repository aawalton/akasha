Haze was also next to the smoking disciple, before facing his head towards the newcomers.

Shuren narrowed his gaze when he recognised the receptionist he once engaged with many months ago.

“Isn’t he on the clock? That senior disciple has some gall to be out here on a smoke break when the day has just started,” Shuren said with snark, half with jest and half with irritation.

The pair both heard bits of the dialogue between the receptionist and Haze as they drew near.

“Alright, I’ll sound around the Kallas Jungles. It’ll be a while though, so tell Old Wan to sort out the ones trying to worm their way through the north,” Haze told the receptionist with a hand on his hip.

“Alright. This is a Rank 3 Battle Missive of the highest difficulty, so prepare well. You’re given access to the Cloud Vault for any gear required for the job.” The receptionist dulled out his pipe, putting it back in his spatial storage.

“Looks like it’ll be a long one, huh.” Haze grumbled, scratching the back of his head.

The receptionist got his back off the wall, caressing the back of his neck. “And the ones who were sent out for the highest grade Rank 1 Grade Missive have returned. Quite the mess from that old man, huh.”

Haze jeered at the receptionist before walking towards Shuren and Ming, shining his pearly white teeth with a wide grin. Until a few metres away where he disappeared from their view.

Before Shuren and Ming could even react, two hands grabbing the outer sides of their shoulders pressed firmly, with Haze’s head huddling between the two. “Both of my juniors have grown so much. I’m glad to see that the recent missive wasn’t too hard on your bodies.”

Shuren couldn’t be bothered forcing his way out of the group hug. “Just a bunch of trash, not even close to Rank 2. Nothing to bother worrying about,” he said, raising a single brow up.

Ming grumbled when he failed in getting out of Haze’s hold.

“Listen, the intel you all got was vital. Forget about that crazy bastard, leave him to the likes of me to deal with. And tell Aerestia for me as well, she did very good. You guys have to be the one to tell her, as fellow comrades.” With a sweet pep talk, Haze let go.

“Don’t underestimate her, Haze. She’s the closest to reaching Rank 2 out of all of us,” Ming replied, rotating his shoulder around.

Shuren nodded in acknowledgement, facing the senior he had known since the first day he entered. Though they didn’t meet much, Haze left a strong impression on Shuren.

He must be a Core Disciple at the very least. In fact, if there was a hidden Grand Disciple of the Seven Clouds, I wouldn’t be surprised if it was him. The Path of Sound, huh. I wonder if I’ll get to see him fight with that one day. I heard from the reports long ago that he helped us in the background when we dealt with that crimson axe demonic cultivator. Something about him sabotaging their tunnel routes to escape.

It was no secret that Haze, famous amongst the Seven Clouds for his eccentric and overly-friendly attitude, was one who pursued the Sound Path.

Haze looked at Shuren for a moment with a wistful smile, then proceeded to rub the top of Shuren’s head vigorously. “Don’t think too hard, junior. Come to me if you ever need help.”

Shuren groaned at the mess of his freshly combed hair, but held his complaints inside. By the time he looked up, Haze had long gone.

“He’s a silly guy, but you can rely on him.” The doors to the Hall of Missive opened as the receptionist said in his bland tone, walking back inside with both hands in the pocket of his robes. Ming looked at Shuren, shrugging both his hands as a reluctant agreement.

They both entered the Hall of Missives together, once again met with the artificially cold room. The formations retained a lower temperature and maintained a dry room due to the crystal board, and strangely, if someone was still sweating while touching the board, it would become unresponsive. It was the Seven Clouds own unique technology, and Shuren had asked around if any other sect had incorporated such techniques, but his own father said he’d never heard of such a thing.

In the far distance of the northern wing, where the Missive Board lay, a white-haired girl with hues of violet stood alone. Shuren’s eyebrows narrowed for a moment, before looking away.

The last thing he needed was to bring her any attention. He just wanted to get those sigils and get back to work.

It was about time for all of the delegates to leave anyway. The Seven Clouds accepted them for one year at a time, in return for the deal they held with the other sects.

“By the way, what exactly were you smoking? Don’t think I’ve ever seen a style like that one.” Out of boredom, Ming asked as he followed the receptionist to his desk.

“It’s a resin extracted from some islands in the Devanthra Archipelagoes. There’s a breed of turtles, the Arborterrin, which shells produce a tree, and every few years this resin seeps out. The locals nicknamed them the Gourd of Seven Paths, because of the seven colours. I used to live there for a while, so I gained a taste for these. Wish I didn’t know, it’s not exactly cheap,” the receptionist explained with a sigh of regret.

“Can I have a smo-”

“No.”

Ming grumbled, he was even willing to pay in return, but was cut off abruptly that he didn’t care anymore.

When the receptionist finally made it to his desk, he sank down on his chair for a moment, sighing before sliding his chair forward. “Now, Elder Spiritweaver was impressed with the intel you all gave. Though the original goal failed, you all receive 3000 sigils.” Beckoning his hand over to the pair’s cloudmulets, the receptionist received it to add on the rewards.

Both Ming and Shuren’s eyes lit up when the vibration in their cloudmulets rung across the near empty hall. At most, the usual missives they would do would give around a few hundred. Though this was a high grade missive with relative risk, in terms of combat, they only had to mow down a bunch of weak demonic cultivators.

Of course, the incident with Lixi was another matter.

“What of my request in the report?” Shuren asked when the exhilaration of his rewards subsided.

“It’s been handed over to another department. They’ve already collected all the evidence, and we usually dispose of the corpses when they’re not needed through burial either way,” the dull-eyed receptionist answered. He stared a moment towards Shuren before continuing. “Either way, they’re all dregs of a demonic cult. Kill them on sight, it’s that simple. We don’t have the time nor infrastructure to verify who joined out of malice or who ended up in it as a victim. At the end of the day, all of them are sent to sow discord and tyranny amongst others.”

With his elbow leaning on the table, a closed fist rested upon his cheek. “Anyone who dares encroach upon the territory of Cloud City, be they heretic beings or bandits, they must die without mercy. Do not let your heart bear the weight of those whom seek to kill you. They are evil.”

Shuren fidgeted with the Ruyi Staff, but nodded, agreeing with the sentiments of the receptionist.

He glanced towards Shuren before continuing, “Even if that evil is something for you to decide, make sure you brand them as such. You must have a reason for killing. That is what separates us from those that massacre and slaughter without a thought.”

The senior disciple faced Ming, before continuing, “If you wish to pursue the Path of your Sword, do not let your heart falter once you leave the Seven Clouds. Keep advancing, for hesitation is death.” He gave a serious gaze into Ming Wu’s eyes.

Ming instinctively swallowed air. “Junior brother will remember your advice.” Shuren followed suit. For whatever reason, this senior disciple of theirs talked with heavy words.

“Blessed by Heavens; The arrogant falters, the diligent conquers.” The senior disciple had his arms crossed as he said those last words. Shuren nodded in acknowledgement as he and Ming Wu left the Hall of Missives.

“I’ll be visiting home, since there’s no lectures today. Later,” with the wave to Ming, Shuren leapt on his flying nimbus and left without waiting for a reply.

Ming crossed his arms as he stood on a freshly formed cloud sword, hovering off the ground. “With over 8000 sigils, I can buy a decently sized spatial ring and still have 4000 left over. After I break through to Rank 2, and get some more guidance, I’ll take my leave.”

Ming gazed around as the mountain became shorter in the distance. Though the barrier to Path Foundation was a massive hurdle for many cultivators, Ming had a relaxed expression, as if that pressure didn’t loom over him. He was confident.

The real problem was Shuren.

Ming had noticed his cultivation realm had gotten significantly weaker.

And it wasn't a backlash, but rather a permanent effect.

"He paid a price for us to survive that day. Shuren, if you're going to count my life as part of that deal, then stop trying to bear it on your own. That'll only insult my pride and dignity as your friend."

When he thought back to the receptionist he had met, Ming’s mouth had slighty become ajar.

“Oh, did I ever mention to anyone else I was planning to leave?”

The Hall of Missives was empty once more, save for one girl at the missive board. Smiling, as always.

Without looking at the Falseborne, the receptionist continued to speak.

“Mired in Purgatory; The arrogant ascends, The diligent devoured.”

Celes didn’t deign to look. That burning light was too brilliant for her eyes to envision. It burnt away the hollow desolation she once saw.

A meaning she herself could not grasp onto.
