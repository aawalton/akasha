Chapter 41Fear of the needle is more painful than injection

Grace placed her hand over her chest. "My apologies for the belated introduction," she said with a light bow. "I am Grace, Lei-rei's guardian angel. I've heard much about you, Mr. Bowen, but now is not the time to socialize. For now, please think of me as her parole officer." Her eyes twitched, scanning him from head to toe—and she stretched out her hand. "The grenades, please. Just for now."

Craft looked down and let his mind settle for a moment. He was hesitant to comply, but when he glanced up at Lei-rei, she wouldn't look at him. If he made one wrong move, she might leave.

He rifled through his pockets and handed over a small pouch to Grace without complaint. She nodded. "Shall we begin?"

Begin what? He turned to Lei-rei on instinct, but he couldn't quite look at her. Even so, in the corner of his vision, he could see her lips part. He imagined something hateful and full of contempt spewing out, because that was what he'd earned.

"I need your help."

His gaze snapped straight at her, and he gasped. Why'd she say something like that? But somewhere under the confusion, he was happy. Of course, he thought. He was happy to pay back what he owed her, and that it was the right way to apologize.

But before he could say anything—slap! Lei-rei's face had turned the other way, and Grace's hand was smoking with a faint blue haze. Craft's mind went blank. Was it alright for someone called a "guardian angel" to inflict a point of damage on her protection target? Still, Grace and Lei-rei looked at each other, understanding something only between them, and Lei-rei sighed, looking down.

Grace caught Craft's gaze. "It is also necessary to protect her from herself."

His mind raced for the sake of racing. He knew nothing, and there was nothing he could do.

"I"—Lei-rei continued, and it was clear she was tempted to say something Grace didn't like again—"I don't know if—"

Flick (Mach 1.5)! Lei-rei's head had been thrown backwards, and there was a wisp of blue vapor leaking from her forehead, but as she looked forward again, she couldn't open her eyes. She might not have felt any pain, but she still held a wince on top of a desire to cry.

It was an expression Craft had seen in mirrors before, during nights when he would be tempted to punch the mirror and let his knuckles bleed just to feel like he was still strong. He would be torn in believing whether he could or could not ultimately escape, swinging between "I will be better today" and "I have no self-control." Pitiful, wasn't it? But negotiating with himself—to do some things better while sacrificing the best of his endeavors—was just the norm.

He was used to that feeling. Lei-rei might have been used to it, herself, but it was different watching from the outside, where the view was unfiltered, and every twitch and strain made it seem like she might break down at any moment. Was this how painful it had been for Raffie to watch him, as well? He must have done her a grievous wrong, then, because he couldn't bear watching Lei-rei for a moment longer.

He extended his hand to her. He couldn't help but do it slowly, as if he were approaching a cat who had learned not to trust; if he'd moved too quickly, and she'd be spooked, but too slowly, and he'd seem too weak to rely on.

Her gaze snapped to his hand. Just a little bit more, he thought, but he hadn't even reached halfway when Grace whipped out a revolver—and shot him in the head with a foam dart.

It was the least deadly, and yet the most surprising. The humble foam dart's power lay in the payload of disapproval it carried, and Craft stared at Grace for it, his eyes widened, his hand frozen in mid-air. "Why?" he silently mouthed—what am I doing wrong?

Grace pointed the gun at Lei-rei and shot her too. She flinched, snapping out of her daze and staring at Grace with an incredulous expression. Was that the face he made, too?

"Mental reset darts," Grace said, though Craft was sure they were just ordinary foam darts. They were just foam, so why did he feel nervous as she reloaded the cylinder? Its mechanisms ratcheted to a stop. "If you say or think anything stupid, I'll shoot you."

Before he could even register a protest, she shot him in the forehead again. He'd flinched, but it wasn't much stronger than a clumsy fly bumping into his face. Funny thing was he still wanted to protest—the words were in his head—but he just couldn't!

Grace shot Lei-rei, too. She flinched likewise, and she even flapped her mouth like a fish, but no words came out—until she managed a faint, "W-what?" She ended up glancing at Craft, her expression a mosaic of surprise, annoyance, and silly paralysis.

Lei-rei, feared assassin, subdued by foam darts—of course he'd laugh. The first chuckle felt rude, but when she chuckled as well, the rest of his chuckles followed suit.

Grace whipped out a second revolver and shot them both at once. "You're supposed to be friends," she continued, "but you care too much to hurt each other."

"That doesn't make sense," both Craft and Lei-rei said, both looking at Grace as they did, but they had to pause and glance at each other at that. That they saw things the same way was expected, but...was it the case that that was part of the problem?

Craft looked at Grace with that question on his face, but she just returned a light gaze for a second, shaking her head. "I'm not your mother, and I'm not Enthusia," Grace continued. "I will not tell you what you need to say. I will simply ensure you cannot leave until you realize it yourself."

Lei-rei sighed out of her nose at that and looked down to the side. She must have known what she wanted to say, herself. That was a bit of an unfair advantage, wasn't it?

"Not even a hint?" Craft remarked. He squinted and shirked away, half-expecting to get shot, but Grace just smiled, and he fully opened his eyes.

"Well, I had a coworker," she replied. How generous of her, he thought, but the mention of a coworker made his spine tingle somewhat, and he could guess who it was. "She taught me something before. On one hand, scientifically speaking, the cumulative amount of pain for carefully peeling off a bandage is less than ripping it off, but that analogy breaks down in the emotional realm. The very preference for peeling makes every second all the more excruciating. 'Make of that as you will'—was what she'd said."

That's a very Amacus thing to say. He did still regret what had happened back then. He'd been arrogant, saying his way was the best for him, but—er—no, apparently not. What a joke, right? That he ought to open up bit by bit and hopefully, painlessly, arrive at that magical end state of "happy"—that was just way too vague to be realistic. To hope that there was no pain coaxed him to become the opposite of his ideal, brave self, and Amacus had evidently known this result from the start.

Well, running away was important too, but where was the sweet spot between courage and cowardice? He looked at Lei-rei, hoping maybe there was an answer written on her face. She could only stare back, but eventually, she began to grit her teeth.This girl's got more of a fight than me, he thought. Her eyes became so fierce that anyone else would mistake it for anger.

She whipped out her sword, and he wasn't surprised. He wouldn't have been mad if she had sent his head flying right then and there, but her steel only kissed his neck—and still, he wasn't surprised. He observed with sad eyes the beads of sweat running down her cheek, and he listened intently to her loud, stifled breathing. He could tell she was almost as much of a coward as himself: he, who had offended her unknowingly; he, who might have made a promise then forgotten it; he, who had become a loose thread in her life.

And because Lei-rei was only almost a coward: "It hurts when you look at me"—she strained to say, speaking in a half-whisper—"and you don't smile."

Was it finally time for her to cut him off?

A dart struck him above the eye, blasting away all the cortisol-stew and drama that he'd worked so hard to build up in his brain. Grief? Fear? Contempt? Disgust? Happiness? Huh? The heck were those? The humble foam dart had unceremoniously hit undo on all of them, and unbridled confusion was the only thing that filled his mind, though only for a moment.

Still, it was enough time that when he snapped out of it, he realized that Lei-rei had already sheathed her sword. Perhaps some great apocalypse would follow, and Grace would help Lei-rei draw and quarter him and stuff his remains in an Iron Maiden...but no such apocalypse came to pass. With each passing second, the more 'nothing' happened, and the more bewildered he became. Grace was still just monitoring him and Lei-rei like a sentry gun, and when he looked at Lei-rei, although he felt shameful toward her, he was surprised. Her lower eyelids were pulled tight, creating a slight bulge beneath her eyes. Her lips twitched repeatedly, and the muscle controlling her chin was visibly strained, giving her whole lower face an unnatural, quivering tension. ...and that didn't make sense.

"And I'm"—she continued, her lips quivering, and she sighed sharply—"I'm tired."

There was no way that someone ready to let him go would look so afraid.

"But," she continued, "I thought, if I'd carelessly moved, I'd—"

Craft raised a hand. She made a small gasp, one which he regretted to elicit from her, but it was alright. He wasn't going to say anything cruel. Even so, he couldn't look her in the eye.

"I realized something about us"—and he couldn't stop his eyes from drooping. "I've always been out of place, but I never thought I was a bad guy deep inside. Told myself, 'hey, if someone asks for my help, I'd help them out, right?'" He paused. Neither Lei-rei nor Grace spoke, and the silence was heavy on him. He just hoped he'd make sense. "And that's my life: kill or be killed, help and be helped—speak only when spoken to. I keep my thoughts to myself. I didn't think they were anything important, but because of that, you never really know what I'm thinking.

"You're right beside me, but you know nothing about me." He balled his hands into fists. If they were destined to split ways after this, he might as well commit one final act of honesty. "Truth is, I want to run away even if I tell you I care, and I feel suspicious of you even if I consider you a friend. I've always been sure that you'd be tired of me the moment you knew, though—that I was too much of a failure of a human being to be worth sticking to—so I...never said anything." And I'm not even sure why I was desperate to prolong this silence.

"Grace," Lei-rei said, "gun."

Huh. He looked up just in time to catch a foam dart straight between his eyes, but this time, he didn't flinch. His gaze was transfixed on hers. He had never seen such wide, sparkly eyes filled with such joy...while shooting him, sure, but that was a minor detail.

He was more surprised when she turned the gun on herself—pop—and a dart bounced off of her forehead.

"Why! I'm the one who—"

"I thought this would be harder! I hesitated— I'm a failure as a swordswoman!"

Well, she wasn't wrong, but, "Look, I hopped over a very low bar— "

...Because they found it hard to speak to others, they had made themselves easy to speak to, hoping that by becoming what they'd needed, the world would be less shittier for it. But they hadn't accounted for the fact that their own silence made the person right beside them more disconnected—and lonely.

"I regret a lot of things," he said, and Lei-rei slowed down, looking away for a second before looking back at him.

"Are you sure"—she paused, looking away again, sighing and shaking her head. She looked at him. "I want to hear it."

It was strange to smile as he recounted his regrets. He'd felt the pain he had inflicted on Lei-rei far more intensely than any pain he could have ever inflicted on himself, and he would always think about this mistake for each new person he'd meet. He would also never forget how rewarding it was to share how much of a failure he had been, and how pleasant it was for someone else to want his story.

Simply, he was regretful, and he was happy.

— End of Book 1 —

... so anyway,

A little later, Grace was walking towards an odd, wrinkled tree. From where three branches should have grown from the top of its stem, two were cut and scarred-over, and only one was allowed to climb skyward. From where that branch should have forked, the rest were cut, and only one path remained and climbed some more. This continued until the forks and branches grew so thin that a needle could have been fashioned from the furthest point, though it was hard to gauge just how far into the sky it had reached.

But the closer she got, the chillier it became. The ground took on a thin layer of frost, and soon, specks of white were blowing past her. There was a small geyser of that misty frost coming from a hole at the foot of the tree, and she stopped right before the ring of ice that had built up around it. The hole itself was small, only wide enough to fit one person at a time, and its icicle-lined rim was sure to scratch anyone bold enough to jump straight into it.

But it was the little black dot she saw while peering through the hole that made her feel cold. That little black dot was standing in the middle of a vast sheet of thin ice.

"Maybe I should just use Rapture on that," she muttered.

"Please don't," Pruned said over her shoulder. "I mean, sis, the two of them back there have a good vibe going, and that thing's going nowhere."

Grace couldn't tear her gaze off the little dot. She knew its continued restraint was a temporary truth at best. "How long has it been?"

"Just last night."

Oh dear Enthusia, my maker, mother, and savior—is there really no way around that?
