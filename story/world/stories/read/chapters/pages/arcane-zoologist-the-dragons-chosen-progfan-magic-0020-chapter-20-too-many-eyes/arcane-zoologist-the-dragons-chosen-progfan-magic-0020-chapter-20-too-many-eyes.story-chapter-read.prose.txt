Despite their encounter with the glauxis, Callie managed to return to Granny Ethel without raising much suspicion.

"What took you so long?" Tristan whined, when she returned.

"I… I ran into Callen at the swamp," Callie said. It wasn't even a lie.

Tristan narrowed his eyes. "Is that so?"

"Knock it off, boy. She got the moss, and that's all that matters," Granny Ethel said as she looked over Callie. It was clear that the elderly woman had noticed that something was off, but she chose not to speak of it in front of Tristan. Callie would explain things to her later.

"You're too nice to her, Granny!" Tristan protested.

"It may seem that way because I have to be twice as strict with you, little troublemaker," Granny Ethel shook her head.

Sadly, Callie didn't get the chance to talk to Granny Ethel without Tristan around, since he had to stay late to fix the labels on the medicine bottles, which he believed would be funny to mix up. Needless to say, Granny Ethel didn't find the joke funny.

When Callie got home, Dad and Mom were both chatting amicably while Dad cooked dinner.

"Welcome home, Callie," Mom greeted her, inviting her for a hug.

Callie obliged, feeling extremely grateful that Mom was on her way to full recovery, despite the bandages still wrapped around her arm. But an unpleasant thought crossed her mind. Mom couldn't have taken the egg, could she?

"Mom?" Callie asked tentatively.

"Yes, dear?" Mom asked, looking down at Callie, as she brushed the hair out of Callie's face.

"D-Did you… What exactly happened with the glauxis?"

Mom's smile vanished, and she beckoned Callie to sit in the chair next to her.

"Callie, I know you like arcane beasts and believe them to be good creatures, things with the glauxis… they were difficult. It was very aggressive from the moment we saw it, instantly launching to attack us. I'm sorry to say that, but this is not something that can be solved peacefully. You have to understand that," Mom said.

"Maybe there's a reason it's angry…" Callie suggested, watching her Mom's expressions carefully.

"Perhaps. But it's not like we can ask for it. We as hunters have to do our best to protect the village," Mom said sternly. "Don't you dare get any ideas of going after it!"

"Maeve, even Callie wouldn't go that far. Right, Callie?" Dad asked, looking at her from the stove.

"O-Of course not!" Callie said with as much conviction as she could muster. She actually didn't even need to search for the glauxis anymore. She met it just a few hours ago.

"Good. Now, while I am making dinner, why don't you get the sauna going? You're covered in dirt, and I feel like I need a thorough cleanse as well," Dad suggested.

"Sauna?!" Callie's voice came out as a shriek, startling both Mom and Dad. If she went to the sauna together with Mom, she would undoubtedly see her mark. That would be bad. "But it's so hot!"

"What are you talking about? You've been walking around in your tunic for the last two weeks. Refused to take it off even in the smithy," Dad pointed out.

"I… I just really like this tunic!"

"Callie…" Mom gave her a significant look. "You have to wash up. Your hair is a complete mess. You need to clean with more than just cold water."

"I… I…" Callie wanted to come up with an excuse.

"I'm sorry to say that I won't be able to help you this time. Ethel said I can't go in the sauna until my wounds properly close up. Can you manage alone?"

Callie blinked. Mom wouldn't be joining her. That changed everything. "I can manage! Don't worry, Mom. I'm nine after all."

Mom and Dad shared a look, likely having caught onto her sudden shift in attitude. She offered them a smile, although it felt forced even to her. No way would they actually buy this.

"Good. Get started on it. I'm sure Callen would love to have a thorough wash too."

Callie zoomed out of the house at once, before Mom and Dad could say anything else. She felt like she had so many things she couldn't tell them that speaking them for too long would make them suspicious. It was actually weighing heavily on her.

How long would she keep hiding things from Mom and Dad? Surely she couldn't keep it a secret forever. Sure, this time, Callie would be washing alone, so Mom won't see the dragon's mark. But what about the next time?

I need to tell them. About the mark and magic, at least. Maybe not about the dragon yet.

But remembering how Mom and Dad had reacted to her story about how she saved Snowglow made Callie pause. They were so mad… If she told them about the mark, then she would have to tell them about Wiggles and Snowglow at the very least. But there was no way they would react well to that…

No, I need more time. I need to show them that arcane beasts aren't all monsters. That begins with finding the glauxis egg and showing that glauxis isn't just mindlessly attacking people.

After her hot and extremely satisfying sauna, Callie felt like a completely new girl. It's been a while since she felt so clean, and that night she slept extremely well. This helped her get up early in the morning, dealing with her chores in record time. Even water carrying.

Friend hurry?

Wiggles seemed confused by Callie's fast-moving through the coop, sweeping away the old, dirty hay, and searching for eggs.

"I'm going to prove to everyone that arcane beasts aren't monsters. To do that, I'll find the glauxis's egg and show that it attacked for a good reason and will leave peacefully if the right thing is done," Callie announced to the mireling.

Egg?

"Did you know, Wiggles, somebody stole the glauxis's eggs? I'm going to try and find it!"

How?

Callie stopped her chicken egg gathering and looked at the mireling. The mireling tilted its body to the side.

"I… I don't suppose you have a secret ability to find eggs, do you?"

No.

"I thought not…" Callie sighed. "But I'll go talk to Granny Ethel. Perhaps she knows something!"

With that said, Callie bid farewell to Wiggles and hurried to the healer's hut. When she arrived, Tristan still hadn't arrived, which gave her a short moment to talk with Granny Ethel undisturbed.

"Callie, that is a very dangerous thing you did! I had a feeling that something wasn't right when you returned yesterday, but to think you would seek out the glauxis. Child, you saw what it did to your mother!" Granny Ethel chastised, hands on her hips.

"But… But… I didn't want it to be killed… Not to mention that Mom said that she would call dragonbloods if it got too close to the village…"

Granny Ethel sighed. "I wish we had rangers in the area. Things would be so much easier then."

"Rangers?" Callie asked. Mom had mentioned them before, but Callie didn't know anything about them.

"They're people who deal with problems regarding arcane beasts."

Callie scrunched up her nose. Whenever Mom said "deal with a monster", it almost always meant that she would kill it. Were these rangers just another bunch of hunters? They didn't sound as bad as dragonbloods, but if possible, Callie would like to solve this peacefully.

"Granny Ethel, do you have any idea how we could find a glauxis egg?" Callie asked, eager to change the topic.

The elderly woman scratched her chin. "I'm not a detective, I'm afraid. I know that an egg from an arcane beast can be sold for a huge sum if the creature inside remains alive. If whoever took it wanted to sell it later, then they would likely keep it in a place where the egg can continue to grow."

"Where would that be?" Callie asked eagerly.

"Someplace warm and dark, since normally beasts like glauxis would sit on their eggs to keep them warm. Perhaps moisture would also be necessary. I'm uncertain about glauxis eggs specifically, but some arcane beasts definitely require their eggs to remain at least a little damp," Granny Ethel said.

"So it likely was taken by a person who wants loads of money, and the egg is kept somewhere warm, dark, and possibly moist," Callie repeated.

"I'm not sure about the money. It could also be that someone is trying to get into the good graces of some dragonblood by offering a rare monster egg as a gift. As I said, I'm not a detective."

"Okay, the reason doesn't matter. I'll just focus on finding it," Callie said firmly. It might take a while to check all the places in the village. It could also be hidden inside somebody's house. That would make it difficult to get to it… Nonetheless, Callie was determined to try.

"Wait a moment, little one! You can't just jump into danger like that," Granny Ethel said firmly.

"Danger? But I'm just going to search for an egg. I'll remain in the village the whole time."

Granny Ethel sighed, then put her hand on Callie's shoulder. "The biggest danger comes from people, not from arcane beasts. Remember that, Callie. I know I can't talk you out of this, and I admit it would be great if we could find the egg and return it to the glauxis peacefully. But you mustn't do your search alone. Go together with Callen, okay?"

Callie nodded. She would actually feel safer with her big brother. "Okay. Callen already knows."

"Good. Avoid people while you search for the egg, okay? Who knows what the perpetrator will do if he finds out that you're searching for it?"

"Per-pe-what?"

"The person who stole the egg. I highly doubt he'll give it up willingly. Please, child, make sure that nobody finds out that you're looking for it," Granny Ethel warned.

Callie nodded, although the serious expression in the elderly woman's eyes filled her with worry. Would the thief really hurt Callie if they found out that she was searching for the egg? The thought never even crossed her mind.

"Oh, Callie. I wish I could just keep you away from this dangerous endeavour altogether. This isn't something a child like you should fret over. Perhaps you could leave this matter up to your brother?"

Callie thought about it for a moment, then shook her head. "What if I can somehow sense the egg with my magic? My mark would always glow when I got near an arcane beast. Maybe the same would happen with the egg. Perhaps, it would be impossible for him to find it alone."

Granny Ethel sighed again. "Yes, I figured you might say something like that. Why do you kids always rush to grow up? You should enjoy your childhood more."

Callie wanted to protest, but Tristan came into the healer's hut at that moment, so they had to drop the subject altogether.

Ethel, having sent Tristan and Callie to the flower meadow for more gathering, hobbled towards the house of the village chief. The news Callie had brought her that morning was very disturbing.

A stolen glauxis egg hidden somewhere within Greyfen. The matter was serious, and Ethel was debating how best to approach this problem without enraging the chief and making him punish Callie despite her best intentions. She was such a kind child, wishing to protect both the villagers and the glauxis.

Everyone else seemed to think her a troublemaker, and perhaps she was in a sense. But they also refused to see past the exterior of arcane beasts. They believed them to be mindless, murderous creatures, yet Callie could see past that. A dragon had even recognized her kind heart.

Unfortunately, this meant that the child would be thrust in the middle of problems that were yet far too big for her to handle. Ethel wished she could forbid Callie from searching for the egg. But she knew that the girl would search for it anyway. The best thing she could do was make sure that somebody remained with her.

It was a good thing that Callie had such a dependable big brother. If Callen was with her, then Ethel could be sure that he would keep his little sister safe. But what of the egg? How to present this problem to the chief without agitating him?

"Ethel, what brings you here?" Aemon asked, after she had arrived. The man had set aside his work immediately to speak with her.

"There's something amiss in the village," Ethel said.

"What do you mean?" Aemon's face hardened.

"You know what happened to Maeve and her hunters?"

"Of course! They found a damned glauxis prowling near the village. I swear… As if we don't have enough issues as it is," Aemon rubbed at his temples wearily.

"I have a reason to believe that it might soon appear in the village itself," Ethel continued carefully.

Aemon stared at her wide-eyed. "Ethel, what are you saying? Why would it come here?"

"You know as well as I do that glauxis don't come here during summer. It has never happened before. There might be a good reason why it has come this close."

"What reason? What do monsters even want? As far as I know, they are only concerned with two things: food and breeding."

Ethel gave him a significant look, hoping that he would jump to the right conclusion himself.

Aemon stared at her for a moment, then jumped up to his feet. "The egg? You're saying some lunatic has taken its egg?!"

"Yes, that's what I believe," Ethel said solemnly. "It would be a valuable prize to anyone should they sell it to a dragonblood."

Aemon sat down. "It makes sense. But even for the possible reward, who would be mad enough to steal a monster's egg? That's suicide! Heck, the whole village could be destroyed in the process."

Ethel nodded. "You must search for it, but carefully. Who knows what would happen if the thief were to be pressured? They might destroy the egg, and then there would be no calming the glauxis."

"Rage of a monster is the last thing we need. Thank you, Ethel, for telling me. I shall search for this. Quietly."

Ethel nodded and turned to leave; some of the weight lifted off her shoulders. She felt it was unlikely that Aemon would find the egg. He didn't have the nose for matters relating to arcane beasts. Callie was more likely to succeed in this regard.

But this way, there would be multiple people searching, which would turn the thief's attention to the adults, rather than the little, curious girl poking about the village. In the best-case scenario, Aemon would find the egg, and the issue would be solved without endangering Callie.
