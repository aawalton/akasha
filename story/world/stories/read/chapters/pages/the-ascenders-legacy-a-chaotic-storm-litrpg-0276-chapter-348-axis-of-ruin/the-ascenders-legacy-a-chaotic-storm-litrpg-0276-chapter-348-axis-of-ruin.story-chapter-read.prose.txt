The text crawled across Aodhán's vision at the exact moment the word left his lips. "Ruin."

But this activation of Ruin's Obedience was vastly different from the others.

Aodhán was centered now. His emotions were dead. Eye of the Storm had evolved beyond peace and tranquility into something far colder. It was clarity now. Precision. Control.

More than that, it was the complete absence of turbulence. Of chaos. Of unnecessary motion.

His storm was no longer explosive or wild. It was surgical. Condensed. So tightly structured it bordered on solidity.

And as lightning gathered from all across the battlefield in response to his call, it gathered without a crackle or roar.

It gathered with purpose—a scalpel rather than a hammer.

Clean. Cold. Clinical.

And when it struck, it was swifter than even Aodhán could have imagined.

From all over the battlefield, ambient storm energy merged into a single, brilliant beam of colorful lightning, no wider than his thumb. It didn't crackle. It didn't arc. It simply struck down on Alaric's dome of Absolute Suppression like a lance and shattered it to pieces.

It didn't disperse.

Alaric stumbled backward, eyes widening slightly at the backlash, shocked by the power in that one attack. He glanced back at Aodhán, a slight frown marring his features as the lance of pure destruction returned to Aodhán as a construct.

Still. Centered. Serene.

But Alaric knew that lance was anything but. Whatever Aodhán had done to the skill, it was incredibly dangerous—a deception of entropy camouflaged as order.

Aodhán had found his center. But it wasn't in Serenity. Serenity didn't birth ruin. It birthed tranquility. And the attack he had just felt had been anything but tranquil.

Aodhán didn't give him time to process the change, though.

In an instant, he crossed the distance between them, violet lightning dissolving in his wake. Alaric activated Still World Mandate, immediately, forcing everything in his immediate area into stillness.

And yet, pain slammed into his chest as the lance struck at full force, skewering through stillness and tissue to graze bone.

Alaric was shocked. He tried to grab the lance, but it slipped from his fingers like liquid to return back to its wielder.

Back to Aodhán.

Aodhán grabbed the lance, and in a tone so flat it was eerie, he muttered, "Ruinous Layering."

Chaos rushed out of his hands to coat the lance in inky black liquid, and though the activation was undramatic, Alaric knew he couldn't let the lance strike him.

Aodhán may have found his center, but that center was not serenity. It was a perversion. And he was determined to eliminate it.

Gray eyes hardened in resolve as they met Aodhán's, and in a whisper, Alaric spoke.

"Impressive. Truly. But wrong."

Then he moved—and despite Zarxan's blessing of speed and defiance, Aodhán barely managed to follow.

CHINK! CHINK!

The sound of metal hitting metal echoed as their constructs slammed against each other in a blur of strikes. Alaric was fast—faster than Sophran had been in that high-speed battle they'd done in his training room.

But while Alaric had tier and experience on his side, Aodhán had Zarxan's blessing. And it was just enough to help him hold his own. Barely.

Defiance, speed, and power pushed his body beyond what it should have been capable of, and without his emotions in the way, Aodhán was flying free—his entire form a blur of crimson and gold as he parried, defended, and attacked Alaric.

But despite the advantages he'd been given, Alaric was stronger.

His spear slammed into Ruin's Obedience like the weight of a mountain, and the lance splintered to pieces. Aodhán felt no fear or desperation, though. He simply gathered another ruin, spending far less energy on this stable version than he had on the ruinous one.

Violet lightning pulsed as he teleported away from Alaric, lightning gathering all around him to form another lance.

Alaric refused to let that happen.

He crossed the distance between them in less time than it took to process the movement—not rushing, not desperate, just there—and his hand closed around Aodhán's wrist with the particular precision of one who had done this exact move a hundred times before.

And an instant later, Aodhán's world tilted on its axis.

He found himself airborne, the earth and sky switching places disorienting. He hit the ground hard enough to crater it, bones reverberating from the force of the attack.

But Aodhán felt no pain.

He was up in an instant, wings flapping as he merged with Varéc, imbuing the precision and control of Axis of Ruin into a new skill.

Storm’s Wrath.

Without nary a boom of thunder, the sky broke open to unleash a floodgate on the earth. Only this time, it was no ordinary water. There were no crashing waves. No foam. No turbulence.

Instead, water fell from the heavens like liquid glass—fluid, smooth, silent.

And with his aspect humming within him like a choir, Aodhán sent all that water toward Alaric.

Not as a wave. Not as a flood.

As blades.

The water didn't crash or roar. It fell—thousands upon thousands of needle-thin hydraulic edges descending in perfect parallel sheets, each one compressed to the thickness of a hair. Their speed, unimaginable.

Alaric activated Still World Mandate again, pouring energy and power into enforcing serenity in his domain. The air thickened. Space resisted.

But this was not lightning. It was water. Water adapted.

The front edge of the descending torrent flattened into a smooth, translucent plane—so polished it reflected Alaric's own gray eyes back at him.

What had been a wall of liquid became blade.

It struck against Alaric's suppression field without sound, but the field itself buckled inward with a sound like grinding stone. The blades pressed in, slicing through the field in perfect unison, like a knife through butter.

Alaric's eyes widened—fractionally—and he moved.

But the blades were faster, each drop singular yet acting as a team.

The first sheet caught his shoulders, slicing clean through cloth, skin, and muscle as effortlessly as a knife through paper. Blood misted into the air, crimson against clear, and Alaric's jaw tightened in pain.

This wasn't water anymore.

It was cutting pressure given fluid form.

Swiveling, he raised a hand to the torrent, Kinetic Dissolution activating to eliminate the force behind the skill. And for a moment, it worked. The water stilled—but then it began to adapt, torrents branching and branching until they completely surrounded him.

Kinetic Dissolution couldn't affect them all, and once more, Alaric's efforts were rendered futile.

Every time he shifted the equilibrium, the blades adapted, adjusting density, edge, and angle in defiance. It was like trying to moderate a living thing.

And it was everywhere.

Like a madman, Alaric burned through essence and willpower to destroy the attack, but each time, water adapted terrifyingly, bladed torrents slicing through his body at every opportunity until the glass itself turned crimson.

Eyes wide in growing panic, Alaric met crimson-gold irises through the curtain of liquid glass.

Aodhán stood just outside his own creation, wings spread, violet lightning flowing on his wings, and expression dead. There was no triumph, no rage or satisfaction in his expression.

Just cold, centered purpose. And a new lance of ruin.

It was inhuman. It was against everything Serenity stood for.

Alaric realized it then. The other transmigrants were dangerous, but Aodhán… he was the reason for this war. The cause of the unusual expansion. The antithesis of order and Serenity.

If he was already this strong now, how much more would he be at the Mythic class? At the Calamitic class?

Alaric's jaw set.

"Enough."

Willpower exploded out of him in a wave, and reality undulated as the rule took effect, halting everything around him and freezing them solid.

Even as he did so, Alaric felt the core of his power shrink in repulsion. He was a serenity cultivator. He shouldn't be shedding blood. He shouldn't be taking lives.

But if he didn't take action now, lives who would have been protected would eventually fall to ruin.

He didn't care that doing this meant betraying his ideal. His power. And everything he stood for.

Someone had to do it. Someone had to restore balance. Unfortunately, the task had fallen to him.

Grimacing slightly as his ideal revolted, Alaric moved through the suspended liquid glass, passing through it like a curtain to stand before Aodhán, who was frozen alongside his skill.

His hand closed around Aodhán's neck and tightened, grimacing at the contrast of white against midnight skin.

He looked into Aodhán's blank eyes and whispered, "Balance must be restored."

And to his utter, complete surprise, Aodhán responded. "Yes, it must."

Alaric stumbled back in shock, but it was too late. Water was already in his veins. In his bloodstream.

They gathered. Condensed. And before Alaric could so much as blink, they ruptured in an explosion of bubbles in his bloodstream.

Alaric saw red.

Back in the elder hall, tension hung heavily in the air as the High-Elders watched the screen with held breaths and tight expressions.

If the war had been chaos before, it had devolved into something far worse.

Mythics died in their thousands every hour. Machines and automatons went up in smoke as quickly as they were created, and what little formations they had left had been destroyed. Reality itself was having to repair itself every second.

It was a slaughter.

But despite all that, they weren't losing. Not yet.

The Calamities held ground firmly, pitting themselves against enemy Calamities to keep them occupied and prevent them from actively participating in the war. The Mythics were also still holding strong.

The Water and Wind sects stood as the main line of defense, while Storm, Lightning, and Shadow were on the attack, doing their best to rain as much destruction as they could on their enemies.

But the real champions were the transmigrants.

Videos of them dominated several screens, from Sophran to Aodhán to Ashscar, and finally, Nymera, whose visage filled nearly every screen, her entire body humming with so much power it seemed inexhaustible as she clashed with Volgrim Kaizen in the sky.

The other transmigrants were impressive. Some, like Aodhán, even more so. But Nymera? She was in a class all on her own, her power far surpassing that of any other transmigrant on the battlefield.

Her opponent, Volgrim, had been exceedingly impressive too, embodying his path of silence far more than he had in his battle against Ashscar. But against Nymera, who needed not even to speak before the storm answered, his abilities were rendered almost completely useless, forcing him to resort to basic earth manipulation, his control impressive.

Just like Kael Varros had been before him.

It was all very impressive.

Yet it wasn't enough.

Tense, the High-Elders watched as the sky split open in fury and dozens of plasma beams speared down, slamming Volgrim into the earth with so much force and power his body formed a crater several miles wide.

Shocked silence hung over the hall.

And then—

"He can't survive that," High-Elder Mystral spoke, her voice a whisper. "No one in the advanced class can survive that."

"No one in the advanced class should be able to summon plasma," High-Elder Tharn stated, his expression shocked. And then, as if resigning himself, he shook his head. "They are all too powerful. If I was the enemy, I would want to kill them too."

No one else spoke, but it was obvious from their expressions that they agreed with him.

Their transmigrants were too powerful for their tier, especially Aodhán and Nymera. Those two were the most powerful transmigrants they'd seen in the history of the sect, and with Zarxan's blessing still running in their veins, it couldn't have been more obvious.

There was no doubt the other sects feared their rise and would love nothing more than to send Calamities to simply assassinate them.

But they could not do that. Not until this battle was over, at least.

Though not stated, the rules of balance wars were known and sacred. There could be no active interference with the outcome of every battle. Zarxan's intrusion didn't count, as that had been widespread across their entire sect.

But though that rule currently served them, the High-Elders knew they weren't out of the woods yet. Should Aodhán or Sophran lose their battle, they wouldn't be able to interfere.

The screen shifted then, showing Volgrim's half-burnt body embedded deep within bedrock. His body twitched, impressive considering the attack he had just taken, but that ended the moment Nymera landed beside him, the mangled skeleton of Kael Varros still held in her arm.

The skeleton had lost its arms and a leg, but it was still a suitable weapon, and Nymera used it dutifully. Bone jabbed into Volgrim's shattered ribs a moment later, and with a burst of lightning, she shoved it in—past tissue and bone to skewer his heart and nail him deep into the bedrock with the skeleton of his ally.

It was a strong statement, and the First Seat was certain many were cheering at the scene. He, however, had a different concern.

For the safety of the others, he would have loved to leave Nymera on the field and let her cause as much destruction as she could, but for her own sake, he ordered, "Recall her from the field. She's done enough."

The messenger nodded, understanding his reasoning, and without hesitation he left for the viewing deck to inform Elder Ghaltris.

After the messenger left, High-Elder Torvas turned his gaze back to the screen, marveling as he watched Nymera carefully pick Ashscar up, tending to his injuries with shaky fingers.

He shook his head at the scene. Such devastating power. Such emotional anchor.

This was what it meant to be truly powerful. What it meant to be a transmigrant.

And he couldn't wait to see just how that blossomed in the future.

Smiling, he turned his gaze to Sophran and Aodhán, the only two transmigrants left in battle, and whispered, "Show them your strength. Show them the power of the storm."

Somewhere in the distance, thunder boomed, and the First Seat sighed.

With Nymera now out of the battlefield, there was nothing left to do but hope Aodhán and Sophran made it.

If they didn't, then there would be nothing left to fight for.

It was a statement they understood all too well.

Your strongest are not untouchable.

"That makes two. Two transmigrants dead."

Yet no one spoke.

What could they say? That they'd been overconfident? That they'd underestimated the transmigrants of the Storm Sect, or that Malverin had been right? That bringing their transmigrants into the war was like throwing sheep to wolves?

No. That wasn't true.

Volgrim had nearly killed the herald before that… that monster had shown up. They'd had a chance before she'd arrived to smite Volgrim down in minutes.

Now, two transmigrants lay dead by her hands, leaving only two left.

Fortunately, the Storm Sect had called her away.

Almost as one, their gazes traveled to the two transmigrants left. Seraphine and Alaric—both still holding their own.

Hope wasn't dead yet. If these two could win, they would even the score and end this war in an instant. The Blood and Earth sects may have lost one transmigrant each, but if Seraphine and Alaric won, the Storm Sect would lose two and balance would be restored.

Sighing heavily at their silence, Malverin glanced at the two screens left and muttered, "There's still hope. All isn't lost yet."

Silence was her only response.

The Sect of Silence.

To restore silence.
