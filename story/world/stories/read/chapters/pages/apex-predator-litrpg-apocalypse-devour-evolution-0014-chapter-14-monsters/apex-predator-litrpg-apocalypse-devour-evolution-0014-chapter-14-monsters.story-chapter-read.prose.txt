Don jumped. The spear with the flaming point tore through the space where he’d just been. It hissed past his ribs. Mid-air, he flicked his claws out with a snap.

He landed lightly and kicked the attacking thug in the face. His neck snapped back, and he groaned as blood started spurting out of his broken nose.

Don gave the man no time to recover. He grabbed the haft of the spear and yanked.

The thug stumbled forward like a clumsy child, face-first into the dirt. Don stepped in and drove his claws into the man's back. He could feel the lungs puncture. The thugs whole body went rigid for a moment. A sharp exhale. Then stillness.

Another flash of motion—Don twisted and slid back as a second spear struck where he'd been standing. Too slow. The man braced to follow up, but Don had already moved.

The red-bearded man roared as he joined the fight, charging in with wild energy.

Don sidestepped the telegraphed attack calmly, no wasted motion.

As the second thug thrust forward, Don’s claws sliced through the wooden shaft with a dry tsok. Half a second later, the man was holding a pointless stick, blinking at it like it had betrayed him.

He never saw the second claw coming. It arced fast and clean across his throat.

So slow.

The man dropped like a sack of meat.

Don didn’t stop. A third man was already closing in, tossing the broken half of a shield toward Don’s face as a distraction. It almost worked. Don ducked, but the next spear strike came tight—closer than expected. It punched into his side with a dull thunk.

Don grunted. More surprised than hurt. He looked down at the shaft sticking out of his side, then back at the thug holding it.

The man tugged, trying to wrench it free.

Don didn’t budge.

He clenched the shaft and smiled.

“Now what?”

The thug froze. His grip loosened.

Don tilted his head. Then he lunged.

One step forward, and his claws punctured the man’s throat. Warm blood sprayed in an arc. The thug dropped without even a gasp.

Only one left.

The bearded man—wounded but standing—let out a guttural yell and hurled his spear.

Don caught it mid-flight. He didn’t hesitate. He hurled it back.

It struck home—deep into the man's shoulder. He fell, rolled, groaned, and still tried to run. Half limping, half crawling, he made it ten feet before Don was on him.

Don grabbed him by the collar and yanked him back onto the dirt.

“No!” the thug gasped, kicking weakly. “Wait—!”

“I warned you.”

“I— I can take you to the camp. The boss will want a fighter like you. I swear—”

“Not interested.”

The man’s eyes flicked wildly. He raised both hands in surrender. “Okay! I—I’ve got potions. Rations. Gear! Just take it! Just—”

“Good,” Don said flatly. “I’ll take them when you’re dead.”

Panic broke across the thug’s face like a cracked mask. His pleading turned to hate.

“You fucking freak,” he hissed, face twisting. “You’re a monster.”

Don didn’t blink.

He swiped once and the man’s throat opened like a zipper. Blood spilled into the dirt.

“Just when I need to be,” Don said.

The man only let out a small wheeze and he was soon dead.

Don looked at the men he had killed. His pulse was even and he felt no nausea. He had just done what had to be done. He didn’t enjoy it, but he certainly didn’t regret it. These men were beasts, and only knew one language — force. So Don had shown it to them. They were dead and wouldn’t cause any more harm.

Now Don just had to decide whether he would Devour these thugs. He was bleeding, so there was that. And what good were the corpses to anyone anyway? Don could at least make their deaths meaningful. He had been the predator and they the prey, but that didn’t mean he would treat their deaths as nothing. He would use them to get stronger. He would also Devour the poor boy with the shield that the thugs had left behind. He had to. If Don died because he wasn’t strong enough, that would be the very definition of darwinism.
