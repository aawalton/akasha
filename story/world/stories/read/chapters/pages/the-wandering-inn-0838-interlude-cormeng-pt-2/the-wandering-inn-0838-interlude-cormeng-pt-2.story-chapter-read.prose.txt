One day, Gothica was sitting in front of the door to Cormeng’s shop, staring out the window. It showed the street outside where he lived, mostly. But for a moment, when people left, she could see Mudhole.
Miserable. Rain again, that damn muck, and a rising area where they were building what would be the City Hall of the town. Just flickers. Gothica was a Level 27 [Midnight Goth] these days, but she was also something else.
A [Window Watcher]. Level 4. She had gotten it in Cormeng’s, watching people go by. Staring at her friend’s cities. At all the places she would probably never be allowed to go.
One of her only Skills was [A Longer Glimpse]. So she could see a bit more into that second. A bit more of Mudhole. Ramshackle housing, a bit improved with Doren’s materials. People in more organized lines, fishing, hauling catches up. A bit less starved, maybe. A crude rope pulley system that let people haul goods across the boardwalk by pulling on wet ropes.
Still terribly shitty compared to Goblinhome—literally, they had no latrines. But Goblins might have thought it a good home.
Gothica knew she wasn’t very smart. Chieftain Rags was always telling her she had to be better. She expected Gothica to be something. That was the scary part. Gothica was just someone who liked being spooky, in touch with her darkness. Saying ‘up yours’ to important people. But sometimes she and Redscar and the others watched her like—like they knew something.
Mrsha did too, but Gothica was afraid of her. She wasn’t sure she could be that person. Sometimes, the other [Goths] asked her questions about whether they’d ever fit in, what to do when people were mean to them, and Gothica never knew if she said the right things.
She was afraid of her class. Of letting it down. And she knew she was a silly Goblin. But even she got things.
It was today. What day? Gothica didn’t know, the summer. What was she, a calendar person? It was bright and sunny in the Unseen Empire. Raining slightly in the mudflats. Today, at exactly this hour, Gothica realized it.
Before anyone else. She thought even Chieftain Rags didn’t get it. Even Shaman Theikha, for all her big smarts, hadn’t quite gotten it. But she, Gothica…she gazed at a Human man who was a bit flabby.
In a respectable Goblin way that said he had enough to eat to get fat. And he was balding, but that was cool; Goblins didn’t really lose hair. You had to respect someone who got old enough to lose it. A bit droopy, a bit clumsy, not the sharpest bean in the knife drawer, like her.
But today, she saw Shopkeeper Doren, the Rulebreaker, owner of Cormeng’s Magical Emporium of Antiquities and Trinkets, and realized one of the most important Humans in Izril was her friend.
He didn’t even know it. He was so good, beyond even Erin, because he fooled himself. It had been waiting to come out of him. For forty years—he’d hidden it. Until now.
He was running about the store, laughing, holding his back, his usually-dour or blank face lit up with excitement. Eyes shining with magic. Real magic.
Here, in the New Lands, where Gothica could feel her own mana pool diminishing with each passing breath. But Cormeng’s descendant didn’t even seem to realize it was there. When he spoke, it was the language of magic. Words not even [Mages] knew. Not Latin, but something else.
And…he was chasing a little Drake girl around the shop. She was giggling like a kid. Laughing, and no one in Mudhole had ever heard her laugh. Eyes shining as she ran, dodging around him, weaving through the shelves of things meant to help people, that he bought and stocked without ever asking for a compliment.
Two steps—and when he was about to catch her, she ran up onto a third step that hadn’t been there before. A glowing, blue circle of light. It seemed to splash, as if Silppie’s foot were a raindrop landing on water, and she ran higher.
“Careful, careful! Remember, rateík, calcne, don’t stop chanting!”
“Ra-teík, calc-ne, oothün—”
Each word was like a tiny drumbeat. And the girl, with that flawless grace of children, was speaking perfectly on-beat. Each step carrying her on the magical platforms, around the shop, until she nearly bit her tongue. Then she shouted, and Doren dove to catch her, landing face-first on the floor…
Gothica caught Silppie on her aura. The shadows whirling to cushion the Drake girl’s fall. Then she was rolling around, eyes shining.
“I did it! I did it!”
“You did! Remember, it’s a secret. I—I have to teach you other spells! And get you more components. Why, why, you could run across the Mudflats with that! Cormeng said he once chased down a pirate ship with that spell!”
“It’s magic! Magic, Doren! How is it possible?”
He was wiping at his forehead as he got up, checking the door he’s sealed so no one could see him gifting the girl with magic. Gothica just watched him as his face lit up. Then fell a second.
“It’s possible because that’s how magic is, Silppie.”
“But no one can cast magic here unless they’re super [Mages], Doren. Alchemist potions don’t work, and artifacts fail…”
He sat down with her on the floor, and Doren gazed around his shop. There was a pride there Gothica had never seen.
“Well, Silppie, this is a secret, but I trust you. So I’m going to tell you something. [Mages] don’t know everything. My great ancestor, Cormeng, found a magic all of his own. And it’s…wonderful. It’s not perfect. He wasn’t perfect. But they should have respected him more. I should have.”
He was gazing down at his hands. Flexing them, and Gothica remembered him talking about his stupid family. His aunt who died of a heart attack which led him to inherit this place.
The only man in the entire world who knew this magic was sitting there. An [Archmage] of one. And why not call him that?
The world had plenty of damn [Archmages] as far as Gothica could tell. The Death of Magic was one, Archmage Eldavin was what everyone called a real one, Chieftain Rags said Az’kerash was still up and about. It felt like every other day someone pulled an [Archmage] out of the toilet.
But there was only one person who knew Cormeng’s magic, and he was right there. And he, like Erin, took a power so few had ever witnessed and gave it to those he found deserving.
“Remember, you have to be very secret, Silppie. I’ll teach you more spells. But promise?”
“I promise.”
He smiled at her, then noticed Gothica watching him.
“Gothica. You—would you like to learn a spell?”
“Me?”
“Only if you want to. I know you know darkness magic, and it’s probably so much better, but I…I should have studied more. I’m sure there are a few you could use. All his great magic is beyond me. I never studied. It’s so hard. But this?”
He was gazing into Mudhole. Gazing, and Gothica could imagine Silppie running across the muddy waters, leaving this hellhole behind. Doren whispered.
“I’ll give it out. It doesn’t cost much. I just need to buy some more reagents and make it easier to teach. It took a whole week, and Silppie was faster than I ever was. Then maybe…there’s so many spells that could be useful out there.”
Magic for the New Lands. Gothica just sat there. Later, she’d probably have to tell Chieftain Rags and stuff. And he’d be important and maybe be too busy to hang out with her. But she saw it and savored it.
She’d seen it first. Gothica was proud of that. Just her…and maybe bonus credits to Lieutenant Capsha. But that was mucky love stuff, and Gothica didn’t truck with that.
 
——
 
Doren was highly stressed after that, but Silppie didn’t tell anyone, and neither did Gothica, it seemed. He asked them to give him time to work on the spell and figure out Cormeng’s magic himself.
The thing was, Doren had never looked into battle casting or how to prepare a mobile version of the spell. He’d taken the lazy route like most of Cormeng’s descendants, and he could set up a pre-packaged, static spell, but now he was highly motivated to teach someone a better version.
“The simplest way will be to make a spellbook.”
“Wat.”
Gothica stopped sipping on a boba tea and eyed him. Doren explained.
“You see, it’s simple. You just make a contained magical vessel—it doesn’t have to be that advanced since it’s just the form of the spell. [Mage] spellbooks work the same way. Then you’d flip it open to the right page, activate it while chanting and using the components—”
“So an actual spellbook? That’s so…cool!”
Most [Mages] only used them as refreshers, notebooks, but Doren had read they had been used as spell foci before wands and staves became all the rage. He could just see it now. Flip a page open, grab components, and blam, spellcasting. You’d have to re-etch the rune each time you wanted to cast the spell so what did you do?
Have fifty copies of each spell ready to go. Immensely annoying, but then you could fire them off. True, it’d need a big spellbook for the multi-page runes…Doren bet Cormeng had used belt pouches and probably been able to grab what he needed on the fly. Activate multiple spells simultaneously? That’d give a traditional caster a nasty shock.
“It won’t beat traditional mage magic. Unless you’re being suppressed or there’s no magic about.”
“Yah, can’t imagine where that would come in handy.”
He just grinned at her. He felt like he was flying, over the moon. This would do it! This would save them!
…But what happened when he gave it to Thorssin or Hwalt and they told people where they’d gotten it from? Even if they didn’t, people would see it, and Lyonette had said they were noticing him.
His stomach hurt, but he knew he had to do it. This was his way of honoring Cormeng. This was his deed, the one they could punish him for, but would save Mudhole. Change the world.
“I always wanted to be this kind of person, you know. Someone grand and powerful. I dreamed of being a mage, actually. Of following Cormeng’s example. I even practiced. Then I tried to duel a Wistram [Mage]. Barely Level 15. He put me in the healer’s clinic for three weeks. I just wanted a shortcut, and I thought Cormeng had to have one to make up for not having levels. But that’s not what this is. It’s hard work. It’s just hard work and thinking differently, and what you get is something no one else has.”
He stared at his hands, wishing he’d known this forty years ago. Well, this was good enough. Gothica was just staring at him. Not sneering. He kept waiting for her to make fun of him, but she patted him on the arm.
“You’ll be really important. Then idiots will try to kidnap you. Don’t worry. I’ll shield you with my heart of immortal darkness. Promise.”
He smiled at her. For a moment, despite the stink of Mudhole, a glowfly on the back of his neck sucking up his sweat, it was perfect.
Then the door opened, and Thorssin burst into the room.
“Doren! They’re gone!”
The shopkeeper looked up, and Gothica sighed. For she could see it too.
 
——
 
Three events of great importance happened that day. The first was Mudhole in an uproar over the disappearance of six hunters.
Thorssin had seen it himself. They’d been combing the mudflats for larger fish, a kind of crawling catfish which was gross but plentiful in meat and could go above the water or in it.
“It was right across from where the water gets ultra deep. If we’re here, it’s sort of to the left and down—there’s these standing stones. I thought some of ‘em looked even like archways, but no one goes there because it’s so deep. I saw six of the [Hunters] chasing the fishcrawler onto the bank. Then one of ‘em stumbled into a stone archway, and they all—vanished.”
“Vanished? As in they were hit by a spell?”
“No, vanished like…like they were somewhere else. I swear I smelled something different.”
Doren was consulting his journals.
“When they vanished, did you feel nauseous? Did your ears pop?”
“Earholes, and no. I did feel ill, but it was more like, you know, when you miss a step? It was like that, but—”
“In reverse?”
Thorssin stopped. People were in Doren’s shop, gossiping, and apparently the MSF was cordoning off the site. Investigations were being launched by all parties. Lavroisse, everyone.
“Yeah, how’d you know?”
Doren lowered the journal, heart beating too fast.
“Because that’s the sign of a dimensional fold. Not teleportation. Teleportation is air displacement unless it’s done well. If you feel movement, it’s because you’re being shifted and you’re off the ground—but that’s only if you’re in it. It sounds like they activated a gateway. A real portal spell. It sounds…like they ran into the Crossroads of Izril.”
Rikka dropped the harpoon she was cleaning. Everyone, Silppie, Gothica, Hwalt, stared at him. Doren glanced around.
“It makes sense, doesn’t it?”
“But—but that’s the <Heroic Quest> that [Innkeeper] posted! That’s not something we’d just find…why standing stones? How can magic work like that?”
“Maybe it spontaneously generated.”
More stares. Doren shrugged, self-conscious.
“That’s how it works. Space wrinkles, and the stones might have literally been yanked into position. If the Crossroads of Izril really are a complete subdimension, that kind of magic is a law that enforces itself on the world. It’s Tier 9 magic.”
“But the passphrase. Don’t you need that?”
Thorssin was grasping at the magical logic of it all, but Doren scratched at his chin.
“Not to open a gateway, necessarily. Unless they were locked to the Passphrase of Imlerith. It could be that’s for something inside the Crossroads of Izril. It might span the entirety of Izril.”
More stares. He held up the journal.
“Look, it’s one of the lost mysteries of Izril. My ancestor had notes on it. If it’s that…”
Then Mudhole had just found a way into one of the most important things in the New Lands. Everyone was abuzz with excitement. And worry; the hunters hadn’t been found, and the MSF was very, very reluctant to get near the gates. They were asking for volunteers to scout it out with ropes.
Thorssin and the others went back to see what was going on, and Doren’s own mind was spinning. He thought this was actually a good thing. Why, the news of the Crossroads might overshadow any odd magic. You could say it was from there. Or maybe it’d get Manus to fast-track Wyverns or a Courier over there.
He was just about to write a [Message] when he realized this might put him on The List for good. What if…he told Lady Rie? Or the Gnolls?
“Er, Cormeng’s. Open a door to the Meeting of Tribes.”
He muttered under his breath. He was about to get up and open the door to get someone’s attention—there was usually someone he could flag down—when the door sprang open.
Hrawr burst into the shop so fast he nearly knocked Doren over. He caught the man, patted him down, and then shook him.
“Doren! Dorendorendoren, how many have you got we’ll buy them all!”
“What? What? What?”
The man was so badly shaken—literally—he was insensible until a second Gnoll leapt in and hit Hrawr.
“You idiot, subtlety! Shopkeeper—is anyone around? Good.”
“They just left. To investigate the Crossroads of Izril. Possibly.”
Doren was pleased to see Hrawr’s jaw drop, but that still didn’t slow the two Gnolls’ roll. Svrei was the second Gnoll, and he had a wand at his side. He was one of the would-be [Mages] training in the Meeting of Tribes, and his fur was standing on end.
“Doren, we just found the most amazing—! You know those bugs, the ones you put in that lovely lantern?”
“Glowbugs? Sure. What about them?”
“They’re—get this, Doren. They’re filled with magic!”
Hrawr was grinning, leaping up and down. Svrei waited. Doren nodded.
“Right.”
The Gnoll threw up his paws.
“They’re more magical than Luminary Fireflies, Doren!”
“I know. Are they useful?”
The two Gnolls exchanged a glance. It occurred to Doren that they hadn’t known. It’d been as obvious as looking to him, but he’d assumed most [Alchemists] could do what Cormeng’s magic did on sight.
Another victory for old Cormeng. He wondered if the bugs would work in Cormeng’s magic, and his heart leapt as Svrei leaned over.
“Doren, we didn’t think the insects had that much magic in them! For the New Lands? Shaman Theikha had a hunch, and she’s having us test every bug. Everything from the ordinary ones on up. But these…Doren, they have so much natural light magic in them that the [Alchemists] think we can use them in countless potions and spells! One of the [Hunters] made a kind of paste and rubbed it on an arrow, and it’s so damn bright! If we concentrate the effect, we could make Flash Arrows or—”
“Or just see in the dark. One cub ate them, and now her pee glows.”
Hrawr put in. Doren blinked. That was pretty powerful. Normally, you didn’t manifest magical effects just from ingesting raw…
“So you want more?”
“We want all of it. And not just dead ones. We want to breed them, but we worry they need something magical in the New Lands. Can you get more? You said people catch them.”
Doren stared at them. Then he went into his back rooms. He came out with the gigantic jar of glowbugs, half still alive. Hrawr fainted into Svrei’s arms. The other Gnoll dropped him. The [Barbarian] leapt up with a grin.
“You’ll be rich, and we’ll have one of the first new great alchemical resources of the era!”
He reached for the jar, and Doren leaned back.
“Absolutely. How much?”
“Excuse me?”
“How much for it? You just told me it’s valuable. It seems to me I’m the only seller around and you really want it.”
The two Gnolls’ jaws dropped. But the shopkeeper was no [Innkeeper] who had no savvy for money. They began laughing, trying to grab the jar, and protesting they’d settle it later—then they ran to get a [Shaman].
Doren was beaming. He was wondering how to work with this—if he told Laniver, then Lavroisse would hoard the stuff. But he had to reward Silppie and get it somehow. Maybe say the lanterns were a huge hit…
More and more excitement. The Gnolls swept back in with a [Shaman] who kept hitting them for jacking up the prices. Doren negotiated hard, then let them have half the jar; the other half was going to Goblinhome. He was waiting for Gothica, who would probably hate this challenge to her authority of the night, when the door opened.
“Hello, Doren.”
Lieutenant Capsha appeared. She paused to wipe her boots and took off her helmet, but she didn’t immediately go over to the counter. She just stood there a second, breathing in and out. Then she walked over.
“Capsha, what a day, isn’t it? I just had some good news—er, just businesswise. How’s…?”
She leaned on the counter, resting her head on his shoulder. He jumped.
“Capsha?”
“I’m…sorry. It’s been a long day. The MSF was up all last night, and then we got word about the disappearances. I’m just—Ancestors.”
Her voice was a bit muffled. Then her head came up, and she was wiping at her grimy face. Rainwater was running down her scales, but she was smiling.
“They never promised us help. You know? Captain Cithoc was pulling all the strings, and I’m—I’m not really in Manus’ army, Doren. I—we weren’t telling the truth. We told everyone, Drakes especially, they were coming. But Manus said it was so far and they couldn’t commit. Same with the Merchant’s Guild. Merchant Lavroisse claimed they were on the way, but I don’t know if he was lying.”
“I…I see. But something’s changed?”
She just shook her head. Smiling as she cried.
“Not yet. But they’ve got to now. They’ve got to, Doren. It was just last night when we found it. In the dredging.”
“In the what? Oh, wait, you dug something up?”
She was nodding.
“We thought it was the sunken wagons at first. But it’s big. Way too big to be just the wagons. So we kept digging, and it was damned hard. We have to clear the muck and put down walls and…but we finally got a good look at it. It’s a huge structure, Doren. Something magical. We can tell it’s magic. Dead gods, there’s even cloth down there. It’s like the muck hasn’t touched it! It must have been on the seafloor! It could be from Tiernas!”
They’d found something under Mudhole. Something so important that Merchant Lavroisse wanted to cover it up, and the MSF was going to report it to Manus right away. Three huge events in a single day.
Capsha just kept leaning against Doren.
“I just…I want to get out of here. Leave and never come back. But this way at least it might be worth something. All the dying and suffering. Doren, don’t tell anyone or I’ll get in trouble. I just wanted—”
She leapt up and saluted when the door opened. Silppie ran in, and Doren beamed at her.
“Silppie! What’s new?”
The Drake girl halted and stared up at him. Her eyes were huge in her head, and her claws were trembling as they held her cloak. Doren’s smile faded, and Capsha focused. The girl spoke.
“They’re all dead. All six [Hunters].”
And then he felt the world begin to slip a bit.
 
——
 
Six [Hunters], varying species, four Drakes, one Gnoll, one Human. Each one killed in a moment. A blow from behind had snapped necks or broken backs.
The scout who’d gone into the portal had come back out moments later. They were shaken, Mudhole was shaken, and everyone was on high alert. There were no footprints nor signs of what had been there. But two details made Doren shiver.
“Their belt pouches were gone. Gone…and someone arranged them in a line. There was a word written in the ground. That’s what they said. We think it was a word. It looks like this.”
There was a copy floating around Mudhole. Doren peered at it. It could be…no, it looked like words. But no one knew what it was.
He…had some inkling, but had to show it to the Gnolls, then Gothica when they came by. It was an unsettling night despite the news about the glowbugs. And it was the Goblins who got back to him first.
Goblin memory. Gothica put something on the counter.
“This from Taganchiel. He says it probably accurate. Hurt his head.”
“What? What is it? This is nonsense.”
Gothica shrugged.
“That’s what I said. But he said it what it translates to. He sure it old Drake. Really old, like the kind they find in Liscor’s crypts.”
“The what?”
“Eh, too hard to explain. Old home. I knew it look familiar; they got other words like this. Anyways. This what it said.”
Doren lifted the paper and sounded the word out.
“Ma-ha-dak. That’s…is that a place? A name?”
Gothica didn’t know. The Gnolls came back with the exact same translation, but neither group’s living memory knew what it corresponded to. No Drake settlement. No person.
That was the first oddity. But there were answers to that. Just not the kind Doren liked.
 
——
 
He was preparing a few magical spells and teaching Silppie them in secret. She was a good student, and if she got them, he knew others would. Doren was still on the fence about teaching the others.
He wanted to, but there was a lot of attention on Mudhole right now. A lot. Still, he’d bought as many reagents as he could and had to fight some weird Gnoll [Alchemist] and a Stitch-girl in Pallass to get the supply. [Alchemists] were crazy.
Lieutenant Capsha and Laniver were regulars in his store these days. They were running [Messages] to their respective groups, the Merchant’s Guild and Lavroisse’s people and Manus. They were nominally enemies, but both seemed more interested in getting out of Mudhole, and the big discovery of the Crossroads of Izril and whatever they’d dredged up was their ticket.
“If only we’d found one. The damn Merchant’s Guild wants to know what we found. As if the Crossroads weren’t enough!”
Laniver complained loudly over some of Hwalt’s rum. The man was oddly generous today. Perhaps it was the hope in the camp—or the nerves of the Crossroads. Both were in plenty. Capsha was also drinking a shot and nodded.
“The same for Manus. But they want the Crossroads. I was just told we might get a Wyvern flight out…only, there are wild Wyverns and worse in the skies. Plus, now they’re telling us to keep this operationally secret! Secret from who?”
Suddenly, Mudhole got daily updates, and Doren kept getting encoded messages he didn’t know how to properly handle. Lady Rie was sending attractive young women to ask if he’d like to go with them to dinner…and the Gnolls and Goblins were cautiously sending their people around to take notes.
Actually, he’d had a number of Drakes and Humans in his store this morning who’d wanted to stick around. One had even tried to hide before he switched over to Mudhole.
The cat was out of the bag, but hopefully there was a giant pile of catnip that overshadowed Doren. Still, not all catnip was good in this analogy.
Doren had just handed over yet another black paper report Capsha was opening. When all correspondence was marked ‘top secret, do not open without maximum clearance, priority’, it all began to lose value. She was sighing as she got up to ferry this over to Captain Cithoc when she froze.
“That’s…”
“Are they actually flying over?”
Laniver’s head rose hopefully, but Capsha just studied the missive. She hesitated, then handed it to Doren. He blinked and then read it.
There were code words on top of the missive, the [Strategist] sending it, dates, and little symbols worked into the very expensive paper that had apparently been teleported to the Mage’s Guild. But the contents of the missive were actually quite short. They read, in white ink—
 
MANUS HGH CMMD—
PRIORITY CAPTAIN CITHOC. Disregard all orders to investigate possible ‘Crossroads of Izril’ gateways immediately. 
Crossroads of Izril gateway is now confirmed. Specialist teams will prepare to secure and fortify area. Expect arrival within 1-2 weeks tops. Do not allow other forces to claim gateway.
HIGHER PRIORITY BY ORDER OF THE SECURITY COUNCIL. Do not allow any forces to activate the gateway. They may secure it, but transit into the Crossroads at this time is to be halted at all costs. 
Any suspicion of the being known as Mahadak exiting the Crossroads of Izril must be reported at once. Coordinates will be attempted to be confirmed for possible remote bombardment if mana drain allows. Do not let the portal activate from your side.
FOR YOUR EYES ONLY, APPROVAL BY DRAGONSPEAKER LUCIVA. The being known as Mahadak dates back to our records from before the Creler Wars. It was a known phenomenon in the Crossroads of Izril, akin to a ghost story—but one that was known to be extant. We do not know how many Drakes were killed by it until the Crossroads closed. Do not let it out, Captain.
END OF PRIORITY MESSAGE. BURN AND DUST ASHES.
 
Laniver was reading over Doren’s shoulder. He appeared sick. Doren felt like throwing up.
“Th-that—that’s not a—you don’t just have ancient weapons of war waiting around for thousands of years. It has to be dead. Right?”
“Maybe it is.”
That comment came from Gothica. Doren was about to tell her this was overblown. It might be a coincidence or the words had been there from before, when she looked at him.
Gothica who had grown up with Skinner and Facestealer. Gothica whose tribe had fought a Mortemdefieir Titan. She looked scared.
“I hate Old Things. Worse than [Archmages] in toilets. Keep popping up.”
No one understood that, but the warning…Capsha grabbed the letter, and Laniver ran out into the rain too.
“Well, well, they’re coming to get you all. You’ll be away from here, and it’ll be secured by the Walled City.”
That was what Doren tried to focus on. Thorssin nodded as Silppie nervously played with the spellbook he’d made for her, a slim tome. She stared at the rune she was etching there, and Rikka glanced at it.
It was Hwalt who didn’t join in the attempts to raise spirits. He took a longer drink from his flask.
“The Crossroad’s not the only damned thing here.”
“What do you mean, Hwalt?”
The man hadn’t been in the store as much recently. He was more filled out, healthier from eating better and getting over his illness, but he seemed a bit sick today.
He was also muddier than normal. And sweaty? He rolled his shoulders.
“I’ve been digging, Doren. That bastard, Merchant Lavroisse? He’s hiring everyone to do the damn dredging. It’s good pay. Not that he pays us in more than food and supplies. You even get luxuries.”
“I see. But the job’s hard, or it’s unsafe?”
Doren could imagine the dredging being perilous if the walls let the muck back in. Or just backbreaking, but Hwalt just spat.
“That’s not it. It’s what they’re digging up. You…you can’t see it. But Thorssin, you seen it? Rikka? Anyone?”
Everyone shook their heads, except for Silppie, who just looked up once. Hwalt rubbed at his shoulders.
“It ain’t a building. At least, not one normal. It’s wide as can be. Huge. Bigger than all of Mudhole, it feels like. And there’s some kinda tarp…it’s so damn tough we can’t get through it. So we have to excavate around, and that’ll take ages. Mud don’t stick to it, though. I—I don’t like it, Doren.”
“Don’t like the work?”
“I don’t like what we’re digging up.”
Hwalt’s voice was grating. He took another drink, and now Doren saw his eyes were bloodshot. And he was shivering but not with a fever.
“It’s off. It’s magic, sure, but it makes me uneasy the longer I look at it. Me and some of the seadogs and one of the adventurers—we all don’t like it. Reminds me of wrecks in the ocean. The kind not even Drowned Folk go near. Lavroisse won’t hear it. He says he’s Pheislant’s son, and he knows more about the sea than I’ve ever dreamed. But he won’t bury it where it belongs.”
A crawl went up Doren’s spine. He saw Gothica’s eyes focusing on Hwalt, and if she hadn’t liked Manus’ warning, she was truly unsettled now.

Or desperate people. He felt it again, like Hwalt’s first visit. This had been fun for Doren. He’d even made money. But for them…
They have nothing when they leave here. Lavroisse is desperate. He needs money. Capsha has nothing but her levels. When they go…

Cost…and Capsha was so worried about High Command. Perhaps on being punished for taking Manus’ authority. Neither one had anything from the New Lands, really. They’d lost so much. It was Doren who had the easy life. He was the shopkeeper.
That’s why Lavroisse hates me. He sees how easy it is. He’s cursing himself for being a fool. I’ve got the connections, the money…

A crazy one.
A stupid one. It’d ruin so much. But—but—
He stared into the darkness, and he could not see this building which they were digging for. But he felt it, somehow. Like it was affecting him, even here. Doren clenched his pudgy fists. Then he did something…
Crazy.
In the darkness, a watching Goblin smiled sadly.

Madness. He had five [Alchemists] try to attack him when he left Cormeng’s to deliver [Messages], and Lady Rie almost pressganged him into an audience with the Unseen Emperor. The other half…

It was his great ancestor’s.

“This is called Platform Step.”

‘Manifestation’.

Not even when morning came.

Mudhole was filled with people lined up for The Grimless Carnival. They stood on the sinking boardwalks, smiling.

Laughing, but not in the way of…regular performers.

At you.

The Ringmaster.

For his eyes.

Tiernas.

Just gone…

Useful boosts. Nothing that touched on Cormeng’s magic specifically. They would gain other chant-spells, more traditional [Conjurer]’s magic, and if they chose, learn Cormeng’s magic. But the Grand Design would never speak one of his spells to them.
That done, it sat there and watched. A great crafter of its own, who had created wonders the likes of which the world saw seldom. Tipping its hat to the other artisans who had made their own works separate from its great efforts. For the first time, the Grand Design abided.
Regretfully, with all the saudade, the wistful desires for what could be—
Proud in its decision.
Then it watched Doren continue running his shop.
 
——
 
More guests. Some made him smile. Some made him duck. Some were annoying, entitled, or desperate, and he dealt with them, not always perfectly. Seldom perfectly, really.
He was just the shopkeeper, and he probably came off as a pretty dour man. Blank-faced, occasionally with a wandering eye.
Quick to remind you that theft was not allowed and who haggled like a [Miser] over prices. Who’d pester you with questions about if you’d seen some standing stones, drag you into a conversation. A pushy, mercurial fellow of a shopkeeper.
Not a bad one. If you were hungry, he’d ask you if you had anything to sell. Why, he’d take some yellow beetles for some food. And if you got him talking, if he liked you, his face would light up a bit, and if he laughed, he’d laugh like a donkey revving up—but then he’d reveal a bit more of the man and shop that was hidden here.
And it was magic all the way down.
However, the thing most of his guests who got to know him came away with was that Doren was a sad man.
Not a ‘sad man’ in that he had nothing going for him. But someone who had suffered a terrible loss. Someone who had gone the distance and seen the cost.
A shopkeeper who had weighed the price and paid it. Who might pay it again.
He fit his store, which seemed to be growing around him, for all it had been so badly damaged and used. The first true heir of Cormeng.
That was Doren Issk. He stood, blank-faced, behind his counter, putting on smiles. Until one day, his door opened.
Then he looked up from sorting coins and paused.
A true smile slowly broke over his face as his guest stopped in their tracks. Doren slowly left his counter and came out, arms spread. He paused to wipe at his eyes as the figure retreated, but Doren didn’t let them run. He smiled at the nervous [Innkeeper] holding a rolling pin behind the guest and the attractive staff ready to let fly with their weapons…then hugged the familiar face.
“Hello, Toren.”
That was how he searched, and sometimes…
Found them.
