Chapter 135: Supposedly Heavenward

Jack rubbed his chin, pretending to consider his dad’s offer. Internally, he felt a combination of victory and dread. He was definitely going to take the offer. But he asked, “Where is it exactly?”

“Platform Heavenward,” his dad replied. “Highly independent.”

“And private.”

His dad shrugged. “That it is. One has to be, with the eyes that are otherwise on us.”

“Mmn. Well, I can’t argue with having contacts like that, Dad. Let’s do it. Can’t promise anything else, but I’ll listen.”

Freddy grinned and hopped out of his seat to pat Jack’s shoulder amiably. “That’a’boy! It’s a golden opportunity, son, you’ll see for yourself. No need to take my word for it when so many others are involved. Not that this whole thing is free of risk, mind you. But the best things we can get out of life rarely are, are they?”

Jack put on a faint smile. “You’ve got that right, Mr. Frederick Laker. Risks and high costs, that’s usually the rub.”

His dad winced and gave him a mildly offended, admonishing look for using his full name, but otherwise didn’t comment, instead patting his son’s shoulder again as he said, “Come on, Jack. We can leave immediately; I have everything ready.”

Jack nodded and rose as his father led him and his two goons out, everyone moving at a brisk pace. The ‘pleasant’ guide of the arcology took one look at Freddy leading Jack and simply nodded, turning away to leave without another word.

“Looks like your new allies come with perks,” Jack commented.

“That they do, son,” Freddy replied without looking back. “That they do.”

Here we go. ‘Betraying’ my dad to save him from these psychos — and help clear parasites out of the collective system. I hope I’m doing the right thing; no matter how much I tell myself that and rationalize, it doesn’t feel like it.

They took an exceptionally generic box-like six-seater van out from the arcology, as it flew up and away into the neutral airspace between platforms. Fortunately, Platform Latterbloom was not in some contiguous independent space as existed by deliberate arrangement in certain zones of the Farmer’s Alliance or the Industrial Fellowship. As such, as soon as they exited the private space, a wealth of information was instantly shared and processed by Memoria from Jack’s recent encounter.

Memoria was excited as she contacted him through direct thought. <This is perfect! The eagerness of a father to bring his son into enlightenment has caused spectacular overreach! — they’re doomed! Just remember how false it is, Jack. Your father, and most of the people you’ll meet, are infected victims who need quarantine and treatment. Don’t drink or eat anything they put in front of you. Do take note, these people tend to carry non-metallic stunners. Otherwise, keep doing what you’re doing. A hellstorm of teams are standing by. Don’t forget to test the Neex channel once you’re touched down. No ‘sending’ until then, and don’t even reply to this message.>

Jack had no reaction other than to internally assure himself — yet again — that this was the best he could do for his father in the predicament.

The seating was such that Jack and his father were in the ‘two and three’ spots, with a driver and three other goons filling up the rest. Freddy had on a pair of smartglasses he was operating with eye and finger movements, no doubt reporting to someone.

Jack took a moment to do his best at passively getting an impression of what metal his dad had on him, without chancing the use of channeled memorite. It wasn’t much, but it was something. The smartglasses certainly had a special ‘feel’ to them. He noted the particulars of his jewelry, too. A gold watch; two rings, one on each hand.

They came to hover a significant distance outside from a small platform on the flank of some of the more obvious floating platform territories of the Farmer’s Alliance, such as the one where everything had begun for Jack, Overflow Three, which was visible as a blob in the distance. Jack’s mental map of things as a pilot was too good not to basically know where everything was, even without the huge central landmark that was the tower.

At a dead stop, almost certainly at the border of Heavenward’s official airspace, his father pulled out what looked like a fat wand-like instrument and said apologetically, “Gotta scan you real quick, son. Protocol. Hold out your arms.”

Jack nodded and did as instructed; the wand was made to hover over every part of him.

His dad frowned and asked incredulously, “You got biological mods done?”

Jack didn’t refrain from wincing. “Yeah… some of the shit I got into, I decided I needed to improve my physicality, we’ll say.”

Nodding slowly, his dad lowered the wand as he chewed his lip. He leaned in and spoke low, “Look, don’t bring it up to them. It’s not disliked or unheard of, but they might wanna know precise details out of caution. I’ll have to poke more discreetly about that another day. I don’t want to go there right now.”

“You got it, Pops.”

“And I need your phone before we go in.”

“Why is that?”

“No pictures, video, outside signals, and so on, not for initial outsiders. That’ll change later.”

Showing reluctance, Jack pulled out his phone and handed it over. “I guess I can’t blame them for being cautious, with some of the things you said.”

“Thanks, Jack. This will be worth it in the end, trust me.” He pocketed the phone and then did something with his smartglasses.

After a minute or so of waiting, the driver called, “I’m cleared. Headed in.” The vessel momentarily began toward the platform.

The instant they crossed that threshold, Jack felt a vague vibe of ‘wrongness’ within himself, like every fiber of his being was suddenly wanting to revolt and go back the way he’d come. It was much like he was a magnet headed for his opposite pole. ‘I don’t belong here,’ was the message his essence was sending out.

It was quite uncomfortable, but tolerable. He imagined it had something to do with the contract interactions and Memoria’s means of tracking, with Jack her ‘island’ of light in the dark unknown. This would be the vector he would ‘scream down’ to signal the cavalry, if he didn’t have the Neex channel.

He was suddenly tempted to call for cavalry the instant they touched down, to maximize the chances of securing his father…

Can I even implicate them further in the first place? I dunno…

Quickly, the complex he’d already seen in the grainy video came into proper view. The platform was mostly drab buildings hugging the inside of high walls as borders, with the massive mansion otherwise dwarfing everything else, located in the center, and with sizable, well-kept green landscape and farmland making something of a donut.

They finally touched down on the roof of the mansion, upon which dozens of other vessels rested.

It was time to contact Neex. He sent a simple mental message. <Heya. Testing, testing, one, two, three.>

Neex was quick to reply. <Reading loud and clear. All is well?>

Here was the moment. Jack made his choice. <All is well. Standby.>

Ultimately, Jack was simply too curious. He might meet someone it would be useful intel to spot; someone who otherwise might get away during a raid, no less. That was in addition to any incriminating discussions or attempts to drug him. If they doped a drink they put in front of him, that was all the better to prove criminal intent.

Leaving all but one of the goons, Jack’s dad led him down an elevator to the ground floor, which showcased a massive and beautiful entrance hall, or grand foyer, with lots of white and gold coloring, including the wallpaper itself. A crystalline chandelier hovered over an ornate table sitting on a fancy rug, and a fresh bouquet of white and pink flowers bloomed out of a vase in the center. The iconic double spiral staircase led to the second level, and statues peeked out of wall alcoves. Servants and guards were ever visible.

Everywhere one turned was opulence.

The parlor was on one of the wings of the complex, and was quite different than most of it, abandoning the gold for a more rustic aesthetic, though no less decorated and grandiose, with dozens of couches and chairs amidst a prominently polished, red-brown interior. Certainly, there was plenty of space for multiple, separate conversations. There was a large, stocked bar along one side with a bartender. A smattering of folk were present, some who noticed their arrival and others who pretended not to. They were varyingly casually-dressed and fancily-dressed, yet always well-dressed. Few wore non-tailored clothing.

On the far side were extensive glass windows showing outdoors, artificially, gently lit up despite the prevalent gloom. Jack knew by both the look of the glass and from seeing from the other side to a degree, that they were totally mirrored on the other side.

Some sort of VIP persona in fancy mahogany-colored attire was entertaining a few others across the room. He was very tall, looming over everyone else, and had a distinctive, perfect face that Jack detected was probably due to alteration via body sculpting. His smile, as he told some story to the others, was somehow incredibly uncanny and creepy. Soulless. Virtually everyone present turned toward the entrance to some degree, but this man was the only one who legitimately did not so much as glance at them. He was far too laser-focused, too tunnel-visioned on himself, or at least his immediate goal.

Mini chimed. <That’s Zachariah Heavenward in the flesh.>

The owner himself. Nice.

“This place is nearly as old as New Humanity,” Freddy commented as they headed for the bar. “Old enough to feel like time slows down to something more naturally suitable.”

“It’s nice,” Jack replied, finding it hard not to be on edge.

His dad glanced over at him and chuckled. “Relax, Jack. No pressure needed. Just be yourself.”

Oh, you wouldn’t like that, Dad, trust me.

The bartender came over with raised eyebrows, and Freddy leaned over the counter, throwing a smile Jack’s way. “Indulge me to try a mocktail, son?”

Jack shrugged, internally reminding himself not to actually ingest it, though he felt it was important to accept. My dad might not even know what they put in these. “Sure, go ahead and hit me.”

“Two non-alcoholic margaritas, if you would, John.”

The bartender nodded and promptly got to work constructing the drinks.

“Who do we have here, Frederick?” came a middle-aged woman’s voice, in a thick, posh Origin accent. Jack turned to see a striking woman in a glittery silver dress with a plunging neckline displaying ample cleavage. Her extensive platinum blonde hair was in a fancy updo, and the rest of her accessories and demeanor screamed loudly of her wealth. The massive, rounded glass cradled in her palm screamed of her inebriation, and the blatant way she looked Jack up and down screamed of her horniness.

Mini chimed. <Cougar alert! Be strong, Jack!>

Jack felt only exasperation. “Yeah, no shit, Mini! Not helpful, thanks.”

“Annette,” Freddy greeted with a too-neutral tone, “this is my son, Jack. Jack, this is Annette.” His lack of further description was pregnant with the unspoken desire to fill in the details he could not while in her presence.

“Call me, Anne, please, darling,” Annette drawled indulgently, and she thrust her hand practically in Jack’s face, in a way that pretty much demanded he take it, not to mention kiss it. “Charmed, naturally.”

Trying not to be too awkward, Jack took her hand and touched his lips to the top, smiling politely as he released it.

Holding his eyes, she pulled her hand away only very slowly.

Jack cleared his throat and said, “Charmed to meet you as well, Anne. And may I say you’re looking very lovely tonight.”

His dad’s warning glance said he should not have made that comment.

Annette beamed, nodding and taking a sip of her drink. “Indeed, you may. My husband — as usual — is not around to do it, so someone should, I think.”

Wow. “Well, I do hope he wouldn’t take exception to that fact.”

Annette leaned her head back and laughed richly, waving her free hand at him. “Oh, no, I assure you, he doesn’t. We’re very modern that way, and in… greater depths of expression, so to speak.” Suggestion was flagrant in her eyes. “On that note, might I say you cut quite the figure yourself, Jack. You have a presence that rather commands attention, begging it to be given.”

His dad’s glance was now more along the lines of surprise shifting into acceptance, as if Jack was confidently doing just what he wanted to.

Jack wore a smile at that, as he willed himself not to sweat. How does this keep happening to me?! “I-”

“Your drinks, sirs,” came the call of the bartender.

So relieved was Jack to have the excuse to turn and take the drink that he almost ended up drinking the damn thing. But at the last moment, he stopped himself and asked his dad, “Pardon, but was there someone I needed to meet here?”

When his dad met his eyes, after taking a sip of his drink, he seemed to pick up the obvious subtext of the question. “Oh, yes, I’m afraid it’s rather urgent at that.” He looked at Annette apologetically. “I’m afraid you’ll have to excuse us for now, Annette. Business before pleasure. But I do promise to turn him loose at the nearest available opportunity.”

Annette smiled and nodded to him. “Of course. Business is business, after all. But I will hold you to that.” Her eyes shifted to Jack’s, and she winked. “I can enjoy the view from afar for a while.”

Once they had strolled far enough away, his dad leaned in and muttered, “Why the hell were you getting her panties in a twist if you weren’t game?”

Jack sighed and rolled his neck. “I, uh… I think I do it without really thinking. I guess. It’s a problem.”

His dad shook his head and chuckled. “Yeah, it is. Well, fair enough; good luck with that. Actually, now that I think about it, you making a little connection with Annette could-”

“I have a girlfriend. So, ah. No.” Even if I didn’t, I don’t think I want to go that deep under cover. My last name is not ‘Bond.’

His dad frowned at him. “Mmn. Well, I know from experience that can be even more complicated. But no matter, things will be as they will be.” He looked over at the party around the big honcho, whom he was still entertaining. “No idea when that’ll be through with, so let’s just bite the bullet and interrupt, eh?”

“Sounds good,” Jack replied.

Along the way, Jack hung back for a moment and pretended to stumble discreetly, spilling some of the drink, deftly without getting any on himself. He looked around awkwardly, and a servant was moving his way, waving a hand at him in silent rebuke, pointing at himself and mouthing, ‘I’ve got it, sir.’ Jack nodded and waved apologetically before catching up with his dad, who turned only after the incident and waved at him impatiently.

Nice. Now it’s not clear if I’ve sipped from it.

As they approached the small group gathered around Mr. Heavenward, all of them obsequiously giving him their rapt attention, the man took notice of the two new people closing in, particularly Freddy. A nearby goon in a suit was also watching, though in a way that looked like he wasn’t. Jack could see the subtle tension, ready to spring into action if commanded.

But Mr. Heavenward broke into a bright smile and flung up his hands. “Freddy, my friend!” he called in a deep, cheerful voice. “What a pleasure! But oh — is this business, actually?” He glanced briefly at Jack.

Jack did not quite end up meeting his eyes, but he still felt a flicker of something exceptionally not right in there, in significant heaviness.

Oh, man…

He sent a message to Neex: <Just a warning to send out: I’m about to meet Zachariah Heavenward, and the vibes are super bad.> He sent a little bundle of the impressions and imagery for Neex to pass along, too.

Neex replied, <Acknowledged; passing this along. Be careful, Jack.>

“Kinda,” Freddy replied to Mr. Heavenward’s question, as he smiled and turned to nod in indication of Jack’s presence. “This is my son, Jack? I mentioned him a few times, and he’s interested in hearing more about certain opportunities. At the least, I wanted him to make some friends here.”

Mr. Heavenward was becoming more and more disturbing the closer Jack got, as he got a good look with his enhanced vision. Though his face was full, the rest of him was far too lanky, with loose clothing that probably hid the fact well from others. He didn’t have on gloves, though, and his fingers seemed very skinny, long, and… off. Uncanny, and slightly unreal, much like his face.

He also hid the subtleties of his alarm well, even though Jack could tell it was there. Something about what his dad said didn’t quite work for this creature, but he smiled his overly pleasant, too-wide smile and nodded with understanding like the contorting, plastic idol of a saint. “Of course, Freddy. What could be a purer desire than to shine a light for our very children, hmm?” He turned to the group he was entertaining and put his hands together with an apologetic expression. “Do forgive me, but I have to pause our discussion briefly. Won’t you have a drink at the bar in the meantime? Or whatever else might tickle your fancy?”

The group was very amiable as they professed their understanding and desire for refreshment, and oh, Mr. Heavenward was so kind, of course, we’d love a drink, that would be divine!

Momentarily, they disengaged, though not without curious flashes in their eyes of people worthy enough to take attention away from them.

Once they were on their way, Mr. Heavenward approached Jack for what was obviously to be a handshake. “Jack Laker, is it? Wonderful to meet you!” He glanced at the drink in Jack’s hand, and this time, the man actually looked genuinely pleased and relieved. “Oh! Was the drink to your liking, then?!” He was a bit too excited in the delivery, his eyes widening happily, his alarm seeming to evaporate.

Holy shit, they did put something in it.

“Oh, I, uh…” Jack started, but trailed off. Unavoidably, their eyes met very concretely just as their hands did.

Jack found it difficult to suppress how much this man disturbed him, from the simple oddity of his presence, to his plastic face, to the oily, wire-like, intense grip of his hand, immediately speaking of unbefitting strength. But the eyes froze him unavoidably, because they were awful and dead. He stared into an ephemeral abyss full of rotting, melting corpses, delivering something between the revulsion of sight and smell.

Amazingly, Q-Loth hardly reacted to the handshake, but it felt like it was staring into those awful, dead eyes right along with him, and not at all pleased at what it was seeing.

The feeling appeared likewise. Mr. Heavenward’s face contorted into a rictus of revulsion and fury. With a vicious snarl and a whip-like, rubbery motion of his whole body in coordination, he flung Jack hard into the nearby window by the hand — Jack tried to tighten his grip instinctually, but that hand turned into something boneless and as slippery as oil, and Jack flew as if launched from an inhuman slingshot.

He hit the window with his back and shoulder, and it gave way, breaking into shards, much of which went with him, as jagged edges raked over him, and he found himself thankful for a heavy jacket.
