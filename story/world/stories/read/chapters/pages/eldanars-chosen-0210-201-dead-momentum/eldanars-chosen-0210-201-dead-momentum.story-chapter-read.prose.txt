The rescue squad tore through the corrupted forest with a frantic, breathless urgency.

There was no time for scouting or methodical grid sweeping. They were a massive, heavily armoured blunt instrument moving at maximum possible speed. Comprising nearly twenty high-tier veteran adventurers, they completely abandoned all stealth. The heavy clanking of steel plate armour, the rhythmic thudding of heavy boots, and the harsh, ragged breathing of the fighters echoed loudly through the rotting timber. They were relying entirely on their overwhelming combined levels and sheer aggression to simply crush any minor undead patrols that stumbled into their path.

The darkness beneath the dead pine canopy was absolute. It was a suffocating, freezing pitch black that seemed to actively absorb the ambient light, pressing against their eyes like a physical weight. They navigated solely by the faint, pulsing glow of Carcan’s amber-topped staff and the small, dancing sparks of residual fire flickering across Brett’s fingertips.

The terrain was a nightmare. The black mud churned violently beneath the heavy steel boots of the fighters, spraying foul-smelling, freezing sludge up to their knees. The air tasted of rust, stagnant water, and the coppery tang of old blood. Every step required immense physical effort to pull their boots free from the clinging muck.

Less than ten minutes into their sprint, the deep shadows directly ahead of them shifted. A pack of a dozen corrupted, rotting forest stags, their antlers tangled with black necrotic vines, stepped onto the muddy path to block their progress.

Josh did not even break his stride. He was positioned at the very forefront of the column and he simply lowered his shoulder, raised his shield, and activated Juggernaut's Tread. Blue lightning flared around his boots, granting him unstoppable momentum. He slammed into the centre of the undead pack like a runaway siege engine. Three of the rotting stags were instantly crushed beneath his heavy steel, their bones shattering loudly as they were trampled into the mud.

Ronald and a pair of lithe, dual-wielding rogues blurred past Josh on the flanks. They did not stop to engage in a prolonged melee. They simply dragged their razor-sharp blades across the exposed throats and hamstrings of the remaining beasts as they sprinted past, leaving the crippled monsters to be finished off by the heavy maces of the fighters following closely behind.

It was a display of ruthless, terrifying efficiency and no one slowed down to see it.

Elara remained at the very front of the formation, running shoulder to shoulder with Ronald. The injured skirmisher was a testament to pure human willpower. Her face was the colour of chalk, entirely drained of blood. Her breath whistled harshly through her clenched teeth, and her ruined right arm hung completely uselessly at her side, the crude cloth tourniquet soaked black.

But her eyes were locked firmly forward. She was driven by an adrenaline-fuelled desperation that allowed her to completely ignore the freezing mud dragging at her boots and the burning agony radiating from her mangled shoulder. She knew exactly how many minutes her party had left, and she refused to let them die alone in the dark.

They ran for another few minutes, plunging deeper and deeper into the thickest, most saturated pockets of the necrotic fog.

Suddenly, Elara let out a sharp, ragged gasp. She shoved her way past Ronald, her boots slipping dangerously in the muck, and pointed her bloody steel dagger towards a dense thicket of dead, thorny briars just ahead.

"There!" Elara shouted, her voice painfully hoarse, cracking with desperation. "The ground gives way just beyond those trees!"

Josh did not slow his punishing pace. He lowered his shoulder once more, bracing his heavy shield, and smashed directly through the brittle, dead briars like a steel battering ram. The thorns scraped uselessly against his breastplate, snapping like dry twigs.

He broke through the dense treeline and immediately dug his heavy boots deep into the earth, throwing his upper body weight backward just in time.

The ground literally vanished mere inches from his steel toes.

The rest of the rescue party hurried to the ragged edge, looking down into a geological nightmare.

The intense, suffocating concentration of dark mana in this specific area had actively rotted the very bedrock beneath the forest floor. The magical corruption had eaten away the limestone, causing a massive section of the earth to completely collapse into a steep, bowl-shaped sinkhole. It was easily a hundred yards across. The walls of the gorge were sheer, completely slick with freezing black mud and a tangled, chaotic web of thick, dead tree roots that hung down like grasping fingers.

At the very bottom of the thirty-foot drop, a scene of absolute, desperate horror was unfolding in the gloom.

The missing Iron Gauntlet party was backed into a corner. They were pressed hard against a sheer, concave wall of exposed, freezing limestone, completely cut off from any route of escape.

And they were on their last legs.

A massive, heavily armoured man, easily the size of Bhel but lacking the dwarf's dense bulk, was down on one knee in the freezing sludge. He was holding a towering, rectangular metal shield that was buckling and denting under relentless, concussive impacts. The metal was glowing faintly with a strained defensive aura. Behind him, a mage in torn, mud-caked robes was blindly firing weak, sputtering sparks of elemental magic from his fingertips, his mana reserves clearly completely exhausted. A rogue and a cleric stood pressed against the stone wall behind the tank, desperately trying to bandage terrible, weeping claw wounds on each other with visibly shaking hands.

They were completely and utterly surrounded.

The bottom of the sinkhole was a writhing, clicking sea of bone and rotting flesh. Hundreds of corrupted native beasts clawed mindlessly at the failing shield wall. There were skeletal hounds, massive rotting bears, and disfigured forest cats, all driven by the singular, mindless hunger of the dark mana.

But the true threat did not stand on the ground. It crawled along the muddy walls of the gorge.

Over a dozen Crypt Weavers, their massive, bulbous bodies constructed entirely of fused human skulls and ribcages, were scaling the sheer drop. Their glowing green abdomens pulsed with sick light. They were actively spinning thick, grey necrotic webs, casting the heavy nets down to trap the adventurers, slowly but surely pulling their defensive perimeter tighter and tighter.

"Light preserve us," Carcan whispered, stepping up to the edge of the sinkhole. She stared down at the overwhelming, impossible swarm, her silver eyes wide with profound horror.

"We do not have time to formulate a flank," Ronald hissed, his jade eyes instantly assessing the impossible mathematics of the slaughter below. "If that tank's shield falls, the horde will wash over them. They are dead in seconds."

"Then we draw the aggro," Josh stated, his voice a cold, flat bark of military command.

He turned away from the drop to face the gathered rescue squad, his storm-grey eyes burning with uncompromising resolve. The latent, humming energy of his newly acquired level twenty-six pulsed powerfully beneath his skin.

"Mages and rangers, line the ridge!" Josh roared, pointing to the edge of the sinkhole. "Do not let those Weavers drop any more webs on us. Keep the canopy clear. Melee fighters, on me! We are going straight down the throat!"

There was absolutely no hesitation. These were seasoned professionals. The veteran adventurers instantly split into their designated tactical roles. Perberos, Brett, Carcan, and half a dozen other ranged fighters immediately spread out along the lip of the sinkhole. Bowstrings hummed with lethal tension, and magical staffs began to glow with intense, gathering power.

Josh looked at Ronald. The feline beastkin had his twin hunting daggers drawn in a reverse grip. A terrifying, predatory grin pulled his lips back over his sharp fangs, his slit pupils dilating in the darkness.

"Try to keep up, Josh," Ronald challenged, his long tail lashing once beneath his cloak.

The beastkin did not wait for a response. Ronald leaped directly off the edge of the thirty-foot drop. He did not fall clumsily. He twisted in the freezing air with impossible, feline grace, using the hanging dead roots to briefly slow his descent, plunging smoothly into the darkness below.

Josh took a deep, steadying breath, filling his lungs with the freezing air. He gripped the leather handle of his shield tightly. He stepped confidently off the edge, falling straight down like a human anvil, closely followed by the other heavily armoured fighters of the rescue squad.

"Fire!" Brett roared from the ridge above, his voice echoing loudly across the gorge.

The suffocating darkness of the sinkhole was instantly and violently shattered by a blinding, apocalyptic barrage of covering artillery fire.

Brett unleashed a massive, continuous torrent of living, white-hot plasma directly from his bare hands. He aimed into the thickest mass of the horde, bypassing the trapped party. The elemental heat was absolutely staggering. It instantly vapourised the freezing black mud, sending up thick plumes of hissing steam. The plasma wrapped around the undead beasts like a hungry predator, incinerating dozens of rotting stags and skeletal hounds into fine ash in a single, devastating heartbeat.

Beside him, Perberos and the other rangers fired a devastating, coordinated volley of heavy steel-tipped arrows. The elven scout aimed with terrifying precision. He ignored the beasts on the ground. He tracked the chaotic, scuttling movements of the dungeon spawn on the walls, putting his heavy broadheads directly into the glowing green necrotic cores of the Crypt Weavers. Three of the massive bone spiders shrieked as their magic was shattered, falling heavily from the walls to crush the lesser undead below them.

Carcan slammed the base of her staff into the dirt on the ridge. She sent a massive, floating orb of pure, concentrated holy light slowly descending into the pit. The orb acted like a miniature sun, illuminating the battlefield in stark, blinding detail and searing the rotting flesh of any ghoul foolish enough to look directly at it.

The sudden bombardment from the sky threw the previously organised undead horde into momentary chaos. The Crypt Weavers shrieked, their hive-mind briefly overwhelmed by the sheer volume of destructive magic raining down upon their ranks.

A second later, Josh hit the bottom of the gorge. He bent his knees deeply to absorb the immense kinetic shock of the thirty-foot drop, immediately triggering his Titan's Anchor skill upon impact with the mud.

A shimmering, high-voltage shield of raw blue energy flared to life around him in a massive dome. The explosive shockwave of his landing, combined with the magical energy of the skill, blew the thick mud outward in a perfect, violent circle. A dozen skeletal hounds and a massive rotting bear were thrown into the air, their bodies completely shattered by the kinetic blast before they even hit the ground.

Josh dropped the anchor immediately, rising smoothly from the mud crater with his longsword drawn.

"Push through!" Josh roared, his voice booming over the deafening hiss of Brett's burning plasma and the shrieks of the dying monsters.

He activated Stormforged Adamantite. Brilliant blue electricity violently interlaced with the residual magical flames Brett had cast upon his blade earlier in the evening. The air filled with the sharp, acrid stench of ozone.

Josh swung the heavy, electrified steel in a brutal, wide horizontal arc. The blade sheared cleanly through the dense, physical bodies of three corrupted dire bears charging blindly from the smoke. The extreme heat of the lightning cauterised the rotting flesh instantly, dropping the massive beasts into the mud as permanent, physical corpses.

Ronald was a blur of lethal, muted green motion to Josh's immediate left. The beastkin did not rely on brute force. He used the chaotic, tightly packed bodies of the undead against themselves. Ronald vaulted cleanly off the broad shoulders of a rotting orc, spinning in mid-air. He drove his twin daggers deep into the glowing green eyes of a Crypt Weaver that had survived the fall from the wall, severing its magical core instantly. The beastkin landed smoothly in the mud, immediately sweeping low to cleanly slice the hamstrings of a skeletal warrior before it could raise its rusted sword.

Behind them, the other heavy fighters of the rescue squad crashed into the fray.

They formed a tight, heavily armoured wall of raised steel shields, swinging massive iron maces and heavy halberds. They slammed violently into the exposed rear flank of the undead horde, acting as a massive, unstoppable wedge of living steel and muscle.

The sheer, explosive shock of the coordinated assault carved a massive, bloody trench directly through the rotting swarm. Rusted iron weapons sparked against pristine steel armour. Ancient bones shattered loudly under heavy mercenary boots. The overwhelming smell of ozone, burning decay, and freshly churned earth flooded the freezing air of the sinkhole.

For the first terrifying, adrenaline-soaked minute, the momentum belonged entirely to the rescue squad.

They were an elite, rested force slamming into the unprepared rear of an enemy that had been entirely focused on a different target. They carved a path of total, uncompromising destruction, pushing relentlessly through the sea of bone towards the failing defensive wall of the Iron Gauntlet party.

Josh deflected a snapping set of rotting jaws with the rim of his shield, bringing the edge of his electrified steel directly down on a skeletal neck, severing the spine cleanly. He kicked the crumbling remains out of his path.

He could see the massive tank of the trapped party just twenty yards away through the swirling smoke and falling ash. The exhausted man’s eyes were wide with absolute disbelief as he watched the wall of fire and steel carving a bloody path towards him. The tank let out a ragged, desperate cheer, finding a final reserve of strength to hold his buckling shield high.

"Hold the line!" Josh bellowed to the stranger, stepping over the burning corpse of a ghoul. "We are coming!"

But the undead were not simple, panicked animals capable of being routed by a sudden shock assault.

They were commanded by the residual, terrifying intelligence of the Crypt Weaver hive-mind. Furthermore, their sheer numbers in the confined space of the sinkhole were simply too vast for a single surprise charge to break them completely. The horde absorbed the initial impact, treating the massive loss of their rear ranks as a mere tactical inconvenience.

As Josh pushed another five yards forward, his boots dragging heavily in the thick sludge, the tactical reality of the sinkhole shifted violently.

A chilling, unified clicking sound echoed loudly from the sheer limestone walls of the gorge. It was the sound of a hundred human skulls grinding together.

The remaining Crypt Weavers had adapted to the new threat. They stopped trying to trap the exhausted Iron Gauntlet party. Instead, the bulbous, skull-fused monsters clinging to the high walls turned their glowing green abdomens outward, aiming directly down at the rescue squad carving through their ranks.

Thick, heavy, grey streams of concentrated necrotic webbing rained down from the darkness above.

The sticky nets completely ignored the magical bombardment from the ridge, falling heavily through the smoke and holy light directly onto the tight wedge of melee fighters.

A veteran fighter holding a heavy mace beside Josh let out a sudden, panicked roar. A massive grey web slammed squarely into his chest. The necrotic material immediately began to harden upon contact with the freezing air, contracting violently and pinning the man's heavily armoured arms tightly to his sides. He stumbled backward, unable to raise his weapon. Two corrupted, rotting wolves instantly leaped upon the helpless man from the shadows, their jaws snapping as they dragged him down into the thick, freezing mud.

"Form a circle!" Ronald screamed over the din of battle.

The beastkin's voice had completely lost its usual cocky, arrogant edge. It was replaced by a sharp, urgent military panic. Ronald narrowly dodged a falling strand of grey webbing, slicing wildly at the grasping skeletal claws suddenly surrounding him.

"Shields outward! Do not let them flank you!" Ronald ordered, his ears flattening against his skull.

The element of surprise was entirely gone.

The massive horde of rotting meat and bone stopped reacting to the rear assault with confusion. Guided by the Weavers, they turned with terrifying, perfect synchronicity. They completely ignored the trapped Iron Gauntlet party against the wall, shifting their immense, mindless fury to focus entirely on the heavily armoured interlopers currently bogged down in their centre.

The unstoppable wedge stalled.

Josh planted his heavy boots, slamming his shield brutally into the face of a charging ghoul, shattering its jaw. But the sheer, bodily mass of the undead pressing against him from three sides was completely overwhelming. He could no longer step forward to reach the trapped party. The pressure was immense, a physical wall of rotting flesh and rusted iron pushing against his shield. He was forced to take a grudging half-step back just to keep his balance.

The frenzied, explosive rescue charge degraded instantly into a brutal, suffocating grind.

They were bogged down deep in freezing, knee-high mud. They were surrounded on all sides by an ocean of snapping jaws, thrusting spears, and rusted iron swords. Thick, heavy necrotic webbing continued to rain down from the sky, threatening to completely immobilise them.

They had successfully reached the bottom of the gorge and drawn the aggression away from the dying party, but the forward momentum was completely dead. The true nightmare of the sinkhole had just begun.
