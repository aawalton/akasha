Noelle shook her head. "It’s fine; it wasn’t that serious to begin with. I was just messing with you."

"Wait, so you made me apologize for no reason?"

"Ahh…" Noelle paused, turning her gaze away from me. "You took a day off yesterday, don’t you have better things to ask? Like, ‘please Miss Noelle, spare me some of your knowledge so that my small brain can grasp yesterday’s lecture and not feel overwhelmed.’"

"You’re changing the topic!" I pressed.

"I’m not, no, no, I could never," Noelle shrugged.

She’s even throwing my own lines back at me now, talk about being a smartass.

"Okay, fine. Please, Miss Noelle, can you spare the supposedly small-brained individual some of your knowledge?" I asked politely… though I believe it might’ve sounded sarcastic to foreign ears.

"What’s with that tone, huh? It’s as if you’re mocking me."

"I’m not, no, no, I could never," I bit back my laughter.

Noelle’s eyebrows shot up, a silent "Excuse me?" forming on her face. Despite her attempt to remain composed, a laugh escaped her. The sound hung in the air briefly before she regained control, smoothing a stray hair behind her ear.

How does that even work? I wondered. How does one stop laughing by fidgeting with their hair? If it were me, I’d probably bite the inside of my cheek—or maybe my lower lip.

We entered Cellera’s campus through the main gate when Noelle finally broke the brief silence between us.

"Fine, I’ll do it, but I’ll need twenty-seven favors for my lesson," she said, being outrageously greedy.

"Five favors," I bargained. I had no intention of being her servant for the day. She’d likely forget about them in a day or two—that’s just the type of person she was. Intelligent, sharp, charismatic, the kind of person everyone liked to be around. But she was also forgetful… forgetful about things she found uninteresting. Pretty sure I fell in that category. So, her forgetting the favors wasn’t a long shot.

"Seven favors and it’s a deal!" Noelle exclaimed as we entered the building. Good thing no teacher heard her, or we’d have been reprimanded for shouting on campus.

"Six."

"Seven."

"Six, and it’s a deal," I gave my last offer.

Noelle’s eyes narrowed at my response. She opened her mouth, probably to insist on seven again, but stopped. Turning her head away, she kept walking toward our classroom before glancing back at me, her hand outstretched.

"It’s a deal," she said.

"…It’s a deal? I guess," I repeated, shaking her hand, unsure what else to say.

With a firm handshake that felt like her attempt to make my hand fall off, we commenced the deal and continued our march to the classroom.

In a moment, we reached our classroom. We were certainly early—no loud chattering voices came from inside. Reaching out for the knob, I pushed the door open and walked in.

I felt a sudden change in temperature; the classroom was colder than the hallway. My eyes turned to the benches; only one was occupied.

A girl dressed in a rather extravagant purple outfit, matching her hair and eyes, sat on a bench, staring at the book. That girl was none other than Seraphina Victoria of the Victoria household. The only daughter of Duke Merivas Victoria, and his wife.

And the legal heir to everything under the Duke’s name… at least when he dies.

She was also the first one to class? Did not know she was this punctual. Are nobles meant to be punctual? I mean, they have hard training and all, don’t they? I mused as I looked at Noelle, who was standing beside me.

"Hey! Why are you looking at me like that?!"

"Like what?" I composed myself. Looking at Noelle as if she were a failed noble was definitely not good.

"It’s as if you are looking down on me… no," she shook her head, "you were looking down on me."

"I was holding back a sneeze," I answered, moving to my desk.

"No, you weren’t."

"Achoo!" I sneezed, then sniffled. "See? I have a cold, don’t you know?"

"That was the fakest—wait, wasn’t I supposed to give you a lesson!?"

"You were, yes." I looked at the clock. It was already 8:40—just twenty minutes remained before class started.

"Darn it!" Noelle yelled as she hurriedly took the seat on Mabel’s bench beside me. "You shouldn’t have intertwined me in this conversation! We have little time!"

Now, while I wanted to argue and say it was she who had intertwined ‘me’ in the conversation, I decided not to, since it was I who would suffer because of it.

Taking my seat at my bench, I placed my bag on the desk and looked toward Noelle, waiting for her to start the class.

Surprisingly enough, Noelle was able to condense the whole two-hour lecture into a ten-minute session with me. Unsurprisingly enough, she could speak for ten minutes without a pause, not sure which of the two was more interesting.

The basic summary I learned from her talked about strengthening magic and how different mana affinities may affect it. Initially, strengthening magic does not depend on affinity—whether a person has fire or water, ‘their fist carries the same punch,’ as Noelle put it.

Though, as mages grow, they might be able to flow a particular affinity’s mana in their body instead of just the formless bright mana. And this changes everything. Now, instead of the formless bright mana that increases muscle strength, fire affinity mana may seep in, causing Fire Fist, which not only strengthens the muscle but also imbues it with fire.

However, this is difficult to master and was typically avoided by mages, who preferred not to be on the front lines in combat.

Afterward, Instructor Serena took everyone to the training ground to show a demonstration of strengthening magic, and demolished a boulder into smithereens with her kick.

"And hey, you know what? Her leg was covered in an aura of earth affinity! She called it Shatter Fist!" Noelle exclaimed excitedly.

There was no reason not to shout anymore; only eight minutes were left before class started, and since everyone was chatting, surely no one would be punished for indiscipline. It was a classic demonstration of strength in numbers.

"Yeah, that sure was something," Mabel, who had come to class just a few minutes ago, chimed in. She was sharing her seat with Noelle. "I really am curious to see how Ice Fist would work. Maybe it would freeze everything with each punch?"

"Shouldn’t you be more concerned about learning the normal version first?" I asked.

"Don’t be a spoilsport! Let a girl imagine!" Mabel immediately shut me down.

Suddenly, a veil of silence enveloped the class, and in walked Instructor Serena. Seeing her, Noelle stood from Mabel’s place and walked back to her seat.

The class was about to start. My eyes turned to the empty bench. Princess Emily was still not back from her visit to the capital. Then again, it’s not like not learning strengthening magic would hinder her.

All it would do is make the final between her and one of those three more interesting.

The class started, and as soon as attendance finished, Instructor Serena asked all of us to line up and leave for Battle Arena One, which was also known as the Battle Dome—or the Inner Battle Arena.

In minutes, Instructor Serena and we reached the Battle Dome.

There were twenty long steps to descend before we reached the main ground. Each step housed several seats, which were filled by common people and nobility when Cellera’s Battle Fest arrived.

As we went down, we found a large circular ground covered in lush green grass, the kind of grass you’d want to get face-slammed on. It was just that soft.

The Cellera Battle Dome was an arena where young mages settled their scores, grudges, and competed to get better at their craft. Whether it was a Battle Fest, a practice duel, or a tag team match, everything took place here.
