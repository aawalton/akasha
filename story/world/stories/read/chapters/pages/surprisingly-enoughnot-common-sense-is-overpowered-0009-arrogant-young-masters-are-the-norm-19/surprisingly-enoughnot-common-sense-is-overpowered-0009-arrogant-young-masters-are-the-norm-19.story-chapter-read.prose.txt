It was the day of the lecture, delivered by one of the Foundation Building elders of the sect.

Ning made his way toward the General Affairs Hall at an unhurried pace. The buildings lining both sides of the stone path stood in perfect symmetry, their tiled roofs and carved pillars mirroring one another so precisely that it almost looked unnatural. It was probably meant to symbolize balance or harmony.

Noice.

Ning gave an approving nod.

Before long, the General Affairs Hall came into view. Stepping inside, he found there were only a few people, since he arrived early.

It was a safe choice, and simply the sensible thing to do. In an unfamiliar place, even small matters like arriving on time were worth paying attention to.

"Brother Ning!"

Zhang Feng's cheerful voice rang out from one corner of the hall. He waved enthusiastically, joined by Wei Zhusang and Lang Rulang.

Ning smiled and returned the greeting with a cupped-fist salute before walking over.

At first glance, nothing seemed unusual. Wei Zhusang, Zhang Feng, and Lang Rulang were practically inseparable these days.

The surprise was the fourth person standing with them.

He was a young man with sharp features, already at the Second Stage of Qi Condensation. His robes were of finer quality than those of the other peripheral disciples.

The young man gave Ning a leisurely once-over before speaking.

"So, you're another member of this so-called Mutual Aid Association." His eyes lingered on Ning for a moment before he gave a small nod. "Hmph. You look... acceptable."

Ning tilted his head slightly, his eyes drifting toward Wei Zhusang in silent inquiry.

Who's this sassy lost child?

Wei Zhusang immediately understood the look.

"Ahem." He smiled awkwardly. "Brother Ning, allow me to introduce Brother Su Fan." Leaning in slightly, he lowered his voice. "He's from the Alchemy Hall."

Ning's brows rose almost imperceptibly.

That explained quite a lot.

The Alchemy Hall.

This man was Rich, with a capital R.

Wei, Zhang, and Lang all came from respectable cultivation families, but their connections had only secured them work in the Weapon Refining and Talisman Halls. Those were nothing to scoff at, since both ranked among the top professions of the Hundred Immortal Arts.

Still, wherever there were people, there were class divisions. Alchemy and formations always sat a little higher on the ladder, even among the four great arts. Both were closely tied to cultivation itself, with spiritual pills and formations capable of making or breaking a cultivator's path.

A disciple from the Alchemy Hall naturally had reason to be proud.

"So it's Brother Su." Ning clasped his hands politely. "It's a pleasure to make your acquaintance."

"Hmph." Su Fan merely inclined his head a fraction.

Yep. Definitely a young master.

Wei Zhusang chuckled awkwardly. "Brother Su's background is quite impressive."

Ning simply smiled. "It's only natural. Brother Su has already reached the Second Stage of Qi Condensation while the rest of us are still at the First. Talent and hard work deserve respect."

Wei Zhusang blinked before giving him a nod of approval. "Brother Ning, I really admire your outlook."

Su Fan snorted, though the disdain in his expression lessened. "Not bad. At least you know how to speak."

Ning resisted the urge to roll his eyes.

Of course, Su Fan had heard every word.

Cultivators possessed senses far sharper than mortals. Whispering only worked against ordinary people. Around fellow cultivators, lowering one's voice merely created the illusion of privacy.

Ning certainly wasn't about to make that mistake.

Just then, a streak of moon-white light descended from the sky.

A flying sword landed lightly in the center of the square, and a middle-aged man in moon-white robes stepped off with effortless grace. As his feet touched the ground, the sword circled him once before sliding neatly back into its sheath on its own.

Ning's eyes lingered on the sight.

Now that's what a cultivator should look like.

The man's sleeves fluttered gently despite the absence of wind, his bearing calm and detached. He carried the effortless dignity of someone who had long since stepped beyond the mundane world.

The disciples immediately cupped their hands and bowed.

"We greet the elder!"

"Enough with the formalities. You may simply call me Elder Lou Ran." His gaze swept across the gathered disciples before he nodded in satisfaction. "Today's lecture will cover the fundamentals of cultivation. Listen carefully. A solid foundation is worth more than years of blind effort."

The square quickly fell silent.

"The purpose of cultivation," Elder Lou Ran began, "is to refine the spiritual qi of Heaven and Earth into one's own strength. Every breakthrough tempers the body, expands the dantian, and brings a cultivator one step closer to transcending mortality itself."

His voice wasn't especially loud, yet every disciple heard it clearly.

"The path ahead, however, is neither smooth nor safe. Demonic beasts, hostile cultivators, ancient ruins, and heavenly tribulations all await those who seek immortality. To survive such dangers, cultivators rely primarily on two things."

He raised two fingers.

"Spells and martial arts."

"Spells transform qi into external phenomena." Elder Lou Ran flicked a finger, and a tiny flame appeared above his palm before becoming a stream of water, then a gust of wind, and finally a small bolt of crackling lightning. "Fire, water, earth, metal, wood, wind, thunder, and countless other manifestations all fall under this path. Most cultivators begin with simple techniques before gradually learning more profound spells."

With another flick of his sleeve, the miniature display vanished.

"Martial arts strengthen the cultivator directly. They refine the body, empower weapons, and allow qi to be wielded through close combat. Neither path is superior. A capable cultivator understands how to make both complement one another."

Ning listened attentively, committing every word to memory.

Elder Lou Ran continued, "As for the realms of cultivation, they progress from Qi Condensation to Foundation Building, Purple Mansion, Golden Core, and Nascent Soul."

He paused, allowing the names to settle in the disciples' minds before smiling faintly.

"What lies beyond Nascent Soul needn't concern you for now. Even understanding the first five realms is more than enough at your current stage. A cultivator who dreams of flying before learning to walk will only stumble."

His expression grew more serious.

"Never become so obsessed with advancing your realm that you neglect your foundation. A weak foundation may allow you to climb quickly, but it will eventually become the chain that prevents you from climbing any higher."

Many disciples nodded solemnly.

Just then, hurried footsteps echoed across the square.

A lone disciple came running through the entrance, breathing heavily as every pair of eyes turned toward the unfortunate latecomer.

The youth stumbled into the square, his robes torn in several places and his face streaked with dirt, as though he had fought his way through half the mountain just to get here.

Ning raised an eyebrow.

Xiao Fan.

Before anyone else could speak, a mocking voice rang out across the square.

"So the trash finally decided to show himself."

Ning turned toward the speaker.

The young man wore embroidered robes far finer than those of ordinary peripheral disciples, similar in quality to Su Fan's. His chin remained slightly raised, and the smug curve of his lips made it abundantly clear that he enjoyed having an audience.

"It's Xiao Hong," Zhang Feng muttered beside him.

Ning studied the newcomer. He'd already heard about him on his first day in the sect.

As expected, another arrogant young master. Do they grow these people in the sect's backyard?

"Is he the one with the high-grade spiritual root?" Ning asked quietly.

Wei Zhusang nodded. "The very same."

A grin slowly spread across Zhang Feng's face. "This should be interesting."

Ning glanced at him. "Why?"

For him, even gossip counted as entertainment these days. Living in a cave did that to you.

Zhang Feng chuckled. "It's a long story." He folded his arms and nodded toward Xiao Fan. "That guy used to be the young master of the Xiao Clan. His father was the clan's greatest genius and the undisputed heir, but instead of marrying into another cultivation family, he chose a woman with no notable background. The elders nearly coughed themselves to death from the shock."

Wei Zhusang nodded. "Not long afterward, both of Xiao Fan's parents disappeared without a trace."

Zhang Feng sighed. "Then came the worst part. Xiao Fan was diagnosed with damaged spiritual roots. His cultivation barely progressed, so the clan stripped him of his position as heir."

Wei Zhusang added, "Xiao Hong's branch inherited everything afterward."

Ning drew in a breath.

Parents mysteriously disappear. Ruined talent. Inheritance stolen. That's already three protagonist clichés.

Zhang Feng scratched his cheek. "But that's not even the strange part."

Ning looked at him. "There's more?"

"It was said that a passing expert later repaired his spiritual roots. No one knows why." Zhang Feng shrugged.

Wei Zhusang spread his hands. "Then the senior disappeared just as mysteriously as he came."

Ning looked ahead blankly.

A mysterious expert too?

He's collecting protagonist tropes faster than a gacha game collects limited characters. Fuck, I might actually be living inside a third-rate xianxia novel.

"I arrived late because three people blocked my path." Xiao Fan's gaze shot toward Xiao Hong. "On someone's orders."

Xiao Hong merely sneered.

"Is that so? Then perhaps you should have arrived earlier." He folded his arms across his chest. "Or are excuses the only thing you've managed to cultivate?"

Even the dialogue sounds like a third-rate script, Ning thought, quietly blending into the crowd.

"You—"

"Silence."

Elder Lou Ran's voice rang across the square. With a casual flick of his sleeve, an invisible pressure descended upon the disciples.

Ning's breathing hitched as his body instinctively stiffened under the weight. It wasn't directed at him, yet merely standing within its range made him feel as though an invisible mountain had settled onto his shoulders.

His eyes, however, shone with excitement.

So this is a Foundation Building cultivator. All this with just a wave of his hand. The gap between realms is even more ridiculous than I imagined.

Elder Lou Ran's calm gaze swept across the disciples.

"This is no place for your personal grudges." His eyes rested briefly on Xiao Hong. "Interrupt again, and you will be punished."

The pressure around Xiao Hong suddenly increased.

His face turned pale before he coughed up a mouthful of blood, staggering back several steps.

Only then did Elder Lou Ran shift his gaze to Xiao Fan. "As for you, regardless of the reason, do not be late again."

"Yes, Elder!" Xiao Fan bowed deeply before stealing a triumphant glance at Xiao Hong, whose face had darkened with humiliation.

Ning quietly watched the exchange.

Yep. That guy is definitely not gonna forget this anytime soon.

By now, Xiao Fan had checked almost every box in the classic protagonist handbook. Ning was nearly convinced he had found the chosen one, though a small part of him still wanted more evidence.

If I hadn't survived my teenage years, this level of existential crisis would've flattened me.

After all, it wasn't every day you realized you might be stuck inside a xianxia novel.

His thoughts drifted for a moment toward parallel worlds, infinite timelines, and the possibility that every absurd cultivation novel he had ever read existed somewhere as a real world.

Fortunately, before he disappeared completely into that rabbit hole, Elder Lou Ran resumed his lecture.

The elder's explanations were surprisingly engaging, breaking down seemingly profound cultivation principles into concepts even newly admitted disciples could understand. Ning found himself listening so intently that when the lecture finally paused, he almost wished it would continue.

Elder Lou Ran looked across the gathered disciples before speaking once more.

"The sect has recently approved an additional incentive for your generation. Any disciple who reaches the Third Stage of Qi Condensation within one year may freely select a cultivation technique from the Sutra Pavilion."

A murmur immediately spread through the square.

"Those who reach the Fourth Stage within that same period will receive an additional opportunity from the sect."

The disciples' eyes brightened.

"In cultivation, those who stop advancing inevitably fall behind. Most of you possess only low- or mid-grade spiritual roots. Bottlenecks during Qi Condensation are unavoidable, and even a brief lapse in diligence may prevent you from reaching the later stages while your bodies are still in their prime. A weak foundation at the beginning of your path is difficult to repair later."

His gaze swept over the crowd one final time.

"Your generation has entered the sect during a rare period. Treasure this opportunity, cultivate diligently, and do not disappoint the sect."

With those final words, Elder Lou Ran stepped onto his flying sword. The blade rose smoothly into the air, carrying him beyond the clouds without another glance.

Rare period?

Ning's eyes narrowed thoughtfully as his gaze drifted toward the protagonist among them.
