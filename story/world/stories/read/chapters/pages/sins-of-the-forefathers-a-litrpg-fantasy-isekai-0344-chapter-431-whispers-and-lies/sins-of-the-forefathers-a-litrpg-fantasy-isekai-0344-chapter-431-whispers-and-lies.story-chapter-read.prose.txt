I streaked through the sky on folded wings, diving faster than I think I ever had before. At this point, I was a fair hand at flight. I knew what I was doing in the air. I knew just how to tuck my body to lower my profile, how to increase my speed. I was accelerating to such an extent that the point of my flaming spear actually started to scream as it split the air before it. The roar of the flames and the din of battle were the only reasons the people below me didn’t hear the screech as I fell.

If it hadn’t been there, I had no doubt the combatants would have been wondering where the strange, calling bird was.

And yet, for all of that…

The werewolf my spear was aimed at still reacted in time to stop me from skewering him.

In the split second before my blade impacted Bleddyn’s opponent, his great furred head snapped up to stare straight at me. Before I could react, one of his clawed hands, crackling with azure lightning, appeared in the path of my spear blade…

And grabbed it out of the air.

I instantly halted, the sudden stop of my screaming descent great enough to cause a small shockwave to emanate out of the point of impact. I almost wanted to gape with a mouth of fangs, as I hung for only a moment, suspended in midair by the very tip of my spear.

The mouth of the werewolf below me split into an open, malicious smile. In his eyes, I could see a spark of absolute hatred. “Finally,” He growled at me in satisfaction.

Off to the side, Bleddyn howled. Seeing an opening, he lunged at the back of the other werewolf, claws outstretched to tear at him.

At the same time, I used the last of my surviving momentum to twist around the length of my spear and tried to lash out at the werewolf’s head with a kick.

Almost nonchalantly, the enemy werewolf took a deep, deep breath…

And barked with a crash of thunder.

I only had a single instant to gape at the incongruity of a werewolf barking at me, before I was blown away by a wall of sound. His bark had been strong enough to cause a pressure wave that flung both Bleddyn and I away from him. Both of our attacks were deflected at the same time. I was lucky that the werewolf seemed to care so little for my spear that he released the blade of it as I was forced away from him, or else I would have lost my weapon right then and there. As it was, I had to retract it as I flailed wildly in mid-air, or else I would have cut myself. I had to use my wings to stabilize the fall, or else I would have gone skidding face-first across the cobblestones.

Oddly, my mid-air tumbling meant that I touched down to the right of Bleddyn, who had used his claws to halt his own repulsion. They left tracks in the stone below us from how deep my friend had to dig them. The two of us exchanged a brief glance as we gained our footing, standing up.

I…just now realized something. This was the first time that either of us had ever seen each other in our full transformed states.

I shook the thought away and directed a wary gaze at the enemy werewolf, who hadn’t moved an inch since he’d blown us away. “Who the Hell is that?”

Before Bleddyn could answer me, a low, furious growl echoed out of the chest of the other werewolf, loud enough to be heard over the battle raging all around us. “Who am I, he asks…” He rumbled, rage evident in every syllable. “How dare you. How dare you! I am the man whose heart you ripped out of his chest, Hart. I am he who swore vengeance against you the moment your filthy blade stole the only thing that mattered to me in this world!”

He roared, the sound echoing out across the entire battlefield. So great was the noise of it that I even saw some of the flames licking the surrounding buildings dance from it. In normal circumstances, I would have expected the impossibly loud sound to have caused a lull in the battle. But…the mind-controlled servants of the Savoy didn’t have that kind of agency, and the defenders couldn’t spare it.

“I…AM DAFFYD!”

My brow furrowed…

And I remembered just who that was.

Daffyd.

That was the name of the Archmage of Clan Calonawr. The one who had been missing since the end of the Construct War. From what I understood, he had disappeared into thin air only days in the aftermath. No signs of struggle. Nothing missing from his home that would have indicated him leaving on a trip. Just…gone.

He was also…

“Your Uncle?” I asked under my breath to the werewolf standing at my left.

Bleddyn growled and spat out a wad of blood to the side. I think I even saw a few teeth in the mix. “Anybody that fights for the Savoy ain’t kin of mine,” He rumbled, slowly standing to his full height. Transformed as he was, it was significant. “I don’t claim ‘im.”

Daffyd snorted, the sound as full of disdain as it was possible for a nearly ten-foot-tall lupinid to utter. “Your opinion doesn’t matter, whelp, and neither do you. You were the warm-up. I’m only here for him.”

I was startled at the clawed finger sent my way, and I couldn’t help but furrow my brow. “What the Hell have I ever done to you to deserve this level of hate? For God’s sake, we’ve only met once!”

Daffyd’s shoulders started to shake, and for a moment, I thought the obviously unstable werewolf was going to explode towards us in rage.

Instead, he started to laugh.

There wasn’t an ounce of humor in his voice. He wasn’t laughing because he was happy.

No, that was bitterness.

Abruptly, the laughter cut out, and Daffyd fixed his eyes on me. Even from this distance, I could see the crazed glint in them. “He was right. Somehow, Anguis was right. I didn’t believe him. I only went with him because of the possibility he could be right,” He rambled, talking more to himself than he was to me. “But you don’t even care. You’ve slaughtered so many now that they don’t even register to you, monster. You don’t even remember.” He lowered himself, crouching into a stance almost as if he were about to pounce. His breath picked up, and actual drool started to pour from his jaws.

And yet, his control over the sky blue lightning sheathing his body in ethereal, energetic armor never once wavered. Even in the depths of whatever madness Daffyd was sunken, he was still an Archmage of skill.

That didn’t bode well.

I tensed, and Bleddyn did the same, falling into a ready stance. Almost instinctively, I extended my spear once more and activated The Scintillant Blade.

“Her name…” Daffyd said, in a rapidly rising tone of pure rage. “Was RHIANNON! AND YOU KILLED HER!”

Wait, what?

Before I could process those words, Daffyd exploded towards Bleddyn and me, clawed hands held wide and ready to rend me in twain. Bleddyn leapt away, but I interposed the length of my spear in a blocking position. Not because I wanted to.

But because I just wasn’t fast enough to dodge.

Still…

It was a mistake. And it nearly cost me my life.

Before my disbelieving eyes, the pure Oninite haft of my weapon buckled under the sheer might of Daffyd’s blow. For a single, solitary second, I held out hope that the weapon I had personally forged would hold. This was the last of my collapsible spears, and it had served me well through dozens of battles. Hundreds and hundreds of monsters had fallen before this blade. This one in particular had been the very spear I had used to slay the soulless corpse of Rhazal, back in Elderwyck.

But…

It wasn’t enough.

The Oninite shattered, and I staggered from the sheer monstrous strength that Bleddyn could bring to bear. I no longer held a spear in my hands. Instead, I was clutching uselessly at two separate, fragmented pieces of a spear. Gears, mechanisms, and springs fell to the cobblestones below as the last of my daggers was sundered. Before I could react…

Clawed hands shot forward and dug into the scaled flesh of my transformed self’s shoulders. Those impossibly strong claws neither noticed the enchanted armor of my coat, or the natural armor of the scales granted to me by Rhazal’s curse.

They pierced straight through and instead sank straight down to my bones. In only moments, it felt like Daffyd was clutching at my collarbones like the handlebars of a bicycle.

A strangled cry of pure agony escaped my lips, and I instinctively lost my grip on the remains of my spear. Nearly losing consciousness from the pain, I stumbled to my knees, eyes wide and unseeing.

I trembled for a moment, before somehow, someway…

My tripartite mind managed to focus and engage the domain of my Middle Lattice. My control…over my automatic bodily functions.

I completely shut off my sense of pain.

In an instant, my entire body went numb. The relief was so complete and total I nearly wanted to weep from it. But I knew I was still in very real danger.

An enormous werewolf’s claws were still buried up to the knuckle in my shoulders, and I could still feel them. The sensation of clawed fingers internally clutching at my very bones was something I would never forget.

Almost hazily, I noticed that I had retained my grip on both Vis Maledicta Exactoris and Might of the Wyrdwood both. I could only attribute that to my Lattices, because I’m sure I would have dropped both when I’d been grabbed. Weakly, I tried to reach up and wrap my transformed claws around the forearms buried in my shoulders, feeling the frayed strands of my muscles twitch around Daffyd’s claws as I did. Managing to wrap my hands around those furred limbs, I tried to do something to dislodge the grip.

But, no matter how my weakened, injured form tried…

I couldn’t budge him.

“Good,” I heard a nearly purring voice echo from above me. Weakly looking up as I struggled against the hold on my very bones, I met the bloodshot gaze of a nearly euphoric Daffyd. He smiled broadly in malicious amusement at my obvious agony. “Struggle. Fight. Beg for your life, and know I will not grant it. This wouldn’t be as satisfying for me if ending you was so simple. But put up at least a little bit of resistance before I kill you, murderer.”

I coughed, and I was alarmed to see that what I coughed was a mouthful of blood. His blow must have been strong enough to rupture something internally, even though he’d been aiming for my shoulders. Still, I needed to say something, and so I struggled through the blood filling my mouth. “I…didn’t…kill…Rhiannon…” I wheezed, barely aware of the frozen form of Bleddyn off to my right. The werewolf looked like he didn’t know what to do, and I didn’t blame him.

“…what?” Daffyd uttered.

I took a shallow breath and nearly choked on it. “I didn’t…kill…your daughter. Nerexxa did…and Rhazal destroyed…her stolen…corpse.”

Through the fear starting to crawl up my spine, I could remember it almost like it was yesterday. The enormous, titanic form of Rhazal, sprawled up on the shattered, central fortress of Elderwyck like a throne. The tiny, grotesque form of Nerexxa, groveling in the crumbled stone of Fort Duality’s courtyard before him, wearing the mutated corpse of Rhiannon of Clan Calonawr like a suit. She had begged the Calamity for her life, citing a relationship to the same goddess as reason to do so.

Rhazal hadn’t agreed, and so he had cast her into dust.

But by that point, Rhiannon had been dead for years. The ancient Vampire, a form of parasitic super-weapon crafted by the ‘gods’ during the War in Heaven, had killed and stolen her body to use as a plaything.

I hadn’t been the one to kill her.

Stumbling on my words, aware that I was losing blood fast, I struggled to convey this to Daffyd. But when I finished…

My weakly beating heart sank when I met his eyes.

He didn’t care. Daffyd…was too insane for me to even reach. Too long gone to even be reasoned with. I’m not sure he had even heard a word I’d said.

Anguis had filled the head of a grieving father with lies, and I was about to die for it.

How…ironic.

“With your death, my vengeance will be complete,” Daffyd whispered to me, a feverish light in his wolven eyes. “Die for me, Nathaniel Hart. Die for my daughter.”

Daffyd howled and clenched down with both of his hands.

In response, both of my shoulders shattered from the monstrous strength of the werewolf, and I…

I lost control over my pain response from the sheer intensity of the reaction. The resulting agony of both my shoulder bones being reduced to dust instantly was too much for me.

I passed out, certain I was about to die for the whispering of the Serpent.

As the world faded to black, I was chased into the land of unconsciousness by something curious.

Three howls.

One of savage victory.

One of furious grief.

And one…

Of defiant denial.
