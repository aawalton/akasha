Don found one of the long halls the Grimoids had built and settled in there. He put down his bundles of spears. He wasn’t going to train his Telekinesis with them, lest they break. He would need them all to fight against Morhsoff.

That was fine though. There was plenty of furniture left behind. Don kicked a long table and it shattered into dozens of wooden bits. Then without further ceremony he lifted half a dozen pieces in the air around him. He started spinning them around him, trying to keep them hovering at the same altitude and moving at the same speed.

Don quickly decided six was way too many pieces. His mind would just get overwhelmed and all the pieces would drop. So he went with three. That was difficult but manageable. First Don sat down and made the pieces circle around him. But the wood splinters were light, and he couldn’t keep a high speed while keeping the pieces in an orbit around him.

So he opted to move around. First he walked slowly. It was pretty easy. But when he started running and jumping around, while keeping the three pieces of wood circling around him the exercise got a little bit difficult.

Don kept doing it for a while and soon added a fourth piece.

I could actually form a technique around this. Float sheets of metal or shields. That could be effective against enemies with projectiles.

Of course Don would have to practice something like that, so that whatever he was using as a shield wouldn’t just be pushed at him when something like an arrow struck it. He would need to be able to preemptively block the strike, like a boxer.

But that was definitely a viable technique to develop. He would need a sufficiently weak enemy or a training partner though. He wondered how that girl, Charlie was doing.

I sure hope she got the hell away from Vance and that backwards camp.

Don figured he would check up on the camp once he had things settled with Jacques. The girl was level headed and had grit. Don could appreciate that. He wasn’t interested in playing any kind of leader to her or anyone for that matter. He wasn’t a pack hunter, but a solo predator. More akin to cat than a dog.

But if there’s a chance Baxter is out there somewhere… Maybe I’ll revisit the pack idea.

Eventually Don sat down again and tried to levitate the six pieces again. He could now manage levitating all of them, but having them circle around him was slow. If he wanted to make this into a combat application, he could only use two objects.

So Don did that next. He unbound the two shields from his bundles and tried to move them around in the air.

Don had enough power with his psychics that the shields weren’t heavy for him, but they had enough heft that floating two objects was difficult.

He dropped the other shield. The single shield was easy to maneuver. Especially in straight lines. Don was good at picking one direction and making the shield accelerate. But now instead of going full gas, no breaks, he tried to halt the trajectory before the shield crashed into the wall. Most of the time he succeeded…

It was great practice. Don spent over an hour just maneuvering the shield, making it accelerate and slow down, and to circle around his body in varying orbits. A shield was actually a perfect weapon for his psionics. He could launch it like a projectile, and it could also block.

What I need is a shield with a bladed edge…

Don scoffed. His mind sure had changed. That was a brutal thought. But it was efficient and correct. If Don ever had a chance to get something done, maybe he should ask for shields with serrated bladed edges. Kind of like reinforced buzz saws.

But for now he would have to settle for spears. Don didn’t want to use the hobgoblin spears from the bundles, but he left the hall and went outside to loot the Grimoids he had killed earlier. They had plenty of spears lying around. Don started picking them up with his mind and throwing them around. He got into a rhythm. It was almost like a psychic dance. Spears flew around him as he threw them at walls and pulled them from the ground. Tsok, Tsok, Tsok.

Don lost himself in the practice. He was much more efficient about his Energy, but it was definitely draining down. The mental exhaustion was mounting, but it was all coming together.

The movements of the spears became snappier. Some of them shattered against the walls. Some of them broke in two, but those two snapped pieces joined the juggling act. Around Don swirled a dervish of wood that exploded outwards like lightning bolts made of wood and steel.

Soon his efforts paid off.

[Racial Skill Increased: Telekinesis]

[Telekinesis Level 6]

But since his Energy wasn’t spent he didn’t stop. He just brushed away the notification and kept pushing his skills. It was beautiful. Don hadn’t really surrendered to improving at something in his previous life. Nothing had been worthwhile. But this was important. This was survival. He needed to develop his Telekinesis, like his life depended on it— because it did. And with the surrender to the craft came a certain beauty. There was elegance to the psychics. Like playing an instrument or dancing. It reminded him of Mal’asha’s deadly grace. She wasn’t what one might have expected of a top level predator. She was sophisticated. Don was starting to realize why.

You could only push so far with brute strength and willpower. Those were Don’s primary weapons, and he would leverage them to their fullest. But he needed to augment his arsenal. Sure, he was a hammer to Mal’asha’s scalpel, but he could be a hammer with a sharpened edge. And here in the grind of the whetstone for a sharper edge, Don found great beauty.

He was having fun.

But the focus required was immense. As his training prolonged, his grip of the spears started slipping. Some of them clattered to the ground as he kept throwing and picking them up. It was fine. He wasn’t trying to be perfect. He was just trying to improve. The work became sloppier and harder, but it didn’t really matter. It was enjoyable, and despite how hard it was, Don kept improving his skill.

His Energy kept draining, but Don kept working. Soon the spears started clattering more and more, and he could barely lift them up. Even still, Don kept pushing. He recognized this point. This wasn’t the point of giving up. This was just a wall. Don had torn through several walls of exhaustion and pain. He knew how to handle this. He had the required will and presence of mind. He pushed through and soon he started managing more spears.

It was hard. Sweat didn’t drop in beads. It poured. Eventually his Energy was drained to 2% and the System started blaring at him. Don wanted to push further, but he had to drop. It was fine. He would allow it. The strain on his mind was massive.

He dropped to his knees instantly when he let go of the spears with his mind. Don managed to shuffle into a nearby shack and fold his new cloak into a pillow before he basically blacked out instantly. His work was done. Tomorrow morning he would get another Telekinesis level, for he was certainly close to one. Then he would rest up and make a plan. And then it was time to finally face the music.
