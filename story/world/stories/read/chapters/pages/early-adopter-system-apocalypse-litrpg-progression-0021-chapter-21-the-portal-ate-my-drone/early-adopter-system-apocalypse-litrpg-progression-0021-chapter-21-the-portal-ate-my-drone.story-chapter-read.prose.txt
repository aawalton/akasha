The next steps Scarlet took would come down to a simple question. Was the portal a ‘right now’ type of problem, or an ‘after a meal and a nap’ type of problem. Unfortunately, she had no prior experience with massive distortions in the fabric of space and time. Experience that would be helpful right about now.

Gritting her teeth she took a single step towards the boundary of the clearing. She wasn’t even sure her toe had fully crossed that invisible barrier – hadn’t even known there was a barrier there – when a prompt popped into her field of view.

[Enter Tier 1 Portal? ][Yes | No]

She startled herself with just how quickly she pulled her leg back.

The prompt disappeared.

She stared at the distorted clearing for a while. Probed at it with her senses, old and new. It wasn’t a large clearing, perhaps three, three and a half metres in diameter. In the centre was the most obvious distortion, that lattice of colour that she had naively assumed was the portal itself. However, she had come to realise that the portal was the clearing. She hadn’t missed this place in earlier scans because it hadn’t existed before. A terrifying and awe-inspiring concept.

She’d felt the force of the portal, the way it warped the world, the way those spatial energies had pressed into her, around her. It had turned ‘space’ from a concept into a palpable force in a way she hadn’t felt before. If it hadn’t been for whatever failsafe the System had in place to prevent people from just being at the whims of these anomalies Scarlet couldn’t imagine the kinds of chaos that could bring.

She looked over at Maus, the Spatial Mouse. The little guy was grooming his face nonchalantly on her shoulder, and Scarlet had to wonder just what kind of force he’d grow into. She went to give his head a little scritch until she realised she was holding the drone.

Her eyes went from the drone to the portal, and back again perhaps three or four times before she made up her mind.

Sending the drone into the distortion would help her answer the question about her next steps.

Scarlet lowered the drone into position with a practised motion. Instead of letting it fly freely, she routed the video feed into her phone through a direct link. It was a very long cable. Then, she activated it with a flick of her thumb before guiding it forward. Though the device was low to the ground, it moved smoothly through the air, its small frame steady as it approached the boundary.

Scarlet watched its progress through the feed.

Just like when it had been flying overhead the cameras weren’t picking up any of the distortion that Scarlet could see. However, the video wasn’t static. Through the screen she watched as the camera appeared to fisheye before stuttering and snapping back to normal, then zooming, or ballooning the picture in a single corner, or point of the frame. It wasn’t pleasant to watch, but it mostly just looked like something in the software was glitching disturbingly, or that the hardware was malfunctioning.

The drone made it almost a metre into the clearing, the feedback getting worse as it went before suddenly, nothing. The screen went black; the drone just disappeared.

It wasn’t crushed, or warped, or thrown out of the clearing. It was just… gone.

The slackened cable dropped to the grass with a muted thud Scarlet couldn’t hear over the pounding of her heartbeat.

The portal ate her drone.

No wonder anything that could get the hell out had fled. As she contemplated what this meant she began absentmindedly coiling the now ruined cable. It hadn’t gone far, and so it didn’t take long for the distorted end to come into view.

She brought the mutilated blob of what could now only charitably be called ‘wire’ to her face. Unlike the clean cutaway point she’d been expecting, the cable looked wrong. Warped, and pitted, like it had been burned, and frozen, and inverted. Simultaneously.

“Yeah,” Scarlet drew out the word. “Nope.”

She turned and began to walk away. No way on this green Earth was she about to enter that portal before she had a meal, a sleep, and a think.

<-…->

Scarlet had re-entered the bunker, cleaned off, and settled in the control centre to eat a rather tense meal. The giant, gaping hole in reality made relaxation somewhat difficult. So, she decided to engage in the time-honoured human tradition of making herself too busy to acknowledge her feelings.

Scarlet got to work.

She ate with one hand as she took another look at the Quest in her Interface. With the other hand she pulled out the notebook she had dubbed 'Preliminary Plans Post-Apocalypse'.

It seemed a Safe Zone was more than just a monster-free area. If administrative rights meant what she believed they meant, then Safe Zones were established settlements. Settlement meant governance, and dealing with the headache that unironically called itself civilisation.

Establishing a Safe Zone also meant the System would be pitting her against the powers that be. Governments were known as ‘administrations’ for a reason, and Scarlet had no interest in spearheading a direct conflict. Not when she had other, better, options.

Could Scarlet deal with people? Sure. Would Scarlet deal with people? If she had to. Was Scarlet inclined to deal with people; was she enthusiastic to take on a role that would so obviously expose her? No. Absolutely not.

She had no desire to be a politician. She was no champion of humanity. Sure, on a collective level she cared. She wasn't cheering on mass casualties, and she wasn’t unsympathetic to some of the horrors caused by the Integration. Would that make her stick her neck out? Not on the lives of millions more.

It might seem callous, however dying to save ten meant she wouldn’t be alive to save hundreds. More importantly to her, she wouldn’t be alive, period. She wasn’t okay with that.

She opened the backend of STAR and found that the engagement had ballooned. Her site was filling a gap; for information, for communication, for connection. More than how to get new skills, or grind Stats, it looked like most people just wanted to know they weren't alone. To share, to be seen, to be heard.

Community. STAR was becoming a community.

She tweaked some code to try and help with the speed issue caused by heavy encryption.

See, Scarlet was great at this politics thing. Give people what they thought they needed so she could get what she wanted. She just didn't want to do it in person.

Inconveniently, for those people to exist, and to continue to spend their time contemplating, iterating, and creating, they required a relatively safe environment.

Unfortunately, the most reliable way to assure peace and stability was through force, or the threat of it. In some cases, words simply weren’t enough. It was why ‘bloodless revolution’ was basically an oxymoron.

Social instability also meant her and Maus’s safety would increasingly rely on their ability to defend themselves. Scarlet had no illusions about the type of threat other humans posed now that social order was eroding. Some people were only waiting for an opportunity.

She’d had enough of that for one lifetime.

Unassailable authority was one solution, though personal strength was optimal. This Quest appeared to offer her an opportunity for both.

Of course, whether people knew she wielded that authority or not was entirely on them. So, she needed her own people. Not just any random people, people she trusted.

That thought was so absurd a spontaneous chuckle burst out of her.

Okay, not people she trusted, but people she could trust to do a job, and people she could subordinate. Yes, that was much more likely.

"You know, buddy, I also have absolutely no intention of being the public face of an entire settlement.” She narrowed her eyes playfully at him. “You are the damage dealer of this little duo we have going on. I don't suppose you'd be interested in taking on some political battles as well?” Maus stared back at her with a vacancy so profound she wondered for a moment if he was sleeping with his eyes open. She probed the bond, only to find he had not been paying any attention at all.

The corners of her mouth twitched.

"Maus," she called. “Buddy,” she tickled the little guy and watched the life return to his eyes. Watched him scratch at his ear before latching onto her hand so he could groom her fingers.

She smiled, emotion seizing her for a moment. She closed her eyes, and breathed through it, focusing on the hum of her computers, the smell of books, and the sensation of tiny paws on her hand.

When she was once more collected, she gently extracted her hand from Maus. She stroked his little head before turning back to her monitors.

She pulled up a secure messaging app, and as she got back to work, she felt something subtly shift.

She checked her log. Her Vitality had just increased? How? Why?

Then she remembered: 'Vitality is increased through recovery.'

Recovery. Just what counted as recovery? She had hypothesised that the Guide had been referring to physical recovery. However, the Guide had never explicitly stated that VIT only affected her body. Even if it did, was her mind not a part of her body? Could she improve her VIT by overcoming mental fatigue? If that truly were the case, then what about emotional recovery, magical recovery?

"What a holistically sadistic little Stat you are," she murmured to herself, scarcely registering that the corners of her own lips had pulled up just a little. She could work with this.

Scarlet pulled out a completely new – 100% necessary, this one had grid paper – notebook and began to make notes.

That increase in Vitality had shown her that her Stats did not divide neatly into categories like ‘physical’, ‘magical’, or ‘mental’. Not only that, but she had been primarily relying on Maus for combat capability. This wasn't necessarily bad, it just wasn't ideal.

What if they got separated? What if – any and all gods forbid – something took him out and then he was on a five-day cooldown?

Integration had rendered moot a lot of her personal safety preparations. If something happened to Maus right now, she wouldn't just be alone. She'd be unacceptably vulnerable.

That psionic trick she'd pulled off with the bat was neat, but she had no idea how she'd done it, and no actual control over it. Yet.

She’d need practice. That meant honing her mind along with her body.

She resolved to head into that breach the next day. Wasn't the Guide always insisting she advance? Didn't she want information? Yes. She had a leg injury, but it wasn’t crippling. Her biggest issue would be the pain, and it wasn’t like she was new to pushing through pain.

Besides, there was no guarantee she’d always be in perfect condition before being thrust into a crisis.

For tonight, she’d send out messages to the people she wanted on the team. In her Planning notebook she began working on a list. She just really hoped the people she contacted hadn't found themselves mauled to death by magical wildlife or some other such inconvenient tragedy in the few hours since the timer ran out.

Then she’d need to allocate her FS and capitalise on her headstart. She’d always planned to enter the portal, she just hadn’t planned to do so this soon. With this Quest, her new insights, and everything she was seeing on STAR, she realised waiting wasn’t an option.

Apocalypse day 2. Plan: Enter portal. Don’t die.
