Azure watched as her wild-haired, yet pristine, beautiful goddess inspected the temple walls and doors, nodding to herself as she did. It was still strange how her slowly bleeding eyes remained closed even as she ‘looked,’ using her invisible third eye to focus.

The closed eyes look wasn’t so bad, anyway. It had an elegance and vulnerability to it. It was kinda growing on her, and as much as she instinctively worried it was a problem that needed fixing, Samantha’s claims to be ‘right as rain’ continued to be proven true.

Aside from that, when I do get to see her eyes, it’s like a special treat. Kinda like undressing her, eh hehe.

Finally, Samantha turned to Azure and raised an eyebrow. “Door, or wall?”

“What?” Azure asked, perplexed.

“To destroy. This place isn’t going to just let us in. I need to break through, one way or another. Fortunately, I’m good at breaking things.”

“Are you sure there isn’t a better way?”

“Oh, there is, love… but we’d need to know the pass phrase, and we don’t.”

“Samantha, we can’t fuck this up, not now. What if breaking in fucks it up?”

“Uhhh… in that case, we’re fucked.”

“Exactly. Do you think I might know it?”

“Possibly. That sounds rather Goldie-like, honestly.”

Biting her lip, Azure thought about it. Something only she’d know? A phrase…

She walked up to the door, squaring her shoulders. She called, “Monster in the Mirror!”

Nothing.

Turning toward Samantha, she asked, “Do you think we have infinite tries?”

Samantha shrugged. “I don’t see why not. I can monitor the realm. It won’t last forever… in fact, it appears to be slightly destabilized more than before, likely from my massive radial destruction magic. I’d say something less than an hour, certainly.”

“So, I can try, for what, up to thirty minutes?”

“Sure. We have to allow a few minutes to be inside, after all. I’ll monitor things… tell you when it's time for us to go forward with my idea, as a last resort. I’m pretty sure I can break through with something, though, especially enhanced with My Future Now.”

“Alright… fuck, what else…” She mined the most recent vision she’d had. Catch phrases? She tried what she could recall:

“I’m unbreakable!” Nope.

“I’ll see your end, Sage!” Nope.

“My preservation is wise.” No? Damn it!

“Wait, I got it, I got it!” She cleared her throat and left a long pause. “Some mercies can’t be avoided.” She waited long, long moments after, expectant.

“That’s not it, Azure,” Samantha muttered.

“Come on, you fucking door! That has to be it!” Azure flopped on the stone floor, pouting and hanging her head. “I’ll never get in there… it’s hopeless…”

“Oh, stop it, you drama queen.” Samantha sighed. “Look, you need to mine deeper than some shit like that. Something more personal. Those words were surface-level crap.”

Azure sat up and faced the door cross-legged, glaring determinedly. “I’ll dive so fucking deep, door! Just you wait, I know I’ve got the answer to this bullshit in this here fabulous crystalline brain of mine.”

“That’s the spirit…”

Azure mined deeper back, finding other things from that other life to spew. There weren’t many positives, but she tried everything… again and again and again, summoning up phrases. Her motivation and inspiration waxed and waned. Samantha ended up sitting down cross-legged at her back, facing the opposite direction, with them leaning against each other. Azure drew from that contact, letting it fuel her with strength.

But it was all to no avail. Nothing muttered worked to open the door.

Azure was soon moaning piteously in her lover’s lap. “There’s nothiiiing, Chestnut!” she cried. “Nothing works! Damn that four-armed snake! Damn all snakes! I hate them! It’s something stupid, is what it is! Like you said, something no one would know, lost forever…”

Samantha just patted her back, not bothering with more admonishment. Likely, she was ‘admonished out’ by now.

I think this is my sixth time giving up…

As Azure’s whines died down, Samantha patted her back insistently. “Up… it’s time we just broke through, babe… sorry…”

“No, no, no, no!” Azure said, suddenly grabbing at Samantha as if to prevent her from doing it. “I-I… can do more guesses!”

“Azure…”

“Babe, we can’t fuck this up! I need to know!”

“Do you, though?”

“Wha-?! Of course I-”

“Bah! Never mind, Azure — there’s no time! I need to break in now, before this place unravels. Don’t you want to at least have the chance that breaking in gives us?”

“I-I… mmnph…” She had no more to say.

Slowly, gently, Samantha slid free and floated over to the door. Azure sat up, watching with horrible anticipation. She knew this wasn’t going to work…

Samantha hesitated, turning back, sighing with a sag of her shoulders. Her eyes squinted open and went watery. Teary-eyed from exposure and the bleeding, she nonetheless met Azure’s eyes with her striking, soul-melting, vibrant blue windows, framed by the perpetual stains of red down her cheeks. “I’m sorry, Azure. We have to try, right? Some things, try as we might, are just lost forever, like skipping stones finally falling into the sea, however good the throw…” She turned back and prepared to cast a destructive spell, holding out her hand. “On that note, here goes nothing!”

Stones lost in the sea…

“Wait!” Azure cried, shooting up onto her feet. “Wait-wait-wait!” The memory I had, mine alone, perhaps…

Samantha frowned and looked back at her. “I can’t wait any longer-”

“Shhh! Hold on… please… thirty seconds!”

The goddess nodded reluctantly and dropped her hand.

Azure walked up to the door, her mind cast to some of her first memories as she tried to collect them. They hurt to dredge up because every piece of evidence in her life seemed to suggest they were illusory or warped.

It was in a rocky hollow on the sandy coast in the Bay of Mengior, near Sovar Ska, near the place that had been the Temple of the Changers. But it was nothing so grand as that… just her supposed childhood, and that of her friend, Jallee.

They carved something on the bottom of a stone, and Jallee claimed it would be there forever, regardless of the prevailing wisdom that storms flooded and claimed things from the hollow. He’d bet her a gold piece.

On that later day, when Azure had returned to the bay and rejected entering the temple ruins, she’d checked for the stone and not found it, finding only some other names written by others on a larger rock, likely by lovers. She’d thought something like ‘Maybe true love will prevail over childhood friends and a stone for throwing.’

She’d not spoken nor thought the words on that stone, then, and perhaps they’d never been uttered or etched whatsoever in the world.

And yet, she recalled them perfectly.

Standing in front of the obsidian door, she whispered, “Jallee and Azure, big and rich and famous, were here.”

With a shudder, the door warped and twisted and folded in on itself, shrinking and turning molten, until it became a tiny, tiny piece of shining ‘something’ clattering on the floor and rolling in a circle. Azure dropped to her knees, watching it, knowing, dreading what it would become. In the process, intuitively, perhaps by some power of the place, her body morphed back into soft, vulnerable form.

She took up the piece, ignoring everything else. When she opened her hand, there was a simple gold coin in her palm.

Tears bloomed in Azure’s eyes, and a sob wracked her. She gripped the coin and curled inward on herself, as Samantha put her arm around her and comforted her.

“Liar,” she managed, breathless, accusing the air with little more than a gasp, but viciously, such was her pain. “Liars! We’re always liars, and I am the biggest of them all, to myself…”

She knew what the coin really meant. She felt it in her core and fiber — Jallee never existed. The stone had never been there, the words had never been etched to be so much as lost. They had no meaning or purpose, but for right here, to unlock things almost wanting to be forgotten, to vanish without a trace.

It was a bitter pill for someone like Goldie to give her, a knife in the gut, ultimately, but at least it was a raw, quick end to the lies.

This is the end of its power over me. I know what this is, Goldie. I know. It’s a warning shot. Damn you, thank you… I see. What does it matter? What the fuck does it matter how I was made into who I am?

Azure peeled her hand open, finger by finger, shaking, clenching her teeth, and finally letting the coin fall back to the floor with a clang. With effort, she turned away from it, closed her eyes, and stood, with Samantha helping her up.

Azure kept her eyes closed as she said as steadily as she could manage, “Fuck the vision, fuck the old life, fuck it all. I don’t need any more of this.” She opened her eyes, looking directly at Samantha. “I just need you. I was nothing before you came along; I’d be nothing if you didn’t. Nothing, or worse, like… like her.” She looked down and nodded — mostly to herself. “She’s still not me. Right? I can believe that. I choose to. I know I was made for you — literally, now — and it doesn’t bother me. I-I said that long ago, when I discovered I was a construct…”

Samantha’s hand brought her chin up, and the goddess was smiling with blatant, undeniable affection and love — a beauty to rival spring’s bloom. “You’re far more, Azure,” she declared, “and you know this. As opposed to nothing, you’re everything. At the least, you know you’ll never, ever convince me otherwise. So, why don’t you marry me, already?”

Before Azure could reply, Samantha kissed her.

Through the open doorway, unbridled energy hummed, waiting.
