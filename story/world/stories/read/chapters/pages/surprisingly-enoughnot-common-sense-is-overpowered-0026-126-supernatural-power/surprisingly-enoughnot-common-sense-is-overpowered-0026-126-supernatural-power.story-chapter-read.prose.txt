Ning was at the Spiritual Farming Hall’s library, a quiet, quaint place filled with the faint scent of parchment and dried herbs. It had taken him more than a month to finally visit. Between finishing his duties at the Scripture Pavilion and managing his fields, he simply hadn’t found the time until now.

As Ning glanced around, his gaze soon landed on a massive scroll displayed prominently at the center of the library.

[Alchemy required spiritual herbs and fruits.

Talisman crafting needed spiritual grasses, bamboo, and bark.

Artifact forging and array formation demanded materials such as spiritual wood.

In the end, farming was the cornerstone of all professions.]

Ning stared at the final line for a moment.

He wasn’t sure whether the declaration was meant to inspire spiritual farmers or comfort them over the fact that almost every other profession seemed to receive more respect.

Either way, the Spiritual Farming Hall clearly had strong opinions about its own importance.

Moving deeper into the library, Ning quickly realized there were almost no cultivation techniques or spells here. Instead, the shelves were packed with notes, manuscripts, planting records, and guides concerning every aspect of spiritual agriculture.

He picked up a thick volume titled A Guide to Spiritual Agriculture and began reading.

Clearing land, cultivating soil, breeding seeds, adjusting fertilizer, controlling pests...

Spirit fields were ideally established around spiritual veins. If no natural vein was available, an area with sufficiently dense spiritual energy could be supplemented with a spirit-gathering array instead.

The initial process wasn't too different from ordinary farming. The land had to be deeply plowed, weeds removed, and ridges properly arranged. The main difference was that soil saturated with spiritual energy was considerably tougher than ordinary earth, while the weeds growing from it were correspondingly more troublesome.

Even the weeds cultivated in this world.

For that reason, clearing new spirit fields generally required at least a low-level Qi Condensation cultivator, preferably one trained in the Spirit-Forging Earth-Shaking Technique. The technique loosened large areas of soil while drawing spiritual energy deeper into the earth, greatly reducing the amount of physical labor required.

Of course, the fields within the Pure Qi Sect had been cultivated for generations, so Ning didn't need to worry about starting from scratch. Newly opened fields outside the sect were another matter. Such land generally lacked both fertility and accumulated spiritual vitality, requiring fertilizers, spells, and formations before it could reliably support spiritual crops.

The simplest fertilizer was surprisingly straightforward: exhausted spirit stones.

Even after a spirit stone had been drained of most of its usable qi, traces of spiritual energy remained within it. Crushing those stones into powder and mixing them into the earth gradually enriched the soil. Other commonly used materials included ash from cinnabar wood and composted spirit beast manure.

According to the book, establishing one acre of first-grade spirit field generally required:

– Twenty exhausted spirit stones, crushed into powder– One hundred catties of cinnabar wood ash– One hundred catties of spirit beast manure

Afterward, a low-grade spirit-gathering array and yearly applications of fertilizer were recommended to maintain the field.

“Pretty general,” Ning murmured.

Most of this was basic knowledge. Useful, certainly, but nothing that would suddenly transform his fields.

Hu Yue had already warned him that the genuinely advanced material was kept separately and required contribution points to access. Ning could only accept the existence of yet another cultivation-world paywall, especially considering the current state of his finances.

Still, the free material wasn't useless.

As Ning continued reading, he found cultivation methods for dozens of different spirit grains. The variety he currently grew was among the most basic, valued mainly for being resilient and easy to maintain. As long as it received regular spiritual rain and proper care, even an inexperienced farmer could achieve a decent harvest.

The varieties listed afterward were considerably more demanding.

Purple Extreme Rice, Blazing Sun Rice, Blood Marrow Rice, Green Bamboo Rice, Pearl Rice, Golden Bell Millet...

Most were high-grade first-level spiritual grains, and nearly all required mastery of the Small Cloud Rain Technique at Great Accomplishment alongside one or more supplementary spells. Some demanded special fertilizers, others required carefully controlled temperatures, and a few were apparently temperamental enough that Ning wondered why anyone bothered growing them at all.

After nearly an hour of browsing, however, something finally caught his attention.

[Frozen Breath Grain]

High-grade first-level spiritual grain possessing an ice attribute. Requires the Hidden Ice Mist Technique during cultivation. Six-month growth cycle. Low supplementary requirements.

Ning immediately read the entry more carefully.

Among the high-grade grains listed in the book, Frozen Breath Grain was unusually straightforward. Compared to Sheer Ice Rice, which required similar environmental conditions but depended on several expensive fertilizers, Frozen Breath Grain was cheaper to cultivate and required far fewer supplementary materials.

Its spiritual potency was somewhat lower in exchange, but that hardly mattered to Ning.

What mattered was the requirement.

Hidden Ice Mist Technique.

For most spiritual farmers, learning an entirely new spell just to grow one particular crop would be a serious investment of time. Ning, however, had never been particularly frightened of learning another spell, especially after overcoming his inner demon and finding that his comprehension had improved noticeably.

He tapped the page thoughtfully.

Six months, relatively cheap materials, and one new spell.

That sounded manageable.

So it’s decided, Ning thought.

...

“Junior Brother, are you sure you want to choose Frozen Breath Grain?” Hu Yue asked later, visibly surprised by his choice.

It was a rather niche crop. Not only did it require an ice attribute, but anyone hoping to grow it also had to learn the specialized Hidden Ice Mist Technique.

There were other high-grade grains with similarly low supplementary costs, and while they also required specialized spells, those spells tended to have uses beyond farming. The Hidden Ice Mist Technique, on the other hand, had very little offensive power. Learning it solely to grow one type of grain was hardly an appealing investment for most cultivators.

“Yes, Senior Sister,” Ning replied. “I cultivate an ice-attributed technique, so I think Frozen Breath Grain will suit me quite well.”

He didn't mention the name of his cultivation art, but everything he said was true.

“If that’s your decision, then it’s fine.” Hu Yue nodded after a moment. “But you’ll need to reach at least Great Accomplishment in the Small Cloud Rain Technique before you can grow it properly.”

“I’m aware. I’m already close, so it shouldn’t take much longer.”

Ning glanced at his panel.

[Small Cloud Rain Technique: Minor Accomplishment (199/200)]

“As expected, you really are talented, Junior Brother.” Hu Yue sounded genuinely impressed. Ning's sect token contained records of his progress in the spells he'd registered, so she had little reason to doubt him. “You remind me of Senior Li Yue when he was younger.”

“Senior Li Yue?” Ning asked, catching the unfamiliar name.

“Mm. He was born with a supernatural ability related to plants,” Hu Yue explained. “Apparently, he can stimulate the vitality within seeds and accelerate their growth, especially when combined with the Spring Breeze Seedling Technique. For a spiritual farmer, it's an incredible talent.”

“That really is amazing,” Ning said sincerely.

An ability that could directly accelerate the growth of spiritual plants was practically a spiritual farmer's dream.

“He entered the sect long before you,” Hu Yue continued. “By now, he's already reached the late stage of Foundation Building.”

Ning nodded, filing the name away for later.

Then another thought occurred to him.

“Senior Sister, since we're talking about promising disciples...” Ning began carefully. “Would it be possible for the Hall to reimburse me for the Hidden Ice Mist Technique?”

Hu Yue blinked. “Reimburse you?”

“Yes.” Ning's expression remained perfectly serious. “Think about it. Frozen Breath Grain isn't commonly grown in the Hall. If I can cultivate it successfully, we'll have another high-grade spiritual grain available, especially one with an ice attribute. That would be useful for trade, alchemy, and increasing the variety of crops produced by the Hall.”

Hu Yue stared at him.

Ning continued before she could interrupt.

“The Hidden Ice Mist Technique may cost the Hall some spirit stones now, but that's an investment in a promising spiritual farmer. If I succeed, the Hall benefits as well.” He paused before adding, somewhat shamelessly, “Besides, Senior Sister, you know how limited the income of a new spiritual farmer is.”

As he spoke, Ning deployed one of the few techniques in his arsenal that required neither spiritual energy nor an incantation.

The Puppy Dog Eyes Technique.

Its effectiveness depended entirely on possessing a sufficiently cute and attractive face.

Fortunately, Ning had been blessed in that particular department. With this face, even Hikigaya Hachiman's dream of becoming a househusband might have been achievable.

Hu Yue looked at him for several seconds before finally sighing.

“Fine, fine. The Elder did tell us to give promising disciples some additional support. I'll ask him about it.” She immediately raised a finger. “But I'm not promising anything.”

Ning's face brightened.

“Thank you, Senior Sister.”

...

“So, what exactly are supernatural powers, Elder Shen?” Ning asked as the old man leisurely sipped his wine.

Over the past few months, Ning had grown quite close to Elder Shen. It hadn't been particularly difficult. All it took was good wine, a few snacks, the patience to listen to an old man's stories, and knowing when to offer a well-placed compliment.

Ning had made a habit of reporting the pavilion's daily happenings to him, and with Old Zhou's spiritual wine occasionally serving as a bridge between them, their relationship had naturally grown closer.

Elder Shen was getting on in years, and like most people, he enjoyed having someone genuinely listen when he talked about his past. Ning, meanwhile, was young, had no clan backing, and rarely bothered with the layered schemes and hidden intentions Elder Shen had probably dealt with throughout his life. Even if the old man could tell Ning was deliberately trying to build a good relationship with him, there was nothing particularly objectionable about it.

Ning gave him wine, listened to his stories, handled the pavilion well, and occasionally provided useful suggestions. Elder Shen gave him advice and answered his questions.

It was a perfectly healthy exchange of emotional and material benefits.

Of course, this might also be thanks to my natural talent for pleasing old men-wait, that sounded wrong.

Ning coughed lightly and immediately abandoned that train of thought.

“Supernatural powers,” Elder Shen began, swirling the wine in his cup, “are innate abilities. They come in all forms. Some are incredibly useful, while others are barely worth mentioning. There doesn't seem to be any fixed rule governing who awakens one. Some believe they're gifts from heaven, others say they're tied to peculiarities of the soul, while another theory traces them back to ancient human bloodlines.”

“I see.” Ning nodded thoughtfully.

That was easy enough to understand. Supernatural powers were somewhat like spiritual roots: some people possessed them, and among those who did, the difference between one person's ability and another's could be enormous.

“Of course,” Elder Shen continued, taking another sip before reaching for the potato chips Ning had brought, “if we're talking about supernatural powers, then we have to mention the Sect Leader.”

“Sect Leader?” Ning's interest immediately rose.

“Yes. It was about a hundred years ago.” Elder Shen's expression grew nostalgic. “The sect was in turmoil. Our previous Golden Core Sect Leader perished, while enemies both within and outside the sect were beginning to stir. It was a bloody, uncertain time.”

He paused for another drink.

“And that was when Ge Xuan rose.”

Ning leaned forward slightly, crunching on a chip as he listened.

“With his supernatural powers, Sword Bone and Sword Intent, and his newly formed Golden Core, he fought the mid-stage Golden Core leader of the Dark Soul Sect. Ge Xuan was a once-in-a-century genius. With a sword in hand, he cut down enemies, suppressed the rebellion, and forced the surrounding sects to withdraw their greedy hands. Much of the stability we enjoy today was built by him during those years.”

Elder Shen sighed, his eyes glinting faintly in the lamplight as old memories resurfaced.

Ning could only imagine how chaotic the Pure Qi Sect must have been back then. Without occasional lore drops like this, it was easy to forget just how much history had taken place before he ever arrived.

“Elder Shen must have been quite remarkable back then too,” Ning said with a grin.

“Of course!” The slightly drunk elder immediately puffed out his chest. “With my spells, who among those in my realm could stop me? When one enemy came, I killed one. When two came, I killed two. Even when ten surrounded me, I still fought my way through! Those were the days.”

Ning nodded seriously. “As expected of Elder Shen. Ten against one, and they were still the ones at a disadvantage.”

Elder Shen blinked before bursting into laughter. “Hahaha! Exactly! Ning, you really do understand me.”

“I'm merely stating the truth,” Ning replied without the slightest shame.

The elder nodded approvingly, clearly delighted. Ning had long since discovered that Elder Shen required very little maintenance. A cup of good wine, an attentive audience, and the occasional reminder that he had apparently been an absolute menace in his youth were generally enough.

“I only hope I can reach even one-tenth of Elder Shen's level someday.”

“Don't sell yourself short, Ning. I see a little of myself in you. You'll surely accomplish something great.” Elder Shen's words were beginning to slur.

Ning judged that the wine had reached precisely the right stage.

“Elder, I've actually been wanting to learn more about supernatural powers, but I haven't found any books about them in the pavilion. Is there somewhere I could read about them?”

After all, the old man was only another cup away from declaring that back in his day, he was Him.

Elder Shen stroked his beard. “Books concerning supernatural powers are difficult to find. Most have to be purchased from outside, and even then they're rare.” He thought for a moment. “You're lucky, though. I happen to have a jade slip on the subject. I'll lend it to you in a few days.”

“Thank you, Elder.”

Ning immediately poured him another cup.

No wonder people said having an old man at home was like possessing a treasure. Elder Shen was doing an excellent job proving the saying correct.
