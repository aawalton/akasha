Edge made his way to the arena with a spring in his step, hoping to squeeze in a training session with Beelo before his first low-rung brawl, which would take place in another six hours.

He had been looking forward to their reunion since returning from the Haunted Vale, eager to discuss his battles with the monsters and see if the man had any advice on how he could improve his performance. Truth be told, Edge was even more excited to let the warrior know he registered as a gladiator. Maybe I can convince him to share some tips on climbing the ladder.

When he stopped by the training annex, he learned Beelo was available as soon as the man finished a lesson that was already in session. Edge paid for an hour’s instruction and waited for his mentor to appear, passing the time by chatting with the warriors seated along the benches filling one corner of the melee-weapons floor. Rue materialized part of the way through, making friends with a pair of beasts while the crowd laughed at their antics.

When the gladiator appeared, he gestured for Edge and Rue to follow him into the private room he was using. They spent a while catching up while going through some drills. When Edge was done soliciting the man for advice for fighting in the ring, he decided to broach a topic he’d been thinking about for the last few days instead of requesting a regular session.

“I’ve got a strange question for you today. I don’t think we’ve talked about this skill yet, since it’s not directly related to wielding a glaive in battle.”

“I’m listening. Show me what you’ve got and tell me what you need.”

With that, Edge summoned the left chain of oblivion. Foebinder penetrated the skin of his wrist and extended into the air, stretching like it had been sitting in the same position for too long.

“That’s an interesting skill. Can you control the chain directly, or does it obey simple commands? I assume you’ve come up with a way to minimize how much it impedes your ability to control your polearm.”

“Yes to all of the above. But that isn’t what I wanted to discuss.”

Beelo’s eyebrows had shot up when he learned the living links were so versatile, but that was nothing compared to the astonishment on his face when Edge finished his next sentence.

“Foebinder is self-aware. It wants you to teach it how to wield Opulence and make the most of its potential.”

“Your chain can learn from experience? That would make it more like a Companion than a simple construct. I know of beasts that can fight with weapons, but I’ve never heard of a skill that can do the same.”

The gladiator circled Foebinder, inspecting it from all sides while the chain regarded him in turn.

“It has incredible reach and its movements aren’t limited by joints like a living body. These links remind me of a prehensile tail, but they’re even more flexible. If Foebinder can truly master weapon combat, you will have a terrifying ally at your side. Fuck it.” He came to a decision. “This is a once-in-a lifetime opportunity. If we pull this off, I’ll be the first person in history to teach a construct how to brawl.”

What followed was a fascinating exchange. As Edge and Rue watched on, Beelo systematically evaluated Foebinder’s capabilities, asking it to perform a range of tasks. They had a natural rapport, and he only had to help translate a few times. The gladiator was eager to test the chain’s limits, including whether Foebinder could improve through training like a regular student.

Edge had taught the skill a few basic techniques, but he was still a novice himself. Not only could Beelo adapt his methods to the construct’s unique specifications, he could show Foebinder how to pull off moves the human body couldn’t even attempt.

He had never seen his mentor so excited. The gladiator didn’t just want to teach the chain to wield a polearm, he intended to develop a unified combat style that would let Edge and Foebinder fight while wielding glaives at the same time.

“With two polearms, you can pull off tricks that would normally require specialized skills. For example, attacking from two directions at once, or having one of you parry a blade while the other goes in for the kill.”

Manifest Chain was equally thrilled. The avatar loved Beelo’s idea and intended to practice diligently inside his inner world. It looks like I need to buy another glaive. Something strong and light rather than expensive, since my gut is telling me Greed can’t buff two weapons at the same time.

They shook hands at the end of their session, and Edge promised to visit again soon.

Obtaining a weapon that could handle high-stage opponents was tricky, even without any frills. He had plenty of time, so decided to confer with the only person he trusted to figure out the details. Beelo had provided some useful insights, but as much as the man loved polearms, he wasn’t an expert in materials and Helena was—let alone the myriad intricacies of resource refinement and weapon modification.

As luck would have it, the craftswoman was in her workshop. She was willing to listen with one ear while she infused a thick slab of truesteel with an alchemical agent whose purpose was above his paygrade.

She found the project interesting in principle but too simple to tackle herself. Fortunately, Helena decided it was a worthy problem for her apprentices to solve. She called Pip and Coal into the workshop so they could negotiate their own commission.

She didn’t say anything while Edge explained what he wanted and haggled for a price. Pip was aggressive and Coal was charming, and they made a solid team. When they came to an agreement and he transferred the down payment, she said, “Don’t worry, I’ll make sure it turns out well.

“We haven’t discussed them before, but these two are a solid fit for the job. They planned out their skillsets with the intention of working together from the start, allowing them to specialize in ways that wouldn’t be practical for a lone smith. They might even surpass me one day, although it won’t be anytime soon.”

Next, the apprentices loaded the schematics they had devised into the machine that rendered an illusion of the finished product.

The glaive they designed was lighter than Opulence but almost as durable. It featured simple modifications to keep its blade sharp and repair damage it picked up in the field. It had high thermal conductivity to pair with Elemental Blade, along with some surprises for anything that tried to pull the polearm free from the chain’s grasp.

While Edge would be able to wield it in a pinch, the balance and attachment points were designed from the ground up for Foebinder’s unique properties, and he was excited to see the finished product. It set him back an even 100,000 creds, which was a fair deal for a weapon that could go head-to-head with stage-four beasts without being annihilated for its trouble.

He still had four hours before his match, which gave him plenty of time to squeeze in some training for his Companion. They made their way to the Hunters’ Association to see what missions were available. Before he entered the beast hunters’ society, he decided to check out the gift King had mentioned.

Edge didn’t trust the man, but there wasn’t any reason to suspect the gesture was more than a straightforward bribe. The Coalition already knew where Puppet Town was, and it would be obvious if there was anything suspicious included with the supplies. Even still, he wanted to inspect the goods before shipping them home, and he was curious to check out the bait King had set to tempt him into agreeing to an alliance.

As promised, there was a storage locker keyed to his Guide in the Association’s vault. When the door slid open, an appreciative whistle escaped his lips. He ran his gaze across stacks of metal, wood, and stone, along with some technical manuals and components for magitech machines. Everything was well-organized and labeled, and when he switched on Dragon’s Greed, he had to raise a hand to shield his eyes from the glare.

Given the volume of the resources and how brightly they were shining, he was looking at several million creds worth of raw materials—a considerable fortune by any accounting. The treasure-sensing skills couldn’t appraise the manuals or mechanical parts, but he had a hunch they were just as valuable.

He still didn’t trust King, but Edge had to admit his offer was tempting. These goods will make a big difference, and he implied this was only a fraction of what the Coalition could provide.

Shortly after, Edge found himself in the beast hunters’ tower, staring at the job board with Rue at his side.

He spent time every day working with his Companion inside his inner world, along with training sessions in World’s End whenever he was in town. His friends pitched in too, teaching the fox the ins and outs of hunting with a crew whenever they were somewhere safe enough for the beast to come out of his den.

However, the fox needed more paws-on practice in tracking, ambushing, and battling wild beasts, which Edge hoped to accomplish by taking jobs within the city limits until Rue was strong enough to join the team during missions in the Lakelands.

He also needs some experience battling other trained beasts, not to mention monsters and human opponents. Maybe I’ll register him with the arena when he evolves to stage two. It’s a good compromise between safety and real-world combat conditions. While he mulled the matter over, he scanned the listings, grinning when he spotted a job that was a perfect test of the fox’s ability.

A mixed infestation of volt rats and gas rats was plaguing one of the processing centers on the northern docks. Rue had hunted volt rats before, but he’d never taken on their gas-wielding cousins. The colony included some stage-two rodents. It might be too tough for the fox to tackle on his own, but with Edge watching from the shadows and ready to step in at a moment’s notice, he was confident that he could keep his Companion from suffering a serious wound.

Just to be certain, he walked up the stairs and entered a store that sold gear specifically designed for beasts. He purchased a magitech device that would create a protective bubble if it detected lethal damage—a weaker version of his Organ-Guard that didn’t require surgery. The external device was less efficient and expensive to refuel, but it was insurance against an unexpected strike and ideal for tacking opponents above the beast’s stage in a controlled environment.

Edge purchased the device and attached it to Rue’s collar, securing it next to the pin he had commissioned for every member of Trapper’s crew before leaving for World’s End. The two of them spent a few minutes browsing the wares on display, checking out some armor he might purchase when the beast he evolved to stage two and reached his adult size.

They returned to the lobby and Edge accepted the job, letting Rue take the lead when they left the tower. Eagerness to prove his hunting prowess was flowing through their bond, along with some speculations about which species of rodent tasted best.

“That’s the spirit.” Edge laughed at his antics. “Pull this off, and you’ll find out soon enough.”
