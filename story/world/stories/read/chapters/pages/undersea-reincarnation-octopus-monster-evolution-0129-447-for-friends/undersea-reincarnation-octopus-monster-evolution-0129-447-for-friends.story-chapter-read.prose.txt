Henry was still for a moment, with only his eyes flicking left to right for any trace of movement while all of his senses and all of his attention were tuned to their utmost, waiting for an attack or a skill, or anything other than what Sense Foe had sent him, which he was still interpreting, but in the meantime, his eyes got nothing. The darkness around him was still stifling, and it was as if—somehow—it had grown thicker. If a moment ago it had simply been due to the lack of light, now it was as if he was swimming in pure black ink. The kind that wouldn’t even refract light off of its surface when a light shone upon it. Just complete and utter darkness.

Henry pulled Maurice closer, and he could sense the crab’s many legs trembling. Sense Foe was still unable to point him in any specific direction. Like a broken compass, the skill couldn’t figure out the source, only that what it had sensed was around. As for Henry’s other senses, none were giving him much to go on. The darkness was muting everything out. Only his aura was trickling in some details and now that he was looking for it and paying attention to it, he could only confirm what Zerathstra had told him the day prior.

Unfettered Echoes’ aura was well-tuned to sensing out his surroundings. And while the image was coming to him slower than what he would like, he still wasn’t liking the picture too much.

The kraken—because he was almost sure this was a single one—was still… unfocused. Like someone rolling over in their sleep, it might sink back into slumber, or it might wake up. Which was why Henry wasn’t casting anything. He just kept his Avatars in reach and waited. While the range of his clones was severely reduced by the darkness, it wasn’t enough to cut him off, so their escape was still possible.

Escape’s not an option, though. Not yet, he thought to himself. They had come down here for the orb. They had to take it. They just needed to move slowly, carefully—

The kraken woke up.

Henry was still frozen as a statue, but there was no denying the shift in the waters around him. His senses were all still cut off, but he felt the attention suddenly surround him, right as Sense Foe’s blaring grew stronger. Though it still refused to give him a direction from which he should expect the creature. And then—

The absolute darkness surrounding them was ripped away, and Henry’s brains stuttered.

Darkness, in physics, was due to the absence of light, just like cold was due to the absence of heat. To chase away darkness, one would have to create a source of light and that’d get the job done.

Instead, here and in front of his eyes, no such light source had been deployed—at least, none that he could discern—and the dark itself was ripped away, like black pigment sucked out of the ink that used to fill their surroundings, leaving a world washed in shades of gray. There were no shadows below or around any tower. No darkness within the homes and buildings. None. All darkness had been banished.

The sunken city of Nymara was right ahead, with its structures and its broken towers, and Henry would have taken the sights in, but he didn’t. Instead, he turned his attention a bit to the right, where some sort of still-intact archway that led into the city stood.

Because that was where Sense Foe was telling him the kraken was.

Henry waited, and he used the opportunity to glance around through his Avatars. The banished darkness hadn’t extended all the way up, from what he could see. It was only the last couple hundred feet or so.

Then a movement pulled Henry’s attention back to the scene in front of him. From around a pillar, a lithe, thin figure swam out. With pale, silver and white scales, the Merfolk that calmly approached was unlike the ones he’d seen before. She wore no mask, so there was no missing the inky black eyes, which matched the mane of black, shadow-like hair that trailed after her, and before Henry could examine her further, two familiar shapes swam after her. Inky black krakens, human-sized. Clones, Henry would bet. So he fired a quick series of Identifies toward all three.

As for the latter, he was pretty damn sure they were ink clones—or an advanced variant of them, considering what Telepathic Sense was telling him. The two were simply echoing the idle curiosity—for a lack of a better term—of the central figure.

“Young. But surprisingly mighty.”

The kraken’s voice was… pleasant. Surprisingly warm and soothing considering the cold water that surrounded them. And it—or she—spoke in the same tongue that the Merfolk he’d encountered spoke with, though it sounded a little more melodious. A little slower.

“Yet I’m not in the mood to entertain at this time,” she continued, tilting her head while her hair continued to dance behind her, coiling as if it had a mind of its own. “What brings you to my domain, young ones?”

While the polite greeting came as a surprise, Henry quickly pulled himself together to return it. He still sometimes expected hostile encounters with krakens, considering the first one he’d ever experienced. Then again, Rocky had shown him that it didn’t have to be that way, and this individual—so far—seemed to be heading in that direction as well.

He should just keep things cordial, say what he came here for and why—because he likely needed a good reason to have intruded on this powerful being’s domain. Then they’d be on their way.

“Your hair’s pretty!” Maurice piped up from his arm, which made Henry’s breath catch. The black and white face slowly turned toward the crab and unconsciously, the arm that held Maurice twitched, ready to pull him away in case the stranger took offense. Though whether Maurice sensed that or not, Henry couldn’t tell. He just saw the crab wave from the corner of his eye and continue as if nothing happened. “Hi! I’m Maurice. This is Henry. What’s your name?”

The Merfolk’s lips had been curving up in mirth, but at the mention of a name, the smile stopped. She stood there, frozen for a beat before her brows furrowed.

“Shadows have no name,” she said, tone distracted. “In my long years of contemplation, I seem to have misplaced mine.” At that, the shadows at the edge of the bubble seemed to quiver in anticipation, while something pulsed out of the kraken. To Henry, it almost felt like the beginning of an upgrade, but somehow different. It felt… both overwhelming, and barely perceptible. It was as if the world itself had trembled around them, which he could only barely pick up upon through Unfettered Echoes. But the kraken didn’t let it go through, and she just waved a dismissive hand, as if used to the phenomenon, and continued her reflection as if nothing had happened. “My name. Her name. This place…”

Then, the first drop of hostility spread out of her, and Henry’s mana moved, but just as suddenly as it had come, it faded.

“Nymara,” the kraken said, and now, there was something else behind the gaze. Anger. Cold and relentless. But old. The kraken’s hostility wasn’t targeted at them, but at this city itself. “My name… is Nerry. No. Naerabel, for strangers. Which you are.”

The two clones swam closer to her, then seamlessly merged back into her body as she floated in place. She hadn’t drawn any weapon, nor had she aimed her aura at them. But still, Henry suddenly felt like there was something sharp hovering above his head.
