Ashton grabbed the looking glass and felt it in his hand. The magic within this item was truly incredible. Deeply complex enchantments that worked with each other in tandem like they were made to sit in one object in the first place.

Whoever made this, this avid reader, was definitely extremely skilled. The complexity and grade of the enchantments generally determined how long it would take people to 'attune' to a magic object; basically, how long it took for them to be able to use the effect imbued into an item.

That meant that it would take a while to get used to this. For most, at least. Usually, people simply learned how to use magic item effects instinctively, but that still required them to use the item for a long time and try out numerous reactions with their own mana.

As for Ashton, the moment he held the object in his hand and began to inspect the patterns within the item more closely, he was able to understand the general principle well, and could use quite a few shortcuts.

Though most of the enchantments were new to him, there were a few that he did know already. It helped him figure out the use of the other enchantments more easily, and quickly let him attune to the item.

"Millie, there's a book on the table in the tent. Could you bring it to me real quick?" Ashton asked. Millie quickly nodded and excitedly leaned back to grab the book. It was one of the items that Ashton wanted to barter with; a simple history book. Something that was probably worth quite a lot.

Right now, although the constellations had access to this world, they weren't exactly the sort that liked to 'share'. So, everyone else was still in the dark, and Ashton was now one of the few access points through which information could be shared.

Due to that rarity, information in particularly actually had a pretty decent value right now.

But for now, this was the best thing to test this looking glass out on. Well, the only thing he really had on hand.

He flipped the book open to a random page. In his 'eyes' right now, this was just a blank page. There wasn't any mana in the ink, so of course. But then, Ashton placed the looking glass onto the paper and let his mana flow inside to activate the magic item's effect.

Immediately, within the glass disc, Ashton could see letters appear as concentrated mana.

"Hm, interesting," Ashton whispered. "It basically just creates a mana duplicate of whatever is directly below it based on the colour. It guess I should be able to even use it on phone screens..."

Of course, Ashton still wasn't actually able to see colour. Rather than representing the actual shade, it seemed like it was more showing reverse saturation. The darker something was, the more concentrated and as such brighter the mana was. It was essentially black and white, though mana technically didn't even have colour in that sense anyway, on top of being negative.

Whatever it was; Ashton was able to read it.

"But it just makes the writing a little bigger. How can you suddenly read it?" Millie asked, narrowing her eyes. "Are you the type of blind that can technically still see, but just really badly? There's a girl at school that was like that. Her glasses were thicker than my thumb. She was really fun, but she moved away a while ago."

Ashton laughed. "No, I'm fully blind. I don't see things physically anymore, remember? I see mana through the wisp. Mana isn't normally visible, so you can't see the actual effect."

"Hm... If you say so." Millie shrugged, turning back to the books that the Salesman had on display.

"You have genuinely no idea how amazing of an item this is," he said to the Salesman with a broad smile on his face. "Thank you. I appreciate you picking this up for me."

The spirit nodded, clearly more than satisfied with this trade. But there was something that both of them were waiting for.

"Will you bring that out now?"

With another brief nod, the Salesman stood up. Their cloak slowly opened up as they pulled out a large wooden box out of it. It was taller than the spirit themself, but fairly thin and narrow in comparison.

The moment Ashton saw the box, he knew the worth of the item.

163 thousand coins.

A broad smile appeared on Ashton's face. Even if this was a gift from his old friends, the Salesman spirit couldn't just give it to him for free. To make this possible, the Salesman had to 'buy' the item from them, though they were allowed to do so on credit, and was now selling the item back to Ashton.

"Shit. Alright, I guess I'll have to really save up for that thing. Could you open it? I want to see what's actually in there."

The Salesman quickly nodded, and carefully placed the box down onto the ground, using all four of their arms to stabilise it. This was probably the most precious item that this spirit has ever handled, and they wanted to make sure that this was treated with the respect that it deserved. Especially considering that it was an item given by multiple extremely powerful spirits.

The box was slowly pulled open, and Ashton was holding his breath as it happened.

"It's just a stick?" Millie asked, confused, but her question was drowned out by the hysterical laugh that just came from Ashton. Some tears soaked into his blindfold.

"Those fucking bastards. They definitely haven't just made this in a couple of days." Ashton looked down at the item. He thought that they would have just chosen a random staff that suited Ashton's current power well enough.

But no, that wasn't it at all. This was Ashton's staff. Or well, a replica. It was weaker than the actual piece, but it was much stronger than when Ashton first made this. It was something that he had enhanced and repaired numerous times with the help of spirits, fairies, elementals, and any other summon that wanted to help out, as long as he was able to pay for the price of their abilities somehow.

By the time that the system came around, it was an item that could barely be graded anymore. It was an artefact like none other. Truly an extension of Ashton's body. The system ended up actually restricting and weakening the item, which Ashton found quite ridiculous, but it was still useful, and the restrictions were slowly being lifted as the quests went on. Ashton was fighting, hoping that he would be able to get his trust partner back someday.

At least, until the Summoner used it to make a deal with a constellation that was interested in it. Ashton had no idea where the hell it was now, but it was taken from him.

Ashton looked at the item with a smile. He ran his fingers over the staff, feeling the engravings. "I made this right around one of my birthdays. Not that I celebrated those often. I had just brought my internal core to the lower third stage. One of the beasts brought me a tree that fell due to lightning, and I carved out its core. The Atsali—a metal elemental—extracted some high-quality ore from the side of a mountain we had set up camp next to, and I basically just melted it down and stuck each end of the stick into it. Over the years, we enhanced it more and more, and fixed up all the mistakes I made. Enchantments, new materials, improvements... and turned it into this beauty. To be honest, after just a few years, there was probably nothing left of the original, but it still carried on that spirit."

Ashton could feel numerous enchantments placed into the staff. This item wasn't quite at the level that it had when it was taken from Ashton. Far from it, actually. It was really mostly a replica in appearance. The base materials seemed to be the same, but this item had never even been enhanced. Plus, the enchantments were well-made, certainly, but... they weren't quite at the level that Ashton left it at, and there were a few fully missing.

Of course, nobody beside Ashton had ever been able to use this item, so the spirits would have had no way to truly analyse all the enchantments.

"Though... the overall effects are still there. And I guess they did take out the leftover remnants from earlier enchantments that I had to work around, so if I can just adjust things a little later, it should be fine," Ashton pointed out. It was definitely worth the money that it cost. Far more than that, actually.

He was a little sad that it wasn't the actual original, but maybe this had the opportunity to be even better. It was a memory that his dear friends had preserved to this extent over thousands of years, and had now passed on to Ashton.

"Hey... there's something scribbled there," Millie said, pointing at the inside of the wooden box's lid.

Ashton turned to her, confused.

"Really?" he asked, looking up at the Salesman. They didn't seem to know about this either until now. Ashton grabbed the looking glass and held it against the lid. There really was something here. It was a little hard to read at first, especially since the box's wood grain was also pretty dark, but after focusing a little bit, Ashton could make it out.

It wasn't just scribbles, it was writing. Not in English, of course. It was written in the spirits' script. They were small circles with multiple rings. Each one had different lengths of lines made up of small shapes. But beyond that, each circle had a small imprint of magic placed into it. Like this, each circle could convey a whole story, with the writer's emotions, thoughts, and intention placed into it.

Ashton felt the subtle magic as he inspected each of the circles more and more. There were so many of them, though the more that he looked at them, there was barely any need for that.

Even though they were all written by different spirits, they all said basically the same thing.

'We are happy you are okay. We love you.'

Each circle was also marked with a clan insignia, so that Ashton would know who it came from. Not that he needed to. The moment he read each message, he knew exactly who wrote this.

"They prepared all this in just a few days..?" Ashton whispered. There was a mark here from the Messenger spirit... they must have been the one to bring this around to all the others.

Somehow, Ashton couldn't wait to go to sleep again and see if he might get called by another one of his friends.
