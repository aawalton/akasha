Alley cupped his hands and blew warm air into them. While the air had been crisp and fresh the entire time Alley had been in the Frostlands, the continent was really starting to live up to its name now that autumn had become winter. He’d had that thought several times before, but as each day passed and the hours of sunlight grew shorter and the temperature continued to drop, Alley had to keep adjusting his standards. Despite being thin and kind of on the ratty side, his Cloak of The Bloodless One did a good job of keeping him warm even as the weather worsened, which was exactly what Alley expected from a magical necromantic artefact Dealer, but the dark material didn't cover his hands or head, and those were enduring enough cold that they were starting to hurt. Even with the increased durability that came with a rank up, Alley resolved that he was going to buy himself some thick gloves after this was over.

The ‘this’ in question was an assembling of all the remaining entrants in the Frostwing Trials. They had been required to gather in the largest and most central of the four arenas in the city as a sort of ceremony to open the second half of the tournament.

This far in the number of competitors that had started in the thousands was down to two hundred and fifty True Deck Welders and five hundred competitors with Trials Decks. The full six hundred and fifty entrants from both divisions were now gathered on the floor of the arena, where they would be shown their bracket position,informed of the location of their next match, and have their decks checked. The whole process took all day, from mid-morning until well after the early sunset.

Unlike when Alley’s group had first lined up at the little offices in the center of the city with hundreds of other hopeful tournament entrants, this wasn’t purely administrative, but a pageant of epic proportions for the viewing pleasure of the tens of thousands of spectators.

As much as Alley didn’t think of himself as the sort who wanted to be at the center of attention, he could appreciate why the tournament organisers did things like this. Not only were many of the tournament entrants more than happy to use their Decks to put on a show, but the swarms of people that made the whole event worth the city-state’s time also seemed to adore the opportunity to get another look at the entrants.

Darius claimed a lot of the appeal was that the spectators wanted to take this opportunity to gamble before the second half of the tournament began. Alley believed him that there must be a significant amount of gamblers in the crowd, but he was certain there was no way it accounted for even half of the people crammed into the temporary arena stands; There were just too many people.

Speaking of Darius, the red-headed hunter was having an obvious ball of a time. Despite his painfully cold hands and face, Alley couldn’t help but smile as he watched his friend wave to the crowd, perform tricks with his spear, and blowing the occasional kiss towards gaggles of women who were clearly too far away to even notice.

At the far end of the arena grounds was a wooden stage that hadn’t been there when Alley had last set foot in the central stadium. The same council of old Frostlanders, featuring one young mayor who had presided over the legal trial after Alley and Darius had permanently killed the draco-lich Faferion, were atop the stage. The group of them were drawing names written on little pieces of paper from sacks half the height of a person, and then reading them out to a group of non-uniformly dressed tournament staff, who then proceeded to write them on one of six enormous boards.

Once the name was written down, a man with a voice amplification device shouted it out so it echoed across the arena, where it was picked up and repeated by a  man using another copy of the same device at the far side of the arena floor. When he saw these sorts of Resonance devices, Alley couldn’t help but stare a little. He might be better educated than most when it came to winning Challenges, Alley was still from a backwater, far from the centers of culture and innovation.

He had to admit, though, they weren’t exactly the most innovative of devices, essentially being a cone that you spoke into, studded with Cacophony and Pressure Crystals. The combination of the two forms of Resonance doubtless created the ability to be heard clearly at such great distances. Though Alley had to assume Cacophony, which was known as the Resonance of sound, was probably doing most of the work.

The entire process of name drawing, being added to a bracket, and then being announced took even longer than Alley expected, but he was actually grateful for that, as the cause was that each name was written on the bracket board a second time in Southern. Once the whole process was complete, paper copies of the boards would be distributed. Alley wasn’t quite sure how that process was being achieved so quickly, but he imagined there must be some sort of paper-writing-on device like the one that had made a list of every Card in his Deck when he’d first entered the tournament.

As the day rolled into afternoon and then evening, only Darius had been put into a bracket, and to both his and Alley’s surprise, got a sizeable cheer from the crowd when his name was called. Alley knew a written account of the first round the Trials players had fought in the forest north of the city had been available for purchase, and Darius had obviously won a series of matches in front of the crowds in the city.

Both of them still shared a look of utter shock, and Kyera all but giggled when the spectators clapped and cheered for the hunter.

The scene of surprise in the group was repeated a few hours later when Alley and Kyera’s names were finally called, though the apprentice Frame rolled her eyes when more than a few whistles and whoops were uttered by sections of the crowd.

“They couldn’t like me for my play.” She grumbled with a shake of her head. “ It has to be because I have tits.”

Both of the young men repressed a laugh. After a few moments getting his face under control, Alley chimed in.

“I like your play. It has to be good to win with that Library of yours.”

The brown-haired woman turned an unimpressed gaze onto Alley while Darius once again fought back laughter.

“I’m taking this from Mr. Ninety Cards? At least I can plan a strategy and not just hope to draw good things at the right time.”

At that, Darius gave up trying not to laugh.

“You both suck.” He said in between what Alley could only describe as cackles. He wasn’t sure if men could crackle, but if they couldn’t, this was absolutely whatever the male equivalent was.

“We both got bigger cheers from the crowd than you.” Alley shot back.

“Pah, that girl Trine, and that old bald guy with the big beard got way bigger cheers than both of you combined.”

The good-natured debate went back and forth for a little while, but now that all three of the living members of the group had gotten their placements within the next round of the tournament, they began slowly making their way to exit the arena. A fair number of the competitors had done the same, though just as many had opted to use this opportunity to continue playing to the crowds.

While technically it was probably the best strategy to get up close to the bracket boards and confirm their full paths to the final rounds of the event, the city would soon be inundated with the paper copies of the brackets anyway, and since they only cared about popularity as a way to jokingly one up each other they quickly agreed that they had been stuck here long enough.

Alley did have to admit he found it interesting to see the groups and individuals that formed or didn’t form out of the hundreds of competitors scattered around the arena floor, but he had been here all day, and most of the novelty of seeing people from all over the world gathered together in a single place had long since worn off.

“Korshei Iverdeen!” Came the call from the pair of voice enhancers when Alley’s group was almost all the way to the west exit of the Arena floor. This was followed by an absolutely deafening roar from the crowd that utterly outdid any of the cheers that any of the competitors had gotten. Once it had died down enough that conversation could resume, Alley looked thoughtful for a second.

“I feel like I know that name from somewhere.”

Darius, who was leading the way out of the arena, nodded over his shoulder.

“Yeah. I’m pretty sure that's the captain of the city guard, the one that was our guarantor to make sure we didn’t run away before that trial.”

“Ohhh right, right,” Alley replied. “ I do remember his name being Korshei, but I didn’t realize he was so beloved.”

“I saw him fight, and the crowd loved him then, too. I think he might be a bit of a local hero around here.” Darius said with a nod as he walked. “ His Deck seemed pretty nasty too, Authority Resonance that let him make people stand still, or do what he wanted.”

Both Alley and Kyera were surprised to hear that, as neither of them had ever encountered or even heard of a Trials Deck using such an intangible Resonance type. As interesting as that idea was,they weren’t going to learn more about it by standing around here, and all four members of the group had already agreed they wanted to spend the evening carefully experimenting with the enchanted items Darius had stolen from Castle Lethe. Denderra, in particular, was excited by that idea, as the possibility of tasting more food via The Messenger’s Cut was never far from her thoughts.
