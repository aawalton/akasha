The next few hours passed in a mix of effort, wandering, and the faint smell of Elynara’s soup – which she still wasn’t convinced counted as soup at all. The spices she had left were mismatched, and only brought up to par thanks to some herbs she gathered on her journey.

The rations she had crumbled in dissolved into an unappealing mush, but she was hungry, so ate it anyways. Elynara was starting to miss orphanage cooking, and even the meals she had while traveling started to sound so very appetising now.

She never expected to stay in the ruins so long either, so by now only the longest lasting rations were left. All the food gifts from villages she got on the way had run out at last.

But then again, Elynara never expected to find the spirit and machine duo needing her help either. Life was just full of surprises when one least expected them.

…And maybe she could go out and hunt some monsters out there soon.

Aru, bless her heart, ate it anyway. Or rather, the automaton girl seemed fond of eating itself, the ‘new’ activity bringing her joy even if the food didn’t.

“It wasn’t so bad,” she said, voice gentle.

Elynara just stared at her. Even her polished plate wouldn’t convince her.

“Liar.”

Aru tilted her head innocently.

“I did not lie. I merely lack a proper comparison.”

“That’s even worse!”

Aru hummed thoughtfully.

“I see.”

Elynara groaned and pushed her face into her hands. Starpuff trilled sympathetically, then took a spark of mana from the mana blob Elynara gave it to eat, and threw it into the soup bowl – which immediately hissed and puffed up into a tiny green cloud.

“…Traitor,” Elynara whispered at it.

Starpuff just spun cheerfully in response, gobbling up the rest of its ‘meal’. Aru’s eyes shone brighter at the exchange.

At least somebody was having fun! Even if it was at Elynara’s own expense.

And with the terrible meal concluded, they returned to searching the place up. After all, they had the bunker to explore, and needed the tools and gear to do so.

That didn’t stop Elynara from admiring the views, however. And who knew, maybe there was still something hidden among them?

The outer ruins had softened under the weight of centuries, their harsh angles turned round by wind and dust. The metal beams looked less like infrastructure now and more like the roots of some massive dead creature, half-buried, half-reaching. Faded glass clung to the frames of windows in cracked sheets, glittering faintly under Elynara’s staff light.

The two of them moved through the half-buried chambers, and picked through debris piles. Shifted aside bone-dry materials, metal sheets, bent rails, and odd scraps whose purpose neither could guess.

Aru walked ahead, scanning each piece with small, thoughtful hums; Elynara trailed behind carrying whatever Aru declared ‘likely useful’. Despite her earlier drive to explore the bunker fast, the automaton girl seemed just as happy to work on doing so safely now.

Which was an improvement already, and eased Elynara’s worries slightly.

They continued, finding strips of flexible plating, as well as pieces of that weird metal-like thing Elynara found earlier on – buckled but intact enough to be reshaped. Some lengths of rubbery material that hadn’t fully degraded. Thread-like metal coils. Faded cloth bundles that disintegrated at the lightest touch. And a surprising number of data crystals, hidden in compartments Elynara would have completely missed, if not for Aru knowing where they were, and how to open them.

Who knew the ancient civilization would be so fond of hiding things smoothly in the walls of all places? With the markings long since faded away, and whatever power supplying them long since not enough to so much as light them up, you simply had to know they were there and leverage them open to get at the insides.

“Filter material, reinforcement,” Aru murmured, crouching beside a broken vent. “We might fashion a partial seal with this.”

“If it doesn’t crumble on us,” Elynara warned, brushing dust off the piece Aru had chosen.

“Yes.” Aru paused. “…We shall test it.”

Starpuff was delighted by the exploration. It kept zipping between them, landing on every object Elynara reached for, chirping loudly whenever she picked up something especially questionable.

Elynara held up a bowl-shaped thing full of holes.

“No idea what this is.”

Starpuff landed inside it, fit itself perfectly into the concave shape, and blinked up at her like it had achieved something miraculous.

Aru examined the object.

“That is a shower head.”

Elynara blinked.

“…Starpuff, get out of there.”

Starpuff puffed up proudly.

Elynara sighed.

“No, you cannot keep it.”

Their scavenging continued with light bickering like that, until they had enough pieces for Aru to begin work, and even found some resistive cloth-like material that endured the passage of centuries. Some of it still crumbled away, but the pieces underneath survived, protected by their brethren.

And now they would find use once more after centuries of neglect.

Aru sat on a flat metal slab near the bunker entrance, the white dust still safely behind the sealed door. She laid everything out in neat rows, her movements precise, gentle in ways that always surprised Elynara given the strength the girl had.

“This will not be perfect,” Aru murmured, fitting two plates together with tiny adjustments of her fingers. “But it will put something between me and the white sand. That should be enough to let me stay in there for far longer. It helps that, for whatever reason, the sand does not carry far. Whatever stirred up into the air does not stay in the air for long.”

“...Probably its disruptive nature slowly cutting through the air currents,” Elynara theorized, Aru nodding at that. The theory was as good as any, at this point.

Elynara watched her work for a moment longer, then sat down cross-legged beside her.

“…You know,” she said quietly, “there’s still so much I don’t understand about this place.”

Aru paused, tilting her head. “What do you mean?”

“Well… the ruins, the machines, the weird script. Everything. But at the same time it’s all strangely familiar.” Elynara poked a small shard of crystal with her staff. “The records I found and whatever Alicia told me talk about Magitech civilizations. And lost ages.”

She thought for a moment longer, wondering how to put her thoughts into words.

“...The script thing is the weirdest. Celestia told me things were bad, but she also gave me knowledge. I have not had trouble reading anything up until now. And yet I can’t read it, regardless.”

She hesitated.

“And seeing you… meeting you… makes the terrible state of the world feel even more real. But not in the bad way! I mean, I’m happy to have found you, and even happier to have you as my friend! I mean that… Um.”

Aru listened carefully, pausing in her work.

“...That I represent what was lost.”

“Ah, yes, that! Um, sorry.”

“Think nothing of it, for it is true indeed.”

Aru considered her next words carefully. While thinking the automaton girl set down the pieces she worked on carefully aside, her hands organizing the pieces idly, before answering:

“You speak of Celestia and this being a Magitech civilization,” Aru said slowly. “But my creators called themselves Elthusians. And worshipped not the Creation Goddess. Or at least not more than the other gods.” She adjusted a strap carefully, eyes lowered. “Save for one.”

“And that was…?” Elynara asked.

Aru lifted her gaze upward – toward the sky that wasn’t visible from here at all – as a complicated expression crossed her face.

“…Dismoria. The Goddess of Souls and Advancement. One of the three Moon Goddesses.” She breathed softly, her voice sounding distant. “A name given to the moon, and the Goddess bearing its name.”

A beat passed.

“Though, I guess…” Aru continued, looking away at the ceiling long collapsed above them. Her voice dropped to a whisper. “…Now, one could best call her: Dismoria, of the Broken Moon.”

The words settled between them like dust.

Elynara blinked slowly, the name hanging in her mind like a bell still ringing.

Dismoria.

The Broken Moon.

She stared at Aru, trying to reconcile the weight of it with everything she thought she knew. Which – realistically – wasn’t much at all.

“I… knew there were other gods,” she murmured. “Somewhere. Probably. But magic took so much of my attention, I never really thought about them.” She scratched at her cheek sheepishly. “And I kept forgetting to ask anybody who might know more. There was… always too much to do.”

Aru hummed softly, not unkindly.

“…The other two moon goddesses,” Elynara added after a moment, leaning a little closer. “Do you know their names?”

Aru nodded.

“Lunaris and Aurelia.”

Elynara tried repeating them under her breath.

“Lunaris… Aurelia…”

The names felt gentle, in the same way moonlight felt gentle. Subtle. Soft.

Good names.

Aru adjusted another piece of scrap plating between her hands as she spoke.

“Lunaris, the Moon Goddess of Dreams and Yearnings. Keeper of dreams and night. She influenced sleep and intuition, granting visions and inspiration.”

Elynara listened quietly, eyes wide.

“Aurelia,” Aru continued, “the Moon Goddess of Knowledge and Wisdom. Keeper of secrets and lore, granting clarity and understanding to those who sought knowledge.”

Elynara let out a long breath she didn’t realize she had been holding.

“…And all this time I thought Celestia handled everything alone.”

Aru shook her head gently.

“No, or at least it wasn’t so simple. Celestia was revered… but not singular. Not for us.”

Elynara absorbed that in silence. There was something almost comforting about it – knowing the world wasn’t quite as simple or strict as she had assumed.

She glanced at the piled trinkets again, then at the strange carved script she’d found earlier.

“There’s something else I’ve been meaning to ask. That writing everywhere – the script – I can’t make any sense of it. It doesn’t even look like letters.”

Aru paused in her work.

“Those are not letters.”

Elynara frowned.

“They’re not?”

“No.” Aru lifted one of the plates, brushing a finger over a line of intricate geometric shapes carved into it. “These are logic gates. Circuitry of mana and logistics. They work, but have no meaning other than to let mana flow in certain ways.”

“Oh.”

Elynara leaned in, squinting at the shapes as if they might suddenly reveal themselves.

“…That explains nothing. But also everything.”

Aru’s lips tugged upward, faintly amused.

“I do not know more,” she added. “Because I was a protector.”

“Right. Not your ‘necessary knowledge,’ huh?”

“…Yes.”

Elynara tapped the nearest crystal with her staff.

“Do you think there’s something about it in the crystals we gathered?”

Aru stared thoughtfully at the pile – at the soft glimmers inside the data shards, the promise hiding under their crystalline surface.

“…Maybe,” she said. “Hopefully. We will see. There is still quite a lot more to retrieve from inside the bunker.”

Elynara nodded and stood, brushing dust from her knees.

“All right then. One step at a time.”

Aru rose as well, her makeshift plating clinking softly.

“One step,” she echoed.

And together – girl, automaton, and a cheerfully floating Starpuff – they continued their search through the ruins, the quiet weight of gods and forgotten ages settling between them, gentle as moonlight.

Aru finished the last strap with a small click, then stepped back so Elynara could see.

It was… something.

The “protective suit” looked like the result of three different ruined closets, five random storage rooms, and a particularly confused workshop deciding to work together out of spite.

Plates of mismatched metal overlapped in odd angles. Rubber sheets had been cut and tied around her arms and legs like improvised armor wraps. A length of transparent visor-plastic formed a crude face shield, held in place by twisted wire.

Someone – Elynara – had stuffed soft cloth into every gap she could find, which gave an unfortunate puffed-out look around the joints.

And underneath it all, Aru’s faint internal glow pressed through in uneven blue arcs, flickering through the seams like she was holding a small lightning storm inside her body.

Elynara rubbed her forehead.

“…I’m sorry.”

Aru blinked slowly.

“For what?”

“It looks like it lost a fight. With itself.”

Aru tilted her head, examining herself down to her boots. The suit made a faint crinkling sound every time she moved.

“I believe,” she said carefully, “that it is functional.”

“That’s not the same as–”

“It is functional,” Aru repeated, gentler this time.

Elynara huffed, but she couldn’t argue.

It was functional.

Hideous, but functional.

And if it kept that cursed dust off Aru, then aesthetics could go jump off a cliff.

They had also managed a few tools between them. Elynara had cobbled together a “broom” – more a wide shovel tied to a metal handle – meant for pushing piles of sand aside rather than sweeping them. It made a satisfying scraping sound across the floor when she tested it.

A larger patchwork bag, reinforced with wire and scraps of plating, sat beside them for Aru to pack anything she found.

Starpuff tried sitting inside the bag once.

It fell over immediately. Starpuff chirped indignantly.

Now they stood before the entrance again – the dark frame of the bunker yawning like a dead throat, the thin layer of white dust glittering just beyond the threshold. Elynara’s staff-light reflected off Aru’s makeshift suit, casting strange, crooked shadows.

Elynara felt her mana hollowing slightly – she had topped both of them off as much as she could, even pushing her core to the edge of tingling discomfort just to cram in a bit more.

Aru shifted her weight, the suit crackling faintly.

“Well then,” she said, voice soft behind the visor. “I’m going. Hopefully this works.”

“It should,” Elynara said immediately. She stepped closer, tightening her grip on her staff as if the act itself might keep Aru safer. “But you come back immediately if anything feels wrong. You hear me?”

Aru’s expression softened behind the distorted suit.

“Yes,” she said. “I will be careful.”

Elynara reached out and squeezed her armored forearm.

“…Good.”

Aru squeezed back, the gesture stiff through the layers of scrap – but warm all the same.

They stood like that for one more breath.

Two.

Then Aru turned, lifted the visor slightly as if in a small farewell, and stepped once more into the pale dust.

The hours blurred together in a quiet, steady rhythm. Not in the dull, repetitive way her mind feared, but in the soft, purposeful way work sometimes settled around her – warm at the edges, alive with small sounds.

Aru disappeared into the doorway again, her mismatched suit creaking softly with every step. Elynara watched until the girl vanished behind the pale glow of dust, and only then let herself exhale.

The door swallowed the light whole.

She turned back to her spot beside the wall, brushing the floor clear with her hand before sitting cross-legged again. The cold stone stole warmth from her legs immediately, but the familiar posture helped. It always helped.

Elynara rested her palms on her knees, inhaled deeply, and let mana flow through her once more.

It felt different today. Same yet not quite.

The threads of mana drifted toward her far quicker than she expected, curling around her fingers, sliding into her core with a gentleness that would have been impossible just weeks ago. The loops of capacity training that once felt tedious and slow now fell into place almost effortlessly.

All the mana breathing and gathering she did over the past few weeks was bearing fruit, far more than she expected it would.

“Already?” she murmured, surprised as her capacity trickled up by a point after just a few loops of pushing.

Starpuff answered with a soft trill, drifting lazily in the air as if it, too, enjoyed the calmer flow.

She pushed deeper into the training, guiding mana to her core, the process ingrained into her through months of practice and stubborn nights of trial and error. All those hours added up, she realized. Even the ones that felt pointless at the time.

Her body remembered more than she thought.

It almost made her smile.

The faint sound of metal scraping stone interrupted her, and she opened her eyes just in time to see Aru emerging again. The suit sagged in odd places, dust clinging to its seams. Elynara hurried to meet her, using one of the cobbled together ‘tools’ to brush away the white flecks before they sank deeper.

Aru didn’t complain. She merely stood still, patient, with a quiet little tilt to her head indicating she was watching her work.

“You’re shaking again,” Elynara murmured, already reaching with her mana.

The warmth flowed into Aru in a steady stream. She shivered once, then stilled. The trembling under Elynara’s hands ebbed slowly, melting back into the girl’s core lines.

Some of the dust had slipped past the suit – into the cracks of her shoulder, under the plating around her ribs. Elynara brushed them out carefully, frowning at each patch.

“It still works,” Aru assured her. “ And it’s much better than before.”

“That doesn’t mean it works well enough.”

Aru blinked.

“…I will make further adjustments.”

They settled together near their growing collection of finds, Aru hunched over torn straps and crooked pieces of plating while Elynara sorted crystalline scraps into neat piles. Their shoulders occasionally touched. It felt… steadying.

The third time Aru emerged, she held something different – it was slimmer, more polished than the usual pieces. She carried it with careful fingers.

“This,” she said quietly, “should help me read the data crystals later.”

Elynara’s eyes widened.

“Really?”

“Yes. But… not now. Once we gather enough, it will be easier to understand.”

She tucked the item away beside the others, almost reverently before continuing.

The entrance itself changed with their work.

The bones that once stared outward in hollow silence were gone – moved gently and respectfully by Aru to the far edges of the room, hidden behind debris so they no longer watched. The white dust had been pushed aside too, small clearings carved out like tiny islands of safety. Even a patch of floor glimmered faintly under Elynara’s staff-light.

But still, the place made her skin prickle.

Every time she approached the doorway, her body reacted first. Her steps slowed. Her breath shortened and her mind blanked. It was just a flutter, barely long enough to unsettle her. Yet Her heart beat harder every single time, as if expecting something to reach out, and pull her into that dark abyss.

Whether it was the draining mana, the memory of those skulls staring her back, or something deeper she couldn’t name, she didn’t know.

She just knew she hated walking near it.

Elynara shook herself awake again, brushing her palms together until sparks flickered from the edges. Starpuff chirped in response, hovering protectively near her shoulder.

“Aru should be out soon,” she whispered to herself, steadying her grip on her staff. “Focus.”

And she did.

Whatever the bunker had become…

Whatever happened to those who sought shelter inside…

She had a friend to protect. Memories to recover.

And knowledge to return to the world.

Elynara curled her fingers around the staff’s warm shaft and breathed in once more.

She hoped – quietly, sincerely – that the ghosts of the dead would understand.

It took them nearly four days.

Four long, heavy days of Aru moving back and forth through the pale doorway, her suit growing more battered and mismatched each time. Four days of Elynara forcing more and more mana through her body until her core felt like a stretched muscle learning a new shape. Four days of quiet murmurs, scraping tools, the faint hiss of white dust, and the hum of mana breathing running beneath everything like a second heartbeat.

By the morning of the fourth day, Elynara’s capacity had climbed more than she dared hope, finally settling at 178, a solid improvement, and she felt it could still be pushed more.

If she hadn’t been so tired, she might have squealed. Well, not really, but it sure made her happy! Perhaps she will surprise-hug Aru sometime.

Her mechanical friend was surprisingly shy with physical contact, and she very much looked forward to her reaction.

Who knew who would be the one squealing then?

Aru’s pile of recovered items had grown too – but not in the chaotic scatter Elynara expected. The automaton girl arranged everything with gentle precision: mementos, metal tags, tiny identity cards with names long worn away, cracked but still readable data crystals, bent pieces of children’s jewelry, and countless small objects no one alive understood anymore.

Every item Aru placed down gave off the faintest echo. A whisper of life. A reminder of hands that once held them.

Elynara tried not to think about it too hard.

The white sand forced her to think about other things.

Even under the suit Aru built, its destructive nature showed itself again and again. The layered plates wore thin, the rubber cracked, the cloth insulation frayed at the edges. Each time Aru came out, Elynara found new void spots eating away at the corners of her friend’s glow – little holes in her mana body she had to fill by hand.

By the end of day four, Elynara was relieved beyond belief they were done.

Aru said there were more trinkets inside – but mostly too damaged, or too inconsequential. If any of them were mementos, they did not look the part.

Any more trips, and the suit would fall apart entirely and they would need to build a second from scratch.

Before anything else, they worked side by side to clean the items – every shard, every tag, every relic – until Elynara’s mana sight showed nothing but normal glow. Mostly. Some little voids still made it through, and she had to blast them with mana to go away – quite a lot of it too.

Those patches were incredibly mana hungry, she could not begin to imagine just what it would take to cleanse the entire bunker, much less the entire Deadzone.

Only when she was absolutely certain the items were clear did she let Aru sort them again.

The next problem was transport.

And that one, thankfully, Elynara could solve.

She dug through her satchel – Celestia’s precious, overly-generous gift – and began emptying it of anything nonessential. Everything she had been keeping ‘just in case’. Spare clothes, cooking scraps, dismantling tools, her old notes… Elynara set them all aside into rough bundles they could throw into bags or carry by hand.

The mementos, relics, and machinery samples, though – those went into the satchel one by one, resting on soft folds of magic that cushioned everything perfectly. Weighless and protected from any further damage. No strain on the items, nor on herself.

Aru still watched with curious interest each time something slipped inside and vanished.

Meanwhile, Elynara placed her own data crystals beside the pile Aru assembled, the only items she did not yet hide in her satchel.

They had plans for them, or at least Aru did.

Elynara really didn’t know how to make sense of the items, the magitech too alien for her to understand. At least without more study.

So she just looked at Aru as she finished organizing the wrecked data crystals and cards recovered earlier, the crystals they found in the ruins, and all the intact ones from the bunker.

There were… a lot of those. A lot more than Elynara expected given that people likely rushed over to the bunker without much time to prepare. But the bunker had been huge too, and housed the entire place…

She shook herself from those morbid thoughts, and looked at the final part of the organized items – and several strange devices Aru claimed could “bridge” information and let her access, read, and even save it.

Elynara didn’t understand any of it.

But Aru… Aru looked the most hopeful since she woke up in this form. The hope was infectious too, and now she was hoping her friend would find what she wanted to find among them.

Out of the nine gathered devices four out of the nine were turned into parts – too damaged to function alone or to be of any other use – but really, they only needed one working device to read all the data crystals. The backup was nice to have though, and access to examples of magitech was valuable enough on its own too, so there was nothing to complain about.

And the blank ones Elynara had found earlier, to her surprise, turned out to be the most valuable of all.

“They can store the most,” Aru had said, almost reverently. “If this device works… we can copy the knowledge from the damaged crystals into these.”

Elynara stared at the plain little crystals, remembering how she almost used one as a makeshift paperweight.

Her stomach growled loudly.

Of course it did.

“I would really like to eat something that isn’t dried ration slabs,” she muttered.

Starpuff chirped in agreement, floating upside-down in misery.

But food had to wait, and she had the feeling they wouldn’t be staying long in the ruins after this.

First they would need to finish their careful organization of the relics, then the copying.

And perhaps, just maybe, it would be time to get some answers.

Elynara swallowed hard. She feared learning what had happened here – feared the truth buried in dust and bone and silence.

But she also wanted to learn. Needed to.

For them. For herself.

For the world.

And for Aru.

Aru’s fingers trembled slightly as she set the last device down, and Elynara’s heart clenched.

She wasn’t the only one afraid.

Together, they sat before the piles of recovered memory, fragments of a lost people lying between them.

Waiting, hushed, nearly holding their breath.

And slowly, softly, Elynara reached over and took Aru’s hand.

“Ready?” she whispered.

Aru squeezed her fingers. Not tightly, but with determination.

“…Ready.”

It was time for the lost memories to live once more.
