Chapter II.VII (2.7)

With her shopping trip proving a failure in this loop, the ship set sail and they began their journey to Porcelain Island. The crew didn't seem to mind the task of ferrying them across the archipelago. In fact, they often made way and bowed their heads to Mitsuko whenever she passed them by, likely as a sign of respect for her saving their lives from the kraken. It also probably helped that Holly promised them all pay from the Hon government, so they weren’t working for free. Though they’d need to wait until after the barrier fell for their paydays. Mitsuko privately made a promise to truly pay them out ten times over if they ever actually escaped this archipelago.

The hardest part of leaving Mauve Island was actually dealing with the Sailor’s Guild. They did everything in their power to keep The Selcouth Sable stuck in the harbor. But the captain was relentless. After their last meeting with him and Holly, the guild relented permission to the ship. Mitsuko hadn’t been in the meeting and when she tried questioning Holly about it, the gnome just winked and grinned mischievously.

They passed The Terror as they exited the harbor. On deck, Mitsuko spotted several familiar faces and she wondered if Sett’s mutiny would still play out without her interference. It hadn’t when she successfully smuggled herself over to Verdant Island. But that was still early in the loop. She didn’t know where the ship disappeared to after it stopped on Verdant.

“Captain insists we take his cabin,” Holly said, standing on her tip-toes to keep her head above the gunwales as she leaned against them to look back at Mauve next to Mitsuko.

“We’re definitely not taking his cabin,” Mitsuko replied. “I like the cabin we have below deck. We don’t need more space than that. You’re like a third the size of a normal person.”

“Excuse you! How rude. I am a normal person!”

“You’re the furthest thing I’ve ever met from a normal person. I’ve actually met Oozes more normal than you.”

“I know exactly who you’re talking about, and he is not more normal than me!” Holly huffed dramatically and turned her nose up. “Besides, the point is moot. I already declined the cabin.”

“Because it was too big?” Mitsuko asked.

“It would have been drafty. You know I prefer cozy.”

“Personally, I would not have minded the extra space,” Juniper added. She stood on the gunwales next to them, listening in on their conversation.

“Of course you want the big room, Ster—Juniper.” Mitsuko stumbled over the name, completely ruining her snark. She cleared her throat. “You get to stay outside. Guard us in case a guardian or acolyte tries to murder us in the night.”

“Guardians and acolytes can’t leave their islands.”

Mitsuko dramatically clamped her hands to her ears. “Oh! I cannot know such critical information about the Prismatic Spiral!  Not without using a special question! Let it be known I was exposed unwillingly!”

Juniper’s eye twitched. But she agreed to stay on deck and watch out for anything suspicious.

Even with the ship’s elementalist pumping the sails with wind, it would take over a day to sail to the island. Mitsuko decided to put that time to use. She and Holly retreated down to their cramped cabin and Mitsuko practiced Deteriorate and Mend on different objects while lounging on her short bed.

Mitsuko cast Deteriorate on a spare healing potion they had. Magical objects always proved more challenging so she decided that if she wanted to level, this was the best way to do so. Uncorked, the potion hardened after time exposed to the open air. Then she recorked the bottle and Mended the contents, returning it to normal. After a few back and forths, a thought occurred to her.

She cast Mend and Deteriorate simultaneously on the potion. She had never cast two different spells at the same time before. The effort involved made her pulse quicken and she widened her eyes. The two spell clashed against one another. It was like mixing oil and water. They didn’t combine. Instead they pushed one another back and forth. She continued to force them at one another.

Holly yelped as the glass vial shattered. Shards of glass sliced into Mitsuko’s hand while both healing potion and blood dribbled from her palms. She stared down at her marred hands. Every minute movement sent a spasm of pain. Shards of red tinted glass poked out of her skin. The potion had healed the wounds while not ejecting the glass first. Weirdly, while she felt the throbbing pain, Mitsuko didn’t wince as she once might have.

“Doesn’t that…hurt?” Holly asked, also staring at Mitsuko’s hands with wide eyes.

“Nah. It’s not too bad.”

This was far from the worst damage she’d taken while in the loop. And by now she knew it was only temporary.

Mitsuko cast Mend on her hands and the potion. The wounds reopened momentarily as the glass exited and then the flesh sealed back together. Her sheets, which had soaked up the blood and potion liquid, lost their stain as the liquid returned to a newly formed glass vial.

“That really is amazing,” Holly commented. She leaned over and plucked the vial from Mitsuko’s hand. Then she held it up to the daylight coming from their cabin’s small circular window. “It looks perfectly fine. I’m not a brewer by any means, but it looks pure. As if it never fell on your dirty blankets.”

“My blankets aren’t dirty.” Mitsuko snatched the vial back. “You’ve been with me the entire time I’ve been on this ship. Who would I have made them dirty with?”

“Your skin?” Holly looked at her like she was an idiot. “You realize you humans have like skin grease and you flake off all the time. Not to mention hair.”

Mitsuko stood and set her hand on the bedframe. Then she cast Mend. The sheets flew about, rapidly repeating all the times Mitsuko had slept on them throughout the trip. Finally, they settled into place.

“There. Clean now.”

Holly eyed the bed dubiously. “What happens to all the stuff like skin flakes? You didn’t have it fly back into your body. Do you just delete it from reality?”

Mitsuko shrugged. “I can create matter so I don’t see why I wouldn’t be able to destroy it as well. I think Sterling said something about this in one of his ramblings.”

“Ah, yes. Your friend…Sterling.” Holly smirked and waggled her purple eyebrows at Mitsuko.

“There are probably around fifty different reasons why that wouldn’t work out, the age gap being one of them. But more importantly than any of those other reasons, I’m simply not interested.”

Holly sighed. “Damn, if you’re not interested in him then he must actually be a decent person.”

Mitsuko glared at the gnome. But before she could defend her dating history, Holly tossed a package at her.

“What’s this?” Mitsuko tiled the long cylinder wrapped in brown paper.

“A map, obviously. I swear, Mitsuko, sometimes it’s like you don’t even know me. Of course I’m going to throw a map at you to distract you from talking about your myriad of terrible failed relationships.”

Mitsuko ignored that last bit and undid the string holding the brown in place. She unrolled the map on her newly cleaned bed.

The shape was that of a canine tooth, though hooked at the tip. It was mostly blank, save for several large triangular mountains and a few notes of interest. ‘Ice Reef’ and ‘Winter Caverns’ both caught her attention. The reef, because it circled around the majority of the island, likely protecting it. And the caverns because that was a decent lead into where she’d need to go one at the island.

“It’s flawed,” Holly said. “I’m not certain the entire north western corner is even accurately drawn. And those mountains are… well, to put it nicely, not drawn well. But this is the best map I could find while we were on Mauve. And it’s far better than any records the Hon Empire had of the island.”

“This one has landmarks, but do you have any idea about where the island settlements are? The port we’re going into?”

“No and no,” Holly replied cheerily. “Also, no topographical information either. Just the random triangles on this map. Once we get closer tomorrow I’ll set up some divination rituals to get a better view of the island.”

“Isn’t the Prismatic Spiral interfering with your divination spells?” Mitsuko asked. She thought she remembered someone mentioning that in a previous loop.

“The dome is messing up a bit of my magic, but I can at least still get bird’s eye sight spells set up. Especially if the target is nearby. Further away and they fuzz out. Also, I tried divining inside that clock tower you mentioned and the headache it gave me knocked me flat on my butt.”

Mitsuko frowned. “So you don’t have access to sight within the temples?” she guessed. “Have you tested out other spells?”

Holly tapped a finger on her chin while she considered. “Well, I specialize in scouting. But how about I test one right now. Try lying to me.”

“Holly, you’re a disgusting ogre of a gnome who will never amount to anything. Everyone hates you. You smell like a clove of garlic that grew from decomposing used socks. And your hair is disgusting like the mangled intestines of a murasaki bakemono sliced open by a rusty serrated saw.”

Holly glowered at Mitsuko. “You’re lucky I can tell that was all lies. Otherwise I’d be very pissed right now.”

To hide her smirk, Mitsuko stood and peered out the circular window of their cabin. Low to the sea, all Mitsuko saw was open water. The gulls that tailed the boat were nowhere to be seen.

But then, in the placid water, Mitsuko saw something churn. Her smile slipped and her brows knit together as she squinted down into the depths below. Her efforts earned her a flash of a crustation’s shell, just briefly cresting the sea’s surface.

“Holly, can you cast a life sense spell?” she asked her friend. She knew it was normally within the diviner’s arsenal.

“Ugh. It’s such a pain. I need to actually use chalk for that one.”

“Please do it. I think I saw something in the water just now.”

“In the water? Hm.” Holly crouched down on the floor and began scribbling the ritual’s base structure with a piece of chalk on the floorboards.

Meanwhile, Mitsuko kept her eyes focused on the sea. Twice more she spotted something crest the sea and into visibility. What looked like a red foot covered in carapace and a green shell that could belong to a sea turtle.

“Okay, I got it. One sec.” Holy slapped her hand down on the complex drawing of symbols. Her eyes glowed white for a second and her mouth moved noiselessly. Then she blinked and pulled back from the ritual. “That’s… certainly odd.”

“Holly, now’s not a good time to be coy with mysterious statements.”

“There are six humanoids below us. Along with plenty of fish.”

“I’m guessing crusts are the humanoids. I’ve run into them before. But if there are only six the crew should be able to handle it just fine. That’s it?”

“Well…there also seems to be something much larger following them. It might be with them.”

“Larger? Can you give me more info?”

“Maybe, ship-sized? A bit bigger than The Selcouth Sable. It might be… a large turtle? Or maybe it’s a whale? I can’t quite tell what that is.”

Mitsuko opened her mouth, about to ask if she had any ideas about the shape of the creature or any distinguishing features. But then something slammed into the hull from below. Holly tumbled over and rolled under Mitsuko’s bed.

Mitsuko grabbed a hold of the wall to balance herself. Their window dipped below the sea as The Selcouth Sable rocked. For a moment, she stared into the depths of the water below.

A white eye stared back at her. One of thousands.
