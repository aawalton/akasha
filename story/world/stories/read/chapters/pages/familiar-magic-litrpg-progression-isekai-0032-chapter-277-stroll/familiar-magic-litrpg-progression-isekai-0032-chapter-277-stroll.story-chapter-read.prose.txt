Selina jumped up, not able to believe her eyes. But there could be no doubt, this was Cara Fletcher, her friend.

“Is this really happening?” she asked, pinching her arm. “Are you real? Am I asleep? Or hallucinating? Maybe those dreadrachni injected me with some kind of poison. I read once that certain types of monsters can do that, though I haven’t seen it myself. Parcel and Newman from the southern kingdoms postulated that–”

Cara let out a loud sigh, shaking her head. The familiar sound made Selina grin, and she rushed forward, gathering her friend up in a tight red hug.

“Oh, how I’ve missed you!” she mumbled into her friend’s shoulder. “I know you wanted to go off with Prince Calevaro. Did you two ever…? That doesn’t matter. I know how he’s like. Did you ever imagine being a princess though? But I suppose that would have made Princess Ellevaro your sister–that would have been challenging. But I suppose you also mentioned helping Valanor? That was always so hard to picture for me. You just didn’t seem the type to help the Church of the Goddess, whatever state they were in. Plus with what we learned about them… But Valanor had big plans, didn’t he? He was going to fix everything. Noble, but unlikely, if you ask me. A world-spanning religion based on lies didn’t have much hope. Also the goddess left! Ethan told us everything. Oh! Ethan is alive! Isn’t that wonderful? He started the Union, then escaped to Earth through a rift! But he’s not there anymore, he’s here, with me! Us! Well, not here here, but on Potentia! Wait, what are you doing on Potentia? Did you come here by accident? They don’t have any alcohol, that must be difficult. But you were trying to quit! Did you quit? Oh, and you have horns! They’re small, but they’re very pretty and stylish. They suit you, I think. But I’m biased. Cara? Cara, you’re not saying anything. It’s becoming rude.”

The archer looked back at her with a slight smile, shaking her head for some reason.

“Cara, what’s the matter? You’re still not speaking–are you alright?”

Cara pointed at her throat, shaking her head slowly. Selina placed her hand gently on the archer’s comparatively small neck. She sent out a questing thread of power to search for injury–a natural precursor to any healing–but felt not wrong. She tried to force the healing spell to cast anyway, but her magic told her that Cara was in perfect health. Still…

“There’s something odd about the structure of your trachea, I can’t tell what, and it won’t be healed.” She stepped back, examining her friend again. “The horns…you’ve been changed, haven’t you? Affected by Potentia.”

Cara nodded slowly, then made an angry gesture at several of the monsters which had finally caught up. They backed away dutifully, but watched Selina with intense eyes.

“What are you doing here, Cara?” Selina asked. “How are you connected to these monsters? Wait, I have a notebook! You can write it down!”

Cara let out a sigh, then gestured around them vaguely. As if to make her point, a howl sounded from deeper into the woods.

Selina nodded. “Right, explanations can wait. Potentia. Demons. So-forth. We should get back to the Fable Train. You’ll need to meet Apollo, and Zakiel, and to a much lesser extent, Strategus. Oh, a new Strategus. Different Strategus? It’s complicated. But I’ll explain on the way!”

She took an excited step back toward the train, wondering if they could still catch Walter. Cara’s hand on her shoulder stopped her, and she turned back.

“What is it?”

Cara made an insistent gesture, then pointed in the opposite direction. Then she turned and strode off, the monsters eyeing Selina warily before following.

Selina looked back and forth between the retreating archer, and the direction where she knew the army waited–no doubt with a concerned Apollo whipping them forward. After a moment of indecision, she raced after her friend.

“Cara! Cara, wait. I just need to go back to my bunker. The metal room-thing that was on Walter’s back. Oh! Walter is my…pet? I don’t know what to call him. But he’s my giant Twilight crab friend. The bunker goes on his back, you see.”

Cara glanced at her with a raised eyebrow, but didn’t slow.

“He’s sweet! Well, maybe he is. I don’t suppose I really know him well, but he’s been a good boy and taken good care of me. And he’s safe! It’s so complicated, but almost-Ethan is in control of him, and he wouldn’t hurt us!”

Cara just shook her head, as if refusing to acknowledge the statement.

Selina hurried to keep up with your friend, quickly examining her in the Astral as she did so. “You’re still Dusk rank, I see. That means this place is as dangerous for you as it is for me.”

Cara gave her a knowing glance, and a half-smile.

“Fine!” Selina said in an exasperated tone. “It’s the woods. The woods are your weird second home, so I’m sure it’s less dangerous for you. But still dangerous! There’s an army of Twilights to protect us back with Fable. Oh! Fable is a girl now. I’m not certain I can explain that one.”

Cara just sighed, but didn’t slow. Selina grumbled.

“The bunker has communication equipment, Cara. I can at least let Apollo know that I’m alright. He worries! And Zakiel will be scared if I’m gone too long. He’s like Ethan, if Ethan was on many drugs. But with a child-like innocence. Which I think is ironic, given he’s apparently some kind of killer. But I’m not sure it counts since he’s only from a potential reality, and not a reality-reality. But I’m fairly certain he killed me in his own dimension. Isn’t that fascinating?”

This time Cara’s jaw dropped, and she even mouthed a few words before giving up. Still, she just pointed more aggressively forward, and Selina could do nothing but sigh, and follow. She eyed the monsters which had now formed a loose ring around them, the dreadrachni in the trees above.

They continued on for nearly two hours. Selina spent the time trying to catch Cara up on everything that had happened, struggling to find the right balance of explanation with someone who couldn’t really confirm how much she was understanding.

“And I think that’s it!” she declared. “I was in Corvale, which was sucked through a massive Dimensional Bleed. Something similar happened to Prince Calevaro, and we both managed to meet up with Ethan! Together we found the lost god of Earth, who is a city, which is interesting, but confusing! We scored some victories against the Followers, and now we’re here!”

Cara nodded absently.

“We need to find The Throne, which is the last vestige of Dusk’s powers. The army is helping us accumulate enough of Potentia’s tainted magic that Ethan can…sit on the Throne, I suppose? That will let him complete the Union, as planned. But the last of the Followers are here too, and we’re fairly certain they have the same plan. They have their own army, and it’s larger than ours, so Ethan keeps trying to find ways to slow them down. Right now he’s looking for the Basilisk, the strange man who turns things to stone.”

Cara let out a relieved sigh, and pointed. Selina, who’d just thought of another dozen things to clarify, looked ahead. The monsters were pushing away boulders from the entrance to a small tunnel, barely large enough for Selina to stand in.

“We’re going in there?” Selina asked.

Cara nodded, leading the way. Selina only hesitated for a moment before following. As soon as she crossed the threshold, she felt the telltale presence of rune magic. She didn’t see anything, but she was certain it was there, and wondered at the purpose of the wards. She didn’t even know Cara was capable of such magics, but she had to imagine the archer had been busy since they’d last seen each other.

She followed her friend deeper into the darkness, even her night vision rune barely enough to penetrate the truly lightless tunnel. The sounds of the monsters behind her made it hard not to feel like she was being chased, but Selina resolved to hold her calm. Cara must have some sort of safe hideaway deeper down, presumably where she could also communicate. Selina just needed to be patient.

Patience soon manifested as more talking, the sounds of her voice echoing up and down the seemingly endless cavern. Cara didn’t seem to mind though, suggesting the place was every bit as safe as Selina had hoped. They kept moving deeper and deeper as the minutes ticked by, with the low ceiling occasionally scraping against her horns.

When they finally saw a light up ahead, Selina couldn’t help but release a long, relieved sigh. That sigh turned into a gasp of awe when they finally emerged into open space, and Selina nearly collapsed from the sight.

This was no hideaway of Cara’s. This was a single, enormous chamber, larger even than all of Corvale. And it was a fitting comparison, as a city sprawled out before her.

She recognized the architecture from the ruins they’d discovered on the surface, although this was far more elaborate, and almost entirely intact. Stone buildings stretched out for miles, many four stories tall or higher, and there spires and towers dotted among them. In the center, standing out among the reddish-grey stone was something she could only describe as a castle, although it might have been more of a fort by some standards.

It was of an odd, twisted construction, built in angles and curves, entirely unlike those she’d seen on Earth. She desperately wanted to go study it, and hoped she’d have time.

The cave had ended in an alarmingly steep set of stairs that descended into the city, with platforms halfway down where more monsters stood on guard. These were tall, golem-like creatures made of the same stone as the buildings, and Selina briefly wondered if her initial assessment may have been incorrect–these might actually be some kind of construct. They stood perfectly still.

“You found a Nator city!” Selina said to Cara with undisguised delight. “We found some ruins, but nothing like this. I might actually learn some of the history of my people! Thank you for taking me here, Cara. I could study this place for a hundred years! I might finally get an understanding of what happened to those of my kind who didn’t make it to Nexum.”

Cara gave an odd smile, but kept walking.

When they reached the bottom of the stair case, more golems stood in front of a tall wall that Selina had seen wrapped around the entire city, and they moved to open a stone door as the odd party approached.

It was only a crack before Selina saw what awaited her inside, and her life was forever changed.

“There are Nator living here,” she said in disbelief, and nearly became the only Dusk rank Bonded to faint.
