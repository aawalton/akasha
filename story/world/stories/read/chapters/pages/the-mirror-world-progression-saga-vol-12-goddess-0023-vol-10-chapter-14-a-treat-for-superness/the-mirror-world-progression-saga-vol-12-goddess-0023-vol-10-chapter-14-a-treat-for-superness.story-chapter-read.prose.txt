Samantha flew out from the city fast and hard, burning energy, and Invoking deeply with [Goddess] to steel herself out of that depressed and grieving state she’d been in. She ‘swallowed deity’ desperately into herself, like a miracle pill.

Perhaps the human part of her needed more of those shreds left of the tender, emotional being, and psychological healing therein, but as deific ‘clarity’ came, she was dubious about that. It felt more like the creeping insanity her brothers and sisters were fighting off. This was just her version of it.

The body of the Goddess flew up. Up, and up, and away. Away from their heat. Away from the confines of mortals.

Why shouldn’t I?! Who among them wouldn’t envy the sky?! Who wouldn’t reach for it, and wave goodbye?

When she was ‘existing’ as a deity in full, things were balanced enough. In equilibrium. She had a ruthless side, courtesy of Rallindin, but she was not nearly so brutal as that — when she made a logical comparison. If anything, the many lives between the First Wizard and Samantha showed a gradual bend toward more mercy and compassion. There was no real casting that aside. There was no return to the original badass, per se.

The steel had been tempered. Refined.

The question continued: how could she transcend yet remain the same? All well and good, the childish notion to maintain the internal status quo of the self. Be who she was, neverending, preserving the ‘Sammy.’ But that was simply not enough. It had never been and never was. She was continually evolving into who she was meant to be, casting aside molted shells of who she was yesterday to transform into what was necessary for tomorrow.

Even more extreme, perhaps: seeds planted by who she was, and they’d bloomed and grown into something higher.

Perhaps everyone had the seed, and Samantha Elizabeth Reine was simply the one who had finally bloomed. There was beauty and synergy in believing that — believing she was ‘Everyone of Earth.’ Their amalgamate. All of the soul and consciousness of that cosmos, simply… moving on, through one tiny seed. Earth hadn’t died. She was Earth.

Gaia.

In this musing on her identity, she was Earth’s entire integration back into the System. An evolutionary timestamp of humanity — which was nothing more than the most dominant macro-organism string by the end. Making the perhaps unfortunate statement, ‘To here, humanity got, roughly.’ Not exceptional, yet not too down in the dirt. A well-fed and fertile product of excess. A daughter reasonably fit enough to become a mother. Not too shabby?

In the material world, Samantha was very high now. High enough to feel the biting cold, and to dispense with breathing. She looked down from the too-dark heavens at a visibly curved world below. She saw the coastline and the mountains. It was lovely. A rare gift, to see it all. Who else had done such a thing on this primitive rock?

It was a treat for fucking superheroes. Or supervillains. Leaving everyone else green with envy, regardless. If only they knew.

It reminded her of when she looked down over Rivermount, after the conquest. Saw the fear and awe, the wonder in their eyes. She hadn’t rushed to disavow them. She hadn’t spoken comforting or encouraging words. She’d just stared down at them and felt the divide. Knew how vast it had become. She didn’t mince. Perhaps as honest as she'd ever been, doing and saying precisely nothing. That silence had been instructive.

Samantha smiled. They accepted me, anyway. They trusted me to rule.

Her head was clear enough now. Cool like still waters. She left another version of herself down below. Frozen away, shed, disintegrated, molted dead and worthless now. Her name — Samantha — meant something new yet again. Perhaps more of where she came from, perhaps less. Both.

To hell with the maudlin blah. There is one thing I absolutely have in common with the majority spinning ‘round where I came from, and here, too: I’ve gotta get back to work.

First things first, she shifted to the Bridge of Edges and popped her body back near Rivermount. Not long after the conquest, they’d found a route in catacombs lodged in foothills southwest of Rivermount, near the mountains there called the Oboors. It looked like underground city ruins. Azure had poked around but found nothing to stir a vision. It was the 8th Transition Point, and she nicknamed it Oboors Underhills.

It was mostly precautionary to be there. Maybe something would happen as she searched for the locale. She got herself aboveground but didn’t bother heading directly to Rivermount from the foothills… She was about to be looking to shift to yet another new spot, because Lovie had mentioned that was how to get to the Consolidator in the first place.

But she had to find it first. She prepared all her long-term buffs beforehand, including her new one, Spell Reflector, so she wouldn’t need to do it ‘inside,’ where things like that tended to get wonky. Something told her she also wouldn’t find it comfortable to go back after finding it, either.

She sent word to Azure to be ready and to get the longer-term buffs prepped, but in her case, she could prep shorter ones right before spitting out next to Samantha. That was the other thing… Azure would be a liability to dig around in weird places where she was going — perhaps in the same way one found it awkward doing surgery on themselves.

She dematerialized to the Bridge of Edges again and started focusing on things like the Southlands, as well as the region between Geirkos and Traesh. That simple regional act done, corresponding to nothing discernible physically, but nonetheless a linkage she detected spiritually, she focused more and more on what she knew of those ruins…

It became obvious very quickly. A place that pulled the spiritual reality toward it; a place that repulsed her subtly. A comparatively large location of the bendy, weird landscapes. It was darker, more warped, as if symmetry began to break around it. This was something like the ‘error’ she’d found before: the imperfection that had led her to the Archer. But this was both subtler and worse in different ways. Nothing was fully broken here; it just twisted more. Began a synthesis, a transition. To a degree, she had to move through that to get where she wanted more specifically.

Great. No choice, though.

As soon as she crossed the threshold, she felt icky. Shuddered. It was like moving into an oil cloud. Subtle differences of the ‘air,’ but unmistakable.

From concord to discord, by one little note.

She walked through the realm as if on a rail made of strange resin or stone, through a wide tunnel the color of all colors at once, just a shade darker. And then darker still. She couldn’t see very far beyond the immediate path, as usual. It twisted through many spaces, every step past many layers of transparent, mind-bending terrain. As she walked, the discord grew louder and louder.

Shadows twisted out from the terrain, shadows that didn’t exist anywhere else in those spaces. They separated from the rest and crisscrossed, making new trickery of the depths she traversed. Her rail and tunnel began to corkscrew and go in many directions at once. She relied heavily on [Deific Pneumamancy] to get her through this. Each ‘step’ was treacherous… Heaven knew where she’d get sucked off to if she took a wrong turn.

Only she could walk this. It required will to stabilize the way, making the correct steps to get to her goal. She was certain they didn’t exist before she made them. Pathfinded. Or perhaps re-pathfinded what was lost.

I certainly made the right choice not bringing Azure.

Everything grew dark, distorted, oily, and corrupt — like the structure was slowly melting in a slow process of transformation. Into what, she perfectly understood that she did not want to know. That was ‘on the other side’ in a way that would corrupt her just to touch it.

She paused, once, almost making an error in her steps. Right past her nose was black, melting wax, interlaced with a still-fascinating inner matrix of immense complexity. It smelled of formaldehyde, mothballs, and acid bogs. It stained her nostrils. It was deceptively solid, as the spot represented a trapdoor. She knew precisely what was beyond it.

The Sage’s path. Entropic network branching into ours.

She suppressed the urge to vomit, even as her eyes were tantalized by the matrix patterns it was showing. Could she understand something by observing this way? She thought she was seeing imagery forming on that surface. No — inside it. Imagery she’d never seen in that place before.

But it felt like her eyes themselves were being corrupted second by second; tainted by those intense fumes.

She felt a warning fire through her nerves. Had it already touched her? No. No, surely not. If it had…

Suddenly, a little nub, a little lump was forming on the surface in front of her nose, as she made cross-eyes to pinpoint it. It was growing out toward her — slowly and painstakingly, to her hyper-quick senses.

Feeling a sudden, intense revulsion, Samantha chose her back step carefully by analysis and force of will. Not quite backtracking — but certainly getting away.

In another moment, she was away from it, trying to shake the creepy, awful feeling that it had touched her. It hadn’t, right?! It couldn’t have. Otherwise, she’d feel it with every fiber of her being. That was pure corruption.

She checked herself copiously. ‘Physically’ in that space, mentally, spiritually, with [Spirit Sight]. Nothing. She did it again. All was well.

She breathed a sigh of relief… and then admonished herself for remaining that close for so long.

Stupid, stupid, stupid! Gotta stay away from the Dark Side. Luke and Leia, give me strength!

She moved on, skirting the edges, making sure to avoid close encounters with the ‘soulmelt.’

Ugh. Don’t like that word. Why did that have to come to me?

As usual, she came to the right ‘spot’ quite suddenly, and it was so strongly pulling her, she almost fell into the well immediately. It was a brief struggle of will to avoid it. She did not look at it, deliberately, right then — if she had, she would’ve entered.

Fighting off panic, she very, very carefully, very, very slowly, took the tiniest step back. Bingo. The pressure disappeared as she stepped into the equilibrium point — between the stability of the confounding path and the vortex, invariably pulling her deeper.

She peered in the direction she would need to step. Nothing but shadows in many layers cloaked the way. It wasn’t one you could look into before leaping. Those two concepts, or states, were wedded and merged.

It was time to make contact with Azure, being the only person she could technically bring with her. No one else could be summoned until they were on the other side. Depending on how she arrived, she might be able to summon Servitors.

“Hey, love,” Samantha sent mentally to her betrothed. “Prepare your final spells and come. I need you to transfer while remaining in contact as much as possible without fusing into me. Clinging, hugging, sharing the exact same space. This place is no less dangerous than the one with the Archer.”
