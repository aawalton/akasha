Standing up, Ferris stretched his mental body. Jade had spent the entire time he was writing his notes rolling around. The slime would’ve been happy patrolling the same area all night, but came over to mimic his stretches. Ferris’ exercises soon turned into practicing his spear forms, which Jade also tried to copy.

Usually, being able to move freely in the soulspace was freeing. Practicing had always been a familiar, relaxing exercise that calmed his mind and centered his thoughts.

Yet now, he couldn’t shake the worry his uncle’s visit had sparked. Bluereef’s spear style was based on the Water Jet ability. Could he ever measure up to other styles without the ability? Should he try to learn something else?

Stopping, Ferris gripped the weapon tightly. Why must I lose this as well? He thought.

With no answer, he called Jade over and left his Heartfire to gather qi from the Air Passage. Ferris wanted to clear it out so that he could try out the gift his uncle had left him: the incense sticks. Hopefully, the work would distract him from his thoughts.

The cloaked demons were still lurking about, stealing chunks of qi where they could. Ferris chased them away when he saw them, but still didn’t have any good ideas to keep them from returning.

The harvest of qi was pithy compared to what he’d gotten from Jade’s Digest River last night—and hoped to match tonight. Even not counting what the thieves stole from the Air Passage, he still got about twenty times as much from Jade. Ferris knew qi was a harsh problem for cultivators before they started delving, and it was easy to see why. Experiments like his paper would have been impossible without Jade.

Once the Air Passage was cleared and all the qi chunks dumped into the pool, Ferris and Jade woke up to the real world. They checked outside the door, and Jade was overjoyed to find that Mother Lily had left two plates of dinner for them.

Dinner was stir-fry with a dark brown sauce. Jade enjoyed it as always, but kept complaining that the different components were too small to be fed individually. Jade wanted to taste and rate each component on its own.

After they finished eating, Ferris pulled out the bundle of incense sticks his uncle had given him. He’d heard about things like this before: resources that could aid cultivation. Inside the bundle were five sticks of red incense. Uncle Winton had instructed him to inhale the smoke while practicing his meditative breathing, which made Ferris suspect it would affect his Air Passage.

Sitting on the floor, Ferris carefully lit one of the sticks and set it on a ceramic incense stand in front of him. After confirming that a wisp of smoke was floating up from the red-hot top of the stick, he closed his eyes and focused.

Meditative breathing was a common ritual for aspiring adepts, and Ferris had practiced it daily growing up. He hadn’t done as much focused breathing since unlocking his soul—instead working in his soulspace. It was almost nostalgic to do it once again.

The stick of incense burned for about ten minutes, but Ferris continued the breathing exercise for twice that. Once he felt he’d gotten any benefits possible, he moved Jade and himself to the bed to seek the emptiness of his soul instead.

After opening his eyes in the soulspace, Ferris and Jade headed to inspect the Air Passage. Ferris did take a quick look around his Heartfire, but nothing had changed. He wasn’t sure what he would do if the Air Passage was the same as before. There wasn’t much else in his soulspace to check.

Thankfully, Ferris immediately noticed a difference as they approached the Air Passage. A hazy smoke hung in the air in and about the trench. Inside, barely visible through the smoke, glowing chunks of qi were everywhere. It was the densest he had ever seen here, even better than after he had traveled the rift to get to Scalefort Bastion.

Walking down into the trench, Ferris and Jade began their second collection run of the night. It was too bad they had to do it twice, but Ferris wanted to maximize any potential gains from the incense stick. He didn’t know if having any qi already on the ground would hurt the results, but it wasn’t worth the risk.

Additionally, they didn’t run into any of the cloaked demons. Ferris wasn’t sure if they just hadn’t returned yet from earlier, or if the smoke scared them off, but he was glad of it.

Despite how excited he was, when they got it all back to the Heartfire, Ferris couldn’t help being a bit underwhelmed. He had filled his bag two and a half times, where a normal day—even before the cloaked demon thieves appeared—he didn’t fill it once.

Despite this, it was less than half of what Jade produced yesterday. The slime’s qi production—with sufficient food—was a level above even a cultivation resource like the incense stick.

Ferris appreciated the gift. If he were just relying on his Air Passage, the cloaked demons would’ve been a debilitating problem instead of an annoying inconvenience. These five incense sticks would have been a critical influx of qi. Still, it made him excited to think about what he could do with more qi than other new cultivators. He needed every advantage he could get. Once he was feeding Jade monsters in the rifts, then the ability would really take off.

After all the qi chunks were dumped into the pool, they went to the slimespace to gather from the Digest River. Ferris was happy to see that every single clam was proudly displaying a qi chunk, ready for harvesting.

Ferris had to create a couple of new acid-resistant chains to use his combination fishing method with Jade. Even with the costs, he gathered more qi than he’d received from the stick of incense.

After filling his bag the first time, Ferris wondered if there was a way to make carrying it back to the Heartfire easier. To drop off qi from the Digest River, he had to walk over, climb up the tower, and transfer to the soulspace. Then do it all in reverse to return. It only took him around five minutes per full bag, but that added up with the amount of qi he collected every night.

Ferris remembered Uncle Winton’s qi rope transfer technique, which relied on moving the core around. He could technically leave the slimespace from anywhere, as long as he had the core next to him. So far, he had left it on the pedestal on top of the Heartfire’s tower. Should he move it closer to the river? Did it matter if the core was in the pedestal or not?

On his next trip back from the soulspace, Ferris quickly tested using the core without its pedestal. He was able to transition back and forth fine, although the core dropped to the ground after he left. Even though using the core alone worked, he wanted to keep them together. Ferris believed the ‘intent’ of the pedestal had kept the demons from stealing it in his soulspace, after all.

Ferris lifted the pedestal up on his back. It wasn’t heavy, although it was a bit awkward to climb down the tower with it. At least the side of the tower was sloped rather than completely vertical, and had many handholds.

Once he got the pedestal to the Consume Pool, Ferris found a place next to a couple of the yellow spires—far enough back to avoid any higher acid levels from eating. He set it down and placed his hand on the core, leaving the slimespace. After a second to confirm everything was normal with the matching pedestal in the soulspace, Ferris returned to the slimespace.

Happily, he found himself next to the pedestal, down by the Consume Pool. The time saved for a single trip was a small thing. But over the course of the night, it would likely save him at least thirty minutes. Ferris intended to collect this much qi every day. In the long term, it would be a massive time savings.

While about halfway through collecting the qi from the clams, Ferris noticed something. This was the first time he was able to fully harvest from every clam in the river two days in a row, but some of the clams had moved. Not all of them, but he definitely remembered fishing from a couple yesterday that were gone today. There were also clams in new locations; he was sure they weren’t there before.

Ferris had never seen the clams move, but he hadn’t spent too much time just watching the river. If he wasn’t actively collecting qi, he wasn’t usually close enough to see the clams. Maybe they could push themselves around like a real clam.

It was one more odd thing that didn’t have an explanation, so Ferris resolved to make a note and hope he would discover more later. At least he now had a way to write these things down. He would add it to the records he already had on the slimespace creatures.

After he finished the harvest, Ferris smiled as he dumped his bag of qi chunks into the growing pool. It was the largest it had ever been, even with his experiments making paper earlier.

After watering the Core Tree and the two seeds in their pots, he was free from the nightly chores.

Ferris didn’t want to stay up too late—he had to work the next day after all. Still, he had resolved to keep a watch on the oath statue until he could better secure it. With that in mind, he called Jade over and set out for the chain demon’s fort.

This was the first time he’d approached it without the bright light shining from the top of the four towers. There were a few groups of chain demons—which Ferris and Jade easily killed—but not nearly as many as he was used to. Compared to before, the fort was practically deserted.

Unfortunately, the pile of chains Ferris had collected was gone. He’d hoped leaving them under the oath statue would protect them from whatever took things abandoned in the darkness, but no such luck. Before, the lanky demon’s corpse hadn’t disappeared, but it had been wrapped up in the oath statue’s white chains. Ferris wasn’t willing to let the white chains grow down into the ground, and still had Jade cut them back each visit. At least he’d learned something. Most likely, the white chains themselves had kept the lanky demon’s corpse from being stolen, rather than the presence of the oath statue.

After leaving the soulspace, Ferris lay in bed holding Jade, thinking about all he had learned that day. With each new answer, he just had more questions.

He did, at least, feel like things were looking up. He knew more than yesterday, and Jade’s qi production was running at full speed.

As they said, the only way to walk the path was one step at a time. He was happy with today’s step forward, humble as it was.
