Once Jia had sufficiently chastised Eui, the two sat down in front of each other to meditate. Eui glanced nervously at Jia.

“Are you sure about this? We were just warned that joint meditation is risky, and last time was—”

Jia fixed Eui with a determined glare and shook her head.

“That was an accident—we weren’t ready for it. Next time we’ll be prepared. I’ve already made up my mind, we’re in this together all the way. Unless you’re having second thoughts?”

Eui shook her head and waved her hands urgently.

“No! I’m not! I’m just nervous, I guess.”

Jia held out a hand with her pinky finger extended.

“We’ll stick together forever. Promise?”

Eui smiled and linked her own pinky with Jia’s, completing the gesture before giving Jia a sly smirk.

“Together forever, huh? Ooh, your boyfriend is going to be so jealous!”

Jia narrowed her eyes at Eui and waggled her fingers menacingly.

“I thought you had already learned, but I guess good lessons bear repeating—”Eui quailed and held up her hands defensively.

“No, please! Once was enough! I’ll be good!”

“You better.”

Jia grumbled a bit before returning her focus to the matter at hand.

“So, you said you had an idea about joint ki meditation?”

Eui nodded and began to explain. The concept was simple enough—like their joint mana meditation, it was based on the way they had created a circuit of ki through the attunement rod. In this case, they would try to create such a circuit through each other’s bodies, hopefully sharing their ki the same way they had shared their mana previously.

They joined hands and began to meditate, synchronizing their breathing and focusing on the flow of ki within their bodies. They had already agreed on a counterclockwise circuit of ki, sending the energy out through their right hands and accepting it back with their left. It didn’t take long to accomplish a circuit thanks to their practice, but nothing happened at first.

Jia could feel the ki as it travelled part way up Eui’s arm, but after that it disappeared from her senses. Focusing on the ki she was receiving, she noticed that she gained control of the ki that Eui was sending her around the same point.

Recalling the difficulty of retaining control of the qi outside her body when she assisted Eui’s awakening, Jia focused on the ki that left her body, trying to maintain control of it for as long as possible. The results were almost too immediate as Eui clenched her hand painfully and Jia’s ki was rejected by Eui’s.

“Ow! Fuck!”

Jia was snapped from her meditation by Eui’s cry of pain.

“Are you ok!? I’m sorry! I didn’t mean to do that!”

Jia explained what she had been attempting and Eui nodded along.

“That makes sense, but as soon as you did that it hurt. It felt like what you described when Yan Zhihao grabbed you.”

“Damn...what are we missing?”

Eui raised an eyebrow and grinned at Jia.

“Well, I can think of one—”

“Not that!”Jia blushed bright red as she smacked Eui in the arm. As if Eui didn’t already tease her enough before Ienaga told them about dual cultivation. Eui snickered at her for a moment before coughing once and schooling her expression.

“Ok, serious idea. You’re losing control of the ki because it’s outside of your body, and I am gaining control because it’s inside mine, right? I think that pain is a result of us fighting over control of the ki. Maybe while you focus on keeping control of it, I need to focus on not taking control. Does that make sense?”

Jia mulled it over for a moment before nodding.

“I think so, but it’s going to be hard to do both at the same time.”

Eui grimaced as she came to the same realization that Jia had. They would need to both focus on maintaining control of the ki that they sent out, while also preventing themselves from instinctively gaining control of or rejecting the ki that they received.

“Nothing to do but practice, I guess…”

Jia nodded and they returned to their meditation. As Jia had thought, it was extremely difficult to concentrate both on accepting the foreign ki and maintaining control over her own. Worse, each slip of concentration was extremely painful due to the increased sensitivity that ki meditation caused.

She found that between the two, it was much harder to accept the foreign ki. If she had to come up with an analogy, maintaining control was like holding her breath. The longer she did it, the harder it became, but there was nothing stopping her from keeping it up aside from her own willpower. Conversely, accepting Eui’s ki was like trying to tell her heart to stop beating. Her own ki was automatically trying to reject it, and it took enormous focus to prevent the reaction.

They spent a long time like that. It was something of a painful seesaw of momentum, where they each got a little bit further before one of them lost focus. The spikes of pain didn’t get any better, but Jia found herself getting used to them. It was a reminder that she needed to try harder—concentrate more. In a way, she was reminded of Qin’s constant negative reinforcement.

After what felt like an eternity, Jia was nearly ready to give up. Neither of them had even made it halfway to a full circuit of ki yet. Then, as a thread of Eui’s ki finally made it up past Jia’s shoulder, everything suddenly changed.

Something seemed to catch the thread of ki, and without any further input from either Eui or Jia, it was pulled in towards Jia’s heart before being distributed throughout her entire body, and finally out through her right hand, where it met her own thread of ki.

Once the circuit was complete, Jia felt like the ki in her body wasn’t hers anymore. Her entire body disappeared from her senses as if she had suddenly ceased to exist. For a bare, terrifying moment, Jia thought she might have just died.

Then, all at once, she felt her sensations returning along with new, foreign ones. Her body lit up with unfamiliar sensations. A new tail with more points of articulation than her own. Longer, smoother hair that cascaded down over shoulders that were narrower than she was accustomed to. Jia could feel Eui’s body as if it were her own.

For a second, she thought that they had somehow swapped bodies. However, she soon realized that the novel sensations were only distracting her from the more common, familiar senses of her own body. She could feel Eui’s hands entwined in hers, even as she felt her own in Eui’s.

The sensation of having two bodies was extremely odd, and more than a little confusing. Their breathing, their heartbeats, and even the flow of their ki had all become perfectly synchronized, and as with the other forms of joint meditation, it was impossible to tell Jia’s ki apart from Eui’s.

After taking a moment to acclimate to the unusual feeling, they began to meditate in earnest. With each intake of breath, the ki in their bodies expanded marginally. Like with mana meditation, their combined efforts seemed to cause an increase in effectiveness that was greater than the sum of their parts.

Jia felt her muscles twitching involuntarily as Eui engaged in some kind of image training, and instinctively she knew what kind of movements those twitches represented and how she would replicate them. Jia focused on her own image training, to consolidate the week and a half of sparring practice that she had neglected to meditate on properly.

The pair meditated comfortably until they began to feel the diminishing returns, as the mana in the environment grew thin from their absorption of it. Jia briefly wondered if her combat meditation technique would allow for longer sessions, but abandoned the thought as joint meditation already required enough concentration without adding more complications.

As Jia came down from the meditation, she opened her eyes, only to realize she’d opened the wrong pair of eyes. She saw herself, eyes closed in meditation, breathing in a steady but relaxed manner. Then, unbidden, her own body opened its eyes. Jia instantly became dizzy as her brain scrambled trying to make sense of two pairs of eyes seeing each other at the same time.

Both girls recoiled, and the link ended abruptly as they released their hands. Jia got reacquainted with the feeling of just one body, which was thankfully her own. Was it possible that they could have ended up in different bodies? Had Eui been in total control of both of them for that moment when Jia lost all sensation? What would have happened if Eui hadn’t completed the circuit?

“That was weird!”

Eui’s shout broke Jia out of her dark contemplations. Eui grasped her head, shaking it.

“I’m still feeling dizzy just thinking about it. I just saw myself seeing myself.”

Jia giggled a bit at Eui’s complaints, which were so incongruous to her own thoughts.

“Just imagine how much more awkward that would be to experience with a guy.”

“Pfft!”

Eui doubled over laughing at Jia’s comment. The laughter was contagious and soon they were both caught in self-perpetuating fits of giggling.

Without any new crises or duels to distract her, Lee Jia’s schedule fell into a routine over the next few weeks. As she tried to catch up in spiritual and martial arts, Jia’s magical studies were reduced to a single lesson a week, plus Dae’s tutoring which was also reduced to a weekly session.

Jia and Eui fell into a pattern of meditating on whatever they had worked on that day. Eui’s original idea of splitting their attention was mostly abandoned as they began to do most of their training and studies together. Eui was even begrudgingly convinced to join in on Dae’s tutoring sessions, though she spent most of them menacing the poor boy.

Elder Qin seemed unimpressed by Eui’s awakening, but agreed nonetheless to include her in Jia’s lessons. Like Ienaga, he insisted that she work on her foundation before learning any techniques. With her qi unlocked, the Foundation Building Manual made a lot more sense to her.

Spiritual arts were more about feeling than anything, and the jade slip had included feeling-based instructions that were impossible to put into words, which she had no way to interpret without qi senses. Lee Jia became very interested in seeing Dae’s transcription when she discovered that.

The foundation building mostly consisted of opening a few elementally neutral meridians, which served both as practice for the more complicated elemental patterns as well as improving the efficacy of meditation. As for meditation itself, with some trepidation Jia and Eui tried to replicate joint qi meditation.

Thanks to their experience with the other disciplines and the parallels between them, it was relatively easy to figure out how to do it. What was not so simple was keeping their focus. Even prepared for it, the overwhelming rush of unfiltered emotion was difficult to withstand. Eventually, they were able to calm down enough to focus on their breathing and meditate properly, but almost every session resulted in tears.

Over the course of the next month, Lee Jia saw a sort of culture develop within the academy. The Qin students were the most numerous, but also the most insular and cliquish, rarely deigning to engage outside of their social circles. The next largest group were the Yamato representatives, who were also the most sociable. Lee Jia assumed this was because, while they were undoubtedly elites, they were less starkly noble than either the Qin or Goryeon groups. Finally, the Goryeon students were entirely represented by half spirits and made up only a small fraction of the students. Of the two hundred or so disciples, only twenty of them were half-spirits.

Lee Jia supposed she couldn’t blame the other groups for being insular. Aside from Takeda Rika, Xin Wei, and Guan Yi, all of her friends were from her home country. Even then, Stoneface was a fairly distant friend and Xin barely qualified as an acquaintance.

Most disciples did not pursue Lee Jia’s path of omnidisciplinary practice, focusing instead on one or two disciplines. Outside of the Qin students and Lee Jia’s inner circle, almost nobody pursued spiritual arts due to the incredible difficulty of awakening. Conversely, the Qin students mostly boycotted the martial arts training, apparently still convinced that Ienaga was holding back some kind of secret.

After a month, all of Lee Jia’s friends had managed to awaken in all three disciplines. Even Xin Wei did, though he slacked off terribly in martial arts. She had been nervous at the idea of her friends using the awakening stone at first, but Eunae convinced her that as long as they understood the dangers, it was their decision to make. In the end, Eunae, Dae, and Takeda were all able to pass the trial of awakening.

Lee Jia ducked under a high kick from Kasai and swept the braided girl’s grounded leg out from under her. Kasai twisted impossibly in the air and managed to recover before hitting the ground, though she landed off balance.

Before Lee Jia could follow up on her unbalanced opponent, her ears twitched and she sensed Satou approaching from behind. She dodged his strike without looking and used his momentum to throw him into the recovering Kasai before backing off to regroup with Eui and avoid a two-on-one.

“Sorry about that, Jia. I tried to hold him off, but he’s slippery.”

“Don’t worry about it! Go left!”

Jia and Eui were already moving before she had even finished speaking, and by the time Satou and Kasai had completely recovered, they had been forced back to back by Jia and Eui’s pincer.

Jia engaged Satou in a flurry of blows that he had no choice but to parry, as his mobility was cut off by Eui engaging his partner in a mirror of his own situation right behind him. Neither Kasai nor Satou could break off for fear that their partner would be immediately overwhelmed.

Satou’s ability to parry was more than a match for Jia’s attacks, however, as he masterfully deflected every strike. Jia knew she wouldn’t be able to overwhelm Satou’s defense—this was not the first time they’d sparred, after all—but she also knew she didn’t have to. Once Eui started mixing her tail into her martial arts, her opponents always either forgot it was there or became hyper-vigilant against it. Kasai wasn’t out of the fight, but she went down long enough for Eui to tackle Satou from behind.

A qi burst was essentially just an uncontrolled explosion of qi that relied on the fact that one usually couldn’t harm themselves unintentionally with their own qi. As Satou slumped over unconscious, Lee Jia considered that since it was the spirit arts equivalent of throwing a good punch it was probably not cheating...probably.
