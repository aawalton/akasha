Ezra rolled his shoulders against the new armor he wore. He’d already taken time to get used to his original new breastplate and other pieces from Smith since he had been accepted into the prospective Knight squad. The difference between having the metal armor and the much lower-quality but less restrictive leather armor he’d worn before was stark, and he needed to take time to get used to the difference.

Now, with Chloe and Jormun’s encouragement, he’d taken as his prize from winning in the tournament an axe and new armor. What he’d worn before was just the breastplate, with pauldrons, gauntlets, and heavy trousers. It was more than protective enough, though there were obvious weaknesses in the stomach, parts of the upper body, and legs. What he’d taken as his prize was a set of half-plate armor, which covered his entire torso, with tassets that dangled down to continue to protect his lower body. Thick greaves protected his legs, and an improved helmet replaced his old one, with a visor that snapped down instead of just an open face.

The armor had the coat of arms of the Duke of Brayton on the left breast. A shorter half-cape hung from his shoulders, reaching just below his waist, with a fancy embroidered coat of arms on the back. Again, this much larger coat of arms was that of the Duke, showing Ezra’s apparent allegiance and loyalty.

“If you keep acting that way about it, I’m going to take the damn armor,” Telemachus joked, kicking Ezra’s foot to catch behind his other foot.

Ezra stumbled and glared at the other Adept, who raised both hands and laughed. His fire-affinity friend had taken quite a long time to finally decide what he was going to take himself as his prize, and had finally decided on a weapon over that of the improved armor.

Since it was a spear he had chosen, Telemachus walked with it in hand, the solid steel haft mostly hidden under straps of leather that were wrapped almost all the way up to the aural yooperlite spearhead. In truth, the general form of each of the weapons was very similar, with a solid metal haft and the aural yooperlite head. Of course, Ezra’s weapon was an axe and Telemachus’s a spear, but both weapons had a glittering edge to them, a faint orange glow that was hardly noticeable until aura was pushed into the weapon.

For now, neither of them were going to waste their inner world’s reserves with a fight coming, instead focusing on continuing on their path forward towards the extermination mission.

“You’re not gonna take my armor,” Ezra growled at the other man as he shoved him roughly.

Telemachus laughed as he stumbled to the side, his steps sure as he very obviously drew on his inner world’s reserves to keep himself from going far. He didn’t at all look bothered as he resumed marching, and the two men continued on their path.

Around them, the rest of the extermination force was marching in time, about a quarter of their numbers comprised of the members of the prospective Knights squad, and the rest made up of experienced career soldiers. Since it was a smaller but stronger group of goblinoids and Dragonkin, nobody who wasn’t at least an Adept was permitted to come. The other commoner trainees had complained about that, but the likelihood of there being several Dragonkin, a dozen orcs, and just as many bugbears had drained from them any overwhelming enthusiasm for throwing themselves into danger just yet.

They marched under the command of one of the Pseudo-Knights that Ezra had only really spoken to in passing. Michel was a man with a soft, gentle name. He himself was anything but. Under heavy armor, his shoulders were huge, massively broad, and his waist just as thick. Ezra was fairly certain that somebody could shove an entire barrel inside of his full-plate armor torso, and his voice was booming and commanding. Ezra had met him a couple times in passing during training, but he was, evidently, Dominick’s right hand man in terms of taking care of things outside of the walls of Brayton. As such, Michel didn’t frequently interact with the others or participate in training, instead constantly working outside of the city.

Every one of the trainees that had reached Adept was qualified to come on the expedition, though it would appear that some of the more politically connected trainees didn’t have to do anything the commanders said. At least one of them was, as Willem had neglected to “take the offer” to come. Michel had, when he learned as much, made a face so overwhelmed with disgust that Ezra wondered what kind of repercussions would be coming the way of the spoiled noble. He was looking forward to seeing it when they returned to the city.

The expedition carried on their way, quiet conversations filling the air as they marched. They left the northern gates of the city at dawn, and it was now well past midday, though they had recently slowed their pace quite a bit. Michel had given the warning that they would be encountering the enemy soon. He sent Basil, the other Pseudo-Knight accompanying the expedition, off on a quick scouting journey, just to make sure that they knew where the monsters were and what they numbered.

Less than an hour after Basil was sent out, he returned, his body language serious and maybe a little worried. As soon as he returned, his heavily camouflaged cloak hanging heavy behind him, Michel ordered the expedition to gather up and listen to the report.

“Four Dragonkin are leading the horde,” he immediately said. “Behind them are thirty orcs, at least forty five bugbears, and about a hundred random goblinoids. There’s something off about the Dragonkin. They aren’t arguing, but they don’t defer to any one of their number. Even so, they seem well coordinated. That combined with the fact that their numbers are well spread out would lead me to believe that they are under the command of an aberrant.”

The mention of an aberrant didn’t seem to be something noteworthy to the rest of the people standing around him, but Ezra had no idea what that meant. Michel nodded severely at that, waiting for the rest of the report from the scout.

“Otherwise, they’re moving in this direction and seem to have recently found some travelers.”

Ezra stiffened at that, and by the tone of Basil’s voice, the understanding that Ezra had that the goblinoids would leave only bones behind was correct. The reminder of the kinds of creatures they were fighting was unnecessary, but Ezra subconsciously pulled his axe out of where it hung on his waist and took it in his right hand. The leather creaked under the force of his grip, and, going off the shifting of the people around him, Ezra could tell that he wasn’t the only one who shared the hatred of the beasts.

“Then we know what to do,” Michel said, looking at the rest of the men and women around him. “Those of you who haven’t fought like this before, remember, everybody here is an Adept. Have a partner, maybe a small squad, but you’re not soldiers marching in formation. Stay together, keep each other safe, and keep communicating. I’m taking the front line. Basil, Alexander, and Liora are on overwatch. Don’t get separated, the battlefield shouldn’t spread too far. If the aberrant appears, Basil and I will take care of it. If there isn’t one, then Basil and I are still focusing on the Dragonkin. Now get in position, we only have a few minutes.”

All of the warriors around him quickly got into small groups of people that they knew and trusted. Ezra glanced at Telemachus, who had already stepped beside him, and then Artemis came forward. She didn’t say anything as she joined them, and neither of the two younger men disagreed with her inserting herself into the squad. Then, when they stood there quietly, she quickly took command.

“Stay together,” she said, hefting her shield and stepping forward. “Ezra, you and I are on the front, Telemachus between us. Your spear is long enough, you can stab at everything that is coming too close or is trying to come around us. We all stay near Richard’s squad. Keep an eye on each other’s flank.”

As she mentioned Richard, she gestured to another career soldier, a man who held just a spear, though he wore armor of about the same quality as Ezra himself. He nodded at Artemis, obviously having worked together quite a bit in the past. His own squad was a group of four, two men holding shields and a woman with a halberd.

“Your inner world is water affinity, right?” Telemachus verified as the two trainees turned their attention towards her.

“Yes,” Artemis nodded. “I’m primarily defensive with how I use it, so I’m planning on you and Ezra to be the two primary killers. I’ll focus on wounding, defending, and crippling. Any questions?”

Ezra shook his head, and Telemachus took a shaky breath. Then the three were ready, and under Michel’s command, were sent to set up a couple dozen meters away from where the commander himself stood. Nobody was in a squad with him, and he pulled a massive sword out from where it hung on his hip, his large steel shield already in his left hand. Then he stood entirely unmoving, his greatsword in one hand and his shield held at the ready.

Nearby, Ezra and Artemis stood together, their shields not quite in the ready position, though they both were looking forward as, though the extermination expedition members were all silent, sound started to swell in front of them. Chittering, shrieks, and inhuman growls filled the air as nearly two hundred monsters approached the squad of only thirty warriors. Only the greenest, the six trainees, seemed even a little nervous, and each of them had at least one veteran standing at their side.

Another handful of seconds passed, and Ezra went through his final movements to prepare himself as the first of the enemies made themselves known. A dozen goblins under the command of an orc with two bugbears swarmed forward. Ezra couldn’t help but remember that it was numbers rather similar to this that had killed Helen, and he felt his adrenaline spike. His hands shook on his axe and shield as his stomach twisted, but there was no more time for nervousness.

Four goblins led the charge, and they threw themselves with snarling faces at Ezra and Artemis. The human defenders’ shields smashed forward, holding back the cudgels and makeshift spears from the creatures. As they were slowed, Telemachus’s spear shot out and pierced right into one of the meter-tall beast’s throats. It fell, gurgling, as Artemis and Ezra both struck out with their own weapons. Ezra’s axe cleaved into and through another skull. Artemis was more precise, stabbing twice in quick succession. Each strike was a deadly blow to the target she struck.

Just as many goblins came forward, though now the bugbears and orc followed, close enough to start to menace the small squad. The goblins were barely distractions as the practiced, experienced fighters struck them down. Again, Artemis’s strikes were swift, each one deadly to the little green bastards.

Then the first bugbear drew close, its disturbingly long arms reaching to grab over Ezra’s shield. A goblin used that same opportunity to strike out at his ankles, and Ezra started to curse.

Telemachus’s spear jabbed forward, taking the goblin in the eye through the slightest gap in Ezra’s defense. Grinning, Ezra raised his shield and ducked low, smashing the edge of his shield into the 2-meter-tall goblinoid’s wrist. Bone didn’t break, but the jarring hit drove off the attack, and as the bugbear’s furry, somewhat bear-like face screwed up in pain, Ezra turned just enough to raise his axe up high and, with just a faint draw on his inner world, cut down.

Wind accelerated his strike as the yooperlite glowed, and the axe chopped the bugbear’s arm off just above the elbow. It screamed in rage and agony, seeming to try to decide whether to continue to attack or to retreat. Artemis stepped forward and stabbed it in the side, between the ribs and the hip, and it bent over. Fear flashed across its face, but as it bent, Ezra drew on his inner world again and accelerated the next attack he made. In an uppercut, he swung the weapon, and the blade of his axe glowed as the aural yooperlite reacted to the aura he infused it with.

The weapon flew up and lodged in the jaw of the monster. In that moment, Ezra was reminded why Telemachus had chosen the weapon instead of the armor as his own prize. The stone of his weapon amplified the effect of Ezra’s aura, and where the weapon lodged into the inhumanly dense bone, wind exploded out in every direction. In that single attack, the bugbear’s head burst in a gory expression of Ezra’s power.

He had no time to celebrate his victory, though, as the other bugbear and the orc arrived at that point. Richard’s voice rose beside them, and the two shield-bearing warriors crashed into the flank of the remaining goblins. Ezra and Artemis didn’t have to worry about the little beasts any longer, but the two large ones threw themselves forward. The orc carried a weapon that could loosely be called a sword, a thick hunk of metal with one edge sharpened to a blade while the bugbear’s claws menaced.

Artemis’s calm demeanor bled away as she roared a challenge and stepped forward towards the orc. It obliged, delivering a devastating overhead blow, but she raised her shield, water swirling on the surface and twisting the weapon as she accepted the attack. The orc’s arm jolted inward, its elbow smashing into its fat belly from the swirling water’s defense. The weapon thunked heavily into the wooden shield, and with that opening, Artemis’s blade chopped into the orc.

Her sword smashed heavily into the orc’s dominant arm, but wasn’t able to amputate it completely. The tendons that held its fingers closed were severed, and the blade fell from unfeeling arms as Ezra engaged the bugbear. From behind, a glowing spear shot forward and crashed into the orc’s chest. The muscle fought to resist the attack, but the yooperlite flared with a secondary explosion, which ruptured the monster’s rib cage. Its internals were exposed as Telemachus and Artemis dismembered it in a few more attacks.

Ezra drew more on his inner world, making sure he was more than fast enough to outpace the bugbear. Instead of enhancing his strikes, he trusted in the strength of his arm and the weight of his weapon, and in a few short seconds, he dismembered the bugbear’s attacks and then, with a full-body swing, decapitated the beast. It fell to the ground, and the three soldiers fell back into a closer stance, evaluating each other to make sure there were no injuries. Richard and his squad pulled a ways back, giving the three their own space again as the fight continued to rage across multiple fronts.

“I thought you said we would be the ones killing them?” Ezra joked as the three enjoyed the brief lull in the fight.

“Goblins don’t count,” she replied, not smiling, but her voice carrying a tinge of amusement. “As for the orcs, fuck them all.”

Knowing what had happened to her older sister, Ezra didn’t try to argue with that. He looked forward, another small group of goblins approaching, when Jormun’s voice distracted him.

The old ghost was commanding and demanding, but never worried. The tone of his voice was somehow terrified, confused, and overwhelmingly furious.

What in the ever-loving fuck is an infernal doing here?
