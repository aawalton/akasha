We stood near the entrance of the mine, staring into the dark passage below. The jagged opening descended into shadow, the tunnel mouth looking like a wound in the earth rather than a proper entrance. From where we stood, I could only see a few meters ahead before the darkness swallowed everything.

“Do you think there are more inside?” I asked.

Ja’a narrowed her eyes, focusing past the entrance.

“I am not sensing anything right now,” she said. Then she added, “That does not mean there is nothing in there, mind you. It only means nothing strong enough is standing out to me.”

That was not particularly reassuring. Vena shifted slightly beside me, her grip tightening on her claymore.

“Could we handle those monsters in this narrow terrain?” she asked.

Raik nodded, not looking concerned.

“Fighting them in tunnels would actually be to our advantage, as long as we maintain formation,” he said. “In open terrain, they could build momentum and attempt to bypass Shingo. In a confined space, they would have far less room to maneuver.”

“What if they come from behind?” I asked.

“That is why Katar and I will be taking the rear,” Raik replied. Then he glanced toward me. “And you, Alice, are upgraded to skull-shattering duty alongside Calr. Vena protects Ja’a and supports the team with healing.”

I gave him a flat look. “Lucky me. What a promotion.”

“It suits your spear,” Kan said with a small grin.

That was fair enough. After a brief final equipment check, we moved in.

Inside, the mine felt strange. At first glance, it looked like a normal tunnel leading deeper underground. The walls were rough stone, reinforced in places by old wooden beams, some partially rotted with age but still holding firm.

There was no natural light, but each of us had a luminous manastone attached to our belts. The light they produced was soft and steady, bright enough to illuminate our surroundings without being too harsh. Unlike torches or fire magic, the gemstones did not flicker or cast aggressive shadows, and more importantly, there was no risk of accidentally blinding each other whenever someone turned around.

As we descended further, I started noticing smaller holes branching off from the main passage. At first, I thought they were natural cave formations, but the more I looked, the less that explanation made sense.

They were too narrow; each side tunnel was only large enough for a single person to crouch-walk at best. They cut into the walls at strange angles, branching off in directions that felt almost random; a couple angled upward, some downward, while many continued horizontally into darkness. Together, they created an absurdly dense network of narrow passages spreading in every direction.

It reminds me of something… something very specific, though I can’t place it yet.

Then movement caught my eye as a skeleton stepped out from one of the narrow tunnels.

The entire group tensed immediately, ready for a fight.

This one was not too different from the undead we had fought before. Its bones were covered in patches of dirt and mineral dust, and instead of carrying a weapon, it had a full metal pickaxe fused directly into one arm. Strapped to its back was a large basket filled with chunks of raw ore.

The skeleton did not even acknowledge our presence. It simply stepped into the main tunnel and started walking in front of us as if we were not there.

Calr instinctively raised his crossbow. Before he could fire, Raik grabbed his wrist and gently pushed the weapon downward. With his other hand, he gestured for silence, then pointed toward the skeleton.

The order was simple. We would follow the monster.

Calr lowered the crossbow with visible reluctance.

We followed the mining skeleton deeper into the tunnel. As we walked, we passed two more skeletons coming from the opposite side. Both looked almost identical to the first, but their baskets were empty.

Like the first one, they completely ignored us. Each of them simply entered another side tunnel and disappeared into the darkness.

That was when the memory finally clicked. I had seen a similar mine structure before. I had even created a few myself.

Minecraft. I almost laughed out loud.

These skeletons were basically strip-mining. The narrow one-person tunnels, the weird branching angles, the obsessive search pattern.

It all suddenly made sense.

They were mining exactly like a player in a resource-gathering game, carving efficient, narrow tunnels toward nearby ore veins.

If my comparison was correct, then these undead could somehow detect nearby metal deposits. Or more accurately, whatever was controlling them could.

That realization was both fascinating and deeply unsettling. If they could sense metal, they could probably sense us, too.

We tightened our formation. The side tunnels were now the real concern.

They were too small for the spinning monsters we encountered outside, but still, if something with comparable speed were to emerge from one of them, we could be in trouble.

Everyone adjusted their positions accordingly, keeping their attention split between the front and the dozens of branching tunnels around us.

The atmosphere became significantly more tense until eventually, the skeleton we were following reached a set of large double doors.

The sight was so unexpected that we stopped walking.

The doors were made entirely of metal, heavy enough that I doubted we could move them easily. They looked almost absurd in the middle of an abandoned mine. Their surface was new and pristine compared to everything else we had encountered in the dead zone. They looked downright eerie.

The skeleton casually set down its full basket. Nearby, there was a stack of baskets lined neatly against the wall. At least fifty of them, around half of them, were full of ore.

The skeleton picked up an empty basket from the pile and turned around to head back toward the tunnels.

Only this time, it finally reacted to us. The moment it came within a few meters, something changed. Its posture stiffened. Then, without warning, it lunged. Apparently, proximity to us was enough to override whatever passive mining behavior it had been following.

Fortunately, it was far less dangerous than the constructs outside. Shingo stepped forward and crushed it with a single hammer strike. The skeleton collapsed instantly, bones scattering across the tunnel floor. The basket clattered beside it.

“That was anticlimactic,” I muttered.

“What did you expect?” Ja’a asked. “It was too weak to even register on my soulsight.”

Still, after the attack, we all braced ourselves.

I expected some kind of reaction from whatever was controlling them. Maybe a swarm of mining skeletons pouring from every side tunnel, or the doors opening with an army waiting behind them.

Nothing happened. The mine remained eerily quiet.

We slowly approached the metal doors. Up close, the doors looked even stranger, like something a noble would commission rather than something you would find in a mine. They were also slightly grayer than normal steel. The double doors were far too heavy to be practical.

Someone had wanted whatever was behind them sealed, or maybe protected.

Katar stepped closer and tapped his blade against the metal surface.

“This is bone steel,” he said. “Death-infused bone combined with pig iron to make steel.”

“While I cannot sense anything beyond it, that might be because of the magical material,” Ja’a said.

“There are no obvious locking mechanisms,” Calr noted thoughtfully.

Kan crouched beside the frame, examining the hinges.

“These were designed to withstand force,” she observed. “A lot of force.”

Calr adjusted his grip on his crossbow.

“So,” he asked, “do we open the ominous giant metal doors in the creepy undead mining complex?”

I stared at him.

“You make it sound like a terrible idea.”

“It probably is,” Ja’a said, her eyes, however, looked excited.

“But don’t you realize it?” She added, “If the door alone is made of magical metal, can you imagine what is inside?”

Raik looked over the group.

“Well,” he said. “Shingo, do the honors. Let’s find out what is inside.”

Shingo gave a small nod and stepped forward. He planted both hands against the heavy metal doors and pushed.

At first, the doors barely moved, groaning faintly as Shingo leaned his full weight into them. Muscles tensed beneath his plate armor as his massive frame forced them open, centimeter by centimeter.

Then suddenly, whatever resistance had been holding them gave way all at once. The doors lurched open far more easily than something that heavy had any right to.

The abrupt shift completely threw off Shingo’s balance. He stumbled forward, his momentum carrying him into the room. Without his shield or hammer, both of which he had set aside to open the doors, he dropped awkwardly onto one knee and threw out his hands to catch himself.

That was when two doom balls rushed him. They launched from inside the room with charged momentum, both spinning constructs heading straight toward the now-vulnerable giant.

Shingo reacted on instinct. Still half on the ground, he extended both gauntleted hands forward.

Fortunately for him, he was not alone.

Kan’s chains shot past us in a blur. The metal links wrapped around both constructs just enough to interfere with their momentum, slowing them by a fraction.

That was all Shingo needed. He caught each doom ball by one of its outer rings, one in each hand. The sheer force still pushed him backward, boots scraping against stone, but he held firm.

I jumped forward immediately. My spear came down in a clean thrust, shattering the skull inside the first construct. At nearly the same moment, Calr fired. His bolt pierced the second skull dead center.

Both constructs collapsed.

The pressure pushing against Shingo suddenly vanished. He exhaled heavily and tossed the inert metal frames aside.

The mute boy’s face said it all. That had been far too close. I looked past him into the chamber as he sat there panting.

The room beyond looked like a forge. Or maybe a smelting room.

The chamber was far larger than I expected, easily several times wider than the tunnel outside. Metal tables lined one side, while another metal door was positioned on the far wall. A large crucible sat near a massive furnace built into the back, glowing faintly with residual heat.

We were just about to step inside when Ja’a suddenly raised a hand.

“Stop.” Her voice was sharp enough that everyone froze.

“The whole forge is a monster,” she said.

We all turned toward her.

“What?” I asked.

“I can sense a large soul, around thirty SB, covering the whole room,” Ja’a said, staring into the chamber with narrowed eyes.

For a moment, we all stood there watching for what would happen next. I expected tentacles or metal chains to burst to life, or something similarly horrific.

Instead, one basket of ore started shaking, then tipped over. Loose chunks scattered across the floor before beginning to levitate. The metal slowly floated through the air toward the center of the chamber.

A few seconds later, the ore was dumped directly into the furnace. Then molten metal flowed outward through narrow channels like glowing blood. We watched in stunned silence as the liquid metal was shaped into rings.

Another doom ball frame. The newly formed rings were plunged into a quenching basin with a violent hiss of steam. Then bone fragments rose from a nearby pile as a skull assembled itself.

Finally, red mana flowed inward from all directions, gathering inside the construct like liquid light. The skull’s eyes began glowing.

Before the new doom ball could even finish activating, Calr shot it.

Crack.

The skull shattered instantly, and the unfinished construct collapsed.

I expected a new skull to start forming, but instead, the forge simply started over again. More ore floated into the furnace. More molten metal flowed. A second construct began assembling from scratch.

“This thing does not seem very smart,” Katar observed.

I stared at him.

“What do you mean? It literally baited us with the door. Do not underestimate it.”

Katar just shrugged.

“No, Katar is right. This thing is limited in what it can do,” Calr said. “I think.”

Raik stepped forward slightly, studying the chamber.

“Do you know what it is and how we destroy it?”

Calr’s eyes fogged over, the telltale sign of him using his Analyse power.

“It is called a spawner,” he said. “At least, it fits the description. It is basically a golem that creates other golems.”

“We can probably destroy it if we find its core,” he continued. “It should have something similar to a golem core hidden somewhere inside.”

“It also shouldn’t have a way to directly attack us, only create and control monsters.” He paused for a second. “At least the ones in the abandoned forge of Nazaklam don’t attack directly.”

As we stepped fully into the chamber, that was when the sound of clacking bones came from behind us. I turned sharply toward the tunnel entrance, only to find that every little hole in the tunnel was now spewing mining skeletons, all of them rushing toward us.

“Well,” I muttered. “That is inconvenient.”

Katar, Vena, and Shingo immediately moved to hold the entrance.

The doorway was narrow enough that only a few skeletons could approach at once, which thankfully turned the endless tide into a manageable bottleneck.

Shingo blocked most of the doorway with his frame and smashed skeletons apart with brutal efficiency.

Katar was helping by dispatching those who tried to slip past the giant.

Vena stood just behind them, periodically raising her free hand in prayer whenever too many monsters gathered near the door. Her Holy miracle was as effective as ever.

The swarm was not a real threat, especially with Vena on our side.

Yet somehow, it still felt like we were falling deeper into a trap.

Naturally, since Raik was resistant to heat, he was the obvious choice to approach the furnace. Kan and I followed close behind as backup, helping search the chamber for anything resembling a core or a control mechanism.

The closer we got, the hotter the room became, and sweat began dripping from my forehead almost instantly.

And that is when the real trap was sprung, the metal doors slammed shut behind us, and the sound echoed through the chamber like a prison gate locking.

Damn it! I knew those skeletons weren’t a real threat; they were only a tool to push us deeper inside, just far enough for the entrance to be sealed. And we fell for it!

To add insult to injury, the furnace roared louder as the temperature immediately began rising.

“Not good,” Kan said.

“We need to find the core fast!” Calr shouted.

“Katar, Shingo, work on opening the door!” Raik called. “The rest of you, find and break the core!”

Meanwhile, the team leader pushed deeper toward the furnace itself, unbothered by the rising temperature. If the core was inside the fire, he would find it.

I started looking around the room, just in case it was hiding in plain sight.

The chamber kept heating intentionally as the forge itself tried to cook us alive.

The heat became unbearable within seconds as breathing started to hurt, sweat drenched my clothes, and my skin stung.

Vena and Calr looked miserable, and even Kan, who had some fire adaptation, was visibly straining by now.

Only Raik looked unaffected.

Out of desperation, I started throwing lightning around the chamber almost at random, hoping to hit something important. Blue arcs bounced across metal surfaces, filling the room with flashes of light.

A burst of healing from Vena swept over me, helping me think more clearly.

I activated my Perception Soulbook and tried to locate the core. It should be made of metal, so maybe I could find it that way.

Everything here is made of metal; this is useless.

I could teleport away, taking Vena or Ja’a with me, then come back for the others.

No, that would take too long. I don't have enough star mana, and recharging in the sunless reach will take far too long.

So I decided to try something stupid.

Holding my spear, I extracted some star mana, closed my eyes, and wished.

“I wish I were safely on top of the core.”

I blinked and suddenly appeared on top of the pile of bones where the doom balls’ skulls were being created.

I slipped immediately, sending bones scattering everywhere.

That was when I found the core; it was a skull made entirely of metal with glowing red eyes.

“Here!” I shouted, trying to stand up from the awkward position I had landed in.

Lucky for me, I wasn’t alone, and Kan was able to capitalize on my reckless action. Her chain wrapped around the metal skull, and the morningstar head on the other end began bludgeoning it repeatedly until the red eyes finally went dark.

“You destroyed it,” Ja’a said. “I can feel its aura dissipating.”

“The heat is not dying down,” called Raik, who was still standing near the furnace. “Is the door nearly open?”

“Not... yet,” Katar replied through gritted teeth.

I could barely remain conscious. My teleportation had placed me even deeper into the forge, making the heat affect me far more strongly. I was about to teleport away again, this time outside this hellhole, when something unexpected happened.

I watched Raik inhale deeply, like he was holding his breath before plunging underwater, and the heat reacted to it, slowly decreasing the more breath he gulped.

One moment, I was being boiled alive, then the next, I was freezing.

The room had actually gotten cold.

Cold enough that I could see my breath… I watched with wide eyes as frost spread across the edges of the chamber walls, and thin ice formed over metal surfaces.

He finally closed his mouth before it would have gotten unbearable.

It was still uncomfortable, especially since the thermal shock nearly gave me a heart attack.

We all blinked, completely stunned at the new development. Raik stood at the center of it all, looking just as surprised as the rest of us.

Then he let out a soft exhale, and warmth slowly began returning to the room, radiating off his body in controlled waves and bringing the temperature back to something manageable.

“Took you long enough,” Ja’a said between breaths.

Kan immediately ran toward him and hugged him.

Vena, on the other hand, ran toward me and started healing me, since I had gotten the worst of it. My side was raw and pulsing with residual heat.

Raik took slow breaths, each one steaming; he looked mildly uncomfortable, as if he still had excess heat inside of him.

He approached me carefully, checking on my condition.

“What changed?” I asked, smiling at him to showcase that I was doing fine.

He rubbed the back of his neck awkwardly.

“Well,” he said, “after your explanation about heat and cold...”

I narrowed my eyes on him.

“What did you do?”

“You said I cannot create cold,” Raik explained. “Only move heat.”

He shrugged.

“So I did. I moved it.”

I blinked..

“I ate it,” he looked a little smug when he said it, “If I can convert mana into heat, then theoretically I should be able to reverse the process and convert heat into mana.”

I stared at him.

This is not how it is supposed to work... was it? You don’t turn calories back to food…

“Did it work fully?” I asked.

“Somewhat,” Raik admitted. “My mana reservoir is slowly being replenished, but Most of the heat is still trapped inside me. I am basically keeping it from escaping and turning the room into a furnace again.”

“It is... somewhat uncomfortable.” His breath was visibly steamy and hot as he spoke.

Ja’a poked his arm.

“Why did you not try it sooner?”

“If the monster were still alive, it would have contested my authority over the heat,” he replied. “If you guys had not killed it, it would not have worked. Its soul was stronger than mine after all.”

“Makes sense,” I said as Vena finished healing me. I was still a bit red, but no longer in pain.

“More importantly, do you sense another monster?” Raik asked Ja’a.

She shook her head.

“Then let’s secure our exit before looting the place.”
