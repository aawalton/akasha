Jade regained consciousness with a splitting headache. She opened her eyes slowly, blinking against a bright light that sent lances of pain through her temples. Groaning, she rolled onto her side, clutching her head in her hands. What was happening? Where was she? The floor beneath her was cold, hard stone. It was far from comfortable, but the cool surface was a balm to the burning heat of her skin. There was a sizzling fire inside her, radiating through her body. It wasn't the soothing warmth of a healing spell, though. No, this was more like she was standing too close to an open furnace.

"Urrghhhh…" She groaned again, shifting on the floor.

"Hold still." A calm, melodious voice sounded nearby. "Jade, you're hurt. Please stop moving so much."

It was only then that Jade noticed sound. There was sound around her, lots of it. The footsteps of people moving around, indistinct conversation, and the crackling hum of the Core crystal. Just as Jade decided she was ready to risk opening her eyes again she heard a familiar incantation. Wondrous healing energy flowed through her body, dulling her many aches and making the uncomfortable heat recede. She sighed, some of the tension in her body easing. Feeling somewhat better now, Jade was successful in keeping her eyes open long enough to see a familiar face looking down at her.

"Hey, Siora…" She said… or rather croaked. Her throat felt scratchy and dry, like she hadn't had anything to drink in more than a day. "What… what happened?"

Jade searched her memories as she asked the question. She recalled the battle against the boss, and the heart-wrenching order to retreat. She'd stayed behind to distract Seraphine, planning to escape once her friends were safe, but it hadn't worked. The boss had cut her off, forcing her to fight alone against an overwhelming force. She'd somehow managed to defeat it, and then… The last thing Jade remembered was bending down to drain the angel, practically glowing with the thrill of triumph and anticipation of feeding. That last part sent a shudder of revulsion down Jade's spine. She'd let her inner demon take control, given free rein to the dark instincts her transformation had implanted in her mind. Whatever happened to her next seemed to have banished them for the moment, but the memory lingered. It left her disturbed.

"The usual, apparently. Fenrin told us that you killed the boss. So, you did something crazy and somehow it worked." Siora said, her tone exasperated. The elf pushed Jade back down as she tried to sit up, silencing her with a raised finger and casting another healing spell. "Except this time, you had to go and one up yourself, didn't you. You drained the angel. Jade, didn't you stop to think before filling yourself with incandescent essence? I've seen what it does to you! And that's just when you get hit with a spell or something." Siora sighed, shaking her head. "You're lucky to be alive."

"There wasn't much time to think." Jade said with a grimace that suddenly eased into a groan of relief as Siora's healing magic took effect. "I was trapped with a boss. It was all… instinct."

The elven alchemist gave her a brief, uneasy look before finishing her work, extending a hand to help Jade to her feet.

"We're just happy you're okay." The elf relented, giving her hand a squeeze. "When we realized that you'd been left behind…"

Jade didn't need her succubus senses to read the guilt in Siora's expression. She lay a hand on the elf's shoulders, doing her best to conjure up a reassuring smile.

"Everything happened so fast. It's not anyone's fault. If it wasn't for Fenrin…" She trailed off, looking around the cathedral. The vast space was now mostly empty, without any monsters in sight. Aylin and Tara were over near the Core crystal bent over a complicated magical diagram, and Aki and Luis were standing at the main entrance. She didn't see anyone else. A sudden spike of concern gnawed at her stomach. "Everyone made it through, right?"

"What? Oh, yes. Yes!" Siora said, putting away her alchemy pouch and looking flustered. "Sorry, I should have led with that. It's just… I'm not used to this. I know I'm the one who insisted on coming along, but this was… more than I was expecting."

"None of us knew what we were walking into." Jade said. She took a few tentative steps while holding Siora's proffered arm. It didn't take long for Jade to stop, concluding she was better off waiting for the world to stop spinning before trying again. "Where are all the monsters?" She asked, leaning against one of the large, cracked columns.

"They stopped spawning once the boss died, thank the goddess. I don't know what would have happened otherwise. I don't think Naomi and Luis would have left you. I don't think I could have either."

The sincerity emanating from Siora's mind was touching in a way Jade had rarely experienced before. To have someone tell you that they would put their life on the line to help you was one thing, but to feel the truth of it from their very soul was another thing entirely. The part of her that held her demonic desires recoiled from that trust, but Jade didn't care. That part of her was back in its place, locked up in the dark corners of her mind. Even sealed away, though, the thoughts and feelings she'd experienced while under its influence lingered like a bitter aftertaste.

"Thank you, Siora." Jade said, trying to push those feelings aside. "I couldn't ask for better friends and companions."

The elf gave her a tired smile and finished checking her over. Once Siora was satisfied that Jade wasn't about to keel over, she went off to continue looking over the rest of their party. Most of the others were still scouting the corespace, ensuring that there weren't any further surprises and gathering loot from fallen monsters. Jade slid down the pillar to sit against it, deciding to take another few minutes to recover. The blinking indicator of several stacked notifications tugged at her attention. A quick glance confirmed none required immediate attention, so she pushed them aside for the moment. She needed a minute away from all of this. Demons, dimensional disasters, monsters, and just the chaos of the Labyrinth in general.

Once Jade felt strong enough, she pulled herself to her feet and strode over to find out what progress Aylin and Tara were making. As she approached the massive, glowing gemstone, Jade was reminded of her conversation with the Ebon Eye agent she'd met with in Illysport. According to him, there was likely a potent relic hidden within the faceted surface of this font of power. A dimensional anchor, which his organization had tasked her with retrieving in exchange for aiding her people. Jade still hadn't decided what to do with the information. She knew next to nothing about the mysterious organization, but the manner in which they'd connected with her, as well as their association with the corrupt nobleman Verres Ledkin, led her certain conclusions.

Whatever their nature, though, Earth needed all the help it could get. Nothing Jade sensed in their meeting led her to believe she was being played. But even if she did want to hand the device over to them, she first had to retrieve it. Doing so without Aylin, a studied expert on the nature of the Labyrinth, noticing would be challenging. That, and it would be like a slap in the face after all the mage had done to help them.

She didn't have to be here. Jade thought, watching as the dark-haired half-elf made a small adjustment to the chalk diagram, she and Tara had drawn beneath the Core crystal. Despite the chaos and danger of the day's events Aylin remained calm and composed, as if the desperate battle had been little more than a minor inconvenience getting in the way of her research. Some of it was a front, but that didn't diminish its value. Right now, their group needed a reassuring presence. Making up her mind, Jade approached them.

"Aylin, can you spare a moment?"

"What? Oh, Jade. Yes." The mage stood, brushing chalk dust from her robes. It must have been from habit, because Aylin's clothes were covered in almost as much mud, blood, and sweat as everyone else's. "We've safely discharged most of the aberrant dimensional energy from the Core.""We'll be able to destroy it safely, then?"

"We won't need to, as it turns out. Tara pointed out that we can just leave the ritual active. It will slowly drain all of the Core's energy over the next hour or so. It's set up to draw out dimensional essence, and that's primarily what Labyrinth Cores channel. The problem here was just that there was too much of it. It will shut down soon after."

"Tara suggested that? I thought she'd have wanted another explosion.""I'm right here, Jade." The tek'kalla rolled her eyes. "Giant explosions aren't the only thing I think about.""I think we've all had our fill of excitement today." Aylin said, smiling slightly."I won't argue with that." Jade nodded.

"So, what did you need?"

Jade hesitated for a moment. How could she know this would lead to the best outcome? The obvious answer was that she couldn't. No one could know that. All she could do was take the path that felt right, and trusting Aylin was certainly that. The demonic side of her, not entirely dormant after so recently being allowed to take over, stirred at those thoughts. Honor and trust went against its nature. Jade pushed those feelings back hard. She had no intention of letting that part of her loose again.

"When we were in the city, I was approached by one of the men that was on that expedition of yours. He said he's part of a group called the Ebon Eye…"

It only took a couple of minutes to recount the details to Aylin, who looked troubled. When she was done, the mage didn't respond immediately.

"The Ebon Eye…" She mused, pacing back and forth in front of Jade. Behind her, Tara continued to monitor the chalk diagram drawn on the cathedral floor. A thin stream of blue energy connected into the large Core crystal, flowing from it to the ground. "What could they hope to gain from this? This whole region of the Labyrinth is in crisis. The Duke may turn a blind eye to some of their activities, but he wouldn't stand for them possessing a critical war asset if the demons do manage to invade."

"You don't seem surprised to hear about the dimensional anchor.""I'm one of the region's top experts on the Labyrinth." Aylin said with a wry smile. "It'd be strange if I didn't know as much as the Ebon Eye about Cores. They probably learned about them from one of my research papers.""Were you going to mention them?" Jade asked, trying to keep the annoyance from her voice. "It sounds like they're important.""If we found one, yes. I didn't want to distract anyone from the battle. Taking on a Core boss with only two parties is hard enough if everything goes well." Aylin panned her gaze around the room, looking over the carnage left over from their desperate battle. "Let alone when you find yourself in this kind of madness."Jade sensed nothing but sincerity in the mage's words. Mollified, she simply nodded her understanding.

"In any case." Aylin continued, looking up at the Core. "This one doesn't have anything I'm interested in."

"There isn't one of those anchors inside?"

"Oh, I didn't say that." Aylin smiled, her eyes glinting. "And as it turns out, I'd be happy to help facilitate its extraction. We wouldn't want your people to miss out on the generous deal they've been offered."

"Well, that's not suspicious at all." Jade muttered. "What are you planning?"

"Something that benefits everybody. For the most part."

Aylin explained her idea to Jade, and then to Naomi and Luis when they came over to check on her. Luis was immediately on board, although Naomi and Jade took a little more convincing. Naomi was too kind to be entirely comfortable with anything sneaky, and Jade was simply paranoid of unintended consequences. Naomi was swayed after Aylin told them more about the Ebon Eye as an organization. As Jade had surmised, they were something between a mercenary guild, an information brokerage, and a criminal enterprise. Too useful to be fully cracked down on, they leveraged their more legitimate dealings to generate the influence they needed to get away with less savory operations. For her part, Aylin's reassurances were enough to convince Jade. After all, the Labyrinth researcher's plans had a better track record than her own.

"Alright, I'm in." Jade said, once Aylin was finished. "How soon can we get started."

"Once the Core is mostly depleted, we can modify the ritual to extract it." Aylin said, gesturing to the still-active diagram. "That tek'kalla mage is quite talented, by the way. I wasn't sure what to expect, many of their people aren't interested in magical theory, but she's quite advanced for her age."

"We got off to a bit of a rocky start, but we're glad to have her. We wouldn't have been able to stop the demons in the last corespace without her."

"I'm still right here, you know." Tara muttered, still monitoring the ritual diagram. She turned away, grumbling something under her breath, but Jade could sense the relief radiating from her. That was something Jade understood. She too had spent a long time searching for a place to belong. Finally finding it in a monster-filled interdimensional dungeon had come as a surprise, but the friends she had around her made the hardships worth it. With that in mind, there was something she had to do.

"Where's Fenrin?"

"Out with Parker and Urgraan, making sure there aren't any more monsters." Luis said. "Why?"

"I need to thank him." Jade looked up at the high ceiling of the cathedral. Several of the rafters were blackened and charred, and a couple were broken entirely. Splintered beams and fragments of wood littered the floor, adding to the carnage. "He's the only reason I made it out of this."

"He told us what happened." Naomi said, touching her arm. "We all want to thank you, Jade."

"I thought you'd be upset. I went and did something reckless again, and it almost got me killed."

"There's a difference." Naomi shook her head. "This time, you took a risk to protect us. When things went bad, you could have been the first to get away."

Jade looked away, embarrassed.

"I couldn't do that."

"We know, chica." Luis clapped her on the shoulder. "That's why we're thanking you."

She nodded, some of the tension in her shoulders finally easing. Nearby, the light from the Core began to diminish, more and more of its magic draining away. They had done it. Made it over another hurdle. Jade shared the relief and triumph she felt from her friends but knew it wouldn't last. It was a miracle they'd emerged without casualties, and there were more dangers ahead. It couldn't be this close again. But that was a topic for tomorrow. Today, what they needed was to rest and celebrate. Jade grinned, watching Tara start to try and explain dimensional physics to Luis and Naomi, gesturing enthusiastically at different parts of the diagram.

This group really is coming together.

It was a good feeling. Excusing herself, Jade left in search of the missing member of their team.
