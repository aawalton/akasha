After a half-hour of Renner throwing daggers at Jareth- with steadily increasing glee, I noticed wryly- both men sheathed their weapons. Early evening light cast the arena in a warm glow as Renner turned towards me, cocked his head to one side, and announced, “Your turn.”

I started. “My… what?”

“Your turn, ladyship.”

“You’re going to throw knives at me?”

He chuckled, grabbing one of the wooden swords. “Not just yet. Draw.”

My fists curled with nervous uncertainty. Once upon a time, I’d thought myself quite capable of wielding a blade. Durst had taught me the stances and footwork of basic swordplay; I knew how to hold a guard, how to shift my balance from positions of attack and defense, and how to parry.

He’d taught me all that… with practice weapons. With a set, steady rhythm.

I’d recently been hit, quite like a hammer, by the knowledge that I’d learned how to ‘fight’ against a man with a gentle heart who had never once had it in him to actually try and harm me.

At my hesitation, he gave a sly grin. “First time? I’ll be gentle.”

Teela cackled. Jareth paced over to join her at the fence with a broad smile plastered across his face.

I gave Renner a withering glare, reaching for one of the shorter, narrower practice swords. It looked similar to the weapon he’d gifted me. “It’s not my first time, I just-”

“Good.” And with that, he was on me.

I blocked the blow with a startled shriek, jumping back. Both hands went white around the pommel. “Wait! Give me a moment to-”

My arms trembled with the force of the next hit as it sent my sword wide. An instant later, flat wood thumped lightly against my ribs. “And you’re dead.”

I fumed. “You’re supposed to wait! Adopt the appropriate stance and-”

He tapped me on the thigh. “Dead again.”

“Renner!” I swung back. It was a heavy, graceless blow and he swiped it away effortlessly.

Wood prodded my ribs. “And again.”

“You’re supposed to-”

“What? Wait ‘til you’re ready?” A thrust poked in, tapping past my attempt to deflect and grazing one hip. “Dead.”

I swiped crosswise. He calmly stepped back. “I haven’t done this in years! Give me a moment to remember-”

“No. No dropping your guard in a fight.” He slashed twice, two diagonals coming down from above. I somehow managed to block the first, then jerked sideways from the seconds. He nodded, advancing. “There. See? You know at least some of this.”

“I know I’m annoyed.” It was true, but so was his claim. The rush of adrenaline was pushing my instincts into memory. My feet knew how to slide along the dirt, my knees knew how to bend for balance, my shoulders knew how to brace against a hit. My coordination and speed were lacking, not to mention my strength, but the steps were coming back.

He gave me a lazy smirk, parrying my next hit and sliding to one side. Blunted wood tapped my stomach. “Annoyed and dead.”

I lunged, swinging wildly. “Would you stop-!”

We continued for quite some time, with me pointedly ignoring the two figures guffawing from the side. I never landed a hit and felt quite like an uncoordinated kitten trying to fight an unconcerned dog.

“Isn’t the point of sparring to help me improve?” I snapped when he finally relented for a moment. I leaned against the fence, posts digging into my lower back as I panted and swiped damp hair from my forehead. “As in, teaching me instead of… whatever this is?”

“Best way to improve is to practice.” Renner chuckled from a few paces away. He wasn’t even breathing particularly hard. The shadows of nearby firs cast the planes of his face into a very flattering light.

Both only added to my consternation.

“Besides, you took a nap earlier. The point is to wear you out.”

“Surely there’s more fun ways of doing that.” I rolled my eyes, then caught the twitch of his lips and flushed beet-red. “Not… oh, you know what I-”

“I’m open to suggestions.” The step he took towards me seemed to cover a very weighty amount of distance. “You have something in mind?”

“No.”

Another step.

I attempted to level a fierce glare, but this faltered upon seeing just how unfairly perfectly the sunlight was highlighting his hair.

“You’re a bad liar.”

My eyes narrowed to slits. No dropping your guard in a fight, huh? Hmm… I drew a deep breath. “Well… okay. Maybe I had a… a thought.”

“Yeah? About?” Another step. He was practically on top of me, and the way his voice lowered sent a jolt straight down my spine.

“Um…” I swallowed and, for a long moment, couldn’t seem to think of anything aside from the fact that he smelled like leather and fir trees. Get it together. He’s the one who’s supposed to be distracted, not me! Say something… suggestive. Surely I can do that. He does it all the time, it can’t be that hard. “Er… y-you?”

Salt. That’s hardly suggestive.

The way his brows rose seemed to indicate otherwise. Leather creaked as one hand slid beside my waist, settling against the fence. “What about me?”

I couldn’t seem to breathe. He was so close I could make out the shade of each individual hair along his jawline. “W-well… I think… that is…”

“Yeah?”

“I think you… f-forgot… your own… advice.”

“You…” He cocked his head to one side. “Huh?”

My sword collided with his ribs. This earned a surprised grunt, and I flashed him a triumphant grin. “Hah! Serves you right.”

Soft, husky laughter made my pulse flutter. “And here I was,” he very deliberately set his sword down, then shifted so his other hand settled along the fence beside my waist, “being nice. And you decide to try and maim me.”

“You’ll live.” I rolled my eyes, trying to sound tart even while my world was quickly narrowing to wickedly gleaming copper and a pair of very nice shoulders. “Also, you? Nice? I’ll believe it when I see it.”

“Oh? Just say the word, ladyship.” He smirked. Warm breath washed across my lips as his hips shifted forwards, grazing mine. “I can think of a few nice things I’d like to do with you.”

My mind went utterly blank. My face burned and my sword thumped to the dirt and I stared at him, fully at a loss for an answer, half of me silently cursing the unfairness of how impossibly good he was at flustering me and the other half wildly imaging some very nice things that we could-

The spell was broken when, realizing with a flood of horror that we had an audience for this interaction, I peeked over his shoulder and caught sight of Teela. She stood turned mostly away from us, hat on the ground and both hands gripping a wooden crossbow. Jareth stood behind her, positioned very closely, with his hands practically covering hers and his feet braced outside her own. He appeared to be showing her how to load and crank the weapon.

Which, I knew from experience, she definitely already knew how to do.

I clapped a hand over my mouth, stifling a giggle, and nudged Renner.

It took him a very moment to drag his attention off me, but eventually he glanced behind. We watched as Teela gradually became impatient with the paladin’s calm instructions. She grinned, stepped free from their intimate position, and raised the crossbow. The bolt left with a hiss and hit a hanging target almost dead-center with a hearty thwack.

Jareth blinked, eyeing the target in obvious surprise, as she chirped a smug, “Told you.”

“He’s…” I hesitated, wanting to choose my words carefully, and watched Renner’s face. “A good man?”

“Good?” He snorted. “He’s full of himself, thinks he’s invincible, and can’t look away from a pretty face.”

“I meant-”

“Perfect gentlemen.”

“Ah. Good.” My chest swelled as I watched the Fae-touched woman eagerly reload her weapon. After a moment’s thought I added, very gently, “You’re worried about him.”

Renner gave his head a slow shake. “He’s a good man, but he’s also a half-wit. Doesn’t think beyond the moment. Never has, and now…” His lip curled and he turned, shoulders stiff, putting his back to the pair.

“Why is it so dangerous to be blessed early?” He didn’t answer right away and I tentatively pressed, “You said something about dreams earlier. And… and…”

“And all kinds of ugly things.” He braced both forearms against the fence, scowling at nothing in particular. “Never seen it myself; hasn’t been a problem in decades. Centuries, even. The Order has a real strict rule about no one under thirty receiving Thalessa’s blessing.”

“And… I assume that rule was put in place for a reason?”

His jaw tightened.

“Something… something bad happens to them? To the younger paladins?”

“Not one thing. Lots of them. Waking nightmares, visions of things that aren’t there, wounds that don’t bleed and don’t heal right. Staring at the sun for hours on end, forgetting how to speak. And… salt, ladyship. Gnawing pieces of themselves off. Eating their own flesh.” He looked away.

A thick knot was quickly forming in my belly. I eyed Jareth in horror. “Ashes. That can’t… surely it’s just stories? That all can’t be real! Why would a goddess’ blessing hurt people?”

The anger radiating off of my companion was nearly palpable, but before either of us could continue the barracks door swung open.

Royce stood in the entrance, expression unreadable. His hair had reached a familiar, not-often-seen level of tousled; a manic blonde fray that indicated he’d been running his hands through it for a good hour, if not more.

He paced towards me. “Hey. I took a walk.”

“And?”

Broad shoulders swelled in an explosive sigh. “And I owe you an apology.”

“Royce, you don’t. You’re allowed to be angry at me. Saints, you should be angry at me! I… I should’ve talked to you earlier. About the…” my gaze cut towards Jareth. He was at the other end of the arena, playfully deflecting on onslaught of wild slashes from Teela. She had picked up a wooden battleaxe and had clearly never held such a weapon before in her life. The pair were laughing and paying us no mind. “The plan.”

Royce rubbed the back of his neck. “Which changed, apparently. Between last night and today.”

“And again earlier.” I winced, then dropped my voice further, leaning in and peering earnestly into his weary blue eyes. “Look, I’ll start with last night. Renner told me the Thalessians will-”

The door swung open with a creak. I snapped my jaw shut as an unfamiliar man stepped from the entryway.

Crow’s feet lined the corners of sharp brown eyes and silver-gray hair threaded the edges of his temples. His jaw was narrow and darkened by thick, nearly-black stubble through which thin lips slashed a harsh red line. A white scar laced diagonally through one eyebrow and another, longer line traced from beneath one ear to the bottom of his collarbone. He wore plain, unremarkable clothes, save for the emblazoned white cloak clasped at his throat.

The figure swept his gaze across the five of us. Then, both hands clasped behind his back, said, “I see dallying with civilians takes precedence over duty. Has the safety of your fellows lost its weight, Sir Jareth?”

His voice held a slight, clipped accent, almost as if he was biting off each vowel. The ‘sir’ had the distinct edge of a leveled blade

The young paladin turned away from Teela, who had hurriedly retrieved her hat, and snapped a salute. “Just following orders, sir. Dame Joselia bade me stay behind and wait for signal smoke. I’m keeping an eye on this group.”

“Ah. Your comrades from earlier. I heard the tale.”

The man stepped out. He was very thin and not particularly tall or imposing, but the simple movement held an odd weight to it. He looked at me for a long while, then at Teela. “Which lady was present at Fellbrook?”

“That would be me, sir. Brin Damelle.”

He examined me for a bit longer, then inclined his head. “I’ll hear your account. Inside.”

I started. “My account?”

“She’s already given it.” Renner’s voice was grim. He moved to stand just behind me. “In full. Joselia heard-”

“You will address your betters as befits their station. And yours.” The piercing gaze stayed fixed on me. “Dame Joselia agreed there were some… oddities to be accounted for.”

I dug my heels into the dirt, fighting a flinch. “I… oddities?”

He gave me a thin-lipped smile. The expression reminded me of a skull’s soulless grin. Teeth bared, but with empty eyes above. “Have no fear, my lady. I only have a few questions. And I’m quite ready for a looser tongue. Please.” He held a black-gloved hand out, indicating the doorway. The fabric around his knuckles caught a dwindling gleam of sunlight and shone with familiar, gut-clenching crimson.

Renner breathed an oath, so soft it was barely a whisper of air against the nape of my neck, then took a step forwards.

The man finally spared him a glance. “Alone.”
