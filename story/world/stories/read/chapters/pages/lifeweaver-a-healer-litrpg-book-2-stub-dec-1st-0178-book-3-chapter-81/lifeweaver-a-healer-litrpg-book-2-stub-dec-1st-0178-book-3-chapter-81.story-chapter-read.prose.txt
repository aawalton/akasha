Splinter was the only other person who heard Luke's comment, and the ratkin glanced over at the portal, his whiskers twitching. "Where does it go, Lifeweaver?"

Sighing, Luke got to his feet and touched the portal. Nothing happened. "Nowhere. That's the problem."

"You've drained the mana of every Lifeweaver in this room for nothing?" Cage asked, his voice full of ill-contained frustration and, Luke thought, hints of fear.

"I opened a portal," Luke said. "Step one is complete. Now we just need a way to connect it to our worlds!"

He nodded to himself. That was it. Through it all, he'd kept hold of the system weave and saw it twist and turn around the open portal, as if it were a living thing that did not like how Luke had used its threads. Even in that chaos, he saw what was missing.

"It's the thread," he muttered.

The portals he'd seen in this state all had one thing in common: a thread connected them to something, to a location. That was beyond Luke. He ignored the raised voices and shouting, at least somewhat confident they wouldn't attack him, not after they'd seen what he was capable of. So, since connecting the portal to a place was out of the question, what then was the answer? There had to be one. No dungeon would be constructed with the sole purpose of keeping the Integrated locked up like in a prison. Would it?

No, there was an answer in here somewhere. If this dungeon wasn't cut off, he might have been able to reach out through The Greater System Weave and reach Earth and the echoes therein, but he'd tried that once before, and it hadn't worked. This place had no connection to Luke's system. He blinked. Wait. Something had happened that might have changed that.

Reaching out through the dungeon weave, Luke grinned. There it was. Ray's thread glimmered in the darkness, running off into the infinite distance beyond. Several others appeared as well, the human Integrated he had healed in this dungeon. That was why they appeared, to give Luke a way home. This also settled the question of whether it was really Ray or not. It was. Also, since his thread now appeared, Luke had resurrected his friend and given him a second chance at life.

Unable to stifle a small chuckle at how he would lord that over his friend for all eternity, Luke looked up, considering the other Lifeweavers in the room. They posed a problem. With threads to use in connecting himself to Earth, Luke was sure he would be able to get the portal working, but he was not about to take everyone else with him. They needed threads of their own to follow.

"Listen up," Luke said, raising a hand to quiet the babble of voices. "I know what to do. Weaver's Echo. Raise a hand, everyone who has the skill."

About half of them raised their hands. That would be a problem. "Those of you who have the skill, have you also healed someone from your system?"

All hands remained in the air. That was something, at least.

"Good. You guys and everyone from their systems go over there," Luke said, pointing to the far wall. "Everyone else, come with me into the corridor."

The two Singers followed his instructions, along with a large group of others. Most of those who didn't have the skill at least had someone from their own system who did, which was good enough. Dot met Luke's eyes, and he sighed, nodding for her to join those by the wall. Their last interaction would be him helping her home. After that, they were through.

Following that, he waved for the rest of the Lifeweavers to go back into the corridor. Once the remaining six gathered around him, Luke explained the situation.

"If you don't have Weaver's Echo, you need to get it. To get home, at least one Lifeweaver from your system must have the skill. If you have a point available, get the skill. If not, get back out there and kill monsters until you level up. Once that's done, the dungeon will provide someone from your system for you to heal, I'm sure of it. Heal them and then return here. Also, you need to hurry. I'll have to wait for you, and I'm on a deadline here."

The Lifeweavers nodded and scurried away in all directions once he'd healed them a little, just so he could get a sense of their location.

Splinter waited for the others to leave before speaking. "It was a tremendous thing you did in there, Lifeweaver."

"Well, yeah."

"Won't you share the secret?"

Luke gave the ratkin a long look before answering. "Tell me about your system."

"Not that I mind, but why?"

"Earth, my system, is being invaded. I'd like to know I'm not providing a future enemy with information."

"A fair request."

Splinter spoke of his homeworld and the different races on it. From what Luke understood, most of them were anthropomorphic animals who all lived in separate kingdoms under some sort of high king that enforced peace. They were good guys, in general, from what Splinter said, but he couldn't guarantee anything in terms of enemies or allies, though he would find a way to put in a good word for Luke and Earth.

"Fair enough," Luke said, and explained.

Most of it seemed to go over Splinter's head, but he took frenetic notes, his whiskers twitching uncontrollably. Once Luke was through explaining, the ratkin put the notebook away and nodded several times.

"Thank you," Splinter said, peering back into the room, lit up by the person-sized pink portal. “You have done us all a great service. In more ways than one.”

"I'm looking forward to working with you in the future," Luke said. "Now go get Weaver's Echo."

"Oh, I have the prerequisites already, just wanted a moment alone with you, Lifeweaver. Perhaps you would also give me the honor of being your first test subject?"

"Sure," Luke said, laughing at the eagerness in the ratkin's eyes. "Let's do this."

Wanting to first test his theory on how to connect the portal to a thread, Luke settled back into a sitting position in the room. He ignored the many pairs of eyes on him and steeled himself for the pain that was sure to come.

After one final, deep breath, Luke reached out to Ray's thread and grabbed hold of it. The pain was immense, but no more than he had prepared for. By now, pain mattered little to Luke, almost as if the pain center in his brain had been fried one too many times, or it was his improved stats doing their thing, or he was just turning into a badass. Yeah, had to be that last thing. No doubt.

Gritting his teeth and ignoring the feeling of being electrocuted, he pulled on the thread and moved it closer to the portal. As it got closer, the thread trembled, almost like a piece of metal just before it enters the pull of a magnet. Something still held it back, but Luke had a pretty good idea what. Just to confirm what he suspected, he attempted to use Weavestep and found that he could not. It had been a risk, since he'd leave everyone behind if it did work, but he'd been ninety-nine percent sure it wouldn't. Or maybe ninety. Eighty at the least. Nodding to himself, he let go and let out a low sigh of relief.

"What happened?" Splinter asked.

Luke gave him a reassuring nod. "It worked. You ready?"

After confirmation, Luke went straight at it, not having lost any mana from the previous experiment. How this was supposed to work with another Lifeweaver's echo, he wasn't sure, but his gut told him it would be possible. First, he reached a thread of mana over to Splinter, guiding it to the ratkin's Meta-Heart.

Noticing Splinter's heart beating so fast he might as well have been running, he whispered, "Calm down and find the echo. It's through the weave. On the other side."

"I see it," Splinter said, his voice a little breathy. "Glittering like summer rain."

"Keep it at the forefront of your mind," Luke said, concentrating.

Any person's use of skills or other abilities was part of the system. You couldn't have an Integrated without the system, after all. So, it stood to reason that, as a Lifeweaver, Luke should be able to get a glimpse of what was happening behind the closed door, so to speak. Watching Splinter with Weaver's Perception alone wasn't enough. To see, Luke also needed to watch the system weave and how it reacted to the ratkin.

"Do you see the weave?" Luke whispered.

"All I have ever been able to do is get faint glimpses of something beyond the physical world," Splinter whispered back. "Now, in here, I see it. Faint but undeniable. It's beautiful."

"And deadly if you're not careful," Luke said as he watched the weave wrap itself around Splinter, like a trap invisible to the naked eye. But this was no trap. It merely reacted to the ratkin reaching out with his senses to find the echo. Following that intention, Luke got results. A shimmer of sorts inside Splinter's Meta-Heart and a twist of the weave, and there it was.

Quick as lightning, Luke reached through Splinter, grabbed the glowing thread, and heaved. Dragging this one back to the portal was much tougher than Ray's, but he got it done. When it was just about to slot in, Luke brought out the key handed to him by the fake doctor Relian and thrust it at the glowing pink portal.

The thread slotted into place.

Splinter gasped, and Luke pulled back, opening his eyes. They'd done it. Even so, the connection with the thread was tenuous at best and wouldn't hold for long, Luke realized.

"Hey, Splinter. What about other ratkin in here with us?"

Splinter shook his head. "It's only me, Lifeweaver. I'm the only one."

"Then you'd better go."

Nodding, Splinter reached out toward Luke with a thread of mana. "May I heal you?"

Understanding what that meant, that the ratkin would be able to find him wherever he went, and in time, maybe even weavestep to him through The Greater System Weave, Luke nodded. "And I you?"

"Of course," came the reply.

They exchanged a brief moment of healing before Splinter pulled back and stepped toward the gate. "Until next time, friend."

"See you," Luke said.

Splinter walked through the portal, and it transported him along the echo thread.

Luke grinned and turned to the others. "I think we just invented the Lifeweaver handshake! So, who's next?"
