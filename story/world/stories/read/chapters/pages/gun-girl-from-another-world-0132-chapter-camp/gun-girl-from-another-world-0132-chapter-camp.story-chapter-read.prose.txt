Camp

*Remmi*

“Hey, you! The girl with the black hair,” comes a squeaky voice from behind us.

Ayre, Korrigan, and I are wandering through a city during a rare day off. The most recent dungeon purification tour has left us drained, so we really need that break. As ol’ Murphy could have told us, though, it seems our weekend is already getting cut short, what with our walk back to the inn getting so rudely interrupted.

At first, Korrigan doesn’t even react, as surely nobody would be calling out to her. Normally, if something is interrupting our group, it’s with a shout about yellow hair. But then a small light rushes before us, causing us all to stop and look.

The light is actually a fairy; tiny, nebula hair and eyes, teal wings you can kinda see through a bit. The short, white dress looks good on her, to be fair, but it’s still not quite the same kind of sprite we’d expect to see around here.

Korrigan points at her face, finally realizing she’d been the one the fairy was talking to, or trying to, anyway. “Me?”

“Yeah, you!” The fairy flitters up to her face level. “You have a ton of magical power in you! Wanna become a Magical Girl and save the multiverse?”

Absolutely not on my watch. I’ve seen this anime, and I’m already smelling danger. I physically place my body between the two of them, forcing the fairy to move back, and I already have my hand resting on my holster. Ayre, too, seeing me behave this way, follows suit, pulling his bow out of its back sheath just in case this bug means trouble.

“Whoa, whoa, whoa, slow down, there, dragonfly,” I speak up assertively. “Korrigan’s a member of my party. You can’t just Kyubey her into some new ‘Save the World’ business when we’re already busy saving this one.”

Korrigan, for her part, does take a cautious step back, as well, clutching her magic staff tightly with how Ayre and I are clearly readying ourselves for combat. I have to hand it to the kid; she didn’t blindly jump on the idea like many others probably would have. But maybe that sort of deal doesn’t have the kind of social engineering behind it here that it does back home.

The fairy is, however, undeterred. Instead, she’s getting a rather mischievous smile that doesn’t seem nearly as intimidated by our prepared hostility as I feel we are due.

“Aaaactually, I want to extend that offer to all of you! Because, damn, girl, you’ve got almost as much power as the tiny one here, and you there, elf … Jeez Louise, we could use an archer like you!”

“I’m not tiny,” Korrigan shouts, obviously offended. With her jutted bottom lip and balled fists at the bottom of rigidly down-stretched arms, she definitely isn’t pouting, nope.

The outburst clearly takes the fairy aback. “Uh, sorry, you’re right,” she apologizes quickly, holding her little hands up in a surrendering fashion. “Look, I got excited here. Could we please go somewhere to discuss this properly?”

At least it seems like the fairy recruiter, for lack of a better term, has come to her senses. And good timing, too, people are starting to watch. Like, more than they usually do with us. And their expressions are confused as they whisper to each other. Could they not see her, maybe?

I give a tired sigh and a roll of my shoulders as I let my hand fall off of the butt of my pistol. “Fine, let’s get to the inn. I’m famished.” I turn my head to my teammates. “Ayre, Korrigan, wanna at least hear her out?”

“I don’t mind,” Ayre says slowly, eyes staying trained on the little fey, “but don’t pull any tricks.”

Korrigan nods, too. “If she doesn’t insult me again …”

“Alright, let’s head back,” I declare, moving to lead the way. “Dinner, here I come! I’m ordering goose!”

* * *

“Damn, that was one hell of a ride,” I say as the four of us step out of the portal.

The huge, stone not-quite-arch is several yards wide and even taller, studded with five mysterious symbols whose glow has already gone out. Honestly, the resemblance to a simplified version of a stargate is kind of uncanny.

The area we step out onto is largely flat and ringed by trees, but before us stands a tall tower on a miniature island. We’re at the shore of a lake, above us a ton of stars in the sky despite it being daytime, and to the left are various pre-fab buildings that look starkly modern by comparison.

It goes without saying that the fairy convinced us to come along. I’m proud to say that none of us are the type to turn down a request for aid anyway, and she assured us that it would only be for a short time, to see how compatible we really are, and such. So off we went and followed a fairy through a magical gateway, that thing you’re never supposed to do even in my own world, and now we’re here.

I look at my companions, and I’m immediately reminded that it’s their first time traveling between worlds. It’s a lot rougher than teleportation, and both of them are looking a little green in the face. Ayre, at least, takes it better than Korrigan does. After a few minutes, their dinner settles back down - maybe it was a bad idea to eat and fly after all - and they, too, look around.

The architecture with those pre-fabs is Western and fairly modern, clearly closer to my house than what they are used to in Furinshao. Of course, by comparison, the stone gate looks about as modern as Stonehenge.

“Welcome to the camp, girls,” the fairy, Suzuran Amberlily, shouts while holding her arms wide. “For the next week, all of you will get a taste of being a Magical Girl trainee here at camp. Since you explained your accomplishments already, I doubt you’ll have to attend any theoretical lessons or boring training on camp grounds. It’s straight into the dungeons with you! But first …” Her energy slumps along with her shoulders as she lets out a sigh. “Paperwork …”

We all give sympathetic nods at the universality of bureaucracy, and are quickly ushered into a wide building with a red roof and colorful glass windows framed by light gray stone. Judging from the wooden sign with metal cutlery, this is the cafeteria.

“Why in here?” I ask. Considering we just had lunch, it isn’t like we need more food. Not that I couldn’t go for a pastry.

“Because I imagine Isabelle is in there,” Suzuran answers.

“Who’s that?” Korrigan asks.

“She’s the Director of the camp,” comes the explanation. “Her mom, once the strongest Magical Girl, made the whole place and all that. She was surpassed by Isabelle herself some time ago. Don’t worry, she’s a chill one.”

Unsure of what exactly to expect, the three of us follow Suzuran, who uses magic wind to open the door, and step inside. The cafeteria is decently lit, with rows upon rows of mismatched chairs, tables, and potted plants. At the other end is a wide counter, behind which works a maid with long, brown hair and ruby-red eyes.

Probably not Isabelle, that one.

The only other two people are a cute-looking, very fashionable witch in an extremely orange outfit, the first blonde hair I’ve seen outside my own mirror in way too long, and also sporting red eyes, and a mature-looking woman with silver-white hair and trustworthy blue eyes in a red-black dress. The latter is probably Isabelle. The blonde is probably a fellow trainee, getting advice perhaps. Maybe we’ll end up working together.

Suzuran flies over and informs them of our arrival, temporary though our stay might be. We are wordlessly seated across from them after the witch girl cleans the table of various girly magazines. Alright, maybe not getting advice. More like putting in a request.

With Ayre to my right and Korrigan to my left, I nod in greeting, raising my hand for a wave. “Hi, I’m Remmi Lee.” And I look to the silver woman, who, frankly, gives me Yorin vibes. I’ll bet she’s way older than she looks, too. “Director Isabelle, I assume?”

Oddly, she looks at the witch, who looks back while trying to hold back a laugh.

“No, no, I’m Isabelle,” the witch manages to fit through the choking. “This is Olivia, our camp doctor and best worker.”

“Oh, sorry,” I apologize. Actually, now that I think about it, I should have guessed as much. No universe’s Yorin would ever be directly in power.

But Isabelle doesn’t take any offense, either, waving it off. “Nah, it’s fine, no worries. Next to Olivia, I really don’t look the part, do I? But anyway, ahem …” She makes a brief effort at straightening herself out to look a little more in charge. “Heya, welcome to Magical Camp, the biggest of the three facilities to train new Magical Girls and support those who’re trainees no longer. I hope Suzuran here already told you what this is all about?”

I nod back. “Yeah, apparently, your world is being threatened by Mah-Gee-Kohs, and you need all the additional firepower you can get.”

She snaps her fingers before pointing them at me. “Straight to the point, I like it. Yeah, Magicos are the worst things you can imagine. They eat emotions, memories, even people, and generally scare the shit out of everyone. They feed and reproduce endlessly like a virus and cause damage like natural disasters. We’ve managed to drive a good amount of them back over the last decade plus, but right now, we’re facing worse waves than ever before, so recruitment drives are up as much as our funds allow.”

Isabelle crosses her arms at that with a frown. “Which, to be clear, is damn stupid, but,” and she gestures vaguely at Suzuran, “the fae have their own rules and don’t care about anything else.”

“Hey,” said fae shouts indignantly, “this is out of my hands, and you know it! I’m just an office worker and field recruiter, and so are most of the others. It’s the higher-ups who still insist on bullshit rules from hundreds of years ago.”

I look at the little bundle of anger, realizing how modern she has behaved since she found us. “Are you domesticated?”

Normally, such a question might be offensive. Not only does it sound like you’re comparing them to a dog, but fairies, at least in the myths I’m familiar with, that take to the mortal realm aren’t viewed well by their former peers. However, she takes the term with surprising pride, holding her hand to her chin like some ohohohime noblewoman.

“Yes, indeed!”

“Ahem,” is the first word Olivia has spoken, as she had so far been eerily quiet. “If we may stay on topic, please? I’d like for the three of you to sign the paperwork. Please be assured, this is merely a formality.”

Yeah, definitely getting the Yorin vibes from when we’ve been clowning for too long.

Ayre, who has also been quiet so far, speaks up too. “I have a question, if I may?”

Olivia gives a nod, clearly now taking over the meeting. “Please, go ahead.”

“Can guys become Magical Girls?”

It’s Isabelle who looks surprised, eyes widening for a short moment. Though again, if Olivia really is the local Yorin, any surprise would never reach her face anyway.

“Uhm … no?” the blonde answers. “The fairy magic only works with girls. Nobody quite knows why that is, but if boys are infused with it, it will at best wane and disappear shortly.”

“And at worst?”

“They explode!” Lively Isabelle delivers that with undue cheer and animation. “But don’t worry, that never happens to girls.”

I hold up my hand. “Stop. Ayre is a guy, so this won’t work out for him.”

Isabelle, Olivia, and Suzuran all look at my long-time partner in shock. The fairy is holding both hands to her mouth in realization that she had apparently royally fucked up, while I’m pretty sure Isabelle’s eyes go to Ayre’s thighs.

“Oh, that’s … not good,” Isabelle understates. Then, faster, “Olivia, wait! We abolished that rule years ago!”

Olivia had already been drawing something from her dress, and having seen her eyes widen, I, too, am halfway to standing and ready to draw, but Isabelle’s hand resting on hers makes her relax.

“I … yes, you are, of course, correct, Director. I apologize.”

“Whew,” the blonde sighs at the crisis being averted, then turns to us again, her gaze a little pleading. “Well, this is unfortunate. But you two are girls, right? Please?”

“Yes,” I confirm, easing back into my own seat as I gesture to the small oni. “To make things proper again, this is Korrigan. And the elf is Ayre. As for me, well, as I said, I’m Remmi. Remmi Lee, pleased to meet’cha.”

Isabelle nods. “Good, good. So you’ll be trainees for a while. Once deemed ready, you’ll get a graduation exam to solve, after which you’ll officially be Magical Girls proper. For the time being, you can stay at one of the dorms, two girls - er, people - per room.”

I raise an eyebrow. I don’t doubt that my team can plow through whatever tests their Sailor Moon wannabes go through with ease, but this is already sounding like much longer than what we’d agreed to.

“Ah, excuse me,” I speak up, “but we only agreed to do this for a week at most. We have our own problems in Toleste. In fact, I’m one of five heroes set to fight the Corruption infecting it. It sounds a lot like your Magicos, with a dash of Zerg for extra fun.”

Once again, the two women look at Suzuran. I have a feeling she’s in a lot of trouble after this is all settled.

“I’m sorry,” she apologizes. “This was the only way I could get them to come here in the first place!”

Isabelle double-facepalms, slapping one into either eyeball with a groan. “Uuuuuuuugh, fine … Hero, you say? That at least means you’re powerful, right? And so are your friends?”

Arms before my chest, I nod. “Yeah, we’ve been pretty active in our world, so we’ve got some decent gains. You guys have a way to test that here?”

Her look shifts to confusion. “... Like … what, exactly?”

“Well, Toleste has an Essence System that measures everyone’s stats and abilities, and assigns a level summarizing it.”

Isabelle is looking at me like I have multiple heads. “Are you talking about a video game or something?”

I don’t think she was expecting me to nod in hearty agreement. “Exactly like a video game! Everyone’s got a level, class, skills, the whole shebang.” I pat my hip, drawing their attention once more to my holster. “On account of specializing in my pistol, I’m the Gunslinger Hero.”

The two women before us blink and look at each other, clearly unfamiliar with the idea of a System being anything more than a gameplay convention, which is weird, because I definitely had Isabelle pegged for someone who would recognize an isekai when she heard it.

“I fear,” begins Olivia, “that this is going to be a long evening to sort through all of this information.” But then she looks to me. “Young Ayre, however, may not be the only one troubled here. Firearms are not a particularly effective weapon next to magic.”

“Why?” I ask, and I can tell by their faces that they pick up on the slight tension I’m giving off at that. This is going to be one of those worlds, is it? Well, not on my watch.

Again, they exchange looks before turning back to me. “It is a simple power differential,” Olivia explains. “Sufficiently advanced weapons can be effective against a magico’s minions, and a Magical Girl centered around them can be acceptable fire support for a proper frontline team, but gunpowder weaponry certainly doesn’t qualify for either.”

My smile feels a bit tight on my face, and I’m sure it shows. “Well, I’ve seen plenty of fireballs, and I can tell you that if you think they’re more impressive than a bullet to the brainpan, you’re running off of Hollywood logic.”

“Be that as it may,” Olivia replies with a little more sternness in her tone, “we have worked extensively alongside armed forces in the past. When we say that firearms are ineffective, we speak from experience.”

“You let me worry about effectiveness, then,” I reply. “In the meantime, don’t think of it as a firearm. Think of it as a wand that casts a very special spell called Magus Dumpus, and leave the rest to us.”
