Trevor didn’t have to wait for the Quest Master to get back to him.

[[Power skill selection!

Congratulations, you have reached level 25! I understand you’re in a rush, so I’ll keep this brief.

You have two options to choose from. This is not because I’m keeping it brief.

Option One: Conditional Withdrawal

You may set conditions on items in your inventory. They can be as specific or vague as you like and can be both activated and changed once per day. If these conditions are met, you may withdraw items from your inventory without needing to focus on them. You can set one condition per 4 stat points in Capacity, of which you currently have 10. Items retrieved with this skill can be set to appear as far away as 1 foot from your body.

Option Two: Monster Messenger

Monster parts, materials, and goods created from either take up 50% less space in your inventory. That includes your familiar’s husk. This effect stacks with Earth Bond and Stacks.

Take your time, but not too much time, and choose wisely.]]

Trevor stared at the two options, forcing himself to slow down and understand them. There was no point in making panicked decisions.

These two were skewed in different directions. Monster Messenger would help him as a straight boost to his carrying capacity. There were several items that used what the skill described, though they weren’t usually made or shipped in bulk. Affecting Clay’s husk would be nice, but it was already reduced by both Earth Bond, just like everything else stone or metal related in his inventory, and Stacks thanks to proper inventory management.

Conditional Withdrawal had a lot of potential. It was certainly the more flexible of the two options, especially since the Quest Master had gone so far as to mention how specific or vague he could be. That also meant he had to be careful. If he wasn’t strict on the wording of the condition, it might go off at the wrong moment. If he set one to summon a shield if someone were to strike him, then would Captain Braidley hitting him hard on the back count?

That would take time to test, but he needed to make a decision now. Only one of the two would be useful in combat and that’s what he focused on. Hans was quick to get them up here, but there was no telling if he’d be just as fast coming back.

“Option one, Quest Master,” he whispered as he looked back at the mirror. The ice golem was getting closer, its eyeless gaze seemingly focused on the recliner.

[[The Power of Inventory has gained a new skill. You can now set the parameters of Conditional Withdrawal once per day. You can currently set 2 parameters.

Would you like to set them now?]]

“Yes,” Trevor said before he paused. “If I set the same condition twice, will they both go off? Or one, then the other.”

[[Both will go off unless you specify otherwise.]]

“Okay. Glad I asked,” he said, sighing in relief that he wasn't about to make a big mistake.

Since he didn’t want to get fancy while he was in danger, he set the same parameter twice. The second one was worded specifically for after the first condition was no longer viable. That would keep them from going off at the same time.

Before he could ask about his Stamina options, the sound of the ice golem’s footsteps became louder and heavier. Trevor checked the mirror to see that the monster was charging, its spear aimed directly at the chair. He dove out of the way.

The weapon skewered the comfortable leather recliner, missing the courier by a foot. Trevor scrambled to his feet as the ice golem lifted the chair with ease. He looked up at the imposing figure, covered in rows of jagged edges. It reminded him of the first time he faced an earth elemental.

This time, he didn’t freeze. He ran forward to close the gap. The monster took a step back and swung its spear again, ignoring the extra weight of the recliner. It was basically a club at that point. The furniture went right for Trevor’s head.

Every instinct screamed at him to dodge, but he ignored it, continuing his charge. He allowed himself a sharp intake of breath and a flinch when the attack got close. There was a flash, and then he couldn’t see it at all.

The recliner shattered against the new and improved bulwarks he had made with Clay and Grunder’s help. It was thicker than the first iteration had been, with slim rectangles cut out at eye level for humans and demons, dwarves, and shark girls. There were no support struts to keep it up. Instead, it was bolted to a Floatstone tile. It hovered in the air where it had been summoned.

With his path forward clear, Trevor dove forward, arm outstretched. He came within an inch of the ice golem before it stepped back again. The monster was quick. Faster than the courier for sure. He could no longer reach it.

Trevor, thinking fast, did two things in quick succession. He changed out his armor for his silky pajamas while trying not to think about how horrible of a decision that was.

Afterwards, he retrieved a slab of Clay’s husk from his inventory so it was on the ground in front of him. It was long and heavy, so it wasn’t going to go anywhere. The ice golem stepped on the edge of it, tilting as it became slightly off balance. Underneath it, the stone’s surface froze over.

Landing on the rock below would have caused him to stop outright, but Trevor slid across the slab with no trouble thanks to its smooth surface. The ice golem tried to shuffle again, but it had to fix its footing first. That act allowed the courier to catch up.

He slammed his hand against the edge of its foot. A shock of cold flew up his arm, but it didn’t prevent Trevor from attempting to pull the ice golem’s shell into his inventory. There was a moment that felt different from how it had before, and then it worked.

The heart of ice fell to the ground beside him. On impact, it released a burst of cold air that forced him to roll over to protect his face. That effect only lasted a moment, and he reached back to swipe at it with his hand. It, too, disappeared into his inventory.

Trevor took a deep breath as he rolled onto his back. He immediately shimmied back onto the slab when he laid on the ice the monster’s foot had left behind. The magic had protected him from environmental factors, but not anything else, and he learned that the hard way.

Raising his hand into the air, the frostbite on his blackened fingers were prominent against the clear blue sky. Trevor winced as he looked at it. He couldn’t flex his hand without pain, but it was a small price to pay when the alternative was so much worse. In that way, he was lucky. His mind focused on his inventory for a healing potion.

“Trevor!” Adabelle called just before he heard what was undoubtedly Hans’ crash landing. “Are you okay!?”

“I’m good!” he yelled back, giving her a thumbs up from his good hand without getting up. “Just fought a monster, so, I’m glad you got back so fast in case there’s more.”

The demonkin and Clay ran to his side just as he retrieved the potion from his inventory. His wife helped him sit up, and he drank it. It tasted like strawberries.

Raising his hand, Trevor watched as the frostbite started receding. It only took a moment for the affliction to heal. He made sure to flex his fingers to make sure there was no lingering pain.

“What happened?” Adabelle asked, looking him over with concern as she helped him to his feet. Her hands and eyes were all over him, checking for more injuries. Clay dove into the husk that their master had been laying on, assimilating it into a form similar to the monstrous dogs one of the Tosan dungeons created. “Are you okay? Why are you in your pajamas? Did you get hurt anywhere else?”

“Calm down, my love,” the courier chuckled, placing his hands on her shoulders. She stood up a little straighter, but stopped fussing with his clothes. A moment later, he was back in his armor, which got a grunt of confusion out of Hans. “A golem came out to patrol, I hid behind a recliner, which is broken now…”

He glanced over at the chair and lowered his head, giving it a moment of silence. Clay stepped in close and leaned their now much heavier body against their master’s leg. He reached down to pet them.

“Its spear went through, I dodged out of the way, then dashed towards it,” he continued. “It tried to step away, so I changed my clothes and pulled out something to slide on to reach it. Then my hand took damage just touching it, which is less fun than the rest of it, but still something I can work through.”

“It sounds like you had to think fast, Trevor,” Adabelle said as she put a hand on his chest. She gave him a warm smile. “I’m glad you’re safe. I’m sorry I didn’t react in time to stop him from taking you.”

“I’m okay, and I get the feeling that this could have happened to literally any other Summoned,” the courier said with a thin smile. He leaned slightly so he could see the yeti better. “Glad you got back so fast.”

“Confused,” Hans said, scratching the back of his head. “Trev says monster. No see monster. Where monster?”

“I obliterated it in a single strike,” he replied, only slightly sarcastically. “The only thing that remained was the heart, and then I smote that, too.”

Hans’ eyes went wide. “Real?”

“No,” Trevor said, deadpan. “And I could have gotten really hurt. Thank you for coming back so quickly.”

“Oh. Adbelle told Hans hurry. Got back up fast fast,” the abominable snowman said as he walked forward, his curious gaze on the bulwark that remained hovering in the air. “Trev okay?”

“Yeah, I’m okay,” he answered with a small smile. He approached the bulwark and touched it, sending it back into his inventory. The yeti was once again startled by the act. “But now I’m with my party, so all I have to do is pick my Stamina skill and then we can go.”

“Go?” Hans asked, perking up.

“No, soon,” Adabelle said sternly, and the yeti grunted but obliged. She turned back to Trevor. “What of your level twenty-five skill?”

“Picked it while I was hiding behind the chair. It really came in handy,” he said with a chuckle. “Quest Master, please send my wife the information on Conditional Withdrawal and show me my new options.”

[[Stat skill selection!

Your Stamina has reached 10, and that means you get to choose a general skill. There are three choices.

The last choice you made for Stamina was Endurance Boost, which lowered the rate at which you became fatigued.

Option One: Greater Endurance Boost

The last time we were in this position I had said that your particular brand of adventure requires a lot of walking. This time around it’s teleporting, but overall not much has changed. This skill further reduces the amount of physical fatigue and soreness you build up over time, making it so you’re basically half-machine.

Option Two: Armored

You’re starting to get used to wearing armor, and not just when larping for your wife. It’s become something that might actually protect you instead of a piece you bust out every so often in order to look cool at parties, or for your wife.

Whenever you’ve covered at least 33% of your body with armor, increase your Stamina stat by 2. If you’re 66% covered, increase it by 5.

You may ask, what counts as armor? Use your common sense on that one.

Option Three: Hazard Protection

Sometimes, you can’t avoid danger. If you can’t go around it, or over it, or under it, then you’ve got to go through it. Poison gas swamp? No problems. Mountainous cold? You can work up a sweat. Desert heat? More like a pleasant late spring day.

This skill offers mild protection from most environmental hazards and some special attacks or properties of monsters.

That last bit was added just now after you experienced it for yourself.

You will still need to be careful, however; I said it protects from “most” and “some,” not “all.” This skill will not protect fully from anything, just help you out enough that it’s a good choice.

Take your time, and choose wisely.]]

“Thanks, Quest Master,” Trevor said as he scanned the list.

“Conditional Withdrawal is quite good,” Adabelle stated as she finished reading the text box. “I can see it opening up a lot of options for you in the future. Did you already set your conditions?”

“Me, too,” he replied with a smile. “And, yes. The first one has already activated, but if I’m about to get hit with a blow intended to be lethal or debilitating, a bulwark would appear to block it. The second one is the same as the first, but with the added clause that the first had to have been used already.”

“So it’s active now?” she asked.

“It is.”

“That’s smart. Good work, Trevor,” she praised, causing him to smile. “And what about your Stamina skills?”

“I’ve been offered Greater Endurance Boost, Armored, and Hazard Protection. I’m pretty sure I already know which one I’m leaning towards, but I’d like to hear your thoughts.”

“The first one is always good, though some people think it’s a waste to take what is essentially the same skill twice. If you do, you’d be able to keep up with me easier,” she explained.

“Well, that does sound nice.”

“As for Armored, two points isn’t worth a skill at this level, but five is,” Adabelle said, taking a step back to look him over. “Are you above or below the second threshold now?”

“Quest Master?” Trevor asked.

[[You are currently 73% armored. I have already sent this to your wife.]]

“Adding five to your Stamina would be a fifty percent boost,” she noted, tapping her chin. “But how would you feel about wearing armor more often?”

“Eh, not that great if I’m being honest,” the courier answered with an apologetic smile. He reached up and scratched the back of his head. “I really prefer comfier clothes, and I’m not sure I like skills that come with conditions. Except for ones like World Walker, but that’s because it’s almost always in effect since I’m usually grounded.”

“Which is a very fair way of looking at things,” Adabelle agreed, though she pouted afterwards. “It does mean I don’t get to see you all dressed up as often, though.”

“And that’s exactly why the Quest Master called you out when explaining the skill,” he teased, reaching up to poke her nose.

“Did it, now,” she said, her pout only becoming more adorable as her tail swished.

“It did, but I think I’m going to go with Hazard Protection,” Trevor replied to get the conversation back on track. “It sounds useful, especially when I travel to new locations, and it could even help against monsters. If just touching one of these ice golems is going to injure me, then it also becomes immediately helpful.”

Adabelle’s expression changed as she reached down and grabbed her courier’s hand. “I don’t like that you’re going to be hurting yourself here. You can stay in the back, if you want.”

“I appreciate the offer, love, but I’m going to see this through,” he told her with a smile. “What’s a little pain to an adventurer, huh?”

“But you’re not an adventurer, you’re a courier,” she returned.

“Sure, but I’m on an adventure, aren’t I?”

“I suppose that’s true,” Adabelle relented as she offered him a concerned smile. “Okay. Go for Hazard Protection, then give me my armor and sword. Hans, get ready, because my man is about to win this competition of yours.”

“Hans can’t wait,” the yeti said, grinning. “We have much fun.”
