Aura streamed into Gary’s abdomen. It swirled around itself like a black whirlpool with nowhere to go. Sparks zapped out. Gary spread part of his concentration to keep it in place as most of his willpower sucked more aura into the whirlpool. What had started easy quickly became difficult. Gary could feel the challenge. He had to constantly maintain his focus to keep the aura from escaping, and he had to endure the feeling of exhaustion that permeated him as his body emptied itself of aura.

By the time he’d gathered twenty percent, he was already panting heavily. His body had gotten used to having aura—it signaled terrible exhaustion, so deep it gave rise to panic. This was nothing to Gary. He was used to exhaustion.

We’re not dying, it just feels like it. A little more. Just one more push, he kept telling himself. He clamped on, resuming the sucking process.

A boom echoed, almost making him jump. The gathered aura spilled, over half of it escaping back to his body. He gritted his teeth, taking a moment to rupture his eardrums again, and making sure he did it as thoroughly as possible. He returned to the breakthrough. He grabbed the aura with steely resolve and pulled it in. It didn’t resist much at first, only hesitating a little before following his command. The more he pulled, the more it resisted, until it fought back like an animal going to slaughter. Gary pulled harder. The aura screamed as it was sucked into the black whirlpool, which grew ever deeper.

He was only at thirty percent, yet the aura fought like a cornered lion. It slipped between his fingers. The exhaustion made him feel as if he’d reached his limit. His brain blared like a siren with the intense need to escape this feeling. Panic rose, trying to overtake his body, and Gary had to fight it as well.

We aren’t dying. We can keep going. One more push.

Thirty-five percent. Forty. Forty-five.

He’d passed the point of failure, but this was just the lowest standard. Even the weakest clan disciples reached fifty to fifty-five percent concentration. Gary was aiming for much higher.

He also had to form the core afterward. Reaching forty percent didn’t mean he automatically succeeded, and the more aura he gathered, the more difficult the next step would be.

The price of genius.

He gritted his teeth and pushed. Fifty percent. Fifty-five. It was getting much harder. The inner scream to give up was getting louder, but it was shouting at the wrong person. Gary had trained for days non-stop. He’d endured two strikes of Sui Duang’s Meat Grinder. He’d seen his planet slaughtered.

One more push. Come on, body. Just one more. We aren’t dying. We can do this.

They won’t fucking break me.

Sixty percent.

The pressure was really mounting now. The whirlpool was compressed and wild, threatening to implode under its own weight. The aura was pushing to escape, but Gary grabbed it with his willpower and forced it in, tighter and tighter. He made space to pour more in. It felt as if his body and mind would tear, but he knew they wouldn’t. It was an illusion. It had to be.

Gather! he screamed in his mind.

He was no longer pulling, but pushing. That was the hard part. The aura resisted him every step along the way, and he had to consciously force it through. The further along he got, the slower he became. The first thirty percent had come quickly. Every five percent now felt like an hour, and he really hoped it wasn’t, because the frog would reach him before he was done.

Sixty-five.

Gary had already reached the point of a decent clan disciple, and he was playing with many disadvantages. Normally, the cultivator would use aura-calming treasures to soothe their aura and reduce its resistance. Gary did it raw. His sixty-five was not their sixty-five.

I could have bought the aura-calming treasures. Fuck me, and fuck this. It’s always been unfair. No matter how hard it gets, I will win. I won’t ever give up. My aura can explode if it wants, but I’m not letting go. I’m dying anyway. What have I got to lose!?

Seventy percent. By this point, pushing the aura in felt like holding a beehive. It stang. He had to pour his whole concentration into it just to keep it from erupting. Bits and pieces were slipping through the cracks, requiring extra effort to refill, further slowing him down. His body felt numb and weak, pushed far beyond its limits. The inner whisper of panic had turned into a loud, over-the-top scream demanding he quit this instant. Gary pressed on.

One more push, he lied to himself. Just one more.

He reached seventy-two, and to his horror, the aura leaks matched his speed of feeding the whirlpool.

This wasn’t an issue of willpower. Aura control was necessary, too. His 29 could only get him so far, at least without aura-soothing medicine.

Gary growled in refusal. Only seventy-two? After everything he’d been through, he’d die as just above average? No! He refused!

This wasn't about survival anymore. It wasn’t about defeating Feng Shen or climbing the tower or escaping this hole. This was about pride. He was breaking through for himself, and seventy-two percent just wasn’t good enough.

GATHER! he screamed, intensifying his efforts. His rate of absorption ticked up, but even while going all-out, he only reached seventy-five percent. This was his limit.

No. I refuse. Gather! Gather! Gather! he commanded, but it was useless. His control wasn’t enough. The aura kept slipping between his fingers, and the concentration fell back to seventy-four. For some reason, this infuriated him so fucking much.

Anything, everything, come to me and GATHER!

A faint blue light danced over his empty body. He suddenly remembered the astral aura in his veins. He had no idea how a breakthrough worked if you dual cultivated body and energy, but he was beyond caring. Aura was aura.

COME TO ME!

The astral aura felt differently than his black one. It was thinner, softer, dreamier. He grabbed it all the same and stuffed it into his whirlpool like a caveman filling a turkey. It resisted, but it stayed. The concentration rose to eighty percent. His veins were empty. He’d consumed his own body cultivation.

More, bring me more! Gary raved, frantically scanning his body. There was one more thing to take energy from. Something that actually held enormous amounts of aura but was absolutely ill-advised to touch.

The Seed of Eternal Flesh. That little red orb which was anchored to the walls of his soul via crimson tentacles.

That was an evil item if Gary had ever seen one. Messing with it—trying to demand its energy—might kill him or mess with his breakthrough. At this point, however, what did he have to lose?

His willpower crashed through the walls of his soul and fell upon the seed. Give me everything, he demanded, reaching in and trying to forcefully take its essence.

That was, very predictably, a mistake.

The seed erupted with power. Its tentacles thrashed, tearing at Gary’s soul walls like hooks through wood. It only affected a small part of his soul, but the pain was far greater than anything he’d ever experienced. He wasn’t prepared. His entire body froze, including the berserk aura in his whirlpool. This was too much. His mind bent under the pressure of such massive pain, threatening to break. If it wasn’t so hardened, it might have.

Gary recoiled. He couldn’t go in there. He just couldn’t. Pain aside, he could tell with perfect clarity that messing with the seed anymore would fracture his soul and mind and injure him irreparably.

He withdrew immediately, but the seed wasn’t willing to let him go. A tentacle wrapped around his willpower. The very fact that this could happen stunned Gary. He pulled, but the tentacle held. It was hungry. So, so hungry. Suddenly, Gary and the seed were connected, and he realized that what existed inside it wasn’t the endless energy he’d expected. It was a void. Desolate, quiet emptiness. And that hunger. Oh, the hunger.

Like a circuit had been completed, the seed saw him as he saw it. It spotted the rotating whirlpool in his abdomen and went crazy. A howling sound came as the seed’s powers traveled through the tentacle, through Gary’s mind, into his body, into his abdomen. A crimson tentacle physically sprouted from the walls of his abdomen, touching its suckers to the whirlpool. With a deep, slurping sound, the whirlpool was sucked into it. It fed the seed—a tiny amount of its emptiness, a drop in its ocean.

Gary didn’t know what to be terrified of first. There was a tentacle in his body. A real tentacle. And all the aura he’d gathered, eighty percent of everything he held in his body, was gone. He was back to zero. He didn’t have enough aura in himself to retry the breakthrough. It was done. Over. The End.

“Gary! Gary!” Goldie screamed in his mind, but the sound was distant. The seed reached through his soul to his connection with Goldie and snapped it. Gary felt extreme grief welling up inside him.

No… Goldie…

He couldn’t move. Suddenly, he was back in the present, lying flat in a coffin of reinforced dirt at the bottom of a hole. He couldn’t hear the bangs, but he could see that the light had grown a lot brighter. Hints of movement emerged where, before, he only saw vines. The frog was approaching. He’d failed. He had to kill himself immediately.

But he still had a tentacle in his body. Done absorbing his aura, it released a pulse, which activated Gary’s aura field without his permission. The tentacle was in his mind. It was in his eyes. It saw what he saw, the reinforced dirt and enemies above. Something reacted inside it as it looked up, but then the feeling was gone, replaced by a much greater sense of joy as the seed spotted the dirt. A squealing sound echoed inside Gary. The tentacle’s suckers opened again, even wider this time, and they sucked.

It was nothing like when Gary drew his aura towards his abdomen. This was on a completely different level. The remaining twenty percent of his aura was peeled off his bones and organs, off his fingers, off the farthest areas of his body he hadn’t reached. Even the tiny bits of astral aura remaining in his veins were affected. Within seconds, all of it had been sucked by the tentacle, leaving him a hollow husk. He felt like a mortal. It was maddening.

But the tentacle kept going. Its suckers revved. Gary sensed his aura field expanding despite having no aura left, even surpassing the greatest width he’d ever achieved. It stretched fifteen feet around him. The wealth of information he received was overshadowed by how little he understood what was happening. He finally got an idea. Part of his remaining aura, which the tentacle had just absorbed, had been emitted as a pulse. Unlike a regular aura field, it didn’t return. It flew off into the distance, dissipating within fifteen feet and somehow feeding Gary and the tentacle information on what it saw. He didn’t even know this was possible.

The tentacle must have been satisfied with what it learned. It opened its suckers a third time, all of them wide like gaping maws. The suction was indescribable. Gary felt like a leaf in the wind, and he no longer knew what was happening. Aura flew at him from all directions. It was ripped off the dirt around him. All the aura it had ever absorbed, which made it hard like iron, was separated and sucked into the tentacle. As it passed through Gary’s body, the feeling was extremely invasive. He felt about to lose himself. Thankfully, it was over soon.

An area completely devoid of aura now existed around Gary. He could sense its emptiness. It was hungry, like the seed. Aura poured in from further out, but it was slow and little—mediocre—and the tentacle didn’t care about that.

All that aura had gathered before the tentacle, greedily getting sucked in. It used Gary’s willpower as a conduit to dive into his soul, where the seed slurped it up. The tentacle in his soul let go, and Gary was flung out of his own soul. Just as he was ejected from it, he saw a little bit of the seed refilling. A tiny bit, though much more than what his meager aura had managed.

Finally back in his body, the first place he checked was Goldie, hidden so deeply in her spear he could barely sense her. She was shivering. The next place he checked was his abdomen. The tentacle was gone as if it never existed, but something else was there. A ball of aura, tightly knit, a larger amount that what he’d previously gathered. Gary estimated this was about ninety-five percent of his body’s capacity—the level he’d be able to reach had he access to aura-soothing medicine and experts compressing his aura from all directions.

The ball was tight now, but with the tentacle no longer controlling it, it was quickly unraveling. The ramifications of all this were staggering, but Gary had no time to consider them. He fell numbly on the ball. Gathering more was pointless—he had neither the control nor the aura. He passed directly into the final stage of this breakthrough: compressing.

This was the hardest part. The more aura a cultivator had gathered before, the harder it was, but the more powerful they’d be if they succeeded. But it was hard. To compress a highly-gathered core, geniuses usually secured the assistance of their seniors, who would surround them and push with their auras to help compress the core.

Gary didn’t have that luxury. The seed had returned to inactivity, his mind was spinning, and he felt thoroughly exhausted and terrified. Most importantly, his ears had healed, and he heard a loud boom which delayed his concentration. He hastily ruptured his drums again. The ball unraveled to ninety-three percent concentration.

There was no time. He grabbed onto the ball and pushed. Pushed, pushed, pushed.

Compress! he yelled, but the ball was stubborn. There was too much aura inside it, and it resisted greatly. Most importantly, it wasn’t his aura. It wasn’t the dirt’s aura, either. This was a crimson-tinted core smelling faintly of hunger. There was black lightning in there too, just enough to make this core count as his and give it a darker hue.

The foreign aura made it harder to control and compress. However, this was the step Gary felt most confident about. To compress the ball was to compress oneself. This was about willpower. And Gary had nothing if not that.

Compress! he roared. Compress!

It went slowly. Gary had lost track of time, every fiber of his being focused on compressing the dark crimson aura. Goldie could no longer speak to him. He wouldn’t know when the frog reached him. If they captured him, they’d make it so that he couldn’t kill himself and then do who-knows-what on him, but it was a risk he was determined to take.

Glory or death.
