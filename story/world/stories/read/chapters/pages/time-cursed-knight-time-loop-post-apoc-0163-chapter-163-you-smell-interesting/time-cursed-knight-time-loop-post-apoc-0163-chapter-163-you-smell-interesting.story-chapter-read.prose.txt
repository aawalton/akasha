The silence afterward was worse than the noise.

Smoke curled from blackened wyvern corpses scattered across the tiers. The air tasted of sulfur and charred scale. Soldiers moved between the bodies, checking for signs of life and finding none.

I wiped the flat of my blade against my thigh and looked around.

That's it?

I'd died in this cavern seventeen times. The rhythm of those deaths was carved so deep into me that I could feel the shape of what should come next, and this wasn't it.

It had taken less than ten minutes, but they mentioned an ancient one.

I kept my sword out.

Hagen's voice cut through the smoke. "Casualty count. Then defensive positions. Nobody advances until the wounded are behind the shields."

Officers moved. Sergeants barked. The machine of House Hexenzeit ground back into order.

I returned to the ritual site.

Brandt was cleaning blood from his bastard sword with a rag he'd produced from somewhere. His metal hand held the blade steady while his living hand worked the cloth along the fuller. He didn't look up.

"You knew," he said.

"I had a feeling."

Ghost materialized from behind the pillar, wiping his short blade on his thigh. He hadn't taken a scratch.

"Next time," he said, "lead with the information."

Near the broken ritual circle, a cluster of Academy mages had gathered around the body of the lead red wyvern. One of them, a woman in research robes with a lens apparatus strapped to her eye, was kneeling beside the skull, both hands pressed flat against the scales.

"Three hundred years," she said, her voice rising with each word. "There hasn't been a confirmed wyvern sighting in three hundred years."

A younger mage beside her was writing furiously.

"The scale density is nothing like the museum specimens."

Funny. The first time through, I tried to tell everyone in this cavern that there were wyverns behind that gate. They called me crazy.

I turned away from them.

Hagen was walking the perimeter with Captain Reinecke. Four rings of mana still flickered faintly along his forearms. His coat was singed at one sleeve. A streak of dark wyvern blood crossed his left boot.

He stopped beside the dead red wyvern nearest the site and studied it the way a butcher studies a carcass, estimating yield.

I walked over.

"Quite a mess," I said. I nodded at the nearest black-scaled body, the one slumped against the supply crate I'd been thrown into. "I assume you'll want these cleared before the column moves."

Hagen looked at me.

"The Department will take specimens. The rest can be burned."

"Burned." I let the word sit for a moment, as though considering it. "Seems a waste. I'd like some for myself."

Hagen's expression didn't shift. He glanced at the nearest red corpse, then back at me.

"Take what you like," he said. "We'll keep a few specimens for the Department. The rest is slag."

He turned and walked away.

I stared at his back.

He doesn't care.

Hagen hadn't come here for wyvern corpses or alchemical reagent. He'd known this fight was a door, not the destination. Whatever he wanted was on the other side, and everything between him and it was debris.

Which meant he had no idea what he'd just given me.

The blood was alchemically active. So were the venom sacs, the scale lattice, the organ tissue. The original Kaspar had run over a hundred experiments with monsters' compounds, and draconic material was the rarest of them all.

I looked at the Academy researcher.

She was still crouched beside the red wyvern's skull, one hand running along the jaw.

"Fifty-fifty," I said.

She looked up.

"On the specimens. Half to the Academy. Half to House Grenzwacht."

Her face fell.

"These are the most significant zoological specimens recovered in three centuries—"

"Which is why you'll want half of them catalogued under your department's name instead of zero."

She glanced toward where Hagen had gone. Then at the wyvern. Then at me.

"Fifty-fifty," she said quietly. "But we select first."

"Alternate picks."

She let out a slow breath and nodded.

I left her to her measurements.

Brandt was watching the corridor beyond the open Door. Black-scaled bodies lay between the pillars where the first company had met the swarm, but the fighting there had also ended quickly. Hexenzeit soldiers were already moving the dead wyverns to the sides to clear a path.

I leaned my shoulder against the stone.

Now I understood why my loops had been so brutal.

The expedition had been built around its mages, and the mages had been mostly useless against their kind.

What they'd needed was steel.

The wyverns could absorb mana.

Hagen was the exception. The chaining was faster than anything I'd witnessed from a human caster. So quickly that the wyverns' absorption couldn't keep pace. By the time a scale drank one bolt, the next was already in it.

If he had six Circles instead of four, none of those red wyverns would have gotten close to us.

I'd rather not think about the day that happens.

Hagen stopped beside Captain Reinecke near the cleared corridor.

"Inform the Archduke that the first corridor is secure," he said. "We enter the second Door in ten minutes."

Reinecke nodded and sent an officer back up the ramp toward the upper pavilions.

"Moving," Brandt said.

The corridor was longer than I remembered.

Our boots echoed.

I counted steps. Old habit.

Now it was stone, and the stench of burned scale was fading.

The second Door appeared at the far end.

It was identical to the first. Massive. Seamless metal. Runes packed across every surface, dense as scripture. The same letters I could read and no one else could.

Hagen stopped in front of it.

Sieghart came up beside him.

Neither of them seemed surprised to find a second gate.

Hagen placed both hands flat against the metal.

Sieghart did the same.

The Door moved.

No ritual. No crystals. No channeled light. The runes along its surface flared once, pale blue, and the seam parted down the center. The halves swung inward on their own weight, grinding against the stone floor, and stopped.

Beyond them lay darkness.

Then a voice entered my skull.

⌜ You managed to kill my children. ⌝

It wasn't the wyverns' voice.

The wyverns had spoken in fragments, clipped and overlapping. This voice was singular. It filled the inside of my head.

Hagen froze.

Sieghart's six rings flared to full brightness.

⌜ Powerful, for humans. A pity. ⌝

The column behind us went silent. I could feel the words press through the ranks. Some stumbled. A Hexenzeit sergeant gripped his wand with both hands and the tip shook.

I tightened my grip on the sword.

A shape moved in the dark beyond the Door.

It walked slowly. The gait was unhurried, almost casual, the stride of a man crossing a room to pour himself a drink.

It wasn't a wyvern.

The face was wrong. Reptilian. Vertical pupils set in yellow irises, scales so fine they looked like burnished leather, a jaw that hinged wider than a human's should. But the proportions were human. Two arms. Two legs. Upright posture. The hands had fingers.

The clothing was what stopped me.

A blazer.

Purple fabric, cut in a style I hadn't seen since my last life. Single-breasted. Two buttons. The lapels lay flat against a shirt of pale yellow linen, open at the collar. No tie. The shirt was tucked into dark trousers that ended at polished shoes.

He looked like a man who had a meeting at two and wanted to wrap this up by one.

⌜ Welcome to my home. ⌝

The voice in my head was perfectly pleasant.

⌜ My other children are occupied at the moment, so I'm afraid I must receive you myself.⌝

He stopped five paces from the Door.

His yellow eyes moved across the soldiers, mages, Magitek, and Academy researchers, then settled somewhere between Hagen and Sieghart without choosing either.

⌜ What would you like? ⌝

He extended his hand.

The gesture was precise. Palm open. Fingers together. The angle of a man offering a handshake across a boardroom table.

⌜ If it is within my power to grant, I will. In exchange, you leave. The arrangement benefits both parties. ⌝

Hagen and Sieghart exchanged a glance.

I stayed where I was, ten paces behind them, beside the last pillar before the Door. My Knights were around me. Roen's iron slab rested against his shoulder. Brandt's living hand was on his sword.

Sieghart spoke first.

"I would like passage." His voice was steady. Whatever pride the suspension had cost him, he'd reassembled it. "To the human base you occupy."

The yellow eyes moved to Sieghart.

⌜ Possible. But only you. Alone. ⌝

Sieghart's jaw worked.

Hagen stepped forward.

"I want to know," he said, "where the portal is. The one the dragons used."

The vertical pupils narrowed. The pleasant businessman's posture didn't change. The extended hand didn't waver.

⌜ Ah. ⌝

A pause.

⌜ That's a pity. I'd thought we might reach a civilized agreement. ⌝

His fingers closed.

The magic came from his hand. One moment Hagen was standing upright with four rings burning on his forearms and his mouth open to say something else.

The next moment he was in two pieces.

The cut went through him diagonally, from the left shoulder to the right hip.

Blood hit the stone.

Blood hit the figure in the purple blazer.

He looked down at his shirt.

⌜ Unfortunate. I liked this one. ⌝

He looked up.

⌜ Now. Can we still come to an arrangement, or— ⌝

The barrage hit him.

Sieghart's six rings blazed white. Behind him, every Fourth and Fifth Circle officer in the column opened fire. Ice lances. Stone spears. Lightning. Wind blades. Fire in every color the spectrum could name.

The body in the blazer vanished.

Where it had been, something else was growing.

The blazer was gone. The linen shirt was gone. In their place was skin the color of old bronze, scales each as wide as a man's chest, ridged, running with a light that came from inside them like banked coals.

A head emerged from the dark.

It was the size of the carriage we had ridden in. The jaw alone could have swallowed Roen whole. The eyes were the same yellow. They looked smaller now, set in a skull that blocked the corridor from wall to wall.

⌜ Very well. ⌝

The voice was the same. Perfectly pleasant. Perfectly calm.

⌜ I suppose we do this the old way. ⌝

The notification was a joke.

My legs wouldn't move.

I looked at the soldiers behind me. Half of them had dropped their wands. A Hexenzeit sergeant was on his knees, both hands pressed to the stone, his mouth open in a scream that produced no sound. Two Academy researchers had turned and were trying to run, but their legs wouldn't cooperate either, and they stumbled into each other and fell.

The mages who could still stand were firing. Sieghart hadn't stopped. His six rings burned so bright they cast shadows across the cavern walls, and the spell he was pouring into the dragon's chest did absolutely nothing.

Move. I told my legs. They didn't listen.

One step. I dragged my boot forward across the stone.

Brandt was beside me. His sword was drawn, but his feet were planted and his breathing had gone shallow. Roen had the iron slab in both hands and was shaking. Ghost had gone very still, the color drained from his face, his pale eyes fixed on the dragon's jaw.

I forced a second step.

The dragon's head turned.

Those yellow eyes found me.

⌜ You. ⌝

A pause.

⌜ You smell interesting. ⌝
