There is only one way to refine yourself.

Seek your limits.

Push them.

The boat cut through the river with little sound save the creak of boards and the swish of Ironwine’s stout oaken pole. It drove them forward, splashing slightly as the Thrysalid’s iron claws hauled back.

It was quiet there in the deep caverns beneath the mountain. A heavy mist hung in every direction, enough to muffle sound and obscure the horizon. It was as if they floated through a dream. He could make out neither shore nor horizon—all he could see was the big-bellied skiff that he sat within, and the stretch of false stars far above.

He flared his Talent. Sovereign Sight was different now. Haker was still cataloging the changes, but he could tell, for instance, that the light shining above was fundamentally different from the fungal colonies and moss he'd seen around Ironwine's home. This light was colder, bearing little in the way of natural illumination. He’d tasted its like before, and often, though most recently in the uncut lump at the bottom of his pack.

Tidal gems.

Pieces too small to mine littered the distant ceiling of the cavern. They gleamed with the potency of the Sea, captured through a thousand different means, until they glittered like lost diamonds. The Gnarl was full of the precious treasure—it was why the island had been settled centuries ago—and mines pockmarked the mountain like a honeycombed hive. The towns and villages around the mountain thrived off its trade, and the Sunken City made its fortune in them.

The flecks themselves were too tiny to be useful, but they could glow for months at a time before going dark. Before that happened, others would light up, creating an eternally shifting night sky. It was famous, held up in story and song, and a prized feature of the Sunken City.

We must be close by now.

Harker leaned back, trying to find some comfort on the hard wooden seat. He was wrapped in a ragged cloak the Thrysalid had provided, but it was too thin to block out the chill that hung in the mist. Repressing a shiver, he pulled his Talent close—there was little reason to soak himself in the icy waters, even if it was only with his senses. For the last day of travel, Harker had made the thrilling discovery that he could extend himself to his maximum range. At some point in that basin of poison, Harker had pushed himself beyond his own limits. Now a five-foot radius sphere around his body could be held without a moment of dizziness. Even amid the slowly rocking skiff, he could easily absorb the thousand details contained in his range. Not everything made it through, but far more than he’d ever considered, all of it filtered through his mind.

It wasn’t perfect. Moving rapidly while at max capacity was still a challenge—the changes came too swiftly to process—but pulsing was still an option. He’d spent hours practicing atop the river, testing the boundaries of his Talent and the limit of the details he could retain.

It helped him forget, for a while, the consequences of his injuries.

Emerging from the mud had cost him a great deal of muscle mass, and Harker had never had much to spare. Ironwine’s care had helped him become functional, but where he was once lean and rangy, now he feared he resembled a bundle of sticks secured by twine.

From his pack, he pulled a cube of dried meat and cheese before devouring them in a few rapid bites. The Thrysalid had provided a great deal of food for his journey, but Harker had already demolished a quarter of it. He’d never felt so ravenous before, in part because of the anxiety that plagued him.

He’d lost a great deal of time, both in treatment and recovery. Numbers ticked down across his forearm, unseen beneath his coatsleeve but always on his mind.

Two weeks left to reach the Travelspire. Two days max in the Sunken City to visit a Charterstone and resupply, then off to the Southern Reach and Antrim. That should take…six days in total. He wasn’t certain of the distances involved, but he’d fantasized about visiting Antrim a thousand times since he was a child. The roads were busy with travelers and well-tended. Six days would suffice.

So long as nothing goes wrong.

Harker was shaky the moment he’d exited the basin.

Steps like a newborn pup and just as reliable, he’d been forced to rely on Ironwine’s aid just to traverse the kitchen. As much as that bothered Harker, Ironwine didn’t seem to balk at such things. There were no recriminations or accusations of weakness. Perhaps it was merely lost in translation. The Eidhrin was as strange to him as a crayfish, both in manner and body language.

Yet Body language was all he had to go on. The Thrysalid was taciturn at best, and often simply gestured to get Harker’s attention whenever he wasn’t smithing. The forge was always going, and small stone and metal creations piled atop Ironwine’s workbench, many of which had no apparent purpose. When he wasn’t working, however, he was caring for Harker. Multiple times a day, he forced Harker to walk a full circuit around the house, propping him up whenever his muscles gave out. Then, half-supported on an iron carapace, Harker would still be expected to finish his laps.

It was painful, but Harker knew all too well the dangers of atrophied muscles. They required movement and time to rebuild. Most of all, however, they required food.

Ironwine cooked several meals a day, though Harker was careful to watch what the poison-blooded Thrysalid considered edible. Nothing like the medicinal mudbath was prepared again, perhaps because Ironwine discovered how susceptible humans were to poison. Instead, he was fed a vast quantity of meat, rice, and long-stemmed plants.

The days passed, clawing at Harker’s unease while his body recovered. It was far more rapid than he expected, and credited the Thrysalid, but Ironwine deflected any praise. His healing, he was told, was entirely due to his own efforts…and perhaps the lingering echoes of the Last Grace elixir. Harker couldn’t feel its influence, but he also couldn’t deny the speed at which he regained a portion of muscle in his legs, arms, and abdomen. He wasn’t any stronger than before—with the loss of mass over his whole body, Harker had no doubt he was even a touch weaker. Yet he possessed a vitality he couldn’t explain, but his Talent could feel. If it weren't for the progress bars and the Boons they rewarded, Harker would have almost thought it a figment of his imagination.

On the last day before they were to set out, the Thrysalid at the table where Harker was devouring a hefty meal. Inscrutible black eyes stared at him above the iron-ridges of his cheekbones. His antenna twitched.

"Your soul is broken."

Harker swallowed a mouthful of long-grained rice. As far as conversation openers went, that was a heavy one. "Excuse me?"

"The Bargains you have made, the stature you have built, they hold it fast for now, but not for long. You know this."

Harker nodded. "Stillwater mentioned it before, yeah.”

“The medicine has healed your wounds, your organs, and your blood."

“Right. It strengthened them.”

"More than that."

Harker frowned. Ironwine was clearly eager to explain. He didn’t have to wait long.

“Your blood was wrong. It has been fixed.”

"You mean the infection?"

Ironwine's antenna flicked downward. "Yes, and no. You are a strange one, Harker Shoalborn. Stature and the Sea gather within you like sands upon a river’s bank. They crowd you, each one fighting for space to thrive.” His small hands tapped against one of the large claws. “Such potency cannot live for long within one flesh. The fall you suffered was only the catalyst for the deeper dangers. Your blood rioted against you.”

Harker bit into a piece of dark bread. He understood all too well. Medicines might be rejected by a patient’s body—some people were simply incompatible with the reagents used, and other options had to be considered. “So you’re saying the mudbath was your way of correcting this issue?”

“Yes. Toxins to nourish the deep poisons in your veins, balanced with antitoxins to draw out that which was harming you. Yet I failed to account for the frailty of human flesh.” Ironwine bowed his head, tendrils lashing. “I apologize for that. Still, you have come through the other side with blood any young Thrysalid would be proud of.”

“I’m not sure I’m following.”

"Between Stature, blood, and the shape of your tributaries, you resemble an Eidhrin. Malformed, perhaps, but it is why your strength returns so quickly. You now bear the resilience of a strong adolescent Thrysalid."

Ironwine seemed proud of this point, but fear pricked at Harker’s belly. “This is a…good thing?"

"Quite. You will find yourself more resistant to poisons, to disease, and might even find true nourishment in them…though I doubt it. Your flesh is entirely human. Still, you may see other benefits as you come into your power."

Harker chewed his bread and the news slowly. Ironwine was correct—this was an excellent development. Poison and disease were always a worry growing up in the northwoods. What concerned him was the mention of his tributaries. You resemble an Eidhrin. What consequences would come from that?

"I'm impressed by your will to live. And by your ingenuity." Ironwine tapped the pack that sat on the table next to Harker. "To use that human medicine in conjunction with the mudbath was inspired…and mad."

A low rattling came from Ironwine's throat, a sound Harker had come to realize was laughter. "But tell me, how did you summon such Waters to the solution?"

Harker clenched his jaw. He couldn’t deny his gratitude toward Ironwine, but he had no urge to reveal his secrets.

The Thrysalid lifted a small hand, waving off the question. "Never mind. I do not seek to sour the Bargains we've already settled. You have lived. That is enough.”

Oddly, Harker believed him.

“As I said, however, your soul is broken. Stature will not be enough to hold it together forever."

"I know," Harker said. "That's why I'm headed to the academy."

The rattle became a concerned rumble. “That place. It is as treacherous as any Court. Treading such depths is a dangerous gamble.”

Harker spread his hands helplessly. “What other choice do I have?”

“Very few.” Ironwine went quiet for a moment. Dark eyes turned inward. "Learn to stand swiftly, Harker Shoalborn. Only then can you swim."

The mist thickened, rolling over their skiff in curtains large enough to obscure Ironwine ten feet in front of him. Yet with that thickness came a burgeoning light. It bloomed within the fog like an amber sunrise, until a lamppost materialized out of the horizon. It arced up over the mist, illuminating their approach and the ancient boards of a single, long dock.

The river sloshed, its slow waves washing against the water weed covered posts. Ironwine poled them closer until, with a single massive claw, he latched onto the nearest board and pulled their small craft abreast. The waves lifted and dropped them, slow and steady, but the Thrysalid held fast.

“We have arrived,” Ironwine said.

Harker stood up, ignoring the faint twinge in his thighs. Carefully, and a little wobbly, he climbed onto the docks. Straightening, he checked over himself and his belongings. His pouches, weapons, and pack were all there, wrapped in the tattered cloak.

He was ready.

"Hold, human."

Harker turned back to find Ironwine leaning over the dock, head nearly level with Harker’s own. Heavy iron claws clung to the docks, and black eyes watched him from beneath inscrutible antennae. “Take this.”

A small hand stretched out, depositing a metal coin into Harker’s hand. It was circular, small enough to fit in his palm, and black as pitch.

"What is it?" Harker lifted it higher. It didn’t reflect the amber light, even when rotated. It was like holding a piece of the empty sky. “Why are the edges sharpened?”

"I am uncertain. Sometimes I know what must be Made," Ironwine said, "and other times the Making knows itself."

Harker lowered the coin. "I don't understand.”

“That will come with time, as all things must.”

“What do I do with it?"

The tendrils that looked so much like a beard beneath the Thrysalid’s eyes and nose parted, revealing a lower face formed of dark iron. A chiseled jaw, chin, and perfect lips spread wide around dark teeth. "Care for it."

Harker had to force himself not to take a step back. "I will."

The tendrils returned, and the Thrysalid leaned back into his boat. "Find the Hammer. I have no doubt that he will aid your next steps. May the Stars light your path, Harker Shoalborn.”

“Fair winds, Ironwine.”

The Thrysalid shoved off, and the mist immediately swallowed him and his boat. Soon the slosh of water was the only indication he even existed, until even that faded. For the first time in months, Harker was alone.

"All right, Sunken City," he muttered, hiking his pack onto a shoulder. "Let's see what you have on offer."
