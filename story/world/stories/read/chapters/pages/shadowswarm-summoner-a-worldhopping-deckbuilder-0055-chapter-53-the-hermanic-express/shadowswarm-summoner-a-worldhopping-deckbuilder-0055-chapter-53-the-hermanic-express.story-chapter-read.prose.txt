Chapter 53: The Hermanic Express

The train left mid-morning, right on time. It turned out, the molekin were a very punctual people, though Ambrose didn’t know if it was a native quality of their culture or imposed on them by the Empire’s quotas. Of course, he and his friends were all ready and assembled ten minutes early, after getting a much more mild and palatable breakfast of toasted bread and eggs. Turns out they did have edible food, but they weren't exciting enough to bring to feasts, apparently.

At first, Ambrose was worried about privacy issues on the train. They weren’t the only ones headed to Bedrock Spire, but they were the only ones with a private carriage reserved for VIPs. It was large enough to fit the eight of them, and almost comfortably too.

The rest of the long train was dedicated to Crag’s shipments of raw ore, spider silk, and cards they’d harvested from the world. That had confused him at first, until Mulch clarified.

“We didn’t used to have cards,” he admitted shortly after the train left the station and took them down a claustrophobic tunnel out of town, towards the center of Stonskarr. “They showed up ‘bout five years before the expedition to seek the surface. That’s kind of where we got the idea, right? We found that the most cards were either really deep down, or really high up. We got curious, and started explorin’.

“And when the Empire came in, they were mostly interested in the cards and our resources, it turns out. We and the other villages harvest what we find, and then give them most of what they need from us. They mix it up, give some of it back, and take the rest for themselves. We’ve got some of our own food, but we gotta import more from them, and most of it comes from other places on Stonskarr.”

“Not unlike what they do on Amaria,” said Raeva, bristling. “Is it like that everywhere?”

“It seems that way,” said Luthor. He sat upright in his seat in the center of the carriage, hands in his lap. The dim lighting made his increasingly rough beard look unhinged. “As we’ve discussed, we’re going to set you and your friends up with as many combat cards as we can, but it takes time and experience to use them well and not lose to better, more skilled wizards. You have to keep that in mind.”

Mulch grunted disgruntledly. “Mychele and Roy’s people have a small collection, but not enough to do anything big or lasting. Get us some cards to give to them and we’ll find ways to fight back in our own way.”

It was all fine, for hypotheticals, but there was one niggling problem Ambrose felt they all forgot. “Are we really going to be discussing all of this in front of the prisoners?” He eyed Jack with the same distrust and contempt he’d felt from the beginning.

“Hey, I’m all for you guys now,” said Dexi’Lynn. She pumped a tiny, scaled fist into the air. “Go Team Rebellion!”

Despite the fact that their carriage was not only private, but had at least three carriages between them and the other passengers, Ambrose winced. “Could you not call us that? Especially not when around other people who might take issue with us. Like the kid who’s been threatening us from the moment we met him.”

Jack scowled back at him, but he wasn’t particularly intimidating. Mulch spoke up for them. “We’ve come to an agreement with him. He’ll be a good boy, won’t you, Jack?”

He looked like he wanted to spit acid at them, but his eyes dropped to his lap. “Yeah. I won’t say anything.”

“Is there anything you can do about it, Ambrose?” Jessica asked. “What about your contracts? Can you get him to sign something with penalties if he rats us out?”

“I already said I wouldn’t,” Jack protested, but no one was listening.

“No,” Ambrose said. “My [Contract] card is common, and can’t do anything worse than unpleasantness. And frankly, if he turns us in, we’re fucked. Making him unable to taste food or sleep well won’t really be much of a comfort if we’re dead or bound to a card. We could throw him out the window…”

Raeleq sighed and leaned forward. He and Mulch had the toughest time fitting in the seats and not taking up too much room. “That’s not who we are, and it never will be. He’s just a child. It’s a risk we’re going to have to deal with.”

“Unless you want to press the issue,” Raeva added, smiling unpleasantly. Dancing together had been fun, but seemed to end with her extra annoyed at him for some reason. It seemed to linger into today.

“No, I just wanted to raise my objections,” he said.

“Objection noted and filed,” said Luthor, almost smiling. “Now, moving on. According to Mulch, the train will leave for Crag six hours after we arrive in the city. It’s like clockwork. In that six hours, we need to find the Levi-Box, get as many decent cards as we can get, and hunker down and not cause trouble of any kind. It’s likely not going to be as easy as that sounds.”

“It doesn’t sound easy at all,” Jessica complained. “What happens if we face scrutiny?”

“We be as vague as possible, and lie our asses off the rest of the time,” said Ambrose, immediately. “Don’t say anything. In fact, if we spend our time being as anti-social as possible and focusing only on the shopping, we could probably slip in and out. Although…Think Bedrock Spire has like, real food?”

Mulch’s nose twitched in irritation. “Luthor and I was talkin’ about it, and we think we should split up into two groups to cover more ground. Bedrock Spire’s pretty big, even if we stick to the Hermanic quarter. We get what we need, slip away into less savory parts of the city, and then get back to Crag.”

Ambrose understood, but that didn’t mean he liked it. “And if there’s a fight of some kind? We had a tough enough time the past few days without fighting a legion of people who might be way stronger than us. What’s our play then?”

“Run like hell?” Mulch shrugged. “It’s dangerous either way. Didn’t figger you for a coward, Ambrose.”

“I’m not a coward. I’m just not the kind of fool to get trapped by a spider and then mistake stupidity for bravery..”

“No,” said Raeva, “you’re the type of fool to try to steal from our world’s most powerful wizard, and get us all exiled.”

For a second, the only sound was the gentle, consistent chug of the train, the gentle rocking as it powered through the rock tunnel. Then Raeleq laughed, as he did more and more at Ambrose’s expense. One by one, everyone else joined in, until even Ambrose smiled.

“Yeah, yeah. You know, part of me hopes that we do get caught and killed, if only so that you stop throwing that in my face.”

“I gotta hear this story,” Dexi’Lynn said, golden eyes glittering with interest. “I’ve only caught bits and pieces.”

Jessica smiled and nudged the kobold with her shoulder. “I’ll tell you later, once we’re back at the ship.”

“SO,” Ambrose said, moving on, “we get to Bedrock Spire, we split up, we go shopping and avoid any potential scrutiny. Is there anything else we need to know, or are we going to spend the trip in awkward silence?”

“It’s only awkward if you make it awkward,” said Raeleq peaceably. “But I suppose you do make everything awkward. With your bad attitude and rudeness.”

Again they laughed. Ambrose just shook his head in exasperation. It was kind of funny. Just a week ago, he would’ve been irritated at being the butt everyone’s jokes…but even he knew he had it coming, and there were no real teeth to any of it. It almost felt like they were starting to get on his level and have fun with being jerks.

The trip continued on, but not in silence. Mulch asked about their home, and they spent a good hour laughing and talking about Rentan Academy. For as bad as their exit had been, there were three years of mostly good memories. Not just of classes and exceeding their previous limits, but the softer, quieter moments around town, in apartments, at bars.

The combination of structure and relative freedom had been so good, and now, facing the exact opposite, Ambrose realized how affected they all were. Now, they had only what plans they came up with, and freedom hinged on their ability to evade detection. Not for the first time, despair punched him in the gut, whispering all the bad things that would happen to them when their time ran out.

He thought of his family, all their families. They didn’t talk about it much, but in the quiet moments, were they dreading what was happening back home just as much as he was? It would’ve been a difficult subject to broach, even if Ambrose hadn’t been…well, him.

They talked at length, while he fell silent, not quite sure how to process it all, and annoyed that it gave him trouble at all.

About an hour and a half into the ride, just as things fell silent again, the train started to shake and the lights flickered.

“What’s wrong?” Luthor asked, immediately alert.

Mulch just laughed. “Nothing, this means we’re getting close. I know there ain’t been much to see, what with the tunnel being dark and all, but you’re in for a treat. I don’t love the Empire, but even I gotta admit they build real well.”

The shaking got worse for a second, and then all of a sudden they were out of the tunnel and in an impossibly open cavern, miles wide. Even more incredibly, they could see almost all of the cavern in crystal clean detail. As dark as Stonskarr tended to be, the area around Bedrock Spire was as lit up as the Amaric capital of Centrago at night.

More than that, it was populated.

The train tracks continued for several miles forward, suspended over a drop of hundreds of feet. Along the edges of the cavern, much like in Crag, homes were built directly into the rock face, on tiered terraces jutting out of the mountain. Hundreds, thousands of them, thick on the bottom and narrowing out to rings of single homes along the top. Impossibly tall, long stairways split up the homes every so often, forming a sort of inverted hive.

And that was just the area around the city.

Bedrock Spire itself…Ambrose had no idea what he was expecting, with a contradictory name like that. The city was indeed much lower than all the raised housing, a monstrosity of rock, stone, and metal forming something halfway between an intimidating fortress and the mountain city of Espoza back on Amaria. A lone tower stretched high into the cavern, looking down on the city and on even level with most of the far off houses. More than that, it was suspended above a sea of magma, giving the bottom of the city an ominous, reddish glow.

The train tracks, when they pulled into the station, would put them about halfway to the top. Far off, Ambrose saw other train tracks at the same elevation, forming a great wheel with each track being a spoke, leading to the center of Stonskarr, and the Empire’s power here.

“Ancestors…” Raeleq said, letting out a sharp exhalation.

He wasn’t alone. Just about everyone had a reaction to suddenly seeing the city. Ambrose himself wanted to act unimpressed or above it, but he couldn’t. It was easily more impressive than Centrago had ever been, even if it was uglier by nature.

Dexi’Lynn let out a high pitched cackle. “It’s always so fuckin’ funny, see how people react. You starting to get it now?”

Yes, Ambrose realized, he was starting to get it. Stonskarr may have been a small, provincial world in comparison, but the Empire’s strength was anything but. Despite all their cockiness and jokes, the danger was real, and it was now upon them.
