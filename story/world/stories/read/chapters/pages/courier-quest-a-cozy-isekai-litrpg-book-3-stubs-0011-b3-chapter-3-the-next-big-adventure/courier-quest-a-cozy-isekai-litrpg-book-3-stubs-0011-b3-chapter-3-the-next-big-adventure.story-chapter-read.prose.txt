Adabelle was a sight to see. Her white dress fit her form, hugging her from chest to hips, only flaring out when it reached her knees. She wore long, lacy gloves that almost climbed all the way to her shoulders, detached from her sleeves. A delicate ribbon decorated with rainbow scales was wrapped around her uninjured horn along with a silver bell.

Her thick, white hair was in a long braid laced with pale peonies. It was worn over the shoulder, settling on her chest. She had opted not to wear a veil, as it was custom in her family not to. Instead, a crown of roses and peonies adorned her head.

A sash around her waist was weighed down by a sword. The plain, straight blade was sheathed in a white scabbard with a green and orange gemstone at its throat, mimicking the eye colors of her and her groom. Trevor didn’t know if that was a common decoration for warrior women at their wedding but, now that he saw it, he couldn’t imagine it any other way.

The swordswoman walked with practiced grace and poise, looking everything like a leopard ready to strike whenever it struck her fancy. Her bell made no noise, and there was no doubt that any ringing would be heard over the hush of the crowd.

Trevor took her all in. He was fully aware of the grin on his face, likely appearing goofy enough that Lailah would tease him later, but he didn’t care. Adabelle was beauty incarnate in her wedding dress, and he was glad that he didn’t have to say anything just yet. The courier couldn’t trust his tongue at the moment.

Her glowing orange eyes were on him and him alone. She wore what he thought might have been a similar smile, though it was certainly tempered compared to his own.

Wayne delivered Adabelle to the gazebo, helping her up the steps. She hugged him tightly, whispering something into his ear, before pulling free with a smile. The dwarf nodded, not saying anything, but as he turned to walk to his seat Trevor could see him dab at his eyes.

That couldn’t keep his attention long, however. Adabelle stood across from him. His eyes locked with hers. It took a few seconds to register the fact that Lailah had started talking.

“Thank you all for coming. We are here on this beautiful day to celebrate the unity of not only two families, but two worlds,” Lailah said, her voice easily carrying over the island. “A twist of fate, whether by design or happenstance, have brought these two together. Trevor Anderson, born of Earth, and Lady Adabelle Ramansa Katine, born of Halvsar, both found each other here in Tosa.

“Despite their differences in birthplace, culture, and station, or perhaps because of it, love has blossomed between this pair of outworlders. Their hearts and souls are already one, united by that feeling, and they are ready to recognize it today, just as we are here to witness.”

Lailah smiled as she looked between Adabelle and Trevor, who still hadn’t taken their eyes off of each other. “Marriage is the next big adventure. You will face challenges that will test your resolve and creativity together. You will learn and explore the future together. There will be nothing that you won’t be able to accomplish, so long as you do it together.

“Because marriage will enhance you. Not only as a couple, but as individuals. It is an experience that you have already begun to partake in, and it will only become grander from here on out. Knowing Adabelle’s indomitable spirit and Trevor’s dedication to the people around him, I am sure they have the strength, patience, and sense of humor required for a long, successful union.”

Shifting some papers on her clipboard, Lailah retrieved one of the pages she had earmarked. “If there are any who oppose this marriage, object now or forever hold your peace.”

This was something that came from both world’s ceremonies. At first, Lailah hadn’t wanted to include it, but she was pressed by the bride and thus added it at the last minute.

In response to the call for objections, Adabelle moved her hand onto the pommel of her sword. The threat was clear; any who objected would have to deal with terrifying consequences.

Trevor barely noticed the move. His eyes were still on hers. They hadn’t strayed an inch.

The crowd was predictably silent, and Lailah continued. Once she began speaking again, Adabelle’s hand rejoined the other in front of her.

“I was asked by Lady Adabelle to share an excerpt from Summoner Jackson’s personal musings, which she inherited when she became the mayor of Tosa,” the elf continued, holding up the page. Trevor perked up. He hadn’t known about this part. “Being summoned into this world is a lonely endeavor. Everyone deals with it in their own way. Some become adventurers, using their strength to protect their new home from dangers both seen and unseen. Others become couriers, serving needs more directly. Both care deeply, no matter how they express it.

“When two worlds collide in such a harmonious way, it’s a beautiful thing. Personalities dance, reacting and shaping the movements of both. Companionship leads to love. Whether it’s platonic, like one of everlasting friendship, or something much deeper, like one I have seen bloom time and time again, it reminds me that loneliness is not the default state of life. One shouldn’t be ashamed to seek out others for comfort, especially if they are Summoned. Those who have found their chosen family, no matter how big or small, traditional or strange, will find peace throughout their days. I wish all those who arrive here to find some measure of paradise, so long as they do it together.”

Trevor’s smile softened at the words of Adabelle’s old mentor. Jackson was gone now, sent off into the next world with a ritual, but he had been good to them. He had also, apparently, known that they would fall in love. His Power, Bookmark, allowed him to visit any place the people he summoned would go while giving him small snippets of their lives.

Some might have called that an invasion of privacy, but Adabelle had given Trevor his own book to peruse. There really wasn’t much other than some vague lines about events that had already happened or feelings felt in the moment. Certainly nothing scandalous.

Lailah put the page underneath the others as she looked up at the crowd. “Trevor and Lady Adabelle have decided to write their own vows, which they will now deliver over a pair of oathing stones.”

Turning to face his best man, Trevor took a pair of stones from Klar. They were smooth aside from where the bride and groom’s names were carved into it along with a type of Celtic knot he didn’t recognize. Something Lailah had assured them was meant to set their vows in stone. It wasn’t a tradition he had known of, but he was excited for it nonetheless.

With a stone in each palm, he reached out for Adabelle. She put her hands in his, one over and one under, and smiled.

“If the groom will start,” Lailah prompted.

Trevor nodded, and then hesitated. He had practiced his vows over and over again, making sure they were the best he could come up with, only to blank out when he finally needed them. Taking a breath, he opened his mouth anyway. Either he would speak from the heart or it would come to him, whichever would work.

“I... have become something of a traveler in this world, to my surprise,” he started, fumbling only slightly. “No matter where I go, though, I know that there is a place to call home waiting for me. That place isn’t Tosa, or even Lucent. It’s wherever you are, Adabelle. You are what grounds me, keeping my head from the clouds. You are my lighthouse in the storm.”

Trevor’s smile grew as he found his groove. “You’ve become the most important person in my life. Looking back at our journey's prickly beginnings, to our budding courtship surrounded by lights, to that first proclamation of love, this has been the destination I’ve yearned for most. Standing up here with you, ready to tell the world, or a small selection of friends, how I really feel about you.

“Though I may wander and travel, I promise to always return to your side. I will always treasure your presence and miss it terribly when we aren’t together. It is with my whole heart that I say that I’ve never been happier, in this world or the last, than when I’m with you. I vow that I will spend the rest of my life enriching yours, just as you have effortlessly done for me. To me, these are not just promises made to you, but privileges I look forward to for the rest of my days.”

“Beautiful,” Lailah said as she turned to Adabelle. “And the bride.”

She didn’t hesitate. “Where I grew up, marriage was always supposed to be something political for someone like me,” the noblewoman started calmly, her voice flowing with ease. “Imagine my surprise when I was whisked away to a place where such a thing no longer mattered. Where I could choose my friends, my family, and, most importantly, who to love. For a long time, I thought myself that same noblewoman who didn’t have a choice. In a way, I still am, though it’s no longer the same as it was back then.

“Because loving you, son of Ander, is not a choice for me. It’s a necessity. An irresistible need. One that I allow myself to fall deeper into every day if only because I know you will always be there to catch me. Whether it’s something as simple as holding me when I’m feeling frustrated, or providing feedback when I need solutions, you have always come when I called for you. It doesn’t matter how far you are, I know you would be by my side the moment you know I need you.”

Trevor couldn’t help but nod, and Adabelle smiled. “You promise to always return home, to the place where I am. I hear your vow, and return it as such. Wherever I am, wherever we decide to lay roots, I will do everything in my power to make it a place worthy of us. No matter how far you range, there will always be more wonders for you to return to. My duties define me by my own choice, but you will always be first in my eyes. We shall share the privilege of enriching each other’s lives for the rest of our days.”

The courier bit the inside of his cheek, trying not to tear up. It was a futile effort, though, and he had to lift his arm without letting go of her hand to wipe his eyes. He could see that Lailah had to hold back a comment. She was sure to tease him later, but for now he enjoyed her professionalism.

“You have heard their vows and promises, and as witnesses carry them with you just as the oathing stones do,” the elf said, addressing the audience. Trevor took the twin stones and placed them in his inventory for the moment. “Now, Klarkartar, the rings if you please.”

“It pleases me a great deal,” the merchant prince stated as he fished them out of his pockets. He winked at the groom as he handed them over.

Lailah continued. “Do you, Trevor, take Lady Adabelle to be your lawfully wedded wife, to have and to hold, in sickness and in health, in good times and bad, for richer or poorer, for as long as you both shall live?”

“I do,” he immediately replied.

She turned to the bride. “Do you, Lady Adabelle, take Trevor to be your lawfully wedded husband, to have and to hold, in sickness and in health, in good times and bad, for richer or poorer, for as long as you both shall live?”

“I do,” she said without hesitation.

“Then, Trevor and Adabelle, as you place your rings on each other’s fingers, repeat after me.”

He passed over the band she would place on his finger, and they slid the two onto the other’s together, repeating the words.

“I give you this ring as a pledge of my love,” Trevor and Adabelle said as one. “With it comes the promise that I will support you today, tomorrow, always, and forever. From here on, we meet the world together as equals and partners.”

“By the power vested in me by the King of Eskretet and the Quest Master, I now pronounce you officially married,” Lailah announced happily. “Trevor, you may now kiss the bride.”

Taking a step forward, he embraced Adabelle. They gave each other one last look of longing before sharing a sweet kiss, with the promise of many more to come.

“Friends and family, it is my honor and privilege to introduce for the first time, Trevor Anderson and Lady Adabelle Ramansa Anderson-Katine gen Tosa!”

The crowd cheered, but the newlywed couple didn’t hear them. They were immersed in each other, the world quiet as they shared their love.
