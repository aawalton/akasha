Vin blinked, only partially surprised to find himself not only in control of his body again, but standing within that familiar dark void once more. The God of Contracts had warned him he would bind Vin as many times as it took if he continued using his connection with mana to escape his contract, so this was to be expected.

What wasn’t, however, was the fact that Ovord was standing beside him.

As were each and every one of his friends.

“Woah,” Scule said. Rubbing his eyes, the petian looked around into the endless, yawning void surrounding them. “…Did we die?”

“No. I’ve been here before,” Shia said, her brow furrowing. “This is where I met with the God of Devotion to receive my divine boon.”

Reginald squeaked and nodded, confirming that he’d been in a similar location when he’d met with the Goddess of Loyalty. Yet before Vin could translate, Lumel gasped.

“Alka!”

Sure enough, rather than her golem body, Alka stood before them in the flesh for the very first time. The Slayer’s short-cut hair and wiry muscles looked just like they had back when she’d been a ghost, and she was currently rubbing at her chest with a weird expression on her face.

“Ugh. I feel like I ate too much, too fast. Which is weird, seeing as I haven’t eaten anything in almost a year.”

“Why are we seeing Alka’s old body?” Scule asked. “Even if we connected her back to the System, she’d still be a golem.”

“In this place, you see a reflection of a person’s soul,” Vin explained, holding up his half-missing left arm. “It’s why I’m still missing an arm in here. That epic monster from the third wave did some serious damage.”

“I feel as though for the sake of politeness, I should introduce myself before we are all killed,” Ovord said. Pressing a fist to his chest, he bowed his head. “I am Ovord, Arch Mage of the Soul and vassal to Vin.”

“Vassal?” Shia asked, raising an eyebrow.

“Killed?!” Scule asked, focusing on a very different part of that statement. “What do you mean, killed?”

“Is it not obvious? I assume we have angered the Gods and they are coming to execute us,” Ovord said simply, as though he was discussing the weather. “I will do what I can to delay them, but I doubt I will be able to buy more than a second or two in my weakened state. If I were whole again and the rest of you were in at least your third prestige, then perhaps—”

“That is enough,” a powerful voice boomed, and Ovord stopped talking when Contracts manifested before all of them. The God looked angrier than ever, and even the chains covering his body seemed to show it. Rather than sit still, many of them were actively sliding along his body or hovering around his form, like a swarm of living snakes just waiting to shoot forward and bite an unsuspecting victim. “Ovord, you will speak no further on such subjects. You know as well as I do they are not ready.”

“Contracts. I see you are well,” Ovord said casually, causing everyone other than the God to stare at the soul mage in shock. “How—”

A chain shot forward from Contracts, aimed straight for Ovord’s chest. In response, a shadow erupted out of the void in turn, crashing into the glowing chain and actually managing to shift the chain’s trajectory by the barest of inches. It wasn’t nearly enough to stop Contracts, however, and the chain slammed into the soul mage. Almost instantly, Ovord was wrapped from head to toe in glowing metal, leaving only his eyes exposed as he was held in place by the angry God.

“I said enough,” Contracts said, turning his ire on the rest of them. Scule was currently shaking so hard that Vin feared he might have a heart attack. Shia and Lumel were wary but determined, each of them looking prepared to try using their magic if it came to it. Reginald was standing between Scule and the God, barring his teeth in warning. And Alka had her fists raised, a weak but determined grin on her face as she prepared for anything.

But before the powerful God could do whatever it was he was planning to do to them, three more people manifested out of the void, one of which Vin knew quite well at this point.

“Contracts! Stay your hand,” Benevolence ordered. Her gaze was hard as she stepped forward to place herself between the irate God and them. “You know as well as I do these mortals have broken no rules. Vin hasn’t even broken the unjust contract you continue forcing upon him.”

“Their only crime is that of being loyal to their friends,” a woman in a brilliant red robe stated, a soft smile on her face as she moved to stand between Reginald and Contracts. “You cannot fault them for that.”

“If anything, their devotion should be rewarded,” a man wearing golden chainmail made of millions of miniature links stated, moving to stand in front of Shia and shield her with his armor-clad body. He held twin swords in each hand that mirrored one another flawlessly, and he pointed them both toward Contracts. “They had no way of knowing what would happen to themselves, yet they moved to help regardless.”

“What the hells is going on?!” Scule stammered, his bulging eyes bouncing from God to Goddess as he took in the four divine beings before them.

“The six of you managed to do something that should not have been possible,” Benevolence answered, not taking her eyes off Contracts as if waiting for those other chains of his to burst into action. “Usually, we are only able to talk to Vin while using his divine boon to repair his body. But due to the mana currently linking all of you together, an exception was able to be made. Despite the fact that none of you have managed to hit your third prestige just yet, you managed to come together and influence the System directly. A feat we believed to be impossible.”

“To be fair, they did have the assistance of a former Candidate. And the mana itself,” the Goddess in the red robe laughed, her robe swirling and burning like living fire as she did. From the way she stood between Reginald and Contracts, Vin could only assume she was the Goddess of Loyalty who Reginald had gained his boon from. Though he was far more interested in what she’d just said than who she was.

“Candidate?” Vin asked, turning to look at Ovord’s bound form. The soul mage’s silver eyes were currently staring daggers at Contracts, as if he had a personal vendetta against the God for some reason. That look combined with the fact that Ovord acted as if he knew Contracts and what the new Goddess had just said was more than enough for Vin to put the pieces together, and he gasped. “Ovord was on track to become one of the Gods, wasn’t he?”

At his words, Benevolence finally tore her eyes away from Contracts, beaming at him with a gaze so powerful that for a moment Vin thought he was staring into the sun.

“This is why I am acting,” Contracts said, his voice low and dangerous. “We all agreed to the same terms when we created this new System. No variations. No fluctuations. We need to ensure that this new world and System goes smoothly. There is no fallback plan if this one fails like the last. We won’t have enough time to create another. Due to a number of outside forces, these six have touched upon powers and answers they should not have. The withered remains of a former Candidate who slipped through the cracks during our haste to create the new universe. A divine prodigy who discovered a way to get around our defenses and communicate, even if only for a few minutes at a time. They’ve drawn the attention of the mana itself, and even gained the ability to pierce Beyond the Veil, which we thought we’d removed from the System entirely! They are too dangerous to the System as a whole to allow them to remain!”

“Wait, you all know about what my master’s been doing?” Shia gasped, staring up at who could only be the God of Devotion in shock. “Can you bring him here?!”

“You see? She has been actively working toward breaking this new world’s boundaries since the very beginning!” Contracts boomed. His chains seemed to triple in size as they shook back and forth and the void trembled. “There are rules that must be followed! Terms which we all agreed to that you are now trying to bypass!”

“You always were a bit of a stickler for the rules,” Benevolence said, her eyes glowing with fire as she turned to grin at Contracts. “The world isn’t so black and white as your beloved contracts that you love to go on and on about. There are nuances and surprises that not even we can predict. Sometimes you just have to roll with the punches.”

“You would risk the lives of every single soul on Edregon,” Contracts said, looking from one God to another as if he couldn’t comprehend why they were standing against him. “If they are not stopped here, there is no telling what they might do. They touched the nucleus. The very soul of the new System itself. Had they made even the slightest mistake, there is no telling what damage they may have caused.”

“They had Ovord’s skilled hand and the will of the mana guiding them,” Benevolence argued. “If anything, you yourself put us all at risk when you tried to halt them at the last second. If the mana hadn’t devoured your chains and Vin was forced to take over, what do you think would have happened?”

“At best an explosion of mana powerful enough to wipe out all of Terra in the blink of an eye,” Devotion said, causing Vin to pale slightly. He hadn’t realized that was a possibility, or he would have had them conduct the soul surgery a little bit further away than the basement of town hall.

“And if that were to happen, all of Edregon would most likely collapse anyway without the Earthers acting as support,” Loyalty added, giving Vin a clear wink before continuing. “The only other surviving settlement is far weaker than Terra is, even with their stolen reinforcements from the last wave bolstering their number.”

“Wait, there are other Earthers?” Lumel asked, looking at Loyalty in confusion. “And how are they important to the survival of Edregon?”

“Do you think I don’t see what is going on here?” Contracts hissed, causing all of them to jump when his chains began to grow brighter and brighter in light until they were blinding to look at. “Even now, you take advantage of this confrontation to break the rules. I have every right to banish you back to the old universe and be rid of you once and for all.”

“You could… except for the fact that Edregon has yet to bring up any Candidates capable of filling our roles,” Benevolence said with a sickeningly sweet smile. “The old universe has fallen to such a state of corruption that you can’t appoint anyone from there, and the Candidates brought over to Edregon and quarantined as an experiment remain incompatible with the new System. That means at the moment, you can’t touch us.”

Contracts froze, his body and chains all halting in place as though he’d turned into a statue. Ever so slowly, the blinding light of his chains began to diminish, until his chains gave off nothing more than a soft glow. The God took one deep breath, and then another, seeming to do his best to curb his temper. Vin didn’t know what ‘roles’ a God had to fulfil, but it seemed as though they had to be important indeed.

“You are correct,” he said, narrowing his eyes at the three Gods who blatantly opposed him. “For now. I will refrain from killing these six mortals. Though only if they swear to never again interact with the new System like they just did. I am done attempting to force contracts upon those who have the favor of the mana. If they decide to go against this order… I will simply kill them and suffer the consequences.”

With that, Contracts turned and vanished, the dark void surrounding them suddenly feeling a whole lot darker in his absence. Silence stretched across the void for the briefest of moments, before Scule cried out again.

“Would somebody tell me what the hells is going on?!”
