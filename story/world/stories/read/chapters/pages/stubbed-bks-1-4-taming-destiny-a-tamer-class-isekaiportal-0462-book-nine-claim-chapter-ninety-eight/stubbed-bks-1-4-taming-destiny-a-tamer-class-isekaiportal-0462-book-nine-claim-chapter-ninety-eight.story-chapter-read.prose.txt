My tense statement sends a ripple of horror through those in the chamber with me, hands tightening on weapons, faces hardening. Although everyone had to know that Thiakar is infected, given the context, somehow the Tier four actually managing to speak through him brings it home in a way none of us were expecting. It proves just how much the hunter has lost – and makes us question whether anything of him is actually salvageable.

My own hand clenches. I won’t consider that. I know better than anyone else how deeply a parasite can embed itself into a person’s systems. And yet a Battle of Wills proved that their souls and consciousness persisted.

Even while I open my mouth to speak to the Tier four, my mind races with ways to save Thiakar.

“Why should we not kill you?” I ask it as levelly as I can. “What kind of use could you be?”

Thiakar tilts his head on one side, the gesture unnerving, accompanied as it is by an unchanging blank expression.

“You have seen what I am capable of.”

“Yes,” I agree. “And nothing of it makes me want to leave you alive to continue.”

“What if that was at your command?” I stay silent and, a moment later, it continues monotonously. “An army of minions at your disposal. Unfeeling. Unthinking. Unable to refuse you. You have destroyed or released all of the powerful beasts I had converted, but with time, I – we – could build an even greater army. No territory would be out of our reach.”

It goes silent again, perhaps to make me consider what I could do with an army like that. I appreciate the pause – it gives me the time I need to control my immediate emotions and keep my expression blank. I don’t know the extent of its ability to dig into Thiakar’s memories – it can obviously use his understanding of language; for all I know it can use the same to read people’s expressions even if it isn’t using them.

I can imagine the picture the Tier four is trying to paint. With enough Tier threes, we could steamroll over the continent, defeating even other Tier fours – and perhaps adding them to our ranks. We could certainly overcome the other competitors – we’ve experienced the power of a beast army that has no sense of self-preservation and seeks only to inflict as many injuries and casualties as it can. Even when individual beasts aren’t powerful, the sheer attrition rate is challenging to overcome. We only won in the last battle because our tactics were superior – and if the army followed our commands, that wouldn’t be relevant.

“What do you get out of the arrangement?” I ask it neutrally.

Markus, you’re not considering this, are you? Valence asks with a hint of betrayal.

It has some tactical benefits, Layton muses.

For now, I just ignore both of them, looking at the Tier four over Thiakar’s shoulder – the one I’m truly speaking to.

“I do not wish to die,” it tells me emotionlessly.

“You said that,” I remind it. “But that cannot be the only reason you are making the offer.”

It is silent for a moment, then Thiakar nods slowly, his face still that eerie smooth blankness.

“All I wish is to grow. To multiply. I do not care what I do as long as that takes place.”

I nod in return, my suspicions confirmed. My lips thin, my sheer revulsion at its offer breaking through my facial control.

Because the thing is that, technically, the beast isn’t offering me anything I don’t already have. I hate to think it, but, at its base, my Dominate Skill is little different from how the Tier four wields its parasites.

At my current Willpower level, if I don’t try to do anything else strenuous, I could hold four or five unwilling Tier threes. I could hold far more Tier twos, and an army of Tier ones would hardly even challenge me. And I’m only at level thirty-four.

Which is why what the Tier four is offering is so repulsive to me. There’s a reason why I’m not currently fielding an army of beasts who are unable to resist my commands, even if it means their own deaths – and it’s not because I couldn’t; it’s because I don’t want to. That’s not who I am.

I might have to make hard decisions at times – I’ve already overstepped the lines I set for myself when I first learned what Dominate could do. Multiple times, even. But that was always with what I would consider to be a good reason – to save their lives, or to save the people under my protection.

This…. Even if I could argue that sending a vanguard of cannon fodder to overwhelm my opponent would save my own people, it would only help in the short term. I learned that lesson well with my first Bonded – protecting people too much can actively hurt them in the end. Everyone needs to grow; this world respects strength alone.

My eyes rake once more over the featureless red blob of opaque slime looming behind Thiakar, every iota of my being screaming at me to kill it and wipe this whole place clean with so much fire that the very stone melts.

Because even if I were tempted by the possibilities the beast offers – which I’m not – it’s not as if I could guarantee that it kept its word. I know that some Titanbends have developed Dominate to not require eye contact, but I’ve never managed to do so. Besides, the strength of its domain suggests that I wouldn’t be able to Bind it unwillingly anyway.

It seems more likely that, far from being content to be under my command, it would just use me to build its army – quite probably slipping its parasites into my own people in the meantime. The chances of betrayal from this beast are incredibly high, especially given its stated objective in life – to grow unrestricted, unstopped. Any obedience it offered me would be temporary at best.

Prepare to attack, I warn everyone I can mentally.

Wait, you’re not going to agree to its offer? Valence asks, sounding a little as though he has whiplash.

Of course I’m not, I tell him impatiently. Now, Layton, work with Mage Vellus and Maxen to deal with the animals in the walls. Valence, lead everyone else to hit the Tier four itself. Bastet, target the Tier four with the Warriors; the rest of you help deal with the animals. I’ll take care of Thiakar. When we’re done with our tasks, we help the others.

Understood, Layton confirms, and the others in my Bond network send their own acknowledgments.

On three, I start counting, while pretending to still be thinking. Two.

Markus, just…if you can save Thiakar…. Valence reaches out.

I’ll do my best, I assure him. One. Go!

Chaos immediately breaks out as Valence and Layton shout out my orders to those not part of the Bond network. Within seconds, Skills and spells have started flying towards the Tier four while Mage Vellus shoots his jet of water at the walls, methodically clearing section by section of slime and revealing the beasts hidden behind for Layton, Maxen, and some physical fighters to deal with. Skills enhance weapons with different shades of light, physical shields raise and magical ones shimmer into place. The temperature starts to shift unpredictably as Layton uses it to aid his allies.

Meanwhile Valence leads the charge to attack the Tier four itself, his greatsword raised, his face set in a mask of determination. I’m aware of Storm’s Shadow leaping forwards into the fray even as Lathani suddenly disappears from my side.

And then the word disappears into grey around me as I catch Thiakar’s eyes and trigger Dominate.

As a Hunter, Thiakar has more Willpower than the average human, but it’s still nothing in comparison to mine. I run forwards, knowing there’s no time to waste.

What’s happening? Thiakar asks weakly, confusion and fear rippling around him as I reach halfway between us. Grateful as I am that he still exists even under the parasite’s extensive influence, it does complicate things a little.

“I’m sorry – I have no time to explain,” I apologise, closing the distance. “I swear I’ll free you as soon as I can.”

Free me? Wait – don’t–

But it’s too late. I’ve triggered the Bond. Seconds after having entered it, the Battle of Wills space fractures around me.

Since I’ve been gone, the chaos has only got worse – spells and Skills fly through the air, providing almost more light than Aingeal. Meanwhile beasts pour into the chamber from the holes in the walls, the Tier four holding nothing back. There are even more entrances than I expected – the walls are riddled with them. But though there are some Tier twos, most of the beasts are Tier ones and those with me are high enough level that it’s becoming a slaughter.

The Tier four is another question. Though it seems to have few offensive abilities of its own, I can see how difficult it is to injure it – attacks sink in without leaving a mark, and slime that is separated from its body reattaches more often than not.

But my companions are determined and, with as many attacks as are landing on it every second, they’re making progress. When the lower-Tier beasts have been dealt with, we’ll be able to whittle its health down faster – as long as our resources last longer than its own.

And in the middle of it all, Thiakar, pulling a bow out of his Inventory and nocking an arrow to it, his eyes on Valence.

Stop! I command him and he freezes in place, a tremor wracking his limbs, ripples under his skin as if his very blood is trying to rebel. Confusion and unease crash through the Bond.

Then his face crunches up in the first expression I’ve seen on him today – pain. The skin on his head is reddening, like all his blood has been diverted there. My eyes wide, I remember exactly what the parasites try to do when all hope is lost.

The battle ranges around me, far more important than one man. Yet I disregard that, charging forwards and grabbing Thiakar’s head with one hand. With the other, I wield my trusty knife, slicing across his throat.

Then, ignoring his terrified choking and diving into his head, I grab the parasite – bigger than any I’ve seen in a human, though not quite as big as in some of the Tier threes – wrap it in magic, and tug it out through the gaping slash.

Dropping it to the ground, I quickly pull my last healing potion out of my Inventory and tip it into the wound on Thiakar’s throat. It heals enough that I feel safe leaving him for now – I need my mana.

“What…?” he asks weakly, slumping against me, shocked numbness replacing the maelstrom of emotions previously present.

“Sorry, still no time to explain,” I apologise then, after a moment of hesitation, I knock him out with a quick pulse of magic, then pull him into my tattoo storage. I can’t spare the focus to catch him up on everything, even mentally, and I don’t want him panicking.

Thanks to the Stealth I engaged before starting the Battle of Wills and the general chaos, I’ve fortunately gone unnoticed. Narrowing my eyes, I assess the battle and where I would be most useful.

Layton, Vellus, Maxen and my companions have the beast flood under control – no more are coming from the walls and those that are in the cave are rapidly being cut down.

Nodding to myself, I pull out my bow and start shooting poisoned arrows into the Tier four. It might be slime, but I know from experience that it’s still flesh. If I can build up enough poison in it, its regeneration rate will eventually be overwhelmed.

Together, we whittle down the parasite controller. Little by little, I see progress – more and more of the slime that is cut away from it remains inert on the ground while its form becomes smaller and smaller.

It’s not all going our way – the Tier four fights back with what it can. Slime constricts around the legs of those closest, and waves of parasites pulse towards anyone pulled down to the ground, seeking to infest them through any open wound or orifice – or failing that, drown them.

One unlucky fighter is tripped so he falls face-first into the slimeball – his legs kick frantically for the seconds it takes his allies to rip him back out and he hacks up red slime for several minutes afterwards. I make a mental note to check him over later – he has to have ingested some parasites there.

But the fighters quickly become wise to its tricks – everyone soon has a shield of some sort, their own or shared with an ally. The parasites find no entry and their controller becomes visibly more and more agitated, beginning to vibrate.

A cheer and pulse of victory comes from those dealing with the minions. I glance over to see the final beast falling to the ground, twitching weakly. With reinforcements coming to join us, the Tier four’s life has become drastically shorter.

Looking back at the much-reduced red lump, alarm flares through me as I see that its rate of vibration has increased drastically.

It’s not really going to…is it? I question for a moment, flabbergasted. It’s one thing for disposable parasites to do this, but the controller? But there isn’t any other explanation I can think of.

“Take cover! It’s going to blow!” I cry as loudly as I can, summoning my rock wings and charging forwards to set them in front of the closest group of mages and archers.

A moment later, we’re painted in red slime as the Tier four, indeed, explodes.

I carefully retract my wings to see nothing where the Tier four was.

Layton steps forward carefully, his eyes fixed on the remains, the slight hints of a hope in his expression.

“Is it–?”

He finishes with a wet-sounding gurgle as sharp claws suddenly emerge from his chest, glistening with his blood.
