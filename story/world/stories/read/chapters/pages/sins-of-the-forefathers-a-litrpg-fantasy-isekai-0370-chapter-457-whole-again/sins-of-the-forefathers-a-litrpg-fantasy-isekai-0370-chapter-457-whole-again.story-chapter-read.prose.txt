Oh, please, please let the surgery finish soon. I’m not sure just how much more of this I could take.

At least if I were awake, I could focus on something other than Middle and Core never shutting up.

It was hard to tell the passage of time in this strange, somewhat unexpected state of unconsciousness I had found myself in. I would almost equate it to how I had heard comas described, but only almost. I couldn’t perceive the outside world in any way, which I was grateful for. I didn’t need to feel whatever Rafael, Renauld, and Josha were doing to my body.

Who am I kidding? I know exactly what they’re doing.

First, they were, apparently, under Rafael’s lead, opening up my neck and sternum for surgery. Then, they were going to be carefully cleaning up whatever bone shards remained within the tissue from the loss of my original collarbones. Once the attachment points were ready, both on what remained of my shoulders as well as my sternum, they would be inserting the alloyed replacement clavicles I had forged in the back of a moving wagon. Then, under careful healing, they would graft those replacement bones to my skeleton.

At the same time, there was plenty of extremely delicate soul surgery needed to ensure the new bones weren’t rejected as replacements by my soul. The last time I had something like this done, I had done that part using Aetherial Melding. It had been very tricky work, although I had been much less skilled at the time.

I was just a bit put out that I wasn’t going to get the chance to try it again. Rafael had just steamrolled right over my objections and decided to do it all himself. I had no doubt he could do it…

But it was my body. I should get the chance to perform risky surgeries on myself if I wanted to damnit.

All that to say, I was essentially trapped in a formless mindscape of unconsciousness while being heckled by my Core and Middle Lattices.

Well, my Core was at least. It was always more of an asshole than Middle. Middle was more busy mulling over everything I had learned about the history of the gods, told to us by the apparent counterpart to Anima.

Someone whom she had never once breathed a word about to me. I…

I was really tempted to try and reach out to her. I had spoken to the Great Spirit of Life before, in a state much like this one. I was positive it could be done again. But that was probably a bad idea, even though I wanted to do it. I couldn’t sense it, but Rafael might have been poking about my soul right about now, grafting on two alloyed lengths of mixed Mithril and Oninite. Considering my link to Anima was attached to my soul, I wasn’t willing to add a single bit of potential instability to the mix.

I…would just have to find a moment later to get that done. But I was going to.

For all that we had shared together, and all we had promised each other…

I was a bit hurt that Anima had hidden the once existence of Hermus from me.

All three Lattices of my mind abruptly stopped their individual contemplations. I had sensed something. Something entirely welcome.

I was waking up. Slowly, a sense of awareness was starting to roll back over my mind, and it filled me with a sense of anticipation.

If I was waking up, that could only mean that the procedure was not only finished, but I had likely recovered enough from it to be allowed to. Everyone involved in the mission to make me whole again was perfectly capable of keeping me under until I was ready to awake. Hell, I could as well. I had learned enough Healing at the Academy to do that much, at least.

I waited eagerly as the waking world began to inch closer, and as it did…

My senses came with it.

All at once, the sensation of the world returned to me, and I could feel myself once again.

And boy did I feel like absolute shit. Every inch of my body felt weak and achy to a degree I rarely encountered. I was cold and numb for the most part, but there was this godawful ache around my upper chest that protested with every slight breath I took. My eyes were closed, but that was because they felt sensitive, like I would be blinded if I opened them.

It wasn’t helped by the fact that whatever I was lying on, it was hard as well, with no cushioning whatsoever.

No, on second thought…that wasn’t quite right. I think someone had put a small pillow under my head.

I just wish they had put a sheet or something over me, too.

As I lay there, trying to work up the will to even open my eyes, the ringing in my ears faded enough that I could hear anything else…

And what I heard was idle conversation, somewhere off above my head.

“…this was somewhere around, oh, six hundred years ago,” I heard a familiar, irritatingly casual voice say. “The woman and I had spent our younger years dancing around each other, denying there was any true interest. It was only after we had gained some distance from both my original Master and Greycton that we allowed ourselves to…experiment.”

“Is that what they called it back then?” I foggily heard someone who sounded like Renauld ask.

“Quiet,” I heard what I was sure was Sylvia scold. “I want to hear this.”

“Thank you, my dear. Now, Greycton was busy off doing his little…pirate thing, and so the woman and I had time to grow fond of each other. Fondess only escalated into love, and love into eventual union. The three of us didn’t see each other again for quite some time, not until the Sea Beast reared its very unfortunate head. Then, there we were all reunited in the combined force raised to combat the Calamity.”

“Just curious. Was the Headmaster already working with Shacklock at that point?”

“Shack-the Madman? I’m somewhat surprised you know that, child. I wasn’t aware that it was common knowledge that Greycton and the Madman had been comrades once upon an age. Their long-standing mutual hatred has existed for longer than some nations.”

I heard Renauld snort. “We heard it from the insane horse’s mouth, actually, Nate and I.”

A sigh was his answer. “So he still lives. Pity. I had hoped the Madman had perished while I was in my response.”

“About that…you might not have to wait much longer…”

Around the time Renauld started telling Rafael about the impending Core Collapse of Shacklock, I finally regained enough control over my body to slowly blink open my eyes and groan.

The conversation immediately stalled, and as the world started to swim into focus above me, I heard chairs being pushed back from a table. Moments later, a vaguely feminine, silvery form appeared in my field of vision.

The very first thing I truly saw upon awakening was Sylvia’s relieved smile beaming down at me.

You know what?

That was fine with me.

Unfortunately, the welcome sight was shortly ruined by the much more ghastly visage of Rafael appearing on the opposite side of my table. The Lich had drawn back his hood and was peering down at me with his leathery face.

“Ah, you’re awake.” Rafael clicked his tongue against the teeth still in his head. Honestly, a kind of impressive amount of dexterity for a walking corpse. “A bit behind schedule, honestly. Perhaps you should invest more in Vitality, Nathaniel? A bit of extra strength in your constitution wouldn’t go amiss.”

“Go…fuck…yourself,” I managed to croak out in between chapped lips and a tongue that felt as leathery as Rafael’s. Thankfully, my wonderful, sweet, divinely kind Sylvia immediately noticed and helped me to both sit up on the edge of the bed, as well as sip at a cup of water. I still felt as weak as a kitten, so I was leaning heavily on her as I did. As I tentatively wet my lips, I felt a furry head bump into my free hand, hanging off the side of the bed.

Looking down, I saw Fade waiting patiently on his haunches. Dutifully, I worked a finger under his own bandages between where his antlers had fallen out, and scratched an itch I was sure was there.

I heard a relieved sigh over our bond, followed by his voice. “You look like shit,” My Familiar cheerfully informed me.

In response, I lightly thumped the same spot with the same finger I had scratched only moments ago. “Little asshole.”

Fade chuckled back at me, and I heard a note of relief in his mental voice. I took that to mean the surgery had gone well. I doubt he would have been so relieved if I were on the brink of death.

I relaxed and allowed my head to fall onto Sylvia’s shoulder. She took my weight with no complaint and instead rested hers against mine. Looking up, caught an almost nostalgic glint in Rafael’s flaming eyes that only lasted for an instant, disappearing when he noticed my gaze. “I’m guessing things went well?”

“Good enough,” Renauld said cheerfully, appearing at the side of the Lich. “The actual physical surgery went like a breeze. Honestly, I’ve never been part of a procedure that went so well. I wish you could have seen it, Nate. This guy really knows his stuff when it comes to surgery.”

Rafael scoffed. “There’s a reason I was always the one who wielded the knife when it came to Healing, young one. The woman has ever had the manual dexterity of a particularly inept primate, even if she has some skill in the mystical part. But yes, your friend is correct, Nathaniel. Everything went as well as planned. Your new metallic bones have been fused to both your physical self and your spiritual self. They should function well as replacements for what you lost in battle. Better than well, actually. I would be shocked if you couldn’t turn near any blade with that alloy if you were desperate enough. The same goes for your left arm. I revised the attachment point as well. You did an adequate enough job the first go around, I suppose. Still, surely you can notice the difference.”

Shifting my eyes, I considered my Primordium hand resting on Fade’s head. I suppose he was right. My sense of touch in my prosthetic arm had always been a bit muted. Before, I would have struggled to tell individual fur strands apart on Fade’s skull. Now, I could feel each individual one under my metal fingers.

I nodded slowly. “Thank you. How long was I out, and how long until I’m back at one hundred percent?”

“About five hours,” Sylvia answered me quietly. “They finished the procedure fairly quickly, but that’s how long you were recovering for. Preceptor Josha had to step out not long ago on business while we wait for you, but…” She turned her eyes towards me knowingly. “Walter stopped by briefly, asking for you.”

I smiled slightly at the news. “Good. That’s…good.”

“As for how long until you’ve recovered…perhaps an hour.” Rafael shrugged his skeletal shoulders non-committedly. “The nature of a Status is to bring a person back into homeostasis at an accelerated state. I imagine it will accelerate if you can get a good meal into your system. From there, perhaps an update to your Status itself will help matters along. Maybe with those points into Vitality, hmm?”

I rolled my eyes, but nodded nonetheless. I could take advice when it was given. Still, I was pretty damn happy that I was nearly done with being essentially infirm. I had spent so long consigned to a clone body that I had forgotten the sense of stability that came with being myself.

It was good to be back.

“After that I-we,” I corrected. “Should go and check in with…everyone, I suppose. Maria, the rest of the Order, the expedition, Bleddyn, the Unchained-”

“Shush,” Sylvia cut me off gently. “Focus on recovery first, before you go charging off after more responsibility. Things are already dying down here in the town. The sounds of the caravan died out some time ago, so I believe everyone has been settled. Once you’re strong enough, then you can shoulder the world. It can wait until you can bear the weight once more. Renauld, do you mind finding us a proper meal out there?”

“Something with meat,” Rafael advised clinically.

I chuckled and decided to take the advice. As Renauld cheerfully consented, I decided to just…relax. Sylvia was right.

The world could wait until I was strong enough to raise my head before I picked up my blade again.
