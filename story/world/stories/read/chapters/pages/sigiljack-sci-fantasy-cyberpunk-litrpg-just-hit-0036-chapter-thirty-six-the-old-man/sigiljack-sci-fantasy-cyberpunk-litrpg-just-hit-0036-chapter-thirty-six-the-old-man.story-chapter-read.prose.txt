“She asked me about it, you know,” Red said as they walked.

John wore his combat suit under a black shirt and jeans, his leather jacket thrown over it. The armored boots peaking out under his denim was the only real tell he was decked out. Ghostwind rode his hip in its scabbard; his PD11 sat high in his shoulder holster under his armpit.

Marvin paced behind them, quiet and watchful. John had left the aerial drone at home. Red might've dropped Claire off at school already, but Mona was still there at the apartment, and John still didn't fully trust the new watch Kaito and Nabe set. The dossier Nabe had sent over the night before looked solid and filled with respectable mercs when he'd combed through it, but a couple profiles lacked pictures and ages. Those were the vampires, probably.

“About what?” John asked.

“Who we were meeting.”

“How much more did you tell her?”

“Just enough to keep her from getting upset,” Red said. “Told her we had work. Told her we needed to see some people to get that work done.”

“Hm.”

Claire was getting more and more headstrong and persistent lately.

They reached the door of the building they'd parked near. There was white paint on the glass, a shop name: Marcus’ Collectibles.

John opened the door and held it for Red. The bell chimed. A steel bar door inside stood propped open past the more friendly front door; John eased past it and breathed in the smell and heavy taste of lacquer and old paper.

The shop was almost a museum in its contents. Antique firearms under glass. Old sabers and dress swords on velvet stands. Bookshelves towering up, packed with first prints and banned reprints despite the fact that no one used paper to read anymore. There were big-game mounts with glass eyes in the corners, some of them monstrous.

The Old Man sat behind his counter, his suit jacket off and folded, his vest neat. Marcus' white hair was pushed back with clay, a few bangs free but not out of place. A cigar burned lazily in his mouth. He closed a naval treatise he was reading and set it on the counter, then blew a lazy smoke ring.

Nearby, and every few feet, a series of  expensive and small air purifiers kept the cigar smoke from even thinking about settling into any of the collectibles or books.

“Boys,” he said, rocking once in his chair like his mannerisms were pure habit and not for show.

His forearms were scarred and strong. The blue of his ice-steel eyes was intelligent, deep, and unflinching.

“Been a while, Old Man,” John said and approached the counter.

“Mhm.” Marcus sipped whiskey from a cut crystal glass. Nothing for show. He did things because he did them. “Surprised to get your ping. Thought you were out of the life, John.”

“Back in. Normal work dried up. Didn't pay enough."

He grunted. “Never seen ‘normal’ pay proper. And I’m older than both of you put together.”

He ticked his cigar toward Red. “But I know you don’t need this. Your shop’s humming. He pull you back in? Owe him a favor or something?"

“Something like that,” Red said. “John’s a hazard to himself, and everyone else, left alone.”

The Old Man nodded once, then looked past them to Marvin. "Rental?"

"Found him and patched him up," John said. "Know anything about him?"

“Older frame, hell... it was old when I was in fatigues, but I served with a few refurbished units. Pre-collapse bones, if you know what you’re looking at. Good steel. Reliable in a fight.” His eyes slid to the neon pink hand. No comment, only the barest twitch of his nose. “You're lucky you got him. I wouldn't tinker with him too much, but it was smart to sleeve the barrel arm."

“Figured people might get jumpy,” John said.

"Well, alright."

The Old Man stood. He walked past Red and John and locked the front door, then hit a light switch. The shop's windows went from clear to a deep, opaque black in a blink. After that, Marcus swung the steel bar door shut behind the glass one and sealed the shop off from the outside world completely.

“Come on."

He led them into a nook between bookshelves. He reached up and took a mounted twenty-point, albino buck off the wall space between two shelves. Then set it on the table. After that, he pulled a specific book out from a nearby case, but it didn't come all the way out, he then crossed to another shelf, and pulled a book that looked completely differently.

After Marcus had angled the second book away from the shelf, a reccessed panel appeared out of the wall where the deer head had been. Marcus removed a lock from a chain at his neck and inserted it into the panel face.

The panel itself slid open and revealed a keypad and hand scanner. Marcus punched in the code, and then laid his palm on a plate. A green light blink, there was a soft click. The panel shut, a section of wall slid aside, and stairs down were revealed where before there had just been the wall.

“You’ve been here,” Marcus said and gestured for the two to go down first.

“Still a neat trick,” Red said.

“Doesn’t get old,” John added.

“You kids are easily impressed.” The smile at the edge of Marcus' mouth said it didn’t bother him. The whole setup matched his rep: a public face of rare books, a private armory beneath.

They went down, Marcus following behind them. At the bottom of the stairway, the old man reached for and flipped a breaker.

Lights rolled up across concrete and clean rows of illegal goods. Crates of ammo were stacked by type across the floor. Shelves of hunting rifles, carbines, SMGs, handguns, and even anti-mech launchers lined the walls. John noticed the glint of a rack of mundane and mono-edged blades. There was another door at the back of the armoury, overbuilt and locked; John had never been inside the room, but he knew it was Marcus' workshop.

“You know how this works,” the Old Man said, smoke drifting around his words. “Look. Don’t touch unless you ask. Tell me what you want. I give you one price.”

That was the rule. There was no haggling, and things were more expensive than other runners would offer, but Marcus never sold junk. Everything the old veteran offered was either retooled or personally inspected and approved by him, and he knew his stuff.

“I’m looking for a battle rifle,” John said. “Not standard issue if I can help it. Preferably accurate for selective fire, but I want to be able to get suppression out of it too.”

“Not a VX-9 then.” Marcus said casually and walked to a shelf, pulled a rifle, cleared it, and returned to set it on a table under a drop light. He waited for John, Red, and Marvin to follow him and approach the other side of the display table. “It's a Kestrel Mk-IV “Nocturne”. Freehold SF spec. Caseless. Electro-fused. Chambered in 6.5×44 MD. There's a mana-dust binder in the pellets it shoots for a cleaner burn, and a flatter arc out past four hundred meters. But I wouldn't push it past seven-hundred for a point target, officially it reaches six-fifty. The battery pack’s factory new. Service life beats the Nine. Hits harder too. No kick. Easier to maintain.” He tapped the magwell. “One thousand credits for it. Ammo’s a credit a round right now. But I'll give you a box of a hundred for ninety.”

“May I?” John asked.

“Go on.”

John picked the rifle up, cleared it, and checked it, felt the tolerances, ran the bolt, sighted. He glanced to Marvin. “Well?”

Marvin lifted both hands, waited for the Old Man’s nod, then took the rifle carefully. The robot re-cleared it, shouldered it away from the others, did two clean ready-ups, and aimed at a dead section of wall. He handed it back to John and nodded once.

“Hnh. He likes it,” Red said.

“Lot of personality in that chassis,” the Old Man said.

John set the rifle on the felt table. “I’ll take it and three-hundred rounds. Mags?”

“Three come with it. Anything else?"

“Grenades,” John said, scanning the shelves for anything else he might want. “Frag and EMP if you have them. Half dozen each.”

Marcus moved away to the grenade crates and fetched two trays. Green frags. Silver EMPs with blue banding. “Ten credits each for the frags. Twenty each for the EMP.”

“Always surprises me how cheap these things are,” John said. “Deal. You have anything that'll beat a PD11 for a sidearm?"

The Old Man kept his eyes dead-locked on John's. “I could upsell you, but you’d be paying what you just did for the rifle to outdo an issued Eleven pistol-wise for what it does. It's a good sidearm for a mass-produced gun."

“I figured.” John’s eyes lingered longingly on a slim gauss pistol sitting on a set of studs with the other pistols. He let it go. “I will take a box of electrostatic rounds. And a box of AP. Also three hundred in .35 MR. I have a security drone that eats it.”

“Done.”

The Old Man’s gaze dipped to Gravewind’s scabbard. “That blade orc work?"

“It is,” John said.

“Sha’vael’s, huh?” the Old Man said, after studying it for a moment longer, no softness in the words, but no outright ice either.

Marcus had known Sha'vael when she, John, and Red had run together. Before Sha'vael had... died.

“Yeah.”

“Not saying you should go looking to change it, but I could give it a monoedge” the Old Man went on.

John paused. "It'd be fragile, would need a magnetic field to stabilize it. Unless--"

Marcus looked at him coldly, as if to say he wouldn't have offered to do it if he didn't know that, but the Old Man finished his thought for him anyway. "Unless it was a nanobot edge coating."

“You have that on hand?”

“Some, but it's not cheap. It'd be five-thousand for the coat on a blade that size, but it'd cut through titanium alloy like butter when I was done."

“I don't have it to spend today.”

Marcus nodded once. No push, no sell. And no promise it would be there to buy later.

"Actually, do you have any collapsible SMGS?" John asked.

“Powder or caseless?”

“Gunpowder's fine if it runs clean.”

Marcus turned and pulled a compact from a nearby rack. “Edgemark S-12. Short piston, suppressed well. Sturdy as anything mechanical ever made. Four hundred credits. And a half credit per round.”

“I’ll take it and three-hundred rounds.”

Marcus nodded and moved to collect the rest of everything they'd talked about.

He stacked boxes, mags, the rifle, the SMG, the grenades in a neat line beneath the lamp.

“That it?” the Old Man asked.

Red thumbed toward his own kit. “You sitting on any .50 cal sabot?"

“Back wall. Third crate down. You want the mixed brick or singles?”

“Brick,” Red said. “Two, if you’ll give a good price. And I'll take some timed charges."

John's eyes scanned over everything he'd bought. It was a lot of money, but now Marvin was outfitted well. And he had both grenades and a new SMG to sure himself up with.

Next stop, the Retainers HQ.
