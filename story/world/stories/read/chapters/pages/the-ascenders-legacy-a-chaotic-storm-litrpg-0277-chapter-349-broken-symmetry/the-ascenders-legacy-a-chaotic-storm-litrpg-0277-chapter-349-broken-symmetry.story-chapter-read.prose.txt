Crixis and the other underlings stood alongside Special Disciples Seamus and Darien within the viewing deck, their eyes hooked on the massive screens as they waited for Special Disciples Nymera and Ashscar to return from the battlefield.

All his life, Crixis had known transmigrants were powerful. Who didn't? He knew their power scaled differently compared to the rest of the cultivation world. He knew they were never to be made an enemy.

And though the last two weeks of war had shown him exactly why they were called specials, the essence behind that title hadn't truly sunk in until today.

Until he'd watched Nymera decimate two transmigrants in minutes. Sophran turn an entire formation of Mythics into a legion of the undying. Ashscar herald destruction upon destruction on the battlefield until he'd been silenced, and finally, Aodhán holding his own against another transmigrant thirty tiers above him.

Or perhaps, more appropriately, thirty transmigrant tiers above him.

Not only were they displaying unimaginable control of their abilities, but they were also holding their own and standing out in a battle dominated by Mythics.

Their instinctual understanding of their paths and skills was obvious in the way they moved, the way they fought. They weren't people who had spent their entire lives in a training room. Or even dungeons.

No, these were people who had fought real, hard battles before. People who had stared death in the face and denied it their lives.

These were people forged on an anvil of ruin.

Ordinary couldn't even begin to qualify them.

Beside him, Rathis Cloudrend flinched as they watched a replay of Volgrim's death.

"By the storm, that is brutal," he whispered, his vapor-like hair billowing as he leaned toward Crixis. "I bet everyone in the Earth Sect just about died at the sight of that."

Kairn, Seamus's brother, chuckled at the mental image, but Crixis couldn't bring himself to do the same. Of course Volgrim's sect members were affected. Nymera hadn't just killed their transmigrant—she'd made a statement with his death. And had that been any of the storm transmigrants, Crixis knew how he would feel about it.

Sighing, he shook his head. "I wish I could be as excited about all this as you guys."

Rathis glanced at him. "You're thinking of them as humans, not as enemies." He glanced back at the screen and grimaced. "Don't do that."

Kairn nodded solemnly. "I bet the transmigrants aren't doing that. Can you imagine the amount of willpower they're all burning? I think Nymera just used my entire willpower pool in that plasma attack."

Crixis glanced at the screen, his empathy slightly discarded as he realized he hadn't even taken the willpower cost of this entire war into account. But beyond even willpower, he soon realized that barely anyone was using their seals either. It was as if they'd made an unspoken arrangement not to.

Frowning, he glanced up at Special Disciple Darien and asked, "Why aren't they using their seals?"

Darien glanced at him, then back at the screen before sighing. "Because as terrible as the war is now, as terrible as it looks, it's not quite as terrible as it could be, and no one wants to escalate things yet." Jaw tightening, he continued, "I mean, they're barely using chaos either, but with the strain it puts on the spirit, it's a bad idea for a war this drawn out."

Beside Special Disciple Darien, Seamus nodded. "No one has used an icon yet either. I hate to say it, but we are still in the preambles of this war." His gaze tightened into a glare as he glanced at Aodhán's screen. "I wish they'd just let us help."

Crixis followed his gaze, swallowing nervously as he watched Aodhán clash against the serenity awakened in a blur of seemingly solid lightning and ruin. Lips parting slightly, he marveled at the fact that despite how inaccessible the war was even for special disciples, it was still nowhere near its peak yet.

Shuddering, he glanced at Su Tianan, who stood quietly in a corner, watching Sophran's battle against the glass transmigrant. Mirelle's death had shaken him hard. It had shaken them all, but while their grief had served as a driving force for transmigrants, it had become a hindrance to Darien and Seamus, who wanted nothing but to help yet were constantly denied despite their status as double inheritors.

Elder Ghaltris simply refused to let them, not wanting to entertain the possibility of another death.

Crixis could understand it, at least to an extent. But he also saw it as an underutilization of power. Special Disciples Darien and Seamus were strong combatants able to rival transmigrants and hold their own, even if only for a short moment.

Their spirits may not be as powerful as those of transmigrants, their abilities less sharp, but they had one thing even transmigrants lacked. Willpower. Darien and Seamus together probably had more willpower than the four transmigrants combined.

And yet, Elder Ghaltris refused to let them help.

Well, at least they could help, Crixis thought to himself as he shifted his gaze away from Tianan. What could he do?

A commotion ensued from the other side of the hall in that moment, and they all turned to see Nymera exiting a portal with Ashscar in hand.

"Healers!" an elder shouted as they rushed to help, and though Crixis doubted there was anything he could do, he rushed toward them too, Rathis and Kairn just a step behind him.

They arrived just as Nymera laid Ashscar down, hands pressed against his sternum firmly.

"He's bleeding profusely," she stated, arms still pressed against his sternum. "Half his ribs are fractured, and he's barely breathing. I think his lungs have been punctured. His liver too."

"We have it, Special Disciple Nymera," one of the healers stated firmly. "Leave him and let us work."

Nymera hesitated only a moment before jerking to her feet, apologizing as she stepped back, giving the healers room to work.

"How about you?" Elder Ghaltris asked as he came to stand beside her. "Are you alright?"

"I'm fine." Nymera shook her head, turning to glance at the screen where she found Aodhán and Sophran still locked in intense battle. Her eyes widened slightly and she turned to glare at Elder Ghaltris. "Why did you recall me when they're still fighting? I could have helped. Send me back."

"No." Elder Ghaltris shook his head. "You've been fighting for two weeks straight. You need rest."

"I'm not tired," Nymera argued.

"You don't have to be. You need rest. Your mind needs a break. Besides, I wasn't the one who recalled you. It was the First Seat."

Nymera paused, then glanced back at the screen, staring silently at the shifting battle for a moment before asking, "Can they win?"

Elder Ghaltris pursed his lips uncertainly. "I fear that's not something I can answer. There are no certainties in battles like this. We'll just have to trust them to pull through."

"We wouldn't just have to trust them if you send us in," Seamus glared at him. "We can help—"

"No." Elder Ghaltris cut him off firmly. "You've asked a dozen times. My answer hasn't changed."

"But—"

"Trust them, Seamus," Nymera whispered, her voice cutting through tension like a knife. Worry marred her midnight skin, and fear shadowed her eyes, yet she smiled. "Trust them to pull through."

She glanced back at the screen, hands tightening. "They'll pull through."

A pause. Quieter. "They have to."

Back on the battlefield, Aodhán was indeed pulling through… debris, groaning as he worked his way out of the crater Alaric had slammed him into.

Suffice it to say, Tidal Rupture hadn’t worked as he’d hoped it would.

While Ruin’s Obedience had become more offensive with the effects of Axis of Ruin imbued into it, Tidal Rupture had done the opposite.

It had gathered. Condensed. And then—

Nothing.

No bubbles. No embolism. No explosion.

Instead, the water had flowed smoothly through arteries and veins, integrating itself into the rhythm of Alaric's body as though it had always been there. And now, as he dragged his broken body from a crater, Aodhán knew he should have expected this.

He had assumed that every skill imbued with Axis of Ruin would become deadlier. He’d been, oh so wrong.

Coughing, he'd barely dug himself out of the crater when Alaric appeared again, his face a mask of fury. Every iota of serenity within him seemed to have been bled dry, and unfortunately, Aodhán couldn't blame him.

If he'd come so close to death, he too would have been furious.

Hands gripped his neck immediately, crushingly, and Aodhán barely activated Lightning Body—Violet before Alaric snapped it. The lance of Ruin reappeared in his hands as he arrived a distance away from the crater, barely raising it in time to block another attack from Alaric.

"You're a scourge," Alaric snarled as he attacked again, serenity essence shaping into a blade that collided with ruin with a sound like the world itself shattering. "A lie wrapped as truth. Your center of tranquility is false."

"I agree," Aodhán said through gritted teeth as the force of Alaric's attacks sent vibrations through his bones. "My center is false. I'm not like you." His eyes gleamed. "But that was never my intention."

His fist caught Alaric in the neck, barely slowing him, but in that brief moment of hesitation, Aodhán jabbed the lance of Ruin into Alaric's chest and detonated it.

Lightning exploded in concentric circles and blasted Alaric backward, flesh erupting in a spray of blood and—

Space froze. A bubble of serenity erupted out and the entire explosion—something that would have killed dozens of Mythics before they could blink—was swallowed up, compressed, and sent back at him.

Eyes widening, Aodhán raised his hands and flared his aura, authority singing through him as he attempted to regain control of the explosion.

Before he could, the world tilted again. Pain slammed into his skull and stars exploded as his head smashed against the rocky earth hard enough to crack.

Serenity engulfed him immediately in suppression—only for a moment, but that moment was enough for serenity to bind his pathways and render him practically mundane.

The next punch blew his jaw apart, and the next cracked his sternum inward.

Aodhán barely managed to regain control of his pathways before Alaric could blow him apart, Lightning Body—Violet activating once more to teleport him out of harm’s way. But with his ideal temporarily ignored, Alaric was unrelentingly feral.

He appeared before Aodhán again, a blade of serenity slamming down to rip him apart. Aodhán didn’t think.

He acted.

[Leech]

But as energy surged from his core, Aodhán couldn’t help but fear he was in for another surprise.

What effect would Axis of Ruin have on Leech?

Miles away from Aodhán's escalating battle, Sophran was also pulling himself out of a crater, his body bleeding and spirit buckling under the flood of vitality surging through it.

Sophran had never fought a war like this before. He'd never kept a skill active this long. But he couldn't stop. He dared not stop.

Despite the pain, Sophran knew his vitality was the only thing keeping him going, keeping this battle going. If he deactivated it now, he was dead. He was—

He glanced up at Seraphine where she stood watching him, analyzing. Cataloguing. Always fucking cataloguing.

The woman was brutal.

Before Reflect, she had been a menace. A challenge to overcome. Now, however, she was an impossibility.

Attacking her was futile, as anything he did was simply reflected back. Even defending himself was futile.

Her defenses were too tight. Too absolute.

There was no overwhelming her with power or matching her in precision.

There was nothing left to do but run.

Yet even that, Seraphine was hellbent on denying him.

"You just don't give up, do you?" she muttered, a hint of disdain on her otherwise calculating expression. "You're like a cockroach. You just won't die."

"Maybe you're not trying hard enough," Sophran growled at her. "Try harder."

Seraphine frowned contemplatively, and for a moment, Sophran feared she intended to take his words seriously. But even more scary was the fact that she seemed to think she could actually try harder… insinuating that she still hadn't gone all out yet while he was here, on the brink of collapse.

Reflect was already impossible to counter. How much harder could she make this battle?

Then she smiled. "Even on death's door, you taunt. So frustratingly living."

Sophran bluffed. "I'm not on death's door."

"Then let's end this farce of a battle once and for all. You can't win. My Reflect is perfect. It isn't just some mirror shield. It is symmetry."

"Spare me the details," Sophran growled as he pushed himself to his feet. "I get it. I'm unprepared for your brand of problem. There's no winning against you."

"Then deactivate that healing and let me slice you apart."

"No, thank you. I could do this all day."

Seraphine glared at him, deeply, thoughtfully, then she smiled. "No, you can't."

She vanished immediately, reappearing behind him to slice him apart. But while Sophran couldn't attack or defend really, his speed was unhindered. Swiftly, he dodged the attack, lightning instinctively slicing toward Seraphine's throat.

She didn't dodge.

And with gritted teeth, Sophran realized he'd been played again.

Pain slammed into him as an invisible hand sliced his throat open, nearly taking the entire head off. Seraphine attacked then, crystal-blue irises fixed on the injury to complete it, forcing Sophran to move even while healing himself.

It was a nightmare, and he barely managed to shift his neck away at the last minute, taking the attack instead on his chest.

It burned, body and spirit groaning as Ancestral Continuance forced them back together for the umpteenth time. He couldn't keep this up much longer.

But he couldn't stop either.

No one was coming to save him. He had to win this battle.

Growling in annoyance, Seraphine attacked again, multiple scissions slicing toward him, and despite his impeccable speed, there was only so much he could dodge.

Injuries piled up. Ancestral Continuance forced them back together.

His spirit bled. Ancestral Continuance pushed more vitality through it.

His body shuddered. His vision dimmed. His mind wavered.

Yet Ancestral Continuance kept him alive.

It was tiring. And though Sophran didn't want to die, he had no energy left to fight.

He crashed into the earth a second later, his descent creating another crater. But this time, Sophran didn't force himself to stand up.

Body shuddering and bleeding vitality, he lay on the ground, spiritually and utterly exhausted.

Slowly, he opened his eyes, vision swimming with a faint outline of Seraphine as she stepped into his vision, crystal-blue eyes sharp despite his hazy vision.

She regarded him for a moment, her expression blank. Gone was the calculation. Gone were the analyzing and cataloguing.

She had won.

Her opponent lay on the cracked earth, too weak to lift a finger.

She had won, but she felt no satisfaction.

The joy of battle was in finding weaknesses and exploiting them. Finding fractures and widening them. And though she had found many of Sophran's weaknesses, she hadn't won. Not truly.

He had surrendered. And that ate at her like a worm in her heart.

Had Sophran kept going, she wouldn't have won. He wouldn't have either, but neither would she, and that truth left a bad taste in her mouth.

Seraphine, however, hadn't come to this battle chasing satisfaction. She had come with an aim.

And satisfied or not, it was time to fulfill it.

Raising a hand to the air, she conjured a glass blade so thin it defied substance and glanced down at Sophran, whose eyes were wide open now.

"This is for balance."

She swung the blade straight at his neck, slicing through muscle and bone to—

The world shook—like a struck bell ringing through the bones of her existence.

Seraphine froze mid-motion.

For half a heartbeat, the battlefield ceased to exist. The screams, the lightning, the shattering glass—all of it faded beneath the overwhelming pressure that rolled across the heavens.

She looked up.

Far above the clouds, something burned—a silent supernova spreading in slow, terrible majesty. Radiant fractures spidered through the firmament like cracks racing across a pane struck too hard.

A glass Calamity had just died.

Seraphine didn't need proof. No glass cultivator needed proof.

They could feel it in their bones—not as shock, or grief—but as absence. A symmetry broken.

Pain bloomed first. Then anger—white-hot anger that speared through her in furious brilliance. A thirst for blood bloomed next, but when she looked back down, ready to slice Sophran apart—

He was gone.

For a moment, Seraphine stood perfectly still.

And then she screamed into the heavens, buckling beneath the weight of her pain.

Glass shards erupted all around her, and the next moment, she turned her gaze to the Mythics all around her.

If she couldn't have Sophran, then she would feed her bloodthirst with these Mythics instead.
