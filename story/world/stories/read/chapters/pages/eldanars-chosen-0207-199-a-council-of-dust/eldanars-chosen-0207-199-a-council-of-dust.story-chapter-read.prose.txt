The party limped into the safety of the forward operating camp, the sheer relief of their arrival threatening to completely unravel them. The camp had exploded in size since their departure. Dozens of sturdy, heavy-duty military pavilions had been erected, creating a miniature canvas city on the edge of the rotting forest. Several large, roaring watchfires were burning brightly around the perimeter, casting a warm, welcoming orange glow against the gathering twilight.

Most importantly, the camp was heavily populated. The perimeter was secured by heavily armed city guards clad in the polished chainmail and tabards of Ashenfall. Several large groups of veteran adventurers were gathered around the central fires, sharpening weapons and studying parchment maps. Torin had evidently sent enough terrifying reports from the frontier to mobilise a serious reinforcement effort.

Josh and Perberos carried Bhel straight towards the largest medical pavilion. The dwarf was awake but incredibly weak, the severe blood loss from the Bone Goliath’s mace having drained the colour from his face. Carcan had finally purged the necrotic infection and sealed the horrific wound, but magical healing could not instantly replace missing blood volume.

"Put me down, you absolute embarrassments," Bhel grumbled weakly as they approached a free cot near the back of the tent. His voice was a thin, gravelly rasp. "I can stand on my own two feet. People are staring."

"People are staring because you are currently covered head to toe in black mud and your own blood, Bhel," Brett pointed out from the doorway. The mage was leaning heavily against the wooden tent pole, his dark red drake-scale gloves resting limply at his sides. "Accept the indignity with a measure of grace. It is preferable to collapsing into the dirt and forcing us to drag you."

Josh gently lowered the heavy dwarf onto the clean white sheets, letting out a long, ragged exhale as the crushing weight finally left his arms. He rolled his shoulders, wincing as his bruised collarbones protested.

"Stay here," Josh ordered Bhel, his tone leaving absolutely no room for dwarven stubbornness. "Carcan, ensure he drinks some water and actually rests. Brett, come with me. We need to find whoever is in command of this lot."

Brett pushed himself off the tent pole with a tired sigh, adjusting his ruined, mud-caked fur cloak. "I suppose hot food will have to wait."

They stepped back out into the bustling thoroughfare of the camp. The noise was incredible after the suffocating silence of the rotting forest. Guards were shouting patrol rotations, armourers were hammering out dents on portable anvils, and the distinct, mouth-watering smell of roasted meat drifted from the massive mess tents.

As Josh scanned the crowds for a command banner, a familiar, sharp voice cut through the din.

"You look like you have been chewed up by a troll and spat out down a mountainside."

Josh turned. Leaning casually against a stack of wooden supply crates was Ronald.

The feline beastkin had not changed a bit since their brutal training sessions in Ashenfall. He wore his muted green and brown ranger gear, the supple leather bearing the fresh scars of recent hunts. His hooded cloak draped over his shoulders, shifting like a shadow as he pushed off the crates. His slit-pupiled, jade-green eyes took in the state of Josh’s dented plate armour and Brett’s pale, exhausted face with a sharp, calculating intensity. A fanged smirk played at the corner of his mouth.

"Ronald," Josh greeted, a genuine smile breaking through his fatigue. "It is incredibly good to see a familiar face. Are you running the scouting perimeter?"

"Someone has to ensure these city boys do not blunder blindly into an ambush," Ronald replied smoothly, his short-cropped ears twitching slightly to catch the sounds of the camp. "You two look terrible. I assume you found something worse than wandering stragglers out there in the dark."

"Significantly worse," Brett stated, his academic tone returning as he straightened his spine. "We need to speak to the camp commanders immediately. We are sitting on intelligence that completely changes our understanding of what’s going on."

Ronald’s fanged smirk vanished instantly. He recognised the uncompromising seriousness in Brett's tone. The beastkin gave a single, sharp nod.

"Follow me," Ronald commanded, turning on his heel and weaving fluidly through the crowded camp.

He led them towards a massive, dark blue pavilion situated at the very centre of the encampment. Two heavily armed city guards flanked the entrance, but they stepped aside immediately as Ronald approached.

Josh pushed the heavy canvas flap aside and stepped into the command tent.

The interior was warm, illuminated by several glowing magical spheres floating near the ceiling. A large, heavy oak table dominated the centre of the room, covered in detailed topographical maps of the frontier and small wooden markers representing troop movements.

Standing over the table, commanding the room with her mere presence, was Caistina Tharistrar.

The elven mage looked as elegant and terrifyingly powerful as ever. Her sapphire-blue robes were pristine, the silver arcane sigils catching the magical light. Her long, moonlit-white hair cascaded perfectly down her back. She held her pristine white staff loosely in one hand, the intricate runes pulsing with a soft, latent energy.

She looked up as Josh and Brett entered, her piercing amethyst eyes widening slightly in surprise.

"Josh. Brett," Caistina greeted them, her melodic voice carrying a mixture of professional authority and genuine relief. "The quartermaster reported that your party had ventured deep into the northern grid sector. I am very pleased to see you both walking. I assume Carcan and Perberos are safe?"

"They are safe, Caistina," Josh confirmed, stepping up to the wooden table. "Carcan is currently tending to Bhel in the medical tent. He took a heavy hit, but he will live."

"Excellent," Caistina nodded, her expression softening for a brief fraction of a second before hardening back into the mask of a military commander. She gestured to the sprawling maps. "We are currently preparing to push the frontline deeper into the woods tomorrow morning. I finally convinced the city council to release a portion of the guard. We intend to establish a secure perimeter and begin systematically culling the wandering undead packs."

"You cannot do that," Josh stated bluntly, leaning his heavy steel gauntlets on the edge of the map table.

Ronald moved to stand beside Caistina, his jade eyes narrowing. "Explain."

Josh took a slow, deep breath, ordering his exhausted thoughts. "We found a dying scout out there. A man named Fenn. He told us the undead were not wandering aimlessly. He claimed they were massing in the deep mountain basin. He claimed they were being led by a commander."

Caistina’s elegant brow furrowed deeply. "A commander? Undead hordes are feral by nature. They operate on basic, primal hunger and residual necrotic programming. They do not follow military commanders."

"That was our understanding as well," Brett interjected, stepping up beside Josh. The mage placed his dark red gloves on the table, pointing to the designated basin area on the map. "We assumed Fenn was hallucinating from a necrotic infection. We pushed forward to the ridge to confirm the intelligence visually. Caistina, it was not a hallucination."

Brett looked directly into the older elf's eyes. "The basin is full. We are not talking about a few hundred wandering ghouls. We estimated tens of thousands of heavily armoured skeletal infantry, wights, and massive bone constructs. They are standing in immaculate, rigid military formations. They have banners. And standing at the centre of it all is an entity radiating a density of dark magic that I have never felt before."

The command tent fell into absolute, suffocating silence.

Ronald stopped perfectly still, the tip of his tail twitching erratically beneath his cloak. Caistina stared at the map, her amethyst eyes losing their usual serene calm, replaced by a cold, calculating horror.

"Tens of thousands," Caistina whispered, the numbers completely shattering their current tactical models. "That is an invasion force."

"It gets worse," Brett continued, his voice taking on a grim, academic certainty. "The magical density of the rotting forest gets worse as you get closer to the dungeon, it’s transforming the area, and makes healing harder.” He stopped, taking a deep breath, “And I do not believe this boss monster we saw was a product of the dungeon break. I believe he predates them. If an entity with the power to organise an undead army already existed in this world, then maybe the portals did not simply overflow by accident. I believe this entity caused the Shattering to create a chaotic smokescreen for his true invasion."

Ronald let out a low, dangerous hiss, his fangs fully exposed. "If that is true, then this entire camp is currently sitting exposed just a few miles from an apocalyptic threat."

"Which is why we cannot push into the woods tomorrow," Josh concluded, his storm-grey eyes locked on Caistina. "If you march this army of city guards and mid-tier adventurers into that forest, you will be walking them into a meat grinder. The undead do not fatigue. They do not feel fear. They will absorb your frontline and add your dead to their own ranks."

The reality of the situation hung heavily in the warm air of the pavilion. The previous plan of a slow, methodical cull was entirely obsolete. They were facing a conventional war against an unconventional, terrifying enemy.

Ronald began to pace the length of the table, his movements sharp and agitated.

"We cannot simply sit here and wait for them to finish assembling," the beastkin argued, his ranger instincts demanding immediate action. "If they have tens of thousands of troops, they will eventually march on Ashenfall. If we strike now, while they are confined to the basin, we have the element of surprise. We use our heavy hitters to punch a wedge straight through their lines. We send stealth squads along the ridges to disable their bone constructs with explosives, and we have the mages bombard the centre to create absolute chaos."

Ronald slammed a clawed hand onto the map. "We cut the head off the snake. We kill this General, and the feral programming of the horde will shatter. They will turn back into mindless, wandering beasts that we can easily pick off."

Josh shook his head firmly, completely disagreeing with his former trainer.

"You did not see the patrol guarding the ridge, Ronald," Josh warned, his voice hard with recent, bloody experience. "We barely survived a fight against fifty skeletons and a single Bone Goliath. They are incredibly well-equipped, and the ambient necrotic magic in the area actively heals their wounds while rotting our injuries. A surgical strike sounds brilliant on paper, but the moment your wedge bogs down in the mud, you will be surrounded by a wall of iron and bone. It is suicide."

"Josh is correct," Caistina agreed softly, her eyes fixed on the map. She traced a long, slender finger along the main road leading from the frontier back to the city. "A frontal assault against superior numbers in highly disadvantageous terrain is the height of tactical foolishness. We would exhaust our mana pools in the first hour. An army of living men requires food, water, rest, and morale. The dead require none of those things."

Ronald stopped pacing, glaring at the map. "So, what is your alternative? We pack up this camp and run away with our tails between our legs? We just surrender the frontier to them?"

"We are not surrendering," Josh countered, his voice steady. "We are relocating to a superior defensive position. We retreat to Ashenfall."

Brett nodded enthusiastically, tapping the parchment map near the city icon. "The timber walls of Ashenfall are towering and heavily reinforced. They act as a massive force multiplier. We force the undead army to march to us. We make them fight on our terms. We use the walls to negate their overwhelming numbers, raining fire and arrows down upon them while our heavy fighters hold the choke points at the gates."

"It is a siege strategy," Caistina murmured, her analytical mind instantly seeing the grim logic. "We let the undead mass break like water against the stone and timber of the city. We use the static defences to bleed their numbers dry."

"It is a coward's strategy," Ronald hissed, his pride clearly wounded by the prospect of a retreat. "If they besiege Ashenfall, they cut off our supply lines. They starve us out. And if the walls fall, the entire civilian population is slaughtered."

"If we die in the basin tomorrow, the civilian population is slaughtered anyway," Josh replied bluntly, refusing to back down. "At least behind the walls, we have a fighting chance to hold out."

The debate cooled, leaving a heavy, oppressive silence in its wake. The sheer scale of the nightmare they were facing was difficult to process. They were no longer arguing about hunting rogue monsters for bounty coins. They were debating the survival of the last major human city in the region.

Caistina slowly stood up straight, leaning heavily on her white staff. She looked older in that moment, the weight of centuries pressing down upon her shoulders.

"Your intelligence is sound, Josh," Caistina decided, her voice ringing with finality. "And your tactical assessment is accurate. I will speak with the guard captains immediately. We will break this camp at dawn and begin a rapid retreat back to Ashenfall."

Ronald let out a frustrated sigh, but he did not argue further. He respected Caistina’s authority, and he knew deep down that the math did not favour a forest assault.

Caistina looked back down at the map, her beautiful amethyst eyes clouded with a profound, chilling sorrow.

"Either way, we need to let the crown know," Caistina whispered, stating the horrific, undeniable truth of their situation. "We have less than a hundred people in the entire city in their third tier. We need more."

The stark reality of her words hung in the air like a physical weight. Less than a hundred highly capable fighters to hold the walls against an ocean of bone and rusted iron. The odds were catastrophic. Unless the King mobilised the royal army and marched to their aid, Ashenfall was entirely on its own.

Josh stared at the map, feeling the crushing burden of responsibility settling heavily onto his bruised shoulders. They were going to have to fight harder than they ever had before.

Suddenly, a faint, familiar chime echoed softly in his mind.

A translucent blue system notification materialised directly in the centre of his vision, overlaying the parchment map on the table.

[Level Up! You have reached Level 26.]

Josh blinked, reading the glowing text as the residual experience from the brutal fight with the Bone Goliath finally processed through the system. He dismissed the notification with a thought, a grim, determined smile touching the corner of his mouth.

They needed more power. And he was just getting started.
