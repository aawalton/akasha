It was, by a margin, the strongest attack either of them had produced so far.

The spear shattered the surface of the Elementalist’s chest, sinking several inches in, and the lightning in its tip discharged in a single burst, penetrating deep within the rift creature.

The Elementalist froze for half-second.

An arc of lightning also forked out, striking the Frigid Knight, although the boss barely froze at all.

Alex saw Beckett withdraw his spear and execute three Lightning Spears in quick succession, crying out, taking advantage of brief window of freeze. The Elementalist’s chest was a shattered mess, and Alex could see blurs of lightning mana rippling within its body.

But the surprise was quickly over.

The Elementalist stepped back, staff shining, and a thin shield materialized between it and Beckett. A shield that to Alex’s eyes seemed a little brittle. Beckett seemed to think so as well; Alex saw the guy try to press his advantage by shattering the shield, trying to charge through.

Which worked.

At least—the shield shattered, but the shattered pieces spiraled backwards like shattered glass, slicing Beckett in a dozen places, splashing blood, injecting ice-mana, and eliciting a gasp.

It was a false shield, intended to be broken at close-range.

Alex couldn’t tell the extent of the damage, but Beckett did recover very quickly, as he was forced to; and as he speared at the Elementalist once more, fire-lightning lances were hardening overhead. Beckett’s movements were a little more start-stop, aided by lightning infusions to fight the freeze.

The Frigid Knight turned at an almost leisurely pace, likely to slash at Beckett, eyes cold.

A Mana Spear CRSHHH’d into the side of its head. A second.

It frowned, like it’d been mildly inconvenienced, a few white scratches against its temple, and Alex’s jaw clenched.

That’s quite a freaking gap.

It was a two-Level difference, but with what he’d been able to accomplish there, it felt like 10 Levels. As a distraction his Spears seemed maybe fine?

But I’m still only scratching it.

It was possible the thing just hard-countered his spears’ strengths; his spears were especially useful against creatures that had softer, squishier aspects amenable to cutting. His suspicion, watching these Ice-creatures, was shattering, concussive-type attacks, like hammers, were likely more effective against their body types.

Unfortunately, he had to work with what he had; he was forced to help distract the Frigid Knight, and the minions Beckett left behind, as Beckett tried to fight through his ice-wounds and finish the Elementalist, fast.

Alex was also trying to avoid getting sliced up by the two minions before him. A task which, with his attention split like this, was feeling increasingly dicey.

CLANG!

Alex let out a tight breath, stepping back, almost slipping on a frozen trail left by the Knight’s ice arc.

What’s wrong with these things?

With Force Reinforcement he didn’t feel at risk of breaking, at least, but he was clearly beginning to get edged out in every exchange; the residual ice on their bodies was melting faster.

His hope was with the Elementalist damaged, the peculiar buff’s effects might be affected as well, but it felt like wishful thinking. It’s probably going to be active for a bit even after Beckett finishes the Elementalist…

A second slice, faster than Alex could deflect, raked him up the side, and he hissed, feeling the ice sink in. And seconds later, a third slash glancing up his back.

As he was trying to help Beckett, he was fighting these things with pretty much just his body.

As he twisted and turned, the iced-up muscles in his side and back flared with pain, and he winced.

It almost felt, as Alex kept staggering back, like Beckett’s assault was an instant of air—and the two were right back to drowning.

At least Beckett did seem to be getting close with the Elementalist. Alex briefly glimpsed how shattered its chest was—he couldn’t imagine it lasting much longer? He hoped, at least.

Which was lucky, since this crazy rush they’d tried already bloodied and iced up Beckett a decent amount in their brief series of exchanges. And Beckett’s own minions were nearly upon him again.

In the background, Alex was also acutely aware the Frigid Knight’s restraints were over half-melted.

Something has to change, radically, before it breaks free, or we’re just fucked.

It felt incredibly clear to him.

There were so many aspects of the fight consider as Alex tried to find more daylight, but before anything else, any ideas he might have to turn the tide were increasingly held up by an unglamorous fact.

I’m just freaking weak.

It’d been true since the very start of this rift; he’d kind of been compensating for it all this time with strategy and trying to use is mana well. But creativity felt clearly insufficient when the power differential, physically and mana-wise, was this stark.

But he also felt what the peculiar mana was flowing within these minions’ bodies; the way it was assimilating, taking on the characteristics of the ice.

I have to admit—as Alex’s blade was almost crushed out of his fingers—I’m insufficient.

His full focus was back on the minions before him; he gazed at them wonderingly, ignoring the pain for a few seconds.

It felt insane to try this now, but the sheer pressure of it also had a sharpening effect; the world filtered through clear and focused. He felt himself slipping into a familiar space of icy clarity.

Please, teach me.

***

Beckett spat the blood out of his mouth, injected lightning mana into the muscles in his sides, and twisted hard, crying out.

An icy blade sliced inches from his neck.

He glimpsed a reflection of his face, his slightly unfocused expression, in the blade.

And then it was past him.

“Alex, what the hell—!?”

Beckett flipped out of the way of two scything blades. He felt so close to taking the Elementalist, and it was like all his air support instantly vanished.

His body wasn’t yet as iced up as it’d been from that first Gelid attack, but the bleeding was far worse; he still felt a few shards of ice lodged deep in his torso. There was no time to dislodge them.

That ice wall was so much more fucked up than I thought it’d be.

It, and the minions’ abilities overall, had felt like a slap in the face, but Beckett didn’t let himself dwell on it; he couldn’t afford to.

Beckett’s eyes flashed to Alex.

Alex’s dark hair was wet, matted to his face, but it still exposed Alex’s wide eyes; he seemed entranced, a little lost in his own world.

What is he—?

Alex had left him little choice but to trust him.

Maybe he trusts me to take care of all four for a little.

He felt a spark of annoyance that Alex had left him like this, but it was what it was.

I can’t dwell on that either.

I have to just—

He gasped as he felt a blade he was trying to twist away from glanced across his back.

I have to finish it.

A few seconds later, he seized a chance.

***

The realization struck Alex.

Force filled a few muscles in his legs. The next exchange was exactly like the one prior. The Force didn’t have any effect.

But he knew, with precision, what it was supposed to feel like. I can find this, I have to.

The peculiar mana shivered.

For the first time that fight its effects began to wane, even with the melting ice feeding it.

Alex focused on the entire clearing once again, and saw Beckett breaking away from the center.

Beckett’s body was littered with new iced-up cuts, but a two-foot-long, still-bleeding gash ripping diagonally up his abdomen was what caught Alex’s attention; he was a bit astonished. When did he get that?

Was it all in those few seconds?

Instantly new shields materialized in front of the minions, helping Beckett open up a little space; he saw Beckett leap over a river of frozen ground left by an ice arc.

Alex felt a sharp stab of guilt. What was I thinking?

“Are you fine?”

“Yeah! I’ll live,” said Beckett, with a bloody grin. “Focus on the fight!”

If Beckett was at all pissed, he wasn’t showing it.

The two of them did exactly that.

It was still one against two, but every second, the minions’ buffs was decreasing; Alex forced himself to set aside his guilt and focus once again on the mana, reinforcing its feeling in his mind.

Guilt he could address later; he needed to seize this, especially in the window while he and Beckett could still fight these things without the final boss’s intervention.

Force surged through his limbs. Alex staggered a little less, but it was because the minions were weakening; his body was unchanged.

His will was forceful, and also very specific. The ice-mana was peculiar because it felt utterly neutral mana, yet it was also specifically structured, he realized. Its identity emerged precisely as it started to flow within the minions’ bodies.

The Force mana in his body flickered, and for an instant he felt a surge of force rising up his legs, up his arms—it vanished as quickly as it’d arrived, but it did manage to let him deflect away the dual-wielding minion, and gouge a two-inch-deep slash into its left arm as he did.

He tried to replicate it the next exchange, and failed.

And again, teeth clenched—come on!

A second failure.

He was managing a few nicks between his tries, but he had no illusions this level of strength would be sufficient. His eyes flickered to the melting ice blocks—thirty seconds, maybe, until it all melts?

The pressure will help. It’s there, I just—

CRACK!

Alex’s eyes widened.

A seam split the left ice-block. CRACK-CRACK!

The Frigid Knight’s sword suddenly brightened. As did the ice-blocks welded to its legs, as did the ice trails littering the clearing.

The Frigid Knight regarded the two of them. It pointed.

Every frozen ice-trail across the clearing exploded.

Alex heard a cry, and the next instant he saw Beckett kneeling, a ten-inch-long shard of ice spearing into his midsection; the guy seemed bewildered by it.

Alex glanced down, and saw several long shards, embedded in his body. One a few inches in his right thigh, the other slicing into his side. The skin around it was frozen bright-blue.

Between twenty and thirty smaller shards sliced up his arms and legs, injecting their own ice.

I can’t feel any of it, he realized. I can barely feel my body.

The only reason neither of them had been struck by more was the ice didn’t discriminate. It’d penetrated its own minions, who also seemed rather frozen-up.

Alex heard Beckett forcibly drag in a breath, lips trembling.

The Frigid Knight drew its blade, turning toward Beckett.

It stepped thrice, and it was before him.

Beckett had to turn to face it. It slashed, sword brightening. Beckett let out a cry as he parried with his spear’s steel haft.

The force visibly went through Beckett’s body.

Alex wasn’t sure how the guy managed to hold onto his spear as he was sent flying. Beckett crashed against the ground a dozen feet away, and his blood stained the snow.
