Black lightning erupted from Gary’s body, singing the tentacles holding him. They spasmed, but that only made them squeeze tighter. The monster didn’t even register the damage.

Nine Songs of the Spear: Mud! Gary thought, shooting a slow strike that cleaved a tentacle in one blow before dissipating. Their toughness was maddening.

Life! Another tentacle was severed just before reaching him.

Rain! Two tentacles were attacked, but the damage inflicted was minimal. They wrapped around a leg each. Thankfully, Gary’s waist and above were left untouched. He gathered terrible lightning, steaming the water around him. The vacuum was harder to create than in air, but somehow he managed.

Fourth Song: Lightning!

The Golden Bolt flew like a missile. It carried terrible momentum as it struck the monster in the center of its open mouth, piercing through it to reach the river floor beyond. Black blood spilled, and a terrible screech shook the water around Gary, so bad he momentarily lost his aura perception.

Gary! Goldie screamed in his mind.

I’m not leaving you! he roared back.

He dove down blindly, guided only by his connection to Goldie. His fingers wrapped around the shaft. He pulled and sensed it break free of something. The water around him was warm now. Sticky.

His aura perception returned, and he found the octopus screeching. The captured climbers had escaped and swam furiously upward. The corpses it had gathered floated away as its tentacles thrashed and whipped the water. Gary’s strongest attack had hurt it, but even when striking its weak point without any defenses, it hadn’t managed to kill the beast.

The pain had made its grasp around his legs weaken, so Gary kicked and thunderstepped away. A tentacle passed by where he used to be, not trying to grab him anymore, but to shatter him. He’d made some distance now, though. The tentacles couldn’t reach him as rapidly as before. He thunderstepped in odd directions, dodging them the instant they entered his aura field. A glancing strike to the leg broke it, but Gary was so agitated he couldn’t feel the pain. He kicked as hard as before. A tentacle fell from above.

“Thunder Thrust!” he yelled, piercing clean through it. Even as shreds of flesh filled the water, he sensed more tentacles rising from below. Many more. He thunderstepped even harder, escaping a hundred feet in seconds. He broke the surface. The battle was wrapping up, only a few stragglers left of the horror forces. Those who’d managed to reach the tentacle side of the river were boxed in by the legion and slaughtered ashore.

The moment Gary’s head reached air, he yelled, “Core Formation Field Boss at the bottom!”

He mustn’t have been the first one, because he could already see the Legion’s Core Formation officers splashing into the protein water. The tentacles thrashed and retreated to protect the main body. A fierce battle unfolded at the river floor, sending ripples all the way to the surface, but Gary was already sprinting for the shore like his life depended on it. He reached it, climbed a hill, and lay his back on the dirt, hyperventilating.

He’d been through life-or-death struggles before, but doing it underwater, where his eyes saw nothing and he could only rely on aura perception, brought a different kind of despair.

“Everyone, assemble!” Boulderus Johnus’s voice echoed from the bridge. “We conquered the bridge and pushed them into the woods, but some enemies escaped in our side of the river and will be hiding in the area! The Legion will handle the two side bridges. Spears, witches, and boxers, spread out and eliminate the strugglers!”

“Fuck that…” Gary muttered, still breathing heavily.

“Boss, boss!” came Osteo’s voice, flanked by heavy, happy steps. “We got a lot of them, boss!”

Gary glanced at his merit points and saw three hundred and fifty. He whistled. “Not bad.”

“The boss said not bad!” the beef boys celebrated.

“We’re not done yet. Let’s keep going,” Gary said, slapping his cheeks and getting up.

“The boss said let’s keep going!”

“You don’t need to repeat everything I say.”

“The boss said we don’t need to repeat everything!”

Gary sighed as he turned to the beefs. “Huh? Where’s the third one?”

“He fell, boss,” Osteo said.

“He died?”

“Yeah.”

Gary stayed silent. Beef boys were caricatures, but…

“Hey, Osteo. Are you alive?”

“What do you mean, boss?” Osteo asked.

“Exactly what I said. Are you alive?”

“Well, yeah.”

“How do you feel about the third beef boy dying?”

“I’m sad, boss. He had dreams and family.”

“What dreams did he have?”

“He wanted to become a chef.”

A chef… Gary pressed his eyes shut. His mind traveled back, to when he’d first entered the tower. At the time, he’d met a paraplegic orc who also wanted to be a chef.

He’d stolen the sword of that orc, leaving it defenseless. Goran’s group had killed it later.

Gary reopened his eyes, finding the two beef boys staring worriedly. “Boss?” Canc said.

“Do you have dreams?” Gary asked.

“I want to be the boxing champion!” Osteo said.

Canc grinned. “I’ve always wanted to be a baker.”

“I understand. I’m sorry for leaving you alone,” Gary said. “I don’t know how real you are, whether you really have dreams and aspirations or you just spawned today, but it doesn’t matter. You’re alive. I’m the one who brought you to life—I think—and I will take care of you. You have my word.”

The beef boys glanced at each other, then raised their fists. “Yes, Boss!”

“Follow me. We’re going hunting.”

Gary took off. Everyone else had already spread into the forest to look for horror stragglers, and Gary didn’t want to lose out. He needed strength. And in this world, to acquire strength, he had to kill.

A blur passed between the trees. Gary angled after it, followed in step by the beef boys, and almost ran face-first into a bunch of tentacle zombies. They wore the colors of spearmen—they’d just died and been reanimated. A hooded robe at the back darted into the trees.

Gary tore into the zombies and blasted them away like ragdolls. They were between levels 10 and 17—worthy opponents once, now small fries. He saw a couple of them getting up as he ran past.

“Osteo, Canc, get them and come after me!” Gary yelled, diving after the robed figure. He only glanced back once, seeing Osteo gripping a zombie from the waist and suplexing it. The level 22 beef boys should have no problem dealing with them.

Trees blurred past. He leapt from branch to branch, barely keeping tabs on the blurry shadow in the distance. A monster came from the side. Gary thrust on reflex, only barely slanting his strike when he realized it was an imp.

“Careful, idiot!” a voice called.

“Asim!” Gary said. “With me!”

“Where are we going?” Asim asked, but Gary had already sprinted past. Cursing, the witch got on his imp and raced after him. The forest opened into a wide grass field. The figure ran on foot, not looking back, the long robe hiding all of its skin. Gary stepped onto the grass as well. His Speed and Strength came into play as he erupted with more speed than almost any Birth of Aura cultivator, yet the gap wasn’t closing. He considered turning back. He pressed on.

“Thunder Step!”

The world roared as Gary accelerated, blending bursts of blinding speed into his already frantic chase. I’m sprinting as hard as I can, and it keeps up, he thought. But I can last longer.

They darted through the grass field, raising dust in their wake. A mortal would only see afterimages. The robed figure shattered a tree to avoid turning. Gary disintegrated its remains. The imp flapped its wings with all its might, but Asim was getting left back. The beef boys had just emerged from the treeline.

The robed figure eventually had enough. It turned without warning, sticking its feet into the dirt and unleashing a blast of green energy towards Gary. He rammed into it spear-first.

“Thunder Thrust!”

The blast erupted into green flames that licked him through his aura. Intense necrosis invaded his system. He could sense his cells dying and regenerating in real time. When he made it through, the robed figure stared at him, red eyes glowing under a hood.

“You made my fallen clansmen into zombies. That is inexcusable. Prepare to die,” Gary said.

The figure hissed. A pair of scaled hands threw its hood back, revealing a face between a reptilian and a zombie. Much of its flesh was rotting, revealing sunken cheeks from which tentacles emerged, tiny and wiggling. An eye was missing, too, showing empty bone. It passed a tongue—a tentacle—over its thin lips, then hissed in speech.

“She sees you,” the thing hissed.

“Holy fucking shit,” Gary said, recoiling from the sight.

“Mother sees you. She knows what you’ve done.”

“Gary, no!” Goldie said. “Rollo Frollo was fine, but you did the mother of that thing?”

“What mother!? I have no idea what it’s talking about!” Gary said.

“Prostate,” the thing hiss-whispered. Its voice struck Gary physically despite its low volume. He almost flew back. Only then did its description appeared, as if the System had taken its time recognizing this thing.

Tentacle Chimera, Level ??

Fear not! Its level isn’t too high, just unknown. These are pretty new monsters. I’d appreciate if you could hang on for a bit before dying so I can assess them.

Gary had no time to ponder. Question marks or not, this monster was clearly in the Core Formation realm. He wasn’t sure he could take it. One way or another, this would be his toughest battle since arriving at this floor.

The monster raised its hands. From inside its sleeves, tentacles flew out, acting as snakes one moment and as worms the next. They opened their mouths—???—and spat green flames.

“Thunder Step!” Gary yelled. The grass where he used to stand burned deep into the ground. The flames didn’t spread to the rest of the field, thankfully, but their power was evident. Gary wasn’t sure he’d survive a single hit of that.

Suddenly, he realized this was the first time in his life he fought straight against something a higher realm than himself. It could easily be the last.

“Thunder Step!” he roared, blitzing next to the monster. “Nail Gun!”

A rain of thrusts disappeared into the monster’s robes. The holes he poked were tiny. Even the fiber resisted him.

A hand swung for Gary. He was seemingly out of its range, but the gyro giraffe so long ago had taught him better than that. He jumped. A tentacle shot out, flicking below him like a whip, and he kicked against the air to arrive over the monster.

“Thunderbolt!” he yelled, driving a hard thrust into its head. Black lightning erupted. When it cleared, Gary saw his spear stopped at a robed forearm—it had dug only an inch into the skin, which bubbled around it. Gary withdrew just before a tentacle snapped up for his spear. The black lightning’s effects were greatly reduced on this monster, barely slowing it down despite the clean hit.

That wouldn’t stop Gary. As long as he possessed a single advantage, he’d drive it home.

“Thunder Step!” he roared, flickering around the monster so quickly he left afterimages. His spear rained thrusts. Even as he dashed, however, that red eye never left him. It could follow his speed. Its hands blurred, blocking every strike with bare flesh and robe. It suddenly delivered a punch, right where Gary was heading. He struck against it. “Thrust of Mercy!”

Spear pushed against a sickly-looking, scaled gray fist. Gary was overpowered. He was flung back, sent rolling on the grass. He got to his feet, watching the monster warily. An old phrase came to mind.

Tyranny of rank…

Tyranny was the term used by climbers to indicate a massive difference in any attribute. Tyranny of speed, tyranny of strength, tyranny of aura… Absolute tyranny was when one opponent exhibited tyranny over the other in every realm of the battle. Tyranny of rank was precisely what Gary faced right now—an opponent far superior due to nothing but their difference in realm.

This monster was just on a different level. Its strength, its speed, its durability, the volume and quality of its aura… Gary could barely keep up in places and suffered terribly at others. His advanced vibration, which terrorized the people of his rank, meant nothing to the tentacle chimera. His attacks were effortlessly stopped. It was simply unfair.

He couldn’t face this before breaking through. Pursuing had been a mistake.

“Go. I won’t chase,” he said, preparing to put away his spear.

The monster gave a smile so wide that its lips passed behind its ears. It laughed. “We didn’t know who you were, but now we do. We must claim you today, immediately, right now. One of us. One of us.”
