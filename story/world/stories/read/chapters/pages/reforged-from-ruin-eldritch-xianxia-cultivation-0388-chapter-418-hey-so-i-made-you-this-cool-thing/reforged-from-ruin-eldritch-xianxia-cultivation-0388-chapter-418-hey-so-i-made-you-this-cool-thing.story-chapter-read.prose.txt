Historically, artificial spirits have not been very good at understanding human needs. It is hard to blame them for this- they are, after all, not human, and do not possess the instinctive and subconscious understandings of things like pain, exhaustion, and perception that we do. So much information is taken for granted in a person’s day-to-day life, enough that it could fill libraries, none of which inhuman spirits tend to have access to.

Unlike Dao spirits, item spirits, or manifestations of locations or events, which intrinsically understand whatever they embody, an artificial spirit has only the information provided by their creators. Given humanity’s eternal ignorance of the details of our own existence, there is often a tremendous amount overlooked, and it’s often hard to find exactly what is missing until it becomes glaringly, painfully obvious.

I once met a spirit designed to protect the household of a bloodline, born from some ancient general or other. The spirit was commissioned to provide absolute security and comfort for those who lived inside its walls, tied directly to the lives of those it was in charge of defending. When the household fell under assault due to a little spat between its ancestor and some other cultivator, the spirit locked down the house, keeping out the attackers long enough for its creator to return and dispatch those that remained. In short, the spirit succeeded absolutely in what it was designed to do.

Upon entering the compound, the general found his entire family, their servants, and every animal in the reach of the spirit’s arrays dead, many of them frozen in their final moments of clawing at the barrier to get out. Turns out, the good general forgot that people need more to live than to just not be stabbed, and while the household had plenty of space, plenty of food, plenty of water, it did not, in fact, have a good source of clean air.

The spirit, of course, had defended them from several gaseous and chemical attacks during the siege, and had changed the arrays to better match those defenses. A good few weeks later, and consequences arose.

The general was rather angry at that particular spirit, much to its confusion. Luckily I was nearby, and, curious about the commotion, managed to offer to take the thing off his hands before he shattered its core. I offered to uncover why it killed his family, which he agreed to, and which I did- and now I have a lovely, entrepreneurial little artificial spirit, and a favor from a general. Altogether a fruitful encounter.

What was I saying? Ah, yes. Artificial spirits only know what information they are given and what information they acquire afterwards. The exact needs and system requirements of people is rarely given in anything approaching its wholeness, which can lead to very interesting conclusions.

-Grandmaster Errath of the Research Division and the Division of Altered Cultivation. Narration recorded by auto-transcriber during dissection exercises.

Geng He is having a bit of a hard time recovering his breathing. This is partially due to exhaustion, but admittedly, the lung that the spirit took from him isn’t helping.

He’s currently sitting in the same space he almost died in, the spikes growing from every surface retracted to nothingness. The floor has turned softer and smooth, and the ceiling above him (which technically is a wall, his gravity is a bit off-center right now) is glowing with a slight bioluminescence, painting the cavern a light blue.

Fighting in perfect darkness, forced to rely only on his senses and the little light he could produce with his techniques, had been hell. An hour, maybe- or maybe more. Maybe so much less. Just the dark, the vomit inducing chaos of changing directions, and the whistling of sharp-edged madness cutting towards him. In that place, everything fell away- every concern about himself, thoughts of competition, bloodlust, need, envy, it all stopped mattering because every ounce of focus was going into not dying. If he missed a cut, something would come out of the dark and take a piece of him. If he failed to account for falling in a new direction, something would come out of the dark and take a piece of him. If he hesitated, used his Qi improperly, was anything less than perfect-

Well. His arm is gone.

He’s capped off the top of it with a chunk of bone, force-grown from his Qi reserves. His core is dangerously low, the previously precarious balance made less so simply because he has almost nothing left. No chance of overloading or poisoning himself, because there’s not enough to overload or poison with. He’s using all he has just to stay conscious and keep his wounds covered in bone.

His arm is gone from the elbow down. Most of his left calf is missing, along with several chunks of his torso, his shoulders, his face. Everything hurts.

But he can’t sleep. He can’t let himself sleep. Even if the chamber has changed, even if the spikes are gone and there’s light now, it’s not enough. The moment he lets his eyes drift closed, that’s the moment that something will happen, will come out of the dark and-

“Hello there!”

He leaps off of the ground, his sword swinging at the floor where he was sitting, made of almost as much bone as metal. The cut slices through layers of flooring, through the softness and into the quasi-muscle beneath-

And cuts the face of the temple spirit in half, carving it open diagonally.

Blood flows from the wound as pieces of brain matter fall apart through the open cut. The eyes of the face go limp and begin to drift apart.

Both of them swivel to center on him, a moment before the face slaps wetly back together and the cut disappears.

“Wow! Good reflexes! I don’t really have neck muscles so I can’t really move, but still, that was a great reaction time, way better than when you started the trial!”

He stares at the spirit. It stares at him, smiling, gormless eyes unbothered.

Everything hurts, and the abrupt movement made several of his bone-scabs cut into the tissue around them. He’s bleeding again. His core is nearly empty.

It still almost hurts worse to bow his head.

“This lowly one thanks the ancient spirit for his training.”

“Oh that wasn’t training!” replies the face. “You guys said you wanted trials, and rewards! And you passed! Congratulations!”

He looks down at himself. He feels all of the missing pieces and the adrenaline still running through every part of him, leaving him frozen. Or maybe that’s the blood loss.

“I-”

“So congrats! Time for rewards! But first, how about we get you back in shape?”

The face starts to stretch, growing up out of the ground. It blossoms into a neck, a torso, two arms, then four, the torso extending until it’s poised atop a long, tall, muscular tail. Each of the four arms extends, far too long even for this new body, the hands at each end developing into more and more complex shapes, all of them sharp, all of them long and fine and terrifying.

“Don’t worry, I’ve been practicing with anesthetics and I think I’ve got all the right nerves mapped!”

Geng He seems like a tough cookie. Also like a whiner, and the fact that he’s not currently allowed to whine is probably really annoying him, which she finds kind of vaguely amusing. It’s a fun little distraction, watching the ways his face twitches when he decides not to say something, which is most of the time.

Goes to show that he’s smart, too! A good listener. She told him not to move too much, and talking would technically qualify. It’s really difficult keeping someone awake while modifying their insides, she’s discovering, but it’s good to practice these sorts of things. And besides, he’s being a very useful test case!

The bone sockets easily into the new joint, sculpted from out of the bone matter Geng He himself generated. Chunks of muscle follow her will and start to weave little tendrils into the ragged stump of his arm, fibers interlacing and melting into each other, one cell-chunk at a time.

Geng He hisses at the pain of connection, a spasm shooting out from his brain to the new fingers. They’re shaped just like his old one, but she’s made sure to modify the insides as much as she can while keeping the shape intact.

“Don’t worry! The itchiness will go away shortly! Just a sign of some lingering attachment issues. I highly recommend you don’t try to tear it off when I unpinch the nerves.”

He wants to say something, but stops himself, instead keeping his entire body very very still. He’s really doing an excellent job listening to instructions!

Her other arms and associated finger-tendrils are still digging through the rest of him, removing the very clever but very sharp pieces of bone stopping his bleeding and remolding the tissue beneath. Targeted pulses of life-Qi motivate his flesh to grow, transforming energy into matter, and she weaves the resulting tissue herself, ensuring minimal scarring from the regeneration. She really did a number on him, but he’ll be fine shortly.

The arm finishes reconnecting, and she makes sure that the improved vascular system won’t draw too much blood from the rest of the body, modifying it on the fly. The nerves connect a few seconds after, at which point the skin blooms up and grows out to connect into a jagged line where the loss occurred.

“There! All done!”

She stops pinching the nerves, at which point a LOT of signals happen all at once.

Geng He gives a shriek, rolling over himself in pain, clutching at the limb. The new hand spasms, its skin slightly lighter than the other arm, its fingers ripping through his robes and bruising at the flesh that it grabs at. Her work is too good to start bleeding again that easily, but if he doesn’t stop…

He impresses her, though. Centers himself, gritting his teeth tight enough that she hears a crack from them. He grabs hold of the new arm and holds it tight, forcing its fingers to hold still- which is harder to do than before, considering how they now bend backwards as easily as forwards.

“Alright, great job holding still!” Raika tells him, making sure her facial muscles turn into a smile. “So I know I said that the rewards were after, but you know, there’s just so many things wrong with how the human body works, and I felt like it was nice to make it better.”

He growls, still clutching at himself, but he does manage to mumble out some words. “What… did you do to me?”

“Well, for one thing, the muscles in the new arm should be about triple the strength of the other arm. Wove them more efficiently. The bone is lighter, to compensate for the weight, but it should be more durable also, just an overall upgrade on that front- made sure the architecture had better structural stability. It also should be more Qi-conductive, hopefully- should be easier to empower, able to hold more energy in it without harm.

“Now, the real experiment is with the spiritual veins. I don’t really know how to make those, so I mostly just built some channels in that should conduct Qi a little better than the rest. Probably going to be hard to get used to, but I used some of your own tissue to grow it, from the bone-stuff, so hopefully it’ll be better at letting your cultivation base through than what you’ve got in the rest of you. Have you considered switching cultivation energies? These ones really don’t seem to suit you.”

“They… are… the best that I have,” he groans. “For the… techniques I’ve managed to get ahold of. Need those energies.”

“Hmm. Fair enough! Ready for your rewards now?”

He inhales, then exhales. Repeats it a few times. It’s a bit rude to make someone wait, but she understands- new limbs are hard the first few times. She’s pretty sure she remembers that.

Eventually, he’s calmed down enough to turn back to her and try getting on his feet. With a painful, slow grunt, he makes it up, and then bows to her.

“This one thanks the ancient spirit for-”

“I’m really not ancient, you know!” she says, frowning at him. “I’ve only been alive, like, an hour. Even the rest of me’s got less than thirty years, I think!”

“I… apologize, great spirit.”

“Don’t feel too bad about it. Anyways- ready for the rewards?”

“Is there… some reason to hurry? I do not wish to dissuade you, spirit, but I might need just a moment to rest.”

“...Oooor you could enjoy your rewards right away, because I worked really hard on them and I have other things to do?”

He flinches, as if expecting a worse rebuke. Instead, she just sits there and waits, looking at him expectantly. She really wants to see his reaction to what she’s made. Besides, he’s fine, she fixed him already.

“I would be grateful to accept your generosity, great one.”

“Great!” From out of her flesh, space starts to warp until a twisting whirlpool of meat opens wide, depositing into her lower set of hands a cube made entirely of bone.

“So, if you imprint this with your Qi, it’ll grow into a set of bone armor that’ll help enhance your strength and speed. You should be able to modify for defense, too, but that’ll be up to you. I couldn’t think of anything for your dream-Qi, but there should be some channels in the bones that you can store light and force inside of. Don’t do too much or it will explode, though, which will be very bad if the armor’s grown to connect with your bones. But if it does! Then you should be able to store it inside your body to hide it, if you need. I know how hard it is to get outfits when your body is shaped differently than normal.”

He stares at the cube, then, gingerly, extends his new hand out to grasp it. A trickle of his remaining Qi moves into it, making the cube vibrate slightly with force, glow, and start to sprout little coral-like protrusions.

“I… this is… how durable can it become?”

“That’s dependent on you. I think it should be tough enough to block mortal steel pretty much as soon as it’s done growing, but in theory, if you can get enough Qi into it, it should be able to do a lot. I don’t know though! I just made it.”

Geng He bows again, this time much more sincerely. There’s no twitching microfeature of his face stopping a stray comment he’s thought of.

“Geng He thanks the honored spirit for providing this incredible gift.”

She smiles wide, grinning at him with hands on her hips. “Damn right it’s incredible! And you’re damn welcome to it. I thought you were a real paper tiger at first, but you really held out that whole time. Even when I got distracted!”

Geng He’s face does that little microtwitch again.

“I’m giving the others their gifts right now, so pretty soon you can all be on your way. Thanks for the whole trial idea. Or… I guess that was more your friend. Yu-something. Either way! Nice meeting you, have fun leaving.”

“I mean… I guess you can stay? I don’t know why you would, though. It’s just an empty room.”

“In this one? No, here it’s just me. Plenty of other stuff, though, I’m still getting a feel for it all. There’s some tunnels in the mountains that are super interesting, haven’t really gotten a chance to explore that yet. Lots to do. Why?”

Geng He can’t quite hide his disappointment fast enough. “I suppose I had hoped to… your gift is a princely one, honored spirit. I apologize.”

She pauses at that last revelation. She likes it, because… it reminds her of someone? Of Herself?

Blockages shut the thought down. Safeguards, so many and in so many directions.

Still! It’s basically confirmation. Raika is and was bold. That’s important.

“Alright! I’ve made a decision. Wait here a second, this is going to be super loud.”

And with that, her body collapses onto its tail, slumping over lifeless as her attention leaves it for other, larger avenues of communication.

Her mind expands as she stresses her system again, feeling the ways it’s already starting to adjust to the new demands and changing to better suit a connection between conscious thought and the landscape. Glorious growth, joyous development- and not her current focus.

She passes by groups battling particularly ornery parts of her biology. She finds a campsite where multiple individuals have begun trading, establishing a base from which to explore a vast canyon that breathes, expanding and contracting in rhythm. She finds a hidden cave where two peoples dressed in very different robes are enjoying some very intimate behaviors, and then expands past them to include the ones trapped and dying inside of an ever-healing corner of the lower mountains.

From the cultivators on a floating palanquin, fighting a flying serpent-thing, to the one that’s fishing at a lake that’s a giant eyeball, to the ones killing each other, to the ones harvesting plants and rare herbs, her mind travels.

Rather than try to change the entire landscape at once like she did before, she instead does something simpler.

There are a lot of surprised noises that come out of a lot of people when she forms mouths next to them.

“So I heard you all like trials! Hello! I’m doing more of them. Stay where you are and don’t say anything if you want to get put into a deadly situation for special rewards afterwards! You have ten seconds!”

There is a lot of stunned silence and wasted moments.

Then the hidden realm explodes into motion.
