'Absolutely annihilate,' perhaps.

All fixed-wing aircraft flew thanks to magic. Even in the Age of Oppression, when most engineers had absolutely no idea about the Tapestry and only took into account atomic models of the universe, they still took advantage of the increased speed and decreased pressure which a properly-shaped wing could produce above it, even if they didn't fully understand why it happened. Adding mana to the picture made it obvious, though - a proper wing functioned as a kind of 'second ground', creating a small bubble of extra-airy Air directly above it. Those Associations cause the wind directly above the wing to move faster and be less dense, creating the physical Force known as lift.

And because Oliver lived post-oppression, he knew all kinds of tricks to enhance the 'false ground' Associations of a wing, though that didn't always make figuring out the right balance of Earth mana to include easy. Too much and the false ground would become too real, increasing its drag and mass and making it want to stay stationary. Too little, and he wasn't getting any real benefit.

But that was where a slow fall was so great! Doubly so with a slow fall intended for gliding. Oliver could cut the influence of gravity on his aircraft by a literal order of magnitude just by ⟨Inlay⟩ing the subskill into some appropriate fabric, and he had a solid run of that flycloth already proofed for more clothing purposes.
