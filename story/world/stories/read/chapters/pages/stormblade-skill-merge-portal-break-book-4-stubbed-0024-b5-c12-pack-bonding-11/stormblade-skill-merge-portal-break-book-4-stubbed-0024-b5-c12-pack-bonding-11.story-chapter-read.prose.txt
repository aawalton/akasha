Ellen was flush with power.

She’d been A-Rank before. Technically, she still had been, even with her core fracturing in the wake of the Siege of Phoenix. Her core had felt complete then.

What a fool she’d been.

This was true power. The truest, most real power an A-Ranker could acquire. Her core wasn’t just fixed. The shadow she’d poured into it, and the work she’d done on it, had strengthened it to its limits. She didn’t have more Mana. Her spells didn’t hit harder, and her aura didn’t inspire more fear, nor her Tides of Darkness more destruction as hungry shadows ripped at her enemies’ bodies and spirits. Nevertheless, Ellen’s strength had increased. Her potential.

And it wasn’t just the potential in her core.

As Kade climbed the Black Tower, she hung back. The portal world seemed almost…eager…to respond to her whims. It was terrifying—not the least because the Black Tower was now a storm world, not a shadow one. It bore a superficial resemblance to the Dark Citadel portal it had been, but that was all.

Tanat caught her eye. One of the alien woman’s brows rose, and her lips parted in an unspoken question.

“It’s like the portal world thinks of me as its mistress,” she explained.

Tanat nodded knowingly. “That would probably be your Dual Skill Progression in action. You and your partner are one, not only in name, but in spirit. You have no doubt noticed his presence in your core, and yours in his?”

“Of course,” Ellen said.

“What a joy that must be, to share so intimately with a partner,” Tanat said quietly. “That link is most likely causing the Black Tower to view you as Kade, and Kade as you. An interesting situation, to be sure. I admit to being envious of your relationship with him.”

“What do you mean?”

“I mean, Eleanor Traynor, that both of you have very complimentary cores, archetypes, and roles, and your bond is shockingly strong. Even Dual Skill Progression rarely manifests this intensely.”

“Ah,” Ellen said. Her connection to Kade was sensitive. She’d felt the moment he’d forged his new Path, even with her fractured core. It had probably started with the B-Rank attempt just before Yalerox arrived in the Hurricane portal, and had grown from there with every interaction they’d had. Every date—both the good ones and the ones to Traynor Corporation galas. Every portal cleared. Every…every night they’d shared.

Tanat smiled widely as Ellen’s face heated up. “If he or I were lower-ranked, I would consider attempting to join your Dual Skill Progression group. Alas, I missed my window.”

“Uh,” Ellen said. Something about Tanat put her off her guard in a way that no gala ever could. “Thank you for not.”

“Of course, Eleanor Traynor. Now, shall we continue? I wish to see this Eye of the Storm.”

Ellen was more than happy to move on—and to never talk to Tanat about Dual Skill Progression again.

The Black Tower was far from finished.

My design had fatal flaws. Lightning escaped its circuits every second, wasting its power and potential. The storm overhead was strong—stronger than anything I could do by myself—but it wasn’t strong enough. I needed something on par with Queen Mother Yalerox’s Eye of the Storm.

And it wasn’t just a weapon anymore. It was a promise now—that Tanat could see what I was trying to create, and that she could access it, and that she could control it. I needed a viable product to show her. It didn’t have to be perfect, but it needed to be functional. Right now, the cage surrounding the Black Tower itself and the cables transferring electricity from the lightning field that had once been a city were the weak points.

I reached out to the cage, and the Black Tower responded. It shifted and bent to my will as I tried to shape it into a replica of Queen Mother Yalerox’s central sandstone spire. That structure made sense as a storm generator, but I didn’t have the God of Thunder’s eidetic memory, and I couldn’t replicate it perfectly.

And then there was Ellen.

She was here now—in the Black Tower—and restored to her full strength. And that had an impact on my work. The dark clouds obeyed my will, and they fell into my design, but her influence was…

“Kade?” she asked.

I opened my eyes and stood from the throne I’d all but melted into. “Hi, Ellen.”

Before it could get awkward, she walked up the dias and wrapped me in an embrace. “Thank you for this, Kade.”

“I made a promise. With you for the long haul.”

“I know, but you could have let me recover naturally. My core was going to fix itself over time, and—

“No, I couldn’t,” I interrupted. A pause. The Black Tower pulsed around me, its will aligned with mine, ready to get back to the work. “Let me show Tanat the Eye of the Storm and explain what I need from her. Then I’ll give you all the time I can.”

Ellen looked over her shoulder, locked eyes with Tanat, and nodded. “Okay, Kade. I’ll be patient.”

“Great. Tanat, welcome back to the Black Tower. This is—“

“Your portal world now,” she interrupted. “I recognized your relationship with it when we arrived. It is almost identical to that Retan had with the Soaring Sabers’ canyon.”

I nodded and pointed the swordswoman to the throne. “Have a seat.”

“You know this may not work, correct? Your bond with Eleanor Traynor is different than your connection to me in fundamental ways.”

Instead of answering, I just waited until she sat down. Then I pointed up at the open top of the Black Tower. “I want to tell you a story about a brave man I knew. His name was Carroll, and he died storming a tower much like this one. It was guarded by a monster called Queen Mother Yalerox and her countless spawn. She attacked my world, laid siege to one of our fortresses, and set out to conquer the desert we lived in.

“Carroll was an A-Ranker, and he was in charge of the fortress’s defense. When we—Ellen, my team, and I—arrived in Carlsbad, we convinced him to join us. Another delver’s attack forced us into Yalerox’s A-Rank portal world.”

“A-Rank? Simple.”

I nodded, wincing. “If we’d been stronger, yes, but only Carroll was an A-Ranker. The rest of us were C-Rank. We fought hard, but he died in Yalerox’s throne room, fighting her bodyguard. His sacrifice—and the sacrifice of my core—helped us beat Queen Mother Yalerox and her ultimate weapon.”

Tanat nodded slowly. “Often, monsters will create weapons or have skills that allow them power beyond their rank, at a cost.”

“Exactly. Now, my core was broken, but my patron and the leader of the Path I walked helped me repair it with Queen Mother Yalerox’s own core—and that led to a realization. Now that I have my own portal world, I have access to the Eye of the Storm, just as she did. The angry clouds overhead are the beginning of that weapon, but they’re not the finished version—and I don’t have time to build it myself.”

Tanat closed her eyes. Power coursed through her, and her aura flared and vibrated as Mana poured into her body. The Eye of the Storm wasn’t finished, but even in its current state, it had the power to enhance its user. Ellen readied herself, but I put a hand on her shoulder. “Tanat’s just testing the Eye.”

“You’re sure?”

Before I could answer, Tanat stood. “This feels like a functional beginning, and I can do what you need me to do. The Black Tower will respond to me. It will be slow, but I can create something akin to the Eye of the Storm.”

“I’d do it myself, but…”

“Yes, I know,” Tanat said. “However, your partner needs you, and you have little time. Consider this repayment for helping me with my Lonely Gate.”

“Thank you, Tanat,” I said. We discussed the Eye of the Storm—Yalerox’s towers, how she’d used the Eye, and what it did, as well as its critical interaction with Stormbreak. By the end of it, Tanat knew almost as much about it as I did.

And then, finally, I left her and focused on Ellen.

We left the Black Tower quickly and returned to Earth.

I had my reasons. For one thing, Tanat’s project could easily require the tower to come down and rebuild itself, and that process could be dangerous. I doubted it’d kill any of us, but we couldn’t afford injuries.

For another, I hadn’t spent any time with Ellen, and we both needed it. It was a sacrifice. The timer was ticking down, and at any moment, the Placid King could make a move. But even so…even so, we both needed it. It was worth the risk. Very, very worth it—especially once we retreated to the suite at the top of the Desert Wind building and locked the door behind us.

Afterward, Ellen rolled over and stared at me. Her eyes were serious. “You really didn’t have to—“

I sighed. “Yes, I did. It’s not just about the promise I made you. I need you with me for what’s coming. I need every ally I can get—Tanat, the Phoenix S-Rankers, and Jessie—but even more than that, I need you by my side when the God of Thunder comes for me. I don’t know how else I can beat him, Ellen.”

“Tell me about Retan,” Ellen said. “What was he up to?”

I closed my eyes. “You’re not going to like it.”

“I don’t care.”

“Alright. I’ll start with the Crone, then. After you and Jessie left, she told me about the ascension process and what it entails.” I spent ten minutes filling Ellen in on everything I hadn’t already explained about what the SS-Plus-Ranked Paragon had told me, answered her questions, then ran through the Mana pillar I’d found in Retan’s treasure hall and what I thought he was doing to his students’ cores.

Ellen didn’t say anything right away. The silence hung, but it wasn’t uncomfortable. After almost two minutes, she cleared her throat. “Retan. That’s your biggest threat, not Eugene or the Placid King.”

“How do you figure?”

“You said the Crone believes that pressure to ‘play’ the ascension game starts at SS-Rank, right? You’re sitting at S-Rank. If you get too much stronger, you’ll end up in the same situation Retan, Eugene, and the Crone are in. That’s the real pressure. It’s not just gaining enough strength to kill the God of Thunder. It’s doing it without losing yourself.”

I nodded and propped myself up on an elbow. “I’ve got a responsibility. I made promises, and I’m going to see them through.”

More silence. Eventually, Ellen kept going. “I see what you’re trying to do. What are you missing?”

“The Stormblade Path is incomplete. Something is missing, but I don’t understand what. A way out, maybe? A way to avoid playing their games. I need to find that and add it in. The Crone uses the Ghostmarket. Maybe I can use the Black Tower similarly. But…it’s not the same.”

I stood up and stared out the window. The sun poured in; in a few minutes, it’d be time to help Jessie get things in order with the Governing Council, the Portal Tyrants, and the Guardians. Not so long ago, I’d been an E-Ranker, with a skill I’d wanted to keep secret from the guilds. I’d been worried about being recruited by them and being drawn into their politics. Now, in the space of a single day, their squabbles seemed incredibly far beneath me.

The guilds had been the most powerful organizations in my world, but not one of their members could stand up to the powers I’d been dealing with, or the ones who wanted to kill me.

“I won’t play their games,” I said. “I’m not going to let Retan’s fate be mine—or become the God of Thunder trying to stop him.”

“I know, Kade.” Ellen stood and put her arms around me, pressing her chin into my shoulder blades. She was warm, and for a long time, neither of us moved, and we watched the sun over the 303 Wall.

Then, eventually, she let go. “I’ve been going over my status, and I’m close. Very close. If we could find an A-Rank portal, I could probably push myself to the cusp of S-Rank. From there, we’d need to find a way to get me there, but I could easily be S-Rank with you by the time the God of Thunder comes.”

I nodded. “We’re going to make that happen. The God of Thunder laid out the proper way to reach S-Rank, and I paid attention to my deviations from it. We’ll get you on a Path you can follow—maybe the Crone’s Path—or we’ll build one of your own.”

She laughed. “First, we need an A-Rank portal, and those won’t be easy to find. I can only think of one we have easy access to.”

“Which one?”

“Guess?”

I thought about it for a while, but nothing came to mind. And eventually, my phone’s buzzing interrupted me. It was Jessie.

Jessie: Kade, you and Ellen need to be down here in ten minutes. We’ve got a meeting with the GC.

Jessie: I’m dead serious about this. You have no idea how many strings I had to pull to make it happen.

Jessie: Ten minutes.

“You’re not just going to tell me, are you?” I asked.

Ellen shook her head. “You’ll figure it out. For now, let’s get ready. Your sister needs your help. Hopefully, by the time this is over, you’ll have it figured out.”
