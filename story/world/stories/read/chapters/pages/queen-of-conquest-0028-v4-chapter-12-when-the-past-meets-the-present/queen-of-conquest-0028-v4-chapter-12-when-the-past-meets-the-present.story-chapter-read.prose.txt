The world was consumed by the screams of the dying and the stench of the dead.

Cries overlapped and tangled together, some sharp and pleading, others wet and broken, choking off into gurgles that ended too abruptly. They rose and fell in waves, echoing through the smoke until it was impossible to tell how many voices there were or where they were coming from. Beneath it all lingered the smell of rot and hot iron, thick enough to cling to the back of my throat.

The earth beneath my feet wept red. Blood pooled and branched outward in crawling tendrils, spreading like a grotesque spiderweb across the battlefield, dyeing broken armor, shattered weapons, and torn bodies in streaks of scarlet. Some of it was fresh and slick, still steaming faintly where it touched the air. Other patches had begun to darken and clot, sticky and resistant underfoot. Every step I took disturbed it, ripples forming in the gore like I was wading through something half-alive.

I stood at the center of it all.

The realization carried no pride. No triumph. Only a hollow weight that pressed down on my chest, as if the battlefield itself were judging me for surviving it.

Thick, cloying smoke pressed in from every side, choking its way down my throat and coating my tongue with the taste of iron, ash, and bile. Each breath felt wrong, too shallow or too slow, my lungs burning as though they rejected the air entirely. It burned to breathe. When I stepped forward, my boot came down on something solid.

There was a wet crunch.

Bone gave way beneath my heel, someone’s kneecap collapsing with a sickening snap. The sound carried too far in the stillness, sharp and final. I barely reacted. I didn’t even look down. Whatever face had once been attached to it did not matter anymore.

I couldn’t tell where I was, though a deep, gnawing certainty told me I had been here before.

The feeling crept beneath my skin, threading itself through old memories I had buried deep. My stomach twisted as recognition brushed the edges of my mind, refusing to fully surface.

There was nothing to see. Even when I squinted, even when I strained my senses, the crimson smog swallowed everything beyond a few feet. Shapes drifted in and out of existence, corpses looming and vanishing at the edges of my vision. Some were slumped in unnatural angles. Others lay twisted together, armor fused by dried blood, weapons half-buried beneath them. They watched me in silence, half-formed accusations that pressed in from every direction.

Then the smoke moved.

Not drifting. Not swirling.

It split.

Straight down the center, as if parted by an invisible blade, peeling back to form a narrow corridor through the haze. The smoke resisted for a heartbeat, curling inward along the edges, before holding still in an unnatural, rigid divide. From within it, a silhouette emerged, broad-shouldered and unmistakably male. His footsteps were slow, deliberate, unhurried, each one heavy with purpose, as though this place belonged to him and always had.

As he drew closer, the details sharpened.

Short-cropped black hair, streaked through with silver. An unshaved beard framing a sharp jaw I knew far too well. Memory slammed into me without mercy. I remembered that jaw clenched tight before his hand struck, before the world went white and ringing, before pain became something ordinary.

His eyes were red.

The same deep, blood-soaked crimson as mine.

The color of the Aedronir bloodline.

Had we stood side by side, he would have been about a head taller than me. I didn’t need to measure it to know. He had always loomed over me, looking down with a gaze that mixed ownership with expectation. Condescending. Possessive. Proud.

“Ahhh,” the deceased King of Aedronir hissed, dragging a hand through his beard. “There you are, Lilith. I’ve been looking for you.”

My throat locked.

The sound of my own heartbeat filled my ears, loud and frantic. I tried to swallow and couldn’t. My mouth was bone-dry, my tongue sticking uselessly against my teeth. My pulse thudded so loudly I was sure he could hear it, see it, feel it thrumming through the air between us.

“You’re,” my voice came out hoarse, “you’re dead.” I looked around, panic crackling through my senses like bolts of wayward lightning. The battlefield felt suddenly too close, the smoke pressing in harder, heavier.

He nodded, thoughtful, almost indulgent, as if humoring a child. “Oh, I was dead.” He pressed two fingers to his chest, over his heart. “I can still feel where your hand tore through. So very clearly.”

His smile vanished.

He stepped forward, the distance between us shrinking too fast, almost warping around him as if time and space meant nothing here. The pressure of his presence bore down on me, dragging old fear and fury up from my mind. “But not everything stays dead, daughter. You should know that better than anyone.”

Then he was gone.

The space in front of me was suddenly empty, the smoke rushing inward to fill the void. Before I could react, his voice whispered against my ear, cold and intimate, each word brushing my skin like a blade’s edge. “You cannot run. You cannot hide. I will come for you and return you to your rightful place, by my side.”

Rage detonated in my chest.

It burned away the fear in an instant, leaving only fury and defiance. I snarled and spun, striking out behind me with everything I had, fist tearing through the air where his skull should have been.

It passed through smoke.

The cloud exploded outward, scattering like a living thing recoiling from the blow. Tendrils of red haze lashed away from my fist before curling back in on themselves.

His cruel laughter echoed from everywhere at once bouncing off the battlefield and crawling beneath my skin. His crimson eyes burned within the haze like the twin suns over Ordite, judging me. “Always so rash.”

“You don’t know me,” I spat, teeth bared. “I killed you once. I’ll kill you again.”

He laughed harder, the sound thick with amusement. Then, dripping with mockery, he said, “Then come find me, oh Queen of Rot.”

“How—”

The smoke slammed into me.

It hit like a tidal wave, driving the air from my lungs. Pain tore through my body, sudden and absolute, as if thousands of thick nails were being driven into my bones all at once. My vision fractured, white light bleeding through the red as my senses screamed in protest.

And then I woke.

I bolted upright in bed, Deathbringer already in my hands, spinning in a wide arc as my senses flared outward. The room blurred as I searched for movement, for intent, for anything that didn’t belong. Shadows clung to the corners, stretching and shrinking with my breath, but none of them moved.

There was nothing.

Only silence, heavy and oppressive, settling slowly back into place.

It came out slow and shaky, my lungs protesting as if I had been submerged for far too long. My grip on Deathbringer loosened by degrees, though I did not lower the blade. My senses remained stretched thin, probing every shadow and corner of the room for lingering intent.

Nothing and no one.

The silence pressed in, broken only by the faint sound of my own breathing. The bed beneath me was real. The walls were solid. The air was cool and clean compared to the battlefield smoke that still felt lodged in my chest.

Questions flooded my mind, tumbling over one another too fast to grasp.

Had that been a dream?

I shook my head. No. It had been far too vivid, far too coherent to be dismissed as a mere nightmare. Dreams did not leave my body trembling like this, nor did they burn themselves so deeply into my memory that I could still taste the ash.

Had he summoned me to his Dreamscape somehow?

That was impossible. My jaw clenched as the thought formed. He was dead. I’d broken his neck, torn out his heart, and burned what was left.

Regardless, dreamscapes required physical contact to enter. There were rules, boundaries that even the strongest minds could not simply ignore.

And yet.

No one in Graedon knew my past. No one here knew what the King of Aedronir had looked like before his death, much less the way he moved or spoke. No one could have recreated that presence so perfectly. It was impossible that someone was manipulating me. They simply could not have had that knowledge.

Unless he was not as dead as the I believed.

I had survived my own execution. I had clawed my way back from a fate that should have been final. Was it truly so unlikely that he had done the same?

But even then, the thought snagged painfully. How could he have known about the Queen of Rot title?

“He was long dead when that happened,” I muttered.

The realization settled like a stone in my gut as the memory finally aligned. The battlefield. The screams. The blood-soaked ground.

The day I realized there were no victors in war.

At that realization the world titled and the fog returned, rushing to cacoon around me.

For a brief moment, my vision blurred and I thought the scene would collapse entirely, that I would be dragged back into the safety of waking reality.

It didn’t.

Realization that I'd never woken up struck me, and I began to scan around me for threats. Irritation that I'd allowed myself to be so easily fooled was quickly filed away for later as the smoke continued to thicken, rolling inward as if drawn by an unseen pull. From the same place my father had vanished, a second figure stepped forward.

My breath caught.

Is that… me?

At first, it felt like staring into a warped mirror, one that reflected me from a different angle or a different time. The resemblance was undeniable. The same build. The same posture. The same burning red eyes.

But as she drew closer, the differences emerged.

She looked younger than I'd been in Ordite, much younger, but older than I would appear now. It was difficult to tell age after a certain cultivation level. Still, I was confident, at the very least, she was younger than I'd been when I'd been cursed as the Queen of Rot.

Her eyes were crimson, but paler, lacking the depth and weight my own carried. The scar on her face was shallow, a thin mark compared to mine that had nearly taken my eye.

Still, none of that was what unsettled me the most.

It was the way she walked. Her gait was aggressive, shoulders squared, chin lifted as if daring the world to challenge her. There was no restraint in her movements, no hesitation. She carried herself like a weapon already in mid-swing, as though destruction was not a consequence but an intention.

Angry.

Uncontrolled.

Just like I had been. Maybe like I still was.

When she spoke, it only confirmed what I already knew.

“So this is where he went,” she hissed, jabbing a finger in my direction. “It was you.”

She stopped, clearly expecting a response.

My brows knit together as I stared at her for a long moment. Then I tilted my head, genuine confusion cutting through the tension. “Who in all the fucks are you?”

“I am who you were. Who you should have been,” she spat. “The new you. I am Andromeda-"

The laugh tore out of me before I could stop it, cutting off her monologue. Loud and cruel, just like my father's had been. “The new me?” I glanced her over again, realization slowly clicking into place as the pieces aligned. I laughed again. "A replacement? How did he alter your True Self during Reformation to match mine?” I asked, genuinely curious. I had never heard of that being even remotely possible.

Her eyes narrowed, and for the first time, a cruel smirk curled across her face. “Father altered nothing,” she growled. "This is who I am. But who are you, Lilliana Silverwater?" Andromeda's face twisted into bitter disgust as she spat the name. "I know who you truly are. What you are. The monster you have always been. You were no daughter to father. Not even a weapon. You were his little monster, the pet he kept at his side to do the work he didn't want. I know everything about you Lilith. Every city you burned, every family you destroyed, every scream that follows you even now, long after you claimed your victory. I know how many lives your hands stole, how many bloodlines you erased. I know the monster you once were, the monster you still are, hiding behind your pathetic crown of a fallen kingdom."

My pulsed spiked, echoed by the powerful drumming of my heartbeat at her words. Not denial. I knew what I'd done. I'd been a Queen of Conquest. That was what I did. I warred and conquered. Rather it was the way she said it, the word monster she spat that my soul raged against. "You hide it well from your comrades, don't you?" she continued, voice like ice scraping against stone.

"They see a Queen. A ruler. Maybe even a friend. They do not see the carnage, the death, the blood that stains every step you take. I see it all. And they will see at all soon too, won't they?" At my shifting expression, Andromeda grinned. "Ah, perhaps they've already seen a taste of how cruel you can truly be. But no, not the full extent. Not yet. Oh, they will though. I know they will. After all, we are the same, aren't we, Lilith? We are beings of war, conquest, and carnage. You won't be able to go long before you begin to show how truly, how utterly monsterous you are." She took a step closer. My body refused to move, my mind struggling under her words. I did what I needed to do.

"I-," I started, but she didn't let me speak.

"How long do you think you can hide it, Lilith? How long before those you call comrades realize their crimson duchess, Hero of Lysoria, is nothing but a killer hiding in their midst? How long before the world learns the truth? The truth about the Queen of Rot?"

When her voice trailed, I tilted my head, swallowing dryly before offering the mirrored version of myself a wry smile. "I am what I need to be. You may call it a monster. Others may too. But I am, who I am. I do what I need to do." I tapped a finger against my temple. "You may be a bringer of carnage. A weapon for father. But I... I am not a weapon. Above all else, I am a queen."

"A queen of nothing," Andromeda spat. "the day will come when your allies, your comrades, your so-called paragons, will see the truth. And when they do, you will fall. You will realize that nothing protects you from yourself.”

I stepped toward her, batting away the whisps of smoke still flickering about, my lips splitting into a wild snarl. “I am Lilith of Aedronir. Queen of Aedronir. The sole remaining Queen of Conquest. I will not fail. I will always rise, even when there is nothing left to rise for. I do not know who you are, but if you serve Niro, our father, then you are nothing but one of his puppets. You’ll realize it soon enough.”

Before I could release my Authority and crush the space between us, her form began to unravel. Smoke peeled away from her limbs, her outline blurring as though reality itself was rejecting her presence.

“Perhaps you were a puppet,” she cackled, rage blazing in her eyes. “But I am not. I am of His true bloodline, not a half-Royal like you, artificial and fake. My mother is the true Queen of His land. Yours wore a crown, but she was born in the dirt. A candidate, like you. Made, not born.”

Her figure shimmered violently as it continued to dissolve. “I will show you that you are nothing, Lilith. Nothing compared to His true bloodline heirs. I am Andromeda, Imperial Princess of Pandoria, primary heir to the Pandorian Empire and the true successor to Emperor Niro.”

Her voice lingered even as her body vanished completely.

“And I will be your death.”

The space around me erupted into blinding white light.

I was seized by a violent sensation of being pulled in millions of directions at once, my senses stretching to their limit, until everything collapsed inward with a deafening pop.
