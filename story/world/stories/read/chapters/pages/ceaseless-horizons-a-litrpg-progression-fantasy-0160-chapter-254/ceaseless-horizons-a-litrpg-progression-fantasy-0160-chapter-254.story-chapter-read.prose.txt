Both the slashes moved at similar speeds, which meant that Melvine could barely see them. One blue, almost dark blue from the sheer compression, and the other a bright crimson.

They should have clashed.

They really were on track to clash.

The slash then passed less than two meters away from the bubble, and the sheer mana radiating off it made Aryan pass out. Isabella’s team was badly damaged from the vicinity due to a potent draining effect, and even if Elias’s team did better due to Lisa’s timely defensive buffs, they still buckled over.

Melvine didn’t notice any of that. The boss wasn’t moving. It stood there, staring at the approaching calamity that was Lev’s lightning slash with wide eyes. Despite the distance, she could see something in the maddened gaze, something she couldn’t hope to understand.

Hooves kicked the ground in urgency. Craters formed with each impact, yet the stag stood rooted, even taking a small step to the side, bringing itself directly in front of the slash.

It didn’t make sense.

Before the attack could land, the Hunter finally arrived at the scene. Bleeding and mangled, it still flew through the air faster than she could track, grabbing the unconscious Lev. His terrifying glaive slipped from his hands, though before it could fall, one of Isabella’s whips wrapped around it.

Then the Hunter punched the bubble and, with a mighty sweep of its hand, pushed everyone through the hole. Melvine felt herself slam into the durable construct’s wall as her vision flopped, and the world became sadistic.

Feedback drilled into her brain. Her Perception skills forced her to notice the absurd amount of lightning mana rushing towards them, eviscerating everything in its path.

The Hunter also recognized the severity of the situation. It dove, dragging the bubble to the ground just as Lev’s glaive returned inside the bubble. Dirt and stone parted, engulfing them again once they were inside the hill and rapidly descending.

And yet, she could feel the calamitous explosion catching up. The hills in its path might as well not have existed, for it continued unimpeded, slowly getting closer and closer. Breathing became difficult as the air heated up like a herald of its inevitable descent.

She closed her eyes, pushing her mist into the bubble and praying for the best. Her perception left no choice but to track the approaching wall of crackling mana.

But it began to slow down. When there were only a few dozen meters left between them, the explosion’s advance slowed down to a crawl.

When Melvine thought she could almost see the lightning through the stone behind the hunter as they swam deeper, their speed finally outpaced it.

A threshold was reached, and the lightning fizzled out in the next few moments. It felt like an eternity had passed, yet they had barely reached twenty meters below the ground.

Something tapped her shoulder. It was a barrier belonging to Lisa, and only then did Melvine realize that the whole bubble was filled with dense mist. She guided it all out of the hole in the bubble, and Rena conjured a fire to bring the temperature back from freezing to normal.

One of the Hunter’s arms was barely hanging onto their body, connected by a few thin muscles. There were deep cuts in the torso, each reminiscent of claw swipes. Dark, black blood oozed out of the wounds, hiding many other injuries underneath a layer of dried blood.

His arms were torn open, but they were much better than the little of his legs she could see through her Perception. The suit he wore at all times had saved him from any external shockwaves, but it did nothing to protect harm from within.

She racked her brain. Lev wasn’t stupid. He was always observing and taking notes, be it fighting or social interactions. She had seen the gears turning inside his head more than a few times whenever he talked to the folks on the expedition.

That left only one answer for why he put himself through this.

Why did Drakys tell him about physiques?

Melvine did another deep scan, just to see if she missed anything. She hadn’t. Lev’s efforts were wasted.

His body was completely mundane. There was no sign of any budding passive skills related to his body, nor were there any hints of improved mana conductivity.

He was doing it all for no reason.

Not even two seconds later, he finally slumped on the bubble floor, having recalled Stargazer back into his palms already.
