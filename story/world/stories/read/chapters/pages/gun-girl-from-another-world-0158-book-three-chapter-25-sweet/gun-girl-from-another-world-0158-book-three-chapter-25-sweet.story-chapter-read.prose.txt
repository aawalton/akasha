Chapter 25

Sweet

I’ve been an engineer. I’ve been a gunslinger. I’ve been a Hero. I’ve been a blacksmith, a wheelwright, and an alchemist. I’ve been a farmer, a diplomat, and a merchant.

Today, I’m going to become an apiarist.

I’ve done my studying up on it and purchased the relevant skills from the System. I knew the gist back on Earth, as I always thought it’d be cool to have a rooftop setup and a steady supply of happy syrup. Now that the essence crop experiment has been verified with the dairy cow and chickens, I could only make myself wait until we’d laid plans to hire more staff before diving into this particular expansion.

I cleared more yard for it, too. I’ve done surprisingly well at filling up the first two acres I cleared when I got here with the growing outdoor gym that has basically become a Ninja Warrior course and shooting gallery, plus the actual firing range. Then there’s all of the crops, including trees, bushes, shrubs, and whatever the heck sugar cane falls under among those, as well as the barn and grazing field. With the new workshop built and the house and surrounding grounds finally staked off, it’s expand or start getting tight.

It’s not a total loss. The lumber will be used in the building of the house (I absolutely blew Daelon’s mind when I suggested frame construction), and I’ve gotten much faster at clearing out stumps, with a few more tools for it besides. I’m not so concerned about the grass this time, though, because I’ve used the Turbo Seed bullets to fill the glade with an array of Essence Clover, as well as several bushes of Essence Honeysuckle and, just because I can be wholly confident they’re not going anywhere, some golden yellow Essence Star Thistle.

Spread out across the beautiful new acre of flowers are three distinct stacks of boxes, each standing up off the ground, with holes in the side and a lid on top. Every one of them is already smeared with orchids. Soon, their new tenants will be here, ready to move in.

“Wow, what a beautiful field of flowers!” Korrigan’s voice reaches me as she comes up from the direction of the house. When she stops beside me, she points to one of the wooden boxes. “Is that where they’re going to live?”

“That’s right,” I confirm. “The trapper should be bringing them soon.”

The child swallows a bit of drool. “How long before we get any honey?”

The question makes me frown, but only because it’s not something I can answer definitively yet. “Normally, you only harvest from this species once a year, but everything seems to go faster and more plentiful when Essence is involved, and their diet is going to be one hundred percent Essence nectar from day one.”

I shift my weight as I cross one arm across my abdomen, though. “Honestly, I’d trade plenty for frequency. I’d like to have Essence Honey sooner rather than later, and with a harvest from three hives being as much as a hundred pounds normally, I don’t have any rational clue what I’d do with three hundred pounds of honey every year. And I’m not sure the hives could support that much mass, either.”

But the idea only excites Korrigan. “You could have honey on everything! Honey bread every day! Honey on roast! Honey on vegetables! You could just eat honeycomb for a snack!”

I chuckle as I reach down to ruffle her hair through her cap. “I think even you would get sick of that much honey, Korrigan.”

“Boy, I’d sure like the chance to find out!”

“I’ll bet.” My attention shifts to a small, human-drawn buggy rolling up the driveway. “Ah, there’s the man bringing our sweet new friends now.”

We meet the man as he sets the posts down and exchange greetings. He’s a local beekeeper. Well, not local to Dabun, but local to the region. I hired him for the three bait hives I can see sealed up in his cart.

“Hey, there, girls,” he greets us. “You all ready for your new residents?”

Now that the prospect is actually here, Korrigan seems to realize how close she is to the goods, and she’s suddenly a lot more hesitant. “Do they sting?”

That drives the farmer to throw his head back and laugh. “Sting?! These things?! No, no. Oh, they will if you leave them no other choice, sure, but they’re the sweetest little things to have needles!” He leaned toward the girl. “Did you know they don’t even sting hornets? One comes buzzing around looking for trouble, they hug it to death! Great, big, fluffy pile! Sit there on it until it gives up!”

“That’s way better than the bees where I come from,” I put in with half a grin. “We’ve got one species of honey bee that’s so territorial, it’ll chase you for over half a dozen hundred paces! They’ve actually killed over a thousand people in just half a century!”

“You shouldn’t sound so proud of them,” the young mage mutters with a shiver. “And you still say you don’t have any monsters?”

“Not a one!”

“Your home still sounds terrifying.”

But the beekeeper just chuckles. “It’s perfectly natural to be proud of your homeland, even if it is brutal.”

When he turns to reach into the cart, I step forward. “Can I help?”

“Please do! Just pick one up and try not to shake them. I’ll grab the other two.”

I do as instructed, and we start heading back to the flower field. “I thought bees had to be moved at night?”

“It’s best,” he confirms, but then glances over his shoulder at me with a smirk. “But, ah, I don’t know if you’ve ever been outside at night, but it’s a bit hard to see where you’re going.”

I give my eyes an amused roll. “Okay, I walked into that one,” I admit.

He nods back. “Anyway, these kids were bundled up last night, and then they got to rest their wings until I could get here this morning. It’s a bit longer than ideal, but it just means they’ll be happy to get out and stretch. It’s better to actually move them into the new hive in the late evening or under a light rain, too, but that doesn’t work so well for other people’s schedules if you aren’t doing it for yourself.”

I think about that timetable for a moment. “Wait, if these were gathered last night, why’d we need to wait two weeks after we placed our order?”

“That’s how long it takes them to settle once you’ve lured them in,” he explains. “Move them too soon, and they’ll abandon the new hive to try to find their way back to the old one. When I say they were bundled up last night, I mean that was when we blocked the entrance to keep them from leaving again after they all tucked in for the evening.”

This is a perfect example of how the education provided by the System is no replacement for actual experience. I know things like that, I understand the bees’ behavior, but I haven’t internalized it like he has. My hive boxes are immaculate, and I even provided different, otherwise meaningless ornamental objects in the proximity of each one as landmarks for the bees to follow, but I had to ask for an answer that was, to him, automatic.

When we reach the edge of the flower acre, he abruptly stops. “... Ah,” he exhales with wonder, “what a splendid field of blooms! I don’t believe I’ve ever seen its like. It’s clear now why you were so confident about being able to support three whole hives!”

Yeah, he didn’t want to sell me that many for a single acre, and suggested I do no more than two. At the time, I’d only told him that I had a lot of flowers, practically choking the entire acre glade. He agreed with the warning that, if he decided it wasn’t sufficient, he’d take one back, and the Empire doesn’t really have a habit of refunds when the mistake is your own fault, but this is his first time actually seeing it.

“Only the best for my new bay-bees,” I pun, daring the System to translate a dialectically-dependent play on words.

Both the farmer and Korrigan wrinkle their noses a bit, as if they'd caught a whiff of cow farts from the barn, but I don't smell anything, and those things should be at a premium from what I'm feeding her. They don't say anything about it, though, and it passes.

“And the hives?” I ask instead. “I'm confident in their quality, but are the designs right?”

As we come up to the first one, he looks it over, checks the entrance, checks underneath, and even checks the lid. When he finishes the quick but thorough examination, he nods. “That they are. Couldn't find a more recognizable bee hive design if you tried.” And he raps his knuckles against the side. “You're right to be confident in the work too. You must have hired a big city carpenter for them. The best for your bees, indeed.”

“Oh, I built them,” I admit. “I have the carpentry and woodworking general skills.”

The farmer's eyes bulge at that in surprise. “You do?! And this is your work?! Oh, I shouldn't have accepted tins from you, girl. I should have had you pay in a few new boxes of my own!”

“If they're that impressive, I'm happy to send some your way. Just let me know how many you need when we're done here, and I'll call it a gift of gratitude.”

“Oh, no, I'd have to pay you,” he insists. “These are made of cypress, not cedar! The number I need, no, even if I purchased them, I would have to insist on cedar.”

It's because cypress is considered to be much more expensive wood than cedar. It's less vulnerable to rot and holds an aroma that the bees find appealing. But it's not more valuable to me, though he's right that he wouldn't be able to afford it if it had a price tag attached. After all, rather than go buy the lumber, I grew my own. Yes, that's right, even the hives are essence wood! Every corner of these bees’ lives will be saturated with Heavenly EssenceTM!

… I pause for a moment, wondering if I'm about to hear from the marketing department, but my vision remains clear.

“Alright, then,” I declare as if he's left me no alternative, “if you won't tell me how many you want, I'll just make twenty-one of the things and have them delivered to your address.”

Both he and Korrigan stop at that.

“... Why twenty-one?” Korrigan speaks up.

“Because everyone knows odd numbers are extra annoying, especially when they're only slightly off.”

The man chuckles and shakes his head. “Alright, girl, I'll admit defeat. You can make the damned hives. But first, we've still got to get these ones moved over.”

And Korrigan and I huddle in close to watch as he casts a spell to make a small cloud of thick smoke drift up from the fingers of the hand he holds underneath the bait hive.
