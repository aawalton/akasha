Runes of blood painted every wall in a complex sprawl of a winding pentagram. Yog masterfully guided Nyxalraith’s blood into the intricate patterns she had devised.

Sprawling arrays of candles bathed the room in a dim light — she’d already gone out of her way to destroy the LED lighting. The shadows flickered, inviting her own into a haunting dance. In the centre of the chamber, where light struggled to reach, three cultists kneeled in a state of semi consciousness. They were Bodytwisters. That’s what a passing glance revealed. Nyxalraith hadn’t really cared much beyond the fact that they were seventh evo cultists and that they were nearby when she’d needed them.

While her blood had proven sufficient for most rituals, she had come to learn just how much doing so could be… less than desirable. It was like signing her signature. Sure, most cultists on Coral wouldn’t know what that meant, but Euphoria certainly would. And she wasn’t about to give him a heads up. She would have gut another cultist and used them for the paint if she thought her blood would affect the ritual circle itself.

Nyxalraith looked upon the site of the ritual with growing anticipation. The weave of patterns exuded an eerie sensation that tickled even her spine, and she was the one that designed it. Despite that, as she let her eyes wander, there was no imperfection to be altered. Her inner voice didn’t cringe like it did so often upon witnessing the cults’ rituals… especially the Technocult’s.

A soft voice slipped past her lips, and the walls began to warp.

The language she sang was unknowable, but each syllable was perfect. She simply knew it. The subtle inclinations and background tones added by her mutated throat rang off rippling steel like whispers of entities that lacked the concept of words.

Nyxalraith only had a few months of Ta’Taish’s lessons, but had taken to rituals like a fish to water. The concepts were both incredibly complex — brain melting puzzles that more often than not shouldn’t work — yet she understood them with only the slightest of effort. It was a language she somehow inherently knew, finally being unlocked.

And the hymn flowing from her chest? That, she’d been working on since the day she started.

Nobody would ever claim Nyxalraith didn’t hold grudges.

Of course, she hadn’t expected to actually put the ritual into effect so soon. There were still parts she could tweak. Those massive pillars from Zandisth’s ritual had given her plenty of inspiration to make Euphoria’s chains particularly vicious. Still, the core was solid. And well… there was also so much of that massive ritual that triggered a sense of revulsion in her. Structures she would never replicate.

Modules.

The Technocult were the worst for it — which made her lessons often painful — but it seemed near every ritual was modular. Only the Scriptures’ special rituals often carved into the skin of the higher creeds seemed to adopt real form. All the rest were bricks, lumped together with force and mortar.

She’d once thought the Technocult’s rituals were ugly… now she saw they were all the same.

Though the Technocult remained the worst.

Ugly, heinous, modular things.

Nyxalraith shivered at the thought. She’d once tried to convince Ta’Taish to cast aside such horrible creations and take upon the proper language. But he’d just given her the most insulting look. As if she was an idiot. So what if ritual designing was considered one of the most dangerous things to mess around with? A random plume of otherworldly spores infesting your skin was well worth having something that actually looked right.

Nyxalraith breathed and sharpened her mind on the task. As confident as she felt, she was still planning to trap a god.

The floor and ceiling joined the walls in their formless wiggle to the tune of her hymn as more of her voices joined in. She pushed herself. Those echoes rolled along her tongue until they formed entirely distinct words all in conjunction with each other. This wasn’t a simple chorus following a single rhythm. This was a dozen lead vocalists, weaving their own rhythms into a symphony.

The effect was immense, but her tongue twisted painfully to accommodate. She would have to see if there was a mutation hidden away in her core that would make her tongue capable.

Screams met her song, but Nyxalraith already had full control over the chamber. The sacrifices, now awake and struggling as they were lit on bright pink fire, had their voices stolen from them. Their screams flowed in underneath Nyxalraith’s hymn, becoming as much a part of the ritual as they were to be sacrificed through it.

Nyxalraith had wanted to be stronger before she engaged in this ritual. Not only would she have used the time to improve the ritual, but she wasn’t exactly at a level where she could confidently influence a god. Even a cowardly one. No, it was only thanks to Little God, Lyaegha’s, existence that she could push forward with the ritual as she was.

He wasn’t exactly going to be an active part of the ritual, but knowing she could tap into his nature without drawing the ire of a Greater God — or worse, their interest — Nyxalraith would have the framework to trap Euphoria. If he wasn’t now looking down on her with a constant physical weight, she would have assumed he wouldn’t even notice. To call upon a god’s aspects did not necessarily call upon them.

Not normally, at least. She was sure there were some unfortunate souls pulped to a drop of blood because one of the natures they’d been calling happened to look upon them.

Nyxalraith could have also tapped into the unnatural forces not controlled by any god… but they tended to be unpredictable. Turbulent. Aspects that a god couldn’t simply shake off didn’t often accept simply folding themselves neatly into a ritual. So often, they led to devastating consequences. Dark Stars, for instance. She wasn’t exactly excited to test whether she could warp one that wasn’t directly linked to her name as she had in her fight against Solan.

Despite all the possible issues that surrounded the ritual she performed, Nyxalraith was more than eager to go through with it. If she wasn’t so pissed at Alysyra, she might have been grateful for the excuse.

Learning that Ari was not exactly safe and sound had come as a shock. Not nearly as much as the gut punch that came from Alysyra. The girl was Invowed to her. Wasn’t it supposed to be impossible for the girl to lie to her? She’d thought small jokes or jabs were the extent of what she could get away with… but apparently not.

Nyxalraith was disturbed by the revelation, but not so much from Alysyra’s betrayal of her trust.

Her clear-cut openness as she admitted her faults and thoughts had been soothing. Once the shock wore off, she could understand that it was more of a mistake than a betrayal. Nyxalraith had, after all, considered the danger of keeping both Ari and Dan alive, what with how much each knew about her. For Alysyra to have similar thoughts? Well, she couldn’t judge her. But trusting her again would take time, even if she knew it wasn’t the girl’s fault. Even if she knew it was a mistake. She couldn’t help the reflexive doubts now floating in her head when it came to Alysyra.

But mostly, Nyxalraith was disturbed by the fact that she’d not even considered Alysyra lying to her. She was Invowed. Nyxalraith didn’t know enough about that to know for sure whether an Invowed would work towards what they thought was best, or what they thought the one they followed considered best. Was that even consistent between the Invowed?

Could Alysyra be keeping more hidden from her because she believed it better that Nyxalraith not know?

That was the question. Alysyra was everywhere on Coral. It wasn’t hard to think that she might have seen or heard something that Nyxalraith would really want to know, but decided it better she didn’t.

But she couldn’t let that infect her mind for now. In a few minutes, when the symphony reached the apex of its crescendo, Euphoria would be summoned. And when he was, Nyxalraith had plans for him.

Ever since that day he’d abandoned her and snatched her claw for the effort, she’d been thinking of how she would pay him back. Maybe she’d carve out that grin of his and make a trophy of it. Maybe she’d keep him trapped for a while, or keep him chained like a hound to sic on her enemies.

She didn’t intend to eradicate him, assuming she even could. Sure, she was going to make the giggling god pay, but what he had done wasn’t as bad as what the cult leaders had done. It wasn’t a true vengeance. Nyxalraith would make him work off that claw he’d taken, and his cowardice, but that was all.

It was funny, now that she thought about it. Euphoria was a god. Trying to tie down a god was one of the most dangerous rituals to perform, and yet Nyxalraith felt entirely confident she would succeed. It was unnatural just how naturally the language of rituals came to her. So much so that she had to wonder what might have happened in her last life if she’d been able to learn. Would she have been able to protect herself from the cults even without her mutations if she knew as much about rituals as she did now?

A moot hypothetical. There was so much that could have gone differently. But now… now she was in control. Nyxalraith had more control over not only her own life, but of so many others within Coral that it was almost overwhelming.

Nyxalraith had never been averse to killing, but even she needed to take a step back and realise just how many lives she’d rendered null in less than half a year. The Fleshsmiths alone put that number into the hundreds of thousands. If she continued at the pace she was, with strikes like the Everseeing Eye ceremony rather than hunting small groups, she knew it would only be a matter of time before she’d reached a million. Whether that be months from now, or years.

No matter how the cults deserved it, that was a lot of life to take. Roughly ten percent of Coral’s entire population, cut down by one person.

Why was she thinking of a potential future number when her current count was already extreme? At least a few full percentage points of the population. That was what? One in every thirty dead because of her direct actions? Really, Nyxalraith should feel more awed by the number, but while there were sure to be some unfortunate innocents caught up in those statistics, by and large, the cults deserved it.

How horrible would the curses be if they weren’t all swallowed by her core? Alysyra, in comparison, had caused the deaths of only thousands, and the curse that had formed as a result was almost debilitating to her. A sickening, occasional wave of necrotising infection. Even with the improved healing ritual Nyxalraith gave to her, her arms barely avoided taking on the ancient rot that permeated the Pharaoh. It would take her a while to build up a name for sacrifice.

Nyxalraith counted herself lucky that she didn’t need to concern herself with curse management. Anyone else in her position would be riddled.

The world groaned and Nyxalraith was snapped back to the moment. The walls had warped, shaped into a secondary pentagram that spun around her in three dimensions, guided by the blood of the base ritual. Now heavily corrupted, the space around her lacked any sense of cohesion. Walls, ceiling, floor… they were all there, and yet nothing but runes burning with the pink light of the ritual’s apex.

Suddenly, the screams that had made an unnoticeable backdrop to her vocals ceased. The cultist bodies exploded. Like a balloon bursting, they each lost their lives at once, but instead of their organs splattering all over what remained of the chamber, they spiralled into an orbit. Hearts, lungs, brains, and every other bit that kept a human body functioning twirled through the air.

There was a crack of lightning. A sliver of light ricocheted between organs a dozen times in an instant, then there was a laugh. Deep, giddy excitement rolled around Nyxalraith. Countless grinning teeth phased into existence, all giggling as if sharing the funniest inside joke.

Nyxalraith couldn’t help but chuckle herself. Not because she was being influenced, but because Euphoria clearly didn’t know what he was being baited into.

The world shook with a crack of laughter so loud Nyxalraith thought she might be hearing it for days to come. Then, just as quickly as it had all arrived, the laughing stopped.

Euphoria didn’t have eyes, but she knew he saw her.

Nyxalraith’s multi-vocal hymn sang its final notes.

Euphoria laughed. No, that wasn’t his name. She saw his soul.

“G̣͈̻̥̖͒̎͂h͍̖͖͕̯͎̹̟ͤȍ̤͙̗͕͔̓͛r̯̖̰̮̝̆̇͑ͦ̿ṷ̳̺̩̙̩̬͖͔͌̍͆͐̑̋k̥̲̠̟ͣͦ̀ͥͭ͒͛̾͋̐i̙̩̹͈̜̮̝͖ͭͬͮl̗̗̞̼͋͂,” Nyxalraith spoke the name, intuitively weaving it into the last of the ritual.

The god’s grin froze. “This… is not funny.”
