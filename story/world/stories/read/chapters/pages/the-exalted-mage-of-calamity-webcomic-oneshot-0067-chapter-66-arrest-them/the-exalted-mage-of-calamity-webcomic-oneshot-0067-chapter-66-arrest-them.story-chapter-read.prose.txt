“Dain! What the hell is going on?!”

Talenick, owner of the Scavenger’s escort group, stood in a fit of rage. His voice boomed through the large conference chamber beneath their headquarters, echoing off the stone walls. Several senior members were gathered in the room, standing in a line, all noticeably tense.

He paced back and forth.

One large communication crystal lay on the table, linked directly to Dain from High Crates.

“Explain what is happening!” Talenick shouted again. “The men I sent to protect Lorence haven’t responded, and I can’t even contact him myself! And now you’re telling me you can’t reach them either?!”

His face flushed with red anger, muscles tense and fists balled. He walked around the room as if he were ready to barrel through anything standing in his way.

“Calm down, Talenick,” came Dain’s voice. “You know how Lorence is. He’s… an elusive one.”

Talenick stopped, turning sharply toward the crystal. His brows drew together. “Know how he is? He’s given us nothing but headaches!” he snapped. “I can barely tolerate the man with his arrogant attitude. Each time I see him, he flaunts his goddamn wealth because he knows we can’t hurt him. All he’s got are those damn mana crystals. Without those, he’s nothing to us.”

He clenched his jaw. “And now things are all going to shit,” Talenick continued. “We’re walking a dangerous line, Dain. I don’t know exactly what you and him have dragged us into.”

“What are you talking about?”

“Are you serious?” Talenick barked. The men around the room flinched. “First it’s that woman poking her nose into everything, forcing us to halt operations. Then you tell me that Lorence is being chased too? You don’t find that strange?”

The line went quiet for a moment.

“What are you implying?”

“Don’t play dumb,” Talenick growled. “I lent you my men to protect him, and now they’ve vanished. That’s two Tier-4s. There aren’t many of those in this city—and now I’m down two of them. He dragged a hand across his face. “And it doesn’t stop there. I can’t get in contact with Lissa either.”

“Lissa?” Dain’s voice echoed back.

“The clerk at the notary office,” Talenick said, already pacing again. “Not that it matters. My men are gone, and now the office has gone quiet too. I’ve already sent two more to check on it, but it’ll take time since it’s across the city.”

His eyes narrowed. “And that’s one of the first places that woman started snooping around.”

He paused, turning back toward the crystal. “There are even more issues. The two men I had trailing her—like you asked—they haven’t come back either. They’ve been missing for a full day now. And then there was that explosion in the slums.”

His frown deepened. “Too much has happened in just a couple of days for this to be coincidence.” His voice dropped, colder now. “Someone is on our heels.”

“What do you propose we do, then?” Dain’s voice echoed.

Talenick exhaled sharply and walked over to the couch, taking a seat across from the communication crystal. The white sphere swirled with mist, its interior churning softly as the voice carried through, distant and almost ethereal.

“I’ve already sent Harton to check the notary office,” Talenick said. “Find out why Lissa isn’t responding. If something’s happened…”

He paused, jaw tightening. His teeth bared like an agitated animal. “Then we prepare new boots and lay low for a while. Shut everything down. That includes you, too, Dain.”

A long silence followed.

“Dain?”

A cough came through the crystal.

“…Apologies,” Dain’s voice returned. “I’ve come down with a bit of a cold.”

“A cold?” he repeated, narrowing his eyes slightly.

“As for the boots…” Dain said quickly, “I believe I may know the cause of all this.”

Talenick leaned forward. “Talk.”

Dain cleared his throat. “That woman who came to see me. The one who wanted to speak with me directly. The same one who’s been ‘snooping’ around. It’s most likely she who caused all of this. She’s probably related to Lorence’s issue, too.”

A murmur came from the scavengers gathered in the room. Talenick simply clicked his tongue. “I figured she was involved,” he said. “Some kind of investigator, maybe. Stirring things up in the open to make us relax and underestimate her.”

“Mm… yes. That seems likely. She’s working with some type of team; that much is obvious. A single woman like that wouldn’t be able to cause this much trouble, let alone take care of several Tier-4 aura users.”

Talenick hummed in agreement.

There was a brief pause before Dain continued. “As for the boots… do you remember Galland?”

“Galland?”

“The merchant serving under me at High Crates.”

He frowned slightly. “Merchant…”

Dain let out a quiet sigh. “The one we framed for the Lilybed shipment three years ago. The altered report. False page at the back?”

That was when his eyes lit up. “Ah, yes. That poor bastard. The merchant prodigy you were keeping,” he said. “But what about him?”

“I believe he’s involved,” Dain replied.

“What?” Talenick frowned. “How? I thought you were keeping an eye on him.”

“I am, but…” Another cough cut through the transmission. “…he’s only part of the problem. I think that woman is tied to his case from back then.”

“The Lilybed case?”

“Yes,” Dain said. “She came to your front desk and asked a clerk about the shipment, didn’t she? Claimed she might have had family involved.”

His expression darkened slightly. “That’s right… I heard about that. Thought it was just a sloppy question. But if she’s connected to that shipment…”

“You remember the details, don’t you?” Dain pressed. “Anything unusual? I’m a bit hazy on it myself. If there were loose ends, we might be able to identify her.”

Talenick’s frown deepened as he thought.

“There were only a few boots on that trip,” he said slowly. “After we killed them, we sold the bodies to Varce. Their IDs were repurposed—no one could trace them.”

The crystal gave a faint, almost imperceptible ripple.

He didn’t notice.

“What about the funds?” Dain asked. “Maybe she’s tied to where the money went. Someone could’ve leaked something.”

“Hah. Like any idiot would do that,” Talenick scoffed. “The shipment went through clean. Only a few Scavengers were involved.”

He gestured toward two men standing nearby.

“Lark and Tristan were on that job. They’re here with me now. I doubt they’d be stupid enough to betray us after all this time.”

The two men shook their heads.

“There were no problems with that trip,” Lark said.

“Yeah. It was just the two of us. We trekked all the way to Lilybed with no issues. Brought back the receipts and recorded everything,” Tristan added.

Another cough echoed through the crystal.

“What about the bandits?” Dain asked. “With that many involved, maybe one of them talked.”

“What?” Talenick said.

“You heard me. If there were that many bandits, surely one of them—”

“What the hell are you talking about?” Talenick cut in sharply. “It was just Lark and Tristan. We didn’t hire any bandits for—”

Talenick went quiet. For a long while, both sides said nothing.

“You… how did you know she came by and asked about the lilybed shipment? There were only three people in that conversation. I doubt the woman would have told you. And the other two don’t work for you, Dain.”

Again, only silence.

“Dain?” he called, his voice deepening into a growl. “Something’s going on, isn’t it? What are you hiding?”

“I told you it wouldn’t last long.”

Talenick froze. “What?”

That wasn’t Dain’s voice. It was younger; lighter. A girl’s voice.

“Oh, beat it. We got what we needed, right?” another voice chimed in, older, and much more amused.

Talenick’s expression twisted. “Who is this? Dain? What is going on?”

“Ahem. Sorry, Talenick,” the second voice said, clearing her throat. “But Dain won’t be speaking to you anymore.”

His teeth ground together. “You—who are you?”

A quiet chuckle filtered through the crystal, faintly distorted. “Hi. It’s Veronica. The woman you tried to assassinate.”

A murmur rippled through the room as the other Scavengers reacted.

Talenick’s gaze hardened. “What did you do with Dain?” he demanded. “You’re going to regret this.”

“Oh, am I?” Veronica replied lightly.

A brief rustle sounded on the other end.

“Hey, everything he just said and agreed to—that’s enough to use as evidence, right?” she added. “Illegal mana crystal trade, forged contracts, tax evasion, trafficking… and being an idiot.”

“How dare you—”

“Isn’t that right, Lord Tomas?”

Talenick’s anger faltered.

A new voice came through, clear and composed.

“Haha, yes. More than enough,” Lord Tomas said. “This should be sufficient to shut everything down.”

Talenick went still. His fists tightened, knuckles paling; the veins along his arms became visible. Around him, the other scavengers froze as well, all tense and stiff.

That was when they all heard noise coming from above them. A stampede of thumps from the stairs, coming closer to the entrance of the room. The sound grew louder by the second.

“Ah, and Mr. Talenick,” Veronica added, almost cheerfully, “half the city guard is already there.”

The pounding drew closer to the door.

“So I’d recommend surrendering,” she continued. “As you said, there aren’t many Tier-4s in the city.”

She paused for emphasis.

“…that is, if you weren’t counting the ones working for the city.”

The doors to the conference room burst open. Both wooden slabs tore from their hinges and crashed to the floor. Every Scavenger turned at once. Some drew swords, others wielded daggers.

The only one who didn’t move was Talenick.

A wave of armored guards flooded into the room, spreading out and sealing the perimeter. Each guard was dangerously armed. Spears, swords, shields; they were all equipped for an all-out fight if the situation came to it.

Then, one man stepped forward.

Dressed in black, his attire was immaculate. His posture was relaxed, almost refined—but there was something dangerous in the way he carried himself. He wasn’t one of the city guards. But he also didn’t need to be. He was someone most people who had lived a long time in the city recognized. One who worked directly for the Viscount himself.

Nolhan.

“Talenick Pines,” Nolhan said, his voice calm, yet carrying the full weight of authority. “Based on reasonable suspicion of forgery, attempted murder, trafficking, tax evasion, organized crime, and organized conspiracy, you and all Scavengers members present are now under arrest.”

The room went still.

“All of your assets will be seized and withheld for investigation,” Nolhan continued. “Any attempt at resisting will be met with violent force. That is your first and only warning.”

It was a standard, rehearsed line, if not a tad aggressive. It was something any criminal would expect from a guard captain. However, there was an edge to Nolhan’s words; it was a warning, as if he dared them to actually resist.

Several of the Scavengers exchanged looks before slowly lowering their weapons. One by one, they dropped to their knees. Guards stepped forward immediately, binding their hands with iron cuffs.

Nolhan moved through the room at an even pace, passing the table and couch until he stood beside Talenick.

“It is over, Mr. Talenick. Young Lord Tomas has won this game of cat and mouse.”

Talenick said nothing as a pair of guards lifted him up from the couch, binding his arms behind his back, and placing reinforced steel cuffs on him. His face was shrouded in a mask of darkness and anger. Every muscle in his body was coiled; he was ready to explode into a destructive rampage.

However, he didn’t.

Nearly thirty guards surrounded him, each one at least a second-tier aura user. Several stood straighter and much prouder. More confident. Third-tiers. Perhaps even fourth.

Nolhan, however—he was known by most. He wasn’t someone a person could fight easily. Not to mention, the Viscount’s son now knew of his crimes. There was no longer anywhere safe in the city for him to run toward.

“Take him away,” Nolhan ordered. “Search the rest of the building. Confiscate any weapons or dangerous materials. Leave anything tied to transport contracts—we don’t know who else is involved.”

He turned to the communication crystal on the table.

“Young lord, have the investigators arrive in half an hour. We’ll be finished with the preliminary sweep by then.”

“Thank you, Nolhan,” Tomas replied. “I’ll make the arrangements.”

The two guards flanking Talenick pushed him out of the room. He walked without resistance.

His face had been fueled with anger as the cuffs were placed on him, but as he walked, his demeanor changed. His muscles relaxed, the tension evaporating. As he stepped through the doorway, he turned his head back toward Nolhan and the crystal.

On his face were no longer bared teeth nor a contorted expression of rage.

No—it was a smile. A smirk. A grin.

“You’re going to regret this, Lord Tomas,” he said eerily. “Things would have been safer for the people if you had simply let us do what we pleased.”

He chuckled. “You don’t know the hell that they’re about to spread.”

The guards shoved him forward, escorting him further out of the room.

Nolhan simply stood there, his gaze focused on the man as he was escorted upstairs.

Behind the communication crystal, far away in City Hall, everyone, including Veronica, sat still.

It wouldn’t be until far later that they would truly understand what Talenick’s words meant.
