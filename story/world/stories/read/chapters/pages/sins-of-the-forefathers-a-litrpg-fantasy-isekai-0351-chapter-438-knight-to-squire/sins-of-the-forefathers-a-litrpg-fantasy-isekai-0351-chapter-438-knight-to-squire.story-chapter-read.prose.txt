Normally, most people would want just a bit of alone time for themselves after such an emotionally charged conversation. Some time they could fully process their emotions in the aftermath.

Not Renauld, though. The Gnoll wanted nothing more than to return to his duties as a Healer. I didn’t blame him, and frankly, could understand it. Bleddyn and the rest had told me quite a bit more than their immediate actions in the aftermath of fleeing Rhoscara.

That had included the composition of our current forces and who was still with us.

There were a lot of wounded among the combined forces of the Unchained, the Herztalian expedition, and the Rhoscaran refugees we managed to evacuate. Many of those people were injured in some way, evenly spread out mostly among the various camps of this procession. Renauld and the rest of the Healers available had no shortage of work.

I didn’t blame him for wanting to get back to it. So, rather than linger around and hover over the Gnoll’s shoulder, I let him be to return to his work.

I think he appreciated that.

But for me?

I linked back up with Sylvia and Walter, who had been waiting for me outside the command tent. After asking them to lead me through the encampment to a specific place within it, I listened quietly as two of the people closest to me expounded on our circumstances.

As we passed an incredibly long line, populated with incredibly weary-looking refugees, all of them looking to fill their bowls with incredibly thin soup…

I sighed at what I had been told.

“A third, huh,” I said quietly.

Walter grimaced while Sylvia nodded grimly. “Yes,” She said, just as quietly as I. “Overall, the expedition lost a full third of its members.”

“That’s not counting all of the ancillary members, by the way,” Walter continued, eyes straying over to the soup line. “That’s just from everyone we brought with us from Herztal. Most of those casualties came from when the Ashen Bride was attacked by Daffyd and the Savoy who accompanied him. I’m…a little ashamed to say that I’m relieved that the Polaris Reach didn’t suffer many losses. Most of us left before the attack when Sylvia rallied us to save you. The largest casualties…came from the Gnolls.”

“There’s essentially nothing left of those whom the Throng ordered to accompany Marcel,” Sylvia sighed. “A scant few warriors, at best. The crew of the Ashen Bride died with their ship.”

I shook my head. Just another regret to add to the pile. Another failure. “And so it goes.”

“And so it goes.” My Squire repeated softly, making the sign of the Gyre with his hands.

My eyes lingered first on the symbol, and then on the face of Walter.

He looked…older, in the aftermath.

Walter was no stranger to suffering. He’d lost everything prior to becoming a slave with me in Addersfield. He had never wanted to speak about what he had seen when those Elven raiders had assaulted the caravan, containing the sum total of his family. The young man had been one of the only survivors left from the attack, and then had gone on to suffer under the yoke of a Dwarven master. He’d nearly died from the twisted machinations of Magnus and had only survived due to my clumsy first attempts at surgery with Aetherial Melding.

And yet, for all of that…

I think the events of Rhoscara had shaken him, to a degree. He had his faith these days, but apparently not even that had been enough to shield him from the horrors we had seen in the streets of the Red City.

Mothers and their children cut down by mind-controlled friends and family. Laughing, sadistic Savoy soldiers spreading flames to everything in sight, mocking the screams of those roasting within their burning homes.

The knight he had chosen to follow, emotionlessly tearing through those attackers, creating lakes of sizzling blood upon the flagstones.

I stopped where I stood, in the middle of the camp. Walter and Sylvia continued past me for a moment before turning in confusion when they saw me. The younger man eyed me with confusion when I approached him, and then tensed when I set a hand on his shoulder.

I met his eyes. “Walter…are you alright?” When he only blinked at me, I did my best to smile at him. “I know we haven’t had time to talk lately, what with everything that’s happened. The preparations for the battle, the confrontation with Anguis, saving me, and then the fight through the streets…I just want you to know. I am here for you if you want to talk. I…know you were a little disturbed by how I fought, back in Rhoscara.”

Walter immediately interjected, his eyes opening wide in sudden panic. “Sir Nate that’s not-I’m n-not-” He clenched his fists tight to stop his stutter, suddenly seeming infuriated with himself. “I’m just not used to seeing you fully cut loose like that. Until now…it’s just been monsters, with the exception of Olag’s ambush. I’ve never seen you so…wrathful like I did in Rhoscara. I…it shook me a bit, but they deserved it.”

Something about the way Walter hissed those last few words…it struck me as ominous. There was real hatred in his voice, and all of it seemed directed at the people I had literally torn apart.

I exchanged a quick, mildly alarmed glance with Sylvia, and then focused back on Walter.

It was hard not to hate people like the Savoy in the heat of battle. But it was another thing entirely to carry around that hatred like a grudge just waiting to strike out at the target. It wasn’t healthy, and I am perfectly aware of just how hypocritical it was for someone like me to say that. I was the king of the glass castle, throwing boulders through curtain walls, trying to decry that with my…issues with slavery.

But I wanted better things than endless, unceasing hatred for Walter.

Sometimes I thought I was tainted. That I had a flaw in my personality that had hyper-focused me on my time collared by Magnus. That something deep inside of me had just been waiting for the moment a sufficiently horrific thing happened to me to come along, and when it had?

It had sunk its teeth deep into the event, refusing to let go as if it were a bloodthirsty pitbull.

That would never let me let go.

I didn’t want that for Walter. He had recovered so well from his time in Addersfield, unlike me. The young man had moved on; he had found a mildly healthy coping mechanism in his faith, his schooling, and his ambitions.

I couldn’t let him wallow in his traumas as I did.

I placed my other hand on his shoulder and did my best to project my seriousness to the young man. “Walter,” I said seriously, causing him to look up in surprise at my tone. “I want you to understand something. I am not a good person.”

Walter blinked rapidly at me, mouth suddenly agape, while out of the corner of my eye, I saw Sylvia narrow hers at me warningly.

I pushed on.

“I am, in the end, a poor role model,” I said, speaking over the half-hearted protestations of my Squire. “I am petty, I am vengeful. I hold grudges past the point where they should be carried. In battle, I am far more brutal than should be acceptable. Those Rhoscaran soldiers…the way I killed them was wrong. It is not the right way to live and to fight. I was trying to send a message of brutality to every single member of the Savoy who thought they could pillage and murder to their hearts' content. My intent was good, but there is a limit to what should be acceptable in a civilized world.”

I sighed, closing my eyes briefly and tightening my hold on Walter’s shoulders. “But we don’t live in a civilized world, do we?” I said quietly, only barely audible over the din of the camp. “We live in a brutal world, where the might of the individual trumps common morality. Those with a greater strength can do whatever they wish. They can alter the course of the world if they so choose. I am, in the end, no better than Anguis in my own way.”

“Nathan,” Sylvia interjected sharply, taking a step forward and drawing the gaze of both Walter and I. “You cannot possibly be seri-”

I shook my head to cut her off. “In some ways, it’s true. We are both supremely arrogant existences, in the end,” I said, oddly at peace with the realization. “We both believe we can choose the course of all life upon Vereden. That we have the right to dictate the fate of all. The only real difference between Anguis and me, despite what he may believe, is that he chooses to wallow in his own selfishness. To embrace it and seek whatever he desires to the detriment of all. While I…reject it.” I took a deep breath, aware of the almost anxious look from Walter and the downright furious one from Sylvia. “I try to redirect those feelings and use them to do at least some good. There are people whom I so selfishly care about on this planet, and it must continue if they are to thrive upon its surface. And so, all I have done…I do out of selfish desire. But you, Walter?” I smiled wider at the stunned young man. “I see a brighter future for you. There is so much capacity for good within you, Walter. You are selfless, where I am selfish. You are kind, when I am cruel. You are forthright, when I am deceptive. You shine, while I lurk. Please, understand that I mean this when I say it. I have no wish for you to emulate me. Instead, I wish for you to see me as I am, and decide…that you will be better than me. What I do is good, at times, but I do it for the wrong reasons and with the wrong methods. One day, you will do good things, for the right reasons, and the right way. Do you understand, Walter?”

In our own little corner of the refugee encampment, silence reigned between the three of us for long moments. Sylvia had an air almost akin to that of an offended cat, bristling but silent in her indignation.

Walter, though, was more stunned than I think I had ever seen the young man. His mouth hung open, and just as he had back on that Rhoscaran street…

He was staring at me like he had never seen me before.

Only this time, it was for a much better reason.

Eventually, the young man drew in a shuddering breath, almost coughing from how unstable it was…

And nodded.

“I-I understand,” He stuttered, for once not trying to attack himself for the impediment. “I’ll think about what you said…Nathan. I still want to be your Squire…but I need to think about things. Can I…be excused?”

“Of course,” I said, smiling softly at him. “Take your time. You know where we were going.”

Almost in a daze, Walter wandered away, disappearing into the crowd of the camp. I focused on the wild, shocked pounding of his heart for some time, until that too, disappeared from my senses.

And then turned back to face the visibly displeased Sylvia.

“I don’t care for it when you speak of yourself that way, Nathan,” The Sculpted woman said bluntly. “You sacrifice so much of yourself, and try so hard that I truly don’t care for it. What does it matter what you do, to a few worthless murderers?”

I chuckled softly to myself and reached out with one hand to cup Sylvia’s soft, Mithril-esque cheek. Gently, I brushed one hand across the surface of her transformed skin. “Oh, my cold north-star,” I said lovingly. “You are, in your own way, just as twisted as I. I’ve found that your people do not experience emotion in the same way we organics do, and so you do not place as much value upon the sanctity of life. It is so very utilitarian, in the end. You are good, and just, and righteous in your own ways…and yet, cold to the realities of the flesh. You cannot understand it, in the same way I cannot truly understand the trials of the Sculpted. Empathy can only go so far. You yourself are only further changed by your relation to Grey. He is an odd, callous man at times. Kind and generous to those he loves, but utterly, utterly without mercy to those in his way. I almost doubt he views them as people, really. The centuries seem to have a way of eroding our common morality.”

Sylvia sniffed, for a moment acting very much like an offended housecat. “Those who oppose us are in the wrong. Our purposes, our goals, they are superior to those that seek to subjugate others.”

I shook my head slightly, but didn’t argue further with her.

When you loved someone, truly loved someone, you accepted them to their utmost.

Including the skewed morality under which they operated.

“As you say. Now…enough of this navel gazing. We had somewhere to be, didn’t we?”

Sylvia eyed me suspiciously for a moment longer before nodding. We walked, hand in hand, through the encampment, utterly unnoticed and unbothered by the refugees within it.

Everyone had their own problems to deal with, anyway.
