“Alright,” I said with a sigh. “I realize that beggars can’t exactly be choosers, but…is this really the best thing we can pull together?”

Bleddyn just shrugged his massive shoulders at me. “At short notice, aye, this is it.”

It, in this case, being what the coalition of the Herztalian expedition and the Unchained had chosen as our new command vehicle. Or, in a strange twist of fate, a new vessel. I don’t know what it was, but for some reason, I couldn’t get away from using converted seafaring ships as ground transport.

Once was an incredibly novel experience. Twice…that was eyebrow-raising in a strangely suspicious manner. Was I cursed or something? If I was more poetically inclined, I would wonder if my fate was just tied to the sea now or something.

As it was, it was odd that I had found a second ship that was being converted into a rallying point for our journey north.

Initially, I had been incredibly confused when Bleddyn had told me that there was a ship down here, miles beneath the surface, that we could install the mobile Ward Stone of the Astray onto. Something that could act as a replacement for the now ashen Ashen Bride, sadly lost in Rhoscara.

For some odd reason, there had been an entire deconstructed ship down here in the storehouses beneath Addersfield. As in, the entire thing had been broken down into all of the necessary planks, boards, masts, and even sails and stored in a particular warehouse on the outskirts of the settlement.

From what Bleddyn told me, it had been a late discovery after he and his people had occupied this place and began sorting through all of their newly won supplies.

The lead belief was that it had been a personal vessel owned by Magnus, transported here by the arrogant, feckless toad, so he could revel in the knowledge that just beneath his feet, he had an entire ship that could be put back together. I don’t know if that was true…but I wouldn’t put it past him.

There had apparently been a debate about whether they should reconstruct it to use as an offshore vessel for the Unchained, but it had never progressed past planning before the Skyfall had hit. Now, though, we had a different use for the gaudy thing.

We were going to reconstruct it…just, we were going to change the design a bit. It was a good thing that most of the Gnolls formerly under the command of Marcel had survived the battle, because we were going to need their expertise. Inspired by the design of the now lost Ashen Bride, we’d smooth the hull out a bit, add some wheels, store the masts in the cargo, and voila!

One brand new land vessel for our use. It wouldn’t be as large as the Ashen Bride itself had been, but we didn’t need it to be, in the underground tunnels of the long road.

We just needed it to move.

My best guess, as I eyed the frantic construction efforts, was that it had, once upon a time, been something at the scale of a three-mast light galleon. I had ordered most of the typically Dwarven gold ornamentation not to be installed in the new vehicle, but even without it, this thing was still going to be a plush ride. It didn’t surprise me that Magnus had wanted his vessel to be the peak of comfort, judging by some of the furnishings I could see getting hauled around. But that didn’t matter to me.

The only thing that did was that we could install the Ward Stone on it…just in case.

I hadn’t forgotten that we would need a command center for the inevitable siege upon Smaragd, once we returned from the Holds. Once we got back, it was my intention for this kludged-together vessel to fulfill that purpose. The central lift down here would be more than large enough to transport it to the surface. We’d…just have to remove the roof of the building up there when we did.

I was knocked out of my planning for the future by a nudge to my ribs. Blinking, I cocked an eyebrow at the amused expression of Bleddyn. “Yes?”

He rolled his eyes at me. “Ye didn’t hear a word I said, did ye? Too busy off in your own world. Probably plotting conquest or some such rot.”

I couldn’t help an amused snort from escaping my nose. “Conquest, eh? Just what do you think of me, that you think I’m plotting conquest?” I paused for a moment. “You know what, don’t answer that. What did you need?”

Bleddyn just shook his head and dropped his teasing. “Did ye have a name in mind for this junk heap? The old name was total shite, and I ain’t gonna ride around in something like that.”

I almost didn’t want to ask, but morbid curiosity overcame me. “What was the original name?”

“Oh, ye’ll love this,” Bleddyn sneered. Not at me, I could tell, but at the memory of Magnus. “That fucker called it ‘La Magnifica Catena’. Knowin’ him, he probably thought that was classy.”

I mentally translated the name from Velancian Dwarfish and joined Bleddyn in sneering.

‘The Magnificent Chain’, eh. Yeah, I wouldn’t tolerate that.

Good thing I had an alternative already in mind.

“Yes, I have a name,” I nodded, wandering up and considering the light, almost golden colored wood of the under-construction vessel. I laid a light hand on it and considered the grain for a moment. “I thought about it some, and…this is really our last chance, isn’t it? You know what’s at stake here, Bleddyn. We can’t afford to fuck up and lose a battle again. There just aren’t enough men available to fight through another loss, nor are there the supplies. This is it.”

That sobered the werewolf up quick, and I could tell this line of thinking made Bleddyn a bit uncomfortable as he almost reflexively crossed his arms. My friend wasn’t the kind of person to dwell in failure, and didn’t like the consider it beyond what was necessary as a leader. He was, in the end, somewhat of an optimist.

I envied that in him, I really did.

“Yeah,” Bleddyn said gruffly. “It is. Everything rests on us winning, and winning soon.”

“In which case…I have a name for this ship,” I said slowly, knocking on the wood. The sound briefly drew the attention of some of the nearby workers, but they didn’t pay us much mind. They had work to do, after all.”

“We’ll call her the Final Line.”

…………………………………

And so it went.

Even in Vereden, I would have expected the construction of an entire ship the size of the newly named Final Line to have taken at least a week. Professions helped a lot when it came to the creation of complex projects such a vessel, but not to the degree you could cut down ship making that much. I had mentally prepared myself for the loss of time, consider it really was needed, but thankfully, I was wrong. Every single day was precious as we ran closer and closer to our deadline for the global collapse of wards, and the combined work crews of the Unchained and the expedition didn’t disappoint me.

They got it done in just three days.

I was told by the viscerally exhausted foreman that it was because the Dwarves who had disassembled Magnus’s vessel had been so meticulous in part keeping. Every plank of wood, every rope, even every nail had been painstakingly preserved by the people who had stored it here. Not only that, but a very large amount of the time it took to create a ship in the first place came down to the preparation of the composite materials. Wood needed to be cut, then shaped, and then treated most of the time to withstand the elements, and then there was the creation of everything that went into binding it together. Not to even mention the damn furnishings to make sure we weren’t sleeping and eating off the floor, over the next few weeks of travel.

Everything being so present and clearly labeled massively sped up the process.

And so, after three days of work, it was done. And…it was certainly a thing, I could say that.

While the Ashen Bride had very clearly been designed from the outset as a barge that could also become a land vessel, this thing was…less so. I could very clearly see the compromises made to accommodate the six massive wheels and their wells, as the adjusted hull design had taken shape. The masts weren’t up, considering how unnecessary they were underground of all places, but they were stored in the hull.

Honestly, I might order them taken out. Bella and I were unsure if this thing could even be made seaworthy in an emergency. They were just unneeded weight to slow us down on our journey.

For all of its compromises, though…it would do. After a walk around on the deck and through the halls, I was confident that this could make the journey. It wasn’t pretty, for all of the oddly plush furnishings in the rooms.

But this could get us north.

………………………………….

Normally, I would have participated in the construction. I wasn’t exactly a slouch when it came to crafting at this point, and I was confident that I could have contributed.

However, I was busy with something else at the time.

Securing a way to actually pull this slapdash beast of a converted ship. The Unchained didn’t have enough horses to haul something like the Final Line, not if we were going to also transport all of our combined troops alongside of it. Final estimates showed that we could fit maybe three hundred of our thousand soldiers within the hull, and even then, they’d be packed in like sardines. We were going to need dozens of other, smaller wagons traveling alongside it to accommodate the rest. Not unlike how the original expedition had braved the length of Velancia, in fact. The problem was, the Ashen Bride had had teams of horses specially bred by the Gnolls to drive that ship, when it was in its land conversion. Sadly, they had either been slaughtered to the last in the battle or perished in the flames. The Gnolls simply weren’t sure as to their fate when I asked.

While I liked to think all of those majestic animals survived the chaos and fled into the Skyfall besieged wilds, it didn’t matter. We needed a way to drive the beast all of our own…and there was only one more source of readily available beasts of burden down here.

I understood that Alveron was reconciling with the fact that, maybe he might have, if Anguis hadn’t been completely lying, been a bit overzealous with how he had approached the problem of Fynneas. I was even sympathetic, to a degree.

But the Elves had not once raised their willingness to accompany our forces to the north, and if they weren’t going to contribute…

It was time to force the matter and make some hard decisions.

…………………………………..

I made sure that I had all my strongest people with me when I approached the Elven encampment, just outside the ‘walls’ of the settlement. I even begged a favor from an aloof Rafael to come with me. The Lich had adopted a very hands-off, observational approach to matters since accompanying me back here. I didn’t see him half the time, and honestly, I only found him lurking in a corner of the settlement with Fade’s help. He was reluctant to involve himself, but he caved when I told him he’d get the chance to meet the last surviving Paragon of Vereden.

I ended up being more thankful for his presence than I’d expected. It was as our visibly armed group was approaching the increasingly agitated Elven guards that Rafael suddenly stopped in place, bringing the rest of the group to a halt. When I turned to look, I was startled to see the Lich had lowered his hood and was eyeing the Elven barricade with an extremely focused, narrowed gaze.

I ignored the gasps of the passerbys at the bared skull of the Lich, and tensed. “What’s the matter?”

Had Alveron sensed our approach and decided to stop being Mr. Nice Elf?

At first, I thought Rafael was ignoring me. “It’s been a long, long time since I’ve felt this…” He muttered under his breath. There was a visible frown on his desiccated face as he spoke, but I could still see the distaste upon it.

“Rafael.” I said sharply, breaking his concentration. As his gaze finally met mine, I frowned at him. “What’s the matter? Are we walking into a trap?”

“…not as such. I suspect I know now why your Paragon has been so reclusive, and you do not require my presence. Nor, in fact, do I wish to meet him any longer. I have no desire to witness this…degradation again. Once was enough.”

And with that, Rafael spun on his bony heel and stalked away. I didn’t even get a chance to blink before he had vanished back into the storage stacks of the settlement.

A shocked moment of silence fell over the group, as our most powerful member suddenly deserted us.

“Do we…turn back?” Walter asked tentatively.

Slowly, I shook my head. “No…it doesn’t matter if Rafael won’t help. We still need those Chamels if we hope to make the drive north. They’re the only unused creatures that can realistically pull the Final Line. C’mon.” I said, rolling my shoulders and turning back to face the Elven guards no more than a couple dozen feet away from us. No doubt they could hear every word we were saying, though it was a toss-up if they could even understand us.

“One way or another, we’re getting inside that camp.”
