Rex was still parsing through everything he had spoken about with Andy as he walked through the forest the following day.

They were a long way away from being able to do anything about their slaver problem. And that meant he needed to stay alive until they had an actual plan. Which meant that today’s hunt couldn’t fail.

He walked straight through the burned section of forest as fast as he could, reaching fresh vegetation and going straight to work, looking for signs of life.

A nice, juicy deer would surely get people off his back for a while. But when he spotted a large, feathery bird up a tree, he figured it would have to do.

Wanting to test his new Feat, he summoned his Mystic Arrow and took aim. An extra point of Strength was noticeable, and he pulled his bowstring back as if it were nothing and loosened.

The arrow whizzed through the air in a second, and stabbed a good half-way through the poor bird.

“Sorry,” he said, retrieving his catch.

Interestingly, he pulled the Mystic Arrow out, and it was still good to use.

“I wonder how long this thing lasts?”

Rex tied the bird by its legs—which was slightly larger than a chicken—and flung it over his shoulder.

“At least I’ve got something. But I really need another kill.”

The other hunters would no doubt claim the bird, and unless he was ready for a fight, he was going to have to give it to them. Rex considered trying to avoid them and going straight to Mulvaney, but he had a feeling that was a strategy likely to get himself in trouble sooner or later. And Mulvaney was unlikely to be happy if he missed out on a kill.

He kept walking, eyes sweeping across the trees as he searched for another bird. He’d have taken anything, but since he’d already got one, he figured he’d try to get another.

He also started to place his gerbil traps around the place. They were unlikely to work on anything larger than a gerbil, but maybe he’d get lucky. Besides, Mulvaney had already made it clear he would be happy with whatever he caught, as long as he didn’t come back empty-handed.

But only a moment later, he spotted footprints.

“Tiny, little prints…”

Rex’s eyes widened as he followed the footprints.

“The goblins. It has to be.”

He kept walking, following the tracks, and soon came to a particularly thick section of brush, behind which they seemed to disappear. But there was more. He could smell something. The sweet, smoky scent of barbecue.

That smells delicious. But the goblins could be in there somewhere. I had better be careful.

He took his time, softening his footfalls and searching for an easy way through. Climbing several branches, he eventually got a good vantage point, where he could see through the scrub.

The goblins, they have a new camp!

He spotted several goblins meandering between makeshift huts that looked like they had scrabbled together with old trunks and leaves. But that wasn’t all.

“Jackpot!”

In the center of their little camp was succulent boar roasting over embering coals.

His mouth was already watering at the sight of the cooking boar.

Stay focused, dammit! That’s exactly what I need to survive another day out here.

Showing up with a cooked boar would be suspicious. But Rex had a feeling that as long as they got paid, Malvaney and the others likely wouldn’t give him too hard a time.

“Twelve. More than I wanted to deal with, that’s for sure,” he muttered under his breath as he spied over the goblin camp.

On the plus side, if he could take out twelve goblins, there was a good chance he might hit level 3, and level 3 was already halfway to being a freedman. If he achieved that, they might actually have a chance of doing something. Possibly even freeing the slaves from Credo’s yoke.

Yeah, I just have to figure out how to kill these little guys.

However, even as he was thinking through his plan, he spotted a peculiar goblin at the far end of the camp. It was dressed in a dark robe dotted with bones and feathers, with a similarly decorated staff in its hand. And unlike the others, it was working over a bubbling cauldron, feeding various reagents to it that Rex didn’t recognize.

Yep, that’s definitely a shaman.

Broad daylight was ideal for goblin hunting, and the surrounding shrub probably helped him more than it did them. The tree he was in was bushy and covered in climbers, while he had a perfect view over their little camp.

But with a shaman involved? Yeah, that wasn’t a scenario he could just rush into. Not only that, but he had only fifteen arrows, plus the Mystic Arrow, leaving very little room for error.

He sat there for some time, going through the fight in his head. They were spread out, and he reckoned he could knock off a few before alerting the rest. But even if every arrow flew true, they would spot him sooner or later.

I would have to start with the shaman. The last thing I want is to be a sitting duck up here as it casts spells at me.

If he was going to take the shaman out first, it would be best to make his way around the camp. As it was some distance from where he was.

Don’t be reckless. I need a backup plan.

Rex decided to climb down and survey the area for anything that might help.

After a bit, he found a rather deep ditch. It wasn’t huge, but it could make for a decent trap if he needed to escape.

First, he scoured the area for good sticks and branches to make stakes out of. He focused on ones that were already fairly sharp, then climbed down into the ditch and buried them, poking upward. If anything fell in, they’d have a nasty time of it.

Then, he got to making the trap itself. He was still experimenting with his spells and was not entirely sure if his idea would work. But there was only one way to find out.

He took leaves, weaving them together by stabbing their stalks through one another. His handiwork was fragile, to say the least, and fell apart several times.

“This better bloody work,” he muttered to himself, increasingly frustrated every time the leaves fell apart.

When he was finally done, he had a blanket of leaves on the ground. He couldn’t lift or move it without it breaking apart, but he had an idea.

Taking a deep breath, Rex raised his hands over the leaves and cast  [ Harden ]. The spell worked quickly, stiffening the leaves, soldering their joints together.

Rex had figured that if a woven shirt could be hardened by his spell, so could leaves. The spell just had to acknowledge that the leaves were one object to work with, and it seemed he had done enough.

“Not bad,” Rex thought as he lifted it up.

He could have made something similar with sticks, vines, and leaves over the top. But not only did he want to test his spell, but it had a perfectly layered layer of leaves with no gaps, although it looked a little too uniform. Not only that, but it was strong enough for him to walk on, and he could deactivate the spell in an instant with his mana thread.

Once it was over the hole, he threw more leaves atop it to make it look more natural.

“That is… perfect,” Rex smiled and dusted off his hands.

With a trap like this, he didn’t have to worry about sticks breaking too early or too late. Or not breaking at all because the goblins were too light. Instead, he could deactivate [ Harden ] whenever he wanted.

Okay, time to go kill myself a shaman.

Rex started walking, but barely made it a few steps before he noticed a feathery creature in the tree nearby.

“Bush turkey! Looks like I’m extra lucky today.”

He didn’t actually know what the birds were called. But for now, decided to go with bush turkey, feeling it an apt name based on their appearance, and where he found them.

He nocked an arrow and drew back, but just before releasing, it fluttered down from the tree.

Hey! Get back here!

Knowing he was going to have to surrender a kill, Rex wanted to score as much game as possible. If for no other reason than to keep himself safe from Mulvaney and Jango.

But the bush turkey was fast. It dashed along the forest floor, weaving through the underbrush with surprising dexterity.

Luckily, Rex was Classed now, and he himself was quite quick. He took chase, but every time he almost got a clear shot at it, it turned. Cursing under his breath, he continued after it. He had struggled too hard to find game just to let one slip through his fingers.

Finally, after several minutes of chase, he got a clean shot and scored his second kill.

“Well, at least this should secure my life for a day,” Rex mumbled as he tied the bird’s legs and added it to his collection.

As he finished up, he noticed the waning afternoon sun. He had been so caught up in everything from scouting the goblins to trap making that the day had run away on him.

That’s not great.

A shaman was bad enough. But with any luck, he would be able to take it out before it cast its own spells. But attempting that in low light?

“Fuck,” he muttered under his breath. Looks like I’ll be waiting until tomorrow.

With everything that was going on around the farm, he wanted to get stronger as quickly as possible. Not to mention, the goblins would no doubt start devouring the boar. But he wasn’t going to take the risk of fighting them at night, no matter how much it annoyed him.

On his way back, quickly checked the gerbil traps. Most hadn’t been sprung, and the two that had had been broken.

“Oh, well. At least I tried.

Despite the fact that it looked like whatever was lured to his traps was too big for them, Rex still took a few minutes to rebuild the broken traps. Figuring that with a little luck, they might just make the next day easier.

It was a bit of a walk back to the farm, though. And by the time he passed the burned section of forest, the evening had grown fairly dark.

Moonlight provided a little light, and Rex was good at finding his way. But he was new to these forests, and it was his first time walking through at night.

He got confused a few times, walking back and forth before finding trees he recognized.

“This is it. I remember you.”

Rex kept walking, but slowed as he heard shouting.

What’s going on?

At first, he had been glad just to hear voices, as he was a little worried he was lost. But the shouting voices sounded too angry, giving him pause.

Steadying his breathing, he made careful, silent steps, finding a good tree surrounded by brush to hide beside as he tried to hone his hearing on the voices.

The shouting continued barely legibly, and he heard crunching steps through the leaf.

“Have you found that little runt yet?”

“Me? No? I’ve been out here all day. Do I look like I’ve found anything?”

“Well, look harder. Mulvaney has already got the shits. The last thing we need is Jango bringing his sorry self out here.”

“I don’t like it any more than you do? But what am I supposed to do? I can’t just click my fingers and make the little asshole appear, now can I?”

Little runt? Who are they looking for?

Rex got a sinking feeling. But he hadn’t done anything. In fact, he had actually done his job for once, and had two pretty juicy-looking bush turkeys to show for it. Why in the hells would they be looking for him?

“YOU TWO!” Shouted another voice, and the moment it got closer, Rex recognized Mulvaney. “What are you standing around for? Bring me the kid! If Credo finds out we let that bastard escape into the woods after burning half the place down, he’ll have our necks!”

Escape?!

Rex’s eyes widened. It was obviously dark, but was it really that late? Did they think he had gone and run away instead of paying off his debts?

What do I do?

He almost stepped out from behind cover then and there. The last thing he needed was a bunch of angry hunters chasing him down. But a pang of killing intent flooding out from Mulvaney’s aura froze him in place.

He got a very bad feeling about trying to confront them. He did not sense the auras of men who were willing to stop and listen. They wanted blood.

His breath stopped, and his heart pounded through his chest. Rex waited until they started walking again and kept back into the forest, using the sound of their footfalls to mask his own.

The moment he had some distance between them, he picked up his speed, running back the way he had come.

What about Andy? What about the slaves?

Rex gritted his teeth. He hated the idea of leaving Andy with those thugs, but there wasn’t a damn thing he could do about it in his current situation.

First, he needed to survive.

Hunters

They could only do so much at night, burdened by the fact that only one of them had the Nightsight Skill. But Mulvaney had insisted that none of the hunters return home until they had brought him, Rex. And so, with deadly beasts lurking in the forest, they made camp.

Even seasoned as they were, it was dangerous, and they kept a rotating watch all night. And in the morning, a thick mist and plenty of dew greeted them. Rising, they rubbed at their backs, aching against the rocks they couldn't clear.

“I’ll fucking skin that kid alive,” one of the hunters growled as he stretched out.

“Not if I do it first.”

“Stop whining and eat up,” another hunter said as he ladled out beans they were boiling over a fire. “As soon as we’re done, we head out and find the little cunt.”

“Aye,” a hunter nodded as he took his beans.

The men might have been gruff and willing to kill, but they also weren’t used to this. Most could count the number of times they had camped out on one hand. It was part of the appeal of their job. Hunting easy prey and living relatively cushy back at the farm. Not to mention the farmhands who were available to answer their every beckoned call. If they had wanted to sleep on the ground out in the wilderness, they’d become proper adventurers picking up valuable contracts in Tartarus and would have far more coin to their name, assuming they survived.

“Hurry up. I can feel the cold in my bones,” one of the hunters spat as he got to his feet.

“Give me a minute.”

The other slapped his comrade across the back of the head.

“Oi, I’m eating! Can’t you see?”

“I said, hurry up!”

The hunters shook their heads, but did as they were told, and collected their gear and got walking, muttering curses beneath their breath.

But they didn’t get far before the hunter leading the small group raised a hand, halting them. “Hold up!” He said. “Looky here.”

The hunter kneeled, pointing to prints and broken sticks.

“Tracks? You think they’re his?”

“Looks like it. If I had to guess, I'd say our runaway boy went this way.”

“Haha. Good one,” the other smiled. “Looks like we’re going home early.”

“Ha. Early? Early would have been last night. I fucking hate sleeping out here. I tell you what, prying out a few screams from that bastard before we finish the job should make me feel better though.”

“You’ll get all the screams you want,” the hunter who had spotted the tracks said, rising to his feet. “But until then, keep your focus on the job.”
