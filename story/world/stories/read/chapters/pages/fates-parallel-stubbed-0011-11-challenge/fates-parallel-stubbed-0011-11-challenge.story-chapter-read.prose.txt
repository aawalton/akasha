Lee Jia stared blankly and blinked up at Yan Zhihao. What? A duel? How did the conversation end up there? She took a hesitant step back from the boy.

“I uh—I have prior engagements today…”

For some reason Yan Zhihao doubled over in laughter at her response.

“HAH! Haha, I do appreciate a sense of humor. This isn’t a simple exhibition like my playfight with that Takeda girl. This is a proper challenge.”

“Okay?”

Lee Jia was still hopelessly confused. Yan chuckled at her expression and continued.

“Allow me to explain. As the challenger I will demand something from you—in this case the secret behind your second awakening. Should you respond to my challenge, you will have a week to prepare after which we will duel. If I win, you comply with my demand. If you win, then you have the right to make a demand of your own from me.”

Lee Jia went still at that. Her ears perked up and faced forwards, Yan Zhihao had her full attention. She narrowed her eyes.

“What kind of demand?”

“Hah! Straight to the point, eh? Anything! As the challenger, I am at an advantage—it is only right that I should be fully prepared to back up my challenge. By precedent, it is usually frowned upon to demand something too greatly in excess of your own wager, but there’s no hard limit.”

Lee Jia furrowed her brow and considered. It was a compelling offer. She didn’t think she stood a chance against Yan Zhihao as she was, but a lot could change in a week. Probably not enough, but a blank check against a secret that didn’t exist was a very tempting wager.

“What if I refuse? If my options are to fight you, or get bullied into giving it up anyway it’s not much of a choice right?”

Lee Jia eyed the group of disciples that had stopped to eavesdrop on the spectacle Yan had created. She had no doubt that many of the Qin disciples would take Yan’s side. Yan Zhihao crossed his arms and frowned.

“Ah, as expected of savages and beasts—I shouldn’t be so disappointed. Did I not say this was civilized? If you refuse then that’s it—you refuse. Then nothing. I don’t think my offer is unreasonable, so you might lose face, but you stand to lose nothing but reputation.”

Lee Jia grimaced. Yan made it sound reasonable enough, but she wasn’t totally ignorant of how these things worked. Lee Jia didn’t have a reputation to lose, so instead her refusal would mean that the first thing anyone knew about her was that she was a coward. It would sour every first impression she tried to make going forward, and it would also reflect poorly on those who associated with her.

“So we duel in a week, if you win I tell you how I awakened my ki and if I win I can demand whatever I want, is that right?”

Yan Zhihao clapped and grinned happily at Lee Jia. It was obvious that he didn’t think it mattered what he offered as a wager.

“Exactly! So how about it? A bit of friendly competition keeps things interesting, no?”

Lee Jia took a deep breath to steady herself. She wasn’t ready for this—she wasn’t ready for anything in the same realm of existence as this, but she didn’t really have a choice.

“Alright, I accept those conditions. I think you’ll be disappointed, though. You should just follow Master Ienaga’s instructions.”

“I suppose we will find out, Miss Beastkin. Our duel will take place at the training field a week from today. I look forward to seeing what a so-called interdisciplinary practitioner is capable of.”

Lee Jia’s eye twitched and her tail was lashing angrily as she answered through clenched teeth.

“My name is Lee Jia. Please try to remember it until our bout.”

Yan smirked and nodded fractionally.

“Lovely meeting you, Miss Lee. I’ll be taking my leave, then. See you in a week. Good luck!”

He turned and strode away and Lee Jia didn’t miss the fact that over half the people who had crowded around them left to follow him. Classic bullying tactics, using the crowd to pressure her. She sighed heavily as she ran towards the lecture hall. What had she gotten herself into?

The lecture hall was open, but mostly empty. A few small groups of mages had gathered together to do some studying. Lee Jia was relieved to see Dae sitting alone in the corner, focused on writing something in one of his many books. Dae looked up and greeted Lee Jia with a smile as she approached.

“Ah! Miss Lee. Glad you could make it, hehe.”

Lee Jia put her hands together and gave Dae an apologetic look, bowing lightly.

“Sorry I’m late! I slept in and got interrupted by Yan Zhihao on the way here.”

“Hehe, don’t worry about it Miss Lee. I’d have been here either way. I like your hair, by the way! I suppose the rumors about your second awakening must be true—and I don’t blame you for sleeping in. Master Ienaga’s training is brutal.”

Dae rubbed an arm and winced, recalling the harsh training. Lee Jia sighed with exasperation as she took a seat next to Dae.

“Thanks, my roommate did it for me. Has everyone heard about that already? Yan was bugging me about it too.”

“Hehe, it’s a small campus and people tend to gossip. I’m not sure how the rumor started, but it spread fast. What did he want?”

Lee Jia clutched her head in her hands and groaned as she recalled her encounter.

“Ugh, he wanted me to confirm the rumor. When I tried to get out of it he used some kind of attack on me that seemed to convince him that it was true. Now he thinks there’s a secret trick to awakening that I am not telling him.”

Dae scratched his chin thoughtfully and began murmuring to himself.

“An attack to confirm—hmm...perhaps a forceful mana intrusion? That should be possible for a qi user, I think. Wait, you said he confirmed it? As in you actually have awakened?”

Lee Jia nodded.

“Yeah...I hadn’t really planned on announcing it like that, but Yan was completely certain. It sounded like he already thought that there was some kind of trick to it that Ienaga was holding back.”

Dae stared at Lee Jia in wonder.

“That’s incredible! Two awakenings so quickly...Master’s theory was—uh, never mind that, hehe. Anyway, was there? A trick to it, I mean.”

Lee Jia frowned at Dae. He seemed to know more than he let on sometimes, but she decided to let it go for now.

“Kind of? Not like Yan Zhihao thinks. It only works for mages and it’s still not exactly easy, and nobody told me how to do it—I discovered it on my own. He’s going to look pretty stupid when I tell him about it after our duel next week.”

“I see! Wait, hold on! What about a duel!?”

Lee Jia leaned forward and draped herself over the seat in front of her, resting her head on her arms as she complained to Dae about her situation.

“Bleh, Yan didn’t believe me when I told him there was no secret so he demanded that I duel him for it.”

“And you accepted!?”

Lee Jia shrugged her shoulders and blew out her cheeks.

“Not like I had much choice. Besides, I don’t really have anything to lose. I take a little beating next week to avoid bigger trouble down the line. Not like I haven’t done it before—heh, kind of like how I arrived here, actually.”

“Wha—but couldn’t you just tell him? If you don’t think you can win the fight, why not just save yourself the trouble?”

Dae’s shocked expression made Lee Jia giggle a little bit. He was surprisingly innocent for a mage.

“Because he wouldn’t believe me. It would hurt his pride to accept something I gave him freely, especially since he considers me to be so far beneath him. If he beats the secret out of me, that’s another story. That’s something he took with his own strength—he’ll be more likely to accept it, then.”

“That’s—a surprisingly shrewd analysis, Miss Lee. You speak almost as if you know Mister Yan personally.”

Lee Jia smiled and shook her head.

“Not him, specifically, but plenty like him. That’s the world I grew up in. I always thought it was different for nobles and mages but I guess not. In a way it’s kind of comforting to know that people are the same no matter where they come from.”

Dae was silent for a bit after that, staring sullenly down at his book.

“So what are you going to do?”

Lee Jia sat up and stretched. What an odd question.

“Right now? I’m going to learn how to read and write so that I can study talismans and learn to cast spells. After that I’m going to learn how to actually use the ki that’s caused me so much trouble. A week from now, I’m going to fight Yan Zhihao, probably get kicked into the dirt and then? Then I’ll just keep going. Keep learning, keep training, keep living. That’s all.”

Dae stared at Lee Jia, jaw agape. After a moment he collected himself and chuckled nervously.

“Hehe, that’s amazing. I envy the strength of your resolve, Miss Lee. I doubt I could be as calm as you are in the same situation. I—I don’t think you should give up! It feels wrong to just let a bully like Yan get what he wants. You’re the first interdisciplinary practitioner I’ve ever met! Who knows what you can accomplish!? I’ll support you, so—”

“Dae, stop!”

Lee Jia was trying and failing to suppress a giggle as Dae got more and more worked up, his eyes glimmering and his dog-like tail wagging excitedly.

“I appreciate it, Dae, I really do. Of course I’m going to do my best! I’m just keeping my expectations realistic. Right now, the best way you can support me is by teaching me how to read and write.”

Dae blushed and looked away, scratching the back of his head as he recalled the purpose of their meeting.

“R-right, hehe. Of course. Let’s get started, then.”

Lee Jia spent most of the afternoon and well into the evening with Dae, learning basic characters, their meanings, and how to transcribe them. She was surprised to find that she seemed to absorb the information near perfectly. Once Dae had explained a character’s meaning and the proper brushstrokes to replicate it, she was able to recall that information perfectly even after hours of building her written vocabulary.

“Ah, that’s because of your awakening. Expanding one’s mind beyond the realm of the merely physical allows for more than just the ability to sense and manipulate mana.”

Dae explained while Lee Jia worked on practicing a particularly troublesome set of brushstrokes, her face scrunched up in concentration.

“Eidetic memory is one of the first perks—ah, that stroke’s angle is four degrees off, try again—of awakening as a mage. If you focused, you could probably perfectly recall every moment since you awakened. No, try again. Remember you’re aiming for a three degree margin of error for starters.”

Lee Jia grumbled as she tossed aside the failed character and started over. Dae was almost a different person in teaching mode.

“So I just never forget things now? Then how come I keep messing up these characters?”

“That’s the difference between knowing how to do something and actually doing it, Miss Lee. Though it’s not quite accurate to say that you never forget anything. There is a limit to the complexity of the concepts you can retain, but the subjective experiences of everyday life and simple language-based glyphs are unlikely to stress that limit, even at the first stage.”

There he went throwing around complicated words and ideas like she was just supposed to know what he meant. Lee Jia had gotten used to it by now, though.

“What do you mean by subjective experience and language-based glyphs?”

“Subjective experience means that you recall events as you experienced them. This is as opposed to objective experience, wherein you would recall events as you perceived them. The difference is subtle, but I can use an example to explain. Imagine a white cat standing on a snowbank.”

Lee Jia missed a brush stroke. Why was that the example that he used? It was so similar to her dream from the other night. Well, she had white hair and catlike features, and it was getting colder, so perhaps it was just a coincidence. Dae didn’t seem to notice her reaction as he continued.

“If you glanced at the snowbank, you might miss the cat. Recalling the subjective experience, you would never be able to remember seeing the cat. However, if you recalled the objective experience and analyzed the memory carefully, you would recall seeing the cat even though you originally hadn’t noticed it.”

“Wow. Can you do that?”

Dae shook his head.

“No, perfect recall of objective experiences is exponentially more complex than subjective memory. We naturally experience the world subjectively, so perfect recall requires us to change the way we perceive the world in the first place. That would require a level of awareness and clarity of mind beyond that of a second stage mage like myself. Probably beyond even those in the third stage, but I can’t be certain.”

Lee Jia nodded along. Finally finishing the character she was working on to her—and more importantly Dae’s—satisfaction and moving on to the next.

“Huh. So you’re only in the second stage?”

“Wh—only!? Ah, but I forget that this place is your first exposure to immortal practitioners, hehe. You have to understand that the academy extended invitations only to the most talented young practitioners in each nation. The overwhelming majority of the students here are in the early to middle phases of the first stage in their respective disciplines. Not to brag, but reaching the second stage at our age is an incredibly rare achievement.”

Lee Jia hadn’t realized it was such a big deal. She chuckled nervously, idly hoping she wasn’t about to pick up Dae’s habit.

“Ahaha, sorry. I didn’t mean to hurt your pride. What about the glyphs?”

“Hm? Oh yes! The basic talismans and formations you are learning use regular written language, but you’ve probably noticed that the jade slips and the formations in your dorm use different ones.”

“Eh, well I couldn’t read anyway so I didn’t really know the difference.”

“Oh—right. Well they do! In theory you could write instructions onto a complex talisman or formation in plain language, but as the instructions grow more complex, the size of the medium and the cost of the mana infusion would rapidly grow to impractical levels. To combat this, mages come up with increasingly complex glyphs and runes which embody more complicated ideas than can be easily expressed in plain language. It would strain my mind to comprehend even a single character of the formation surrounding the academy, much less recall and replicate it.”

“I see.”

Magic was a lot more complicated than Lee Jia had realized. She supposed she’d just have to learn these things one step at a time. No doubt her improved mental faculties would help in that regard. For now, her mind was reeling from all the new information so she decided to change the subject.

“What stage do you suppose Yan Zhihao is in?”

Dae seemed to ponder the question seriously for a moment before answering.

“It’s difficult to judge based on limited information and different disciplines. My guess would be early second stage or at least peak first stage.”

Lee Jia grimaced. How was she supposed to fight someone that advanced?

“I kinda get early, middle, and late phase. But what do you mean by peak? Is there a difference between that and late phase?”

“Hmm, how to describe it? Consider the mana gathering stage of a mage. The differences between mages in that stage can be measured quantitatively by the amount of mana in their aura. The more mana they have, the later their phase within the stage.

“The border between two stages is more complex. It requires the mana in a mages aura to go through a qualitative change. Such qualitative changes are what define the stages of any form of cultivation, and are commonly referred to as bottlenecks. They’re called that because breaking through to the next stage is often much more difficult than simply building your way up. The peak of a stage is the point at which no further growth within the stage is possible, but the practitioner has not yet broken through to the next stage.”

That made sense to Lee Jia. In a way, she imagined that breaking through was probably a lot like awakening all over again.

“Why do you think Yan has to be at least at the peak of the first stage? Isn’t there a huge difference between stages?”

Dae tilted his head back and forth as he considered her words.

“Yes and no. The difference between stages grows exponentially. Early on, a higher stage of cultivation is challenging but not insurmountable. The main reason I placed Yan Zhihao where I did is his qi. From his duel against Miss Takeda, we can surmise that the quantity of qi available to him is well beyond what I would expect from most first stage cultivators.”

“Really? How can you tell? For all you know he had way more than what we saw. I have no idea how to even guess how much he was using.”

Dae scratched his chin and shrugged.

“True, but that’s why I estimated the peak of the first stage as a minimum—it’s far more likely he’s in the second stage. That said, I doubt he had much more qi available to him than what you saw in his duel against Miss Takeda.”

“Oh?”

“Generating a raw element like that is quite taxing. The amount of mana required is quite high, and all of his techniques seemed to rely on generating flames. I am guessing the cost is somewhat circumvented by a high affinity to fire, but the fire breathing attack he used to finish the fight is not something that can be performed at will. It consumed his defensive aura, right? Probably a trick to conserve qi—I’ll bet he was almost entirely exhausted at that point.”

Lee Jia thought carefully about what Dae was saying. If generating fire was costly and Yan’s entire strategy relied on doing just that...would it be possible to beat him through attrition? Lee Jia looked up from her calligraphy as the beginning of a strategy began to formulate in her head.

“Hey Dae, is it possible for a mage to use talismans created by another mage?”

“Hm? Yes, of course. Some talismans and formations are even made for mortal consumption, remember?”

Lee Jia nodded. Of course—that only made sense. She grinned as the first steps of her plan started to come together.

“Dae, can I interest you in a trade?”
