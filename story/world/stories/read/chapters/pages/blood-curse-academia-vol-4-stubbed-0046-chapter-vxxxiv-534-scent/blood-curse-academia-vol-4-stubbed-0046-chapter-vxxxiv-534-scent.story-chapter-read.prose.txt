Chapter V.XXXIV (5.34) - Scent

“Can you name the most common mistake novice illusionists make when attempting to hide from a predator?” Professor Ignis stood at the head of their lecture hall, hands clasped behind him and brilliant red hair combed to the side. In front of him was a table of unmarked tomes.

“They get seen through because of someone’s spellsense?” Yon guessed. As per usual, the older student sat beside Kizu in this class.

“No.” Ignis gave a short shake of his head. “That would lean over into a fault in their divination abilities rather than their illusions. It’s an obvious defect that all illusionists know of and will have means in which to work around. Anyone else?"

“They don’t properly take into account their surroundings and they get caught,” Emilia answered. Unlike Yon, she sounded confident rather than guessing at what Ignis was looking for. The Tainted girl was sitting up at the front, her back straight as she dutifully participated in the lesson.

“Closer. But still not the heart of the issue.”

Kizu tapped the tip of his quill against his notes as he considered how Emilia’s answer was closer to the truth. It left a blotch there that grew steadily as it covered the parchment. He had to stop himself as it overtook a few of his written words. After a few seconds of silence, Kizu decided he might as well give it a go and raised a hand.

“They try to make themselves unknown.”

Ignis smiled. “Precisely. I assume all of you have dreamed of instant invisibility?”

In response to his question, there were murmurs of agreement from the students.

“As A students in my class, undoubtedly you’ve all attempted it,” Ignis continued. “And yet, you’ve all failed. It’s because you’re attempting to delete yourself from the world, rather than blend in with it. Now let me ask you another question, is it harder to blend in with the shadows of a dungeon or a brightly lit library bustling with busy students and filled with shelves of colorful bookspines?” He didn’t even wait for their response before continuing. “Every detail in your surroundings creates more complications for you as an illusionist. This truth should be known to each of you at this point. So let me ask you another question, how does this apply to illusions beyond the visual medium?”

“Trying to mask your scent,” Kizu realized instantly. “If you’re in a heavily perfumed room, you don’t want to remove the smell from yourself but blend in with it. Otherwise, an opponent with enhanced senses will still notice.”

“True,” Ignis said. “But learning how to replicate a scent is a mighty task. Any other solutions?”

“You send out a scent of your own over an area to compete,” Yon said. “If a summoned dog is trying to track you, then if you cover an entire room with the scent of a spice, then you’ll be able to hide far easier than if you try to just make yourself smell like the original subtle differences existing within the room.”

“Well answered,” Ignis praised. “From that example I assume you were one of Professor Oasaji’s victims in the last challenge?”

Yon bowed his head, his face flushed as he muttered confirmation.

“I didn’t say that to embarrass you about the loss. Nearly every peer of yours in this class lost alongside you. Rather, your answer is a sign of a keen mind. You’ve immediately connected our lesson today with a recent failing and realized how you could have done better. That is the goal of studying. This is the entire point of our academy. You fail here so you can succeed in life. You’ve done well.”

Yon raised his head at the praise but remained unhappy. “Failing still hurt my Combat ranking,” he muttered quietly. “Which, in turn, hurts me in life as a whole.”

Only the closest students to him heard him. And, of those students, only Kizu glanced at Yon.

“For the rest of class today I want you to study scents,” Professor Ignis continued, addressing the class. “By the end of the semester, I want each of you to have at least three you can create and spread at the drop of a pen.” Ignis examined each of them carefully. He set a hand on the leather tomes on the table in front of him. “In the classrooms next door I have set up a few example scents. Feel free to browse both them and the books I have here at your leisure. I will also be present to answer any further questions. Everyone understand your instructions?”

The students all murmured their agreement. Chatter broke out as they all took Ignis’s backwards step as confirmation they were free to talk amongst themselves.

Kizu leaned back in his seat as he considered what Ignis had said. If they were meant to master at least three scents by the end of the semester, then he should choose his options with care.

The rest of the students stood and did as Ignis had suggested. Many stepped out of class while others opened tomes. Kizu heard a declaration of surprise that pages of the tomes were enchanted with scents.

“You have something in mind?” Yon asked. The lean older boy tugged on his scraggily goatee as he looked down at Kizu.

Kizu didn’t miss the bags under his friend’s eyes. As a fifth-year student, Yon was likely running up to the edge and pushing his rankings as hard as possible right now. Only a bit longer until the school year ended and sent him out into an unknown world. A world where Hon and most of Tross were cut off  from one another. Not to mention the chaotic mess of fire and destruction that was the current continent of Edgeland.

“I want them to be distinct,” Kizu said. “Not just like three different spices. I need them to be broadly applicable.”

“I suppose that makes sense,” Yon agreed. “Any specific ideas?”

Kizu shook his head. He needed to narrow it down more. “What about you?”

Yon smiled. “I’ve always enjoyed my mother’s pumpkin bread. I thought this might be a nice excuse to indulge in some baking. I know some of the cooks from a project last year and bet they’ll give me access to the ovens and ingredients if I ask with a good excuse.”

“Do you have time for cooking right now? It’s the end of the year.”

Kizu regretted the words the moment they came out. He physically saw Yon’s shoulder’s slump as an invisible weight fell on them.

“I’ll… figure something out,” Yon mumbled.

“Food isn’t a bad idea though,” Kizu said quickly. “I mean, it’s helpful even beyond deception. I bet it can relax people and put them at ease.”

Kizu thought back to some of the smells from his childhood in the Hon Basin. He was reminded of the bat strew the crone had been fond of cooking. Sometimes she’d cook up a big pot with other mystery meat and they’d eat only that for a week straight. He shuddered as he recalled plugging his nose to get the thick chunks down his throat without gagging. Often, insects would get in it while it sat out and they’d be thrashing about within the while it slipped down his throat. One time, a centipede had fallen in the stew and stung the inside of his throat as he gulped down a bowl of the gruel. It had been agony. He still recalled the crone cackling as his throat swelled closed.

Suddenly, he was irritated as he thought about how he could remember the experience. Why couldn’t the crone have at least blocked that from his memory when she was sealing things away?

“And people wonder why I like sweets,” he grumbled under his breath.

“What was that? Did you say something?” Yon asked. The older student was currently tugging at his scraggily goatee with one hand while his other was outstretched before him, a swirl of illusionary colors over it.

“Nothing. What are you doing?”

Before Yon could answer, the spiraling rainbow solidified into a small orange loaf for a moment, then it returned to the jumble of color.

Yon’s shoulders slumped and he sighed. “Wanted to see if I could recreate the smell from memory. No luck.”

Kizu decided to give it a shot. His mind went to the smell of a campfire. It was not only a strong scent, but persistent and would cling to him for days. That should fit what he needed.

Biting his lip in concentration, Kizu focused all his effort on recreating the smokey aroma. He didn’t stretch out his hand like Yon had. Instead, he closed his eyes in hopes of blocking out all the surrounding distractions.

“What the—puhh!”

Yon’s exclamation turned into a gag and Kizu heard him stumble away.

Unfortunately, Kizu was not immune to his own illusion. The heavy scent of smoke nearly knocked him over. He reached out and steadied himself on the back of the chair next to him. His eyes snapped open to help him find his bearings.

The smell, he’d been expecting. But what he saw with his watering eyes took him by enough surprise that he forgot to keep channeling his illusion.

A thick cloud of smoke swirled around him, tendrils whipping about like he was caught in the center of a gray tornado. It was nothing like what a common campfire spat out. The illusionary whirlwind was vicious in how it cut across his vision.

It faded and, with it, the pungent scent let him breathe easy once again.

Every student remaining in the room was shooting him a look of concern. Several of them were also now plugging their noses and stumbling away, looking woozy.

It was only then that Kizu recalled their first day practicing sound illusions in Illusions A. On that day, nobody else had removed their blindfolds when they were all attempting auditory illusions. They likely had no idea about the visual illusion side effects that were common when attempting a new medium. While his whirlwind of smoke was nothing compared to some of what the rest of his class had created on that day, they’d been blind to what they’d been doing.

Kizu huffed as he dismissed his smoking whirlwind. At least his attempt hadn’t filled the room with a parade made up of half a marching band and silent clowns.

Kizu just gave his classmates an embarrassed wave and they slowly returned to their studies. He heard a few of them muttering complaints about how he’d already created a smell. That made Kizu relax a bit and even smile slightly in satisfaction. A far cry from mastering the medium. But he already had a foothold in it.

He let out a sigh of relief, taking a brief moment to appreciate the lack of smoke in the clean air.
