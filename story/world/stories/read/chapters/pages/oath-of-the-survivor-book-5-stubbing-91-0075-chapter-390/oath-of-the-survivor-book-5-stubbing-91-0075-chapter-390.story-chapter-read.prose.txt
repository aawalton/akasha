Chester Drake had gone on many expeditions, seen many wondrous things, and fought through more dangerous situations than most people would find in a lifetime.  And still, he was shocked when his Seismic Sense took in the world around them.  The very crust of the moon felt alive.  Thick mycelic tendrils wove through the crust, carrying with them a tide of fast-moving fungal constructs, all barreling directly in their direction.

Ahead of him, Mayhew strolled forward, seemingly entirely unaware of the danger.

“They’re coming!  We’re under attack!”  As he shouted, Chester’s mana tried to wrest control of the ground around them, but the mycelium held it fast, resisting his efforts.  Then the tendrils erupted from the ground, binding their ship and attempting to tear it to pieces.  They’re going to tear us to pieces before we can even get into formation, Chester thought, swearing as only a few small stones were wrested free from the clinging fungus.

Then, everything seemed to stand still.  Golden light bathed the area in front of him, as a whisper of power rushed past.  An oppressive blanket of energy covered everything, and for a moment, Chester thought it felt… hungry.  And then the moment passed.  His control of Seismic Sense destabilized as the mana he put into the skill was drawn in, and many of the small stones he’d managed to raise began to waver in the air.

The hair on the back of his neck stood up as he felt something brush over him, and then the surrounding fungal constructs, the grasping tendrils, and even the mycelium beneath the ground withered to dust for hundreds of meters all around.  There was no flashy attack, no explosion of fire or crackling ice.  He stood with wide eyes as Kyle Mayhew continued to walk, stepping over the collapsed husks with no more effort than going for a morning stroll.

More of the constructs and tendrils charged their position, and each met with the same fate.  Once they crossed too close, they were obliterated.  In all his life, Chester had never seen anything like the simple carnage laid bare before him.

Chester’s mind went unbidden to the island operation where they’d picked Kyle up in the aftermath of his fight against the strange arthropod.  A large part of him had hoped Mayhew wouldn’t survive the encounter, as even back then, the young man was dangerous.  Volatile.  Now, seeing him effortlessly dispatch an attacking force numbering in the thousands, Chester knew that they’d saved a genuine monster.  When Mayhew turned back toward him, he flinched involuntarily.  Kyle’s voice was entirely unbothered as he asked, “Are you coming?”

Kyle quirked an eyebrow as he looked back to the ship, and the stunned Chester standing just outside the ramp.  “Are you coming?”

From his vantage, he could see a very annoyed-looking Skippy standing in his position in the outer edges of the middle of the formation, while the other D Grades looked around, trying to process what had happened.  Garth and Tillienne were part of the rear guard, and had likely missed seeing the full scope of the initial attack.  Or more accurately, what remained of it.

With the benefit of Genesis Engine, Aspect of the Tree: Root, and the improvements to his attributes, Devourer’s Domain and Aura Force were now able to cover a radius of just over three hundred meters.  He’d kept it reined in to only a hundred and fifty or so, but the results still spoke for themselves.  The fungal constructs had no real Willpower to speak of, and all he’d done was incorporate a small amount of Conceptual Death, coupled with a widespread application of Wither, and they all crumbled.

The other thing he’d noticed as he expanded his domain skills was a small haze of fungal spores reaching into the atmosphere.  Kyle had no doubt that they served as an early warning system, which allowed DeRosa to prepare his ambush the moment the expedition was detected.  He’d already destroyed the spores, but it was clear that any element of surprise was entirely lost.  DeRosa knew that the ship had landed, knew their location, and could bring all of his resources to bear.

That partially explained why a continuous wave of the constructs was pouring in toward them.  Without any knowledge of who was attacking, DeRosa was clearly planning to either seize a decisive victory with overwhelming force, or otherwise endeavor to exhaust the expedition’s resources before they could get too close.  If Devourer’s Domain and Genesis Engine weren’t sustaining the domains almost entirely on their own, it would have been a good strategy, too.  Not that anybody on the expedition knew how long he could keep the skills active, either.

“Has anybody ever told you that you take all the fun out of everything?”

Kyle turned to face Skippy, who had walked up to him.  Tillienne wasn’t far behind, and Kyle could see Garth and the others shuffling nervously around the ship’s entrance.

“I’ve heard it once or twice.  Quite the welcome we’ve received, huh?”

“It certainly is,” Tillienne agreed, watching as another group of fungal tendrils broke through the ground, then crumbled to dust.  “How do you think we should proceed?”

“Well, the element of surprise is shot.  With some of the spores in the air, he knew exactly where we were going to land, and already had an ambush ready to go.  On the bright side, most of the fungal growths nearby have been cleared out.  I’m not saying that he won’t be able to grow more, but if I’m able to clear out a slightly larger perimeter, it should make it easier for the D Grades to defend the ship.”

“What’s stopping another army from coming up from below?” Skippy asked, a serious note returning to his voice.

Kyle considered for a moment.  With the obscuring formations destroyed, he and C.H.A.D.D. were able to get a better sense for the state of the area.  “The subsurface has been cleared of fungi for a good distance.  It’ll take time and energy to clear out the dead debris and provide new growth.  If we start our incursion, I doubt he’ll have the energy to spare for a strategy like that.”

Tillienne looked at the nervously shuffling D Grades.  “Do you think they’ll be up for it?”

“They’re going to have to be,” Kyle replied with a shrug.  “They have a few Fire Mages, not to mention a handful of real elites in the mix.  With the surprise ambush thwarted, this isn’t really much more dangerous than they should have been expecting in the first place.”

“Cold and calculating, this is a new side of you, Kyle,” Skippy said.  “I don’t disagree.  But you’re going to have to let me hit at least a few of the constructs along the way.  It’s no fun if you just kill them all.”

Tillienne nodded.  “We can certainly take some strain off of you as we advance.  No need to show all your cards along the way.”

Kyle nodded.  “I appreciate that, I’ll leave them to you as we travel.  Now, we should get moving.  You get the updates to Chester and the team.  I’ll clear out a slightly wider perimeter, and then we’ll look at heading out.”

“Understood,” Tillienne said with a nod, gesturing for Skippy to follow as she made her way back to the group of D Grades.  Kyle saw Rochelle and Garth looking his way, but he turned to his work.  Right now, he just wanted to destroy more of Victor’s constructs, and there were plenty in his range to let him work out his frustration.

In the distance, Kyle saw a couple more tendrils rising, though these didn’t approach any closer.  Trying to get a sense of my range, huh?  He was tempted to extend his domains just a bit to destroy them, but recognized that as a wholly unhelpful and petty move.  Can’t let my anger get the best of me here, he admonished himself as he went back to clearing the perimeter.

[DR. MAYHEW, NOW THAT THE FORMATION BELOW US HAS BEEN DISRUPTED, I AM DETECTING THE EDGES OF SEVERAL OTHER REPEATING PATTERNS.  WITH SMALL DETOURS, WE SHOULD BE ABLE TO BREAK THOSE FORMATIONS AS WELL.]

“Thank you, C.H.A.D.D.”

Through Pack Leader’s Instinct, Kyle could tell that the drone was trying to keep his anger focused on productive things.  And it was working.  I owe you one, he thought, making the small detours to break a couple more obscuring formations.  Then it would be time for the battle to really get going.

Victor DeRosa was having a bad day.  When his atmospheric sensors detected an incoming vessel, he’d foolishly hoped that he’d find a group of scientists or explorers, ripe for quick capture and ascension.  With luck, he could have even commandeered the ship, allowing him to begin colonizing other moons, other systems.  Instead, he found a crew of powerhouses.  His initial ambush was wiped out before he’d truly had a chance to understand how.  That was both vexing and problematic.

So, like any good scientist, Victor put more resources to the task.  From all sides, all angles, he continued to mobilize his forces.  After all, he had the bodies to spare.  He turned his senses to watch them crumble and fall, raising telescopic stalks outside the field of death to observe the slaughter.  As he continued probing, he started to get a vague sense of familiarity.  This is similar to the technique that killed Reynolds, he realized.  After watching dozens more crumble, he was certain of it.  The technique was less potent in some ways, but covered an incredibly wide area.

It was a mystery that flummoxed Victor for quite some time, though the loss of Gregory Valentine and subsequent disaster on his burgeoning colony dwarfed it.  Now, he would have a chance to properly study it.  And, of course, test his countermeasures.  He’d given some thought to how he’d handle techniques of this nature, and field testing always proved helpful.

As he watched the trio of C Grades left the ship at high speeds, moving toward one of the caverns on the surface.  So they’ve done their homework.  A pity.  DeRosa had hoped to remain undetected for a while longer, but this outcome was not altogether unexpected.  The presence of a few C Grades would certainly mean a hard-fought battle, though not one he deemed unwinnable.  After all, Victor had the defender’s advantage.  Even if his troops were all but worthless in an encounter like this, the land itself belonged to him.

He turned his attention back toward the ship, and the group left behind to guard it.  A handful of D Grades, not weak, per se, but much more vulnerable.  And if I can reach them…  A smile cracked his twisted face, new possibilities opening up in his mind.  Perhaps this wasn’t going to be such a bad day after all.
