In the three additional days it took for Rulfar to finish recovering his stamina, Asher made quite a lot of progress. While the mornings were spent hanging out with Samantha or training with Mornamol, his evenings were spent over in Whikoga, hard at work learning the ins and outs of Permanence’s second-tier ability.

Their findings were simultaneously incredible, and a bit of a letdown.

The first thing of note was confirmation that Asher could only influence one thing at a time with his skill here on the material realm. In order to utilize his second-tier ability in any other way, the apple he’d originally stopped from aging continued to age once more. Thankfully, those missing days didn’t all catch up to it all at once like he’d originally feared, but it was still a bit disheartening. Part of Asher had hoped that he’d just be able to halt the aging process on two different people at the same time. That way he could offer to keep Brian from growing old and dying of old age as well.

Obviously he wasn’t planning to pitch keeping him physically twelve forever, but somewhere in his early twenties like themselves.

Despite that drawback, they did discover a few curious interactions his skill had on things other than age. For one, Asher had suddenly become a pretty decent back-up healer in a pinch. A quick trip to the healers was all it took for him to confirm he could cause the blood pouring out of a wound to halt as though the artery had been sealed shut, or for poison to stop spreading before it could travel too far through the body. He couldn’t heal others with his skill or prevent injuries in the first place, but he could keep things from getting worse.

He could also do some seriously weird things with the world around him. All he needed to do was focus his ability, and he could stop the spread of most things, keeping them in a permanent state. He could halt the rate of a spreading fire, keeping it from growing any larger so long as he willed it, regardless of how much fuel was around. Keep the level of light in a room stable even if the lone torch illuminating it was snuffed out. Prevent a man from growing hungry even if he didn’t have anything to eat for hours on end.

The more he experimented, the more unique ideas he came up with to utilize his skill. Permanence quickly revealed itself to be incredibly versatile, and Asher grew even more excited to see precisely what its third-tier effect would end up being one day.

With Rulfar back in action and a firm understanding of his own skills once again, Asher wanted nothing more than to get right back to hunting down liches.

However, there was one last item on his to-do list he needed to take care of first.

“I do not appreciate having this dropped onto my lap out of nowhere and then being left in the dark for days on end,” Draken said slowly, the air turning cold as the death aura of Whikoga’s deadliest gang leader brushed up against Asher. “It is only because of our professional relationship and past experiences working together that I do not order my people to toss you out of my base this instant.”

“And I think he likes me!” Smiles said, the severed head grinning as though he didn’t have a care in the world. “Hey, Asher! Welcome back!

“Thanks, Smiles,” Asher said, taking in Smiles’ new setup. His cushion that had once been on Draken’s desk was now sitting on a small end table over in the corner of the room. There was a protective glass dome resting over the severed head, though if it was to try and keep Smiles in or the world out, he wasn’t sure. “What’s with the dome?”

“He kept trying to roll around,” Draken drawled, narrowing his eyes at the severed head.

“You kept turning me to face the corner!” Smiles argued, laughing like it was all just one big joke. “It is a nice corner, but I got a little bored after the first few hours of looking at it. Asher, do you have any more fun memories you can give me? Draken likes to drink a lot. And I mean a lot, a lot. Have any memories of being drunk?”

Asher raised an eyebrow at the gang leader, who merely sighed and closed his eyes.

“Asher, I am begging you. I don’t know how much longer I can do this. Please tell me you are here to take or destroy this thing. I’m about five minutes away from throwing it out the window. Why did you even want it saved in the first place?”

“Other than the fact that Smiles played a critical role in helping me take down the King of Death by leading me through the maze that was his workshop while I was actively dying, he’s not just some random undead,” Asher explained, tapping Smiles on the forehead and pushing the memory of the first time he’d tasted alcohol into the severed head. Unfortunately, Asher wasn’t a huge drinker, so he couldn’t do much more than share the taste. “He was created over fifteen hundred years ago, from the parts of a hero that tried to slay the King of Death before he’d even created the Deadlands. Sure, he’s a little… lost, these days. But I don’t know, I just felt bad for him. The King of Death was preparing to rip out my own brain and soul in order to construct a new undead as well. If things had gone differently, I could have become the next Smiles.”

“Truly?” Draken asked, giving Smiles a new look of almost respect before he shook his head. “Regardless of what he once was or what he’s been through, he is driving me up the wall. He is simply too curious. I can’t just keep him here in my office and hit him with my Death Aura every few hours. To be honest, it is exhausting. I have to put everything I have into it each time, he absorbs death energy like a sponge.”

“Alright, is there some other source of death energy we can get him? I don’t know, some monster part or magical gem that would solve the issue once and for all? I even have his body in my rift, we could potentially get him connected again and he could become one of your trusted enforcers, like Crush or Thunder!”

Part of Asher thought it a tad weird that he was basically trying to convince the leader of Whikoga’s largest gang to take an incredibly powerful weapon onto himself, even if it was also an incredibly annoying one. But while Draken might be a gang leader, his gang wasn’t like any Asher had ever seen. Most importantly, Draken ran a very tight ship. His people were well cared for and kept the peace around town, ensuring there wasn’t anything the common folk had to worry about. The gang survived by stealing from the upper class and nobles in the third and fourth ring. It was far riskier work than what the other gangs did, but had much bigger payouts.

“Being an enforcer sounds fun!” Smiles said, already having shaken off the latest memory Asher had shoved into his brain. “So does having a body again!”

“I can’t believe I’m even considering this,” Draken muttered, pinching the bridge of his nose as he sighed. “Okay. If you can make it so I don’t need to exhaust myself every few hours to keep him alive, I’m willing to try teaching him how to… well, be normal, I suppose. As to if such an item exists, I’m not sure. As my focus is on running this portion of the city, I don’t really know too much about monsters.”

“Leave that much to me,” Asher promised. “I don’t know much about monsters either, but I’ve got plenty of resources I can check with.”

“Are we finally ready to go after that missing lady or whatever her name is?” Moxy asked after he’d gathered his team in their usual lounge room Donvath had lent them. “Christian is begging me to give him a break from all the sparring, so for the sake of our relationship, I think I need to go beat up something else.”

“The Lost Lady,” Samantha said, her eyes widening as she smiled at Moxy. “And wait, you two are official? Congratulations, that’s so exciting!”

“You finally wore him down,” Rulfar said, a look of sorrow on his face as he shook his head. “That poor man…”

“Focus guys, Draken’s on his last legs here,” Asher said. “No, we’re not going after The Lost Lady. Soon, but not quite yet. First, we need to find some source of death energy in order to keep Smiles alive.”

“The freaky, talking severed head you showed us?” Moxy asked, giving him a curious look. “It’s still kicking?”

“For now, but it won’t be much longer if we don’t find a new source of death energy for it,” Asher explained. “I’ve Incorporated a pretty large index of monsters from the library, but it is large, and I don’t even know where I would begin reading through it. Do any of you know of a monster with what we would need?”

“I know of plenty of undead, but nothing that would radiate that kind of power,” Rulfar admitted, crossing his arms as he leaned back in his chair in thought. “Would such a monster even exist? What did people think the King of Death was if not a lich? That seems like a good starting point.”

“There were a lot of theories, but the most popular one was some sort of death dragon,” Asher shrugged. “Apparently, dragons are always matched with an element. Nobody’s ever heard of a death dragon before, but it’s possible. But also not an option for us.”

“Can we have Draken create an artifact to mirror his Death Aura skill?” Samantha proposed. “We know the skill is enough to keep Smiles ‘alive.’ Wouldn’t that work?”

“Don’t forget, Death is technically still one of the forbidden elements of the Noala Kingdom,” Asher reminded them. “Draken gets a pass because it’s his origin element, but I don’t think we’ll find an enchanter willing to bend on that. It’s one thing to work with forbidden elements to create simple, personal artifacts like my Ring of False death, but it's another thing entirely to make something capable of instantly killing people.”

“Aren’t you pals with the High Prince?” Moxy pointed out. “Just tell him to order an enchanter to do it, or whatever. Problem solved.”

“I can’t order Donvath to do anything,” Asher drawled. “Not to mention I doubt Draken would be willing to potentially weaponize his own Death Aura into an artifact. He takes his power very seriously.”

“What about Richard?” Rulfar asked. “He still owes each of us a favor. Could he do it?”

“He actually owes me two, but no, I already checked in with him first,” Asher admitted. “Richard never ended up learning any death skills, and that means he can’t make us the artifact we would need. Which I suppose isn’t surprising as, again, forbidden element.”

“What about our new wizard that doesn’t suck?” Moxy asked. “Kroult was it, right? Whatever happened to him?”

“Donvath said he was going to put him through the mother of all interrogations, and that was the last I heard about it,” Asher said. “Can’t say I blame him either. If I was the leader of a whole country, I wouldn’t be all too thrilled about the idea of a lich running around freely within its borders. Especially one that used to be on the Council of Death, even if in name only.”

“Either way, Kroult admitted he wasn’t a very powerful wizard,” Samantha pointed out. “I doubt he’s the answer we need.”

“It sounds like the answer we need doesn’t exist anywhere,” Moxy snorted. “Not on this realm at least.”

Asher, Rulfar, and Samantha all blinked as one, slowly turning to look at Moxy as she picked at something between her teeth. It took her a few seconds to realize they were all staring at her before she raised an eyebrow. “...What?”

“Moxy might not be the brightest, but she’s got a point,” Rulfar said, his poor chair creaking dangerously as he leaned forward and gave them all a wide grin. “The object we need might not exist on this realm. But I’m willing to bet it does in another.”
