Sarna glanced around the table and suppressed a sigh. Intake days were amazing and horrible at the same time. Thankfully she'd only had to deal with five evaluations this year, five near rubber stamps that required a degree of care and knowledge.

"Thank you Elienne," Dean Jean said as the woman finished giving her opinion on yet another group. "That leaves the members of the Coordinator Dorm. I will remind you all that certain aspects are not to be discussed without prior clearance. Those who are not aware of those aspects, consider yourself lucky for the moment."

"What's to be said," Marten groused. "You let them all in regardless of what actual background they had. Put them all in the same dorm without proper evaluation. My lessons will be a mess for at least a year."

"I'm aware of your opinions Seer Marten," Jean said. "Your objections are noted however those two groups had to be isolated, the Family groups have the appropriate degrees of actual talent and backing and-"

"And you put those two in there with them because your old friend had my job and their sponsors were the Hall's darlings."

"That is enough," the Dean snapped. "They were placed there for rooming constraints, a solo Delver is unheard of, a pair only slightly less so. We can fit both on a single floor without upsetting space allocations."

"I have a few notes," Sarna said softly. She knew Leo was glaring at her, reminding her she needed to make herself heard. Easy for him to say, the man couldn't be quiet even if he tried.

"Go on," Jean said.

"The Hammerswells are exactly what they seem on the surface. Family trained, brothers born back to back and a cousin who has the family pride in spades. Their healer will hopefully keep them in line."

"Expected."

"The Blehmans are going to have trouble dueling. Someone convinced them they could get away with an Archer for Overwatch."

Cara rolled over, adjusting her blankets once as she tried to get comfortable on the floor. The School had provided private rooms for all of them but here she was on the floor. Hopefully they'd be able to get things adjusted quickly, otherwise she was going to rip the mattress off her bed.

She knew Keldren wouldn't complain, he probably didn't even notice the difference. Not that he'd fit on the beds they were given. She did fit on the bed, she didn't have to sleep on the floor to have enough space. But there was only one bed in Gwen's room and of course that meant it was Gwen's.

She turned to face the other way, hoping her shoulder would find a spot this time. Her throat still hurt from choking on her words, her whole body ached from the momentary disobedience that Gwen had orchestrated. She'd known it might happen but that didn't make it hurt less, didn't make being used hurt less.

I'm sorry.

The words slipped into her head, the band between her and Gwen snapping into place despite how rarely they used it. It wasn't normal to be that open with someone all the time, to let emotions slip through unfiltered, to know someone that way. Using it in combat as a group, or even as a pair while training was one thing. You could focus on the moment, keep yourself from distractions.

Using it to whisper late at night was something else entirely, something better suited to their first few years after Awakening when they still spent more time out of the Depths than in. Morgan established the bands in the depth, kept them all coordinated. That was part of his role, his specialty. A private connection would prevent the other, and if there was an emergency those moments could be deadly.

I… Cara? Gwen sent, the words feeling almost fragile.

It hurt. It wasn't just choking.

It… you didn't say!

Was I supposed to wince? To curl up in pain around strangers, even if you called them family?

She wasn't being fair, she could feel her own pain and frustration threatening to break the band. She could feel how it was affecting Gwen, how the words on their own were verging on cruel.

You should have told me. You could have-

I couldn't say no.

I would have understood.

Flipping back over Cara glared at the bed, her eyes finding Gwen's despite the near perfect darkness. She didn't try to filter how she felt, she didn't try to pretend. They'd had this discussion in a way before, she'd tried to explain but-

Gwen. I couldn't, she felt Gwen flinch and bit her lip. She didn't want to hurt her but she had to. The oath, the geas. I know you wouldn't have forced me to. But you needed to know, you needed the answer and it was the simplest way.

Cara-

No. You needed to know, I could get you the answer. All it took was choking and fighting to stay standing as though nothing was happening.

She could hear Gwen moving, hear the bed shift as her feet hit the floor.

Get in bed, she sent. Sleep.

No, Gwen sent.

She sat up, only slightly surprised at how close Gwen was.

Don't make me-

Get in the bed Caraene, Gwen sent.

Cara tensed, felt her body start to ache as she tried to resist the words. Gwen had- was- She stood up, ignoring her blankets as she all but flung herself onto the bed and tried unsuccessfully to get right back out.

Gwen sat down next to her, nudging her over until they found a way to have space for both of them.

Gwen, she sent, her emotions seething.

You need a proper bed.

This is your bed. And you promised you wouldn't.

That hurt more than anything. Gwen had used the geas. She'd used her name, not to Name her but simply to ensure the order took fully. She'd used it to make her get into the bed and not sleep on the floor.

They'll say something.

They won't come in to my room, not unless there's a disaster.

She wasn't wrong. Respect for privacy was something they'd all had ingrained in them. How could it not be when most of their time was spent in the Depths without any to be found. Nobles and their worries about propriety, about privacy.

She sighed. The bed was comfortable. Gwen's silent tears weren't.

She'd lashed out, she'd let her own emotions run free and taken it out on her. She'd hurt her Gwen.

I'm sorry, she sent.

No, Gwen sent back. I am. I used you, and then I- Sleep where you want.

Cara felt the command fade. She considered going back to the floor, considered how the mattress felt, considered how Gwen felt. She rolled onto her side and pulled Gwen to her. Her sworn charge, the woman she'd spent almost every remembered hour of her life within earshot of, made herself smaller, curled into her arms and lay there.

How did you know you could say it? Gwen sent.

Say what?

That I was jealous. I haven't blushed that much, felt that embarrassed, since…/

You trusted them. You aren't competing with them.

I'm not? Gwen answered. She's in the direct line, same as Bern and me.

You're not. Or at least you don't want to be.

They might be though. But that wasn't for Gwen to worry about. If they were a threat Cara would deal with it, no matter what it cost her. She wouldn't be able to do anything about the rumors or concerns of the royals, but she'd deal with Anya, and Seb, if she had to.

Anya hadn't asked what her being in the royal line meant. They hadn't asked any questions that Cara had expected. All they'd asked about were mundane details that might affect interactions in class or in the dining hall. They were clearly curious, clearly wanting details. She'd seen the look in Seb's eye, the same look she'd seen in Morgan's more than once.

They wanted privacy Cara, Gwen sent.

Cara stilled, supremely aware of her fingers in Gwen's hair, her other hand on her side in a loose embrace. She hadn't said anything, had she? Hadn't reacted in some way that made it obvious what she was-

Stop being so nervous. No one is going to barge in, she added. They weren't what I expected either. They kept blushing though, and were clearly unhappy to be opening the door when you knocked.

Cara tried to relax, tried to calm down. The band helped, knowing Gwen was safe, knowing the tears were gone, the pain was fading away. The band made it worse…
