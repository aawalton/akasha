Chapter 140: A Need to Be Said

General Mask said with gravitas, “My title is coded simply as ‘the PM.’ Coded because most think that refers to the Prime Minister, which is a deliberately arranged deception. The real meaning is something you all need to understand before you go out there, because like every faction has an Archon, every faction has one of us, a term and title that is not of our choice, but a translation from the core System, which required this when Memoria was first born. The title is Prime Mover.”

Once more, the mysterious masked man left his words to hang. Indeed, Jack and every other graduate were hardly breathing, rapt at attention to this rare revelation of authority.

Who all knows this, I wonder? Every full ANP, certainly.

“This is highly classified,” the Prime Mover continued, “but my stance is that every jacket-wearer is owed the knowledge before going out there. The PMs are always the strongest individual combatants, and we are deeply integrated with the reflection of our Archon — but I won’t get into that any further at this time. PMs rarely leave their territory as the risk is too great, and their power diminishes. They typically find borders to fight on. If you even think you’ve encountered a foreign one? Run. Pray, while you’re at it. Ironically, they’re more dangerous than an Archon on the borders, who have to reach out indirectly, just as ours does.

“My personal involvement as PM is a complex matter. These days, I actively deal primarily with emergencies and existential threats.” Jack felt like the man was looking right at him during this comment. “I hope it goes without saying that if you are unlucky enough to hear an order coming down from me, it’s important.” He shrugged with a hand. “That’s all, Agentus.”

An official suddenly called in a loud, exaggeratedly rough command, “High salute for the PM!”

Everyone in the place did a salute, including the recent, jacketed ANPs.

The PM returned the salute. “You’re dismissed. Go and make us proud; level up. We’ll be watching, as always.”

The at once stunned and elated new members of the Agentus were swiftly shuffled out of Central Command, through the airlock, back into the hallways.

It really was like the bridge of a sci-fi capital ship in there. It made him suddenly daydream of the whole tower rising into orbit and shooting out for the stars. He literally chuckled at himself. Now we’re getting fanciful, even for me.

Out in the cover of the hallways, elated cries and chatter exploded among the youthful new agents. Highfive threw an arm around Jack, laughing joyously. “Can you believe it, bro?!” He was shaken vigorously. “Can you?!”

“Yes!” Jack said, trying to fend the big guy off. “No! What do you even- Whatever, just stop shaking me, damn you!”

“Alright, that’s enough roughhousing and chatter,” the guide commanded with mild disapproval. “Let’s take a walk — a professional walk — to the elevators, and head over to Commons.”

Everyone exchanged glances, muttering about that last word, but no one seemed to know.

They proceeded down typical liminal corridors to a wider opening with a central feel to it, most obviously by having a large cargo elevator that was flanked by four smaller elevators. The cargo elevator opened for their admittance, as their guide didn’t even miss a step entering it.

Everyone filed in, keeping relatively quiet, and the elevator went… up — something likely not everyone knew, because the movement was deliberately subtle. If anything, it naturally felt like it went down. However, Jack’s senses through the metal structures of the tower were special, and he’d been paying attention in the back of his mind all the while, mentally mapping things. His senses informed him the group was still in the general vicinity of Central Processing. When they stopped, he placed the location on the bottom end, or immediately under it, which meant that Central Command was significantly under all of it and separated, deep in the tower, encased in absurdly thick walls of metal.

Makes sense. It’s like redundancy — the PM and his leadership structure reside in one place, while the core of Central Processing leadership, more operationally open, is housed in another. Maybe an enemy could target CP, but good luck even fathoming the other place. Before I went there, I never would have. I’d bet anything CP has a less peculiar, decoy location for Central Command.

Mini suddenly chimed at this point. <Congratulations on being in the fairly rare class of agents we have to report as knowing too much about tower locations, Jack. Take note: referential tower location information is highly, highly classified, especially regarding Central Command. Don’t share it with anyone, ever, don’t try to ferret out more, and preferably stop even thinking about it.>

Jack blinked a few times from the exchange, somewhat shocked by it. He hadn’t thought it was that big of a deal for those on the ‘inside.’ Then again, it wasn’t like he wasn’t paranoid as hell, himself. If it were up to him, wouldn’t he institute the same sort of rules? And it suddenly made sense why very few people ever went there…

Given pause by this whole thing, lost in thought, someone had to grunt and nudge him forward to exit the elevator. Briefly, he replied to Mini, <Understood, but no promises on the thought thing.>

<I may have suggested it, Jack, but trust that I know the probability of you doing so approaches zero.>

Soon, the environment took his attention, as it did the others, a few gasping in amazement.

They were in a huge hollow ring of the tower, stepping out from the central core elevators into another world, as seen from something of a large, metal observation deck. Free-floating orbs generated warm light all around. The very edge of the tower surface could be seen from their vantage, complete with numerous pockets of thick windows, which had to be mirrored on the other side, and disguised even further, because this blatant space was not publicly known.

Below, there was an earthy bottom, and numerous park-like greenery and various buildings, even some houses scattered around. At first, what he thought were pillars rose from the floor to the roof, but he soon realized they were towers with rooms inside, from the existence of various windows.

It was not a crowded place by any means, but activity could be seen in people lounging here or there, but mainly by the existence of small platforms zipping around. They were metal disks with a railing; the agents using them were always standing with their hands firmly placed, because the speed was significant. One disappeared above into a quickly opening panel, revealing an alcove that closed behind him. Another sped over to a large station structure embedded into the tower wall. He thought he recognized it, somehow, just from the positioning.

“Is that Midstation?” he asked no one in particular as he gawked. He’d dropped personnel off there occasionally, but never got to see much. He glanced around and ended up looking back to see their guide raising an eyebrow at him just as the elevator door closed.

Well then.

“Yes, it is, Agent Ferro,” said a familiar voice to his front. As he turned to see, just about everyone else present was doing so, too, becoming aware of a man in a suit and mirrored smartglasses who had somehow escaped their notice — Agent Baham- no. Agent Cancun, the man who’d once interrogated him before he’d signed the contract. Only pairing the voice with the face succeeded for recognition, so similar were the two.

Standing near the railing of the deck with his hands clasped at his waist, Agent Cancun continued, “Welcome to the Commons, available only to high clearance agents. With a few exceptions, you can go where you want here, for recreation or business. Containment zones for safe, lighter power practice or sports events are allowed in some cases, in specific authorized spots, if you prefer the decor here. They aren’t free by default, but take note, you have a limited credit as ANPs to encourage keeping up your skills. The towers have offices and meeting rooms, mainly for smaller teams, and Midstation One and Two are operational or personal aerial exits, depending on individual circumstances.”

He pointed to the roof. “Immediately above you are extensive private quarters you’ve already been moved into. You can reach them either through Central Processing above that, or from here, via a platform or other supernatural means. Memoria makes some extra allowances here for the sake of speed. Ironically, efficiency gains make the net costs minor. I’m authorizing you all for the interface access… now.”

Jack became aware of a tiny new window in his mind that essentially asked the question, “Where do you want to go?” and was clearly prepared to help him get there on a dime. This was advanced stuff with no voice interactions present, just a thought portal, like tossing intentions into a swallowing void, which was… a bit awkward for him.

“Some of you may not be as practiced as others at using this,” Agent Cancun continued, “but rest assured that it’s intuitive — it was designed to be. You’ll get used to it.” He gestured with his hands to either side, and the balcony and platform seemed to grow out and clone itself fluidly, into two self-contained floating platforms, without apparently stealing material from the deck. That was just an appearance, Jack knew instinctively and logically — there was simply extra material underneath moving to fill in.

I could do all this myself, actually. Would be good practice. But getting used to the interface should come first, as I’m sure this’ll be faster for a while.

The platforms floated upward, spun around a few times, then sank right back down into the deck, as if they’d never been. Cancun dropped his hands. “This suffices for your briefing, Agentus. Any questions? Otherwise, I hereby turn you loose.”

A young, blue-haired woman who hadn’t been paying proper attention asked, “Yeah, um, how do we get to our new quarters, again?”

Agent Cancun gave her a flat look; then again, he gave everyone those at all times. “You ask your interface to take you, Agent Conflux, and then it happens, like magic.”

Highfive, still grinning and looking around as he had been since they’d entered, asked, “What’s the allowance for training?”

“Ten hours a week anywhere allowed in the tower, with accrual up to a month if you’re abroad. If you’re out longer, you can request more credit, which will be granted if you’ve leveled up significantly and thereby need the dedication.”

Highfive wheezed as he took on an appreciative expression and slow-clapped his hands. “Oh, man, that’s awesome, hell yeah, bro! This is great!”

Agent Cancun frowned slightly at Highfive’s commentary, likely due to the unprofessional expressions, but said nothing further.

“Can we ask about the PM?” Délight asked.

“Yeah, just how much is he our hidden patriarch?” Bo added, which caused a few snickers.

Cancun replied simply, “I can’t answer anything about that.”

“I have a question,” Jack said. “Why were you my interrogator not long ago, but also here? Is it just who is free at any given time?”

Cancun turned to Jack and might’ve raised his brows slightly, though it was hard to tell with the shades. “Do you perhaps think I’m here for your sake, Agent? A familiar face for a special boy?”

Jack tried not to sneer, turning into a ‘polite smile’ as he shook his head and shrugged. “It’s just simple curiosity.”

“We have our skill subsets, but this is busy work anyone could do. We like to spread that around, you could say.”

Because the man had been slightly insulting anyway, Jack also asked, “Did you really need to take a shit back during the interrogation?” Suppressed laughter and snorts ensued from this, naturally. “When you ran off.”

Cancun didn’t appear insulted, though. He shook his head. “I said it to deliberately excuse myself in such a ridiculous manner, you saw it as an eccentricity of character, instead of potentially being suspicious of my sudden departure, and thereby potentially more on the guard when Agent Bermuda entered. A small matter, but that’s the truth.”

Jack nodded slowly. “So, you didn’t actually blow up a toilet while I was getting scammed?” More amusement from some of the others, as well as a few incredulous frowns. The blue-haired girl who had asked the dumb question, ‘Conflux,’ was holding both hands over her mouth, shaking as she repressed her laughter.

“Do you really feel scammed right now, Agent?” He gestured out at the gorgeous, exclusive environment.

“Not really, but you didn’t answer the question, Agent Cancun.”

Cancun gave him a level look. “No, Agent Ferro, I didn’t blow up a toilet. I went to work elsewhere.”

“A shame. It would’ve been a synergistic use of time.”

“It certainly would’ve been one for the logs. Now, then, any further questions?”

Jack blinked as he realized a sneaky pun had been dropped.

Hey, he’s maybe not so bad, after all.

No one had anything further to ask, and Cancun strolled off for the elevators.

A few people immediately started on their ‘platform magic’ to head up, with one girl veritably screaming as it rocketed off. Most remained loitering indecisively in small clusters on the deck.

Conflux walked up to Jack and put a hand on his shoulder as she leaned downward and shook her head despairingly. “Why?!” she asked in a mock strangled voice before meeting his eyes. “Why did you do that to me, with the toilet humor? Now, of all times?!”

Grinning, Jack shrugged. “I am what I am. But did you catch the pun he… dropped?” At Conflux’s confused and pre-amused look, Jack repeated, “One for the logs?”

Conflux’s eyes went wide, and her jaw dropped. “No!” She guffawed and shook her head, finally cringing painfully. “Ugh! Awful, it’s just awful, why am I laughing, damn it… damn you…”

Chortling himself — mostly from her infectious humor — Jack held out his hand. “Ferronaut. Call me Jack outside the mask, though.”

Conflux shook his hand as she nodded. “I’ve heard. Call me Flux. I’m actually going to use that as the radio handle, anyway. I was surprised to find it unclaimed, and it would be confusing, right?”

Jack noticed Thread Bear, Snapper, and another subdued girl walking up behind her, and it clicked. “Conflux. Are you the renowned offensive booster of Snapper, then? The Catalyst?”

Flux grinned and splayed her hands in a smug pose. “That’s me! My reputation precedes me, I see. If you’re curious — which you gotta be with those weird questions — my boost is versatile, uuuum, medium strength? But takes continual focus. No fire-and-forget for moi.”

Jack nodded thoughtfully, then snapped his fingers. “Conflux like an actual river?”

“Exactly! We both flow into a mutual, greater, continuous power!” She thrust a palm out, ballooned her cheeks, and let out a big splash sound.

Jack couldn’t help but laugh at that. So did Highfive, nearby.

“Well, you’re overly adorable,” Bo said, to which Conflux blinked, unsure how exactly to take it.

Jack nodded toward the rest of Conflux’s crew and said, “Congratulations on the jacket, guys.”

Thread Bear smiled politely and nodded, as Snapper gripped the lapels of his jacket and tugged them smugly. “We look damn good in them, don’t we?”

“You best believe it, bro!” Highfive said with a grin, miming Snapper’s maneuver, with equal, if not greater, smugness. They shared mutual appreciation for each other.

Jack cleared his throat loudly. “Ahem! Regardless of our aesthetics, I think we all have a date with our accommodations in order-”

“Ahhh!” Conflux scream-squealed and jumped up with a sudden, toothy grin. “I’ve gotta see it, yes, yes, yes!” She spun around and practically ran, though grabbing the other girl of the group in the process, who was left wide-eyed in horror as she was tugged along. “Let’s go, let’s go, me first, me first!”

Everyone watched them get on a platform ‘together’ and fly off, Conflux throwing her hands up and yelling like she was on a rollercoaster.

Thread Bear turned to look at Snapper warily, but the other man had a knowing look as he put a hand on Thread Bear’s shoulder and shook his head. “I think we should do this alone.”

“I’m pleasantly surprised you see it that way, Snap,” Thread Bear said, blinking in disbelief.

Jack turned to his teammates, who had wistful looks in their eyes. “Sooo… I take it the official personal quarters are a big deal?”

“Yeah, man,” Highfive replied. “Everyone knows they’re awesome. I guess this place is technically classified, but you can’t help but hear how Agents often don’t bother getting houses outside the tower.”

“Unless they marry someone and have a family,” Bo added.

“Right. Anyway, it’s more than that — it’s something permanent, right? Or can be. It’s not a place to stay for a while and move out…” He trailed off, lost for further words.

Jack smiled. “I get it, Blondy. To a degree, I’ve already been there with the whole ‘independent, totally my own place’ sort of move. But now that I think about it, this does feel different. That must be magnified for you guys.”

Sober, somewhat uncomfortable nods were exchanged. Bo finally sighed and shrugged, “I think we’re all in agreement about doing this solo, then. I damn sure don’t plan on letting anyone else see how I react. Bad enough, what happened in that damn ‘star cruiser bridge’ place we were in.”

Jack snapped his fingers and pointed at her. “Exactly! That’s exactly what it looked like. Anyway, don’t sweat it, Bo. I forgot what happened with you, anyway. I was too self-absorbed. Right, Fiver?”

Highfive’s brow knitted together. “Huh? I remember everything, and Bo kinda br-”

Jack cleared his throat loudly in interruption and glared at Highfive insistently, raising his brow high.

Given pause, Highfive glanced between Jack and the suddenly subdued Bo, at which point things finally clicked. “Actually, ah, shit, it just slipped away! I can’t remember anything. It’s like you say, Bo — I have the memory of a fish. Oh well.”

Bo nodded uncomfortably, not meeting their eyes. “I’m… gonna go now.” She started walking off, then hesitantly muttered, “Thanks, guys,” before rushing off and forming a platform.

Grinning to himself, Jack made for the edge of the deck himself, patting Highfive’s shoulder along the way.

“Hey,” Highfive called, forestalling him.

Jack turned to see Highfive holding out his fist for a bump. He bumped it.

“Appreciate all you’ve done, bro,” Highfive said significantly, briefly looking away and collecting himself before meeting Jack’s eyes with a deeply serious cast. “I may’ve said it before, but I don’t think I’ve gotten the chance to express just how much I do. I’m bad at this stuff, and it probably sounds weird, but… You really pulled my soul out of the gutter when I was down and out. I don’t know how else to say it, but it’s true, and it needs to be said. I’m a full Agent Non, now — I’m pursuing my dream in confidence again — all thanks to your leadership, coaching, networking, strategy, and so on. It’s all because of you.”

Jack was touched by the admittance but also felt slightly embarrassed by the deep, personal praise. He shook his head slightly. “Not all me, Fiver — we all helped each oth-”

“I know, know,” Highfive said, holding up his hands. “But you brought the lion’s share, Jack. You are the mastermind — you’re just on a different level. Everybody else knows it, and you should, too. I just want you to know, I’ll follow you straight to hell and will always have your back. We’re in this together forever, whatever happens. You’re my brother for real. You need me for anything, I’m there like lightning, guaranteed.”

Jack nodded slowly, resisting any lengthy response as he realized it wasn’t warranted. Finally, he offered simply, “Likewise, Blondy.”

After a long pause with them both looking off, Jack smiled and said, “Now go enjoy your new pad, bro.”

Highfive returned the grin as he rubbed his hands together. “Ohhh, you know I’m about to soak it up in my riches, baby!” He hopped over to the edge and started finger-drumming on the railing. “Presto chango! Uhhh, how does this work again?”

Shaking his head in exasperation, Jack called, “Throw your thoughts into the interface’s black hole,” as he moved over and smoothly formed a platform himself.

“Holy shit, it’s workiiiiiing — ahhhh — wooooo!” Fiver zoomed away, still having the time of his life.
