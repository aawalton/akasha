Gilbert gave Sakura a careful look from head to toe and nodded almost imperceptibly.

“That’s much better,” he said. “Now you at least look like a person.”

Sakura snorted, though without any real irritation. She ran a palm over the new vest layer, still getting used to how strangely well the clothes fit her.

Gilbert hesitated for a moment, then pulled a weapon from behind his belt, hidden by the folds of his cloak. Sakura had not noticed exactly when he had managed to put it there. Most likely after he had regained access to his Inventory. It was a curved blade. Not too long, but not short either. The blade itself was thin, with a smooth curve, and the hilt was silver, its metal slightly darkened, with a simple but beautiful pattern near the guard.

Sakura went quiet at once. Gilbert held the weapon out to her, hilt first.

“This is for you,” he said. “From me personally. If you practice and really learn how to use it, this blade will serve you well.”

Sakura took it in both hands, carefully. The weapon was lighter than it looked. For a moment, the sheer reality of it all made her uneasy. Her new life...

“Thank you,” she said quietly. Then she turned her gaze to Saorex. “And to you too. For everything. I appreciate it.”

Saorex looked at her with his black eyes so calmly that, as always, there was no way to tell what he was thinking from his face. But he accepted her gratitude all the same, in his own way.

“This is-s all I can do for you,” he said. “Ahead of you are many more trials-s. And they will be far more dangerous-s and cruel.”

Sakura tightened her fingers around the hilt of the blade without meaning to.

“I believe that,” she muttered.

Saorex tilted his head slightly, as if her reaction did not surprise him.

“The main thing is-s not to forget that cruelty is-s not the only path, even when it seems-s to you that there is-s no other choice.”

Sakura looked at him in silence for several seconds, then slowly nodded.

“I’ll remember.”

Saorex looked away first, as though everything that needed to be said had now been said.

“Good. Then leave my work-s-shop and do not bother me over trifles-s.”

“We’ll go,” Sakura said quietly.

Gilbert gave a faint huff and gestured for her to follow him.

They returned to the chamber Saorex had given them to rest in. Pampli lay in his place, yawning. Sakura sat down on the edge of one of the bundled web blankets and hid the blade in her Inventory. They rested for a short while and ate a little in silence. Then Sakura glanced at Gilbert and noticed how focused he had become. Even by his usual standards. It was as though none of that dry irony he always hid behind was left in him.

“This is the best time to discuss what we do next,” he said.

Sakura straightened at once. They really did need to talk about it.

“Yes. You’re right.”

Gilbert dropped to one knee. With his fingers, he cleared away a thin layer of dust from the stone floor and began drawing lines in it, marking symbols Sakura did not understand yet. He was sketching a map, very roughly.

Sakura leaned closer.

“This is our area,” he answered shortly. “Very simplified. Here,” he tapped one of the marks with a finger, “is Mebrel. Here are the ruins of Crowlis. Here are the forests stretching farther north and east. These are the old roads. This is the river. And here...”

He drew the line farther, toward an uneven circle.

“Ogrom.”

The name sounded almost as grim as it looked on the improvised map.

Sakura frowned.

“Let me guess. We’re heading to this Ogrom?”

“Yes.”

“I already don’t like it,” she said honestly. “The name alone feels dangerous.”

“And you’re not wrong,” Gilbert replied dryly. “But let me remind you that kidnapping a lawkeeper with magical runes was your plan. Ogrom is where that will be most realistic.”

Sakura glanced at him.

“But it still won’t be easy.”

“Of course not. It’ll be very, very hard.”

He straightened one more line on the dusty floor and then continued more seriously.

“Our situation is bad. Worse than you think. After the escape from Mebrel, it won’t just be the locals hunting us. Everyone will be looking for us and trying to kill us.”

Sakura listened in silence.

“Gustav Merren is not just some city bastard with a title,” Gilbert said. “He’s an influential politician. His influence has spread far beyond Mebrel for a long time now. He leads mage extermination squads in this region and works directly for those who benefit from making sure not a single spark of magic ever catches again.”

Sakura frowned even more deeply.

“So he matters more than he seemed to.”

“Much more.”

Gilbert dragged a finger along the edge of the improvised map, smudging the dust a little.

“Before you appeared, everything was simple. All he had to do was track down low-level mages and turn them over to the authorities. But now Crowlis’s magic has resurfaced.”

Sakura’s gaze dropped involuntarily to her own hand. Somewhere there, beneath the sleeve of her clothing, the ominous mark was hidden.

“So now everyone’s going to be thrown into a panic because of me.”

“Yes,” Gilbert said. “That’s why we need a city where we can stay as unnoticed as possible. Ogrom is the only option.”

He tapped the circle that marked Ogrom. Sakura studied the rough outline in the dust.

“Tell me.”

Gilbert nodded.

“Ogrom is poor, filthy, and full of crime. It was abandoned to fend for itself long ago, because there are too many obsidian creatures nearby. Trade routes avoid it whenever they can. The authorities pretend the city exists, but prefer not to look in its direction. People survive there however they can. Smuggling, underground markets, questionable workshops, mercenaries, people without names or pasts — there’s plenty of all that.”

“That sounds like a terrible place.”

“That’s exactly why it suits us.”

Sakura made a face. Gilbert went on.

“It’s easier to disappear in Ogrom. People ask fewer questions when you look strange. They barely even care if you’ve hidden your name from System recognition. But there’s another side to it. Finding a lawkeeper there will be harder than finding some peddler dealing in every kind of filth.”

Gilbert fell silent for a moment, then smirked.

“And before we head there, we’ll have two days. Two days to raise your level. I think that will help. Because you won’t be able to use your class magic. We’ll need something else.”

“What something else?” Sakura asked, surprised.

“Scrolls. So-called external magic, not your own. I’m going to teach you how to use scrolls.”

Sakura looked at Gilbert, once again thinking about how skilled this world was at surprising her. How much still remained that she did not know? And how much more would she have to learn?

“Fortunately, scroll magic isn’t complicated. I think you’ll pick it up quickly.”

“With a teacher like you, I don’t suppose I have much choice.”

“Unfortunately, as long as I’m wearing these bracelets, I won’t be able to become a very good teacher for you. But that’s a temporary problem.”

Sakura smirked and nodded.

“Well then, let’s not waste time. Teach me about scrolls.”

Gilbert looked at her as if that was exactly the answer he had been waiting for.

“Not here,” he said. “If you trigger a scroll the wrong way, the strike could go anywhere. Better not test how patient Saorex is when someone starts burning down his cave.”

Sakura glanced at the walls and the web blankets, and the thought immediately seemed reasonable.

“Fair enough. I’d rather not make Saorex angry...”

Gilbert gave the faintest smile.

“Exactly. Come on.”

They left the chamber, passed through several narrow corridors, and came out through a side opening, not the one they had used when they first entered the caves. Outside, it had already grown noticeably darker, but the vast sky of Veyr still held onto a muddy remnant of light. The forest was quiet and far too watchful, the way it often seemed to be. Gilbert led Sakura a little farther from the entrance, to a small rocky ledge between the roots of old trees, where there was no webbing, but plenty of flat stones and the dry trunk of a long-dead tree.

“This will do,” Gilbert said.

Pampli, of course, came with them and immediately settled off to the side on a rock, watching with wary interest.

Sakura crossed her arms over her chest.

“So. Scrolls.”

Gilbert summoned a leather case from his Inventory and opened it on the stone between them. Inside lay rolled strips of thick paper, thin plates like waxed parchment, and several narrow wooden slats marked with burned symbols.

Sakura leaned closer, examining everything carefully. At last, Gilbert began to explain.

“A Magic Scroll is a sealed magical construct, already assembled, stabilized, and ready for use. In practice, the medium itself does not matter. It could even be an ordinary stone. Scrolls are simply the accepted form for sealed magic.”

He took one of the scrolls, though he did not unroll it fully, only showing her the edge.

“With ordinary magic, you build the spell yourself, limited by the capabilities of your class. You form the flow, hold the structure together, and direct it. That takes control, mana reserves, practice, and most often time. With a scroll, it’s different. Someone has already done all of that for you. A mage or a scribe folds the spell into a stable pattern, weaves it into the medium, adds limiters, and seals it. The one who uses it later does not create the magic from nothing. They unlock what has already been sealed.”

Sakura listened in silence, trying to remember every word.

“That doesn’t sound complicated. You just need to learn how to unlock the magic woven into the scrolls, right?” she asked.

Gilbert raised his eyes to her.

“It’s not that simple, Sakura. You need a proper impulse. Intention. Sometimes a little mana, sometimes blood, but most often contact with the user’s will. It depends on what conditions the creator wrote into the scroll. If we simplify it.”

Sakura shifted her gaze to the case.

“I hope we won’t have to spill blood today.”

“We’ll see. So. Are you ready to move on to practice?”
