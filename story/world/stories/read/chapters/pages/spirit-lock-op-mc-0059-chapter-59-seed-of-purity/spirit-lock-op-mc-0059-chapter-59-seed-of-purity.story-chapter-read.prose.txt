“That’s a good reaction,” Jeela said, gently lifting the pearl. “The Seed of Purity is widely considered a jewel. Its burn is gentle, but no one who has used it has reported issues with power output or incompatibility. It’s truly priceless.”

Hayden accepted the catalyst carefully. “Obtain as many of these as you can, and I will pay for them out of my auction proceeds—and I will do so generously.” He fixed his eyes on her. “Do not sell them to anyone else.”

Jeela felt electric dysphoria spread across her skin. It was clear that he was offering her the carrot or the firing squad. Sweat dripped down her chin. “It’s quite difficult to obtain these.”

“I will give you ten percent of my profits from the auction for one hundred of these,” Hayden said.

Jeela’s jaw locked up. Hayden was offering her between 10 and 200 trillion credits per catalyst. His gaze said he knew what he was offering—and that she would be foolish to cross him.

“I will buy them if you survive the rift,” Jeela said. “It will also take time.”

Hayden’s icy gaze thawed, allowing her to breathe.

“I’m glad you like it,” she said.

For a split second, he stared at the catalyst in confusion, as if he was lost as to what to do. Then, his bored expression returned. “Let’s continue.”

She forced down her anxiety and nodded. “Let’s go over the barrier spirits…” She trailed off as she pulled out two boxes. “I have something priceless, even by galactic standards.”

The first box was the size of a suitcase, and it contained a thick stick that bent the air with wind pressure.

“I introduce Cyclops: a siege spirit that creates a vicious cyclone of wind blades around the user and their party. It’s both a defensive barrier and a devastating weapon, allowing the user to create a maelstrom of destruction as they walk.”

“Cyclops, huh…” Hayden mused with the hint of a smile. “Walking within the eye of the storm…”

Jeela smirked. “You like that?”

“I find it delightful.”

“So do I. Just be aware, its cora requirements are intensive. Even A-rank rifters struggle to maintain this for more than thirty seconds. No matter how efficient you are, there’s no way that you could maintain it for more than five seconds with your gate closed.”

“Oh, it’s not for me,” Hayden said. “And the clear spirit?”

Jeela pulled the box back defensively. “Do you know how much that cost?”

“These spirits will be used to protect my life,” Hayden said. “Who uses them is irrelevant.”

Jeela winced and pushed the box back. “Fair… Let’s continue.” She reluctantly opened the next box. “Here’s the Clear Barrier spirit. As with the Clear Line spirit, we have a large surplus of these. Should I worry about selling them?”

“Yes. These will soon become treasures. Stockpile them and my school will buy them.”

“Understood.” Jeela stared at him for a moment before pushing him a third box. “And lastly… a cora crystal. Can I ask what this is for?”

Hayden opened the box and looked at the orange crystal. It was the size of a dagger and as thick as a sword’s handle. He looked up. “An explosion.”

Her eyes widened. “Hayden, don’t tell me—”

“In the rift,” Hayden clarified. “There’s better tools outside the rift. Now…” He motioned his hand for her to continue, and ticked her off.”

Jeela dropped a bag onto the table. “Here are your elixirs. It doesn’t include your next core expansion elixir. Your request was… difficult.”

“That’s fine,” Hayden said. “I appreciate the effort.”

“If you appreciated me, you wouldn’t have hid major secrets.”

“I’ll trust you with the truth—once you trust me. I don’t have time to hear people debate and counter my every move. And that’s what you would have done if I told you the auction’s date, yes?”

Jeela laughed bitterly. “Of course I would have.”

“And there lies the problem. Now, please, place a little faith. I’m far more competent than you think.”

Jeela believed him—if only because she wanted to believe him. Her situation made her feel small and insignificant, a child amongst abusive adults who were on the verge of destroying each other—and taking her with them. There was a certain comfort in one of those adults being Hayden, though. He was capable—to what degree wasn’t known, but he was capable. He also exuded a contagious confidence that made her feel safe. It was strange, but she trusted him, so she said—

“I’ll assemble your team.”

—and left the room, giving him the bold confirmation of compliance he craved.

Hayden stared at the Seed of Purity catalyst when she left. Can there really be something better…? he wondered. Or is there something else within that rift?

He had been under the impression that the catalyst he needed was in the rift, but he was just handed a celestial treasure. The catalyst he was holding was at the ideal point between flexibility and purity on the catalyst quadrant, making it compatible with every spirit in the multiverse—with fine precision.

There was nothing greater—at least that he could know of.

It occurred to him that he had misinterpreted his situation. The Digital Universe was a boundless treasure trove, as the number of rifters and immortality-seekers was minuscule compared to Elysian, which was both vastly larger in terms of population and spirit-focused. It was conceivable that he could buy a multitude of celestial treasures for next to nothing—if not everything he desired.

The old god on Riaka’s shore must have known that—but pointed to the rift regardless. For a man who could manipulate time—something Hayden didn’t think was possible—to point to the rift, and go so far as to bear the costs of telling him, it had to be special.

Hayden felt excitement as he pocketed the Seed of Purity catalyst. Even if there were a better catalyst in the rift, it would be overkill—

The one Jeela gave him was enough to make him a god.

Kayline and Lina were practicing in the gym when Hayden entered, surprising them both.

“Hayden!” Lina said excitedly. “I didn’t think you were… What’s wrong?”

Hayden lugged a large bag into the room and heaved it onto their table. “I’ve brought gifts.” He extracted a neon orange elixir.

“What’s that?” she asked.

“A core expansion elixir,” he said. “It’s for Lina.”

“Wait what?” Kayline cried. “If she does that—”

“I have a meeting in a few hours, so please don’t waste my time.” Hayden handed the bottle to Lina. “Drink this. I’ll oversee the expansion.”

“Why do I need an expansion?” Lina asked. “I thought my flow-through was infinite.”

“It is. But there are other uses for your core, namely using the Clear Barrier spirit. It's disregarded as weak here, but with your coran capacity, it will have extraordinary power.”

Lina’s eyes twinkled. “Seriously?”

“Yes. Now drink.” He then handed the Cyclops box to Kayline. “I need you to assimilate this before you take your expansion elixir.”

Her eyes sparkled with excitement. “What is it?”

“Open it.”

Kayline stumbled back when she opened the box. The wind pressure exuded was twice that of the catalyst he gave them. She immediately shut it. “W-What the hell is that?” she asked.

“Something to protect you and your sister,” Hayden said.

“I could tell you that much! What’d this even cos—”

“A lot,” Hayden interrupted. “Now please. While I have exercised immense patience, it’s time to focus on your role. The wealth, energy, and rarity required to execute that role are none of your concern.” He turned to Lina, who had finished drinking the elixir and had sat down. “Stand up and start running.”

Lina tried to protest, but stopped herself. Then, she stood and began running laps.

“Sorry,” Kayline said, feeling guilty for doubting Hayden so much.

He shook his head. “You’ve done well. Just finish strong.”

She nodded resolutely. “Okay.”

Hayden summoned Gluttony to help stabilize Lina’s core. It had been a few minutes, and she was pouring sweat and stumbling—she needed the help. He then balanced Kayline’s core to facilitate the assimilation. Once she finished, she drank an expansion elixir and ran laps as well.

Hayden watched them with a strange glint in his eyes. They would soon be in grave danger—and he would need to protect them. He regretted having to enlist children—even if it were to their benefit—but the sisters were the difference between triumph and absolute defeat. So, he would do what he could to train and protect them well.
