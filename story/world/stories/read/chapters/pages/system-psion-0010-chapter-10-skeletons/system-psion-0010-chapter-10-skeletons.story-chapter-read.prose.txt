Some found himself face to face with walking stereotypes.

He quickly realised something was different the moment he saw the new group of undead in the distance. For one, they were notably not dark skinned, nor were they wrinkled.

In fact, he would go as far as to say they were the complete opposite of that. Not only were they white, they also had no skin. Samuel took in the skulls and empty eye sockets with a mix of horror and fascination.

Skeletons.

They were perhaps the quintessential undead race. When the notification mentioned the lich prince, he had expected the entire area to be filled with skeletons of all types. The sight of the revenants at first definitely had him second guessing himself. But now, he felt vindicated. Perhaps all those video games were not completely wrong. Though he knew better than to take that feeling too far.

Despite the differences in species, the skeletons did not seem notably smarter than their revenants, moving in largely similar patterns. Still, he approached cautiously. Perhaps they would have some kind of strange magic or another such nonsense he was not prepared for. He would not approach until he was sure. That way nothing would take him by surprise.

He studied them for some time, but unfortunately, there was no variation in their actions. They seem to just be walking randomly in some strange imitation of a patrol. A fairly ineffective one at least.

It only took a few minutes for him to get tired of waiting. There was not really anything more he could gain from watching. Other than simply wasting time. So it was better to simply strike and prepare for the worst. Though his mental energy had not completely recovered, the new stat points he put in psi were enough to make up for it. Plus, for some reason, it seemed like his regeneration rates had increased the more points he had. He was not sure about it at first, but after watching his reserves for some time. He was pretty certain.

It was still not high enough for him to stop worrying about resting between fights, but it was good enough. Enough that he did not have to wait an entire day anymore.

Samuel decided to go in strong, choosing not to use just a little amount of energy the first time. There were seven skeletons whose power levels he did not know, and he thought it would be fairly unwise to try testing them with only small amounts of energy without knowing what they could do. He had learnt during all his fights that day that just a few seconds of mistakes could cost him dearly. Which was why he hit the skeleton with enough force to smash in a revenant’s skull.

The amount of mental energy he used was enough to one shot any of the revenants he had faced earlier except the level 5 ones. Though even in their cases, it definitely brought them close to death.

That was why he was worried when the strike did nothing more than leave a small crack on its skull. His eyes widened at that.

This had just become dangerous.

Fear started bubbling up his chest, which he quickly shut down, swinging his stick once more. This time it was to the chest. Perhaps its ribs would be more brittle. He was proven right just a moment later as the rib he hit smashed into pieces. Unfortunately, the skeleton seemed completely unaffected by that. It was also notably faster than he was expecting.

The skeleton gripped his hand before he could stop it. And he felt his bones creaking from the force.

It was strong.

It's second hand pulled back and punched him in the face before he could stop it.

Thankfully, he did not think anything was broken. But he knew there would definitely be a nasty bruise. Of course, that was only a fleeting thought in his mind. He reacted instantly out of reflex, letting a burst of telekinesis strike the skeleton point blank in the face.

Unlike the last time when he had done this to a revenant, the skeleton did not die. Not for the first time, he wondered if he had bitten off more than he could chew.

He managed to dodge backwards. Then cursed himself when he realized his mistake. His first strike had already shown that the skeleton’s skull was exceptionally strong. Its ribs, however, were a lot less strong. Yet he had managed to target the strongest part instead of going after one of its weak points in his panic.

He took a deep breath, then met the skeleton once m	ore with his stick. This time he aimed at its chest. The ribs exploded outwards, shards of bone flying everywhere and leaving the skeleton unable to maintain its balance.

The moment it fell to the ground. He went after it once more, not letting it get any time to balance itself. This time he used smaller bursts of telekinesis. Smashing the spine as hard as he could. It took him nearly ten tries but eventually, its skull was separated from its body.

Samuel didn't have time to ponder what just happened, because the moment he was done with the first one, the other skeletons were on him.

A notification threatened to fill his vision, but he blinked it away instantly.

He was glad he only had to fight the first one alone, but it seemed like his luck was about to run out.

Unfortunately for them, Samuel was not about to go down. The past few fights had started reawakening long unused instincts within him. Instincts that let his ancestors become apex predators.

Skeleton met stick and telekinesis blew its ribcage to pieces. It fell to the ground, but he did not have time to finish it before having to fend off another one. Soon all six skeletons were on the ground, none of them dead, but all struggling to get up. He wasted no time, spending most of his reserves to sever their spines.

It was only when he was finally out of combat that he could finally catch a breath. As if realizing that, a host of notifications  came piling in all of a sudden.

You have slain [Skeletal Squire Level 6] x5.

You have gained a level. You are now Level 5.

Telekinesis has reached level 4.

Samuel sat on the ground, his mental energy running low as he looked at the pile of bones scattered around him. He went over the fight, cringing internally as he noted some obvious mistakes he had made. Not for the first time, things had gotten truly dangerous for him at some point.

He had no grand delusions about his physical combat skill. While he was neither unfit nor untrained, there was no denying that he was no peak specimen of physicality. His telekinesis had undeniably been the primary factor behind his survival so far, letting him simply brute force his way through his enemies by blasting them into smithereens.

It was a strategy that had kept him alive so far. Until now.

His battle with the skeletal squires, however, had thrown a big wrench in his relatively smooth sailing advancement. One that he suspected would only grow bigger the farther he progressed.

Facing five skeletal squires at his current level took came dangerously close to using up his mental energy reserves.

He was still confident that would be able to finish them if his reserves were full with little danger, but he doubted that would last. Eventually, there would be a group of enemies that would outlast him.

Samuel though about his current predicament long and hard, knowing he could not afford to continue without figuring out a way to fix his critical weakness. His first order of business, after identifying his broad objective, was to consider why he spent so much mental energy in the first place. Given how unrefined his control was, he knew there were certainly more efficient ways to handle his situation that would not leave him at risk of running dry on psi.

Unlike the revenants he had faced, the biggest issue with the skeletons was the inscreased durability. Whether that was because of their higher levels or something to do with their species, he was not sure. Perhaps it was both, but it did not matter much.

What he needed to do, was to find a way to get around that. He had managed to figure out the easiest way to take them out, which was seperating their spines. It was a rather obvious weakness, but even that took more mental energy than he was comfortable with spending in a single fight.

Thinking further about his actions, he tried to figure out what made seperating the spine so energy intesive. If he could figure out a way to reduce the amount of energy it took to sever spines, then he would find himself free from his problem and free to continue delving.

The answer hit him after a few minutes spent pondering fruitless solutions. He was thinking about the difference between the skeletons and the revenants when something hit him.

Revenants were made of rotting flesh, and from his observation, even their bones were not free from decay. That, he suspected, made them more prone to being smashed apart with blunt force.

To handle the much sturdier skeletons though, he would need to be more precise than the telekinetic equivalent of swinging a club. He needed to figure out an easier way to sever the spine. It was in the middle of that thought when the obvious solution came to him.

A sword.

After all, what better way was there to sever something than with a sharp blade? The more he thought about it, the more certain he became that this would be the answer to his problem. He already knew how effective bladed telekinesis could be, having seen it first hand against the snakes.

Looking back, he would definitely have struggled far more if he only had the stick available to him. Blunt force could only go so far when he wasn’t facing something with actually decaying bones.

Having finally figured out a way out of his current predicament, Samuel found himself faced with a very important question.

How exactly was he going to turn his mental energy into a blade with his subpar control?
