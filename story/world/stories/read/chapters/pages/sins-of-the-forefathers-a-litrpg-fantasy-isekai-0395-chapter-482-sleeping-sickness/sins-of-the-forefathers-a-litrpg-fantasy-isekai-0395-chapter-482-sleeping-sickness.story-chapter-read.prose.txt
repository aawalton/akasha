Consciousness was slow to return to me. All parts of my split mind felt like they were swimming through thick molasses as I slowly swam back to consciousness. The odd thing was…

I’m not even sure I was exaggerating with that word.

Swam.

The first thing I felt as awareness returned to me was that I was…sticky. Yes, that was the right word. My entire body felt like I had been dipped in an oddly sticky oil. It clung to every inch of my prone frame, as I lay upon something…something hard. The sensation of the warmth around me, sliding over my sticky body was…well, it was decidedly uncomfortable.

The second thing I felt was something nudging me. It was soft and insistent as it pushed itself up against my cheek over and over. The sensation was familiar, actually. I swore that I recognized that specific feeling.

…Fade?

Total awareness returned to me all at once, and my eyes popped open in a flash. A ragged, strangled breath tried to escape me, only for something to enter my lungs and send me into a coughing fit. I lurched into a sitting position as I tried to cough out what had slithered its way inside of me, as barely functioning eyes stared down at my palm in bewildered, half-dazed dread.

A puddle of blood and spit sat in my leather-clad, Primordium palm. It took several blinks of disbelief for me to realize that, no, actually, that wasn’t my blood.

It was the blood all around me.

I was sitting in a pool of blood. A literal pool of blood. The sticky crimson liquid had filled the entire room that I sat in, with a pool of the liquid nearly five inches deep. Moments ago, I had been lying unconscious in it, as best as I could tell. An equally blood-soaked Fade was standing over me, his huge lupine form towering over my sitting one, his head bent down to my level to stare at me in worry. As far as I could tell, the blood of the pool must have gotten into my mouth and lungs while I was out of it. It had certainly gotten into my ears, and wasn’t that a deeply unpleasant sensation.

I shook my head wildly, flinging blood everywhere from my sodden hair as I did, and knocked against both sides of my head to get it out. I’m not sure I succeeded, but I cleared some out.

Oddly enough, that was when I finally heard Fade’s mental calls to me, even though I didn’t even need my ears to hear that.

“Nate!” He cried, palpable relief in his voice. “You’re awake! I mean, I knew you would eventually but I wasn’t sure when and I was starting to get worried and I didn’t know what to-”

I held up a hand to stop him, coughing a few times more as I did. Fade stopped his rambling as I spoke in a hoarse voice. “I-I’m okay, bud,” I managed to stumble out. “What…what happened?”

Fade looked around uncertainly for a moment, and I followed his eyes. I withheld a strangled gasp at what I found.

Every last member of the battle group I had been traveling with was out cold around me, floating in the pool of blood.

Including Sylvia.

A strangled, panicked gasp escaped me as I struggled to my feet. It was more difficult than I’d been expecting, because the blood on the stone was incredibly slippery. I had to grab onto Fade’s pelt to get enough leverage, and when I was finally upright, I nearly tripped over my own feet in my haste to reach my lover. I slumped to my knees when I reached her, reaching out a trembling hand to her prone body.

Her ever-present leathers were ripped and torn in several places, some I knew from the battle through the Hold, some that looked new. Just the sight of her unconscious body, halfway floating in the blood around me, was enough to bring back the memory of an old anguish. There was another time in recent memory when both of us had been rendered unconscious, and I had awoken to her unconscious body after all.

Back in Elderwyck, when Rhazal had risen, and we had fallen from great heights. We’d both been injured from the fall, but her to a much greater extent than I. Sylvia had nearly died, and in some ways, she had died that day.

The old Sylvia, that is.

The one from before the amnesia.

My failures had cost her that continuance of self, and it had taken the Sculpted woman nearly a year and a Breakthrough before she was fully recovered. If that had happened again…if I lost Sylvia again to the consequences of my own godsdamned ineptitude…

I don’t know how I would cope with that. I’m not sure I could.

My breath came in short, sharp gasps as I reached out a trembling hand to slip under her golden, blood-stained hair, and lifted her head.

I didn’t blink as my movement caused the daughter of Greycton to stir from her own unconsciousness. Instead, I just kept a desperate gaze focused on her fluttering eyelids, as sapphire orbs graced the world once more.

I don’t think I would ever be fully capable of describing the relief that rolled over me, then, as those eyes settled on me…and lit up in recognition.

“Nathan…?” Sylvia breathed.

I lowered my head, and didn’t even care about the blood staining her lips as I captured them with my own. Sylvia was visibly and audibly confused by the impromptu kiss, judging by the muffled question I felt under my lips. Still, she eventually leaned into it, and I was unspeakably happy from the affirmation.

I smiled as I pulled away from her, gazing down at the Sculpted woman in my arms with visible relief…

And then Sylvia coughed a lungful of blood onto my face, from only a foot away.

My smile froze on my lips, as the blood dripped down my chin.

Ah. That’s right.

Sylvia’s last Breakthrough had given her lungs that weren’t entirely vestigial. It…made sense that she might have gotten some blood in them as well.

A sheepish, apologetic smile crept onto Sylvia’s lips and, almost involuntarily, both of us started to laugh. We leaned into each other, falling into a hug as we did.

If there was an element of hysteria in our laughter, I don’t think anyone would blame us.

Even as we pulled away from each other, I helped Sylvia to her feet. As I did, I looked around the room. As I’d noticed earlier, the rest of my battle-group was here in the blood-flooded room as well. Oddly enough, a head count revealed that it really was everyone lying in the blood. Nobody had been swept away…

Ah. That’s right. I had been wondering just what the Hell had knocked out all of us, apparently at once. It was only now that my worry for Sylvia had been assuaged that I remembered.

It had been shortly after we had heard and felt the impact of the initially falling spire onto the one where we had slain Rhogann. In moments, it had felt like the entire world had started to shake. Although we hadn’t seen it at the time, I think Rhogann’s spire had collapsed as well. Then, the chain reaction of two enormous edifices of stone falling apart like…

Honestly, for a moment there, I think there might have been some kind of chain reaction. I was positive that at least one more of the spires had collapsed. Maybe more, frankly. All I knew was that so much blood had come pouring into this spire that most of the lake must have been emptied from displacement.

I wasn’t even sure if this spire was still intact. For all I knew, the only remaining part of it was just this room.

The pressure and volume of blood that had poured through the entryway had been like a raging river. In moments, it had reached the ceiling, completely submerging us. Then, we had discovered something really distressing.

Somehow, the blood of the lake had some kind of…soporific effect. Up to this point, nobody, for obvious reasons, directly consumed any of the blood from the ominous lake. It had only been when we were literally over our heads in the liquid that we discovered that it would put you to sleep if you drank any, voluntarily or involuntarily.

In moments, we had been knocked out. All of us, even me. I had been sure that we were only moments away from being washed away by the flood and dashed against a wall further into the tower. Only…

We all looked fine. Everyone looked fine, like the blood tide had up and vanished after putting us all to sleep.

Wait.

“Why is everyone facing upwards?” I said aloud, baffled. Every single person in this room was face up, and not face down in the blood where they could have drowned. If the blood had a sleeping effect on it, it was entirely possible that breathing it in face down was a death sentence. You would just sleep through your own drowning. I’d been a bit worried about it, but…

“Ah,” Fade spoke up again, splashing his way over to me. “I did that. The blood never knocked me out.”

I turned my head sharply to look at my Familiar. “It didn’t knock you out?”

Fade shook his head. “Nope. I was still awake while everyone was snoring. Sena too. When the blood receded, I went around and flipped everyone over so they wouldn’t drown. Sena and I talked, and we decided I would stay behind and both guard everyone, and try and wake you up. Meanwhile, she’d go out there and try and make contact with the other battlegroups. Maybe try and find Bleddyn, Olag, or hells, even Rafael.”

I crossed my arms and frowned. While he’d been speaking, I’d been relaying his words to Sylvia. The Sculpted woman was just as confused as I was and joined me in frowning. “How long ago was this?”

Her response was a furry shrug. “Maybe about five minutes, honestly. You were only under as long as it took me to actually try and wake you up, Nate. The moment I touched you, you started stirring.”

I stilled as I understood what he was implying. “The blood doesn’t just have a soporific effect…”

“It has one to keep you sleeping unless you’re disturbed, as well,” Sylvia finished grimly.

I cast an eye out at our sleeping comrades. “Sneaky. Demented, downright malicious, and just a bit evil, but it’s also effective.” I heaved a sigh, unfolding my arms. “Alright. Even gabbing. We need to wake everyone up, and then decide what we’re doing next.”

“That’s going to depend heavily on what the situation is like outside,” Sylvia said quietly.

I nodded. What else needed to be said?

She was right. I didn’t want to think about it, but if we were instantly knocked out by that blood.

What had happened to everyone else?

…………………………………….

It took us maybe twenty minutes to fully rouse everyone in the battle-group, and assure them they weren’t either dead or dying. It took a bit to convince Renauld to not obsess over the potential side effects of consuming the blood, because if I was being honest, that worried me too.

I didn’t want to suddenly sprout-well.

I was going to say scales, but I already had some of those.

Instead, we’ll go with snake fangs, from nearly drowning in the blood of the serpent Calamity that had been pinned to the roof.

I say had…because it was only partially up there now.

As much as I wanted to venture deeper into the spire, find the nearest Rendarch, and have Fade extract some information for us, we needed to see what had become of the world outside the spire. The door had been blocked with a large piece of stone, almost completely blocking it off. That solved the mystery of just why we had been saved from being swept away, since it had blocked the flow after we had been knocked out.

How the Hell had Sena gotten out of here? When I asked Fade, he looked at me like I was stupid.

“She shrank.”

Eyeing the single tiny, rat-sized opening between the rock and the doorway, I understood.

And honestly, I was a bit embarrassed about. I forget she could do that sometimes.

To be fair, it had been a while since Sena and I had seen each other.

In the end, the rock ended up being a pain in the ass to move, and we needed to break it down using the warhammers of a few of the other warriors. It took me five minutes of wailing on the stone in my transformed state to shatter the rock, and when we did…

We stepped out into a transformed Karag Korvok.

Like I said.

The serpent had been nailed to the roof of the mountain before.

Now it was only suspended by the central spire, thrust straight through its heart.
