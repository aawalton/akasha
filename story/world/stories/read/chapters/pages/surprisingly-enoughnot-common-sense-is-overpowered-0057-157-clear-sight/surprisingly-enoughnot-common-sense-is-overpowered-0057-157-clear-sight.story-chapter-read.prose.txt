Ning walked into the Moon Dew Pavilion.

Every counter was crowded with disciples. Some clutched genuinely bruised arms, while others had minor scrapes but behaved as though they had barely survived mortal combat. Since the Moon Dew Pavilion functioned as both a pharmacy and a shop selling various cultivation materials, it inevitably descended into chaos after every round of the competition.

Ning slipped through the crowd using the Turtle Breathing Technique, blending into the flow of bodies as he made his way toward the inner counters.

There, he spotted someone familiar.

Si Sihua.

She wasn't standing so much as draped over the counter, half leaning against it with hollow eyes as though she were merely going through the motions of being alive.

When she noticed him, her eyelids lifted slightly.

"Oh, Junior Brother," Si Sihua said, raising her head a fraction.

Ning glanced around. "Senior Sister... you work here too?"

"I don't," she replied flatly. "I was forced."

"...Forced?"

She clicked her tongue irritably. "Yes. My aunt said that since the Mission Hall is nearly empty right now, I need to help her manage this place for a while. Something about me being 'experienced in these matters.'"

She even made air quotes, her disdain obvious.

"I was supposed to be watching the matches," she added bitterly. "Instead, I'm here explaining the difference between bruises and internal injuries to idiots."

Ning nodded sympathetically. "No wonder I didn't see you at the arena."

"Exactly," she grumbled. "You think I'd willingly miss the chaos?"

A disciple nearby tried to ask her about ointment ratios. Without even looking at him, Si Sihua grabbed a bottle and tossed it over, landing it perfectly in his hands.

"Next."

Despite her complaints, she was terrifyingly efficient. She identified injuries at a glance, cut off bargaining attempts before they could properly begin, and then resumed leaning against the counter as though none of it required any effort.

"And why are you here, Junior Brother?" She turned her attention back to Ning. "You don't look injured."

"I want to buy a second-tier spiritual liquid for my ocular technique. Is there anything suitable?"

"There are a few options," she said lazily. "Depends on how much you're willing to bleed."

She placed three bottles on the counter.

"This one is Moonfall Condensate. It's collected from high-altitude cliff basins during a full moon. Strong potency, but absorbing it is quite rough. It'll feel like someone's sanding your eyeballs."

She tapped the second bottle.

"Silver Veil Nectar. Extracted from night-blooming spirit flowers grown in mist valleys. Gentler than the first, but its effect is slightly weaker."

Then she tapped the third.

"This one's new. Moonlight Spirit Dew. Refined from Frostlake condensate and diluted lunar essence. Easy to absorb, minimal backlash... but more expensive."

She glanced at him.

"I recommend the third one."

Ning nodded. "Price?"

"Eight hundred spirit stones."

She said it casually.

Ning felt his heart twitch.

Eight hundred spirit stones was not a small amount.

Si Sihua watched his expression with mild amusement. She knew he had no backing and was usually short on spirit stones, so naturally, she wasn't going to let the opportunity pass.

"What? You look like you just lost your soul."

"I will after I buy it," Ning replied.

"Hm? I thought you'd bargain." She raised an eyebrow.

"I would with others," Ning said with a smile, "but Senior Sister is the one in charge."

"Hng! One hundred points. You really know what to say." She lazily waved a hand. "Fine. Give me seven hundred. Who told my aunt to force me here? I'll take revenge this way."

The last part carried genuine resentment.

Ning understood. As one of her friends, he knew Si Sihua's greatest ambition was simply to live leisurely. In a world obsessed with cultivation, struggle, and ambition, she wanted nothing more than to become a salted fish and float through life without unnecessary effort.

Simply put, she had the same dream as a middle-aged man forced to work overtime.

"Also..." Si Sihua suddenly perked up. "How did you earn so much? Junior Brother, teach me. If I can earn enough spirit stones myself, my aunt won't be able to threaten me by cutting my allowance."

"I just gambled on the Outer Sect Competition," Ning said.

Si Sihua stared at him.

"You what?"

"I placed a bet. It paid off."

"Are you trying to ruin yourself?" she demanded. "Don't you know gambling is dangerous?"

What followed was a surprisingly thorough lecture on the dangers of gambling from someone who had spent the last several minutes trying to avoid doing her actual job.

"It was a calculated decision," Ning said sheepishly once she finished.

"That's what everyone says before they lose their house."

"I don't own a house."

"...Fair."

She rubbed her temple. "Don't tell me you went all-in on some underdog. You wouldn't have won this much otherwise."

Ning's smile deepened slightly.

Si Sihua narrowed her eyes at him, clearly sensing that there was more to the story. She held his gaze for several seconds before eventually sighing.

Thinking too much was exhausting. She had already given him a warning, and that was more than enough effort for today.

"If you win big again, come treat me," she said lazily. "And if you lose everything, don't come crying to me."

Ning nodded seriously.

After all, could it really be called gambling if winning was guaranteed?

...

Ning had earned around 1,900 spirit stones from the bet.

After witnessing the strength of the other competitors, he knew he needed to improve. While Ning was generally quite protective of his spirit stones, he also understood that resources only had value when they could be transformed into strength.

For now, the fastest way to increase his power was to refine his Pure Eyes technique. Of everything he practiced, it was the only one he could realistically push through a major improvement within the limited time available.

Pure Eyes was a method of gradually transforming ordinary eyes into something extraordinary. Even its cultivation process was unusual, requiring the eyes to be repeatedly nourished with spiritual liquids and essences.

Fortunately, Ning had spent the past year building a solid foundation, regularly nourishing his eyes with spiritual rain liquid and occasionally using first-tier essences. His progress had been slow but steady, and now his eyes were finally ready for a qualitative transformation.

Ning uncorked the jade bottle, releasing a cool fragrance into the room. He tilted his head back and carefully allowed a single drop of Moonlight Spirit Dew to fall into each eye.

The effect was immediate.

A searing chill spread from the surface of his eyes and pierced inward. It wasn't exactly painful, but it was deeply uncomfortable.

Still, Ning endured it. Compared to those supposedly protective eyelashes that were meant to keep things out of his eyes yet somehow always ended up poking into them instead, this was at least doing something useful.

He began circulating his qi according to the Pure Eyes technique.

Spiritual energy gathered within the delicate meridians around his temples before flowing toward his eyes. The Moonlight Spirit Dew didn't clash with his qi; instead, the two gradually merged as the spiritual liquid was drawn deeper into the surrounding meridians.

Behind his closed eyelids, Ning could faintly perceive a silver glow.

The nourishing phase over the past year had gradually strengthened his ocular meridians. Now, the second-tier spiritual liquid was pushing that accumulated foundation further, refining the meridians and altering the way qi circulated through his eyes.

Tiny pulses spread from behind his eyes as the initial cold gradually gave way to a faint warmth. Ning continued circulating his qi, carefully guiding the remaining medicinal energy through the same route again and again.

Eventually, the resistance he had felt at the beginning disappeared. His qi completed another circulation without obstruction, and Ning immediately noticed that something had changed.

The transformation was complete.

Ning slowly opened his eyes.

"...This is too clear."

The difference was immediately obvious. The very quality of his vision had risen to an entirely different level.

The first stage of Pure Eyes was Clear Sight.

At this stage, Ning could perceive fluctuations in spiritual energy with far greater clarity, but that was only one part of the improvement. His depth perception, focus, and dynamic vision had all been enhanced.

Ning turned toward the window and looked outside.

The world seemed sharper.

Cultivators already possessed eyesight far beyond that of ordinary mortals. They could see clearly across great distances and follow movements that would appear as little more than blurs to normal people.

Now, Ning's vision was simply ridiculous.

He finally understood why characters always seemed to move differently after awakening some special eye ability. It was like going from 4K to 16K resolution. Everything was sharper, smoother, and easier to follow.

Curious, Ning turned toward the mirror.

His normally pale blue eyes had become a striking, gem-like blue, bright enough that they almost seemed unreal.

Ning stared at his reflection for a moment.

Not bad.

Unfortunately, after only a few minutes, the brilliance began to dim. The vivid blue gradually faded until his eyes returned to their normal appearance.

"Hm. It's good in every way... but it consumes qi quite rapidly."

He could feel the drain clearly. Maintaining Clear Sight required a constant supply of qi, and even with his refined control, the consumption was significant. He could use it during combat when necessary, but keeping it active indefinitely was out of the question for now.

Ning exhaled softly.

Alas, beauty had its price.

And this time, the price was spiritual energy.

....

The second round of the Outer Sect Competition shifted to one-on-one matches, with only twenty disciples remaining in the running.

Ning stepped onto the stone platform and looked across the scarred bluestone, only to find Lang Rulang waiting for him on the other side.

"I didn't expect you to be my first match," Ning said with a helpless sigh.

Not only was he stuck fighting in the opening bracket again, but his opponent was one of his own companions. Fortunately, this round allowed each contestant two losses before elimination, so even if one of them lost here, their chance of entering the inner sect wasn't over.

Lang Rulang stood upright with one hand resting lightly on the hilt of her sword.

"Same here." Her eyes briefly moved over his stance. "Don't hold back."

Ning blinked. "Hmm?"

"I'm just making sure you don't pull your punches now and then spend the next three months complaining that you could've won if you'd fought seriously."

Ning chuckled. "I won't." Then, remembering something, he smiled. "Turns out Zhang Feng was right about you. You're incredibly earnest."

Her brows twitched.

"That idiot. What nonsense has he been saying about me?"

"Just that you have an icy shell but a warm heart."

Lang Rulang clicked her tongue. "I'll deal with him after the competition. As for you, I'm going all out."

"Just what I wanted to hear."

The bronze gong rang, signaling the start of the match.

[Hidden Ice Mist]

Ning moved first, his fingers passing through a practiced sequence of hand seals as cold qi surged through his meridians.

Hidden Ice Mist spread rapidly across the arena, and within seconds, thick freezing mist had swallowed most of the bluestone platform, causing Lang Rulang's figure to disappear from sight.

Ning immediately retreated deeper into the mist and reached for the black bow on his back. Turtle Breathing suppressed his presence while Shadow Steps softened the sound of his movements, allowing him to circle around and create distance before drawing the string.

The first arrow tore through the mist.

Lang Rulang reacted quickly, but without a clear view of its approach, the arrow still sliced through her sleeve and left a shallow cut across her upper arm. She lowered her stance at once, trying to judge the direction from which it had come, but Ning had already moved.

His second arrow came from a slightly different angle, this time passing low enough to graze her thigh.

Lang Rulang immediately understood what he was doing and pulled out a yellow paper talisman.

The Wind-Repelling Talisman activated, releasing a concentrated burst of air that tore a narrow corridor through the surrounding mist and briefly exposed Ning's position.

By then, however, Ning had already shifted several steps to the side.

His third arrow came from the new angle and struck her bronze shoulder guard with a sharp clang, knocking her slightly off balance. Before she could fully recover, another arrow was already approaching.

Lang Rulang crushed a second talisman.

The Swift Step Talisman took effect, and her speed suddenly increased as she rushed through the thinning mist.

Ning's gaze remained fixed on her movements. Rather than aiming at where she was, he adjusted slightly ahead of her path and released.

The arrow flew directly toward her chest.

Lang Rulang shattered a third talisman mid-stride. For half a breath, the Mirror Veil distorted her outline, causing Ning's arrow to pass through the fading afterimage before striking the stone behind her.

Ning calmly reached for another arrow.

He had known from the beginning that fighting an accomplished talisman maker would be troublesome. Even with the competition limiting the number of talismans she could use, five different effects were more than enough to completely change the flow of a battle.

Lang Rulang continued closing the distance despite the cuts on her arm and thigh.

Another arrow came for her, but this time she raised a small bronze disc. The Iron Shell barrier formed just before the arrow struck, stopping the tip and forcing her back half a step.

Four talismans.

About fifteen meters remained between them.

Ning fired again, aiming low at her exposed ankle. Lang Rulang used her fifth talisman, and a sudden lateral burst from the Wind Escape Talisman threw her sideways, carrying her out of the arrow's path. The shaft still scraped across her calf, but by the time she landed, she had achieved exactly what she wanted.

She was close enough.

All five of her permitted talismans had been used, but the distance Ning had spent the entire fight maintaining was gone.

"Now I've got you."

Her sword swept upward.

Ning didn't bother reaching for another arrow. He slung the bow over his shoulder and pulled the spear from his back, bringing it down just in time to meet her blade.

Steel rang across the arena.

Lang Rulang immediately pressed forward. Her swordsmanship was precise and well-practiced, with each attack aimed at practical targets such as his wrists, elbows, throat, and the gaps in his defense.

[Four Sided Stability]

Ning kept his footing firm while using the shaft of his spear to redirect her attacks.

She slashed low toward his knees. Ning rotated the spear and caught her blade, lifting it away before she could complete the cut, but Lang Rulang immediately changed direction and thrust toward his throat.

Ning shifted his head just enough for the blade to pass beside his neck, then drove the butt of his spear toward her ribs. She abandoned the thrust and stepped back before the counterattack could land.

Ning followed without hesitation.

Their weapons collided several more times in quick succession. Lang Rulang was clearly the better swordsman at close range, but Ning had no intention of fighting only with his spear.

A patch of stone near her left foot suddenly heated.

Lang Rulang instinctively shifted right, only for her boot to land on a thin layer of frost left behind by the Hidden Ice Mist. Her footing slipped slightly before she recovered with a stomp, but Ning's spear was already coming for her.

She knocked it aside and immediately created distance, jumping several meters backward before raising her sword.

The air around the blade began to move.

Ning's expression grew more serious.

[Falling Gale Sequence.]

Wind gathered around Lang Rulang's sword.

It was her family's inherited Earth-grade martial art.

The first slash sent a compressed crescent of wind toward him. Ning raised his spear and blocked, but the force still pushed him backward.

The second strike followed almost immediately, carrying the remaining momentum of the first, and before Ning could fully recover, the third was already coming.

Lang Rulang continued without giving him any room to breathe, chaining the fourth and fifth strikes together as the pressure behind her sword steadily increased.

Ning quickly understood why the technique was called a sequence. Each attack carried the momentum of the previous one forward, allowing the force to build as long as Lang Rulang maintained her rhythm.

His arms began to ache under the repeated impacts. The shaft of his spear flexed dangerously, while stray edges of compressed wind slipped past his defense and left shallow cuts across his cheek and shoulders.

At this rate, simply blocking wasn't going to work.

[Pure Eyes: Clear Sight]

Ning activated Pure Eyes, and his pale blue eyes brightened as qi flowed into them. His perception immediately sharpened, making it easier to follow everything from the placement of Lang Rulang's feet to the minute changes in her shoulders and the flow of qi gathering around her sword.

More importantly, Ning could finally see what he was looking for.

Falling Gale Sequence depended on rhythm. Each strike had to connect smoothly into the next so that the accumulated momentum wouldn't disperse.

Ning didn't need to overpower the entire technique to defeat her. He had a plan.

He took a short step backward and activated Soil Refining.

The stone directly beneath Lang Rulang's front foot softened for barely an instant, causing her heel to sink less than half an inch.

Under normal circumstances, such a tiny change would have meant nothing, but in the middle of a technique where every movement depended on the previous one, it was enough to slightly disrupt her balance.

Her next slash came a fraction slower.

Ning immediately took the opening.

His spear shot forward.

[Falling Spear: Overturning the Dragon]

The iron spearhead struck the flat of Lang Rulang's blade just as she was gathering qi for the next attack, breaking the rhythm she had spent the previous strikes building. The wind surrounding her sword dispersed before the next strike could form.

Ning stepped forward and drove power from his legs through his waist and into the spear. The spearhead slid along her blade, forced her guard aside, and stopped an inch from her collarbone.

For a moment, neither of them moved.

Lang Rulang looked down at the spearhead before slowly lowering her sword. "...You broke my rhythm."

"Yes."

That had been Ning's plan from the beginning.

He knew perfectly well that trying to overpower an Earth-grade martial art with his current cultivation would be stupid. That was why he had spent spirit stones gathering information on the Lang family's martial arts before the competition.

Falling Gale Sequence became stronger the longer its chain continued. As long as Lang Rulang maintained her rhythm, every strike would make the next one harder to stop.

Ning only needed to create a small enough disturbance at the right moment and then take advantage of it.

Even then, his arms were aching from blocking the earlier strikes, and several of his meridians burned from the amount of qi he'd used in such a short period. His body refinement had allowed him to endure it, but he had no desire to find out what the next few strikes would have felt like.

The presiding elder's voice echoed across the arena.

"Winner: Ji Ning."

Lang Rulang swallowed a healing pill before looking around the arena. Frost still covered several patches of stone, scorch marks remained near where they had fought, and the small patch Ning had softened beneath her foot was slowly returning to normal.

Ning lowered his spear, still trying to decide what he was supposed to say after beating a friend, but Lang Rulang spoke before he could come up with anything.

"A fight has a winner and a loser. Today, I lost." She wiped a little soot from her cheek, apparently taking the result better than Ning had expected.

"You make it sound much easier than it was," Ning said. "I'm serious. If I'd let that sequence continue much longer, my spear might've actually broken. If I hadn't prepared the terrain beforehand, I probably wouldn't have found an opening to stop you."

Lang Rulang studied him for a moment before a trace of amusement entered her expression. "Are you trying to comfort me?"

"A little." Ning smiled. "Is it working?"

"Not particularly, but I appreciate the effort." She sheathed her sword and shook her head. "You really do know how to use that mouth of yours."

Ning placed a hand over his chest. "I only speak the truth."

"Of course you do." A small smile finally appeared on her face as she turned to leave. "Enjoy the win. Next time, I'm taking it back."

Ning slung his spear across his back. "Good. I'd be disappointed if you didn't try."

Lang Rulang walked off the platform, leaving Ning behind as the formations began resetting the arena for the next match.
