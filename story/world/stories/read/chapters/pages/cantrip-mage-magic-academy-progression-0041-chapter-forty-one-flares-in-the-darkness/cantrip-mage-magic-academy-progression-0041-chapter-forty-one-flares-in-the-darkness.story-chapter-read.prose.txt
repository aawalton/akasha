A Briefe Historie of the Alemic Tongue was a thick book. Its spine was nearly as wide as Eola’s palm, and Colin shot her a frustrated look when she picked it up and handed it to him. “What am I supposed to do with this?”

“Carry it? All I’ve got is my bandolier and spellbook,” Eola said. Patrice snorted, Colin tucked the book away, grumbling about its weight, and Eola breathed deeply, taking in the aftermath of the battle.

Roth was still down. Not hurt, but down. The Dalyn’s mantis blade had sliced through his flute perfectly, leaving a clean split with the grain, and he cradled the two halves in his hands as he continued to cry. Eola watched for a moment, struggling with herself. The instrument clearly mattered to Roth, but at the same time…it was uncomfortable. He was so rough and angry whenever he wasn’t playing or singing that Eola often forgot he felt anything else.

The Dalyn’s scythe-like arm lay maybe ten feet away, in a small pool of ichor. The blade was amazingly sharp—sharper than anything a living thing had grown had any right to be—but when Eola touched the arm behind it with her boot, it folded around her toe, and more green-black goop squirted out of the severed end.

Dalyn blades could be worth a pretty penny; according to Instructor Clearance, alchemists would occasionally buy them. But as Eola pondered the blade, Tagg put a hand on her shoulder. “Don’t bother, Miss Lemiene. That was a baby.”

“A baby?” Eola shuddered. Instructor Clearance hadn’t mentioned that.

The old librarian paused, then poked the scythe with the tip of his spear. “No, not a baby. Close to full-grown. Juvenile, though. Its blade’s not secreting oil yet. That’s what alchemists want.”

“Oh,” Eola said quietly. The Dalyn had towered over her, and the Davek had out-muscled Patrice.

Or had it?

She looked over at the tall girl, who stood smugly, leaning on her long hammer. Her helmet was off, and her face was flushed red as she breathed deep, quick breaths, but a look of smug triumph shone through the beads of sweat. “No matter what Colin says, I got it! It was already dying when he finished his ritual!”

Colin rolled his eyes. “If you say so, Patrice.” The burnt insect smell was…a lot. Patrice must have a pretty strong stomach if—

In the distance, a yellow orb rose over a floating section of the library.

“What does yellow mean?” Colin asked as he stared at the tiny Bright Ball.

“It means someone’s using last year’s colors.” Tagg’s weight shifted off his spear. He leaned forward, eyes locked on the Bright Ball. “Or it means…Lan, you ready for a fight?”

Instructor Tarik shifted his cloak, and for the first time, Eola caught a glimpse of his sword-belt. Or…Eola supposed it was a wand-belt. One silver-and-chromite stick hung from each hip, resting against the professor’s leather greaves. What was he going to do with two wands? No one could sketch two runes at once—could they? They’d never asked their professor about his own attunement; he might have something special. Eola stared, then tore her eyes away to watch the fading Bright Ball.

“Can you stay and watch them?” Tarik asked.

Colin shuddered. “You’re going to leave? You can’t leave us in here alone!”

“They can, and they will if they have to.” Patrice touched his shoulder. Her hand lingered for a while. “We’ll be fine.”

Tagg shook his head. “If it’s red, we’ll both go. Otherwise…it’s not just the students in danger, Lan.”

“I know.” Tarik went silent, and all five of them waited, watching the library sky as Roth slid his broken flute into his satchel and gently closed the flap. He didn’t watch for another flare, and no one tried to make him.

Eola wasn’t shocked when the red Bright Ball appeared a moment later, over the same floating island. She was surprised when Instructor Tarik swore, though. “Curse Y’aer! Apprentices, do not leave this room. We’ll be back in…fifteen minutes?”

Tagg nodded, looking grim.

“Fifteen minutes,” the professor continued. “Stay safe, stay quiet, and do not pick fights without us here. Use Roth’s magic if you have to.”

He drew his twin wands and, moving them together, sketched two identical runes. Eola’s eyes widened; he was casting multiple Second Order spells. A pair of blue-white wings erupted from his back as he finished; a second appeared on Tagg’s.

Then they stepped onto the long room’s railing and dropped away, just inches from where the Dalyn had retreated.

“I wish I could fly,” Patrice said longingly.

Roth glared at her. “Give it a try. You’ve got a better chance than any of us.”

“Har har. Make fun of my attunement more, why don’t you?”

Colin nodded. “That was pretty rude, Roth.”

The angry boy didn’t say anything else, much less apologize, and Eola watched as the two mages’ wings caught them and they soared toward the far-off platform. She was already counting down inside her head. Fourteen fifty-one. Fourteen fifty. Fourteen forty-nine. Meanwhile, the stink of burnt Davek wafted over the group, and she tried to ignore it.

The floating room with the red beacon drifted behind another, and Eola lost sight of Tarik and Tagg. The four apprentices could do nothing but sit and watch.

Fifteen minutes had never felt so long.

It took fifteen minutes, then a second fifteen minutes, before a new stairwell connected to the room’s arched entrance. It was a long spiral leading downward, and Eola’s eyes lit up when it clicked into place. “I think this is the way to our next book.”

“Shouldn’t we stay here and wait, though?” Colin asked.

Patrice shrugged. “It’s been over half an hour. Whatever’s going on, they’re both going to be there for a while. I bet it’s a fourth-floor monster breaking through or something. Either way, the test will end when it’s supposed to. I vote we keep going and find these books.”

“But…” Colin trailed off. Then he seemed to find his courage. “Did either of you know Master Tarik was that strong? He’s got to be an Archmage for sure.”

“He is,” Eola said absently.

That fact was the only reason Eola hadn’t already started her march down the spiral staircase toward the five-pointed, star-shaped room below. If whatever was happening was enough to keep Archmages and Truemages occupied, the smart thing to do was stay put. They’d come back. When they did, the test could continue.

She paced the shelves, hand on her sword, staring at the stairwell. It’d leave soon, and when it did, they’d be stuck here. Meanwhile, as Patrice had said, the test was still in progress, and they wouldn’t find the remaining three books by sitting around here. Time was running out. Colin and Roth had both refreshed their souls with ‘purified’ Mana, and so had Eola. Patrice, of course, hadn’t used any of hers; in fact, she didn’t even know how much she had, since she’d refused to even cast Mana Mark during her exams.

A decision had to be made, and Eola made it before it was made for them.

“Patrice is right.” Eola pointed at the stairs. “Let’s get moving.”

Colin moaned, glanced back over his shoulder as if the full mages were about to come soaring back. Patrice pumped her fist and hefted her long hammer over one shoulder.

And Roth took one more look at his damaged satchel and wrecked flute, sighed, and pushed himself up from the place he’d been sitting since the fight. “Fine. Our funerals.”

Eola ignored him, stepped onto the spiral staircase, and started working her way down, smallsword first. They were back on the hunt—and pushing into unknown territory.

As Eola led the other apprentices down the stairs, her focus was only half on the room below.

The rest was split three ways.

First, and most pressing, was whatever threat had called Instructor Tarik and Librarian Tagg away from them. Headmaster Tomlish had said that warning Bright Balls were a call for immediate help, and that anyone able to respond should as soon as possible. Were they supposed to go help, too? What could four first-year students with their list of attunements even do against a fourth-floor monster? Instructor Clearance hadn’t even bothered to teach more than the names and physical characteristics of the most common ones. According to her, if they encountered one, the best thing they could do was run.

Most of the rest of her thoughts, however, were on the spellbook attached to her bandolier, and on the incomplete Forsooth Script mark she’d been working on. It was a puzzle—and worse, it was one she felt she needed to solve. It was a feeling in the back of her skull, like if she couldn’t understand it, something was going to go horribly, horribly wrong.

The fact of the matter was that her First Order equivalents weren’t up to snuff. They were expensive, and while some—like Chromite Cloth—were powerful, her spell-chains had never reached even close to the same level of efficiency most other students could muster with Ordered Magic. She’d be better off sticking with her cantrips.

But that wasn’t an option. She needed something to even the odds, and for now, spell-chains were her only option.

And then there was Colin. Just…Colin. A small, but very noticeable, part of her mind was still stuck on him.

Eola was so distracted that she didn’t even notice the stairwell was about to move until it started shifting under her. “Everyone, down!” she yelled. Then she followed her own advice and started running.

She almost made it.

Instead, she found herself skidding to a stop, hand on the railing—which, thankfully, stayed solid. Patrice slammed into her back, and her arm jarred painfully. Luckily, Colin and Roth were a bit behind, so she didn’t have to hold everyone back.

Below her was a long, long drop into the lower floors.

“The whole stairwell’s moving,” Colin said. “They’re not supposed to do that, are they? They’re supposed to pivot, not disconnect from both sides at once.”

“Where are we going?” Patrice asked.

“I don’t think there are rules here,” Roth said, “at least not ones the library has to follow.”

As Eola peered into the distance, the other apprentices’ voices faded into a jumbled tangle of words. She didn’t bother trying to decipher them, and all her thoughts about both the full mages’ battle and her modifying mark fled her mind. She only focused on one thing; the floating room emerging from a cloud of shelves, and their likely landing spot.

It was one of the surprisingly rare multi-story rooms. This one included only the one stairwell arch, but an in-built flight of wide, tile steps connected its lower half to its upper. So, too, did half a dozen shelf ladders. The running fountain in the room’s center was, Eola thought, a bit much in terms of decor, but it was distinct. The fact that it was a dead-end was even more so. After a minute, the stair connected to the open arch, and Eola stepped off.

“Thank Y’aer for solid ground,” Colin said as he joined her.

Eola wasn’t so sure about that. The lower floor seemed safe enough, but she’d caught a glimpse of unweeded shelves all along the wooden upper floor. Worse, Atta’s fur was all up along her back, her tail had broomed out, and she stared at the tile steps. “Is this room in our notes? Does anyone know where we are?” Eola asked.

One by one, the other apprentices shook their heads. Colin pulled out the list and skimmed it, then folded it back up. “No, and no. This room’s not on our list. Wait it out?”

“Definitely,” Eola said. “We’re not alone.”

“Feral books?” Patrice asked.

“Yes. Let’s keep near the fountain. Fighting them is a waste of Mana.”

Patrice’s hammer head bounced against the tile with a loud crack as she sat down. “Alright.”

It was only a matter of a few minutes before another stairwell linked up, but in that time, Eola made a note in the crowded margins of her spellbook.

The room with the fountain has the worst weeding problem ever. Stairs linking to it: Spiral counterclockwise down.

Then they started a long climb back up a double-width staircase, climbing toward a room that didn’t match any of the shapes on their list of books for the Exam. Eola didn’t care. She’d seen the number of feral books in the fountain room, and right now, just about anywhere was better than another minute of watching for the signs of an attack.

So far, they’d avoided anything worse than a few bruises and some paper cuts, and she wanted to keep it that way.

The room it connected to was…strange.

Shelves lined the floor’s edge—or at least, most of it. One lay on the tile floor, overturned. But there were no books scattered on the floor. The other shelves were upright, but just like the downed one, they’d been stripped completely. Every single one of them was also covered in countless thin nicks, like razor-thin blades had been ripped across them. Whatever had done it, the library hadn’t healed the damage, or even been able to mitigate it.

“I’ve got a bad feeling about this one,” Roth said.

Eola ignored him. She sheathed her smallsword, slid her wand back into its spot on her bandolier, and jogged to the downed shelf. A handful of books—or at least their covers—had been trapped below it, but every page had been ripped out of them, leaving nothing behind.

As she searched the downed shelf, Patrice strode past her. “This shelf over here’s supposed to be ‘The Birth of Magic,’ but there’s not a single book here. What’s that supposed to mean? Did the librarians curate this section?”

“No,” Eola said quietly. Her heart pounded in her chest. She knew exactly where they were and what had happened here. This was the room she’d been in when the paper monster had attacked. She’d had to flee from it, bringing only Magick of the Dawn and Records of Lord Card’s Greenarbor with her. Those books had turned out to offer next to nothing she didn’t already know, but Atta had grabbed something much more useful. She’d swallowed a single page from a nondescript, brown leather notebook and had ‘gifted’ it to Eola after she’d recovered.

And the last time Eola had seen that notebook, the fallen shelf had been mid-collapse.

“Help me lift this,” she said, pointing at the shelf. If the notebook—if Varin’s childhood journal—was here, she could get started on her self-given quest to put it back together.

And if there was even one page in it…well, the one page that Atta had given her had been eye-opening enough. Eola couldn’t wait to read more of the Great Mage Varin’s apprenticehood thoughts.
