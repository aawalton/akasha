Caleb spent the rest of the day with Revin and the others, forced once more to head to the tavern to celebrate at the end of the night. He passed out in his father’s home, and in the morning headed back to the mill to await the iron delivery Dimel had promised. He’d given Caleb the meta steel ingot along with some cold steel as an advance on the delivery.

Caleb made efforts to show some level of exertion as he carried the bars out of town in a thick canvas sack, but once outside of view, he used his Imperium to aid in holding the metal up. On top of letting him use his willpower, adding 20 more stat points to aid in the lifting it also distributed the weight evenly across his whole body. Caleb found that lifting using only a fraction of his magical potential did wonders for the weight distribution alone.

At the mill, Bog greeted Caleb like an excited puppy, running back and forth in the grass around him and ripping up deep furrows with every step.

“Calm down! Calm down! I missed you too.”

Caleb had been more than a little worried that the dragon would stray away from the mill at some point during his long time away, but he was happy to see he had occupied himself by eating his way through the pile of scrap he’d once considered his prime stock. Looking at the remnants of what was left, it was not the loss it would have been.

Along with the eating came pooping. All over the yard—thankfully not in the mill—lay piles of dragon dung. Now with a more regular diet, it was like regular animal poo, but burned and filled with slag. Along with the poop were piles of cooled molten spit. It seemed that in Caleb’s absence the young dragon had gorged himself, eating far more than he had the ability to digest. Caleb quested out to these cooled piles and was pleasantly surprised to find that the metal within them had been refined to the highest-quality cold steel he’d ever given Bog.

“We are going to need to make some designated bathroom areas,” Caleb said, eyeing the mess.

Chastised by the comment, Bog got to work digging up the cooled metal vomit and dragging them into a pile. He refused to touch his poop.

***

Caleb spent some time getting the place in order before allowing himself to get lost in his work. Vaguely he planned to turn the mill into a home for himself and Kelia. For now it was a simple two-room affair with a forge in the main mill room and a cot set up in a small side room. Its fireplace had been a late addition by Caleb, as a fire in a mill would be a recipe for an explosion. He’d built the forge into the wall between the mill room and the bedroom, and had used the same chimney for both.

Once he’d cleaned the place up—except for Bog’s poops, he was going to make the dragon deal with those—he got to work planning out his week.

He had to make a full suit of armor for himself, plus some pieces for his brother and wife.

Elders. My wife. Caleb marvelled at his sudden drunken turn in fortune. Why does she even put up with me?

He would be the first to admit he could get distracted and neglect her, but she apparently thought it a feature more than a flaw, and would use that time to go off on her own, checking in on him to make sure he wasn’t neglecting eating.

Caleb pushed the introspection aside and decided to get to work. He had a lot to to work on, but he had one thing he wanted to forge first.

His real journeyman piece. He’d thought about it and decided he should have made a hammer out of the meta steel. Had his master not given him another piece, he would have reforged his blade into one—as much as destroying that blade would have hurt.

He needed a new hammer. He had the meta steel hammer from the cave, but that one had a low affinity. He hoped—though he had no idea how he’d get it—to work on magic steel. Judging by how much heat that blue steel took to smelt, he was going to need every benefit he could get his hands on.

So he got to work. This metal was in much better condition than the last piece, but still he folded the steel. This would be his number-one tool for years to come, and he’d not accept any flaws within. Aided fully by his magic, he was able to rapidly cut and fold the steel eight and then nine times in half the time it had taken him to do eight the just the other day.

Free from the eyes of others, he was able to work inside his Vortex. With the corrosive air pushed out and away from him, he didn’t need to worry about the air compromising his welds. With full access to his magic, he could hold the steel in place and guide his hammer swings to the perfect location using his Imperium. Each blow pushed the natural mana out of the piece until it was pure and empty, calling for an enchantment that Caleb resisted the urge to add.

When the shape was formed, he used Arcane Power Strike to punch a hole in the center of the block of steel. Then inspiration struck him, and he continued to shape the hammerhead, turning the back side into a long and wicked point that served no purpose in a forge.

The blade he’d crafted had been beautiful, but it really had no place in his life. As his master had always told him, “Use the proper tool for the job.”

Spears were for fighting beasts. Swords were for fighting men.

The only men Caleb was likely to face were the Frigidi, and a steel-aspected sword would do little against their ice armor. No, if he wanted to arm himself to fight them, he’d need to make a cold-aspected hammer, and in the meantime, this would work nearly as well.

With the head of the hammer finally shaped, Caleb moved over to the quench. He planned something risky.

Hammers needed to be hard. But hardness was a range. Blades were hard, but strike a blade with a hammer and the blade would bend. Blades couldn’t be too hard or they risked shattering. With the large block of steel that was a hammer, that was less of a concern, and a higher hardness could be sought. But higher hardnesses came with their own risks. The faster the metal cooled, the harder it became, but the greater the risk of flaws forming in the quench.

Water quenched faster than oil, but with it came the risk of a broken hammer. Things could be done to lessen the risk, and that wasn’t accounting for Caleb’s new abilities.

But what can be done to increase the reward?

He heated his hammerhead up to quenching temperature and then set it aside to cool to room temperature. He would repeat this process as many times as it took before attempting the quench to remove the internal stresses he felt within.

While he did that, he performed a test. He took a series of pieces of cold-aspected steel and heated them to quenching temp. While they heated, he filled up his trough with water and pulled the bag of magical cold-aspected flecks from his pouch, pouring it into the water.

Using his Imperium he forced the flecks to swirl around in the trough until it spun like a whirlpool, then plunged the first of the metal pieces in.

The metal chunk exploded just as the ice had in the cavern, sending water spraying everywhere. Instinctively, Caleb pushed the metal shards that flew at him away, and they flew to either side of him, harmlessly bouncing off the stone walls of the forge.

“That was more violent than expected,” he told Bog, who’d been watching with interest.

He tried again, this time using his Imperium to hold the metal together in the water. Once more it exploded, but not as violently as before.

On and on he continued his work, collecting the shards together and forging them back into billets as he ran out of stock. All the while he heated, cooled, and reheated the hammerhead, looking for the slightest flaw.

In the end, he had success when he reduced the amount of the magical cold steel—which he’d mentally dubbed quenching steel, finally having a good name for it. With two-thirds removed, he successfully quenched a hammer without it completely exploding.

Next, he used normalized cold steel, and found that he could quench this perfectly if he held it together with his mind.

To test out the method, he took the newly hardened block of steel and, using an Arcane Power Strike, brought a spare hammer down on it. The handle of the hammer shattered and a deafening ping rang out through the forge. Caleb became disoriented momentarily, and when he could focus again, found the hammer he’d swung had cracked down the center, while the piece below sat unharmed. His anvil too had sustained damage and there was a small dent in its top.

“Craters,” he cursed.

He hated repairing his anvil.

Finally satisfied he stood a good chance of not completely destroying his latest masterpiece, he pulled it out of the forge for the last time and plunged it into the water.

He brought all his will to bear as he fought against the internal forces working within the steel, forcing it to come together cleanly. The effort was harder than with the cold steel, but he had the willpower for it, and when he pulled out his spiked hammer, it was perfect. The watered steel pattern was beautiful across the surface, giving it the illusion that it had once been made of wood but was transmuted into steel.

He stared at it awestruck, and Bog eyed it greedily.

“It needs a handle,” he decided, and moved to his pile of wood before thinking better of it.

Why use a wood handle when a steel handle would be better?

He quickly got to work, forging a handle out of a piece of cold steel he had. He made it slightly longer than was appropriate for a working tool and refrained from quenching it, instead letting it normalize until all the internal stress was gone. Once it was done—and purged of its chaotic mana—he got to work fitting it to the head.

When he was done, he held a mix between a war hammer and a smithing hammer. The handle was unpolished black steel while the head was a mix of dark steel and bright white lines from the watering. The handle was a full sixty centimeters long, fifteen longer than the longest smithing hammer he’d ever used.

He placed the ultra hardened cold steel test piece on his anvil—after placing a block of steel beneath it to protect the surface—and swung.

The cold steel shattered with an explosion of sound, sending projectiles in all directions. Even with his Imperium held at the ready, Caleb was unable to deflect the shrapnel completely and his leather apron was peppered with metal.

As soon as they recovered from the shock of the sound, Bog ran all over the room, liking the scattered remains of the cold steel off the wall, anvil and ground.

Caleb rolled over onto his side in pain, grateful he’d worn his apron and chastising himself for his stupidity.

Despite it all, he was ecstatic with the result.
