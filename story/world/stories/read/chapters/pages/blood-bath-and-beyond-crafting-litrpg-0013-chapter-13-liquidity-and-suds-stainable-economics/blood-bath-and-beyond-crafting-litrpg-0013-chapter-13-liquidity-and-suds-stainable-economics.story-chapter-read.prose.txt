I limped up to the roof. Whatever poison was in that blasted toad’s tongue burned like acid.

“Look!” I declared, slithering across the tiles and thrusting the two blood stained mana crystals under the gargoyles’ snouts. Their eyes widened, nostrils flared, heads swivelling like something from the exorcist. Could they smell them?

I was surprised they did not wolf the crystals down immediately. They looked like a pair of very ugly stone dogs confronted with unexpected forbidden treats. Four stone eyeballs returned to me, the longing palpable. Definitely puppy dog eyes.

“Go on,” I said, encouragingly. “They are for you!”

There was no more need to convince. The crystals disappeared amid much crunching and cracking and swallowing. For a long moment both gargoyles returned to stillness, and I wondered if they had gone back to their gargoyley meditations.

Was that it? I let out a huff of disappointment and started to get up. I was in dire need of a wash. Again.

Wistful shuddered. Then Eggs.

Their eyes flashed pure, incandescent white, as bright as stars, then the light faded, gone, as soon as it had arrived.

“Thank you,” said Wistful, “I have no means to repay you for such a princely gift, but Madame [Witch], I am in your debt.” His voice was no longer stilted and clipped. He spoke with the urbane clarity I remembered from the tower wall.

Eggs whooped agreement. His back shook, his feet tapped. His bat-like wings sprang out from either side of his shoulder blades, and he crouched low, as if ready to pounce into the air. Wistful’s large, muscular arm shot out, holding him down.

“No, young one,” he said. “Save the mana for when it is needed.”

Eggs made a gravelly noise of complaint but settled down, hunching over, returning once more to absolute stillness. The nobbled arc of his back made him look particularly monstrous.

Their stillness was particularly uncanny after the burst of movement and I found myself wondering, what they would look like in a fight?

“I flyff,” Eggs mumbled. “Me flyfff good. Show Rachel.”

“She has seen you fly.”

“I have seen you fly,” I said. “You were excellent.”

The gargoyle’s ghastly lips tilted upwards.

“As I was saying,” said Wistful, not taking his massive stone paw off Eggs’ head, “Madame [Witch], I propose an alliance. A system formalisation of our already mutually beneficial arrangement.”

“Where I bring you mana, and you protect me and my base?”

“You have read my mind. What a delight! As I said, mutually beneficial.”

“Agreed,” I said. The gargoyle and I shook on it, ceremonially.

“And mefff,” said Eggs, from beneath Wistful’s paw. I patted his leg and we shook on it likewise.

Blue light flashed across my vision.

[The level 8 gargoyle known to you as ‘Wistful’ has become an ally.

The level 6 gargoyle known to you as ‘Eggs’ has become an ally.

Congratulations! Rachel’s Base has reached level 2! Base bonus increases by +1

Allies now have access to base bonus, and may share system information, if they choose to do so.Congratulations! Rachel’s Base has reached level 3! Base bonus increases by +1

Radius of base bonus increases by 5 feet from hearth. Base radius is now 55 feet.

All poultry and livestock within base radius receive resistance to mange, pox and infection.

Give Wistful and Eggs access to base bonus? Y/N?

*This allied status will persist until one of the parties chooses to break the alliance]

“Yes,” I said. “Give them access.”

I scanned over the notification again, taking it all in.

Nice! Ooh poultry. I could have chickens. That would make Eggs happy. And take care of my breakfasts, but first I needed to find chickens… or someone who could sell me chickens. And it looked like having allies helped level the base? That was interesting, I wonder how far I could push that? Of course, creatures I wanted to ally with didn’t grow on trees.

Wistful and Eggs seemed as happy as I was with the arrangement.

“Now that is settled, we need to talk,” said Wistful.

“Okay,” I said, a little startled. It seemed we both had many questions.

“How many mana crystals were in the dungeon?” he asked.

“A few more. But I’m not strong enough to break off any of the larger ones. And they are guarded by a giant level 12 toad.”

“Excellent, excellent,” he said, a little absently. “All in good time. This is good news for us all! Mana crystals grow where there is a great concentration of mana—”

“Door hot,” Eggs cut in.

My brow wrinkled. “I beg your pardon?”

“The portal creates a mana disturbance,” explained Wistful. “A concentration of magical energy. But the crystals take years, sometimes decades, to form. This is serendipitous indeed! The dungeon must have been hidden for a goodly while. If adventurers had been there recently, the crystals would have been harvested long ago.”

“The door did say something like that,” I said. Wistful’s bestial face and form were in such direct contrast to his tone it was startling. If I looked away, I could imagine I was talking to some slightly stuffy Oxford don in a tweed hat.

“Eggs?” said Eggs, accusingly.

I paused, thinking. “I haven’t seen any eggs yet in the dungeon,” I said after a moment, “although I am keeping my eyes peeled.”

“Hmph. In therff reallyyy good egg. Special egg. Biiiig egg.”

“I’ll look for it.”

“And just because I get the impression you don’t fully understand the value of your gift,” said Wistful, “let me explain. You could have sold those crystals for at least a gold noble in any decent sized city. As it is, each one had more mana than my compatriot and I could generate in half a year of solitary meditation at our current levels. Mana crystals have many purposes amongst both mages and those rich enough to use their services.”

“Not a lot to buy out here in the woods,” I said, with a shrug. “But that’s good to know. It would be nice to be rich. Assuming that a gold noble is riches?”

“Two gold nobles would be a peasant’s wages for a year.”

“Okay - and how much is, I don’t know - basic food?”

“Eggs!”

“It has been a while since we visited any civilisation of note, perched as we were on the late enchantress’s castle ramparts, but even the wicked need to eat. Last I heard, a penny would be adequate for a dozen eggs. A loaf would be half a penny, maybe a penny and a quarter. A side of bacon would be around 15 pennies.”

“How many pennies in a gold noble?”

“240 pennies, or 20 shillings in a gold noble.”

“Oh, okay, wow.”

“10 shillings in a rose gold noble.”

“Huh…”

“A saint is 8 shillings and 6 pence and a florin is-”

“A penny for a dozen eggs,” I interrupted. “I’ll remember.”

Eggs nodded in approval.

This was good information to have before I interacted with what I was going to optimistically call ‘civilisation’. It was important that I didn’t stand out, not if I wanted to stay hidden from the other archetypes. I also needed to know how much was reasonable to charge for my soaps. Once I made them, and found a town to sell them in…

“Speaking of cities – how far are we from the nearest?”

“Lughnanas is about four hundred miles away, give or take, depending on which route you go. That’s the closest city.”

“And the nearest town? Settlement? Or village, or town, or camp, or… monastery, whatever. Town would be best, I think. But which way are the people? I want to make soap to sell, but yeah, no point if there’s no one to sell it to.” I would have to reassess my plan to stay here if I had to travel four hundred miles to find customers.

“There are one or two towns and villages to the east, about eighty miles, then of course, the enchantress’s keep about fifty miles away, as the gargoyle flies, although it would probably no longer qualify as a ‘community’ in the strictest sense. Whether or not you would care to mingle with the remaining inhabitants is questionable. It is likely a moot point. I doubt anyone there is left alive. Or much of the keep left standing.”

“There were humans there?” I asked, feeling a bit weird as the memory of that crumbling tower flashed across my mind once more.

“In a manner of speaking. I feel the very nature of the concept of ‘humanity’ bears a closer examination in the face of this question, one which we should examine most—”

“Barrwff,” said Eggs.

“Barrwff?” I repeated.

“Ah yes, Barrow-in-the-Mist, an arguably charming but rather rustic settlement of a few hundred souls. Some call it fair, others call it a carbuncle upon the face of the forest. Somewhere between village and town. About thirty or forty miles west. Might that do for your needs, Madame [Witch]?”

“Rachel,” I murmured. “Call me Rachel, please.”

“I fffly Rachel!” declared Eggs, his clawed paws tapping again.

“If the mana crystals are as precious as Wistful says, I think it would be better for you to save your energy,” I said. “At least until I am strong enough to prise off the rest. I can walk. I think. With these wonderful new legs!” I brandished my shapely, but bruised calf muscles at the pair. They did not seem particularly impressed, but then some of their muscles were the size of watermelons.

“It will be good knowing you are looking after things here.” I thought about everything for a while, all three of us looked out over the forest. “If you don’t mind me asking,” I said. “Why is it you—” I looked at Wistful who had finally taken his paw off Egg’s head, “why are you so much more…” I searched for a none-insulting word, “…eloquent than Eggs?”

“Hey! I talk ver good,” said Eggs.

“My friend is young,” said Wistful. “But most importantly his life so far has been spent in the company of the elements and other gargoyles. Opportunities to develop human language skills were limited. However. Do not underestimate him. His powers of mana manipulation are advanced for his years. I, on the other hand spent my youth on the library spire, where I was privy to many intriguing conversations. Before the old enchantress descended into complete madness, of course.”

“Ah,” I said. “Well, fortunate for me.”

“Indeed.”

I quizzed Wistful for a while and discovered that he knew a great deal about some things (the cost of maintaining a moderate sized castle, the lineage of war horses, the nesting habits of eagles, how to mix oil paints, how to summon demons via human sacrifice, or how to tile a roof) but nothing at all about others (chicken raising, farming, soap making or witching in general). Still, it was wonderful to have any information at all.

The next day I tried myself against the toad again but soon decided I needed to level up elsewhere before facing it. My strategy of hopping in and out of the portal did not work as well, the horrible, warty thing’s [Charm] effect was devastating and its blasted poisonous tongue regenerated between fights.

The encounters left me bloody and bleeding, and I did not see myself making measurable progress flinging myself at the slimy git. I decided to forgo the fight till I was stronger, but that also meant the crystals were off limits. The remaining crystals were much larger than the ones I had stolen, and I needed to increase my strength once again before I could get at them, even without the toad.

Sitting on the edge of the roof, as had become my habit, I watched the sun set and the moons rise and pondered my next moves. Out in the forest came the now familiar half scream half whinny of the insane unicorn, reminding me of another task before me.

The gargoyles could help me kill it, but my gut told me - toad first. I could at least see the toad’s level. Also, as I glanced down at the mottled purple and yellow of well... most of me… my collection of wounds was growing. I had acquired a limp, and the wound on my arm still throbbed. I was still functional – (and I was convinced I was healing at a faster rate than I would have on Earth) but slowly and surely, I was becoming increasingly injured.

Tutting I peered down at my body. Now I was looking closely I was a bit worried I was going to be left with a ton of scarring. A break from fighting didn’t mean I had nothing to do.

Plus, I was not here to be some asshole fancy pants martial artist edge lord. No - I was here to make soap. Magical soap. Soap making levelled my class, right? I did not have to get so dirty. Fighting monsters was invigorating, but I needed to focus on why I was here. Where my true witchy power lay.

Time to figure out soap magic.
