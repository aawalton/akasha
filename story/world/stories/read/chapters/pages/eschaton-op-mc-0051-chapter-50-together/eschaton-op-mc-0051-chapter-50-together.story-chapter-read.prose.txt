I step off the cargo truck and walk up to the Lieutenant in command of the harvesting team. He seems shocked to see me and quickly gives me a salute, which I wave down.

“Machina! Uh, sir, what are you doing here?”

“I’m your reinforcements. Do you still need them, Lieutenant Carlson?”

He nods rapidly. “Yes sir! But why are you here, sir? There’s got to be stronger Fractures than this one.” He gives the Fracture a nervous look. “I’m sure we’d be fine…”

“The other teams are overloaded, and I’m always willing to help out where and when I can.”

“Well, we’re happy to have you, sir. Really. All of us on the ground feel a whole lot better with you around. Feel free to do what you think is best. I won’t try to tell you what to do, sir.”

“I’ll take point, in that case.”

I lead the way into the Fracture, trucks rumbling behind me.

I hold the growling, rabid metal wolf by the neck. The Junker Wolf is the size of a car. It looks like cobbled together out of parts found in a junk yard. Rusty steel jaws look like they’ve been covered in dried blood.

I squeeze, crushing its neck into scrap metal. I toss it behind me lazily, and half a dozen harvesters jump on it. Even the metal it’s made of is magical, making it extremely valuable, even as rusty as it is. The saws the Typicals use have blades made from magical steel, it’s the only way Typicals can realistically harvest magical materials. Mundane sawblades wielded by them have a tendency to explode otherwise. Sparks fly as they carve up the slain monster.

“Goddamn, sir. The news doesn’t do you justice,” Carlson marvels.

“I try my best.”

Another Junker wolf pounces from behind a pile of wrecked cars. The entire Fracture is a giant junkyard. Towers of crushed, rusty cars reach into the sky.

I spike the Junker Wolf into the ground. It hammers a crater into the ground, and doesn’t move again. Tiny pieces of steel bounce off my armor, but nothing reaches the Typicals behind. I flattened it like a junkyard car crusher. The Lieutenant peers around me with a look of awe. He glances at the crater the Junker Wolf lies in before giving his service rifle a disgusted look.

“Do you even need me here?” The singular 2-Star, Lieutenant Cooper, huffs.

“Of course,” I tell her. “I can’t be everywhere. I promise, I’m as glad to have you here as you are me.”

“I doubt that,” she mutters. “You’re fucking Machina, I’m just some punk 2-Star.”

“Everyone is important. Everyone! Do you think we’ll get through this if we don’t work together? It doesn’t matter what I’m capable of. Together we can move mountains! Humanity has done it before without magic. We can do it again. We will do it again, and as many times as it takes.”

She straightens, and hefts her mace onto her shoulder.

“Okay. Together,” she says.

“Come on, there’s a clearing up ahead. Let’s clear this place out. I’ll draw them out, and we’ll put them down.” I say.

The Lieutenant rounds up the combat platoon, and we push ahead into the football field sized clearing. It’s a parking lot with a dozen tow trucks in various states of disrepair.

Come and get me.

I flare out my Aura, an esoteric scream ringing out. Carlson and the harvesting team shivers as it washes over them. As Typicals, their ability to sense Auras is lacking at best, and non-existent at worst, though the sheer power I wield is more than enough. Every day my Aura grows a little stronger. Cooper is staggered by it, and I internally wince.

Hotter, faster, more. Never cooling, never slowing, never lessening.

The power scares me. But it scares me less than the thought of failure.

The scampering of metal claws on dirt and scrap metal echoes from all around us.

“Into the center, come on!” Carlson orders.

A pack of Junker Wolves, dozens strong, pour into the parking lot. The Bradley lets loose with a chunk-chunk-chunk, and it blows two into scrap. Magically durable metal or not, 25mm High Explosive rounds are full of serious hate.

I put the new skills Matt has drilled into me to use. My sword comes up in a smooth arc that zips through the air. I cut a wolf in half. I kick the remains into another, staggering it. Cooper charges into it and crushes its skull with her mace.

I catch a wolf that leaps for her, and smash it into the ground. A boot stomps it flat, and it dies with a squeal of pained metal. The Bradley scraps another Junker Wolf with its rhythmic hammering. I step forward, and chop downwards, my sword carving through another wolf.

I catch a wolf by its scruff mid leap, and toss it in front of the Bradley’s main gun. Accounting for the average human reaction time, I give the order.

“Fire!”

The wolf is blown apart by the second and third rounds mid-flight. The infantry erupts with hollers and roars of defiance. A wolf leaps onto the Bradley and starts to gnaw on it with metal teeth, sending sparks flying.

A soldier pulls a grenade off his chest, yanks the pin out, and stuffs it into the monster’s chest cavity. He dives away, and the grenade goes off. It blows the wolf in pieces, throwing metal shrapnel all over the parking lot.

The monsters’ howls and screams fill the air as we slaughter them all.

Never stood a chance.

The harvesting hauls what’s left of the pack of Junker Wolves away. Trucks, filled with magical scrap metal, rumble off for the Fracture’s entrance. Cooper is sitting against the Bradley, resting. She devours a pair of MREs in rapid succession.

“What?” She says to a couple of Typical soldiers. “Magic takes a lot of calories, alright?”

Carlson pulls his helmet off and wipes his brow. The entire combat platoon is exhausted, but mostly unharmed. A few cuts and bruises are the limits of the injuries, caused by shrapnel.

“Whew. That was something. 3rd Platoon, back on your feet! Come on, come on!”

They all stand up with a groan, and Cooper tosses aside her third empty MRE tray.

“If you wanna sleep in a bed tonight, let’s start scouting. Let’s find the good stuff!”

He then turns to me. “Sir, you’ve got a bit more work to do, I think. We’ve got this covered.”

Off for the boss.

I step into another clearing, nearly sixty feet across. Along the opposite side is a huge car crusher, though it lies inactive. To my left is a crane, with a heavy duty claw on the end.

The boss stands up from lounging on a crushed car. It’s a humanoid, looking like a metal bomb suit. It quickly lights a huge blowtorch fed by two backpack tanks. A twenty foot long blue flame roars out of the nozzle before it settles into something about half as long, but thin as a knife. Four piles of scrap stand up and shake themselves off. The Junker Wolves growl like a revving engine.

I could steamroll this boss in an instant. Not even the addition of the Junker Wolves would slow me down. But I’m not in blitz mode anymore. Saving the world isn’t a rush to the finish line, it’s a marathon. Rapid-fire destruction won’t save us, only understanding Fractures and monsters will. Time for a little experimentation.

The Junker Knight makes a few hand movements to the pack of wolves, and they slowly circle around me, outside the reach of my sword.

It’s intelligent? Is it… alive?

Flashes of my doppelganger from the Amiens Fracture flicker through my mind.

“I don’t suppose you’re alive, are you?”

It doesn’t acknowledge my words, continuing to order the wolves around.

“I suppose it wouldn’t matter if you were, though.”

Every monster I’ve come across, excluding this Junker Knight and the Amiens Fracture have been nothing but animalistic. They acted on instincts and hunger, not calculating intelligence. I hate monsters, true, but they are what they are.

If something was intelligent enough to understand me, to respond to me, and still continue on its murderous path? Well, that’d be worse. They’d be monsters in the truest sense.

I force back a wolf that snaps forward with a lunge. It quickly scrambles backwards, but not for free. Its hasty aggression costs it an eye, and it howls in pain. It sounds like an overheated engine, whistling and chugging in agony.

The Junker Knight’s hand movements become more intense, and it’s clearly annoyed by the wolf’s preemptive attack. I brandish my blade, continually backing up. Letting them get behind me would be a lethal mistake— for someone else, at any rate. But it’s good practice in any case, and others wouldn’t fight like me. I want to hold back for this fight. It’ll help me get a better idea of how others might perform, or what they’ll need to do to win.

The monsters stop advancing, ordered by the Junker Knight. It looks up for a moment and snaps its fingers.

The crane roars to life, and the claw above me drops down, spread wide.

They backed me into it!

I lunge to the side, straight into a leaping wolf. I cut it in half longways. My blade’s tip whizzes through air, slinging transmission fluid and oil over the clearing. Two more wolves lunge for me, maws wide. Rusty metal teeth as long as a man’s forearm form a bloody grin.

The footwork Matt taught me lets me weave around them. Even after slowing myself down—slow and steady—I’m still able to dodge them. One misses entirely, landing on the ground and whipping around for another attack. Just a moment before I slaughter the second one, a gout of white-hot flame cuts through the air between us.

I jump backwards, towards the wolf that missed. Normally I’d power through the blowtorch. Whatever the Junker Knight has wouldn’t even scratch me, but this is an experiment. Not a battle.

The wolf behind me clamps its jaws around my leg, and tries to pull me to the ground. I grip it by the neck and rip it off. It refuses to let go, and its jaws are ripped apart as they give way first.

Oil pours from the injuries, and it howls in pain. I hurl the monster at the wolf that lost an eye. They collide in the air with a boom. Metal parts and rent steel goes flying. Before I can follow up, the Junker Knight forces me back with its blowtorch again.

The razor thin blade of flame is about as long as my sword, around twelve feet. For the first time, I don’t have the range advantage.

The Junker Knight seems content to let the wolves take the brunt of the fight. It stands back, only attacking to keep me from following up on the wolves when they make mistakes or are at risk.

Maybe it has limited fuel?

If it does have fuel, I’m not sure I want to run it dry. It’d be magical like everything else found in a Fracture, and I’m curious what Morgan could do with magical fuel.

The last undamaged Junker Wolf growls at me from behind the Junker Knight. The blowtorch sputters to life, and another blade of flame stabs out. The Junker Wolf jumps through the white-hot flame, and its metal skin quickly glowing with heat. I sway to the side, and cut it half.

I grab a piece and throw it at the Junker Knight. It’s too slow to dodge the makeshift projectile, its bulk making it clumsy. It goes sprawling to the ground. Before it can scramble to its feet, I charge forward and plunge my sword through its head, careful to avoid the gas tanks on its back. It twitches once before going still.

I carefully remove the tanks. Despite not being able to see through the metal casing, I can still feel the sloshing liquid inside. With that, I can estimate how full it is.

Mostly full.

Assuming the tanks started completely filled, noting how long it held down the trigger, and how full it is now, I can calculate how long it can attack before the tank goes empty.

Two minutes.

I store the canisters away, happy to note I can store liquids. At least when they’re contained in something else, at any rate.

I wonder if I could just scoop up a lake?

Cooper and a squad of harvesters enter into the clearing. She surveys the battlefield, quickly directing them to points of interest. I pull the two Fractal Shards from the reward orb, and toss them to her.

“2.3 Amp, best guess.”

“You sure you don’t want them? Fracture salvage laws means whoever clears it gets the goods,” she says.

“We cleared this together, remember? Just because I killed the boss doesn’t mean I did it on my own,” I remind her.

“Right, yeah. Well, thank you.”

A flurry of gun shots thunder out, and both of us snap to a twitchy Guardsman. He quickly swaps out the mags, still looking at one of the Junker Wolves.

“I-i-it moved! I swear!”

A dozen smoking dents litter its hull, and both myself and Cooper chuckle. It was the one I ripped off a leg off of and threw into another one. What remains of its jaws reside in its ribcage.

“Well, I’m off. Things to do.”
