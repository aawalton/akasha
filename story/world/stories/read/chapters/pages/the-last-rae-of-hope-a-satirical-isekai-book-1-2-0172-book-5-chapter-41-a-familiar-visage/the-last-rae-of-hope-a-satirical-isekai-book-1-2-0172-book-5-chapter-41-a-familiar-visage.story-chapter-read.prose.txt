I’d better see what Tweedledee and Tweedledumb are saying to Nora.

“After this, I can schedule some time for bed, right?”

The hallway I found myself in wasn’t one I had been in before. Mirrored portals lined both walls at irregular intervals, none of them showing anything that would make them distinct from one another. Chester, however, seemed confident he knew where he was going, so I didn’t question him.

I wasn’t prepared for him to stop in the middle.

“What’s going on—ugh?!”

A noxious smell filled the room, so very pungent it burned my eyes.

“What is that?!”

Chester walked over to a nearby mirror, raised one paw, and pressed it flat against its surface, revealing the chaos behind it. Nora was there, flanked by a very pleased Bodhil and Beigaldi.

Bodhil gestured off to the other side of the room. “Our General’s plan, of course, is the most efficient—”

“Kokraskaarta! Skraakshna! Skrraaaaaaaaaaaaaakkk!”

They didn’t!

I burst through the door, finding the smell was considerably worse on the other side.

“I had nothing to do with this!” Nora said immediately.

“I know,” I said, turning to Bodhil and Beigaldi. “No possession, of anyone, ever! Not even Skreethi!”

They gave each other a smug look, then feigned chastisement.

“Of course,” Bodhil said.

Beigaldi nodded. “Naturally.”

Then they both left, abandoning all responsibility for the chaos they caused.

Then I turned to face the Skreethi Matriarch herself, bandit eyes and all. “I am so, so sorry for whatever—”

“Krestakkok!”

Oh, I’m sure she called us that one before.

“Ma’am…” I started, my hands gesturing wildly. “I—”

She drew herself up to full height, her tail thumping dangerously.

Nora’s eyes widened before she grabbed her staff, striking the floor twice in succession before it started to glow. “We can talk about this, right?”

The Matriarch paused, then pointed at me with a sharp claw. “Awkward One,” she addressed me, her muzzle slightly out of sync with the words Nora’s spell shared with us. “I was removed from my burrow in the middle of the night, transported without permission through a series of disorienting pools of water, and deposited in this hallway without so much as an explanation! This is not how one solicits the attention of the Empress of the Skreethi!” She paused, the hackles on her back relaxing. “However, as you have contributed meaningfully to our community in the past, I will hear your plea. What favor do you seek of mine?”

She grimaced. “Let me explain—to both of you.”

The Matriarch eyed Nora, her sharp teeth catching the light. “I was summoned on behalf of your servant?”

“Well, at least there’s that,” she said with a mysterious sniff. “Go on then.”

“What’s Chairo?”

Nora groaned, but then the Matriarch laughed, a loud, barking sound. “I know what Chairo is. I like to see you uncomfortable.” She glanced at me. “You must work to be less uncomfortable. Your heart rate is too fast.”

“What is the Purpose of gaining entry to the City of Many Humans? My ancestors did not find it of any value. The scraps of food were tempting, but they were too much work to obtain.”

Right. Birds weren’t inhibited by it!

“We need some Skreethi to put some specially sealed black stones around one of the holy crystals—I mean—”

They can get past the barrier. In the sewers, they’d be mistaken for giant rats! Well… Maybe some of the smaller ones, anyway. And planting ereborite along the holy crystals is genius!

But the people inside will panic the moment the holy barrier drops…

Oh yeah, it’s all coming together!

She remembered the drawing in Nora's journal?

It was finally, finally time for bed. The bastion didn’t necessarily show me what time of day it really was, but I absolutely needed sleep. Even the hairs on my toes (yes, it’s normal to have hair there, even as a woman) screamed for it.

I had just gotten comfortable when there was a knock at my door.

“May I come in?” Olethros asked.

“Anytime,” I replied, sitting up.

He appeared in the room like he always did, looking at my beanbag chair. I leaned over and fluffed it out before plopping onto one side of it. “Do you want to talk?” I asked, patting the free half.

He shrugged, the casualness of it exaggerated to the point I knew it was calculated. “I had a conversation with my brother,” he explained, sitting down. “We reached a temporary accord. That’s all.”

Before I could respond, his head fell heavily on my shoulder. His arms came next, gingerly at first, then tightening around me. There they stayed, clutching as if I might try to escape.

I hugged him back in silence.

It couldn’t have been easy. Years of anger and misunderstanding don’t resolve with a single reveal, and there was no way they could work through everything in the few hours that had just passed.

He wouldn’t tell me this, of course, and I wouldn’t press him about it, either.

After a while, Olethros exhaled deeply.

“I don’t know what will come after,” he admitted, relaxing his grip a little.

“Me neither,” I replied.

He settled in closer, put his head under my chin, and fell silent once more.

This... isn't too far yet, right? I'm not letting him get too far ahead of that talk I eventually have to have?

Eventually, he murmured, “Show him to me, please.”

“Your brother?” I asked, confused.

He sat up. “My father. What he looked like—before—”

I waited.

Oh.

And to borrow a phrase from your vernacular, I intend to become my father’s most unreasonable facsimile.”
