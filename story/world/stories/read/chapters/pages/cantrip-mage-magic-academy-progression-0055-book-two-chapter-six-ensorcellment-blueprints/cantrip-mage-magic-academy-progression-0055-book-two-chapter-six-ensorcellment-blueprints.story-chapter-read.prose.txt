By the end of the term’s first week, Eola’s muscles had transformed from sore to numb.

That wasn’t a blessing, though.

Maestro Yarrowbloom had been going easy on them in Magical Dueling the previous term, and his two days a week of Spellsword Tactics turned out to be full-contact, unarmored sparring and lessons. The bruising impacts left her almost as stiff as Lieutenant-Librarian Fringe’s physical conditioning, and she was exhausted by the end of it. Nor was Patrol Duty any better. The class was like Introduction to Monsters, but with far more information taught far more quickly. It was enough to make Eola’s head spin.

Extinct Tongues was the opposite. Instructor Tarik’s focus on Old Alemic was almost relaxing, and that gave Eola time to think. Equally importantly, Old Alemic was one of the languages Varin had used in his journal. It was one Eola could already translate about seven of every ten words—though all that meant was that the three she couldn’t had to be her focus.

And then there was her assignment from Lannith.

She covered sheet after sheet of parchment with sketches and notes, poring over Ensorcellment Projects for Emerging Mages to double-check her work and come up with a rough list of materials, techniques, and processes. Every free moment of time went into Eola’s sketches and descriptions.

Well…not every free moment.

A few minutes every day were spent with Colin.

But by Fifth-Day, when Eola crept down the narrow stairwell and entered the demi-Srilla’s lair for her scheduled meeting, she was pretty confident in her overall plan. She sat at a wide table and unfurled her notes as Lannith loomed over her.

“A wand?” The armored demi-Srilla’s helm pored over the parchment. One plate-mailed finger hovered over the sketched diagram, tracing the ink and tapping here and there. Eola waited for almost a full minute as Lannith took in the design until, finally, the monster’s finger left the page. “Pen, please. I can mark this draft, correct?”

“Yes,” Eola said, scrambling for a pen and ink, but before she could pull hers out, a long quill appeared in the professor’s hand. As the quill ran across the page, bright orange lines followed. “How did you do that?”

“You are Lemiene, correct? Lemiene, you have picked a challenging project for a beginner. Ensorcellment is not an easy field to master, and a weapon like a smallsword would be an easier project to learn on.” The demi-Srilla paused, then continued working. Eola’s parchment filled up slowly with orange. “However, I sense your interest in this project. It is wholehearted and earnest. Why?”

“I need this wand to…for Truemage.”

“Be honest.” The monstrous professor’s tone had shifted, and Eola was suddenly very aware of how unprotected she was in her blue robes and cloak.

“My…my parents need my help, and I need every scrap of Mana I can get to save them.”

“And you are aware that you, not a craftsman from the world outside, must perform every step of the ensorcellment? This wand is more complicated than most second-semester projects. Forging two separate metals, bonding them to wood without destroying it, and the alchemical processes for preparing the ensorcellment surface…a complicated project indeed.” Lannith continued to speak, looming over Eola, pen resting on her suddenly heavily marked notes.

But the monstrous professor had shown Eola her true colors. It didn’t give a single one of Y’aer’s curses about Eola’s parents, about her need for Mana, or any of that. All it had cared about was that she had an answer. “It’s complex, but I can do it.”

“Good.” The armor jerked upright so suddenly Eola’s hand dropped to her belt, where her smallsword should have been. Lannith paid the motion absolutely no mind. “Chromite and silver. Won’t need steel or iron. Fortunate. And…oak?”

“Yew?”

“Yew! Yes, yew could work. Yew has serious magical properties. Might also force Clearance into visiting. I enjoy talking with her. What else? Bloodstone. Winterstalk musk. Amber.” The list went on for a minute, and Eola focused on memorizing it, even as the demi-Srilla’s hand traced the words onto the paper. “Solvents. Varnish—kaneseed oil or yellow tar. And of course, manuals. You’ll need to double-check all of this. I’m not you, and this project must match your needs. You’ve done some research, clearly. Which book?”

“I have Ensorcellment Projects for Emerging Mages, but it’s checked out from the library,” Eola said. Her notes were covered in orange now, to the point where she could barely see her original sketch.

“Good. You will need others, though. Fresh paper.” A paper zoomed across the room as Eola stared. She hadn’t seen Lannith cast a spell, but that was clearly magic. Before she could comment on it, the professor started writing again, armored fingers holding the pen deftly as half a dozen books’ names appeared, one after the other. Then it flipped the page and kept writing, and Eola’s stomach sank, Lannith’s strange, mysterious magic forgotten.

She recognized those coordinates.

The library’s third floor.

Eola talked to Tagg first.

It’d be one thing to convince Patrice to go with her. That girl was, in many respects, a glutton for battle, and she probably hadn’t had the chance to swing that hammer around since their near-disaster of a Library Exam last term. Eola was pretty sure all it’d take was the promise of cracking a Dalyn/Davek pair, or at least fighting some feral books, and she’d be in. She’d also said she needed to get back to the third floor.

And Roth…well, Roth would be easy. He needed the same books she did, after all.

No, the real problem was Colin. Roth might’ve had it worse during the Library Exam, but her…Y’aer, it was still odd to think about. The word sort of squealed in her head, like a crank without oil. Her boyfriend hadn’t gotten off easy, either—and he was so…soft. No, not soft. Gentle. He had a few rituals that could end fights, but combat wasn’t his thing; he wanted to be a healer.

If she wanted to convince him to join her on a quick expedition into the third floor, she’d need to come to him with a plan. Not just a description from Lannith, either. She’d need a route, or at least the suggestion of one.

And for that, she’d need Tagg.

The old librarian was still hard at work, leaning on his spear and watching the gate and tunnel between the first and second floors. His beard was, if anything, a little whiter than it had been, but his wrinkled face still broke into a grin. “Miss Lemiene. Study session on the second floor tonight?”

“No, not this time. I’m here for information. I need to head back to the third floor, and—“

“The Scholar’s Cult?” The smile faded from Tagg’s face, and his brow furrowed.

“Yes, but not exactly. I am worried about them. Eddarn especially. But I’m looking for a specific route to the following location.” Eola held Lannith’s list of books out, back face up, showing him the description of a ten-sided floating room with a large fountain in the center. According to the monster, the fountain emptied into the air, and the water faded to nothing within ten feet of the room’s base. Also, according to Lannith, the real treatises on ensorcellment could be found there.

“A route, eh?” Tagg’s lips pursed. He nodded slowly. “It’s not my place to give routes, normally, but you’re right that the cult seemed to have it out for you. I can’t escort you up there myself, but here.”

He wrote quickly, describing a trio of staircases for Eola to follow and the room she’d have to wait in until the right stairs and room linked up. When he was finished, Eola folded the paper up carefully and deposited it next to her Atta statuette. “Thank you.”

“Be safe, girl,” the old librarian said, and Eola nodded, half-bowing, as she disappeared back into the first floor.

Eola hurried across a campus that was already cold even this early in the evening, slipped through the dormitory building’s doors, and marched to Colin’s door. Her hand paused, inches from his door. She swallowed nervously; she’d never been in his room. Then she knocked.

“One moment.” Colin’s voice sounded muffled, and as she waited, something crashed in his room. “Y’aer’s curse! One moment!”

“Take your time, Colin,” she said.

When he finally appeared, it was in the form of a single eye that peered through the cracked door. He looked red-faced, from the little Eola could see. “What can I…what can I do for you, Eola?”

“I was hoping for a quick, private conversation.”

Patrice snorted from the couch.

Colin glanced over his shoulder, pursed his lips together, and seemed to steel himself. Then he nodded. “Come in. But not a word, alright?”

He opened the door, and Eola stepped inside. Before she could properly get her bearings, he’d closed it behind her. “Welcome to my room,” he said awkwardly.

The first thing Eola noticed was the smell.

Familiars weren’t, strictly speaking, magical animals. They were real animals—or the impression of one, drawn from the void and bonded with their masters. That meant that, for all intents and purposes, familiars like Colin’s Nera needed to eat, drink, and relieve themselves. When you added that Nera was a weasel to the mix, it made for an…interesting…aroma. Eloa’s nose wrinkled, and Colin slowly drew a rune in the air. She recognized the core of the spell.

“Air Mesh, with a Permanent symbol, but no Personal one? What else is in it?”

Colin shrugged, trying and failing to seem relaxed. “I dunno. It’s the First Order ritual Filtration Ward. Only thing that keeps Nera bearable, honestly. I’d kill for your window.”

“I see.” Eola finally started to look around. When she did, she wished she hadn’t. Colin had explained that, despite his systematic approach to combat and magic, he was a bit disorganized as a person.

The crashing sound had been a rack, complete with a collection of school cloaks, robes, and scarves. They’d landed halfway on the desk, catapulting papers everywhere, but those had only landed on stacked up piles of books on rituals, siege magic, and medical runes. Those were stacked on top of what looked suspiciously like last term’s final few weeks of homework.

Eola raised an eyebrow, and Colin groaned, face reddening. “Not a word.”

“Oh, Colin,” she said.

“Not a word!”

“Fine. But only if you promise to let me help you with this later. You shouldn’t…I mean, this is…how do you keep yourself organized for classes?”

“I…I manage,” he said. Eola recognized the phrase and winced.

Then she waited. Awkwardly. There was a lot of that all of a sudden. After a minute, she cleared her throat. “Can I give you a hug?”

“Yes, please.”

It helped. A little. Then, when he wiggled like a mouse caught in Atta’s paws, Eola let him go and explained her plan. It took less than a minute to get him onboard.

Having a suitor had a few advantages, it seemed.

“So, that’s why we need to get to the third floor,” Eola finished ten minutes later.

It had taken that long to get Roth out of his room. He’d been silent, and if looks could kill, the glare he’d given her when she kept knocking would have ruined her chances of ever rescuing her parents from Lord Card’s dungeon. But he’d at least agreed to hear her out.

And a good thing for him, too.

“Well, I’m in,” he said immediately. “Need those books, too. We’ll share ‘em.”

Patrice shook her head slowly. “You’ve really thought this out, Eola.”

“I have. I know the floating room we need. I know how to get to it. It’s a slow strategy, but it’s one that, if we do it right, will mean passing through only four rooms—not counting the entrance.” She was on the couch, Colin’s hand around her shoulders, every part the picture of what she’d sworn a year ago they wouldn’t become. “I’m not saying it’ll be safe. There are Dalyns and Daveks, Srillas, and mites in there, and Tagg didn’t tell me anything about those, but—“

“What about the Scholar’s Cult?” Patrice interrupted.

Eola paused mid-sentence. She’d expected this argument from Colin, not Patrice, and she’d been caught off-guard. “What about them?”

“Didn’t Eddarn get away? And they were after you. They set a whole trap last term specifically to catch you, Eola. You can’t just ignore that.”

“I’m not ignoring it.”

“You are.” Patrice stood up, arms on her hips. “You put in all this planning work to make the trip a success, and that’s really impressive—especially given how you handled your first trip to the third floor last year—but you’re ignoring the Scholar’s Cult. They’re the most dangerous thing on the floor. Tell me you’ve at least considered them. Please.”

The redhead’s eyes bored into Eola’s, and as they did, Eola caught the hint of a tear. Patrice wasn’t angry. She was scared.

Eola gently slipped Colin’s arm off her shoulder and stood up. She shook her head. “You’re right. I’m sorry. I’m scared of them, too. But we’re going in at a weird time, early in the year. It’s a short trip. Not quite in and out—we’ll be stuck in one room for between thirty seconds and forty-five minutes depending on the stair schedule, and again on the way out—but close to it. I think, as far as third-floor trips go, it’s the safest one we’ll ever make.”

The common room was silent for a few seconds, and Eola looked around at her friends. Colin had stayed on the couch. Roth lay on the floor, hands behind his head, humming. And Patrice’s eyes wouldn’t leave hers. She took a knee and tapped the parchment with Tagg and Lannith’s writing. “I need your help, but if you truly don’t think we’re ready, I’ll wait until tomorrow. We can come up with a better plan by then.”

Quiet.

Eola tried not to fiddle with her robe as Patrice stared. Then, after an eternity, the redhead nodded. “Good enough for me. I’m in. Tonight.”

“Great. I’ll get ready. Meet here in fifteen minutes?” Eola asked.

“Sure.”

Roth cleared his throat. “Colin, you need help with your armor?”

“Yes, please.”

Eola retreated to her room, pulled her gear down from the loft, and laid it out on her bed. The bandolier for her dad’s wand and her spellbook. The belt and scabbard, with her mom’s smallsword in it. Her breastplate—sized for her mother, but when she put it on, the straps tightened around her.

She breathed a sigh of relief.

Both because they were still alive, but also because, in the end, her friends trusted her. It hadn’t been Colin that had driven that point home, though—or even Roth.

It had been Patrice. Patrice, who, as far as Eola could tell, was the toughest, most self-assured person—at least outwardly. This term, she’d been seeing a different side of ‘Lady Patrice of House Clerk.’ This Patrice was less confident. She was more nervous. She wasn’t interested in swinging her hammer around like last year. Something had changed. Was it her relationship with magic? Or was there something else?

In the end, though, it didn’t matter, because Patrice and the others trusted her, and ever since that disastrous Library Exam, she’d changed, too.

The four apprentices assembled in the suite’s common room. Roth had his hide coat and a satchel stuffed considerably less full of instruments than last time. Colin carried his smallsword and belt in one hand, counterbalancing his own satchel of supplies. He wore the same ill-fitting armor he’d had last term. That was one more thing Eola would have to address with him; even a siege mage needed the proper equipment to do his job. And Patrice had her suit of plate on.

Eola looked them over, then nodded. “We’re ready. Let’s go get those books.”

“And let’s hope the Scholar’s Cult isn’t on the third floor,” Patrice said quickly.
