20th Peregrinor

This ocean is dangerous, but it is also strangely beautiful at times. Today, I was enraptured by several shoals of bioluminescent fish that drifted past us just after sunset. They were multiple colours and rippled in an eye-catching way that kept me watching for almost half a candlemark.

I stopped watching, incidentally, when one of the seamen informed me that the fish were not actually real, but illusions created by a leviathan lurking beneath us. Anything drawn in to prey on the obvious targets would find itself quickly consumed.

I vaguely recall declaring in a previous entry that I would not be surprised by anything else this ocean has to throw at me? I take that back. I felt very small indeed at the thought of us floating above a leviathan that, if what I recall of the reports of the creatures is correct, is probably multiple times the size of my ship.

The sailor, perhaps seeing my unease, reassured me that such a leviathan was clearly too small to attack us. That helped, at least until he added that if it had been large enough, it would already have attacked.

Beautiful or not, I can’t wait for this journey to end.

In other news, I discovered what exactly happened after the point when my memory of our drinking two nights ago became blurry. Valence is never going to live down the fact that he proposed to the server who came to clear up the mess we had made – and that the server accepted and reminded him of it hopefully the day after.

I don’t know who was more embarrassed – Valence or the server when he turned him down immediately.

Yes. Him. The server was male.

That actually led to an interesting revelation of a difference in Moriaxar culture that I’d somehow passed over entirely. It seems that, for all the prejudices and rigid class structures here, there is no prejudice against homosexual couples. I suppose that is partly due to the lack of any religious dogma opposing them – if anything, the reverse is true. Warrior and Healer, patrons of the second month, Sanguaris, are both male and are said to be on-and-off-again lovers. Likewise, Peace and Justice, patrons of the fourth month, Justicaris, are both female and in a loyal and committed marriage.

Anyway, in a world where magic exists, apparently heirs aren’t a problem, even for a homosexual couple. If a lord marries a man, or a lady marries a woman, they can go to a Healer and undergo fertility processes that allow them to have biological children. No magic can make a man pregnant, but in the case of male couples, the child is carried by a surrogate who does not contribute genetically. At least, that’s the understanding I’ve gained, though Thessalanor looked at me oddly when I mentioned genetics.

For those less wealthy, Healers may not be an option. But there’s always finding a donor or surrogate in the normal way – or adoption. Sadly, there are always plenty of children in need of it.

I am not entirely sure how to feel about it. Glad, of course, that it does not cause discord or conflict – there’s enough of that as it is. But I’m a product of my own culture, and so it still feels a little strange.

It’s not that I’m anti-homosexual or anything – I had a friend at school who was gay. But it was an issue that was so hotly debated back where I came from, something that was so divisive. It feels weird for it to be such a…non-issue.

Whether in magical phenomena, beasts, and people, I am reminded daily how much I still have to learn about this world that has become my own.

11th of Malvora — Rest Day

Today has been rather interesting for a day that’s usually spent quietly. The first half of it was spent visiting those sentenced for acts of sabotage (see my first entry for details). It seemed appropriate, given that Malvora is the month patronised by Redemption and Curse.

So far, all have kept their heads down and quietly got on with what they’ve been ordered to do. They aren’t well-liked, which is understandable. They are living and working alongside those they were willing to sacrifice – and everyone knows what they did.

Still, I haven’t seen much true remorse. Not yet, anyway. Yet there are signs of change.

The one responsible for sabotaging the food seems less certain now – less convinced of the reasoning behind his actions. Even the Warrior who once boasted of his role appears unsettled when confronted daily by those he condemned.

I have kept my distance, and I suspect that has helped. Without me as a convenient target – the ‘oppressive noble’ – they have been forced to see the others for what they are: ordinary people.

I hope that the others will follow his lead by the time we reach the Lost Continent. For their sake, I hope they make progress – both in combat skills and in relationships. Three of them will be on the front lines when we arrive, and without allies, they will not survive long.

I’m definitely rooting for them – though, admittedly, it’s not only for their own sake. I’m aware that several of the other lords – and probably a good number of the others on my ship – are watching them closely, either expecting or hoping for them to fail and prove their point that it would have been better to execute them or wrap a collar around their necks.

I have to admit that I have a similar but opposite hope – like the slaves who I have offered the hope of freedom if they work for it, these condemned are test cases. If they succeed in redeeming themselves….

On the other side – and far more positively – I distributed some more Class stones today. Five new Warriors have joined our ranks – mostly Common classes such as Warrior and Archer – as have three new crafters: a Cook, a Fisherman, and a Butcher. Maxen, was, of course, chosen for a Warrior Class. He has well-earned it. All will be valuable additions, both now and when we reach the continent.

I still have a number of stones remaining – mostly Common, with a few Uncommon ones – from Tiria and those entrusted to me by Nicholas or taken as bounty from Kyrian’s fleet. I won’t hesitate to give them out when deserved, but I won’t just give them to anyone. By making them something to earn, it inspires others to improve and become better. That might be worth more than the value of the stones themselves.

Two of the recipients were among the former slaves – one from Moriax, one from Garous’ ship. Both have fought hard and proven themselves repeatedly and so it was with gladness in my heart that I handed the stones over – and saw the awe in their eyes as the stones melted into their skin.

When I stop to think about it, it feels odd to make such decisions that determine the course of someone’s life, good or bad. Of many peoples’ lives. In those quiet moments, a little voice speaks up from inside, questioning why I am the one making these decisions. What did I ever do to deserve it?

I try to remind myself that it’s less about what I did to deserve it in the past, and more about the fact that I have the power now. I therefore need to prove myself worthy of it in the future.

It helps. A little.

16th of Malvora

Tiria and Keshaka have finally made progress, though not on my Kindle or phone charging problem. Instead, they have created something that is almost as exciting, and probably more immediately useful: a way of tracking time.

I explained the basics of clockwork to Keshaka. She, possessing a far more practical understanding of mechanics than I do, adapted the concept and integrated it with magic.

The result is a necklace with a dial that looks fairly like a clock face – that probably comes from the drawing I talked her through of a clock face with its hour, minute, and second hands.

The proto-clock draws on the ambient mana I emit, maintaining a steady rate of movement. Apparently, calibration took several days to ensure consistency regardless of fluctuations in my mana output, but they succeeded.

For the first time since arriving here, I can reliably track time even without the sun. Given the heavy fog of the past few days, this has been invaluable. Though, I was less pleased when I accidentally set the alarm to ring in the middle of the night and woke up everyone in my cabin – and the dorm room below…. Everyone else wasn’t exactly ecstatic either.

Note for anyone reading: become familiar with all functions of new magical devices before putting them on.

10th of Luseris

I’m frankly amazed at how much damage a storm can do.

It’s not quite as devastating as the Tier four roc was, but the combination of lightning, wind, and torrential rain has significantly reduced our sailing capacity.

Sor’az’s ship nearly capsized under the waves, and Josiah’s fared little better. The larger vessels handled the seas more effectively but became prime targets for lightning strikes that ripped apart sails, shattered three masts, and caused several fires despite the anti-fire enchantments.

If Luceris were the month of lightning, I might be tempted to believe in divine influence. As it stands, it is supposedly the month of Luck.

If this is luck, I would hate to see misfortune.

We will need extensive repairs, which puts us further behind schedule. At this rate, it may be another month before we reach the continent. Cabin fever is already setting in and I’m not alone in aching to set my feet on something that doesn’t move.

On the brighter side, I managed to spend part of the storm observing the lightning while not frantically running around and trying to mitigate its damage.

While on the samurans’ world, I felt close to understanding it – close enough to attempt shaping it myself. Now, I feel even closer – perhaps some meditation will help.

I don’t need another elemental Skill, but the idea of throwing around lightning bolts like Zeus is…tempting.

It might be worth spending some time experimenting. If I get the chance.

17th of Luseris

No success with lightning shaping so far.

It feels as though there is some barrier I have yet to figure out a way around.

From what I have gathered – through discussions with Sarran and Nicholas, mostly – my method of learning differs from most. I need to understand the relationship between magic and the natural phenomenon before I can replicate it. Only then does the System recognise that understanding and grant the Skill. Most people start it the other direction – learning how to shape the element with a single, narrow Skill and then sometimes broadening it as it ranks up.

At present, I can’t even figure out the difference between lightning magic and, say, fire.

Still, this isn’t the first time I’ve struggled to learn an elemental Skill – Earth-Shaping took time too and Air-Shaping required observation of how Windy did it before I could replicate the effect. With time, I’m confident I’ll be able to learn it. The question is whether it’s the best use of my time when I have so many other things to work on.

On another note, Keshaka and Tiria made excellent use of the storm: they have successfully generated electricity!

Now comes the more difficult task: controlling it – voltage, amperage, and whatever else. It would be easier if I were an expert, but I’m not. So they’ll just have to test with the yo-yo. I’ve memorised the internal features so I can repair the metal circuit if they blow it to pieces, but until they can regulate the electricity reliably, there’s no way I’m letting them anywhere near my more delicate devices.

2nd Terravar

Sor’az’s soothsayer has sent another warning: a magical storm is coming, worse than the one we experienced just over a ten-day ago.

That is not heartening. We have only just finished repairs from the last one, and I’m not eager to discover what ‘worse’ looks like.

There is, however, some hope. We’re supposed to reach the Lost Continent in less than a tenday. What's more, if we can stay ahead of the storm, we may avoid it altogether. Still, we will need to remain vigilant – magical storms can apparently have unpredictable effects on wildlife even after they have gone.

At least this time, the other lords are more inclined to listen since the soothsayer proved entirely accurate last time with the Tier five and we all almost died because we didn’t listen soon enough. Elarion even insisted we burn extra resources to reach the continent as quickly as possible, which saved me from having to argue the point.

After several monthly drinking sessions where we talk about everything and nothing, I feel that I know the other leaders of this expedition far better than when we began. They know me better too, which has eased much of the tension between us.

Particularly between Elarian and myself.

I am grateful for that, and I hope it continues as time goes on. With everything I suspect is waiting for us on the Lost Continent, we can’t afford internal discord.
