As Luke’s body crumpled to the floor, a warm glow spread across the courtyard. A soft radiance enveloped Victoria, Kayson, and Tristan, emanating from their bodies like the gentle flicker of fireflies.

For a moment, the three men stood in stunned silence, forgetting even Luke and Victoria’s unconscious forms as they bathed in the divine light.

“W-What is this?” Kayson murmured, staring at his hands.

From their bodies, a black mist began to rise—sinister and unnatural. It stood in stark contrast to the light, like a shadow recoiling from the sun. Just looking at it made their hearts tighten with unease, stirring fear and guilt they couldn’t quite name.

An alarming amount of the dark haze lifted from both Kayson and Tristan, swirling upward before dissolving into the air, fading as though it had never been.

The scene felt surreal. Only when the glow began to dim did the men snap back to reality.

Kayson rushed to Luke’s side, crouching beside him with palpable concern. After a brief inspection, he exhaled in relief.

“He’s just unconscious…”

“What about her?” Richard asked, nodding toward Victoria’s still form.

Kayson turned to the woman. She lay peacefully, her chest rising and falling with soft, steady breaths. Cautiously, he stepped closer, half-expecting her to lunge at him again.

“I don’t think you need to worry, Kayson,” Tristan said gently, offering a small smile. “That light—it’s purged the evil that had taken hold of her.”

“Was that divine light?” Richard asked, his voice hushed.

Tristan gave a slow nod, though doubt lingered in his eyes. “Perhaps. I’ve heard tales of the gods blessing those they find worthy... but I can’t claim to know the details. We may have to wait for young Luke to wake before we get answers.”

Richard frowned, glancing down at Luke. “You think he caused this?”

Tristan placed a reassuring hand on the larger man’s shoulder. “You probably don’t know this... but Luke is blessed by the gods. I wouldn’t be surprised if he petitioned them to save Victoria—and cleanse us of whatever had latched onto us.”

Richard’s eyes widened. He took a step back, reeling. “Luke? Blessed by the gods? That’s... that’s impossible. He’s so—”

“Weak?” Tristan finished, a grin tugging at the corner of his lips.

Richard looked sheepish but nodded nonetheless.

“Blessings come in many forms,” Tristan continued. “They’re not limited to physical strength alone. Tell me, what kind of seventeen-year-old could lead an entire army out of a siege without a single casualty?”

He stepped forward, his voice gaining strength.

“Who else could guide that same army across enemy territory—unarmed, undersupplied—and not only return home, but also rescue you, and the second prince from enemy hands during the same campaign?”

Tristan swept his arms wide, gesturing to the palace around them.

“And now, that very same boy has purged the kingdom of traitors—without plunging our forces into a civil war. In one decisive stroke, Luke has preserved our strength and given the Marxx Kingdom a fighting chance at survival.”

He paused, lowering his arms as his tone grew reverent.

“Even if it’s a narrow one.”

“Tell me, Richard. Do you still think Luke is weak?”

The question hung in the air, suspended between them.

“…No,” Richard admitted at last, his voice quiet. “I guess not.”

Kayson rose to his feet, his expression firm as he looked up at the larger man.

“The first time I met my brother, I thought he was weak and underhanded,” he said, a faint smile tugging at his lips. “We dueled—sword against bow at close range—but somehow, he still came out on top. Though… the means were a little sneaky.”

“Oh?” Tristan arched a brow, curiosity lighting his features. “How did he pull that off?”

Kayson shifted uncomfortably, his hand instinctively dropping toward his crotch. “I’d rather not talk about it.”

Tristan let out a chuckle as Kayson cleared his throat and pressed on.

“Wounded in pride and ego, I challenged him to a game of chess. The stakes were simple: the loser would become the winner’s subordinate. I thought that only by crushing him—by making him kneel beneath me—could I satisfy my bruised pride.”

Tristan gave a knowing smile. “So that’s how you ended up following him.”

But Kayson shook his head. “No. Luke won the match, but he refused to accept my vow. I still remember the look on his face—annoyed, almost insulted. And I’ll never forget what he said next.”

He adopted a mocking tone, clearly imitating Luke. “‘Do you think I need to trick and scheme people into becoming my subordinates? Only those worthy and willing may have the honor of serving me.’”

Kayson laughed softly at the memory.

There was a brief silence before Tristan burst out laughing, a deep, hearty sound that wiped the exhaustion from his face. “What a domineering bastard. Spouting that kind of nonsense to the son of a viscount.”

Richard’s gaze drifted to Luke, still lying unconscious beside Victoria. He looked so young now—barely more than a boy. The contrast between his age and his accomplishments was stark, unsettling even. How could someone so young carry so much weight?

Yet, in the short time Richard had known him, Luke had demonstrated his worth time and again. The retreat at Xiandong. The daring rescue of both himself and the second prince. The decisive quelling of the traitors within the kingdom. Each act had reinforced the same truth.

After a few moments of reflection, Richard nodded, the lines on his face easing as a sense of conviction took root.

“There’s no one better to lead the kingdom,” he said firmly.

Then, without ceremony, the large man stooped down and lifted Luke’s limp body, slinging him over his shoulder with practiced ease.

Just as he straightened, a sudden flare lit up the sky.

A pyre—igniting swiftly into roaring flame—cast long shadows across the palace grounds. Thick black smoke curled into the night sky like a warning. It was a signal: the call to arms, an alert to the soldiers and citizens alike. An attack on the capital was imminent.

Dread would spread across the city. Panic would take root. But it would also force the remaining defenders into action.

There was no other option.

With the entire imperial court now gone, the kingdom was without leadership. And this—this was the moment to declare a new leader. One capable of rallying the people and defending the Marxx Kingdom’s last hope.

“We should go,” Tristan said, turning toward Victoria.

“I’ll take her,” Kayson offered, stepping forward and lifting her with unexpected tenderness.

Tristan didn’t protest. He simply nodded, then followed as the three of them moved toward the next chapter of their rebellion.

The group moved swiftly through the palace halls, their footsteps echoing in the stillness. As they passed the doors to the imperial court, a grim tableau awaited them—a haunting reminder of the night’s chaos.

Bodies lay scattered across the marble floor in grotesque disarray. Blood smeared the ground, mingled with brain matter and viscera. Though the thick smoke had finally dissipated, black soot clung to the walls and ceiling like the residue of a nightmare that refused to fade.

They walked in silence.

From beyond the palace walls, the distant cries of panicked voices grew louder. By now, the four pyres—signals of an attack on the capital—should have been lit. Their flames would serve as both a warning and a rallying cry to soldiers and civilians alike.

As they exited the palace’s grand front doors, the scene outside confirmed their fears.

A crowd had gathered.

Hundreds of civilians milled restlessly before the gates, their anxiety palpable. Murmurs surged through the mass, growing louder by the second. Had it not been for the presence of soldiers—who were also arriving in greater numbers—the scene might have devolved into chaos.

The moment they stepped outside, the crowd surged toward them like a wave breaking on the shore, voices raised in desperation.

“What’s happening?!”

“Why are we under attack?!”

“Where is the King?! We demand answers!”

The cries pierced the air like arrows, each question steeped in fear and confusion.

Standing under the weight of those pleas, the group from the palace hesitated. A cold dread settled in their chests.

How could they possibly explain that the King they so desperately sought was already dead?

But they wouldn’t have to.

Just as the crowd threatened to reach a boiling point, a cry rang out—followed by startled gasps and shouts of pain. Heads turned as a massive figure carved a path through the sea of bodies, the crowd parting around him like the Red Sea.

Riann had arrived.

The towering Master of Arms strode forward, a hand resting casually on the pommel of his sword. His sheer presence dominated the courtyard. Broad-shouldered and battle-worn, his expression was one of absolute command.

In an instant, the crowd was subdued.

His reputation preceded him. Both civilians and soldiers fell quiet under his gaze, cowed by the weight of his authority.

“People of Ralis City,” Riann said, his voice deep and resonant, ringing across the square with unwavering force. “Our enemies have infiltrated the kingdom—corrupting us from within like a festering wound. They sought to destroy us with a borrowed knife, turning us against each other in secret.”

He paused, letting the silence stretch, his eyes scanning the crowd with steely resolve.

“The King is dead,” he declared. “And the imperial court has fallen with him.”

His words struck like thunder.

Tristan’s eyes widened in disbelief. He hadn’t expected Riann to reveal the truth so openly—not yet, not without a plan to soften the blow. With their leadership in shambles and the enemy closing in, panic was the last thing they could afford.

The reaction was immediate.

Gasps rippled through the crowd, followed by a flurry of murmurs. The civilians were stunned, too shocked to respond at first—but the whispers soon turned to shouts.

“He’s lying!”

“What do you mean the King is dead?!”

“No—this can’t be happening!”

The soldiers, too, began exchanging worried glances, their disciplined lines breaking into confused clusters of conversation.

The tenuous calm teetered on the edge of collapse.

Seemingly unbothered by the rising tension, Riann pressed on, his voice steady and grim.

“These enemies are coming to finish the job with a force marching from the south,” he declared. “They expect to find us already broken—our walls divided by civil war, our blades turned against one another. That is the battlefield they hoped to create.”

He took a step forward, his gaze sweeping across the shaken crowd.

“They plan to storm our gates, to rape and pillage our homes before reducing this city to ash.”

A heavy silence followed his words, but it didn’t last.

The murmurs returned—harsher now. Fear bled into the air like ink in water, staining every breath. The crowd began to sway, like a dam straining against mounting pressure.

Tristan narrowed his eyes at Riann’s broad back, baffled.

He failed to see how this was meant to help them.

One wrong move, one panicked scream, and the entire square could unravel. If people began to flee, they’d face an impossible choice: deploy soldiers to contain the chaos or allow the gates to open, risking an unguarded escape that would weaken their lines.

Neither option ended well.

“I hope your uncle knows what he’s doing…” Tristan muttered, leaning in toward Richard.

Richard didn’t answer immediately. His brow was furrowed in deep thought.

“This is the most I’ve ever heard him talk,” he replied, half in awe, half in concern.

Up front, Riann raised his voice again, this time with thunderous force.

“Will you all let that happen?!” he roared. “Will you lie down like dogs and let them take everything?! Will you let them burn your homes and slaughter your kin?!”

The crowd froze.

No one answered.

Fear had chained their tongues. The abrupt announcement of an imminent invasion, paired with the death of the king and the imperial court, had shattered their sense of reality. They were leaderless, frightened, and teetering on the brink of collapse.

Then a voice rang out, clear and sharp:

“I will not roll over like a dog!”

A soldier stepped forward, spear clenched in both hands. His plumed helm caught the flickering torchlight, casting shadows across his determined face.

Tristan recognized him instantly—Blake, one of the lieutenant commanders Luke had handpicked during the expedition.

His presence had an immediate effect.

“We will not roll over!” Blake shouted, raising his weapon high. “We’ll kill every last one of them!”

Several soldiers behind him rallied, spears thrust skyward with booming cheers.

“We’ll kill them all!”

The momentum shifted.

Up at the front, Riann gave a single nod. His expression remained stoic, but there was the faintest twitch at the corner of his lips—almost a smile.

“All of you,” he said firmly. “Spread the word. The Marxx Kingdom will not fall today. We will make our final stand, and from these ashes, we will rise again. Our blades will carve our mark into the world once more…”

His voice dropped slightly as he turned his head toward Richard—and then to the unconscious figure draped over his shoulder.

“…with a new King.”

The words settled over the square like the final note of a war drum.

“You heard the man!” Blake called out, motioning with his arm. “Move out! Spread the word! And if I catch any of you slacking, I’ll kick your ass myself!”

Despite the crudeness of the order, his tone held no malice—only fire.

The energy in the crowd shifted.

The fear didn’t vanish, but it was tempered now—molded into something sharper, something useful. Determination replaced despair as the people began to move, filtering back into the streets, carrying the word like a spark through dry grass.

Hope flickered to life.

Soon, only the soldiers remained, continuing to gather from the east with disciplined urgency. Their armor clinked as they assembled, rows of steel forming in preparation for the storm on the horizon.

“Master Riann, are we assembling in the main square?” Blake stepped forward, bowing his head respectfully.

Riann nodded. “You did well, Blake.”

Blake was about to reply when his gaze landed on someone nearby. Recognition flashed in his eyes as he spotted Tristan—only for that light to dim upon noticing the fatigue etched into the man’s face.

“Vice General, where—?”

He didn’t finish the question. His eyes shifted to the unconscious figure slung over Richard’s shoulder.

“He’s fine, Blake,” a deep voice answered. A firm hand settled on Blake’s shoulder. It was Richard. “Take your men to the main square. Keep the others in line until we arrive.”

Blake nodded, lips pressed in a thin line, and turned back to his unit. He barked orders with the commanding presence of a seasoned officer, leading his men past the palace gates. Yet even as he moved with purpose, he cast one last lingering glance at Luke—his commander, his leader.

As the last of the soldiers departed, Riann finally turned toward his nephew.

“Is he okay?” he asked, raising a brow.

Richard sighed, shifting Luke’s weight on his shoulder. “Honestly… I don’t know. Some strange things happened after you left.”

“He’s just unconscious,” Tristan added from nearby. “Luke will wake up soon.”

Riann gave a slow nod, though concern flickered behind his eyes. “He better. We’re a little short on generals right now. Losing someone like him might be more than we can handle.”

“How many men do we have in the city?” Tristan asked, dragging a hand across his weary face, trying to shake off the exhaustion clouding his thoughts.

“Not sure,” Riann admitted. “We’ll know soon enough once we reach the main square.”

With that, he stepped forward and pushed open the palace gates, motioning for the others to follow.

As Kayson passed, his eyes drifted toward the unconscious Victoria in his arms. He didn’t speak—but the silence spoke volumes.

They moved through the streets of Ralis, now cast in eerie contrast to its usual bustle. Flames from the pyres flickered above the rooftops in all four directions, their glow stretching long shadows across stone walls.

All around them, doors slammed shut. Windows were barred. The city's usual symphony of merchants and laughter had been replaced by hurried footsteps and whispered prayers.

Soldiers streamed toward the central square, quickening their pace the moment they caught sight of Riann. No one paused to greet him—not out of disrespect, but reverence. His reputation preceded him, and no one dared delay in his presence.

The group walked in silence, each man lost in thought. Still, there was something unspoken they all shared: grim determination. They knew what lay ahead.

At last, the winding streets gave way to the broad expanse of the main square—the heart of Ralis City. It was where all roads from the three outer gates converged. Normally, it was a hub of life and commerce: wagons creaked beneath produce-laden tarps, merchants hawked exotic wares, and laughter rang out during festivals and public gatherings.

It was also a place of judgment—where punishments were dealt, floggings carried out, and traitors met the executioner’s blade.

But now, there were no cheering crowds. No music. No clamor of trade.

Instead, the square pulsed with the slow, metallic rhythm of armor and boots. Hundreds of soldiers funneled in from every direction, drawn by the pyres’ call. They moved with purpose, forming ranks and awaiting further orders—an army being molded in the shadow of impending war.

“Make way!”

Riann’s thunderous voice rolled across the square like a battle horn, sending a visible shockwave through the assembled ranks. Soldiers froze in place, heads snapping toward the sound. A heartbeat later, they scrambled to clear a path, eager to avoid standing in the way of the Master of Arms and the group behind him.

Eyes tracked them as they passed—particularly the unconscious man and woman being carried—but no one dared to speak.

The group advanced toward the northern end of the square, where an old stone platform stood like a forgotten monument. Even beneath the flickering glow of the pyres, the deep cracks along its surface were clear. Time had worn it down, but the weight of history clung to it still.

As they neared the base of the platform, Richard suddenly halted, letting out a surprised grunt.

The body slung over his shoulder stirred.

“Victoria…” Luke’s voice was hoarse, barely above a whisper, as if pulled from the depths of sleep.

Richard gently lowered him down, placing him atop the platform with unexpected care—like one might seat a drowsy child. Luke blinked slowly, vision clearing in fragmented snapshots. His thoughts, however, lagged behind.

He squinted into the darkness.

A sea of armored men stared back at him—watching in silence, their expressions a complex blend of awe, confusion, and expectation.

“…Is this Valhalla?” he muttered, voice dazed.

“Luke, are you alright?” Kayson stepped forward, Victoria still nestled carefully in his arms.

But Luke ignored the question. His eyes had already locked on the sleeping woman.

“Is she…?”

“She’s alive, Luke. You saved her,” Kayson said gently, shifting slightly to show her peaceful expression. Even in slumber, her face looked serene—free of pain, free of the torment that had plagued her.

Luke exhaled slowly, the tension bleeding from his body. His shoulders relaxed for the first time in what felt like days.

He stared at her—this beautiful, complicated woman he had failed once before. Never again.

Seeing her safe was all the reassurance he needed. His mind, unburdened, snapped back into focus.

A glance at Riann… a shared look with Tristan… bits of information assembled themselves rapidly. He could infer some things—but not everything.

“How long since the pyres were lit?” he asked sharply.

“No more than fifteen minutes,” Tristan replied.

Luke gave a slow nod and inhaled deeply. Then, without another word, he closed his eyes—and let his soul drift free.

Instantly, his exhaustion vanished, like casting off a weighted cloak. In this liberated state, he soared above the city square, unseen and untethered.

The sensation was exhilarating—clean, lucid, powerful. The sluggishness of his mortal form no longer held him back. From above, he looked down at the capital’s winding streets, watching as soldiers flooded toward the main square in steady waves.

At a glance, he counted roughly twenty-five thousand.

Too few, he thought grimly.

His gaze shifted southward, rising higher still.

Beyond the edges of the city, past the scattered farms and watchtowers, an unnatural darkness loomed on the horizon. Blacker than the night sky, it crawled across the land like rising fog—silent, steady, suffocating.

He didn’t need to look closer. He knew what it was.

And he knew who commanded it.

Just as Luke prepared to return to his body, something pulled at him from the east—a subtle tremor in the fabric of the world that struck his soul like a hammer.

Shock gripped him.

Suppressing the rising surge of panic, he shot higher into the sky, his gaze tearing through the darkness like a hawk searching the distant horizon.

Then, it appeared.

A blinding flash of white light split the night—pure, radiant, and utterly out of place. It shimmered like polished silver beneath moonlight, cutting through the gloom with effortless grace.

A few seconds later, it came again—this time in a different rhythm.

He watched, frozen in his soul form, as the white aura continued to pulse through the night sky in an odd cadence. It wasn’t random. It was precise. Familiar.

A strange sense of recognition brushed against the edges of his consciousness—no, deeper than that. It touched his very soul.

I’ve seen this before…

The rhythm. The timing. The pulsing intervals…

It was like the flapping of wings.

And then, as if summoned by the thought, a sound echoed through the sky.

It wasn’t heard with ears—but felt. A trumpeting cry, majestic and mournful, filled the heavens.

A crane.

Not just any bird—but the Crane.

Luke hovered in stillness, letting the moment sink in. A sharp breath escaped him, then a glint returned to his eyes. The corner of his mouth twitched upward.

Then, without a word, without warning, he snapped back into his body.

Fatigue crashed into him like a wave, every nerve in his frame protesting. But he didn’t care.

Not now.

“HAHAHAHA!”

His laughter burst forth uncontrollably, echoing across the square like a madman’s cackle—wild, sudden, and infectious in its intensity. He threw his head back, laughing as though he’d been freed from a cage.

To the others, it was unsettling.

Tristan, Kayson, Riann, and Richard all exchanged uneasy glances. No one dared speak. The square had fallen silent, the gathered soldiers now staring at the young man on the platform—wide-eyed and uncertain.

Then, Luke lowered his head.

His expression had changed.

A wide, feral grin had replaced his laughter. His eyes gleamed with something dangerous—hope sharpened into a weapon.

“He’s coming,” he said, voice low, but crackling with energy.
