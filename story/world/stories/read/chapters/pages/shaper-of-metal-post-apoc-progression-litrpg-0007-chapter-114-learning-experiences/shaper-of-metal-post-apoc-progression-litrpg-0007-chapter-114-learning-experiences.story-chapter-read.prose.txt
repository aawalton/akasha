Chapter 114: Learning Experiences

Briefly, they went over different strategies when it came to dealing with the ‘defense’ of the fort, but for the most part, just decided to experiment directly and get to some action. Their virtual enemies immediately started by attempting to bait out a response to Chime’s sound-blasting of the walls, which Jack immediately responded to by indirect dart bombardment and shattering shrapnel for some random AoE fire, all blind, and all very hard to pinpoint a source location, though he was obviously moving around on the towers. The others tried some ranged fire from cover, sometimes from actual arrow slits, but their enemy did not get into it or engage them. Every time, it was just Chime working across a far distance…

This guerrilla engagement was mostly pointless and a waste of energy, but Chime was better at it than the rest of them put together. Apparently, what he was doing didn’t require line of sight, and he still managed to cause pretty intense blasts through weird angles, bouncing sound off walls and such as he used the fortress like another instrument of his orchestra. It wasn’t damaging the walls, but it messed with them plenty. Jack and Bo went almost totally deaf, and the general wear-down was not at all fun. The environment began to feel downright oppressive.

And then, they started to notice visual distortions. Very subtle, but it progressively messed with their heads, causing split-second zone outs.

Wait. Oppression. She’s doing something to us across the open!

At that point, they quickly abandoned their guerrilla defense pretenses and retreated inside the main fortress to force their enemy to come in. Come in, they did — Bo had to block a nastier sound wave as they all tried to immediately follow with a counter-charge.

But the air flashed with hypnotic light before they could move, and that oppressive feeling intensified, overwhelming their senses, weakening them to the bones, effectively stunning them all at once. Délight had buttered and basted them up for it the whole time they’d been waging their guerrilla war. Slowly. Boiling the frogs in their own pond.

A moment later, Hiccup sprinted like a blur and got to Bo a split second before she could recover. Perfect timing. The body displacer delivered a coup de grace that likely would’ve put her fist through Bo if not for it being a simulation.

Though the intense hypnotic wave could not last forever, it was still difficult to come out of, slowing them even as they began to move and adjust. Bo’s quasi-interrupted counter on Chime allowed him to follow up, coming in, locating Inkblot, and blasting him, ruining any chance at him counter-attacking.

Highfive and Jack tried a team-up counter on Chime… but there was Defiant, screening out Highfive, as Chime mounted a furious defense with blasting sound, preventing the now unfocused Jack from getting metal on him for anything more than a moment. Jack still bled him on an arm as he adjusted to sneak a blade through the defenses, but that was all.

And that was pretty much it. Jack got focused on from their enemy’s whole offensive might. He put up a dogged fight, but took a nasty injury that plagued him and slowed him down. They alternated between battering Inkblot and Jack to keep them from mounting a decent offense, and finally, Jack was tagged out, with Inkblot soon to follow.

When it was over, the team sat around on the floor shaking their heads, saying nothing for a while, huffing and puffing — well, besides Bo, who, somewhat thankfully, hadn’t expended herself much.

Jack wondered if they were all annoyed about the same thing. Délight. The alterations to the faceplate hadn’t stopped her. Maybe it forced her to take more time, but she must have been able to sense what was happening and adjust. She had still pulled off a nasty feat of strategic timing. ‘She’ being a sim. If this held up as part of her real ability suite, they needed to block all ‘open’ visual access until immediate, physical engagement.

Finally, Jack looked up and met each of their eyes in turn. “Alright, no guerrilla shit. Cat and mouse. We hide and force that shit right off. Yeah?”

Inkblot gave a simple thumbs up. Bo shrugged with the most minute of effort possible, which Jack knew for agreement.

Highfive had a sour look on his face. “I make a shitty mouse.”

“I know, Blondy,” Jack replied in sympathy. “But they beat us at long range. I could put my fields out there, but we can’t expose ourselves. Maybe I can mess with them as they move to get to us.”

“Manufacture traps, then?” Inkblot asked with a raised eyebrow.

Jack nodded. “Even if it doesn’t work, at least they’ll be paranoid and hurried coming at us. Hypothetically.”

“Now we’re talkin’, Big Iron!” Highfive said with new spirit, clapping his hands.

“The only thing… there’s so much surface area to cover. I’m going to need to practice my ass off creating fields quickly. The more area covered and the faster I can get, the better.”

“As soon as it becomes an issue,” Bo interjected, “Chime will start detecting those fields and blasting them.”

Jack shrugged. “If they’re thick enough. I can make them quite thin and still get info out of them. Regardless, that’ll slow them down and give us more opportunities. It isn’t like I can’t move fields… though without information running through them, I may not be able to move them where they need to go.”

“Wish we could see their asses from inside,” Highfive commented with a sigh.

“Hey, actually, why the hell can’t we?” Clearing his throat, Jack turned his head to the side and called out, “Sim Chamber! Can we have cameras in our fortress?”

The smooth AI-like voice chimed back immediately, blanketing the area. “You may requisition cameras and bring them with you to the field as part of feasible location security. But you must set them up as part of your seven-minute prep time, as the base cannot be altered prior to then.”

Jack, Bo, and Highfive all looked at each other with wide eyes, like they had just gotten their hands into a jar of cookies. Inkblot simply nodded in satisfaction.

“Hell yes!” Jack exclaimed. “Can we have drones, too?”

“No.”

“Rigged explosives?”

“No.”

“Trap stuff?”

“You’ll need to be more specific, Junior Agent.”

“Right. Well, I can just make stuff with metal if I want to do that.”

“Correct. Miscellaneous implements of other materials are also feasible.”

Highfive asked, “Can we have vehicles? A tank? Artillery? Mountable machinegun? Mortar system? Grenades? Archimedes heat ray? Robot armies?”

“No to all of those things, Junior Agent.”

“Aww. Not even like, one small robot army? Like, hand-sized robots?”

“No.”

“Damn.” With everyone staring at him, he shrugged with his hands. “What?! Worth asking.”

Jack had his brows furrowed. “Am I hearing things, or did you say ‘Archimedes heat ray?’ I’m hearing things, right?”

“Naw, bro!” Highfive got an excited look on his face as he scooched over closer and gestured out with a hand, from the sun, down. “It was a big ass mirror, and it, like, focused the hot sun to burn shit like a giant magnifying glass! And look at that sun! It’s bright as hell!”

Jack, brows remaining furrowed, nodded and took a deep breath. “I can’t believe you knew about this, and I didn’t.”He grinned suddenly. “But you’re right, that’s awesome!”

“I know, right?! I remember it ‘cause I was obsessed with it as a kid, and then the other day-”

“Wait.” Jack interrupted Highfive, turning his head to the side again. “Why can’t we have mirrors, Sim Chamber?”

“You can secure mirrors. There is simply no ‘Archimedes heat ray.’ Not in stock or even technically in existence at all.”

“Got it. Hmm. Actually, it's probably not worth it.”

“I’m glad you finally figured that out, genius,” Bo said sarcastically. “I already requisitioned a bunch of small wireless cameras. Should be here in an hour.”

“Perfect. Let’s drill our prep period over and over, I say. Until we’re efficient. I need more metal, though. I’ll make models of the cameras for now, so send me the look…”

They proceeded over the remaining hours they had left, drilling down the seven-minute prep period to get everything they wanted done. Ultimately, they decided to haul some soundproofing insulation that Jack could fit between two layers of metal walls, which they would use for a smaller room within the ground floor of the keep. The best stuff for compactness seemed to be a type of expanding spray foam that Inkblot suggested. Highfive could handle doing that while Jack did other shit, then come back to contour the final layers of their new room. Ideally, they’d open and attack the approaching enemy after they moved into the keep, right through the wall, possibly after Chime tried his little tricks, possibly before to pull an ambush.

As Jack created fields and set up simple, triggerable traps, Bo would place cameras inside, and Inkblot would place them outside — they wanted the sneakiest guy doing that part, though maybe it didn’t matter during the prep period. They both got pretty efficient, even with two-dozen different camera placements, so Jack started having them haul metal to different spots to save time.

The majority of traps were just carefully-placed and variably well-hidden metal Jack could use when enemies were near. Seven-minutes was difficult to work with, especially considering he was not a ‘trap master’ by any means. Primarily, he hoped to make them paranoid and slow their approach. At that point, they had no hope of any form of ambush, and Team Highlife could counterstrike at an opportune point when they were grouped. Ideally, directly from the protected room. Their enemies needed to come to them, and fortunately, though they had good range and overall speed, they were not that well suited to hit-and-run. To finish anyone off, they relied on intricate teamwork that required them all to be nearby.

Jack would try at least one or two things, as his efficiency kept increasing at the basic stuff. It was just a matter of experimenting and practice.

The drills were mad scrambles as they trained, and a lot of repetitive hard work, but what they set up was well worth it, to Jack’s mind. Highfive was completely shit with the spray foam at first, making a big, chaotic mess, but after the fourth or fifth reset, he was getting the hang of it and even seemed to be enjoying himself and taking pride in his handiwork.

It was something of a shame they were ‘wasting’ most of the concept of defending a heavy fortress, but obviously, that was just a terrible idea considering who they were facing. It might’ve even been an antiquated pastime — there were liabilities to the ancient ways, in modern warfare. Perhaps that was the lesson intended for them. The fortress did have the advantage of an extremely inconvenient mass in multiple layers that had to be navigated. A very thick shell to get to the nut. They couldn’t keep the shell together by any means, so the best bet was making sure the enemy expended themselves before inevitably getting it, and then making the ‘getting’ more than they bargained for.

As they were finishing the last drill, collectively pooling in the front courtyard, wiping their sweaty brows in the simulated heat — and Jack was considering moving on to the real test — Mini chimed in his head. <Thirty minutes until the mandatory dinner! Please cease all training and get ready.>

Highfive groaned. “Here we go! Another chance for Délight and her crew to mess with us. Yay.”

“I’m counting on it,” Bo said, face sweaty as she huffed and puffed from exertion. In her case, the particular type of fatigue was the easier kind. She hadn’t complained even once. “Didn’t you say you wanted to mess with Defiant, anyway, Fiver?”

Highfive blinked. “Oh. Right. I did, didn’t I?”

Bo rolled her eyes and turned to Jack. “Anyway, when we get back, enough drills. We got it. We need to move on to testing against the sims again. For all we know, this won’t do shit, and we’ll need to adjust.”

Jack frowned, but nodded at the same time. “Yep. Agreed. We’ll do our prep for real, fully gear up and everything, with the sims coming for us in seven minutes.”

Everyone nodded their agreement.

They broke to go take showers and such. Augur was sullen at being banned from going, as he wanted to ‘see what happened,’ but he got to eat dinner with Ira, so his protests were a bit muted.

Remembering he had some absurdly strong, musky cologne he’d gotten as a gift years ago, Jack threw copious amounts of it on. Nothing would throw off a face-to-face stare-down like eye-watering levels of cologne! He was just that kinda troll.

Jack smirked at the hypothetical image of a dude getting in his face and suddenly wishing quite desperately that he’d not.

Wrinkling his nose at the burning smell hitting his nostrils, Jack nonetheless slapped his hands together and spoke into his bathroom mirror. “Let’s do this, champ!”

🌑 🌒 🌓 🌔 🌕

Jack put his dinner order in before arriving, then met up with his teammates at the table, to which he was narrowly the last to sit down.

Bo sniffed the air with a perturbed look on her face. “What the unholy hell is that smell?”

“Some cheap, musky shit,” Highfive muttered, looking around externally for the source. “Bet it’s that guy. He looks like the type.”

“I’m afraid the source is much closer to home,” Inkblot said, giving Jack an uncharacteristically perturbed and judgmental look.

The other two did double-takes following Inkblot’s eyes, and they looked both shocked and offended at the implication.

“No,” Bo exclaimed in horror. “It can’t be!”

“Settle down!” Jack called, fighting off laughter as he took his seat. “I just want anyone who tries that facedown bullshit to pay for it.”

“And yet, we’re paying for it right now! Gah!” Bo covered her nose and mouth. “Where the frag did you get that shit, anyway? A country feed store?”

“I actually don’t remember who, but it was a gift. Anyway, it isn’t bad in small amounts. I just deliberately overdid it.”

Highfive was shaking his head. “Bro. This ain’t it. It just ain’t, man.”

Jack grinned devilishly at his friend. “I’m glad you like it. You can borrow it if you want.”

“I could wrestle a skunk and be less skunky, so, yeah, no thanks.”

“Your loss.”

A random guy walking by made a loud scoffing noise and grimaced, looking at their table in disgust, like it had a bag of shit hidden somewhere. He shook his head and kept walking — notably faster than before.

Bo was glaring at Jack with her inner shirt up over her nose. “The one time we need to show solidarity as a team, so I can’t send you to the shame corner where you belong…”

Jack just kept grinning, intent on being proud of himself for his brilliant tactical acumen.

Their food came. Jack ate greedily, and, despite his protestations, Highfive did as well. Inkblot ate slowly and methodically, as usual. Bo only picked at her food, lost in thought in between casting eyes across the area.

The table of their enemy could just barely be seen between the interposed bodies of other mess hall denizens. What flashes Jack saw, it was mostly Chime and Hiccup, giving cool glances their way as they ate. He caught sight of Délight once, speaking what seemed low-key passionately with her eyes flickering over the table, an elbow propped and pressing three fingers to her temple. Whatever it was she was expressing, she was deep in that expression.

A moment later, his view was blocked.

It was strange. This was not the sort of image he expected of her. And they hadn’t come over yet. More evidence of her change.

He knew Defiant was sitting next to her, but he was always blocked from Jack’s view.

Bo seemed to be getting more and more agitated. After another twist around, she shook her head in frustration and met Jack’s eyes. “You seeing that? They’re arguing. Screw it — let’s capitalize and go to them. Yeah?”

Jack blinked and met the eyes of his other teammates, gauging the mood. Highfive nodded slowly, once, and it was enough that an understanding subtly passed between them. They took their last drinks, wiped their faces, and stood, letting the chairs screech loudly and deliberately.

This announcement brought many eyes to them, and their enemy’s table responded quickly after looking over and seeing them slowly approach — all but one. As her teammates stood up and made their chairs screech, too, Délight took a deep, frustrated breath and sank down in her seat defeatedly, as if they had just ruined a long-term work. She swallowed whatever she was about to say and dropped her head down, shaking it and sighing.

Wow. Are we the bad guys, now? Maybe that’s a good thing for a night and a day. Sorry, Délight, but you should’ve expected to suffer for your sudden change of heart. Ideals cost you.

Jack was amazed to see a few younger students seated at a table between the two groups, currently looking between them with wide eyes. They promptly picked up their trays and their things and scurried away.

He tried not to laugh, to maintain the collective intimidating swagger of the rest of his crew. This shit is like out of a movie!

Inkblot suddenly connected with a personal memlink. “You’re overdoing it. Strutting. Relax. You don’t care how you’re seen. You’re above it all. Superior. No trying necessary.”

Jack just sent back a vague acknowledgement, otherwise taking his teammate’s advice and getting into that mindset, relaxing a bit at the very least. It wasn’t hard to stop caring, considering that was the default for him. As it was, he was play-acting to look ‘tough.’ Perhaps that was unnecessary, then?

The enemy team had ended up hanging back way more than they likely wanted, because Délight was refusing to rise despite their consternation. It definitely threw their game off. Délight had her elbows on the table, two hands at her head, with her eyes not looking at anyone. Her hair was currently in a ponytail, with numerous strands broken free and dangling in her face.

Just before they reached the table, Chime, Hiccup, and Defiant recovered from their disruptive uncertainty to come around and meet their enemy interlopers, everyone stony-faced and cocky. Defiant immediately went for Highfive, which was pretty comical, considering Defiant looked like a figurine next to the giant, the top of Defiant’s head barely coming up to his rival’s chin. Highfive had just the right face painted on for this: dismissive and mocking. Defiant was unaffected, however, his eyes unwavering and steely.

Jack resisted the urge to comment in the team gestalt that he was staring down Highfive quite defiantly. Womp womp womp.

Unsurprisingly, yet another girl taller than Bo stared her down — Hiccup’s expression was definitely one of more convincing spite than Highfive’s. On the other hand, Bo’s height hardly seemed a detriment, because she had a far more menacing aura to her.

And then Chime approached Jack, ignoring Inkblot — which made sense. No one wanted to try staring that guy down. It was like staring into a black hole. Nothing would come of it.

Technically, that was perfect for Délight, as she would have a different approach to begin with. If anyone might throw that guy off-balance, it was her. But she refused to rise.

Chime’s cocky face twisted into something else as he neared, and he stopped short of his target, grimacing and putting his hand over his mouth, looking at Jack incredulously, not believing the source of the smell at first.

Hiccup was also distracted as she sniffed the air and immediately grimaced, her eyes reluctantly moving away from Bo’s gaze. “What the steamin’ shit is that smell?!”

Chime looked at Jack in disgust and shook his head. “What the hell is wrong with you, man?”

Jack grinned the most shit-eating grin in the world, still entirely shameless. “Everything, loser. Everything.”
