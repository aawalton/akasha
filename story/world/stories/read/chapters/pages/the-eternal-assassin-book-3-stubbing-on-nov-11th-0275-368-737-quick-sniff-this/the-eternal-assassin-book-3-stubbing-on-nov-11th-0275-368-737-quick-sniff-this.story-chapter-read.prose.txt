With barely five minutes to go before the destruction of almost half of the Noala Kingdom, there wasn’t a whole lot of time for talking or strategizing. Asher and Samantha arrived at the conference room just in time to witness Donvath shouting at the Eight Crests’ team what they needed to do to save Dormaul. As soon as Mako confirmed they would do what they could to save the mountain city, Richard grabbed them and the four vanished in a flash of violet.

Makes sense. My Eternal Mark is located in the center of the mountain city’s third layer. I doubt they could go from the middle of the mountain up to the surface in only five minutes.

Once he realized he probably wasn’t going to be used as a transportation service, Asher focused on his own problems. Grabbing Rulfar the moment he rushed into the room, he dragged the confused bloodhound over to Groln and his team.

“We think we should trade members,” Asher said without preamble as he pointed toward the blue woman giving him a curious look. “We’re heading to Poltar, which is a city on the water. Rulfar’s not going to be of any use there, but he can help you fight a giant golem.”

“Sarah, your call,” Groln said as he eyed up Rulfar. They could all hear Donvath frantically shouting what he needed the members of Amanda’s Order to do in the background, but Groln ignored the panicked High Prince. “What do you think?”

“I’ll join them,” Sarah smiled while she finished securing a bracer on her arm. Only then did Asher realize her weapons of choice seemed to be dual bracers, each with a large, scythe-like blade curving out to the side. “If you need a warrior in the water, I’m your gal. What are we fighting?”

“Enraged sea serpents,” Asher said, blinking at the brilliant flash of violet light that indicated Richard had returned from dropping off Mako’s team. “It’s a lot to ask, but if you and Samantha can keep the city safe from the sea serpents, I can try to hunt down the lich controlling them. You should have at least some backup from the Malrron family. They’re the nobles in charge of the city.”

“Let’s hope they can fight,” Moxy snorted as she jogged over to them and gave them an excited, feral grin.

“Leave it to us!” Sarah said, smiling and throwing an arm around Samantha’s shoulders. She was careful not to accidentally cut her head off with her wicked bracers, which Asher was incredibly grateful for.

“You’re up to taking on a giant stone golem?” Groln asked Rulfar. The demonic man had a slight frown on his face as he stared down at Rulfar, clearly weighing whether or not he thought the new temporary member of his team would be of any use. Rather than respond, Rulfar pulled his great axe off his back and activated his battle form. His already large body grew taller and more muscular as fur stretched over him and his features shifted, and Groln’s eyes widened a tad when the seven-foot-tall bloodhound went from being shorter than him to suddenly towering over him by a good two feet.

“I’ll do what I can,” Rulfar grunted, now forced to keep his head bent so that he wasn’t scraping it along the ceiling.

“You’re next,” Richard panted. Wiping sweat from his brow, he motioned for Groln’s team to gather round. “I don’t know where the golem originates from, so I’m placing you in the center of the city. I figure you’ll spot it easily enough once it is constructed.”

“Understood,” Groln said. The royal slayer looked like this sort of last-second mission with no real explanation was second nature to him, and he threw Sarah one last grin. “Good luck out there.”

The moment they were gone, Asher threw up a small Spatial Lock, not willing to waste any more time than they already had. Sarah recoiled slightly and gave the near-invisible barrier a strange look, but there was quite literally no time to explain. Focusing on the mark he’d left over in Poltar, the world shifted as they traveled halfway across the kingdom in an instant.

Originally, Asher’s Eternal Mark in Poltar had been left in a tavern that was recommended for people new to the city. Though rather than continue to pop into a tavern and scare the hell out of the room’s current occupants anytime he needed to return to the city, he had switched the location of his mark before long. Now his mark took him directly outside of the small home that belonged to Sefta, the noble woman who they had befriended during their stay in Poltar. The home itself was almost constantly moving around the floating city as Sefta tried to hide from her mother, which meant Asher wasn’t all that surprised to discover they appeared on the edge of the city rather than in the center where the home had been last he’d journeyed to Poltar.

“We’ve got four minutes, if that, before the attack starts!” Asher told them the moment they appeared. “Let’s hurry!”

“I can create an underwater whirlpool below the city to stop the serpents from smashing straight up from underneath!” Sarah said as she flashed them a confident smile. “Don’t worry, I’ll be able to keep it from damaging the city. It might just spin around a little bit.”

“Do it!” Asher nodded, watching as Sarah turned and executed a flawless dive straight off the side of the city into the water. Turning to Samantha, he threw his arms around her, pulling her close for a quick hug. “Are you going to be okay without your bow?”

“I’ve practiced fighting underwater with just my wings. It’s not my preferred style, but I’m pretty dangerous down there,” she grinned before giving him a kiss and shoving her bow and quiver into his hands. “Hold onto these?”

“Always,” he said, handing her a potion of Darkvision to drink in return. The moment Samantha vanished into the water, Asher turned toward Moxy. “Find a member of the Malrron family as fast as you can and explain what’s about to happen. We’re going to need their help!”

“Can do!” Moxy said, giving him a lazy salute before turning and blasting herself up onto the roof of a nearby building with a burst of air. Based on the crash of shattering roof tiles it didn’t sound like she’d bothered landing gently, and she began shouting at the top of her lungs as she ran into the city. “Malrron family, I challenge you!”

Asher could only shake his head, but he didn’t have time to admonish her. Not to mention it was the middle of the night and there wasn’t anyone wandering the streets, so that might actually be the fastest way to find the people they needed.

Focusing on his own task, Asher slipped into the astral and ran into Sefta’s house. It had been months since he’d last seen the noble woman, and he hoped beyond hope that she’d returned from her trip to the capital. He was currently down a bloodhound and needed some way to find the lich responsible for the mayhem that was to come.

Luckily, he happened to know that Sefta was also quite skilled when it came to tracking.

“Sefta!” he shouted after he reappeared in her living room. His eyes scanned over the tiny trident darts lodged into the wall beneath the dartboard and the scattered plates containing food remains that really needed to be washed. Sefta’s little hideaway home was basically the definition of a bachelor’s pad, which felt appropriate seeing as she was only hiding in the first place because her mother kept trying to set her up with noble men without her permission. Just as he was preparing to pick one of the random doors and risk barging into her bedroom, the first door splintered as a water-propelled harpoon blasted through it, shooting straight through where he’d been standing a moment ago before slamming into the wall hard enough to crack it.

“Is that you, Four?” Sefta called out. Cracking her neck, she casually strolled out of her bedroom, walking over the shattered remains of her door without a care in the world. He could only assume his shout had woken her up, because the noblewoman was as naked as the day she was born. Sefta didn’t seem bothered by that fact in the slightest, however, as she looked around the room with a large, serrated grin on her face. “Finally come back to Poltar, have you?”

“I hope to God that’s not how you greet your mother when she surprises you,” Asher said, causing Sefta to blink when he returned to the material realm.

“Asher?” she asked. It took her a few seconds to recover from her initial shock, but once she had, she belted out a deep laugh. Walking over to him, her grin turned from predatory to excited as she punched him in the arm. “It’s been too long! Here for a late-night sparring session?”

I’d forgotten that Sefta was almost as bad as Moxy, Asher thought while he shook his head. Her aura was the same as ever, revealing itself to his eyes as a dark blue whirlpool of thrumming energy that longed for battle. If it weren’t for the anchor chains representing her family’s expectations holding her back, she probably would have turned into some wild slayer years ago.

“No time for sparring, I need your help,” he said, trying to figure out where to even begin. “Sorry to drop in like this, but Poltar is in danger, and we need to track down a lich that is about to try and destroy the city. I’ve got some powerful warriors already prepared to intercept, but there’s a giant nest of enraged sea serpents probably already on their way to the surface. They can fight off the serpents, but the attack’s not going to end until we finish off the lich. I remember how you were able to track down the demons that attacked those caravans months back using your Sea Serpent element. Think you can find a lich?”

“A lich?” Sefta repeated, frowning as she ripped her harpoon free from the wall and slung it over her shoulders like a baseball bat. She still seemed unbothered by the fact that she was completely nude, and she made a face before shrugging. “I track stuff via blood. Sea serpents can smell blood from miles away, in or out of the water. Liches don’t even have blood, right?”

“They have a little… it’s just all nasty and rotten,” Asher admitted. He’d beheaded enough liches to know by now that their ‘blood’ was little more than a thick, brown sludge. “I’ve got the remains of another lich on me right now. It’s not the same person’s blood, but it should be equally rotten. I’d be willing to bet that thousand-year-old blood has a pretty distinct scent.”

“You’re just carrying an ancient corpse with you?” Sefta asked. Raising an eyebrow, she looked him over. “...Where?”

Rolling his eyes, Asher reached into his Personal Rift and dragged Eupher’s corpse out. For her part, Sefta didn’t even seem surprised by the sudden appearance of a horribly emaciated human being. Sniffing curiously, she recoiled back, retching as she shook her head.

“Realms above, that thing is foul! Yeah, if there’s another being close by with blood as nasty as that one, I’ll be able to find it no problem.”

“Perfect!” Asher said, letting out a sigh of relief he hadn’t realized he’d been holding. He hadn’t exactly had a backup plan in the event that Sefta hadn’t been home or proved unable to track the lich down. “Can you please put on some clothes and lead me to the lich? We don’t ha—”

A cacophony of eerie, warbling screeches that seemed to echo in Asher’s own soul radiated from beneath their feet, sounding like an army from the underworld rising up to grab their ankles. His heart sank as he recognized the sound from the time he’d spent hunting in Poltar’s waters. He’d heard a single sea serpent roar before, but he’d never heard what sounded like dozens of them crying out at the same time. If Moxy hadn’t managed to wake any of the Malrron family yet, she wouldn’t have to worry about that part of her mission any longer.

The lich’s attack had officially begun.
