… "Say, High Elders, have you ever heard the words 'Miracle Rain' before?"

The entire hall fell silent at Elder Qarris’s question, and the High Elders exchanged glances in confusion.

“I fear you have lost us, Elder Qarris.” The First Seat spoke after a moment, his brows scrunched in curiosity. "What is this rain you speak of?"

Elder Qarris's weathered face creased into a smile. "It's a skill, First Seat. A transcendent skill created by one of our own transmigrants."

"What?"

Gasps echoed across the hall as the elders processed his words in shock. A transcendent skill was a skill created after the formation of one's inner world. In other words, it was a skill that could only be created by calamities.

But even among Calamities, transcendent skills were a rarity.

To suggest that a transmigrant at the advanced class had created one was just—

"Preposterous." High-Elder Velora barked out a laugh. "Surely, you exaggerate, Qarris. This is a joke."

Everyone turned their gaze to Elder Qarris, expecting to see a smile on the man's face that would hint that this was all a stupid joke. But Elder Qarris didn't smile. Instead, his gaze remained calm as he stared back at the High Elders.

"I do not believe I am exaggerating, High Elder Velora," Qarris said quietly, his ancient gaze piercing. "Now, I may be wrong, but I'm certain of what I saw. I am not exaggerating."

Unease spread through the hall as elders exchanged weighted glances. Two weeks ago, none of them would have hesitated to say such a thing was impossible. Even transmigrants could only bend the rules of cultivation so far.

But after all they had learned of Aodhán in the past few weeks, none of them were willing to take that risk.

All except Zarxan.

"Elder Qarris," he began firmly. "I do not mean to call you a liar, but it is impossible for any of our transmigrants to have such a skill. They are all still at the advanced class."

"And yet I know what I saw, High Elder Zarxan." Qarris replied, his gaze hard. "I wouldn't be here if I didn't."

Silence descended on the hall once more, then Elder Tharn spoke. "Okay, say you're right, Elder Qarris—"

"I am right, High-Elder Tharn. I know what I saw. I know what a transcendent skill looks like."

"Okay, let's all calm down." High Elder Mystral spoke up, raising a hand to instill order. "If Elder Qarris says he knows what he saw, then he knows what he saw." She glanced at the aged elder. "Please tell us, which transmigrant created this… miracle rain. Was it Sophran?"

"No, High Elder Mystral." Elder Qarris shook his head, then glanced at Zarxan. "It's Aodhán Brystion."

The chamber erupted once more, and Zarxan sighed as if he'd already seen this coming. If there was one person who could turn the rules of cultivation on their head, it was Aodhán.

But to turn it this much was extreme even for Aodhán, and while Zarxan didn't push back, he quietly held his reservations.

As did the other High Elders.

Elder Qarris was powerful, but he was also very old, and old people made mistakes.

The First Seat must have shared his thoughts because he sighed deeply.

"You promised us good news, Elder Qarris," he said. "How does this Miracle Rain help the war effort?"

Elder Qarris straightened at that, seeming to gain a couple decades as he smiled. "I think it would be better if I just showed you."

Zarxan perked up at that, his eyes narrowing curiously as Elder Qarris pulled out a memory crystal from his spatial space and raised it into the air. The other High Elders leaned forward too, their expressions curious as Elder Qarris began to speak.

"This is the memory of the librarian, Elder Kudrov, and the marvel she witnessed just before she called me."

He activated the crystal then, and despite his reservations, Zarxan found himself leaning forward, his gaze intent as a scene from the library was projected for them all to see.

The memory showed Aodhán from behind, authority radiating from him as nine droplets of golden rain fell in a perfect circle around him, their power tangible even through the memory.

Silence blanketed the entire hall as they all watched the droplets splash against the heads of nine disciples, all of whom seemed to gain inspiration immediately.

The memory faded a few minutes later, and in the silence that stretched, Elder Qarris spoke. "The miracle rain is an inspiration catalyst, a skill so powerful it induces instant inspirations on all whom it touches." He looked directly at Zarxan and added, "As of the time I left the library, all nine disciples had gained seals."

"What!" The First Seat cursed as the hall erupted in disbelief. "Did you just say seals, Qarris? As in multiple?"

"Yes, First Seat." Elder Qarris replied, his smile wide. "At the time I left, the majority of them had gained two seals already, with Elder Marcellan's son gaining three."

The hall erupted once more, but this time, it wasn't with whispers.

Noise filled the air as several elders began talking at the same time, their eyes wide as the implications of Elder Qarris's words settled upon their minds.

Zarxan himself couldn't believe it.

The branding of a seal was sacred—an agreement between a person and the Origin Plane of their affinity. A skill that could facilitate that couldn't be just an ordinary skill.

He glanced at the other High Elders, unable to believe that what he was seeing was true. That somehow, someway, it seemed Elder Qarris could be right.

And if he was, if Aodhán had truly created a transcendent skill at the advanced class, then how much more powerful would the skill be at the Calamity class?

His mind had barely put the image together when Elder Qarris spoke again. "This is my good news, High Elders. If we use this skill in the war, it could revolutionize everything. If it's this powerful in the hands of an advanced cultivator, how much more powerful would it be in the hands of a Mythic or a Calamity? How much more effect would it have on the disciples?"

His words were like gasoline to a fire.

And the hall practically exploded with noise—disbelief, astonishment, and shock merging into a cacophony that all seemed to echo the same thing.

How had Aodhán managed to create such a skill?

The question didn't stem from disbelief or skepticism but from a desire for understanding—not just for themselves but for the sect as a whole. And after a long moment of chaos, a firm voice finally broke through.

It was Zarxan's.

"Has the skill been tested before this?" he asked, his voice thundering within the hall. "Do you know if it has any side effects or dangers?"

The hall quieted at that, their gazes turning to Elder Qarris, who seemed stumped by the question, having never thought that far. Fortunately, he wasn't alone, and after a moment of hesitation, Mirelle stepped forward, bowing.

"Good evening, elders and High-Elders. If I may be given the opportunity to speak?"

"Go ahead." The First Seat spoke when he recognized her, and she nodded.

"The skill has been tested before. I have tested it, and I can say it only has one drawback." She hesitated a moment. "The twisted effect."

For a heartbeat there was silence, then High-Elder Zarxan spoke. "Then it has no drawback save for the natural effect of gaining seals." He let out a shuddering breath and fixed his gaze on her. "You said you tested the skill. How many seals did you gain from it?"

"Just one, High Elder Al'ruhn." Mirelle replied carefully. "But it wasn't Aodhán who created the rain I tested. It was Darien, and it was nothing compared to what Aodhán created today."

"Darien?" High-Elder Velora asked, her eyes sharpening as she glanced at the other High-Elders. "That isn't possible. Even if we agree that a transmigrant created a transcendent skill due to his peculiar nature, a double inheritor couldn't have replicated it. This is not a transcendent skill."

That realization caused another eruption of noise within the chamber, with Elder Qarris coloring in embarrassment.

But his embarrassment didn't last long, nor did their judgment, because the skill not being a transcendent skill only made it better. It meant the skill could be taught. Replicated. Perhaps even perfected.

Almost as one, the elders turned their gazes to the First Seat, who in turn glanced at Zarxan. "What do you suggest, High Elder Al'ruhn? He's your ward."

Zarxan stared at the First Seat for a moment, then at the elders below before nodding, his voice thundering in the silent hall.

"Invite Aodhán to the hall. Perhaps we can learn more about this rain skill from the creator himself."

Almost immediately, Elder Ghaltris and one other elder rushed out to carry out his order, and Zarxan sighed.

In less than two weeks, Aodhán had wrought more changes on the sect and the Origin Plane than he, a Calamity, had managed in the past three centuries.

And he was still only in the advanced class.

Just how much more impact would he have made by the 100th tier?

How much more would he work if he could be convinced to stay to the end?

How much more powerful would the sect be with him at its head?

These questions raced through Zarxan's mind, and he realized with even more certainty that he had to convince Aodhán to stay. To leave his family in Lutia and stay.

His fate was obviously tied to the sect.

He just had to make Aodhán see it.

After his discussion with Elder Vasmos, Aodhán set out to find Dravion Ashscar so they could head to the library together. He said goodbye to Elder Kobi and Elder Sedgwick, who informed him that Elder Luseph had taken off a few minutes earlier for something urgent.

He wondered what the urgent matter was, but since they didn't seem too worried, he didn't dwell too much on it. He thanked them, and with a core sense spread out, he began his search for Dravion.

He found the crimson-haired transmigrant about five minutes later, talking animatedly with a swarm of early-tier Mythics, their gazes attentive as he described his time in the world rift to them.

It was a weird sight, to say the least.

Mythics, regardless of rank or power, weren't supposed to look up to Specials. They were elders, for crying out loud. Yet the deference these elders showed Dravion was undeniable. It was obvious in their gazes, the tilt of their necks, and their entire posture—as if they could bow to him right there and then.

At first Aodhán wondered if it was because Dravion had gained his familiar at such an early tier, but he soon dismissed that thought. If that was the case, then the elders would treat him the same way since he'd done the same thing.

But that wasn't the case, which meant there was more to it.

Drawing closer, he called out, "Dravion." Are you ready to go?"

Dravion turned to glance at him, his smile waning a bit. "Oh no, sorry, we were just getting to the good part of the story."

The elders nodded in agreement, and Dravion's smile brightened once again. He glanced back at Aodhán. "Do you want to join us?"

"No." Aodhán shook his head. "I was heading to the library to check on Sophran and the others."

"Oh." Remembrance flashed in his eyes, and for a moment he seemed to consider following. But Dravion eventually shook his head. "No, you go ahead. I'm not ready to leave yet."

Aodhán stared at him for a moment before nodding. "Okay."

He bowed at the elders seated like children around Dravion and turned away, feeling a sudden disconnect in his emotions. In the last few hours since he'd known Dravion, he hadn't been given much reason to cultivate a friendship with him.

Not only was he impolite, but he also seemed to care more about his reputation than his friends, which was a no in Aodhán's book.

Turning his gaze to the skies, he reached out to Varéc and felt a surge of excitement thrum through Psychic Link. He smiled at that, genuinely happy for Varéc, who seemed to have found his first friend in Giselle.

He wasn't just happy. He was ecstatic. And in that hovering, protective way parents usually were, Aodhán decided to take a look. He created a lightning platform and stepped on it, eager to see what a friendship between familiars would look like.

However, he'd barely floated a dozen meters above ground when an elder suddenly appeared before him, his gold insignia marking him as important. But it wasn't just the insignia. The man radiated a powerful aura, with crimson-slashed silver hair and a gaze seemingly cast in stone.

He was dressed in a battle robe crafted from layered stormfiber, and when he smiled, Aodhán thought he recognized the man even though he'd never seen him before.

"Good evening, elder." He bowed in greeting. "How can I help you?"

The elder smiled. "Good evening, Aodhán Brystion. I'm Benjamin Ghaltris. I hear you met my daughter at the Tempest trial, Serapha."

"Oh." Aodhán's eyes widened in realization, and he smiled. "I did, sir. How is she doing? I heard she made it into the core sect."

"She did." Elder Ghaltris nodded. "But she would have been more comfortable in the special sect. She's a special child, you see."

Realization dawned on Aodhán, and his smile wavered slightly. "I already have an underling, Elder Ghaltris."

"And you can have one more," he replied without hesitation, his smile widening. "Just think about it."

Aodhán grimaced but nodded. "I will."

"Good." Elder Ghaltris smiled. "Now, for what I really came here for. I have an invite for you from the High Elders. They wish to speak to you concerning your Miracle Rain skill and the specials currently locked in seal chambers in the library basement."

Aodhán paused, surprised at how fast the news had reached the High Elders. He doubted he would be getting in trouble for it, as he hadn't done anything wrong. Still, he asked, "Is something wrong?"

"Don't worry, you're not in any trouble or anything." Elder Ghaltris spun a portal of lightning into existence and gestured for Aodhán to go in. "They simply want to speak to you."

Aodhán glanced at the portal, which was more of a gate than any portal he had ever seen before, and nodded. He could see the elder hall clearly from this side, and with a sharp inhale, he stepped through.

They arrived at the hall in an instant, and a few seconds later, the massive doors were pushed open, releasing a tangible wave of power into the corridor. The wave engulfed him, oppressive and suffocating even without intent, and when he stepped into the hall, he realized why.

Aside from the six High Elders, the hall was filled with dozens of powerful Mythics, many of whom stood at the same level of power as Elder Ghaltris. These were the pillars of the sect, the ones involved in the running of the sect, and even with their auras leashed, all of them together was a sight to behold.

Exhaling deeply as eyes turned to him, Aodhán bowed deeply. "Good evening, High Elders. You summoned me."

"No," the First Seat spoke, his voice thundering against the walls of the room, yet warm. "We invited you, Aodhán. You could have declined the request, and we would have borne no grudge."

Aodhán didn't believe that for a second. He smiled. "I could never say no to you, First Seat. What do you need from me?"

The High-Elders all glanced at Zarxan, and Aodhán followed their gaze, his expression scrunching in worry as his gaze locked with Zarxan's, noting how troubled he looked.

"Good evening, Aodhán." Zarxan began, his voice gentle yet bearing an undeniable undercurrent of power that sent chills across the hall. Aodhán shivered despite himself, and Zarxan smiled. "It's barely been two weeks since you joined the sect, and already, you've proven to us all how truly special you are."

The High Elders all nodded as he continued. "First, you toppled every record ever set on the Mountain of Tempest, you gave us the idea and blueprint to create the chaos interface, and now, it has come to our knowledge that you possess a skill capable of granting seals to others. A skill that in the right hands could turn the tide of this war in an instant. A miracle rain. Is this true?"

Aodhán only hesitated a moment before nodding. "It is, High Elder Al'ruhn. But it doesn't grant seals to others. It simply condenses whatever fragments of insights they've gathered on their own into an inspiration. It isn't a miracle rain. It is simply a catalyst."

Zarxan nodded. "Even better. But that's not the only thing it does, is it?"

"No, it is not." Aodhán replied, then proceeded to describe the effect Miracle Rain had had on the war against the Sunstonians. He described the enhanced clarity, the enhanced speed, the sharpened instincts, the world slowing…

Shocked whispers rose with everything he said, and when he was done, High-Elder Mystral asked, "So you mean it's possible to avoid granting seals to disciples on the battlefield?"

Aodhán smiled. "Not entirely, but I've learned that it depends on the amount of power I infuse into the rain or the quantity of raindrops I'm infusing."

"Wow." Zarxan shook his head, his expression proud. "Did you create the skill yourself?"

"Yes, I did."

Zarxan nodded. The hall fell silent. And a heartbeat later, he said, "Since you created the skill yourself, then you can teach it." His gaze grew somber, uneasy. "Can you teach it to us?"

Everyone turned to Aodhán immediately—elders and high elders alike—their rigid postures bellying their anticipation and unease at asking such a direct favor from someone younger and weaker.

Unlike in Lutia, where the sharing and teaching of skills meant very little, here in Gordon, where paths and spiritual insight were taken more seriously, asking for the details of a person's skills was like asking for information on their path.

And in a case like this, where Calamities were the ones doing the asking from a mere advanced awakened, it was even more unsettling for them. Aodhán, however, had no problem sharing the details of his skill. He had shared it with Sophran and Darien already. Refusing to do so now only meant delaying the inevitable.

Besides, he wanted the sect to have this skill. If it meant losing fewer people to the war, Aodhán was more than ready to do it.

What he wasn't ready to do, however, was to pass up a good opportunity for growth in the name of altruism. If he could gain something from this, then he most certainly would.

Smiling, he bowed. "I would be happy to share the details of the Miracle Rain… on one condition."

Blooming smiles halted midway, and a sudden tension thickened the atmosphere. Silence reigned for a moment, then Zarxan asked, "What is your condition?"

Aodhán nodded. "I will teach you all the skill, but in turn, you'll each owe me one infusion to be collected at my discretion until I reach the Mythic class. That is six bestowments for the next sixty tiers." He smiled. "That's what I ask."

It wasn't a simple request. Aodhán knew. He was asking Calamities to bestow their energy, willpower, and quintessence upon him. It was so much more than what he had done in the library, not just because he was weaker than them, but because the energies of Calamities were steeped in concepts and understanding so far above anything he could imagine.

Even if they used the same number of seals, the droplet they would produce would far outstrip any he could conjure. What he was asking for was a treasure beyond compare—six treasures, to be exact.

So, no. It wasn't a simple request at all.

But despite knowing all that, Aodhán still didn't realize the gravity of what he was asking for. Beyond just the concepts and energy that surpassed his own was the meaning behind the action of every infusion—a process that the High Elders had realized was far from ordinary.

Everyone Aodhán had bestowed his Miracle Rain upon had been changed by his power in one way or another, their paths stained by his understanding of the storm. For most people like Yue or the soldiers of Ragnarok, it wasn't too obvious. But for people like Aldric and Crixis, Aodhán's understanding had become a building block in theirs—the trajectory of their paths forever altered by that single infusion.

Aldric had gained a title, and Crixis had had his entire path changed.

The process of infusion wasn't ordinary at all. It was an imprinting of approval on the path of a person. A karmic tie. An anointing.

And if the Calamities, with their exceeding knowledge and perfected paths, did the same to him, they would leave an imprint on his path too. An imprint that would not only approve his path but reinforce it—imbuing him with knowledge, power, and insight far surpassing anything even Mythics could handle.

A path of Ruinous Authority backed by the power of six Calamities.

The repercussions would be disastrous, especially for someone with a newborn path.

And though Aodhán hadn't thought that far, the Calamities certainly had, and they hesitated.

Heavy silence reigned over the hall for nearly a minute until High-Elder Kaelthorn, who hadn't spoken a word all morning, finally did. "That… that is a very big ask, Aodhán."

"It is." Zarxan responded gravely, his gaze even more troubled than before. "You do not realize what you're asking."

Aodhán frowned, still not understanding. "I'm sorry. Am I missing something?"

"Yes, you are," High Elder Mystral replied, her gaze hard. "We are Calamities, Aodhán. It's not just an infusion you're asking for. It's karmic—"

"Mystral." The First Seat cut her off with a glare before turning his gaze back on Aodhán. "Tell me, Aodhán. What happens if we say no?"

Almost impossibly, the tension in the atmosphere thickened. The silence in the hall grew loud, and the gazes of dozens of Mythics fell on him like tangible weight. Aodhán glanced at the High Elders, his gaze searching theirs for a clue into what they were thinking.

He found none, and after a moment, he whispered, "If you say no, I'll still do it."

"Then my answer is no." The First Seat stated immediately, his voice thundering, and a moment later, High-Elder Mystral did the same, shaking her head. "It's a no for me too."

Aodhán stared at them in confusion for a moment before turning his gaze to the others, seeking an explanation. His gaze landed on Zarxan a moment later, but Zarxan's gaze held no answers. He stared blankly at Aodhán for a moment, worry brimming beneath the surface, and then he too spoke.

"No."

That single word struck Aodhán like a slap to the face, a betrayal, and he felt a spark of anger suddenly ignite within him. The High-Elders were playing a game he couldn't understand. They were testing him, and when the First Seat spoke next, Aodhán confirmed it.

"What do you say now, Aodhán?" the First Seat asked, a small smile on his face. "Are you still willing to teach us the skill?"

Aodhán glanced back at the First Seat, anger simmering within him. "I could have changed my conditions if they had been so unfavorable."

"We don't want you to change your conditions. We want to know if you'll teach us without conditions. Will you teach us?"

"Not until the other High Elders give me their response."

Another beat of silence stretched within the hall, many looking at him in shock at his tone, but Aodhán was too angry to care. He fixed his gaze on the First Seat, willpower surging within him to keep his body from shuddering as the silence grew louder.

And then High-Elder Kaelthorn spoke. "Yes. You can have my infusion."

"Mine too," High Elder Velora added, her smile wide. "Our paths resonate."

Aodhán smiled back at her and turned his gaze to the last elder. "What will your response be, High Elder Tharn?"

High-Elder Tharn regarded him for a moment, his gaze slightly troubled, before he too nodded. "Yes, you have my infusion."

Aodhán let out a sigh of relief at that, his smile wide. For information he would have given out for free had things gotten to that, three Calamitic infusions was a great profit.

He glanced at High-Elder Zarxan, his smile still on his face. But Zarxan didn't smile back.

He didn't smile back because, despite it all, despite the obvious game they had just played, Aodhán didn't understand.

He wanted to tell Aodhán what was going on. Why the other High Elders were so excited for this war. Why he'd said no to his request. But Zarxan knew that doing so would only chase Aodhán away rather than draw him close.

Aodhán wasn't ready to choose his affinity over his family. Not yet.

And until he was ready—until the sect became his only home—he was a liability.

Unfortunately, the other High Elders didn't understand that either.
