The very detailed plan I was constructing to brutally murder the blue box is tragically interrupted by the sound of people groaning and scrambling upright. Judging by the dramatic gasping and knee-holding, they must have collapsed earlier.

Tragic.

Honestly, watching them struggle to recover from their embarrassing lack of fortitude is slightly painful. They’re drawing more attention to their failure by being loud about it, like someone defending themselves after stealing the last chip from a blessedly perfect bag of chips.

I graciously decide to overlook their social crimes and finally glance around, only to be met with a world that is… more. Beyond the usual human range. I can see individual blades of grass swaying as a breeze ghosts over them, the veins in every leaf decorating the surrounding forest, every wrinkle on the clothing of the now-not-quite-human man standing ten meters away. And beyond him, dozens more, all wearing the same expression: a perfect blend of confusion, horror, and existential dread.

I blink.

…Interesting.

My gaze drifts across the collection of bodies lifting themselves off dirt and grass. Matching what I see to my extensive and totally healthy familiarity with fantasy races—information which I absolutely did not acquire while doom scrolling comparative ear charts from DnD homebrew forums—the picture becomes clear.

Elves with ridiculously pointy ears helping stout dwarves upright. Tiny gnomes and faeries staring in quiet fear at the now towering humanoids around them. Foxkin. Catkin. Wolfkin. A few things that look like someone randomized a character creator and hit “confirm” without hesitation.

I even spot someone with wings and a tail, almost draconic.

Everyone—and I mean everyone—has one of four weapons strapped somewhere on their person: longswords, wands clipped to belts, bows with quivers slung across backs, and long wooden staffs. The sight of several gnomes and faeries eyeing their comically miniature swords fills me with brief pity.

Brief.

Because something infinitely more important catches my attention: me.

My comfy black pajamas are gone, replaced with a short beige tunic that ends just below my hips, flowing into a matching skirt that stops just above my knees. Underneath, shorts. Thank every star in the cosmos.

I can still feel my favorite underwear—extremely comfortable, as all things should be, maximizing the eleven-and-a-half hours of beauty sleep crucial for maintaining my status as the most perfect, humble being alive.

A snug belt sits at my waist, keeping the tunic from misbehaving in the breeze, and holding a sheathed sword at my left hip. My feet are in simple leather boots with white socks peeking out.

And of course—trailing behind me—a snowy white fox tail sways lazily, poking through a conveniently placed gap in my tunic. Instinctively, I reach up and confirm: yes, there are extremely fluffy ears sitting atop my head.

I move my hand to the side of my head, discovering that my previous ears are completely gone, replaced by the soft fur of my newly acquired fox accessories.

Interesting.

Likely connecting to the same ear canal.

Highly convenient.

My mouth also feels… different.

I run my tongue over my teeth and pause as I find my canines are longer now. Sharper.

Noticeably so.

…

Mildly concerning.

Naturally, I react exactly the way any rational person would upon discovering they have been forcibly transformed into a fox girl.

I shrug.

Could’ve been worse.

Reluctantly, my attention shifts back to what the System said—specifically the cheerful part about “endless hordes of undead” and “working together to survive.”

Glancing at the jittery mass of newly-minted fantasy races awkwardly wiping grass and dirt off themselves, the logical conclusion becomes glaringly obvious.

Without hesitation, I spin on my heel and begin walking toward the forest full of monsters and probable death.

Dramatically stabbing a goblin to death is almost certainly going to be less of a headache than engaging in small talk.

Fortunately, everyone is too emotionally compromised to stop me. Which is ideal, otherwise I’d have to stab them too, and social murder is way harder to justify on day one.

Just kidding.

I would never stab someone over something so small.

…Probably.

Within moments I disappear into the treeline, following one of several rough paths leading away from the clearing. Once I’m well out of sight, I break into a run—letting the motion burn the last traces of irritation out of my bloodstream.

After a few minutes, I veer off the path and stop beneath a tree with a particularly inviting branch roughly five meters up. The foliage obscures it well—perfect for hiding.

I map the path upward automatically. A hop, a grab, a shift of weight, and then a fluid climb I’d like everyone to appreciate but sadly no one is here to witness. Tragic.

In seconds I’m perched comfortably on the branch, back against the trunk.

Time for a plan.

Mental checklist:

Step one: check status.

Step two: figure out how Inspect works and use it on everything.

Step three: acquire a weak monster and stab it repeatedly, for scientific data.

Step four: investigate the strange flow inside my eyes.

I nod once—a dignified gesture—and will the window to appear.

Instantly, the blue box fills my vision again.

My upgraded senses? Obviously Vulpine Senses.

The stamina drop? Running and climbing.

No last name? Good.

My satisfaction is short-lived, because now comes the real challenge: figuring out how to use Inspect.

I try mentally poking it the same way I summoned the status window.

Nothing.

Okay. Maybe I just think the word? Without saying it aloud. Because saying Inspect at people mid-conversation feels like the social equivalent of licking someone’s forehead.

So, I think it.

Inspect.

The system responds immediately.
