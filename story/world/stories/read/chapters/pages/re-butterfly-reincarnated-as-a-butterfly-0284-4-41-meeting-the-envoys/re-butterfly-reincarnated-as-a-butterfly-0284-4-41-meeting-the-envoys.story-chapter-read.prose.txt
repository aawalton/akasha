“Are you sure you want to make them wait outside?” Adon asked, raising an eyebrow.

He was in his humanoid form, following Rosslyn around as the Queen finished readying to meet their demon guests.

“This is not a settled city with everything hidden behind closed doors,” she said in a calm, steady voice—but Adon could hear the tension hiding underneath. “It is a military outpost. Every preparation for war that we have made since we arrived here is on full display once they make it past the walls. I have sent word that the armaments closest to the gate must be hidden away as best we can so the demons have no glimpse at our preparations even when we open the front door to leave. They will understand our reasons. A people who spy as much as they do… If they pretend not to understand, I frankly do not care. We have lost too much to their insidious ways already. I will not give up any of our advantages in preparation or surprise to make them feel welcome. They are not welcome. Their presence is an evil that we must tolerate, in the hopes that it prevents a greater evil. For every moment they are in proximity to our fortifications, I have ensured that they will be watched. We will not tolerate anymore of their tricks, their treachery, their spying, their…” She trailed off, fuming, clearly trying to get control of herself again.

Adon had never heard his fiancee’s voice sound more full of hate than it did as she spoke of the demon envoys.

He couldn’t say he thought she was wrong or overreacting, but it was just slightly jarring.

It’s possible that I don’t feel as strongly about the Empire as they deserve, he thought. Considering all the destruction they’ve wrought. Even if they were fleeing from the monsters, terrified, would it justify all the people they’ve killed to carve out more territory for themselves?

His mind flashed back to Samson. He wondered if Goldie would want to attend today’s meeting with the unwanted guests. Would she be able to keep control of her own hatred, which surely burned just as hot as Rosslyn’s? After all, it had been her son she had lost to their assassins.

People were supposed to lose their parents at some point. That was part of growing up. But there was something more horrible and unnatural about a parent knowing her child died protecting her.

“Do I look ready?” Rosslyn asked after a moment. She wore a strange sort of medley of masculine and feminine garb. Battle armor, yes, but with makeup. She had a sword at her side, but there was a jeweled butterfly pin in her hair. Every inch gorgeous.

The Queen was a dazzling study in visual contradictions.

“You look fierce and beautiful,” Adon replied, gazing at her with undisguised admiration. “You’ll terrify them if they have any sense.”

“Flattery is not what I need right now, Adon,” she said, shaking her head slightly. But there was a little pink in her complexion that hadn’t been there before, and she walked out the door of her room with a little extra spring in her step.

As Adon followed, others gathered around them.

The Queen politely but firmly dismissed all but a few.

Adon sensed telepathically that she and King Janus had previously extensively discussed what they would do in the event that the Empire sent a party of envoys. The plans had been set in stone for this eventuality for weeks, before they even had the notion that the Empire’s hold over Wayn might be weak.

King Janus and the rest of the highest military councillors met them near the gate. Adon felt a little sad at observing that Goldie was indeed not attending the meeting. He hoped someone had informed her that the Empire’s people were out there. Somehow he hadn’t found time to do it himself.

But Frederick was in on events as they happened almost invariably. He would have told her. The Dessian heir was not attending today’s negotiations either, though.

Almost all of those here were Parmonian generals, but a couple of Rosslyn’s people were in attendance—including Angus—and the High Priestess had reappeared. She had spent most of the last several weeks in relative seclusion.

Adon had guessed, without any evidence for or against, that she might be recovering from the miracles she had performed. After all, King Janus had claimed that they shortened her lifespan.

But she seemed perfectly healthy now.

“Greetings, Your Highness,” the High Priestess said.

“Your Holiness,” Rosslyn replied, instantly curtsying despite not being in a dress. “Thank you for lending us your wisdom and support.”

“Of course. Your struggle is all of our struggle.” She turned to Adon. “Butterfly, you are well?”

“I cannot complain, Your Holiness,” he replied.

“And what good would it do, anyway, right?” asked King Janus, laughing and putting an arm around Adon’s shoulders.

“The Goddess gives us each no more than we are meant to bear,” the High Priestess replied, seemingly agreeing with Janus for once.

“Is everyone ready?” Rosslyn asked. “As much as I do not mind keeping the envoys from the Empire waiting, I would also like to get this over with.”

Everyone around her nodded, and a few affirmed aloud.

“Yes.”

“Born ready!”

That last, from one of the Parmonian generals, amused Rosslyn, although she did not smile. The situation was too serious for that.

“Let us go and meet them, then,” she said, gritting her teeth. She turned to one of the soldiers who manned the walls and raised her voice. “Open the gate!”

In an instant, the fortified wall sprang to life, and the massive gates swung open.

Rosslyn, Janus, Adon, and the High Priestess made up the front line of the human side’s delegation. They were leading with their strengths, showing how united and diverse their force was. They had the legitimate ruler of Claustria, the ruler of Parmonia, a member of a nonhuman species as given away by his pointed ears, and the leader of the religious authority for the continent.

Bringing Frederick as well would have signaled even more strength, but Adon realized that the reason they hadn’t done it was to avoid putting Dessia in an awkward position that might have forced the Duke there to declare his neutrality before he had decided whether to enter the war or not.

Behind the ruling elites were almost exclusively military personnel, with only a couple of advisors and diplomats at the edges. The size of the party when Adon counted was intimidating. The demons had brought eight envoys. The humans had twenty people.

As the butterfly passively read the minds of the men and women around him, he realized that every detail of this response to the Empire’s delegation had been thought through. There were servants going around outside the fortifications, discreetly bringing tables and chairs to a designated clearing in the woods. The number of personnel on the human side was intended to intimidate the imperial envoys and make them feel smaller, despite the fact that they were their country’s elite and that their Empire was the largest known power in the world.

The largest known power, because no one knew the true extent of the Monster Kingdom or the mechanisms by which it worked, other than that it operated under one ruler with more influence over its subjects than any other monarch.

As the party stepped through the gates, Adon got his first close look at the demon envoys.

The eight he had counted before were all still there. The leaders standing in the center had long horns that looked almost ceremonial. But from the reaction he sensed from the generals behind him as they saw those horns, Adon understood not only that they were functional but that the size of them correlated somewhat with the individual’s power. The butterfly had already known these were the cream of the crop for demon society, but now that he recognized there was an external signifier, he couldn’t help but wonder how that would affect the development of a civilization.

As the human party finished passing through the gates, the giant doors immediately and quickly shut behind them. Adon noticed a few of the demons looking through the opening—a few of them, he sensed, had primarily been dispatched for spying purposes—but not the leaders. Their eyes remained fixed on the humans in the lead.

There was a smug air about the demon leaders. Adon sensed that they had low expectations from the gathered group of humans. It was mainly the people behind the front lines they were looking down on. Parmonia had brought its most competent military commanders, but the imperial standard for power and competence was different. They assumed that physical and magical power were linked to actual competence. If one was the best in one, then one was the best in all.

And besides the people in the front line, there was no incredible individual powerhouse on the human side. The generals had been chosen for their wits and experience, not their strength in single combat. It was a way in which the humans were culturally different from the demons.

Adon learned much about the demon culture and modus operandi that had only been obliquely or shallowly mentioned to him in the preceding weeks and minutes, all in the time it took the human party to walk up to the party of demons.

Rosslyn stopped when she and the other front liners were six feet from the front group of demons. As she planted her feet and ceased moving, everyone else stopped.

The butterfly was a little impressed by the unity of movement, but like everything else about the human delegation’s actions so far, it had been planned ahead of time.

“Your Highness, the reigning Queen of Claustria I presume,” said the leader of the demon delegation. He dipped his head in a shallow bow, which Adon sensed was deliberately disrespectful—implying that she was of diminished status and authority since they had occupied half of her country.

She simply nodded back in response, not even lowering her eyes, to signal that the envoys were beneath any deference she might show.

“Messengers—or rather, nobles—of the Demon Empire,” Rosslyn said, slipping in her own subtle disrespect. “To what do we owe the… To what do I owe this visit?”

“We are here on a mission of mercy, Your Highness,” said the leader.

“And why should we grant you any mercy?” the Queen asked. Everyone on her side laughed except for the High Priestess. Even Adon faked a laugh, though the joke wasn’t particularly funny. This too was planned.

“You misunderstand—” began one of the demons to the side of the leader, before the actual one in charge of the delegation gave him a cutting sidelong glance.

It sent an unambiguous message: Shut up, you fool.

The other demons were not pleased with Rosslyn’s joke either. Adon was surprised at how only the leaders and the ones who were probably spies at the edges were capable of keeping their cool. The others gritted their teeth or even cursed quietly under their breath.

The Empire had not sent their most subtle peacemaking envoys.

“We are empowered to make peace, if that is of interest to you humans,” said the leader once the fake laughter had faded.

Through Telepathy, Adon heard the complete phrase, Filthy humans, that the envoy had diplomatically held himself back from uttering.

None of this race of people is actually able to think of themselves in the way normal diplomats do, the butterfly thought quietly. They all already feel like they’re lowering themselves by negotiating with humans at all. It’s a wonder they didn’t send any of the humans they have working for them. But I guess that wouldn’t project strength, from their point of view.

“Let us walk, and when we arrive at our destination, we will discuss how the Empire might prevent itself from suffering disaster,” Rosslyn replied.
