When he emerged from the Memory Sphere that Lamphrey had summoned, he knew where he was without even having to open his eye.

It was the smell that hit him first. The smell of dank, damp walls, corn chips dipped in mild salsa, and an unwashed sink full of grimy dishes.

The stench of familiarity.

Lamphrey’s transportation sphere dropped him off at the Core Memory where he knew Sys would be waiting. What he didn’t know, however, was what the memory contained within would be.

But when he got there, he wasn’t surprised.

He flopped into a room – four grey corners coated with sparse decorations, a Yucca plant loitering in the corner beside the open bathroom door, and a shabby couch set before a tv that was definitely more expensive than every other item in the place combined.

Outside a window peppered with dead flies, the sounds of a modern city awakening to the new day – complete with car horns and a few choice expletives – filled the air. And that told him exactly where he was.

He was in his old apartment back in Boston.

And that meant that the shabby, scruffy looking human who sat on the couch, console controller in his hands, could be noneother than him. Ethan Hawke. The human from earth.

He flopped towards the couch, unsure of really how to begin.

But his old self merely lifted his eyebrows dimly at his presence.

“You with the IRS?”

Ethan ruffled his brim.

“Unlikely. I’d need hands to take your money.”

“These days?” his old self scoffed. “You never know…”

There was then a moment of recognition – Ethan could see it – that flashed in the face of his old, goblin-like self. It was accompanied by a flash of lambent green that came over human-Ethan’s eyes for just a few seconds.

Enough to show that Sys was still in there – though dim, and probably barely even cognizant anymore.

“Room for one more?” Ethan – the hat – asked.

And the Bostonian Neet responded: “Meh. Pull up a couch. Just don’t mess up my hair, yeah? Takes me ages to get it just right.”

Ethan sighed in the face of his old sarcasm, blinking at his disheveled locks that looked more like a bird’s nest than a fine head of hair. At least he wasn’t balding like he’d always feared.

“You’ve got nothing to worry about…”

He flopped onto the couch with little fanfare while his old self continued punching buttons, glued to the blade-wielding warrior he was puppeteering.

“Two-player?” he asked absent-mindedly.

“Solo RPG,” was the response. “Some dudes made a multiplayer mod recently but it kinda kills the whole experience.”

“You don’t like playing with others?”

“It’s not that. It’s just that other people get in the way of the whole fantasy of the thing. You know, ‘chosen one’ and all that. That whole shtick doesn’t really work if there’s two or three other ‘chosen ones’ hanging around, right?”

“I see.”

“That’s why MMOs are dying, man. Too much drama, too many scammers. Too many dumb kids more interested in going viral than just enjoying something on its own terms. Way I see it, we’re all better off alone in our own worlds.”

There it was again – that flash of green across both his pupils that told Ethan that Sys was still in there, somewhere.

Looks like Soul Wipe has already done some of its job, Ethan pondered. Now, he’s reverted to his most basic form – the tiny fragment of me that he was bound to by Jun.

He stared at those glazed-over eyes, trying to sense any part of Sys that recognized him and still understood what was happening.

But his old self gave nothing away. Instead, he kept on playing his game without even turning to see the magical hat that was staring so intently at him.

Ethan wasn’t really sure how to proceed. He’d come here – he knew – for a reason, and that reason wasn’t to try to halt what he’d already set in motion. Already he could see birds disappearing in the city skyline outside – merging with the undulating white clouds that were slowly lowering themselves like soft pillows onto the rooftops of each high-rise apartment nearby.

He knew what that meant. The lesser Memory Spheres had probably all been dissolved at this point, leaving the Core for last. It meant that maybe the Soul Wipe was finally starting to affect his own memories, now. As he looked about the room, surveying each crack, each broken floorboard, each boarded up or plugged hole in the ceiling, he felt his vision grow hazier and hazier. It was happening, alright. He was going.

Clink.

He swiveled on the couch to see his that human-self had procured a rather sizeable glass bottle of Jack Daniel’s Tennessee whiskey and was in the process of pouring himself a liberal amount into a dirty shot glass on his lap.

Ethan watched him lift the glass to his bearded lips, smile to himself, and then down the thing in a single gulp. Then, with a little approving nod, he reached for the bottle again.

“You drink alone, too?” Ethan asked.

His disheveled self seemed to take this question as a provocation of sorts. He rolled his eyes as he downed his next glass.

“Oh, excuse me, mother. Would you rather I was out drinking in some dive-bar with a couple of corporate stiffs from downtown? Ogling some pretty little thing in a low-cut dress and slurring my best pick up line, hoping I could get her tipsy enough so that she could stumble back here with me? I suppose then you’d get those grandkids you always wanted, right? I suppose then I wouldn’t be such an abject fucking failure in your eyes.”

“It was just a question.”

“Except it’s never ‘just a question’ with you, is it?” the human Ethan growled. “You’ve always gotta have some fucking insinuation attached.”

Ethan couldn’t be sure, but he got the sense that the sudden alteration in his old self’s mood wasn’t down to the alcohol. That rage bubbling to the surface at the slightest question could have been Sys in there, still active, still vengeful. But there was also something odd about his past-self’s response.

This conversation – it had happened before.

Yeah…Ethan thought as he looked long and hard at his old self who had by this point gone back to his game. This is a Core memory of mine, after all. Which means…did mom call me at some point and ask that? And…did I really speak to her like that?

Truth be told, he could barely even remember his parents faces, no matter how hard he tried.

But he had just remembered – vividly – the feelings he associated with them through his old self’s outburst.

“…I don’t usually have guests over,” his human self suddenly muttered. “You planning on staying long? Because all I got is leftover takeout…”

Ethan found himself blinking randomly.

“No,” he said. “No, I’m – just passing through.”

His old self merely grunted before reaching for his bottle again.

“What about you?” Ethan asked.

“Me? Heh. I’m heading the same place I’ve always been – the ass-end of nothingburgh on the way to nowhereland.”

Was I really this pretentiously miserable?

The question hung.

Ethan was sure he hadn’t asked it aloud, but his old self suddenly seemed to change in the moment the thought ran through his head. He started chuckling into his glass, setting down his controller, grabbing his knee with his free hand. Then, after he’d downed his next shot of whiskey, he stared into the bottom of glass at his distorted reflection.

“Even the Gibdos from Zelda scrub up better than you do…”

He twisted on the couch and finally turned to face his visitor for the first time since he’d arrived, a wry smile stretching across his face.

“You know what I should do?” he asked, a dark chuckle escaping from his throat. “You know what I should do – literally – tomorrow?”

He leaned in closer, almost tripping off the side of the couch. Ethan could smell his own fetid breath in his face. But that wasn’t what sickened him right now.

What really sickened him was that he knew what was coming.

He knew the truth that he’d been avoiding ever since he first came to Argwyll.

“I should pick out a busy road,” his old self said, laboring over each word like it was a prisoner’s confession. “Rush hour – morning, maybe, on the way to work. And I should cross right into the middle and just – stop. Just stop. I’ll look forward the whole time so I don’t see what’s coming. Shouldn’t take too long for nature to take its course. And then – smiling – I’ll feel it: bang, a car going into my sides, into my ribs, maybe throwing me half way across the road. Maybe a truck – if I’m lucky – that’ll make it quick. Squish me under its tires, roll me like a pancake along the asphalt. Course I’d already be gone by the time they find me – if I’m lucky. Yeah. And I bet it’ll barely even be an issue. Sure, some poor sod’ll do the cleanup job, but these days they’re hiring machines to do that kinda stuff – y’know, the dirty shit no real people like to do. Not that it affects me, sure, for me it’ll be simple: one moment of pain, perhaps, and then basically just sleep. Forever. And ever. And ever.”

His face twisted as he said this. Little rivers of whiskey dribbled down his bearded chin as his mouth contorted into what could have been a manic smile or a scowl at Ethan.

But the hat that sat patiently and listened to this outburst wasn’t scared in the slightest.

“…and you’d tell yourself that it was because you had no control over your life,” he said quietly. “You’d look at yourself in the mirror and hate seeing what stared back, because it wasn’t you. It hadn’t felt like you in a long time. And it was convenient to blame that on the world – on all the social and financial expectations that you could dredge up to explain why you were miserable.”

He watched his old self frown. Evidently, that wasn’t the response he had expected.

“But the truth is that you had abandoned the world ages ago. You’d stopped seeing the world as anything except this apartment and the job that funded this lifestyle. And you hated it – it, and you.”

His old self wiped a sweaty hand along his grizzled mouth.

“This is the life I want to live. But I can’t just live it, can I? Because there’s always someone saying how I could do better, be better, and I can’t -”

“Oh, don’t bullshit me!” hat-Ethan shouted – seemingly still able to conjure up a small remnant of the [Roar] Skill. He watched his old self fall back against the end of the couch before continuing, “I’ve been there. And I’m not perfect – not by a long shot – but I got through it by realizing that there’s more to this world than just me!”

His old self let out a surprised gasp, but he wasn’t deterred. He was just as stubborn a bastard as he’d always been.

“Fine! Great! What a hero. You think you’re better than me. Is that what you’re getting at? Is that what you want to hear?”

“Of course not. We’re both heading for the exact same destiny, after all, right?”

“Every mortal heads for the same destiny!” he scoffed – and now the glowing eyes were back again, lambent green reflected in the shot glass he was bringing back to his lips. “We’re born, we live, we die. We can’t control any of that. We can’t control anything. In the end, we’re powerless. In the end, it’s all pointless.”

“I disagree.”

The old Ethan – the version of him that truly was Sys – cackled with an alien voice that no longer sounded like his own. Now, it was an eerie amalgamation of system and human.

“Oh, go on, then. Tell me how I’m wrong. Tell me that you actually got the control you were looking for over your life. Tell me what you would tell yourself if you really did go back, and sit beside your past self, just before that fateful day.”

It was Sys who was asking. It was Sys who he’d been having this whole conversation with. Now, that was clear. And, powerless as the parasite now was, he still demanded an answer that he expected Ethan didn’t have. In the end, perhaps he wanted just this small, meaningless victory before he perished.

But he would be disappointed. When it came to his question, Ethan barely had to think about it.

Because after everything he’d been through, the answer was just so plainly obvious.
