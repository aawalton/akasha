As soon as Yoon arrived with a tray of freshly-brewed tea, the meeting commenced between the five individuals. Yoon introduced Aida to Mimi and George and gave a succinct summary of her request, mentioning that she and Tyda were interested in investigating the mysterious sleep disease Aida had encountered. Dev had looked a bit startled to be pulled into the impromptu discussion, though he hadn’t protested too much despite the stack of papers in his arms indicating he was in the middle of a lot of work.

The discussion—it was more of an interrogation, actually—completed in a whirlwind of impressive-sounding words from the new Gullbeaks employees that left Aida more confused and less confident that the Adventuring company would discover anything she wouldn’t be able to on her own.

Mimi, who was in charge of the reception area where they received official community reports, was grim and politely skeptical. “Do you know how many wild theories and tips we get in a day? If we even hint that we’re looking for something that specific, you can be certain that specific report will be the only thing my girls hear for the next three cycles.”

The matronly woman punctuated her point with a blunt finger stabbing the air. “I’m not saying there’s nothing behind your theory, dear, but I don’t see how we can actively search out information for it from our side.”

George, the Gullbeaks’ newly appointed Information Network head, was a little more encouraging, though his demeanor still didn’t quite inspire any confidence. He pushed his glasses up his nose bridge in a thoughtful tic. “We will certainly keep an ear out for what you describe in the community, though some of our struggles mirror Mimi’s. We cannot directly mention that we are looking specifically for this kind of phenomenon, else we will color the community’s perceptions and dilute our information collecting efficacy.”

While the two other staff members hemmed and hawed about the difficulty of doing the job correctly without jeopardizing their other responsibilities, Dev looked up at Aida. “I understand why you’re interested in pursuing this line of investigation, but… how certain are you that the two phenomena are related?”

Aida swallowed as all eyes turned to her. Behind the three pairs of newcomers’ eyes, Yoon, who was leaning against the far wall, nodded encouragingly at Aida. The faith she saw in the woman’s eyes made her feel like a con artist, especially after how adamantly the heads of the Gullbeaks’ information department seemed to be against investigating her theory.

If I’m wrong, then I’ll have wasted more than just my time or theirs. It will be everyone who works for them as well.

But she wasn’t wrong—there was a reason for everything happening. And regardless, it was better to expend all this effort on trying to answer how Ezra went into a coma than not. She hardened her resolve.

“I’m certain.” Dev gazed unblinkingly at Aida for a long moment before nodding.

“If you’re that certain, then I’ll have the Talonwings speak—discreetly—with our contacts among the higher families. We might hear as much gossip as Mimi and George, but at least the network will be smaller.”

“Thank you, Dev,” Yoon said, nodding at him. “Mimi, George, please just keep Aida’s observations in mind. We never know what might be relevant.”

Aida exhaled as everyone stood up to leave. Despite how optimistic she felt when she first received approval from Tyda and Yoon about this embarking on mission, she now felt like a fraud after making her case.

Strangely enough, trying to convince other inconsequential side characters to band together to rescue a main character felt more wrong than convincing heroes to do the same thing.

The door clicked shut, pulling Aida from her thoughts. She blinked up at Dev, who was watching her with concern. There was no one else in the room.

“Are you all right?”

“I’m fine,” Aida said, slightly defensively. She inhaled, trying to calm herself down.

Don’t take your frustration out on him.

As much as she wanted to avoid accidentally triggering anything fate-related with him—why take chances?—it wasn’t his fault that he was intertwined in whatever prophecy Bruce or Gemma suggested.

Though it would still be wise for her to make it clear where she stood in regard to all that nonsense.

“Actually, I am frustrated.” She looked up into his ice-colored eyes. “I’m frustrated that I’m just some stupid side character who doesn’t know what’s supposed to happen in her story. I mean, I know that technically nobody knows what’s supposed to happen… but as far as everyone else is concerned, there isn’t an already-written story for them to follow along with. As far as everyone else thinks, they are writing their own story as they live.”

Dev gave her a slow blink. “Is this about what Professor Bruce said?”

Aida twitched irritably. Dev grinned, which made her even more annoyed.

“So, you do feel something for me, don’t you?”

Not by my own choice.

She huffed. “Isn’t it irritating? Not being able to choose.”

Dev leaned his chin on his palm thoughtfully. “Well, nobody ever chooses to fall in love. Right?”

Aida froze, biting her lip. He wasn’t wrong; all of the best romance stories didn’t have the lead start out with the intention to fall in love with a specific mark. In fact, the themes of all the best love stories revolved around falling in love when someone least expected it.

And most importantly… she knew she wasn’t supposed to have developed any sort of feelings for Ezra, and she still did anyway.

“Look, you can blame whoever—or whatever—you want if it makes you feel better. But I don’t agree with you.” Dev tapped his stack of paper on the desk, lining them up neatly before he stood up. “I know my feelings, and it’s not because of fate or whatever other foretold prophecy Bruce or Gemma brought up.

“I have eyes, Aida. I can see—and feel—that you’re tense around me. I know it hasn’t been long since you and Ezra broke up, so I can understand why you’re feeling confused. But I don’t agree with your perspective about being trapped into your future.”

Dev paused in the doorway, lifting his chin as he thought. “It would be a shame if you really thought the things you accomplished were due to divine design. Especially considering your starting point.”

He glanced at her over his shoulder before quickly glancing away. “Anyway, I don’t want to repeat what I’ve said before… but I want to remind you that in everybody else’s eyes, you’ve already defied destiny. Why let the fear of what might be destiny control the rest of your life?”

Without another word, Dev strode away from the small conference room, leaving Aida speechless.

But that’s different, she wanted to protest. I wasn’t ever going to do anything that disrupted the flow of the overarching story!

Every expectation she had broken as Aida was because it was all for herself; it wouldn’t have affected anyone else’s life in any meaningful way, because she wasn’t as important of a character as someone like Sue.

But now that she started on this train of thought, she was caught by the hypocrisy of her justifications.

She was a side character whose only role, theoretically, was to help move the plot along so the main characters could do what they had to do in preventing the catastrophic downfall of Wyndian society. She fully embraced that role with no qualms, turning to the Gullbeaks for help, and even committing to the task of getting in touch with Ezra via what was purportedly a dangerous meditation technique.

That’s because I don’t want to end up dying if Sue and team can’t save us.

What if, in order to save Wyndia, she had to give herself up? Becoming one with Dev? After all, that surge of power she experienced when she and Dev faced down the adolescent eel was unlike anything she could ever imagine. Even Dev had said he wouldn’t have been able to replicate that feat with the ice needle, not even after a lifetime of training.

If Sue can’t save us… will Dev and I have to do it?

A shudder ran down her spine, thought it didn’t shake her resolve. Rather, it strengthened it.

She now saw things more clearly. She shouldn’t be fearing joining with Dev; rather, if everything went well, she wouldn’t have to join with him. But preemptively worrying about losing her autonomy was an exercise in futility. She—and they—all had much bigger problems to worry about than whether their decisions were governed by themselves or a higher being.

“Oh, sorry—are you still using this room?” A Gullbeaks employee poked his head in hesitantly, half in and half out. He looked her over, frowning slightly since he didn’t recognize her.

“No… my apologies. I should get going now.”

“AIDA! You’re finally here! That meeting took a while, huh?”

Sue was waving energetically at Aida from the center of the training field. She was surrounded by several mildly-singed training dummies, and Kawa was standing beside the door with his arms folded.

“Welcome,” Kawa said, moving aside to let Aida enter the training yard. “I’m glad you’re finally here. Mind helping with putting the flames out?”

“Oh, sure.” Aida pulled her wand out of her bag, looking between Kawa and Sue expectantly. “What’s happening, exactly?”

“I think Suelina found her weapon, so we’re practicing with it.” Kawa’s grin stretched across his face. “Can you take the other end of the training yard? I’ll cover this end.”

“What’s your weapon?” Aida called to Sue as she crossed over to the end Kawa pointed her to.

“You’ll see,” Sue said cheekily. She was dressed in her reddish-pink accented leather armor, and aside from two slim holsters crossing her lower back, Aida couldn’t see any weapons.

Daggers, maybe?

“All right, Suelina, whenever you’re ready,” Kawa called, motioning for Aida to prepare as he crouched to the ground. He placed his palms against the earth and looked to Sue, waiting for her to indicate she was ready.

Sue took a deep breath, resting her hands against the two handles for three breaths. On the fourth breath, the mana around her snapped into place, making the air feel unbearably oppressive. On the fifth breath, Sue looked to Kawa and nodded.

The ground exploded around Sue as several stalagmites speared out of the ground at her. Sue flowed easily through every formation, her blades whipping around her in a blur of clean swipes and strikes, leaving debris and rubble in her wake.

As soon as all of the sharp statues were destroyed, Sue sent waves of flames arcing towards the dummies standing at the edge of the arena, igniting them with no more effort than lighting a candle.

Aida applauded furiously, feeling her face split with the excitement of seeing Sue in such perfect form.

She made that all look so easy.

“How was it?” Sue was looking at Kawa with apprehension, worried about the score he would give her. He nodded his approval.

“That was the best you’ve done yet.” He turned to Aida, who was approaching them after having doused the dummies on her side. “May I present to you: the Blaze Dancer.”
