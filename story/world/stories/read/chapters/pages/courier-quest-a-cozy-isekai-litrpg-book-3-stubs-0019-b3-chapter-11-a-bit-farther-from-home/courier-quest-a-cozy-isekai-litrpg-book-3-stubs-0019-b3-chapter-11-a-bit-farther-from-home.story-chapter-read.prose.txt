The next morning, Trevor and Adabelle woke up feeling refreshed from their two days of solitude. All of the exhaustion from the wedding, both physical and mental, had melted away, which had been the entire point of their trip to the middle of nowhere.

They were lounging in the chairs on the terrace they had first arrived at after having cleaned up their messes. Adabelle was already in her armor, which she moved in as naturally as her usual clothes, while her husband would be able to change into his in an instant. It wouldn’t be long until Lora arrived, and Trevor had put the call out to Clay through their bond that it was time to return.

He had been checking in on his familiar every so often, but left them to their own devices. The earth elemental had been all over the place, digging through the rock cliff and along the surface, simply exploring a new environment with new kinds of stone.

At least, that’s what Trevor thought they were doing.

Clay bounded across the terrace and skidded to a stop in front of them, doing a little twirl as they watched. The familiar was four times the size they had been when they left, completely covered in gold with a giant, uncut diamond sticking out of their forehead. They stood up straight, held up their arms, and absolutely shone in the sunlight.

“Dang, little buddy,” Trevor remarked with a whistle, standing up so he could kneel next to them. “You’re looking spiffy. Where’d you find all that?”

The earth elemental preened at the attention, headbutting their master affectionately before pointing downwards.

“The Wildlands? Or inside the cliff?” Adabelle asked.

Clay’s body shifted to become blocky and tall, revealing a few more gemstones within their bulk. They had a replica of the house at the top. The gold gave way to some of their usual marble, which appeared as an arrow pointing towards a spot just above their halfway point.

“That’s definitely the cliff,” Trevor said. “And it still impresses me how well you can control the stuff that doesn’t make up your husk now. Good work, Clay.”

They continued stretching out like that for a few moments, as it really was tiring moving so much new material, before shaking themselves. The gold transformed into uniform bars, since the familiar was well acquainted with Trevor’s Stack skill, and clattered to the floor along with a few precious gems. All except the diamond, which the earth elemental held in their hands.

“That’s an extraordinary haul,” Adabelle praised as she reached down to pick up one of the gold bars. Clay blocked her, instead offering the diamond. She blinked in surprise. “For me?”

Nodding, the earth elemental pointed at Trevor.

“For me, to give to him?” she asked, confused.

Clay shook their head and pointed from the courier to her.

An amused smile crossed her lips. “For me, from him?” Adabelle tried. This time she was met with a vigorous nod, and she giggled. “Well, thank you Trevor, for this wonderful gift.”

“All in a day’s work, I suppose,” he said as he laughed at his familiar’s antics.

“And thank you, Clay. It’s very pretty,” she continued, reaching down to pet the earth elemental’s head. She slipped the diamond into her belt pouch, which had her newly attuned Couple’s Cube secured inside. It disappeared into Trevor’s inventory for safekeeping.

Satisfied with a job well done, Clay allowed their master to pick up everything else as they climbed up onto Adabelle’s lap. They rolled over immediately, being careful not to mess up the noblewoman’s skirt, and gave her access to their stomach for belly rubs. She quickly acquiesced to their demands.

“I thought my familiar was being a fantastic wingman, but look at them. They’re taking all the glory,” Trevor playfully complained, enjoying the smugness coming through their bond. “Clay is far too smooth.”

“At least they’re giving you the credit,” Adabelle laughed.

“True.”

Setting the last of the gold bars into his inventory, Trevor enjoyed the way they snapped into place. None of them were particularly large, but Stacks still reduced the amount of space they took up in a satisfying manner. Clay had been really good about that ever since the first time they cut their husk into pieces for their master to store.

“Good morning to the newlyweds!” Lora sang as she appeared on the terrace. The Summoned was all smiles, and the stars comprising her pupils almost seemed to twinkle as she looked at them. “I hope your stay has been satisfactory with no less than six out of five stars.”

“It was perfect, Lora,” Adabelle said as she stood up. “I hope you were well while you were gone?”

“Oh, me?” she asked, grinning as she brushed the green ribbon on her belt. “I’m always well, thanks! The boss tells me that I’m to wait on you all, but it looks like you’re all ready and set to go?”

Trevor nodded. “We’re set. Cleaned up after ourselves and triple checked to see if we left anything. Got it all.”

“Oh no! You didn’t have to clean up, I’m bringing in a crew to do that later,” Lora said.

“It’s more to help me than anything else, honestly,” the courier said sheepishly. “Back when I could, I used to travel and split hotel rooms with a few friends and, sometimes, randoms when I went to conventions and tournaments. Making sure everything was tidy allowed me to check the room over so I wouldn’t forget anything. It’s a habit now, and no trouble at all. I’m not saying we deep cleaned the place, just made it more manageable.”

“Okay, that doesn’t sound too bad, then,” Lora replied with a smile. “I guess all that’s left is to ask if you want to go to your accommodations or straight to the dungeon! Our Quest Giver knows to expect you within a certain time frame, but not exactly when. Figured you could have some flexibility.”

“We should go to the dungeon,” Adabelle said, barely containing her excitement. “I mean, the only reason we would need to go to where we’re staying is to drop off our bags, but Trevor already has that covered. It would be better to go to the dungeon, collect what loot we can, and see what will fit.”

“That makes sense. No reason to put it off,” the courier agreed. There were some butterflies in his stomach, but it wasn't a strong feeling. He was enjoying his wife's mood. “Your enthusiasm’s infectious, lady.”

“I’ve been telling you for ages that I wanted to go on an adventure with you,” she replied, standing up with Clay in her hands so she could set them on Trevor’s shoulder. “I just knew you’d hate the ones around Tosa because they’re a little intense, and the ones around Eskretet aren’t much better. From the sounds of it, you’ll excel here like you did back in Vilderchtif. Griffin wouldn’t have advocated for it otherwise.”

“That’s actually true!” Lora said, pointing at Adabelle as she nodded at Trevor. “The boss had me take her and Lailah to… every one of the dungeons you may or may not be going to in order to test them out.”

“Really?” he asked, tilting his head. “I feel like they would have said that back when they were giving us the rundown.”

“I’m pretty sure Griffin was already annoyed that he told you about the first one and Klar didn’t want to get in more trouble, dear,” Adabelle said as her tail wrapped around his wrist. “It’s going to be fun. And, if it’s not, we can leave. Let the usual Summoned handle it. It’s your honeymoon as much as mine, and you get a say, too.”

“Lady, I’m already in,” Trevor laughed. He wrapped his arms around his wife and pulled her tight. “Let’s take this topic and bury it. I’m fine. How could I be anything less when you’re at my side?”

“That is true, I will protect you no matter what,” Adabelle replied. Clay stood up on Trevor’s shoulder, puffing out their chest. “Yes, we will both protect you no matter what.”

“Aww, you three are so cute,” Lora sighed.

“They’re doing all the heavy lifting in that regard,” Trevor was quick to say. Before his wife could protest, he turned to the teleporter. “Ready?”

“Born that way,” she answered happily.

“Then we should be off,” he declared, holding a hand out for Clay. The familiar jumped into it, curling up, and he sent their husk into his inventory. The earth elemental’s black core fell into his palm, where they vibrated with excitement. Trevor held up his familiar with a smile. “I think someone else is excited to go dungeon diving, too.”

Once Adabelle was done passing off her magical items to Trevor, Lora touched the two of them on their shoulders and scrunched her face up. A moment later, she nodded to herself. There was less of a pinch this time, as if they were getting used to teleporting, and then they were in a completely different location.

The dark brush that had surrounded the terrace was replaced with thin, tall tropical trees. Waves lapped over the beach beneath the wooden walkway they were on. There were several islands across the water, some with houses, but none had any way to get to them other than by boat.

“Same ocean, different place,” Trevor said as he began retrieving his wife's items for her. “A bit farther from home this time.”

“Much, much farther from home,” Adabelle agreed as she replaced her sword and belt pouch. “The Magrovan Peninsula is on the southern end of the continent. We have officially traveled farther from Tosa than I ever have before.”

“That’s so exciting!” Lora remarked, letting go of them both to start pointing out a few things nearby. “As the boss said he would, I brought some purification salt for Clay. Nothing magical, but it’ll still work just great. If you head down this path, then you’ll make it to the dungeon. Arny, the Quest Giver, has a shack just outside and he’ll give you the low down on what to expect inside.”

With a smile, she pointed in the other direction from the dungeon. “Now, I’m not going to rush you, but there’s a cafe back this way that I’m going to wait at. Easy to find, just follow the walkway. I’ll take you to where you’re staying once you’re done with the dungeon.”

“Is it close by?” Trevor asked.

“Only about a tenth of a mile or so. So, kinda close, not really far,” she answered while waving her hand. “The mats dropped by the ghosts mean that this is a pretty lucrative place to start a ghost hunting business, you know? From there a community sprung up around it. Thus, the Locker was born!”

The courier couldn’t help but snort, and Adabelle looked at him curiously. “Is that funny?”

“Yeah. I can tell someone from Earth named this place because there was a story of a ghost ship called the Flying Dutchman,” Trevor explained as he stepped away from her towards the beach. “It was captained by Davy Jones and, while I’m not sure where the lore came from, to call something Davy Jones’ locker was to mean the bottom of the sea. Ship graveyards and stuff, if I remember right. Seems fitting for a place known for its ghosts by the ocean.”

While he spoke, he summoned Clay’s stoat body as well as two of the five slabs of their husk before setting them down on top of it. The rock was heavy, and it sunk into the sand before the earth elemental melded it all together. That was the big reason why Trevor hadn’t wanted to put it on the wooden walkway. While his familiar was configuring their body, he went to get the salt.

“Anyway, I’m gone. See you two later!” Lora said before skipping off down the path.

Adabelle was quick to turn back to Trevor once she was out of earshot. “Are you sure you’re okay with this?” she asked, quietly worried. “I don’t want you to have put on a brave face for Lora.”

“Oh yeah, I’m fine,” he answered as he poured salt onto his familiar. Clay created a funnel to prevent it from bouncing off of them, taking it all inside. “I’m never going to lie about being uncomfortable just because I think it’ll impress other people, even you. Well, not when it really matters like this.”

“Okay, I just wanted to make sure is all,” she said. “If you say you’re fine, then I won’t worry about it any longer.”

“I had plenty of time to mull it over, come up with solutions and hypotheticals, and it’s all good, lady. Promise.”

Trevor finished pouring the salt on Clay as the earth elemental finished changing the shape of their husk. The form they chose was humanoid, though headless, and about the size of Rashie. Two arms ended in shields that were coated in white salt. Their legs were thick with cleated feet in order to prevent them from getting pushed around. Now that they were larger, they pulled the two Summoned into a hug.

They reciprocated the familiar’s affection before the newlyweds took each other’s hands and started heading towards the dungeon. The wooden walkway led them down the beach towards where there were some short cliffs in the distance.

Their visibility went from the point where they could see the horizon down to basically nothing as a dense fog crept in. It got so bad that the wooden walkway and each other was all they could see. Trevor’s grip on his wife tightened a little bit, but she patted his arm to soothe him. Clay moved in front of them, ready to block whatever they could.

A rhythmic creaking noise came from ahead, and a dull light called to them through the fog. The path headed that way, so they continued. Just as Lora had said, they found themselves at an old worn shack.

Sitting in a rocking chair on the front porch was an orcish man with a shaved head. His skin was wrinkled with age, and he gave the newcomers a sharp glare as he whittled at a piece of driftwood. He wasn’t even paying all that much attention to his knife, but it worked deftly to carve without catching his fingers.

“This is a dangerous place you outsiders have come to,” the orc said, his glare becoming more critical though it didn’t come with the same weight that Trevor had felt from other Summoned. “This here dungeon’s not for the faint of heart. If you’re not prepared, then there’s no shame in turning around. You sure you city folks can handle it?”

Adabelle scoffed at the challenge, clearly ready to go, but Trevor smiled as he observed their Quest Giver. He put a hand on Clay as he went ahead to tell him it was okay. The orc raised an eyebrow as he approached, setting the knife on his lap.

“Hi, I’m Trevor,” the courier greeted with a smile, offering his hand. Bowing was more of a Lucent tradition thanks to the Japanese origin of the first King Eskretet, and he knew most of the rest of the world preferred to shake in greeting. “This is my wife, Adabelle, and my familiar, Clay. It’s nice to meet you.”

The orc looked down at it for a moment before breaking out into a big smile. “No doom and gloom, then? Alright, I can respect it,” he laughed as he took the hand. “Gotta put on a performance for some of these outlanders, you know? Good to meet you all. I’m Arnusal, but you can call me Arny. Let’s talk dungeon, yeah?”
