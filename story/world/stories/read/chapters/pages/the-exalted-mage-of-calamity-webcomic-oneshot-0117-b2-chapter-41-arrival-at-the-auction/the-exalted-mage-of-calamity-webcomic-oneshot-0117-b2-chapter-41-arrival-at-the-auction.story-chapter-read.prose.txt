After Veronica’s notes were verified, she and Gwen were granted access to the upper floors of the auction pre-showing. As expected, there weren’t many people on the higher floors, but there was no shortage of items for them to inspect.

With Sage having memorized the entirety of The Isles of Avalon, Veronica made sure to note any materials or items that might prove useful in opening the portal to the island.

There were plenty of dragon-related items up for auction, though their prices varied wildly. Fortunately, she didn’t need anything specific. As long as she bought something cheap, it would work well enough.

Spirit plants were also plentiful. A few had no medicinal properties and simply existed to look pretty.

The remaining materials were too inexpensive to be placed in the auction. She could buy them from almost any merchant house.

Of course, there were also a few items that might have suited Gwen and Nasha. However, her budget was already growing tight, assuming she had calculated the costs correctly. Much of what she could afford depended on how much her spell theories sold for.

Afterward, the two left the auction house and regrouped with Nasha. Most of their time from that day onward was spent training. Gwen still needed to practice her new phoenix spells while incorporating what she had learned from Fia Twindale’s books. Nasha, on the other hand, was already completely comfortable using Selene’s Tear.

In the meantime, Veronica contacted Hugh and asked him to delay the Mage Association’s release of her notes. She couldn’t afford for them to be revealed yet—not until after the auction.

The remaining two days were also spent taking more guild commissions. Veronica did also spend some time conversing with Galland, making sure that the two of them were aligned on what exactly their merchant company, Zenith, would be up to.

For the time being, Galland was focusing on getting as many connections as possible. Trade and profits would be lower, but he’d in turn try to secure as many partners as possible. After Veronica reassured him that she’d pay back any remaining funds he’d let her borrow from the company, Galland eased up on aggressive trade contracts, foregoing the short-term profits.

And just like that, it was time for the Pelotema Grand Auction.

“Looking good, sweetstuff,” Nasha said.

Veronica studied herself in the mirror, wearing the gown Nasha had chosen for her. White and moonlit-blue fabric covered her body, while a snug black underlayer cupped her chest. Two slender straps crossed her torso and looped loosely around her neck, leaving her shoulders bare. Fine gold lines curled across the bodice like enchanted vines, gathering at her hip beneath a small triangular belt that connected to a light skirt falling in cascading layers.

Veronica turned. “You really like the open-shoulder look, don’t you?”

Nasha smiled and stepped closer. She wore a floor-length red silk gown, sleeker than her usual performance dresses. Its strapless, corset-like bodice was embroidered with subtle floral patterns, while a slit ran along one side, offering a clear view of her long, slender leg.

“Everyone has their preferences. Don’t like it?”

Veronica glanced back at the mirror. “No… it’s good. I’m just not used to wearing something so…”

“Sexy?”

Veronica frowned. “I was going to say beautiful.”

Nasha chuckled.

Footsteps approached from the adjoining room.

“How do I look?” Gwen asked, stepping into the doorway.

She wore a pale green gown, simple but carefully tailored. A ribbon cinched her waist, and the long skirt moved softly around her ankles. The dress lacked ornate filigree, jewels, or elaborate patterns, aside from a little embroidery along the neckline and cuffs. It looked plain beside Nasha’s and Veronica’s gowns, but not in an unflattering way. It suited her.

“You look lovely,” Veronica said warmly.

Gwen smiled. “Thanks. I’ve never been to anything this grand before. I don’t want to stand out too much.”

“Are we ready to go, then?” Nasha asked.

The three left the Maristar Gold Inn and hired a small carriage.

The moon was out, but Maftem didn’t feel quiet the way a city normally would at night. Most of it still seemed wide awake. The streets remained fully lit, shops along the main avenues were still open, and small groups of people moved beneath the lights, most dressed far better than the average passerby.

Tonight, everyone seemed to be traveling in the same direction.

Several carriages rolled along the road ahead, each polished and decorated with crests, ribbons, or small mana lanterns attached to the sides. Some belonged to nobles. Others likely carried wealthy merchants or guild representatives. Guards rode alongside a few of them on armored horses, while others walked beside the carriage doors with swords at their hips and eyes constantly scanning the street.

The closer they came to the auction house, the heavier the traffic grew. Carriages slowed, forming a long but orderly line down the avenue. Auction attendants stood along the road, directing drivers toward different drop-off points. The entire street had been arranged for the night, with temporary barriers, additional lamps, and guards posted at nearly every corner.

After almost ten minutes, their carriage finally reached the broad steps of the Pelotema Grand Auction House.

Hellen brought the carriage to a smooth stop.

“We’re here, ladies,” she called back.

Veronica opened the door and stepped out first, then turned to offer Gwen a hand.

Gwen accepted it carefully, lifting the edge of her skirt as she descended. Nasha followed, moving with a natural elegance that made it clear she had spent a lifetime wearing dresses.

“Thank you, Hellen,” Veronica said, turning back toward the driver.

Hellen grinned from the front of the carriage. “No problem. I’ll come back in three hours if you need a ride.” She turned the carriage and drove away, presumably to pick up other clients.

Although the auction house would make a fortune tonight, the city’s carriage drivers probably would as well.

As the three walked toward the entrance, several people turned to look at them. They didn’t stare all at once, but it was enough for Veronica to notice. Perhaps they were drawing a little too much attention.

Gwen leaned closer. “Is it just me, or are a lot of people staring at us?”

“A little,” Nasha said with a smile. “Three beautiful ladies arrive at the same time? Of course we’d get stares.”

“I don’t know if I’m that beautiful,” Gwen said softly.

“I think you fit more of the ‘cute’ archetype,” Nasha said. “A little tomboyish, too.”

“Um… thanks? Wait—is that a compliment?”

Nasha shrugged. “It’s the truth. Some men are into that. Who knows? You might find your knight in shining armor here.”

“Wait, really?”

“After the auction, people will have to wait in the lobby to collect their items,” Veronica said. “Considering how large the event is, some of them may be waiting for quite a while. There should be opportunities to meet people.”

At the entrance, two guards in black formal armor stood beside the open doors. Their uniforms resembled those worn during the pre-showing, but tonight they had additional silver cords across their shoulders and small badges identifying them as auction security.

One guard looked at Veronica, then Gwen, then Nasha. His gaze paused on all of them, but only briefly.

Then he coughed into his fist and straightened. “Invitation or pass?”

Veronica reached into her small bag and withdrew a glossy black card edged in gold, with the Pelotema Grand Auction seal stamped in its center. Jansen Pelbrant had given it to her after her two manuscripts passed verification.

The guard accepted it with both hands and placed it against a crystal plate beside the door. The card glowed. A moment later, the crystal chimed softly.

The guard’s posture became more respectful. “Verified VIP. Miss Veronica Everwells and two guests. You may enter.”

“Thank you,” Veronica said.

The guard stepped aside. “Enjoy the auction.”

Nasha smiled faintly as she passed him.

The guard coughed again and stared straight ahead.

Inside, the lobby was much busier than it had been during the pre-showing. All the display cases were gone, replaced by standing tables covered with plates of food. If they hadn’t known better, they might have believed they had entered a ball rather than an auction.

Accommodating the guests like this was a smart decision. The more relaxed and comfortable they became, the more likely they were to spend.

After several minutes of snacking near one of the tables, an attendant approached them.

“Miss Everwells?” she asked, looking between all three women, uncertain which one she was supposed to address.

Veronica nodded.

The attendant bowed. “Please follow me. Your balcony has been prepared.”

They followed her toward the central staircase and climbed to the second floor. But they didn’t stop there. Instead, they continued to the third floor and proceeded along a long hallway leading deeper into the auction house.

Several nobles passed them in impeccably tailored suits, along with wealthy merchants and even—

“Knights in shining armor,” Gwen whispered, almost giddy.

“Okay, okay. Calm down,” Veronica said.

After several turns and passing numerous rooms, they entered the auditorium. The third floor formed a ring around the main auction hall. Partially enclosed rooms with balconies extended from the walls, each overlooking the stage below. Some were separated by low partitions and velvet ropes, but none were fully enclosed. They offered enough privacy to convey status while remaining open enough for everyone to see who occupied them.

The attendant guided them along the ring until they reached a balcony near the center. Three cushioned chairs waited behind a polished railing, along with a small table holding fruit, tea, and a written catalogue.

“This will be your balcony for the evening,” the attendant said. “To place a bid, raise the numbered paddle on the table. If you have any questions about the items, a servant briefed on every lot tonight will be stationed nearby to assist you.”

Veronica picked up the catalogue and glanced at the first page. “Understood.”

“If you require anything else, please call.”

The attendant bowed and stepped away.

Gwen slowly approached the railing and looked down.

The main auction hall stretched wide beneath them. Rows of seats filled the lower floor, already occupied by merchants, mages, guild representatives, and wealthy guests. At the far end stood the auction stage, with a polished podium in its center and several guarded doors behind it.

Then Gwen looked toward the neighboring balconies and the VIP guests occupying them.

“You know, I wondered whether they’d run out of VIP rooms with so many items in the auction. But now that I’m seeing it, there must be over two hundred balcony seats.”

“This place certainly looked smaller from the outside,” Nasha said. She looked at Veronica. “Magic or something?”

Veronica hummed. “Maybe. It’s difficult to tell since I can’t see the enchantments, but you’re right. The interior does seem much larger.”

Gwen leaned farther over the railing and narrowed her eyes at another balcony roughly twenty spaces away. “Hey—girls. Isn’t that him?”

Veronica and Nasha followed her gaze.

“Lord Vaun,” Veronica confirmed.

He sat in his own VIP booth, dressed in his usual dark green coat. Three butlers and two armored knights stood nearby. Beside him sat a woman with long black hair wearing a blue gown—presumably his wife.

“Hey,” Gwen said, turning back toward Veronica. “Since this is called the Pelotema Grand Auction, does that mean the duke will show up?”

Veronica tilted her head. “I’m not sure. Although the auction bears his name, I don’t think he owns or manages it directly. But considering how important the event is, he may attend.”

She looked down and opened the catalogue. Nearly three hundred items were listed for the evening. Some would take several minutes to sell, while others would likely pass in seconds.

The first several lots weren’t particularly impressive—mostly enchanted tools and minor monster materials meant to appeal to commoners and those of lesser wealth. Still, they offered a chance to acquire unwanted materials cheaply. If Veronica had more money, she might have considered buying some, but there was nothing she urgently needed among them.

After turning several pages, she found the materials she had come for. To open the entrance to Avalon, she needed at least three items from the auction. The remaining ingredients could be purchased or traded for through a merchant house or mage tower.

And she desperately wanted the raw starmetal ore.

The only problem would be finding someone capable of refining it. Still, the sooner she acquired it, the better. Starmetal was exceptionally rare. Once she reestablished her position and power, finding suitable candidates to forge her staff would be easy.

She still didn’t know how much her spell notes would sell for. In the future, parts of the theories had been co-authored by Maeve, her mentor, who had handled most of the rights and financial arrangements.

Hopefully, they would earn enough.
