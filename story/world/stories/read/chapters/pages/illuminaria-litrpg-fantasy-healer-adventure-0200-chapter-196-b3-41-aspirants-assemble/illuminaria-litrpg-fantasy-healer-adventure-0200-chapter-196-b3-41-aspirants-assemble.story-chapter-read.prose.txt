Chapter 196 / B3-41: Aspirants Assemble

As the three of them headed west across the city’s central square, Joe was amused by the thought that he was walking across Founder’s Plaza with one of the actual founders. Margen looked every bit the legendary hero. He was dressed in a red tabard trimmed with gold over black leather armor, highlighted by garnet-colored accents, as well as actual garnets. Doomherald hung across his back in what had to be a magical scabbard, since there would be no practical way to resheath the weapon back into the intricately woven wrappings around the blade.

Within the man, Joe could still see the Erlking’s cursed affliction etched into his muscles and organs. There were dozens of black, tumorous-looking knots riddled throughout Margen, yet surprisingly, they were not growing. There was a spell-effect containing the pools of Mummy Rot that Joe could detect with [Witch Sight], but other than knowing it was some sort of {Stasis}-based power, he couldn’t identify it.

On the other side of the powerful warrior was the captain of the Adventurers Guild, trotting along on legs far shorter than either Joe’s or Margen’s. Myllo was wearing a green plaid suit that Joe would never have been caught dead in, but somehow the korrigan made it work. His prestigious coif of red hair was mostly tamed by beaded braids. In one hand, he bore a walking stick made from a near-black wood topped by a silver, raven-headed cap. Joe realized that Myllo must be in a weird spot, as that cane clearly suggested an affiliation with the Winter Court, and yet he was now one of the Aspirants of Spring.

The three of them walked down Godsway towards the steep rise that separated the west district of High Park from the rest of Fort Coral. The first church they passed was the great and raucous cathedral of Mairree. Joe had to admit he fully approved of a church that celebrated friendship and festivities. Having Ayl the Brewer’s temple next door was no accident, and Mairree’s congregation had already started their worship hours ago. From noon to midnight, this boisterous church was one big party every day.

As the hero drew near, excited shouts filled the air. Joe watched dozens of bets being paid off between those who had believed the rumors of Margen's return and those who were very reasonably skeptical of such an outlandish claim. The living legend strode over and shook hands, but he made it clear that he could not join them just yet. He promised to do so tomorrow. Joe knew that by then, Margen would have been formally presented to the city and treated by Mercy Suku, Saint of Cliffside.

Once free of the Mairree’s throng of revelers, Joe began to take note of the amazing churches that filled Godsway, the main avenue through the temple district of Godsrow. A stunningly beautiful temple to the goddess Ahloower, the goddess of enticement, charm, and seduction, refused to be overlooked. Not only was the structure visually appealing, but the vivacious priests and priestesses standing on the edge of the roadway also drew attention and patrons. Across the boulevard from ‘She who is Pursued’, was her husband Ardhor, the Fervent One. The myths state that the god of passion and zeal pursued his love across the void, pulling many of his fellow gods in his wake. Once he caught her, he sought a place to woo Ahloower and found Illuminaria. Ardhor and Ahloower are credited with leading the gods of emotions to join the first primal gods, beings like Blu, Urth, Greahn, and Stown.

Godsway ended at the steep rise that led up to High Park. Slanting across the escarpment that separated Fort Coral’s most affluent district from the rest of the city were two main roads: Park Lane climbed to the south, and the wide avenue High Street headed north. The trio turned right and followed High Street upwards.

The street was far more resplendent than its simple name suggested. For starters, it was unerringly straight and smooth, wide enough for three wagons to ride abreast of each other. Ornate lanterns burned with colorful mana even during the day. The hue of the streetlights shifted subtly in concert with each other, reminding Joe of the string of LED lights he had running around the ceiling of his dorm room in college. There were little paved patios with benches every fifty or so feet where one could stop to catch one’s breath or just sit and admire the elevated view over the small metropolis and Coral Bay behind it.

At the top of the long climb, he found himself in what felt like a completely different city. Expansive lawns stretched like carpets of deep green velvet; each flawlessly trimmed and bordered by low walls of polished stone. While these short barriers offered little in the way of physical impediment, [Witch Sight] showed Joe that they were layered with some of the finest wards he had ever seen.

The air was filled with the perfume of manicured flower gardens. Each tree left along the lane was a perfect specimen, adding the mellow rustle of leaves in the afternoon breeze. The fantastical topiary was carved into works of art.

Every home they passed was its own masterpiece: great stone and wooden mansions with columned porticos and tall windows aglow with golden light. They sat on cobbled driveways sweeping in elegant curves from the cast-iron gates along the road, which were adorned with intricate crests.

Myllo clearly knew which estate they were looking for. He led the three of them to a gate emblazoned with a symbol of a tophat with the letter ‘B’ cut from the center of the accessory. As Myllo spoke their names to the empty air, Joe watched magic awaken in the barred barrier’s hinges. The heavy gate swung open for them soundlessly.

Walking down a multi-hued tiled carriage drive, Joe noted the great house ahead was unlike any of the ones he had seen so far. It was large and impressive, as the others had been, yet it had a refinement the others lacked. The differences came from a whole handful of little things: the slight curve of the rooflines, wide, peaked windows and rounded doorways, carved accents, and the layering of paint to form a subtle ombre instead of a flat color. Where the previous mansions Joe had seen sported large gilded, marble statues or just leaned into how massive they could make their dwellings, the Barrburrow estate chose elegance through effort over bludgeoning the onlooker with indulgence. All together, these fine touches made the masterfully crafted manor outshine even the most opulent demesne they had passed so far.

A butler met them at the door and led the trio through the house. The inside had the same care put into its furnishings as the exterior. The furniture was chosen to complement the spaces they were in, and the painting and tapestries actually made Joe want to stop and admire them instead of just passing them by.

The elven servant brought them to a set of open paneled doors and ushered them into what clearly was the master study of the home. The two walls were lined with bookshelves, and fire burned in the hearth of the adjacent wall. Joe was surprised, since it was a warm late-spring day, that is, until he spotted wards blocking the heat, allowing one to enjoy the ambiance of the flames without making the room uncomfortable.

The master of the house sat behind a massive desk covered with ledgers. He was not at all what Joe had expected. Joe had heard he was a leprechaun, and so, in his head, he had instinctively drawn a picture of a jolly, green-clad little man. Dorian Barrburrow was nothing so banal. While he was not quite as sinister as the Warwick Davis depiction of a leprechaun, the master trader was far from the cherubic Saint Paddy's Day stereotype. He looked like he would fit right in with the pirates under a Jolly Roger.

Black tattoos covered his hands and under his eyes. He had a sharpness to him, with bat-wing ears, pointed eyebrows, and a small, angular beard. Shrewd eyes assessed all three of them. So astute was his gaze that Joe was stunned by the message from Hawking that popped up.

[No One] and [Ideal Alias] have failed to block an assessment. You have been assessed.

Joe had long ago turned off the notifications for failed assessments since all anyone saw when they id’ed him was either whatever he had queued up in [Ideal Allias] or nothing if they got past that skill. This was the first time since he had arrived in Illuminaria that someone had an insight skill strong enough to punch through [No One].

There was one other person in the room. Standing by the corner of the wall with the door, stood a tall, fierce-looking fey. There was something about the guardian that reminded Joe of Corran Liogen: the elongated features, his rangy build, the way he held himself. Age had mellowed the old fey knight, making him seem more wise and serene, yet Joe could imagine in his prime, Corran would have had this man’s sharp-edged alertness.

A falcon sat on Nallorakk’s shoulder. The raptor seemed to be studying the new arrivals as keenly as its master was.

“Welcome,” Doriain announced, rising from his desk. “Right on time. Thank you.” He strode over to Myllo and embraced the guild captain. “Cousin,” he greeted. Stepping back but still holding the korrigan's upper arms, he added, “Tell me true, Myllonell. Did you plan to pick this fight with our queens, or did you stumble into it?”

“Definitely the latter, Dorian. We found a necromantic corruption leaking from a warded valley just a few miles away that not a single person in Fort Coral could ever remember visiting. It turned out to be the prison of the Erlking, who had become thoroughly corrupted by undeath. Delving into politics of the Feylands was never even a consideration.”

“Well, we have that going for us then. We can claim the right of truth, then. Attempting to troggshit our way through this mess would have been considerably harder.” Dorian turned to face Margen and extended his hand upward to the hulking warrior. “I have long admired tales of you leading your people to this far shore, General Margen. It is an honor to meet you.”

“I’m not a general any longer, sir. But thank you all the same,” the big man rumbled.

“Well, I would not get too comfortable without the title. With the pirates out of Defiance growing bolder every year, the city council has been proposing a military leader for quite some time now. The only stumbling block was that they could not agree on who should fill the role. I think your return will solve that dilemma unanimously.”

He then turned to Joe and proffered his mitt once again. “So, you’re the wild card in this whole mess.” Joe grasped his hand, yet instead of shaking, Dorian placed his left palm over the clasped digits. “They know your heritage?” he asked.

“Yes, they both do.”

“Fair nough. Then I can say I’m not surprised a changeling is the one who tipped the kettle over. When you look hard enough at Annwn’s momentous moments, more often than not, you’ll find one of the hidden folk played a role in bringing it about.”

Dorian gestured for them to sit beside the fire, choosing a burgundy high-backed armchair for himself. He needed an upholstered stepstool to hop into the unusually elevated chair, but the customized seat left him looking Joe in the eye. Joe could see how someone as shrewd as the master trader would understand the subtlety of not being looked down on. Of course, Margen still towered over the group, and Myllo now looked like a furry kid sitting with the adults, but given the size disparities among the three of them, there was never going to be an even height.

“I guess our first order of business is to settle how many of us plan to contend for the crown,” the leprechaun stated as he settled into his chair, steepling his fingers.
