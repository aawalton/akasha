Chapter II.XXIV (2.24)

Mitsuko did her best to create as few waves as possible as she transported Zeaqu up to his tower in a cart. After the guild member pointed out which tower was his, Mitsuko decided to forgo the main roads on the island and push the cart down side alleyways where she was less likely to be seen or interfere with anyone’s lives. This was a quick, opportunistic scouting mission to get a base idea of what Amber Island was like and she didn’t want to alert the guardian of her existence here just yet. Though she couldn’t help but to worry that the mere fact that she was transporting the wizard rather than forcing him to interact with the island as he normally would trigger cascading changes in the loop.

At the very least, Mitsuko was confident that Zeaqu wasn’t one of the acolytes. Juniper had mentioned before that they weren’t able to leave the island. Though, Mitsuko also knew from her run-ins on Ashen Island that people could still be corrupted by the guardian’s influence and turned into an acolyte after their arrival to the island as well.

Still, she thought it unlikely and if she let complete paranoia consume her, she’d never get anything done.

“Who are you?” asked an elderly man with a hunched back, floppy hat, and a cane that appeared to be supporting more of his weight than his legs. He stepped out of a doorway and into the yellow stone alley. He eyed Mitsuko with suspicion.

“My name is Mitsuko,” she replied. Only after the words were out did Mitsuko think to herself that maybe she should be using an alternate identity.

“Mitsuko,” the man repeated the name like it was something disgusting. “What are you doing in this street? Only true wizards are allowed here. And you don’t look like a wizard to me. Are you a thief? Here to steal our secrets on spellcraft? What’s in that cart?”

Mitsuko couldn’t hold back a laugh. “I couldn’t steal any magical secrets you have here, even if I wanted to. I have only one natural affinity and I doubt it’s something you wizards are capable of. All your vast spell knowledge is useless to me.”

She shouldn’t be revealing so much about herself. The wise thing to do would probably be to get on her hands and knees and apologize for her existence. Maybe her new powers had gone to her head and changed her way of thinking these last few months, but the thought of groveling before someone like this disgusted her.

The old man’s eyes narrowed. “Why are you here?”

Mitsuko gestured at the cart. “I’m delivering bananas to Wizard Zeaqu.”

“Bananas. Hm.” The wizard hobbled over then kicked the cart.

“Hazbaz! Stop that!” Zeaqu shouted from inside the crate. “You’re bruising my fruit!”

“Ha!” the old man cried out, triumphant. “Not just transporting bananas after all! You’re smuggling this hack. What are you doing inside there, Zeaqu? Hiding from responsibilities?”

Zeaqu popped his head out of the top of the crate and glared at his fellow wizard. “Hiding from Piano Pipper. Return to your rocks and leave me be!”

Hazbaz blinked. “Oh. In that case, I sympathize. Carry on with your antics, young wizard. Good day to you.”

Hazbaz hobbled away, down a different alleyway. As he stepped around a corner and out of sight, he yelled at an alley cat.

“Just a bit further. Two blocks,” Zeaqu said. “Get me in my tower and I’ll be safe.” He then popped back down into the crate.

Finally, they arrived at the yellow stone tower. Mitsuko tipped the cart and the crate fell out at the doorstep.

“The bananas!” came a muffled cry from within the crate. “They’ll be bruised!”

“Whoops. The wheels knocked into the stone steps outside your front door.”

The crate burst open with a plume of purple smoke. After a few seconds, it dispersed and revealed Zeaqu standing amongst a pile of bruised bananas, smoothing his robes with one hand and tugging on his beard with the other.

“A rough journey, but I successfully evaded my foe thanks to your assistance.” He stepped forward and the door swung inward as he approached the threshold. “Join me inside. Allow me to host you for you.”

Mitsuko followed him through the doorway and stopped midstep. The entire first floor of the tower was decorated with different fruit and vegetable shaped furniture. She poked the nearby coatrack that looked to be made of burdock root. The wood felt normal.

“All an illusion, my dear.” Zeaqu flopped down on a pumpkin shaped plush chair. “We all have our oddities here on this island. Be grateful I’m not one of those obsessed with manure and dung beetles.”

“You’re joking,” Mitsuko said flatly.

“Nope! That particular obsession has stolen the passion of nearly a dozen wizards. Everyone loves something. Socks, kendama, flesh tumors, parchment, kangaroos. We host a wide variety of passions. Hazbaz, for example, collects extraordinarily old rocks with leafy patterns.”

“What causes this?”

“They say it’s something in our water. Every long term resident here has an obsession.”

“Who do you mean when you say ‘they?’”

“The wizards obsessed with water, of course!”

Mitsuko edged around the room, glancing at the paintings of faces made out of fruit. “Is this magic related?”

“Sometimes! But not for me. Druidic and flora altering magics hold no appeal. I simply like fruit and veggies.”

Mitsuko made a note to herself to bring her own water source to the island in all future loops. Especially if she ever brought Holly here. She shuddered to imagine what sort of obsession the gnome might develop.

“You mentioned you were out studying the barrier. Is that your specialty?” Mitsuko asked.

“Oh!” Zeaqu’s eyes lit up. “Follow me upstairs.” He darted over to a spiral glass staircase at the center of the room and swiftly climbed up.

Mitsuko, averting her eyes so as to not accidentally look up the wizard’s robes through the clear glass stairs, followed after him.

The next floor up was filled with glass objects. Scrying orbs, magnifying glasses, mirrors, and even swords hung on the wall. Every piece of furniture in the room was likewise made of glass. Tables, chairs, shelves. Only the yellow walls remained crafted of stone, and even they had several large windows cut into them.

“I am known as the glass wizard. While my current obsession is, indeed, edible flora, my magic is entirely dedicated to the creation of glass and its utility. As such, they deemed me the best wizard on the island to investigate a clear barrier. And I deemed it an opportunity to receive my fruit shipment despite the chaos of the outside world. Several of my trade contacts have been barred entry due to suspicions about the barrier’s appearance.”

“Fascinating,” Mitsuko muttered, her eyes transfixed on a glass sword dyed orange. “You created this all just through magic? No equipment?”

“I have a few things down in the basement in storage, but I’ve mostly surpassed the need for physical tools. I’m quite good at what I do.” Without waiting for a response, Zeaqu stretched out a hand and a glowing orb expanded and melded over his open palm. In a matter of seconds, it had folded over itself a hundred times over and transformed into a small humanoid shape and cooled off. “It all comes down to the elemental creation of ground up quartz then incinerated and liquefied before cooled into a hard surface. Well, I suppose that simplifies a few things. But that’s the gist of it.”

Zeaqu jabbed his hand forward and dropped the glass doll in Mitsuko’s hand. As she looked closer at it, she noted it had an uncanny resemblance to herself. Roughly the same bangs and face. Though maybe she was seeing similarities that weren’t truly there.

“Thanks,” she said. She dropped the doll in her pocket.

“The chance of it breaking in there is not zero. It’s not magical glass,” Zeaqu warned with a waggle of his finger. “If shattered, it might very well slice through the fabric and cut into your skin.”

“I’ll make certain that doesn’t happen,” she reassured him. Though, in truth, she was unconcerned because she could simply undo any damage with barely more than a thought. “So the first floor is your… fruit room. The basement is storage. This floor is where you keep your glass creations. What’s higher up in the tower?”

“Oh! Let’s continue the tour!” Zeaqu darted back over to the glass staircase and dashed up. Mitsuko followed after the excitable wizard.

She entered into the most mundane space she’d seen since arriving on Amber Island. A simple office. Stone floor and walls, a wooden desk with a pile of parchment and an ink pot, and a bookshelf lined with leatherbound tomes. This room could be dropped in a wealthy household across the world and would struggle to stand out.

“My study!” Zeaqu proclaimed proudly, arms raised. “Here I confront the tedium of paperwork Amber Island forces upon me.”

“No windows,” Mitsuko noted. “Why doesn’t a glass mage have any windows in his study?”

“To keep my mind clear and pure.”

Mitsuko decided to interpret that statement as that they would distract him from his paperwork.

For a moment, Mitsuko had the irrational urge to pluck one of the papers off the desk and read it. But that would be extremely ill-mannered. And, judging by what she knew of Zeaqu, the papers were likely nonsensical. It wasn’t as if this random wizard would have the information she needed at the top of a stack of papers on his desk. That would all likely be stored away elsewhere.

“Is there a library here on this island?” Mitsuko asked abruptly.

Zeaqu stroked his beard. “Of course we have a library. Dozens. Any specific subject caught your interest?”

“A history of the archipelago and its people. Especially Porcelain Island.”

“Historical records… hm.” Zeaqu’s hand got snagged in a tangle in his beard and he nearly tumbled over with the force of his stroking. He coughed and set his free hand on his desk to steady himself. “How about we continue my tour? I will be able to point out the best places in which to pursue that knowledge from the supreme vantage point atop of my tower.”

Mitsuko agreed and they ascended up to the next floor which was a bedroom with a side room closed off with a chamber pot and wash station. As were the next two floors. Each was colored differently, red, then orange, and finally, yellow, but they came down to the same basic layout. The seventh floor was a kitchen and dining area. Mitsuko noted that nearly all the food in the kitchen was plant based and dominated by veggies and fruits, with hardly any bread, oats, or rice in sight.

“Here you go!” Zeaqu passed her a stick of yellow celery. “This is my most delectable celery. Cherish it.”

“Thanks?” Mitsuko, not knowing if eating something edible would be seen as cherishing in this wizard’s eyes, carefully stored it in her shirt’s inner pocket.

“Out here is the garden,” Zeaqu said, stepping over to a pair of double doors. “Follow me!”

The doors banged open, scattering a flock of yellow birds and revealing the magical Amber Island.

Mitsuko followed the wizard onto a large balcony covered in patches of yellow vegetables and took in the sight. In the distance, a ring of mystical yellow fog blocked the horizon but she could still see the sea churning as they passed through the water. There were a dozen similar yellow towers scattered throughout the island’s landscape. For the most part, it was very urban and Mitsuko suspected it might actually have a larger population than Mauve, despite that island itself being considerably larger. However, the environment took a sharp turn at Amber Island’s center. A large forest blotted out everything, with yellow foliage consuming the manmade structures there. Even from a distance, Mitsuko could see streets ending abruptly at the edge of the forest and the remains of a few toppled towers, absorbed by the island’s nature.

“What’s going on over there?” she asked, pointing at the park.

“Oh, our last governor blew up a few days ago. Nothing to worry about.”

“Your governor… blew up?”

Zeaqu waved dismissively. “Happens all the time. His soul got converted into life energy and expanded rapidly until it expired. Less than twenty casualties this time, so a far better result than eight years ago when our governor combusted in an explosion and left a crater in Amber. That took us an entire year to patch up and messed up the island’s hydrodynamics so we slowed down considerably.”

As Mitsuko took in that information, she spotted a familiar gull deftly dodge around the earlier yellow birds while it flapped over in their tower’s direction. The uncanny glow of its brown eyes revealed it to be Juniper.

“Did the library get eaten up by the new forest?” Mitsuko asked. She’d need to look into the island governor’s death a bit more as well, it could hardly be a coincidence he died around the same time as the Prismatic Spiral’s start. But, for now, she wanted to remain focused on Porcelain. If she happened to gather additional information as well, that was great, but one objective at a time.

“Only one!” Zeaqu declared happily. “And it was a library dedicated to fashion trends, not the historical records vault.”

Juniper fluttered to a stop and landed on a massive yellow squash.

“Shoo! Shoo!” Zeaqu waved his arms in the air.

Juniper gave the wizard an unimpressed look.

“How do you stand strong against my pesticides' repelling effects? Fine then, stand your ground and I will transform your feathers into stained glass,” Zeaqu warned her. His hands glowed as he prepared a spell. “We will test your fortitude and stubborn willpower against the might of my spellcraft!”

“That’s my familiar,” Mitsuko lied. “She was supposed to help get my ship to turn around but failed.”

Zeaqu’s arms flopped down. “A familiar? Why didn’t you say so? Are you that witch Zena invited to the island? She’s been obsessed with witches these last few months.”

“No. I’m not a witch,” Mitsuko replied simply. “What were you saying about the library?”

“Oh! Yes, you want that one over there.” He pointed at a mound of dirt. “It’s called the memory vault. Lots of history buried in there.”

“Thanks—”

Zeaqu rushed forward, shoved Juniper aside, and scooped up the squash into his arms. “Ah, ha! Safe, now!” He cradled the vegetable as he then answered Mitsuko’s unasked question. “It just occurred to me that the bird might defecate and spoil my wonderful vegetable. I must retreat with my spoils. Do not permit your familiar the destruction of my garden.” With those parting words, he dashed back inside, still embracing his precious squash.

“I see this island is as silly as I recall,” Juniper said. “You have found yourself in quite the peculiar place, Champion Mitsuko.”

“I saw an opportunity.” Mitsuko took out the celery from her pocket and bit into it. The juice inside was quite good. Sweeter than normal. She offered Juniper a bite in a peace offering. The bird hopped up to her and snapped a piece off with her beak. “The events that led up to getting here would be hard to recreate. So while I’m here, I’ve decided to do a bit of research to see if I can’t find another path here for future loops.”
