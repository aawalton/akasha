"The wayfarers from Ziemrot," the woman's voice carried the gentle crackle of a well-tended fire. However, her eyes danced with hidden knowledge.

Aira exchanged a quick glance with Lila, whose amber eyes had widened with surprise. Alliot stiffened beside them, his posture shifting subtly to place himself between the approaching woman and his companions.

"I am Ronorrh," the woman continued, her fiery eyes assessing them with calm interest that contrasted with the hostility they'd faced in Ziemrot. "High Shaman of Lamcada. Word travels quickly between our cities—sometimes through official channels, sometimes through whispers on the wind." A slight smile played on her lips. "Though I suspect Khanorrh's message painted you in rather... unflattering terms."

"That's putting it mildly," Lila muttered. "Pretty sure he'd have us smoldering like pine sap if he caught us again."

Aira, who received all the details of the dialogue through her bond with Lila, was surprised to notice Ronorrh's eyes flicker with amusement at her green-haired friend's words. The High Shaman inclined her head slightly. "We in Lamcada prefer to form our own judgments. The fire teaches us that what appears dangerous from a distance often proves... deliciously entertaining up close. Old Khanorrh never appreciated a good performance, did he?" She gestured toward a path leading deeper into the city. "Come. We have quarters prepared for you. Rest, explore our city, and when you're ready, we can discuss what brings you to our fiery embrace."

As they followed Ronorrh through the gleaming obsidian streets, Aira leaned close to Lila. "She picked up the connection with you right away," she said through their party chat. "Did you notice?"

"Clear as sunrise, I noticed," Lila replied, her eyes fixed on the High Shaman's back. "Every shaman's got their branches covered in different ways. I'm just not sure which tree to climb or which is hollow to the core."

"We have some tensions with Lamcada," Alliot commented. "As far as I know. Not that I paid much attention to… politics before. Not that I trust everything Ainorrh said anymore…" His voice was tight with what might have been suspicion or wonder.

"Well, don't forget that even if you ignore politics," said Aira, "politics don't ignore you. And politicians are all too happy to use you. That's something that doesn't seem to change, whatever world you live in."

Later that evening, after settling into their quarters—a beautifully crafted structure with walls of polished volcanic glass that somehow remained cool despite the ambient heat—they gathered to assess their situation. The Splinter of the Worldroot sat between them, its emerald glow casting strange shadows across their faces as it pulsed in response to the foreign elemental energy of Lamcada.

"We need to talk about our skills," Aira said, eyes focused on the rhythmic dance of light beneath the Shard's surface. "We need to talk about many things, really. I don't even know what to begin with. But skills aren't less important than anything else. I've been monitoring our progress since we left Ziemrot. Between the journey and our escape, each of us accumulated a reasonable amount of skill points."

Lila nodded, running her fingers through her green hair, which now had prominent gray streaks. "Got more cracks than a cliffside in my skill system. I've got skill points piling up, and I don't know what to do with half of them."

"I've never had to make these decisions before," Alliot admitted, his usual confidence wavering. "In Wentouk, progression is gradual. Deliberate. You master one ability before considering another." He glanced at Aira. "Your connection with the Nexus is different. More immediate."

"I… I can try guiding you through it," Aira offered, reaching for her wristband. "I know that your people progress through rituals and obscure procedures known only by shamans. But maybe I can do something for you?"

"Thank you for the offer, Aira," said Alliot, his fingers nervously working at his collar. "I appreciate it. But I'll pass for now. This all..." he gestured vaguely at the space between them, "That's too many fractured relationships at once. First, Ziemrot's betrayal, then questioning Ainorrh's motives, and now bypassing the rituals that have defined our progression for centuries? I'm not sure I'm ready to break that tradition as well."

"What about you, Aira?" Lila asked. "You've got a mountain of skill points yourself."

"I've been considering upgrading my Energy Manipulation to level ten," Aira replied, her fingers tracing patterns in the air as she accessed her interface. "It would require three thousand skill points, but it would double my detection range and significantly enhance my control over elemental energies."

"Would that help with the ritual here?" Alliot asked, eyeing the Splinter cautiously.

"I don't think it will affect it drastically," Aira replied. "But what's even more important, I don't have those three thousand points yet. I'll have enough when we get the second shard. But I'm uncertain if it's the right allocation. If I spread those points across multiple skills, I'd have more versatility, even if each individual ability would be weaker."

Lila leaned forward. "What about our bond? If you upgrade Two is a Crowd, would that strengthen what we can share?"

A thoughtful expression crossed Aira's face. "That's a good point. At level six, the bond might allow for more seamless skill sharing. And it would only cost 648 points."

"I've been thinking about new skills," Alliot said, uncomfortably shifting his weight. "Until now, I've focused my development on defense and tracking. But after what happened in Ziemrot..." His voice trailed off.

"Well, it's still a couple of levels before you get a new skill," said Aira. "Unless we can find a quest that will grant you one as a reward. But also, you want options that don't rely on your air affinity. Something that would work even near a foreign Source. Not that you have any influence on what you get. But maybe…"

Alliot nodded. "What?"

"We have more and more confirmation that I influence the people around me," said Aira slowly, deep in thought. "Maybe… there will be some changes with your next skill as well? I don't know. And I don't have any ways to test this theory besides just moving forward."

Aira gestured for them to gather closer. "Alright, let's work through this systematically. Lila, with your current level, focus on strengthening your three core skills before acquiring new ones. You've got another one, right? And you'll probably get one more after we finish this part of our shared quest. But we'll explore that when you have a bit more skill points and a wider choice."

She looked at Alliot. "For you, the recommendation is the same. We wait. Hopefully, the System, in it's benevolent wisdom, will grant you something that would allow you to function near foreign Sources without the adverse effects we saw in Ziemrot."

The night was unlike many previous they spent marching. This time, they dedicated the dark hours to mediation. However, it was never really dark in Lamcada, with flowing lava illuminating the enlightened city. The next morning, they turned their attention to another mystery. Alliot persuaded Ronorrh to lend him some materials about Ziemrot's old language. He sat cross-legged on the floor, the pages of Lila's grandfather's journal spread before him. His brow furrowed in concentration as he traced the angular script with his fingertips.

"I was right. It's a changed form old dialect. From the area near Ziemrot," he confirmed, comparing symbols in different passages. "I'm not a scholar, though. So, bear with me. It's evolved from what I studied and from what I can find in these reference books. The language is similar, but many terms have shifted their meanings."

Lila knelt beside him, her amber eyes scanning the pages with renewed interest. "Can you make out any of it?"

"Fragments," Alliot replied, carefully turning a brittle page. "Your grandfather was meticulous—there are detailed maps marking patrol routes near Ziemrot, sketches of defensive formations, notes on trading patterns, even pressed plant specimens with annotations about their magical properties." He paused, his finger stopping on a passage where the ink had been applied more heavily, as if written with heightened emotion. "This is interesting. Here, he describes what he calls 'moonlight meetings'—encounters with enlightened individuals who ventured beyond the city walls after dark. Unofficial exchanges of knowledge and goods."

"Bit of a root-walker, wasn't he?" Lila murmured, pride mixing with surprise in her voice. "Always knew he went further than the other rangers, but this..."

"There's more," Alliot said, turning to a page filled with the phrases and expressions Lila had grown up using. "These sayings—your catchphrases—they're listed here with notes."

"Let me see that," Lila leaned closer, her eyes widening.

Alliot translated haltingly: "'Forest expressions... similar to mountain speech... same roots...' The next part is unclear, but I think your grandfather was researching the Ziemrot expressions."

"Why would he do that?" Aira asked. "How could he do that? I thought enlightened do not speak to humans. I thought they can't speak to humans."

"Huh… That's right," Lila said. "I didn't even think about that. I'm still too human, I guess. For me, talking to… them… is natural."

"So, did he have regular contact with the enlightened?" Alliot asked, studying another passage. "Look here—there are entire conversations recorded, with notes on proper responses and cultural references."

"Lost in the lichen completely, I am," Lila said, disbelief coloring her voice. "Don't know what's worse—praising the Elders or speaking like torch-blind Khanorrh. Both seem twisted as bramble in a windstorm. So, did he learn this bark-talk from the enlightened?"

"Not exactly," Alliot replied. "The humans in that region apparently took the mountain-centric language of old humans and adapted it to forest imagery. 'As steady as bedrock' became 'solid as oak.' 'The mountain remembers' shifted to 'the forest never forgets.' The human language evolved with generations. But we, enlightened, do not change as swiftly as short-lived humans. That's why Khanorrh and other people of Ziemrot still speak that old language from before the Dawn of Enlightenment. That's why it's hard for me to understand these diaries."

"Two cultures sharing a linguistic root but expressing it through their own environment," Aira observed. "Amazing."

As Alliot continued translating what he could, a picture emerged of a man who had found ways to navigate the dangerous boundaries between human and enlightened territories—a ranger who had learned to speak the language of both worlds while belonging fully to neither.

"So my grandfather wasn't just exploring," Lila said softly. "He was... building bridges. Still, it doesn't give us an answer how he was even able to talk to them."

"It seems so," Alliot confirmed. "Though there are sections I can't translate. The human dialect has diverged too much from what I studied."

"Still caught in the wind," Lila murmured, running her fingers over the pages that contained her family's secret history. "Even when I think I've found answers, they just lead to more questions."

"Maybe Ronorrh can help," Aira suggested. "If Lamcada truly has maintained closer ties with humans, they might have scholars who understand these dialects better."

Later that day, they explored the city more thoroughly, each absorbing different aspects of Lamcada's unique culture. The seamless integration of fire into daily life was evident everywhere—from crafting to art, from engineering to construction. As they turned a corner into what appeared to be a marketplace, Lila halted so abruptly that Aira nearly collided with her back.

"Bones and bark!" Lila whispered, her amber eyes widening. "Are those... humans?"

Aira followed her gaze to a bustling workshop where several figures worked at forges and anvils. Some had the telltale glowing eyes of the enlightened, but others—their eyes normal, their movements more fluid despite the heat—were unmistakably human.

"It can't be," Alliot murmured, disbelief coloring his voice. But it was. Humans, working alongside the enlightened with an ease that bordered on familiarity. The two groups communicated through gestures and written notes, but the cooperation was undeniable. It wasn't hard to distinguish between them—the glowing eyes being the most prominent characteristic that marked the enlightened.

"I never imagined this was possible," Alliot admitted, his voice barely above a whisper as they watched a human blacksmith and an enlightened artisan collaborating on a blade that gleamed with both physical and magical perfection. The human said something, and the enlightened responded with a nod and what appeared to be a laugh. "In Wentouk, the very idea would be considered dangerous. Heretical. We're taught that meaningful cooperation is impossible—that humans lack the capacity to truly understand our ways." His hands trembled slightly. "Yet here they are, creating something neither could achieve alone."

"High on the cliffs with their own importance," Lila observed, watching the easy camaraderie between species that were supposed to be eternal enemies. "Makes me rethink what we read in the diaries. They have to have a way to communicate. We need to talk to Ronorrh!"

"You are right, and I hope Ronorrh answers our question," Aira said. "But also, the important question is 'why?'. What purpose does isolation serve? And who benefits from it? That's a question for both people of Wentouk and Ziemrot, who took even stricter stance. Even while we know it wasn't always like that. Even if your grandfather was old, Lila. His whole lifespan was just a blink of an eye for Khanorrh."

They continued their observations, gathering information and forming connections with both enlightened and human residents. Each conversation revealed more about Lamcada's approach to elemental magic and its relationship with the surrounding human settlements.

By evening, they reconvened at their quarters, minds buzzing with new insights and growing questions. The Splinter of the Worldroot sat at the center of their small circle, its glow seeming to respond to their discussion as they mapped out what they'd learned and what mysteries still remained.

"We need to speak with Ronorrh," Aira said decisively, watching the Splinter's emerald light pulse against her palm. "Tomorrow. No more circling around the truth. If Lamcada has found ways to communicate with humans while other cities maintain isolation, we deserve to know why."

Lila nodded, her amber eyes reflecting the Shard's glow. "Torch-blind obvious there's more beneath the surface here than a few friendly trades. The way humans and enlightened work together—it's like watching two parts of a forest thrive side by side instead of choking each other out. It's like two paths are coming together. Our paths, our journeys merge here, in Lamcada."

"It changes everything," Alliot said quietly, his expression troubled. "If this cooperation is possible—has been possible all along—then the barriers between our peoples weren't inevitable. They were choices."

"Choices made by those in power," Aira added, her voice hardening. "And I want to know why."
