In his time at the gas station, Eric had done a little wandering; not much, but enough that he’d mapped out most of the region immediately surrounding the gas station. He’d seen no further spots of strong mana like that of the dungeon he’d plunged earlier within about half an hour of the station. He didn’t know how dense dungeons were or how common safe rooms were, but the only way to find out was to get walking. He’d checked his map last night, but the thing only showed him the world around where he walked, like a true open world video game’s map. He could only fill it in by walking… or, if open-world video games had taught him anything, by climbing random towers.

Eric surveyed the sky hopefully just in case, but no towers presented themselves. He’d have to expand his map the old-fashioned way.

Ah, well. That’s what I figured I’d have to do anyways.

He picked a direction at random and set off. Cy napped on his shoulder as he walked. The little rabbit barely weighed anything, and it was no effort to carry him on his shoulder. He wore his coat open and his hood down, the leather rapidly heating up as the day warmed, but if he raised the hood, he could hide Cy within the hood as well.

As he walked, he kept Mana Sight active. A fair share of cockroaches, snakes, and other small monsters lunged at him out of the undergrowth, but he beat them back with the wooden bat he’d gained from the dungeon… and, occasionally, his bug-stomping boots themselves. He also practiced Firebolt along the way, though he was careful not to use so much mana that he grew exhausted. Slowly, it went from a fizzling embarrassment to a sizzling spark. It splashed off the average cockroach’s back, but provided a serious distraction to the few furred enemies who launched themselves at him from the trees; squirrels with saber-teeth, or gray-furred rats with wild red eyes. It wasn’t the one-hit destructive bolt he’d been hoping it would be, but he was still mastering it. It could still get better.

Between his higher stats, Regeneration, and the rapidly-improving Firebolt, the basic walk-around monsters weren’t much of a threat to him anymore. Some even began to avoid him; he saw the mana-gap of snakes scurrying away from him rather than trying to ambush him. The cockroaches were too dumb to flee, but easy killing. He squashed another, rubbing its head into a stone to fully execute it, and twisted his nose in disgust. “At least they aren’t swarming.”

“Of course they aren’t. I’m not using the Swarm spell,” a sleepy Cy interjected.

Eric froze. He looked at the rabbit on his shoulder. “That was you?”

“It was.”

“You tried to kill me?”

Cy put his paws up in a rabbit’s facsimile of a shrug. “Kill or be killed, Player. To be fair, I only did it the once. Once I figured out you weren’t hostile to me, I didn’t try again. I don’t have anything against low-level Players. You’re just part of the Game. But if you’re a threat to me, I’ll fight back. And a little rabbit like me doesn’t have a whole lot of life to chunk through if you decide to attack. If I don’t make the first move, I end up as rabbit stew.”

Eric opened his mouth, then closed it. It did make sense, he supposed. If not for his sister’s love of bunnies, he probably would have attacked Cy on sight, rather than waiting to see if the rabbit was actually a threat. Even as a level 1 Player, he was still a human fighting a rabbit. The human won that fight. Cy was right: if he didn’t make the first move, he was a dead bunny.

Of course, the problem was that he’d only let Cy live because he’d thought the cute little rabbit was a non-threat. If he’d realized Cy was the one attacking him, he would have absolutely fought back and killed the little rabbit. He pondered for a minute, considering everything that he’d just learned. Just because someone attacked in panic and self-defense didn’t mean they were necessarily hostile; simultaneously, just because he couldn’t tell who was attacking him didn’t mean they weren’t attacking him.

Then again, I don’t think most monsters are like Cy. I get the feeling he’s special.

“I truly thought I was dead when you survived the Swarm. That spell took all of my mana, you know. I can only cast Swarm once before needing to recharge for a few days ever since they took my mana. But luckily, you’re a rational Player who respects the perfection of the cunicular form, so we naturally came to a point of mutual respect.”

“Cunicular…”

“It’s a fancy word for rabbit,” Cy informed him, preening himself.

Eric frowned. “Wait, hold on. What do you mean, “they took your mana?””

“Isn’t it obvious? The Great Game feeds itself, young man. Mana, life force, call it what you will, but that’s what you’re staking on this game. If you lose, you’ll be just like me.” Cy paused. “Well. Not quite as handsome, obviously. But yes. Reduced to a monster.”

“Hold on. All these monsters were people?” Eric asked. He lifted his boot and looked at the cockroach scum there. What kind of person did you have to be to get turned into a monster roach?

Cy waggled his paw. “Sort of? Do you remember the opening message? The one about life energy?”

“Yeah.”

“I’m not sure, but I think it’s more like the Game harvests the energy of the worlds that lose, then forms monsters from that. Some monsters, anyways. Those… nasty bug things, I’m not so sure. I think they’re just enlarged versions of the local fauna. The same with the slithery hungry tubes and the weird less handsome quadrupeds. I suppose it’s really the dungeon monsters I’m referring to.” He paused. “Don’t feel too bad about killing them, though. Regardless of how the Game forms them, it strips their intelligence and self-awareness, and reduces them to what can truly be considered monsters. Most dungeon monsters are little more than killing machines.”

“Then why weren’t you in a dungeon?”

Cy harrumphed. “What kind of rabbit would I be if I couldn’t twist my way free of almost any cage? Ridiculous. Naturally, a rabbit archmage of my caliber escaped dungeons long ago.”

“Wait—you’re an archmage?”

Cy stilled, then turned his head, grooming his ear long-ways. “Was. A long time ago. Now, I can barely muster up a Swarm. Oh, how I’ve fallen.”

Eric twisted his head to look at Cy. “I’m a mage. Can you teach me spells?”

“Let me put it like this. Have you learned any spells from scratch so far? Truly studied and learned them earnestly? Or has the System fed you spellbooks?”

“That…” Eric fell silent. He had nothing to say to that; he hadn’t. Of course he hadn’t. He hadn’t known magic was real until a short while ago, so how could he have? And even if he wanted to, he just didn’t have the time to learn magic from scratch. The world was over. He had to level up and defeat dungeons fast to get both power and attention. But just because he needed to take the shortcut didn’t mean he couldn’t also take the long route. He could double down. Learn spells through the System and learn manually from Cy. His eyes glimmered with resolve. “But I can learn.”

“You can’t even cast a System-granted Firebolt correctly, and you can learn?” Cy scoffed dismissively. “Let me know when you’ve trained yourself to cast a Firebolt, and if it’s up to snuff, maybe, just maybe, I’ll try teaching you some of my all-important spells.”

Eric walked on, but the fact Cy had just casually revealed kept bouncing around in his head. He couldn’t just stay silent. Cy had evaded the question once, but he wasn't going to give up. “If dungeon monsters are killing machines, why are you normal?”

“Normal? Don’t be ridiculous. I’m far superior to normal.” Cy flipped his ear dramatically, then continued. “In earnestness, I was able to use a very complicated revival spell you couldn’t dream of learning to cast myself forward at the very end of my world’s Game, when the world was forfeit and our life essence was harvested. I’ve carried it forward ever since, hopping from Game to Game. If not for that, I’d probably be just another monster.”

“How? If they stole all your mana—”

“Stole. Past-tense. But monsters are allowed to accrue mana and cast spells—the Admins just never foresaw someone having enough magical prowess to send their consciousness from Game to Game.  I am an unforeseen anomaly. My usual modus operandi is to find somewhere quiet to cave up, absorb mana for the entire Game, then cast myself forward to the next one when it ends. You’re very fortunate to have left the Tutorial early enough to catch me out of my hidey hole!”

“Why? What’s the point of just… surviving like that?” He understood wanting to live—understood it very much, in fact. But what was the point of living as a helpless rabbit in life after life, cowering in a hole, accruing mana, and praying Players didn’t find you and kill you? What was Cy’s objective? Why suffer like that? Surely it wasn’t just for the sake of survival.

“Someone from as primitive a world as yours could never understand my grand plans.”

Eric glanced at him. “You don’t have a plan, do you.”

“Silence!” Cy snapped. “Do you want me to teach you magic or not?”

“Of course, Master Cy. Cy’s plans are the most complex and incredible. My weak brain is at fault!” Eric said woodenly.

“Well, as long as you understand.” Cy went back to preening.

Eric stared at Cy, a little overwhelmed. He’d just been hit with an absolute bomb of knowledge, and he didn’t really know what to do with it. If Cy was really telling the truth, then didn’t he have to keep his new familiar a secret from Joe and the other Admins? How could he even do that when Joe could read his mind? And what did all this mean? He’d known that his life force would be forfeit when he lost, but becoming a monster, that was… that was… honestly not the most groundbreaking revelation? Sure, it was shocking, but it wasn’t like he couldn’t see it being a thing. Like Cy said, the Game had to feed itself somehow. Of course it would use the harvested life force to spawn its monsters. He just hadn’t thought about what happened after Earth… if Earth lost. But it’s not going to lose. I’m going to make sure we win this Game. I’ll be the one to break the cycle.

And then he froze. Wait, is this forbidden knowledge? Am I allowed to know this?

Don’t panic, kid. I know about Cy. I let him live because he’s funny.

O-oh, Eric thought, a little startled by the voice in his head. But then, his grand plan…

Yeah. Let me know when it starts. I’m waiting. Joe somehow transmitted a sigh across the System, although Eric was sure he didn’t hear anything. Anycase, only real thing to watch out for is to make sure he doesn’t start blabbing about stuff he shouldn’t know during an epic fight or something. The show makers hate that. They’ll cut it for sure. It’s the number-one surefire way to get no screentime.

Still, it’s kinda crazy how he’s the only one, and I just happened to find him and make him my familiar.

The only one. Yep.

Eric pressed his lips together. He’s not the only one?

I’m not the only bored Regional Admin, so that’s a big fat nope. I was gonna warn you about Anomalies later, but since you’ve run into one, might as well: sometimes, monsters retain their consciousness. It’s cute when it’s a pompous rabbit, but imagine if that snake boss you fought had full grasp of its original reasoning power.

“Oh,” Eric said aloud, the full shock of it hitting him all at once. He’d won partially because he’d manipulated the snake into taking down the tent, but it was only so easy because it was basically just a big mindless monster. If it had awareness and intelligence on par with a human, that would change everything.

Yep. I’m a big softy with a sense of humor, but some Admins are downright sadistic. Watch out. Anomalies can very well be the end of your run. Joe paused, then continued, thoughtful: Make for great battles, though. Lots of clips from those. If you want to get to the top the traditional way, go fight some Anomalies.

And where would I find those?

Fucked if I know. That’s your job, kid.

“Don’t call me… ah, whatever.”

“What was that?” Cy asked.

“Nothing.” A cockroach burst out of the undergrowth, hissing furiously. Eric short-hopped over it, then stomped it before it could get away. It dropped nothing. That had been a theme, recently: enemies not dropping anything. He got the impression that if he was too strong, the monsters stopped dropping prizes. It wasn’t a total shock; even in the relatively small part he’d engaged with so far, the Game had a lot of mechanics to prevent camping or grinding. As valuable as grinding could be to levelling, it didn’t make for good viewing. There was a reason why “grinding off-stream” was a common practice amongst game streamers.

The people… beings, whatever… who lost the Game became its monsters. He wasn’t totally shocked by it. Maybe he should have been, but it was just the logical conclusion. What else would happen to the people who lost? Ordinary death, I guess? Was becoming a monster better or worse than just dying?

It was the kind of deep philosophical question that he wasn’t equipped to handle while running on days of gas station food, dealing with steel-chewing cockroaches, and trying to learn a new spell at the same time, all while hopefully heading in the right direction to find another safe zone. It lurked in the back of his mind for a bit, but there just wasn’t enough bonus processing power available in his brain for him to figure it out. He tucked it away for later… or maybe asking someone else.

Let’s focus on what’s right in front of me for now. Once I get everything up and running… then, maybe then, I can figure out how I feel about all this. He walked on, continuing under the dappled sunlight of the interleaved trees.
