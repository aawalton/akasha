The transition from the freezing, open air of the valley to the enclosed, suffocating atmosphere of the Goblin Caves was instantaneous. The portal spat Josh and Brett out into the wide, roughly hewn stone tunnel. The air was thick and heavy, carrying the distinct, stagnant scent of old iron, damp earth, and unwashed bodies. Torches sputtered in crude iron brackets bolted to the walls, casting flickering, erratic shadows across the uneven floor.

It looked exactly the same as it had when they had first trained here. The layout was identical, a permanent, unchanging pocket dimension generated by the system.

But the two men standing in the tunnel were vastly different.

"Well," Brett said, his voice echoing slightly in the damp cavern. He casually adjusted the collar of his fur cloak, looking down the dark corridor with absolute indifference. "This brings back a few memories. I believe I nearly lost a boot in a mud puddle right around that first corner."

"I recall you screaming quite loudly about a spider, actually," Josh replied, a wide grin spreading across his face. He rolled his broad shoulders, feeling the comforting, heavy weight of his plate armour. "Shall we take a leisurely stroll, or are we setting a pace?"

"Bhel challenged our clearing time," Brett stated, his deep red drake-scale gloves beginning to glow with a fierce, internal heat. "I intend to make the dwarf weep with profound, bitter inadequacy. We set a pace."

Brett opened his hands and his tiny, serpentine Cinder-Sprite familiar materialised from thin air, looping happily around his wrist before diving directly into his palms.

"Stay close to me," Brett advised smoothly.

They began to walk, their pace a brisk, purposeful march.

It did not take long for the dungeon to react to their presence. As they rounded the first major bend in the tunnel, a screeching horde of three dozen common goblins poured out from a side cavern. They were small, ugly, and armed with rusted cleavers and crude wooden clubs, their yellow eyes wide with aggressive, mindless fury.

Months ago, a pack this size would have forced the party into a desperate, frantic retreat to find a defensible choke point.

Today, Brett did not even break his stride.

He raised his right hand, his palm facing the charging green tide. He tapped into the latent power of his Scorched Veins skill, bypassing the normal casting time entirely. A massive, roaring torrent of pure, concentrated fire erupted from his bare hand. It was not a fireball; it was a continuous, devastating cone of elemental destruction that acted exactly like a high-pressure flamethrower.

The flames washed over the narrow corridor, instantly incinerating the front ranks of the goblins. The creatures simply vaporised into ash and golden dust before they could even scream. Brett casually swept his hand from left to right, painting the entire tunnel in blinding, searing yellow light.

Within five seconds, the corridor was completely clear.

"Fascinating," Brett murmured, examining his smoking glove. "The ambient mana density of this broken instance is actually amplifying the thermal output slightly."

"Keep moving," Josh laughed, stepping over a small pile of smoking ash.

They tore through the dungeon like a pair of vengeful gods. The sheer difference in power between level twenty-five adventurers and low-level dungeon fodder was staggering to witness.

Whenever a group of monsters clustered together, Brett simply unleashed another continuous, devastating wave of fire, turning the damp stone corridors into literal blast furnaces. If any goblins were lucky enough to spawn too close, or if a heavier, thicker-skinned orc managed to stumble through the flames, Josh was there to meet them.

Josh did not even bother drawing his longsword. He kept his heavy steel shield raised, moving forward with the unstoppable, crushing momentum of his Juggernaut's Tread skill. When a frantic goblin leaped at him, Josh simply stepped into the attack, driving the heavy steel rim of his shield directly into the creature's face. The physical force behind his tier three strength shattered bones instantly, sending the broken bodies flying backwards into the darkness.

It was a casual, incredibly efficient slaughter. They barely had to communicate. They moved as a single, lethal unit, mapping the familiar twists and turns of the cavern from sheer muscle memory.

Less than forty-five minutes after stepping through the entrance portal, they reached the end of the line.

The crude, twisting tunnels opened up into a massive, cavernous antechamber. At the far end stood a pair of enormous ten-foot doors of blackened bronze, etched with scenes of battle. Runes pulsed faintly across the ancient craftsmanship, a single line of crimson energy tracing the join.

"Forty-two minutes by my count," Brett noted, checking a pocket watch he had acquired in Ashenfall. He looked highly satisfied. "I believe Ronald's original solo record was just over an hour. Bhel is going to be furious."

"Only if we kill the boss quickly," Josh pointed out, stepping up to the heavy bronze doors. He placed his gauntleted hands flat against the metal and pushed.

The doors groaned heavily, scraping against the stone floor as they opened inward, revealing the final chamber.

The boss room was illuminated by several massive iron braziers burning with a sickly green flame. The Goblin Chieftain rose from its skull throne, a hulking, grotesquely muscular seven-foot silhouette. It resembled its smaller brethren but was terrifyingly, unnaturally stretched. It gripped a notched black axe bigger than Bhel, its yellow eyes gleaming. It offered a jagged, rotting smile and a wet, choking laugh.

Josh did not let the creature finish its dramatic introduction.

He turned his head slightly, locking eyes with Brett. "Mine."

Brett stepped back with a polite, obliging sweep of his hand, completely content to let the fighter expend the physical effort.

Josh turned his attention back to the massive, laughing Chieftain. He felt the familiar, eager spark of his class abilities ignite within his chest. Bright, violent arcs of blue static electricity erupted across his heavy plate armour, snapping and hissing in the damp air.

He triggered his Adamantine Breach skill.

Josh crossed the fifty-foot chamber in a single, unstoppable blur of motion. He was a human lightning bolt, leaving a trail of scorched stone in his wake.

The Goblin Chieftain's wet laugh was cut abruptly short as Josh slammed into it with the force of a runaway carriage. The heavy steel shield took the massive monster squarely in the chest, lifting it entirely off its feet and driving it violently backwards into its own throne of skulls. The bone throne shattered into a hundred pieces under the impact.

Before the stunned Chieftain could even attempt to raise its massive black axe, Josh drove his longsword upward. The blade, supercharged with the raw, crackling electrical energy of his Stormforged Adamantite skill, pierced the monster's thick hide just beneath its ribs.

The static discharge was blinding. A massive shock of blue lightning tore through the Chieftain's internal organs, instantly stopping its heart.

The giant goblin went completely rigid, its yellow eyes rolling back in its head, before dissolving instantly into a massive, glittering mountain of golden dust.

The fight had lasted less than three seconds.

A moment later, a heavy, iron-bound chest materialised in the centre of the room with a soft chime, signifying the dungeon's completion. Almost simultaneously, a swirling, bright blue portal ripped open against the far wall, offering their exit.

Josh and Brett both looked at the heavy loot chest.

"Any interest?" Josh asked, deactivating his lightning aura and rolling his shoulders.

"Absolutely none," Brett scoffed, already walking towards the exit portal. "It will contain nothing but low-tier copper coins, a rusted dagger, and perhaps a minor health potion. Our goal was simply to bleed the ambient mana from the instance to stabilise the exterior rift. The janitorial work is complete."

Josh laughed, entirely agreeing with the mage's assessment. He sheathed his sword and followed his best friend, walking straight past the unopened chest and stepping through the blue light of the exit.

The world shifted, and they were immediately deposited back into the freezing, muddy air of the valley outside.

The sudden appearance of two men emerging from the portal caused the two parties guarding the entrance to flinch violently, their spears and swords raising instinctively. However, recognising Josh's familiar, battered plate armour and Brett's distinct fur cloak, the guards quickly lowered their weapons, breathing heavy sighs of relief.

"Clear," Josh announced to the guards, offering them a reassuring nod. "That instance is completely scrubbed."

He looked past the guard perimeter. Near a stack of wooden supply crates, Carcan was sitting comfortably on a folded blanket, sharing a steaming cup of tea with a human cleric. The elven healer looked up as the portal flared, her luminous silver eyes immediately finding Josh in the gloom. A bright, genuine smile broke across her pale face, and she raised a slender hand, offering a warm wave.

Josh felt a sudden, familiar warmth bloom in his chest. He smiled broadly, raising his own gauntleted hand in return, and gestured for Brett to follow him over to the crates.

"You survived," Carcan noted playfully as they approached, handing her empty teacup back to the junior cleric. "I trust the mighty goblin hordes did not give you too much trouble?"

"It was essentially a very loud, very fiery walk in the park," Brett sighed dramatically, finding a clean spot on a nearby crate to sit. He began meticulously brushing imaginary dust from his sleeves. "I barely even needed to stretch my magical reserves. The thermal conductivity of those damp tunnels is quite remarkable, however."

Josh leaned against the crate next to Carcan, looking back towards the swirling purple light of the entrance. "Did the short one and the pointy-eared one make it back yet?"

"No," Carcan confirmed, her smile widening in amusement. She smoothed her pristine white robes. "You are the first to emerge. It has only been forty-five minutes. I believe Bhel boldly predicted they would be waiting for you."

"Excellent," Josh grinned, crossing his arms over his breastplate. "This is going to be incredibly satisfying."

They sat and chatted with Carcan and the cleric for several minutes, enjoying the cold, fresh air after the oppressive dampness of the dungeon. They discussed the logistics of setting up the camp again, and the work that would need to be done to make sure it was safe.

Roughly twelve minutes later, the portal flared violently once more.

Bhel and Perberos stepped out into the mud.

Bhel marched forward with a confident, arrogant swagger, his heavy boots stomping through the freezing slurry. His twin axes were drawn, and his beard was slightly singed, but he looked incredibly pleased with himself. He scanned the immediate area, his dark eyes searching for the human duo.

When Bhel spotted Josh and Brett sitting casually on the crates, sharing a pot of tea with Carcan, he froze completely.

Josh did not miss the opportunity. He pushed himself off the crate, threw his arms wide, and let out a massive, booming cheer that echoed across the entire valley. "The mighty dwarf finally arrives! We were beginning to wonder if you got lost in the dark!"

Bhel’s confident swagger evaporated instantly. His broad, tanned face fell into a mask of utter, profound disappointment, followed quickly by a scowl of deep defensive indignation.

"You cheated," Bhel accused loudly, stomping over to their crates and pointing a thick, calloused finger directly at Brett. "You used some sort of illegal spatial magic to bypass the corridors. I know you did."

"I assure you, my stunted friend, our methods were entirely conventional," Brett replied smoothly, taking a slow, thoroughly obnoxious sip of his tea. "We simply walked very fast and burned everything in our path. It is called efficiency. You should look into it. Plus, I don’t have access to any special magic as you well know."

"We got bogged down by the sheer numbers!" Bhel complained, crossing his arms and glaring at Josh. "The corridors were completely choked with the little beasts. I couldn't swing without hitting three of them. And then my left axe got stuck securely between the ribs of a particularly stubborn cave troll at one point. Took me a solid minute of pulling to wrench it free. Perberos had to shoot six goblins just to give me breathing room."

Perberos, who had followed the dwarf over silently, simply offered a small, barely perceptible shrug, confirming the story. The elven ranger looked entirely unbothered by the loss, clearly treating the entire exercise with his usual cold professionalism.

"Excuses, excuses," Josh teased, clapping the dwarf firmly on his broad, iron-plated shoulder. "The record stands. Humans win."

Bhel let out a low, rumbling growl, kicking a clump of frozen mud with his boot. He looked back at the swirling purple entrance portal, his competitive spirit flaring fiercely.

"Right," Bhel declared, hefting his axes and turning back to Perberos. "We go again. Right now. I know the exact layout now. I can easily shave fifteen minutes off that time if I just use my momentum charges instead of stopping to hack them to pieces."

"Bhel, you literally just stepped out," Carcan chided gently, standing up from her blanket. "You need to rest. Drink some water. Eat something."

"No time for eating!" Bhel insisted stubbornly, already marching back towards the entrance. "The portal is still bleeding mana! It is our civic duty to clean it! Come on, Perberos!"

The elf let out a very faint, deeply suffering sigh, but obediently turned and followed the highly motivated dwarf back to the entrance.

That brief, comedic exchange set the strict, gruelling rhythm for the rest of the day.

They became the most overqualified magical janitors on the continent. The two pairs took it in turns, staggering their entrances to ensure they generated separate, isolated instances. They ran the dungeon, slaughtered everything inside to burn off the rampant ambient mana, killed the boss, exited, took a brief fifteen-minute break to drink water and chew on salted meat, and then plunged right back into the dark.

It was a tedious, repetitive grind, completely devoid of any real danger, but it was absolutely necessary.

As the sun began to sink below the western ridges, plunging the valley into a deep, freezing twilight, their efforts finally began to show tangible results. The portal, which had been a violent, erratic vortex of aggressive purple energy all morning, began to calm. The violent flares slowed, and the colour slowly shifted from a bruised purple to a dark-blue.

The rift was starting to return to its natural state, though they still had a lot of work to do.

Josh stepped out of the exit portal for the seventh time that day, his muscles aching with a deep, satisfying fatigue. He met Bhel and Perberos near the supply crates. They were all covered in a fine layer of soot, dried monster blood, and golden dust, but they looked victorious.

They approached the central fire, where Carcan had secured them a space. The relief in the camp was palpable. "Right," Josh said, taking a bowl of hot stew from Carcan with a grateful nod. He looked at his exhausted, filthy, but entirely unbroken party. "We eat, we set a basic, two-man guard rotation just to be safe, and then we sleep. Tomorrow, we finish clearing out the dungeon, and hopefully head for home the day after.”
