Two hours before dawn, the golden trio met Rick at the edge of town.

Iris looked annoyed and about to set something on fire even before breakfast. Cyrus was still half asleep and letting himself be dragged forward by her impatience, while Hakon wore the silent displeasure of someone too disciplined to complain but not nearly disciplined enough to enjoy waking at such an hour.

Rick, meanwhile, saw nothing wrong with it. According to him, the best part of the day should be spent hunting, not walking toward a hunting ground like lazy merchants on an afternoon errand. If they wanted to reach the forest, scout, prepare, and still have good light for the actual work, then leaving before dawn was only sensible.

The trio disagreed. Repeatedly.

Rick ignored them.

Their differences did not end when the sun finally rose, either. The farther they went, the more clearly Rick understood why hunters preferred to work alone. A party was slow. Even when everyone moved with purpose, they still moved heavily, talked more, reacted slow, and took longer to settle every small disagreement. Once they entered the forest proper, the problem became impossible to miss. Rick could not have measured the lost time exactly, but he was certain the trio would slow him by at least a few hours every day.

He did not complain, since that had been part of the agreement.

The day before, they had settled on a plan to ambush a grey wolf pack that roamed near the border of Rick’s current hunting ground. The beasts were only copper-tier, but there were several of them, and numbers had their own value. A pack like that would still bring back decent loot.

Unfortunately, the closer they came to the area, the more obvious it became that the original plan would not work.

They were too noisy.

A lone hunter could sometimes pass near wolves without drawing immediate attention if the wind, terrain, and luck all aligned. A four-person group, however, had too many chances to give itself away. A snapped branch here, a whispered complaint there, the wrong step at the wrong moment—small things, but in the forest, small things killed plans.

So Rick stopped them before they entered the wolves’ territory and proposed a change.

The trio complained, of course.

Cyrus complained openly, Iris intelligently, and Hakon mostly watched them and made short comments. But none of them pushed very hard after Rick made the reason clear. If the original plan had become unworkable because they could not move quietly, then arguing beyond a certain point would only confirm the problem.

Fortunately, their number was useful once the new planning started. What Rick lacked in raw combat strength, he made up for in local knowledge. What the trio lacked in familiarity with this stretch of forest, they partly replaced with more eyes, options, and hands to execute a layered attack. After several minutes of back-and-forth, the new plan finally took shape, and Rick felt far better about it than he had about the first.

The foundation of it still came from knowledge. Ever since the bookstore, Rick had become almost embarrassingly fond of books. He had seen what proper information could do for a hunter’s chances, and now he trusted it more than rumor, pride, or instinct pretending to be wisdom. So when he went to meet the trio the day before, he had used one of his reading credits to look up grey wolves in the beastiarium.

That had changed the whole hunt. Grey wolves were carnivorous wind-essence beasts that preferred windy plains near a water source. The nature of their essence made them especially fast. That alone would have made them dangerous, but in a pack, speed became much deadlier. They did not merely chase. They overwhelmed. In open ground, they could run down prey quickly enough that mistakes would be fatal.

Fortunately for Rick, they were not living in open plains. This pack had claimed a small valley south of the mountains. The place had fewer trees and stronger crosswinds than the surrounding forest, with a stream running through it. It suited the beasts well, but it also gave them predictable ground. The valley was still bordered by forest on its sides, which made it one of the few places nearby where an ambush had a real chance of succeeding.

The first time Rick had found the valley, he had been tempted to try something reckless.

Fortunately, temptation was not the same as stupidity. A hunter pursued by grey wolves through broken forest did not enjoy good odds, and Rick had known it. But that had been before. Now he had a party, and even if they could not execute a proper hunter’s ambush, they could still get close enough to build something effective.

By around noon, the group reached the last stretch before the valley.

Rick left the trio at a landmark they could not mistake and went ahead alone to scout.

The depression began near the feet of the mountains and ran back toward the Wall for perhaps six kilometers. Crossing it at full speed would take more than ten minutes, which meant it was too large to search carelessly. Fortunately, Rick did not need to search all of it. He only needed to confirm where the pack currently stood.

That took less than a minute.

The wolves were at the stream, roughly one kilometer north of his position, closer to the mountain side than before. Rick studied them briefly from cover, marked the wind in his mind, and returned at once.

The plan would put him in danger. There was no point pretending otherwise.

Even so, the trio would be in the worse position once the real fighting began. If things went badly, Rick at least knew where the nearest shelter lay and how to reach it. The adventurers would have to hold against a pack in unfamiliar ground. He had no intention of abandoning them over the first difficulty, but neither did he plan to die foolishly for people he had only just met. If the situation turned hopeless, then hopeless was what it was.

Fortunately, the wolves’ position favored them.

Half an hour later, the preparations were complete.

The trio took their places around the chosen ambush site. Iris had the most important role in the opening move. Dried grass and other flammable material had been laid in intersecting lines through the clearing, forming a rough grid that could be ignited quickly with her fire cantrips. Cyrus and Hakon positioned themselves where they could strike any beast forced into confusion or trapped by the sudden flames. Rick’s part was the simplest to describe and the most difficult to perform.

He had to bring the pack in.

So he returned alone to the valley’s edge, confirmed the wolves’ new position, and took out a slinger.

There were five of them. A little closer now, but still well within the bounds of the plan.

Rick loaded the slinger and began sending small stones toward the pack. He did not try to hit them. That would have required luck and wasted time. The goal was only to offend.

It worked immediately.

The first few stones struck dirt and rock near the wolves, drawing sharp heads up all at once. The next sent them into a low, furious frenzy. Then one of them spotted Rick through the brush, and the whole pack broke into a sprint.

A moment later, Rick was running too.

There was a vast difference between imagining oneself fleeing wind-essence beasts and actually doing it. On one side lay planning, courage, and reasonable confidence. On the other lay the very real sound of five fast predators crashing after you through the forest.

Rick nearly dropped the slinger from fright.

In fact, he did drop it a few breaths later.

He had roughly half a kilometer to cover before reaching the ambush point. Under ordinary conditions, that was manageable. Under these conditions, it felt impossible. The wolves were at least three times faster than he was, and the only reason they had not already reached him was the forest itself. Trees, brush, and uneven ground did for him what speed could not.

Even so, the run was hard.

Rick threw himself between trunks, over roots, and through bushes that slashed at his arms and face. Branches whipped across his clothes. Loose stones rolled underfoot. He took several cuts and more bruises than he would bother counting later. Behind him came the howls—close enough now that he no longer needed imagination to supply the danger.

Then he saw the landmark. The agreed place.

He did not slow.

If anything, he forced more speed into his legs. The wolves were too close now for caution, but he still had to angle his path carefully enough not to ruin the preparation lines. If he trampled through the wrong patch and broke the trap, then all of this would become a very stupid story told briefly over his corpse.

The distance vanished.

Rick heard one wolf behind him more clearly than the others, close enough that the sound no longer blended with the pack. It had singled him out. The beast was only a few strides back when the ambush site suddenly came alive.

Fire burst upward in intersecting lines.

The dried grass grid ignited almost all at once, orange-red flames racing through the prepared paths with violent speed. Heat struck Rick’s side. Smoke twisted upward. The peaceful bit of forest transformed into a burning snare in the space of a breath.

Unfortunately, one wolf had already crossed the line that was meant to shield him.

Iris had done what she could. She had needed to wait until Rick crossed the final strip before igniting the whole thing, and that final heartbeat had been fatal. The wolf reached him first.

Rick did not waste time cursing.

He threw himself toward the spear they had prepared near the edge of the grid, hit the ground hard, and snatched it up in one desperate motion.

Surprisingly, the wolf did not press the attack.

The beast could have torn his throat out with one bite. Instead, the moment the flames rose fully around it, instinct won over hunger. It abandoned the pursuit, turning abruptly and trying to find some path away from the fire.

Rick got his feet under him and leveled the spear anyway.

The wolf barely cared that he existed now. It was panicked, disoriented, horrified. The beastiarium had been right. Grey wolves were fiercely averse to fire. The moment their instincts judged it a real threat, running came before killing.

Good.

Because now the plan had actually begun.
