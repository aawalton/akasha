The pink ether sword changed shape into a slender, refined blade. Its surface was perfectly smooth and straight, etched with countless floral patterns so fine they could only be seen up close. Yet when planar flowed through them, they shone as if they had truly come alive.

A strange fragrance spread out, as though the scents of ten thousand flowers had been crushed together into incense. It was so overpowering that no ordinary living creature’s sense of smell could possibly sort it apart. Human senses were duller than a dog’s; the human brain simply was not built to process that much scent information.

But what this divine weapon did was send the information of scent directly into the opponent’s senses, straight into the brain itself. The instant the Lord of the Mountain inhaled the indescribable floral scent, the data came crashing down and compressed inside its mind.

“Hrk—!!”

The Lord of the Mountain inhaled sharply. Its brain was flooded with too much information at once, making it dizzy. Rina launched into the Peach Blossom Dance. Every thrust of her sword shot forward in a straight line, beautiful yet filled with hidden force. The Lord of the Mountain flipped backward, sending two leather balls flying into tree trunks so they would rebound toward Rina.

Something is wrong with this human woman. Her divine weapon is too strange, the Lord of the Mountain thought. Its instincts screamed at it not to touch whatever power she wielded. With every swing of Rina’s blade, peach petals scattered through the air. Just a few movements of her sword were enough to dye the whole world pink.

“Hundred-Flower Poet Song, secret art — Fragrance Across Ten Thousand Li.”

Rina released a massive amount of flower-element power all at once. The already-thick floral scent intensified, not twice over but tenfold.

“You insane human woman! Are you trying to make me die from flower stench? If you want to fight, then fight with power!”

“Fight with power? What a shallow way of thinking. You want an ordinary human woman like me to measure raw strength against a demon like you? Try being born a normal human woman first, then say that line to me again. I’ll even help send you off for your next reincarnation.”

Rina raised Yuanyang and cut down the balls flying at her from every direction. Petals—not just peach blossoms now—filled the air in dazzling profusion. The young woman began to dance through her forms, relying on a flexible body and muscles honed by repeating the same techniques for years upon years. With bizarre footwork and fluid movement, she closed in on the Lord of the Mountain.

This was far too abnormal. The Lord of the Mountain did not remember Jinyo being this difficult to handle. And yet it was the same sword, the same sword art, even the same secret techniques.

When a Lord of the Mountain died, its memories and abilities passed on to the next strongest Eltharoc. It was a kind of rebirth through succession. That meant the current Lord of the Mountain possessed all the combat experience of its predecessor against Jinyo—Hundred-Flower Poet Song, flower-element power, even the Punishment Wave.

But all of that experience was useless against Rina Erindale.

Is it because of the divine weapon? This woman’s divine weapon has some mysterious power.

It did not know the power of the divine weapon called Yuanyang. Jinyo had never used it. No—Jinyo could not use it, and never would.

Each divine weapon had its own personality, likes, and dislikes. The sword Kawi-gan... no, Yuanyang, hated men and loved women. The more a woman excelled in Hundred-Flower Poet Song, the more it adored her. Rina’s understanding of that sword art had reached the grandmaster level. She stood only one step away from becoming a Sword King. If Yuanyang did not love her, then whom would it ever love?

I can’t see. My senses are being stolen.

The Lord of the Mountain’s breathing turned ragged, its chest heaving. It hurled dozens of balls infused with Punishment Wave at Rina. They ricocheted off the trees in chains, creating a web of attacks centered on her—yet not a single ball struck her.

Impossible. Don’t tell me my senses are so compromised I can’t hit her. No—that’s not it. I’m not even aiming. I’m just throwing them out to trigger a chain reaction. And still none of them hit? How is that possible?

It had to be a nightmare. It had to be.

Rina stood still as a statue, her very existence insulting its own. As a servant of Heaven, it refused to endure such humiliation any longer.

The Lord of the Mountain lunged straight at her. It concentrated its power into a spinning force around its fist and unleashed a punch that split the air with a shriek. Rina was forced to dodge. She stepped on blossoms and leapt among the trees, but even she was not as fast as the Lord of the Mountain.

This Lord of the Mountain was not like the last one. Instead of focusing on muscle and brute strength, it specialized in agility and endurance. It pursued Rina like a clinging ghost.

“So because your senses don’t get fooled in close combat, you gave up ranged attacks and switched to melee instead? But isn’t it a little stupid to walk straight into my striking range?”

Hundred-Flower Poet Song, Fourth Form — Rose Thorn Whip.

planar took shape as a whip and lashed across the Lord of the Mountain’s body, carving countless burning cuts into its flesh. The illusion woven from planar was so vivid it bordered on reality, nearly more than its body could endure. The Lord of the Mountain steadied itself, focusing on regenerating muscle and skin.

Yes—it bore an immensely powerful boosting curse. As long as it kept eating cursed fruit, it would remain immortal, incapable of dying. The Lord of the Mountain unleashed a rapid chain of punches. Rina spread her legs and dodged in a bizarre stance, then spun behind it and slashed for the back of its neck. It summoned a ball in time to intercept the strike.

Why could it not land even one hit? The Lord of the Mountain truly could not understand. Rina’s reflexes were too good. Meanwhile, it felt its awareness and physical strength beginning to deteriorate.

“This is bad.”

It’s definitely bad. She’s no easy prey. Could it be true, what she said—that she’s killed many of Their Excellencies? This filthy, lowborn human woman?

The Lord of the Mountain was shaken. It began to suspect that Rina was one of them—those madmen tasked with hunting down the beings from Heaven, the ones who opposed Heaven without cherishing their own lives.

I see now. Because she constantly has to deal with Their Excellencies, she cannot afford to be hit at all. She must have invested everything into reaction speed!

It believed it had discovered one of her weaknesses. All things humans called Unknowns shared one trait: attacks that ignored defense and a technique called Punishment Wave.

Punishment Wave was practically a basic skill of those from Heaven. Higher-level Unknowns also possessed Double Impact. Every attack weakened the target’s physical limits. Rina’s unit’s strategy was absolute: never get hit, because even a single mistake put one at a catastrophic disadvantage. Against the power of the Unknown, defense meant nothing.

Sense Mind. Danger Perception. Evasion.All of these skills had already been raised to the highest level a normal human could achieve.

If Rina had devoted everything to evasion, then her body itself had to be fragile. One hit—just one—and victory would be within reach.

That was what the Lord of the Mountain thought.

It chased Rina in a frenzy. Hundreds of leather balls were controlled midair, spun, and launched without even being thrown. It no longer cared about precision or maximum speed.

Yes. Just one hit. One hit and Rina would be crippled all over.

Rina flipped through the air, dodging every leather ball rebounding off trees, grass, even rocks. Her flexible body, trained since childhood, seemed made for such movement. The supple agility that no man could ever match was a difference impossible to overcome.

If Nephrin had been there, she would have stood gaping. It was no different from watching an Olympic gymnastics performance—the kind of flexibility that let her rotate and move freely, creating unimaginable forms through control of planar and illusion.

Hundred-Flower Poet Song, First Form — Peach Blossom Dance.

Rina’s sword flashed again and again. Peach blossoms budded, burst, and bloomed, spreading pain throughout the Lord of the Mountain’s body. She did not stop after one form but chained the techniques together. That was the brilliance of Hundred-Flower Poet Song: it could flow continuously from the first form all the way to the last without interruption—but only with a body that possessed immense flexibility, strength, endurance, concentration, and speed.

I can’t see. I can’t tell what is what anymore.

The Lord of the Mountain’s mind went blank. Its body regenerated and was damaged over and over again. This woman did not even want it dead—she was using it as a practice dummy.

“Your brain’s reaching its limit, isn’t it? The pain, the flower scent, the nausea, the urge to vomit, the inability to think—everything feels slower and slower. Every sense you have, whether smell, taste, sight, or even thought itself... if you can’t escape my illusions, then prepare to die.”

Not a single attack would ever reach her.

The inability to distinguish reality from illusion—this was the terrifying power of the divine weapon Yuanyang. When fighting with this blade, if the enemy became afflicted with Infatuation, then continued strikes would build up flower-element power inside the target’s body. Once that accumulation passed a certain point, Infatuation would become Brain Leak.

Every stat—strength, planar, intelligence, reflexes, willpower, and charm—would become -100.

Which meant the moment Brain Leak took hold, all stats dropped by 600 at once.

“Hundred-Flower Poet Song, Tenth Form — Ten Thousand Falling Petals.”

Rina danced the sword form, and ten thousand flower petals gathered into a whirling storm. Here, before all, the mightiest technique of the Hundred-Flower Poet Song school was revealed.

The swirling petals passed through the Lord of the Mountain’s body. It did not even cry out. Its sense of pain had already been stolen. The petals born of illusion sliced and peeled at its skin, creating countless tiny cuts all across its body.

The flower storm spun at high speed, like ten thousand blades stabbing, carving, and flensing flesh, until the Lord of the Mountain’s body turned red. It tried to regenerate, but the damage came faster than its healing.

“Aaaargh!”

Its soul screamed. It did not hurt—its cry came from the deepest layer of instinct, from something more primal than pain. It was forgetting. Its senses—no, even its brain—were being overrun by the thoughts of that human woman.

No. It could not even think of escaping or running. Its brain was ordering it to stand still.

This was bad. Truly bad.

“Aaaargh!”

“You’re noisy.”

Rina controlled Yuanyang and struck three times in a single second, severing its head. The enormous thing toppled from its shoulders. Its body tried to regenerate, but like every Eltharoc, it gradually withered and lost all power, becoming nothing more than an empty husk, no different from a dried root.

Rina felt no joy in defeating it. Even if it had been the true Unknown, she still would not have been happy.

“Yuanyang... so you really chose me.”

Rina picked up the sword and spoke to it. The pink blade reflected the moonlight in reply.

“Why didn’t you choose the master’s son and choose me instead...? No, of course. One is a man, and the other’s swordsmanship is pathetic. But please—give Jinyo a chance.”

No answering gleam came from the divine weapon. If the sword had a personality, then Yuanyang was a proud young lady whose mouth never matched her heart.

Rina absorbed the experience points she had gained from killing the Lord of the Mountain. Because it was a newly reborn Lord of the Mountain, it was only level 40—still ten levels below the one Jinyo and the others were fighting. She immediately ran back toward the fields. Who knew? Even killing the leader did not mean the situation would calm down.

“Yaaagh! Die, damn you!”

All the survivors grabbed farming tools and fought back. Jinyo knew chaos was erupting outside. There were wounded people who needed help, but he absolutely could not stop the sword dance. The monsters had attacked in a massive swarm—the largest ever seen. If nothing was done, hundreds would die.

Esther slashed his sword and sent flames cutting through the throats of every pumpkin-headed creature that came seeking death. They were fearless and revolting. There were too many of them, and their humanoid shapes only made them more nauseating to cut down.

“Sir Esther, watch out!” Luciria shouted from behind him.

A living human face, its eyes nothing but white.

“Whatever. If I forgot, then it can’t be anyone important.”

Esther did not spare it another thought. He turned back to fight the other monsters, leaving the split-open face there to rot.
