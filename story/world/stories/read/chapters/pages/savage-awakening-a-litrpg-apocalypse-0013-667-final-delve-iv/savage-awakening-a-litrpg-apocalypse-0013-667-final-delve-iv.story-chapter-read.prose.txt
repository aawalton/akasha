They sensed the auras before they entered the realm—a Prime aura and then a partial aura which they knew signaled one of Stegar's mech-suits. Side by side.

They talked it over and quickly decided to engage.

Zane shattered the distant System steel, exposing a realm of sterile white colors. Then he landed, Reina's avatar not far behind.

Their initial inspection of this floor, before they'd landed, had found nothing concerning—although he knew a cursory soul-sense scan alone could be faulty. Still, pretty much the instant he landed he was certain he'd be fine.

The room actually reminded him of the sparring rooms in the Silver Winds' facilities, a massive cube of spotless white all around, broken up in grids.

Reina caught his eyes and gave him a nod. Her initial assessment had been that this floor had been repurposed to be an alchemical testing space of sorts. They knew Malzareth liked its experiments.

The enemy was stationed just twenty miles away. Since the room was all flat planes, they could see each other quite clearly.

One was a Stegar body, just as expected—Stegar's 2nd, judging by all its bulky plating. Stegar's bodies were numbered according to combat strength.

Then there was a Prime Summons, a knight with silver eyes wielding a shield and a thick, six-foot-long blade edged in Destruction-white.

Zane finished his inspection and concluded he had no particular reason for concern.

Stegar attacked first.

It moved with a speed that should've been impossible for its size. Except its universe flexed, drawing a tight circle around it, and there was an immense expulsion of essence—a distortion of space.

It hyper-jumped.

But before it could complete whatever attack it was attempting, Zane had already stepped out of the way.

Space seemed to collapse before him as he did.

He slashed, using its own momentum against it. A blinding line of Supernova light and that body went careening into the far wall.

He wondered if that was meant to be some kind of distraction; it worked to a degree. The Prime Summons had phased over in the time he took to strike. Its blade arced for his neck but he easily stepped away again.

Seven hundred-plus shards of Destruction slicked the edge of the blade as it passed by. A wall of pure force extended, carving the length of the room.

Then Zane seized the blade, heedless of its sharpness.

In the same motion he wrenched it out of the Summons' hands and turned it back on itself. He would've punched it right through its chest piece if it hadn't twisted at quite impressive speeds, leaving it with just a crushed left flank.

He would've done more except he was forced to confront a rush of metal. Stegar's 2nd was already in quite terrible shape, slashed nearly in two, but it still attacked then—a clear bid to let its companion escape.

He caught Stegar's body, dropping the sword as he did, and slammed that tank-like mech-suit into the ground with such force it trembled the walls of the testing chamber. He treated it to two firm Supernova Fists.

Then he stood.

The moment he’d dropped the sword, it’d flown away. The Prime Summons had reclaimed the weapon.

He noticed something else—an instant before the Summons had dodged just then, its eyes had brightened.

When it engaged him now, it moved almost like it had precognition—like it suddenly had a perfect understanding of all of his movements and moved to counter accordingly.

It had a fairly basic skill set, centered around sword skills and force-slashes. It simply wielded them strangely well all of a sudden, with a level of acuity none of Malzareth's Summons had demonstrated. It felt like he was fighting a master swordsman.

Toward the end of the fight, the Summons tried to sneak in a strange Skill—one that wreathed the blade in green lines of Fate. In that instant Zane felt, with a strange certainty, that he couldn't dodge the strike; it had an aura of inevitability to it. It'd land wherever he tried to dodge.

He didn't mind parrying it with an axe and then finishing the Summons with his next.

All in all, he didn't find the fight particularly noteworthy. After Stegar’s body went down the Prime Summons lasted just three or four exchanges, and he estimated it was slightly stronger than Gilgoroth had been.

He expected it'd take quite a lot more to take down a higher-tier Prime. It still did help solidify his understanding of his capacities right now.

"You weren't truly fighting a Summons just now," said Reina, inspecting the spot where it'd fallen. Her brow was creased slightly. "You could see the moment it changed, couldn't you?"

He nodded. He'd figured—its sudden cognitive boost and perception increase was quite a big tell.

“I’m almost certain that was Malzareth itself,” she said.

If there was any doubt before, now it definitely knew they were here.

It wasn't necessarily something he was concerned about; she seemed to take it in stride as well. They'd expected it around this point.

"How did the Steps feel?" she asked, considering him.

"Natural," he told her, and she nodded.

He'd been tripping over himself when he'd first worked the spatial elements of his new Stellar Collapse Concept into his steps. He had to rethink his relationship to space and distance a bit for it to make intuitive sense—at first his body just didn't expect space to collapse like it did.

But he'd spent a few months in-Dilation Machine trying it out, in between his various other shard escapades, and ironed it out. With its effects, the attacks just now had very little chance of genuinely hitting him. Even setting aside his body’s new reaction speeds.

After he worked the spatial aspect into his other Skills, like his Slash and his Hammer, he'd seen his accuracy and attack speeds take quite a substantial leap.

Near the end of that training block, he rarely missed when he sparred top-tier Dragonspire elites.

The Stellar Collapse Concept also supercharged that loading phase of his Supernova Law, which granted him a sizable pure power boost. It was a nifty Law, that.

"I think that was a test," Reina murmured, and he nodded. He’d come to the same conclusion.

It wanted to see what he was capable of. Which was why he'd shown some restraint in that fight.

They continued on their way.

He shattered the floor and then shattered the ceiling of the next floor—and saw just how close they were. He was pretty sure he knew exactly where they were in the tower.

Once they dropped into the floor, they confirmed it.

This was a Lair. A resting place for Primes.

But this was a Lair that'd clearly lost something.

The floor was riddled with giant black spikes that looked like thin diamonds, each a few miles high, crisscrossing endlessly, making a kind of vast thicket—with a few clearings scattered throughout. A silvery, soul-colored mist hung over it all.

The spikes felt like empty containers the moment Zane sensed them. They were hollowed out. He was certain they'd once been filled with essence or Corruption.

The two of them had dropped into a clearing piled high with treasures—soul-based treasures and Origin-grade obsidian items, piled up in gleaming hills.

They were quite certain this had once been Gilgoroth's Lair.

They encountered no resistance there, which was slightly surprising given the fight they'd had a floor before.

"We're making good time," she said, which tracked—all was pretty much to plan so far.

They made their way to the next floor. He sliced straight through the Superdungeon once more, and found the material here slightly thicker than the rest.

The next floor was yet another Lair. They landed in a massive hall of what seemed to be a temple-like structure. The hall was made of a kind of high-grade black marble, which only highlighted the starkly white items the hall held.

Skulls were scattered about the hall, and a large number of Sacred Bones simply lay there. Before them lay an impressive-looking throne of bone, a throne painted with blackened blood. Beyond the hall’s pillars stretched dunes of white sand and a peculiar black sun, outlined in white, hung in the distance. The sands gleamed strangely in its light.

The width of that throne was a tell by itself. It didn't take any particular deductive skills to tell this was Cromm's Lair—the only difference was that throne hummed with a peculiar essence. The same ghostly-white essence he'd seen the Starved use... the sands outside hummed with it too.

He realized it wasn't actually sand. It felt the same as the throne in the Astral Plane—those were dunes of bone dust.

Reina didn't seem to like the look of the sands, but she didn't comment.

They didn't dwell on any of it. While the floor didn't feel 'hollow' like Gilgoroth's floor, its owner wasn't here.

They decided to proceed just as they'd done so far.

When he shattered the roof of the very next floor, he revealed a world of diamond.

Miles on miles of crystalline mountains and valleys, all quite natural-looking. The crystals were strange—some of their sides were totally transparent, while others were complete mirrors.

After he opened up the realm, his senses could search inside.

Which was how he'd felt the aura. It simply wasn't doing anything to hide its presence; then again, the Mirror Dragon never had. It'd simply never felt the need to.

"It's just... waiting." Reina looked a bit perplexed. "I can't sense anything suspicious from here."

"It feels like a clean floor," he said.

She hesitated. "I'd be surprised if it truly was. It's not an accident that it's there. It just can't be. If it's been placed there, Malzareth must be confident it's not in true danger."

He nodded. He'd also be a bit surprised if there wasn't more to this. Risking a Stegar body to test him was one thing, but the Mirror Dragon was its strongest Prime.

Reina was silent for a bit. And then—"Two Primes are still distracted, several floors up," she said. "If you can get concessions here—maybe force out its Supreme, or even take it down..."

"I'd be in strong position for the final fight," he concluded.

"I've just received the Scryers' reports," she said a few moments later. "It is the only signature down there, but that still doesn't preclude any arrays."

"If they're there, I'll deal with them," he told her. At his stage, there weren't many arrays that could seriously hamper him. "I still think I should take this."

She looked him over. “I do think it’s likely underestimating you," she said.

And with his spatial Laws—the way he could move now—the way he saw it, it'd be quite difficult to take him down. If they wanted a chance, they'd need to give up a concession. At least a Supreme, if not a Prime—and if it did that, he had what he wanted.

They’d decided. She topped him up with essence and Blessings. The Mirror Dragon was still standing there, unmoving.

Ultimately he felt he was quite clear-eyed about the risks here. He still engaged.

He stepped through the void, and twenty seconds later, landed on what they knew to be one of the final floors before the Throne Room.

And there, he faced Malzareth's strongest Prime.
