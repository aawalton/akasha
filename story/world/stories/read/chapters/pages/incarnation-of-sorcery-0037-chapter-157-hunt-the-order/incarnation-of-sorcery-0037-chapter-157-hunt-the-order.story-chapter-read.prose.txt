It took a decent amount of time for Ryland to finish assisting the Rhellians. He didn’t want to leave things half-finished, so he had used [Inscribed Renewal] on the vast majority of the Rhellians who had still possessed the Soul Glyph of Unity on their manasouls. He had only stopped when the number of people left was something the current infrastructure could handle.

Considering Ryland had needed almost a week to finish it all despite his rapid pace, he was a little worried how long Mavar might have taken.

Then there was the other issue. With how rapidly they had gone ahead with the plan to free the Rhellians from the effects of Mavar’s Inscription, they hadn’t fully set in place what to do after it.

The confusion and mass panic were starting to get out of hand. Mavar was doing his best to contain it all, and he had called in as much assistance as he could. There were two major problems with that, however. One was that freeing all the Rhellians meant there were too many of his own assistants and retainers in the confused group than in the helpful group.

But the bigger issue was that of trust. Since this entire operation was about restoring belief among the foreigners that Mavar had never had any malicious intentions with his Soul Glyph of Unity, he couldn’t just use his people to keep things in check.

Which was why a lot of the foreigners had volunteered to help, which the Demiurge had graciously accepted.

It was quite nice seeing the Mage Knights from Arcoryx and Jure assisting in making sure everyone was calm. The Academs from different academies were all explaining things to the Rhellians who weren’t easily capable of understanding what was going on. They were all staying neutral for the sake of ensuring there were no real problems.

Ryland had to admit that the aftereffects of the procedure were quite intriguing. Having never applied his Inscription-dissolving spell at such a deep level, he found himself taking an academic interest in it.

Basically, the responses varied.

Forget the technical aspects of the Inscription, the majority of Rhellians didn’t fully grasp that their very souls had essentially been tampered with. They didn’t understand that magic had been performed on their manasouls. That their entire lives had, from most perspectives, been manipulated so that Rhel and its Demiurge could control everything.

There were quite a few, however, who did understand that. These were usually the people one would expect to know more about the situation.

Several Academs from the Rhellian institute, several dignitaries and high-ranking officials and mages, and so on. People who were aware of the truth at a superficial level, if nothing else. People who might have even pried into the matter themselves, to whatever extent had been allowed by Mavar.

One of the most surprising cases was Yulkaviel.

“I was hoping something like this would eventually come to transpire,” he said when Ryland met him. Or rather, when the old elvhenan had sought him out after Ryland’s participation in the procedure was over.

“I do recall you hinting as such,” Ryland said, remembering the rather veiled conversations they had at Arcoryx before their departure for Rhel. “However, I don’t think either of us could have predicted that something this… drastic would occur. I certainly didn’t.”

Yulkaviel laughed. It was a brittle sound, there for a moment before it broke into nothing. Like many of the other patients, he looked pale and wan. Drawn. As though the realization of it all was sitting heavy on his heart and mind. A blanket was wrapped around his thin frame, much like someone affected by a jittery fever.

“I couldn’t be upfront in my earlier state,” he said. “But now… I do feel a lot freer. But also, far more scared than I had expected. I… don’t know what happens next, Ryland. Where do we, as a people, go from here?”

In all honesty, Ryland could predict the different paths that would now manifest. There were many who would try to take advantage of the Rhellians’ current vulnerable condition. A lot would perhaps simply evacuate Rhel itself, leaving in disgust and fear. Some would belligerently remain, despite disapproval.

And quite a lot might just prefer to go back to exactly what it had been like before.

“But why?” Yulkaviel asked when Ryland had shared his thoughts about the final bit. “Why would anyone ever want to leave behind this freedom? This individuality?”

Ryland sighed. “There are many who prefer the security it comes with. Many who would rather leave all the important decisions, all the pressure of needing to make your own choices, up to someone else. Do you deny that there were those who could truly claim to be happy in the prior state of Rhel?”

“I cannot,” Yulkaviel said with his own sigh. “I myself know many who are as you describe. It seems unfathomable on the surface, but…”

The old elvhenan shook his head, clearly unable to believe how one could be willing to give up the ability to think critically just for something akin to safety and security and happiness.

“It’s not uncommon,” Ryland said.

Yulkaviel looked at him sharply. “There are other Realms where people are controlled like this?”

“Not in the exact same fashion. Not with their leaders Inscribing Soul Glyphs onto a person to control their thoughts and emotions. But such directness isn’t always necessary. There are many leaders who use the base urges of their constituents, who amplify and manipulate the thoughts most people would like to keep hidden, just to influence and direct them.”

“But how can one fall for such a thing? Surely, with the freedom of thought, one can notice when one is being lied to or manipulated?”

Ryland tutted in disappointment. “Do those lies really matter if you believe your greatest fears are being addressed and the path to your happiness is seemingly cleared open? If it is reaffirmed that you are never at fault for the ills of your world? If your easiest option is to believe that someone else will eradicate problems that will never affect you again?”

Yulkaviel didn’t look like he was fully convinced, but he didn’t argue the point. Plus, there were more important matters at hand anyway.

Ryland left the guidance of the populace of Rhel to the Rhellians. He had already helped enough, and he would help again if needed. But first, he really, really needed to put a stop to whatever the Flourinus Order were preparing for on Rhel.

Finding them took longer than he would have preferred. In truth, Ryland couldn’t even be certain that the Order was still on Rhel after their last interaction with the Demiurge had shown that they no longer held Mavar’s favour. If anything, they were now being hunted down.

Sadly, the hunters that Mavar had sent forth hadn’t discovered anything concrete regarding their presence on Rhel.

Which was why Ryland had needed to act. Thankfully, his efforts had a positive result.

He floated over the desert to the northeast of Rhel. An endless sea of white sand stretched out towards the horizon, the heat baking the very air and turning the atmosphere into an oven. Gracious was here. He was sure of it. The proof was quite evident to his Incarnation eyes.

For just a moment, Ryland deliberated on how best he could get his quarry out of there. Then he channelled his mana.

“[Alchemic Reconstruction],” he incanted.

The desert turned into a sea of glass.

It was quite the sight, if Ryland was being honest. He had done quite a few things to deserts in his time, even at gargantuan scales too. Usually, they were either using the sand for one purpose or another, or reforesting the desert to turn it into a more habitable place. This desert, the Whiteblight Sands, used to be far larger. It was his efforts that had reclaimed enough of the nearby land for people to repopulate.

Right now, however, Ryland wasn’t focused on creation. He had a different goal entirely—flushing out his target by turning the entire area into glass.

Of course, Gracious would have been able to survive the process. They had been living inside a glacier, which was honestly much the same consistency, if significantly colder. But it was that difference in temperature that Ryland was relying on.

And sure enough, as the entire region as far as Ryland’s regular eyes could see attained a reflective sheen of glass, Gracious was finally forced out.

“How?” Gracious the First said as they emerged from the pit of the volcano Ryland had found them at. “How did you rediscover us again?”

Gracious the Second echoed the First’s surprise. “We’ve been blanketing the entire area in defensive Inscriptions and spell circles for the entire week now! It’s preposterous we’ve only been able to stump you for a few days at best.”

“Few days?” Ryland shook his head. “I don’t know what gave you the impression it took me that long. I only started looking for you about two hours ago. Then I saw the sheer intensity of Glyphs in the local area and figured where else could you possibly be?”

Gracious’s head twisted back and forth as both the First and the Second wanted to stare at him.

“I was busy fixing the little mess you’ve caused on Rhel,” Ryland said. “But fear not. My part in it is done. You now have my full attention.”

They clearly didn’t appreciate that. Their form started warbling and shifting, turning into the consistency of watercolour on a canvas. In fact, as their manasoul unfurled into its proper form and their magic came into being, everything around them started turning into a painting for Gracious to control as they willed.

“I suppose this was inevitable since you were on Rhel,” the First said.

“But that’s alright,” said the Second. “We’ve been ready for this eventuality.”

“Yes. We’re not Pious, as you’ll see.”

Ryland released the chains on his manasoul. The faster he took care of the issue, the faster he could return in case something else occurred. Rhel was in a precarious state at the moment. His magic flared out too, turning the world to Conceptual Glyphs.

“If you answer me quickly,” Ryland said. “I’ll be happy to let you go.”

“Really?” Gracious the First asked. “You’ll let us go? Just like that? And you expect us to believe that drivel?”

“Well, I don’t expect you to believe anything at all, seeing who you are in truth. And I admit, I’m only sparing you this time. Here and now. When next we meet, eradication awaits.”

“Ha. I knew it. [Titanic Overture]!”

Magic exploded out of Gracious, turning them into an even more intense smudge of paint. Except, the world itself was becoming a canvas now, and from its pages, a monstrous titan of white and gold sizzled into being. It was the same creation that had held up Mavar’s apocalyptic spell that day. Now, it was aiming straight for Ryland with its burning fist.

Ryland, however, had become an Incarnation. The world to him was the truth. Everything was constructed from Conceptual Glyphs, shining and brilliant, radiating out more and more power.

And in this form, he had no trouble at all simply locating the Glyphs of Connection keeping the enormous being intact and shutting them off.

Of course, it didn’t happen instantaneously. For a creature taller than a mountain, there were an uncountable number of Glyphs holding everything together. Which was why, because it took Ryland a little longer than was ideal, he received a direct smack with a fist of energy bigger than the keep in the centre of Rhellian Institute.

“[Eternal Bastion],” he incanted with more than enough time to spare, leaving himself unharmed as the energy caused a thunderous explosion around him.

“No!” Gracious cried. “Salty! Your fall will be avenged! [Earthen Obliteration]!”

The thing about painted magic of the kind Gracious was using was that it could appear from everywhere and nowhere. Their main spell circle was imbued into the shifted reality they had enforced, and one of its characteristics was hiding away any further spell circles they went on to cast.

Which meant Ryland could predict neither the exact effect nor the exact location of the spell. All he knew was that he was the main target.

Chunks of earth big enough to pass for small mountains came flying at him from multiple directions. Left, right, down, up, it didn’t matter. They broke through Gracious’s canvas, shooting at Ryland like meteors.

“[Translocate],” Ryland incanted.

He himself wasn’t disappearing. Dodging had never been Ryland’s style. Not after he had discovered the neat ability to simply rift away an opponent’s attack and throw it back on them.

Which was exactly what happened with Gracious. Every single mountainous piece of earth that appeared out of nowhere got eaten up by the dozens of Ryland’s rifts appearing out of thin air, which caused all the rocks to slam straight at the original caster all at once. Gracious screamed as they were pelted by literal mountains one after the other.

“[Reinforcement]!” Gracious the First shouted, voice echoing through the world.

They were desperately trying to make their magic stick together, to draw the lines of energy into one cohesive whole again. Reconstructing that colossal giant of writhing energy would keep them safe against their own barrage of rocks. Even Ryland was sure of it.

Which was why he popped up right next to the site of destruction with a [Translocate] and cast another spell.

“[Eye of the Archfiend]!” Ryland incanted, his manasoul churning into even greater potency as he called forth a spell he hadn’t used in ages.

Twin rifts cracked open on either side of him, each shaped like an eye. And eyes were exactly what bulged out from within their depths. Mad eyes, each bigger than Ryland was tall, a frightening yellow veined with the black of insanity.

In the next instant, power burst out of those eyes like Ryland had rifted open windows to a supernova. Come to think of it, he did have a spell that could do exactly that.

But for now, the eyes of a Calamity forced out a wave of total obliteration. Waves upon waves of scarlet force emerged from the rifts with the mad eyes. They ripped apart the very air, pulled apart the entire titanic beast of energy into thin, ineffectual strands.

“No!” Gracious the First screamed. “How dare you turn a Calamity upon us?”

“[Meteoric Ascent]!” cried out Gracious the Second.

A blink of an eye later, they were bursting out of the flying debris and the blanket of crimson destruction, shooting skyward as even more magic convulsed out of their manasoul.

“[Rebirthing Star Shower],” Gracious the First cast.

“[Honing Canvas]!” cast Gracious the Second.

The first spell turned the sky itself into nighttime, pulling down the stars into a violent deluge of power. Gracious’s second cast caused them all to target Ryland very ferociously, honing in on him just as their caster wished.

Ryland could have tanked them all with [Eternal Bastion], but the problem was the “rebirthing” part of the spell. Even if they failed to breach his barrier, Gracious had ensured that they wouldn’t simply explode and dissipate. The explosions would continue gathering around Ryland until he was blinded and then the Order would take full advantage.

The best bet would be to simply destroy Gracious long before their spells could reach him. So, Ryland did just that.

“[Endless Evolution],” he incanted as his Soul Glyph of Genesis once more came to life. “[Wings of Salvation].”

Power surged out of his manasoul like a storm made of pure lightning. From his back, enormous curved bones grew out like the curls of Glyph Prints, energy firing from cracks all across the glowing white appendages. Ryland’s wings then shot him so fast across the sky, the air quite literally cracked. Gracious’s stars were left far behind, Rhel warping at the world-bending speed Ryland was capable of.

“[Wound Corridor]!” Gracious screamed desperately.

An ugly tear in reality consumed him, spitting him out in the distance. But Ryland was already there, popping out of a rift with the same momentum he had entered it with, nearly catching his target.

Sadly, they had disappeared again.

What followed was a rapid chase as Gracious tried to use a combination of painted spells trying to distract Ryland while he himself used bursts of speed to escape as best as he could. Thankfully, Ryland was faster, and with [Omniscience], he could cut through even the fake Order mages and go right after Gracious.

“[Closed Canvas],” Gracious growled.

If the world had been merely a painting before, reality itself now turned into a complete sludge of paint where definitions blurred into each other, enough to leave most affected by the spell insane.

But Ryland was wise to such things already.

“[World Node],” he cast. He was moving even as he raised both hands forward. “[Awakened Avatar].”

The whole world turned into a spray of points, including this broken canvas that Gracious had turned everything into. But Ryland had no intention of letting them call the shots, so he had turned the mana surging out of him into a being reminiscent of Gracious’s titanic construct, just a thousand times more intense.

This being grabbed a hold on reality through the nodes Ryland had instated, then ripped the whole world apart.

“No!” Gracious screamed as the painted, smudged reality came crashing down around them.

Both the First and the Second were so overwhelmed by it, neither of them were aware that they were finally slowing down, too distracted by the ease with which their high-Tier magic had just been smothered to nothing.

That was exactly when Ryland appeared before them, thrusting through the sky like a vengeful meteor, [Everlife Spectre] once more gifting him the spectral, bony arm he needed.

Gracious failed to react in time as Ryland’s arm stabbed right through their chest, more magical paint exploding everywhere.

“And now,” Ryland said. “We pull out the real nuisance.”

With a severe tug, Ryland pulled a roaring Chivalrous out from his initial target.
