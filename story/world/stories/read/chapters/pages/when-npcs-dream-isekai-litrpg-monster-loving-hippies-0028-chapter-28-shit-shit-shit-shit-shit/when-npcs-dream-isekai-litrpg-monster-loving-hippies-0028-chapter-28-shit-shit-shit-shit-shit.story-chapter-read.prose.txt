Shit shit shit shit shit.

How the fuck did it come to this?

Had Juliette made the right choice? Was there any other way? Maybe we could’ve pulled Kim Jong Un from the bag and made him sting Miguel? No… no, his venom was tailored for armadillo-care-bear biology. It wouldn’t affect humans the way we needed. And anyway, it didn’t matter now.

Now… now I need to calm the fuck down.I had to support Juliette. She was probably on the edge of panic too — she had just murdered someone. In cold blood. And she’d done it for us. For me. I needed to be strong for her. At least pretend to be strong. I could probably do that.

“Hey,” I said gently, putting a hand on her back. “You didn’t have a choice. If Miguel had contacted Arjun's group, they’d lock us up in the best case scenario. You did what had to be done.”

She didn’t answer. She just stared blankly, looking utterly miserable.

“We need to go now,” I said. “Wash our faces, go downstairs, smile, and pretend nothing happened.”

“Spunkrag-chunderarse… Broomfart-knobflapper…” Duchess whispered. “Was that supposed to happen?”

“No,” I whispered back, then turned to Samar. “We need to leave. Get far away before they realize we killed their wizard.”

Samar, pale and silent, nodded slowly.

I turned back to Juliette, who was still little out of it.“Juliette. You understand?”

“Y...yes,” she murmured. “Wash face. Go downstairs. Smile. Leave this fucking place.”

“Good.” I helped her up. “Oh shit. I need to grab something.”

“What? Now?” she asked, confused.

I walk to Miguel’s bookcases—the shelves he’d said were about gas. My hands shook as I pulled volumes, flipping through pages, tables of contents—fuck, not enough time. Maybe this one was about diatomic molecules… maybe that one too.

I shoved five books into my bag until it bulged, heavy as hell. But I wouldn’t get this chance again, and I had an idea I really wanted to test.

After that, I looked around the room, searching for any evidence of murder we might have left behind. Most of the blood had vanished with the body, but here and there I spotted a drop, which I immediately rubbed away with my sleeve.Maybe they’ll assume we only kidnapped him or something—enough to buy us some valuable time.

“Okay, we can go,” I said. “Juliette, will you…”

“Yes,” she said more firmly. “I can do it.”

We headed downstairs.

Farhad was still there, reading some history book. I think I knew how to play this.

“Oh hey!” he said, looking up. “You’re back already?”

“Yeah, we saw the craziest thing,” I said, acting amazed.

“The lightning?” Farhad guessed.

“Miguel said it’s not super useful in combat, but… it looked awesome.”

Farhad chuckled. “Miguel is modest. Only a month into this art, and already with results. Think of the amazing things he could achieve in a year!”

With high probability, he’ll still be dead, I thought grimly.

“Yes! Very talented,” I said, forcing a grin. “We learned a lot.” Then I added casually, “But then he suddenly got an idea and buried himself in his books again.”

“He does that,” said Farhad. “Sometimes disappears for days.”

Oh, did he disappear. Good. Very good.

“Miguel mentioned you need us to run errands?” I asked. “From the magic shop?”That was basically my grand escape plan.

“Yes, it would help us greatly,” Farhad said. “We have a limited time window before the system considers you part of our group — and thus, criminals. We desperately need some magic-enhanced gear for the Kamatouu assault. The right items could mean the difference between life and death. I don’t want to lose more people. This won’t be like our attack on the keep — this time, we’ll be ready.”

“Of course,” I said. “I can go now, if you want.”

“I would like that, yes,” Farhad said, and disappeared into a side room. When he came back, he handed me a small sack and a list. “Sixty gold coins, and a list of what we need. Go to the Arcane House of Zerathis & Malovar — second-largest building in the city, you can’t miss it. When you return, Miguel can demonstrate the items for you — you’ll love seeing them in action.”

“Sounds awesome,” I said.

Then I added, casually: “Hey Samar, wanna see a real magic shop?”

“Sure,” she choked, trying to sound excited.

“I’ll tag along too,” said Juliette.

“Cool. Come on, Big Bird — not leaving you behind.”

“Wouldn’t the guards attack a monster?” asked Farhad. “The Arcane House is full of them.”

“The system recognizes her as tame,” I said confidently. “We already checked. It’s fine.”

“I see,” said Farhad, placing a hand on my shoulder. “Thank you, friend.”

No need to thank me, really.

“Sure!”

And just like that, we were out of the keep.

A massive stone lifted off my chest. These guys were way too trusting. They should’ve insisted at least one of us stay behind while the others shopped. But they hadn’t faced any hostile humans yet. They assumed all players were united against the system or monsters. They’d be way less trusting after today.

“Fuck fuck fuck,” Juliette muttered as we walked. “I thought I was gonna break down and cry any second.”

“Good thing you didn’t,” I said.

I heard a soft sniff. Samar was crying quietly now.

“Sorry,” Juliette said. “It’s this world… or maybe just me. I’m sorry.”

“N-no,” said Samar, “I understand why you did it. I’m not blaming you.”

“You’re weird. WEIRD!” Duchess chimed in. “You cry from your eyes when sad?”

“Yes,” I said.

“So weird!” she squeaked. “You meet Naked-Nula you don’t like, pretend to be friends, kill them, and then cry!”

“Look,” I said, annoyed. “We didn’t want to kill Miguel. He was about to contact Arjun’s group and— never mind. It was hard for us too.”

“Whatever,” Duchess snorted. “But now they’ll come after you. They’ll try to kill you. Worse, they’ll try to kill me.”

“I know,” I said. “We’ll… think of something.”

“We should head toward the magic shop area,” Juliette said. “In case they’re watching us. Then bolt for the city wall and make a run for Kamatouu.”

“We need that shop Juliette,” I said. “If we want any chance of surviving a future fight with them, we’ll need magic items — and we’ve got the money.”

Juliette looked thoughtful. “Okay. You’re right. But we need to be fast. If they realize Miguel is gone… or try to contact him… they’ll come for us.”

“Can we get an hour?” I asked. “I could probably spend a week in there, but maybe we can do it in an hour.”

“Fine,” she said. “Let’s hurry.”

We walked fast and soon stood in front of an incredible building. It didn’t look like a shop. It looked like a small cathedral — with towers, carved stonework, and a massive sign:

The Arcane House of Zerathis & Malovar

Two intimidating guards flanked the entrance. These guys were serious.

“Wait,” said Samar. “It’s Kim — he’s been in the bag too long. He’s pissed.”

“Screee!” Kim screeched angrily.

“There’s a small garden near the store,” said Samar. “I’ll let Kim breathe a little, and I can also get a view of the keep from there, see if they come out to look for us.”

“Good thinking,” I said. “Wait for us there.”

We entered.

It was… breathtaking. The interior glowed with a soft, golden light that came from nowhere in particular. Shelves climbed to the ceiling, stacked with shimmering crystal bottles, leather-bound tomes with gilded spines, and all kinds of strange trinkets.

A refined NPC approached — tall, with elegant robes and a low, courteous voice. “Welcome to the Arcane House of Zerathis & Malovar. How may I assist you?”

I turned to Juliette. “Let’s start with the items on the list. They’ve probably done their research — that’ll save us time.”

We showed him the list:

Sixty gold total. But we didn’t need all of that.

The rings and the armor were probably what we were looking for—items to increase general strength.The Stranger’s Guise amulet and the Amulet of Gentle Tides were likely meant to help bypass their criminal status, though I doubt that would actually work.The Plate of Far Voices was just receiver for the Echo Song Crystal — which was probably the name of their phone-like device.

And the Hungerless Elixir… that was just stupid. For that price, they could have asked us to buy them food—much, much more of it.

Aside from The rings and the armor , I really needed was something to help me with magic circles. With magic device and enough practice, maybe I’d finally have a chance at creating a half-decent, stable magic circles.

After some back-and-forth with the assistants, we finally found something perfect for me—the Charm of the Binding Sigil. The catch: it cost a small fortune. Twenty fucking gold.

I was hesitant about this, but Juliette looked at me and said she trusted I’d make good use of it. She told me to go for it. I loved her even more in that moment.

I also really wanted the phone device, and some durable, lighter magic spears… but we didn’t have the time or the money for those.

We ended up buying:

Total: 58 gold. We kept two coins.

I also spent a few minutes browsing through the trinkets on the nearby shelves. I couldn’t resist—I was too curious. But they were just… like, super random.

One day, I swore, if we survived and got filthy rich, I’d come back here, go through every damn trinket, and build the ultimate cheating exploit combo. But for now—our hour was up.

By the time we left, evening had fallen. Shadows stretched long, and only a little light remained. Not terrible timing—if they tried to chase us, the darkness would slow them too.

We picked up Samar and Kim. The armor took up a lot of space, but was surprisingly light. Each of us strapped one to our backpacks — even Big Bird, despite endless complaints and squawking.

We kept to main streets with guards, but stayed out of sightlines to the keep. Eventually, we reached the city gate, waved farewell to the guards, and walked out.

The trees were just ahead, Dubadilu hopefully waiting there, when a sharp voice cut the air:

“Wait!”

We froze, turned. Two players from Farhad’s group—a boy and a girl—stood behind us.

Shit. We were screwed. Level twos, probably. They could snap us in half. Best bet: drop the gear and sprint into the woods, pray Dubadilu could help in the fight.Stupid. We should’ve worn the armor already. Could we put it on now?

“Hey!” Juliette hissed at me. “Calm down. If they wanted to attack, they wouldn’t shout ‘wait.’ And look—they’re not moving.”

She had a point. I tried to breathe.

“What do you want?” Juliette shouted to them.

They hesitated. Then the girl spoke. “We want to leave Farhad’s group. We want to go with you.”

That took us all by surprise.

“Juliette,” I said quietly, “it could be a trap.”

“I know,” she replied. “But… it might not be.”

“There’s another problem,” I added. “They’re criminals. If they took part in the assault, the system might keep their status. It could spread to us.”

“I know that too,” Juliette said, tense. “Let me think.”

Then she called back to the couple. “Why? Why do you want to leave?”

The girl stepped forward, visibly nervous. “Farhad is a good man,” she began, “but he’s wrong to go against the system. I think he’s going to doom us all.”

She paused.

“And... some of the men in our group—they’re acting like thugs. Treating the NPCs like garbage. We don’t want to be around that.”

That was actually a very good answer.

“And the keep assault?” Juliette pressed. “Did you take part? Did you kill guards?”

“I’m the new guy,” the boy said. “Joined after the attack. And Charlotte didn’t fight. We think… maybe if we leave, the system might forgive us.”

Juliette paused, then shouted, “Okay! Give us a moment.”

She turned to us. “What do you think?”

“They are Broomfart,” said Duchess. “I don’t trust them.”

“You don’t get a vote,” Juliette said flatly.

“I believe them,” said Samar.

“Geshem?” she asked.

I wasn’t sure at all. “I’ll trust your judgment.”

“Okay,” Juliette said, turning back to the couple. “If you join us, when we reach Kamatouu we won’t hide you. I don’t know if your criminal status will stick, but you’ll have to face the guards. Maybe even end up in jail.”

“We accept,” the girl said.

“There’s something else,” Juliette said, suddenly quiet.

“I… I did something. Before we left the keep.”

“Juliette,” I whispered, “why—”

“I won’t keep it secret if they join,” she said firmly.

She raised her voice. “I killed Miguel.”

The couple froze, exchanged a look.

“It was cold blood,” Juliette continued. “There was a reason—but not a great one. What matters: we killed Farhad’s second-in-command, betrayed him, robbed him. He will come after us. After you too if you join us.”

Long silence.

Then Juliette added, “It’s going to be really dark really soon. You have about two minutes to consider what I just said, and decide what to do.”

That shook the couple’s earlier conviction. They whispered to each other, frantic, and I don’t think they agreed.

Then finally the girl said:

“Miguel was an asshole. What he did… never mind. It wasn’t right to kill him. But… that’s not our business. We still want to come.”

They approached.

“Wait!” I said. “Show us you’re unarmed.”

They raised hands. No spears, no swords. Could be knives.

“Come slow. Hands out.” I leveled my spear. I wasn’t making another stupid mistake today.

They obeyed. We searched them—found knives, confiscated them.

They stared at the Armadillo Care Bear and the Scorpion Suricata with wide eyes.

“Oh, this is just Dubadilu,” I said. “And that’s Kim Jong Un. They’re… tame. Sort of.”

“If you say so…” said the boy. “I’m Santiago. This is Charlotte.”

“And I’m Lady Scheelha Pam Fizzlesteck, Duchess of Dingleberry Downs, the Third!” Duchess shrieked.

“OHMYGOD IT TALKED,” gasped Charlotte.

“Don’t remind me,” I sighed. “I liked her better when she didn’t.”

“Shut your Spunkrag, lowly Naked-Nula!” she retorted.

Santiago and Charlotte looked at each other. Probably already regretting joining us.

We walked in silence for a while, found a safe spot to camp, and collapsed. It had been a long, awful day.

We’d escaped Farhad’s group — for now. But this was not the end.

Not even close.

Because we had just pissed off a large group of superpowered humans with magic, resources, and grudges.

How much did we piss them off?
