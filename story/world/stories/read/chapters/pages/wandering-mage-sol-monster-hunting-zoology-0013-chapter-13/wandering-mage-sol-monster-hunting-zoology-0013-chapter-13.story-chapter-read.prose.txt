Tressa led them away from the Farron Moth breeding grounds, heading closer to Falidorn as she knew what to expect there better than she did this far into the forest; who knows what kind of monsters they could run into. She’d already seen so much she never had before just by traveling a little further from Falidorn’s walls. She could only imagine how much existed in the far reaches of the world.

Once they were safely away from the moth nesting grounds, she used Lumas to send little lights dancing around them and lighting their way, letting them shine comfortably.

“They are almost like fireflies,” Lisha said. She reached up, trying to poke the light nearest her, but her finger went through it, though it seemed to bounce away almost like it was alive.

“How's your leg?” Lisha asked, pointing down.

“Leg uh… it’s okay,” Tressa said, then she let herself levitate to show her she could if needed. “If not okay.” Then she banished the spell and continued walking normally.

Eventually they reached a bridge that was familiar to Tressa and in much better shape than the last they’d tried to cross.

“Let's just set camp here,” Briadon said. “I'm not crossing that in the dark, no matter how good her magic is. And after that whole incident with the bridge earlier, I'd rather stay away from water period.”

Lisha sat down and agreed. “I second that.”

Tressa looked across the bridge, then back to them, as it wasn’t her intention to have them cross anyway. “No cross,” she said, and pointed. “Falidorn. High Elven Capitol.” Then she pointed the opposite way. “Um… Gone?” She didn’t know how to tell them that’s the way they’d have to go if they wanted to leave the Elven lands.

Lisha nodded. “Then will this be where we part ways?”

“Um…” Tressa looked across the bridge and back to them. It was still quite a walk to Falidorn, something she’d rather do in the morning. Especially with how tired she was. If she was going to face the music, she’d rather do it after getting some sleep…

She looked at the two monster hunters. It had been only a day since she’d known them, but already she had experienced such an amazing adventure in their company. Did she really have to go home at all?

Yet, she didn’t know if Lisha and Briadon would want her to join their travels in the first place. It was probably a stupid idea.

However, as she opened her mouth to respond, her stomach spoke up first, letting out a long, loud rumble.

“Gah!?” Tressa blushed and looked down at her tummy, putting her hands over it as if to quiet a barking pet.

They both laughed, Lisha trying a little harder to hold herself back, as Tressa nearly panicked at the embarrassment.

“Hahaha, that's right, you haven't really eaten, huh?” Lisha asked. “Want to stay for food?” She made a gesture as if shoveling food into her mouth. “Eat?”

Tressa smiled. “Yes!”

Tressa found a rock to sit on as she watched in fascination as they put together a campsite. She pulled out her sketchbook, summoned her mana quill, and took her last chance to get drawings of the minotaur and statiki as she watched them work, her Lumas spell keeping the campground lit.

When she was done with that Lisha was just starting to work on lighting the fire under the pile of sticks she’d gathered into a fire pit.

“Lisha… um… this,” Tressa said, holding up her staff.

“What is it, Tressa?” Lisha asked.

“Um. Away,” Tressa said, then closed one eye and bit her tongue as she pointed the staff. Basic elements were not difficult even without practice, but conquering fire was still one she was least experienced in.

Still, as her mana flowed and she focused it into a spell, she succeeded at shooting a small fireball into the kindling under the thicker sticks.

“Ha! That’s handy,” Briadon bellowed. “Best part of traveling with a mage right there!”

“Yet last time we were partied with one, you never stopped complaining,” Lisha said.

“Only because he never stopped complaining! At least this elf here seems like she knows how to rough it!”

Lisha’s tongue flicked, and her scaly lips curled in a smile. “Yes, that is true.”

Tressa turned to a fresh page and took the Scrollow Bird out of the bag Briadon had put it in and started sketching some of its details. Particularly, its feathered yet bat-like wings interested her, and the unusual quality of its neck feathers.

Soon, there was a roaring fire, with a small pot being held over it with sticks Lisha had found on the ground and created a makeshift frame with. They were clearly very experienced with surviving in the wild, they did everything as if it were a mundane chore and second nature to them. They filled the pot with water from the river, then Briadon peeled and cut potatoes and other veggies he had in his satchel into the pot.

Then, when Lisha unwrapped something from a cloth and threw it in, she suddenly slapped her own face. “Oh no! Wait, can elves eat meat?” she asked. She looked at Briadon.

“I don’t think so,” Briadon answered.

“I’m a fool. I didn’t even think to ask…” Lisha muttered to herself. Then she looked over to Tressa, who looked up from the bird corpse and paused her sketching to stare back curiously, not sure what the problem was.

“Tressa, is meat okay?” Lisha asked.

Tressa stared at her blankly. “Meat?”

Lisha pointed at the pot and mimed chewing again. “Meat. Animal. Is it okay?”

“Food?” Tressa asked.

“Yes. Food,” Lisha said slowly. “But meat. Look.” Lisha pulled another bundle and showed the jerky that was wrapped inside. “Can elves eat m-e-a-t,” she asked, saying the word slowly as she showed it.

Tressa blinked. What a strange question. “Meat? Yes. Eat uh. Meat, herb, vegetable, fruit.” She listed off these simple words in the Common tongue.

Lisha sighed with relief. “Oh, good. I worried I’d ruined it.”

“Huh, thought elves only ate plants,” Briadon said, now chopping onions into the pot.

“Maybe that’s just wood elves?” Lisha guessed. “I guess High Elves can eat anything?”

Tressa tapped her chin. “Yes!” she eventually said, though, she wasn’t quite sure what she was responding to.

After Lisha tended to the stew for a while, it was finally ready and Lisha poured some into two bowls and handed one to Tressa.

“I get rock?” Tressa asked.

“What?” Lisha chuckled.

“Rock?” Tressa pointed at the bowl.

“It's a bowl,” Lisha said, wondering if that was what Tressa was saying. “Um, not a rock.”

“Rolb?” Tressa tilted her head.

“No, b-o-w-l.” Lisha said, sounding out the word. “Say it with me.”

“B-o-a-w-l,” Tressa said, mouthing each sound of the word the best she could. “Boawl.”

“Close enough. But yes,” Lisha said as she put the bowl in Tressa’s hands.

“You eat?” Tressa asked Lisha.

“I will after you. Only have the two bowls.”

Tressa looked down, feeling a bit guilty that Lisha would have to wait for her to finish first. But her stomach let out another growl, and she dug in. Once she tasted it, she’d barely blowing on the hot stew before shoving more into her mouth.

As she ate, she let out a grateful sigh as the warm food landed in her belly. It wasn't anything special. The jerky had softened thanks to the boiling water, and it had a thick broth from the flour Lisha had added. Even with the vegetables, it was cruder and not as flavorful as the stew Narella would make, but it was still so good in its own way.

“Mmmmm,” she hummed in delight, leaving Lisha and Briadon to laugh.

“I know. Lisha can turn anything into edible food,” he boomed, pounding his stomach in joy.

“Well, thanks. And you burn everything,” Lisha shot back. “That, or turn it into poison.”

“At least I try,” he said, tossing back the last of his broth in a single gulp and filling another bowl. He’d clearly end up eating the most of the three of them, which was fair considering his size.

“Oh, right, before I totally forget, this is yours.” Lisha dug into her pouch, produced a gold coin, and passed it to Tressa.

“Me?” she asked, holding the coin.

“Hahaha, yes. You helped us, after all. If it wasn't for you, we never would have gotten that last bounty.”

“No. I draw.” She tried to hand the coin back. After all, the deal she had made was that they would let her draw and research them, which she was doing.

“No. No, I insist.” Lisha waved her hand. “Even if you turn it down, I wouldn’t feel right.”

Briadon huffed in agreement. “You earned it, elf. Just take it.”

Tressa nodded and slipped the coin into a small pocket on her satchel. She then finished the last of her bowl and handed it back to Lisha, who gratefully took it, filled it again, and began to eat.

Lisha paused as she finished half the bowl. She looked up, and Briadon's ears twitched.

Tressa caught their movement and realized they had heard something she hadn't. Focusing, her ears twitching slightly as well. She did hear it. Something rustling in the bushes.

“Don't look around,” Lisha said quietly, locking eyes with Tressa for a serious moment before going back to eating. Tressa blinked and watched how Lisha’s foot nudged the bottom of her halberd, causing it to slide across the log it leaned against, ever so closer to her hand.

Briadon as well let out a casual yawn and a long stretch that brought his arm closer to his club that he had laid nearby. “What a day,” he muttered under his breath.

Tressa flinched and resisted the urge to start looking around. She didn’t know their words but it was clear from how they were behaving, they were worried about something. Something attacking them?

She caught a slight movement out of the corner of her eye. Something metallic caught the light of their fire. She tried to act natural and trust in Lisha and Briadon as they seemed to be far more alert than her.

“So, Tressa, what's your plan now that you helped us?” Lisha asked.

Tressa bit her lip, trying once again to understand the common tongue.

“Home? Uh… Travel. You?”

Just what do they think is out there? Are we in danger? I guess I should play along, but…

She listened closely, and she could hear it too, slight rustling and an occasional snap of a branch.

“Either go home or travel?”

Tressa nodded. “Home. Uh, mother and father.”

“I see, so you are looking for permission?” Lisha asked.

Tressa shrugged as she didn't understand, and Lisha let it go.

With a yawn, Lisha set her bowl down a few seconds after Briadon, putting herself in a better position to quickly grab her weapon and get to her feet.

“Once again, Lisha, you make the best food,” Briadon bellowed.

“Thanks. Did you enjoy as well, Tressa?”

Tressa nodded with an anxious smile. “Uh y-yes! Good. Yum.”

The two laughed, acting normal, but then there were snapping branches all around them, and the two suddenly burst into motion, grabbing their weapons and jumping to their feet.

“Stay behind me,” Lisha hissed, swinging her halberd around with both hands, the blade catching the firelight.

Three figures descended on them, one grabbing for Tressa, but Briadon’s foot came up, kicking one attacker away. But the other two got their hands on her, and Briadon would have clobbered them with his club if he didn’t seem to worry about hitting Tressa.

“W-Wait!” Tressa tried to stop them, but everything was happening so fast. The next thing Tressa knew, she heard a shout as Lisha spun in behind her, the hands that grabbed her coming loose as Lisha knocked them away, possibly injuring them.

Tressa let out a cry of pain as she tried to catch herself on her hurt ankle, and she hit the ground. But then, as she looked up, Lisha and Briadon standing on either side of her, she saw dozens more figures coming to the edge of the firelight, all with bows drawn.

Then, a familiar voice spoke in the common tongue.

“I suggest putting your weapons down unless you want to become full of arrows.”

Tressa looked to see him standing in front of all of them, arrow nocked and ready to fly at any moment.

“A-Aldmern!?” Tressa gasped.
