The morale was low, and somehow, the temperature was even lower. Goldman led the way with Vela beside him, holding his candelabrum with the magical flames resisting the cold.

They all carried their feet through the snow while climbing the weird and irregular ground. Harry, being the only one, was happy to finally be able to walk again and not feel useless. As most things in the loop — except the Metas — the young thief's bruises and severe injuries were completely healed. With his tie now intact and his glasses swallowing the wind in front of him like a shield to his eyes, Harry was annoyingly happy. Much for Madeline's delight.

Brun, since the past loop, had been using her Emblem to get a better understanding of the mountain and the safe spots. Although it wasn't raining rocks at the moment, it was nice to know of a shelter nearby in case things went south. The old woman, however, kept an expression that Harry knew a lot; concealed panic. Is the path not safe enough? Harry thought to himself as he walked alongside Madeline, the last two of the group.

Behind the gentle pirate was Brun and Petrikov, who had an odd expression, almost as if he was a tourist enjoying the deadly trip. Daniel and Manes followed right behind, bickering about the weather.

"Argh!" Madeline broke Harry's focus. "This place is so... strange! The ground is bumpy."

"Well, what did you expect from a mountain?" Harry replied without looking at her. "A cozy and symmetrical space?"

"No, I mean..." And she tripped again. "Look, I've climbed mountains before, unlike you. I know what I'm talking about. This isn't normal. The snow is sticky and the terrain is... squishy? I don't know how to describe it, but it's weird and I don't like it."

As Madeline kept complaining, the Locksmith realized she had a point. The ground was squishy, almost like it wasn't rock they were stepping on. The snow also had a strange texture and appearance. Yes, it was white as it should be, but, if inspected closely, one could see it had the shape of an... hourglass? Why would it have the shape of an hourglass?

The two thieves, against their will, were put as the last in line. One because Madeline could create a wall in case any of them fell, securing their safety. And two, because Harry could create his doors and passages, assuring no member of the group were ever in danger. At least they shouldn't be.

Even though he was cured, however, it took a lot of him to use his Emblem in order to climb the mountain. It would be faster, however, it would also drain him and leave him in the same spot he was before the loop restarted. Goldman was also reluctant in using his Emblem to facilitate the trip. The mountain's terrain would make it hard to guarantee a linear path. That, combining with the fact that he would have to tend to other seven individuals, made the group drop the idea.

With a meek presence, Petrikov walked alongside Brun as he cherished the snow in his feet and stared at the blue sky. His existence in the Tower still perplexed Harry. Either way, the professor's bucolic way of life changed with the arrival of said Tower. The Locksmith longed to know what the man was like before. He kept talking about the kids in his class, and how they were so tenacious, but Harry knew better. What group of kids could survive... this? The Locksmith added inwardly.

It was comforting, though, to see the man's happy demeanor since meeting the group. He found a little piece of the Tower he could call home, and a group of creatures he could call children. Was it all it took for a man to be happy? Harry still remembered his talk with Prism about the nature of trust. Was he being too naive to believe that his companions trusted him or was this his imagination?

Crash! The sound of the hotel collapsing was heard as they all looked back and forward once again, each determining the time it would take to reach the top.

"Shit, this is taking too long..." Madeline complained once again, holding her brush tightly. "How long did he said until the rain?"

"I don't know..." Harry replied. "I guess thirty minutes or something? Brun knows about this, and she hasn't yet taken us to a shelter. I'm sure we're fine. We just need consistency."

"I hope she knows what she's doing." Madeline said with a worried expression.

"...Why don't you stay with her and ask about it?" Harry suggested. "I can walk alongside Petrikov, it's no problem."

"What about the formation?"

"Well, Goldman isn't looking back right now..." The young thief squinted his eyes. "So I guess it doesn't count, does it?"

"...Thank you." She gave a warm smile which made Harry's heart beat faster for a moment. The change was then made as Petrikov was now talking to himself alongside Harry. Something in the professor's demeanor gave Harry a bit of hope, though, so he didn't stop the man.

"What to say about the world if the so-called 'threats' they claim are young people trying to change it?" he said, seeing a confused expression on Harry's face. He then decided to clarify. "Well, I see people like you, Madeline, and even Manes, and I wonder; from what I've been told, this was supposed to be a prison, right? A place to purge those too impure for the world, the dregs, the incomprehensible, the villains. Yet, you don't seem like villains to me; you're just individuals. Nothing more, nothing less. I imagine villains could be individuals too... But you understand what I mean. Ordinary people, good people."

The Locksmith then chuckled as he avoided Petrikov's gaze. "I don't know if 'good people' would be the best description for someone like me."

"Why?" He asked with a smirk. "Are you a thief by any chance?"

"...Does borrowing the constitution count as theft?" Harry asked, embarrassed.

"Oh, shit...!" Petrikov, to Harry's surprise, laughed. "Did you really do that? Well, where I come from, thieves only stole from the nobility, which didn't matter much to me, anyway. Yeah, call me morally irresponsible, but they were pricks, weren't they? Stealing resources from an entire country to maintain their relevance amongst themselves. You don't judge a bear for eating a deer without first understanding the forest. Right?"

"I... I think I agree." Harry replied. "I didn't steal because I wanted to, you know? I'm not a kleptomaniac, no matter how hard they tried to turn me into one. I just wanted attention... But what about you? How were you holding up here for so long? Don't tell me you've been drinking that water during all this time."

"Well..." He glanced to the side, and immediately Harry felt the atmosphere change. "I'm not even sure how long I had before you guys showed up. Don't get me wrong, I like the mountain and the Metas, but I've been through so much to get here. A place where I wouldn't be taunted. You're the first people I've seen since stepping here. It's just... It gets lonely out here, you know? I still miss the laughter and teaching. I know that someday I'll return and all will be fine. Now that you guys are here, I know it'll be a lot easier."

"Don't know if that could tranquilize you..." The young thief began. "But if we get out, the government will have to pay a whole lot of chimes to anyone who has been thrown in here because of their mistake. You miss your town? Drumond, right?"

"Would you believe me if I said I don't know? I remember the kids, obviously, but the town itself has kinda... faded? It's like if a fog covered my memories. I've been here for so long that I think my mind threw it all away. It isn't useful, right? Even the faces of friends and families are muddy. Maybe I only love the idea that, out there, I had a life. That I was something. Even though, we keep on living. One loop at a time."

"Is that why you always start the loop inside the hotel?" The Locksmith asked, something that had been stuck in his throat ever since he saw the hotel collapsing. "Do you hope that someday you'll have the courage to let yourself sleep and finally rest?"

"I..." He paused for a moment. Petrikov sighed deeply. "Every day is a gamble, you know? After understanding the Metas and doing everything I wanted, the beginning of each loop always gives me extra motivation. It's an adrenaline rush knowing if I'll wake up on time or if one day my mental confusion will overwhelm me to the point of forgetting about the hotel. When you put it that way, it's almost as if my subconscious is looking for a hit of a deadly desire..."

Petrikov, after a few seconds of silence, his face an enigma, bent down and picked up a clump of snow. In his hands, he formed a snowball and tossed it aside. It then struck a rock as he smiled.

"But now I don't feel that way," he said. "Look, to you, I may just be a dying man, lost in a realm beyond his control, and I may even be, but... I'm alone, man. Damn, just seeing you here will give me hope for countless more cycles. For that, I thank you."

"Don't you want to come with us?" Harry asked. "I mean, it'd be nice to have you with us to the next floor. I don't doubt your abilities, I mean, you've had the strength to stay here for who knows how many cycles! I'm not the one leading the group, but I'm sure Goldman would at least consider it."

"...Are you serious?" His eyes gleamed. "I, well, I'd never survive out there, but—"

"Careful!" Goldman's gruff voice rang in the group's ears.

Harry then turned, ready to use one of his doors. He saw Goldman ahead, defending against several boulders with his body. They were small, almost like pebbles, but at high speed even a leaf could be fatal.

They all continued on their way, now with much more concern. Seeing no major threat, Harry decided not to use his Emblem for now, fearing he might jeopardize Goldman's leadership.

"..makes sense!" He heard Brun and Goldman yelling. Madeline, alongside them, looked horrified with the old woman's explanation of something as she looked at the snow.

What are they talking about? Harry added inwardly as he turned around, not noticing anything out of the ordinary. It'll take some time before the rain really starts going. We should have plenty of time to reach a safe spot. This isn't about the path...

Petrikov, also confused, tried to understand the situation while closing his eyes and taking deep breaths. He shook his head as he continued walking. Slow and steady... He thought to himself.

Goldman then turned to speak to Daniel. At that moment, Harry's heart nearly stopped. During all the time he had spoken with the gentle giant, he had never noticed panic on his face. Not even when they fought Conrad. The man always had a smile, or a variation of it. It was almost a brilliant ray of optimism in everything he said, and that is why his expression terrified Harry. He spoke to Daniel calmly, softly, almost as if he didn't want to arouse suspicion in the others.

Madeline saw Harry's confusion, and although it was hard to hear, she pointed to the snow. The Locksmith then started digging, much to Petrikov's perplexity.

After a few seconds, he said to himself. "What am I supposes to be searching for? This isn't..."

And then he saw it. A human hand. Then two, then three. Harry, aghast, looked up and saw the snow that preserved dead bodies.

A face stared back at Harry, right below his feet. The face of a child. Before Harry could advise against, Petrikov saw it. It was one of the children who attended his class.

"No..." He said to himself, tears already coming out.

"Come on, Petri." The Locksmith held his arm and they followed the group.

I am in hell. The young thief repeated to himself. I am in hell.
