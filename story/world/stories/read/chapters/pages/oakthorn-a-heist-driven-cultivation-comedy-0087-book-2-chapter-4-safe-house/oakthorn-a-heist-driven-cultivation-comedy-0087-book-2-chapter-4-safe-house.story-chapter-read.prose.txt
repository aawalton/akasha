Roots stretched across the tunnel ceiling like petrified veins, the ancient network having found its way through solid stone over countless centuries. A single mage light bobbed ahead, casting long shadows that danced and writhed with each step.

“Are we there yet?” Chubbs asked for the seventeenth time in the past hour, her tiny fingers sticky with some unidentifiable substance she’d found on the tunnel walls.

Cade shifted the sleepy dragonling on his shoulder and exhaled slowly. Twenty-six hours underground had frayed even his legendary patience. “Not yet, princess. Soon.”

Their makeshift camp had provided just enough rest to keep them moving, but not nearly enough to wipe away the bone-deep exhaustion that came from fleeing a city of angry mobs and religious zealots. The narrow tunnels had eventually given way to more structured pathways with actual support beams and carved walls, but that only made the journey feel more like being digested by some massive stone serpent.

“I could carry her for a while,” Rayka offered, nodding toward Chubbs who was now attempting to pocket a glowing mushroom.

“No, no,” Cade said quickly, scooping the fungus from the toddler’s hands and replacing it with a tiny carved wooden horse Jer had fashioned during their brief rest. “Last time you carried her, she turned your hair into feathers.”

Rayka’s hand instinctively went to her head. “Fair point.”

Astrid moved with unsettling grace ahead of them, her staff occasionally tapping against specific stones that pulsed with faint blue light at her touch. The young and petite draugr girl hadn’t shown a hint of fatigue despite the grueling pace she’d set.

“Not much further now,” she called back, her voice echoing unnaturally in the cramped space. “Another mile or so before we reach the main thoroughfare.”

Orro snorted from his position at the rear of their group. “Thoroughfare. As if we’re taking a pleasant stroll through a market district. Last I checked, moist underground tunnels don’t have thoroughfares. Just shallow graves.”

“Someone’s upset to be away from the Verdant Sea, I can tell,” Astrid remarked in a sing-song drawl as she shot a wicked grin over her shoulder.

Cade saw Orro tense out of the corner of his eye, a nerve clearly struck.

“How’d—” Orro growled, looking ready to force answers out of their undead guide, but Cade interrupted him.

“How much further, Ms. Clover?” he asked politely.

Astrid’s black orbs glanced back at him. Malice lingered just behind the civility she wore like a mask. His mind flashed with the image of the splattered gold ranker.

“Not much farther, Son of Ruin.”

“Is anyone else concerned about how casual she is about letting us use these super secretive smuggling tunnels, or is it just me?” Gavin’s voice resonated directly in Cade’s mind through their focal stones, the telepathic connection feeling like cool water washing through his thoughts.

“Everyone with a functioning brain stem,” Elena replied, her mental voice as sharp as her actual one.

“Stay alert,” Nora added, her presence in the mental link feeling like the calm center of a storm. “She’s been too cooperative.”

Bunny stirred on Cade’s shoulder, his scales shifting from sleepy white to an anxious silver-blue in the tunnel’s lighting. The dragonling’s scarred tail tightened around Cade’s neck—not enough to constrict, but a clear warning.

“Bunny feels it too,” Cade projected to the group. “Something’s coming.”

As if on cue, the tunnel ahead widened dramatically, opening into a vast underground chamber that stole the breath from Cade’s lungs. Massive stone columns carved with intricate runes supported a ceiling so high it disappeared into darkness. Railroad tracks—actual, metal railroad tracks—ran through the center of the chamber, disappearing into tunnels on either side. Cade had read of such industrial progress, but most gods prohibited such innovations in their territories.

Who would want mortals to be able to flee them at high speeds or, gods forbid, no longer need their constant oversight?

“Welcome,” Astrid announced with a sweeping gesture, “to the Clover Clan’s humble transportation network.”

“Humble,” Jer muttered, craning his neck to take in the impossible architecture. “Right.”

Evie moved closer to Nora, her eyes wide and unfocused in that way that made everyone around her nervous.

“The darkness has roads that shine,” she whispered, her voice taking on that musical quality that Cade was coming to understand preceded her prophecies. “Beneath the crowns of kings, the hidden throne—”

“Not now,” Nora cut her off gently, placing a steadying hand on the siren’s shoulder. “Save your strength.”

Cade caught Nora’s eye and raised an eyebrow. She shook her head slightly—whatever Evie was sensing wasn’t an immediate threat. And Nora didn’t want their draugr host to know any more of Evie’s secrets than she already did.

File her doomsday proclamation away for later, then.

“This is...” Cade gestured at the massive subterranean station, “impressive. How far does your network extend, exactly?”

Astrid’s smile was all teeth… Sharp teeth. “Curious, aren’t we? Let’s just say that while the gods may rule the heavens, the Clover Clan has mapped the bones of this continent quite thoroughly. Over the course of the past millennia, we’ve become the biggest crime syndicate in the world, give or take a few centuries.”

“The ‘biggest crime syndicate in the world’ is quite the claim,” Nora commented, her voice carefully neutral.

“Not a claim. A fact.” Astrid tapped her staff against the ground, and a small handcar rolled seemingly of its own accord from a side tunnel. “The gods have their domains, kings have their kingdoms, but everyone—everyone—eventually needs something they’re not supposed to have. That’s where we come in.”

Cade helped Chubbs onto the handcar, trying not to flinch when she poked at what looked suspiciously like a detonation rune etched into the metal.

“Not this time, Chubbs,” Cade prompted as he moved her hand from the rune protected by a glass box. “I’m sure there will be buttons to press later, okay?”

“Cadey?” The little girl looked up at him with wide eyes that sometimes seemed too ancient for her cherubic face. “Am I bad?”

The question hit him like a physical blow. He crouched down to her level, aware of Nora tensing behind him.

“Why would you ever say something like that?” he asked, eyebrows knit together in concern.

She looked away from him, tapping one of her heels against the other in a soft rhythm. “You always tell me no. That means what I want is bad. That makes me bad too.”

“Oh, Chubbs,” Cade said, his heart wrenching at the words. “Your curiosity is just a bit… extreme, is all.”

“Is that why I’m so different?” Chubbs asked, her doe eyes staring directly into his soul.

“You’re special, Chubbs. One of a kind. And we wouldn’t have it any other way.” Cade replied more sincerely than he’d expected.

“But why?” she persisted, her tiny fingers now tracing the curve of his jawline.

“Some questions don’t have easy answers, kiddo.” He ruffled her golden curls, feeling the raw power that thrummed beneath her skin. “We’re all just figuring it out as we go.”

Over Chubbs’ head, he caught Nora’s conflicted expression—the truth perched on her lips, held back only by the thinnest thread of restraint. He gave a slight shake of his head.

Not yet. Not until they were somewhere safe, if such a place even existed.

“Everyone in,” Astrid called out, breaking the moment. “Next stop, the most crime-free city in the realm.”

“Why do I feel like that’s not a coincidence?” Rayka’s voice whispered through their mental link.

“Because nothing ever is,” Cade replied, settling in for whatever fresh hell awaited them at their destination.

The handcar moved at a speed that defied physics, magic humming through its metal frame as it shot through the underground tunnels. The wind whipped Cade’s hair back, stinging his eyes despite the protective barrier Astrid had erected around them.

“So,” Cade shouted over the rush of air, keeping one arm firmly around Chubbs who was giggling maniacally at the speed, “tell me more about this family business of yours.”

Astrid’s laugh was almost lost in the tunnel’s roar. “What would you like to know? Our latest haul? If we’re all draugrs?”

“Yes. Yes, I would like to know that,” Jer answered her question but was quieted by an elbow to his ribs. “What?! I’ve never met a draugr before her. Is curiosity a crime?”

“Yes!” Elena, Rayka, and Nora all answered the acrobat at once.

“Yes!” Chubbs shouted over the din of their passage.

Cade chuckled and patted the tiny goddess on the head affectionately.

“Let’s start with how the biggest criminal empire in the world stays under the radar,” Nora interjected, her knuckles white where she gripped the handcar’s rail.

“Who says we’re under the radar?” Astrid’s eyes gleamed unnaturally in the darkness. “The best place to hide is in plain sight. The Clover Trading Company is one of the most respected merchant houses in the realm. We pay our taxes. We contribute to charities. We even help the crown catch ‘dangerous criminals’ from time to time.”

“Just not the ones on your payroll,” Elena muttered.

“Naturally.” Astrid nodded as if Elena had made a perfectly reasonable observation. “It’s all about balance. For every smuggler we sacrifice to the authorities, we gain the trust to move ten more shipments unchecked.”

The tunnel walls blurred around them, occasionally opening into massive chambers where other tracks intersected, creating a dizzying network that seemed impossible to navigate without a map.

“You’ve mapped the entire continent,” Cade said, eyes narrowing as realization dawned. “That’s how you move so freely. You have access points in every major city.”

Astrid tapped her nose with a finger. “Smart boy. My great-grandmother started the tunnels three centuries ago. Each generation has expanded them. The gods are too busy squabbling amongst themselves to notice what happens beneath their feet, and mortals rarely look down unless they’re already falling.”

“Pretty poetic for someone who beats people to death with a magic stick,” Gav commented.

“I contain multitudes,” Astrid replied dryly. “And it was only one person. The rest were merely... inconvenienced.”

“Why not just kill them all? Why torture them?” Orro’s voice cut in, chilling the conversation.

Everyone turned to Astrid. Her smile grew thin.

“Because if I killed an entire battalion of Lifekeepers, there’d be the four hells to pay. But if they return to their camps, defeated and humiliated by a lone warrior, their spirits will be broken. That’s how the Clover Clan has survived throughout the rises and falls of kingdoms. Break the spirit, break the man. Blood rarely needs to be spilt if you can manage that.” Throughout her entire explanation, Astrid’s eyes never left Orro’s.

“Some spirits don’t break,” was all Orro said in response, the conversation clearly over.

The handcar began to slow as they approached a station carved directly into natural bedrock. Glowing crystals illuminated elaborate murals depicting scenes Cade recognized from historical texts—except these versions showed hooded figures in the backgrounds, pulling strings, moving pieces.

“Modest,” Cade said, eyeing the ornate carvings with a mixture of distaste and begrudging respect. At least they knew what a color palette was, even if theirs was red and black.

“History is written by the victors,” Astrid replied with a shrug. “We just keep better records.”

As they disembarked, Chubbs toddled over to a particularly graphic mural depicting what appeared to be the assassination of a king. She traced the carved blood spray with a chubby finger.

“Pretty red,” she said happily.

Rayka quickly scooped her up. “Let’s find something less murdery to look at, shall we?”

“Speaking of murder,” Cade said casually, “we’re approaching Honor’s Light, aren’t we? Bit risky for a criminal enterprise to set up shop in the famously incorruptible capital of Honor’s domain.”

Orro’s entire demeanor shifted at the mention of Honor, his posture tensing like a drawn bow.

“The most effective camouflage is absurdity,” Astrid said after a beat too long. “No one looks for a snake’s nest in a lion’s den.”

“What was that about?” Cade directed toward Orro through their mental link.

“Later,” came the terse reply, vibrating with an emotion Cade rarely detected from the assassin: rage.

Unadulterated and old rage. It was like watching an ember thought dead reignited with a single gust of wind.

A rumble passed through the stone around them, and dust sifted down from the ceiling.

“Ah, right on schedule,” Astrid said, looking upward. “That would be the twice-daily prayer procession passing overhead. Thousands of devoted followers marching through the streets to honor the god of, well, Honor.” Her smile was sharp enough to cut. “If they only knew what was beneath their self-righteous feet.”

A set of stairs carved into the bedrock led upward, spiraling into darkness before being swallowed by what appeared to be a trap door.

“That’s our entrance,” Astrid explained, starting up the steps. “Welcome to Honor’s Light, the shining jewel of moral superiority and, coincidentally, home to our largest operation.”

“This is insane,” Elena’s voice slipped into their shared telepathic link. “We’re walking into the headquarters of the world’s biggest crime syndicate, in the middle of a city dedicated to the god of Honor, while carrying the reincarnation of a dead goddess.”

“Just another Tuesday, then,” Jer replied, but the humor in his mental voice was strained.

“Stay sharp,” Cade instructed as they ascended. “We don’t commit to anything until we know exactly what we’re walking into.”

The trap door opened into what appeared to be a wine cellar, racks upon racks of dusty bottles extending into the gloom. Astrid led them through a maze of vintage years and rare origins before stopping at a seemingly random section and pulling a specific bottle.

With a mechanical click, an entire rack swung inward, revealing a corridor illuminated by the same blue-tinged magical lights they’d seen in the tunnels.

“Subtle,” Orro commented.

“Says the man currently wearing thirteen concealed weapons,” Astrid shot back without missing a beat.

“Fourteen,” Orro corrected automatically, then scowled.

The corridor led to an elevator—an actual, mechanical elevator with brass fittings and an ornate gate. As they crowded inside, Chubbs reached for the control panel with alarming enthusiasm.

“I help!” she declared, seconds away from potentially sending them who-knew-where.

“How about you help me instead?” Cade intervened smoothly, lifting her up to see out the small window in the elevator door. “You can count the floors as we go up.”

While Chubbs happily began counting (getting stuck at “four” and repeating it several times), Astrid pressed a sequence of buttons and pulled a lever. The elevator lurched upward with surprising smoothness.

“My father is eager to meet you all,” she said as they ascended. “He’s particularly interested in your... unique circumstances.”

“By which you mean the angry mobs, the religious fanatics, or the fact that we’ve been blamed for the destruction of a city older than dirt?” Cade asked dryly.

“All of the above,” Astrid replied with a smile that didn’t reach her eyes. “The Clover Clan has a vested interest in the balance of power. When gods start reincarnating and fragments of divine essence start attaching themselves to mortal thieves, well... that affects business.”

Cade’s blood ran cold.

“She knows,” he sent urgently through their mental link. “About me. About the Remnant.”

“Play it cool,” Nora replied instantly. “We need to know how much they know, and what they want.”

The elevator came to a stop, and the gates opened onto a sight that momentarily robbed Cade of speech. They stood in what could only be described as a palace, disguised as the top floors of what, from the outside, probably appeared to be a perfectly respectable merchant company headquarters.

Marble floors stretched beneath crystal chandeliers. Priceless artwork adorned walls covered in silk damask. Servants—or more likely, highly trained operatives disguised as servants—moved efficiently through the space.

“Four!” Chubbs declared triumphantly, having finally moved on from her counting loop.

“Close enough, kiddo,” Cade murmured, his eyes locked on the wealth of information laid out before him.

Every piece of art, every servant’s position, every hidden door (and he counted at least seven) carefully cataloged and filed away.

“This way,” Astrid directed, leading them toward an expansive gallery off the main hall. “Father thought you might appreciate some of our collection before your meeting.”

The gallery made Cade’s fingers itch with professional appreciation. Display cases lined the walls, each containing artifacts that would fund a small kingdom. Ancient weapons with telltale shimmer of enchantment. Jewels the size of chicken eggs. Scrolls and codices so old the parchment had turned amber.

“Just browsing,” Jer quipped, earning him a sharp elbow from his sister.

“Look but don’t touch,” Astrid advised. “The security measures are... excessive.”

As if to prove her point, Chubbs—who had wiggled down from Cade’s arms—reached for a particularly shiny golden orb. Before anyone could stop her, her tiny fingers brushed the display case.

Nothing happened.

The entire group collectively held their breath as Chubbs pressed her face against the glass, leaving smudgy cheek prints.

“Pretty ball,” she declared, then toddled off toward the next shiny object.

Astrid’s expression was priceless—a mixture of confusion, alarm, and grudging respect. “That’s... not possible. Those wards would reduce an adult to ash.”

“She’s special,” Cade said with a shrug that he hoped conveyed nonchalance rather than the cold panic gripping his spine. “Kids, am I right?”

“The wards aren’t responding to her divine essence,” Nora’s voice filtered through their mental link, tight with concern. “That shouldn’t be possible unless...”

“Unless the wards were designed by someone who knows exactly what she is,” Cade finished the thought. The implications sent ice through his veins.

Astrid was watching them too carefully, her eyes narrowed in calculation. Cade forced a relaxed smile and gestured broadly. “Quite the collection. Your father has excellent taste.”

“He’ll be pleased you think so.” She began leading them deeper into the gallery, past increasingly rare and dangerous items. “Most of these have been in our family for generations, though we do occasionally... acquire new pieces of interest.”

That's when Cade saw it.

Hanging innocuously on the far wall, between a jewel-encrusted ceremonial dagger and what appeared to be the preserved hand of a demon, was a map. Its aura was old and stagnant, but there was no mistaking the Destruction magic leaking from it.

Not just any map. The map.

Ancient parchment stretched across a frame of blackened wood, the edges crumbling with age. Markings in a language that should have been incomprehensible to him blazed like fire in his vision. His steps faltered as every instinct screamed at him to go closer, to touch it, to take it.

A map to Destruction’s domain. The original domain, not the scattered fragments that survived the god’s demise. He knew it like he knew his own name.

Holy shit. Holy actual shit. Cade’s internal voice reached a pitch he didn’t know was possible. That’s it. That’s actually it. The map to the original domain where the rest of the shards would be. Right there on the wall like a cheap tavern decoration.

Outwardly, he managed to keep his composure, save for a slight hitch in his step and what he was sure was a momentary widening of his eyes. He forced himself to keep walking, to not stare, to not give away the earthquake happening inside his skull.

“Cade?” Rayka’s concerned voice filtered through their link. “You look the same as when Dad told you that you could stay in the Skyreach library overnight. What is it?”

“On the wall,” he replied, keeping his mental voice impressively steady. “Don’t look directly at it. Third display from the left. It’s a map to Destruction’s original domain.”

“You’re sure?” Nora’s mental voice was sharp with alarm.

“Absolutely certain. The shard... it’s reacting.” He could feel it inside him, the fragment of divine essence burning against his core, reaching toward its counterparts depicted on the ancient parchment.

“Something caught your eye?” Astrid asked, too perceptive by half.

“Just admiring the craftsmanship,” Cade replied smoothly, gesturing vaguely toward a nearby display of jeweled daggers. “My sister has a weakness for sharp, sparkly things.”

Rayka played along beautifully, moving toward the daggers with well-feigned interest. “Guilty as charged.”

Chubbs, demonstrating her impeccable timing, chose that moment to trip over her own feet and tumble directly into a pedestal supporting what appeared to be a crystal skull. The pedestal wobbled alarmingly. A bright red pulse started to build around the base of the stand. The thief practically felt the angry enchantment about to make them all into fine dust.

Cade moved faster than thought, catching both Chubbs and the skull before either could hit the floor. The bright red flashes dissipated in a begrudging fashion the thief really did not appreciate.

He set the toddler on her feet and replaced the skull with the careful precision of a man disarming an explosive.

“Nice reflexes,” Astrid commented with raised eyebrows.

“Survival skill when you’re dealing with her,” Cade replied, ruffling Chubbs’ hair affectionately. “She’s our crew’s latest demolition expert. You should see her resumé.”

“I flying!” Chubbs declared happily, apparently interpreting her near-fall differently.

“More like falling with style,” Jer quipped, scooping her up and settling her on his shoulders where she immediately began using his hair as reins.

Astrid watched the interaction with calculated interest before continuing toward a set of double doors at the end of the gallery. “Father is waiting in the reception room. He’s quite eager to discuss his proposition.”

“We need that map,” Orro’s voice slipped into their shared consciousness.

“Obviously,” Cade replied. “But first, let’s see what daddy draugr wants with us. Knowledge is leverage.”

“And we’re in desperate need of some leverage right now,” Elena added grimly.

The double doors swung open to reveal a reception room that put most royal chambers to shame. Marble columns supported a domed ceiling painted with scenes of what appeared to be the founding of the Clover Clan. Crystal chandeliers cast rainbow prisms across plush carpets and velvet-upholstered furniture.

But Cade barely registered the opulence, because there, standing beside a distinguished-looking draugr whose silver-shot hair and regal bearing could only mark, was a face he’d hoped never to see again.

Bernard, gold-ranked leader of Gavin’s old crew, looked up from his conversation, and his perfectly practiced smile showed just a few too many teeth.

“Cade!” he called out, voice dripping with false warmth. “What an absolutely unexpected pleasure.”

The rest of Bernard’s crew—the infamous Stone Britches, possibly the world’s most pretentious thieves’ guild—loitered around the room with the forced casualness of predators trying not to spook their prey.

Cade felt his plans crumbling like sand between his fingers, but his smile never faltered. “Bernard. I’d say it’s good to see you, but my mother taught me not to lie to the elderly.”

Behind him, he heard Nora’s almost imperceptible sigh.

Bernard smiled congenially, but there was a feral glint to his gaze. “I see I’ve been sat at the kid’s table. How… Quaint.”

A well-dressed draugr stepped forward, his undead eyes glittering with amusement. “Ah, the infamous Cade Stormhollow and his crew.” His gaze drifted to Astrid with something like fond exasperation. “Welcome to the heart of the Clover Clan. We have much to discuss. My name is Jameson. I run this branch of our family’s business.”

Well, Cade thought as he met the gaze of the most powerful crime lord in the realm while a divine toddler drooled onto the priceless rug, and their only escape route was blocked by another crime lord who wanted him dead. This is going to be interesting.
