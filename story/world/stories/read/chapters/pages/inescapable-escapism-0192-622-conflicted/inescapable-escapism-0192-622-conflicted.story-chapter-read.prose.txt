As I entered the canteen, I could feel my eyes automatically seeking Seth out. I knew he should have been there. At lunch, he’d told me he had swimming last thing, and we’d planned on eating dinner together, just like we did every night. His class finished twenty minutes before mine, so I should have been able to spot him sitting at one of the tables, but I couldn’t find him.

Perhaps he’d gotten bored of waiting, a voice in the back of my head mused. He may have decided to eat without me, and I couldn’t exactly blame him for changing his mind. Twenty minutes was a long time to just sit there and do nothing. I’d done it whilst waiting for him before, but I understood if he hadn’t been able to do the same. Or just didn’t want to.

I glanced over my shoulder at the tables behind me once more as I neared the front of the queue, trying to make my movements as subtle as possible. I didn’t want to seem as though I was searching for someone. That felt a bit pathetic, like I was too much of a coward to eat alone or that I was such a loner that I only had one friend at the Academy. It was kind of true, of course. The rest were still in the induction wing.

A group of trainees that had been standing in the middle of the room finally moved aside, revealing Seth seated behind them, and I let out a small sigh as relief washed over me. I didn’t want to admit how anxious I had been about the thought of having to eat alone again or how much better seeing him there made me feel, but the difference was significant. I could even feel a bit of a smile pulling at my lips as I looked at him.

Seth didn’t seem to have spotted me yet. He was staring into space, a distant expression on his face, and I couldn’t help but glance around to make sure I wasn’t holding up the queue before looking back at him.

He looked good. I almost didn’t want to admit that either. It felt weird to think that about a friend, even one I might have had feelings for, but he did. His dark hair was still a little wet from the pool, but it was starting to curl as it dried. He didn’t have particularly curly hair normally. That only happened after he’d been swimming, and whenever I noticed it, I was always gripped with the urge to run my fingers through it.

I shook my head, trying to push the thought aside. I couldn’t be thinking things like that, I told myself. He was my friend, and it was weird. If he knew, it would probably make him uncomfortable, and I didn’t want that. I cared about him too much.

Taking a deep breath, I forced myself to stare straight ahead and not look back at him. It was hard, though. I was still just standing in the queue, waiting to get my food, and there wasn’t much else for me to do. I was alone. I didn’t recognise the trainees standing on either side of me, and even if I did, I didn’t have the confidence to just strike up a conversation with them.

As I reached the front of the queue, I couldn’t stop myself from glancing towards Seth again, checking to make sure he was still there, and he was, thankfully. He had barely moved from the last time I’d looked at him, his gaze still faraway. Somehow, despite how long he must have been sitting there, his plate was almost full. He was just picking slowly at a slice of garlic bread, seemingly lost in his thoughts.

The urge to know what he was thinking about gripped me. I longed to abandon my tray and go straight over to him, to ask him to tell me his deepest thoughts, but I knew I could not. He could have been thinking about anything, and it would have been weird for me to ask. Plus, then I’d just need to rejoin the back of the queue, and the buffet was already starting to run low.

My class had ended later than most. The majority of the trays were more than half-empty, but luckily, there were still plenty of vegetarian options for me to choose from, and I moved quickly. I didn’t want to take too long to fill up my plate. I felt bad. Seth had already been waiting so long for me to join him, and I knew it wasn’t my fault that my class was scheduled to finish later than his, but that didn’t matter. I still didn’t want to keep him waiting any longer than I had to.

I neared the end of the buffet, my steps slowing as I reached the drinks table. Craning my head, I glanced at Seth again, trying to work out whether I needed to grab anything, but my gaze fell on the table in front of him. An almost full jug of water had been placed in front of him, next to two cups. A smile pulled at my lips as I turned away from the drinks and began to walk towards him.

Whatever it was that Seth was thinking about must have been interesting because he didn’t react as I approached. It wasn’t until I’d almost reached the table, just a couple of steps away, that he blinked quickly and glanced around. His eyes found me immediately, and he smiled.

My stomach tightened, and I had to look down. The way he smiled at me made me feel weirdly off-balance and out of my depth, but not in a bad way. It was a little scary, but I liked it, and I recognised the feeling. I felt the same way when Duncan smiled at me whenever we were alone in his room.

But I couldn’t think like that, I told myself as I pushed the thought aside. Seth is not Duncan, and we are not dating. He is not my boyfriend. Maybe one day he could be, but for now—

“Hey,” I said, interrupting my own thoughts.

“Hey! How was observation and memorisation?” he asked before dropping his piece of garlic bread and loading his fork with spaghetti bolognese.

“It was good! How was swimming?”

Seth had been eating ravenously. It was clear he was starving, but my answer had made him pause. His fork froze halfway to his mouth, and he stared at me in confusion for a few seconds before speaking.

“It was?” he asked. “Are you being sarcastic?”

His question made me laugh as I grabbed my own cutlery and glanced down at my food, debating where to start.

“No,” I told him, spearing a piece of broccoli before continuing. “It was actually pretty good!”

I took a bite as Seth continued to stare at me in confusion, savouring the flavour. Broccoli was normally kind of boring. Whenever my mom had made it for me when I was a kid, she’d just boiled it, but the piece I was eating had been fried in chilli, garlic, and butter, and it was delicious. Immediately, I regretted only grabbing a couple of florets rather than emptying the rest of the tray.

“Really? Why?” Seth asked, his brows drawn together and his eyes slightly narrowed. “I mean, I’m glad. Obviously. I just… don’t get it.”

I covered my mouth with my hand as I laughed before hurrying to swallow my mouthful.

“That’s absolutely fair,” I told him. “Like I expected it to be boring too, but it wasn’t just a normal class. We had a training exercise thing.”

Seth’s eyebrows rose.

“Oh, really? We haven’t done any of those yet. What was it like?”

I’d just put a forkful of pasta into my mouth when he’d asked that question, and I had to chew quickly before I could answer him.

“Good! Kind of scary, though,” I said. “It was meant to be a simulation of what it’s actually like to be an analyst, so Cara showed us a recording of a real mission and some pictures of a target, and we had to watch out for him to appear so we could warn the field operative on the ground before he got to her.”

“Huh. That sounds pretty fun, actually,” Seth agreed as he reached for the jug that he’d placed between us. “Do you want some?”

“Yeah, please,” I said, pushing the other cup towards him with a smile. “It was! Like, it was kinda boring at times because we were just sitting there and watching CCTV feeds, and nothing was really happening for ages, but it was good, you know?”

“Yeah, for sure. I mean, I feel like I’d get bored too, but how did it go? Did the class spot the target in time?” Seth asked.

“We did!” I told him, hesitating before correcting myself. “Well… I did. Cara picked me to go first, and I think next class, someone else is going to have a go with a different simulation.”

I wasn’t sure why, but admitting that had felt kind of scary. I was worried that it would sound like I was bragging about how well I’d done or something, and I didn’t want Seth to think that.

“Oh, really?” he said, his eyebrows rising again. “Wait, how does that work?”

“She’d set up like a mock GSOD station at the front of the room, but there were only two screens, and then the CCTV feed was also showing on the projector behind me so everyone else could see it,” I explained.

Seth grimaced as he paused to take a bite of his garlic bread.

“Wait, so you were facing everyone else?” he asked. “God, that sounds terrifying. Like, I know you were probably focusing on the mission, but like… I would have hated that.”

He laughed, and I joined in. It was a relief to hear Seth say that. It had been really intimidating, especially at first, and it was nice to know I wasn’t alone in feeling like that. Seth admitting that he would have found it scary too made me feel more normal.

“Yeah. I mean… I did forget about them pretty quickly, but it was kind of terrifying.”

My stomach swooped again as Seth smiled at me.

“But you did it, right? You spotted the target?”

“Yeah,” I told him, looking down.

“Well done! That’s awesome.”

I felt my face starting to heat up at his words, and I couldn’t meet his gaze. Instead, I kept my eyes fixed on my plate as I stabbed some veggie meat.

“Thanks.”

“I mean, I’m not surprised at all,” he continued. “Like, I’d completely trust you as my analyst.”

My eyes snapped to his face. His expression was completely earnest, and I was taken aback.

“Really?” I couldn’t help but ask.

“Yeah! I think knowing you were in my ear and watching the CCTV would make me feel way better if I were in the field,” he told me.

I could only stare at Seth as I processed his words. I felt weirdly touched by them. Knowing that he’d trust me with his safety like that, with his life, made me feel even more flustered than I’d felt before, and I didn’t know how to respond to it.

Seth seemed to realise what he’d just said. His eyes widened slightly, and he let out an awkward laugh, looking away from me again.

“Thanks,” I said.

That was the best I could do. I wanted to tell him that it meant a lot to hear him say that, but I couldn’t find the words. My thoughts had begun to swirl again, and I was drowning beneath the vortex.

It had been scary enough during the training exercise, and that wasn’t just because everyone was staring at me. I knew it was just a simulation. There were no real consequences if I messed up, but that would be different if I were doing it for real. If I lost focus or made a mistake, someone would die, and that someone could be Seth. It could be Abbie or Katie. They were both going to become field operatives too, and I could be assigned to a mission with either of them.

My chest began to tighten as fear gripped me. I wouldn’t be alone. Not straightaway, anyway. I’d spent enough time in the GSOD to know that. Trainees always started out with a tutor helping them out. Then, they were paired with another trainee for a while, until they became confident with what they were doing, so it wasn’t like I’d be thrown into it immediately. If I did become an intelligence analyst, I’d have a chance to work my way up to that.

But I still wasn’t sure whether I’d ever be able to. It felt too scary. It was too much pressure, and I wasn’t ready for it. I’d never be ready for it. Perhaps it would be better if I became a field operative, I found myself thinking. Then, only my life would be in my hands. I wouldn’t have to worry about making a mistake that could kill someone. I’d just need to worry about me.

“So, um, did it help?” Seth asked after an awkward pause.

I blinked and stared up at him. I’d been so lost in my thoughts that I wasn’t sure whether I’d missed something that he’d said, but I had no idea what he was talking about.

“Did what help?”

Seth smiled, but he still looked slightly uncomfortable.

“You know, the training exercise,” he clarified. “Like, did it help you make your mind up about your specialism? It sounds like it went really well, so…”

He trailed off, and I smiled at him to show I understood. Somehow, it was like he’d read my mind. He seemed to know exactly what I was grappling with, and I had no idea how he’d done it.

“Oh, yeah,” I said before quickly correcting myself. “No, I mean. I don’t know. I think if anything, it’s kind of made it harder, you know? Like, don’t get me wrong, I did enjoy it, but it also made me kind of… I don’t know.”

I was rambling. I knew that I was. Nothing I was saying was really making any sense, and I didn’t know how to explain the thoughts whirling through my mind. They were confusing even for me, and I felt silly for feeling so conflicted. I didn’t want to say how I was feeling out loud.

The whole point of the Academy was to become a field agent, an analyst, or to go into research and development, or any of the other options. We were meant to choose a specialism and stick with it, but I couldn’t do it.

“It made you kind of…” Seth repeated, prompting me to continue.

I let out a breath, my eyes flitting towards his face. His expression was gentle. He seemed genuinely interested in what I was thinking, and for some reason, that just made me feel worse. My stomach churned. Guilt sat heavily in the pit of it, and the room spun lazily as the urge to flee gripped me.

There had to be a way I could phrase it without sounding stupid.

“I just…” I started carefully, trailing off and taking a deep breath before forcing myself to speak again. “I worry that I’d regret it if I became an analyst. Like… it would still be cool. I think I’d still enjoy it, and I’d be helping to protect people and make a difference, but… I don’t know. Part of me also wants to just… be out there. I just feel like it’s not the same, you know?”

I glanced up at him nervously, a grimace on my face. I was worried that he’d laugh at me or just not understand what I was saying. It wasn’t particularly clear. I felt like I could have done better. I could have explained my thoughts more coherently, but I hadn’t.

Part of it had been intentional, though. I’d left out the terror that had gripped me at the thought of messing up and costing someone their life because I didn’t want to scare him. If I did change my mind and choose to become an analyst, I might have to work with him. I’d be absolutely petrified the whole time, but I didn’t want him to feel that way. I wanted him to be as comfortable and confident as possible.

“So… why don’t you become a field agent like me then?” he asked with a shrug. “I mean, I feel like you’d be great at that too.”

Relief slammed into me. His reaction made me feel a little better, but it was short-lived. He didn’t seem to judge me or offer me empty reassurances, which would have been just as bad. He just gave me an alternative, and it was tempting, but I still wasn’t sure.

“Yeah, maybe,” I said with a smile that felt halfhearted, even to me.

Although I did feel like I’d enjoy being a field operative, part of me was still unsure whether I could actually do it. I didn’t know how I’d perform under that kind of pressure, and I was worried I’d freeze or fall apart. I could snap completely and ruin the mission, and I had no idea what the consequences of that would be, but they could also be bad.

“You would,” Seth insisted. “Like, you’re crushing it in all of your classes, right?”

My nose scrunched up as my gaze returned to my tray. I was doing okay in them, but I wasn’t sure if I’d say I was crushing it. I hadn’t been told I was doing badly, at least.

“I mean… I think so,” I replied, my tone unsure.

“Exactly!” he said before pausing. It seemed like he was debating saying something else. His eyes darted between his food and me before he spoke again. “And… I mean, having you by my side in the field would make me feel better too. Like, I know you’d be completely fine by yourself, obviously, but I don’t know… I just think it would be fun to go on an assignment with you.”

I looked up at him, surprise pushing every other emotion out of me. His cheeks had grown steadily more pink as he’d spoken, and he seemed to be pointedly staring at his tray, but I couldn’t look away from his face.

He was flirting with me, I realised. His tone made that clear, and perhaps it shouldn’t have been so surprising. We’d flirted a bit before, obviously, but that felt different. He was never quite so open about it, and saying that he wanted to go on a mission with me, that he thought it would be fun, felt… bigger.

The silence had stretched out for too long, though. I didn’t know how to respond, but I needed to think of something. I couldn’t just continue to stare at him. He already looked uncomfortable, and I wanted to flirt too. I wanted him to know that I felt the same way.

“Thanks,” I said. “I think it would be fun too.”

As the words left my mouth, a wince flitted across my face. It felt weak compared to what he’d said, but his gaze found mine, and a smile began to stretch slowly across his lips.

“Maybe one day?”

“I’d like that,” I replied, but my voice came out too quiet. It was almost a whisper, and the audible wistfulness in my tone caused embarrassment to flood me. I cleared my throat before changing the subject. “So, um… how was swimming?”

I’d asked him that when I first sat down, but he hadn’t replied. I think I’d distracted him by talking about my class, and I felt bad.

“Oh, yeah. It was fine,” he said as he looked down at his food again. “Pretty good.”

“Yeah?” I asked, taking the opportunity to shovel some of my dinner into my mouth as well.

“Yeah. We didn’t use that hellish wave machine this time. We were just doing lengths, so it wasn’t too bad. I mean, at least I didn’t get slammed into the wall again, so I think my bruise is starting to heal.”

He sent me a smirk, and I returned it.

“Oh, good. How’s that feeling now? Does it still hurt?” I asked.

I wasn’t sure how long ago it had been. I’d been in and out of the world, so it was hard to tell, but it felt like only a couple of days had passed since he’d mentioned being thrown against the wall. He hadn’t brought it up since, but I was still worried.

“Eh,” he said with a slight shrug. “I mean, it’s not too bad. I’ve had worse. I think it just looks worse than it feels.”

“Really?”

“Yeah. Like this whole side is basically purple now,” he told me, gesturing to the right side of his body.

Concern nibbled at me, and I felt my eyebrows draw together as my gaze flicked towards it. I couldn’t see the bruise he was talking about through his shirt. He’d started to show it to me the other day before catching himself, so I hadn’t actually seen it, and I had no idea how bad it was, but I was worried.

“Are you okay? Do you need to go see the doctor?” I asked.

Seth let out a soft snort, his expression bordering on embarrassed.

“I… already have, actually,” he admitted.

The furrow between my eyebrows grew deeper. I didn’t know that. He hadn’t mentioned it to me before, and that made me feel bad. Did he not feel comfortable talking about that kind of thing with me for some reason?

“You have?”

“Yeah,” he said awkwardly, not quite meeting my gaze. “Jenna sent me right at the beginning of class once she saw the bruise. She wanted to make sure I hadn’t cracked a rib or something.”

“And?” I asked immediately.

“I’m fine. Nothing’s broken,” he told me, hesitating before adding, “Well, just my pride.”

A snort escaped me, and his lips twitched. He seemed proud of himself for making me laugh.

“Good,” I said. “Well, next time you go against the wave machine, I bet you’ll make it to the end without getting slammed against the wall.”

“We’ll see.”

Seth’s gaze met mine, and he smiled again.
