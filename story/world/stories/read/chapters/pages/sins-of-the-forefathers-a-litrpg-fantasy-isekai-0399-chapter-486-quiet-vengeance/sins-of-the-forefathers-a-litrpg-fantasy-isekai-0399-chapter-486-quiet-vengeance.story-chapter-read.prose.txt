The flames that followed Serekh’s self-immolation were fast and powerful. They crossed the distance between the dual-layered shields Meia and I had so hastily cast in the space of an eyeblink. The intensity of the heat was enough to outright vaporize the deep pool of blood that had flooded this entire complex nearly up to our knees in the intervening space. I don’t know if it was the inherent nature of that sticky liquid that colored the flames that raced for our position, or if it was merely the corrupted nature of Serekh’s chosen method of vengeance upon us.

Either way, the fire that crashed into the flat pane of Meia’s Aegis Spell was a sickly crimson color.

And it was hungry.

The flash of light that occurred at the moment of impact was startling in its intensity, and I could see Meia’s entire body tense up at the strain of the force of it. Blood colored shot across the entire broad face of the Spell in a blinding wave of flame, dominating the entire half of the room that separated us from the Serekh’s sacrificial spite. I watched with clenched teeth as it actively clung to the surface of the shaped Mana, visibly questing with tongues of sickly crimson fire for weaknesses in the Aegis.

All the while, I could tell from the sweat on Meia’s brow that the force against the barrier was increasing with every moment that passed. Not only that, but there was visible bleed-through impacting Meia. The flat surfaces of her held-out hands were starting to smolder, and the smell of cooking meat drifted over to me as the flames slowly cooked her palm from feedback.

Yet, the Half-Orc Sorceress never once flinched from the no doubt agony she was experiencing. She merely bared both teeth and fang at the fire that sought to consume us.

Eventually, though…

“Brace,” Meia snarled out of the corner of her mouth.

I knew what that meant. I tensed for the blow that was coming, and still…

Still, I wasn’t truly ready.

Meia’s Aegis shattered into a million shards of broken Mana, and the fire roared forward to crash into the spherical surface of my own secondary barrier. I only barely noticed her slumping in place next to me, smoke drifting up from her palms, and then it was my turn.

My eyes immediately widened at what I felt. There was the expected force I could feel raging against the matrix of the Spell, battering at it like a thousand thousand barbarians seeking to topple the gates of a castle. There was the heat transfer, somehow creeping its way through the matrix of the Spell to assault me. Unlike Meia, though, I had almost unconsciously chosen my Primordium fist to channel my own Aegis, so my flesh was safe from the onslaught of flame. Still, the leather glove that covered my prosthetic almost immediately burned, exposing the pure white and rainbow of the arm to the room.

But…there was another element to fire, as it raged against the barrier.

I could feel him. Serekh was dead; there was no doubt about it.

But he was still there.

The Rendarch’s furious spirit was the driving force behind the fire that even now ached to consume us. His was the driving will behind the inferno pressing insistently against the Aegis I had cast, protecting not only Meia and me, but the entire mass of soldiers I had dragged into this mess, huddling closely together behind us. There was no physical form on the other side of the barrier for him to batter against it with. And yet, in my mind’s eye…

I could see him. Though I had never once laid eyes upon Serekh’s face before he had chosen to sacrifice himself in a bid to murder us, I knew this phantom’s face for what it was.

A monster.

Serekh’s hardened, flayed muscle had been transformed into flame, and yet I could still perceive the individual strands of tissue that had once existed. A long, thin, almost horse-like mutation of Dwarven features glared at me with a burning hatred as he pressed his very burning soul against the shell of my Spell. Twisted claws clung to the surface of the shining blue Mana, in death somehow transformed into sharpened points that endlessly scratched at it, searching for the tiniest chink in our armor.

And upon its brow shone a star. What might be called a third eye, blazing brighter and deeper and denser than all other sheets of flame that assailed us. Even through the bright white of its glare, I could still make out the shape of a great lidless eye, refulgent in its hatred.

I don’t know if the Rendarch had possessed such a thing in life, and I didn’t care. It didn’t matter anymore.

The only thing that did was that I could feel those claws scratching away not only at the surface of my shield, but at my Soul as well. I hadn’t even known it was possible to affect a Soul in such a way, across not only the divide of life and death, but the physical distance between his spirit and my body, separated by the Shield.

Yet I still felt those claws scratching and scratching against the outer reaches of my Soul, transferred somehow across the matrix of the Spell.

In the core of my being, within my very Soul, I felt the great crystal tree, with its bound star above its boughs, which represented me-that-is-myself shudder. Faint scratches opened up upon the crystalline bark, a wound that I could feel upon my very spirit. It wasn’t life-threatening…but the pressure increased with every moment that passed.

Had Meia experienced this same thing? Had the revenant that Serekh had become assaulted her soul just as it had mine?

Well.

I couldn’t be shown up, now could I? She hadn’t once faltered before this attack.

Neither would I.

I barred my own teeth at the pathetic ghost within the fire, only distantly aware of the blood that coated them.

My sense of time went…hazy, then. I was concentrating so fiercely on maintaining the barrier protecting us, micro-focusing on the slow drain to my Mana, that I no longer noticed the passage of seconds.

I ignored the pain that Serekh’s ghostly clawing at my soul had caused.

I ignored the heat of the flames, transferring into the metal of my closed fist.

I ignored the fruitless flailing of the impotent specter who had spent his life for no good reason.

Because, eventually…the fire died down. The crimson flames of whatever death curse Serekh had conjured gradually lost potency, and the last embers of Serekh’s own Soul simply…burned away. The disembodied, nearly invisible spirit opened its monstrous maw wide in a soundless wail of frustration…

And wisped away in the stagnant air of the archives.

I didn’t realize, at first. I was so focused on maintaining the Aegis that my first indication that things had changed was the feeling of a hand falling onto my shoulder.

That caused my concentration to abruptly shatter, and with it, the last thin remnant of my Spell. My eyes fluttered open from where I had closed them, just in time to watch as the azure Mana of my shield fell into a thousand sparks of light, which faded in moments. With the loss of the barrier separating us from the rest of the room, a rush of dry, hot, nearly summery wind nearly bowled me over from the force of it. At the same time, the blood that had been blocked off from my shield rushed forward to fill the empty space on the other side, where all of it had been boiled away. Hissing, iron steam filled the air as the sticky liquid rolled over red-hot stone, cooling it in moments. Coughing followed the new crimson mist in the room, and I didn’t resist as the hand on my shoulder dragged me along with it.

Nobody wanted to inhale steam created from blood that had placed everyone into near-inescapable comas, after all.

I slumped against one of the walls outside of the room where we had nearly all been roasted alive, peripherally aware of others doing the same around me. But that didn’t catch my attention.

What did was the sight of what had happened to my prosthetic.

Primordium, from what I understood, was a mythical metal. Far more than actual Mithril. It was a forgotten myth, at that. The metal was unheard of to an extent I’m sure only a few people on Vereden even remembered it existed. And most of them were Spirits.

I had watched this metal quite literally contain an artificial star of mingled Mana and Ki, in the depths of the Netherim bunker.

And for all of that…

I watched with dull eyes as the hand of my prosthetic glowed a cherry red through the darkness of the flooded archive. From the tips of my fingers, up to the articulation point of my wrist joint, the entire thing was suffused with all of the heat that had bled through the barrier. The air around it sizzled and spat from the damp atmosphere I found myself in.

What was going on with the Korvoks that they had the power to do something like this? That much fire and heat, capable of doing something like this, would have been capable of flash vaporizing us in an instant. I was…a little surprised, and a lot proud, that Meia and I had managed to effectively shield our people from the blast.

I sighed tiredly, allowing my head to thump back against the blood-stained stone wall supporting my back.

I’m not sure just how much more of this I had in me. That had been an insane drain on my Mana. I needed to take a backseat on any more fighting until it came time to confront the leader of these damned cannibals.

Otherwise, I’d just be dead weight.

Almost absentmindedly, my hand tried to drift down to the inside of my coat lapel, where I was storing a variety of personal potions. Among them, another butter-yellow energy potion I’d no doubt be feeling later. I only remembered at the last moment that my literally sizzling fingers would likely destroy the glass.

Oh, screw this.

I thrust my prosthetic hand into the waist-high pool of blood I sat in, and watched as the liquid started to boil around it. I ignored the heat that transferred all the way to my left side. It wasn’t much anyway.

Turns out, blood was a surprisingly good insulator. Who knew?

It took nearly five minutes of quenching before the bubbling died down. I’m not sure what I expected when I raised my arm from the pool, but it certainly wasn’t what I saw.

The formerly white metal of my hand, streaked through with rainbow hints, had changed. It was now a blood red in color. There were no more scintillant hues to be found in my hand, all other shades but the red of it having been stripped away.

Actually, wait.

The rest of the arm was different, too. It was still primarily the white of Primordium, but in the past, the rainbow hues had shown when light reflected off of it.

Those were gone now, as well. Instead, it only reflected the red of the blood it had been quenched in. Honestly, it…the result was that I almost appeared to have veins of fresh blood running up and down the length of my false limb.

What did that mean?

Brow furrowed, I idly wiggled my fingers and found that the prosthetic still functioned perfectly fine. I hadn’t lost any range of movement; I still had full sensation within the limb, and there didn’t appear to be any lasting damage done to the structure of the metal.

Good thing, too, because I wouldn’t know the first thing about how to repair this thing.

I didn’t have any more time to goggle at my now red hand before a new one suddenly thrust itself into my field of view. It was familiar, though, in a shade of grey I had stood shoulder to shoulder with only minutes ago. Only, it had been in far rougher shape then.

Now, the palms were simply covered in burn scars.

I followed the hand up to find Meia staring down at me with an unreadable look on her broad features.

Wordlessly, I took the hand and leveraged myself up to my feet. Once there, the two of us merely stared into each other’s eyes for a moment and didn’t release our grip on each other. Instead, we changed the grip from a near handshake…

Into that of a warrior’s clasp.

I hope she could see the new respect I had for her in my gaze, the same way I could see it in hers.

Meia Itzelan nodded at me, and I returned it.
