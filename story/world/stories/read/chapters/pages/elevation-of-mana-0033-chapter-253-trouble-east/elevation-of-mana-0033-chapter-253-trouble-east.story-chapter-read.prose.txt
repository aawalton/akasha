Years had passed since I'd come to this world, nineteen of them in total. While this city had grown to an incredible amount over my time here, it still felt so new..

“Hey Op,” A friend, Iru, said as I passed, joining me as I began to head towards the city outskirts. “Going to see the farms again?” he asked.

“Headed towards them yeah, lot of people getting hurt during the harvest, going to see what we can do about fixing them up, care to join?”“Well I'll head out there while we go, but I'm really much more for ripening the plants. You know I never really could get the deeper parts of healing people down,” Iru mused.

“Because you don't like people all that much.”“True, true, plants don't lie and deceive.”

“Nor do they make truth or love,” I countered. “Plants may well be beautiful, but they don't know nobility or justice.”“Going to start writing me poems or songs?” he joked, sending a small punch my way, that I dodged with ease.

We walked together out the gates and into the fields beyond, and my how they'd changed. All around there were rows upon rows of different things, tubers, grasses, even a few orchards here and there. Some of the plants were clearly analogues of those I was familiar with if slightly different. The wheat, or whatever grain it was, was smaller as far as I could tell, harder, not as rich or good. The tubers looked almost like potatoes and carrots, but they too were small, fibrous, and more varied than wha tI was more used to seeing.

Ongoing were the projects to improve the crops and their yields. Each harvest was examined, the best of the fruits kept for seed or planting, the worst eaten or given to livestock. Livestock too were undergoing a similar process, with a few species that had been captured being brought in and bred into something more useful to people. Mostly there were boar and goats, but there were rumors of the other cities having different livestock too.

The grass crunched under our feet as we strolled past fences and pens made from small green branches, and towards the fields.

“Still hate that people have to live out here,” Iru said as we neared one of the little villages about half an hour outside the city.

“Well, they don't have to, they choose to. Imagine if they had to walk into the city every day, what would happen then? An extra long walk, carrying their tools or risking them getting stolen while they're away? This is their choice, we should respect that choice.”“Well yes, but it still isn't great for them.”I simply shrugged, what could I do? There were no easy answers to some problems, and this was one of them. It meant that these folks were away from a lot of the city services, but too they had more freedom.

With care we approached the village that was first on our trek, one we'd spend the better part of the day working on. I furrowed my eyes as we approached, there were a lot more people here than I would have expected, some that seemed to be from outside, based on their clothing and method of presentation.

“What's going on?” I asked as we approached.

“Good shaman, the people here have told us of your kindness. We have many injured,” a man who seemed to be a leader said as he stepped forward, thick hides covering his body. He was quite formal, almost begging, which for a lot of tribes dealing with someone who did magic was something that happened. “Several tribes from the east have moved out from the mountains there and are pressuring those of us who live further west.”“Those things happen yes, did something go wrong with their hunting?” I asked, it was the most common reason for such disputes.

“I'm unclear on their reasons, but it seems some beasts have been attacking them. They tried to describe them to be, but I was unfamiliar with what they spoke,” he said, head bowed.

“I see, we'll help your people for that is our way, but our leader may wish to talk with you soon. If there are some monsters to the east we should know of this, and hear of those they displace.”“Yes, not like we're just going to let you die,” Iru said with a smile, puffing out his chest.
