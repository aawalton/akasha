The crowd had already grown by the time Lira caught up to it.

She kept the burgundy curtain draped low over her hair as she slipped between two washerwomen and a few teens who had been scavenging in the junkyard, all of them watching the same narrow house.

"So he really did come back," said a middle-aged woman.

"I knew he would," added a younger girl around Lira's age. "Prince Aric is the best."

"He didn't look well after healing the boy yesterday," said an older man with a concerned voice.

"That's because he healed two of them," nodded a second man. "A boy and a girl."

Lira settled into a gap near the back of the crowd, close enough to see, but far enough to disappear, and kept listening as the people around her painted a picture of what had happened over the past few days.

Apparently, Henry showed up first, found the children, failed to help them, and came back with Aric.

Lira remembered Pallan's rebuke about letting kids die, and Henry must have taken it to heart. He had taken it more to heart, in fact, than Lira, who had yet to fully consider what she was going to do with her newly created healing spells.

Her thoughts were interrupted by a commotion near the front of the house. An older man in commoner's clothes, a general laborer, perhaps, from his build and the way he carried himself, came out first, carrying something that took Lira a moment to recognize.

A child, nine years old maybe, probably the laborer's son, looked like he weighed nothing. The father had one arm hooked beneath his knees, the other braced across his back, and the boy's arms had looped weakly around his neck to hold on.

The boy's legs hung wrongly over the father's arm, the calves visibly thinner than normal.

The crowd went silent as Aric followed behind them, wearing a butcher's apron for some reason, while Deren walked beside him holding a bag of roasted meat. Henry trailed them both, hand on his sword, eyes already moving across the gathered crowd.

"Not there," Aric said as the father stopped three steps past the doorway. "A little further away. My magic won't work here."

The man looked at him, confused.

"Trust me," Aric said, gentler this time. "Just a little further."

Lira was also confused.

Why can't he cast spells here?

"Pika. Maja. Syneth."

Her eyes slipped into Mage Sight, and the world layered over itself in threads of drifting currents. There, hanging directly over the house's front step, was a patch of mana disturbance, thick and slow-moving, like oil that refused to mix into water.

Lira had seen disturbance before; her dust-gathering enchantment wasn't clean, but it was never this dense. She had read that higher-tier spells could cause a disturbance large enough to block magic casting, but she had never seen one before, since she had limited herself to tier-one spells.

The father carried his son another dozen paces, until they were completely outside the cloud of disturbance and surrounded by stable mana.

"Here is good enough." Aric held up a hand.

Lira froze. This put them too close to her for comfort, and she realized that choosing a spot away from the door had backfired.

Aric set one hand against the boy's forehead.

"Eldra. Yorad. Cipos. Kalaba. Velar. Moren. Kaldris."

Under Mage Sight, she watched the mana move. For half a second, the natural current of ambient mana shifted; it flowed inward toward the prince from every direction, drawn in like water circling a drain, gathering and compressing inside him, making him glow brightly to her magically enhanced eyes.

The dense mana light moved from Aric's navel through his arm to the boy's body, and finally exploded outward as a wave of disturbance that washed over the area, turning the world around Lira into a glowing cloud.

For half a second, the space immediately next to Lira remained clean, before it too was overwhelmed. A heartbeat later, her own Mage Sight guttered out entirely, unable to be sustained without clean ambient mana, leaving her with a minor headache.

She had to take a second to analyze what had just happened. The bookmark tucked into her satchel, carrying both Mana Obfuscation and Mana Stabilization, tried to fight the wave of disturbance before thoroughly losing the battle. Her Mage Sight lasted longer because, unlike enchantments, it drew on both her internal reserve and ambient mana.

A gasp from the crowd returned her focus to the unfolding scene. She saw with her mundane eyes the muscle on the boy's leg being restored, filling in from nothing, the wasted calf thickening, the turned-in ankle straightening degree by degree until it simply sat where it was supposed to be.

The boy made a sound between a laugh and a cry as he wiggled his toes, and his father's arms nearly buckled from the sudden added weight before he caught himself and dragged the boy tighter against his chest.

Lira analyzed the spell Aric had cast.

Eldra was the only word she hadn't pinned down yet. She had seen it before, twice, in Sustained Flame and Fireball, always sitting beside Fila. Maybe something related to fuel or intensity.

Yorad. Cipos. Kalaba. Body, Amplification, Life. It was her own General Healing spell, originally copied from Deren's, and it had become the root of the healing spells she had developed afterward. It made sense that an advanced tier-two healing spell would contain the same combination.

Velar. Clarity. She had added that to half her own spells for the same reason it was added here, to keep the magic from doing more than it was told.

And then, the two words she would never have thought to add.

Moren. Kaldris. Past. Retrieval.

She understood immediately what they were doing there, and understood, just as immediately, why she had never thought to add them herself.

Moren and Kaldris together meant using the past as a template, reaching backward to whatever the body had been before and pulling that shape forward into the present. It was elegant, and it solved every problem her Controlled Healing spell idea had stalled on; she wouldn't need to worry about misremembering the shape of a bone or the angle of a joint. The caster didn't need to know anatomy, or anything about healing for that matter. The spell only needed a healthier version of the target to have once existed, and it went and got it.

Lira had spent two weeks nearly losing her mind over humor theory before she learned the body-as-fortress model, and even then she had needed to see it with her own eyes and reconcile that knowledge against every past contradiction before she could even begin creating workable healing spells. Aric's spell skipped all of that.

Lira could have created a similar spell; she already had, with the book restoration spell.

The difference was that she already knew how to restore a book. It was a task she had done hundreds of times. And even with the increased efficiency granted by the improved visualization and familiarity with the task at hand, the spell still left her light-headed and starving after mending a single damaged cover.

Efficiency aside, conjuring something out of the past instead of using what was already there always came with a great cost to the caster.

She had nearly fainted restoring a book. A book didn't need the same weight in material as a boy's muscle frame, let alone a fully grown adult.

Case in point, Lira watched Aric sway, almost falling to his knees if not for Deren's support. The knight caught him under one arm before dragging him toward a chair that had been brought out by a teenager for precisely this reason. A jug of something, water she guessed, or maybe juice, was pressed into Aric's hands, and Aric drank from it greedily.

Lira couldn't help but notice that his cheeks had gone slightly hollow and that his face was pale.

Had she created a similar spell, a tier-one spell perhaps that used even less ambient mana, she would have most definitely died casting it.

Henry's eyes swept the crowd again, slower this time, watching for threats.

Lira lowered her gaze before it could meet his and let the current of the crowd carry her one half-step further back.

She stood there a long moment, not moving, watching the boy take his first true steps in maybe a month or two, while his father and mother sobbed without any attempt to hide it.

She watched as Deren unwrapped some roasted meat he was carrying, and she finally understood the need for the apron as the normally neat prince ravenously wolfed down the food like a starving beast.

She considered casting her own General Healing spell on Aric. She had reasonable confidence it would put color back into his face faster, as the body recovered from the effects of starvation, probably by accelerating the transformation of new food into muscle and energy.

But in the end, she didn't move. Well, she couldn't have, even if she had wanted to; with the cloud of disturbance still covering them, there was no room for any spell to be cast.

Still, that wasn't really the reason she didn't move, and she knew it.

She thought about the fact that a prince had just walked into a slum and nearly killed himself to give a nine-year-old boy his legs back, and had done it yesterday too, for two other children.

She thought about her own four new spells, still barely tested but probably capable of doing a similar job at a fraction of the cost. Cure Disease would have removed the underlying cause of the illness, and Close Wounds would have helped with any internal damage. Good food, rest, and her General Healing would have accelerated the rest of the recovery. It wouldn't be as fast or as miraculous as the tier-two healing spell, but it would come at no personal risk.

She thought about whether it would be worth letting Aric learn of her discoveries, whether the risk of him knowing her secrets was worth giving him the tools he would need to help even more people. Whether a prince who could nearly kill himself for a stranger's son would react to the truth of her the way she badly, foolishly wanted him to react. Or whether he would see, underneath the librarian who found him books about a lost empire, a girl who had strangled a man in his sleep and left a forged confession beside the body.

Whether Deren, earnest Deren, who still thought she had given him the Sword Saint manual out of nothing but kindness, would ever look at her the same way again if he learned his own sister had been dangled as bait to force him into killing a man Lira needed killed.

Maybe if she had done things differently from the start. Maybe if she had made herself known, let herself be seen as useful, instead of acting alone in the shadows.

Sadly, a Lira who would have done that never existed. The girl who had walked through the castle gates six months ago had no reason to trust nobles, and every reason to hate the lot of them.

And now, watching Deren force water into Aric's shaking hands while an entire street wept and cheered around a boy who could finally stand, she understood, with a certainty that hurt more than she had expected, that the door to cooperation was probably shut by now.

It had closed a little further every single day she hadn't opened it.

It probably closed the day she killed Thadwick; she doubted it could still be open after Salvek's framing.

It definitely couldn't be open while she still planned on killing Kavard.
