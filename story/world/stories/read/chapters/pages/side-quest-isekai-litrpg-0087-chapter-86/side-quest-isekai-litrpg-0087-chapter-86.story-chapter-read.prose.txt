Null Pulse!

Moments after Logan discovered the prowler, it exploded in a gory burst. As the blackish green blast roiled in slow motion behind him, Logan stretched one arm to tuck Nugget close to him and propelled himself toward the ledge. To cover the distance, though, he had to charge Null Pulse for at least one second. In that time, the hot blast stung his back.

With half his back scoured in the plume, he finally released the skill and rocketed toward the ledge’s safety. The first few moments, with his slowed perception of time, had him traveling at the same velocity as the blast, which was effectively cooking his skin.

Acid Resistance is level 17!…Acid Resistance is level 21!

Heat Resistance is level 19!Heat Resistance is level 20!

But then he finally surged faster than the cloud of hot ichor and landed on the ledge… where he promptly smacked face first into the wall.

Pain Resistance is level 43!

His vision was blurry with the disorientation, but Nugget’s whimpering at his side slammed him back into reality. Nugget had taken more damage than him, but he was just under half health himself. He pulled two fine healing potions from his inventory. He tipped one vial down Nugget’s mouth.

Nugget’s breathing evened out, and the golden dragon nuzzled into a small ball in the crook of Logan’s arm. Nugget was healthy, but drained.

Logan swigged the other potion, and the pain of the blistering burns on his back faded away. He could feel air whipping around the rocky corners of the ledge, though, and he knew his shirt must be riddled with burn holes on the back.

Commotion sounded below as the guards approached, weapons drawn. Some shouted out orders, while others barked threats at potential intruders. Beneath their words, they all sounded scared.

One wrong glance, though, and they would see Logan on his perch. He tucked lower, hiding as best he could behind a rocky outcropping. He debated sneaking along the ramped ledge to where he was supposed to meet the team, but he hesitated. Even when he discovered that prowler, none of his quests had advanced. Had he misunderstood how the new bond worked? Had the bond even truly been successful?

Coughing came from the far end of the circular tiled platform, where Logan had just bounced from. He squinted, and then shock hit him.

Scrambling over the ledge was not a diseased prowler, but two people Logan recognized: Jorek Hane and Dahlia Krett.

Jorek was the one walking, and with Dahlia draped over his shoulder. She was either dead or unconscious. “Help,” the man said with a feeble wheeze.

The guards were stunned speechless, and Jorek hacked up phlegm twice before repeating his cry for help. He swayed as he staggered another few steps.

A guard shuffled forward, glancing every which way as if another explosion might come at any moment. Logan ducked when the guard’s line of sight skimmed his position. He waited a few seconds before peeking back up to see the guard approaching the fallen Jorek.

“Chief Inspector Hane? Is that you?”

Instead of answering, Jorek crumpled to the floor in a heap with Krett on top of him. The man appeared weak, a stark contrast to Logan’s memory, despite their limited interactions.

The guard straightened, suddenly emboldened, and shouted over his shoulder. “Quick, someone, get a healer!” He raced forward to the fallen duo.

Meanwhile, the other guards repeated the cry, passing it back through the throng of guards crowding the narrow entryway to the pedestal.

From this vantage, Logan could see that any spectators who hadn’t fled from the blast had already been dismissed by guards at the rear. Truthfully, he wasn’t sure if Jorek should be healed. For all Logan knew, he could have been the one behind the blast in the blight.

Jorek moved in a pitiful fashion, freeing his arm just enough to raise up a polished orb about the size of an egg. It glowed like a green-and-purple bruise. Jorek’s arm collapsed under the effort even of holding that up, and Logan only had enough time to see the label distortion crucible.

Quest Update! Save the Waterwoods!

You have discovered a key item related to the fall of the gnomish Waterwoods.

Logan waited for the update to say more, but nothing came. Crap.

Technically, the Waterwoods quest he received from Mariv was separate from the blight quest. But Mariv had always believed the two were somehow related. Plus, Logan had just seen a blight-infested prowler explode into enough gore to challenge a B-level horror flick.

If Logan was going to figure out what was going on, he needed to get close.

The guards were still fumbling with their cries for healers down the line, and some guards at the rear looked like they were running to find someone.

Logan would have to beat them to the punch.

He dashed down the ledge, two strides at a time, and was relieved to see Cassandra and the others waiting there.

Cassandra wrung her hands. “What in the spheres—”

“Here,” Logan said as he handed Nugget to Senna. With Logan’s own clothing in burn-riddled ruins, she was the only one with a cloak on who could smuggle Nugget to safety. “Get him back to the inn. And Cassandra, come with me.”

He grabbed Cassandra’s wrist and tugged her up the main walkway, toward the chaos.

“What? Where are we going?”

Logan shouted out to the scrambling guards, who were fanning out to find stragglers who could help them. “Healer! I have a healer!” He lowered his voice for Cassandra. “Something strange is going on, and we need to get information. It’s tied to a quest. A big one.”

The nearest guard shouted back to the main group that he found someone and flagged Logan over.

Cassandra followed. Even confused, she kept her composure. “Logan, what was that blast? Was that your doing?”

“No. It was a prowler. But now it’s not. I’ll explain it later, but be on your guard. He might be linked to the blight.”

“He who? Who’s hurt?”

They had reached the guards and were practically carried up and over the pedestal onto the circular platform as if they were crowd surfing. Logan didn’t need to answer Cassandra’s question because the guards parted, and she gasped.

“Chief Inspector Hane?” She shared a nervous look with Logan. She was probably just as curious as Logan about how such a prominent figure in the Adventurers’ Guild might be tied to the blight.

Cassandra ran a few steps ahead of Logan. The guards were so intent on peering over one another they didn’t stop Logan as he followed. Cassandra slid to her knees and her hands glowed over Krett and Jorek both. Jorek’s breathing steadied, and he let out a relieved sigh.

“Thank you.” He looked up and then frowned in confusion. “Miss Varesh? Where are we? This is not Farrowstead. Aren’t you supposed to be investigating the hearthfires there?”

Logan shared a confused look with Cassandra. It felt like it had been ages since they handled that Farrowstead quest.

Cassandra pressed the back of her hand to Jorek’s forehead as she fussed over him. “We’re in Caerwyn. What happened? Are you okay? We heard an explosion and then...” She gestured vaguely at him, a look of uncertainty on her face.

Logan, meanwhile, craned his neck to analyze the item Jorek had lifted a while ago. His current vantage point was all wrong to catch a glimpse. Nimbly, Logan tucked under the elbow of a guard, who stood on tiptoes, and then crossed behind the back of another guard for a better angle.

The chief inspector cast his eyes about, as if coming out of a daze. “Caerwyn… Indeed we are. Hm. What are you doing here, then? I thought you were on the assignment in Farrowstead.”

Cassandra told Jorek that was weeks ago.

“Weeks?” Jorek shook his head. “But that was just... What day is it?”

Behind them, the guards parted as a familiar voice called out. “Jorek? Is that you?”

It was Cassandra’s father, First Reconjurant Elias Varesh.

Jorek sobered at the sight of him. “Elias. I must speak with you immediately.” He glanced at the crowd. “Alone.”

“Of course,” said Cassandra’s father as he waved the guards away.

He moved to help Jorek lift the unconscious Dahlia Krett, which gave Logan an opening to spot the fallen item. Logan slid in between two onlookers, twisting his body to get a good glimpse. But then Krett suddenly trembled with a seizure.

“Damn,” said Jorek. He pressed his fingers to Dahlia’s temple. His palm and her forehead glowed, and the woman stilled. Fatigue overtook him, and he slumped back to the ground.

The folds of his clothing covered the mysterious item, concealing it from Logan’s eyes, and Logan sighed. He hoped everyone was okay, but he really needed to see what that thing was, and it sounded like Jorek was about to head off to a private meeting with the first reconjurant.

“I don’t know how long I’ve been keeping her stable,” said Jorek. “I thought it was just one day, but if it’s been weeks…”

Cassandra moved in. She met eyes with Logan, and he got the clue to follow along.

“No wonder you’re so weakened,” she said. “You don’t have the energy to stabilize her when you’re fighting to keep yourself standing. Let me help.”

Jorek nodded vaguely, but his attention went back to Elias. He expressed his urgency again.

With a nod, Cassandra’s father raised his voice. “Everyone, go. Except you, Cassandra. Tend to his wounds. Guards, clear the path for us.”

As the closest guard extended an arm to escort Logan away, Cassandra called out.

“Wait!” Her eyes were wide, and it was clear she was trying to piece something together in the moment. “I need him.” She pointed at Logan.

“What for?”

“For…” She glanced down at her hands. “For a bonus to my healing. He’s in my party. And we have a special bond.”

Logan and Elias both raised their brows, and the first reconjurant glanced at Logan. Cassandra, meanwhile, coughed lightly as pink tinged her cheeks. Logan pretended not to notice, but couldn’t help giving Elias a sheepish smile.

Cassandra’s healing was more effective now, but Logan knew that was because she was level 27. But her mask still made her appear lower-leveled, so the team bond was a plausible explanation. And if it bothered that scoundrel father of hers, Logan was okay with the lie.

The guard still had his hand on Logan’s shoulder, waiting for Elias’s order.

“Very well.” Then he squinted at Logan. “Oh, you. You were the one who charged the ceremonial prism, aren’t you?”

At that, Logan’s jaw dropped. The man seriously hadn’t even recognized him? He quickly recovered and just nodded.

“I see. Well, grab the prism now. We’ll need it charged for the next ceremony anyway, and you already know the process, so we might as well get that taken care of. Then follow us.”

Logan sat beside Cassandra in a room with mahogany panels and velvet-cushioned seating. She divided her attention between her two prone patients. Krett was still unconscious, but Jorek was propped up against pillows to speak with Elias.

The two men hardly noticed Logan or Cassandra, and Logan couldn’t help but feel like Elias already might not recognize him again if he were to spot Logan now. They had all just gotten situated, but the first reconjurant wasted no time.

“What’s this about, Jorek? You were supposed to report back on the blight, but we lost contact with you completely.”

“We were sabotaged,” said Jorek with a somber face. His hand rustled in his cloak as he searched for something, and Logan tried not to be obvious as he watched from his periphery.

“By whom?” asked Elias as he leaned in to view the item Jorek presented.

Name: Distortion CrucibleType: MechanismLore: Custom-built distortion item designed to skew resonances and unleash the calamity sealed away within the Ruined Wilds. Be wary of its volatile nature.

Elias picked up the crucible and turned it over in his hands, casting a glance at Krett halfway through his inspection. “This design doesn’t match the diagrams the Gnashridge branch sent us.”

“No,” said Jorek. “It doesn’t.” He then tapped a corner, where it looked like the small rod had been welded. “See how this scorched mark starts wide where the two metal pieces meet and then narrows? That only happens when metal is exposed to an enormous heat blast, but then the blast is immediately neutralized.” He leaned in. “Whoever did this needed to get close enough to a mana stabilization shield with a pressure valve strong enough to do the job.”

Logan’s mind immediately raced to the Mages’ Guild training area. He had seen that exact kind of thing happen while he was scouting with Alden.

Elias stopped handling the item and raised a brow, apparently coming to the same conclusion. “The Mages’ Guild? Did they find out about our advances in mana scar detection?” His knuckles whitened around the rod. “Is this their idea of convincing the bureau to pry another of our inventions from our hands? We are the ones who developed the first adaptable obsidian inlay prototype for the gnomish sigilry! If we lose the lead on this, guild funding will never recover!”

Logan frowned. The distortion crucible’s description sounded a lot like what the reavers had been planning. And then there was Amatra’s secret message for the Mages’ Guild, plus the hidden reaver he had detected inside the guild walls. If rival politics were a part of this, they were only the surface of something far more sinister.

Mariv had suggested Logan not reveal the Amatra quest to either guild unless he found a compelling reason to trust one or the other. While he wasn’t about to tell Jorek or Hane anything about his suspicions of the reavers, he now felt sure Amatra was working with them.

“I agree,” said Jorek. “We need to find someone in our own ranks who is still low enough they can be in both guilds. Then we need someone else who can stay outside the guild and report back to us.”

Despite the seriousness of the situation, Logan and Cassandra each let out a short laugh.

Jorek and Elias looked at them.

“So actually,” said Cassandra, “our other teammate, Alden, is already a member of the Mages’ Guild.”

“And,” Logan added, “they currently think I’m working as his scrubber.”

A smile crawled across Elias’s face. “Is that so?” He lifted the distortion crucible in his hand and waved it at Logan. “Then take your time charging that prism.”
