The outskirts near the Narak Mountains were in chaos. Not the usual kind with roars and territorial disputes. No, this was something else.

Apex predators, beasts that had terrorized these lands for generations were fleeing their hunting grounds like their fur was on fire and their insurance didn’t cover olfactory trauma.

The cause?

Arthur.

To be specific, Arthur had absolutely marinated himself in monster poop.

The breeze picked up his scent and, like a vengeful spirit, carried it downhill for a solid mile.

Even the mana-hawks abandoned their cliff nests. A tuskbeak howler dry-heaved into a bush. One poor fang-rat keeled over mid-scamper, twitching once before accepting death as the kinder option.

Arthur, crouched in the dirt and proudly reeking like fermented nightmare, grinned. “I think it’s working.”

“They fear your stank,” Drogan muttered. “You smell like a corpse that lost a bet.”

Arthur beamed. “Exactly. Not bad for Plan - Smear & Terrify.”

“This isn’t a strategy; it’s a hygiene war crime,” Drogan groaned. “You do know you’re supposed to fight monsters to level up, right? Not evacuate ecosystems.”

Arthur shrugged, still strutting like a bad cologne commercial. “Later. Step one: survive. Step two: find food. Step three: train. Right now, I’m on step one-and-a-half: don’t die hungry.”

His stomach agreed with a noise that sounded like betrayal.

“But nothing edible grows near the mountains,” he continued. “Unless I want to lick rocks or suck moss again. And honestly, I’m two bites away from losing the will to live.”

The remnant groaned like a dying whale. “You’re going to be the first Legendary Class user in history to die of poor life choices.”

Arthur smirked and pressed forward, his stench parting the wilderness like a holy curse. Hopefully, just beyond the ridge, he’d find food.

Maybe even dignity.

Though at this point, he was okay settling for something edible and mildly non-toxic.

On the outer edge of a city estate, one of many that ringed the kingdom’s third-largest city. A creaking cart rumbled to a halt just beyond the reach of the protective walls.

Its burden: a reinforced iron cage containing fifteen prisoners.

Men and women, young and old, sat pressed together in tense silence. Some wept. Some prayed. Others stared blankly ahead, hollow-eyed and too numb to care.

Among them was Lillian, a gaunt twenty-year-old with desperate eyes and a threadbare tunic. He had been caught stealing food for his wife and two-year-old daughter. No one had listened to his pleas. No one cared. Now, his only chance was survival.

The driver dismounted, unlatched the cage from the cart, turned the oxen-like beasts around, and returned through the gates without a word.

Left behind, the prisoners were alone.

Alone… and bait.

An hour passed. Then two. And finally, the monster-repelling potion on the cage bars lost its power.

A low growl rumbled across the field as two Fangmaw Direwolves emerged from the wilderness.

They were massive - easily twice Lillian’s height, all teeth and sinew and scarred hide. Their eyes gleamed with hunger. Their tongues lolled in anticipation.

Inside the cage, panic bloomed like wildfire.

“They’re coming!” someone hissed. “Run as soon as the door opens! It’s the only way!”

Lillian clutched a carved wooden bead on a fraying string around his wrist - his daughter’s old teether. He whispered her name once. Then his wife’s.

And then...

CLANG.

The door flung open.

Screams erupted as the prisoners burst free, clawing over each other in desperation. Lillian ran, legs numb, lungs burning, heart thudding like a war drum.

The city’s main gate loomed ahead. Less than a hundred yards.

He heard a man fall behind him. Heard teeth sink into flesh. He didn’t look.

He just ran.

Thirty yards.

Twenty.

Ten.

And then...

He was inside.

His foot crossed the gate threshold.

Relief flooded him, for a second.

Then pain.

A searing slash down his back. Something massive hit him from behind. His knees buckled. His vision spun.

The world went black.

---

Rowler Drelmor stood on the wall above, arms crossed, his armored cloak flapping gently in the wind. He didn’t flinch when the young man collapsed inside the gate.

His eyes were locked not on the boy. But on the beast that followed.

The Direwolf had passed the threshold.

“Monster’s in. Close the gates. Formation to kill zone. Engage.”

Steel rang out behind him as his elite squad snapped into motion. The tank dropped his shield, readying his body to intercept. The support mage cast Slow on the Direwolf and Speed Boost on the warriors.

Rowler raised one hand and uttered the incantation.

Frost Grasp.

Ice cracked over the stone as it leapt to the Direwolf’s front paw, rooting it in place. The beast roared, lashing out with murderous intent.

The warriors didn’t hesitate. They flanked fast, blades biting into the exposed neck.

Blood sprayed.

The monster bucked.

Rowler cast a second spell - Frost Muzzle. Coating the beast’s face in ice. It reared back, blinded and suffocating.

The warriors struck again.

In seconds, it fell.

Dead.

---

Rowler exhaled.

“Eight seconds,” he muttered.

His team reset their stance, bloodied but untouched. The crowd on the wall gave no cheer. This wasn’t for show.

It was practice.

The Great Hunt was coming.

And he was ready.
