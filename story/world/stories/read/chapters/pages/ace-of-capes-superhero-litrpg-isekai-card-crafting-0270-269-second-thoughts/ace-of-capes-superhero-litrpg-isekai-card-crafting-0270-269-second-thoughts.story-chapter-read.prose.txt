Stella Firebringer rolled over in her bed, sighing and staring at the ceiling.

She couldn’t sleep.

It was probably past midnight, and she had a ton of things she needed to do tomorrow. More press. More training sessions. Will probably need to avert a crisis or two.

Oh, and PTA meeting for Tamsin and Thor.

Mane had attended most of them in the past, but she thought she should attend this one, so that her younger kids would have one memory of her being there for them.

Gosh, the guilt was eating her up from so many angles today.

She inhaled, trying to breathe through it as Dr. Shaw told her to. Mane was awake, now. She could sense it, both because of his breathing pattern and also because he never could sleep well whenever she was feeling this anxious.

He was giving her space, though, to process her thoughts in silence and deal with the things that were plaguing her. She loved him for that.

“An interior decorator,” she said suddenly.

“Mmm?” he murmured, now that she'd finally opened up the conversation.

She turned to him. “I would have made a good interior decorator, don’t you think?” She gestured to the ceiling. "I'm the one who thought of putting that pattern up there. I helped decorate this place so it wouldn't feel like a mausoleum.” It had been her style that had turned this manor into something livable, with actual couches that people could sit on comfortably, and not the cold, decorative ones she had back in her parents’ home.

“You would be a great interior decorator,” Mane admitted.

"Or a personal trainer?" She liked to imagine herself living those other lives sometimes. Nice, normal, uncomplicated lives. "I could manage a gym."

"I can see that," Mane responded.

"Or a sports coach? Maybe owning a sports team?"

"Might have helped get the Blackhawks out of the ditch they're in."

She winked at him. "Or an assistant chef."

"Absolutely not," Mane said instantly. "Let's not get crazy now."

That made her chuckle, and he smiled.

“What if I quit?”

Mane's smile froze. He seemed to hold his breath. He’d never heard her say this before, not even during the worst of times. When she’d been having panic attacks nearly daily, when exhaustion hollowed her out, she hadn’t even entertained the idea of leaving a life of heroism behind.

But Stella felt she'd gotten to the point where…something just had to give. She just couldn’t do this anymore.

“Are you thinking about it?” He kept his voice neutral, but she knew there was some hope in it.

Mane had never asked her to quit her job.

Not at the beginning of their relationship, when he saw how much of a toll it took on them.

Not with how many date nights she'd missed, and not when the burden of childcare had fallen on him.

He'd never complained or put pressure on her.

He’d only tentatively asked her on several occasions why she wanted to be a Hero in the first place, and even though all her reasons were weak, he’d let it be, patiently waiting for her to see it on her own.

“I mean, realistically, what's the worst thing that could happen if I simply decide not to be a Hero anymore?" she asked, spreading her fingers over the soft downy blanket.

“Your Grandmother would be pissed,” Mane said, slowly. “She’d call at least ten family meetings and rant for hours. She’d invoke the memory of your dead parents, and she’d lay the blame, until you want to scream."

Stella swallowed, clutching the blanket. “I think I can take that.” Her guilt from her parents' deaths was certainly an incentive, but there was an even stronger force battling it.

She didn’t want her children to live the same life she did.

Though she’d given them a choice, both Theo and Torin had followed in her footsteps and become heroes because of her. Because they believed the Firebringer oath her grandmother loved to recite—that their family’s duty was to serve, to be an example of heroism.

But there were other ways to serve.

Other ways to be good.

Her kids needed to know that.

She needed to model that for them.

Watching how much of a toll it took on Torin and Theo, how often their lives were in danger…maybe Thor and Tamsin deserved to see her take a different path, so that they could decide for themselves whether or not they wanted to take on the obligation.

And so that Torin and Theo could see that it was okay to change their mind.

“The Association would crumble without you.” Mane followed up with the second reason.

Steall snorted. “It’s crumbling anyway.” That much was clear to see.

There was constant infighting between the non-Hero and Hero factions, both sides in an obvious bid for power.

There would probably soon be fighting amongst the Elementals, too.

With Vacek gone, both Wavelost and Shadowsbane wanted to take the reins, and while those two were close enough to each other to come to an agreement, Groundsmage also wanted to be in charge and wanted Stella's backing.

She couldn't give it to him.

While she liked Thor Groundsmage a lot and was grateful for him having saved her life (so much that she named her son after him), she truly didn’t think he was right for leadership.

Neither was she.

“You won’t be able to save lives anymore if you quit," Mane said. “People will die, and it will be all your fault.”

Stella bit her lip.

She knew Mane wasn't saying it to be cruel. This was a conversation that they’d had before, many times, but typically it was Stella giving those answers after Mane asked her what the worst thing that could happen was.

This mental exercise was a role reversal, and now she was seeing how flimsy her previous answers were, how none of them actually reflected what she wanted.

Did she ever even know what she wanted?

She was in this so deep that she couldn't remember a time she wasn't a hero, or training to be one. She didn't know her 'why', and before all that, she didn't know who she was.

She'd been too young to remember.

Why did she want to be a Hero? To keep people safe? Could she honestly tell herself that she was doing that right now?

Lexie’s words had been echoing in the back of her mind all day, and Stella had felt the shame clinging to her.

Lexie was right. Stella wasn’t being a hero. She wasn't making any of the hard decisions.

She was letting others make decisions for her and following orders.

She was, at best, an effective soldier.

What had happened to the Queen of Dragons? She used to be so brave. She used to do interviews and say whatever she wanted. She used to occasionally defy orders and do what was right.

But they’d worn her down. These past two years had worn her down, and she was just tired of everything and ready for it all to be over, whatever that meant.

All that to say, she wasn’t in the right mind space to save anyone, maybe even to save herself.

Mane put a hand on her belly, letting her know he was still there. She turned to him, gazing into his eyes, the moon falling over his expression.

"Meeting you in that bar was the best thing that ever happened to me,” she told him.

He grinned, his eyes twinkling with the memory. “Because of my pretty hair?”

"Yes, but your personality isn't half bad either."

She recalled going to his first bar, where he'd worked as a cook and occasional bartender. It was her first time at a bar, and she'd gone with Lara, who had pointed out the guy with the pretty hair.

Lara…

Maybe that was when this all started.

Where she'd lost her way and her sense of direction. Her sense of what she wanted.

Lara had always been the one to make her pay more attention to her needs and do what she wanted rather than what her parents' voice in her head told her to do.

She'd always encouraged Stella to think for herself, to break the rules.

With her death, Stella had buried the defiant part of her, so she didn’t even know that woman anymore.

“Whatever you choose,” Mane spoke up. “We’ll ride it together. Like we always have.”

Stella smiled. "Like we always have.”

Then her system started ringing.

It was her brother. Luther was in District 2 or 3, dealing with the situation over there.

She thought he would hate it. It was extremely rural, mostly tropical, and didn't have the city bustle that he typically liked.

For some reason, he didn’t seem to mind the assignment much. Stella had thought that he would be calling every five minutes to complain to her, but so far, that hadn't happened.

Maybe because Districts 8 and 9 were in such chaos, he was happy for the break.

Although Stella suspected there was more to it.

Whenever they talked, Luther sounded more and more….relaxed? Was that the word? Or maybe it was just that he sounded less tense than normal, which was strange considering the crisis they were all in.

Stella vaguely thought he'd finally found some kind of spiritual ascension over there.

Mane thought maybe Luther just met someone he liked.

Stella thought about that as she answered and said, “Hey. Is your new girlfriend–or boyfriend–happy about you calling someone this late?”

“Grandmother’s dead.”

She stopped smiling. She sat up in bed. “What?”

“Just got news, like two seconds ago.”

“Why wasn't I called?”

“I don’t know. I guess Philomena assumed you’d still be busy in the Shatters and hard to reach.” Philomena was their mother’s governess and their grandmother’s best friend. Stella didn’t know what to say.

“Stella? Are you there?”

"Uh huh." She choked out. Once again, she felt the noose tightening around her neck.

Lexie found the Alchemist in a similar state to Vulcan.

Hung up in nothingness, powerless, and with his eyes closed.

The space looked similar as well, except she felt more features were attached to this dimension. It restricted her ability more, probably as a side effect of dulling his Alchemy, so he would be less likely to escape.

Lexie was sure Pvilycht could figure out how to take him out of here with enough time.

Before she could ask him to, the Alchemist's head snapped up.

Though frail and haggard, when the old man looked at her face, there was no trace of fear or madness. He didn’t seem to be on edge as Vulcan was.

He looked sane, controlled, almost like he was enjoying a nice, relaxing vacation and not trapped in a pocket dimension.

He smiled. “I take that blood on your face to mean that my associate’s dead.”

Lexie wiped some of the blood off her cheek. “Yes. You’re about to join him.”

“I don’t think you're going to do that.”

“Funny, he told me the same thing. Yet I did it anyway.” Part of Lexie still couldn’t believe the Vulcan was dead. That she'd killed him.

Not to say she wasn't happy about it.

The Eldritch enjoyed the action, the victory.

But the human half still struggled, particularly with what he’d said about her mother.

Was it true? It wouldn't have made a difference if it were, but...was it?

She shoved the thought to the back of her mind to deal with later.

“I thought about keeping you alive, though,” she said. “Because I think we might have similar goals, but I just don’t like your methods."

“Do you not?” he asked. “Even after everything, do you not understand why I had to go to the lengths I did?”

Lexie pressed her lips together. Now that she could see the larger picture and knew what the Fae intended to do with the galaxy, she couldn't fault him entirely, especially since she'd considered doing much of the same things he'd done.

But she couldn't forgive what he'd done to Tate. For that alone, she could not see them being allies.

“I am told you’re not from this world," the Alchemist spoke up while she was thinking.

“Yes.” She wasn’t shocked that Vulcan had told him. “Isn’t knowing that information supposed to kill you? Well, I guess it makes sense because here we are.”

“Your father knows too, and he’s not dead. For the same reason, you’re not going to kill me.”

“Oh yeah, and why is that?”

“Are you familiar with soul contracts, Lexie?” he chuckled. “Well, of course you are.”

“What about them?”

“Your father and I signed one,” he said. “And it stipulated a clause for mutually assured destruction. Meaning that if you, or anyone associated with him, including his friends at the association, kill me, then he'll die too.”

Lexie raised an eyebrow. “Do you really expect me to believe that?”

“It’s the truth. I can show you the contract. Well, maybe not in here, but if you take me to wherever you killed my friend, then I can show you. Or you could simply ask your father.”

The problem was that Lexie’s father wasn't on Earth and could not be contacted to be asked.

"I'm sure he's witnessing everything that's happening now, even if he can't control it," The Alchemist said. "It's his dimension after all. His miniature world. We're just existing in it."

He laughed to himself like he'd made a joke, and that led to a coughing fit.

“Why would he sign a contract with you?" Lexie asked.

“Because he loves you." His voice was strained as he hacked. "And wants to save your life. Now would you repay him by killing me, and him by extension?"

"I don't believe you."

The Alchemist coughed one final time before saying, "Are you willing to bet your father's life on it?" He turned his head. "Look around. Or rather, feel around. Those extras you sense aren't just there to keep me from escaping. They're there to protect me, too."

Lexie bit her lip. She was wavering. That wasn't good.

“Vulcan tried to sell me the same line about him knowing where my mother was."

“I don’t know what contracts he has with your mother,” Alchemist responded. "As I said, I’m very happy to show you my contract. Although there might be things in there that he’d rather you not see."

"What do you mean?"

"Well," he said. "There was a part of our deal regarding Vacek, and that is likely why he was the only one Aiden was unable to rescue from my trap."

Lexie's breath caught.

"Come now. You didn't think that was a mere coincidence, did you? You should know your father better than that."

Lexie bit her lip. Yes, somewhere deep down, she'd suspected that her father had done something to encourage Vacek's demise, or at the very least, he had not saved him.

But hearing confirmation....

It didn't matter. She knew he had his reasons. She even understood some of them. In a way, they were on the same path...

Doing just about anything, no matter how heinous, to protect each other.

But somehow, Lexie could still not believe her father killed Vacek in cold blood.

It didn't sound like him.

"Ask him about it," The Alchemist said. "Or better yet, ask your disciple about it. He was there and saw the whole thing unfold. He might be able to better tell you."

That's right. Pvilycht was there. He would know.

Lexie was frozen in indecision. Leaving right now felt like such a waste of time and effort, but if what he was saying was true and she inadvertently hurt Aiden with her actions, she wouldn’t be able to forgive herself.

She was already plagued by what Vulcan had said about her mother, and she was hoping that it was a lie.

She stared at the Alchemist for a little longer.

She acknowledged that a part of her hesitation was because, on a deeper level, she wasn't as eager to kill him as she'd been to kill Vulcan. Her grudge against Vulcan was much more personal and intense. With the Alchemist, it was mostly just a necessity.

She needed to think about this.

"I'll be back," she told him as she was about to leave.

"I'm sure you will," he responded. “I’m so happy to have met you, Lexie Sparrowfoot. I have a feeling we will do great things together.”

Lexie didn't say anything as she left.

She didn't talk to Pvilycht once out of the pocket dimension. He'd been waiting at the entrance of the portal for her, and she could have asked him the second she emerged, but she didn't.

Lexie went home first. She knew she should immediately confront Pvilycht about the information, but she wanted to kill two birds with one stone and get Yasycht's demand out of the way, in case she needed to come back to the pocket dimension.

It was morning, Uncle Max was just getting out of bed when Lexie met him at his bedroom door.

Max jerked back and peered. "Lexie, what the hell?"

"Sorry," she said. "I just wanted to know if you still have your dungeon detector?"

"Um..." He yawned and still seemed groggy as he pointed in the general direction of his backpack that was on his...well, Aiden's floor. Aiden's room was usually so neat, but even with the door cracked open, Lexie could see clothes, weapons, and scraps of metal strewn about. He'd certainly made himself comfortable, real quick.

"It's not a hundred percent accurate," Max warned. "Lots of false negatives."

"That's okay. I just need to be in the general vicinity. And you have a map of where the dungeons regularly appear, right?"

"Why do you ask?" he eyed her suspiciously.

"No reason." She thought she sounded pretty innocent, but he didn't buy it.

"If I refuse to give it to you, you're just going to steal it and go off on your own anyway, aren't you?"

"Probably," she admitted. "I learned that from you."

He mumbled something else about never having kids and simply walked down to make coffee.

Nevertheless, Lexie found both the map and the dungeon detector, a tool that looked kind of like a calculator, in his backpack.

Then she packed a few things for her trip and teleported with her orb.

She knew that the Heroes would eventually find out what she'd done to Vulcan and all hell would break loose. She was ready for the backlash, but she needed to fulfil Yasycht's deal before that, because she needed him at full capacity, and their relationship to be solid, in case the Fae got involved.

Lexie also called Doyle and told him to meet her later today as she teleported to the first dungeon.

When she got to the first dungeon, a level 4, she summoned Pvilycht, and he showed up with his book once more.

"Okay," she told him. "As we figure out how to get into this heart, tell me. What happened in that pocket dimension between Dad and Vacek?"

Aiden knew what Lexie had done.

He could feel her do it, but he’d been powerless to stop her. He could also hear the conversation she'd had with the Alchemist.

It was time for him to tell her everything.

He sighed. It was a good thing, maybe. He couldn't keep the secret forever, and they'd promised a long time ago to be more honest with each other.

Besides, more than anyone, Lexie would understand why he did what he did.

Although he was still scared that a part of her would see him as a monster.

Aiden glanced at the Fae crowding around the ship and keeping watch for attacking orcs. It was just about fixed, and they would be leaving soon. Which meant that he would be on Earth soon.

Good.

Because what Lexie had committed was a very serious crime, and though he would try to shield her from it, shit would hit the fan very soon.
