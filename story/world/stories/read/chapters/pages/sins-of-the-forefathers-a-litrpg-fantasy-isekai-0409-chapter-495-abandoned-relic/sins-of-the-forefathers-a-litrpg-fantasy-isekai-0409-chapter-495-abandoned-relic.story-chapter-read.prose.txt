I’m not ashamed to say I gaped just a bit at the small, brass snake no bigger than one of my arms. “But…” I whispered helplessly under my breath. “I thought…you were…”

“You thought I was what, exactly?” Nehushtan tilted his tiny, triangular head at me. The light from the throne his resting place was thrust into reflected off of his eyes, and I could see that they weren’t just amber colored. They were actual amber gems, in another reflection of how he almost reminded me of the Sculpted. “It’s amusing that you even have thoughts, failure. I thought your kind were little more than-”

Whatever entirely undeserved insult the cantankerous old…Spirit? Had been about to deliver was cut off, as his eyes tracked over my shoulder and lit upon something. His head then snapped over to stare in such a readable expression of incredulity that I was a bit impressed at the craftsmanship of his form.

I followed his gaze to see that it had lit upon the approaching form of Dain, followed by the majority of my captains. The rest of my forces seemed to be either inspecting the tapestries or holding a defensive position back near the door.

At the very least, those who were gawking at the bloody banners were doing so in groups.

“What is he doing here?!” Nehushtan literally hissed in outrage. His eyes snapped back over to me, and it was easy to read the incandescent rage within them. “Are you a fool as well as a failure?! Why haven’t you killed him yet?! Surely you must have an entirely sufficient understanding of these degenerates at this point! What could possibly possess you to be so casually strolling with this creature?!”

“Nate, is this…?” Bleddyn murmured into my ear as he came to stand at my shoulder. He crossed his arms as he studied the tiny form of Nehushtan, with an entirely unimpressed look on his face. He wasn’t the only one, of course.

Still, there was something I found curious. Rafael glided into the room, still as silent as he’d been since we’d stood together and gazed upon the Vereden. I hadn’t actually thought he was following us, as absorbed in his thoughts as he’d been. The Lich only gave the throne and its adjacent occupant an uninterested glance, before curiously enough…

His eyes latched onto the second vault door in the room with a startling intensity.

Meanwhile, Dain had fixed Nehushtan with a long-suffering, put-upon expression. “Great Guardian, I see your temperament hasn’t improved in my absence. How are you otherwise, this day?”

Nehushtan bared small, needle-like bronze fangs at the Korvokhan in response. “You know how I am, demon. My answer to you and your despicable kind has not changed in centuries, and it never will. Agony. I am in agony, and it is still worth it.”

Dain sighed and gestured with one hand to the defiant serpent. “You can see why I’m so happy to meet you, Nathaniel.” He shook his head in resignation. “I suspect you’ll have far better luck in getting this one to cooperate.”

I only spared the Korvok a brief, disinterested glance before I devoted it back to one of the people I had come here in the first place to rescue. “Nehushtan, what is going on here? I thought for sure that the Calamity up above was you. I…I even saw it look at me, and I swear that those were your eyes.”

A measure of Nehushtan’s anger and indignation melted away. Only a bit, though. He spared only one more venomous glance at the Korvokhan before dismissing him as I had. “That is me,” He said wearily. “One of my bodies, at least. But it would be more accurate to call it a war form. A projected body that I am meant to create in times of crisis, to better protect that which I am charged with. Only, these animals captured that body, and have spent centuries bleeding it for their own vile purposes.”

“That which you’re charged with?” Sylvia murmured under her breath. Her eyes widened, and her gaze darted off to the right-hand wall where Rafael floated silently, staring at the vault door. “The ship…?”

Meanwhile, Renauld pushed forward out of the group of my captains with a wide-eyed expression of realization on his furry face. “Nate, isn’t that just like-”

“Tatsugan…” I breathed. I paid no mind to how the Gnoll deflated, as I understood at the same time. “You’re exactly like Tatsugan.”

Tatsugan. The dragon-like Calamity that had haunted the mountain of Gorenzan for millennia. The dragon that had turned out to be anything but. Its real form had been a small, arm-length, almost snake-like wyrm that had dwelled within the star-heart of the Aetheric Fusion Reactor, within the Netherim bunker. People had wondered for countless years how it had returned to life as often as it did. Only to turn out that the dreaded dragon Tatsugan was nothing more than a projection.

Just like Nehushtan and the enormous snake Calamity impaled upon the Heartspire.

“Tatsugan…” Nehushtan tilted his head in thought. “Ah. Yes. That thing. A pale imitation of myself, crafted by that traitorous, insipid little wretch Lucretia. I believe she took up the name Dihexia in the aftermath of their little tantrum. She was always fascinated by that capability of mine, in the old days. A pity she wasn’t more interested in more moral applications of her great might.”

‘Little tantrum’. It wasn’t hard to infer what he was talking about there, but…

I feel like it was just a little bit of an understatement to refer to the War in Heaven as a tantrum of all things.

One problem, though.

“If they,” I jerked a thumb over my shoulder at the Korvok I could feel lingering not far behind me. “Are holding your ‘war form’ captive, why have you just dispelled it? Before I killed him, Tatsugan had full control over his projected body. If he was just a poor copy of you, why not do something about it?”

Slowly, Nehushtan shook his head in the negative. “I wish I could,” He said, old weariness in his voice. “The ritual they’ve impaled me upon was designed to prevent such a thing. After all, how would they drain me of my blood if I could simply deny them access to that form?”

I see. I was starting to get a better understanding of the whole situation now, and I liked it less and less. However, there were still things I needed to know, if I was going to untangle this knot.

Time to be direct.

I turned in place, locking eyes with a patiently waiting Dain. As always, he smiled at my regard, but I ignored that. “Dain, no. Ul-Korveth, Korvokhan of Clan Korvok…what is it you’re after?” I took a single step forward, bringing me closer to him. “Why did you bring me here? Why is it you’re so happy at my presence? What do you want from me?”

The words hung heavy in the chamber for a moment, and I saw many of the milling soldiers straighten up from their idle examinations. In the meantime, Dain himself tilted his head as he regarded me thoughtfully.

The smile on his lips changed, then. A hunger crawled into his expression that was far more familiar to me, after all of the time we’d spent in this hellhole named Karag Korvok.

For a moment, he looked decidedly like one of the Skarned.

“I want what every Korvokhan has wanted before me. No,” He shook his head slowly. “I want what every true son of Clan Korvok has wanted.”

Matching me, Ul-Korveth took a step forward, putting him barely a handsbreadth away from me. We were close enough now that I could feel his breath on my face. Oddly, I found the smell of it disturbing. Not because it smelled like the bloody, cannibalistic meat they liked to indulge in.

No, instead, it was almost floral.

“I. Want. That. Ship.”

I let a calm, even smile appear on my face, as several uncertainties were suddenly clarified. “What a coincidence. So. Do. I.”

To my surprise, that seemed to be exactly what Ul-Korveth had wanted to hear. The feral edge that had crept into his countenance eased, and he stepped away, straightening his pure white robes as he did. “Delightful. After all, with your help, we can both get what we want, with no further need for violence. Tell me, Nathaniel,” He abruptly switched verbal tracks. “Do you know why we’ve been draining Great Guardian Nehushtan for a very, very long time?”

My eyes narrowed. “I suspect because it’s a powerful reagent, and you use it to empower yourselves. Everything about Korvokian society seems to revolve around power inherent in blood, flesh, and the exposing of it.”

To my surprise, Ul-Korveth snorted. “A layman’s understanding of the Red Truth, but it’s understandable considering your lack of indoctrination into the faith. No, actually. You are correct that the Guardian’s blood is a powerful reagent, and it is central to many secondary rituals. But that was it. The bloodletting of the war form is a secondary concern. The primary function of the ritual you and yours nearly,” He stressed. “Nearly wrecked, is to weaken the true self. There is a very strong connection between the pitiful flesh we pinned to the roof of our mountain and the command stave that Nehushtan resides within. With every drop drained from him, his will and ability to resist grow weaker. It’s merely been a very…long process.”

“Command stave?” Sylvia spoke up, coming to stand at my side against the Korvokhan. He didn’t seem bothered in the slightest. Ul-Korveth merely smiled at her as well.

“Of course. You…all of you, let me take a wild guess,” He chuckled lowly. “The Great Guardian contacted you somehow, perhaps bouncing a message across the blockaded Concord to reach the Precursor. And that’s how you came to be here, to come to his aid. Am I correct?”

“Just about,” Renauld said sarcastically, joining Sylvia at her side in staring the Korvokhan down.

He merely nodded in response, as if the answer was a foregone conclusion. “Just as designed, then. The Guardian’s leash was always just the slightest bit loose. Enough so that he could just barely send a pleading message of help to the world, across the realm of Spirits, as if he were actually one of them. But there was only ever one kind of person he would reach for.”

“Lemme guess,” Bleddyn grunted, planting himself at my right side. “Precursors.”

“Just so,” Ul-Korveth inclined his head. “With the absence of both the gods and any of the Netherim, there was only ever one choice that was pure enough to suit his programming. A Precursor. There are so few of them that arrive on Vereden these days that we’re unsure if he’s ever contacted any of the previous ones. He won’t answer that question for us, of course. But if you asked, Nathaniel, he is bound to answer.”

I regarded the Korvok for a moment quietly, before shifting my gaze over to the serpent in question. “Nehushtan?”

The brass snake almost looked like he was grinding his fangs together for a moment, as I felt Fade stalk over to sit at my feet. “Yes, I have. But none have ever been as receptive, or perhaps placed themselves in as deep peril as you, to where I’ve spoken to you multiple times. None before have answered the call.”

“Because his bindings prevented him from giving an actual reason,” Ul-Korveth laughed mockingly, visibly enjoying the misery of the serpent. “I’m sure it was sufficiently vague that you disregarded him previously. But now…now, the very world around us is at risk of death, and he promised you a weapon. And it is a weapon, of course.”

“That ship is a weapon?” Bella slinked up to stand by Bleddyn, hooking her thumbs in the leather belt that held her cutlass.

“Oh, most assuredly so,” Ul-Korveth waved a hand almost dismissively. “But of course, while I’m sure that’s why you’re interested in it, it’s not why we are. It’s a plus, to be sure…but the Korvoks desire control of the Vereden for an entirely different reason.”

He leaned forward, a true glint of avarice appearing in his emerald eyes.

“We want off of this abandoned relic of a planet.”
