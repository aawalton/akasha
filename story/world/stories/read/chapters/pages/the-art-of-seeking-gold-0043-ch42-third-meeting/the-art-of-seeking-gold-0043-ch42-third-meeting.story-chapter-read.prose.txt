The village passed by in fragments. Amy's eyes tracked each detail from her position on Mira's back, comparing them against memories. It was all just so... perfect.

"You know," Mira huffed between breaths as she jogged, "you really need to eat more. You are so light! My mom says you city girls are always worried about weight," Mira continued, adjusting her grip on Amy's legs and turning her head to see her. "But honestly, Amy, you really do look pale. Are you feeling okay? You're not sick, are you?"

Amy's jaw tightened, and her eyes wandered away from the girl. "I'm fine," she managed to whisper.

"That's what you always say," Mira said with a small laugh. "But I'm serious! Did you have breakfast? I've got another rice ball if you're hungry. Pickled plum, is that okay? It's in my—"

Mira nearly stopped jogging.

"Oh, no. No, please tell me I didn't..."

"What?"

"My bag! Amy, I left my bag at home! All my homework, my textbooks, my lunch—everything!"

"..." Amy stared at Mira in silence, observing the girl's panic slowly escalating.

"Oh god, this is the worst day ever." Mira's pace had picked up again, but now jogging faster than before. "First, I oversleep, then I'm late, and now I don't even have my bag. And it's too late to go back and get it. Ughhhh! Mom specifically reminded me to check before I left, and I was like 'yes, mom, I will, mom,' and then I just ran out the door like an idiot and—"

Amy stopped listening.

It wasn't intentional. Mira's voice just sort of... faded into background noise, becoming part of the ambient sound of the village around them. Her gaze drifted away from the back of Mira's head and across the buildings they were passing.

Beautiful. It was all just so beautiful.

Too beautiful.

Amy's gaze lingered on the left side of the street. There was a small house with blue shutters and a garden that was supposed to be abandoned, but was now fully well-maintained.

An elderly woman knelt among the flowers. Beside her, a golden retriever sat patiently, watching its owner work.

Mrs. Henderson, a woman who spent most days either tending the plants or painting. She was supposed to be dead around seven or eight years ago. Heart attack, sudden and unexpected. Amy remembered attending the funeral and crying. She had been almost like a granny to her, and whenever her parents fought, she let her stay in her house and paint.

But here she was. Alive. Healthy. As if nothing had ever happened.

The wrongness of it settled over Amy. Her eyes lingered on the woman and her dog... until the woman looked up.

Mrs. Henderson's head turned slightly, then, out of complete chance, she saw Amy.

Amy froze as she saw the old woman smile warmly and lift a hand to give her a small wave. Her muscles tightened. Her brain short-circuited for a full second. Then, almost automatically, she raised one hand from Mira's shoulder and hesitantly returned the gesture.

Mrs. Henderson's smile widened, satisfied, and she turned back to her garden as if nothing unusual had happened.

Amy's hand fell back to Mira's shoulder. Her gaze dropped to the road, expression unreadable, but a faint crease formed between her brows.

"—and then my dad said I needed to be more responsible, but how am I supposed to remember everything when there's so much to— Ow, Amy!" Mira's voice woke Amy from her trance. "Easy there, you're gripping kinda tight," she said, glancing back over her shoulder.

Amy blinked, realizing her fingers had unconsciously clenched around Mira's shoulder. She loosened her grip, muttering a faint, "Sorry."

"It's fine. I'm sorry too. I know I ramble a lot. My mom says I could talk the paint off cars if I wanted to."

The paint comment made Amy think of Mrs. Henderson again, but she pushed the thought away.

The village center was giving way to slightly larger buildings now. They were getting close.

"Almost there!" Mira announced, her breathing heavy, but her pace not slowing. "Just around this corner!"

She wasn't wrong. As they rounded the bend, the two-story building came into view, significantly larger than most structures in the village. This was one of the few rural villages that had its own high school, Amy remembered. Most kids from the other areas had to travel hours to reach one.

And currently, there were students everywhere. Standing in small groups, chatting near the entrance, clearly waiting until the last possible moment to go inside.

"Excuse me! Sorry!" Mira called out, picking up speed despite her exhaustion. "Sorry! I'm late! I'm really sorry!"

Students jumped aside as Mira barreled past. Amy caught glimpses of surprised faces, heard scattered laughter and comments.

The voices blurred together. Amy kept her head down, her hand traveling as usual towards her satchel where Libris rested.

The school's entrance loomed ahead—a wide doorway with steps leading up to it. Mira took them two at a time.

Eventually, they reached the top. Mira carefully lowered Amy down, and the moment Amy's feet touched solid ground, Mira bent over, hands on her knees, gasping for air.

"Finally..." she wheezed between breaths. "Amy... you really need to eat more..."

Amy stood there, swaying slightly. Her legs felt like jelly, and her head buzzed with residual magic use. But she did feel better now that she had rested for a bit.

It took Mira a few seconds to catch her breath, and once she did, she turned towards Amy with surprising energy.

"Okay, okay, let me just—" Mira's hands moved to Amy's uniform, straightening the collar, smoothing down wrinkles that had formed from the piggyback ride. "Your uniform got all messed up. Can't have you going in looking like that; Madam Chen likes you too much, and right now you are our best bet in case we get caught."

Amy stood still, letting it happen. There was something surreal about this moment. Here she was, being taken care of by the girl she had driven to suicide.

Nothing in this world made sense.

This isn't reality, she reminded herself. This is just a puppet wearing Mira's face.

"Your hair, too," Mira—no, her clone—said, brushing the loose blonde strands out of Amy's face. She tucked some behind her ears and smoothed the rest down. "There. Let people see that pretty face of yours."

Amy let her, watching her face the whole time.

The clone then hurried to fix her own uniform. She pushed her glasses up and tried flattening her curls with both hands, though they looked about the same when she gave up.

"Okay. Ready?" she asked, still catching her breath.

Amy turned toward the open doors. She could hear people talking inside.

They were going to class. That was apparently what she was supposed to worry about now, along with the homework in Mira's missing bag.

After everything outside this trial, after the monsters and trying to keep everyone alive...

She had to go to school.

"Amy, are you sure you're okay?" The clone leaned closer. "You've barely said anything all morning."

She kept touching her glasses as she waited for an answer. Mira used to do that when she was worried. Amy hadn't remembered it until seeing her do it again.

For a second, she almost believed it was her.

"I'm fine. Really." Amy made herself look away. "Let's go inside."

"Well... tell me if you need the nurse, okay? We can go there first if you're sick."

"Okay."

"Promise?"

"Promise."

That seemed to satisfy the clone. "Good. Come on, then. We're late enough already."

She took Amy's hand.

Amy followed her inside.

Most of the students had finally gone to class. A few were still in the hallway, though nowhere near as many as there had been outside.

The clone led her past bulletin boards full of student drawings and notices about upcoming events. There were motivational posters, too. Amy hadn't read those when she actually went here, and she wasn't about to start now.

She knew all of this. That was the problem.

A boy farther down the hall was staring at her.

Amy looked back, and he almost dropped the papers he was carrying. He caught them against his chest, his face red, then straightened as though she hadn't just seen that.

The baker's son, she realized. His name wouldn't come to her, though she didn't spend much effort trying to remember it.

He seemed about to say something. Amy waited, but all she got was a nod before he turned away.

Whatever. She had enough to think about without him, such as whether she was expected to spend an entire school day here. Honestly, at that point, just give her the chaos spiders.

"Here," the clone whispered, tugging her sleeve to get her moving again. "That's our classroom."

They stopped at the door. Through its little window, Amy could see the class already sitting down, and the woman at the front.

She was in her fifties, with gray showing in the hair she'd pulled into a tight bun. Dark blazer, dark skirt.

Madam Chen. Amy hadn't seen her since quitting school.

"Listen," the clone whispered, moving against the wall so she couldn't be seen through the window. "She doesn't do roll call until the middle of class. We wait until she's looking away and go straight to our seats. If she doesn't see us come in, we were never late."

Amy looked at her with an unimpressed gaze.

"It's worked before!" The clone said defensively. "Well, once. Okay, half a time. The point is, it's our best shot. Unless you have a better idea?"

Amy didn't. Honestly, she didn't care enough to formulate one. For her, all this was... inconsequential.

"Fine," she said simply.

"Okay!" The clone smiled. "Just follow me and—"

The door opened.

Neither of them moved.

Madam Chen stood there with her arms folded. She looked at the clone, then at Amy, leaving them plenty of time to realize she'd probably heard the plan.

"Miss Mira. Miss Amy." She checked her watch. "Fifteen minutes. Is there a reason you're still standing outside?"

"Madam Chen, we— I can explain."

"You can come inside." She stepped aside. "Both of you."

The class turned to look as they entered. Someone whispered something, and Amy lowered her head, reaching for her satchel before she thought about what she was doing.

She could feel Libris through it. Still asleep, but still there, which helped a little.

"Your seats," Madam Chen said, pointing at two empty desks in the middle of the room.

The clone hurried to hers. Amy took the chair to her left. Sitting down helped, at least.

A few of the students were smiling. The ones who looked sorry for them bothered Amy more, though. She wished they'd all just turn around.

"Well, you two have everyone's attention." Madam Chen picked up the thick book on her desk. "You might as well help with the lesson."

Help how? All Amy wanted was to put her head down for a few minutes.

Madam Chen wrote 'Advanced literature analysis' on the board. "We'll be looking at symbolism in classical texts. Before I explain it to everyone, why don't you two show us what you know?"

Beside her, the clone had gone pale.

"Ten questions," the teacher continued. "You have fifteen minutes. Both of you pass, and I won't punish you for being late. Fail, and we'll have another conversation."

The clone made a noise so pitiful that Amy glanced at her.

The teacher brought them a sheet each, setting both face down. "No talking, and keep your eyes on your own work. You may begin."

"Ma'am?" The clone stopped her before she could leave. "I forgot my bag. Could I borrow a pencil?"

Madam Chen stared at her long enough to make Amy uncomfortable, then went back for a pencil and an eraser. She put them on the clone's desk without a word.

Then she returned to teaching the rest of the class, leaving the two of them to it.

Amy turned her paper over.

What the hell? This was university work. She was tired, yes, but not so tired that she'd forgotten what a high school quiz looked like.

Discuss the role of the unreliable narrator in post-modern literature, with specific reference to—

She stopped reading. The clone was breathing too quickly beside her, staring at the questions as though they were about to kill her. Amy didn't like seeing that expression on her face, or how her hand shook when she picked up the pencil.

Amy looked back at the quiz, almost amused now. Of all the things this nightmare could have made her do, it had chosen this.

Fine. I'll play along.

She closed her eyes and reached for her ability. There was very little mana left, but surely she could manage a literature quiz.

Fate's Road answered, and she followed the golden threads.

It was easy. She knew what to write, down to the examples Madam Chen would want and the wording she'd approve of. Amy hadn't even read the texts in question.

She opened her eyes and picked up her pencil.

Her pencil kept moving through answers she wouldn't have known where to start on a minute ago.

Seven minutes later, she was done.

The clone was still on question three. Amy watched her erase an answer, start another one, then erase that too.

Amy watched her start the same answer again. Alright, fine.

She used her ability again, looking for a way to get the clone through this as well. This time, the answers included a few mistakes, enough to make it less obvious that someone had done her work for her.

Amy cleared her throat. The clone looked over just as Amy adjusted her paper, and in the next moment, the sheets were exchanged. Rather impressive, actually. She hadn't known she could move her hands that fast.

The clone shook her head at her, eyes wide. "No," she mouthed. Then again, more urgently, "No."

Amy ignored her and started writing. The clone kept glancing over, though at least she was careful enough not to let the teacher see.

It took another few minutes. When Amy finished, she slipped the sheet back and took her own paper in return.

"Time's up." Madam Chen came down the row to collect them.

She took the clone's sheet first, then Amy's. After reading a few lines of Amy's answers, she stopped beside the desk.

Amy waited while the teacher checked one paper against the other, frowning.

"Well. You both seem to have done rather better than I expected."

Yeah, she suspected something. Amy could hear it.

"Quite thorough, too." Madam Chen looked at her. "You can manage all of this, Miss Amy, but you can't manage to arrive on time?"

Even the whispering had stopped. Nobody wanted to miss this.

Bunch of gossipers...

Madam Chen looked down at the papers again. "Still, you've both passed. I said that would settle the matter, and it does." She didn't sound particularly pleased about keeping her word.

The clone finally seemed able to breathe. She turned toward Amy and mouthed a smiling "thank you."

"This time," Madam Chen added, still looking at Amy. "Don't expect me to do this again."

She went back to the front and dropped the papers on her desk. "Now, where were we?"

Amy had no idea. She leaned back and closed her eyes, too tired to bother following the lesson. She could still hear Madam Chen talking, pencils moving, and the students starting to whisper again.

"That was pretty impressive."

Amy's eyes opened.

The voice had come from her left side, from the desk beside hers. With a sigh she turned her head, expecting to see another student-shaped illusion.

Instead, she found herself looking at the Goddess.

The Goddess had put on the same uniform as her. She'd even tied her golden hair into a ponytail and opened a notebook on the desk. Amy doubted she'd written a word in it. Those star-filled eyes were on her, and the woman looked far too amused.

Amy couldn't stop staring at her.

The Goddess waved. "I might have to nerf that ability," she said, grinning at Amy. "Only a little. For balance. You understand, don't you?"
