Jiho knew for a fact that Prestige Academy’s substantial increase in security personnel was because Merlin had divulged the events of their meeting.

He called it a meeting because he had not meant to frighten the boy. After all, they were two sides of the same coin and were most certainly destined to help one another on their paths to greatness. All Merlin needed was just a little nudge in the right direction.

However, it seemed the academy had underestimated how much security they needed to put on standby in order to prevent him from finding his way into the academy grounds. Not only was getting past even thousands of guards only troublesome enough to cause him to break a single sweat, he was not a stranger to the place.

After all, it had been his school once.

Jiho was seated in the stands like the rest of the crowd, dressed in a black t-shirt that hugged his body and accentuated his physique, completed by black pleated pants. He crossed his legs and blended in just like every other person present, and with a bucket of popcorn he watched every single fight of the tournament, taking his time to develop some interest in those who competed.

It was hard and kind of disappointing. The majority of them were of a substandard level, and he couldn’t help but wonder if Prestige Academy had gone soft on their style of teaching.

The Prestige Academy he recalled used to be relentless and filled with vigor. Mages pushed themselves to be better, always raring for a fight. He couldn’t count how many times he had witnessed a first year challenge a second year to a duel out in the courtyard. During his relatively short time at the academy, the buildings were always being repaired, because fights were a daily occurrence. Everyone wanted to be stronger than they were a second ago, motivated by the prospect of the return of the Cataclysm, and that they wanted to be ready when it arrived.

The current kids enrolled in Prestige Academy, however, were just far too lackluster for him to take seriously. Even worse was that the first ranked mage academy in the world was just as weak—maybe even weaker—as Prestige Academy, considering they were inching closer to losing their spot at the top.

In the grand scheme of things, the weakness of the current mages was a great thing for him. He could scratch them off the list of potential threats to his plans.

Of course, Jiho’s distaste didn’t pertain to every single student. For example, a few caught his eyes. The second years, Nam Sun-Hee, Sakamoto Ken, and Will Hall. And in the first years there were Kim Yiseo, Park Yuri, Lee Jaehyun, Chima… For some trifling reason, Jiho felt an overwhelming strength exude from Chima in comparison to the rest, both when he had watched his participation in the first round of the tournament, and during his duel in the second round. Even though the boy wasn’t an S-Class Mage, he seemed to have more potential than those who were, and appeared in Jiho’s mind as someone to take note of.

However, there was one person who stood above him in the equation.

Nora Tyrrell.

Being Merlin’s sister, Jiho had kept an eye on her right as soon as he had taken notice of Merlin. She was the definition of a prodigy. High scores and technical skills, and an absolute maniac with magic. Her fight with the Blood Mage from Nikita Academy had also proven to him that the Tyrrell twins were simply standout characters from the rest. It was to the point that he’d had to research their parents, wondering if they were the children of S-Class Mages. The result of the research had made him laugh. Despite being born from ordinary parents they had turned out to be the most standout students in their year. It was not even a stretch to say they were prospects to be the most standout students in the history of Prestige Academy.

Which was why Jiho was a little bit bummed when he found out that Nora was out of the tournament due to the injury she had sustained in her fight with the Blood Mage. He wanted every opportunity he could get to study her. After all, if there was anyone who could talk Merlin out of taking his side, then she would be the one. If push came to shove, killing her would be the only option.

Jiho sighed as he tossed a couple of popcorns into his mouth and chewed. The arena had turned to the epitome of a wreckage. Debris was lying everywhere, and the medics who rushed onto the scene to scoop up the two girls who had just been engaged in the craziest duel Jiho had witnessed in the tournament tried their best to avoid stepping on a protruding spike of cement. They succeeded, hauling the unconscious Park Yuri and her opponent Naomi Chen away with haste.

It was the most exciting match Jiho had watched, just topping Chima’s slightly, and that was because he had gotten to witness something fascinating. Park Yuri’s solar flare spell. It was a very good reason why he had penned her down as one of the people to take into consideration as nuisances.

Regardless, so far, Jiho was yet to see a reason to pat himself in the back for leaving the comfort of his place and coming to the academy to watch the tournament.

Mouse had told him that there was no reason for him to do that, and yet he had insisted, claiming that he could feel something spectacular would happen, and he wanted to witness it in person.

Most of the time, his gut was never wrong. He was starting to feel like this time was one of the few times it was.

But he waited patiently. The boy of uncertainty was yet to have his duel.

And for a fact, Jiho was not disappointed when the time came.

Merlin’s fight with the Sound Mage, Marcus Reed, brought a smile onto Jiho’s face. He couldn’t help but instinctively lean forward all through it as he studied every move of the Deficient Mage. His exquisite mana control, the output, and the way he used it to exploit his surroundings, coupled with his sharp thinking.

And then came the cloak of mana, inciting Jiho’s expression to loosen in excitement.

He had never seen anything like it before—no one had, evident from the shocked look from even the instructors around, the rest of the students, and every single person in the crowd.

The boy had taken mana control to a whole different level.

Despite being a Deficient Mage, Jiho had been a student of Prestige Academy once; he had studied mana just like everyone else. Even after leaving the academy, he hadn’t stopped. He’d always loved magic. He’d always wanted to be a mage.

Reality, however, was often far different than how one hoped it would be.

But that was besides the point.

Mana itself couldn’t take on a shape. It could serve as the catalyst for one’s magic to take a shape, like Chima had done with his fire giant, but transforming it into something tangible was an absurd feat and required a control over it that had never been seen before.

Until Merlin Tyrrell, of course.

Having taken every chance he could get, Jiho had studied Merlin to some degree, and he knew that the boy was restricted to a position whenever he had to nullify a spell. He had always seen it as a weakness; the inability to make any action and dependence on others to make use of your strongest and, really, only ability.

Jiho had tried to come up with several ways to bypass that setback. Since Merlin was to join him, he couldn’t have the boy being a weakness.

However, the boy had proved once again that he was well aware of his own limitations and how to overcome them.

With Merlin shaping his mana into a cloak, he was able to nullify any magic that came upon him without flexing a single finger, and that allowed him to be more proactive in his fight.

He took no time overpowering Marcus Reed, and that didn’t surprise Jiho one bit. The boy had fought a flock of Ice Wyverns alone. As long as magic couldn’t make its way to him, he was way above every single person in his year.

Just as Merlin was about to put an end to his duel and give Prestige Academy their well deserved win, he lost control of his mana.

Jiho shifted slightly on his seat as his brows furrowed.

For the first time, he looked just like everyone else in the crowd, dazed as he watched Merlin tumble to the ground in a fit of pain, screaming as he rubbed his hands across his body like he was trying to put out a fire.

Jiho’s head was filled with questions.

He had watched mages lose control of their mana several times and transform into Berserkers. But nothing he had witnessed looked just as painful as what he was watching now.

His eyes glowed blue indistinctly, and Merlin’s mana channel became visible to him. It was erratic, moving wildly through the boy like it was trying to escape out of him, and because of that it generated far too much momentum for the boy’s internal system to handle.

Jiho could stop the pain. His fingers twitched as he thought about doing that. But then he thought of the several consequences that could arrive from that. The most notable of which was his presence becoming noticeable.

He held himself back.

The academy would take care of the boy.

But before Jiho’s eyes could return to normal, he noticed something unusual.

Merlin’s mana channel suddenly enlarged, and immediately, the erratic movement of his mana was revealed to be as a consequence of compression.

Merlin’s mana hadn’t been trying to escape his channel, they had simply become far too much for his channel to handle, like gas in a compression chamber.

Jiho’s heart skipped a beat at the sight, and for a moment he thought he was losing his mind.

There was no way.

His mana had grown?

In the next instant, a harrowing scream escaped Merlin’s mouth, and with it was a burst of flames which manifested over him.

The medics rushing onto the arena froze, the instructors and his opponent, the crowd, and even Jiho.

Their expressions only further tightened when mana seeped out of Merlin’s palm and a thin layer of ice spread out from him towards the right end of the arena.

It was only then that the boy’s pain subsided. The aftermath, however, was a deafening silence in the crowd.

Jiho could feel his heart pounding in his ear, and before he knew it, a smile pushed his lips apart.

His gut hadn’t been wrong; he had just witnessed something spectacular.

Jiho reduced the intensity of his mana and stood up; as he walked out of the stands, not a single person noticed him, and as soon as he arrived on the stairs that led towards the tunnel out of the arena, he pulled out his phone and called Mouse.

“Round up everyone,” he said as soon as she picked up. “We’re speeding up the arrival of the Cataclysm.”
