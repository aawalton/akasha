Chapter 23: Fire and Stars

Three days. That's how much life I had left. Sleep was slow in coming, a light doze more than a deep slumber. Even then, pain never left me. Coiled in my lungs, nestled in my chest, it was like a serpent of hot coals, its scales burning whenever it moved. Each breath felt like swallowing razor wire dipped in acid.

I had gotten good at breathing in just the right way to prevent the worst of the agony. Shallow, controlled breaths that didn't stress the crystallized poison eating away at my lung tissue. Even a little relief was welcome when every second felt like drowning in fire from the inside out.

I had been attempting to craft more difficult potions for a while now. Failure was what greeted me for my efforts more times than I cared to admit. The hotel room's floor was littered with broken vials and the remnants of attempts that had gone wrong. Some had simply failed to activate, remaining inert liquids with no Flux response. Others had done worse, creating foul-smelling compounds that made my already damaged lungs seize up in protest.

I kept trying, crafting resonance potions just to give myself some kind of success. They were simple, reliable, and gave me a small boost that made the next few hours marginally more bearable. Now, I found it amusing how much we celebrate mediocrity just to feel better about ourselves, instead of admitting the truth that we despise losing.

The resonance potions were participation trophies. Gold stars for showing up. They didn't advance my crafting skills meaningfully, but they made me feel like I was accomplishing something when everything else was falling apart. Better to pretend I was making progress than face the reality that I might die here, poisoned and forgotten in a hotel room that shouldn't exist.

After a certain point, I found I had a new area displayed in my status information. The Flux had begun tracking something I hadn't seen before.

Profession: Essence Crafter (Initiate): 8

I was gaining experience, though the Flux would not tell me how much I earned for each successful creation. Apparently it just decided when I warranted a level, parceling out advancement like a miser counting coins. I needed to check periodically to see how far I had come and how much further I needed to go.

The arbitrary nature of it frustrated me. In construction, you could measure progress. See how many square feet of flooring you'd laid, count the number of studs you'd framed, track your advancement toward completion. Here, I was fumbling in the dark, hoping each attempt brought me closer to the apprentice rank I needed to craft the antidote that would save my life.

At the moment I was working on a sort of rage potion. It wasn't an established recipe in the book, but I was taking a risk. If I could create something not documented in the tome, something based on my own understanding and intuition, I was hoping the Flux would recognize the innovation and grant me the levels I desperately needed.

The book had been clear that original creations carried more experience than following established recipes. But it had also warned that experimentation without proper knowledge was dangerous. Components could interact in unexpected ways, creating everything from mild irritants to deadly toxins. Given that I was already poisoned, the irony of potentially killing myself with a failed experiment wasn't lost on me.

The other door led to a warehouse area, filled with all kinds of parts and other items. Shelves stretched up into shadows, loaded with containers, tools, and materials I couldn't begin to identify. Including Flux Beast parts preserved in jars and cases, organs and appendages that glowed with their own inner light. I left them alone for now, choosing instead to focus on making potions. I would work on making other, more complicated things at a future date.

If I lived.

The warehouse felt like a promise and a threat. All those materials represented possibilities, paths to power and knowledge I could explore once I survived this trial. But they also reminded me how little I understood about this craft, how vast the knowledge I lacked truly was. I was like a child playing with tools he didn't comprehend, hoping to stumble onto something useful before I hurt myself.

For the rage potion I was using valerian root, Flux water, and crushed salamander teeth. As I crushed the teeth with a mortar and pestle I'd found in the warehouse, I pulled on the passion, the anger I could sense within the material. I was finding that what an ingredient was had a lot to do with what aspects it held, what energies it could contribute to a creation.

Teeth were meant to eat, to tear and grind and consume. But they could also be used as weapons, especially considering these were salamander teeth. The creatures they'd come from were predators, beings that killed with fire and fury. Salamanders were related to the element of fire, or so I associated them with based on old mythology and the way these particular specimens glowed with inner heat. Fire. Passion. Rage. The connections felt right, even if I couldn't articulate the underlying principles.

Valerian root was said to cause irritability at times, though it was mainly used for sleep and relaxation. I wasn't an expert in herbalism, but the book had a comprehensive list of ingredients in the back of its pages that detailed their properties and common effects. According to those notes, valerian could induce vivid dreams and, in larger doses, agitation and restlessness. I diced up a small portion of the dried root, releasing a sharp, earthy scent that made my nose wrinkle.

The combination felt logical in my head. Fire for passion, valerian for agitation, Flux water as the binding medium. Together, they should create something that enhanced anger, made rage burn hotter and more focused. At least, that was the theory. Practice had a way of making theories look foolish.

The Flux water was boiling in my makeshift cauldron, bubbles rising through the liquid like tiny stars being born. I felt that boiling was appropriate for a rage potion, heat calling to heat, passion answering passion. I added the diced valerian root first, watching it dissolve into the roiling liquid with surprising speed. The water took on a slightly murky appearance, as if storm clouds were forming in miniature.

I followed the root with the crushed salamander teeth, sprinkling them into the mixture like seasoning. The reaction was immediate and dramatic. The liquid turned a deep crimson, darker than I'd expected, almost the color of dried blood. Steam rose from the surface carrying a scent that was part metallic, part organic, part something else entirely.

I frowned as I studied the mixture. I was expecting a brighter red, something that spoke of fresh anger rather than old fury. This looked like rage that had been allowed to ferment, to sour and deepen into something more complex and potentially more dangerous.

After letting it simmer for what felt like the right amount of time, I carefully poured the finished product into a vial. The liquid remained that same dark crimson, thick and viscous in a way that reminded me uncomfortably of actual blood. I held it up to the light, studying the way it moved, looking for any sign that the Flux had recognized my creation.

No notification followed.

Another failure.

I set the vial down with more force than necessary, frustration building in my chest alongside the constant burning of the poison. How many more attempts could I afford? How much time did I have left before the crystallized toxin finished its work?

The questions gnawed at me as I began cleaning up the failed experiment. Each failure brought me closer to death, each wasted hour was time I couldn't spare. But I had to keep trying. The alternative was giving up, and I'd already decided that wasn't an option.

I just had to be smarter about it. More careful. More precise.

The next attempt would be different. It had to be.

The hours that followed blurred together in a haze of pain and increasingly desperate experimentation. I tried variations on the rage potion, adjusting proportions, changing the order of addition, altering the heating process. Nothing worked. Each failure was marked by the absence of any notification from the Flux, no recognition of successful creation.

My movements became increasingly clumsy as exhaustion and pain took their toll. Twice I knocked over vials, sending their contents spilling across the hotel room's carpet. Once I nearly burned myself on the heating element, my concentration slipping as a particularly violent coughing fit seized my damaged lungs.

Fire ate my lungs.

Two more days had passed. Death was imminent, and I could not move. Flame was my world, as I felt my lungs being eaten away bit by bit. The crystallized poison had spread, creating networks of burning agony that pulsed with each heartbeat. Every breath was a struggle, every moment conscious was an exercise in enduring the unendurable.

Somehow, I ended up on the ground. I didn't remember falling, didn't remember my legs giving out. One moment I was standing at the table, trying to focus on another failed creation, and the next I was sprawled on the carpet, staring up at the hotel room's ceiling through eyes that felt like they were filled with sand.

The Flux screen I was looking at mocked my weakness, displaying numbers that told the story of my failure.

Profession: Essence Crafter (Initiate): 9

Close. So close to life. So close to the apprentice rank that would unlock the recipes I needed. I could feel my skill growing with each attempt, each failure teaching me something new about the craft even as it brought me closer to death. I was all but certain now that apprentice rank was at level 10, just one level away from salvation.

The rage potion had killed me. No, that wasn't right. My stupidity and needless risk had killed me. The arrogance of thinking I could innovate when I barely understood the basics. The desperate gamble of trying to shortcut my way to survival instead of building my skills methodically.

I would die in this dungeon. I would die a failure of a husband and father. I would never find the revenge I'd promised myself in the dark hours after Lynn and Jackson's deaths. I would not be around to protect Elisa, to see her grow up, to guide her through the hostile universe that had swallowed our old life whole.

The weight of that failure was heavier than the poison in my lungs. I had abandoned my surviving daughter to chase power, to follow voices in my head that promised strength. And for what? To die alone in a room that existed outside normal reality, choking on my own crystallized blood.

Death approached with measured steps, patient and inevitable. The old man's scythe was coming for me, ready to reap what I had sown through pride and poor judgment.

But as my vision began to dim, as the fire in my chest reached a crescendo that threatened to tear me apart from the inside, something strange happened. The pain didn't intensify. It transformed.

The burning in my lungs became something else, something that felt almost like starlight. Cool and distant but somehow comforting, like lying on my back in a field and looking up at the night sky. The agony that had been my constant companion for days was replaced by a sensation of floating, of drifting through vast spaces between worlds.

Strange. Why did death look like a sea of stars?

As consciousness faded, as my body finally began to surrender to the poison that had been eating me alive, I found myself wondering if this was what Lynn and Jackson had seen at the end. If they too had found peace in the spaces between heartbeats, comfort in the vast darkness that waited beyond the pain of living.

The stars grew brighter, closer, until they filled my vision completely. And in their light, I thought I could hear voices calling my name.
