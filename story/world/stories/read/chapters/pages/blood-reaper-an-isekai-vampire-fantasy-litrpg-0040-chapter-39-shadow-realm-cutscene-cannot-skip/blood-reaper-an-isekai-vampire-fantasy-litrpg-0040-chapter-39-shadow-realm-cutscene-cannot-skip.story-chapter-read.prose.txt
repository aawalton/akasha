Ace couldn't sleep.

It wasn't from the stress of watching his squad stumble through his drills all day, nor was it because of the bruises forming on his forearms from blocking all of Olivia's blows. It wasn't the anxiety of watching Rachel and Marcus struggle to control the wild pulses of magic that whistled and snapped from their palms in feral bursts. And it definitely wasn't from watching Tara practice her healing abilities, given that she was the only one who seemed fully in tune with her new magic.

Nope.

It was because the damn sun wouldn't set.

He slumped against the cave wall, watching twilight's persistent glow paint the Highlands with muted purples and deep blues. Despite the late hour—it was probably midnight by now—true darkness never really claimed this strange region. The perpetual twilight cast long shadows across the rocky terrain, giving the landscape an otherworldly quality that matched his growing unease.

Why vampire clans made the Highlands their home, he would never know. Maybe it was because other vamps wouldn't bother them in a place untouched by darkness. Or maybe it was because this was where baby vamps had to escape from the higher-powered monsters and murderers, given that the Highlands had mostly low-Leveled creatures inhabiting its barren stretches of grass and rocks.

"I can heal your bruises," Tara said from nearby.

He tilted his head to find her sitting on a boulder by the entrance, keeping watch while the others slept.

"Save your power," Ace insisted. "The Soul Meter takes a while to fill back up. I've dealt with worse than bruises and aches before."

She shrugged. "Suit yourself. You really need to stop fighting sleep, though. You need rest."

"I'm fine." The words came automatically.

A lie.

As silence settled on the cave—save for Marcus, snoring again—and a familiar tension hummed between him and Tara.

Before he could stop himself, his gaze shifted down to her waist, and his thoughts wandered to places they shouldn't have gone.

He rubbed his eyes to rid himself of the daydreams flashing through his mind. She was a teammate, and a sergeant didn't fraternize. It disrupted the team dynamic, and their ability to work together was about the only thing keeping them alive right now.

"There's a difference between hunting and having a death wish," the healer said quietly. "And I've been wondering where the line between the two really is."

When Ace opened his eyes, she was already watching him intently. Her fingers drummed against her thigh, as though she was waiting for him to respond.

But he didn't have anything to say.

"Try to sleep," she said again.

This time, she turned her back on him and hugged her knees to her chest as she watched the Highlands. The ivy covering the entrance swayed in a subtle breeze, shifting like ribbons hung from rafters.

Ace watched the deepening shadows at the cave entrance, fighting to keep his eyes open despite the bone-deep exhaustion. The perpetual twilight of the Highlands cast strange, elongated shapes across the stone floor—shapes that seemed to shift in ways that defied natural movement. He blinked slowly, trying to focus on the strange patterns they made across the rough ground.

His eyes stung. He needed sleep.

Bit by bit, the cave walls slowly blurred and faded. The soft sounds of his teammates' breathing flickered and died. The air grew colder, heavy with an unseen presence.

Then something seized his consciousness—an irresistible pull that jerked him violently from the twilight state between waking and dreaming.

The force flung him through the darkness, his entire body weightless as he spiraled deeper into a cold void. Ace struggled against the sensation, his military training kicking in as he fought for control of his body, his mind, his breath—anything.

But the pull was too strong.

One instant he sat against the cave wall fighting sleep. The next, he stood in a realm of living darkness.

When he finally regained control of his body, Ace instinctively dropped into a combat stance—feet shoulder-width apart, knees bent, hands raised. Only then did he realize his Crimson Sword was already in his grip, though he had no memory of drawing it. The weapon's faint red glow provided the only light in the absolute darkness surrounding him.

Shadows with texture and depth encircled him, swirling like ink in water. The familiar but unsettling sensation of the Shadow Realm enveloped him—but this was different from when he’d slipped into it before. The air felt heavier, the darkness more substantial.

It felt possessed.

Something—or someone—had dragged him here against his will.

Whispers brushed against his consciousness, too faint to understand but undeniably present. Ace pivoted in a slow circle, every nerve on edge despite his exhaustion.

"Show yourself," he demanded.

But the darkness swallowed his command.

No response came, even as the shadows before him rippled and parted. Three tendrils of darkness separated from the mass, their movements precise and directed. They lashed toward him with vicious speed.

Instinct took over. Ace dodged the first, sliced through the second with his blade, and raised his arm to block the third—a mistake. The shadow-tendril wrapped around his forearm like liquid ice, burning with cold to the point where he thought his arm might freeze solid. He bit back a yell of pain as the shadow tightened its grip, probing at his skin as if looking for a way inside.

He drove his sword through the tendril, severing it. The shadow dissipated with an audible hiss, but the icy burn lingered on his skin.

More tendrils emerged from the darkness, dozens now, whipping toward him from all directions.

Fine.

Time to slaughter whatever the hell this was—because he wasn’t going down easy.

Ace shifted into the hyper-focused state of combat. His vision narrowed. His movements became more clinical to preserve energy. His breathing evened out, calm and controlled.

“Bring it,” he said under his breath.

The darkness attacked.

He slashed through the nearest tendrils, rolled under another, and leaped over a third. His body moved on pure tactical reflex, the blade an extension of his arm as he cut through shadow after shadow.

But they kept coming.

Faster, and more numerous than before.

A shadow caught his ankle, and another wrapped around his wrist. They yanked in opposite directions, threatening to split him apart. Pain ripped through him as they tested his joints. Desperation surged through him, and he focused on the shadows themselves—not as enemies to be fought, but as substances to be manipulated.

As something he could control.

Ace gritted his teeth and focused on the writhing shadows trying to rip him apart. He'd seen enough horror movies to know where this was headed.

"Get the hell off me," he growled, trying to push his will against the darkness.

Nothing happened. The shadows only tightened their grip, sending jolts of icy pain up his arms.

As a surge of adrenaline shot through him, he leaned back on his training. Unknown threat. Unfamiliar terrain.

Adapt or die.

Despite the rush of dread that jolted through him as the shadows tugged again, he closed his eyes. His full focus settled on the sensation of the shadows against his skin. They felt almost liquid, but with just enough substance to grab.

He had to stay calm. The more he fought them, the tighter they got.

Taking a deep breath, he tried again, imagining the shadows as an extension of himself rather than an enemy. He pushed his consciousness into the darkness, commanding rather than fighting.

The shadows vibrated but remained locked around his wrists.

Nope.

Apparently, that wasn’t the right approach—but he was on to something.

He changed tactics. Instead of pushing outward, he visualized pulling the darkness into himself, absorbing rather than repelling. The shadows shuddered, loosening slightly before constricting again, tighter than before. A painful prickling sensation shot up his wrist as his fingers went numb from lack of circulation.

"Fuck this," he snarled, abandoning finesse for raw determination.

He channeled every ounce of his military discipline into a single, focused command:

Let go!

The shadows recoiled as if he’d struck them, unwinding from his limbs in a swift, fluid motion. They hovered nearby, undulating like agitated snakes, poised to strike again.

Though Ace felt a surge of triumph, it didn’t last long.

The tendrils regrouped, multiplying and growing denser. They rushed toward him from all sides, no longer targeting just his limbs but forming a swirling vortex around his entire body. The darkness thickened, coalescing into massive, clawed appendages that reached for his body.

Ace reacted instinctively. He extended both hands and pushed his newfound control outward, summoning the untouched darkness around the tendrils until it formed a barrier between himself and the attacking void.

The tendrils slammed hard against the makeshift wall, and to his relief, they froze. More than that, they obeyed. The strain on his mind was immense, like holding back a flood with nothing but willpower.

But it was working.

A mischievous grin spread across his face as he took a step forward, maintaining the mental barrier. The shadows writhed in protest but couldn't break through.

In fact, they retreated—and a slow clap echoed through the void.

"Impressive," said a voice that seemed to come from everywhere and nowhere. "Most newcomers simply get consumed."

The tendrils shriveled and fell, parting to reveal a figure that stepped from the edge of the darkness. Tall. Imposing, with features too perfect to be human. The stranger's ancient eyes evaluated Ace with unsettling intensity.

"Who the fuck are you?" Ace demanded, his sword raised toward the newcomer. "And why the hell did you attack me?"

The figure tilted his head, studying Ace with something that might have been amusement. "Such hostility. ‘Attack’ is such a strong word. I was merely testing your abilities. Nothing more."

"Testing?" Ace glanced at the hovering tendrils still surrounding them. "That's what you call this ambush?"

"An evaluation," the stranger corrected. "One you passed, might I add. Well done."

Instead of answering, Ace scoffed in disgust and sheathed his sword. If the need arose, he could always summon his Duskblades—and they would probably work better in the Shadow Realm anyway.

The stranger circled Ace slowly and kept a measured distance. His movements were fluid, predatory, and ancient—like a hunter who had long outgrown the need to prove himself.

“Who are you?” Ace asked.

"Darkmoor," the stranger said simply.

The name hit Ace like a physical blow. Darkmoor—creator of the Crucible. Even the System tensed when she spoke the name.

"Bullshit." Ace adjusted his grip on his sword. "From everything I’ve gathered since coming here, it seems like Darkmoor's dead."

"I very much am." The stranger's mouth curved into a humorless smile. "What you see now is just an echo—an imprint of my essence on the Shadow Realm."

The darkness around them rippled in response to his words, as if confirming his identity.

"Fine, say you are Darkmoor,” Ace said with a shrug. “Why am I here?"

"You're marked," Darkmoor responded, his eyes trailing over Ace's form as if seeing something Ace couldn’t.

"What the hell does that mean? Who marked me?"

Darkmoor simply smiled, offering nothing.

"I'm not in the mood for cryptic bullshit," Ace said. "Either give me answers, or I’m leaving.”

The shadows around them responded to his anger, pulsing with his elevated breathing. Darkmoor glanced toward the writhing darkness, and his expression shifted to one of genuine interest.

"The shadows respond to you," he observed. "Unusual for someone so new to their power."

"Guess I get a gold star,” Ace said with a mocking bow.

He wasn’t in the mood for… well, whatever this was.

"I brought you here because time is short," Darkmoor said, his tone sharpening. "The System's plans are accelerating. The System grows ever stronger. The Crucible awaits, and you—" he gestured at Ace, "—remain woefully unprepared."

"Thanks,” Ace said dryly.

"You must make a choice, soon," Darkmoor said. "But first, you must earn the knowledge necessary to make it. After my little test, you’ve earned the answer to one question. Ask."

Ace had dozens of questions swarming his thoughts: Who marked him? What did that mean? Why was the System afraid of the Shadow Realm? What was waiting in the Crucible? What was Victor becoming? Was Victor truly a threat—and what was the System turning the man into?

But one question seemed more immediately relevant than the others.

"What are all those figures that I see when I use Shadow Realm Immersion?" he asked. "Every time I come here the shadows seem more... aware of me afterward."

Darkmoor's expression shifted, almost imperceptibly, to something like approval.

"A practical question. Good." He set his hands behind his back and began to slowly pace back and forth. "Your Shadow Realm Immersion skill is not merely a tool. It's a doorway—one that opens in both directions."

The darkness around them rippled, forming images that illustrated his words—doorways opening, figures passing through in both directions.

"Each time you enter the Shadow Realm, you leave a trace of yourself behind, like footprints in sand. And each time you return to the System’s world, you carry a fragment of the Shadow Realm with you." Darkmoor circled Ace as he spoke. "These fragments accumulate. They change you. Enhance your connection to shadows. Make it easier to return."

"That doesn't sound like a problem," Ace said.

"It wouldn't be—if you were the only one who could follow those footprints." Darkmoor's expression grew more serious. "But there are entities in the deepest shadows—hungry things, ancient things—that can sense these connections. Every time you use your magic without proper protection, you risk drawing their attention."

"What kind of entities?"

Darkmoor paused mid-stride and sighed. “Dangerous ones, too many and too varied to name.”

“That’s not really an answer,” Ace pointed out.

“I am aware,” Darkmoor replied. “But for now, it’s all I can say.”

Ace swore under his breath. "Fine. How do I protect myself when using the skill?"

"You don't," Darkmoor said flatly. "Not until you learn to control the shadows rather than simply pass through them."

"And how exactly do I learn that?"

The ancient vampire's lips curved into a cold smile. "That is technically a second question, but I admire your persistence. I'll give you a hint. Your Abyssal Armory skill already draws on shadow manipulation. Learn to control what you store and how you retrieve it. Master that, and the rest will follow."

The darkness around them suddenly shifted, becoming agitated. Darkmoor's head snapped up, his expression alert.

"For now, we’re out of time," he said abruptly. "Something approaches—something that should not know that you exist. Not yet, anyway."

Before Ace could protest, Darkmoor made a sharp gesture. The shadow around them condensed and slammed into Ace with the force of a battering ram. He flew backward through the darkness, his connection to the Shadow Realm severing in an instant.

His back slammed against a jagged surface, and Ace jerked awake with a strangled gasp. Cold sweat drenched his clothes, and he shivered. The cave's rough ceiling materialized above him as reality reasserted itself.

As his eyes adjusted to the sudden twilight, a far more disturbing realization dawned. Even though he’d left the Shadow Realm, the shadows around him didn't move right. They lingered too long, shifting independently of the twilight's glow from the cave entrance. They reached for him, the ends shaped like claws, before they dissolved in a rush of smoke.

“What the fuck,” he whispered to himself.

The horrifying truth settled in his core, cold and clear: his Shadow Realm Immersion skill hadn't just given him access to another realm—it had given that realm access to him.

And something hungry was now watching him through the veil between worlds.
