Elynara woke to the soft morning light slipping across the ceiling beams. She blinked, eyes dry, mind moving like thick syrup. Her body felt heavy, like she’d already spent hours working, though she knew she’d slept well.

Sitting up, the blanket fell into a pile around her waist. She groggily stared at the wall, letting her eyes unfocus while her thoughts tried to catch up. Something about last night’s dreams tugged at the edge of her mind. There wasn’t much she remembered from them, only the sense of running, of doing too much all at once.

“Feels like I’ve been busy all night.”

She yawned, scrubbing a hand across her face, trying to shake the emptiness clinging to her thoughts. Nothing came, just that quiet heaviness pressing against her shoulders.

Starpuff circled into view, flickering softly in the morning light, carrying its usual smug energy. It hovered near her head, then dropped down to boop her cheek.

She blinked.

It hovered, then gave her nose a quick tap, making her eyes cross.

“Really?”

And just as she looked up to glare at it, Starpuff decided it was the perfect time to ram into her forehead. The puff of mana didn’t hurt, but the suddenness of it jolted her hard enough to make her flail backward, almost falling off the bed.

“Ah-!”

She caught herself, heart skipping, before a laugh broke out, rough and bright. The grogginess cracked like ice, leaving her blinking as she pressed a hand to her forehead.

“Okay, you win. I’m up.”

She stretched her arms high, fingers reaching for the ceiling, her back popping in satisfying snaps down her spine. She rolled her shoulders, letting the tension drain out with a slow breath.

A quiet thrill started to build, curling in her chest as the morning settled in.

Today.

“I’m going out of town to explore for the first time.”

She swung her legs over the side of the bed, toes brushing the cool floor, before she stood. Starpuff zipped around her, flickering with excitement, trailing tiny sparks of mana as it circled her head.

She moved to her bag, pulling it onto her lap as she knelt to check everything again. Her staff leaned within reach, the wood warm against her hand as she steadied herself.

She unbuckled the top of the bag, checking each item with quick, practiced movements. Dismantling tools, tied and wrapped so they wouldn’t rattle. Her notebook, its edges already slightly worn, tucked next to a folded cloak. A small ration bundle, tied with twine, enough for a week if she were careful. Her coin pouch, checked and rechecked, tucked into the folded clothes in her satchel of holding.

She pulled the straps tight, checking the seams for any fraying. The small knots she tied to shorten the straps were still holding, the adjustments from her last trip into town making the bag settle snug against her back.

Everything was ready. Everything was in place.

She paused, letting her fingers rest on the bag’s edge, letting herself breathe as the excitement in her chest hummed in quiet pulses.

Ready.

She glanced around the small room, the morning light catching on the few belongings she had, on the staff leaning by the wall, on the quiet stillness she had carved out for herself here.

“Let’s get going.”

Starpuff flickered close, hovering near her shoulder, its glow brightening like it was nodding in agreement.

Elynara smirked faintly, reaching up to give the small spirit a quick flick with her finger.

“Don’t knock me over this time.”

She pulled the bag onto her shoulders, staff in hand, and stepped toward the door, ready for the day, for the road, and for whatever the world would throw at her next.

Elynara headed down the corridor, adjusting the strap of her satchel as the soft morning air drifted through the church’s open windows. A pair of children darted past her, giggling, their small feet slapping against the floor.

“Miss Elynara, good morning!”

She paused, lifting a hand to wave back, their bright faces turning her small smile real as it settled on her lips.

“Morning,” she called after them, the warmth of it following her as she stepped into the hall.

Father Alaric was near the entrance, crouched beside a crate of vegetables, checking each one with the same calm patience he did everything else. The quiet confidence in his movements made her pause for a moment, grounding herself before stepping forward.

“Father Alaric. I wanted to let you know I’ll be going out of town for a few days.”

He looked up, his steady gaze meeting hers before his mouth curved into a warm, grounding smile.

“Stepping out into the world again, hmm?”

He rose, brushing his hands on his robes before placing one broad, steady hand on her back. It was a comforting weight, neither heavy nor light.

“Go with the goddess’s blessing, Elynara. Take care, and take your time.”

She wasn’t sure if it was encouragement or a real blessing, but a quiet warmth settled in her chest regardless.

“Thank you.”

She moved on, letting out a slow breath as she stepped into the dining area. Mina was there, sitting with a small bowl of porridge, Pufflet sprawled at her feet like a fluffy guard, ears flicking with each small noise.

Without thinking, Elynara bent down and hugged Mina from the side, her arms sliding around her before her brain caught up.

Mina let out a startled squeak, freezing before she started giggling, her shoulders shaking.

“What was that for?”

Elynara pulled back, rubbing the back of her neck as a small, embarrassed laugh escaped.

“Just felt like it.”

They settled at the table together, the morning quiet around them, spoons tapping gently against wooden bowls as they ate. The porridge was warm, simple, comforting, paired with the soft crust of bread that smelled faintly of herbs.

“I’ll be leaving town for a few days,” Elynara said between bites, trying to keep her tone casual.

Mina’s eyes went wide for a moment, her spoon pausing mid-air.

“You’ll be careful, right?”

Elynara nodded, swallowing before letting a small grin tug at the edge of her mouth.

“Promise. I’ll even try to bring you something fun back from the farm.”

She paused, a quick thought flickering through her mind.

Not that she had any clue what “fun” looked like at a farm, but… surely there was something, right?

Mina laughed, shaking her head as she lowered her spoon.

“I’ll hold you to that!”

They finished the meal in comfortable quiet, the warmth of the food settling in Elynara’s stomach as the quiet hum of the church morning moved around them.

When she stood, she rolled her shoulders back, letting out a slow breath as she felt the right kind of nerves buzz through her arms and legs, the promise of the day ahead making her pulse quicken in a good way.

She caught Mina’s eye, giving her a small nod. Mina responded in kind, smiling as Pufflet tried to climb into her lap, nearly tipping the bowl.

Elynara turned, stepping toward the doors, the light of the morning spilling in to greet her. She paused for a heartbeat at the threshold, letting the fresh air brush against her face, before stepping out, ready for whatever the world decided to throw at her next.

…And for now that ‘whatever’ turned out to be sunlight assaulting her eyes on this cloudless day. Certainly nothing raising her hand to block the offending sun couldn’t fix. Perhaps one of those days she should get herself a hat?

She grinned as a thought struck her. She would be a proper-looking mage then! …Or a witch. Yep, she was totally going shopping soon!

And with such silly thoughts circling her mind, she arrived at her destination in no time flat.

Elynara stepped into the guild with a lightness in her stride, the morning air still cool against her skin. Sunlight filtered through the high windows, catching on dust motes drifting lazily in the open space.

She glanced around, expecting to see Marcus near the quest board, but he was surprisingly absent for once.

From the back, however, came a muffled thump followed by a pained grunt and a low voice. She would bet some good coin that was Marcus correcting someone’s stance in the least gentle way possible.

“Guess he’s busy,” she murmured, hiding a small smirk.

At the counter, Alicia was, for once, unoccupied, her pen tapping idly against a stack of neat papers. It was a day of firsts, it seemed!

Elynara approached, letting her satchel settle against her hip as she leaned forward.

“Morning, Alicia.”

Alicia glanced up, a small smirk pulling at the corner of her mouth.

“You’re early.”

Elynara opened her mouth to ask about the requests, but Alicia set her pen down with a deliberate click, her clear gaze meeting Elynara’s.

“Actually, before anything else, I have an announcement.”

That caught Elynara off-guard. She blinked, tilting her head, curiosity sparking as she shifted her weight.

“Oh?”

Alicia’s lips twitched, and for a moment, there was a rare warmth in her eyes that Elynara had only caught glimpses of before.

“Your adventurer rank has been updated. Because of your consistent, steady work and how seriously you’ve taken your requests, you’re being advanced.”

Elynara’s eyes widened, the quiet words slipping out before she could catch them.

“Really?”

Alicia reached under the counter, retrieving a small badge tied to a clean, dark string. It was simple, a smooth copper piece marked with the guild’s emblem, gleaming slightly in the light.

“Hold still.”

Alicia untied the worn wooden badge from Elynara’s satchel strap, her movements precise, before replacing it with the copper one, adjusting it so it rested neatly.

Only now did she remember the little Adventurer’s Manual explaining adventurer ranks - listed as Wood, Copper, Iron, Silver, Gold, Mithril, and Orichalcum - she was totally supposed to read through.

There were just too many things that happened since then! Yes, that would be her excuse if anybody asked. But for now Alicia was happy to distract her from her musings once more:

“It’s not much, especially at the lower ranks, but it’s a marker of your capability. Copper rank means you’re recognized as capable by the guild. It opens up a few more requests, and people will take your presence a bit more seriously.”

Elynara lifted her hand, brushing her fingers lightly over the badge. It was cool, solid, and small as it was.

“Thank you.”

Alicia shrugged, but Elynara didn’t miss the way her mouth twitched, the pleased glint in her eyes before she turned away to shuffle the papers on the counter.

“Some even say it’s the first step towards becoming a full-fledged adventurer. Keep it up.”

Elynara glanced down at the badge again, letting the moment settle, a small, warm pulse in her chest. It wasn’t grand, but it was a step forward, and one she had earned herself.

She let herself smile.

Elynara was still touching the cool copper badge, letting the small weight of it settle in her mind, when Alicia cleared her throat.

“Now, about your reason for coming.”

The warmth of the moment shifted back to the steady rhythm of guild business, and Elynara dropped her hand, giving a small, focused nod.

Alicia pulled two request slips from a small stack, tapping them lightly against the counter as she explained.

“There are two open requests near the farms. Both give you a week to complete, so you can pace yourself.”

She laid them out neatly.

“One is a request for the collection of some rare herbs needed for the apothecary; their information is attached. The other is a minor culling request for pests and small monsters that have been getting too close to the outer farmlands.”

Elynara’s eyes skimmed the slips, mind already shifting into practical planning. Before she could ask about the area, Alicia leaned in a fraction, lowering her voice just enough to soften the space between them.

“If you need a place to stay, I know a Forest Folk family that runs a farm out that way. Good people.”

Elynara blinked, looking up. Alicia’s expression didn’t change much, but there was a quiet intent behind her words.

“Tell them I sent you. They’ll feed you, give you a place to sleep. Just a small fee or a bit of help around the farm in exchange.”

It took Elynara a moment to process it, the simple generosity of it sinking in. Alicia didn’t have to prepare any of this. She just… did.

A warm flush rose to Elynara’s cheeks, and she stumbled over her words for a moment.

“Thank you. Seriously.”

Alicia waved a hand, a dismissive gesture that couldn’t hide the small, betrayed smile tugging at the edge of her mouth.

“Don’t make me regret it.”

Before Elynara could reply, the back door of the guild banged open, letting in a sharp draft and the heavy thud of boots.

Marcus stepped in, rolling his broad shoulders, dragging two groaning adventurers behind him, each clutching their arms or rubbing sore ribs.

“And that, kids,” Marcus announced, loud enough to carry, “is why you don’t swing your sword like a fish flopping on land!”

The adventurers mumbled quick thanks and shuffled off, leaving Marcus standing there, hands on his hips, looking far too pleased with himself.

His eyes landed on Elynara, and a wide grin split across his face.

“Ah, our rising star!” he boomed. “Heard you got your Copper badge. Congrats!”

Heat rose in Elynara’s cheeks again, and she looked away, mumbling under her breath.

“Thank you…”

Before she could dodge, Marcus reached out and ruffled her hair with a heavy hand, ignoring her quiet protests as she tried to duck away.

“Keep it up. Just remember, squishy mages need to watch their surroundings, yeah? First real trip, first real test.”

Elynara smoothed her hair down, but paused, letting the advice sink in. She drew in a quiet, steadying breath, the nerves in her stomach shifting into determination.

“Got it.”

She glanced between Alicia and Marcus, giving them each a small, genuine nod.

“Thank you. Both of you.”

Alicia hummed in acknowledgment, while Marcus just gave her a thumbs up, already turning toward the back to collect whatever poor soul was next on his training list.

Elynara checked her pouch, adjusted the strap of her satchel, and tapped the end of her staff on the ground once - a small habit to center herself - before stepping toward the door.

She stepped outside, the crisp morning air meeting her with the promise of the road ahead, ready to begin her first real journey beyond Oltenar’s walls. She adjusted the strap of her satchel, ensuring the weight settled comfortably against her hip, and shifted her staff in her grip until it felt right in her hand.

Starpuff obviously had to choose this moment to fly zigzags right before her very eyes, as if her cross-eyed look amused it endlessly.

Blinking rapidly, she couldn’t help but smile, mock-upset at the prank.

“Yeah, thanks for that too, you silly rascal.”

She stepped off the guild’s steps and headed toward the town gate, boots scuffing lightly against the dirt road as she moved with purpose.

In the back of her mind, she half-expected to see Josh leaning near the gate, arms crossed and smirk ready, waiting to throw out some cheeky send-off.

But when the gates came into view, it was a different guard on duty: a middle-aged woman with her hair tied back, polishing her spear with methodical care. She looked up as Elynara approached.

Elynara blinked, the moment of quiet disappointment passing through her before she caught it, letting it slip away.

“Ah… good morning,” she said, offering a small, polite nod.

The guard returned the nod, a faint smile creasing the lines near her eyes.

“Heading out? Stay safe, miss.”

Elynara’s smile returned, small but genuine.

“I will.”

She stepped past the gates, boots touching down on the packed dirt of the road as the walls of Oltenar fell behind her. The world felt bigger immediately, the road stretching forward, lined with green grasses heavy with dew, wildflowers catching the light, and the faint scent of damp earth rising from the remnants of past rains.

She paused for a single breath, letting the breeze push past her, carrying the scent of wet grass and the distant rustle of leaves.

Then she set one foot forward, staff tapping against the earth, and started down the road, the soft hum of Starpuff at her side as the world opened up ahead of her.

Elynara stepped onto the road out of Oltenar, letting the gate close behind her with a soft thud. The sky was a blanket of layered grey, clouds drifting low, but the air was crisp, fresh, and alive.

The world felt bigger already.

She adjusted her satchel, tugging the strap so it wouldn’t slide, then settled her staff across her shoulders, hands hooked lightly over each end. The copper badge on her strap tapped against the fabric with each step, a small, comforting reminder of her progress.

Birds called overhead, one landing near a flower patch, feathers ruffling as it eyed her. She tilted her head, studying it for a breath before it flew off, wings flapping sharply.

“Yeah, you go on.”

A faint scent of damp earth and crushed grass rose with the breeze, mixed with the sharper, sweet note of some flower she didn’t know. Somewhere in the grass, insects hummed, the vibration brushing against her awareness like a background rhythm.

A distant call echoed across the fields, low and throaty. She paused, staff shifting in her grip, but the sound didn’t repeat.

Probably not a monster. Didn’t sound like one at least.

The quiet of the road, the rhythm of her steps, and the steady weight of her bag made something settle inside her. A small, unexpected burst of energy rose in her chest, and she found herself skipping once. Twice.

Her staff bounced, and Starpuff flickered into view, swirling in delighted loops, matching her skips with tight, invisible spins.

She stumbled to a halt, cheeks hot, eyes darting around.

“Stars, I hope no one saw that.”

Starpuff pulsed, smug, floating near her shoulder. She blew out a breath, half-laughing, rolling her eyes.

As a means of distracting herself, and maybe also as a face-saving tactic, she began pulling mana into her palm. And after allowing it to form into a small, stable sphere, she let it hover above her hand as she walked, bobbing with each step.

Mana shaping on the move. About time I tried this.

She added a second sphere, careful to balance them. They wobbled, flickered, and she narrowed her eyes, adjusting her grip on the staff.

A sudden dip in the path jolted her, and the spheres flickered dangerously. She caught her balance, muttering under her breath.

“Steady…”

Starpuff lunged in, nipping at one of the spheres and popping it with a soft crackle of mana.

“Hey!”

It pulsed again, satisfied, before circling her head once and drifting just out of reach.

Elynara let the second sphere fade, rolling her shoulders as she let her mana settle. Her pace slowed for a few steps, then picked up again. She wasn’t done.

“Right. Let’s see how far I can push this.”

She gathered a small stream of mana again, this time shaping it into a slow-rotating ring. It was deliberately awkward, just to test her limits. It wobbled with every step, jerking to the left as her foot hit uneven ground. She corrected. Again and again.

Starpuff drifted beside it, watching.

“I see you looking. Don’t bite it.”

It pulsed.

She kept the ring alive for twenty steps, then let it go before pulling mana again and shaping a longer thread. She tried wrapping it around her hand like a coil, then dragging it behind her like a trail of slow-moving light.

Halfway through the experiment, the trail tangled and collapsed.

She grunted, wiped a hand across her brow.

“Still counts.”

For the next half hour, she walked while rotating between spheres, lines, coils, and rings, pushing her control to the limit. Every mistake gave her something. Every failure was logged quietly in the back of her mind.

Starpuff remained an annoying, occasionally helpful presence, flicking a shape when she held it too long or nudging one just slightly off course.

A test in its own right.

She stopped once, kneeling beside the road to drink from her flask and let her mana partially recover.

After a short break, she stood and resumed, splitting her focus between her left and right hands this time. One simple sphere. One shaped flame. She held them for five steps. Then ten.

“Better.”

As the second hour approached, her slowly-emptying core began to dull from exertion.

She eased off, letting her mana rest again.

Now and then, she caught herself adjusting the balance of her satchel. Her fingers twitched as if forming a shape even when she wasn’t pulling in energy.

The rhythm of her walk stayed steady.

Mana, walking, mana, walking.

Each practice run felt sharper than the last.

Eventually, fences began to appear along the path, wooden posts leaning in different directions but still holding their lines. Fields stretched beyond them, damp soil freshly tilled, rows of vegetables catching the light through scattered clouds.

Small barns stood scattered across the fields, smoke rising thinly from one chimney in the distance.

Elynara slowed, her breath steady, posture relaxed but alert.

She stopped by the road’s edge, staff set lightly against the ground, and took in the view.

The hum of insects was quieter here. The breeze smelled of cut grass and damp earth.

A small, satisfied smile curled across her face.

“It’s a good day.”

She shifted her staff to her shoulder, feeling the weight settle.

“And the start of another mystery and adventure.”

Starpuff brushed her cheek, a warm flick of mana and approval, before zipping forward to scout.

And she was happy to follow.
