The scientist met Nick’s gaze and smiled wide.

“Welcome, lad. My name is Zero. If this isn’t some kind of cosmic joke at my expense, you are here to learn a method of merging the digital and magical arts that no culture has replicated since I invented it several millennia prior. It turns out that growing up in a world where the physical sciences take a backseat to magitech renders individuals fundamentally incompatible with arcane engineering.

“I am deeply interested to learn why the hall believes you have what it takes to wrap your brain around the computational concepts required to rewire magical circuitry and create code that can automate most aspects of the spellcasting process.”

“Nick Henry. I’m honored to be here and excited to find out what your Heritage has to offer. I might be able to shed some light on why I was given this opportunity. My people come from a planet that didn’t have mana until the System arrived a few months back. By that point, we had already invented vehicles that could traverse the solar system, established worldwide communication and research infrastructure capable of storing, manipulating, and transmitting vast amounts of data, and developed a scientific method to solve complex problems without using magic.

“We were exploring the possibilities afforded by advanced digital constructs that could replicate some aspects of human intelligence, such as neural networks and large language models. This is the time I’ve seen computers since integration erased our civilization, although the System and the displays mounted to its obelisks seem to have some overlap.”

Zero’s grin grew during this brief explanation, cementing Nick’s certainty that he had found a kindred spirit.

“Your people were forcibly awakened to Magic during integration, and it happened relatively recently?” Nick nodded by way of reply. “But you’re already C Grade and Tier 2. Not to mention those insane attributes, skills, traits, and far too many bloodline slots regardless of anything else. That background explains a great deal, but I’m having a hard time grasping how you advanced so quickly. Tell me your story in full so I can obtain the context required to tailor my instruction to your understanding, then I’ll provide an overview of what my training will look like and, if everything checks out, we can begin.”

Zero must have sensed Nick’s hesitation, because he was quick to offer reassurances.

“Don’t worry, I’m contractually bound by the System to maintain total confidentiality of anything you reveal while receiving my tutelage. More than that, you can trust me to have your best interests at heart, now and always. Receiving my legacy makes you my heir, and once your instruction begins, our fates will be bound together. If you rise, I will be richly rewarded. If you fall, I will suffer in turn.

“Going forward, I will support you to the full extent of my abilities until you’ve learned everything I have to teach, which isn’t going to happen anytime soon. You don’t need to take my word for it. There should be a new codex entry about Heritages that will confirm the truth of my claims.”

Sure enough, there was. When Nick read the entry, he breathed a sigh of relief. Zero was playing him straight. The man had every reason to be a helpful as possible, as it would benefit him too. The information gave Nick a renewed appreciation for just how big a deal receiving a Heritage was. It represented an incredible investment on the part of his mentor—time, resources, and risks that he couldn’t even begin to fully grasp in this moment.

Regardless of the details, the takeaway was Zero would be his stalwart ally going forward. Armed with that knowledge, he was willing to trust the scientist with his full story, including the aspects of his profile he had excluded from his application.

The scientist’s eyes went wide as he absorbed Nick’s tale, not interrupting other than asking an occasional clarifying question until the account arrived at the present.

“I see. Our meeting was more fortuitous than I imagined, as this sheds light on a great deal of what the gods have been up to behind the scenes. The System and its allies must have been preparing for their showdown with the Mad God for ages, and now the seeds they’ve planted have come to fruition.

“The Ascendant is in desperate need of champions at this critical stage. It explains why you were thrust into so many situations with an extreme level of risk and reward, although I shudder to imagine how many have perished for every success like yourself. I hadn’t realized the Labyrinth was a Seed of Corruption, but now I understand why so many gods were willing to look past their differences and pool their resources. My home is located in a distant corner of the multiverse, but this threatens it too. Even if I wasn’t willing to take you as my heir, I’d do everything in my power to ensure your people prevail.”

Nick thought his interrogation was over, but it had only just begun. Now that Zero understood the circumstances enabling his explosive growth, the scientist requested an exhaustive report regarding the state of Earth’s technology pre-integration. He grilled Nick on computers—everything from components to power sources to operating systems, peripherals, and personal data devices.

They moved on to servers, satellites, and data centers. Zero nodded with approval at Nick’s description of the internet, streaming services, smartphones, and the global communication grid. After taking a break for lunch, they wrapped up the session by discussing machine learning, large language models, and all the ways humanity had begun to integrate those technologies before the System arrived.

Under his mentor’s guidance, Nick was able to recall a considerable amount regarding several programming languages, including the commands he used to build websites, interact with his hard drive, modify his operating system, and troubleshoot problems with his hardware.

Zero was fascinated by Nick’s life as a professional gamer. He hadn’t run into cultures that had created video games other than his own, although magic could generate elaborate simulations that overlapped with virtual and augmented reality. The man recreated some classics from scratch based on Nick’s descriptions and had them up and running within minutes.

Seeing some of the games from his childhood, even if they weren’t perfect copies, filled him with a wave of nostalgia and made him wonder how his friends and family were doing, if any of them were alive at all. He was drawn out of the past and into the present when the scientist clapped and rose from his chair.

“I never thought I would say these words, but you are an ideal candidate to receive my legacy. Your knowledge of how computers work is woefully insufficient, but with that high Mind and Creativity and my mentorship, we can remedy that in short order. What’s important is that you comprehend the benefits electronic technology provides, how code interacts with a wide range of devices, and how science can be augmented with digital tools. I’m thrilled that you’ve received rudimentary training in research methodology during your formal education, although I wish you had more practical experience constructing and conducting experiments.

“Regardless, we will accomplish great things together in the centuries to come. In the short-term, I can help you survive the peril that threatens your world, contributing to the universe’s salvation in the process. I can’t remember the last time I was this excited. Well then, lad, why are you standing there? It’s time to get to work!”

That statement marked the start of the most intensive training of Nick’s life. Zero intended to bestow the first stage of his legacy fast enough to defend Earth from the monster stampedes, although the man wasn’t sure if Nick would make it back in time for the start of the first wave.

He was anxious to protect his people, but he was confident Team Earth could hold out against the weakest monsters without his help, particularly since the System would be switching the barrier on and off to divide the horde into manageable bites.

Zero believed Nick would be able to defeat the Tier-3 spider after forming a Mana Array, which was the goal of the initial stage of his tutelage. Now that Nick understood just how powerful Heritages were, he was certain that securing access to the hall was the best way he could help humanity in the time he had available, not to mention the personal gains he would receive from inheriting Zero’s legacy.

While the monsters were a deadly threat, Earth wouldn’t be safe until Slaughter’s forces had been dealt with too. After seeing them in his vision, he knew his present power was insufficient to take on the deity’s elites—even after the explosive growth he’d experienced from absorbing the dreadbeast monarchs.
