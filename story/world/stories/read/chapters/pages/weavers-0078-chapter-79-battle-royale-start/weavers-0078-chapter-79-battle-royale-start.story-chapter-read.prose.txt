Chapter 79 - Battle Royale Start

The ground shook.

Kaelin staggered, her boots skidding on stone as the entire floor of the colosseum rippled beneath her feet. Across the wide circle, dozens of students wobbled, some nearly toppling as the dry, compact earth cracked open. Beneath, some wetter soil pushed upward, rolling in great swells as though a very large mole had clawed its way toward the surface.

Professor Reed stepped forwards in the main speaking platform, normally chaired by commentators during matches, and raised both arms. Her violet cloak streamed like banners behind her, hair catching in the updraft as she waved her hands like she was conducting an orchestra, with grace and elegance.

The ground obeyed her will. Smooth stone warped into jagged ridges. A hill rose like the back of some enormous beast, splitting the colosseum’s centre with a grinding roar. A single deep crevasse yawned open, plunging down into darkness that seemed bottomless.

Kaelin dropped into a crouch, breath sharp, her palms pressed to her knees to steady herself. She could feel the vibration of the threads running through the earth, thrumming in her bones as if her own heartbeat had synced with Reed’s magic. The professor wasn’t holding back. Not today. This was the true pinnacle of magic.

With a flick of Reed’s wrist, grass surged through the cracks, weaving into tangled mats slick with dew. Vines snapped forward, uncoiling like snakes, lashing across stone and ridge alike. Great trees erupted skyward, trunks splitting rock with thunderous cracks, their branches spreading wide to cast jagged shadows across the battlefield. The smell of crushed leaves and raw earth rolled through the arena, thick enough to taste.

The crowd roared, their voices rising in a tidal wave of excitement that crashed down on the combatants.

Kaelin looked behind her, curious as to how the spectators might be able to watch. But no sooner was her question answered, as the stands themselves seemed to rise up from the ground. Professor Reed must have been tasked with doing this as well.

Kaelin's right hand tapped against the rock subconsciously, matching it to her pulse. The fingers on her other hand twitched against the hilt at her side, a nervous rhythm she couldn’t stop. She scanned the field as it transformed, she couldn't see much due to the foliage, but from what was nearby she could see boulders for cover, roots to help make traps, trees for ambushes. It wasn’t just an arena anymore. It was more akin to the wilderness.

Professor Reed’s voice rang out, clear and commanding, carrying even over the noise.“This will be your final test. No grades are given, but the winner shall gain the privilege of being known as the strongest in the entire academy, and not least the youth of Erundal.”

Kaelin swallowed hard. The words sent a thrill racing through her chest, equal parts terror and exhilaration. Around her, students shifted into stances, drawing steel, bracing for the chaos that was about to unfold.

Her gaze flicked to the far edge of the arena. Zephyr stood calm as ever, threads already dancing at his fingertips, his expression unreadable. Kana sat up in the stands, staring Aric down, tensing in case of any slight change in expression.

And Kaelin? She took one more breath, deep and steady, forcing the shake from her hands. The earth itself had been remade beneath her feet, and she had no illusions about what this battle would demand from her, as well as what it might draw out from the other competitors.

It wasn’t just about strength. It was about cunning. And survival. Like the tortoise and the hare, staying back and watching from a distance might just be the best strategy.

The roar of the crowd swelled again as Reed’s hands fell, the final signal.

The entire colosseum erupted into cheer.

The ground was still quaking when the blades clashed.

A student from the northern wing hurled a volley of rocks and stones across the field, only for it to be swallowed up by a wall of earth. The battle had begun in full, loud and bright and merciless.

Kaelin ducked behind the base of a newly formed pine tree as a spray of stone shards embedded themselves in the bark where her head had been.

“So much for showing some restraint,” she muttered under her breath, glancing toward the professor’s platform. Reed was watching from above, arms crossed, the faintest smile touching her lips.

Kaelin’s eyes flicked back to the field. Already, some students were grouping up, five to the west, three to the north, shouting orders as if this were a military exercise. It was madness. There could only be one winner. And yet, watching them, Kaelin couldn’t help but smirk.

“Reminds me of that play…” she murmured to herself. “What was it called again? The Hungry Game? By, uh… Suzanne Collins? No... Willhelm Shakespeare? Whoever it was, doesn't matter.”

Kaelin jumped again as a set of bramble whips struck the tree, far too close for comfort. She clenched her fists with a newfound resolve, pressed a hand against the trunk, and began to move.

Speed, distance, time. That was all that mattered. She just needed to keep moving.

She dashed between trees, the sound of chaos following her like an echo. Her boots barely touched the roots before she was moving again, weaving through brush that hadn’t existed five minutes ago. Her eyes flicked from target to target, calculating.

She wasn’t the strongest here. She knew that, but she didn’t need to be. At least... not physically.

A group of three students, one from Mind, two from Nature, blocked her path ahead. She didn’t wait for them to notice her. Threads shimmered in her mind, she imagined them fine with a yellow-ish tint, and time started to bend beneath her fingertips.

She whispered the pattern under her breath.“Slow—”

And so the world slowed.

Leaves hung in the air like drifting glass. The motion of the three students froze mid-step, expressions caught between determination and surprise. Kaelin darted forward, heart pounding, her movements slicing through the stillness. She slipped past them slashing their achilles tendon with the weapon whcih sad sat in its hilt by her side. After a second had passed, the spell collapsed, everything snapping back into motion with a heavy whoomph of displaced air.

She could only maintain the spell for a single second at the most, but it was all she needed. The combination of this and an ambush worked far too well.

They spun, realizing too late as they fell to the ground in a heap, pained screams echoing from their lungs.

Kaelin didn’t look back.

While saying the words for the spell weren't necessary, they sometimes helped her visualise the spell, especially in tense situations such as this one.

Another pair of duelling students tumbled from a nearby ridge, one bending one of the trees over to the side with her magic to work as a shield as the other rained down jagged pieces of rock. A third joined in from nowhere, driving a blade into the ground and sending vines whipping outward. Kaelin vaulted over them, grabbing a low branch and hauling herself into the canopy.

From above, the arena looked even more alive—shifting pockets of battle, miniature wars unfolding and ending in seconds. The hill Reed had summoned earlier now divided the battlefield into quadrants, and students were being funnelled toward choke points like prey.

Kaelin crouched against a branch, catching her breath, the faint pulse of time threads still burning beneath her skin. She could feel every heartbeat of the forest. Or was that just her imagination?

Either way, she needed to last. That was all. Survive long enough to matter, and maybe even win.

Kaelin heard a rustle from below.

She peered down through the leaves. A boy probably the same age as her, but broader, was creeping forward, a longsaword raised. His threads glimmered faintly green; Nature magic. His gaze swept upward, toward her.

“Don’t even think about it,” she whispered.

He threw his hand out—roots erupted from the ground, twisting toward the tree she perched on. Kaelin reacted instantly. Her eyes flared silver as she reached out with a thought, brushing the threads that tied the branch to time’s flow.

Decay.

The wood beneath her cracked. A pulse of accelerated age spread through it like ink in water. The bark greyed, the sap dried, and the entire branch crumbled away beneath her just as the roots lashed toward it.

Kaelin dropped.

She hit the ground in a crouch, rolling once, branches and dust scattering around her. Before the boy could react, she cast another spell.

The air thinned around her, adn she darted forward

He had no idea what was about to happen. Kaelin slipped past him, every step light and fast. The spell broke around her as she gave a jab to the back of his neck, rendering him unconscious.

“No hard feelings,” she muttered, not turning back. “You were in my way."

She ducked under a low arch of roots, boots crunching against broken stone, her breath coming faster now. Each spell tugged at her reserves, a little more each time. Using Time wasn’t like wielding a blade, it demanded focus, precision, restraint. Push too far, and the recoil could crush her mind like glass, albeit only temporarily.

Her ears caught the sound of another fight breaking out somewhere near the ridge. She crept toward the noise, peering through the foliage.

A group of five, again, teaming up. They were using the terrain well, herding loners into a trap near a pit Professor Reed had formed earlier. She watched as one poor student was caught by a web of vines, his weapon wrenched from his grip and flung into the abyss which was the large crevasse running through the centre of the arena.

Kaelin frowned. “That’s going to get ugly fast.”

Her instincts screamed at her to stay out of it. But her mind was already working, deciding on routes, weighing up possible futures. She could use this chaos. Remove some of the competition, find a better position, there were many advantages.

She let her magic loose just beneath the surface, but she didn't trigger any, just getting ready to.

“Alright,” she whispered. “Let’s dance, shall we?”

She sprinted forward, hitting the edge of the slope at full speed, then threw herself sideways into a slide. Dirt and grass tore beneath her as she skidded to a stop behind one of the ambushers. Before the girl could react, Kaelin reached out and tapped her shoulder, just gave it a touch.

Time stuttered.

The girl froze mid-turn, her own attack hanging inches from Kaelin’s face. Kaelin ducked under it, spun, and shoved the girl backward. The spell snapped, momentum reclaiming its grip, the girl’s own swing carried her off-balance and straight into the pit.

Kaelin didn’t stay to watch.

“My bad,” she called out, voice light despite the pounding in her chest.

Another student shouted her name, maybe she was finally getting some recognition. Before she could find out, she bolted into the trees, adrenaline sharpening every sense. Her lungs burned. The scent of sap and her own sweat hung heavy in the air around her, mingling with the revolting scent of some shit she must have slid in next to the slope.

How it made its way there was anyone's guess. The only animals around were humans after all...

All around her, the event continued. The once-flat arena was now a battlefield of smoke, dust, and noise.

Kaelin paused beneath a canopy of leaves, forcing herself to breathe slow. The world shimmered faintly at the edges of her vision, a warning that her future sight was bleeding through without her permission. She shut her eyes tight, bringing herself back to sanity.

"Phew, that was close. Can't let it get out of hand." Kaelin muttered.

A crash snapped her back. To her left, a tree splintered under an intense impact. She turned just in time to see a pair of students charge toward her through the dust left in its wake. One held a large stone gauntlet, the other, nothing. He seemed to be a time wielder judging by the way the other one was able to put so much power into his punch.

“Great,” she muttered. “More idiots, announcing their presence.”

She spun, darting deeper into the faux-forest, twisting through roots and debris. The air hummed as spells flew past. One slit her sleeve, managing to draw blood, another blasted a crater into the earth just behind her. She skidded around a boulder, boots slipping in mud, and whispered another quick spell.

The world slowed again. Or more like it had sped up around her.

Kaelin exhaled and launched herself up the ridge, leaping from rock to rock. As she deactivated the spell, sounds and movements jolted into motion around her.

She crouched there, half-hidden by a fallen log, heartbeat thrumming like thunder in her ears.

From this height, she could see almost the entire field. Dozens still fought, some wounded, others retreating. Reed’s trees loomed tall, swaying in the wind of battle.

Kaelin’s gaze swept across the arena—and froze.

Near the far wall, Aric was standing with the other professors, arms folded. His expression was anxious. He seemed like he was about to explode, shuddering and vibrating like an alarm clock.

Something about it chilled her. He wasn’t focused on the battle at all. His head was up in the clouds, but somehow he still seemed focused on something. She looked over to where Kana had been standing, but she was already gone.

Kaelin tore her gaze away, a shiver crawling up her spine.

"I Just gotta trust Kana, I know she's got my back. If anything happens, I can leave it to her. At least until this is all over."
