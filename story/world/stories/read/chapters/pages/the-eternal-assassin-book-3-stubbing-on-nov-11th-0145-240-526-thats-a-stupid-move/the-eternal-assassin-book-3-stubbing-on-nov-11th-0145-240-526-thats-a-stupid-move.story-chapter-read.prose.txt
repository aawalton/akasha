True to his word, the assassin spilled everything to the nearest city guard when Asher walked him over to one. Chit, Asher learned he was named, calmly and succinctly described all the different people he’d killed over the years of working in the Assassin’s Guild, and the startled guard who had been patrolling immediately shouted for backup before securing the man and scribbling down everything Chit was saying. Even now, Asher half expected the assassin to wait for the opportune moment before making his move, but it seemed he truly did want to go to prison.

Asher supposed when the alternative was the Assassin’s Guild hunting you down, a life in highly secured prison might not be the worst option.

Three more guards sprinted onto the scene, racing over to the mass-murderer with a variety of movement-based skills. Right before they could tackle him, Chit turned and mouthed ‘ashes’ to Asher, while nodding back toward the processing plant he’d been discovered within. That was the last thing he managed, as he was immediately slammed into by three guards who took him to the ground and began further cuffing him with a variety of manacles. Asher thought the reaction would have been a tad extreme, other than the fact that Chit had quite literally just admitted to almost a hundred murders during his time with the Guild.

Rather than sit around watching Chit’s fate, Asher left them to it, wanting to check on the processing plant before the guards could get Chit sorted out and taken away. When he’d asked earlier how exactly Chit was planning to share the location of the other cells, the assassin had merely promised him he’d use a skill once he was safely within the arms of the law.

Slipping back into the building, Asher blinked as he immediately noticed what Chit had done. The large, empty floor was now covered in a fine layer of ash. Except rather than just one blanket layer covering everything, the ash had been shifted around quite carefully, creating a large-scale map of the fourth level of Dormaul.

That’s an impressive skill… Asher thought, wondering just how capable Chit was if he could do something like this not only without looking, but entirely at range. Unfortunately, he wasn’t able to Incorporate the map made entirely out of ashes, so Asher yanked some paper out of his rift and did his best to scribble down the locations. Just as Chit had promised, the assassin had highlighted seven known locations for the Guild, even going so far as to add an actual finger pointing to one of them. Asher understood the hint, and he grinned in anticipation of finally getting to take down yet another one of the Guild’s cornerstones. Eight and Talon had been powerful opponents to go up against, so he had high hopes for this latest Finger.

With his new targets in mind, Asher did his best to kick the ash-map apart, not wanting any of the guards to stumble into the processing plant looking for evidence and get to the Guild cells before he could. That done, he slipped right back out of the plant, making a beeline for the first cell. If he played his cards right, he just might be able to get in a quick warm up or two before going after the Finger themselves.

To his surprise, the first cell Chit directed him toward was actually a building shockingly close to the city’s main entrance. Only a few blocks from where he’d first entered Dormaul with Samantha and Brian, Asher peered at a squat building that looked abandoned. It really went to show how little the nobles of Dormaul cared about the fourth level when the Guild was able to hold ground this close to the entrance to the city. There were two or three ‘homeless’ people lounging around outside the building, each one with the assassin class and dark auras that reeked of death. Anyone with Identify or a similar skill should have picked up on this cell immediately, meaning it was either blatant corruption, or the guards were exceptionally lazy.

Using Distortion to slip around to the back of the building, Asher looked inside with Astral Dip, grinning as Chit’s lead proved itself to be legit. The first room he poked his head into was filled with potential targets. There were two assassins working on loading up some sort of poison into darts at a shoddy table in the middle of the room, another assassin scribbling away at something in the corner, and a fourth assassin practicing with throwing knives along the far wall. Each of the four assassins only held a single element to their name and were on the younger side, meaning they couldn’t be senior assassins. Though based on their assortment of dark and pain-filled auras, they were certainly doing their best to get there.

Which made them the perfect appetizer.

Pulling his head back, Asher walked along the outside of the building, correcting his position. Then, he slipped into the astral, stepping entirely into the building and finding himself immediately behind the assassin scribbling to themselves in the corner. Peering over their shoulder, Asher shook his head at what they were drawing. As far as he could tell, it looked like the assassin was attempting to come up with a cool tattoo design that incorporated the nine people they had already killed. They’d circled their poorly drawn dragon with nine spikes jutting out of its back so far, and crossed out both the wolf with nine claws and the man with nine daggers jutting out of his back.

Deciding to do tattoo-artists everywhere a favor, Asher cancelled his skill and returned to the material realm. Using a tiny Spatial Lock to block sound, he slid Nail into the assassin’s back, ignoring the startled wheeze of air as it fled from the dying man’s lungs.

Asher grabbed the man’s shards and vanished again, only having existed in the material realm for barely a second in order to carry out his first kill. The assassin now lay heavily against the wall with his clipboard and quill still in hand, looking to anyone as if they were just resting. Asher barely needed to bother, however, as the others were far too engrossed in their own tasks to notice anything.

Deciding the knife-thrower was probably the most distracted, Asher ignored her for a moment as he walked over to the table, peering down at the vials of poison the next two assassins were busy dripping into darts. The man and woman combo had similar auras and similar faces, leading him to believe they were related. He briefly wondered what properly fighting against a brother and sister assassin team would be like, before shaking his head and deciding to do this right. He wasn’t planning on giving any of the liches a chance to fight back if he could help it, which meant at least every now and again he needed to practice a far more delicate method of assassination than his regular one so he didn’t screw things up when it really mattered.

He wasn’t able to close the full distance between him and the table before he was forced to drop Astral Dip, but that was fine. He was only fifteen feet away, so he formed a tunnel of compressed space, stepping through and blurring into existence between the two assassins before slamming his two daggers into each of their necks. Again, his Spatial Lock blocked the sounds of them dying and their chairs scraping against the floor from reaching the knife-thrower across the room, and he let their torsos slump down onto the table, scattering the poison-filled darts. While he could pull their bodies into his rift, he wanted to leave a message for anyone else who thought trying to rebuild the Assassin’s Guild after he was done with them was a good idea.

Snatching each of their shards, Asher didn’t even bother slipping back into the astral as he approached the final assassin. This one probably wouldn’t have made it very far even if Asher hadn’t shown up, seeing as they thought practicing their knife-throwing with their back to a handful of other assassins had been a good idea. His class removed the sound of his footsteps, and Asher quickly found himself standing just behind the woman as she hurled dagger after dagger into the crude target scratched into the wall with what looked like a person’s face in the dead center. This cell clearly wasn’t well funded, and out of pity, Asher let her finish her current set of knives before he struck.

Managing to hit a bullseye with her final knife, the blade lodging itself perfectly into the poorly-drawn face on the wall, the woman pumped a fist, smiling to herself at her accuracy.

Her final moment on this realm was one of celebration as Asher’s dagger claimed her life just as easily as the three before her. The last of the regular assassins fell to the floor, and Asher looked around the room, nodding at his efficiency as he collected her shards. While taking out targets silently and without a fight wasn’t his preferred method of assassination, even he had to admit it had its uses.

Just as he was deciding how he wanted to handle the senior assassin who should be somewhere within the Guild cell, the far door swung open and a man with a rough beard stepped out, going to shout something before his voice died in his throat.

[Assassin] - 2/3 elements

There we go, Asher grinned, watching as the man’s eyes first bounced from one dead assassin to the next, before finally landing on him. The senior assassin’s aura was crumbly and dense, giving the sense of crushing any who stood before him and burying them alive under his weight. Snarling, the man cracked his neck, grabbing a hammer off his back as he stared at him.

“The Reaper,” he spat, tapping his hammer into one hand as he narrowed his eyes. “Six said you’d be back sooner or later.”

“Six was right,” Asher laughed, already beginning to wonder what the man’s elements might be. “I see you already know my name. Care to share yours? Otherwise I’ll just have to remember you as ‘hammer guy’ whenever I think back to the different senior assassins I’ve killed.”

“Don’t think just because you managed to take down an alchemist and a bird that you’re hot stuff,” the assassin said, his eyes never leaving Asher’s own. “Though I do appreciate you coming here all on your own. Killing you will make the Head promote me as Finger over whichever of the two cities I want.”

“Going with ‘hammer guy’ after all, huh? Not what I would choose, but I suppose it’s your tombstone.”

“They call me Rubble,” the assassin grinned, revealing yellowing teeth as his entire body suddenly rippled, becoming hard and rocky as the man effectively turned into a humanoid pile of gravel. The loose gravel trailed up his hammer as well, making it nearly three times larger and more like a massive maul than anything one would use in construction. “Let’s hope you’re stronger than you look.”

With that, the talking was done. Rubble made the first move, lunging forward far faster than Asher would have expected from a talking pile of rocks as he swung his new maul directly at Asher’s head. A quick use of Distortion made the powerful swing fall short as he expanded the space between him and his opponent’s attack, and Asher took advantage of Rubble’s opening, ramming Nail straight into the man’s side. The masterwork iron blade punched through the stone without issue, but when he was forced to leap back to dodge a follow up strike, he noted the lack of blood on his dagger.

A skill just like Loratta’s Slime Body, he thought, curious how to best go about taking down a gravel-man. He could tell that the hole his dagger had punched through the man’s side wasn’t refilling with rock thanks to Sever the Thread, but he certainly didn’t seem fazed by the attack. Not even bothering with Astral Dip, Asher used Distortion to continue forcing Rubble’s telegraphed attacks to fall short, all the while dancing around the room as he contemplated his options.

Cutting off his head would probably be easiest, but I’m not actually sure if even mithral can cut through solid rock all that well. It can certainly punch through it, no doubt about that, but cut? Maybe not. Still, can’t hurt to try.

Asher waited for another of Rubble’s large swings, choosing to slip into the astral and let the attack phase straight through him. As soon as the maul had passed, he stepped closer, returning to the material realm as he slashed out with Crescent. Though rather than letting him test his improved strength and Crescent’s cutting ability against solid stone, Rubble apparently had another plan in mind.

The moment Crescent so much as scraped against the man’s throat, he exploded.
