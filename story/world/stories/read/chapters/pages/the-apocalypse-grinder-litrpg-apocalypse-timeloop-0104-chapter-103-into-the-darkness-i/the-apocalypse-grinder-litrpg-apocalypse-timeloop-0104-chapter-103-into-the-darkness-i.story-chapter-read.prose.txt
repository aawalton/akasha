Ronan had taken the juggernaut class a second time. He’d told himself that it would only be for one iteration, in order to earn the merit for evolving to an epic class, but that hadn’t worked out.

To him, it seemed a fair compromise to at least keep the class until he could accomplish said achievement and pocket the chronos merit it would grant him. Then, and only then, would he devote all of his time and focus towards mastering system-less magic.

Gurz’krax the elite goblin warrior blinked, and Ronan crossed the distance of ten paces by the time it opened its eyes. The elite warrior raised its greatsword a single millimetre before it was decapitated.

Sacrificial strike had dealt a wounding blow to the elite warrior, and from then on it had been a losing battle for the tenacious goblin. In the end, it was only a lv.20 goblin. Ronan had faced far worse and lived—or, well, died—to tell the tale.

Transference of momentum was a universally devastating force, no matter the weapon used to direct it into his target. Ronan stood still, offering the chivalrous goblin warrior a moment of silence out of respect for how it carried itself in their tense duel.

His newly tiered-up sword mastery meant he would be even deadlier in a war of attrition. Plus, from the moment he reached tier II, the sword had settled into his grip with a little more ease.

Before, it had felt like a tool he used regularly, and was comfortable wielding. Now, it was as though he’d been training for half a decade and the sword was beginning to become a part of his natural movements.

Against the assassination attempt of the goblin rogue, Ronan wondered if a sword gave him an increased chance of survival compared to a club. He would soon find out.

As it turns out, a sword isn’t much more of a deterrent to a determined assassin than a flaming club. Although, Ronan was sure his blind counter-attack was far more lethal this time.

Partly because the sharp edge of a sword was a more precise instrument for applying pain than a club, but mostly because he hadn’t waited for the goblin rogue to strike first. The moment he’d taken the same number of steps into the darkness as he remembered taking in the last iteration, he preemptively swung his sword, and felt it biting into flesh.

Unfortunately, he’d still been assassinated. Whether that was because he and the goblin rogue had struck simultaneously, or because there was more than one goblin rogue hiding in the shadows was a mystery that remained unsolved.
