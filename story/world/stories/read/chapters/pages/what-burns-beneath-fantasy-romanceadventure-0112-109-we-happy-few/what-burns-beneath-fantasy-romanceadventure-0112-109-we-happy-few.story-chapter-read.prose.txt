Dear Bernard was a balding older man with deep crow’s feet and narrow features. He sat cross-legged amidst a spread of black blankets and soft pillows with a book in one hand and a steaming mug in the other. A silver-tipped cane and a leather satchel lay beside him.

He eyed me wryly as Alora stalked back across the chamber, every step resounding hard against the glass. Heated voices echoed once she rejoined the distant throng of Fae.

“They’re like a big, dysfunctional family.” Bernard set his book down with a raspy chuckle. Then he leaned over, voice dropping to a conspiratorial pitch. “I suppose that’s what happens when a species falls to its last legs, eh? They all come together, whether it’s love or hate in the binding. Less than twenty of them left, and salt if some of them truly don’t have anyone else.”

Settled among the pillows, with my view of the water lessened, my heartbeat grew steady. I eyed the man curiously.

“They’ll snarl and snap, but things won’t get too ugly. How can they, when they’re stuck together for eternity?” He squinted at the distant argument. “Though dear Alora might draw blood on your lord.”

I glanced around. Velidan had not given instructions about speaking to other humans. But Bernard and I were the only two figures nearby, so I answered softly, “She already did.”

Thin, lined lips quirked into a pleased purse. Then Bernard cleared his throat, folded his hands, and said much more gravely, “Young lady, I am incredibly sorry for the situation you find yourself in. Please know that you are very safe right now; dear Alora is not the strongest creature here, but she is the oldest and most respected. A matriarch to the rest, you could say. You’ll suffer no harm in her presence.”

The statement was only reassuring for a moment. Right, but presumably Velidan gave me all his rules and warnings for a reason, not just to rattle me. Which means he doesn’t expect Alora to stick around.

He continued, “I pray the rumors of your treatment were exaggerated, and that you find contentment with your new life. I can offer very little, at the moment, but all that I have is yours.”

I curiously inquired about what exactly Bernard had. He showed me a book of sonnets written by satyrs, his mug of spicy-smelling tea, etched playing cards, a sheaf of vellum with charcoal for sketching, and an assortment of snacks he assured me were safe to eat. All this furthered my confusion and led to questions about who Bernard was.

He told me the tale as we watched Fae mill and chatter, all of them ignoring us. He was a widower fisherman from the outskirts of Respite who, about six years ago, went to bed one night and woke up dangling in the claws of a great gray beast. After an ordeal that he calmly declared he would not speak of, he’d been placed for auction in the Pit.

The lady Alora had bought him in blood. He gave no details on what this meant, but he lifted his chin when he said it and claimed that afterwards she’d been kindly asked to never return to the Pit again. She’d spent weeks helping Bernard recover- apparently Velidan’s powers of healing were exclusive to him- then offered to return the poor man home.

But he’d been rescued by ‘a creature from dreams and myth, and a beautiful one at that’ and asked to stay with her instead. She’d been stunned. They’d bickered about the logistics of his staying, and enjoyed the bickering so much that they kept at it for the next six years.

“Are you feeling better?” a soft voice interrupted. Liana approached us, cerulean light rippling across her features and large, liquid eyes shining. She sank to the blanket beside me. “Lord Aravis said to stay away from the argument, earlier, but I could tell you were very frightened.”

“I’m alright now, thank you. That,” I nodded to the floor, still leery of it but no longer struck numb, “was just… jarring.”

“You won’t have to walk around much,” Bernard assured me, shifting with the rustle of cloth and the creak of aging bones. He pointed at a long, iron-wrought table and two dozen cushioned chairs towards the cavern’s center. “We’ll pass time over here while they all growl at each other over breakfast. Oh, speaking of which… here, Li.” He slid the sketching materials towards her.

Her face brightened.

So it’s a routine. All of this. High Fae gather together for one reason or another, and some of them bring humans along. And we sit in the corner and behave ourselves, exactly like salted pets. The swell of anger faltered as I considered the two people beside me. Bernard and Liana seemed… well, like calm, regular people. They weren’t broken or dispirited, unless they were hiding it extraordinarily well.

I looked further up the room, to where the last two humans- twin blonde women a bit older than me, both clad in blushingly scant ribbons of fabric- sat strumming harps. My mouth went dry every time I looked towards the pair. They wore bright smiles and blushed beneath any glances, but I didn’t think for a moment that their mirth could be genuine. Velidan’s sickening rumble of plaything came to mind.

Saints. How much do things change when Alora and the other kind Fae leave? Perhaps I don’t want to know.

“They’ll come over in a bit,” Bernard said wryly, following my gaze. “I bet they’re dying to meet you. Just don’t be…” he bared crooked teeth in a wince, searching for the right word. “Offended.”

My nose scrunched. “Why would I-”

“Do you require anything, Liana?” Boots clicked against glass as Aravis approached. He knelt beside her. One large hand cupped her cheek.

I stiffened, watching for her to flinch at the touch, to show a flicker of fear, anything. But she stayed perfectly calm.

The young woman gave him a faint smile. “No, thank you. I’m very content.”

“So I see.” His mouth twitched as he watched her carefully etch thin curves of charcoal down the vellum. The expression on the Fae’s handsome face was so nakedly adoring that I nearly blushed, feeling as though I was intruding on a private moment. He pressed a kiss to her empty palm, rose, and drifted away.

I watched with parted lips and a thrumming heartbeat. I don’t think she is pacted. What if some of them genuinely care about each other?

“What are you drawing today, Liana?” Bernard’s voice was very soft.

“I don’t know, yet.” She paused for a breath. The fingers of one hand curled, then slowly traced the smooth skin of her wrist. “But I think it will be something very nice.”

My neck prickled, and a quick glance around showed Velidan in a small group nearby. He was watching me thoughtfully. His gaze trailed to Aravis, then flicked back to me. He said nothing and seemed entirely absorbed in listening to a black-haired man’s chatter, but the question in one quirked brow was clear. ‘Well?’

I sucked in a breath. Right. I’m here to try and overhear things, to try and pick up clues about tonight. Not to sit on a pile of pillows.

I was very aware of the heavy, amused gaze that watched as I inched from blankets to glass. Time had lent me courage and I made my way towards Velidan on working, if shaking, legs.

My spine was ramrod straight by the time I reached him. I braced for the laughs, the leers, for someone else to yank at my cloak. Velidan handed me his drink without so much as a glance. I took it with wide eyes and baited breath.

Then I stood there.

I spent the next half-hour as an end table. Apparently anyone in arms’ reach expected me to hold their wine. This meant I could no longer clutch my mantle, and it was left dangling open as I stood there with full hands and burning cheeks. But, aside from a few glances, my existence was ignored.

At first I was attentive. My ears pricked anytime Embriel opened his mouth, but I heard nothing that seemed significant. The men- who made up the vast majority of the gathering- all congregated in one throng. They discussed regional food supplies with grimaces, snarled over the dwindling numbers of lesser Fae and which ones needed protection versus which ones were ‘better used in the Pit,’ and argued about terrain boundaries.

There were only five High Fae women, and they lounged nearby. I couldn’t overhear any of their conversation. Saffine wore a gown of spiderwebbed topazes and chattered effusively while Alora glared at her. Saerith stood rigid, pearly eyes distant and lips a bloodless line. Two red-haired women who looked like sisters seemed very bored with it all. Vessa was nowhere in sight.

I started noticing more things below me as the conversations droned on. The expanse of water was as terrifying as it was amazing. Some of the seaweed bore vivid blooms of teal and pink. Small, glowing fish trailed by in small schools, leaving paths of light beneath my feet. Occasionally something large would glide close enough that I’d gasp at the vibrant, shimmering scales.

“Excuse me.”

I snapped my gaze up. Embriel’s boots clicked as he hurried down the cavern, towards…

I blinked.

A rotund, red-faced human man in a dirty apron was peeking in through the archway. He was goggle-eyed and wringing his hands. A frizzy-haired woman in a flour-spattered frock appeared beside him. She took one look at the throng of mesmerizing outfits and beautiful faces and squeaked, ‘saints above!’ while clapping both hands to her cheeks.

Embriel swept to them, beaming and dipping a little bow. The trio spoke quietly.

I peered up at Velidan, who looked as puzzled as I felt. Then there was a gentle tug at my mantle. I turned to two identical, doe-eyed faces. Blonde tresses, the flowery scent of perfume, and an astonishing amount of exposed skin pressed towards me.

“May we steal her, my lord?” One of the twins whispered, clasping my hands.

“Please?” The other murmured, looking through her lashes at Velidan. “We’ll be very sweet.”

I don’t know what message I conveyed to him with my eyes, but the wicked glee in his own seemed to indicate that he found it very funny. He practically purred an approval, and I was led back to the pillows.

Bernard watched our approach with a helpless, slightly apologetic smile. Liana didn’t look up.

One of the women smothered a giggle. “Lord Velidan is very handsome.”

The other gazed longingly at my necklace. “He’s given you such lovely things. Lord Ravar gives us nice gifts, too.”

“He’s given us our very own seamstress! And a maid, though she hisses at us a lot. But he tells her to be nice. You’re so beautiful, I’m sure lord Velidan is very good to you.”

They tittered and leaned close, whispering it all in a deluge while they stroked my hair and told me how lucky I was. I sat squinting at the pair, biting back every answer that tried to claw its way out and trying to figure out if this was some kind of bizarre joke.

“Girls.” Gentle steel edged Bernard’s voice. He gave them a stern look. “Our new friend is still adjusting.”

The twin on my left blushed. “Oh, I didn’t mean to be rude. I’m Shay.”

“I’m Sera.” The one on my right smiled encouragingly. “What’s your name?”

“Brin.”

Sera’s eyes were shining. “How did lord Velidan find you, Brin? Did he come upon you by moonlight?”

Shay sighed, draping herself across the pillows. “Oh, it must have been so romantic! Were you bathing in a stream?”

“No.” My gaze flicked towards Velidan, who was alternating between watching Embriel and tilting a wicked smirk my way. “I wasn’t.”

Sera’s full lower lip disappeared beneath pearly teeth. “We heard that terrible story. But of course it can’t be true!”

“Ladies.” Bernard’s tone sharpened.

Shay rolled to her belly with a gasp. “Was it bandits? Cruel men? We heard you’d been tied up!”

“Ladies.”

Sera squealed. “Did lord Velidan save you from them? And then he brought you to his home and promised he’d keep you safe forever!”

I gaped at the shining faces before me. Them, too? How can all the humans here be happy?! Although… ashes, maybe it’s because only the happy humans are allowed. Maybe anyone who mistreats a human has the good sense not to bring them when Alora’s around.

“We lived in Crossbern, beyond the northern mountains.” Shay twisted a strand of hair around one finger. “Lord Ravar found us in the wintertime. We were very poor, you see, and had gone deep in the woods to forage, but a terrible storm fell.”

Sera looked with blatant adoration towards a broad-shouldered Fae. “He was so kind, even though we were terrified. We screamed at him for a long time and he just smiled. Then he helped us find shelter.”

“He said he’d wanted to come see the mortal realm, and that he very much liked what he saw!” Shay giggled, shifting to her knees. “Then he stole us away! Well, he asked very nicely if we’d like to be stolen.”

Sera beamed. “Now we live in lord Ravar’s home. It’s always warm there, and he makes sure we have enough to eat.”

Shay took my hand. “But we’ve heard lord Velidan’s home is the nicest of all! The others are very jealous. Will you tell us about it?”

I shook my head through the disorienting deluge of information. Another flick of my eyes showed Velidan chuckling, though whether it was at his conversation or mine, I couldn’t tell.

“I’ve heard there’s a garden there.” Liana’s gaze traced the path of a small red fish gliding beneath the glass. “I had a garden, once. I grew flowers and herbs, and it always smelled so lovely. Lord Aravis says he’ll find me another one soon.”

“There is one,” I said. “It’s incredible.”

“I’d love to hear about it.”

I obliged, quietly describing the enchanting beauty of Velidan’s home. The humans around me listened in rapt silence.

“Most of the Court’s land isn’t like that,” Bernard murmured when I finished. He nodded towards covered platters being brought in by stout, gray-skinned little creatures. They looked like goblins, but denser and with sunken, broad features. “A few of the lords and dryads and such have managed crops, and your lord Velidan donates a fair bit to the market, but the food still grows foul in many places. It makes a lot of the lesser Fae sick.”

I thought of last night’s bleeding mushroom, then of Velidan’s comments about roses with teeth and forests that tried to murder the living. My stomach churned.

“Thank you all for your patience!” Embriel boomed from the archway, where he stood beaming with a hand on each of the red-faced humans’ shoulders. “My good friends here weren’t used to Caravain’s kitchen. Entirely my fault.”

“And who exactly are your good friends?” Alora’s eyes were sharp, sweeping across the pair with obvious suspicion.

“In due time, Alora,” he purred, grinning from ear to ear. “Please, everyone, have a seat.”

Fae began drifting towards the table. Embriel and the two humans with him moved towards its head, where three chairs sat very close together. The woman’s face turned scarlet as he pulled a chair out for her.

“We’ll sit here and eavesdrop,” Bernard whispered to me, winking. “If they start arguing about regional boundaries, try not to fall asl-”

“I did mean everyone.” Embriel was smiling towards our little group. “Mortals, please. Join your masters.”

Masters. I swallowed the bile as I turned an uncertain gaze towards the equally uncertain faces around me. Apparently this was a new occurrence.

Silver hair whipped against Alora’s cheek as she looked from our host to us, then towards the flustered people at Embriel’s side. Her eyes narrowed even further.

Sera and Shay hesitated, but only for a moment. Then they gave little squeals and flounced over, joining the broad-shouldered Fae they’d spoken of earlier.

I gathered Bernard’s cane for him. He climbed up with the pop of bones and a little groan, then moved- slowly, with a heavy limp- towards Alora. She moved to help, one ivory hand bracing his side with every step.

A little whimper made me turn back. Liana sat white-knuckled, biting her lip so hard that a drop of blood had formed. Her chest rose and fell very quickly.

I’d scarcely reached for her when, with a rush of air and a flash of gold, Aravis had the young woman in his arms. He smoothed hair from her forehead and murmured something in a low, soothing tone. She shivered and clung to him, pressing her cheek against his chest.

Is she sick? I watched the pair, then let my gaze fall as her sketch fluttered down. The curves of charcoal and careful shading clearly showed a smiling, bearded face. Oh. Perhaps it’s someone she lost. Or someone from before she came here, however that happened.

Chatter continued around the table as I paced over. Velidan sat at one end, near Embriel and the flustered pair of humans. He ignored me as I slid into a seat beside him. Plates of covered food sat at each setting and a vaguely meaty smell hit my nostrils. The short, stout gray creatures stood with their faces lowered behind each chair.

“I know I’ve been tightlipped about the reason behind today’s little get-together.” Embriel braced both palms beside his plate once everyone was settled. His glinting eyes traveled the length of the table with deliberate slowness. “But it’s one I’ve been very excited about. A concept I’ve been building towards for quite some time, and one I think many of us will find appealing.” He placed a hand on the flour-spattered shoulders on either side of him. “Harmony.”

The gray-skinned creatures stepped forwards and, as one, swept the coverings away from each platter.
