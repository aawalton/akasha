Rudy read the top block of text twice.

His eyes snagged on the small addition next to his race. Asura-strain. He glared at the words—trying to force the system to elaborate—but even after a solid minute, no new panel appeared.

“Maybe it doesn’t work wirelessly? What about this?” He mumbled and reached out with his finger, poking the floating ‘Asura strain’. The interface remained completely unresponsive, offering no tooltips, no clarifying pop-ups, and absolutely zero explanation regarding what this meant for his long-term health.

With a frustrated grunt, Rudy fished his tempad out of his thigh pocket. He needed to write this down before he forgot. He swiped across the screen, hunting for a basic notepad application.

He found a convoluted contact manager. He found a scheduling calendar. He found a half-broken warehouse inventory tracker. Nowhere on the device was there a simple, blank page to save a single sentence.

"Who actually designed this piece of garbage?" Rudy muttered to the empty room.

He opened his message thread with Jin instead. His heavy thumbs clattered rapidly against the glass, dumping his thoughts without bothering to organize them.

Asura-strain. What does that mean? Am I growing horns? Also, Eternal One sponsorship. I remember the dungeon. I figure we're fine, but is there something I need to know? What are the three question marks after the name? Why doesn't this system explain anything literally? This interface is terrible.

He hit send, the outgoing chime echoing faintly in the quiet cell.

He let his head thump back against the beanbag, staring at the ceiling. The sponsorship issue didn't actually bother him that much. He remembered the dungeon vividly. That vast, crushing presence had observed them with genuine curiosity. It carried none of the scheming he usually sensed from the nobles, and they were the closest match to him for a god.

His gaze drifted back to the blue screen, to the Quests tab.

He stared at the glowing word. Every guardsman, every history lesson, and basic common sense dictated that Quest Bearers attracted the kind of attention that reliably ended in a brutal, public execution. Poking around in that tab was begging for trouble.

Rudy dragged a hand down his face, exhaled a heavy breath, and tapped the letters anyway.

◈ QUESTS AVAILABLE: 1

Rudy jerked backward so violently that he nearly flipped the massive beanbag over. He caught his balance at the last second and forced himself to sink back into the beanbag.

“Son of a bitch!" he muttered to the empty room. "That’s not Cool."

He snatched his tempad off his chest. His thick thumbs hammered against the cracked glass, firing off two messages to Jin in rapid succession.

I have a quest. A freaking goddamn quest! WITH A CAPITAL Q.

Please tell me this is normal.

He dropped the tempad onto the floor and forced his eyes back to the glowing blue text hovering in the air.

“Tch,” He clicked his tongue. “Fuck it. Let’s see what this is.”

Rudy read the block of text three times. His eyes tracked the letters more slowly on each pass.

Half the words felt like they belonged in a boring history lecture he had slept through. Four Bastions. The Olden Empire. The highly specific, terrifying phrasing around necessary sacrifices made his stomach twist. He was definitely going to corner Jin and demand a very long explanation about that part.

But one line cut completely through the lore-dump and hit him squarely in the chest.

Soul Truly Beyond the Stars of Fate.

He remembered that. Remembered the title.

It was Jin’s title. Right there in the quest text, as if the world itself were describing him by name.

Rudy rubbed his jaw, feeling the rough stubble scratching against his palm. His thoughts drifted back to the dungeon. He remembered the amber sky, the crushing pressure, the sheer, idiotic terror of diving into a death trap simply because Jin asked him to. He remembered the moment Jin had shown his mantle reflection to him.

A theory began assembling itself in Rudy's brain. It was loose, cobbled together from half-heard conversations and his master's and Joe’s grim warnings.

Quest bearers attracted attention. Powerful people kill any and all quest bearers before they can access whatever the quests connected them to.

Which meant the Z.A.C. interface, the sponsorships, the rewards. Rudy hadn't earned this access by being inherently special. This entire cascade of broken rules radiated outward purely from his proximity to Jin. From choosing to follow—no, he was pretty sure Jin kidnapped him—into a dungeon.

It was the kind of theory that sounded like a crackpot idea until you wrote it down and realized it explained everything you'd seen.

“This… and that… oh, and that!” His thick thumbs hammered against the tempad, dumping a stream-of-consciousness rant into his message thread with Jin. He didn't bother with punctuation, formatting, or basic coherence. He just needed the panic out of his head.

He hit send before the rational part of his brain could veto the action.

The device chimed almost instantly. Jin had replied with a single, highly judgmental raised-eyebrow emoji, followed by a second text:

JIN: What the hell are you doing?

Rudy scowled, his face flushing slightly as he typed back rapidly: just ignore it. Or read it and ignore it. Going through my status. See ya~

He locked the screen.

“Fuuuu~” Rudy dragged a heavy breath into his lungs. He braced himself and mentally tapped his status icon.

He sat with it for a long time.

He ignored the weird, newly formatted letter grades assigned to his stats. He ignored the ridiculous string of titles. His eyes remained locked on a single, glowing line of text under his cultivation path.

Progress: 99%

He'd known it was close—had been able to feel it the way you could feel a word sitting on the edge of your tongue—but seeing the number made it real in a different way.

The first realm of the Nine Hells was exactly one percent threshold away from completion.

Rudy pressed his palm to his chest again. Felt the Asura heart do its steady, heavy work beneath the bone.

Nascent Asura, the system text had said. Avatar of Colossus.

He closed the panel, leaned his head back against the bean bag, and stared at the fluorescent lights humming overhead in the empty training cell.

It was a deceptive peace. Outside, in the dark city, thousands of abominations were spreading through streets that used to be full of people. The cult was pulling back to something that would be harder to hit than everything they'd destroyed so far.

The final wall was still running its countdown, and he had no idea what part he would have to play in Jin’s plan.

But if there was one thing he knew.

“Oh well, I'd better get stronger.” He said. “And let’s start with the breakthrough…”
