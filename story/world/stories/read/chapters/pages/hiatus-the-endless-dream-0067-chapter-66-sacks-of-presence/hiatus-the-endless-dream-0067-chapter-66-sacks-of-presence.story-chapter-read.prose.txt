My parents and I didn’t fight often. My dad preferred to let his disappointment be known, let you know what you could do to fix it, and then leave. If you fixed the thing he stopped looking at you like a project. With him it was a matter of when you’d fix the thing, not if. My mom was a different story. She liked fighting. She was no proponent of physical violence, even when I was smaller and weaker than her, but any combination of words was on the table. It felt like when tensions rose we put our love to the side for a moment and almost everything was fair game. I’d never win our little spats. She spoke too fast and with more intensity than I could ever muster. She’d rattle off admittedly creative insults.  To this day I think she’s the only one whose called me flat-footed and shiftless in the same breath. There was one time we were arguing, about what I don’t exactly remember—I just know it had to do something about the news—and she turned to me and said with all the exasperation she could manage, “Doe, I don’t know much you do like.”

That one came close to breaking an unknown rule of some unspoken agreement we both had about arguing. It hit a little too close to something. When I said it’s like we’d put our love for each other to the side I meant that in all ways. After the argument was done we’d take that love back and it’d be unharmed by any of the words we said. That one came close to actually affecting something in me. It was those words I thought of  that haunted me as I stared at the crowd of people.  There, in fact, wasn’t much about the current situation that I liked

I felt heat drain from my face and my heart drop. Maybe it was because I didn’t like crowds, more likely it was because of the paladin and the mob. I wasn’t sure why the paladin put me on edge. I chalked it up to the fact the last Instance had involved an evil God and a cult and Paladins typically came attached with a God, or from what I had just recently learned an Instance Holder. The mob making me nervous made more sense. Mobs by a rule were dangerous. Didn’t matter if you were part of the mob or its target, if there was a mob around, you were in danger. This mob was also desperate, its scent hung heavy in the air, but oddly enough it didn’t overwhelm the crowd. They weren’t happy, individuals were panicked, but it felt like they considered this a temporary set back instead of abject doom. I didn’t know how that was possible.

Then the Paladin hugged Minerva and the desperation receded a little more. Ah, they were hopeful. That’s what it was. I hadn’t recognized it at first due to the atmosphere, but them being hopeful just doubled my anxiety about a crowd this large. I didn’t want to be around when their hopes were dashed, but at the same time, if there really were a hundred thousand Visitors in the area I couldn’t leave them be either.

I felt a headache hit me at that number. A hundred thousand. How was that even possible? The number made me a feel a little numb. At a certain points numbers stopped having meaning to the human brain. Ten thousand? A hundred thousand? Conceptually I don’t get the difference. Either way, they’d still mob and kill me.

That thought simultaneously made me afraid and not. I didn’t want to die, but the more I thought about it the more I realized it completely lacked repercussions. I couldn’t die forever and if I did get mobbed turning off my pain receptors would just take a thought or a little bit of Presence.

The thought rolled around in my head as I scanned the crowd to get a gauge for numbers. There were less than five hundred. In total they had less Presence than me. If they tried to mob I could just kill them. Hells, even if they had more combined Presence than me they didn’t know about Belief . They’d naturally get in each others way.

Ah, that’s must be why Instances Holders even bother with worlds sometimes. Then stronger I considered that it might be the same reason many pretend to be gods. Maybe not though, the Spider I accidentally summoned. When it killed I got the Presence.

My thoughts kept spiraling down that rabbit hole as Minerva hopped out of the car to the applause of the mob. When she had said she was looking for people, she had listed three. Even though she told me exactly how many Visitors there were from her world, I hadn’t considered the consequences of that. What did I do with that many Visitors? Think. I had too many thoughts moving. They were conflicting and turning my mind into a jumbled mess. I needed to break down the problem.

Defining the problem was easy. There were 100,000 Visitors near me who were cursed to stay in the Dream while they looked for a specific Instance. Nothing good could come from 100,000 desperate people. 100,000 Visitors. A million Presence.

Really, I had three options. I could help them find this Instance to break whatever “curse” they were dealing with, I could fuck off and leave them to their own devices, or I could start killing and stealing Presence. I eliminated leaving them on their own right away. If the situation of my own world were any less dire, it would’ve been my go-to, but I couldn’t afford to waste anything right now. These people were an asset; how I went about this just determined the type of asset.

“Dorian,” Joey said as he stared at me from an open car door. The engine had stopped. Minerva had already joined her people. Mina had joined her, still pretending to be from their world. “You’re making that expression again.”

I furrowed my brow and leaned to look at my reflection in the rear-view mirror. I didn't know what expression he meant; I thought I looked rather apathetic. “What expression?” I asked.

“The one you make when you’re about to hurt someone,” he sighed. “Nervous?” he tacted on at the end.

If it were anyone else, I was liable to tell them to fuck off. Not Joey. “Yeah,” I admitted. “I met another group of people before I met Maerin. Two actually. Both told me how dangerous other groups can be in the Dream. It sounds like there’s some espionage between worlds sometimes.” I thought back to the scene the Orlan had described to me of my brother. How he rained meteors and monsters down on a world. “It’s dangerous if people from other worlds find out where we are, and there are 100,000 of them. I don’t want them to find our world.”

Joey looked towards Maerin, “Why’d you bring her and the rest then?” he asked. I could tell he was genuinely curious. It was hard to believe. In his shoes, I would’ve been furious with just that amount of information. Not only did you kill your own friend, my girlfriend, but you also risked our entire world for them? It’d seem insane. Then again, Joey wasn’t me, just like I wasn’t him. We understood each other, but just what each other would do. The why always escaped us; the conclusions we drew just felt, and were later proved, right.

“Their world disavowed them as did their Instance Holder. There wasn’t any risk.” Joey nodded.

“Gotcha, I see the issue.”

I nodded, my anxiety easing somewhat.

“Why’d the Dream put them up to this you think?”

I gave a half nod, “I don’t think it’s the Dream. The Dream doesn’t make Instances, it doesn’t give quests. If it tells you to do something it won’t do it through anything other than brute force. She leaves Instance Holders to do all of this stuff.”

Joey nodded slowly again, rolling around the words as if they had a taste to them he couldn’t place. “We have to find time to go over all this in more detail. Minerva made it sound like her world is done for. They also don’t seem to know much about the Dream. I’d like to help them if I can.”

I knew he would say that. I dreaded it, and I hated that he said it. I couldn’t even muster the energy to call him stupid. “That’s a lot of people. Their bodies are still in their world. Even if we help them, when they die over there they die here too.”

“Same with Maerin?” he asked.

I glared at him, hoping he wouldn’t continue down this line. “No, all of their bodies are in our world now. Part of being disavowed.” That last part was an assumption, but if it wasn’t I’d eat my foot.

He nodded his head, “Can Visitors disavow their own world?”

I sighed, resigned. “I don’t know. I wouldn’t be surprised.”

Joey nodded, “Give it some thought for me? I don’t know as much about the Dream stuff. Anything I can do to help in the meantime? Otherwise I’m just gonna go get the lay of the land.”

“There’s a lot of risk. Even if they disavow their world they still have the goal they’ve been set. That may not disapear, and there will always be a leak, snake, or we might show a completely unrelated party where our world is.”

Joey nodded, then frowned. “Woah woah. I didn’t say we should bring them to our world! I was just thinking their goal is to find an Instance entrance, right? We can help them look then they can go back home, but if we can’t maybe they can disavow their world as a sort of “out”, so that they won’t die. I can’t imagine living in the Dream, but it’s better to try than not.”

I stared at him before nodding. I didn’t actually hate the plan, but it wasn’t helpful either. This fell under fucking off, just with extra steps and effort. It seemed like a waste.

“What is it?” a voice called from behind Joey. He turned to see a gaggle of children staring at the convertible with confused looks.

Joey’s face basically blinded the kids as he lit up, “Oh this thing? It’s like a horseless carriage.”

They didn’t seem to understand. Joey started trying to explain more as simply and non-technically as one could if “little magic hamsters running in the engine” was an indicator. I got out of the car. Even though she wasn’t actively using it, I could still feel the mental bridge to Maerin. I could talk to her any time if something happened. If I separated from Joey, I couldn’t.

I thought of the [Communication Stones] item Jeren had and almost slapped my forehead when I remembered they were basically just walkie-talkies. There were probably a pair in the glove compartment of the car. Perfectly reasonable for that to be the case. I focused on the image and sure enough, when I opened it up, a pair sat there. I hadn’t imagined anything else in the compartment, so there wasn’t anything else. I didn’t just want a way for him to communicate with me in an emergency, though. I wanted to be able to get there easily as well. I just needed it to not be potentially lethal like a two-way mental bridge.

A mental compass. One that led me to the other walkie-talkie and vice versa, as long as I actively used it. I liked it. I thought about it. It was a simple concept. I wasn’t trying to break reality or do any crazy magic. Just a compass that points to a location, and not even all the time. It was small. I could do it without Presence.

I repeated that like a mantra for a moment. I turned on one of the walkie-talkies and felt a physical tingle, like a bug crawling on the ridges of my brain pointing to the device. I moved the walkie-talkie around and sure enough, I felt the disturbing, ticklish sensation dance across my skull.

“Heads up,” I lightly called to Joey before tossing one of the devices to my friend. “Found these in the glove compartment. I’m gonna go look around, call if you need anything.”

“I got my phone for that?” he questioned, reaching down to his pocket to pull out a phone. He squinted before realizing that there was no signal.

I laughed and walked away. Some of the children's parents had wandered over, wanting to be close just in case anything happened next to the heavy steel contraption, which people from my world preferred to call a car. As I walked away, looking at the parents doing their best even in this hopeless situation, I didn’t feel ashamed that I had just thought of them as Presence a moment ago. Or that I had considered killing them all. I just no longer had any desire or thoughts of doing it anymore.

Joey wanted to help them and I didn’t have any issues with the principle. I just needed to figure out how to not make all that effort go to waste.
