Tressa stomped through the forest, twigs snapping as she forced her way through the thick brush.

“Act more elegant! Act more ladylike!” she mocked. “You are a High Elf! And a noble’s daughter at that! Stop wandering the forest like some barbarian. Damn you, Father! The world doesn’t end at our city’s walls!”

She crouched by a lake, snatched up a rock, and flicked it across the surface, sending it skipping dozens of times. “High Elven society is so stifling. There is no way I'm joining the temple. I'm going to explore this world!”

She threw another rock. This one didn't skip but bounced once at a sharp angle and then dropped back in with a plop.

“Huh?”

Something moved beneath the surface, its shadow growing until suddenly—

An enormous fish burst from the water with rows of sharp, dagger-like teeth. It snapped at the air in anger before diving below the surface once more.

A smile spread across Tressa’s lips until a giddy laugh slipped out. She conjured a mana quill as she fell against a mossy spot of ground, pulled out her sketchbook, and began to sketch.

“Haha! Tuplur fish! Rare sighting.”

She quickly scribbled in a loose approximation of what she had just witnessed. This would never be a still model she could take her time with, and she hoped it would jump up again so she could get a better look at its teeth. The records in the High Elven library did not mention the multiple rows of teeth, so surely little was properly known of this creature as a whole.

As she watched it swim along the surface and then dive out of sight again, she tapped her chin with the mana quill, thoughts racing through her mind.

“The water doesn't seem deep enough to be a suitable environment for such a large fish. Maybe it leads to an underground cave system and out to one of the larger lakes? Perhaps it comes here for a safe place to lay eggs? Maybe it even leads to the Thimberman lake?”

She smiled and quickly jotted down her theories, already planning to check the library for any records of the local waterways. If such a cave system were unknown, this could be a huge discovery!

She lost track of time as she continued scribbling away. Over her centuries of youth, she’d filled countless of these sketchbooks. Although not all contained simple research of plants and animals. She had other secret books filled with her writings and experiments with mana and old runes. If those were ever discovered, she’d be dragged before the council like a black sorceress, and her research being confiscated would be the least of her problems.

But she truly felt it unjust that there were laws against who might do such research on magic in the first place. She believed knowledge should be equal for everyone, which is why she chose to ignore such arbitrary restrictions that were, in her opinion, made falsely under the pretense of safety.

Today, however, Tressa wasn’t playing with dangerous rune crafting. All she cared about right now was seeing what she could learn about this fish!

The Tuplur fish came up again, swimming along the surface, and she took the chance to get a good look at its fins and scales to add to her sketch. Just as it dove down again, she heard a voice.

“Lady Tressa! Here you are.”

She jumped, startled, then sighed and muttered, “So much for my escape…” Looking over her shoulder, she found a tall elf with silvery blonde hair. He wore dyed violet-blue leather armor with an emblem on his chest that looked like an eagle holding a scroll inside a sun—the uniform of her father’s private guard. Between his garb and intense expression, the young elf came off as much more of an authoritarian than he actually was.

“What do you want, Aldmern?” Tressa grumbled.

“Your father sent me to retrieve you. He would like a word.”

“Tell him no,” she growled, but knew if Aldmern were here, she would have very little choice but to go back. She hated the fact her father was part of the high council. He could command other elves to do his bidding, and they would never disobey him.

“If he finds out I didn't bring you back, I'll be reprimanded.”

“Then say you couldn't find me.”

“Ha, then my tracking abilities would be questioned, and I can't have that either. After all, I have a reputation to uphold,” he explained with a dignified smirk.

She made a face. “Can I stay a while longer, and then we go back?”

“Perhaps. I found you quickly, after all,” Aldmern said, finding a tree to lean against. He folded his arms and crossed his legs, resting his broad shoulder against the rough bark.

Tressa narrowed her eyes at his subtle boasting, but at least he wasn’t forcing her to leave. Though his interruption had ruined her focus on the Tuplur Fish, which was now long gone.

“Thank you,” she said. Then she heard a cry from above that brought her eyes to a bird she knew all too well perched amongst the canopy, and she decided to sketch it. She flipped to another page in a section with other sketches and older notes of that species and began adding to it.

Blue Mimic

A blue bird. Seemingly nonthreatening, lacking direct offenses of its own—unless you eat it without preparing it properly! Its skill at mimicking sounds allows it to lure prey towards other stronger monsters so that it may scavenge the corpses later.

After tracking one for a full day, I got the rare chance to see it in action. It mimicked the mating call of a Corson Fawn and lured it right to a Snapvine. The fight didn’t last long, and the small Blue Mimic soon had a feast.

They have solid blue feathers, sharp claws, and bodies about the size of a large potato. As far as I can tell, they are native to the east around Falidorn and the Thimberman mountains.

“So why do you bother drawing all these pictures of birds and monsters?” Aldmern asked with a yawn. “Doesn't seem to be much point. Better off hunting them.”

“Well, let's say a traveler knows not what I know. If I can share that, then we can save lives.”

“Hm. I suppose. But I don't suspect sketching that bird in particular will be crucial to the survival of our people.”

“I recently discovered they are edible, so I’ve been interested to learn more about how to properly track, cook, and prepare them.”

“Blue Mimics are edible?”

“The meat is quite tender. All you have to do is boil it for three minutes, then drain and season and grill.”

Aldmern nodded.

“Boiling gets the poison out.”

“Poison!?”

“That’s right,” she chimed smugly as she finished sketching and jotted some quick notes on another page about where and when this one was spotted. “Though it is not a deadly poison, it just upsets your stomach and makes you hallucinate. I learned the hard way. Finally figured out I had to boil it. Took a couple of tries.”

“Tressa!” he snapped.

“What?” she answered back as if it was of no concern.

“I thought you'd know better than to try eating strange things!”

She mimed a moving mouth with her hand. “I thought you’d know better than to try eating strange things.”

“Tressa…” he growled.

She burst out laughing. “I’m sorry! You’re just far too easy.” She then met his scowl with an innocent smile. The habits of monsters were not the only things she'd taken care to study.

Her smile turned softly mischievous as she rolled one shoulder and twirled a strand of hair around her finger, her voice softening. “It’s sweet of you to be concerned, Aldmern. You’re always looking out for me.”

The gesture had its intended effect. He coughed and turned to hide his blush. “Bah… You were easier to handle when you were younger. No… actually, I take that back. You were far more difficult… or… maybe about the same…” he trailed off, reaching up and cupping his chin, trying to decide which was worse.

She giggled. “Well, how else can we learn what's safe other than to try it?”

He forced his eyes to remain off of her as he muttered, “I still don't want to eat a poisonous bird. Even if you boil it.”

“Mmmm, but it tastes so good. You're missing out.”

“If your father found out, he might have the council lock you up.”

“If he was going to do that, it would have already happened. Rumors of his daughter going insane might hurt his reputation, soooo… I think I’m safe.”

“Still, Lady Tressa, it's best to be careful. Keep testing your luck, and you may push it too far one day. Your father is a veteran of the Mad King’s wars after all.” He walked over to the water and crouched down to search for a rock.

“I know. As if he does not constantly remind me of it. But I’d rather think of the future than the past,” she said, carefully watching her pen as she shaded in the design of the blue mimic’s feathers, trying not to remember the Mad King’s face. “Though you still haven’t said what grave matter has Father sending the greatest tracker in all of Falidorn into the woods after me today.”

Aldmern flicked a rock at the lake, but didn’t quite get a proper skip. “Two of the elders came asking for you,” he said as he searched for a second rock.

Tressa’s pen stopped instantly. “The elders? What for?”

“I don’t know. I only know he called for you, and when you were not found to be in the manor, he sent me.”

Tressa felt her heart sink, but she tried to shake it off as paranoia. Her secret sketchbooks were well hidden. Nobody could have found them.

But what if my research in the library had made someone suspicious, and they went snooping?

She watched Aldmern as he tossed his second rock, with a little more success this time, as it skipped twice.

He’s not overly urgent, but they wouldn’t exactly tell him if that is what this is about… And I am years from being called for my trials, so what could it be?

“Well, let’s not keep them waiting any longer, then,” Tressa said calmly, shutting her sketchbook and banishing her mana quill. Aldmern looked surprised to hear she was coming so easily. She smiled and explained, “I wouldn't want to get you in trouble.”

“Thank you, my lady,” he said with a bow, and they began heading back. Once she was walking at his side, he asked, “By the way. How did you make that rock skip across the water like that?”

“Once I’ve finished with the elders, perhaps I can show you.”
