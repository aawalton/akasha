For the first time in—I don’t even know how long—I felt proud of myself.

It was weird.I savored the feeling like a sweet, foreign fruit.

If I died today—which was totally possible—I was glad I got to feel that one thing first.

Few seconds after my punch landed, Geshem slowly opened his eyes. He looked confused and disoriented.

I had his hands already locked in a tight grip behind his back.

Geshem had no claws. No fangs. No stinger. And the Naked Nula were physically way weaker than my kind. Plus, with his hands in mine, there was no way he could make any magic incantations.He was helpless.

Soft. Pink. Cute. Helpless.

“Ahhh, my head!” he groaned. “Not again with that fucking feeling!”

“Sorry,” I murmured. “Really. Sorry.”

“Shooya... why?” he growled, as I stood him up and started marching him forward, in the direction he’d come from.

“I changed sides,” I said quietly. “I think…”

“You… you think?” He said.

“Yes… probably.” I admitted.

“Oh…” Geshem said, and he looked like he was thinking deeply about something.

“Look, Shooya,” he began, “I appreciate you wanting to help and all. But if we keep walking this way, we’ll get to Lori soon, and when she sees you dragging me like this, she’s gonna shoot you. On sight. She’ll kill you, Shooya.”

“I… I know that…” I said.

Geshem had been kind to me before. But now we weren’t on the same side anymore. I knew he was trying to manipulate me. Scare me.

Of course, Lori shooting me on sight was a real possibility. I could totally see it happening. And yeah, I was completely, utterly terrified.

“You know,” Geshem tried again, “if you come to Kamatouu, they’ll accept you. They’ll let you join, for sure. We’re actually a really nice band, and—”

“Please,” I cut in, trying to muster a harsh tone. “You’re not Stormcrows. Be quiet.”

That shut him up.He looked like I’d just slapped him.

Yeah. He was in a terrible situation. Forced to betray his own friend. And the more I thought about it, the more I felt sick. Mind control was a truly horrible thing.

We kept walking for a bit, and then—

“ARRHHHRRR!”

A horrible scream split the air like lightning.

Lori stood ahead of us, her teeth bared, an unfathomable rage in her eyes. Pure fury.

That demon again.

She held her bow aimed straight at me, just a flick of her finger away from piercing my carapace and all my precious, mushy organs inside.

I stopped where I stood. I lowered my head. But I didn’t let go of Geshem’s wrist.

And no, I didn’t have a plan for this moment.And yes, I Probably should have.

“Umm, Lori, can I—” Geshem began, but Lori immediately growled again. A terrible sound. It shut him up instantly. Lori won’t let Geshem bullshit her.

I needed to say something. Convince Lori I was on her side. That I wanted to help.

But I was so scared.

“I… I…” I started, trembling. “Sorry… I…”

No, this won’t do. I need to be able to utter a few damn sensible words — my life depended on it.

Come on, Shooya. Come on!

“I WANT TO BE GOOD!” I shouted. Stupidly.

“I mean… I…” I mumbled again.

Then I started crying.

Naked Nula cried with water leaking from their eyes. We just… trembled awkwardly. That’s exactly what I did.

Lori came closer. Still furious. Still aiming her bow. But not shooting. Not yet.

“Go, then—before my heart hardens like winter bark,” Lori growled.

NPCs talk weird. But I understood. She was telling me to keep walking with Geshem. “Before my heart hardens” probably meant “before I change my mind and kill you.”

So I kept walking, dragging Geshem along, and Lori followed right behind us.

Every time I slowed down even a little, I heard the creak of her bowstring stretching.

“Go, then—before my heart hardens like winter bark,” she repeated.

I quickly picked up my pace.

We walked further, and further, and I was so scared I thought I might faint. Then the road split in a Y.

“Where… should we…?” I asked.

No answer. Just the sound of Lori’s bowstring tightening.

Shit.

I took a step toward the left turn.

“Arrrhh!” Lori growled again.

“OK! The right! Sorry!” I squeaked, and turned to the right.

We walked some more. It didn’t take long before we reached the edge of Kamatouu.

Not the main town, though—we came up near the forest’s edge, to a small cabin in the distance.

Once we reached it, I wasn’t sure what to do.

“She wants us to go inside,” Geshem said. “Lori’s a forester. This is her cabin. I live here too, with her and also with… well…” He stopped, swallowed, and looked deeply pained.

I decided he was telling the truth. He didn’t have a reason to make Lori angrier than she already was.

We entered the cabin. Lori came in right behind us.

“Thieves, Papa! Brazen as wolves!” she called out.

No one answered.

She shouted again, louder. Still no reply. The cabin was empty.

“She’s looking for her father,” Geshem explained. “But he probably went out hunting. She… doesn’t know how to handle this.”

Geshem took a deep breath. He looked like it hurt.

“She’s an NPC,” he said. “This isn’t a situation they’re supposed to handle. I… I know this is my fault. I didn’t want to cause her pain…”

Lori herded us into one of the rooms, bow still drawn. Her expression was furious—but also something else.

I’d learned a bit about Naked Nula expressions from traveling with Freya. I think Lori looked… lost.

Now that she had us here, she didn’t know what to do next.

She needed her Stormcrow friends. But she couldn’t go to the main town. Probably afraid Geshem would manipulate someone into letting him escape.

But now she was stuck.She couldn’t trust me.And she definitely couldn’t trust Geshem.

“ARRHHH!” she screamed in frustration.

It was scary.But also heartbreaking.

I needed her to trust me. That would solve everything. But she was an NPC. They were different. I wasn’t sure I could make her trust me.

Still. I had to try.

I gathered every shred of courage I had.“Bring rope, please,” I said. “I will… tie him.”

Lori was silent for a few seconds. Then she rushed out of the room and came back almost instantly, tossing a long piece of rope at me. bow already back on us.

I started tying Geshem’s legs together, then his hands behind his back. But when I tried to secure them, he didn’t hold them close enough on purpose.

“Stop that,” I hissed. “If you don’t, I’ll hit you again.”

He stopped struggling.

I tried to tie his hands and fingers so he couldn’t make incantations. Maybe it would hold — maybe not. Then, with the rest of the rope, I tied him to a wooden post at the side of the room.

Then I heard a sound. Crying.

Not normal crying—it sounded weird. Like… no. That didn’t make any sense.

But it was exactly what it sounded like.

A crying creature entered the room — a baby.

A Naked Nula green baby!

I loved babies already, and I thought Naked Nula were peak cuteness.So it’s hard to describe how I felt toward that tiny pink-greenish blob of softness.Its cuteness was unbearable. Despite everything, warmth filled me.

I wasn’t sure what the deal with the green baby was at all. I was way too dumb to figure that one out.

All I knew came from the look of distress on Lori’s face. she seemed even more lost and tense than before. The situation was collapsing on her.

She apparently needed to protect this baby.She also needed to keep her bow on me.But She needed to keep an eye on Geshem too.

It was impossible situation for her.

I didn’t know what to do.

So I did what I wanted.Without thinking.

I slowly walked toward the crying baby.

Lori tensed. Her bow creaked. She aimed at me. Growling low.

Yeah, maybe I was gonna die. But I’d already decided.

I reached the baby and picked her up. She was soft and smooth, like a little living cloud.

Lori hadn’t shot me yet.

I sang to the baby. A soft, old lullaby my nanny used to sing to me in bed:

♪ Hush, my sweet, now close your eyes,Outside the wall, the Rebel cries.Don’t you frown, don’t you fear,We will make his blood run clear. ♫

♫ Little fist and little stone,Crush the bones until they’re gone.The Emperor wants the world kept neat,So we smash the naughty with our feet. ♪

♪ He used his mouth to shout bad words,Now he is food for little birds.We take the knife and give a turn,So all his lessons he will learn. ♫

The baby stopped crying.

It liked the song.It looked at me with wide eyes and reached out to touch me. It liked my carapace.

“Yes… my carapace is smooth and interesting!” I told the baby. “And you know what? The carapace isn’t even the coolest part. The wings are!”

I lifted my outer wings—those hard shield ones—clicking them apart. Then my lower wings unfurled, thin and gleaming, catching the air.

The baby was ecstatic.It had never seen anything so magical.

It touched them, investigated. I think It could’ve played with them for hours.

I forgot I was under threat. I was dumb like that.

But when I looked back at Lori—Her bow was lowered.

By ignoring the threat and helping with the baby, I had apparently gained her trust. At least, a little.

I looked at Geshem.

His eyes were tearing up. He looked somewhere between relieved and miserable—if I was reading his Naked Nula face correctly.

Lori stepped closer to me.

“Papa says trust is a blade,” Lori said. “Give it to the wrong hands, and you’ll bleed for it.”

“You can… trust me,” I managed to say. “I want to help.”

“Don’t turn your back on that one,” Lori said, glaring at Geshem. “Stillness doesn’t mean harmless.”

“I… I will,” I said. “I’ll guard him. With… with my life.”

And I meant it.I wanted to help.I wanted them to accept me.And I wanted… what was the word?

Maybe… repent?

Lori stared at me for a long moment. Then, she turned to Geshem—and looked even longer.

“I know your hand never meant to strike,” she said. “Yet the bruise blooms all the same.”

That broke Geshem. He started crying softly. “I’m sorry. I’m sorry.”

Lori took the baby from me and left the cabin. Probably to bring her friends.Then we could take care of Geshem properly, keep him safe until his body cleansed itself of Uul’s control. Uul said it would take around four days.

Geshem stopped crying after a while. He looked calmer.

“Thank you, Shooya,” he said. “For helping my family.”

“Is… the baby…?”

“Her name’s Miriam,” he said. “She’s mine. The mother is a goblin. She thought Miriam would be better off growing up with humans—I mean, Naked Nula.”

I couldn’t imagine how painful that must’ve been — to see his daughter like this, to think of himself as a danger to her. It had to be crushing.

“She’s… lovely,” I said. “You’re lucky… to have her.”

“Thank you,” he said. He looked happy. Then his eyes watered again.

“How did you know,” he asked, “that picking up Miriam would make Lori trust you? That was… honestly, a huge risk.”

“I didn’t know what to do,” I answered truthfully. “So I just did what I wanted. Picked up the cute baby. I’m dumb like that.”

He smiled faintly. “You are dumb like that.” And somehow, it sounded like a compliment.

We sat in silence for a while. Then I noticed Geshem’s expression change. He wasn’t sad anymore. He looked… cheerful again.

Which meant I needed to be more careful now.

After a bit, he said, “The rope on my hands. It’s too tight. Stopping my blood flow. Can you… loosen it, just a little?”

I got up, walked over, looked at the rope—

—and tightened it even more.

“Ouch!” Geshem yelped.

Then I picked up a large stick from the floor.

“If I see… even a hint, of a magic circle,” I said, trying to sound hursh, “I’m gonna… put this stick right through your Naked Nula big nose!”

I gasped, covering my mouth. I surprised myself with that vulgarity.

I stayed extra vigilant after that. I checked the ropes again and again. Looked at his fingers. Watched his eyes.

I had no idea how much finger space a mage needed. I didn’t know anything about magic.

And honestly, it was terrifying to guard a gas-poison mage under enemy control.

But I would do this job right.Lori trusted me.

Thankfully, not long after, Lori returned — with Miriam in her arms, and three more Naked Nula. Two male, one female.

The female had a scorpion cat on her shoulder.That creature scared the hell out of me—but I remembered Uul saying that the Stormcrows used to tame them.

Geshem looked at his friends.

At first, he smiled. Then his face twisted in guilt. Anger. Shame. Then he looked at Miriam and lowered his head.

“A BUG LADY!” the female Naked Nula shouted, rushing over to me. “This is like, the second time I’ve seen one! You’re SO COOL!”

“Ah… thanks?”

Lori turned to her friends.

“You’ve a good heart. I know it,” she said. “Help me, and I won’t forget it.”

“Of course, Lori,” said the tall Naked Nula. “You did a great job bringing him in and subduing him. You probably saved his life again. We’ll take care of things from here. I swear. We’ll keep him safe. You can trust us.”

“You’ve a good heart. I know it,” Lori said again, this time looking at Geshem. “Help me, and I won’t forget it.”

Then she left, baby in arms.

With Lori and Miriam gone, Geshem seemed more steady.I guess without his daughter and girlfriend around, he felt less crushed by guilt.

“Hello, friends,” he said. “You can probably guess — or Lori told you — but I’m compromised. Like, really compromised. You can still talk to me, just take what I say with, uh, a grain of salt.”

“We will,” said the tall one. “Can you at least introduce us?”

“Oh, sure,” said Geshem. “The bug girl over there is Shooya. She’s got a low self-esteem, but she’s actually cool. And brave. You’ll love her.”

Then he gestured to his friends.

“This is Hyun-Woo, Nayeem, and Samar. Oh—and the scorpion cat is Kim.”

“It’s… really nice to meet you,” I said shyly.

“Likewise!” said Samar.

“Yes,” said Hyun-Woo. “I don’t know you, but I’ve got a feeling you’ve already helped us a lot.”

“Lori trusts you,” said Nayeem. “And that says a lot. So—thank you.”

I didn’t know what to say.Did they really trust me… just like that?

It felt weird.

Not even an hour ago, I was their enemy.

If I were a Naked Nula, my eyes would’ve been leaking buckets.

Nayeem turned to Geshem. “How are you holding up?”

“I’m happy sometimes,” he said. “Sad sometimes. Confused. S… self-hate. It’s bad.”

“We’ll get you through this. I promise,” Nayeem said, reaching out to put a comforting arm around Geshem—

“DON’T!” I shouted.

He froze.

“Please… be careful,” I said. “Your friend—Geshem—is… very dangerous!”

Nayeem’s eyes widened. He stepped back.

“She’s maybe exaggerating a little,” Geshem muttered.

“Shooya is not exaggerating,” Hyun-Woo said firmly. He turned to Samar and Nayeem. “Geshem is a powerful mage. And he’s clever. And crafty. We must remember—he can be deadly when he wants to be. And right now, his loyalty isn’t to us. It’s to our enemy.”

“I would never hurt you!” Geshem said. He sounded honestly hurt. I think he meant it.

But Hyun-Woo didn’t even glance at him.

“Shooya,” he said, turning to me. “How much time until he’s clean?”

“Four days… maybe five, to be careful,” I said. “That’s what I got from Uul.”

“Uul?” asked Hyun-Woo.

“The controller creature,” I explained. “Flying fluff ball. And… a creep. From the north continent. His plan is to—”

“NO!!” Geshem suddenly screamed. His eyes wide. “DON’T YOU FUCKING DARE TELL THEM!”

All his friends looked at him with sad expressions.

“Be quiet, Geshem,” Samar said. “Or I’ll tie your mouth.”

Geshem blinked in shock. “…Sorry, guys,” he whispered, ashamed.

So I started telling them.

About the mission. The airships. The controller bamboo. The fear Uul had of the Order of the Phoenix. The alliance of his kind. The plan to take over the northern bird kingdom.

And most important—Uul’s horrifying confidence that, once the bamboo people arrived, it would be game over. The continent would fall within a week.

That was huge. The Stormcrows needed to know. They could contact the Order right now, use their phone-device, warn everyone.

I was almost done telling them everything I knew—

Then I heard a faint whoosh sound.

My antenna then picked up a tiny vibration in the air.

“HE’S CASTING!” I shouted. Somehow he was doing it without a visible magic circle!

But his friends didn’t even flinch.

Samar just walked up to Geshem — and punched him square in the face.
