Tiffany stands up and shakes Director Lam’s hand.

“Director, I’m glad we were able to come to an agreement. We’re going to do great things together.”

“Not as glad as I am, I assure you, Captain Barrett. We’re going to make great strides in research thanks to your help.”

Tiffany, followed by John and Danny, exits the California Academy of Sciences, having just completed a deal with them regarding the sale of monster parts.

“You know, I never thought I’d be happy making a fraction of what we used to be,” Danny says glibly.

“Was it the lifetime membership that really made it for you?” John replies.

“Not all of it. But the Academy is a cool place that does good work. It’ll be nice to do something good, right? Something more than just endlessly treading water.”

Tiffany turns on her phone, only to find several dozen missed calls and twice as many half-panicked texts from the Guildmaster of all people. She goes pale as she reads them. She breaks into a sprint, and her team looks at her nervously as they scramble to catch up

“Oh, we’re so fucked,” she says.

“What?”

“Machina wants to see us. Two hours ago.”

“Ah,” Danny says. “Yeah, we’re boned.”

“It’s gonna be fine, Machina is a reasonable guy. Stop whining you two.”

The trip from the Academy to the Guildhall took a little less than an hour—fortunately the trams and buses were on time—though it felt like much more than that for all three of them.

Once the tram pulls up at the station for the Ferry Building, they quickly exit and hurry off into the Guildhall. The receptionist greets them at the front door.

“Hurry, come on. The Guildmaster is still with Machina over in the warehouse building.”

“What does he want?”

“I don’t know, he just said he wanted your help with something. He didn’t tell me what it was, only that it wasn’t an emergency.”

All three of them again hurry off, going to the northern Pier 1. What used to be mall of stores and restaurants was knocked down and rebuilt into a massive warehouse used to store monster parts and other goods before being sold off to the highest bidder. Their breath fogs in the chill, but it doesn’t bother them. They’re used to far more intense environments than a refrigerated warehouse.

“Ah, the Cerulean Guard, it’s good to see you,” Guildmaster Carrigan says once they meet up.

“Sorry it took us so long,” Tiffany apologizes. Machina waves it off, a rare display of movement. Normally he stands eerily still.

“It’s not a problem. It’s important, but not an emergency. I know you’re busy.”

“Before anything more substantial is said, perhaps a change of scenery? We have a few meeting rooms in the Guildhall for occasions like this,” the Guildmaster suggests.

“Alright,” Machina agrees, and a minute later everyone but Machina is seated around a large table on the 3rd floor of the Ferry Building. There isn’t a chair large enough for him, and so he stands at the head of the table.

“I’ll take my leave now,” Guildmaster Carrigan says, and floats out the door.

“It’s good to see you three again,” Machina starts. “I have a plan, and I’d like your help with it. I want to clear some of the stronger aquatic Fractures off the coast, and I want to invite you three along to help me out. I should say there’d be more involved than just clearing the Fractures.”

“You want us to do what now?” Danny asks, eyes wide.

“I’d like your help in clearing some Fractures,” Machina reaffirms.

“…Why us?” Tiffany asks. “We’re only 2-Stars. There’s a 3-Star team here in the Bay Area. You could ask them.”

“I don’t know them, and I know the kind of people you are. I think we’d work well together. I’m not going in looking for valuables to take and then leave. This wouldn’t be about the money. This would be about making the world a better place. It won’t be glamorous or glorious, but it will be important.”

“Sir,” Danny says with an unusual amount of respect, “You’re, well, you. I think just about anyone would be happy to work with you. And while I’d hate to lose out on work, those Fractures in The Deep—I assume that’s what you’re here for—are a bit over our pay grade. They’ve been nothing but back to back Cat-3s. That’s extremely dangerous, even for us.”

“That’s not to say we’re saying, no, sir,” Tiffany says jumping in. She kicks Danny under the table. “Just that something like that can’t be taken lightly. But what would we be doing, anyway?”

“In short? Science. DARPA has put together a variety of scientific instruments to help humanity understand more about Fractures. There’s quite a few of them, and I could use a hand to help handle all of them. We’re also looking for anything that could be useful for a certain project that myself and Morgan are working on.”

“And what would that be?”

“Have you heard of the research project Morgan and I started?”

“Uh, I think I’ve heard a few rumors,” Tiffany says. “Something about a farming tower? Food production? Something to help with the impending food crisis.”

“Yes, that’s pretty much it. We’re looking to retrofit skyscrapers once used for office space into massive hydroponic buildings. We’re looking at feeding tens of thousands of people, and that’s just with mundane plants. We have no idea what could be possible with magic involved.”

“So, what, we’ll be looking for flora samples?” John asks.

“Yes, and others. Our main focus wouldn’t necessarily be on bringing back the most valuable items, but the ones most important for research, whatever that may be.”

Danny sits back in his chair and whistles. “Wow. That could be… big. Game changing.”

“Yes. But I’d like your help with it. I could do it all myself, but it would take a lot longer. And time is valuable these days.”

Tiffany coughs uncomfortably. “While of course we’d love to help, I hate to ask this, and please don’t take this the wrong way, but what’s in it for us? Life isn’t free, as much as we’d all rather it be.”

“You can have whatever isn’t taken for research. I might want a few monsters corpses or interesting pieces here or there, but you can have the Fractal Shards and the majority of whatever we find.”

Danny sucks in a breath. “That’s a lot. Are you sure you don’t want any of it? You do know how much even a 2 Amp Fractal shard goes for, right?”

“No?” Machina says, cocking his head. “I haven’t really thought about it.”

The Cerulean Guard looks among themselves uncertainly. “Sir,” Tiffany says. “A 2 Amp Fractal Shard goes for ten million dollars, minimum.”

“We don’t get them,” John adds. “All of the Fractures around here are either shallow Cat-1s or the very deep Cat-3s, and we focus on the shallower Cat-1s.”

“There’s no Cat-2s?”

“No, not in the Bay Area. Well, maybe if you go inland enough, but they’re pretty rare. Really, a vast majority of the Fractures form in the ocean, and about half of them are Cat-3s in The Deep.”

“I just looked up The Deep, and I assume you mean the end of the Monterey Canyon?” Machina says.

“Just now?” Danny asks incredulously. “There’s been quite a few expeditions down there. You read all of it?”

“Yes. I’m connected to the internet,” Machina says, tapping the side of his head.

“Well, what you’ve read is correct. Two miles straight down,” Tiffany confirms grimly. “Mile high sheer cliff walls. Crushing pressures, near-freezing temperatures. The only thing capable of going down that deep are Empowered or specialized submarines.”

“You can go that deep?”

“We can. Our power sets are focused on the ocean. Not all 2-Stars are the same. We’d get our asses handed to us on land, but in the ocean? Even the greatest of predators would do well to stay away from us. We’ve been down there a couple of times, though as apart of a larger group. Not even the Pacific Raiders—the local 3-Star team—go down there often.”

“Why not? They should be able to deal with Cat-3 threats well enough.”

A dark look clouds Tiffany’s face. “If it was just a single Fracture’s worth, you’d be right. But there’s always a lot of Fractures down there, and probably have been since the Apotheosis. By the time we realized there were Fractures that deep, several had probably already ruptured. Even getting to the Fracture is a challenge in The Deep. The Pacific Raiders can only reliably clear one of them a week. They get banged up pretty badly every time. It’s a goddamn miracle all of them are still alive.”

Flames silently boil in Machina’s helmet. “Oh,” he says simply. “I guess we’ve got a lot of work cut out for us. Well, assuming you’re in.”

“Sir,” Danny says nervously. “There’s eleven Fractures last we checked. There’s no way we could possibly clear that many. Some of them are close to rupturing. Days or even less. No matter how highly you think of us, we do have limits.”

“I understand,” Machina says. “I won’t push you. I would like your help, but I do know that I’m asking a lot.”

Something electrifies the air. A great heat fills all three of them while Machina continues unabated.

“Things might look rough right now, but there’s still hope. We can do this. It’ll hard, I know. But nothing good is ever easy. This would save so many people from starvation, not just here but all around the globe. We could save the world.”

“Could we have a minute to discuss this, sir?” Tiffany says, biting back a desire to leap up and sprint for the beach. She grips the table to ground herself so tightly her knuckles go white. Both John and Danny are clearly vibrating in their seats, equally as amped up.

“Of course.” Machina gets up and leaves the room. The door clicks shut behind him.

Danny hisses. “Holy fuck, his Aura is intense. I damn near jumped out of my seat and made a break for the Pier.”

John holds up a shaking hand. “You aren’t kidding, take a look. I feel like I just chugged a six pack of those trucker energy shots.”

“Well?” Tiffany says, her leg jittering under the table. “What do you guys think?”

Danny chews his lip. “Well, right now? I’d say yes and we should actually start right goddamn now.” He takes a deep breath and tries to relax.

“But if I’m being a bit more objective about it? I dunno, guys. Like, I won’t lie, this could be big for us. And I’m not just talking about the money we’d make from the Fractures, science-focus or not. Just picking up the scraps from Machina will be worth a pretty penny, let’s be real here. This will put us on the map. Working with Machina is a golden ticket to the big leagues.”

“Can we live up to that? We’re just 2-Stars.”

“He seems to think so.”

“I think we should,” John chimes in quietly. “This is work worth doing, right?”

“Taking on Cat-3 in The Deep is not something we can do lightly,” Tiffany points out. “We just mentioned that. A four man 3-Star team regularly get beaten black and blue clearing just one.”

“And no small part of that is just getting to the Fracture, I know. But with Machina’s help even clearing just one could mean the world of difference. That’s big money in the bank for top of the line gear—and think about this: he’s friends with Morgan le Fay. The Sorceress. The Queen of Magic, the Artist of Magery—”

“Okay, okay, I get the point, John,” Tiffany interrupts.

“Imagine the kind of gear she could make for us. Even if we have to fork over our own materials for it—guess what? We’ll be able to scavenge for those, too. They’ll be as high a grade as could ever be reasonably acquired."

“He’s got a point, Tiff. Good gear can really make or break a team, and if I’m being honest…”

Tiffany hangs her head. “Yeah, I know our gear is lacking. But taking on the lower—” John holds up a hand.

“I’m not telling you off. We all decided to go down this road. We chose to punch down on Fractures beneath our weight to save lives, and the stuff we get from it hasn’t been valuable enough to get us better gear. Even the cool million a 1 Amp Fractal Shard gets us isn’t much in the grand scheme of things.

“I think we should accept his offer, but first request a few pieces of gear. Or maybe an advance, I don’t know. But this is a risk worth taking, no matter which way you look at it.”

“Okay then,” Tiffany says after taking a deep breath. “Let’s do it. Let’s help Machina save the world, I guess.”

“I think you’re being a bit dramatic.”

“Shut up, Danny. I’m having a moment.”

“Sorry boss.”
