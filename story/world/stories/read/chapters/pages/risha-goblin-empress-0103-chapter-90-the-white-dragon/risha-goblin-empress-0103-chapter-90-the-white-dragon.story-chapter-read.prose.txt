Risha set the small vine-child down and stood from her chair, “What has the Sapling been teaching you, little one?”

The change in topic seemed to work as the girl frowned, “He’s teaching me about plants, and the names of a bunch of old, dead Lin.”

“It’s good to learn about your people.” Risha nodded. She wished that she knew more about the goblins of the past, and the history that the ancient shaman was a part of.

“He wants something from me, and I don’t like it.”

Risha paused, and turned to the child, “What do you mean by that?”

The child sat back in the chair and looked at Risha with a seriousness that came from having to see too many terrible things at too young an age. “The way he talks about everyone here, and the way he changes when he talks to me… I don’t like it.”

Risha looked to Leaf-Watcher, “What is she talking about?”

The wolf’s eyes narrowed, “This is news to me.”

Risha frowned and looked at the girl who’d grown distracted by a pile of half-made arrows that Risha kept on her desk. “Don’t leave them alone.”

The wolf bowed her head, “Yes, Mother.”

Risha crossed her arms, then let out a sigh as she walked over to the girl, “Do you want me to teach you how to make them?” She motioned to the arrows that the girl was looking at.

Aliya perked up, “Yes!”

Risha sat at the desk, then motioned for the girl to sit next to her. She had so much more energy than she used to, and it made Risha smile to see her squirming in her seat.

Risha had all the tools she needed, and she enjoyed showing the little girl how to cut notches, twine and glue the arrowheads, then insert the feathers.

Once the girl had managed that much, Risha explained to her the small runes she liked to etch into the shaft. Her lessons with the Ancient Shaman had helped her gain a deeper understanding of the strange writings, even if she was nowhere near his depth of knowledge.

They stayed like that for a while, crafting together, until little Aliya started to yawn.

Risha glanced over to Leaf-Watcher and Talo, who were playing a game of tiles as they waited.

Talo caught her gaze. He stood up and walked over to the vine-child who was rubbing her eyes.

She squeaked in surprised as Talo picked her up, then laughed and snuggled into the Shade’s arms, “I don’t wanna go.”

“Mother, other meetings.” Talo said with his usual difficulty.

Risha smiled as she stood up, “He’s right little one. Risha will teach you more later.”

The girl pouted, but another yawn took over her body before she could say anything else.

Talo bowed his head to Risha, then walked from the room. Leaf-Watcher stayed behind, her eyes meeting Risha’s. “I don’t like the Sapling.” the words came out a growl, rumbling through the large kobold.

Risha looked at the towering White Lupus Kobold, “I’ll request a new teacher.”

“How long will that take?”

Risha stayed quiet for a long moment. “I don’t know. I don’t know what I’ll do if they refuse.”

“You do not need them.” Leaf-Watcher stepped forward.

Risha glared at the wolf, and the wolf backed down. “Risha doesn’t need another enemy either…” Risha tapped her fingers against her desk, “What if Aliya went on a trip? The Saplings aren’t the only ones who knew the Lin, and I don’t think the Sapling teacher could follow her there, so far from the sun.”

Leaf-Watcher tilted her head, “Who?”

“The Heart in our undercity.”

“Is that wise? Do you know the Spark well enough?”

Risha hummed, “Maybe not, but whatever the Sapling is teaching her, I want another’s voice to counter it.”

Leaf-Watcher was quiet for a long moment, “I will kill a Spark to protect her, if I must.”

“Good. I’ll request a new teacher from the Saplings. In the meantime, take her down.”

The White Wolf bowed low, then left the room.

Risha had made hard choices in an empire distracted by war, but she had not forgiven the Saplings for their attack, and she trusted her daughter’s instincts. If she had to fight the Saplings and their great tree, she had the power to do so now.

A knock on her door made her look up. The warrior opened the door, then bowed her head, “A Scaled is here, Mother.”

Risha looked past her to see a familiar kobold, this time transformed into a Scaled. Risha rose from her desk and watched over.

The form she’d taken had black scales covering her body, her eyes elongated into diamonds, while her nose was two slits. A long serpentine tail extended from her back.

“What’s your name this time?” Risha asked, motioning her in.

“Sira, Mother.” Mist-Prowler said, glancing over to the goblin warrior who could still hear them. “She has asked me, as a loyal Scaled, to respond to your message, and escort you to her.”

Risha motioned for the goblin warrior to close the door, “She could not come to me?”

“She still wishes to keep her presence quiet, but it is also a move motivated by her pride.”

Risha sighed, annoyed at the politics that she was about to navigate. She motioned for Mist-Prowler to wait so that she could change. She was still wearing the robe she’d worn to meet Elder Kulos after her bath.

The Coyote bowed, her scaled tail moving expertly to keep her balance.

Risha changed quickly into furs and leathers, then finally threw her black panther fur cloak over her shoulders. Kliks woke up for long enough to crawl into her hair, then fell back asleep. Finally, Risha tied her chain-swords to her waist, and hid their handles with her cloak. She paused for a second, then grabbed the bone necklace the Ancient Shaman had helped her carve and clasped it around her neck.

Risha pulled her hood up, and walked over to the kobold-turned-scaled.

Mist-Prowler- Risha mentally corrected her name to Sira- waited for her patiently, then turned to lead her out the door without a word.

Risha kept her hood up as they traveled through back alleys to a simple building. Sira knocked several times, then waited.

Moments later, the door opened, revealing a large Scaled warrior with white scales. He greeted Sira with a bow of the head, then turned to Risha. He paused for a second, then bowed his head again, slightly lower.

It wasn’t the bow of her loyal followers, but it was still more than she expected.

Sira led her past the scaled and into a backroom, where a woman sat on a luxurious chair. Her hood was pulled back, revealing two beautiful horns that curved back. White scales marked a face that otherwise took on human qualities. Striking light blue eyes met Risha’s gaze as the Dragon and the Mother observed each other for a long moment.

Finally, Elderoa, the White Dragon, bowed her head the smallest amount, “Mother Risha, it is an honor to see you again.”

Risha glanced over to Sira, who set a chair down for her, bowed and slipped away. Risha sat in the chair, then looked back to the Dragon. She smiled, exposing her pointy teeth, “I’m surprised to see you here. I was sure that the Scaled city would keep you busy.”

“My sibling watches the city. He likes to keep to himself, but he will do a good job while I’m away.”

Risha nodded, wondering at the dragon who watched the city. She hadn’t been able to meet too many of them before she’d departed to deal with the Kobold Elders. “That’s good.” Risha studied the calm woman, a polite smile on the Dragon’s face giving nothing away, “Why are you here?”

The Dragon tilted her head, “Am I expected to stay quiet in my city? We need things, iron for our weapons, crystals for our mages, all the things that keep my city prosperous.”

“You don’t need to come here in person to get those.”

Elderoa scoffed, “Only the humans have accepted us wholeheartedly, and that is because they do not see the difference between goblin, kobold and scaled. Your people, however, they hold things from us. Wealth, food… knowledge.”

Risha focused on that last word, sensing that there was something that the dragon meant by it. “What knowledge?”

Elderoa watched Risha with a questioning gaze, as if she were trying to piece together what made the Grand Mother move. “Your Elder Oltak. I’ve seen the items he keeps on him. You yourself, have runes inscribed into the bones around your neck the likes of which I’ve never seen.”

Risha reached up to the bone necklace, then focused on the other thing Elderoa had mentioned, “We do not hold food from you. Our farmers brought all they could.”

Elderoa frowned at the change in topic, “You misunderstand. We don’t grow hungry, but there are too many things we cannot grow in our swamps, that is why we must establish trade here.” She leaned back in her chair, “I will be honest with you, since you are a direct goblin. You’re creating a school here, for your mages. I want to send my people here to attend, and I want them to learn your secret magics.”

Risha stared at the dragon. She was fine with them coming to the school, but there was something about the conversation that bothered her. “It’s not only my people that keep things from you. You see yourselves as different, you do not accept us. The invitation was always open to you. Your people are my children.”

“I must protect them from you, Risha, Mother Of Monsters. They-”

“No.” Risha’s voice settled over the room, her spirit expanding behind her. The Dragon’s own power rose to match hers, and the very wood of the house vibrated with the power the two women brought to bare. “I am not your tyrant, Elderoa. I am your guardian, your mother, just as I am to those you protect.”

“You have yet to prove that.” Elderoa’s voice was calm, and she did not fight the mother with her magic, simply rested in her chair with the power she wielded. “And until you do, I will not bow.”

The two women glared at each other, neither one breaking the contest of wills.

Finally, Risha relaxed her spirit, and the room calmed. Mist-Prowler let out a tense breath from behind her.

“Elder Oltak is a smart goblin. He has discovered much that I do not know. The runes that I know,” Risha reached up to her necklace, "are not so easily shared.”

Elderoa opened her mouth, but Risha held up her hand.

“I will take one of your mages, and I will teach them what I know with some conditions.”

“Name them.”

“Bring ten Scaled, I will choose one. They cannot share what they learn without my permission. I have many secrets, some of which could bring harm to us all if they got into the wrong hands.”

Elderoa stayed quiet for a long moment, then nodded.

“One other thing.”

The Dragon’s eyes narrowed.

“I want you to pay me in hundreds of shinies.”

The Dragon blinked, then laughed before quickly hiding it. “Done.”

Risha smiled.

Her shinies had grown in size! They would be the envy of all goblins everywhere.

Risha stood, ready to leave, then paused a question coming to her mind, “How old are you, Elderoa?”

The Dragon paused, then to Risha’s surprise, looked embarrassed before lifting her chin. “I am the oldest of my clutch. I’ve passed my seventeenth year last summer.”

Risha blinked in surprise. Considering the age of Mother Iris, she’d just assumed that all dragons were ancient creatures. The words of the dead dragon that Wind-Whisperer had relayed to her came to mind.

“He fought for us, on the condition that I relay a message to you mother. Save the Dragon’s children.”

Risha looked at the dragon in front of her, “Did your father die on the battlefield?”

Elderoa’s eyes sharpened with anger, “He was brought down by your Draconics, Risha.”

“I see.” Risha walked over to the woman, and she backed up in surprise. Risha slowly placed her hand on the woman’s cheek, then bowed her head. “Risha is deeply sorry. Risha owes your father a strong debt that she will not forget.”

The dragon woman blinked back tears, unable to move away from the kind touch of the Mother.

Risha pulled her hand away, then walked towards the door. “Find your ten scaled by tomorrow, and I will choose one.”

Sira followed Risha out the door as the two of them left.

Elderoa stared at the closed door for a long time, replaying the moment that had just happened in her mind, trying to place it together with her hatred.
