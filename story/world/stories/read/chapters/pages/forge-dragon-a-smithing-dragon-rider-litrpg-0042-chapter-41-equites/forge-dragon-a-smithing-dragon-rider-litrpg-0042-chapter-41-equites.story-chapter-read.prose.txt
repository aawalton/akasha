Chapter 41: Equites

Canus refused to answer any of Caleb’s questions after that pronouncement.”

“You have your orders, and I refuse to get in the middle of a personal matter among my Equites,” he said, using an unfamiliar name Caleb wasn’t in the state of mind to question.

In the end, Caleb was forced to comply with Canus’ wishes lest he receive disciplinary actions. The brothers returned to their home, informed their father of the news. Caleb packed a small bag and then went out to his forge.

As he’d hoped, he found Kelia there waiting for him, pacing up and down in front of the door.

“Where have you been?” she demanded when she saw him, both worried and relieved.

“Me? Where have you been?” Caleb said back. “And why did you join the legion?”

“Me?! Why did you join the legion?” Kelia said back in turn.

“I had to save the village from being conscripted!”

“Well so did I!”

There was silence after that for a moment.

“I’m glad you’re okay, but I need to know what happened,” Caleb said, embracing her.

“Me too.”

They sat down beside each other on an old wall surrounding the mill. Caleb went first, though he really wanted to know what Kelia had said to make her recruitment into the legion worth a third of a village. He knew his wife was impressive, but not that impressive.

“Are you alright?” Kelia asked seriously after he’d recounted the story of the battle.

Caleb breathed in deeply and then blew it out as he thought of what to say.

“I don’t know. At first it was so easy—killing these kids that didn’t stand a chance against us and never saw me coming. And then it was so hard and I thought I was going to die a dozen times. Then at the end when I knew I was going to win, I just kept thinking about how I was about to kill that guy. I’ve killed a lot of people already in such a short time and I’m either repressing all that really well or am okay with it. This was different. I wasn’t rushed. I was going to kill him. I knew it, and I think he knew it too. I’m just glad I didn’t have to see his face behind the armor.”

The sat there in silence, just holding hands, until finally Caleb could take it no longer. Caleb noticed she felt much colder than usual. She always felt a little cold to him, but now she felt colder than the dead.

“What in the world happened to you?”

“I got a class,” Kelia said shyly with none of the excitement that the statement deserved. “And… I learned some things.”

“After you guys left, I fell asleep in that nest. I woke when the sun rose through the ring to system messages. I got offered a rare class, and just like you after I picked it I was given those strange messages about elevated access… I have a bloodline.”

A flood of emotions went through Caleb, but then the implications of the bloodline became clear.

Her mother.

“I am a Frigidi—partly at least, but enough to confirm my mother was what everyone thought she was,” Kelia said, whipping a tear away.

“You didn’t know?” Caleb asked.

He’d always been fairly certain that Kelia’s mother had been Frigidi, but he’d not cared and thought Kelia had known.

Kelia shook her head.

“Dad talked about her a lot, but never said she was from anywhere other than ‘outside the village.’”

Kelia continued talking about her class, clearly not ready for the current conversation.

“I’m a Frigidi Frost Druid,” she said. “And a Frigidi Blooded.”

She went on to explain her class and bloodline abilities.

Her bloodline granted her the ability to use her constitution and agility in place of her willpower and acuity when controlling cold-aspected mana. Her class gave her a skill that made her cold-aspected magic more efficient based on what portion of her mana pool was converted to cold mana.

Caleb was fascinated by the descriptions her class gave her. It hadn’t even occurred to him that mana had aspects, but as soon as she pointed it out it became clear to him and he could sense it.

Magical Perception has increased to level 7.

The class also gave her the skill Beast Bond, which was the same as Dragon Bond as far as Caleb could tell but for Avis. They got to share affinities, and Kelia gained Avis’ wind affinity while Avis gained Kelia’s Frost Affinity.

She gained Mana Manipulation and mana Sight but she’d quickly evolved the latter into Magical Perception by studying her surroundings.

Lastly she gained the skill Frost Bolt. Rather than explain it, she flicked her fingers and a jagged bolt of ice formed in front of it before shooting away. It crashed into the stone wall of the mill and shattered into snow that quickly vanished.

“It freezes the air until it’s solid,” Kelia explained.

Caleb had sensed it happen through his own Wind Affinity. A section of the air had simply vanished from his perception as it stopped being anything remotely resembling the wind.

“Keeping my mana pool pure cold is difficult, but I think I’ll get better at it.”

Caleb examined her and saw she had a mana pool held tight to her body. He could sense the cold aspect of it, but could also tell now that other aspects were mixed in.

He looked at his own mana pool and saw his mana was roughly a third metal and two thirds wind with some others he couldn’t identify mixed in. She was restricting the pool and unlike his which had a constant transfer of his pool absorbing ambient mana and releasing excess into the environment, hers had a slow controlled transfer in with little to no transfer out.

“Wind mana is everywhere,” she explained. “It’s hardest to keep out due to its prevalence, but since I have the affinity it’s easier to keep out than others.

“How do you think you got the class?” Caleb asked

“I think… it was that frost breath attack. I don’t know how to explain it, but I’m fairly certain surviving that was what did it. I was offered a few classes. A laborer, ranger, even a wyvern hunter, but the Frost Druid just felt right.”

“Plus it was a rare class,” Caleb put in.

“Yes, and that.”

“So what do we do now?” Caleb asked, she tended to have the better long term view of the two of them.

“We joined the legion Caleb. That’s not really up to us anymore.”

“True, but… what will that look like?”

“Well, we won’t have to worry about making a house, that’s for sure. We’ll be pe paid very well. If we survive our ten year term as Equites we can retire wherever we choose.”

“Ten years?” Caleb asked, shocked. “We signed up for ten years? And what’s an equites?”

Kelia sighed and shook her head.

“Did you even ask what you were signing up for?”

“Ummm, no. I didn’t really feel like I had a choice.”

“Equites is high tongue for a knight. We are to be held back in reserve for when an enemy hero appears on the battlefield. We’ll also serve as scouts. That’s why Canus’ was so eager to have me—regardless of the rare class. Their ast Equites scout went missing. Our first job will be to track him down.”

“Is that Flavius guy with us?”

Kelia shook her head.

“He’s a Bardi. The slave guard. Typically there are a handful of them. They serve much like we will but aren’t trusted to scout. We only have the one.”

“Why’s that?”

“Because we are in the middle of nowhere. Canus was sent to crush rumors of a rebellion from one of the villages deeper in the mountains.”

Caleb’s village was far from the only one ostensibly under the empire’s control. They were the only ones in the region that had a semi-reliable path to initialization, but there were dozens of villages their size or greater. The other villages however were further away from the glacial flows and safe from the raids of the Frigidi. Caleb wasn’t sure how they managed keeping beasts at bay without initialized, but manage they did as evidence by their survival.

“Which one?” Caleb asked.

He’d only visited the nearest, Frostside with his master to trade for more steel, but was familiar with the names of the rest.

“All of them,” Kelia said.

“How? Why have we not had word of it?”

“I don’t know. I don’t even know how the empire heard of it, but they are here to investigate, and we have to help.”

After that, the moved onto packing up as they were supposed to be doing. Kelia already had a bag with all her earthly possessions which amounted to little. She spent most of her time roaming the forest and anything she valued she kept on herself daily.

Caleb found while packing that he no longer needed most of the tools of his trade.

He had a new hammer. The legion would supply an anvil. He no longer used tongs, bellows or flux. So he packed up a small bag of brushes and chisels and other tools he’d yet to find magical solutions for, and carried them with. Bog tried stacking his pile of chew logs in a way he could drag, but Caleb put an end to that.
