Carmack sat in his strategy room, going over his maps as he awaited his council. Finally, their elite vanguard was starting to show its fangs, and it was time to enshrine its power.

One by one, Carmack’s assistant led Aphexia, Gorn, Cella, and the lieutenants into the room. They all gathered around his 3D stone strategic map, waiting for Carmack.

“Thank you, everyone, for coming on short notice. As you all know, we have been preparing our military forces and strengthening them. We arrived only months ago, and found ourselves horribly incapable of defending ourselves against the horrors of the Undercavern. But that is all changing now. The Guardsman rival project is well on its way. The tailors advise they are about forty percent through the scale armor contract. We have also established a promising new training facility, and are well on our way to developing our vanguard force into something truly formidable.”

“Impressive,” the lieutenant said, glancing and nodding at one another.

“With all due respect, hero, it’s not just skills and armor that we need,” Captain Cella said. “We need manpower. Half the time my men are buggering about helping with the laborers’ tasks. How are we meant to probably defend and enforce the law with this kind of disorganisation?”

“Noted, Captain. It is unfortunate that you and your men have to work, but we have little choice. Establishing our settlement here has been a massive undertaking. But as the pieces fall into place, so will your duties. And lastly, we’re not facing off against goblins and petty thugs here. Poorly trained manpower will just get themselves killed in these caverns. We need to be smarter and better trained than the Lydrus of old.”

“Poorly trained? It was you who lost three men already, wasn’t it?” Cella countered.

“Captain,” Commander Gorn raised his voice.

“Relax, it’s fine,” Carmack interjected with a raised hand. “Sorry, Captain. It is unfortunate, isn’t it? Every loss we suffer is painful. By the way, how many men did you lose back in Lydrus? Perhaps we should have a vigil.”

“That’s not the point! Lydrus was different!”

“Sorry, but I’m afraid it is. The fall of Lydrus is the reason we are here in the first place—and not in your beautiful former city. Despite having far greater numbers than we do now, you failed to hold the city. To be blunt, your soldiers were not strong enough. Your tactics were not superior enough, and your leadership was not wise enough. We can’t allow that to happen again. We can’t allow ourselves to make the same mistakes time and time again. Building up numbers means little if those troops lack skills and quality equipment. So, I’m sorry, Captain, but I’m just not willing to watch this settlement and its people suffer through the same mistakes.”

“Why you? Such insolence!”

“Well said,” Commander Gorn interrupted the reddening Captain Cella as his nostrils flared.

“I hate to agree with the hero, but he’s right. Your guardsmen are… lacking,” Aphexia said with sarcastic air quotes.

“You’re insulting the guard? This is outrageous. The men who laid down their lives for our great city? And you two, what do you think of this?” Captain Cella turned to the lieutenants.

“I lost friends in Lydrus… good men. Brave men…”

Captain Cella’s face brightened as the lieutenant spoke grim words.

“But the hero is right. The moment they broke through our walls, the battle was over. We simply couldn’t stand up to them. I watched as individual demons took on multiple knights. I saw entire squads of guardsmen wiped out by a single monster. We lost that day because we lacked the combat ability to stand up to our enemies. No, I don’t want to relive that. Not even if admitting it is shameful,” the lieutenant shook his head. “I’m with you. Hero Carmack and Commander Gorn, I will follow whatever orders you give me. We need to get stronger, and fast.”

“And you?” Carmack turned to the other lieutenant.

“I agree. We can’t waste any more time. This is the only way forward that makes any sense. Things I saw that day… the terror. People carrying their dead children,” the man’s voice quivered. “That can’t be allowed to happen again. No matter what!”

“I can’t believe it!” Captain Cella shouted. “Turning against your own people! It’s a disgrace! Have some damn respect for what we stand for!”

“Calm down, Captain.” Commander Gorn narrowed his gaze at the red-faced captain. “No one is turning against anyone. All of us are in agreement that we need to get stronger. Unfortunately, risks come with that goal. And we have to accept that we might take casualties. This is what it means to be a guardsman. So the people don’t have to suffer. If you can’t stomach that, then perhaps you shouldn’t be part of the guard.”

Cella looked around the room for protest, but none came. He realized he was alone in his outrage and took a deep breath. This wasn’t a battle he could fight at this moment, and losing his station would throw their plans into peril.

“Sorry… I apologize for my outburst,” Captain Cella lowered his head. “Please continue.”

“Good. Let us carry on,” Carmack said. “On this map, I have marked watch stations along the tunnels that lead to the undead catacomb and the beryl mine. I have marked where we will station sentries. I have also requested that the clerks form another mining group to take over the yaddik camp. However, unlike our other mines, this one will be quite some distance from the spire, and as such, the project will be a little more cumbersome, and the manpower required will be more exhaustive.”

“What are you suggesting, hero?”“We have invited almost two hundred and fifty former slaves into our society. Now, I doubt any of them will be in a hurry to head back to the mines. However, we don’t need them to. If they can take up other jobs within the spire, we should be able to free up some manpower without affecting our current responsibilities.”

“That could work,” Commander Gorn rubbed his chin. “But they were in rough condition. I doubt we’ll be getting much from them anytime soon.”

“Yes, I assumed as much. We may have to take a temporary hit to some of our activities until then. Look, we’re going to be busy, and it is unlikely that the guard will be able to avoid labouring jobs anytime soon. Unfortunately, this is a sacrifice I must ask you all to make. Sooner or later, something down here will bear its fangs against us, and if we’re not prepared for that fight, we’ll fall the same as your former city did.”

“I think we’re all in agreement that the guard can help where needed,” Commander Gorn said and turned to the others. The lieutenants nodded, but Cella glared back for several seconds before sighing and agreeing.

“Good, then you’re all dismissed. Please, take a copy of these maps and prepare your men accordingly. Each station is marked with one of your names. If you see your name, it is your responsibility to provide men to man it. Now go. There should be no confusion about what you have to do.”

It would take some time before they were organized and ready to occupy the mine. Once his home was cleared of company again, Carmack instructed his assistant not to interrupt him and opened his skill tab.

The list went on and on. There were literally hundreds of useful skills. Too much choice was a legitimate problem in his unique position.

Before letting his mind wander into the countless options available, Carmack reviewed the Enchanting skill tree. Within it, “Enchanting Mana Control” was particularly tempting. Having greater control over his mana when enchanting and therefore, his efficiency when using it would no doubt provide a massive boon. He doubted mana control as his only problem, though he did believe it was likely the largest contributing factor for his less-than-perfect enchanting test run.

However, he wasn’t about to pick the first interesting skill he spotted. There were many skills, and the problems they faced were vast. Perhaps there were other options that would increase their chances of survival more.

Normally, people only saw available skills to purchase based on their experiences. For example, a spearman would see spear-related skills to purchase, not axe-related skills. Even with the considerable amount of time he had spent investigating the skill sheet, making decisions was still far from easy.

Carmack almost envied normal people. Of course, he didn’t really. He understood the potential his situation put him in. Still, it was no easy task scanning through hundreds of skills that opened up into expansive skill trees. Especially when he didn’t have to purchase supporting skills if he didn’t want to, it wasn’t just about finding powerful skills, but also about finding skills that would complement his geomancy.

Carmack had always been good at healing, thanks to his distant troll heritage, but he had also been physically stronger before coming to this world. The prospect of having to fight someone or something who was truly strong was worrying. In his current state, he was vulnerable, yet he couldn’t stop pumping his stat points into Mental. The more mana he gained, the closer he would get to casting spells of higher circles, and even with all his points in Mental, at low Iron rank, the strongest spells he could cast were 4th circle.

If Carmack was forced to start evening out his stat distribution, it would likely take until Ruby or even Diamond rank to use 12th circle magic. Since the ranking system went from Bronze to Iron, Gold, Titanium, Ruby, Diamond, Adamantium, and Celestial, it put him in some ways off his goals. But not if he could find skills to bridge that gap.

Going back and forth over the skill list, Carmack caught sight of something that appealed to him. Biomancy. Opening the skill tree, his eyes widened. This was what he was after. He could purchase spell skills that could enhance his durability, speed, strength, and just about anything else related to the body.

Yet he paused.

He needed several Biomancy skills, he knew that without a doubt. But was now really the time to focus on enhancing his only physical capabilities?

No… not yet.

Carmack slumped and returned to the Enchanter skill tree. He knew he had weakened the power of Aphexia’s new sword because of his lack of ability in enchantment. Luckily, both the ruby and the monster core used to create it were outstanding specimens, and because of that, it was still a potent weapon. However, items like that wouldn’t continue to just fall into his lap. He needed to be smart about this. If he started pumping out low-quality magical items, then their settlement wasn’t going to gain a reputation as some kind of magical item mecca.

No, he needed to be better than that.

Sighing, Carmack scrolled the expanded skill tree. He realized it wasn’t just mana control that he lacked. Lesser Core Enchanting might be required. The three cores he had looted from the spider webs were just that, lesser monster cores. They were still valuable, and in the right hands could make good magical items that would fetch good coin.

I might struggle with Greater Core Enchanting in my current state, but I should have more than enough mana for Lesser Core Enchanting. He mused over his options, but it seemed obvious what he should do. Eventually, he would need both this and  “Enchanting Mana Control.” But “Enchanting Mana Control” wouldn’t change the skill he was using. Greater Core Enchanting would still drain his mana regardless of how efficient his mana control was, and that would limit him.

We’re likely to be relying far more on Lesser Cores for the backbone of our economy.

Carmack scratched his chin as he hovered over the skill. There was nothing effective about constantly draining himself to enchant a single item, and if they were to build a real economy out of magical items, he would be busy.

How irritating. This is what I get when trying to skip a step.

With a reluctant sigh, Carmack purchased “Lesser Core Enchanting.” This was perhaps not the funnest of decisions, but it was the wisest, he knew. His entire plan for the settlement had been built around the idea of making magical items, and that just wouldn’t work if production couldn’t be scaled up.

This was the way, Carmack knew. He had been powerful on Avalock, but it hadn’t been enough. That wasn’t a mistake he would make again. This time, he would build a worthy army and civilization.
