The oak’s bark felt warm beneath Cade’s palm—not the ambient warmth of sunlight, but something internal and vibrant, as if he’d pressed his hand against a living creature’s pulse point rather than mere wood. Power thrummed through the ancient material, resonating with something deep inside of him.

In the span of a heartbeat, reality fell away.

When Cade’s eyes cleared of the blinding flash, he stood in an endless expanse of billowing clouds and golden sunlight. The heavens stretched in every direction. It was a breathtaking skyscape that seemed to exist beyond the constraints of the physical world. Ribbons of luminous energy—threads of vibrant color that defied simple categorization—swept through the mist surrounding him, weaving intricate patterns that extended beyond what his mortal eyes could follow.

Crimson. Emerald. Sapphire. Amber. Violet. More colors than he could name, some seeming to exist in spectrums he’d never witnessed before. The colored streams pulsed with raw energy, converging at a single point directly before him.

The oak.

“Well,” Cade muttered to himself, “this certainly beats the view from Elysia’s hanging gardens. Though substantially more confusing.”

Here, in this otherworldly vision, the tree stood even more magnificent than in the physical realm. Its bark gleamed like burnished bronze kissed by dawn’s first light, leaves shimmering with an inner luminescence that cast prismatic reflections across the cloudscape. The branches reached skyward with a deeply satisfying symmetry, and Cade noted that each limb was positioned as if sculpted by a master artist rather than grown naturally.

At last, here was empirical proof that art was alive.

His gaze roamed the masterpiece of a tree, slowly drifting down its trunk. There, between roiling clouds of a poofy white substance, was what truly captured Cade’s attention. The tree had no roots.

Well, there was something in place of what should have been the roots.

Instead of burrowing into soil, the oak’s base seamlessly merged with the colorful streams of energy that flowed through the cloudscape. The vibrant ribbons wove themselves into the tree’s foundation, feeding directly into its core while the tree itself seemed to anchor them in place.

Cade squinted, trying to make sense of the impossibility before him. The colored streams reminded him of something he’d seen before—in books, in tales whispered among cultivators, and in legends he’d half-dismissed as fancy.

“I mean, they’ve got to be,” he breathed, recognition dawning slowly. “These colored streams are ley lines.”

He’d always imagined them as abstract concepts, not literal rivers of power flowing through reality. Yet here they were, tangible and brilliant, feeding directly into the oak like tributaries into a mighty river.

Movement at the tree’s base drew his attention. Black thorns—glistening like obsidian and sharp as daggers—erupted from the clouds, wrapping around the colored streams. Wherever they touched, the vibrant energy dimmed, its natural flow redirected and channeled upward into the tree. The oak itself seemed to shudder in protest, its branches trembling despite the absence of wind.

“That can’t be right,” Cade muttered, moving closer to examine the phenomenon.

The thorns didn’t match the tree’s organic structure. They moved differently, and appeared to grow differently too. Instead of extending outward from the oak, they were reaching toward it from the surrounding cloudscape. Here, amongst the clouds, the thorny vines reminded him of vipers slithering out of tall grass.

Wherever they made contact, the tree’s natural radiance diminished, replaced by a sickly emerald glow that reminded him all too vividly of Mordred’s corrupting influence.

Understanding began to form in Cade’s mind, and he cursed as the pieces started to fall into place.

“They’re not part of you, are they?” he asked the tree, certainty growing with each passing moment. “They’re parasites.”

The thorns were foreign invaders, forcing their will upon the oak and the ley lines it anchored. They were transforming what should have been a natural nexus of power into something else entirely.

Cade’s mind raced ahead, connecting implications that grew more disturbing with each logical step. If these thorns could corrupt this newly formed tree, twisting it into something unnatural, then what about the other trees? What about the divine thrones themselves?

Life’s power stemmed from the Oakthorn Throne beneath Elysia—that much he knew now. But if the “thorns” were parasitic additions rather than natural features...

“The Oakthorn Thrones aren’t natural at all,” Cade whispered, horror dawning as the full picture emerged. “They’re corrupted. All of them.”

Cade started to pace. The ground was frustratingly soft and cozy, feeling totally at odds with the unfolding horror before him. He tapped a finger against his chin as he thought of each counterargument to his discovery.

Maybe the roots were normal? Maybe it wasn’t a parasite, but some other form of ancient symbiosis? But if that were true, why did this tree seem to reject the thorn’s influence? Worse, if the thorns were normal, why did they remind him of Mordred’s power?

If his theory was correct, then the gods themselves were keepers of these twisted trees, subjugating these convergence points of ley lines for their own gain. They weren’t protectors of the world—they were exploiting it.

No wonder Death had been so desperate for him to destroy this oak before it fully manifested. A free, uncorrupted tree would threaten the entire divine power structure.

Movement caught his eye, and he dropped into a fighting stance. A figure emerged from behind the oak, stepping with ethereal grace through the swirling mists. Its form was composed entirely of crackling crimson energy. Though featureless, its presence radiated a familiar power.

The figure gestured emphatically toward the writhing thorns, its intent unmistakable even without words.

Free the tree.

He reached deep within himself, bypassing his familiar destruction magic to draw directly on the crimson threads of chaos that had always been part of him. If there was even a slim chance that the goddess Destruction could sense her own power inside of him, he wouldn’t risk using it to free this tree. Chaos, however, seemed to be unchecked by the gods.

The power surged forth, raw and untamed yet responding to his will with surprising precision.

He visualized the chaos cords forming a lasso of sorts, and they formed it near-instantly. Cade directed the chaos energy toward the nearest cluster of thorns. Upon contact, the obsidian spikes hissed and recoiled, emerald smoke rising from them like steam from a kettle. He whipped the lasso around a large cluster of the thorns, and the cords bit deep into the black vines.

“Don’t like that much, do you?” Cade murmured, redoubling his efforts. “Turns out chaos doesn’t play well with others.”

One by one, the thorns withered and crumbled beneath his assault, releasing their grip on the ley lines. When one cluster withered and died, he reforged his lasso and attacked the next vine. With each victory, the streams grew brighter, their natural flow restored as they returned to their proper paths. The oak itself seemed to respond with bright flashes of prismatic energy. Its branches stretched higher, and its trunk grew more robust with each passing moment.

The crimson figure observed silently, radiating what Cade could only interpret as approval. As the last of the thorns disintegrated, Cade turned toward his mysterious companion, questions forming on his lips.

“Okay, so who are you? Are you the source of my—”

Reality slammed back into place with a jarring suddenness.

Cade staggered backward, snatching his hand away from the oak as the cathedral around him trembled violently. He let out the breath he’d been holding. His internal clock gauged that only a few moments had passed during that vision.

But his work in that strange cloudscape had clearly worked, as the tree before him was practically vibrating with power.

The oak’s trunk expanded, bark rippling like liquid as it grew thicker and taller. Multicolored roots burst from the stone floor, shattering ancient masonry as they drove downward with unstoppable force. Branches stretched outward and upward, crashing through what remained of the cathedral ceiling to reach for the open sky.

“What have you DONE?!” Death’s voice cut through the chaos, slicing into Cade’s mind like frozen steel.

Cade whirled and observed the deity hovering at the edge of the ritual circle. His form flickered with unrestrained fury. Shadows gathered around him, frost spreading across the stones where his essence touched the physical world. Yet for all his rage, he made no move to approach the rapidly expanding tree.

“Oh, you’re still here? I’m just doing a little landscaping,” Cade replied, backing away from the oak as another section of the ceiling collapsed nearby. “Removed some parasites. You know how it is.”

The cathedral groaned ominously. Ancient stones shifted as the oak’s roots penetrated deeper into the foundations. A fissure split the floor beneath Cade’s feet, forcing him to leap aside as it widened into a chasm that revealed glimpses of the crypt below, already being crushed by the tree’s unstoppable growth.

“FOOL!” Death’s form began to retreat, floating farther and farther from the oak’s growing influence. “You have no idea what you’ve unleashed!”

Cade watched with growing interest as Death backed further away, seeming almost afraid of the expanding tree. “That’s where you’re wrong,” he called out, ducking as another section of ceiling collapsed nearby. “I’m starting to understand plenty.”

Death’s retreat was confirmation of his theory. The deities feared uncorrupted trees—feared what might happen if mortals discovered the truth behind their divine ‘thrones.’

“I see I was wrong about you,” Death snarled, his form now barely visible at the cathedral’s edge. “You are not a tool we can trust to be left alone.”

“Kettle.” Cade pointed at his chest. “Cauldron.”

He pointed his finger at Death.

But the god of endings had already vanished, unwilling—or unable—to remain as the oak’s domain expanded.

The tree’s power rippled outward in visible waves, transforming everything it touched. Where moments before had been corrupted stone and ancient evil, life now bloomed with vibrant intensity. Moss crept across broken pillars, flowers erupted from cracks in the stone floor, and vines twined around crumbling arches with impossible speed.

It would have been beautiful to witness, had the cathedral not been disintegrating around him.

“Time to NOT die,” Cade muttered, gauging the distance to the nearest exit as the floor buckled beneath his feet.

A thunderous crack split the air as another support beam gave way, bringing down a cascade of stone directly in his path. Chunks of masonry the size of wagons crashed down, narrowly missing him as he threw himself sideways. The young thief rolled across the heaving floor before springing back to his feet in one fluid motion.

“CADE!” Elena’s voice cut through the chaos, barely audible above the cathedral’s death throes and falling debris.

He spotted her at a shattered window on the second level, waving frantically. Jer stood beside her, the Soulkeeper’s Crown clutched tightly against his chest. They’d found an escape route, but reaching them would be a challenge worthy of the Tournament itself.

The floor between Cade and his crew was rapidly transforming into a maze of fissures and collapsing stone. A direct path was impossible—he’d need to improvise.

“Just hang on!” he shouted back, already scanning for a viable route.

A fallen column spanned one of the wider chasms, providing a potential bridge. Cade sprinted toward it, leaping onto the smooth stone surface as the floor behind him gave way completely. The column shifted beneath his weight and began to slide toward the abyss.

“Of course,” he muttered.

He increased his frantic pace to a full sprint, wind tugging on what remained of his tunic and cloak.

Cade reached the far end just as the column tipped past the point of no return, and he launched himself into the air with a desperate shout. His hands caught the edge of a stone balustrade. His muscles screamed as he hauled himself upward.

“If I get out of this, I am taking the world’s longest nap,” Cade promised himself.

The floor beneath him started to teeter to one side.

“Maybe I’ll try accounting. That sounds less life-threatening than this,” the thief grimaced as he forced himself back to his feet.

There was no time to rest. The balcony crumbled around and beneath him, with stones breaking free to plummet into the abyss below. Cade sprinted along what remained of the walkway, jumping to catch a hanging chain that had once supported an ornate chandelier.

The chain groaned under his weight, links stretching ominously as he swung in a wide arc across the cathedral’s central space.

“Shit, shit, shit!” he yelled.

At the apex of his swing, he released his grip, hurling himself toward a half-collapsed archway that might—if the architectural gods were feeling particularly generous—provide a path to the window where his friends waited.

He crashed onto a narrow ledge, momentum carrying him into a roll that barely kept him from plunging over the edge. Stone crumbled beneath his hands as he scrambled for purchase, finding just enough grip to pull himself onto more stable footing.

“Graceful,” Jer’s voice called from above, the acrobat leaning precariously from the window ledge.

“Little help?” Cade replied, eyeing the fifteen-foot vertical climb with growing concern as more of the cathedral collapsed around him.

Jer grinned and unfurled a length of rope, revealing a miniature grappling hook attached to the end. “Came prepared for once!”

The hook clattered against the stone beside Cade. He grabbed it, giving the line a firm tug to test its stability before beginning his ascent. The wall shuddered beneath his feet, chunks of geriatric stone breaking free with each step.

Ten feet up, the section of wall directly below him gave way completely. Cade dangled from the rope, swinging wildly as his boots scraped against the crumbling surface.

“Pull!” he shouted, feet scrabbling for nonexistent purchase.

Elena and Jer heaved on the rope, hauling him upward inch by agonizing inch. His shoulders burned with the strain of supporting his entire weight. His fingers cramped around the coarse hemp. Just as his grip began to slip, his free hand caught the window ledge.

Elena seized his wrist, hauling him upward with strength belied by her slight frame. Jer grabbed his other arm, and together they dragged him through the window like a particularly stubborn fish.

“Cutting it a bit close, aren’t you?” Elena demanded, already backing away from the increasingly unstable wall.

“Thought I’d—” Cade panted, heart hammering against his ribs. “—add some dramatic tension.”

“Mission accomplished,” Jer replied, eyes widening as he glanced outside. “But I think we’re just getting to the good part.”

The entire cathedral seemed to be imploding now, collapsing inward on itself as the crypt beneath it disintegrated. The oak tree continued to grow at an impossible rate, its expanding domain accelerating the destruction as its roots crushed what remained of the ancient structures above and below.

“Go, go, GO!” Cade shouted, shoving his friends ahead of him as they sprinted along a crumbling exterior walkway.

The path ahead ended abruptly where part of the structure had already fallen away, leaving a twenty-foot gap between them and the next section. Beyond that, Cade could see Nora and Gavin waving frantically from stable ground, Chubbs clutched protectively in Gav’s massive arms.

Behind Cade and the twins, the walkway began to break apart. Stones dropped away into oblivion like some demented countdown. Ahead, the gap seemed to widen with each passing second as more of the structure succumbed to the oak’s relentless expansion.

“We have to jump!” Elena yelled, barely audible above the cathedral’s death throes.

“You first!” Cade seized her arm, channeling just enough wind magic to give her the boost she’d need.

Elena didn’t hesitate. She sprinted forward, launching herself across the gap with the grace of a pregnant goat. Her body arced through the air, momentarily silhouetted against the storm-darkened sky before her boots hit solid stone on the far side. She stumbled, caught herself, and turned back to face them.

“Jer, you’re next!” Cade positioned himself at the edge, hands already glowing with wind magic.

The redhead clutched the Soulkeeper’s Crown tightly to his chest, eyes fixed on the daunting gap. “If I die, I’m haunting you forever,” he promised, backing up for a running start. They met eyes.

“Too soon?” Jer inquired with an embarrassed grin.

“Way too soon, man,” Cade agreed, summoning more power to his hands.

Jer grinned even wider and winked right before launching himself forward. His legs were pumping as he built up speed. At the last possible moment, Cade released a concentrated burst of wind magic, propelling his friend across the chasm with an extra boost of momentum targeted at the small of his back.

Watching the acrobat’s leap reminded him of a dragon diving toward their prey. Powerful. Graceful. Deadly. He plummeted onto the far ledge in a nauseating twist and flip, the crown clutched protectively against his chest as he rolled to a stop.

Cade shook his head. “I’m not doing that.”

The walkway beneath Cade’s feet was rapidly disappearing, giving him no runway for a proper jump. What remained would barely support his weight, let alone allow for a running start. He’d have to rely entirely on his magic.

He channeled destruction magic toward his feet, waiting until the last possible moment to unleash it. The walkway crumbled at his heels, stone falling away into nothingness as he balanced on the very edge of what remained.

“Come on, Cade!” Elena shouted, both she and Jer reaching out across the widening gap.

With a final gulp of air that might well be his last, Cade unleashed the destruction magic in a controlled explosion. The concussive force launched him forward just as the last stones fell away beneath him.

For a terrifying moment, he was airborne with nothing but a yawning chasm beneath him, arms outstretched toward his friends. The gap was wider than he’d estimated—too wide, he realized with a sickening lurch in his stomach.

He wasn’t going to make it.

Then Jer did something either incredibly brave or incredibly stupid. He threw the crown behind him right as he leapt forward, arm extended beyond the edge, gloved hand reaching for Cade’s.

Their fingers brushed, missed, then connected with bruising force. Jer’s enhanced gloves adhered to Cade’s skin instantly, creating an unbreakable bond. The acrobat’s body slammed flat against the ledge, nearly dragging him over the edge as Cade’s momentum threatened to pull them both into the abyss.

Elena dropped to her knees, grabbing Jer’s legs to anchor him in place. “Are you fucking kidding me, Jer?!”

“Just… lending a hand,” her twin grunted through the pain.

Together, they hauled Cade upward, inch by excruciating inch. His body scraped against jagged stone, tearing his clothes and leaving bloody furrows across his exposed skin. The pain barely registered through the surge of adrenaline and the desperate gratitude that his friends hadn’t abandoned him.

The cathedral shuddered violently, the entire structure groaning like a wounded beast. Stones rained down from what remained of the ceiling, forcing them to duck and weave as they sprinted along the narrow corridor that might—if fortune decided to stop kicking them in the teeth—lead to safety.

They emerged onto a broad stone terrace, the cathedral’s front steps stretching away beneath them toward the marshy ground. Fifty yards ahead, the rest of their team waited with expressions ranging from frantic worry to outright terror.

“COME ON!” Gavin bellowed, waving his arms as if his gesticulation might somehow speed their escape.

The ground shuddered beneath their feet, the tremors growing more violent with each passing second. Cracks spread across the stone terrace, widening into fissures that revealed the collapsing crypt beneath. The entire structure was imploding, drawn inexorably inward by the oak’s expanding domain.

“Let’s go, O’Keefe’s!” Cade pushed his friends ahead of him, falling into step behind them as they sprinted down the worn steps.

A massive chunk of stone crashed down directly in their path, forcing them to leap over the obstacle without breaking stride. More debris rained around them, some pieces large enough to crush a person entirely. Cade dodged left, then right, serpentining through the deadly barrage as he kept his eyes fixed on his team ahead.

Thirty yards. Twenty. Ten.

The earth itself seemed to be collapsing behind them. The ground sank inward as though some colossal weight were drawing it into a massive sinkhole. The steps they’d just traversed cracked apart, massive stones tumbling into the widening chasm.

Five yards.

Nora stretched out her gauntlet hand, face set and eyes wide. “Faster!”

Cade lunged forward, hand outstretched to meet hers. Their fingers connected just as the ground beneath his feet gave way completely. Nora’s grip was like iron, her incredible strength more than enough to haul him forward into the relative safety of his team’s embrace.

“RUN!” he gasped, not wasting breath on thanks or explanations. “Keep going!”

Together, they fled across the blighted marshland, putting as much distance as possible between themselves and the imploding cathedral. Behind them, the collapse accelerated, the ground sinking inward with increasing speed as the oak’s domain expanded ever outward.

They’d barely reached what might charitably be considered safe ground when a sound like thunder rolled across the marshes. The imploding cathedral reached some critical tipping point, the entire structure vanishing into the earth with a concussive blast that knocked them all off their feet.

Cade hit the marshy ground hard, air driven from his lungs by the impact. He rolled onto his back, gasping for breath as he stared at the sky. Storm clouds swirled overhead, and lightning flashed between them in arcs of brilliant white and gold.

Then, through the gap where the cathedral had stood, something new emerged from the earth.

The oak tree rose like a titan awakening from ancient slumber, its massive trunk widening and stretching upward at an impossible rate. Branches unfurled toward the storm-dark sky, leaves sprouting and multiplying in rapid succession. Roots spread outward beneath the soil, their progress visible as the ground rippled like water disturbed by a stone.

“Holy shit,” Gavin breathed, staring wide-eyed at the transformation.

“I think ‘holy’ is exactly the right word,” Nora murmured, her expression a mixture of awe and wariness.

“Language,” Jer and Elena said at the same time, then shared a warm smile with one another.

Chubbs stirred, still unconscious, on Gav’s shoulder. Behind them, Cade spotted Rayka and Orro holding hands as they both gazed at the rising oak tree.

The ley line convergence continued to grow, reaching proportions that defied natural law. Its trunk expanded to the width of a small house. Its canopy spread outward to cover an area that could have sheltered a village. Light pulsed from its core, rippling outward in a visible dome that transformed everything it touched.

Withered grass became lush and green. Stagnant pools clarified into crystal-clear springs. Flowers—real, vibrant flowers—erupted from soil that hadn’t supported life in centuries.

“Cade… What did you do?” Rayka asked, her voice barely above a whisper as she stared at the transformed landscape.

“Just killed some god-weeds,” Cade replied simply, his gaze fixed on the massive tree. “It wasn’t what Death claimed it was. The thorns were corrupting it, trying to twist it into an Oakthorn Throne. But the tree itself... It’s something else entirely.”

The dome of energy expanded outward, approaching their position with steady inevitability. When it drew within fifty yards, however, every instinct in Cade’s body screamed a warning. His magic surged unbidden, reacting to some primal threat he couldn’t consciously identify.

“Back up,” he ordered, scrambling to his feet. “Everyone back up, now!”

They retreated hastily, putting another hundred yards between themselves and the advancing dome. As if sensing their withdrawal, the energy field slowed, then stopped, creating a perfect circle of vibrant life amid the blighted marshes.

Within that circle, the oak towered in majestic splendor, a living monument to possibilities Cade had never imagined. Yet despite its beauty, every fiber of his being warned against approaching it. This was a domain unlike any he’d encountered. If Life’s Domain had been a candle, this was an inferno of radiant power.

“Is it dangerous?” Elena asked, eyeing the transformed landscape with suspicion.

“I don’t think so,” Cade replied carefully. “Not inherently. But it’s... different. I suspect it’s not accepting visitors right now.”

He studied the dome of energy, noting how it rippled with subtle patterns that reminded him of the ley lines he’d seen in his vision. This was a place of power—raw, untamed, and unfettered by divine interference. For now, at least, it was probably safer to admire it from a distance, even if a part of him really wanted to ignore his own advice and go poking around in there.

A final tremor shook the earth, followed by a deafening concussion that rolled across the marshes like a physical wave. The oak’s domain pulsed one last time, settling into a steady, rhythmic glow that reminded Cade of a heartbeat.

“Well,” Jer said finally, breaking the stunned silence. “I think I can safely say this wasn’t in the job description when Jameson hired us.”

The redhead reached behind his back and produced the Soulkeeper’s Crown with a theatrical flourish. “At least I managed to grab the right crown, right? Kind of hard to keep track of magical artifacts when you’re running for your life.”

The crown gleamed dully in the strange light, emerald energy swirling within its metal confines like a trapped spirit. Mordred was sealed away once more, denied the freedom he’d so desperately sought.

“Is that thing safe to carry around?” Gavin asked, eyeing the artifact with understandable wariness.

“About as safe as anything else we do,” Cade replied dryly. “Which is to say, not very, but we’ll manage.”

Relief and exhaustion crashed over him in equal measure as the full weight of what they’d accomplished—and what they’d discovered—settled on his shoulders. The gods had been hiding the truth about the Oakthorn Thrones, perhaps for millennia. The implications were staggering, paradigm-shifting in a way he wasn’t prepared to fully process.

But for now, they were alive. They’d survived, rescued Chubbs, and prevented a potentially catastrophic ascension. It would have to be enough.

Cade strode forward, seized the front of Jer’s tunic, and for one alarming moment, it looked like he might throttle the acrobat. Even tensed, and Elena instinctively stepped forward to intervene.

Before she could, Cade whipped his other arm forward and pulled Jer into a crushing embrace.

“You damn fool,” Cade muttered, voice thick with emotion that he’d deny later if questioned. “Don’t you ever sacrifice yourself again.”

Jer stiffened in surprise before his arms came up to return the hug with equal fervor.

“Wasn’t planning on making it a habit,” he replied, the quip undermined by the tremble in his voice. “Did you see Elena’s face? Pretty sure she was about to kill me herself.”

“Still might,” Elena called from behind them, though her usual sharpness was softened by obvious relief.

They stood like that for a long moment, two brothers in all but blood, surrounded by a family forged through fire and danger and unwavering loyalty.

Behind them, the oak’s domain pulsed with newborn power.

Everything was going to be fine.
