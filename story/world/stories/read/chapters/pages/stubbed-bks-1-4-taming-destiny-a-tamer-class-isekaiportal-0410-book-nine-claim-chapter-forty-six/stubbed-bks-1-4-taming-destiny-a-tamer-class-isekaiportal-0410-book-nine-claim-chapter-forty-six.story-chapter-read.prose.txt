“From what the scouts have reported back, we seem to be heading directly towards this spot,” I say, laying down the piece of parchment I’ve been working on and pointing to a section of the roughly-drawn coastline. It’s not one of the official maps that we were given after entering the competition – it turns out that we are significantly further north than any of the explorers went. Instead, I’ve been using my limited map-making abilities to transmit the information my scouts have sent me.

Fortunately, we haven’t gone as far adrift as I’d worried we might have, and we’re definitely still heading towards the right landmass. I can be so certain because, as Sirocco and Ivor went down the coastline to the left, they encountered landmarks I was able to place on the map. The biggest were a mountain range with one particularly identifiable because of its distance from the other mountains and its distinct shape, and a large grove of trees, discernable even when surrounded by other trees. They’re still going further down the coastline and continuing to send images, though they’re starting to reach the edge of the range for which that will be possible.

All of the lords lean forwards to inspect the map.

Elarion frowns as his gaze flicks between my map and the official ones.

“It seems to me that we’re not far from the grove that was identified as full of magically-powerful wood,” he comments.

I shrug.

“That’s what the images I’ve received from my companions indicate – at least assuming this is the area marked on the map and not a similar looking one at a distance.”

“Is that likely?” Josiah asks dubiously.

I shrug.

“Everything is possible, but the mountain’s pyramid-like shape is distinct enough that it seems unlikely that somewhere else would have both mountain and grove within the same distance as is indicated on the map.”

Josiah nods, his frown easing and the look in his eyes becoming thoughtful. Next to him, Valence’s expression turns downright eager.

“We should create our settlement near that, then,” he urges. “Mana-imbued wood is highly valuable and would be incredibly useful for building defences and weapons, not to mention everything else we’ll need.” Given that he comes from a territory that has very little forested area, I can imagine he knows well the cost of importing such material. Though, given how the area is forested as far as the eyes can see, I don’t think we’ll suffer for lack of wood, even if more mundane than might be available in the grove.

“Have you not considered that such a grove would be protected?” Josiah points out. “With our luck, it would be a Beast Emperor there, not a King, as bad as that would be. We are not well-placed to face such a challenge right now. We must settle the land first. Survival over profit – at least to begin.”

“How we begin is how we will continue,” Valence argues. “If you are unwilling to face risk for gain, why are you here?”

His challenge rings for a moment. Then I shift and all eyes turn to me.

“Josiah makes a good point,” I agree. “This is a foreign land and one that we know is thick with beasts. We have to assume that any decent resource will have its guardian. And when making landfall is not the time to upset the entire ecosystem by trying to take territory that is probably highly contested.”

Valence looks like he’s sucked on a lemon, but he reluctantly nods.

“You speak reason,” he admits. “However, I would urge us to take control of this resource as soon as we can. If we do not, others will,” he warns.

“I would second that,” Elarion agrees with a nod. “It is indeed likely that the grove will have fierce guardians, but I would be happy to lead an expedition to face its guardian – once we are better established. My family has a great deal of experience with magical wood – and the groves from which it comes.”

I see the calculation in his eyes, the faint gleam of greed that reveals exactly where his offer comes from.

“We can hold those kinds of discussions later,” I suggest. “For now, perhaps we can make landfall here?” I point out a stretch of coastline that offers a break in the cliffs that otherwise block most of the coast on this section of the Lost Continent. “As far as we can tell, there are no significant resources right nearby – at least, nothing that my companions were able to sense from the air. Thus, it shouldn’t be an area that is too heavily contested. Hopefully it is an area where we can establish ourselves. There is also a hill nearby that could offer us a defensive advantage if we choose to settle there.”

“The forest reaches almost down to the water’s edge,” Valence muses. “Unless we wish to build on the water’s edge itself, we will have to clear space for our settlement.”

Elarion flicks that thought away with a casual gesture of his hand.

“That will not take long – many of my retinue have experience in logging. Then, the trees we fell can be used to construct our first settlement. It will reduce the immediate draw on the resources we brought with us.”

“Resources are an important point,” Layton adds in, taking the discussion off at a slight tangent. “We all have low food stores, especially since some of our spatial and preservation enchantments were damaged during the journey. Establishing lines of resource collection as soon as possible is important. If our people are busy building houses, they are not hunting or foraging for food. Perhaps we should envision staying on the ships a little longer until our food situation is better.”

No one looks happy about that idea – neither am I. The thought of being so close to land that we can smell it, yet remaining on the ships in accommodation that is feeling increasingly cramped, especially for people who aren’t lords and don’t have a two-room suite all to themselves…. Yet we don’t dismiss it out of hand because, in many ways, it’s a sound suggestion.

“It would enable quick retreat if we find that this quiet section of coastline is anything but,” Josiah points out slowly. “The only thing worse than clearing trees and building a settlement is suddenly having to abandon it because we’ve encroached on the territory of a powerful Beast King – or Beast Emperor – who takes exception to it.

“It would also allow us to change our minds if we do more exploration and find a better location later,” I add. “Much as I don’t like to admit it, I think Layton has a good point here. Exploration and establishment of supply lines has to be our first priority; creating a permanent settlement is important, yes, but only when we know for sure where we want to settle. Also, consider how we will want to make satellite settlements for all of you – we need to choose wisely to try to capture as many complementary resources as we can.”

Since this is a topic we’ve already discussed in previous meetings, I don’t need to go into more detail than that.

Reading the expressions of those around, I can tell that we’ve reached an agreement. How that will look like in practice will depend on what our scouts find out when we make landfall.

I turn to Captain Sor’az who’s been very quiet so far – so has Captain Garous, but that’s completely par for the course. The damayar, however, is frowning at the maps we have available.

“Penny for your thoughts?” I ask him while Valence also inspects the maps, Elarion talks to his steward, Layton and Josiah discuss arrangements quietly, and Garous frowns thoughtfully to himself.

The damayar blinks then frowns up at me with confusion.

“Ye think me thoughts‘r only worth a copper?”

I smile, a brief quirk of the lips.

“It’s a saying where I came from. Anyway, you’re being awfully quiet – I’m wondering why. If you’d like to share.”

Though the rest of the lords have all relaxed around themselves and me – courtesy of the various social engagements we’ve managed to sneak in over the course of the journey – the damayar have always held themselves at a slight remove. Not unfriendly, not exactly, but…reserved. Sor’az has been willing to come over and share a cup of tila with me, but not if anyone else is involved, and not frequently. I have only been invited once to his ship in return.

“Ah’m jus’ wonderin’ the part o’ me an’ my crew in this,” he admits. “Though me kind be good at settlin’ new land, ah have no desire te be caught in a war ‘tween humans.” He hesitates. “Ah’m considerin’ searchin’ fer a place far away from the rest o’ ye, but I know we’ve got obligations te t’ rest o’ ye.”

I shrug.

“We signed a contract for the journey, not the destination,” I point out. Sor’az inclines his head slowly, his eyes holding wariness mixed with hope.

“We did…but ah didn’ expect ye te hold te tha’ limitation.”

Meaning he expected me to push for more than I’d contracted for, I translate. I sigh – the damayar have very low opinions of humans. Probably deserved, though. I don’t doubt that what happened to Sor’az in the courts was only one action among many that created this wariness among the sea-faring folk.

“We signed a contract; I will stick to it,” I tell him simply.

Sor’az eyes me cautiously.

“An’...ye’d be content wit’ tha’?” His words hold more meaning than is obvious – he’s very clearly recognised that even his kind will benefit from a connection to a larger group – enough that he seems more concerned with keeping on good terms with me than insisting on his rights according to the contract we signed.

I’m tempted for a moment to push for something more. The damayar have proven their expertise on the seas over the last few months and I’m leery of losing that. If I release him from the contract now, arguably he could return back to Moriax without a second thought. I doubt it – part of his reason for offering to come along with me in the first place was because he wanted to explore the Lost Continent.

But even if he does, that’s his right. I’m not going to break a contract just because it’s a little inconvenient for me.

In the end, all I do is shrug.

“What does it benefit me to keep you somewhere you don’t want to be?” I ask honestly. “I have no interest in making you want to murder me in my bed.” That gains a flicker of a wry smile – from our discussions over tila, I know that I’m not far off the mark in my guess there.

“That said,” I continue, and see him tense, the faint smile disappearing as if it had never existed. "Assuming you don’t decide to turn around and go back to Moriax, I would appreciate it if, during your exploration, you could send back regular reports of what you’ve seen – that would be very helpful for us. I’d be willing to negotiate some sort of payment rate in return.”

I hear a disbelieving hiss from one of my allies. Several more look vaguely disapproving, though they know me well enough by this point to be able to guess that this would be my reaction.

I return my attention to Sor’az. “If you’re willing to remain allies, and decide to settle somewhere, we could negotiate some sort of mutual defence. Either way, you might like to consider the range at which the mirrors are effective.” If he goes too far, then he won’t even be able to signal that he needs help – or anything else.

Sor’az inclines his head slowly.

“Yer an unusual human,” he comments. Some might take it as an insult; given what I know his opinion is of humans, I’ll take it as a compliment. “Thank ye.” It’s heartfelt.

I smile back at him, though I’m sure there are traces of sadness within it. Once again, I feel depressed at being thanked for not being an unreliable, untrustworthy bastard. That should never be the default expectation.

“No problem.”

Leaving behind a thoughtful So’raz, I return to the discussion with the other lords. We have a good deal of detail to discuss and prepare for.

*****

Actually reaching land seems to take an age. I suppose it’s my impatience and that distances on the flat ocean are deceptively far.

After our discussion in my cabin meeting room finished and all the lords returned to their own ships, I went out on deck and haven’t left it. Thanks to delegation and my Bonds, I’m able to communicate the information I need without taking my eyes off the tantalising cloud-like lump on the horizon for more than a few moments at a time.

Slowly, almost secretively, the land unveils its mysteries. Details fade into view from one moment to the next, first the cliffs, then the forest, then, little by little, the mountains beyond. If I hadn’t had experience of something similar on Earth, I would have called it magic, an enchantment slowly slipping away as we get closer. But it’s not – just the effect of distance, distortion from the ocean, and clouds shifting over the land mass. It’s a day of mixed cloud and sun so, from time to time, a spear of sunlight spotlights something new in the land.

Its sheer vastness is something else that is only revealed little by little. The small smudge on the horizon expands until it is the horizon, a coastline that stretches as far as the eyes can see in both directions. And then that enormity is hidden again as we get closer and a small section of coastline becomes all we can see.

We are fortunately unbothered as we move forwards at half-speed. The enchantments on our ships that reduce how obvious we are to the beasts around have always been a priority to keep in good condition, second only to enchantments that we need to keep afloat. They haven’t succeeded in keeping us entirely safe, but Lizil has assured me that if they weren’t active, we’d have never made it half the distance due to the number of beast attacks. Whether the beasts near the shores of the Lost Continent are less observant, more occupied with other matters or, unlikely, aren’t present, I don’t know. But I’m grateful for it nonetheless.

We adjust our course several times as we receive information from the scouts we sent out. Not only my Bonded, though Sirocco, Ivor, and Noir are part of the group, those sent include Keshaka’s mechanical flyers, and two human Classers who are capable of extended flight.

Finally, we’re close enough that we have to stop moving forwards for fear of potentially ripping holes in the keel of our ships. We anchor ourselves with the enchanted anchors that somehow grip onto the water currents instead of needing to secure themselves on actual rocks.

I climb into a row boat with several others. The mixture of grim and excited expressions on the faces of the others as we’re lowered down to the water matches my own feelings perfectly.

We row towards our chosen landfall point, a semi-protected bay, with three other rowboats from Layton’s, Elarion’s, and Valence’s ships, the presence of multiple Classers making rowing a far faster and easier task than it would be with only baseline humans. I am tense; so are the other ranged fighters and mages. The rowboats don’t have the same protections as the other ships – if something is underneath us we might not know about its presence until it tries to take a bite out of us.

I trail my fingers in the water, connecting with the currents of the water with magic. I watch for threats, paying keen attention to anything bigger than a human below us.

Alarm spikes through me as something perhaps double the length of the boat but a lot thinner emerges from the sea bed beneath. A hiss of warning passes through my lips as it arrows straight towards us.

Then, perhaps sensing that we are nothing to mess with, it turns and propels itself away just as fast. I lean back again, signalling to the others in my boat that the danger has passed – for now.

I stay alert and watchful but there are no more close-calls.

Finally, we reach the shore. Excitement and nerves combine to make me feel vaguely nauseous. But I push both away as I step out of the boat and into the knee-deep water, my feet touching yet another continent for the first time.

It’s almost lost its excitement – like flying in an airplane eventually became routine after so many flights. But in this case there is far more riding on this first step. It’s not just the first step onto a new continent, but a major milestone in the competition.

This is it.
