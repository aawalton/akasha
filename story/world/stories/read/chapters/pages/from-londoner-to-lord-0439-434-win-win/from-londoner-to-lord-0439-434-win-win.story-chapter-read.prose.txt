The next afternoon, Kivamus was working on a blueprint after lunch. The weather was hot and the clouds had mostly cleared by today, leaving only some cotton wool type clouds in the sky. It was still early summer but he was already starting to feel the reality of Duvas’ words when he had said that the summers in this place were hot and humid and muggy. The peak of summer was still more than a month away, but it was already getting stifling in the evenings.

Earlier in the morning, when he had been walking in the manor, he had seen the grins on the faces of all the guards, servants, and maids, all of them thanking or bowing to him with gratitude and relief. News must have already spread throughout the village last night about the starting of payments in coins. He had heard the guards and servants talking about visiting the alehouse after their shifts, while the maids had been discussing some trinkets sold by a village merchant which they could finally afford, as well as buying some better clothing from any visiting merchant.

Lucem and Clarisa had been running around with grins, announcing that they would also be able to buy fresh buns from the village baker until someone told them that they wouldn’t be paid. They were too young to be working, after all. However, Madam Helga had told them that she would give them some allowance from her own wages so they could buy some good things along with their other friends from the village, like Maisy and Elsie—daughters of Kigeir, the grain merchant. Syrene had also promised Clarisa that she would give her some extra coins but not to her brother, which had made Lucem puff his cheeks in anger.

Kivamus had laughed contentedly when he had heard about it from their mother. All of this should have been normal, and yet for the past six months none of the villagers had seen a sight of a coin in their hands since the day Torhan's men had snatched it from their hands, but finally, the time was here. The irony was that it was coin looted from Torhan himself which had allowed the manor to start payments in coins again. Bet the bastard didn't expect that when he raided the village in the autumn!

The villagers didn’t know much about the recent counter raid by the guards, apart from some wild rumors—ranging from the guards having gone to kill a mighty bakkore up in the mountains, to the men being sent to the coast to kill some pirates—but those living within the manor knew all about the raid, and its success. Hudan had made an announcement to the new guards yesterday about their total victory, while the servants and maids had been listening from nearby. That news, coupled with the fact that they would finally be paid in coins within a few days, had made everyone giddy and changed the atmosphere to a festive one. It made Kivamus vow to work harder to keep these people safe and happy.

He stepped back from the table and looked at the progress of the sketch. Hmm... Decent, but some improvements were still needed.

The inner door of the manor hall opened, making him think that Duvas was finally here with the calculations, but it was Syrene.

“Hey. How’s it going with the medicine?”

Syrene shrugged as she took a seat on a nearby chair. “The last batch of losuvil leaves we got has already been processed. Now I just have to wait for a week until the powder is dry so I can press them into tablets. But apart from that, there’s nothing for me to do.” She laughed. “I am kinda bored these days.”

Kivamus chuckled as he took a seat as well, deciding to take a break and leaving his work for later. “Consider it as a few days of much-needed rest. You've been working nonstop since the hunters started bringing the leaves in early spring. Once the encampment is ready, you will also have to go there to supervise the medicine production, so you will hardly get any rest then. So use this free time to enjoy your days with your family.”

Syrene smiled. “I am...”

“That’s good. You will also start getting paid within a few days, so that should help too.”

Syrene frowned. “I don’t need it, I already told you that. I won't even know what to do with all that money... I am just fine as long as I have a roof over my head and get food.”

“That doesn’t nearly compensate you for the kind of work you are doing with the losuvil leaves. You deserve that money—probably more than anyone else in the village. That medicine is a big part of why we are finally able to pay the villagers.”

“But I have never worked for money in my life! I was too young to help around when my family lived in Cinran, and at that inn on the northern road, I helped Ma wherever I could in cooking or keeping the place clean. But I have never worked for wages... After coming here, I've been doing what I can because I want to. I know my work stops people from being in pain and even saves lives, and that is enough compensation for me.”

Kivamus exhaled. He could understand her point of view, but it didn’t feel right for her to think like that. He explained, “But there are things you cannot do without money... Once you start getting paid in a few days, you can visit the market square regularly to buy some good food or clothes for yourself. The visiting traders will start bringing more stuff to sell the very moment they hear that the villagers can afford it now. You can even buy gifts for Lucem and Clarisa or your mother.”

“I know, and I already promised them that, but the manor makes sure that all the workers get decent clothes, and we probably get better food here than in Cinran. Why don’t you use that money for other things? I don’t know the details but I’m sure money is tight for the manor even now. That will make me feel better instead of me stashing coins under the floorboards. That might have been a good idea if I was working for someone else and could be laid off any day, but here in Tiranat I know that me and my family will be taken care of, and I don’t have to hide money for that.”

“That’s...” Kivamus took a deep breath. How could he not pay someone who had contributed so much to the village?

He tried again, “If you save that money, you can use it to visit Cinran or even Ulriga in the future. Usually a young girl like you wouldn’t want to limit herself to life in a small village like Tiranat."

He knew that he did not want someone like Syrene to even start thinking about moving away from Tiranat, but he did not want it to feel forced.

Syrene chuckled. “That’s funny. Even if I had all the money in the world, I wouldn’t move from Tiranat now. My family is here, and this is my home now. I have lived in Cinran in the past, and it may be a much bigger town, but this village is far, far better than that. Lucem has never been this happy in the past, and Mother is also relaxed and satisfied here.” She snorted. “Her time spent talking with Teacher Gorsazo might have something to do with it, but either way, my family is happy here, and so am I. I have no desire to move away. That’s why I said that I don’t need the money. I’m doing just fine without that.”

Kivamus smiled, feeling relieved that she didn’t want to move out of Tiranat. “Well, we can think of some solution for that money.” He thought about another idea. “Perhaps you can use it to invest in the village.”

“Invest?” Syrene looked confused. “What do you mean?”

“This is something far in the future,” he explained, “but I want to open a bank here. That’s a place where people can keep their savings and earn some interest on that. Basically, it’s like you keep 100 silver coins in the bank for one year, then at the end of the year the bank will give you a few extra silver coins for that investment.”

“Oh, I didn’t know that was possible. So it’s like a moneylender then? But how will the bank even earn that coin?”

“Because the bank will invest it or loan it to whoever needs it. For now I am keeping the village craftsmen on our own payroll, and paying for the raw materials myself, but in the future they will have to work independently. So let’s say, a village craftsman thinks that if he buys a few good tools and hires some apprentices, he can craft more stuff and earn more money. But that will need coins which he doesn’t have. So he can come to the bank, and ask for a loan. The bank will say that we will give you 100 silver coins for a year, and in return we expect, let’s say, 110 coins at the end of the year. If the craftsman thinks he can make more than 10 silver coins of profit, he will agree. At the end of the year the bank will also get a profit of 10 silver, and from that they will pay, let’s say, four silver coins to its investors, like you. So basically, everyone gains something here. The investor gains a profit of four coins for an investment of 100 silver, the bank gets six coins in profit, while the craftsman gains whatever he has managed to earn above those 10 coins he had to pay to the bank as interest.”

Kivamus looked at Syrene, wondering how much she understood, but she nodded confidently after a moment.

"That makes so much sense! This is what you call a win-win for everyone, isn’t it?"

Kivamus grinned. He shouldn’t have been surprised that she understood everything immediately. She was a smart one.

“It is indeed a win-win. It doesn’t always work like that, and sometimes the craftsman or even the bank will have to take a loss, but in the long term this is something good for everyone. Of course, we are in no position to open a bank here right now, but that is one of the things I want to do in the future. So that’s where people like you who don’t have any immediate need for money can put their savings while earning an interest on it, while helping the village at the same time.”
