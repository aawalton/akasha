After eating, they rented two rooms from the guild, one for Briadon and one for Lisha and Tressa to share. Since most nights tended to be spent out on the ground, they took their opportunities to live in luxury. Not that the guild dorms were luxurious by most standards, but certainly compared to what they were used to.

After that, the three went up to the girls’ room to plan their route.

Briadon sat on the floor with a map spread out, while Lisha lounged back on the bed and Tressa sat cross-legged near the end, her bags and a few books scattered around her. She leaned forward curiously, hugging a pillow in her lap as she examined the path from above.

“Hmmm.” Briadon stroked his beard braid. “So we should take the Trenmin path. That way we pass through Etrin Springs.”

“The kobold city?” Tressa asked, remembering Etrin Springs from one of the merchant’s stories. She remembered him telling her of meeting kobolds there.

“No, it’s not the kobold city itself,” Briadon explained.

“Those are the dragon people, right?” she asked excitedly.

“Yes, supposedly they are descended from dragons, or drakes,” Lisha explained. “I’m not sure of the legend, but I know it is well debated what their exact relation is. But they are skilled crafters, and they make good potions. We can also trade the rest of our horned rabbits there with no worries.”

But Tressa was past thinking about the practicality, sitting up with sparkles in her eyes as she imagined them.

“Don’t go getting any ideas, Tressa,” Lisha warned. “We’re on a time crunch. We can’t stop and waste time having them teach you anything.”

“But…”

Lisha put up a hand. “At most, we may sleep there for a single night. Just make sure you actually get sleep.”

“Yeah, I’m not carrying you,” Briadon said.

“You carried me before, and it is not so hard for you,” Tressa complained. She lifted the pillow from her lap. “I’m like a pillow to you!”

“Yes, but I’m not your personal porter,” he snorted. “I only carried you that time because you were injured. Not because you willfully deprived yourself of sleep!”

“He’s right,” Lisha chuckled.

Tressa deflated, her bottom lip sticking out like it normally did when her ideas got shot down.

“Don’t worry, we can stop in for a longer stay sometime later, when we are not on a time-sensitive bounty and pass through there again,” Lisha assured her. “The kobolds have been quite welcoming to us in the past.”

With the route settled on, he pulled out a smaller disposable copy of the map and began charting their planned course on it. They would turn that in to the guild before leaving in the morning. It wasn’t necessary to do so, just a standard precaution so that if they didn’t return within the time limit of the contract, the guild would send out a search party to find out what had happened to them. In the event that they were deceased but their belongings were found, the guild’s duty would then be to retrieve their belongings and send them back to their homelands.

Tressa thought back to when she’d filled out the guild registration paperwork many months ago. There was a section that asked where her belongings should be sent in the event of her death. It was such an odd thing to be asked, she felt, but it was normal for these people. In truth, there had been some close calls in her short run as a monster hunter, but it was crazy to think that people actually died doing what she was doing now. She didn’t want to imagine what that would do to her mother and father should her belongings make it back to them and not Tressa herself.

How would Aldmern react? She didn’t believe his friendship was completely fake, but she still couldn’t forgive him for tracking her with an anchoring stone all these years. It was too big of a deceit and an invasion.

“Hey Tressa, what’s with the face?” Lisha asked.

“Oh, nothing. Just thinking of home again.”

“Still homesick, huh?” Briadon asked, focusing on charting the map.

“As I said, you will get used to it,” Lisha said, reaching over and patting Tressa’s shoulder.

“I don’t know if it is homesickness. I don’t care to go back yet. If we planned to go to Falidorn, I wouldn’t want to right now…” Tressa shrugged. “It’s hard to explain.”

“I think I understand,” Lisha admitted. “You can miss a place, having it deeply on your mind, yet not actually want to go there.”

Briadon finished marking the map and held it up. “Here.”

“Got it sorted out?” Lisha asked, her tongue flicking as she moved to the side of the bed and looked over his work.

Sometimes Lisha charted their course, and sometimes Briadon did, but they always checked each other’s work because one of them might notice a problem or think of something the other had missed.

Tressa moved to look at the map from over Lisha’s shoulder. It seemed simple enough, but she could tell there was a lot about it that she wasn’t getting. She figured as she traveled more and learned the lands better, she’d be able to help with this part eventually. For the time being, the most feedback she could offer was what places she personally wanted to go to, which often had little to do with having a successful hunt.

“Looks good to me,” Lisha said after a moment, then laid the map down on the bed to show Tressa. “Let me explain what we’re doing. For the most part, we are headed here,” she explained, tapping the forested area to the southwest. “That’s where the Bewler Bears can usually be found, at least from the intel I’ve managed to gather. The sawtooth boar can be found almost anywhere, so we’ll go after the boar first. Shouldn’t take more than a day.”

“How can we find them if they can be anywhere?”

Lisha looked at the map and tapped her chin. “Well, they like horned rabbits. We could use some of the meat from the nest we wiped out.”

“Sawtooth boars eat meat?” Tressa asked, much more interested in this part. She shuddered and reached behind herself for her satchel. “That’s creepy. A pig that eats meat.” Though as she said that, she was pulling out her sketchbook and turning to a fresh page. “What else are sawtooth boars like?”

“Hm, well, they’re twice the size of normal boars,” Lisha explained. “They can roll into a ball and charge at you like a circular blade. That’s where the saw part comes from. And they can also use earth magic.”

“So they have ranged and melee combat?” Tressa asked excitedly.

“Yes, that’s what makes them dangerous. Hard to find an easy way to take them without it being an actual fight,” Lisha said.

Briadon huffed. “Quick for boars too. Especially when they’re rolling at you.”

“Hmmm…” Tressa tapped her chin with her quill after writing that down.

“Their fur is coarse and kinda bristly,” Lisha went on. “Their eyes are yellow or red, and they eat anything between meat and plants.”

“So they’re omn… omni…” Tressa sighed, knowing the word in Elvish but not Common. “What is it called? When you eat both?”

“Omnivore,” Lisha said.

“Omnifour.”

“No, om-ni-vore.”

“I get it,” Tressa said, writing it down in Elvish as she was more interested in picturing this creature and adding it to her sketchbook than another language lesson. “They have sharp teeth, or no?”

“Sharp,” Lisha answered. “They are also worth a decent amount, but the smith will be getting the loot.”

Briadon nodded. “Unless we get a chance to catch more than one, I wouldn’t mind trading its parts. Smith himself might even take some.”

“They have ummm…” Tressa hooked her fingers downward in front of her nose. “These things?”

“Tusks?” Lisha asked. “Yes, about half the length of my arm, and they have two sets. One small and one large. The smaller one is in front, so when they roll, the tusks act like blades and can shred things very fast.” She gave a toothy smile. “And their meat is tender and good.”

“Can we eat it?” Tressa asked, quickly writing that down.

“If we do end up hunting a second.” Lisha laughed, knowing Tressa would want to do that. “But we have to be careful, there is a ban on hunting them in certain areas, as they keep other species from ruining the territory. For example, keeping deer populations from growing too large.”

“I see…” Tressa said in fascination, writing that down. Her own people had certain laws about that as well, though it was more the Wood Elves who paid attention to such things and advised the High Elves, who would enforce the law accordingly. Tressa briefly mused that it was a job she might not hate, but truthfully, now that she had experienced being an adventurer and monster hunter, she couldn’t imagine being satisfied doing anything else.

She finished sketching the creature she imagined and held it out. “Are they like this?”

Briadon let out a low whistle, and Lisha’s tongue flicked. “Not bad, Tressa. That’s pretty close from just our description.”

“Well, you said it was like a boar, then explained the differences,” Tressa said. “So I drew a boar from memory and added those things.”

“It’s not exactly right,” Briadon admitted. “But pretty damn close.”

“What about Bewler Bears?” Tressa asked, flipping to a fresh page again.

“Well, they are huge. Taller than me!” Briadon started. “Massive claws, sharp teeth.”

“So like a normal bear?”

“Worse. It’s said that their claws can rip through hardened steel. Most who come across them unprepared end up dead.” Lisha’s tongue flicked. “But we will be approaching with caution and a plan, of course.”

Tressa's quill scratched across the paper as she wrote down the details that Lisha and Briadon said to her.

“So sharp claws,” she said.

“Not just sharp—like swords. And huge. Their paws are about the size of Briadon’s head,” Lisha joked.

“What else?”

“Well, it’s said that its roar has a stunning effect, so it paralyzes the body. But that’s as much as we know. They are territorial and aggressive if you disturb them.”

Paralyzing roar? Does it use mana? Tressa wondered, then jotted down that question. She hoped to see it use that attack, because she would be able to see the mana and know.

“What of the habitat?” Tressa asked.

“Not known. And it’s hard to find them. But we know they like to live in caves like normal bears, and they can dig their dens,” Briadon said.

Tressa nodded. “So they are really unknown. Are we sure this is safe to go after?”

“We’re professionals!” Briadon laughed. “And the guild approved it, so everything should be good. This might even bump us to gold rank. We should be close in any case.”

Tressa nodded. Aside from handling the business and contracts, the guild also tried not to let hunters go on hunts they were not prepared for, and the ranking system was utilized for that purpose. If a hunt was judged to be gold tier and you were only silver, then they wouldn’t approve it.

“Well, in any case, we ought to get to bed,” Briadon said.

Lisha nodded. “I’m going to head down for a few minutes and turn this in,” she said, waving the map.

“Why don’t you just do it in the morning?” Briadon asked.

“Well, I want to go for a walk anyway. Tressa, you’re welcome to come along if you like.”

“No that’s okay,” Tressa said with a yawn.

She said goodnight to Briadon, then changed into more comfortable clothes, draped her cloak over a chair, and curled up into bed, which would be just big enough for both her and Lisha. The wool blanket was a bit uncomfortable. Even the softness of her bedding back in Falidorn is something she’d taken for granted, but such things didn’t make her happy. She’d take a scratchy blanket and adventure over serving in the temple, library, or council any day.

Still, unable to sleep, she eventually stood and cracked open the shutters. The sky had darkened, and the stars overhead were beautiful. Sometimes when they camped outside, she would get lost just staring at the stars as she drifted to sleep.

As for the city, it was odd to see buildings without glass as she had grown up around. When she had explained that to Lisha and Briadon their jaws had dropped as only the rich could afford glass. They had been amazed and almost didn't believe her, until she had described in massive detail the grand library.

She smiled to herself at how different the world was from the elven lands.

Something pulled at her chest as a tight feeling wound its way into her. She did miss home. She missed her mother and even her father’s ramblings and lectures. She missed gossiping with Leimia, getting food from Narissa, and even dealing with Maikia.

As for Aldmern… unfortunately, many of her memories of him had been soiled by discovering what he’d done. She almost wished she had never found out the true secret to the self-proclaimed greatest tracking in all of Falidorn. She left without confronting him about it, and she couldn’t imagine what she’d say to him if she ever saw him again.

She tried to go back to bed, but found herself pacing, feeling anxious with thoughts of home. Both the good and the bad.

Finally, she grabbed some paper and her quill and went to the little desk the room provided. She began writing a letter in Elvish.

She wrote to her mother of the hunt she was going on next but downplayed how dangerous it might be. She wrote of the smith being impressed with her notebooks and the dwarf who tried to steal her bag. She said a few things about Briadon and Lisha, telling her what good friends they were, and finally she ended the letter by admitting she didn’t know when she would be back, but she was living her dreams in a way she had never imagined before. She told her mother she still missed her and asked her to send her regards to everyone.

She signed the letter and folded it up, planning to pay the guild to send it by eagle, which is how she’d been sending and receiving letters from her mother so far.

Writing the letter helped her anxiety a little, but she still didn’t feel ready for bed and thought maybe she should have taken Lisha up on going for a walk.

Instead, she grabbed one of her blank sketchbooks she planned to copy her drawings into and translate into common. She flipped through, seeing dozens and dozens of monsters she’d observed over the years. Gob Frog, Mera Wolves, MeraMega, Deliworms, Farron Moths, Blue Mimics, and many, many more.

She stopped on the Saltiserp and thought that’d be a good one to copy.

The Saltiserp was one of the largest serpents known around Falidorn, with the biggest recorded specimens reaching more than a hundred and fifty feet long. They made their dens in caves along the mountains and hunted mostly in the forests near the base, feeding on large birds and Gob Frogs. Their scales were difficult to penetrate, and their folding front fangs could grow nearly as long as Tressa was tall. Fortunately, they rarely wandered far from their dens, and the Trackers had learned that smoke and fire could irritate and disorient them if one ever needed to be driven off.

That made it exactly the sort of creature Tressa wanted to include. She wanted to start with the ones whose information might actually save someone’s life. Saltiserps were uncommon outside the eastern Timberman Mountains, and much of what was known about them came from generations of Elven observation. A hunter who encountered one without knowing how tough its scales were, or that smoke could be used against it, might not live long enough to learn from the mistake.

She carefully copied the drawing, tiredness finally beginning to creep in on her, and she thought of how much work it would be to finish copying even one of these books. Copying certainly wasn’t as fun as actively taking field notes, either. Would it even be worth the gold of selling them?

But since Lisha wasn’t in the room to help with the translations, she just focused on the pictures for now.

It really began to impress her, realizing that when the library had multiple copies of a book, it was because someone had to painstakingly copy it by hand, not to mention those whose duty it was to restore the burned books.

"If only there were some magic so you would not have to do this by hand..." she muttered to herself.

Then her hand froze as that thought sank in.

A book copying spell?

She folded her arms, abandoning her drawing to sit back and think it over.

A book-copying spell. That would take a process that took months, if not years, and turn it into mere days, maybe even hours. If books could be reproduced at such speed, knowledge could be spread unlike ever before.

It sounded like such a simple thing, but the implications were massive. That was truly something that could change the world. Would a spell like that satisfy the dragon?

How could that possibly work, though?

Transferring a perfect image of a page from one to another would be quite difficult. Even in writing, there were so many small intricate lines; she couldn't even begin to fathom a spell that could do it accurately.

Unless...

She stared at the end of her quill. "Magic ink? Is there something I could infuse into the ink in the first place?"

What she began imagining was certainly interesting. She imagined you'd still have to make at least one copy by hand using the magic ink, but then each copy after that could be instantly transferred with a spell. But what would the magic ink consist of? Could it be repeated endlessly? No, that absolutely violated the laws of magic.

She would need to write with magic ink, transfer the impression through a new source of ink, and place that impression with the fresh magic ink on a new page.

“No, there’s no way,” she muttered in Elvish, not even realizing she’d begun pacing around the room as she continued talking to herself.

“What if the ink could be spilled on the page and only stick to where other ink is?” she pondered, her eyes widening at the idea.

She ran back to the desk and flipped to a blank page and quickly jotted that down.

"Spill the new ink; it only attaches to the old ink, then press the blank page overtop. Could that work? Doing one page at a time would take quite a while, but it would still be far faster than copying by hand.” She grunted as she ruffled the back of her head. “But then there is also a concern of the ink drying before it transfers, or smudging too much. So an additional rune to keep it wet may be necessary… Is there another way? Stacking too many runes into one spell makes it less stable. I shouldn’t rely on runes for something as mundane as wetness when there could be another approach.”

She then started pacing around again, playing with the end of one of her braids and even chewing on it.

“But what would I even make that kind of ink out of in the first place? Utilizing magnetism may be the key… Positively and negatively charged ink? That seems simple enough in theory…”

Some time later, Lisha finally came back to see the elf still pacing around, muttering to herself in a language Lisha didn’t understand. Lisha just stood in the doorway, blinking, and watched as Tressa shouted in Elvish, ran to the desk, frantically wrote something down, and then resumed her pacing.

“Tressa?” Lisha asked.

No response.

Lisha’s scaly lips curled into a grin, having seen her friend get like this before. She took off her armor, set everything to the side, and got in bed. She figured she’d give the elf ten more minutes. If she didn’t stop by then, she’d tell her to go to bed.
