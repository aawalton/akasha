Carter…

Carter was lost.

Not physically. The Desert Wind Guild’s building wasn’t hard to find, and it was even easier to navigate. He’d put his hands behind his head and closed his eyes in one of the atrium’s armchairs twenty minutes ago, and for all the world, he looked comfortable.

But inside, he was lost. He’d been Caleb Richter for a handful of months, and in that time, he’d lost contact with everyone he’d known. In the last day, he’d tried to make a return as Carter, only to find that all his old teams had moved on. His first one—the one he’d entered the Lithic portal world with so long ago—was partially retired and D-Rank still. The ones still delving didn’t have a spot for him, and he was too strong for them anyway.

And the other team…the one he’d ambushed Kade’s team with in that Dark Citadel world…well, they didn’t want anything to do with him. They were still, nominally at least, with the Roadrunners. He couldn’t go back to them.

So…no Roadrunners. None of his old teams. They’d had a memorial for him, and his parents were…struggling…to understand why he’d faked his death. Most of his friends were out of the picture, too.

The end result of all of it was that Carter Richards was lost in a way Caleb hadn’t been.

On the other hand, Deborah was dead, and with her death, Caleb could die, too. And Carter could live.

It should have been a triumphant moment. Instead, Carter found himself empty and purposeless. And until he’d sent that email to Guildmaster Jessie Gerald, he’d continued feeling that way. It was still there, of course, but—

“Delver Richards, B-Rank Archer?” A woman’s voice asked.

He opened his eyes. She was Hispanic, unarmed, but serious-looking. No badge on her arm. No indication of guild allegiance.

“That’s me,” he said, sounding a little surprised about it.

“I’m Yasmin, Support for the Desert Wind Guild. Jessie’s ready to talk to you.”

“Great,” Carter said. He stood up, took a deep breath, and followed her into the heart of the Desert Wind Guild.

We’d set up in Jessie’s office.

She never used her office if she didn’t have to. Luckily, it was cleaned pretty routinely, so it hadn’t gotten a layer of dust or anything too embarrassing. I’d pulled a chair up next to hers, behind the glass-surfaced desk, and she’d opened up both Carter Richards’s and Caleb Richter’s delving histories.

They were surprisingly extensive. Before Carter had ‘died,’ he’d been a busy bee with the Roadrunners. I read over the reports from both the Lithic and Dark Citadel portals, plus a few dozen others. He’d pushed about as hard as anyone could. Officially, he’d died during the portal surge, and his body hadn’t been recovered. That happened—typically when the corpse was destroyed.

Then Caleb had come to town. He’d been extremely helpful in low-rank portals, and he’d gradually grown in power, but he’d studiously avoided anything in the Roadrunners’ territory. Those low-rank portal runs hadn’t stopped, either. He’d kept helping newbie groups through E and D-Rank ones until the Siege of Phoenix. A gradual ascension up to B-Rank, then a stall-out. His bizarre Unique skill merge was to blame for that. Overall, a good guy. Participation in and elimination from the Fallen Delver’s Tournament, service on the 303 Wall, and so on.

That definitely didn’t square with what I knew about him, but Jessie didn’t seem surprised. “He was always a good guy,” she said.

“Even when he threatened you and broke into our apartment?”

“Well…he was almost always a good guy.”

Eventually, we’d read enough, and Yasmin helpfully dragged Carter in.

He looked terrible. Not physically, but his eyes. I’d seen it before—in Jeff. Carter had no purpose, and he’d given everything to his revenge on Deborah Callahan. Ironic, that I was the one who’d made that happen. He’d asked me to do it, and I’d done it.

“What do you want, Carter?” Jessie asked.

Carter flinched. “I assumed you got my email?”

“I did. That’s not what I’m asking. I’m asking what you want.” Jessie had changed in the last fifteen seconds. She’d gotten harsher. Colder. More blunt. “Your track record as Caleb Richter would get you into this guild, but I have concerns about Carter Richards. Convince me you want this.”

“I…I had a lot of friends as Carter. Or at least, I thought they were friends. Teammates with the Roadrunners. Some of them are retired. Some won’t return my calls. Others have moved on to new teams. No one’s left. And the Roadrunners don’t trust me—both because I disappeared for months and because I was one of Deborah’s people.” Carter swallowed.

“That’s not an answer.”

He looked at the black glass desk. “You’re right. It’s not. I’m trying to rebuild from nothing, and that’s not easy. The reality is that, if I want to start over, I either need a guild that can understand where I’ve been or I need to head west to the coastal cities. Of all the Phoenix guildmasters, you’re the one who most understands what I’ve done. I figured if anyone would give me a chance, it’d be you.”

I raised an eyebrow. My aura was suppressed, but even so, my anger was barely contained, and the simple motion had weight to it. He went silent, and I filled the silence. “I’m skeptical. We have a lot of reasons not to trust you.”

“Kade and I need to know what you were thinking for each of the following incidents,” Jessie said. “Answer honestly, and we’ll talk it over after you’re finished. First, Deborah Callahan. What made you choose to align yourself with her?”

Carter went quiet. I fidgeted with my hands. The Storm’s Blade was a quick summon away, and I’d feel better with its grip in my hand, but…no. This situation didn’t call for it, and Carter wasn’t geared for battle. He was in a suit, not armor. No bow, no daggers. No weapons at all.

“She saw potential. A-Rank potential. That’s an incredible offer—assistance in pushing to the second-highest rank out there.” Carter paused. “Would you have taken everything if it were offered to you, and you had nothing?”

“That’s it?” I asked.

“Yes. That’s it. It was a simple decision. Power or obscurity. I couldn’t put together the build myself. She and the Roadrunners helped me assemble it and figured out the right training regimen to make it happen. I owed her after that.”

“And part of that was the Dark Citadel?”

As I asked the question, his eyes met mine. “Not the Lithic portal?”

“No. That was out of line, but not as bad as the Dark Citadel. What happened there?”

“I wasn’t strong enough to tell Deborah ‘no.’ I thought I had authority over the team, but I didn’t. When we went in, I realized I was just along for the ride, and by that point, it was too late. All I could do was what I did.”

The room went quiet. After a handful of seconds—Jessie was using the same trick I’d used on her—Carter continued. “My goal wasn’t to kill you. It was to get you out of Deborah’s way. If we’d had the opportunity to talk, maybe I could have found a solution that worked for everyone. But we didn’t.”

“So then you faked your death instead of standing up to her?” Jessie asked.

“Terri and Lizzie.”

“Who?” I asked.

“My teammates. They both had long recoveries ahead of them after the Lithic and Dark Citadel portals, and if I tried to betray Deborah, she would have gone after them. I couldn’t risk it. The only way to make sure they kept getting the support they needed was to disappear. It wasn’t about me—at least, not entirely. If I could have talked to the Light of Dawn or something, maybe things would have gone differently.”

“And, finally, you threatened Jessie, broke into our apartment, and tried to blackmail me into fighting Deborah Callahan as a B-Ranker.”

“Yeah, that was stupid.” Carter laughed. There wasn’t a shred of joy or amusement in it, though. “I thought…I thought that if there was anyone I knew who might be able to take her out, it was you. It was stupid, though. You didn’t have a chance against her—not until the tournament.”

“One last thing,” I said. It hadn’t been on the plan, but I needed to know. “After you broke into our house, you went right to the Rime portal break at Arrowhead with us. You knew we didn’t trust you, but you still put yourself at risk. Why? What were you thinking?”

“First, that you were going there, and I needed a team to help me. Second, the Arrowhead break could get people killed. Deborah was a monster, but she didn’t screw around with portal breaks. No one does. We’re all on the same side in the face of that kind of hatred. And third…I needed you to live so you could kill Deborah.”

I didn’t trust him. No way. But at the same time, that was the same reasoning I’d given Ellen when I’d let him ride along with us, and he hadn’t let us down against the red-tinted ice pillar and the monsters guarding it.

“Alright,” Jessie said after a moment of quiet. “Thank you. We’ll get in touch with you in the next day about whether we’ll accept you as a member of the Desert Wind Guild.”

“That’s it?” Carter asked. “You’re not going to ask about my role, or how I could help your teams?”

“No. Frankly, if you join us, you’ll be with Jeff Carlton’s team for serious delves in the B and C-Rank range. He likes running with lots of archers,” Jessie said.

“We’re more concerned about your past than your future,” I added.

“One of the guild members will escort you back to your car,” Jessie said. The phrasing and tone were dismissive, and after a moment, Carter stood and left.

“That was…faster than I expected,” I said when the office door shut.

“Well, yes. I told you it would be.” Jessie winced as she settled back into her chair. She tried to reach for the wheels, then stopped, sucking in a breath. “Kade, I’m going to…I’m going to need some help here.”

“Got it. Bed?”

“I think so. It’s been a day, and I knew it’d be a bad one. Listen, though—what’d you think about Carter’s answers?”

I started maneuvering Jessie’s chair toward the office door and the elevator beyond it. This wasn’t my first time helping her when it had gotten really bad. Back in our tiny apartment, when she’d been at her worst and there hadn’t been any space for her to hide it, I’d had to help her a lot. That didn’t mean I was looking forward to the process, though—or that she was. “I think he’s being as honest as he can be.”

“But is that good enough? Can we trust him?” Jessie pressed.

We slid into the elevator, and I pushed the button for the general dormitory floor. As the elevator rose, I thought. “No. And even if we can, I don’t know if we should.”

“I sort of want to, though.”

“Why?” I asked, mostly so I didn’t have to answer it myself.

The silence stretched as we opened Jessie’s door and I helped her out of the suit and into a pair of pajamas, handing her the pieces and wincing with my back to her while she slowly and painfully got them on.

“I have no idea. Thanks, Kade,” she finally said after almost fifteen minutes. “I know the time matters to you. Sorry.”

“It’s fine. You really want to bring him into the guild? Seriously?”

“Yes.” She swallowed and slowly squirmed her way under her blankets. “He used to be an asshole, but I think he’s just a lost, confused person right now. He needs some hope, and we could give it to him. But I see what you mean, too. I don’t know if we can trust him, and like you said, I’m not sure we should. I’ll…I’ll leave it up to you. Let me know what you decide, Kade.”

I nodded. “I will, Jessie. Hope you feel better soon.”

“Thanks.”

I shut the door, took a deep breath, and headed for the gym. Ellen would be there, no doubt testing her A-Rank body and reforged core, and I desperately needed to talk to her about this. More importantly, though, we both desperately needed a portal to clear. B-Rank would be acceptable, while A would be better.

Sure enough, she was in her shorts and sports bra, bench-pressing close to nine hundred pounds and doing rep after rep. I waited until she’d finished, then ran the Carter situation past her. When I was done, she nodded. “I think you take him onto the team. It’s better to have an uncommitted ally than a staunch enemy.”

“That’s not much of an argument,” I said.

“It is, though. Spot me for a minute.” She slid on four more plates on each side, bringing the total up to early eleven hundred. I got my hands in position, and she started lifting. “This will be…my last set. So, the thing about…Carter is…he’s sort of…you know what, just a minute.”

She cranked out six more reps for an even ten, then racked the bar. “The thing about Carter is that he’s not as dangerous as he thinks he is. He wants to be a good person. The situation with Lizzie and Terri—that’s what’s convincing me. He didn’t do all this because he felt like he was in danger. He did it because he thought his people were.”

“So that’s different?”

“Absolutely. It’s about intent and motivation. Carter didn’t do it for power. He did it to protect his healing teammates. That’s much more respectable. But that’s half of the puzzle. The other half is that he’s got nothing left. He’s a lot like Jeff was right after the convoy run. He needs a purpose, and getting him on Jeff’s team gives him one, along with a mentor figure.”

“Hmmm,” I said noncommittally.

“Think about it. You’ve got time. But right now, I need to put this new core to use. Anything on the GC channels?” Ellen asked.

“Not that I’ve seen. I’m keeping my eyes open for anything we can clear together. That means A or B-Rank. As soon as something pops, I’ll—“

My phone buzzed. Then it buzzed again. I took a quick look, stiffened, and answered it. “Jessie?”

“Kade, uh, you and Ellen need a portal, right?”

“What’s going on?”

“Well, the Guardians, they’re…I guess Kellen didn’t like what happened today. They’re making a move.”

“On who?” I asked, blood running cold. If they were gunning for us…

“The Portal Tyrants. That Elwood guy just called me. I was working on sleeping, too. If we get a full-blown guild war…that’d be…bad.” Jessie’s voice sounded labored, like she was fighting through her pain just to talk. That was probably why she hadn’t texted—her fingers probably hurt too much for that.

“Okay. How do we help Terrel Young, then?”

“Well, I have an…idea. It’s a stupid one, though.”

“What?”

Jessie paused. “Well…”

“Jessie?”

“You two need an A-Rank portal. The Wickenberg Portal’s right there…and the Guardians just attacked our ally. Put two and two together.”

I shook my head slowly. “Okay. You’re on speakerphone. Ellen’s here. What’s our plan?”

She told me, in short, choppy sentences. It took less than a minute, and when she’d finished, Ellen’s grin split her face almost from ear to ear. “This is the stupidest plan I’ve ever heard, Jessie,” she said.

“So you’re out?” my sister asked.

Ellen shook her head. “Heck, no, I’m not out.  But if we screw it up, Phoenix dies. Still…yeah, we’re doing this. Kade, get your stuff together for a portal run. I’ll be ready in ten minutes.” She took off, heading for the elevator.

“Kade, I was thinking about…” Jessie started.

I cut her off. “Don’t. Go to sleep. We can tell him he’s in and introduce him to Jeff and Sophia tomorrow.” Then I broke into a jog and followed Ellen up toward our suite. We had a war to win, after all.
