Flowing water gently trickled down the pile of boulders, splashing into the pool at its base. Jade sat at the edge of the pool, watching the small waterfall cascade from one stone to the next. The scene was altogether too serene, it just didn't fit the gravity of their situation. How many times was this going to happen to her? Every time she thought she finally had everything figured out, had a goal to strive for, someone pulled the rug out from under her feet. First it was waking up in the Labyrinth, alone and afraid. Then, turning into a succubus. After that, it was learning what the Labyrinth was doing to Earth, and that the entire system was breaking apart. All the while the shadow of the demon threat grew, culminating with the chilling realization of the true threat they represented.

And now, this. Once again, it was Jade's job to stop this. It wasn't that anyone was demanding it from her. Hell, she doubted anyone would even rebuke her if she just walked away from this insanity. But she just… couldn't do that.

Maybe that's why the Labyrinth offered this race to me…

If it was an echo of Elaria's will, perhaps it had taken her measure before she'd even reached the Damos and the Sanctuary.

It didn't matter. Nobody else could stop the demons from escaping in time to prevent a war, and it wasn't for lack of trying. The goddess had conjured more illusions, showing them the forces the people of the Labyrinth were mobilizing. They would confront the demons that made it through the dimensional tears leading from their home world, but that was all they could do. Jade had no frame of reference for what a cosmic conflict would entail, she barely understood the reality of wars on Earth, but she knew enough. The breach to the demon world was on Earth's front door. Any conflict in this part of the Labyrinth could spread onto their world all too easily. In some ways, it already had.

If there was any chance of stopping that, any way she could prevent unimaginable devastation from sweeping away everything she knew, she had to try. Even if it meant walking straight into the lion's den.

"You're going to do it, aren't you."

Naomi approached, lowering herself to the ground to sit beside her. Her words were resigned, not even a question.

"I have to."

"You don't. But you will anyway. That's what makes you special."

Jade snorted softly.

"I'm not special. Just too stubborn to know when to quit."

The others gathered around, emerging from their own contemplations. Elaria remained on the other side of the glade, resting in her hammock. She'd give them as long as they needed.

"Sure, it's dangerous." Luis said, plopping down on her other side. "But everything we've been doing here is. With this we could fix everything, once and for all! That's a better chance than I thought we were going to get."

"I think you're downplaying this risk, a little." Fenrin said with a shake of his head. "But I agree. Even if it's a long shot, it's one worth taking. The lives we could save…"

"Hold up." Jade said, raising her hand. "You're making it sound like you're coming with me."

"Of course we are." Luis said, looking at her as if she'd just stated the obvious. "We're a team, remember?"

Jade looked on in confusion as the others nodded.

"The whole point is that I'm the only one who can sneak into the demon world." She jabbed a thumb at herself, flapping her wings for effect. "In case you've forgotten, you aren't demons. If you try to walk through an army of them, you'll be torn apart."

"We'll figure something out." Luis said with a shrug. "We have to, because I'm not letting you go without us."

"Me, either." Naomi agreed. She clenched and unclenched her fists, and Jade tasted the depth of her anxiety. But she hid it well, letting nothing show on her face as she met Jade's eyes with a look of steely determination. "You've run ahead of us too many times, Jade, but it's always worked out better when we go in together."

"Didn't you already come to this great realization that working together as a team and believing in your friends is better?" Luis asked. "We wrapped up that story thread ages ago, it's too late to rehash it now."

"This isn't a video game, Luis." Jade said, feeling a strange mixture of annoyance and relief. Behind Luis, Tara and Siora both gave her firm nods. "You lot… how would that even work? There's no way you could sneak in with me. And even if you could, I'm not going to be fighting anyone. We- I, just need to get close enough for the artifact to do its job."

"We all know it isn't going to be that easy." Tara said, crossing her arms and blowing a lock of white hair out of her face. "Lady Elaria said that the demon lord stays in isolation, but what if you run into trouble on the way to his palace? They attack their own just as often as they do everyone else."

"Not to mention the relic won't be that easy to use." Luis reminded her. "Having more of us there would help sell this better."

"Maybe…" Jade reluctantly agreed, slumping back against a rocky ledge. Her tail flicked anxiously, twitching back and forth. When had she started doing that? "But that still doesn't explain how you'd even get there with me."

"Oh, I can take care of that." Tara said. She was back to tapping away on her suit display, swiping through a series of pages. "I've told you about our scout fliers, right?"

"Those hover plane things?" Luis asked. "Man, I'd love to fly one of those someday."

"You're in luck." Tara grinned. She tapped a button and a holographic image of a small, sleek air ship appeared above her palm. Jade inspected the slowly rotating schematic. It sort of looked like a futuristic version of the cylon raiders from that old Battlestar Galactica show, all sweeping curves and predatory points.

"The Elorinth conclave has been developing a stealth variant, capable of masking itself both visually and magically." Tara continued. She made another motion and the image flickered away, becoming transparent. "We have a working prototype aboard the Intrepid, my home ship. We'd probably still need Jade to cause some sort of distraction before we could slip through, but if she can stir up a little trouble to keep them occupied on their side of the gateway, I'm certain this could let the rest of us sneak in."

"That's crazy." Jade protested. "You don't need to take such a huge risk-"

"And you do?" Fenrin asked, raising an eyebrow.

"That's different, I-"

"You didn't sign up for anything." Naomi said, cutting her off as well. "You didn't want to take that demon race, and you definitely didn't agree to go on a suicide mission to save the rest of the Universe when you did. If you really are set on doing this, at least let us make the same choice too."

"Besides, the demon hordes will probably be pretty upset once their lord crumbles to a pile of ash." Tara added. "The goddess said she can keep a gate open until you get out, but flying over a bunch of angry demons in a fast, invisible ship is clearly a superior way to escape than trying to walk out on your own."

Jade couldn't argue with that point. Assuming she went through with this and got that far in the plan, she would have to count on her shapeshifting powers to help her escape. But even if the closest planar gate to Vastrozun's palace wasn't far, she had to assume his destruction would draw demons like moths to a flame.

"Can you even get us on that ship?" Jade asked, eyeing the tek'kalla skeptically. "Won't your people already be using everything they have to keep the demons from escaping into the Labyrinth?"

"I doubt they've started deploying their prototypes, we have plenty of other resources to draw on. I could make our case to my father, he's one of the Kasta clan leaders. But…"

"But?" Siora asked, cocking her head.

"That would probably take too long, if he listened to us at all. I have something of a reputation for recklessness among my clan." Tara said, awkwardly shuffling her feet.

"Imagine that." Naomi said dryly. Tara ignored the comment.

"There's a clause in the Expeditionary Task Force codex that allows a member to immediately requisition required resources when responding to an emergency, with authorization to be approved after the situation has resolved. I was thinking I'd invoke that."

"You want us to steal an air ship from your clan?" Jade asked, incredulously. "While they're fighting a war?"

"It's not theft." Tara insisted, holding up a finger. "It's an emergency requisition. And a state of conflict is the best time to ste- ah, requisition a military asset. The heightened state of readiness will ensure it's prepared for immediate deployment."

"Perfect!" Luis clapped his hands. "See? We already have a plan."

"Naomi?" Jade appealed to their voice of reason. Surely, she wouldn't go along with this. To her surprise, Naomi simply gave her a smile.

"If you're doing this, Jade, you're doing it with us. I'm not going to leave you to fight alone."

A memory flashed in Jade's mind. Her desperate battle against Malice, and Naomi charging in just in time to save her. She remembered the vision shown to her at the shrine of Growth, whose very essence now filled the air around her. In her old life, she'd made the mistake of not listening to her friends before, too.

Jade made her decision. She would put her trust in her friends.

They relayed their hastily concocted plan to the goddess as a group. When they were finished, a ghost of a smile graced her lips, lightening her weary countenance.

"You are the ones taking the risk. If you believe this path will lead you to success, then you have my full support."

She extended her hand, the splinter of Destruction resting in her palm. The jagged crystalline fragment no longer radiated the waves of intense power it had earlier. Instead, it was practically invisible to Jade's magical senses. The goddess gave her a nod of encouragement, and she reached out, gingerly taking the artifact from her. There was no pain, not even a hint of discomfort as she closed her fingers around the relic. Now that she was holding it Jade could sense the immense, tumultuous power of a caged storm held within the gem, but it was locked away. Not a drop of power leaked from its seal.

"The essence is safely secured, you need not fear it." The goddess said. "Just be careful not to channel it too often, as I said. No more than five times, to be safe. There is only so much I could store within a container as… unique… as this one."

"I understand." Jade murmured, tucking the crystal away in a belt pouch. Locked away as it was, nobody would be able to sense the relic. That was a relief, the last thing they needed was someone catching a whiff of vast cosmic power and trying to steal it. "I can't imagine overdoing it would be good for my body, either."

"Most likely." The goddess agreed. She looked Jade up and down. "Although, your spirit is infused with a touch of celestial power, which will certainly help you resist the side effects of using it. I take it you were the one who defeated Seraphine, then. I can… feel her echo, within you."

Jade didn't need supernatural empathy powers to sense Elaria's sadness, and a spike of alarm shot through her.

"Do not worry." Elaria said gently, shaking her head. "She, and many others, chose to become Labyrinth spirits, ready to test new Wayfarers and keep the seals intact. But this world has grown in ways none of us expected, and their task has not been an easy one. I hardly recognize the Seraphine who has returned to my side, and she is in great need of rest. I only hope that her strength will aid you in the days to come."

Jade glanced down at the splinter of power and squeezed it softly. A flicker of violet energy crackled between her fingers.

"I… think it will. My spirit feels lighter, now. Less oppressive. Since the day we fought her, I find my…" Jade hesitated, struggling to find the right words. It was difficult to give voice to these feelings in front of her closest friends. "My demonic instincts." She finally continued. "They're easier to control, now. Less present in my mind."

Luis lay a comforting hand on her shoulder. She leaned into it as the others gave her encouraging nods.

"I am glad. Go, then, and carry a spark of light into the darkness that awaits. What little power I have remaining will be yours, but I can do little more than ensure the way home remains open for you. The Labyrinth has siphoned from us, just as it has from the worlds of the cosmos."

Elaria gestured, and a doorway appeared in the center of her tranquil glade.

"The hopes of a thousand worlds fly with you, even if they do not know it."

Before they departed, the goddess gave them an entry key for the fourth floor of the Labyrinth. While she couldn't control the systems directly, it had diverged quite a bit from her original design over the centuries, she still had enough access to do that. Time was of the essence, and they couldn't afford to spend a week or more hunting down enough boss monsters to assemble a key of their own. Their levels were now high enough to tackle fourth floor challenges, and where they were going, levels would only mean so much. They were each stronger than the average demon now, and could take on elites as a group. But no one would be fighting their way to where Vastrozun waited in his palace. This time, a subtle approach would serve them better.

Which is my specialty, of course. Jade thought to herself, following Fenrin out of the goddess's grove. Tara's ship had better as good as she says…

They emerged back in the Sanctuary they'd met Damos in, although the Labyrinth attendant appeared to have departed in their absence. Jade couldn't fault him, he probably had better things to do than wait around for them to return, but part of her wanted him to see them off. He'd been there at the start of her journey, a figure of authority that seemed to have all the answers. Now, she really did have those answers, and the weight of it all hung heavy on her shoulders.

But there was one silver lining. Before agreeing to carry out this task, Jade had asked the goddess to find a way to help her brother. A small request, perhaps, considering what they were walking into, but in times like this, one needed hope to cling to. The goddess had no power left to do anything directly, but there were talented healers among the followers of Growth. Elaria had promised that, as soon as the crisis allowed, she would see to it that one was dispatched. There were no guarantees, even when it came to magical healing, but Jade drew strength from that hope, nonetheless. It gave her the courage to keep putting one foot in front of the other. That, and the presence of her kind, loyal companions.

Jade glanced at her friends, subtly sampling their emotions. As expected, they were turbulent. They walked in silence, each processing their divine encounter in their own ways.

"You really-"

"Nope." Luis interrupted her with a slap on the back hard enough to send her stumbling forward. "No telling us to back out." He flashed her a grin. "We're comin' with you."

"We can't let the fate of the world rely on your ability to be discreet." Naomi teased.

The others laughed. There was tension behind it. Fear, even. Their course charted them into waters more dangerous than any they'd encountered before, yet not a single one of her friends wanted out. She could feel their resolve, taste their commitment. They knew that she couldn't walk away from this, and they wouldn't walk away from her.

"…Thank you."   Jade paused at the Sanctuary door, one hand on the handle. "All of you. You're all crazy. But I couldn't wish for better friends."

"You have to be a little crazy to make it in the Labyrinth." Fenrin said.

"I don't know what you're talking about." Naomi said, drawing herself up in mock offense. "I'm perfectly normal. You're the weird ones."

"Don't you blast monsters apart with your guitar?" Luis raised an eyebrow at her.

"That's just a power chord." Naomi sniffed. "Anyone can play one."

"Let's save the praise until we have the stealth ship." Tara suggested, following Jade out of the Sanctuary. It was nighttime in this part of the Labyrinth, and faint beams of silver moonlight penetrated the forest canopy to bathe the land in a dim gloom. She flipped her visor over her eyes. "If we can't find a way to steal it, we won't be getting very far."

"I thought we were requisitioning it?" Fenrin asked.

"…that's what I said."

"How well guarded is it?" Jade asked.

"Not very. It shouldn't be too-"

The teardrop pendant hanging from Tara's neck flashed yellow, and then quickly darkened to red. It took Jade's mind a moment to shift gears and remember what that meant. It was her necklace of demon detection, the same one that had revealed her nature in Ashar's shop when they'd first met. They'd finally figured out how to filter Jade's presence out of its search criteria, which meant…

Jade tore her daggers free from their sheaths. She was already spinning around, sweeping her senses through the surrounding darkness as the others came alert. Just in time. A blur of motion shot towards her, moonlight glinting off the short blade held poised to strike. They'd only just returned to the Labyrinth, and the fight was already coming to them.
