Ren yawned, loudly. The sound of it filled our small room. It was the third time she had yawned in the span of three minutes. "Why the hell are we all here again?" She rubbed her eyes with the back of her wrist. "It's not even past the first bell. It's too early to do anything."

Ash had found the three of them with surprising ease. I had set her on the task, but I had assumed it would take longer than it did. It seemed I was the only one of us who would get lost if they took a few steps outside.

The five of us stood in our room. Corin was between Ren and Edda, his face aimed at the floor. That hadn't changed. Ren stood half in front of him with her arms folded, between him and us. That had changed. Edda stood on his other side.

"We want to try again," Ash said. "With Corin. We've practiced a bit since yesterday."

Ren's face hardened. I watched her jaw tighten and her shoulders set. She stepped further in front of the boy. "You've practiced since yesterday," she said dryly. "This has to be some kind of joke." Her voice had lost its usual volume. It was low and sharp instead. "Lysanthia already tried. She already said she couldn't do it. So what the hell is this? You think it's going to be different after…what, one day? That doesn't even begin to make any sense."

I raised a hand. "This time will be different."

Ren looked at me and then she looked back at Corin. It was Edda who stepped forward. The blue-haired girl's expression did not change, but her voice carried a weight that her face would not show. "What exactly will make this time different?" There was an edge to her words, no matter how she hid it.

I looked at Ash. The faintest smile found me before I could stop it. "It is simple," I said. "This time, Ash will be doing the work that matters. I will merely be assisting her."

The two girls were silent. I doubted that either of them understood. I did not explain further. I stepped toward Corin and looked down at the boy. He did not raise his head.

"There will be danger to this," I said. "There will be risk. I suspect there will be pain as well, more than there was last time." I folded my arms. "But the chance of success is real. Are you willing to accept that much, child?"

I watched. The boy looked up at me. A small tremor went through him. It started at his wrist, and died at his elbow. It was one I recognized, because I had been hiding my own. The Ember Core hummed beneath my ribs.

The boy nodded, slowly. I thought of Shera. She had also accepted a risk just like this one. Perhaps I was starting to understand something about humans after all. About the strength that even the weak could carry. I turned to Ash. "Well then, Ash. Shall we begin? We should-"

"Wait." Ren's voice cut through. "How do I know you two won't make this worse?" Edda reached for her arm. Ren shook her off. "You both better be serious about this. If not, then this is fucked up. You can't just…you can't just keep saying something that doesn't make any sense." Her voice had somehow gotten both quieter and louder at the same time. "I was willing to try this once but getting my hopes dashed twice? You better not. Or I'll never forgive you."

I had something cutting to say. I looked at Ren and did not say it. "You don't have to trust me. Neither will you have reason to forgive me."

Ren opened her mouth. The redness in her face only grew. A small hand reached out and grabbed onto her shoulder. Corin spoke first. His voice was quiet. "Ren…If there's a chance, I'll take it." He looked at Ren. "That's enough for me. Please."

Ren stared at him. Something passed between the three of them that I could see the shape of but did not fully understand. Ren's jaw worked. She stepped back. Edda looked at me and nodded.

"Well," I said. "It's decided then."

Corin sat on his knees in front of us. Ash and I sat in front of him. Before we began, I looked at all three of them.

"What you see in this room," I said, "you are never to speak of. To anyone. Ever. Perhaps I should have said this last time as well. I am saying this now. You will agree to this, or we will not start."

Ren and Edda looked at each other. Their shared look did not quite convey the weight of what I asked them. The boy did not look anywhere but straight ahead. The two girls nodded. Corin only faintly nodded. That would have to be good enough.

I turned to Ash. "You do not yet have the fine control for this on your own. You are going to follow me." I held up my right hand. A wisp of Ruin trickled from my fingers into Corin. The boy flinched. I followed it with a wisp of Cradle from my left. White light pooled behind the black, crossing his skin into the flesh beneath. "Can you feel it? With your senses, you should be able to."

Ash leaned closer. Her eyes narrowed. She nodded slowly. "I will find each place," I said. "I will show you the place where the wrongness and the channel are the same. I will need your power there."

"I can do that, I think," Ash said slowly. The Hero looked uncertain. I did not know why. I would not ask something of her I was not sure she could do.

"Good. I will erase what you have cut, and fill the gap that remains. You need to only follow."

This would take some time. The boy had hundreds of small and twisted channels, and each of them would need to be corrected one by one. Perhaps Ash might manage such a thing in one stroke someday, but I knew she could not yet. Still, with our three powers, we could do this. I was sure of it.

I had done many complex things in my long life. Never with another. Never where the critical step rested in someone else's hands. To know that I must place my hopes on another should have filled me with dread. It did not. If it was Ash, then it would not.

I took a deep breath, and focused. I guided a wisp of Ruin to the first knot. "There," I whispered. I did not know why I whispered.

Ash nodded and leaned in close. She raised her left hand and drew a line in the air. Slowly. Too slowly. The line passed through the space above Corin's arm and nothing happened. I felt the wrongness sitting exactly where it had been, untouched.

"Ash," I murmured. "Try again. Envision it in your mind. This is more difficult because you cannot see directly, but this is something you can do."

Ash slowly nodded as she drew again. This time, I felt the wrongness shudder. I felt it through the Ruin.

"Again. You were closer this time."

Ash's third attempt caught. The wrongness peeled away from the channel beneath it, and the separation was not clean. Corin grunted. The sound was sharp. Ren stepped forward. Corin held up his hand without looking. "This is nothing."

I felt the wrongness through Ruin. It tried to claw back toward the channel it had been separated from. Perhaps it was a thing alive. I had suspected I would find something like this. It was good that I'd had the Requiem ready. Ruin erased the wrongness before it could enter the boy again, and I filled the gap that remained with the Cradle.

The impression came. The same one from yesterday, of watching two backs growing smaller on a long road. I ignored it. The first channel cleared. There were hundreds more to go.

The second was harder. The wrongness here was thicker, more deeply fused. The channel hardly resembled one at all. I guided Ash to it and she drew her line. The cut was imprecise. Her slice caught a sliver of healthy channel alongside the wrongness. Corin hissed between his teeth.

"Focus, Ash," I said. "Only what I show you. Nothing else. Feel the line of my power."

Ash nodded slowly and adjusted herself beside me. Her hand trembled once and then steadied. The next cut was cleaner. I burned what she severed and filled the gap.

The third channel. The fifth. The eighth. The corrections I needed to give Ash grew fewer. By the tenth, she needed none at all. Her hand moved where I guided, and the wrongness parted from the channel beneath it perfectly. I followed behind her power, burning what was severed, healing what remained. The wrongness wasn't always there. Sometimes Ash simply severed a channel that grew in a tangle, and I had to join what she cut behind. That was perhaps more difficult than the rest.

We entered into a kind of rhythm, one just between us. Ash's Parting, followed by my Ruin and Bloom. Over and over again. At some point, we stopped speaking entirely. There was no longer a need to. I would find a knot, and Ash would sever it before I had time to say a single word. I would follow with my two powers, and we would move on to the next. Ash leaned closer. I leaned into her.

There was an impression every time I used my powers, and these impressions were different from the visions I had seen the last time I had used either mark on this boy. I saw a hand pressing against a window, watching rain. Watching the sunrise and sunset. Watching the sun. The hand was always watching the window, apart from whatever lay on the other side. I saw a traveling pack being set on shoulders far too small and frail to carry it. I saw the boy's mother, and her mark. It was a pale pink I could hardly see in the vision. And her mother before her. And her mother before that.

The Ember Core ground inside me. Pain was a constant thing, but the level of pain was not always the same. Right now, it was worse than usual. Enough for my body to betray me, every now and then. Some small twitch here, some breath too sharp there. Nobody could have noticed. Nobody but Ash. I knew this because her shoulder pressed harder into mine. We continued.

We healed more and more of these channels. Twenty. Thirty. Fifty. We healed so many that even my nearly boundless Essence was starting to thin. Using Ruin or Bloom one time cost me little. Practically nothing compared to when I only had mana. The problem was just how many times I had to use it, and just how precisely I had to use it each time. Each knot I found led to three more, branching beneath the ones I had already cleared. Doubtless, Ash was under the same strain I was. Perhaps hers was greater.

I said nothing, and neither did Ash. We simply worked.

Ren watched from the far wall. She had been there for some time. Edda sat beside her. At some point, Edda's hand had found hers.

Ren could see the two strange flames now. One so black it was darker than dark. The other so bright it was painful to look upon. Ren hadn't seen either yesterday, not with the way Lysanthia had kept her hands pressed against Corin's skin. Right now, Corin sat perfectly still between the two strange flames. And Ash was doing something too, though Ren did not have the faintest idea what.

The two women had been talking at the start. They weren't talking now. They hadn't said anything in a long time. Every now and then, Lysanthia winced in pain. Ren didn't see whatever hurt her. Maybe her own power did. Each time, Ash leaned into her, just slightly. It wasn't the kind of thing Ren would have normally noticed, but she was looking right at them. And it was impossible to miss.

Ren's mother used to do that. When Ren was small and burning with fever, her mother would press closer, as if the warmth of her body alone could pull the sickness out. It was stupid, but her mother used to believe in a lot of stupid things. She used to believe that if you asked the world hard enough, then the world would answer you. Even if it had to answer you in a way that made no damned sense.

Lysanthia winced again. Ash shifted closer. It had taken her mother three days to walk to Lord Relen's estate to beg him for grain after the fields burned. She almost hadn't made it at all, from what she'd told Ren later. The lord had laughed. Worse, he had nearly put her in chains for wasting his time.

The mess bell rang. Neither of the two women seemed to hear it. Ren stood, and she and Edda brought food and water. Ash drank without looking up. Lysanthia didn't drink at all until Ash forced a cup into her hand. Ren set the tray down and went back to the wall.

Her mother had died that winter. She had fed Ren instead of feeding herself. Even at the end, she had believed someone would come. She had hoped, and hoped, and the hoping had eaten her alive from the inside, the same way not eating had eaten her from the outside.

Nobody ever came for you. Not without expecting more than they gave. You had to do everything yourself. Ren had learned that lesson well.

When the traveling Inker came through and Ren's mark blazed brighter than anything that village had ever seen, she'd thought things could change. She went to the Academy. She clawed her way to Sigil in two years. She worked herself to the bone, because in Arianeth, even someone like her could become someone, if only she advanced.

Nothing changed when she reached Line. Nothing changed when she reached Sigil. The sneers followed her through the halls. It seemed Ren had still been naive, even when she'd thought she was doing the opposite. Edda had followed her, and then Corin. Corin's mark had come out wrong. It was only because Ren was considered an asset that they didn't kick him out immediately.

Ren still didn't know why. What was the point when they were going to do it sooner or later anyway? All she knew was that nobody would care when it happened. It was them against the world. Always.

Corin let out a small groan. It was the first sound he had made in over an hour. He took a deep breath and steadied himself again on the floor. Ren had begun to hope. She knew she shouldn't. She hoped anyway, because she couldn't help it, and because Corin wasn't nearly as good at hiding his pain as he thought he was.

Two strangers sat in front of her friend with their hands doing the Divine only knew what, and Ren was hoping again, and she hated herself for it. Edda's hand squeezed hers. Ren squeezed back.

The hours had stretched, and the light from the single window had started to bleed amber, and then slowly fade into darkness. The room was dim. The lantern had burned low. Ash and I sat in front of Corin. We had gotten closer during the work. Ash was leaning on me slightly. Whether from exhaustion or something else, I did not examine. The Ember Core ground beneath my ribs. Ash leaned into me again. I let her.

We fixed the last of the boy's corrupted channels. Ash separated the final thread of wrongness, and I burned it with Ruin. The Cradle followed and healed the mana channel into what it should have been, had the wrongness never touched it. With it came the impression. The one I had seen before. A door that led to nothing. That door slowly closed. Perhaps the boy closed it himself. Or perhaps there was simply no reason to have a door that led to nothing any longer.

I withdrew my hands. They were faintly shaking. I could not stop them. "It is done," I said. My voice came out as barely a croak.

The Hero's arm was still around me. I was leaning against it more than I had at the start. She was leaning into me too. It was a wonder neither of us fell. My core groaned inside me, and with it came pain. I breathed through it. Was it my imagination, or was the pain worse than it had been in the morning? Either way, it did not matter. We had healed the boy.

He slowly stood, staring at his arm all the while. Ren and Edda were both leaning in to look. On the boy's arm, where before there had been something so pale it had been hard to see, there was a green Spark. The mark flickered as we watched, and then it started to pulse, slow and steady. This mark would not go dim again.

Ren threw her arms around Corin, so hard the boy almost tipped over entirely. She made some kind of choking sound. Then she was speaking, but the words all jumbled over each other until I could make out none of them. Edda was weeping. Then Corin was weeping. Then Ren joined them. Ren's weeping was the loudest. Compared to her, it was hard to hear the other two. None of their expressions were ugly things.

I had ordered demons to stand in formations as straight as drawn lines. I had never seen three of them hold each other like this. Perhaps this was what they had always looked like, when I was not watching.

Ash tapped my side. I looked at her. "You're still here," she said. "Think you might have some right to see this time?"

I looked at her, and then I looked at them. All three of these demonbloods were my blood, however far removed. And I had done something for them, something that was not destruction. Something that would not come to haunt them, in time. "I do not mind the sight," I said, slowly.

It took a long time for the three to compose themselves. Ash had fully leaned into me by then, and her left hand rested atop my right. We did not rise, even as the three slowly did. Ren wiped at her face furiously. Edda's expression had gone back to normal, slowly. Corin's had not.

Ren stepped forward. She opened her mouth. No doubt to say the usual flood of crass words I had come to expect from her. None came. She stood there with her jaw working and her eyes red. Then she took a breath, and she bowed. She folded at the waist, deeply and slowly, and she held it. Her red hair fell forward and hid her face.

I stared at her. It was perhaps one of the deepest bows I had ever received, coming from a girl I'd thought incapable of one. "Thank you," she said. Her voice cracked on the second word. "I don't know how we'll ever repay this."

Edda and Corin bowed too. Theirs were less deep, but no less surprising.

I looked at them. Looked at Ash. "Raise your heads. You do not need to bow. I did not ask for one," I said. "It is a Queen's duty to care for those beneath her." I paused. "And do remember that my retainer deserves the credit as much as I do. More so, in truth. I could have done nothing for your friend without her." The three raised their heads slowly. "You will not mention what happened here, to anyone," I said. "You now have a mark, and people will have questions. You will make your own stories. You will make sure the story makes sense. Neither Ash nor I will be mentioned." I paused. "Is that understood?"

"Y-yes," all three of them said, tripping over each other to say it first. I had no doubt at all that eventually this would lead back to us. Still, it would take some time, at least.

It was strange. To receive gratitude instead of deference. How very alike, and yet not quite. "Now, you three may leave," I said. Ash had yawned beside me. Or had almost yawned, before she had pointedly closed her mouth.

They turned to leave. Corin was last. He stopped at the door and turned back. "Thank you, Your Grace."

He left. I stared at the door. I stared at it for some time. Your Grace. A title for royalty. A title that befitted me, though I had heard 'Your Royal Darkness' far more often. The title had been mine once, though nobody in a thousand years had likely used it. The boy could not have known what it meant or how apt it was. I did not know why he had said it at all. Perhaps some things survived in the blood after all.

"It seems…your kingdom is growing, Your Grace."

I exhaled through my nose. Even I could tell that the Hero was mocking me. "I am not building a kingdom, fool." I sighed. "It seems these demonbloods make even less sense than normal demons did."

I slowly rose. There was a creak from my leg as I stood. Ash followed, and there was a creak from hers too. I tried to close my hands, and it took more effort than it should have.

I went to check the egg. It was nestled between the sheet and the pillow, just where I had left it. I sat on the bed, and held the egg in my lap. The gold shone in the light of the lantern. For a moment, those black lines did not look nearly so dark. Had they been thinning? I could not say. I had been watching them for so long that I no longer trusted my own eyes.

I pressed the egg tightly against my chest. It was pulsing, as it always was. No, it had never pulsed this quickly before. Perhaps the thing inside it was displeased with me.

"I am here," I whispered. "I apologize for not being able to attend to you today." I adjusted the egg against my chest. "You are quite the demanding creature, aren't you? Do not think that your impatience goes unnoticed."

The already fast pulsing grew faster still. The room was very quiet. I heard Ash take a step towards me. Then I felt it. Through my hands. A vibration, so faint it might have been nothing. Then again, stronger. A third, stronger still.

I looked down. There was a single crack on the surface of the egg. It was thin, and long, almost as long as one of my fingers. Even as I watched, that one crack grew until it split the shell from one side almost to the other. It glowed, and for one moment, it looked like a vein of sunlight.

Crack.
