"Nothing?" Luke said, his answer a question in and of itself. Throughout his dealings with the Singers, the lack of notifications wasn't something he had given much consideration, but when he checked his interface to see his Level still stuck at 49, he couldn't help but feel more than a little flabbergasted. Destroying the entirety of the Singer civilization and becoming responsible for the death of billions had netted him a grand total of zero experience points. Frowning, he checked the notifications, and from there, things just got stranger.

Congratulations! You have reached Level 50!

Congratulations! You have reached Level 51!

Congratulations! You have reached Level 52!

And so on all the way to the eye-watering Level 145. But then...

Experience revoked by order of The Council.

"You didn't get a single level?" someone asked. Through the haze, Luke didn't catch who.

He shook his head, rereading the last notification again and again. Some external force, this 'council', had reached its hand in and yanked the levels away. That someone else held that much power over him was disconcerting, and that was putting it mildly. Once The Greater System opened up to Earth, there would be questions in need of answers, a whole lot of questions. And the answers better be good.

"The system didn't count what you did as being responsible for their deaths, then," Milla said.

"I guess not," Luke agreed, knowing that wasn't the whole story, but not knowing if telling everyone was the right move. He needed some time to sit with it and consider what it all meant.

Nick appeared by the entrance to the guildhall, flanked by the rest of his guild. They looked bedraggled, but all of them were bright and wore the sort of smiles you get after receiving good news. "Did you hear?"

"Hey," Milla said, raising her hand in a little wave. "Just got the news."

"What news?" Ray asked, having returned after putting Cage in a cell. Apparently, The Band had cells.

"The Singers have offered unconditional surrender," Nick said. "It's over."

Ray walked up and thumped Luke on the back. "It was over the moment my boy Luke here wiped out their entire planet."

Luke caught a thoughtful look from Curtis, but the Hollow didn't comment.

"What will be our adversaries' fate?" Lemira asked, stepping out of the small crowd. A small, gray, winged lizard sat on her shoulder.

"Depends on the country, I guess," Milla said. "There's no one person or organization that can answer for everyone on Earth."

"A lot of people are dead," Nick said. "I bet a lot of people will want heads to roll."

This time, everyone turned to look at Curtis, who scowled. "What?"

"Anyway, it's out of our hands," Milla said.

They all fell silent for a beat before Curtis shrugged. "Well, I'm heading out."

After watching him go, Lana spoke up for the first time since they'd all returned. "What now?"

"We have six months or so before the next step," Luke said. "We should make the most of it."

"Dungeons?" Ray asked.

Milla nodded. "Dungeons. But first, we all had better get some rest. You all did great against the Singers. We lost some friends, but the rest of us need to go on. I'm hearing whispers of some sort of League of Guilds on Earth that's meant to be a global alliance of sorts. We need to be on that, and for that, we'll need to get even stronger. Get to work, everyone! But also rest!"

Hannah grabbed Luke's arm with a glint in her eye. "You're coming with me."

The crowd dispersed, everyone seeming a little deflated, like they couldn't quite believe it was all over. About the same time as Luke and Hannah reached the plaque that would take them out of guild headquarters, Luke's interface went dark. Not blank, like when he didn't focus on it, but black as night. By the look of everyone else's faces, theirs had just done the same. A second later, a person appeared, one he knew well.

"Guide," he muttered.

The timing of his appearance and what had just happened with the Singers couldn't be a coincidence. What played looked a lot like a television broadcast, except Guide was just standing there in front of total darkness. When he spoke, it both sounded out in the interface and arrived as system messages.

I must begin by apologizing. Despite my best efforts, certain elements have been able to apply pressure to your system. This is an affront to everything The Greater System is supposed to be and the values it represents. Unfair advantage was given to those named 'Singers' along with guidance and even resources far beyond the limits imposed on us. You have shown excellence not just in survival, but in the elimination of your opponent. Know that I am proud of your efforts. An inquest has been launched into this matter.

"What about my levels?" Luke muttered.

As compensation for all hardship suffered by this system, certain allowances have been made. From this point on, your contributions to our common goal will award you a slight increase in experience. Key individuals from Earth will be called upon for a new type of mission, and you have all been given the information you need to adapt your current technology with system-compatible sources of power and communication protocols.

Guide gave a brief pause, then continued.

A system has gone dark. While I’m sure this will be celebrated on Earth, it means there are fewer to stand with us against the void. Your strength is of the utmost importance, and that truth is now greater than ever. Grow stronger and come together, Integrated of Earth, for there is much hardship to come. Thank you for your attention.

"I like the part about getting more experience," Nick said after they'd all taken a beat to digest the message from Guide.

Milla scoffed. "Have you seen the information package? With this, I'm sure we can get the power going again."

"And Wi-Fi?" someone asked.

"Probably."

A round of cheering rocked the main hall of guild headquarters, but Hannah nodded for Luke to follow her outside.

“What’s up?” Luke asked once they were outside. Hannah looked tired, and her face was speckled with blood and dirt. Even the shadows playing in her hair seemed spent.

She gave him a long, searching look. “You’ve been through a lot. Just want to make sure you’re… I don’t know. Doing okay, even if that sounds too small for what just happened.”

“I don’t know how to feel about it all, if I’m being honest. The system did give me levels, but then rescinded them because of something called The Council.”

“The Council?”

Luke shrugged. “That’s what it says.”

“So… What? It means you’re not responsible for their deaths, I guess?”

“What the system thinks doesn’t really play into it in here,” Luke said, tapping himself on the side of his head. "But I'm honestly feeling... Fine. Maybe that's strange, since I don't even have the mental block anymore, but it was them or us, and I wasn't about to let Earth be destroyed."

"I get that," Hannah said. "But..."

"Give me some time to mull the whole thing over, alright?"

Her expression didn't change, but she nodded nonetheless. "Alright." After that, a smile crept onto her face. "Maybe you feel like having a shower?"

"Oh?" he asked, raising an eyebrow.

Hannah wriggled her eyebrows. "I know I could use one."

"Oh!"

Luke had gotten his Shelter back from Cage along with the other items, but Hannah was faster in placing hers, and they entered it right away, happy for the brief respite from crazy, world-ending disasters. Much later, he lay on his back, staring up at the ceiling of Hannah's shelter, but not seeing it due to the pitch-black darkness. Her soft, peaceful breaths told him she'd fallen asleep. Soon, he would do the same. Exhausted was not a strong enough word to describe the state of him in that moment, but at least now it came with a sense of contentment. They'd beaten the Singers. It had happened so fast in the end. Just a single decisive moment ended the conflict. That weapon must have been part of the unfair advantages Guide mentioned. A system ending system of death like that couldn't be something the Singers cooked up on their own.

The sound of a slight shuffle made Luke's breath catch in his throat. Smudge? He'd deactivated Weaver's Perception when they entered the Shelter, wanting to stay in the moment without distractions. Now that he resumed its focus, Luke found that they weren't alone.

"Lifeweaver," a familiar voice called from within the darkness.

Luke set his jaw. "Intruders."

Not one or two, but all five of them. How they had found him, Luke didn't have a clue, and their way into the Shelter without being spotted was a mystery, but there was no doubt about it. They'd escaped the blast from the weapon somehow, and they'd come to exact their revenge. Too bad for them, a friend had just arrived as well.

"I knew you'd come," Curtis said, his voice coming from everywhere and nowhere.
