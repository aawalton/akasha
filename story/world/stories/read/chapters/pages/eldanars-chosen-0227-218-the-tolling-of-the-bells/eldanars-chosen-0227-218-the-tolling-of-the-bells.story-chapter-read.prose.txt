The vibration of the rocky ridge was no longer a subtle tremor. It was a violent, continuous grinding that rattled the teeth in their skulls and made the freezing dirt dance beneath their stomachs.

The five hunters lay completely flat against the frosted earth, staring down into the valley. The sheer, impossible scale of the undead army marching below them defied all logical comprehension. It was a suffocating, undulating ocean of rusted iron, rotting flesh, and bone, stretching for miles in every single direction. The heavy, rhythmic thudding of more than ten thousand pairs of skeletal boots moving in perfect, unnatural lockstep echoed like a continuous roll of thunder.

For several long, agonising minutes, nobody spoke. The human members of the squad were entirely paralysed by the primal, existential horror of what they were witnessing.

Perberos broke the silence. The elven scout did not whisper, nor did his voice tremble. His tone was perfectly flat, carrying the cold, uncompromising edge of a sharpened blade.

"I am not retreating," Perberos announced, his luminous silver eyes fixed intently on the dark, swirling mass at the absolute centre of the horde. He slowly reached over his shoulder, his long fingers resting lightly against the fletching of his heavy, armour-piercing bodkin arrows. "I am going to find the locus of that dark magic, and I am going to kill the general commanding this force. I will shatter their formation before they ever breach the timber walls of Ashenfall."

Lyra, the lithe half-elven rogue, violently wrenched her gaze away from the sea of bone to stare at Perberos in profound disbelief.

"Have you entirely lost your mind?" Lyra hissed, her cynical eyes wide with genuine panic. She pointed a trembling finger down into the valley. "Look at the sheer density of that formation. There are ten thousand monsters down there. What exactly do you think five of us can do against an extinction event? This is not a heroic ballad, Perberos. This is suicide."

"It is a tactical strike," Perberos corrected smoothly, not looking away from the valley. "An army of this magnitude requires a massive, centralised intelligence to coordinate their marching speed and siege assets. If we sever the head, the body will rot. The horde will scatter into a disorganised swarm."

"I cannot help you do that," Lyra argued, her voice dropping into a harsh, desperate whisper. She gripped the hilts of her twin throwing daggers tightly. "I am a close-quarters skirmisher. My blades are short, and my armour is non-existent. For me to be of any use in an assassination, I need to be within ten feet of the target. I would have to walk through a horde of tightly packed skeletal knights just to get within striking distance. I will be ripped to pieces before I take five steps."

Garth, the grizzled human tracker, spat a thick wad of dark chewing tobacco onto the frost. He wiped his heavily scarred chin with the back of a mud-caked gauntlet, his face still pale with shock.

"If we are currently taking volunteers to run back to the safety of the city walls with an update and then hide in a deep cellar," Garth grunted, forcing a grim, gallows humour into his voice to mask his sheer terror, "then my boots are already pointing south. I will happily deliver the bad news to the magistrate."

"You are not going anywhere, Garth," Ronald interrupted sharply.

The feline beastkin pushed himself up into a low crouch. His ears were flattened completely against his skull. Ronald’s pragmatic, brilliant mind had finally overcome the initial, paralysing shock of the horde. He was rapidly calculating distances, marching speeds, and the terrifying odds of survival.

"Perberos is correct about the tactical necessity of a strike," Ronald stated, his jade-green eyes narrowing as he scanned the flanks of the undead army. "If that unified force hits Ashenfall, the city will shatter. However, charging blindly into the centre of their formation is a fool's errand. We will be crushed."

Ronald turned to the elven scout, his voice ringing with unquestionable military authority.

"We are not attacking them head-on," Ronald commanded. "The sensible path is to flank the enemy. We will fall back from this ridge, take a wide detour through the eastern forest, and fall in directly behind their marching column. We will track them from the shadows, match their pace, and wait for an opening. When the commanding entity exposes itself to direct the siege, we take the shot from extreme range."

Ronald then turned his sharp gaze towards the two remaining members of the squad.

"But the city council needs to know exactly what is marching towards them," Ronald continued grimly. "Finn is carrying outdated information, especially the enemy's numbers and capabilities… along with our plan. Someone needs to return to Ashenfall immediately and warn Caistina that they have hours, not days, to prepare."

Ronald looked directly at Lyra and Silas. "One of you needs to take that message back."

A heavy, fraught silence fell over the small group on the ridge.

Lyra and Silas exchanged a long, deeply conflicted look. They were veteran adventurers, bound by a strict code of loyalty to their squad. Neither of them wanted to be the one to turn their back and run while their comrades marched into the teeth of an apocalypse. It felt entirely like abandonment.

Silas, the seasoned human ranger, slowly let out a breath that plumed white in the freezing air. He reached out, placing a thick, calloused hand gently on Lyra's leather-clad shoulder.

"You need to go, Lyra," Silas said quietly, his voice a low, comforting rumble. "Your assessment of the situation was right. This is going to be a long-range engagement. You need to be close to use those daggers effectively. Garth, Ronald, Perberos, and I can engage targets at two hundred yards. We are the ones equipped for this specific mission."

Lyra looked down at her short blades, her cynical eyes softening with profound, reluctant agreement. She knew Silas was right. In a sniper's duel against a dark general, she would simply be a liability, another body for them to protect.

"Take a wide berth," Ronald advised, offering the lithe rogue a serious nod. "Do not follow the main dirt road. Swing far to the west, stick to the deepest shadows, and run until your lungs bleed. Tell Rochelle they will likely arrive by lunchtime."

Lyra sheathed her daggers with a sharp, metallic click. She looked at the four men remaining on the ridge, offering them a final, solemn salute.

"Give them absolute hell in the dark, boys," Lyra whispered, her voice thick with uncharacteristic emotion. "I will see you on the walls."

Without another word, the half-elven rogue melted backward, sliding silently down the rocky slope of the ridge. She hit the freezing mud at the bottom and instantly vanished into the thick, swirling necrotic fog, sprinting wildly towards the distant safety of Ashenfall.

Ronald watched her disappear into the gloom, ensuring she was safely away before turning his attention entirely back to the impossible task at hand. The beastkin crouched low beside Perberos, resting his clawed hands on his knees.

"Right then," Ronald murmured, looking at the silent elven scout. "We are flanking the horde, and we are waiting for a clear shot at the general. So, what is your plan to actually kill it?"

Perberos did not blink. His eyes remained locked on the churning, boiling mass of rotting meat and rusted iron marching relentlessly through the valley below. His ethereal face was a mask of cold, uncompromising resolve.

"I am going to draw my bow," Perberos replied softly, his voice echoing with lethal certainty. "And I am not going to miss."

Miles to the south, the freezing, pitch-black silence of the night was violently shattered.

Clang-Clang. Clang-Clang. Clang-Clang.

The sound was absolutely deafening. It was the massive, heavy bronze warning bells located in the central watchtower of Ashenfall. The frantic, rhythmic tolling rolled across the slate rooftops of the city like a physical shockwave, cutting cleanly through the thick wooden walls of the Guildhall dormitories.

Josh jolted violently awake in the pitch-black room.

His heart was hammering wildly against his ribs, a sudden, massive spike of pure adrenaline flooding his veins. For a fraction of a second, his exhausted, disoriented mind thought he was back in the muddy sinkhole, surrounded by the screaming horde. He scrambled blindly in the dark, his hands grasping for the leather handle of his heavy steel shield.

"What is happening?" Brett yelled from the darkness across the small room.

The elemental mage was already out of bed, but his panicked awakening had gone poorly. He had managed to tangle his long legs in the thick, woollen blanket and was currently swearing loudly as he hopped around on one foot, desperately trying to free himself.

"The town bells," Josh grunted, finally locating his thick, padded gambeson on a nearby wooden chair. He began pulling the heavy, quilted fabric roughly over his head. "They only ring the main tower bells at this hour if there’s some kind of danger. Something has changed."

The ambient noise outside their heavy wooden door was rapidly escalating. They could hear the frantic pounding of heavy boots sprinting down the carpeted corridors, the muffled shouts of panicked adventurers, and the sharp, metallic clatter of weapons being hastily drawn.

Josh did not bother attempting to strap on the complex layers of his heavy chainmail or his full steel plate armour. Securing the straps perfectly required time and assistance, neither of which he currently possessed. He simply fastened the thick leather buckles of his breastplate over his gambeson, grabbed his heavy heater shield, and snatched his swords from the bedside table.

Brett finally kicked free of the tangled blankets. The mage hurriedly threw his new, shimmering crimson Ember-silk cloak over his shoulders, his long fingers glowing with a faint, anxious red light.

Josh kicked the heavy wooden door open and stepped out into the corridor.

Carcan was already waiting for them.

The elven healer stood perfectly still in the chaotic hallway. The shy, vulnerable embarrassment that had painted her cheeks a bright crimson just hours earlier was gone. She had completely retreated behind her hardened, professional battle persona. Her pristine pale green robes were immaculately arranged, and she held her amber-topped wooden staff firmly in both hands. The crystal was pulsing with a brilliant, steady golden light, casting long shadows across her aristocratic, stone-cold features.

"They are ringing the siege alarm," Carcan stated, her melodic voice perfectly level, betraying absolutely no panic. "The timeline we were given has clearly changed. We need to get downstairs to the main hall immediately."

Josh nodded, his storm-grey eyes serious. He took the lead, his heavy boots thudding against the expensive carpets as he led the mage and the healer quickly down the winding, heavy oak stairs toward the ground floor of the Guildhall.

As they descended, the noise grew from a muffled din into an absolute, deafening roar of frantic chaos.

They reached the bottom of the stairs and stepped into a scene of pure, unadulterated desperation. The Guildhall had completely abandoned any pretence of being a simple meeting place for wealthy adventurers. It was now a frantic, overflowing militia muster point.

The sheer volume of people crammed into the massive stone building was staggering. It was not just the high-tier veterans and heavily armoured mercenaries anymore. The city guard had clearly conscripted everyone capable of lifting a piece of iron.

Josh saw local blacksmiths wearing soot-stained leather aprons, nervously holding crude, unpolished iron spears. He saw bakers and merchants gripping rusted broadswords and heavy wooden clubs. Terrified young men and women, who had likely never faced anything more dangerous than a wild dog, were being corralled by hoarse-voiced guardsmen. The air was thick with the smell of nervous sweat, burning torches, and raw, unfiltered fear.

"Stay close behind me," Josh ordered, raising his heavy steel shield slightly to create a solid wedge.

He used his massive, muscular bulk to physically carve a path through the surging, panicked crowd. He ignored the shouting and the shoving, pushing his way steadily toward the front of the grand hall.

Through the sea of terrified faces and raised weapons, Josh spotted a beacon of calm. A brilliant, pulsing orb of pure white magical light floated steadily above the chaotic crowd near the main administrative desks. It was Caistina Tharistrar’s staff.

Josh pushed through the final layer of conscripted militiamen and broke into a small, hastily cleared circle near the front of the room.

Caistina stood tall, her sapphire-blue robes commanding immediate respect. She was flanked by Rochelle, the stern head clerk of the Guild, and a heavily armoured captain of the Ashenfall city guard, whose face was pale and drawn with severe stress.

But it was the fourth person in the circle that made Josh's blood run completely cold.

Sitting slumped on a wooden chair, shivering violently despite the ambient heat of the crowded hall, was a remarkably young, human scout. He was covered from head to toe in a thick layer of freezing black mud and foul-smelling necrotic sludge. He was clutching a dented metal flask in his trembling hands, taking ragged, desperate gulps of water.

Josh instantly recognised the boy. It was Finn, the eager young ranger who had departed the city gates just hours ago as part of Perberos's elite stealth squad.

Behind Josh, Carcan let out a sharp, ragged intake of breath. The elven healer's hardened battle persona fractured instantly. She stepped out from behind Josh's broad shoulders, her silver eyes locking onto the shivering, mud-caked boy. She gripped her amber staff so tightly her knuckles turned pure white.

"He is alone," Carcan whispered, her voice trembling violently with a sudden, overwhelming surge of panic. She reached out, her fingers digging painfully into the thick leather of Josh's gambeson. "Where are the rest of them? Where is my brother?"

Josh placed a heavy, reassuring hand over hers, offering a silent anchor, but his own heart was hammering with dread. He stepped closer to the administrative circle, straining to hear the hushed, urgent conversation taking place over the deafening noise of the panicked militia.

"...ran until my lungs were bleeding," Finn was gasping, his voice barely a hoarse whisper. He looked up at Caistina with wide, terrified eyes. "Ronald ordered me back the moment we saw the landscape shifting. The dark magic... it is flooding the plains. It is eating the grass. The black sludge has reached miles further south than you estimated."

Rochelle slammed her thick leather ledger onto the wooden desk, her stern face tightening with severe anxiety. "If the corruption line has advanced that rapidly, it means the ambient necrotic energy anchoring the horde is significantly stronger than a standard dungeon break."

Caistina did not look at the clerk. She kept her amethyst eyes fixed intently on the shivering young scout.

"Did Ronald get a visual confirmation on their marching speed?" Caistina demanded, her melodic voice cutting through the boy's panic with sharp, uncompromising authority. "Are they moving at a standard shambling pace, or are they coordinated?"

Finn swallowed hard, wiping a streak of foul-smelling mud from his pale forehead. "They were not shambling, Lady Caistina. The ground was shaking. They are moving with military discipline. Ronald said... he said they are on a forced march."

The heavy, armoured guard captain let out a low, deeply pragmatic curse. He slammed his mailed fist against the wooden table.

"A forced march," the captain repeated, his voice thick with absolute horror. He looked at the elven mage trainer, his eyes wide. "If they are maintaining a disciplined, forced march through the night, and the corruption line is already that far south..."

"Then the timeline we gave the city council is completely wrong," Caistina finished softly, the brilliant white light of her staff flickering slightly, betraying her internal dread.

She turned away from the boy, looking out over the chaotic, terrified sea of poorly armed bakers, merchants, and low-level adventurers currently flooding the Guildhall. She looked at the fragile, desperate faces of the people relying entirely on her magical strength to keep them alive.

Caistina took a slow, deep breath, her aristocratic features hardening into a mask of pure, lethal determination.

"We do not have days to prepare," Caistina announced, raising her voice just enough for the surrounding veteran fighters, including Josh and his party, to hear the devastating reality of their situation. "We do not even have until tomorrow evening. If they maintain that pace, the vanguard of the undead horde will hit the walls of Ashenfall by midday."
