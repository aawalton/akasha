After a slight hesitation, the Guardian responded. “We don’t comment on membership to our spaces.”

Whispers rippled through the stacks, making me freeze.

Lenna tilted her head to one side, and went quiet.

“… but I can say that the Knowledge Bearer is involved with the organization.”

The change in demeanor surprised me, and I glanced up. Were they interfering yet again?

The Guardian cleared his throat, drawing my attention. “What type of device do you want?”

On the shelves were books, tablets, and scrolls, plus things that reminded me of crystals.

I had no freaking clue, but I snagged a tablet, since Lenna got a book. For sure I didn’t want a crystal.

The Guardian nodded, then turned and walked away from us.

Lenna gasped, drawing my attention. “Look at this…”

Her book had a list of volumes that she could ‘check’ out. It couldn’t be the entire collection, but it was a start.

I tucked the tablet into my inventory, and to my shock, it didn’t take up any space.

“Noodles?” I asked, as my stomach growled yet again.

“Sure,” said Lenna, still flipping through the book.

I led the way back down the stairs, feeling something staring at me from above. That feeling vanished when we exited the building.

The map directed us to continue down the street, and off we went.

Lenna was lost in her book, but still keeping up with me. The space people gave us helped that.

Then the noodle shop appeared when we turned a corner. A small counter, with an overhang, stools, and someone with bowls, cooking. A meatly smell hung over the area, with a tang of rich broth.

My mouth watered, and I picked up the pace. Thankfully, Lenna noticed, and we snagged the last two empty stools.

The menu overhead translated as I looked at it. I picked the easiest thing: the special. It had meat, eggs of some sort, lots of noodles, and something that was spicy.

“What can I get you?” asked a cat-like person behind the counter.

“What do you recommend?” asked Lenna, before I could order my choice. “Beany sent us in this direction, and we’re new.”

“My spouse, actually doing their job.” They nodded, and pointed to a few options. “Good. Good. Then spicy good. But if you don’t eat meat, I don’t have any options for you.”

“Meat’s fine,” she said, reading the three good options. “I’ll go with the second one. I like eggs. They are hard to find on my world.”

“And you?” they asked, looking at me. The yellow cat eyes stared at me.

“The special. I eat a lot.”

“A double, got it.” They turned and pulled two bowls off the stack, and put three clumps of super thin noodles into a big pot. They moved in the cramped stall like a dancer. From the noodles, to meat that they pulled out of a different pot and chopped up, they were fluid and practiced. Then the noodles went into the bowls, followed by the meat.

Extra eggs went into one bowl, while the other received extra meat, and some blue sauce. They chopped two different things that looked like veggies, and tossed them into each bowl. A different sauce went into Lenna's bowl, while mine got something small and chopped fine sprinkled over top.

“Here you go,” they said, setting the bowls in front of each of us.

A notification popped up asking for payment. I sent it over without a problem. More expensive than in the marketplace with Kabi’s people, but not bad.

Different utensils sat on the counter. Forks, spoons, tongs, knives, and pointed sticks.

I grabbed a fork to play it safe, along with a spoon.

First, I sipped the broth.

A soft heat smacked me in the face, followed by salt, then rounding out to a perfect broth. Next, the noodles, which were perfectly done. The meat was something I hadn’t tasted before, but it reminded me of the Paras, and was delicious.

I forced myself to slow down twice when I realized I’d started rushing.

Lenna spoke up, almost making me jump. “This is really good. The eggs are soft, but tasty.”

“I prepared them yesterday,” the cat person said. “They are from a planet with small lizards. They lay twenty at a time. The lizards taste horrible, but the eggs are amazing.”

“And the meat?” she asked.

“From a massive creature that moves very slowly. It eats everything in its way. No intelligence, but they are farmed. It's common to find it in restaurants. Though, this part of town is mostly aimed at carnivores. There are other areas for those who don’t eat meat.”

The others sitting at the booth finished and left without saying anything. They were short, lizard-like creatures who ate with knives and held the bowls up to their faces.

I finished my bowl faster than I wanted, but I didn’t feel like I needed more.

“That was amazing,” I said, leaning back slightly. I would lick my bowl if it wasn’t rude, but didn’t know what manners required in this place. “Can I get two more servings of that, including the bowls?”

“You want travel food?” they asked.

“If possible?” I wanted some food in my inventory.

“I have something better,” they said, then vanished down an archway at the back of the shop.

“This is really good,” said Lenna, with half her bowl left. “I feel bad, but I don’t think I can finish it.”

“Next time, remind me to give you half a serving,” they said, as they appeared again. “It costs less, and I don’t like food waste.”

They set a gourd-like container in front of me. Two of them, in fact. “These are enchanted to stay good until opened.”

The price popped up, and I gladly paid it.

I added them to my inventory.

Lenna’s bowl was whisked away before she could stop them. Then she received a cup with a lid on it.

“Here you go. Make sure to finish it today.”

“Oh, I will. It was just so filling,” added Lenna.

“It’s hard to figure out servings for all the beings that visit, but I will remember, half serving for you, and double for you.” They stared at both of us for a moment, then shook their head. “Normally, I’m pretty good at figuring out what each wants, but those without fur are harder.”

“Thank you for the meal!” I said, slipping off the stool

Lenna followed, the book still clutched in her hands. “I’ll eat the rest later!” she promised.

“That was epic,” I said, as I paused after heading back to the corner we had turned to get there. “Now, I need to find Strange.”

“So, what actually happened, since it doesn’t seem like you are in a huge rush?” asked Lenna, putting the book into her inventory.

“You want the entire story?”

She glared at me.

“Okay, so… Strange got a delivery quest from Noseen, to bring three goons to me. They were the bad guys, like from the attack on Kabi’s world,” I explained.

She nodded.

“I couldn’t take them, I was way underleveled. Strange helped me escape, but stayed behind cause the quest didn’t complete for some reason. No clue what happened.”

“Did you come into contact with them?” she asked, as we walked along.

“I only saw two of them…” That might be it, I realized. I shook my head. “Still, he is way under-leveled compared to them.”

“Strange knows what he’s doing. I bet he's having fun right now.”

I poked at the bond and, yes, Strange was excited. The bond trembled though, and suddenly he was much farther away. I stumbled.

“I think they just used another portal.” I huffed, and picked up the pace.

“But, is he okay?” she asked again, this time with a knowing look.

“Yes, he's fine. Excited even…”

She chuckled. “He loves exploring new places, and who knows how many worlds they crossed?”

She wasn’t wrong, but I didn’t say anything as we continued down the sidewalk. The buildings changed every block. Some were metal, some wood, some short, some long. There wasn’t any planning from one to the next as far as I could tell.

One we passed even had steps to go down to enter.

The sidewalks became more crowded as we approached what had to be another massive plaza with portals. The link to Strange broke off in two directions here. The stronger one went to the plaza, while another continued off to the left.

I headed to the plaza.

Signs pointed in various directions, but we followed the flow of people going into one of the smaller portals. People exited it on the left, while we entered on the right.

I blinked on the other side, since we were still in the megacity as far as I could tell.

“This place must be massive,” I muttered, glancing around, though I didn’t dare stop moving with the stream of people moving out of the area.

I followed the connection between the two of us down a couple of blocks, and the number of beings on the lower sidewalks lessened.

“This is way too many people,” said Lenna. “Like, how do people find their way around, besides the maps…?”

“I bet people stick close to their neighborhood. Think of the city as a massive forest. Everyone has their place, but stays in the same several blocks. There is everything you need close by.” At least, I assumed it was that way.

I turned down a street that was huge, yet there were less tall buildings in this direction. The city actually included open space, with grass. Some smaller trees dotted the air, along with a few fountains. Music echoed from somewhere.

The bond led directly across the massive park, to something on the other side.

“This is nice,” said Lenna. She was focused on the smaller trees.

I walked onto the grass, and took a breath of fresh air. The mana made me feel like I’d had a cup of coffee. “Woah.”

“Woah, indeed,” she said, as we crossed the massive lawn. Trees and bushes divided the park into different sections, but we didn’t linger. “I feel like if we pause, we’ll be here all day.”

“Or longer,” I added, seeing some complex game with a ball being played by several different species. When my people made it here, this would blow their minds. I couldn’t wait to see John speeding through the air on his hoverbike.

My father would hate it. Too many people, and way too like Earth.

Benny wanted open spaces, and dirt to grow things in. He’d appreciate the manicured lawns, but not want to stay.

I liked the noodles, but wandering the city wasn’t going to help me gain levels, or figure out what was happening with Strange.

By the time we made it to the other side of the park, Lenna took out her leftovers and started sipping on them.

On the other side, across a few streets, stood another smaller plaza with portals. Yet, the bond with Strange didn’t pull me in that direction. It bent down another couple of streets, then turned down a smaller street like the one we’d used when we’d arrived.

An archway led to smaller courtyards, with only one portal in each.

Finally, we reached the single one that the connection led to. Lenna finished her noodles, then nodded at me.

We both stepped inside the courtyard together.

“Warning, this plant has some mana fluctuations," whispered a quiet voice. “And the Great Ones are watching it.”

My head snapped to the small creature, near the corner of the area, covered in dirt and blood. “Fluctuating mana?”

[Jenilla, Caretaker, Protected.]

“Dangerous for those not used to such things, the fluctuations are slowly degrading the planet.” The small thing didn’t move from the shadows.

“Will Lenna be safe?” I asked, thinking of the mana well.

“The planet is within her abilities,” replied Jenilla. “Why do you enter this world?”

My thoughts went to Strange, then a flash of a mountain top. “I need to find Strange, my bonded… and fulfill a promise I made.”

It stared at me with gaint eyes. “Worthy goal, but dangerous.”

“Dangerous doesn’t bother me,” I glanced at Lenna, who stood frozen. “What the…”

“Good luck, Devourer. May you find what you seek.”

By the time I glanced back again, the Caretaker was gone, and a shiver ran down my spine.
