Someone fetched them within a few minutes and I took my time looking at them. Chien was right, they were primitive. It had been a long, long time since I'd done much with this kind of thing but seemingly a lifetime ago my father had been a stone-knapper, the one for our village. That eye in mind I turned each over in my hand, looking at the details.

There were clear differences, these had been broken off of a larger stone, probably with a blunt stone, not an antler, and the edges were much less refined. They were left raw, perhaps because the person who'd made them didn't know how to clean them up. Then there was the place where it was to be connected, ground down, maybe with the edge of a harder rock, but not broken cleanly or evenly. These were truly early stone age tools.

In those moments I almost saw him again. My father, sitting on a log, deerskin draped over his leg as he worked, what good times, what simple times, times I missed.

“Boss, you okay?” I heard.

“Hmm?” I looked up, and felt the small tear on my face. “Oh yes, sorry, old memories.”

That sort of thing was rare, but it happened. Old memories didn't fade, and I could play through them whenever I wanted. I could see the last time I saw my parents, I could hear their voices. It still hurt, somewhere deep down, somewhere that didn't fully heal. It hurt there, every day a fresh pain on an old wound, but we grew, we moved on, we saw more and did what we must.
