“I’m alright,” Veronica replied. “But who are you?”

The armored man dipped his head in a small, courteous bow. “Cyrus Hikingvale. A-rank adventurer. My father is the guildmaster of this branch.”

Veronica’s eyes shifted over him once more.

Hikingvale.

She didn’t recognize the name immediately, but that didn’t mean much. There were many influential adventurers throughout the kingdom, especially in larger cities like Maftem. Unlike nobles, their power was not always tied to land or bloodlines. Sometimes, it came from reputation, monster kills, completed commissions, or just raw combat prowess.

From the way the other adventurers watched Cyrus, the Hikingvale name carried weight here.

Being the guildmaster’s son certainly helped, but she doubted that was the only reason. The aura he had displayed earlier was enough to earn respect on its own. And judging by the ease with which he had appeared and handled the situation, he was likely involved in guild affairs despite introducing himself as a regular adventurer. Entwined, but with no formal office. Hence the introduction.

“Veronica Everwells,” she said. “E-rank.”

“Miss Everwells, then.” Cyrus straightened. “I apologize for the conduct you had to endure inside our guild. People like him do not represent us.”

“It’s fine,” Veronica said, glancing toward the door where the guards had dragged the unconscious brute away. “I won’t blame the guild for one idiot. Especially since you stepped in personally.”

“I’m glad to hear that.”

His gaze briefly passed over the torn commission parchment still in her hand, then returned to her face. If he found it strange that an E-rank adventurer had thrown a C-rank swordsman across the guild hall with a single spell, then he did well to hide it.

“Though I am curious,” Veronica said after a pause. “How did you know what happened so quickly?”

Cyrus turned slightly, looking past her toward the clerk counters. “The clerk informed me.”

Veronica followed his gaze.

The woman behind the counter was still dealing with the two adventurers from before. Her eyes glowed faintly with a pale blue light, thin lines of magic circling her pupils as she held up one of the damaged goblin ears between two fingers.

A revelation mage.

That explained it.

The clerk noticed Veronica looking and gave her a quick wink before immediately turning her attention back to the two adventurers in front of her.

“For the last time,” the clerk said, her smile vanishing as she faced them again, “I cannot confirm a full goblin kill from half an ear and what appears to be a burned strip of leather.”

“That’s clearly an ear!” one of the adventurers protested.

“It is disgusting, and I can’t accept it no matter how much you try to dirty it or burn goblin blood into it.”

Veronica almost smiled.

So the guild had revelation mages watching the counters. Perhaps not everywhere, and perhaps not all the time, but enough that anyone causing trouble directly in front of the clerks was an idiot.

Then again, that man had already proven himself one.

“No wonder you understood so quickly,” Veronica said.

Cyrus nodded. “We do what we can to keep the guild orderly. Unfortunately, with auction season bringing in more adventurers than usual, some believe they can behave however they like.”

“Clearly.”

His eyes dropped once more to the parchment in her hand. “You were registering that commission?”

“Yes. I’ll be taking it with my party.”

“Silverwolves.” Cyrus glanced at the request. “One of the more troublesome E-rank commissions. People underestimate them because of the rank, but pack beasts are dangerous when handled carelessly. Six wolves attacking together are far more dangerous than six wolves attacking separately.”

Veronica remained silent.

She already knew. But she recognized the warning for what it was. If she truly were an ordinary E-rank adventurer, it would have been sound advice.

Cyrus’s eyes briefly flicked toward the shattered table behind them before returning to her.

“Though,” he added with a faint smile, “considering what you demonstrated just now, I suspect you’ll manage well enough.”

Veronica gave a small nod.

“I should return to business,” Cyrus said after a moment. “But if you require anything from the guild, you may ask for me. Consider it a small apology for today’s troubles.”

Veronica raised a brow. “You’re granting me a favor?”

“Nothing major,” Cyrus said quickly, his eyes flattening. “I won’t bend guild rules for anyone. But if you need to expedite a payment, or if you believe your party is ready for a commission above your current rank and require authorization, I can usually arrange such things.”

Veronica hummed, nodding her head ever so slowly. “Then I’ll keep that in mind.”

Cyrus smiled faintly. “Good. I hope your hunt goes well, Miss Everwells.”

With that, he gave her one last polite nod and headed back toward the stairs. The adventurers nearby quickly made way for him as he passed.

Veronica watched him go.

At minimum, he was equivalent to a seventh-tier aura-user. Whether he had reached the eighth was harder to determine from such a short display. His control was precise; he had compressed his aura cleanly, restraining two targets without crushing the surrounding adventurers.

It was impressive. Perhaps she would make a connection with him later on. But for now, it wasn’t too important to dwell on.

The line finally moved.

After several more minutes of arguing, the two adventurers at the counter accepted half payment for their damaged goblin ears and left with bitter expressions. One muttered something under his breath, only to go silent when the clerk’s glowing eyes shifted toward him again.

Veronica stepped up to the counter.

The clerk smiled. “Good afternoon. My name is Emma. Registering a commission?”

“Yes.” Veronica placed the torn silverwolf request on the counter. “And thank you for your help earlier.”

Emma waved a hand. “No problem. Us girls have to look out for each other.”

Veronica glanced toward the door. “Does that happen often?”

“Inside the guild? Not usually. Most men with that sort of personality have enough sense to keep their hands to themselves when they’re standing ten steps away from a revelation mage.” Emma sighed, then began smoothing out the torn commission parchment. “Outside the guild is another matter. Harder for me to help there.”

“I see.”

“I wasn’t expecting Cyrus to come down at that exact moment either,” she added. “Good timing, really. If the guards had handled it, there would have been a lot more shouting and paperwork.”

“You make it sound like paperwork is to be feared more than a brawl breaking out inside your guild.”

Emma placed her palm against the side of her cheek, sighing. “Because it is. You can throw out the trash, but documenting it is a whole other inconvenience.”

Veronica couldn’t argue with that.

The clerk examined the request, then reached beneath the counter and pulled out a thin registration ledger. “Silverwolf Pack Extermination. E-rank. East rocky creek. Estimated six to eight targets. Two hundred vix per confirmed kill. Left ear required for each kill confirmation.”

Veronica nodded.

“Party size?”

“Three. Myself and two companions.”

“Ranks?”

“I’m E-rank. One of my companions is also registered. The other is not an adventurer.”

The clerk looked up. “Non-registered participants are allowed up to E-rank commissions, but the guild does not offer injury compensation to unregistered helpers. They also cannot claim completion records unless they register before submitting the commission.”

“That’s fine.”

“Names?”

“Veronica Everwells. Gwen Finch. Nasha Lowell. Nasha is the one already registered.”

The clerk wrote them down, then tapped the ledger with one finger.

“What is Nasha’s rank?”

“I’m… not sure, actually.”

“Hm. I see. Then just make sure she comes back with you once you are done with the commission so I can accurately update her records.”

A small guild stamp on the side glowed faintly, recording the commission acceptance into the branch’s archive.

“Expected completion window is three days,” the clerk said. “If the pack moves or someone else clears it before then, you will be notified when you return. If you fail to return within the window, the commission will be reposted.”

“Understood.”

“Any questions about the terrain?”

“None. I believe I learned enough from the commission to get a feel for the area.”

“Very well.” The clerk slid a small stamped slip across the counter. “Bring this back with the left ears once the commission is complete. If there are more than eight wolves, the guild will still pay per confirmed kill, provided the additional corpses belong to the same pack.”

Veronica took the slip. “Thank you.”

“Good luck, Miss Everwells.”

Veronica tucked the commission slip away and walked back through the crowded guild hall. A few people glanced at her as she passed. Some with curiosity, others with faint amusement. The rest simply memorized her face, as if making sure to stay cautious.

Not because she was strong. No, there were likely several people in there much, much stronger than her at the moment.

But with Cyrus stepping in to defend her, no one would dare mess with her.

For the time being, she was untouchable.

The moment she stepped out of the Adventurer’s Guild, the noise of Maftem washed over her once again. The sounds were characteristic of a bustling city. She stepped onto the sidewalk and began walking, pulling out her map to examine her next route.

Maftem had four mage towers, each with its own tower master.

Mage towers were places of research and gateways to the mage association. You could go there to buy reagents or potions. Spellbooks could also be bought there, along with magical trinkets and artifacts. Everyday items like a flamering, a ring that could start small flames, or lamps that could adjust their brightness were sold as well. Of course, anything more powerful required much stronger standing with the mage association. No one could walk in and simply buy a teleportation scroll.

After scanning her map, she decided the mage tower east of the city was the best place to go. It was quite a distance away, so she waited on the sidewalk for a passing carriage.

Her first two attempts at hailing a carriage ended with her stopping the wrong kind.

She had seen people do it before and assumed any carriage that stopped was meant for public transportation. Pay a fee, and they would take you somewhere in the city.

But no.

That was not the case.

A minor noble and a merchant’s carriage both stopped because they had spotted a young woman waving at them.

Because who wouldn’t?

On her third try, Veronica finally found a transport carriage.

“Oh my, you really must be new to the city,” the female coach driver said, chuckling behind her hand.

Veronica sat beside her at the front of the coach, her face lightly red from embarrassment.

“Don’t feel too bad. If no one’s told you before, then it’s an honest mistake,” the woman said. “Just for your know-how, public transports have these markings here.”

She turned around and tapped the top of the wagon cabin. At the front sat an insignia: a boat with a paddle beneath it.

“This symbol was used for ferrymen who took people across the water. But now it’s used for any type of transport service.”

Veronica turned slightly, noticing it. “Ah… I see. Thank you. I’m not completely used to all of this.”

The woman smiled. “Don’t worry about it, dear. Name’s Hellen, by the way. Pleasure to meet you.”

She swiped her hand over the crystal at the front of the coach, and the wheels began to move.

The carriage was similar to the one they had used to traverse the Valley of Silence, but it was a much newer, more expensive model. It was slightly more compact and, likewise, used no horses to draw it. The coach had cooling and heating artifacts to regulate the temperature, along with a floral scent gem that wafted over them.

It was very pleasant.

“You sure you don’t want to sit in the cabin? You can lay down and take a rest if you need. It’ll take us about another twenty minutes to reach the eastern mage tower.”

“No, it’s fine. I’ll stay up here. It’s been a long time, so I’d like to see the city with my own eyes.”

Hellen smiled, though there was some slight unease in it.

Veronica wasn’t sure why.

[I believe it is customary for passengers to ride in the cabin, rather than sitting in the coach. It is unusual to sit with the rider. You may be impeding her personal space.]

Veronica opened her mouth for a moment, just slightly, but no words came out.

A few beats passed.

Then she turned to Hellen.

“Actually, I’ll sit in the cabin.”

Her twenty-minute drive to the eastern district was largely spent snacking on sweets from the cabin. From an assortment of chocolate cookies to honey-glazed rolls and exquisite frosted cakes. She only noticed that they had arrived when Hellen knocked on the cabin, getting her attention.

Veronica looked through the window, and there it was: the eastern Maftem mage tower.

The spire stood twelve stories tall, much grander than Ronswick’s five-story tower, but lower than Annesheim’s sixteen. She could see many mages in robes walking in and out, each carrying potions, books, papers, or leather bags of all sizes and shapes. It seemed business in Maftem was much greater than Ronswick when it came to mages and magical items.

Galland did mention they had grown more popular recently, taking a significant share of the market. However, it was impossible for them to reach this level of business in such a short time.

Veronica stepped out of the cabin and paid Hellen three hundred vix for the ride. It was quite expensive, but it turned out Hellen’s carriage was a luxury one. That was why there had been snacks and confections inside where she sat.

That surprised Veronica. She thought those were included no matter the service, like food served at an inn.

Did commoners really just sit in wagons, doing nothing but wait? What kind of service was that?

“Will you need a ride back?” Hellen asked.

“Ah, yes. Now that you mention it. Is it fine if you come back in about an hour?”

Hellen nodded. “Sure thing. I’ll stay around the area and come back in an hour. I’ll only wait five minutes, though. If you aren’t here, I’ll have to move on and get other clients.”

“That works for me. Thank you.”

Veronica gave a light wave as Hellen drove the carriage down the street. She then turned, gazing from the tip of the spire down to its base, where two large double doors stood open.

“Hopefully the tonic won’t be too expensive,” she murmured.

She took several steps and entered the mage tower.
