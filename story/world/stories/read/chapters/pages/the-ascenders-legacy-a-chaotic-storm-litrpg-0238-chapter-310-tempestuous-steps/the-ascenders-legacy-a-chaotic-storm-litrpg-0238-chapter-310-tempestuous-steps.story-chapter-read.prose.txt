The next morning found Aodhán staring at the ceiling—gray light streaming in to bathe his irises with the cold reminder that the night was over.

After Zarxan's departure the night before, he'd been unable to sleep, his nerves firing and mind racing with the consequences of the actions he would have to take today.

Trying to distract himself had been futile.

At first, he'd spent the early hours practicing his runes, and for a while it had worked—the focus of perfecting a rune arresting his full attention—until he'd mastered it, and like a dam breaking open, all those worries had come rushing back.

He'd tried to tune them out by learning another rune—YITHAR—the rune of amplification, but his mind had been disinterested, or perhaps too distracted to pay attention.

He'd dropped his practice note after that and pulled out Gorvak's sword to study its runic structure. But even that hadn't held his attention for long. And so he'd turned to his bed for the oblivion of sleep.

That had been more than six hours ago.

And yet, he hadn't slept a wink. His mind had refused to let him.

He'd spent the night tossing and turning, breaking down and rebuilding his determination to go through with this madness despite having settled his resolve the night before.

The irony wasn't lost on him.

Barely a year ago, he'd been termed rash and impulsive. And now, he feared that the only word he could use to describe himself was indecisive.

Just yesterday, he'd resolved to thrive. To go through with the war, to become so powerful the storm of conflict wouldn't drown him.

But now, after hours of thought, his mind was weary and his resolve brittle.

That was only because he knew what a war looked like. He had seen the consequences himself—the blood, the deaths, the sorrow, the pain. Even for victors, war took its toll. How many outer sect disciples would survive a war between sects? How many inner sect disciples? How many elders?

And even if they won, how much would they have lost first?

The sect indeed stood to gain a lot from the whole thing. Zarxan had explained it all to him yesterday, but the truth was that he had the power to put a stop to all of this.

If he left the sect right now, there would be no war. No death. Balance would be restored. And millions wouldn't have to lose their lives.

The ball was in his court. His actions today could change the future of Gordon forever.

And yet, Aodhán couldn't bring himself to just leave.

In only two days, this storm-drenched mountain felt more like home than any other place he'd ever considered home before. Here in the sect, he was surrounded and enveloped so intimately by his affinity that it felt like being hugged twenty-four seven. His sense of belonging—of family—had never been stronger.

Where would he even go that was better than here? Even in the outer sect, the essence density was so high he could feel it all around him. How much better would it be at the special sect? How much faster would he grow? The resources he would gain? The knowledge he could access? The rivals and friends he would make? The power he could gather.

There was so much he could gain here that he would never gain cultivating elsewhere.

But was that enough to justify a continental war with dozens of other sects seeking their downfall?

That was the last question Aodhán had asked himself, and sadly, even after several hours of deep thought, he had no answer to give. Eight months ago, he knew what answer he would have given. He would have been against it. He would have chosen to be the hero. To be selfless.

But now, he was conflicted.

And as the morning slowly brightened around him, he realized just how much this world had changed him. Where was the wide-eyed boy who'd believed solely in good and bad? Who'd so easily labeled wars as evil and stupid? Where was that boy now when he needed him?

Exhaling deeply, Aodhán draped an arm across his face to shield his eyes from the morning light, feeling mentally exhausted. The initiation trial would begin very soon, and he still had no answer to give.

He needed more time to think. Unfortunately, he had very little time to do so.

A few more minutes passed, but when he still had no response, he finally decided to get up. Grunting, he rolled out of bed and made his way to the bathroom for a shower, his breath hitching as the ice-cold water contacted his skin.

His aspect hummed in response, and with a single thought, the freezing shower turned scalding.

A deep exhale escaped his lips as his muscles relaxed instantly—steam rising off his skin in a cloud of warmth and pleasure. He spent far more time than he needed to within the downpour, shaping several droplets of water into massaging streams that kneaded like hands across his shoulders and back.

A soft groan escaped his lips as the tension slowly slid off him, liquid tendrils gently pressing against pressure points. The sensation was like nothing he'd ever felt before, and half an hour later, when he finally turned off the shower, Aodhán felt far more relaxed for the day ahead.

Squeezing water out of his hair, which had lengthened significantly, Aodhán stepped out of the shower and was soon dressed in what he considered an appropriately somber attire—black pants, a midnight cloak, and a cowl to cover his face and hair.

His gaze caught his reflection just before he pulled down the cowl, and his hands paused. He stared deep into his own eyes—twin pools of liquid gold ringed by bands of crimson and black—surprised at the amount of pain, strength, and tenacity he saw reflected in them.

Brennon and Baxter's deaths flashed across his mind, and he quickly looked away, feeling that pain intensify to the point of tears before slowly subsiding. Breath shuddering, he looked back at the mirror, eyes bright with unshed tears.

And in that moment, he suddenly knew the answer.

Unsurprisingly, it was a selfish one.

There was no escaping the war, not as long as he remained within the sect. But he couldn't bring himself to leave, not when he had no idea what awaited him on the outside without the sect's protection.

For the first time since his arrival in Aeflym, he was safe. He would be accepted and praised rather than hunted. For the first time, he wouldn't have to lie and lie just to hide his identity. He would be free to be who he was, and he wanted that more than ever.

But beyond even that was a hunger for power, not just for power's sake, but to reach a point where he could be forever and truly safe. And that point was the hundredth tier. Zarxan had said the world tattoo could no longer be taken from him then.

That freedom—that security—was what he wanted, and if a war would spark for it, then it was better to fight alongside thousands of friends, comrades, and family rather than be hunted alone.

And until he reached that point, Aodhán was most certainly not ready to leave.

The decision settled in his bones like stone—heavy and immovable. His chest tightened for a heartbeat, then released, and suddenly he could breathe again. The weight hadn’t vanished—the guilt still ate at him, but at least now he knew why he was doing this.

He knew what he stood to gain even though it was selfish.

Determination crystallized within him in that moment, and his gaze hardened. Whatever trials the elders had planned, he was ready. And whatever came after that, he would simply roll with it.

Raising his head high, he pulled the cowl off his face and whispered, "You are stronger than you know, Aodhán."

A mental resonance thrummed within him in agreement, and Aodhán smiled. "I see you finally grace us with your presence, Varéc. Care to explain what you've been doing all night?"

An image of a snoring dragon appeared in his mind, and Aodhán snorted. "Of course you were sleeping. Here I was, unable to sleep, and you were snoring away."

Varéc growled within his spirit, and Aodhán chuckled. "Well, thank you. I do look striking for someone so sleep-deprived. Are you ready for today?"

Varéc responded with a surge of determination, and Aodhán nodded. "Good. Because we are facing the world today, and I cannot do it alone."

He picked up Gorvak's sword from where he'd discarded it and shoved it into his spatial storage alongside his practice notes. He stepped out of his room a moment later, and though he would have preferred a solo walk to the initiation center, Aodhán waited, sensing Crixis's core as he paced nervously around his room.

Once. Twice. Thrice.

And then the door opened to reveal Crixis, nervous tension apparent in his features. That tension, however, turned to shock as his gaze landed on Aodhán, taking in the severity of his bloodline manifestation, mouth slightly agape.

"You're in the advanced class," he stated a moment later, nodding slowly as he gathered his thoughts. "I suspected as much with how easily you took down Gorvak, but…" His gaze strayed to the golden lines on Aodhán's neck, and he swallowed visibly. "That's an epic-ranked bloodline, at least."

Aodhán smiled, not bothering to correct him. "Good morning to you too, Crixis. Sleep well?"

"Not in the least. You?"

"Same."

Crixis snorted. "You don't look it at all." He glanced at the golden lines once more and muttered, "That's going to draw a lot of attention in the outer sect. Perhaps walking is a bad idea. I suggest we fly."

"Sure." Aodhán shrugged, and a few minutes later, they were zipping through the air on a construct of crackling electricity Crixis had created. Despite cutting through the air with remarkable speed, it still took them over two minutes to reach the initiation center—a round, cathedral-like building located at the base of a lightning-weathered mountain far larger than any other mountain in the region.

Aodhán raised his gaze in search of the mountain's peak, heart pounding at the sheer scale of it. The mountain was silver rather than black, its color stripped away by incessant lightning strikes, its peak lost somewhere within roiling clouds and rumbling thunder.

For a moment, he forgot to breathe—the sheer scale of the mountain pressing against his chest in a reminder of how small he really was—of how small everyone truly was in the face of true power.

A powerful aura emanated from the mountain—a pressure so tangible Aodhán could feel it in his bones. On one side of the mountain, steps were etched, rising until they were absolutely lost in the clouds.

Aodhán feared he already knew what the mountain was, but he asked regardless, heart thumping. "Is that the trial mountain?"

Crixis smiled. "Yes. It's called the Mountain of Tempest. That's where the promotional trial takes place every year."

"And everyone is expected to climb it to the peak?" Aodhán asked in disbelief, unable to fathom it, and Crixis laughed.

"Heavens, no! There are 1,501 steps on that mountain, and the pressure intensifies with each step." He frowned and added, "It's a spirit trial in a way because climbing it relies more on spiritual strength than any physical advancement."

They settled to the ground a few meters away from the initiation center—a grand building made from white marble—but Aodhán barely spared the building a glance, his gaze still fixed on the mountain.

"How are the steps divided?" he asked after a moment. "What step does one have to reach to make it out of the outer sect?"

Crixis frowned. "Step 249, I think. Anyone who makes it past that is promoted to the inner sect, which runs all the way to the 499th step."

Aodhán nodded, mentally doing the math. "500 to 749 makes you a core disciple, and 750 to the 1199th step makes you a special disciple."

"Exactly," Crixis nodded. "But from what I hear, making it past the 200th step alone is a chore, which is why outer sect members are so numerous."

Aodhán glanced back at the mountain, wondering what the 1200th step and above represented, and if it was possible to get that far. Before he could ask, however, someone else arrived, settling her storm platform beside them.

It was a girl with long, storm-gray hair, her core blazing with even more intensity compared to Crixis's despite her lower tier. She was dressed in what seemed to be a battle suit crafted from layered stormfiber, the material catching light in sharp, lightning-like fractures with every small movement.

She smiled when she noticed their attention and raised a finger to her forehead in greeting. "Storm bless you, brothers. I am Serapha Ghaltris. A pleasure to meet you."

Aodhán and Crixis mimicked the gesture, introducing themselves in turn.

"You're here for the initiation too?" Crixis asked, and Serapha nodded.

“I awakened last week. It's time to join." Her gaze flicked to Aodhán and lingered. "Perhaps I should have given myself a bit more time."

Aodhán raised an eyebrow at her, and she ignored his expression to fix her gaze on the mountain. "Impressive, isn't it? My parents used to bring me here every year, and I still haven't gotten over its grandeur."

"Me neither," Crixis chuckled, glancing at her. "I didn't realize you were from the shelter."

"My parents are elders," Serapha stated with a smile. "I lived with an unawakened sister in the North shelter." Her smile turned sad, and she shook her head. "I'm going to miss her."

"You can always visit," Aodhán spoke, inserting himself into the conversation, and she turned to glance at him.

"Are you from the shelter too?"

"No," Aodhán responded without elaborating.

Serapha glanced curiously at Crixis, but before she could ask any more questions, a bell tolled from the cathedral as its massive doors opened. They began walking toward it, feeling the subtle weight of the mountain's aura press down on them with every step they took.

They arrived within the cathedral a minute later, and there they found over a dozen figures, all of whom were outer sect members, as indicated by their bronze lapels, save for two—a man and woman draped in obsidian robes.

"Those two are elders," Serapha pointed out discreetly, even as an outer sect disciple welcomed them, saying, "Welcome, soon-to-be-comrades. Your initiation will begin shortly. Please be patient."

"How many of us are being initiated today?" Crixis asked.

"Just one shy of a dozen," the disciple replied, gesturing to an array of seats. "You can sit while you wait."

The disciple had barely finished speaking when Aodhán sensed three blazing cores suddenly enter his range. They arrived at the cathedral just as Aodhán took his seat, and he looked up to see them come in a moment later—all girls, and judging by their uncanny resemblance, they were triplets.

Their cores blazed with an intensity even brighter than Serapha's—all three at the 11th tier of advancement. However, there was one among them whose core blazed even brighter than the rest, shining with an intensity that set her apart.

She wasn't just stronger than her sisters; she also had more willpower than anyone at her tier had any right to have, marking herself as an inheritor.

Aodhán's gaze widened slightly in surprise, then intrigue, as the triplets came to sit beside them. They picked a row of seats near Serapha and promptly began whispering to themselves.

Aodhán had never seen triplets in AEFLYM before, let alone ones with the same affinity. And so, while they whispered amongst themselves, Aodhán observed them—not with his eyes, but with core sense—studying the interaction between their cores in fascination.

Time passed in that manner as more initiates trooped in, rushing to take seats. One thing of note was that they were all between the 10th and 15th tiers. No one was lower or higher, except him.

And because of that, he drew attention, not only from the other newbies but from the elders too.

As soon as the last initiate arrived, placing their total number at eleven, the elders came to address them.

“Welcome to your initiation, newbies," one of the elders took the stage, a bright smile on her face. "I am Elder Rita, and I know you're all expecting a simple trial today. However, I fear we've been ordered to discard the usual spirit and combat tests in favor of something more challenging."

Crixis tensed beside him, small arcs of electricity flickering across his fingers, but Aodhán feared he already knew what was going on and was therefore not too surprised when the elder announced, "Today's trial will take place on the Mountain of Tempest."

Shocked gasps erupted from the gathered initiates, but Aodhán just shook his head.

"Of course," he sighed. "They were skipping right to the hardest part."
