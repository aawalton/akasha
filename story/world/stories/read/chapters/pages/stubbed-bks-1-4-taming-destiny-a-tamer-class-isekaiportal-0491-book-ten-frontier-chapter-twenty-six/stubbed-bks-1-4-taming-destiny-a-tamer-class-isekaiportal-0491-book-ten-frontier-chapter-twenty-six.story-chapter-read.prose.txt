By the seventh mark, Gulnis is already ready to leave. Punctuality has served him well for his whole career – and this meeting with two Great Heirs is set to be one of the most important.

Perhaps I’ll have completed the set before the end of this competition, Gulnis muses idly to himself while he combs perfumed oil through his hair to tame the wayward strands. I’ve met with more than half of them now.

As a son of a merchant, trade is in his blood. Yet even his father, who started the Mithril Trading Caravan, had never dreamed it might become the trusted company of Great Houses.

Gulnis reminds himself of that, lets his ambition swell within him, and then leashes it. Heir Titabend and Heir Forestheart have proven themselves experienced players of the game – that fills him with a different kind of hunger.

A quiet knock falls on his cabin door.

“Come in,” he calls.

Oisella slips inside and gives him a nod. She’s closely followed by Rithan.

“Ah, you’re ready. Good.” Gulnis scrutinises their appearance. Neat hair, clean and colourful robes. Satisfactory. The black circles under Rithan’s face are less so. Gulnis narrows his eyes at the man.

“You weren’t spending all night tupping that cabin lad again, were you?”

“I wasn’t,” Rithan objects. Gulnis gives him a pointed look, not believing him. “Not the cabin lad,” he admits.

“Come here,” Gulnis orders his younger cousin, reaching for his face paints. It’s not the first time he’s needed to use them to conceal signs of tiredness – it won’t be the last, either. If only his father hadn’t promised his brother to keep a junior partnership for his son. Gulnis would far rather have another like Oisella – someone who had worked her way up from the ground floor and was as canny as Gulnis himself. “Next time, sleep before an important negotiation. And Prosperity help you if you mess this up.”

“I won’t. Anyway, you should be grateful,” Rithan tells him sulkily as Gulnis uses the skin tone concealer. “I was finding out information!”

Gulnis pauses, his eyes narrowing.

“Information? From who? How?”

Rithan gives him a sly look.

“Wouldn’t you like to know?” he teases. Gulnis raises a threatening hand. Rithan isn’t necessary for the negotiation. If something…unfortunate befalls him between now and breakfast, Gulnis is sure he can make excuses. “Fine, fine,” Rithan hurries to reply, eyeing his hand nervously. “You always complain about my hunger for tupping, but this time it’s come in handy.” Gulnis just looks impatient. “You know those guards down on the quayside?”

Gulnis closes his eyes, dreading what’s coming next.

“Don’t tell me…”

“What? I just approached three taking a break. One was amenable to…” that sly look is back, “sharing secrets between the sheets. Not that there were any sheets involved.” He pauses, waiting as if for praise.

Gulnis exchanges a look of exasperation with Oisella. This boy…. Still, if it’s got them some useful information, that’s more than his promiscuous tupping usually brings.

“If you don’t want to be scrubbing the decks with the slaves until we get back to Goldport, you’ll share whatever useful information you got – now,” Gulnis threatens.

“Fine,” Rithan sighs with a pout that shows his age more clearly than anything else. “This territory has been through a great deal since they arrived – and during the journey, actually. Though I’m not sure I believe the story of encountering a Beast Emperor.” Rithan looks thoughtful. “He might have been trying to make up for spilling first.” Gulnis makes an impatient noise. “Anway, yeah, they've had attacks from three Tier fours – and killed two of them. They got caught in the same manastorm we did, and have had issues with illness and limited food.”

Gulnis strokes his short beard.

“Yet he purports to have the solution to growing food safely here,” he muses calculatingly. And it would be easy enough to determine if he was false – easy enough that only a fool would try to pass a failing method for a true one.

With that kind of information, he should be able to push for a demonstration – which might allow him to glean information for free. Or he can argue down the price due to it being untested.

Gulnis regards his young cousin thoughtfully. “For once, your loose robes have come in useful.” Rithan smiles proudly. “You’re still going to scrub the decks for the next two days.”

Rithan’s smile drops and he pouts.

“Why?!”

“Because you didn’t inform me ahead of time about your intentions. I would have directed you to ask specific questions that would have potentially rendered more useful information for me.”

With Rithan muttering angrily in the background, Gulnis and Oisella launch into a strategy discussion. They have made a business out of always getting a good deal in whatever trade agreement they have made, and they aren’t planning on changing that now.

By half past the seventh mark, the three merchants have been notified that the escort is waiting and emerge onto deck. Gulnis speaks quietly with his guards, organising three to come with them. Hopefully the local lords will not take offence. A guard for each of them is not unreasonable, especially in a dangerous place like this.

Gulnis swallows as he approaches the delicate bridge made of ice that still spans the distance between the ship and the quayside. It’s a marvellous creation, and that it hasn’t melted overnight – and in the absence of its creator, at that – is testament to just how much magic and skill were put into it. As is to be expected of a Great House heir. However, it’s still partly see-through. And the water beneath does not look welcoming.

Setting a careful foot on the bridge, Gulnis gains more confidence as he feels its solidity.

Within a few flames later, he is approaching the unit of eight Classers who have been deployed to escort him and his party. The leader, a large and muscular man about a head taller than Gulnis and with a steely gaze complimented by their grey colour, steps forward.

“Ser Gulnis?” he asks, his baritone voice holding a clear note of command.

“That’s me,” Gulnis replies, unintimidated. He has met more powerful people.

“I am Captain Thorne. I am charged with your safety. Your guards are unnecessary.”

Gulnis smiles politely at him. There is no need to be rude – one catches more kelnaps with sugar water than with vinegar.

“I’m sure that you and your men are indeed accomplished. However, it would give me peace of mind to have some familiar faces with us. Perhaps your lord can tell us himself if he objects?”

Captain Thorne shifts uneasily, but nods after a moment.

“Very well. I will leave it as my lord’s decision.” Gulnis’ smile widens. Passing the responsibility on is a tried and tested way of avoiding both conflict and censure.

They soon set off, all fourteen of them, heading towards a hill that is only a short distance away from the bay. They follow a trail that is more worn down by passage than intentionally created. Gulnis eyes the rugged road with displeasure. He hopes that the lord will be willing to create something rather more hard-wearing if he truly wants to engage in trade – when it rains, parts of this trail no doubt become a quagmire.

And then they crest a small rise and Gulnis feels his breath catch at the sight ahead of him.

Wheat, fields of it. Small fields, yes, but three of them. Golden, ready for harvest. Indeed, there are figures working on their outskirts, scythes slicing easily even through the sturdy stalks. Something common in Moriax but here?

“Gulnis…” Oisella says with an urgent tone in her voice.

“I know,” he replies with a warning note and a quick glance at the local guards around them. She presses her lips together. Rithan’s expression is more obviously marvelling, but, for all his faults, the boy is pretty good at recognising when not to give away an advantage.

They walk up the wider, paved road up to the gate now obvious in the side of the hill. The slope seems a more deliberate construction than the trail they’ve been following until now. Gulnis suspects that Earth Mages have been involved in its creation – it’s far too even, and the drop down to the farming area on the other side doesn’t look natural. Plenty of Earth Mages with them, he notes. Perhaps it isn’t so impossible that they have found a way to grow wheat. Though I do wonder what is different between them and Greenfield – they also have a good deal of Plant and Earth Mages.

After that first moment of gawking, he does his best to keep an eye on the wheat fields without being too obvious about it. Their harvest goes quickly, and when they’re done, other people – Mages, Gulnis would guess – move in and start doing something to the land.

If he were closer, he might be able to gather more information. As it is, he learns enough just to feel more frustrated.

That they’ve successfully grown wheat doesn’t mean that it’s edible, he reminds himself. The mages in Goldport, Greenfield and New Forestedge have managed to grow wheat – and other vegetation. The issue is when it comes to eating it. Something about the process of growing seems to turn edible plants into poison.

Would the lord order his people to keep growing fields of it if it were not edible? Gulnis pulls up his storage space and checks that he has pills to clear out all sorts of poison – New Forestedge had indicated that they helped with the poisoning from plants, but evacuated one’s bowels in the process. Gulnis hopes not to need to use them, but would prefer to be prepared.

As they approach the settlement gate, an aura suddenly falls upon them, a sensation of being ants beneath the gaze of an immense predator.

Gulnis’ body freezes without his mind’s permission, and his bladder and bowel quiver, clearly wondering whether to empty themselves to improve his chances of escaping.

Around him, the three guards retained by his company grab for their weapons, searching about for the source of the threat. Angry betrayal sweeps through Gulnis as he realises that the local Warriors don’t seem at all concerned. Have we been lured into a trap?

It’s short-sighted, but not beyond the realms of possibility that the local lords would see the riches his vessel and its contents offered, and decide that they would rather take it than pay for it. Gulnis forces his hand to move, reaching into a hidden pocket on the inside of his robes and clutching the crystal within it. If he crushes it, his lady will be notified. Reinforcements are highly unlikely to come in time to save his life, but he can trust that he will be avenged.

“Put your weapons away,” Captain Thorne warns them, his voice tight. Gulnis narrows his eyes at the man. He’s not as nonchalant as the merchant had originally thought him. Though his hand isn’t on the sword at his side, it’s clenched and shaking slightly with tension.

It’s not a trap?

Forcing his body to cooperate, he follows Thorne’s gaze toward the forest’s edge. For a moment, there’s nothing but trees. And then, a moment later, a massive predator appears.

Gulnis swallows even as he hears Rithan swear. Since two of their guards do the same, Gulnis decides not to reprimand him. The beast has teeth as long as any of his guards’ swords, and a white gaze that promises death even at this distance.

“Captain?” Gulnis asks, his voice full of fearful tension as the beast starts ambling towards them.

“He shouldn’t attack us, but we’ll wait here for a few flames until he clears away,” Thorne replies, his eyes fixed on the predator.

Gulnis exchanges tense glances with Oisella and his own guard – ‘Shouldn’t’ isn’t exactly reassuring. He releases the crystal and palms a defensive device that, if needed, will hopefully create enough of a distraction to let them escape, but holds it in a shaking hand for now.

The beast comes closer and Gulnis gulps as his Appraisal gives him a sense of just how dangerous the creature is. He’d thought it was Tier three, which would be bad enough, but it’s not – it’s even more powerful.

“Captain,” he starts with a shaking voice. What is it about this settlement and Tier fours? “Why is a Beast King approaching your settlement? And why isn’t anyone doing anything about it?”
