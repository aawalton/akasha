Callie ran home as quickly as she could, clutching her arm. She was trying desperately to keep her growing magic contained. But it felt that with each new petal, more and more energy swelled within it. As if each time, a larger piece of some invisible dam was broken off, letting more water through.

Temper your heart, temper your heart!

"Callie?" Callen noticed her rushing into the house and hurried after her. "Is everything alright?"

Dad, who had been hustling in the kitchen, looked at her with concern. "Callie?"

"A-Another p-petal… I don't think I.. I can hold it for long…" Callie breathed deeply, trying to keep her magic calm.

But it was clear that she wouldn't be able to contain it, so she decided that if her magic would burst out, it should burst out in a safe form. Not fire or ice or anything else destructive. It would be better if it were flowers. Just like that time with the thunderfoxes.

While Callie was spooked by the sudden burst of energy, it wasn't difficult to let happiness dominate her feelings. Villagers had accepted the frost caterpillars even though they were arcane beasts. They were praising them and even going out of their way to make sure they would have the best places to pupate.

So when some of Callie's magic burst out, it came as waves of warmth. Like a warm spring breeze, carrying the scent of freshly bloomed flowers. She held back as much energy as she could, but when Callie opened her eyes… The whole living room and kitchen have been turned into a small meadow.

The floor got covered in a thick layer of moss with wildflowers sticking out in clusters. The kitchen table had sprouted several branches with green leaves. Dad and Callen were both covered in a carpet of small blue flowers. They both looked at Callie in shock.

"I-I'm sorry…" she muttered.

Dad blinked several times. "At least this didn't happen outside…"

"This will be hard to clean up…" Callen added, pulling some of the flowers off his sleeves.

"Sorry, sorry," Callie muttered again, pulling a patch of moss off the floor, worried she might have damaged it with her magic. Thankfully, it came off cleanly.

"How did this happen?" Callen asked, crouching down and pulling patches of moss too. "I wonder if we can use this in the stables as bedding."

Dad pulled the flowers off himself, then reached down for the moss. "It is nice and soft. Gale might appreciate a bit of extra warmth. But more importantly, what happened, Callie?"

"It happened when everyone was putting frost caterpillars in safe places to pupate. One of them thanked me, and I think another petal grew," Callie said, and folded up her sleeve.

Dad and Callen came closer. The mark had indeed grown the fifth petal, at this point looking almost like a full flower with just one petal missing. It was still glowing, although the magic was calming down now.

"Callie, is everything okay?" Mom came running into the house and paused, seeing all the moss.

She gaped at the moss, gaped at Callie's mark, then hurriedly slammed the door shut. "For heaven's sake, how did this happen?"

"It was the frost caterpillars," Dad said with a sigh. "I really thought that the bugs wouldn't trigger anything like this. Things were fine for the longest time…"

Mom sighed heavily. "At least nobody saw it… But your magic is undoubtedly growing stronger. Callie, you have to avoid any new arcane beast at all costs. Oh, and we should tell the chief about this. After we clean this up…"

The family looked around the living room and exchanged a glance. This would take a while.

After every bit of moss was pulled away, flowers gathered, and any rogue branches cut off, the family could breathe easily for a moment. Callen took the moss to the stables. The branches would be used as kindling, their leaves fed to the chickens. The birds were delighted to have a little bit of greenery in the middle of winter.

The problem was the flowers. There were quite a lot of them, but they weren't the kind you could feed to chickens. Most of them were the blue frey plants, which Granny Ethel would use in poultices to help with broken bones. So it was agreed that the flowers would be taken to Granny Ethel, while Mom would go and explain that Callie had another petal appear on her mark.

Thankfully, the weather wasn't too bad anymore. It was cold and a bit windy, but nowhere near as bad as during the blizzard. Callie could walk on her own by following Callen. However, as they walked towards the healer's hut, she couldn't help but feel dejected.

"What is it?" Callen asked, likely noticing her sullen mood.

"I-It's nothing…"

"Is it about what happened at home?"

Callie nodded. "It… It just feels that every time I understand magic a little better, every time I get it under control, it grows stronger and I have to start everything from scratch…"

"Hey, that's not true! You're definitely improving. We just have to make sure you don't get overwhelmed. You should probably avoid any new arcane beasts for a while," Callen said.

Callie nodded. She knew that. She understood that. But telling her to stay away from the new arcane beasts… It was like telling a starving man not to eat. Callie wanted to know everything she could about arcane beasts.

But then again… I don't think any other arcane beasts are living nearby. Except the mysterious swamp king, but I certainly wouldn't go and seek it out on purpose. It lives in the Deep Forest, and it is forbidden to go there. I don't even want to go there!

Callie felt fully convinced of this fact, so she managed to keep walking with her head high.

When Granny Ethel saw all the frey plants Callie and Callen had brought, the elderly woman basically danced in delight.

"You're a lifesaver, Callie. I had run out of these a while ago. They only grow during late autumn, as you likely remember, but since winter came early this year, they never had a chance to bloom." Granny Ethel took the bundle of flowers, taking them into her medicine room.

"But how come I can grow these random flowers out of seemingly nowhere. It's like there were seeds hidden in our floorboards. And why did I grow moss? Why not grass or blueberries?" Callie pondered.

"I'm afraid I don't know the answer to that. Frankly, I can't believe you managed to just grow plants out of nothing. This is the kind of magic no amount of training could ever achieve. Something that only dragon magic can create."

Callie felt both proud and terrified by it. Hiding her magic would no doubt become even more difficult. Would she even be able to hide? Maybe she should just stay in her room so nobody ever—

"Whatever you're thinking, stop!" Callen said, gently flicking Callie's forehead.

She clutched on to it and glared at her brother incredulously. "What was that for?"

"You were worrying your little head over something silly again. Weren't you? Don't worry, everything will be okay," he said with a smile. "Your big brother will always protect you."

"But what if more villagers find out? What if they tell the dragonbloods?" Callie asked.

Granny Ethel hobbled over to Callie and gently tapped her head. "Do you really think that somebody would sell you out to the dragonbloods the moment they discovered the truth?"

"I mean… yes? Maybe?" Callie could certainly imagine some of the villagers doing it. Like Gerda's and Aurelia's moms, for example.

"Now, now, no need to be so critical. Especially after what happened during the mana blizzard. Everyone is grateful for what you've done. Besides, we don't sell out our own, especially not to dragonbloods," Granny Ethel soothed. "But obviously, it would be better if we could keep this secret as long as possible. Especially from outsiders."

"Hey, not all outsiders are bad," Callen chimed.

"Perhaps not, but it's best we stick to the people we know. Now, why don't you head back home? I'd love it if you and Tristan could come by tomorrow and help me deal with these flowers."

Callie nodded, and they headed home. She wondered if it would really be fine if some of the villagers discovered her magic. The chief knew, and he was rather accepting of her power. But then again, she couldn't hope that everyone would react the same way.

Even if they wouldn't sell her out to dragonbloods, it could still cause a lot of problems within the village. Callie promised herself that she would continue practicing her magic as much as she could to better control it.

Despite the appearance of a new petal, a month passed by peacefully for Callie and her family. The time was cold, but there were no more mana blizzards, so everyone was managing to get by.

While food was sparse, nobody was outright starving, especially not Callie's family. Mom would go out to hunt for boar or deer, while Callie would continue to secretly grow carrots and even potatoes.

The first few days after gaining the new petal were by far the most difficult. She was overgrowing the carrots all the time, her magic just bursting out of her like a flood. It took a while for her to manage to gain control of the energy and let it out as a small trickle instead.

Once she got the hang of the carrots again, she tried potatoes. They turned out to be easier to grow than carrots. If she accidentally released too much magic, the potatoes would come out smaller, but they would still be good to eat or used for growing more potatoes.

If she got the stream of magic right, then she could grow enough in one good to make a whole dinner for their family. They could even trade some of their vegetables away to the neighbors to get some milk and cheese. So they were actually doing quite well this winter, despite it arriving early.

Today, Callie was given a bit of free time because of her steady improvement with magic. Yesterday, she had been called to the chief's house, where she showed how much she was able to control it. She lit a fire, grew a flower in a pot, and froze a bit of water in a bucket.

Ice wasn't working out well for Callie, whoever. It was all or nothing. Either she couldn't freeze stuff at all, or she froze the whole thing. The chief concluded the same thing, so he told her to hold off on ice magic for now. They didn't need more frozen stuff during winter.

After begging Tristan, she managed to convince him to come with her to the forest. She still wasn't allowed to wander around alone.

"Do we have to, carrothead? We see them every winter," Tristan grumbled, but despite that, he kept walking ahead of Callie, stomping out a path for her.

"Yes, but we didn't know they were arcane beasts before. It's different," Callie said, urging him to hurry.

The boy groaned but kept going. Their goal today was to observe the hatchlings of frost moths. They usually took about a month to pupate, so they should be emerging very soon.

Callie had her journal with her, ready to draw and write down everything she saw.

"Wait, there! I see it!" She grabbed Tristan excitedly and dragged him to the spruce tree. This was the same tree where she had placed many of the caterpillars, including the one that made the fifth petal appear on her hand.

"Calm down! There will be thousands of them. We're not going to miss anything," the boy grumbled.

But Callie couldn't quell her excitement. She crouched down near the tree and stared at one of the cocoons. It was moving! The frost moth was about to emerge.

Less than a minute later, the end of the cocoon broke, and a tiny little head peeked out, followed by wiggly legs.

"They're so cute!" Callie whispered, clutching Tristan by the sleeve.

"Don't shake me! They are just moths!"

But Callie didn't care for his grumbling. She took out her journal and began drawing. The cold winter air didn't bother her as she sketched down the moth right after it crawled out of the pupa. Its wings were crinkled up, but slowly they began to expand. Callie absorbed every moment of the process.

Name: Frostmoths

Appearance:

Initially, they live as caterpillars–long, white, and covered with white fuzz. After they pupate, they turn into beautiful fuzzy moths with large white wings at the bottom of which they have long tails. Males have longer tails.

Behaviour:

Caterpillars appear with the first frost and feed on it until they are large enough to pupate. During approaching snowstorms, they abandon trees and even their cocoons to climb to lower, safer ground.

Abilities:

Sense snowstorm

Eat mana frost

Warnings:

Harvesting their cocoons before the moth hatches will kill it.

Callie was so happy that she could feel her magic swirling inside her with joy. But thanks to all the practice she had, she was able to keep it well contained.

Fly.

Fly. Fly.

Fly. Fly. Fly.

The frostmoths chanted as they waited for their wings to expand and take their full shape. The first ones flew high into the sky as soon as they were ready, and before long, Callie and Tristan were surrounded by moths.

"They are so beautiful!" She gasped.

"I suppose… it's not so bad…" Tristan muttered, looking slightly embarrassed.

They both looked at the sight for a long while, until finally the boy began complaining that he was cold.

"Let's go home!"

Callie nodded reluctantly. While she was cold too, part of her still wanted to remain here and watch. However, just as she turned to leave, she heard a familiar voice call out to her.

Callie! Friend! Help!

Callie instantly stopped. That was Snowglow's voice. Was she in trouble?
