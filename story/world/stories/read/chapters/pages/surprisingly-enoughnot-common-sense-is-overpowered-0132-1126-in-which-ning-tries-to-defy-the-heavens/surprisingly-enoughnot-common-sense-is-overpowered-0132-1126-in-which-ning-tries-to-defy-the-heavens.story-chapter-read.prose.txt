Blue flame. That was what he wanted to see.

Ning looked at the small Fireball hovering above his palm, then at the familiar words on his panel.

[Fireball (Mastery)]

He had reached Mastery just before the alchemy competition. To accomplish that, he had even set Scorching Purge aside and focused nearly all his attention on Fireball. In the end, the effort had proved worthwhile, especially when it came to controlling his flames during the competition.

But Mastery was the limit of the technique as written. Continuing to practice it would only refine what he already knew, so if he wanted to progress further, he would have to experiment with ideas the original technique had never accounted for.

Fortunately, Ning had another world's worth of ideas to draw from.

Flame color was one of the first things that came to mind.

The Fireball above his palm burned orange-yellow, similar enough to ordinary fire that Ning had never thought much about it before.

On Earth, flame color depended on several factors, including temperature, the substances being burned, and the conditions of combustion. Ning remembered that the blue flame of a gas stove generally burned hotter than a yellow, soot-filled flame because the fuel mixed more efficiently with oxygen.

Of course, temperature alone didn't determine a flame's color, and he couldn't quite remember all the details.

"If only there were a technique that could let me recall all my memories with perfect accuracy."

Unfortunately, despite searching for quite some time, Ning had yet to find one. For now, his rather unreliable memories would have to do.

Unlike ordinary fire, his Fireball wasn't burning any fuel. Qi circulated through the technique and somehow manifested as flame, which raised an interesting question.

Could changing the qi circulation alter the flame's color and properties?

Ning had no idea, but he intended to find out.

Mastery gave him an enormous advantage. He understood the technique well enough to alter individual sections of its qi circulation and know when not to do something that would lead to an explosion.

Some adjustments made the flame larger without making it stronger. Others made it brighter or simply consumed additional qi for no useful result. Ning tested each change several times before discarding the ones that led nowhere and keeping the few that produced something interesting.

Eventually, a faint trace of blue appeared near the center of the flame.

Ning stopped changing things.

The color was barely visible beneath the surrounding orange, but he had seen it clearly. More importantly, he knew exactly what he had changed to produce it.

He repeated the same circulation, and the blue returned.

Ning activated Pure Eyes and began studying the Fireball more carefully. The altered flame did seem hotter, but that alone wasn't enough to explain what he was seeing. The qi within the blue portion was behaving differently as well, circulating through the structure of the technique with less of the instability he had observed in several earlier attempts.

It was all quite interesting.

The change in temperature seemed connected to the altered qi circulation, though Ning couldn't yet tell which was responsible for what. Perhaps the blue color was merely a consequence of something more important happening within the flame.

He watched the two colors flickering together above his palm, already considering his next experiments.

Earth's knowledge was useful for coming up with ideas, but Ning knew better than to assume its principles applied perfectly to a world governed by qi.

Still, if he could discover what actually governed spiritual fire, perhaps he could eventually create something beyond Fireball itself.

"So the next step would be trying to comprehend its True Meaning?" Ning pondered.

Much like weapon arts, spells possessed a path of progression beyond ordinary technical proficiency.

For weapon arts, there were realms such as Scattered Hand, Penetration, Unity, Transformation, and eventually Intent.

Spells followed a similar principle, though their higher progression depended more heavily on understanding the forces of the world that gave them form. A cultivator who developed a deeper understanding of Fire could apply that knowledge to every Fire technique they practiced, and the same held for the other four elements.

The first step toward that understanding was Insight.

Fireball, Scorching Purge, and Crimson Flame Control all relied on Fire despite their different effects. If Ning could identify the principles they shared, he might begin understanding Fire itself.

"My Mastery should help with that."

After all, he'd already mastered Fireball, and his panel had confirmed that his understanding was producing actual results. That was reassuring, considering how often his ideas came from another world, so in a way his knowledge was also being validated at the same time.

He had min-maxed rather shamelessly over the past several months, pouring disproportionate amounts of time into alchemy, Fireball, and Withering & Flourishing because they were immediately relevant to the competition. Aside from his cultivation itself, several other areas had inevitably received less attention.

Now he could begin correcting that.

Ning rose and headed for the colder section of his fields.

The temperature dropped noticeably as he approached.

Frozen Breath Grain filled the largest plot, its pale silver-green stalks swaying beneath a thin layer of mist. Frostveil Cabbage and Snowthread Radish occupied the lower terraces, while a newer row of Blue-Ice Bulbs grew near the edge.

All four crops preferred the cold, though their individual requirements still needed some attention.

He crouched beside one of the Blue-Ice Bulbs and pressed two fingers into the soil. It was too warm. Not enough to harm the crop, but warmer than he wanted around the roots.

Ning tapped his foot.

[Hidden Ice Mist Technique]

He circulated the technique, guiding a pale stream of cold mist between the rows. Ning kept it close to the ground, thinning it whenever the cold became too intense and redirecting it before frost could accumulate excessively on the leaves.

The technique required far more control this way, but that was precisely why he liked practicing it here. He could improve his proficiency while tending crops that benefited from the cold.

He was hitting two birds with one stone.

After several minutes, familiar words appeared before him.

[Hidden Ice Mist (Great Accomplishment: 145 → 146/400)]

Ning glanced at the increase and continued walking. One point wasn't much, but after so many months of training, he'd learned to appreciate even the smallest improvements.

They all accumulated eventually.

By the time he finished tending the cold fields, a thin veil of mist still lingered around the Frozen Breath Grain. Ning made another short note about the amount of qi he had used, checked several leaves for signs of excessive frost, and finally moved on to the last spell on today's schedule.

Golden Finger.

Ning stopped near the edge of the field, where he had placed a large stone as a target, and circulated qi toward his hand. A faint golden sheen gathered around his index finger before concentrating at its tip.

[Golden Finger (Great Accomplishment: 25.788/400)]

He thrust his finger forward.

A thin streak of golden light crossed the distance and struck the stone with a sharp crack, leaving behind a small but surprisingly deep hole.

Ning walked over and ran a finger across the edge of the indentation.

Golden Finger was his weakest among the basic Five Elements techniques. Despite reaching Great Accomplishment through persistent practice, his progress had slowed a lot.

The problem was his understanding of Metal.

He knew how Golden Finger worked, gathering Metal qi at his fingertip before releasing it in a narrow beam, almost like firing a gun. But that only explained how to perform the technique, while the underlying principles of Metal remained a mystery.

Back on Earth, metal was a category of material, but the Five Elements clearly represented something more.

Cultivation texts associated Metal with hardness, sharpness, gathering, contraction, and severance, among other philosophical concepts.

Ning circulated Golden Finger again, this time observing how the qi converged toward his fingertip instead of focusing on the resulting attack.

Concentration was clearly important, but was that a fundamental property of Metal or merely a feature of Golden Finger?

With only one Metal technique to study, he couldn't tell. If he learned several different techniques, he could compare their principles, much as he intended to do with his Fire techniques.

Looks like I'll need another Metal technique eventually.

Until then, he would have to make do with what he had.

Ning released the gathered qi.

The golden streak struck the stone beside the first hole with another sharp crack.

Four of his fundamental Five Elements techniques had already reached Mastery: Small Cloud Rain for Water, Soil Refining for Earth, Withering & Flourishing for Wood, and Fireball for Fire.

Only Metal remained.

Ning raised his hand again, watching golden qi slowly gather around his finger.

The Pure Qi Sect had countless teachings concerning the Five Elements, but one particular saying appeared almost everywhere.

Yin and Yang divide Heaven and Earth; the Five Elements give rise to the myriad things.

Wood birthed Fire, Fire birthed Earth, Earth birthed Metal, Metal birthed Water, and Water nourished Wood. There was also the restraining cycle, with each element suppressing another. These relationships appeared throughout cultivation, from spiritual plants and alchemy to formations and martial arts.

If he could deepen his understanding of all five, insights from one discipline might help him comprehend another. Knowledge gained from farming could improve his alchemy, while something he'd learned from a spell might eventually prove useful in a completely different technique.

He wanted everything he learned to build upon everything else.

Golden light flashed from his finger.

Crack!

Another hole appeared in the stone.

Ning lowered his hand and flexed his fingers before circulating the technique again.

The same reasoning applied to Yin and Yang, though his understanding of those was even shallower.

Hidden Ice Mist, Jade Skin and Icy Meridians gave him a starting point for Yin, while Lightning Cry Arrow offered a glimpse into Yang, which was often associated with lightning and the power of Heaven. He had his reasons for choosing these techniques.

Ning knew that truly mastering all of them was still a distant goal, but he had to start laying the foundation early.

Ning continued practicing for another hour, repeatedly sending streaks of golden light into the increasingly unfortunate stone.

He was preparing another attack when a familiar caw interrupted him.

Ning glanced over his shoulder and found Fenghuang watching him. "So, you're awake."

The raven kept a more reasonable sleeping schedule than his master.

Caw!

Fenghuang's response came louder this time.

"What do you mean I should rest?"

Through their faint connection, Ning could sense the concern behind Fenghuang's cry. The raven was still worried about yesterday's loss.

Ning's expression softened. To be fair, the bird had a point.

Yesterday's loss still bothered Ning more than he cared to admit. Fourth place was objectively an excellent result, and Ling Xin had been pleased with his performance, but he'd still wanted to reach the top three.

When he'd woken up that morning, part of him had been tempted to forget his schedule, spend the day eating something delicious, and resume training tomorrow when the disappointment had faded.

That was precisely why he'd forced himself to follow his schedule today.

Ning knew himself well enough to recognize how dangerous I'll do it tomorrow could become. One bad day could easily become two, and before long, his mind would start producing increasingly convincing reasons why the third day wasn't particularly convenient either. From there, it would only be a downward spiral.

So he'd developed a simple rule for days like this.

Just start the damn thing.

He didn't have to commit to an entire day of cultivation. One Fireball, one spear form, or even a single row of crops would do. Once he got moving, continuing usually became much easier, and if it didn't, at least he'd accomplished something.

Ning fired Golden Finger again, adjusted the circulation slightly, and repeated the process.

From the outside, I really do look like a genius, don't I?

The thought amused him, especially because he couldn't exactly deny it. Between farming, martial arts, spells, and alchemy, his progress probably looked downright ridiculous to anyone watching.

Ning knew that consistency had played a large part in his progress, along with the learning habits he'd developed in his previous life. Know Thyself complemented those habits perfectly, making it easier to identify mistakes and improve through repeated practice.

But ultimately, the most important thing was to keep grinding, even on days when he barely made any progress.

Such was the Dao of Grinding. A little progress every day, and eventually, even mountains could be worn down.

Caw.

Ning glanced toward Fenghuang. "I said I was almost done."

Caw.

"...One more."

Golden qi gathered around his finger as Ning adjusted the circulation one last time before firing another beam.

Seriously, whoever named this technique Golden Finger had a strange sense of humor.

After all, in cultivation novels, a golden finger was supposed to be a cheat that made everything easier. Yet here he was, working himself to the bone just to master a technique called Golden Finger.

...

"What are you doing, Junior Brother?"

Ning looked up from the patch of earth he had been digging beside the courtyard wall and found Ling Yinran standing several steps away, her gaze moving between him and the handful of soil currently disappearing into his storage pouch.

He had already collected a decent amount by then, judging by the disturbed ground around him, while Fenghuang watched the entire process from a branch overhead, not concerned in the slightest.

"Senior Sister, did you know dirt is actually quite useful in a fight?" Ning asked, scooping up another handful and examining it before sending it into his storage pouch as well. "Throwing it into someone's eyes is admittedly pretty scummy, but if it works, it works."

Ling Yinran blinked. "What?"

"And it's not just dirt. Look at that boulder." Ning pointed toward one of the rocks. "Depending on where I'm fighting, I can use it as cover, block someone's line of sight, force them to approach from a different direction, or maybe move it myself if the situation allows it. Same with the trees, walls, uneven ground, loose stones... there's quite a lot lying around that people don't usually think of as part of a fight."

Ling Yinran listened as her junior brother continued listing perfectly ordinary pieces of the courtyard and increasingly questionable ways they could be used against another human being until she finally stopped him with a raised hand.

"Junior Brother, are you all right?"

"I'm totally fine. It's just that Master kicked me out while she and Grandmaster discussed whether I have to participate in the inter-sect competition, so I decided to make myself useful while waiting." Ning tossed another handful of dirt into his storage pouch. "After all, surely nothing could go wrong, right, Senior Sister? Ahaha."

Ling Yinran fell silent as she watched her junior brother prepare as though he were about to be sent to war. Clearly, he didn't have much confidence in Ling Xin's negotiation skills.

Still, as his senior sister, Ling Yinran knew what she should do.

"Don't worry, Junior Brother. While Aunt may not look like it, she's always reliable at critical moments." Ling Yinran paused briefly. "Yes. Surely."

That did not sound reliable at all.

That wasn't exactly the reassurance Ning had been hoping for.

Fortunately, or perhaps unfortunately, they didn't have to wait much longer. The door soon opened, and Fu Xuan stepped into the courtyard with Ling Xin following behind her, only for the older woman to stop after two steps when she noticed Ning standing beside a freshly dug hole with dirt on his hands.

Fu Xuan's gaze lingered on him for several seconds before she sighed. "...I'm not even going to ask."

Ning gave a slightly embarrassed smile and cupped his hands. "Greetings, Grandmaster."

Ling Xin, naturally, possessed no such restraint. She leaned around her master and looked Ning up and down before asking, "Why are you playing in the dirt, my dear disciple?"

"I wasn't playing in it."

Ling Yinran looked at him.

Ning met her gaze for a moment, then quietly looked away. "It's nothing, Master."

Before Ling Xin could investigate further, Ning circulated a little qi through his robes. The enchantments woven into the fabric activated with a faint ripple, and most of the dust and loose soil slipped away. After brushing the remaining dirt from his hands and hair, Ning once again looked like a respectable inner disciple worthy of having jade in his nickname.

Ling Xin still looked suspicious, but Fu Xuan spared him from further questioning.

"Anyway, we've finished discussing the matter." She turned toward Ning, folding her hands behind her back. "You won't be required to participate in the inter-sect competition."

Some of the tension left Ning's shoulders.

Fu Xuan watched him for a moment before sighing. "I've learned something from this experience, though. The next time you need someone to negotiate on your behalf, don't send your master."

"Master!" Ling Xin protested.

Ning slowly turned toward her.

"You asked her to convince me that there was little reason for you to participate," Fu Xuan continued. "Instead, she spent several minutes explaining how talented you are and why you would perform exceptionally well if you went."

"I was establishing context." Ling Xin crossed her arms, entirely unapologetic. "My point was that my disciple would obviously rock the other sects' disciples, so why should he need to go around proving it? He can always beat them some other time."

Hearing that, Ning rubbed his forehead. Truly, his master was chaos incarnate.

"There is a reason Tang Feiyu handles negotiations concerning your alchemy business," Fu Xuan added.

"I'm perfectly good at negotiating."

Fu Xuan just sighed. "The main reason I agreed was that the alternative involved letting you accompany Ning to the competition, and I have no desire to visit another sect to explain why several of their disciples are suffering from strange pill-related injuries after meeting you."

Ling Xin's indignation disappeared. "Hehe."

Ling Yinran slowly turned toward her aunt. "What do you mean by hehe?"

W-was I just saved by my master's strange reputation?

For a moment, Ning was speechless. He wasn't sure whether to laugh or cry at the realization, but whatever the reason, he'd managed to escape the situation.

He quickly cupped his hands toward Fu Xuan. "Thank you, Grandmaster."

Fu Xuan shook her head. "You've already shown enough through the alchemy competition. You said you would reach the top three and fell short by one position, but sometimes things simply don't go according to plan."

"See?" Ling Xin quickly said. "I convinced her."

Fu Xuan ignored her.

"My original reasoning was simple. If you reached the top three after learning alchemy for only a little over a year, the resulting fame would be useful. Instead, you placed fourth, but after seeing how the competition unfolded, I don't think the difference matters nearly as much as I originally expected."

Ning tilted his head slightly.

"You created an entirely new pill during the second stage, then performed extremely well during the peak first-grade pill refinement. There were disciples who ranked above you without attracting nearly as much attention." Fu Xuan gave him a measured look. "You missed your stated goal, but your actual performance accomplished most of what I wanted from the competition."

Ning considered that for a moment before cupping his hands properly. "I understand, Grandmaster. I was too focused on the ranking itself to look at it that way." A small smile appeared on his face. "And naturally, much of what I accomplished was thanks to Grandmaster's guidance."

Fu Xuan nodded approvingly.

"And mine," Ling Xin added.

Ling Yinran sighed. "You mostly taught him how to make explosions."

"I taught him plenty of other things!" Ling Xin turned toward Ning for support. "Didn't I, my dear disciple?"

Ning became deeply interested in a tiny speck of dirt remaining on his otherwise spotless sleeve.

"...My dear disciple."

"Senior Sister does have a remarkable memory."

Ling Xin's eyes widened. "Traitor."

Fenghuang chose that moment to caw from the branch above them.

Ning looked up. "See? Even Fenghuang agrees."

Caw.

"Both of you are traitors!"

Fu Xuan rubbed her temple at the sudden change in topic.

"Regardless, the competition accomplished what we needed. With enough fame and achievements, we can push for your core disciple status immediately after you reach the Foundation Building realm, which would make our plan to turn your reputation into a deterrent much more effective."

Ning's amusement faded. He had known this was part of Fu Xuan's plan, but hearing her state it so plainly made the purpose clearer.

"Because then anyone moving against me wouldn't only be dealing with Master's protection," he said.

"Exactly." Fu Xuan nodded. "Ling Xin can protect you as her disciple, and I can support you from behind the scenes, but those protections still depend heavily on us. Core disciple status would make the sect's investment in you official."

Ning nodded.

"At your current cultivation, the Eternal Severance Art is more likely to make you a target than protect you. You're still at Qi Condensation, and anyone powerful enough to fear your future potential might decide to eliminate you before you grow stronger."

Ning's expression grew more serious. "So the deterrent only truly begins working once I'm strong enough that eliminating me is dangerous in itself."

"Precisely. Foundation Building would already change that calculation a great deal. If you eventually reach the Purple Mansion while continuing to progress as you have, then you would become a proper threat in your own right." Fu Xuan paused, her gaze sharpening slightly. "Until then, keep the inheritance you received from the Heavenly Origin Tower quiet. The fewer people who know exactly what you obtained there, the better."

"Yes, Grandmaster." Ning inclined his head. "I had intended to keep it hidden as much as possible, but I'll be even more careful."

"Good." Fu Xuan's expression eased. "You're more thoughtful about these things than Ling Xin, at least."

"Master!" Ling Xin felt she was getting bullied by everyone today.

"Which is also why I'm fairly certain there's another reason you don't want to attend the inter-sect competition." Fu Xuan calmly ignored Ling Xin. "You aren't usually the type to refuse an opportunity like this without thinking it through."

Ning hesitated.

There were several reasons, but explaining most of them would require a conversation he had absolutely no desire to have.

After a moment, he gave a small nod. "Grandmaster is correct. I do have certain reasons, though some of them are... difficult to explain."

Fu Xuan studied him briefly. When it became clear that he wasn't going to elaborate, she didn't press.

In truth, Ning was curious about something.

It had started with the insight into karma he'd gained through his interaction with Su Fan.

Before the alchemy competition, Ning had declared that he would participate in the inter-sect competition if he failed to reach the top three. At the time, he'd said it without thinking too deeply about the consequences, and now that he'd actually failed, he was beginning to regret making that promise.

Then, just when he'd been considering ways to avoid participating, he'd suddenly gained an insight into karma, of all things, concerning the relationship between one's words and actions.

Seriously? Of all the possible times?

Could it have been a coincidence? Of course.

In fact, Ning was perfectly willing to acknowledge that he might simply be overthinking the whole thing, using some half-understood insight into karma to justify his disappointment over losing the alchemy competition.

He was quite capable of being paranoid, thank you very much.

But that only made him more curious.

If the plot really was trying to drag him into Xiao Fan's orbit for the upcoming competition, could he still avoid it by consciously taking precautions?

Before becoming properly acquainted with Xiao Fan, staying outside the plot had worked surprisingly well. Ning had minded his own business, cultivated peacefully, grown his spiritual plants, and generally enjoyed a fairly comfortable life.

Then he'd become acquainted with Xiao Fan.

Not long afterward, Ning had found himself in a string of deadly situations.

So Ning had decided to treat this as an experiment. He would deliberately avoid the inter-sect competition and see what happened.

Would that be the end of the matter, allowing him to continue living his peaceful life?

Or would some ridiculous coincidence occur that somehow dragged him into the competition anyway, regardless of what precautions he took?

He couldn't avoid danger forever, nor did he necessarily want to. There were opportunities worth taking and risks worth accepting, especially in a cultivation world where strength determined so much of one's future.

But Ning would very much prefer to be the one making those decisions.

Because there was a rather important difference between willingly walking into danger and finding himself dragged into it despite having taken every reasonable precaution to stay away.

And if the plot insisted on dragging him along anyway?

Well, at least he'd know just how screwed he was.

Of course, there was also another, less philosophical reason for avoiding the competition.

Ning didn't want to get slimed.

At this point, he could no longer deny the horrifying truth.

He had become something of a rival to Xiao Fan.

And Ning knew exactly what happened to the protagonist's established rival when the story moved from the comfortable local region into a bigger tournament filled with previously unknown geniuses.

They got slimed.

Some terrifying disciple from another sect would appear, Ning would walk onto the stage carrying all the reputation he had painstakingly accumulated over the last few years, and five minutes later he would be embedded in the arena floor while everyone stared in horror at how easily the newcomer had defeated Ji Ning.

Then Xiao Fan would inevitably have to fight that person later.

Absolutely not.

Ning had already done that when fighting Qiu Han once. He had no desire to experience it again.

Actually, speaking of Qiu Han, he would almost certainly be attending the inter-sect competition. He was one of the strongest disciples of their generation, possessed an impressive supernatural ability, and had exactly the sort of reputation required for such an important event.

Perhaps this time Qiu Han would find himself on the receiving end of the same treatment he'd once given Ning.

That would be quite funny, Ning mused.

Fu Xuan's voice pulled him from his thoughts. "There's one more thing."

Ning blinked and turned his attention back to her. "Yes, Grandmaster?"

"That new pill you refined during the competition." Fu Xuan moved toward the stone table and took a seat, gesturing for the others to join her. "Have you decided on a name?"

Ning followed and sat opposite her. "I have."

"Good, because I suspect that one is going to sell extremely well." Fu Xuan rested one hand against the table as she spoke. "Fortunately, the formation around the stage obscured enough of your refinement that nobody should have been able to determine the exact ratios simply by watching, but you still revealed more of the process than I would have preferred. Be more careful with that in the future."

Ning's expression became slightly sheepish. "Understood, Grandmaster. I was too focused on completing the refinement at the time and didn't pay enough attention to what the spectators might learn from watching."

"I know, which is why I'm reminding you now rather than scolding you for it. Until we've properly recorded the formula and decided how we intend to handle production, there's no reason to make anyone else's work easier."

"I'll remember."

Fu Xuan seemed satisfied. "Good. So, the name?"

"Wandering Mind Pill."

She repeated it once under her breath, considering how it sounded before giving a small nod. "Wandering Mind Pill. Yes, that's a good name. It fits the effect quite well."

Ning smiled. "I'm glad Grandmaster thinks so."

Ling Xin, who had taken the seat beside her master, looked less impressed. "I still think it could use something grander. Something like Golden Serenity Myriad Thought Transformation Pill."

Ling Yinran stared at her aunt. "That's such a long name."

"First impressions matter," Ling Xin insisted. "A good pill needs a name with presence."

Ning frowned. "Wandering Mind Pill has presence."

"It wanders."

Ning had no retort to that.

Fu Xuan put an end to the matter before it could spiral somewhere else again. "Wandering Mind Pill is fine. More importantly, there's something else from the competition that attracted quite a bit of attention."

"What was it, Grandmaster?" Ning asked curiously.

"Your alchemy style."

"Oh." Ning blinked.

"Several people noticed that the way you approach refinement is quite different from the usual methods, and by this point, it's personalized enough that you should probably give it a proper name."

Ning had a feeling he knew what was coming.

Fu Xuan raised a hand before he could defend himself. "And no, Seven-Eight Style is not a proper name."

Ning's face fell slightly. "Grandmaster, is the name really that bad? I thought it fit quite well."

He even held himself back from calling it the six-seven style.

"The reasoning behind it is good," Fu Xuan replied. "The name needs work."

"That's what I said!" Ling Xin added helpfully.

Ning turned toward his master. "You wanted to call my pill the Golden Serenity Myriad Thought Transformation Pill."

"Exactly. I have excellent naming sense."

"Master, I don't think that supports your argument." Ning had no desire to be lectured on naming by his master of all people.

Ling Yinran covered a small smile with one hand.

Fu Xuan shook her head. "The important point is that your approach is recognizable now." She tapped one finger lightly against the stone table as she continued. "If you keep developing it, people are eventually going to call it something whether you name it yourself or not. I suggest doing so before Seven-Eight Style somehow becomes permanent."

Ning nodded slowly. "I understand what Grandmaster means."

He still felt that Seven-Eight Style was being treated rather unfairly, but he wasn't going to argue the point when Fu Xuan was clearly correct about the more important issue.

Calling it merely an alchemy style was becoming inaccurate. Ning approached almost everything the same way now: cultivation, farming, martial arts, spells, and alchemy. Know Thyself helped tremendously by giving him clearer feedback, but the method itself had gradually become his way of learning about this world.

Ning rubbed his chin thoughtfully.

If Seven-Eight Style was unacceptable, its replacement should cover all of that.

"Then..." Ning looked up. "Let's call it Know Thy World."
