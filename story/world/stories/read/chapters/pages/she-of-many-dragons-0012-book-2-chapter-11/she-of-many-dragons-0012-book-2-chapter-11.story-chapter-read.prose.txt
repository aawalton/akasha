Bella was a little... odd.

As they retraced their steps back through the forest, Alice reminded herself that it had taken some time for Natura to integrate with the rest of the team. In some ways, they were still working on it, though it was much better than it had been before.

But it was painfully obvious that Bella didn't want to associate with the other dragons. Every time someone asked her a question, such as when Iggy excitedly wanted to know about her skills, she would hesitate for a long moment and glance at Alice as if for permission. She would only answer once Alice gave it.

Part of Alice wanted to pull her aside and have a private chat, but in the wasteland left behind after the fires, privacy was hard to come by. And it would be best if Bella got over her reticence and figured out her place herself.

Bella continued to be hesitant to explain the details of her skills until Alice stepped in and specifically asked. Then she was more than happy to elaborate, puffing up her chest in pride.

Her Smoke Form was self-explanatory. If she were in danger or needed to pass through a solid object, her body could temporarily turn into a smoke-like insubstantial being.

Numi perked up at that. “Wow, that's amazing! Let's test it out.” She scuttled around in increasingly wide circles for a minute until she came up with a bit of rock, still ashy from the soil. “I'll throw this at you, and you show us your Smoke Form.”

Bella bristled. “You will not be throwing anything at me.”

“No, you will not,” Prim agreed. She gave a stern look to Numi. “At least not while Alice is holding Bella. The rock will pass right through and hit Alice in the chest.”

“Ohh.” Numi looked sheepish.

“Bella shouldn't do anything that she doesn't want to,” Alice told Numi. “She only just hatched and should be allowed a good meal and rest before she works on her skills.” Though she looked around and frowned. “It may be some time until we can get out.”

“It is fully acceptable for you to see my skills, Alice. After all, they are there to serve your cause,” Bella said.

Alice winced. “Dear, I would much rather your Smoke Form protect you.”

“Perhaps when it gets stronger, it will protect both of us.”

“All of us,” Alice said. “We are a team.”

A soon-to-be very hungry team. Best to get a move on and see if they could work their way out of this forest. She placed Bella on her shoulder—the one Prim wasn’t occupying—and ignored the way the two dragons bristled at each other. Alice was not going to make a new hatchling fly on her own.

Natura pointed the way toward what she sensed was a living forest. As it seemed to be in the opposite direction of the now far-off fire, this was agreeable to everybody.

About an hour later, they came across a stream. It was a pathetic thing, damned on two sides with built-up ash so badly only a trickle of water flowed on the inlet and outlet sides. In the middle swam a hissing, extremely angry ball of blood eels.

Bella perked up. “Alice, I can show you the strength of my Curse of Ash.”

She sounded so very much like Iggy at that moment that Alice smiled. “If you feel you're up to it. Please remember that those eels jump from the water, and they bite.”

She glanced at Iggy significantly, tilting her head toward the dark dragon. Iggy nodded back. He would fly behind her and be a guard in case anything went wrong.

Bella leaped from her shoulder. Smoky wings caught the air, trailing a little ash on every downstroke. Nevertheless, her flight was easy and graceful, and she quickly made her way to the eels.

The eels had gotten themselves in such a state that their bodies briefly broke the surface of the water. This made it easy for her to swoop down and touch one. There was a gray puff at the moment of contact. Then another and another as she dragged her claws briefly along the mass.

It took only an instant for the eels to realize that they were under attack.

The mass of eels hissed in anger, and quite a few tried to jump at Bella. They would have succeeded in catching her, too, except for the hundred other eels trying to do the same thing. They got in each other's way, which gave Bella the moment she needed to flap desperately to the side.

Two eels, however, managed to fling themselves upward, and Bella was rather new to flying. She didn't know the tricks like wingover-turning that the others did.

Alice began to cry out, but Iggy had been watching for danger. He shot down and positioned himself between Bella and the eels.

The eels struck his scales and bounced off, unable to get a grip on his Ultimate Armor.

Within moments, Bella was high enough for safety. But she was not pleased. “What was that? You dare interfere!?”

“Yes, I dare. You are new and untested,” Iggy shot back. “Furthermore,” he added, when Bella opened her mouth to reply, “Alice told me to watch over you. Clearly, it was needed,” he sniffed.

“Bella,” Alice said, and held up her arm. Her dark little dragon returned to her, sulkily glancing over her shoulder at Iggy.

Alice wanted to ask if she was all right but had a feeling that would injure her ego.

“What did your skill do?” Prim asked, her focus still on the eels. “I don't see a difference.”

Bella perked up a little. “You will soon. I have placed my mark on them.”

Sure enough, there was soon a difference in the twisting ball of eels. A number of them had blackened spots on their brilliant red bodies. Those spots only became larger until, after a minute or two, the black seemed to engulf the eels. Then they were seen no more.

“It necrotized them,” Bella said, with pride. “It rots them from the inside out. A fitting death for any enemy.”

Alice swallowed hard. “It is… impressive.” But privately, she thought it was a terrible death for anything except for the blood eels.

“I don't care how they die, as long as they do,” Natura said, with unusual fierceness. “Luckily, I believe that will be soon. There is more ash being washed down the stream by the moment, and they will soon suffocate.”

Bella looked to Alice. This was an opportunity.

“Go and finish them off,” Alice said, “but Iggy will back you up, and you will not complain about taking proper safety precautions.”

Bella raised her head proudly but didn't argue.

Iggy, to his credit, did not gloat but escorted the younger dragon to the eels and even murmured a tip or two on how to quickly dive and ascend again.

Prim leaned over to whisper in Alice’s ear. “She is very fierce and eager to please you.”

“I'm afraid that she is very insecure,” Alice murmured with a frown.

What in the world could have made such a powerful hatchling so eager to please, and yet so fearful of upsetting her?

****

Bella gained two levels in her Curse of Ash from her destruction of the eels. She returned to Alice's shoulder, out of breath, but pleased with herself.

Alice made sure to pile on the praise and not show any of her inner disquiet to the hatchling.

At least Natura was happy, considering they had eliminated even more of the pests from the forest.

They moved on, picking their way through the burned forest. Thankfully, there weren’t signs that any tree wraiths had escaped the fires.

They found no sign of wildlife, either.

Bella had curled up in a dark little ball for a nap, and Alice put her hand over her to keep her from slipping off her shoulder.

Then Bella suddenly stirred under her fingers. “Someone's coming. Someone dangerous.” She shivered with anticipation.

Everyone stopped and looked around. There was no one in sight, only the charcoal remains of trees and ashy fields.

“I don't sense anything,” Natura said with a scowl.

“Not down here. Look up there.” Numi had scuttled halfway up a burned trunk and pointed toward the sky to the east.

There was a small line of objects on the horizon.

“Birds?” Iggy asked. “What do we have to fear from birds?”

“Those aren't birds,” Bella said. “Can't you hear their wingbeats? Dragons.” One lip raised upward in a sneer that showed sharp teeth. “But not our kind.”

“What does that mean?” Alice asked.

“I think it means that we should not be seen until we know if they are friend or foe.” Iggy flew down and landed before them. “Natura, we are in need of a cave or some shelter.”

Natura hurriedly dug into the deep ash and placed a forepaw on the ground. Then she shook her head. “There's nothing.”

Alice looked around, but all the covering trees down to individual blades of grass were burned away. They were entirely exposed out here.

I should have thought of that, she silently berated herself. Then again, they had been traveling for days without meeting anyone.

Her dragons were watching her, so she put on a brave face. “Then we have no choice. We must meet them. After all, we did come out to meet dragons.”

“I think that hiding until we know for certain of their intentions is wise,” Prim said, then hopped down from Alice's shoulder. She stood on her hind legs and extended her wings. “I will obscure us. Gather around close. This will be difficult, and I may be tired at the end.”

Alice thought for a moment, glancing up at the rapidly growing points of darkness. In only the last few seconds, they had resolved themselves into individuals. They were flying quickly. There was no direct evidence of danger, but while studying them, it was as if a shadow crossed over her heart.

Perhaps we should meet on my terms, she thought.

With that in mind, she nodded her assent to Prim.

Prim, wings still outstretched, closed her eyes. Alice didn't feel the dragon's power settle over her. Yet, she at once recognized a change in the world. When she looked around, she saw a slight distortion in an arching bubble over all of them. It was as if a fine pane of glass separated her and her dragons from everything else.

"I have made us look like the rest of the forest," Prim said, eyes still closed. Alice got the impression that she was concentrating very hard.

They all fell silent as the dragons loomed larger in the sky. They moved at great speed, and it quickly became clear that they were much larger than Orris— even bigger than Natura’s wood dragon parents.

They flew in a V-formation, every wingbeat in sync with the others. A uniform steel gray, their heads were like arrows. There was something martial in the way they held themselves, like a finely honed weapon.

Alice found herself holding her breath as they flew past, and she swore she heard a whistle as those sharp wings cut the air.

Their necks were down, sweeping back and forth, looking, searching.

Bella stood up on her shoulder, straining as if she wanted to fight but held back at Alice’s request. "They're searching," she hissed.

"For what?" Alice asked.

Her dragon didn't answer.

At a signal she didn’t see, the gray dragons split off from one another and flew at a lower, slow glide. Each skimmed over the burned forest so low in places that it almost looked as if their bellies would scrape the tops of the burned trees. One flew directly overhead.

Despite the fact they were hidden by illusion, everyone crouched. Alice gazed up as it passed. The gray eyes against gray scales showed no mercy, and Alice got the impression that if this dragon found what it was looking for, it would strike it down.

Iggy growled very, very quietly. Unlike the others, it seemed that he could not stay still and swam through the air within the bounds of Prim's illusion, ready to put himself between them and Alice.

"They are conducting a search pattern," he said, flexing his claws.

I know, she wanted to say but instead held her breath as another dragon skimmed right over them.

Prim's illusion held, though Alice could see how tense she had become.

An odd thought occurred to Alice. If she had an open token, could she reach out to one of the searchers and claim it as her own?

Well, she didn’t have an open token, and the dragon would still have to agree.

The dragons crisscrossed over and near their position several times. But it didn’t seem they were particularly focused on their spot, but the burned area in general. From the smoke in the air and the line of fire in the distance, that area was still growing.

The gray searchers moved on as night fell.

Trembling, Prim let down her illusion.

Alice scooped her up, cuddling her close. "Dearest, I believe that you saved us.”

“If they come again... I'm not sure that I will be able to hide us as effectively," Prim panted.

"If they come again, would we even see them?" Numi asked, with an eye to the night sky.

"We would hear the whistle of their wings," Bella said. “Then I will blacken their hides with my power.”

“I would see them," Natura said, ignoring Bella. "The darkness is perfectly natural. It doesn't bother me."

Alice turned to her. "Then help lead us out. I think that we must escape this forest as soon as possible."

"But what are they looking for?" Numi asked.

No one had an answer.
