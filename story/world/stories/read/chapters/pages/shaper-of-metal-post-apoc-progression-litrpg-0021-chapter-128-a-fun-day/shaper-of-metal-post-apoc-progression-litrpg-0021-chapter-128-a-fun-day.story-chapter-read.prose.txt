Chapter 128: A Fun Day

Jack fished around for the perfect mulberry bush to start with. Finding it in a darker corner, he gestured at it and smiled. “Here we are. Do the honors of a taste test?”

Neex nodded once again in solemnity and approached the bush, leaning down and curling this way and that, inspecting, as if judiciously deciding the perfect first berry. Finally, she plucked it and straightened, holding it between forefinger and thumb and looking to Jack for approval.

He nodded sagely.

Opening her mouth and slowly placing it on her tongue, she lifted her fingers away, closed her mouth, and bit down with a mild crunching, bursting sound coming from inside.

She breathed in, closed her eyes, and chewed slowly. Finally, she swallowed. Sighed. “Mediocre.”

“What?!” Jack exclaimed in alarm.

Neex peeked an eye open, then grinned impishly with berry-stained teeth. “Gotcha!”

“Wha-! Yooou…” Jack sputtered. He squinted his eyes, glaring and frowning with a deliberate, exaggerated admonishment.

Neex responded by smiling even more cutely, still self-satisfied, moving to pluck some more berries and pop them in her mouth. She chomped away, swaying her head and shoulders sideways to and fro — more self-satisfaction on display.

Jack put his hands on his hips and tried to maintain a stern frown, but found it impossible, broke into a snort, and gave up. How could he even pretend to scold when she was being that adorable? “You win this round, you overly-cute gremlin.” He finally moved to grab a berry and take a bite. Very ripe, tangy, with a counterbalance of sweet. Ideal.

Neex paused at his words with a berry still going to her mouth. Her eyes slid sidelong toward him, going wide, but quickly darted away as she finally chomped the berry.

“Okay,” Jack said as he grabbed another berry off the bush, “this is the rule: pop one, stow one in the basket. Otherwise, we’re about to eat this whole damn bush!”

“Roger that, sir!” Neex proclaimed, saluting with a comically stern face.

They walked through the colorful zone that was the nearby historical district, along the cobblestone streets, and mostly window-shopped the aesthetic, even soothing shopfronts displaying all sorts of wonderful things to throw your money away on for the sustenance of what you might call quaint or kitschy culture, depending on your tastes. Neex’s tastes were more that everything, everywhere in sight, was awesome.

Jack bought Neex things, because how could he not? Keepsakes and knick-knacks of questionable authenticity. There was an accursed, impossible-to-miss-or-avoid place that sold grab bags of such things, and boy, did she like that idea.

With the supposedly well-adjusted human girl happily wearing pearlescent ‘Hollywood star glasses,’ sporting a rainbow bead necklace and a garish ‘teddy bear ring,’ and toting a heart-shaped pink purse at least half full of junk, Jack eventually managed to steer her in the direction he’d planned. Was he carrying a bag of junk himself? Yes, but it was a small one. Was it for him? Of course not.

Little Bite was a small and quaint place out of the way in Mulberry Heights, along a turn-off down the main road that was so narrow it was probably better described as an alley.

And that was why it was called Quick Alley. Most places down Quick were little more than food and produce stalls, always painted in garish colors and styles. Jack, being in his disguise as Mikey, had to resist waving in familiarity at ‘Meatman Bob,’ sitting at his perpetually frozen-in-time sausage and jerky stand in his eternal too-form-fitting, off-white wifebeater. Bob was understandably distracted by the beauty on Jack’s arm, at least.

Neex’s chosen form drew eyes. In addition to her various naturally magnetic qualities, she was simply out-of-place, and a bit like a shut-in out slumming. Her very clashing recent accessories did little to help (and so much for the style guide she’d espoused). Her head was constantly turning, drinking in decor and people as they were reduced to a snail’s pace. Staring too long at times. She’d stop completely if he let her, but he was honestly ready to eat and getting hungrier by the second with all the incredible smells hitting his nose.

In any case, her sticking out like a sore thumb worked out, as Jack was still slightly worried about recognition in these parts. He figured it was best not to talk to Bob, who seemed to know and remember everyone like a psychic. Even if he couldn’t truly pinpoint ‘Jack’ within the disguise, he’d probably feel a suspicious familiarity.

It also worked out because Jack felt like a superstar with her as his date. It was a raw, undeniable satisfaction deep in his gut, giving him the urge to smile like a smug idiot.

Via the bond, Neex mentally exclaimed, “Ohhh, they’re just so normal and going about their daily activities! It’s just like in the videos! Though this is the first time I’ve seen a human pick their nose in public. Fascinating. But I’m really here. Among them. Thank you so much for this, Jack! It’s a lot different than seeing just the disciplined military personnel in the tower. And all, under the open twilight sky… It’s so nice.”

“No problem,” Jack replied. “Yeah, I’ve always loved my city. This is refreshing after being away; I’ve missed it. Certainly was the best part of being a cabby.”

“I’m sorry you’ll be pulled so far away from it soon, and largely on account of me.”

“For you? I’ll make that trade any day and come out like a bandit — even if I never saw this place again.”

The very human face of the very alien girl turned to him at that, and soon flickered away with a bashful smile. “I’m quite certain that won’t be necessary, Mr. Laker.” Warmth through the bond further let him know it was a very appreciated statement, though. He had not meant his powers at all, and she knew it.

Little Bite was another super-colorful building with an unassuming sign as a label, but it was a walk-in place. There was a small line going out the door, and sweet smells tantalizing from within.

“We’re lucky,” Jack proclaimed as they got in line. “Usually, the line is twice this long. Worse in the daylight.”

“Oh, I see,” Neex said. “It smells wonderful!”

The line slowly but slowly moved. A humbling experience after so many privileges in the ivory tower, his time as a cadet treated like a sacrament, never to be squandered. Here, they were just two people in a mass; ants in the hill.

People constantly squeezed in and out of the door to get ordered take-out. That was Jack’s prior standard here, but he knew Neex would want to sit among the people for the authentic experience.

Neex kept sneaking glances at a middle-aged man behind them. And then she finally bit the bullet, turning and offering her hand. “Hi! I’m Nichola! How are you today?”

The man seemed shocked to be addressed, coming out of the standard, almost meditative silence that was ‘minding one’s own business.’ However, it was a very pretty and disarming girl saying hi to him, and — considering what she was doing — possibly ‘special’ in some way, so he worked himself out of his stunned state to take her hand and nod politely. “Good.” An awkward pause, and he added, “You?”

Neex blinked. “Me? Me what?”

The man’s eyes flickered, and he cleared his throat. “How are you?”

“Oh! I’m great, thanks for asking, sir! We just picked some berries.”

“I see that. The park? Surprised they ain’t picked clean by now.”

“Oh, no, we went to this secret place full of bushes, it’s over by this-”

Jack cleared his throat loudly, turning with a smile and interrupting with, “I know a guy. The family doesn’t even pick the damn bushes. A shame letting them go to nothing but the birds, you know?”

The man nodded slowly. “I hear ya.”

Neex’s eyes were flickering in confusion, but she got the hint, soon smiling and nodding. “Did you want some, sir? Mikey tells me they go well on the waffles and the french toasts, and there’s plenty of berries here.”

“Ah, no, thank you, miss. Nothing sweet for me ‘till after dinner. Black tea and an omelet for me. Best in the city. Best most things they serve, really.”

Jack nodded. “I’d come here every day except for the price and the hassle. Mostly the hassle.” Not that the price means anything now.

The man smiled faintly. “Yup. This is my off-day. Wanted to sleep in, but here I am. Got time to kill.”

“Well, enjoy your food, sir.”

“You too.”

They got past the door after a bit, filing into a cramped, diner-like atmosphere that had Neex sweeping her head and absorbing every little minute detail of the decor. The walls were covered in old photos and other bits of retro art. They ended up letting their new acquaintance cut in line to take a single seat at the counter and waited on a table for two.

Finally, they were seated, and both of them ordered french toast, which included a ton of toppings in separate little cups. Neex’s eyes widened at the incredibly fat, fluffy treat put in front of her. Not surprising — there was truly nothing like it anywhere else.

Neex sampled each thing with her tongue before deciding on what went on the actual toast with the berries. Jack was already eating, and she mimicked him smushing the berries and mixing things together. Once she started eating, she kept at it with firm, mechanical focus, brow knitted together, nodding and making possibly involuntary pleased sounds in her throat. She got through it quickly.

When it was finished, she dropped her fork and knife and gave a thumbs-up with a smile. “It was incredible!”

Laughing, Jack nodded his agreement as he finally threw down his napkin. “That it was. So… I was thinking, if you would indulge me, I’d like to take you to the Hanging Gardens, the official and absolutely beautiful-”

“Yes!” Neex exclaimed excitedly, clasping her hands together and curling them inward toward her neck. “I have heard of this wonder of humanity and greatly wish to see it! I would love to go!”

“Well, alright then. Off we go, I suppose.”

Neex’s eyes widened in sudden hesitation, and she quickly grabbed her nearly untouched cup of dark tea. Her eyes flickered rather greedily at all the people around, so close together that people had to shift themselves to let anyone anywhere nearby. In a hopeful tone, she asked, “A-after we finish our tea?”

Jack grinned and held up his cup, as if for a toast. “After that, sure. But not too long. Lots of people are waiting, after all.”

“Oh. Yes. True.” Nonetheless, she took the cup in both hands and slid down slightly in her seat, sipping and watching the people, listening to the din that was dozens of simultaneous conversations mixed with the calls of orders and the general hustle and bustle of the crew at work. Clearly, she was going to take advantage of what time they had.

I think Memoria has a competitor for Humanity’s Biggest Fan.

The Hanging Gardens of New Babylon were essentially art come to life. However that depictions might’ve differed from reality, Memoria had it made as glorious as it could ever be imagined. White marble made a mountain of tiered gardens saturated with many resplendent colors of plantlife, most of the tiers walkable through continual pillar supports. Fountains were never out of sight, nor were statues of Assyrian or Babylonian style, including legendary human figures, gods, and, particularly, mythological creatures like the Mushshushu. What walls existed were always painted brick and included more art, often gold on blue.

It was surrounded by water, as if in the middle of a river, and the tiers were fed by various Archimedes Screws in continual operation — big open screws that turned and pulled water upward. They visited a hand-cranked version feeding a drinking well, and Neex was all smiles as she operated it, gushing about tales of the purported inventor.

Yep. She’s a huge nerd.

For the most part, the place was simply a visual delight to walk through. They also ate some overpriced garden-borne fruit, including medleys you couldn’t hardly find elsewhere, as the place focused more on variety than cash crops. It was the only place one could eat figs, for instance. Eh. They were alright.

Neex adored everything, stopping to read the copious plaques and marveling at the many plant species she’d never seen. She had a notebook with her too, writing in it often, apparently writing down ideas that the things there gave her. Carnivorous plants especially fascinated her, which wasn’t surprising considering how huge a nerd she was.

After a while, they moved on. The temple next door, the Ziggurat of Marduk, was only partially open due to some religious thing, but the religious folk who ran and owned the place were big on the Statue of Marduk always being available to the public, so they went in to see it. It was a towering wooden statue of the deity, covered in gigantic jewelry, and under guard twenty-four seven both to prevent theft of the valuables, and to avoid fire, since it was technically flammable. Jack imagined it would be exceedingly hard to do, though.

As she stared waaay up near the base of the thing, amidst a smattering of other tourists and people coming to pray, Neex leaned close to Jack and whispered, “It’s very nice. A one-of-a-kind sight. Do you pray?”

Jack stifled a snicker. “That’s a negative, Nichola. I’m not even close to a believer.”

“And what about Memoria?”

“Oh, I rather believe in her, suffice to say.”

Neex grinned. “Me too.”

Jack next took her to the other high-priority spot: Downtown Atalanta, particularly the Runner’s Strip, as it approached the lunch hour. It was plenty busy already, and the biggest hubbub he could conjure for her.

Thankfully, she wasn’t hard to please, perpetually filled with wonder and excitement. They had a genetically engineered, super-sweet golden apple. They saw and heard a random screaming match between two angry drunks. They chatted with much more friendly people, such as a clown and a cotton candy vendor with the biggest smile on the planet — Neex was a close second. They helped an older, profusely sweating man move some rugs into a shop, for which he thanked them profusely, as his assistant had called in sick. ‘They’ bought more junk. Jack caught a small, hard ball out of the thin air, and no matter how hard they searched and asked around, they could not find the owner. It became another souvenir!

They hit the limited fairgrounds, riding the classic Ferris Wheel (Neex required some persuading and was a bit spooked for some reason), also going through a mirror maze called ‘The Goddess’s A-Maze-Ing Cycle!’, and then doing bumper cars. Neex loved side-swiping him, giggling like the gremlin she was every time, eyes shining with glee. Hilariously, Neex was afraid of the rickety-looking rollercoaster and refused to ride it.

Jack swore she would ride everything in Rollercoaster World one day, and she would love it. It was more of a whole-day vacation commitment, though. You had to book that shit in advance to get the sweet deals.

As they walked out of the fairgrounds, back to the Strip to go get some decent food, Jack was still needling her about it. Neex shook her head emphatically, even as she hugged his arm. “Nooo!” she pouted dramatically in denial of his sworn intentions, hanging her head and pretending to stumble around as though weakened by the mere thought. “I caaan’t!”

Jack nodded firmly, all in all remaining an unmovable, stoic pillar. “It’s happening, Nichola. Just accept your fate. You’ll thank me like one stinkin’ ride in, trust me.”

“Noooo…”

“Trust.”

“Mmmph! B-but they’re too much! Mechanical dragons you cram yourself into the guts of! Too many moving parts needing to work in perfection thousands of times in a row. The stars only know how many screws have popped loose over time and wear and tear! They’re absurd. I want no part of them! Th-they offend me.”

“You rode in the equivalent of a fraggin’ jet with me, gremlin girl! And you were smilin’ ear-to-ear.”

“That’s different.”

“How?”

She blinked and looked over at him with an earnest expression. “Because I don’t trust rollercoaster operators absolutely. I trust you absolutely.”

Jack almost missed a step. It wasn’t as if he didn’t know that, basically, but at that moment, for whatever reason, it really caught him. He felt his ears burning. He looked away, as if something else caught his attention, and gave a noncommittal grunt to cover. “Yes, well, we’ll see, I guess. Not today, in any case.”

Neex curled adroitly, almost inhumanly, like a snake, to study him from around his body, her tacky star sunglasses flipped up. She grew a slow, indulgent smile as her eyes scanned his face. “Have I detected human blushing?! Y-your ears!”

“Not at all,” Jack said smoothly. So very smooth. “It’s the weather getting chillier.”

“False! It would not happen in a span of seconds, in combination with your stricken facial expression after my words, and looking away, and that slightly embarrassed grunt! The timing would not be so connected.”

Crap! She’s getting more observant! “Pfft. You’re overthinking it. Anyway, if you’re going to be all logical, what about the chances of a serious accident on a rollercoaster?”

“I-... mmmph! You’re changing the subject!”

Jack just shook his head, and Neex kept peering at him in satisfaction with her lips bunched up, like the cat that ate the canary.

Damn. But that’s alright. I’ll have my revenge. My secret weapon: the water park. It sounds so innocuous, so innocent. But one watery thrill ride later, and she’ll be hooked! Muhuhahaha.

They were busy having some lunch at the best burrito place on the planet, with a great view from the rooftop and no rush required, when Jack got a message, apparently straight from Memoria, straight into his brain. <Jack, I know exactly what you’re doing right now, and I’m sorry to interrupt.> A feeling of genuine regret and hesitation came through. <I know it’s important for both of you. However, recent news is pertinent and potentially urgent, and you’d be upset if I didn’t pull you aside about it nigh the instant I knew.>

Fighting a sudden sinking feeling, Jack chewed and swallowed his mouthful of burrito and put it down on the plate, glancing at Neex.

She instantly recognized he’d gotten some kind of notice. She smiled supportively and reached across the table to take and squeeze his hand, leaning in to say, “It’s alright! This has already been one of the best days of my life, one I’ll cherish forever. There will always be more to do later.”

Jack nodded reluctantly and smiled back, then looked to the side as he responded back to Memoria. <Alright. What is it, Mother?>

<I’m not going to tell you this way, Agent. You need to come in. Shortly thereafter — hopefully — you’ll be getting to work.>
