The droid look around with interest, seeming as out of place in John's workshop as it did everywhere else.

“I’m not going to hurt you, so just sit right there,” John said, tapping the back of the metal chair.

The military droid lowered itself onto it. Hydraulics whined. The aerial drone idled on John's work bench, inert for now.

John, meanwhile, sat down on his favorite stool."I'm coming over."

John rolled in on his swivel stool until his knees touched the droid’s shin plates. Up close the damage looked worse. The chestplate had been spread open and torn, though not to a high degree. Thick ballistic laminate bowed outward around a jagged oval. The rest of the armor still had an old-issue, military patena matte to it. He’d seen similar plating in the field, just never on a frame this dated.

He leaned into the split chestplate and peered inside, taking the opportunity to get a better look than he'd been able to in the Undercity.

Blue light pulsed within the droid's chest cavity. A crystal heart. Black veins spidered off the soulcore into the droid’s wiring and circuit boards. There was a rhymatic pulse through the core and into the circuits, then back again into the core. Power fed, and power returned. Like a real, non-organic sort of heartbeat.

John dragged a finger along the torn edge of the droid's plating. “What even did this to you?”

The droid looked down at him. It shrugged. As if something that could tear open plating that should be resistant to everything up to concentrated rifle fire wasn't a big deal.

“So you’re definitely alive, aren’t you,” John said, mostly to himself. “Or getting there. That’s for sure a soulcore growing in your chest.”

The droid’s single optic whirred, confused.

“Athena,” John said telepathically. “Now that we can really look at him… you still think so too?”

She manifested next to him, pale blue and seteadier than she'd been in the past few hours. Her pupiless eyes reflecting the blue glow faintly emanating from the droid's damage. “He has a weak thread echo. So yes, in a way he is alive. But the echoes lean numeric more than linguistic in their sound.”

“You can read his mind?” John asked, surprised. "See that it's more numbers than words?"

“No. No more than I can read anyone else’s. I can feel the resonance of his echo across my own. There is no context, I only somewhat I feel what its thinking feels like. Just like you can see what a person looks like."

“So, it's what: half alive then?”

Athena folded her arms, thinking herself. “It is a safe assumption. He may possess proto-emotions built over encoded processes. The best parallel I can make is a mana-generated consciousness is growing on his programing like a symbiotic mold."John rocked back on the stool, looking back to the droid. “I don’t have anything that matches your original plating. And I’m not sure I'm comfortable digging around inside you if you’re more than just a machine. But I can seal the hole.” He tapped the cracked edge in question. “If you want.”

The droid traced the gap on its chest with its fingered hand, then nodded.

“All right,” John said. “I’m good with metal and circuits. But fair warning, I have zero experience beyond tactical care with anything living. That core, and the rest of you, looks a lot less like flesh and a lot more like some sort of weird glass though. You probably need an engineer more than a doctor anyway.”

The droid tilted its head, some concern being readable in the motion.

John smirked. “I’ll leave the hole if you want.”

The droid gave a small, definite shake of its head in the negative.

“Thought so.” He grabbed his welding kit and visor, then fished a handheld plasma cutter from a drawer. Tools clinked onto his workbench. He tipped open the crate of cyber-scrap Kaito had sent and hauled out a heavy metallic cyber leg with a rectangular armor plate skinned over the front.

He set the leg on the table. “This has a decent metal. It’ll stop pistols, maybe short bursts from a SMG. But it won’t match the rest of you in durability, so don’t go catching rifle fire with it. Not unless you want that core you have to get hit.”

The droid watched while John stripped the leg’s plating, popped fasteners, slid out the panel, then measured against the wound by eye. John thumbed the plasma cutter alive, dropped his visor over his face, and trimmed the metal to fit the droid's damage. Sparks hissed as he worked.

When John was satisfied, he brought the plate over to the droid and carried his welder in his other hand. “Last chance to back out.”

The droid shook its head again.

“Hm. If this hurts, flail or something,” John said, bracing the patch metal against the droid.

Arc light bloomed. The droid flinched at the first spit of sparks from John's welder, then settled. John rolled the bead slow and clean. No slag, no voids. He tied the edge to the original armor and chased a final pass to seal micro-cracks.

He killed the torch and lifted his visor. His patch job sat flush. The weld line read clean. The color of the two metals didn’t match, but from three feet it might pass as just a fluke of lighting.

Still got it, he thought to himself but didn't say out loud.

The droid pressed its one palm to the repair, then reached out and set that same hand on John’s shoulder. It gave one short nod. Then a thumbs up.

“Glad you’re happy,” John said, surprised at the droid thanking him so vigorously.

He glanced at the cylindrical, hollow stump of the robot's other forearm. A gun barrel where a hand should be, the caliber small. He then looked back to his scrap crate. A tangle of hands, wrists, and limbs glinted back.

“How do you feel about getting an actual hand? Something you can use when you’re not shooting. We could refit the barrel of your gun under the palm. Then I’ll tackle your leg?"

The droid tipped its head, then pointed a question at the crate.

It took John a few seconds to figure out what the droid was trying to communicate to him.

“You want to pick it?”

A fast, eager nod came back in reply from the automaton.

“Go ahead. But if what you choose can’t host the barrel, I’ll have to shoot it down.” He said.

The droid stood happily from his chair. Then rummaged like a small child in a thrift bin of stuffed animals, careful even with its bulk but clearly excited. It sifted through steel and polymer until it found a forearm with a hand molded in a bright, candy pink.

The only splash of neon color in the whole box, John realized, besides some construction yellow.

John blinked. “You sure?”

The droid's optic brightened as it cradled its selection. It gave another excited nod.

Athena looked up from where she leaned on the workbench, her mouth curving up. “That is somewhat endearing. Are you going to tell him no, John? We both know that arm looks quite modular and tweakable."

John held out a hand to the droid, sighing at Athena's minor jest. “All right. Let me see it.”

The droid passed the arm to him like it was a fragile sculpture. John took it and turned it over in his hands. The arm was of a newer-generation than his own. Tight tolerances. Good motors. Someone had paid for quality and then painted it bubblegum pink for whatever impulsive reason.

He didn’t remember pulling a pink-limbed ghoul apart, but then again that job had been hectic.

He looked back at the droid's barrel-forearm. “I'll need to plug both you and this into my terminal and make sure you're compatible. Best case your bus protocols talk with minimal translation. Worst case I write a whole sheet of patch code drivers."

Athena’s voice touched his thoughts, cool and practical. "We should isolate his bios and the aerial drone's behind a sandbox environment either way. Your firewalls and diagnostics are overdue for an upgrade. We have neglected them."

"You’re right," he answered her silently. "I'm mostly not so much worried about him, but the Grin drone is next and I’m not trusting that thing without a quarantine protocol."

"It will not take us long to create and spin one up," she said.

John set the pink arm on the bench within the droid’s sightline. “Change of plans. I’m going to have to code some before I wire the arm into you. Want me to fix your shot leg first? Looks like bad servos more than anything inherently structural. Shouldn't be hard to get you walking straight again."

The droid’s optic lingered on the pink arm like it longed for it. Then it shrugged.

John actually laughed a bit in response. “We’ll get there.” He gestured up and squared the droid in place. “Stand for me.”

John circled the droid, meaning to move to examine the robot's bad leg until his eyes caught a faded decal on its right shoulder plate instead. He leaned closer, expecting to see a unit crest of some kind than might help him identify where and when the droid had served.

Instead, he saw a tiny helmeted figure with a plume and black skin.

“What’s this?” he murmured.

Athena drifted to look over John's own shoulder, eyes brightening in the way they did when she dove into the threadnet. “It appears to be a depiction of a pre-collapse children’s animation. A martian character.”

“There aren’t any martians,” John said telepathically, thinking to himself that Mars had long ago been colonized, and more recently been abandoned behind an impassable threadstorm that had breached into actual realspace. He squinted at the old, cracking ink of the decal. “Guess the cartoon could have come before we went there if it's pre-collapse, though.”

“Yes. The character predates interplanetary settlement by centuries,” Athena said, her eyes still glowing. “However, the fictional universe it existed within remained popular nearly until the collapse itself.”

“Someone probably thought it was cute to slap him on you,” John said to the droid. “Might mean you’re older than we thought.”

The droid craned its neck to look at John again.

“You remember anything about this?” John asked.

It gave a slower shake of its head this time. Slower and uncertain.

“Perhaps it represents a designation he once went under,” Athena offered.

“Think it could jog anything?” John asked her. “But I guess even if it did, he couldn't tell us. Never known a frame like this to ship with a voice or the digital infrastructure to support installing one. I could maybe get him talking, but it'd be a hell of a lot of work..."

“I do not know,” Athena said. “The character's name in the fiction was Marvin, if you still wish to attempt to ask him about it.”

John sat with what she'd said for a few second, then nodded. He looked up to the droid. “The name Marvin mean anything to you?”

The droid did not move for three long seconds. Then it nodded as well. And slowly tapped its own chest.

“That’s your name?” John blinked. “You’re Marvin?”

Another nod.

John let out a sigh. “Nice to meet you properly, Marvin.” He straightened. “Your name stir anything else up in that head?"

The droid shook its head in the negative again, much more sure of itself this time.

“All right. Let’s fix your leg, Marvin,” John said. “After that maybe we can work on your new hand. And then maybe I'll buy you some heavier firepower to hold with it. If you still want to do the whole shooting things with me, thing?”

John wasn't really impressed with the droid's gun arm. It would be a useful side-arm, especially if they concealed it beneath a new hand. However, it didn't pack much stopping power. And John would've been shocked if Marvin hadn't come pre-installed with weapon handling software.

The droid’s posture brightened. It mimed shouldering a rifle with enthusiastically earnest care and shot off a few fake bullets. If it could, John guessed the droid would've made pew-pew noises.

“Glad that makes you excited,” John said, amused.

Marvin froze, almost sheepish, then rubbed at the new chest patch like he could dust his own embarrassment off of himself.

“Don’t worry about it,” John said, rolling his stool to his larger tool chest. “Let’s get to work on your leg."
