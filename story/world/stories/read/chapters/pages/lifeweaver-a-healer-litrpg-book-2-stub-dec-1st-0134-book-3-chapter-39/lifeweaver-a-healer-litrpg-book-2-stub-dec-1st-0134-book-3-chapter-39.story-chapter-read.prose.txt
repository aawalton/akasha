After explaining to Milla why he wouldn't be able to follow the others back to headquarters, Luke told Lemira and Elijah he needed to go and why.

"I will join you. As a part of The Band, it is my duty to see to the safety of all its members," Lemira said. The words were haughty, but the sincerity on her face was more telling.

"I don't know who this Curtis fellow is," Elijah added. "But if a fellow needs help, I'm not one to shirk my duty."

Hannah, who'd walked up to the small group talking, as well as Ray, who'd just returned from his assassination mission. They, too, wanted to come.

"With Curtis, that's a full party," Ray said, grinning.

"Fine," Luke said. "But we'll need to move fast. Haven't been able to reach him."

"How will you know where to find him?" Lemira asked as they hurried toward the portal that would take them out of the dungeon.

"I can find anyone I've ever healed."

"Creepy ability, that," Ray noted.

Lemira didn't share his opinion. "A healer able to find all those who might need his help again? I think that is a fine thing."

Nash: "Hey Luke, where are you going? Need some help?"

Luke: "I'll meet up with you guys later. We'll need to move fast to find and help a friend."

Nash: "Speed, huh? Well, that's fine. I'll think of something for next time."

Luke wasn't sure what the man meant, but there was no time to waste in finding out. As they stepped out through the portal to land back in the city, Luke groaned.

"Sorry, guys, but I'll need to go alone."

"Why?" Hannah asked.

"He's way out near the edge of the city. It's too far to run."

Ray raised an eyebrow. "So, how were you planning to get there? As far as I've seen, you're not able to fly yet."

"I've got my ways," Luke said as he sat down on a nearby bench, careful to avoid a small pile of bird crap.

"Fine. Be that way," Ray said, crossing his arms over his chest. "Keep your secrets."

"What should we do?" Lemira asked.

"We could steal a car," Ray suggested.

Hannah kept an unimpressed look on her face. "I can travel."

"To a person?" Luke asked.

"Well, no," Hannah admitted. "It doesn't work like that. But you can show me on here." Holding up her phone, she opened the map and pointed to it. "Where?"

Luke showed her.

"That is pretty far, alright," Ray said.

Elijah looked thoughtful. "I can make that run in half an hour."

"That fast?" Luke asked.

Lemira set her jaw and held up a hand. A bright glow surrounded it for a moment, then her llama-monster popped into existence. It looked a little different from before. Rather than ferocious and somewhat unhinged, it appeared placid and calm. Its legs were longer and its frame a little narrower than before.

"I will ride with Elijah," Lemira said.

"Can that thing carry two people?" Ray asked, looking a little apprehensive. "I'm not fast over long distances."

"Certainly," Lemira said, giving Ray a look as if weighing him. "Just keep your hands to yourself."

Hannah chortled. "She's got you pegged already, Ray."

"No one has ever pegged me, dammit," Ray said, before raising his hands to show he'd be on his best behavior. "Why does everyone think I'm some sort of deviant?"

"The vibe," Hannah said.

"Quiet down now. I have to concentrate to find my way to Curtis," Luke said. "I will see you there. Be careful. If you get lost, reach out. If I don't answer, message Milla, and she'll help you get back to headquarters."

Having informed them of what he was about to do, Luke got to it. He'd done this exact thing once before, or a much more difficult version of it, but didn't relish the thought of making another attempt. Last time, blood had pretty much exploded out of everywhere on his body. It hadn't been a pleasant experience, to say the least. He'd need to use that skill again.

Manipulate Thread [Passive]: Reach out a trembling finger and touch the thread.

Calming his mind, Luke entered a meditation-like trance after failing a few times since Ray kept talking while they mounted the llama. With that first step done, Luke immersed himself in that state of nothingness without trying to reach any particular goal. There was no speeding this up. Anything worth doing is worth doing right. Soon, he floated in the vast field of nothing that was his blank mind, and with a nudge of focus, he willed the threads to appear.

Faint at first, they appeared. Only when Luke focused on a specific thread did the weave present itself in all its glory. He wasn't quite sure how he was able to tell, but he knew this was not the same weave as the one he saw on the brink of losing consciousness. This was not the weave of The Greater System. No, this was the weave of his system. The system that had integrated him and everyone else, the system that had elevated Earth and set them free from the dreadful monotony and normalcy of life.

With the fixed idea of finding Curtis in mind, Luke sorted through the infinite number of threads, relying on the echo part of Weaver's Perception to find just the one he was after. There. He'd found it. The single-mindedness and focus reveal a thread different from all others. Well... Perhaps not different, but it brought with it a sense of intent, almost like Luke's will made the fabric of that particular piece of the system a little more noticeable.

It was enough for Luke, who grabbed hold and used Weavestep without hesitation. A surge of power ran through his body in the moment he disappeared, and the world shifted around him. All the air was forced from his lungs, and it felt like his heart would explode in his chest, but he'd done it again. Luke had traveled along one of the threads of the system to arrive right beside his destination. Curtis.

Who was in the sorrier state, Luke wasn't sure. Blood poured from his own eyes, making it difficult to see, as well as from his nose and ears. Blood also exploded from his throat, erupting in a gushing stream. Most of that ended up on Curtis's ravaged body. As luck would have it, the Reaver was unconscious. If he'd been awake, Luke would have found himself at the end of a long string of curses. Perhaps even violence. No, it was best for everyone that Curtis was taking a little nap. A nap that would have seen him dead if he'd been left alone for another hour or so. That part wasn't ideal, but it was nothing a little healing wouldn't fix.

A chunk of his midsection was missing, torn out of his side, clothes, flesh, and all, but the edges of that massive wound had been burned to the point of blackening. The stench of it still filled the air of what appeared to be a supply closet.

That single wound wasn't all, of course, but it was enough to account for the unconsciousness. Either it was that one, or the way the left side of his skull appeared to be broken. Rather than ponder the seriousness of the wounds, Luke set to healing them.

Moving along, that thread had taken so much mana that Luke didn't have enough to just use Weaver's Renewal and then hold up his hand for a high-five, at least not on his own. The patient in question didn't have much to spare either, which was a bummer. He'd have to go about this the manual way, at least for the first step.

Blinking again and again to get the blood out of his eyes, Luke went to work. One thread for the heart to keep it pumping, one for the lungs, and one for the meta-heart, Curtis was ready to have his skull checked out. The wound in his side meant he was missing a bunch of flesh. Suturing the wound shut would lead to a weird stride and a host of other problems, even if it worked. Since that was out of the question, he would instead focus on the skull. Since the skin on Curtis's head was unbroken, that, at least, meant that all parts were present.

During a brief period of boredom when Luke was about thirteen years old, he picked up a hobby of doing 3D-puzzles. They were difficult to get right, but he'd persevered. After spending a good couple of weeks on a 500-piece replica of The Statue of Liberty, and even finishing it, he'd taken a long look at it and then smashed it against the floor, breaking the damn thing back into what felt more like a million little pieces than 500.

Piecing the bits of skull back into place inside a person's head with nothing more than what amounted to a malleable needle and thread was a steeper challenge by far, even if he controlled them with his mind. Good thing he was older and far more skilled by the time he sat down to piece together the puzzle that was Curtis's skull.

Luke hummed to himself as he worked, finding that he enjoyed the process. As far as Weaver's Perception could tell, there was no one else nearby, so it wasn't like he needed to stay quiet. This brief moment of silence and concentration would do him good, Luke was sure. Blinking, he froze. What sort of thinking was that? A friend was close to dying, lying slumped against the wall and bleeding out. Callous didn't even begin to describe it. Had Luke been through so much that even this didn't move him?

As he got back to the work at hand, ignoring the mess of now-drying blood, he considered that question. At that point, Luke had healed a person whose head was all but gone. Yes, he might've forgotten who he was, but Elijah still lived. Losing limbs was nothing. Having mana meant Luke could come back from almost anything, and there was plenty of that to go around. Was this nonchalance around death and the injured something brought about by his own increased power? If not, then what?

Luke knew this was not who he'd been before touching the orb and becoming Integrated. The block-thing Guide mentioned putting in his head. Was that responsible? It wasn't out of the question, but somehow Luke didn't think so, at least not that alone. Either way, he would need to be careful, or he might lose what it meant to be human, if Integrated could even be considered human anymore.

"There," he said under his breath, having finished the work of fusing Curtis's bits of skull back together. Some blood had pooled between the skull and the brain, but it was a simple enough problem to solve. Having drained that and then examined the brain itself to make sure it wasn't damaged, Luke checked back in with his mana pool.

Nodding to himself, he also checked Curtis's mana. "Should be enough."

Mana regeneration was a topic he hadn't spent a lot of time investigating because, well, there just didn't seem to be any downtime in his life, ever. Other people would've figured out how that all worked, he was sure. A cursory search online might even be enough to find the information he was after, but even that wouldn't do much more than confirm what his gut was telling him. Ever since picking the Lifeweaver class and starting out at Level 1, it was as if he could intuit what was happening inside his meta-heart, the piece of him responsible for mana regeneration. When he was fighting, regeneration slowed. Sitting down and taking it easy? Mana ticked up faster. Meditating? Even faster.

The act of healing was somewhere between relaxing and meditating, as long as he wasn't doing it in the middle of a fight. When other people were involved, that was when the topic started getting complex. If Luke were healing someone, and another person fought right next to them, the mana regeneration suffered. If he was healing the one doing the actual fighting, it was even worse, but still not quite as bad as when he fought himself.

Experience, he decided. It was experience that let him get a rough approximation of his mana pool at all times. Though when he wanted to be precise, it was best to just either check his interface or gauge the level of mana in his own meta-heart through Weaver's Perception. Curtis's mana was a little more difficult to get a good read on through that skill alone, but a thread of mana solved that issue. With one of those bad boys inside the metaphysical organ, Luke could read anyone like an open book. At least mana-level-wise.

So, perhaps it wasn't that he hadn't found the time to investigate mana regeneration. It might just be that he didn't need to. Using the skills and senses at hand gave him enough information about the state of himself and those around him.

"Rise and shine," Luke said as he used Weaver's Renewal, having wrapped the injured area in Curti's midsection with a thread of mana first. Doing it the new way, activating the skill inside his patient's meta-heart, was more powerful and ensured the entire body got in on the action, but the mana cost was staggering. Best to keep some mana at hand in case of emergency.

Curtis stirred as his flesh grew back, the burned bits falling off like the charred bits on a flaking hot dog. Ew.

"How are you doing, big guy?" Luke asked.

"Fine," Curtis said with a grunt. He brought a hand up to give his skull a gentle prodding. "Bit of a headache."

"I bet."

Curtis looked up at Luke, then frowned. "You fought them, too? You look like shit."

"The Intruder?"

"I'll take that as a no," Curtis said, checking the now-healed wound in his side. The new flesh was a little pinkish and would stay like that, almost like a huge scar. Not even Weaver's Renewal was perfect, Luke had found. "It was Intruders. Like I said. Multiple."

"I'm going to need a little more info than that, Curtis."

"Yeah, yeah," Curtis said, groaning as he got to his feet. Lots of bruises and scrapes still remained. Those would have to wait. "I was following one, like I said, but I guess he knew. Lured me into... not an ambush, maybe, but a lot of them waited for him. Once I got here, they knew and rushed me. They're strong, Luke, very strong."

"Damn," Luke said, unsure what to add. "Sneaky."

That got a chuckle out of Curtis, which made him groan in pain again, which, in turn, made Luke burst out laughing. Once their laughter died down and Curtis stopped wincing, he put a smile on his face. "At least I found what they've been doing."

"What?" Luke asked, following Curtis through the door out of the closet and into a staircase.

Starting down the stairs, Curtis waved for him to follow. "Come, I'll show you."
