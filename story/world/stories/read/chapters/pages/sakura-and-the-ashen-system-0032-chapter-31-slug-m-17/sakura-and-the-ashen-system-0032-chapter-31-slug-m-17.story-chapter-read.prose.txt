Gilbert pushed the surviving door panel. It gave way with an unpleasant metallic screech.

Inside, the place turned out to be more spacious than Sakura had expected. The room was long, with a low ceiling and rows of broken tables. Pipes jutted out of the walls. Some had burst, and dried white growths hung from them. Shards of glass, metal trays, broken clamps, old masks, and bones lay scattered across the floor. Far too many bones.

Slime was everywhere here.

It had seeped under the tables, hardened on the shelves, sealed the cabinets, and coated the old tools. At the far end of the hall stood a huge tank with cracked glass. There was nothing inside except whitish streaks and dark stains on the bottom.

Sakura slowly walked between the tables, trying not to kick anything.

"So many deaths must have happened here..."

"Very many," Gilbert replied, examining the broken frame of some device. Then he found a cabinet whose door had been jammed shut by dried slime. He looked at it for several seconds, then raised his hand. A thin white-blue arc snapped from his fingers and struck the lock neatly. It cracked.

He opened the cabinet. Inside were bundles of records. Some had rotted, some had stuck together, but several metal cases had survived. Gilbert pulled one out, opened it, and took out thin plates with lines etched into them.

"Records," he said.

Sakura came closer. Part of the text was ruined, but separate phrases were still readable.

PRIMARY SAMPLE STABLE.

SLIME HAS A PRONOUNCED PAIN-RELIEVING EFFECT.

EUPHORIC RESPONSE OBSERVED AFTER MAGICAL PROCESSING.

Sakura felt her face darken on its own.

"So it started as medicine."

"A good intention. Not a good result."

Gilbert turned the plate over. There were more lines on the other side, written in smaller text.

REVENUE FROM PRODUCTION IS INCREASING.

PRODUCTION VOLUME MUST BE INCREASED.

SAMPLE RESPONDS MORE STRONGLY TO PAIN STIMULUS THAN TO FOOD STIMULUS.

Sakura inhaled slowly.

"So then someone decided pain was more profitable?"

"Yes."

Gilbert said it evenly, but something hard entered his voice. Sakura did not think he was surprised. Most likely, it was not even disgust, but recognition.

Sakura looked at him, but did not ask. She already knew enough not to dig her fingers into that place. Gilbert had once been locked in a similar place too, where people were turned into a resource and proof of someone else’s truth. Where they were hurt and someone calmly recorded it in papers. Behind Gilbert’s calmness and smile there had always been that terrible fact: he was the last mage of the south, a man who had survived something Sakura could not even fully imagine.

They found several more records. Most of them only confirmed what was already becoming clear. Something had been kept under the city. At first, it had been studied, then forced to produce more slime. Then, judging by the fragments, something had gone wrong.

The text on one plate had survived better than the rest:

SLUG M-17.

Slime production: unstable.

Pain response: excessive.

Signs of understanding: increasing.

Sakura stared at those lines for a long time, then said quietly, "Even a slug in this world got screwed by bureaucracy."

Gilbert gave a short snort. Not exactly a laugh, but close.

"You are reading a record about a monster that is most likely still somewhere here."

"I understand. It’s just that if I don’t joke, I’ll have to start crying. Trust me, that would be worse."

"I can’t imagine you crying. Since the moment we met, despite everything happening around you, you still haven’t cried."

Sakura exhaled heavily, unable to find anything to say. All of this was too complicated for her, and she preferred not to think too deeply. Otherwise she really would cry, and that was exactly what she did not want. She would find time to process all this horror and despair someday later. So Sakura simply looked away and glanced at the tank again. At the cracked glass. At the old clamps set into the floor. At the chains by the wall, covered in a whitish crust.

Why couldn’t everything have been different?

Why couldn’t she just fall into a normal sewer, walk through it, find an exit, and get out? Why did everything around her have to turn into some insane mess with laboratories, narcotic slime, dead people, and a creature that had started as a sample, then became a monster and someone’s product?

"Why is everything this bad again?" she finally asked out loud.

"Because this is the kind of world you ended up in," Gilbert replied.

Sakura slowly turned her head toward him. From her words and actions, Gilbert was already starting to guess that she was not from this world. As always, he was frighteningly perceptive.

"Yeah. I got thrown pretty far off course," she answered.

"That must be a very fascinating story."

"It’s a very unusual story. And I’ll definitely tell it to you when we have time for long conversations without fear of death."

Gilbert laughed quietly. Maybe because Sakura’s words about “time for long conversations without fear of death” sounded too much like a fairy tale. Something that might never happen.

They continued through the laboratory.

Beyond the first hall was a second. Empty cages stood there. Not human cages, more likely meant for large animals or creatures. Some bars were bent outward, and marks from impacts still remained on the walls. Round holes gaped in the floor, leading down into dark pipes. Slime slowly drained into them, thick as saliva, as if the dungeon itself were still trying to digest something.

Sakura tried not to step in the slime, but it was getting harder. Sometimes it stretched after her sole in thin threads, and everything inside her tightened from the sight. What if it was eating through her shoes? The bodies covered in slime had often looked as if they had taken a little bath in sulfuric acid.

"How many years do you think this crap has been here?" Sakura asked, looking at the whitish streaks on the wall.

Gilbert lit the nearest clot, but did not touch it.

"I don’t know."

Sakura suddenly let out an almost pleased breath.

"I love when you say ‘I don’t know.’ Really. It helps me feel a little less useless."

Gilbert looked at her.

"You are not useless, Sakura."

"I’m a person who almost died recently because of a ring in a wall. Let me have my version."

"You did not know how the mechanism worked. That is different. I think your potential will reveal itself soon. It simply needs more time."

Sakura half-smiled, but almost immediately grimaced again. The sickly sweet rotten smell that had been following them for a while was getting stronger. It clung to her throat, clogged her nose, and was already starting to throb heavily in her temples.

Gilbert noticed it too. He stopped, looked around the second hall, and walked over to one of the overturned lockers. Inside, under a layer of dust, were several rough cloth masks like the ones the collectors had worn. Most had rotted or hardened from old slime, but two were still folded in oiled paper and looked almost whole.

"So that’s why they had masks," the mage said. "The slime is toxic. Better not test how much longer we can breathe it in."

Sakura nodded. Gilbert shook the dust off the masks, then handed one to Sakura.

"Cover your nose and mouth. Tightly."

Sakura took the mask with two fingers, clearly without enthusiasm. She had no desire to press such old fabric against her face. But she had even less desire to be poisoned by the smell.

She tied the cloth over the lower half of her face. The odor did not disappear completely, but it grew duller, heavier, and no longer struck directly into her throat. Gilbert put his mask on too and raised the flame again, suddenly making her stop.

At first, Sakura heard nothing. Then the sound repeated.

It was a rasp, quiet and wet.

They exchanged a look. Gilbert raised the flame higher again, making it brighter. The light stretched down the corridor and pulled a side room out of the darkness, almost completely overgrown with slime. There, beside an overturned table, lay a man.

Sakura froze.

He was alive. Still alive.

The man lay on his side, half-sunk in the whitish mass. His clothes were the same as the collectors’ clothes: a dark cloak, torn in places as if burned. His mask had been ripped off and lay nearby. A hook hung at his belt. Slime covered his legs, chest, one arm, and part of his face. Where it had touched his skin for too long, the skin looked gray and softened.

Sakura took a step forward, but Gilbert immediately stopped her with his hand.

"Careful."

"He’s still breathing."

"I see. Still careful."

They came closer, avoiding the thickest trails. The man heard them or sensed the light. His eyelids twitched. He tried to turn his head, but all he managed was a hoarse inhale.

"Hey," Sakura said, crouching beside him without touching the slime. "Can you hear us?"

The man’s lips moved. Gilbert leaned a little closer.

"Are you alone here?"

The man made a sound that resembled laughter, but it was too weak and wet.

"They left..." he forced out. "Abandoned..."

Sakura looked at his body and understood at once: he would not get out. Not on his own, and not with their help. Even if she had an entire bag of potions instead of a few pathetic leftovers, his body looked like one open wound. Still, something inside her jerked painfully.

"What happened here?" Gilbert asked.

The man coughed. The slime on his chest shuddered. Sakura nearly recoiled from it. A sharp disgust ran through her, and she tried to suppress it.

"It... woke up..." the man rasped.

"M-17?" Gilbert clarified.

The man’s cloudy eyes focused on him.

"Don’t call... it that..." he whispered. "It doesn’t like..."

Sakura felt an unpleasant chill between her shoulder blades.

"It understands?"

The man tried to smile, but all that came out was a painful distortion of his face.

"More... More than we think."

Somewhere deep in the tunnels, a wet, heavy sound rang out. As if an enormous body had slowly shifted over stone.

Sakura went still. Gilbert heard it too, and his face immediately hardened.

"Where is the exit?" he asked quickly. "Which way do we go?"

The man twitched the fingers of his free hand, trying to point somewhere toward the corridor.

"Water... lower discharge... if the grate... isn’t closed..."

"Where?"

"Past the laboratory... down... then follow the red marks..."

He coughed again, harder this time. Sakura clenched her teeth, understanding that every word was literally tearing the last scraps of life out of him.

"Does it attack everyone?" she asked.

The man slowly turned his eyes toward her. Even that seemed agonizingly difficult for him.

"If they came... to take... it knows."

"We didn’t take anything."

"Still... people... come to take..."

Sakura found no answer to that, and then she heard it. A long, wet squelch, a heavy slide over stone that made the whole corridor seem narrower.

The man suddenly started breathing faster. Real terror appeared in his eyes, alive and sharp even through the pain.

"Don’t shine... too bright..." he breathed. "Don’t make noise... don’t cut... don’t burn..."

Gilbert immediately lowered the flame. The light shrank to a dim white spot near his palm. The mage looked sharply toward the passage.

"It’s close."

The man weakly grabbed the edge of Sakura’s sleeve. His fingers were cold, slippery with slime.

"Run..."

Sakura froze, staring at his hand.

"We..."

"Run," he repeated, almost soundlessly. "If it... closes the passage... you won’t get out..."

His fingers loosened. His head dropped heavily onto the stone. He tried to breathe in one more time, but that breath never came.

Sakura understood it at once. The man had died. He had simply stopped breathing in an old laboratory where he had been abandoned.

For several seconds, she did not move. Gilbert put a hand on her shoulder to bring her back to reality.

"Sakura."

She slowly lifted her head. Cold white light was seeping from the dark passage ahead. At first, Sakura even thought someone was walking there with a lantern. The light fell across the stones in an uneven patch, slid over the damp wall, trembled as if someone’s hand had covered it for a moment, then spread wider again.

Only then did she realize it was wrong. The light did not behave like a lantern. It crawled over the floor, low, thick, slowly filling the passage from edge to edge. Along with the light came a squelching, bubbling sound that sent a tremor through her whole body.

Sakura felt her mouth go dry.

Something enormous was slowly crawling toward them down the corridor.

First, the front of the body emerged from the darkness: wide, murky white, almost translucent, with dark stains inside that looked like old burn marks beneath skin. Then the rest of the mass appeared, heavy and shapeless, filling the passage almost from wall to wall. Dark veins and frozen bubbles moved slowly inside the cold glow, as if something had once boiled in that slime and then stayed there forever.

Slime ran down its body in layers, fell onto the stone, and spread across the floor in thin glowing puddles.

On the creature’s sides, Sakura could see metal rings grown into the flesh and broken pieces of old clamps, proof that this monster had once been shackled. Traces of someone’s confidence that such a thing could be held at all.

The creature stopped at the entrance to the room.

The giant glowing slug slowly raised the front of its body, and the whole corridor filled with a thick, wet smell of pain, chemicals, and something sickeningly sweet that reached them even through the masks.

Sakura barely breathed. Instinctively, she focused on the creature.

Pale text flashed before her eyes:

SLUG M-17

Level: 28
