Ning stepped into the Spiritual Plant Hall's assessment grounds.

At a glance, the crowd was roughly what he had expected.

Most were older disciples, their cultivation hovering around the fifth or sixth stage of Qi Condensation. They were broad-shouldered and sturdy, with weathered skin and calloused hands that made their profession rather obvious.

Judging by appearances alone, the competition looked... rough.

Ning quietly moved to one side and began observing the other participants.

His peace lasted less than a minute.

"Well, what do we have here?"

A man strode over, his robes neat but worn and his eyes carrying poorly concealed hostility. "Such a young face. Are you sure you've even fulfilled the requirements to take the assessment?"

Ning gave him a puzzled look. "And you are...?"

Such a classic arrogant opener. Did I accidentally activate a protagonist halo or something?

As everyone knew, once that thing switched on, it came with a complimentary debuff: unsolicited criticism from complete strangers.

"I am Xin Fu," the man declared. "I've cultivated spiritual fields for twenty years. Two acres of Basic Grain, eight acres of mid-tier grain, three basic farming arts at Great Accomplishment, and the Earth-Shaking Hoe Technique at Minor Accomplishment."

He puffed out his chest slightly.

"And you? Did your family buy you a place here? Another rich second-generation brat pushing aside people who actually worked for their position?"

His voice was deliberately loud, and several nearby farmers immediately turned to watch.

Ning glanced around and understood.

Only five certificates were issued to outer sect disciples during each assessment.

Professional certificates originally came from the alliance of Nascent Soul sects governing such matters throughout the Eastern Wilderness. Cultivators normally had to travel to designated cities to undergo the appropriate assessment, but affiliated sects such as the Pure Qi Sect received a limited quota allowing them to conduct examinations themselves.

The resulting certificate was recognized beyond the sect and could even serve as proof of one's professional standing and identity.

Naturally, five slots weren't nearly enough.

One more participant meant one more competitor, and if someone wanted to reduce the competition before the assessment even began, the young newcomer who didn't look much like a farmer was an obvious target.

The surrounding disciples clearly understood as well.

"Old Xin is at it again."

"He does look rather young. Perhaps he really has some backing."

No one intervened. If Ning withdrew, that was one less competitor for everyone.

Ning sighed inwardly.

He had been trying rather hard to play the mob character, but avoiding trouble was never entirely up to you. Sometimes, trouble simply walked over and introduced itself.

"Rich second-generation?" Ning repeated, looking genuinely surprised. "Senior Brother, if you know where my family's fortune is hidden, please tell me. I've been looking for it myself."

A few chuckles immediately came from the surrounding farmers.

Ning smiled faintly. "Unfortunately, my family are mortals."

Xin Fu's expression stiffened at the laughter, but he quickly snorted. "Then what gives you the confidence to compete with people who have farmed longer than you've been cultivating?"

That was a more reasonable question, although the delivery left something to be desired.

"I've been farming for three years," Ning replied. "Basic Grain, Frozen Breath Grain, and a few other crops. The Hall accepted my application, so I assume I'm qualified."

The mention of Frozen Breath Grain drew several surprised looks.

"Frozen Breath Grain?"

"After only three years?"

An elderly farmer stroked his beard and gave Ning a more careful look.

Xin Fu's expression darkened. "Words are easy. Who knows how much of that was really your own work?"

Ning raised an eyebrow.

Now that was a rather stupid thing to say.

"My plots were assigned by the sect. My harvests were inspected by the sect. My missions were recorded by the sect." Ning paused. "So if you're saying my achievements are fake..."

He left the rest unsaid.

The atmosphere changed immediately.

Several farmers who had been enjoying the argument suddenly stopped smiling.

"Brother Xin, mind your words."

"Don't involve the Hall in this."

Xin Fu's face changed. "I never said the records were fake!"

"Then we agree." Ning smiled faintly. "I earned my place here, so there's no reason I shouldn't take the assessment."

Xin Fu opened his mouth, but Ning gave him no opportunity to continue. He simply cupped his fists and walked away, leaving Xin Fu staring after him. Several farmers exchanged amused looks, while one older disciple shook his head with a chuckle.

"Old Xin picked the wrong seedling to trample today."

The remark drew another round of laughter, and Xin Fu's face flushed red.

"Who said I'm afraid of him?!" he shouted after Ning. "Didn't I work my way up step by step? Didn't I toil like an ox? I've dug ten acres of land for twenty years! Twenty years! I've worked like a dog!"

Ning heard him clearly, and despite himself, the corner of his mouth twitched.

Still, twenty years spent working the fields was nothing to laugh at. Xin Fu might have chosen a rather stupid way to protect his chances, but at the very least, the man's pride hadn't come from nowhere.

Ning could respect the effort.

The attitude, however, needed work.

...

Ning returned to an empty corner, his expression calm. This time, he circulated the Turtle Breathing Technique to its fullest extent and did his best impression of a dead man, making it clear that he had no interest in attracting any more attention.

As for Xin Fu, Ning regarded the whole thing as nothing more than a minor episode.

There was a reason he hadn't simply ignored the man. This was the Spiritual Plant Hall's assessment ground, and an elder would almost certainly be overseeing the examination. Perhaps one was even watching them already.

Under normal circumstances, Ning would've been perfectly happy to let a few insults pass. Here, however, Xin Fu had openly questioned his qualifications in front of the other candidates. If Ning had simply backed down and walked away, it could easily have looked as though there was some truth to the accusation.

Sometimes avoiding trouble meant keeping your head down. Other times, backing down only invited more of it.

With the matter settled, Ning closed his eyes and quietly waited for the assessment to begin.

It wasn't long before an elder entered the grounds. A wave of Foundation Establishment pressure spread outward from him, and the scattered conversations immediately died away.

"The Spiritual Plant Hall assessment consists of three stages," the elder announced.

"The first will test your knowledge of spiritual plants. You will identify the plants presented to you, explain their effects, and describe the proper methods for cultivating them."

"The second will test your spiritual farming techniques. You may demonstrate the technique you are most proficient in, and your performance will be judged accordingly."

"The third will test your ability to diagnose and treat spiritual plants."

His gaze swept across the gathered disciples.

"When your name is called, enter the assigned examination room."

With that, the assessment officially began.

"Xin Fu!"

Ning's eyes shifted slightly.

The burly farmer from earlier straightened his robes and entered one of the examination rooms. Nearly an hour passed before the door opened again and Xin Fu emerged, his earlier bluster nowhere to be seen.

His expression was grim.

Ning glanced at him once before looking away.

Apparently, twenty years of farming didn't make the assessment easy.

Fortunately, Ning hadn't come here expecting it to be easy either. He had prepared thoroughly, and now there was nothing left to do except see whether that preparation was enough.

"Ji Ning!"

The call came from Examination Room Three.

Ning entered and found an elder seated behind a wooden table, several jade slips and sealed boxes arranged beside him.

The elder glanced at his registration before getting straight to the point.

"Let's not waste time. Tell me about Skybeak Root."

"Skybeak Root comes from the root and stem of the Thick-Veined Chrysanthemum Vine," Ning answered. "It grows best in shaded ravines near cold streams, particularly where Yin energy gathers. Its medicinal nature is astringent and slightly warm. When combined with Yin-Raising Leaf, it can be used to refine the Freezing Cold Pill."

The elder gave a small nod.

"What is the use of a nine-inch Black Root?"

"When does Fleece-Flower become poisonous?"

The questions came one after another, barely giving Ning time to pause. He soon settled into a steady rhythm, answering what he knew directly and taking a moment to think whenever a question required more detail.

To his surprise, it was easier than he had expected.

"Black Root Ginseng belongs to the Earth element and prefers shaded environments, usually beneath forest canopies," Ning continued. "Its grade rises considerably with age. Ten years is generally considered First-Tier mid-grade, fifty years high-grade, and a hundred years supreme-grade. Once it reaches three hundred years, it can be considered a Second-Tier spiritual plant."

The elder listened without interruption.

Most of the questions weren't particularly obscure. Ning had encountered nearly all of the relevant material in the library, and he had spent considerable time studying anything related to spiritual farming.

He also hadn't prepared blindly. Once Ning decided to take the assessment, he had asked Old Zhou and several farmers who had attempted it before about the questions they remembered. There was no guarantee the same questions would appear again, but certain subjects came up often enough to reveal a pattern.

Plant properties. Growth environments. Elemental affinities. Common diseases. Medicinal uses.

Once he knew what the examiners tended to focus on, preparing became considerably easier.

After nearly an hour, the questions finally slowed.

The elder reached for a plant on the table.

"The Dao of plants and vegetation is unpredictable," he said. "For the final portion of this stage, you will identify several ingredients while blindfolded."

Ning blinked. "Blindfolded?"

"Yes." The elder smiled. "A true Spiritual Plant Master cannot rely solely on sight. Through touch, scent, intuition, and experience, one should be able to recognize a plant and determine the most appropriate way to cultivate it."

"..." Ning gave the elder a deadpan stare.

What do you mean, intuition?

Ning had a very strong feeling that the elder was bullshitting him, but unfortunately, he had no evidence.

"Anyway, this artifact will block your vision completely. You may rely only on your other senses."

The elder handed him a black blindfold.

Fortunately, Old Zhou had warned Ning about this particular part of the assessment, so he wasn't caught completely off guard.

He tied the blindfold around his eyes.

A moment later, the elder placed something in his hand.

"What is this?"

Ning carefully turned it between his fingers, examining its shape and texture before bringing it closer to catch its scent. Finally, he focused on the subtle qi fluctuations within, matching each detail against his memory.

"This is Red Flame Herb. It's the main ingredient in Flare Flame Powder."

"Correct."

Another ingredient.

"This is Snow Weed. It's used to preserve the freshness of spiritual plants."

"Correct."

"This is Tiger-Striped Leaf. It enhances blood and qi circulation."

"Correct."

"…This is a Mimic Seed," Ning said after a pause. "It's used as a substitute ingredient in alchemy."

"Correct."

…

"Hm?" Ning frowned slightly. "It's short and thick, yet unusually sticky… Elder, what is this?"

The elder froze.

I'm the examiner, he thought. Why are you asking me?
