Henry considered what Maurice just told him, while below them, the wetlands slowly leveled up into green pastures and rolling hills.

“[So you think our fusion attempts are failing because we don’t… perceive where we’re about to land?]”

By now, Henry and Maurice had regained their human appearance, just for the ease of movement, but that wasn’t what they looked like. Not exactly. Two eagles flew side by side, high in the sky. It wasn’t exactly an eagle’s flight, as Henry was carrying them both with the usual Telekinesis. To a cursory Identify, they would show as mere C-ranks, which hopefully wouldn’t attract any A-rank’s attention. As for anything of their perceived rank that wanted to fight them, Henry could just shove them aside. If a human appearance was trouble, and the kraken appearance was trouble, then he would try a third option.

“[Yeah! Blink always needed us to see the target one way or another. So that’s probably what’s missing, right? For example, when the turtles came to this world, it was because they heard their own kind’s call for help, right? And maybe, when they take in the soul of people—like yours—they somehow get access to worlds they didn’t know before? I don’t know. That last part I’m not sure about. But maybe we can think of a test? It’d need to be toward something that Find the Path points at, and it’d need to be a place that we know.]”

Henry hummed, considered the angle Maurice was speaking of.

“[A test like that would confirm how the long-range teleportation actually works. But we would still be unable to use it to go somewhere we haven’t been. Also… I wonder if it’s limited by place. Could we not teleport to someone we know? Find the Path could point us in the right direction, and the familiarity with the person might do the rest. After all, the turtle’s ‘call for help’ can’t be that precise.]”

“[We could test that too… but…]”

“[Not we. Just me. You couldn’t test this,]” he stated, matter of fact, “[as you might end up being thrown too far again. But if I get teleported somewhere unexpected, I can always switch back to an Avatar.]”

Maurice was quiet for a second. “[Fair enough,]” he admitted. “[By the way, are we close? How are the whales? You said if the Path toward them changed fast, that they might be near, right?]”

Henry had been keeping an eye on the whale path, true. While the angle to the orb changed visibly with the detour to retrieve Maurice, by maybe five degrees or so, while the one pointing toward the whales hadn’t seen as much change. But it had changed, which meant they weren’t that far. Likely, they were somewhere past the coast of the Reach, which didn’t narrow it down at all. Unfortunately, that wasn’t the only thing that had changed.

Somewhere through the morning, when Henry and Maurice had been working on their fusion, the entrance to Wolf’s Den had been closed. Where once a tunnel led down the city, Henry saw only a rune-engraved boulder in its stead. He wasn’t sure what that meant exactly, but he didn’t bring it up. As for Maurice’s questions…

“[I can tell you that the orb is closer to us than they are. But we might still see them before we leave. As for the tests, let’s do that when we’re taking a break. For now, our priority should be to pick out a few decent A-ranks and dispatch them as quickly as possible.]”

Henry didn’t get any objections there. The two were keenly aware that they needed the levels. With all of these A-rank humanoids that kept on pestering them, it’d be best if they got to the rank themselves.

So they would hunt. Catch anything hostile on their path to the orb and if they were lucky, they’d make it there in their new rank. Just in case the orb’s movement spelled trouble.

Knowing his luck, it very well might be.

The office was deathly quiet when Sel’Veth finished her report, and illuminated by the soft blue lights that lined the walls, she waited, arms behind her back. The whole debacle had taken place barely fifteen minutes ago, but already, things were in motion. After having made sure their warriors were safe, she had come here directly, only to find Gar’Dak already pleading his case to the Depth Lord and now, she stood next to the bloodthirsty maniac, ignoring the smirk she was sure he was barely holding on to.

As for the Depth Lord, he just turned the memory pearl in his hand, its glowing lights reflecting off of his scales as if he had been holding a rainbow in his palm. The fact that her fellow Depth Guardian was here without having heard her report told her everything she needed to know.

“Did you send anyone after them?” the Depth Lord asked, his pale gray eyes meeting hers. There was none of his usual mirth in his gaze. Three of their A-ranks had been pretty badly injured. Crushed bones and concussions had been pretty evident, while the rest had been forced on the defensive. Even she had been contained, if temporarily.

“No, Depth Lord. I wished to report the matter first,” she said. She had been tempted, but they’d offended the kraken enough by capturing its companion. With its intelligence and its strength, she didn’t dare make things worse. It could easily make their life miserable. After all, more than one Deep city had seen its end at the hands of krakens and their grudges.

And if this one hadn’t even stepped in A-rank yet, then we need it as an ally, not an enemy, she thought to herself. It escaped no one that this damage had been done by a single, high B-rank. At least, the Depth Lord seemed aware enough. But she could tell her colleague wasn’t taking the matter seriously.

“I sent a scouting team to locate the beasts, Lord,” Gar’Dak announced.

That immediately made her grind her teeth. The clam-for-brains likely thought he could tame the kraken by force, but that was only because he was underestimating its intelligence. This was no wild, extra smart kraken. This one was an anomaly. One that heavily invested in its cognitive abilities. If that hadn’t been obvious by its proficient use of language, then it should be by the number of clones it had controlled, or how it opted to dispel all of them lest one was approached or captured.

It was smart. And it had been pissed. Now Gar’Dak wanted to piss it off even more. Sel’Veth opened her mouth to re-state that fact, only for the Lord to raise a hand.

The words died in her throat. Sel’Veth prayed to the ancestors that he wasn’t about to back the demented fool next to her.

“The kraken had managed to escape you, Guardian Sel’Veth?”

She nodded.

“The two of you will go.”

The words struck the thoughts out of her head, and without realizing it, she turned and found Gar’Dak staring back at her, his smirk gone, replaced by a frown.

The two turned away, ready to object to the Lord’s command, but he forestalled them once more.

“Extend an invite. Show it goodwill, if it deserves it. Then report back. Depending on what news you bring back to me, I’ll decide the next steps. For now, leave me.”

The command couldn’t be clearer, so Sel’Veth turned and swam out of the office, ignoring the annoying presence at her back.
