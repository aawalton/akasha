Matt is up in the gym’s control room for the illusionary arena.

“Alright, a single fast opponent, like you wanted. We’ll start off at average 3-Star speeds.”

A featureless, mannequin-like fighter forms in front of me, wielding a longsword. It immediately tries to charge through my guard, but I quickly force it back. Despite its 3-Star speed, it’s still much slower than I am.

Like earlier with Matt, it tries to slap my sword away. I whip it around and carve off its neck in a smooth motion. The entire engagement lasts less than a second.

“Right. I guess we’ll go a bit faster. The next one should be at 4-Star speeds, and a quick one at that. Let’s see how you do.”

The next opponent is suspiciously similar to Duelliste, the French 4-Star who weaved around me like it was nothing. It wields a rapier and a parrying dagger in its off hand. It blurs in close, before I can get my guard up. I quickly switch to a half-sword grip.

A lightning quick thrust rips through the air. The mannequin barely twists out of the way. I still leave a gash along one side of its head, but it counter attacks with a strike to one of my knees. I twist and catch the strike on a thigh armor plate instead.

The rapier slides along the plate and the mannequin overextends. I lash out with a punch. It quickly rolls under it, and I use the brief moment to leap away, getting more distance. A warding slash as I back away forces it farther, finally giving me the distance I need.

It tries to sneak inside my guard again, but with my newly-taught skills, I can read each movement before it makes them. Each shift of its weight, every change in position is a tell. Even with its magical capabilities and artificial nature, there are still rules it must follow. Matt has taught me those rules, meaning I can finally predict what it will do.

The simulated attacker has the edge in raw speed—I’d rather not rip apart the building. Unlike the simulacrum, I cause shockwaves when I move quickly enough. My advantage in analysis and decision making doesn’t cause such collateral damage, and it keeps us at a near impasse.

I slowly force it towards the corner of the arena, not giving it a moment of respite. While I’m better than I was, I’m still far from an expert. It shows when I fall for a feint. I swipe too low, expecting it to duck. Instead it flips over my sword, and lands on top of it. Before I can react, it sprints up the length of my blade and lunges for the gaps in my helmet.

It freezes a millimeter away.

“That was much better, Machina. Room for improvement, of course. That feint got you good. Another round?”

“Absolutely,” I reply.

“First, some pointers, then go again. You can be faster than that, Mach. You need to apply that speed.”

“If I go any faster, I’ll start tearing up the arena.”

He shakes his head.

“That’s because you’re accelerating too fast. Slow and smooth. Just like the greatsword, your biggest advantage isn’t your speed, but your momentum. You just need to build that up as you go. Come on, let’s try again.”

He sets up the simulation again, and I ready myself. It reforms in front of me, the featureless mannequin holding a rapier in its main hand, and a parrying dagger in its off hand.

“Begin!”

I react first, stepping forward with a wide slash, and it ducks under the swing. The air screams and the tip of my sword zips over the target. I stay on the attack, drawing on the experience of an earlier fight, one where I had to escort a harvesting team through a Cat-3 Fracture. I was forced to fight amongst much weaker people, both Typicals and Empowered alike. I think I might have touched on part of what Matt is suggesting to me.

Time to drill it in.

I press the attack, each swing and step coming faster and faster. With every second that passes, I gain both momentum and speed. Despite simulacrum’s more nimble form, I slowly overwhelm the training dummy. It desperately tries to deflect an overhead chop with its rapier and parrying dagger, but my sword cleaves straight through both blades and slices the simulacrum in half. I pull back on my sword before it can carve into the floor, and the dummy vanishes in a puff of smoke.

Matt gives me a round of applause, and I return it with a melodramatic bow.

“Good work! Well done. Now, I did notice a few areas to clean up, if you have the time…”

I rest my hefty blade onto my shoulder, the now familiar presence of it comforting.

Never thought I’d find a weapon comforting.

“I don’t have anything else planned for now. Let’s keep going.”

After descending from the raised control room, he guides me through a few movements.

“Now, take a look at what I do. If they come at you from a low side position, step and twist like this…”

After a few more hours of training with Matt, I return to Morgan’s gallery. Now that the more dangerous part of the experiment is completed, her few apprentice mages—It’s strange how normal that is now—are crowded around her. She’s so busy lecturing them that she doesn’t notice my presence until her students quiet down.

Even among other Empowered I’m given a level of deference I find uncomfortable. The constant news articles comparing the Ascended to demi-gods clogs the internet and the airwaves. Our strength, power, and the feats we accomplish with our gifts are things that would have been relegated to fiction two months ago. Still, I am not a god. I might be a hero, but only through effort, not because I have power.

“Hm? What’s going on—oh, hi Mach. Back already?”

“It’s been hours,” I say, chucking. “Have you even eaten today?”

She waves me off.

“I’m an Ascended. Not eating for a day is nothing. Besides, I have these,” she says, shaking an empty energy drink can.

She frowns at the silent can. With a flash of will, the can vanishes, disintegrated by raw Potentia. Before she can pull another can out of her enchanted coat, I pick her up by the scruff of her collar and lift her to eye level.

“Enough of that. Come on, Morgan. You need to eat.”

She bats at my arm, but I don’t budge. Her students see where the wind is blowing, and file out ahead of us, headed to the communal dining hall. She pouts, folding her arms in front of her chest. Her boots hang in the air.

A game we play. A comfortable reminder for ourselves that despite our power, we’re still fallible. Still material.

She rolls her eyes after a moment, and I let go of her in recognition of her silent acceptance. She doesn’t drop to the ground, instead her greatcoat flutters in an ethereal breeze as she floats under her own power.

To be an Ascended is to ignore the laws that once governed reality.

I hate the comparison to demi-gods. But it’s hard to deny. How else would you describe someone with such power?

Dinner, for those who eat—meaning everyone but me—is simple but filling fare. A basic stew, prepared by Morgan’s first foray into magical automatons. The kitchen golems are made of mundane metals, with only their cores and a few critical components being truly magical. They bumble about the kitchen, slow in their tasks but unrelenting. They’ll need to be charged by a mage in a day or so, but it’s a simple task.

The stew itself is entirely vegetables. The monsters might avoid human constructions and infrastructure, but they attack farm animals. The ranching and dairy industries have been virtually destroyed at this point.

There’s still some meat, of course. Money is power, and those industries had quite a bit of both. Mercenary companies have been contracted to defend remote dairy farms and beef ranches. I find it wasteful and self-sabotaging. Even the small bands of a handful of independent Empowered could clear Fractures, defend outlying farming towns, or escort farmers while they harvest their crops. A handful of cows can’t feed the world.

Morgan could certainly afford to purchase meat given her family’s wealth, but both of us are of the same mind.

Such a waste. For the price of a steak, Morgan could buy a few tons of cheap scrap metal. Who knows what she could do with it?

Farms growing crops have been mostly left unmolested, fortunately. A vast majority of the monsters that escape from rural Fractures are based on carnivorous predators. Giant wolves as big as cars don’t care much for corn.

The golems take care of the dishes, and Morgan goes back towards her gallery, followed by her gaggle of apprentices. Together, they work to unlock the secrets of magic. They publicly release whatever they discover.

Keeping things close to the chest will only get more people killed. Only by working together will we all get through this.

I return to my now regular patrols of our little compound outside Denver. I walk the bounds looking for any curious monsters. All the while I trawl the internet. I tell myself I do it to keep an eye on things, but I think it’s because I can’t look away. Maybe I’m addicted to doom scrolling at the speed of light or maybe it’s self-inflicted punishment for not being everywhere at once.

“In the weeks since what has been called the Blue Screen Attack, Conglomerate forces have been steadily forced back. ROK loyalist forces have claimed that Seoul is entirely under control. Conglomerate media says…”

The news cast goes on, and I flick most of my attention to something else.

“Uganda and Kenya have both fallen to the Empire of the Sun’s surprise attack. Self-proclaimed Emperor Awet Berhane had promised no further wars after taking Sudan and South Sudan. Four days ago, he broke that promise and advanced his troops, each army led by an Ascended vassal…“

The Empire of the Sun continues their relentless march across Africa. Awet Berhane—I refuse to call that monster an Emperor—is hellbent on uniting the entire continent under one flag. He claims to bring order to a continent beset by chaos, but all one has to do is look at the razed cities to see that lie for what it is. Mogadishu was burned to the ground after the residents tried to resist his armies.

I shudder at the death toll. Even a single death is too much, but that many? With literal monsters pouring from holes in reality, it’s easy to forget that there were plenty of monsters here already.

It’s frustrating to only be able to sit here and do nothing about it. I want to help, I do. But how? The Empire of the Sun doesn’t have the digital infrastructure that the Conglomerate had, and what little they do have isn’t critical.

I can’t run off and try to fight a war all by myself. No matter how strong I am, I can only be in one place at a time. I desperately want to stop Berhane’s reign of terror, but I can’t do it alone. Everyone else is too busy looking after themselves to fight a trio of Ascended over a continent they never really cared about to begin with.

So I stay here instead, helping where I can. I train, and I watch the world crumble around me. Running off halfcocked and without a plan won’t get anything done. So I wait for the calls for help that always come. They never end, really. But it’s more than just clearing them to end the threat. Now we explore them. Even heavy construction equipment is brought in to take everything that can reasonably be chopped up and hauled out.

The call comes in, like they always do, and like always, it’s Holden. She’s become the liaison between myself and the government. Lieutenant McKinley is too low rank for their tastes, I suppose.

“Hello, Director Holden.”

“Good evening, Machina. Another Fracture has just formed in downtown Denver, Category 2. You’ve been requested. Everyone else is busy or healing up. Will you assist?”

“Of course, Director. When?”

“Tomorrow. I’ll send you the address. Thank you for your efforts, Machina. The United States government appreciates your sacrifice.”

“No thanks are necessary. This is what I do.”

“On the contrary, they are. Lest my peers take you for granted. Have a good evening. Goodbye.”

She hangs up. I don’t really know what to think of Holden. She heads the National Security Agency, which means she doesn’t think highly of rights, privacy, or the Constitution.

At least she’s not sycophantic like virtually everyone else I have to deal with in the US government. Small victories, I suppose.
