“Good afternoon, Miss Suzy. May I offer you a cup of tea?”

“That sounds delightful, Beatrice! You’re a lifesaver!”

“What the hell are you doing?”

I nearly jumped. The girl was back. I hadn’t noticed her—too absorbed in my... self-therapy.

But instead of getting flustered, I was just tired of feeling awkward and embarrassed. I also wanted to have a single normal conversation in this place without crying or begging.

So I looked at her, smiled, and said, “Well, it’s a tea party, as you may see. Suzy just got her refreshment. Would you fancy a brew?”

She stared for a second, then pointed to one of the twisted straw figures. “Is this supposed to be Suzy?”

“No, that’s the table,” I answered with dignity. “I’m more proficient with paper clips, but as you can see, my options here are limited.”

“I see,” she said, sitting down on the cold floor. “Well... sorry for running away earlier. I probably should’ve heard you out.”

It looked like she wasn’t going to run this time. Maybe because I was actually acting like a functioning human being.

New strategy: be normal!

“Well, it’s not your fault,” I said. “I panicked. And acted creepy. I can be... less weird.”

“So how’d you really end up locked in the cell?” she asked.

“I started talking to Hildi,” I said, “The barmaid at the tavern. She was acting like an NPC—didn’t respond to some of the things I said, like she was coded. I wanted to test whether I could touch her. Then I wondered if I could… walk her around.”

“Walk her around? Like she’s a dog?” she asked, accusatory.

“It was… not very nice of me,” I admitted. “But I thought of her as an NPC. I didn’t think it would have consequences.”

“Well, you’re at least partly responsible for being here. You did assault her!”

“If you call pulling an arm an assault!”

She stared at me, then sighed. “Fine. I guess you’ve been punished enough.”

“I… I have.” I looked down.

Silence.

“So, what’s your name?” she asked at last.

“Geshem,” I said. “It’s Hebrew. Means ‘rain.’ You?”

“Juliette.”

“Where are you from?” I asked, trying to place the name.

“Lyon,” she replied.

“Lyon? Like…?”

“In France, of course.” She gave me a slightly amused look. “Honestly, I assumed you were French. You speak it fluently.”

Wait—what?Oh. The system translates!

“I speak English,” I explained. “I’m from New Jersey. To me, you’re speaking perfect English right now. The system must be translating everything so everyone hears their own language.”

“Ohh,” she said. “I was wondering why all the elves here spoke French. But there’s still something I don’t understand… We move our lips a certain way to make sounds—so how does the system deal with that? In dubbed movies, the words often don’t match the lip movements, and it looks terrible. But here, everything looks perfectly synced.”

“You’re right—that is interesting,” I said. She was smart. I hadn’t even thought about that. “So, like… if you say a short word in French, but it translates into a longer word in English, what do I see? Your lips moving for the short word, or the long one? Like… uh…”

I blanked on French vocabulary.

She jumped in. “I’ve got one. Beautiful. Three syllables in English, just one in French.”

She said it.

And I saw it. Her lips clearly formed the word belle—just one syllable. I even heard it, too. Belle. But somehow, my brain understood it as beautiful. No weird dubbing effect, no mismatched lips. It all just… worked.

“That’s actually insane,” I said. “I saw and heard the French word—but the meaning translated in my head, like magic.”

“Wow, that’s really cool,” she said. “Wait—say what you heard: Belle.”

“Belle. I heard the actual French word,” I said. “How did you even do that?”

“When I said it, I was thinking about the name Belle, like from Beauty and the Beast.”

She paused, looking a little unsettled. “So that means… this system isn’t just translating sound. It’s reading our thoughts. That’s… kind of creepy.”

This was fascinating. And it was magical.

It wasn’t something that could be explained by modern technology, which disproved the high-tech amusement park theory.Sure, it wasn’t 100% proof—Juliette could be lying about the translation stuff—but I didn’t think she was.

“So,” she asked, “you mentioned a system. Do you know anything about this place? Why we’re here?”

“Well,” I said, “I was playing video games in my room yesterday, and the next thing I know, I’m standing on a hill near the town. Everything I know is just guesses and theories. Was it the same for you?”

“More or less. I was at a restaurant three days ago. Then suddenly I was here. Thought I was going crazy, or maybe hit my head. But I managed to calm down, asked some people around, and eventually got a job as a maid in the mansion. It’s not too bad… but everyone here acts robotic. You’re the first real person I’ve met. You called them… NPCs, right? I don't really know what that means.”

“You’re clearly not a gamer,” I noted.

“Definitely not,” she said. “Is that relevant?”

“Hugely,” I said.

Then I explained—about isekai, about NPCs, about Skyrim, and how this world fit the tropes. I also told her that usually, these worlds have some kind of level or skill system, but so far, I hadn’t seen any sign of one.

“I see,” she said, thinking. “So… how do we get out? Get back home?”

I looked at her for a long second. “I’m not sure we can go back. And if we can… in these stories, it’s usually after beating the final boss or finding a rare item. Not something we’ll be doing anytime soon.”

She looked stricken. “But… my friends. My little sister—she really needs me. I can’t just disappear like this…”

“I’m sorry,” I said softly. “That must be really hard. There’s also a chance that we’re just… copies. That our real bodies are still on Earth. But I have no idea. I’m as clueless as you.”

We sat in silence for a while. She looked lost in thought. Sad.

“To be honest,” I said, “my life back home kind of sucked. So when I got here, I was actually happy. Well, until… you know. Everything. But once I get out of this cell, I want to learn everything about this world. And I promise—I’ll help you. As much as I can. I mean, if you want me to.”

She looked at me, almost surprised. “Thank you,” she said.

Then her eyes lit up like she remembered something. “Wait here!”

She ran off.

Obviously I was going to wait.

I sat there, heart pounding. I didn’t think she would abandon me… but watching her disappear still made my chest tighten.

She returned a few minutes later—with a tray. A tray stacked with food: fruit, roast chicken, potatoes. Beautiful, delicious-smelling food.My mouth filled with drool before I could even thank her.

“Shhsshoo,” I tried to say, already chewing. I swallowed hard. “Seriously, I was starving. You’re an angel!”

I was close to tears. No joke.

“Oh, it’s nothing,” she said, a little embarrassed. “The cook gives me meals whenever I ask—perk of working here, I guess. Honestly, I don’t even do that much. I slack off most of the time, and no one seems to notice or care. They also pay me two silver coins a day, but I have no idea if that’s a lot or not.”

“Interesting,” I said between bites. “The NPC behavior triggers only with specific events. Like, maybe if you do zero work for a week, they’ll fire you. But until then, nothing changes.”

I pointed down the hallway. A guard was doing his rounds and briefly glanced in our direction.

“See that guy? He obviously shouldn’t let people feed prisoners. But his action script only seems to trigger if I make noise or try to escape. Since feeding me isn’t one of his triggers, he just ignores it.”

“You think we can… exploit the system?” she asked. “Well, you did already try it with Hildi"

“Okay yes,” I groaned. “Please stop bringing that up.”

“But yes,” I added, “we can exploit things—but we have to be careful. Hildi did try to murder me with a kitchen knife. She came really close, too.”

“That was probably so funny,” Juliette grinned.

“Not funny. Not even a little.”

“Juliette,” I said, suddenly more serious. “Do you think you could… get me out? Like, maybe grab the cell key from the guard somehow?”

She shook her head. “That’s not a good idea,” she said firmly. “It’s way too risky—for me and for you. The guard could kill me on the spot if I’m caught. And for you… if this system works the way I think it does, escaping would probably label you as a fugitive. That could make things a lot worse than just serving out your week.”

She had a point.

“Yeah… I guess I’ll just have to wait out the week. It’s not that long, really.” I tried to sound convincing.In truth, the idea of staying here for a whole week terrified me.

Juliette noticed.

“Look,” she said gently, “it won’t be that bad. I’ll bring you food, blankets, even books—so you don’t go insane from boredom.”

“Are you sure? That might count as stealing.”

“I have a room in the mansion. I already took stuff to it, and no one cared. I doubt anyone will notice if I gave them to you.”

“Well…” I felt my chest loosen. Relief. I could actually survive like this. “That would be amazing. Thank you.”

“I’ll also come visit a few times a day,” she said. “I mean, you are a nerd—but you’re more interesting than these NPC robots.”

“Ah. Thanks… I guess?”

“Well, I better get back to work. Can’t risk getting fired.”

“Yeah,” I agreed. “Juliette… you really saved me here. I don’t know what I would’ve done without you.”

“Well, let’s just say you owe me. See you later. You can continue your lovely tea party.”

And so I did.
