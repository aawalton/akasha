Not a second after activating Vital Sight, Archie slammed into the door, splintering wood around the crude, medieval metal latch as he barreled inside. The skill stayed active for a heartbeat longer – long enough for him to tag every living thing in the shop.

Five human life signatures, all hovering around level 100, exactly at it, and one outlier at level 3. The only other shapes in the room that he was able to see after deactivating Vital Sight were three bodies sprawled across the floor, charred and hacked apart, and much to Archie’s annoyance, in such a short time today, he was yet again greeted with a heavy stench of charred flesh.

The scene was already mid-collapse into chaos. Henry stood near the counter, one arm clutching a trembling and crying Camilla tight to his chest, the other gripping a broadsword wreathed in roiling Dark-Flame.

Archie’s boots were already pivoting before the details of the shop fully registered in his brain. Adrenaline Rush surged through his limbs, having dialed it to 20% in an instant, ballooning both his strength and speed beyond the normal limiter the skill could offer.

He layered Savage Charge into his lunge, throwing every ounce of weight and speed into the punch that tore across the shop. The blow caught the nearest of the rugged-looking guys closest to him clean in the side of the head – caught entirely off guard by Archie’s sudden appearance and explosive movement.

The thin black cloth mask did nothing to soften the blow – Archie’s fist sank in, caving the skull like it was made of cake. Bone tore free from the neck, spraying blood and fragments in a jagged fan across the polished wooden walls and across the bodies of everyone nearby.

“Archie!” Henry barked, startled, eyes going wide at the sight of his brother storming into his newly claimed shop – a warm, fantasy-modern blend of polished oak counters and glass display cases, now smeared with gore.

*[Human Lv 100//Street Rogue Lv 100//Bloodhound Extortionist Lv 101] has been slain – XP has been given.*

The two remaining attackers froze for the briefest instant, staring at the headless ruin of their companion. Then training – or desperation – snapped them back to motion. One lunged at Henry, blade raised, while the other came straight for Archie.

Archie met his charge head-on, fury evident on his face at the scene of his brother and three-month-old niece being attacked. The man’s sword came down in a crude diagonal slash. Archie caught it square on the knuckles of his gauntlet; the steel snapped with a sharp, ringing crack, causing the rugged-looking thief’s eyes to snap wide at the sight of his blade snapping in two.

Archie’s follow-up punch buried itself in the attacker’s sternum hard enough to make his spine bow towards him.

Before the man could stagger back from the blow, Archie’s fingers knotted in the man’s scarf and yanked him towards him. Their foreheads met with a wet and sickening crack that caved in the front of the man’s skull. Archie didn’t even pause before slamming both fists sideways into opposite sides of the man’s skull, splattering Archie’s face and upper torso with even more bone, blood, and brain.

*[Human Lv 101//Street Thief Lv 100//Gaslighting Vandal Lv 103] has been slain – XP has been given.*

Behind him, Henry almost sidestepped yet another thrust of his assailant’s sharp sword. The blade clipped deep into his thigh as he planted his stance, as if he went back any further than he did, Camilla, who was still being held in his left arm, would be in range of the blade’s potential retaliating strikes – a drunken attacker was just as dangerous, if not more so, than a sober one.

However, much to the chagrin of the drunken thief, the pain only sharpened his swing of his blade. His head whipped forward, faster than the man could react to, and smashed his forehead into the man’s face with a meaty crunch, disorienting the man instantly, and within that same motion, Henry’s Dark-Flamewreathed sword came around in a vicious arc, taking the man’s head clean off.

The shop fell into a sudden, ringing quiet, save for the wet patter of blood dripping to the wood floor and the soft, choked sobs of Camilla, whose face was buried into Henry’s shoulder.

Archie let out a low, breathless chuckle as Henry exhaled hard, sending his sword back into Spatial Storage before leaning his weight against a reinforced glass display case.

“You’re a goddamn lifesaver,” Henry muttered, still rocking Camilla gently in his arms and trying his best to dissuade her from crying. “If you hadn’t walked in when you did, things would’ve gotten really messy.”

“I was waiting for Camilla to teleport in so my compass could actually lock onto her,” Archie said, wiping the gore from his visor before pushing it up to meet Henry’s gaze. “No… that sounded accusatory. Sorry, I should’ve been here sooner, but I got held up by a couple of things that popped up.”

“It looked that bad to you?” Henry grimaced as he placed Camilla out of her blanketed cocoon and into a new one that was untouched by blood.

“… Not in the way you think,” Archie shook his head. “You could have taken them, they were all weak D-Grades – you held your own in our training fight we had behind your home. I just lost my… composure when I saw you covered in blood, holding Camilla, and being outnumbered… You can’t blame an older brother for acting like this, especially so considering…” Archie said, trailing off towards the end, as he did not want to continue with his sentence.

Henry, as he was wrapping up Camilla in her blankets, somberly looked at her crying face and felt his heart clench slightly at the words that went unsaid. “Yeah, I get you.”

Snapping himself out of his funk, Archie gave the corpses, blood, and whatnot that stained the area around them an accusatory look before activating Forgesmith’s Flame and willing it to devour the remains of the thieves.  After a few minutes and a couple of windows being opened, Archie deactivated Forgesmith’s Flame. With a flex of his mana, all the items and equipment the thieves wore lifted themselves off the ground and landed atop one of the glass display cases.

“Thanks for that,” Henry said, holding out a glass of water for Archie to drink with a string of mana and placing one for himself on the table beside them. “I’ll need that to figure out why they decided to attack me.”

“Extortion,” Archie said after taking a sip of the cool water. “The first one I killed, his profession was called Bloodhound Extortionist. So, that should narrow down your suspects.”

“Not really,” Henry shrugged. “My QL said I literally just opened shop just about…”

“Huh… It’s been about…” Henry trailed off, shifting Camilla slightly so he could dig out his Aetherglass. He flicked the screen awake with his thumb. “Just over an hour since Camie and I ported into the shop. Did you land in Creavenboor too?”

Archie shook his head, moving to close the door behind him, the latch hanging useless where he’d broken it in. “Nah. Got dumped in some city called Greeber. Shitty name, I know.” His grin was wry. “Drove here on my bike.”

Henry perked up, adjusting his stance to keep rocking Camilla. “Oh, so you were teleported nearby then. That’s good – maybe our luck’s finally turning around.”

“Not exactly ‘nearby,’” Archie smirked, trailing Henry through the door behind the counter into the back workshop – an organized chaos of wooden beams, steel panels, racks of tools, and half-finished contraptions scattered across workbenches. The air smelled faintly of oil and scorched metal, every surface marked by some ongoing project. Blueprints and sketches covered the far wall, layered in a chaotic collage of various machinery made out of wood, metal, and the like.

“I mean... technically, no. But when you’ve got a sick-ass bike like mine that can cross five hundred and fifty kilometers in under an hour, I guess everything’s nearby.”

Henry blinked at him, then hissed out, catching himself before Camilla could hear the word he almost used. “No f–… no way. That’s faster than any racecar on Earth.”

“Yessir,” Archie grinned. “When I was upgrading it at your house, I wasn’t kidding when I said my bike’s fast.”

Henry shook his head, half in disbelief, half in envy. “You have to make me one of those.”

Archie’s smile widened. “Once I get the right materials and a power source just as good as the one mine runs on, I’ll forge you one.”

Henry chuckled, easing Camilla down into a padded cradle set up beside his workbench. “Yeah, right after I scrape together whatever coin I can make from this shop.” His voice hardened slightly, eyes flicking toward the doorway to the shop. “This is one interesting way to set up a QL.”

“Tell me about it,” Archie glanced toward the front as well, remembering back to just over an hour and a half ago when he woke up in the tavern, Mugtopia.

“Anyways, did you and Camilla teleport together?” Archie asked, his voice low, cautious.

Henry nodded, his eyes softening as he looked down at Camilla, still swaddled in blankets. “Yeah, thank God… well, thank the System for that. To say I was relieved would be an understatement when I found her still in my arms after being teleported into that dark room – the one where we have to choose a QL. After that? Yeah, understatement.” His voice dropped almost to a whisper, eyes flicking back to the babe. “If she hadn’t been teleported along with me… I don’t even want to think about it.”

Archie gave a slow, understanding nod. “True that.”

A few seconds of solemn quiet stretched between them, an unspoken acknowledgment of how screwed everything might’ve gotten if Camilla hadn’t teleported with Henry.

Then, as though she was able to detect the mood of the room, Camilla woke up from her short little nap and began to cry, breaking the solemn mood that arose between her father and her uncle.

Henry’s head snapped up, and he began rocking her once again, keeping his voice soft to calm his daughter down, “Hey, hey, it’s okay, everything is fine now, little flower. It’s just us here.”

Archie stepped closer, glancing down at his niece with a rare softness that seemed to catch even him by surprise. “She’s a loud one, just like her old man,” he smirked.

Henry smiled faintly, rocking her slowly. “Yeah.”

The tiny cries grew a bit louder, but Henry’s steady presence seemed to soothe her just enough to keep it from turning into a full-blown tantrum.

Archie’s eyes flicked back toward the door that led to the shop. “I’ll be staying here for a while on my own QL,” he said quietly. “And in doing so, I’ll keep an eye out for anyone wearing the same colored clothes or markings as the people that attacked you and Camilla.”

Henry nodded, his jaw tightening. “And if you find out anything, tell me because whoever it was that ordered this will die by my hands.”

Camilla’s sobs softened, her little fists scrunching at the blanket as Henry settled deeper into the chair by the workbench.

Watching what had occurred in front of “Woods R Us” was a figure that stood atop a tailor’s shop four buildings down. The elf’s long ears twitched faintly with every distant sound of the festival still echoing through the streets, though her attention never wavered. Her darkish purple skin practically melted into the black leather of her fitted coat and hood, perfect for her duties as a Shadow Enforcer of City Lord.

She tapped two fingers against the side of her neck, just below the jawline where the thin brass ring of her comm-bead sat hidden under skin. “… Affirmative. While I cannot hear them on account of various wards activating around the residence after the conflict,” she murmured, keeping her voice low, “there is nothing to worry about. The man running around, identified as Creed Burrows, appeared to be rushing to aid a merchant associate after using some sort of magic tool to pinpoint his location.”

Her gaze drifted slightly, tracking the movement of a carriage passing between her and the shop, before settling back on the now-quiet window.

“No,” she continued after a brief pause, “no familial relation at all. Creed Burrows is a tanned-skinned human male with dark blue hair and similarly colored eyes. His merchant associate, Trafford Hill, who was protecting his newborn daughter from drunk-out-of-their-mind D-Grade thugs, refugees from Breena Town attempting to take advantage and rob during the festival, is a human male with fair skin and bright pink hair and eyes.”

She fell silent again, scanning the street with a slow sweep of her eyes, watching as a lone, nervous-looking figure who’d been watching the front of “Woods R Us” and awaiting their teammates to emerge from the shop turned tail and began running down the alley – finally having processed the death of their teammates.

“Yes, City Lord,” she said after a moment, her tone crisp. “I will continue to keep watch and will report back if anything abnormal occurs.”

The comm-link clicked silently beneath her skin, and she let her hand fall back to her side, expression unreadable as the noise of the festival swelled faintly in the background.
