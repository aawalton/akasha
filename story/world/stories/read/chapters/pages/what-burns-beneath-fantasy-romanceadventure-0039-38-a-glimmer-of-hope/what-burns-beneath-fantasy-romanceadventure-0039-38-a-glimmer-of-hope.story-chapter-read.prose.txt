I reached the town Watch just past midday. The air had grown steadily warmer, not to the point of pleasantness but enough that I was no longer shivering at every gust of wind. The Watch building was one story and wide, built from whitestone and with a polished iron shield affixed beside the entrance. I could just make out a fenced-in training yard behind the building. There were practice dummies spaced out along tamped-down dirt, and several colorful archery targets hung from a massive oak tree.

I’d thought a lot about what to say. Imagined different versions of the truth that could paint the situation in a more believable, digestible light than simply declaring that people in Snowmelt were feeding people to a monster.

I’ll settle for most of the truth. That Teela’s missing, and that I believe there’s a Fae lurking outside somewhere nearby. But I don’t think it’s wise to mention Gil, or that other people may be involved. The last thing Teela needs is for the Watch guards to go locking people up instead of looking for her. Or trying to beat the information out of Gil when he can’t answer.

Besides. Sister Embra and Brother Pel may have accepted Gil’s story about the glass cutting him, but I think a Watch guard would have more questions. And how would I even begin to explain the broken window, the blood in Gil’s room, and the water everywhere?

I reached the wooden door when quick, light footsteps came pattering from behind. A familiar young man slid to a stop just a few paces away. He had tawny, unkempt hair and peach fuzz along his jaw.

It was the man Teela had danced with after dinner.

“Hi!” He immediately turned red. One hand rubbed at the back of his neck. “Ah, sorry. I didn’t mean to startle you.”

“It’s alright.”

“Right. Sorry.”

We spent a moment staring at each other.

I cleared my throat. “Did you need something?”

He flushed again. “Right! Yes, I… ah, I don’t know if you noticed me last night, but I… my name’s Terrence. Terrence Black, I live up on… well, that doesn’t really matter. I was wondering about your friend from last night. Teela?”

My shoulders stiffened of their own accord. “What about her?”

His eyebrows drew together. “Well, we made plans to have breakfast this morning. Over at Maude Millie’s place. She bakes fresh bread every morning, you see, and dusts it with sugar from…” He trailed off, taking in my expression, and winced. “I just, ah, wondered if she was feeling alright? She wasn’t at the inn when I stopped by. And then I heard some folks talking about, er, about you and Sister Embra and Brother Pel racing through the streets this morning. I was worried something had happened.”

I swallowed. He looked so earnest that I couldn’t help but feel sorry for him. “I’m sorry, Terrence. Teela… seems to be missing.”

He blinked at me, as if not quite understanding what I’d said. “Missing? You mean… as in… wait, missing?”

I pulled the borrowed cloak tightly around my shoulders. “Our bedroom window was open when I woke up this morning. Teela’s things were still there, but… she was gone.” I watched his face closely, looking for some indication of guilt. A flicker of a smile, or a smug glint to his eyes…

No. He just looked puzzled.

“I’m sorry, I don’t… Maybe she went for a walk? The forest can be very pretty this time of year, you know, and-”

“Her boots were still there.”

“O-oh. That’s… er…” He rubbed the back of his neck, looking around awkwardly. “Missing?”

I placed a hand on the door’s iron rung. “Follow me inside, if you want. You can hear the whole story for yourself.”

He balked but, when I opened the door, hurried in behind me.

We entered into a spacious room built for practicality over charm. There were several desks bearing neatly stacked vellum and glass inkpots, and a singular bookshelf lined with yellowed parchment and dusty, cracked-leather tomes. Whetstones and folded oils cloths lay, neatly stacked, upon two wooden barrels. A sheathed, belted sword rested atop each desk.

There were two other people in the room. The first was a broad-shouldered man with fair skin and an enormous red mustache. He wore black woolen trousers and a navy vest bedecked by wooden buttons. He sat cross-legged at a desk, reading a piece of parchment aloud. Upon my entrance he fell silent and raised small, sharp eyes. They swept me from head to toe as he rose.

“Good day, Miss. Always a pleasure to see a new face in town.” The man’s voice was like a rockslide; deep and rumbling. He tapped two fingers against his forehead, dipping into a bow at the waist.

I smoothed my tunic down, sucking in a deep breath of air. The room smelled like oak and metal. “Good morning, sir. Are you captain Davith Rell?”

“I am indeed, Miss. How can I be of service?”

I was acutely aware of Terrence, standing just beside me. His eyes were wide and fixed on my face.

I cleared my throat. “Captain, my name is Brin Damelle. I’m here to report a crime.”

Davith’s bushy red eyebrows shot up. “Oh? Trouble on the road, my lady?”

“No, sir. I came into Snowmelt last night with two companions, and as of this morning one of them is missing.”

He blinked at me, looking momentarily shocked, then narrowed his bright eyes. They swept me over again, as if trying to judge whether this was some kind of joke.

I stepped forwards. “We stayed in the Snowswept Inn. This morning, I woke to see that our bedroom window was open. Teela- that’s her, my missing friend- was nowhere to be found, and all of her belongings were beside her bed. Her clothes, her boots, everything.”

The captain stroked his mustache, looking thoughtful.

The room’s other occupant, a thin man with cropped russet hair and a faded gray vest, rubbed his chin. “Ah, not to cast doubt, Miss Brin, but this is a very safe-”

“When I asked Master Lewen if he’d seen her, he was confused. He didn’t remember her- or me, for that matter.” I looked from one man to the other solemnly, “And when I looked beneath the window I found a handprint. Not a human one.”

Davith drew a sharp breath, nostrils flaring. “Ashes, my lady, you can’t think-”

I didn’t give him the chance to finish. Teela didn’t have time for it. “I found rotted plants, too, and followed them into the woods until I reached a witchwood tree. It had been torn to shreds.”

The captain grew very still. “Torn, you say? A crime, a terrible one indeed, and not a soul in Snowmelt would do such a thing.”

I thought again of the scored bark. The wounds made by an axe. If only you knew, captain.

The other man cleared his throat. “A crime, or an animal of some kind.” Flinty gray eyes swept from me to his captain. “If it was a monster, the tree should’ve destroyed it. More likely that it was wildlife, or even lightning.”

“It wasn’t anything natural, it was deliberate.” I wrung my hands, looking at the captain pleadingly. “Sir, please. Teela wouldn’t have wandered off, and with everything else I found… there’s something terrible going on.”

The captain shook his head, muttering, and grabbed the sheathed sword on the desk beside him. “Well, my lady, I’m not worth my station if I ignore such claims, and a missing woman is indeed cause for alarm.” He waved for the other man to grab a sword, which he did.

Terrence piped up, voice high but firm, “Captain Rell, I’d like to help look for her, too.”

Davith gave a curt nod. “The more eyes the better.” He strode forwards and opened the door, broad shoulders at attention. “I do hope this is all a grave mistake. Perhaps we’ll find your friend wandering about in the square, shopping for new clothes and boots.”

My heart swelled with relief as I stepped back out onto the cobblestones, but I didn’t have it in me to return the faint attempt at levity. “It’s not a mistake. I don’t think there’s a moment to spare.”

He blew a heavy sigh. “Ashes. Well, my lady, let’s start at the inn. Not to doubt your word, but I’d like to see this handprint for myself, as well as talk to the good Master Lewen. Get the story for myself.”

“And… and then, captain?”

Chilly midday air ruffled the large man’s mustache. He pulled the door shut behind us, motioning for me to lead the way. “If all is as you say, we’ll gather the other Watch and send through Snowmelt asking for volunteers. This is a good town, my lady, with good folk in it. Like Terrence, here. We’ll get search parties rounded up and scour the area for your friend. And mark my word, we’ll find her.”
