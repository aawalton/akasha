Together, Ashton and Millie walked into the gym. People had gathered here for breakfast and to discuss the tasks for the day, but the moment that the two of them stepped in, the air became tense and the noise died down.

Ashton understood why, but it was still a bit annoying. They were acting like him and Millie were in the wrong somehow.

But that was alright. The only reason why they were here was to talk to Rory. Frankly, Ashton was pretty disappointed in him, just letting people treat Millie the way they did, like she was some wild animal that could jump them at a moment's notice.

Honestly, to Ashton, it would be better if she acted like that. Millie was so full of energy when he met her, but she became so quiet now.

"Rory," Ashton said, stepping up to the man's table. He wasn't eating like most others here, and was just discussing things with the other people in charge of different aspects of the school camp.

"Good morning, lad, lassie." Rory smiled and looked past Ashton at Millie, who was halfway hiding behind him.

"Can we talk?"

Rory nodded and stood up. "Of course. I've been waiting for this all morning, anyway. There're a few things I want to show to ye."

He was trying to smile and act normal, as if the hole that Ashton created in the wall here yesterday wasn't still just staring at everyone in here.

Rory practically pushed Ashton outside into the hallway, bringing him to a place where there was a bit more peace.

Ashton stood there, facing Rory. Millie was still hiding behind him.

"So?"

"Hm?" Rory looked back at Ashton a bit perplexed. A slight laugh escaped his mouth. "Lad, you came to me to talk, didn't you?"

"What difference does that make? You know what I want to hear."

From the strained expression twisting Rory's face, it was clear to Ashton that they were on the same page. And that was even considering the fact that Ashton's ability to read people wasn't exactly... perfect. But this wasn't something ambiguous. This wasn't something that gave Ashton any space to double-guess himself.

Rory was fully and utterly ashamed.

He slowly looked down at Millie, and then lowered himself to one knee. His stats had clearly increased a bit, so he no longer moved like a man close to retirement age.

"Millicent. I am truly sorry for the way you've been treated here. There is no excuse for it, and things have been hard enough for ya already. And I want to make sure you know that this is not a fault of yours," Rory started, briefly tilting his head to look up at Ashton, as if for approval. "It is up to us grownups to protect all the precious children of this place, and we have failed you. But I promise, that I will do what I can to change this place, so that the next time ye're here, ya can run around this place with a smile just like you used to."

Millicent grabbed onto the back of Ashton's cloak, and just stared at Rory. This went on for a few moments. These were probably the second-longest five seconds that Ashton had ever experienced. Before finally...

"Okay."

Her response was as nonchalant as it was unsurprising. Ashton could tell that Millie was bothered by the things here, but the way she dealt with these things was shockingly detached from her own emotions. It was pretty clear what was happening.

Millie could see that Rory was sincere. She could see the soul; the heart of the person and the changes in their emotions reflected within it. Ashton did wonder what Rory looked like to her, but surely she could tell that he meant what he said.

Rory was almost startled, as if the response came through a letter attached to a boxing glove to his face.

"Welp, let's go, then," Ashton said, shrugging. "You mentioned you wanted to show me something?"

"Huh? Hold on, is that... it?" Rory asked, looking up at Ashton as he stood back up.

"What, did you not want her to forgive you?"

"Ooh, I can yell at you if that helps," Millie suggested with a smile, sounding almost excited at the idea.

"No, no, if you're alright with this... then so am I," Rory nodded. "Uhm, I will show you, then. It just happened this morning, here."

He began to walk down the hallway, still a bit startled, but Ashton stopped him for just a moment. Since Millie was right there, Ashton had to lean in and whisper into the other man's ear so that she wouldn't hear.

"One last thing, though... you better actually follow up on what you said." Ashton smiled broadly as Rory turned to him, nodding quietly. And so, with that, they were able to move on.

As long as Rory pulled his weight, there was no reason for him to complain. But if Rory let himself be walked all over, then this wasn't going to work. A place like this school was important. It was currently still one of the safest places, and it seemed like a large amount of the kids in this town that still received protection from the system were here in the first place.

The ones that were above primary school age were more scattered about, many of them being at Market Street, the University, and even Calibur, but this place still had the largest amount of surviving children in a single location.

It was a place that simply had to be protected.

And considering that fact, what Rory showed him next gave Ashton mixed feelings.

It was the hallway with the magical vending machine. But now, there were two more standing right beside it.

Considering that the kiosk had popped up in Market Street, it made sense that other 'shops' would develop further.

The truly shocking, almost frightening, thing was really just what was actually contained in these things now.

The one on the far left was the exact same as the one before. The available random boxes had gone up from white to now having one of them be green. Ashton was considering buying one, but he figured there was little reason for that.

He still had two white boxes with him anyway. There were some things he wanted to test out with these first with those two before dealing with any other random boxes, especially since the green ones were a lot more expensive than the white ones.

There would probably be some new items in rotation here. New potions, new candies, that sort of thing.

But the most interesting part were really the other two.

While the first vending machine held all the classic stuff that Ashton expected, of the other two, one of them held 'survival' items, and the other 'class' items.

Survival items were things like matches and other fire-starters, first-aid items, rope, bags, light sticks, and simple tools like shovels and even small axes. They mostly seemed to be mundane items, since Ashton couldn't feel much magic from them, but some probably had simple magical enhancements that made them a little bit more effective than normal.

It seemed like a good array of items, though Ashton was a little confused why this vending machine was here of all places. It was generally useful to have everywhere, but other locations might be able to make more out of this.

But maybe that was the point... creating conflict.

Shops were an extremely valuable resource, especially because they could keep evolving like this over time and changing into something more and more valuable.

Right now, there were loads of things that could be scavenged from the ruins of this town, but before long, that wouldn't be the case anymore. All the most useful things would be gathered by the survivors. Meanwhile, shops were practically infinite sources of useful items.

Before long, despite the steep cost, getting some of the specialty items in here might be easier than scavenging. The other option was creating them yourself, which was easy enough, but people here would take quite some time to figure everything out. The worst part was that the exact sort of people that didn't cooperate with others well and as such couldn't easily trade with those that could make those items, were the exact sort of people that would try and attack a camp and steal magical items like these vending machines.

There were plenty of people that didn't care that they were putting children in danger, either.

And the second new vending machine made that fact even scarier. It was something that Ashton hadn't seen before.

It was a 'Class Item' vending machine. There weren't many items sold here; they were the magical, white boxes that were given out by quests or boss-kill rewards.

There were options between tools, weapons, armour, clothes, accessories, and a few more niche options. And all the items coming from here would be personalised for one's class. For those with more than one, they could probably choose, or it would give out something that combined aspects of all one's classes.

"Ridiculous," Ashton muttered to himself as he stood in front of the vending machine.

"What?" Millie asked, looking up at Ashton confused. Clearly, she didn't quite get what the big deal was. Neither did Rory.

He was just standing there, a few steps away, not quite sure why Ashton was so perplexed.

"I've never heard of something like this before," Ashton said, though he finished the sentence in his head. 'Of course, they'd obviously come up with new things over the past couple thousand years.'

Finding generic items was already hard enough, but finding things that were practically tailor-made to you? That was almost impossible in a world like this.

"Who knows about this so far?" Ashton asked, but Rory was still confused.

"Hm? A few of the teachers, though I figure it has probably already spread around a bit by now. They popped up just past midnight last night."

"Shit, okay... Try to keep it on the down-low, and tell everyone not to post about it on the community, at least for now. These vending machines are ridiculously valuable," Ashton said. Even he wanted to just take one of these and drop it down into the Living Abode, but that wasn't an option.

While it was dangerous for this to be here, if the people of the primary school camp got their shit together, they could get a massive advantage and really protect this place from enemies.

There were probably some stray shops around that Ashton could take for himself, but this one had to stay here. There were also the magical slot machines at the casino. One of them was already being used for magic practice at Calibur, but the others should still be around.

"Being valuable's not a good thing, I'm guessing?" Rory sighed, and Ashton nodded.

"Not in this case. Just don't spread it around too much. Preferably, also move these to a separate room instead of just keeping them here, a place that you can lock."

Rory thought about it for a moment, and then nodded. "Alright. There's a place I have in mind, a supply closet down the hall. Not even I'd really use it often since it was so out of the way. If we clear it out, there's plenty of space."

"Good. I can have the Drunken Bear do it right now," Ashton said, quickly having the Backpacker get out a bottle of gin.

He had been feeling anxious about this since he woke yesterday, but they were entering a dangerous phase for this town.

Right at the start, there might have been people taking advantage of the chaos to do disgusting, terrible things and indulge their own urges. But that was usually at the level of individuals and small groups. Those were still scattered around, but usually weren't a real threat to larger, organised groups like the real 'camps'.

But now that those camps were starting to stabilise, and the people in charge were holding powers that they literally could not have imagined having just last month, they would grow hungry for more.

It was impossible to stop all conflict going forward, especially if the future quests were as he remembered them to be, but Ashton still wanted to avoid the absolute worst-case scenarios.
