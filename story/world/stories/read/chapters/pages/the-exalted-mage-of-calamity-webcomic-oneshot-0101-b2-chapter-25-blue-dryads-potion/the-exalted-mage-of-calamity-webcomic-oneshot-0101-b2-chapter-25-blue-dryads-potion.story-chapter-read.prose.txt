The entrance hall was much wider than Ronswick's mage tower. Its floor was made of pale gray stone, polished smooth enough to reflect the glow of the hanging mana lamps overhead. Several mages walked through the space, some alone, others in small groups, carrying rolled-up notes, sealed cases, ingredient jars, or stacks of books bound with leather straps. There were even a few familiars, such as birds carrying bags or small dogs with scrolls in their mouths.

A few people glanced at her, but none lingered for long.

Veronica stepped farther inside and approached the front counter.

Behind it stood not people, but stone golem puppets. Each was roughly the size of an average adult, with smooth, rounded heads, segmented arms, and glowing mana veins embedded throughout their bodies. They resembled the wooden puppets back in Ronswick, though it was obvious these were far more advanced, crafted by a much more experienced mage.

“Welcome to the Willow Mage Tower,” one of the stone golems said. Its voice was clear but slightly gravelly, like someone speaking through a hollow tube filled with a few rumbling pebbles. “Please state your name and purpose.”

“Veronica Everwells. Initiate mage,” she replied. “I'm here to speak with an alchemist about purchasing a Blue Dryad's potion.”

The golem creaked softly as the light within its mana veins pulsed. It wrote something in a ledger before looking back up at her.

“Your visit today has been recorded, Veronica Everwells.”

Although it wrote in a physical ledger, the golem was likely already connected to the tower's information network. It could search her records in real time and access everything it needed. The ledger was probably there only as a backup.

“I will inform Lady Rennia,” it said.

Behind the counter sat several thin brass pipes running up the wall before disappearing into the ceiling. A small cord hung beside each one, marked with a different colored tag. The golem turned, reached for one of them, and gave it a firm pull.

A burst of steam shot upward through the pipe.

A sharp hiss echoed overhead, followed by the faint rattle of something moving through the mechanism hidden inside the wall.

Veronica watched with mild interest.

Not pure magic, then. A message system assisted by pressure tubes, likely connected to specific rooms or departments. Simple, but effective. Much faster than sending an apprentice running up three flights of stairs whenever someone arrived. Though, in many ways, it still lagged behind Annesheim. There, a warp pad would have taken her directly to the alchemist instead.

A few seconds later, a faint chime rang from the pipe.

The golem turned back to her. “Alchemist Rennia has been contacted. Please proceed down the left hall. Take the stairs to the third floor. First room on the right.”

“Thank you.”

“You are welcome. Please refrain from touching active research equipment without permission. Please refrain from entering restricted laboratories. Please refrain from consuming unknown reagents. And please refrain from public displays of affection between the hours of 12:00 PM and 7:00 PM.”

Veronica paused. So… you can have public displays of affection after seven?

“Do… people need to be told all that?”

“Yes. Each warning is due to a previous incident.”

“I see.”

That was concerning.

Veronica followed the directions, heading down the hall to the left. The tower grew quieter as she moved farther up. Doors lined the corridor, each marked with a small metal plaque. Most were labeled with department names, storage numbers, or warning symbols.

The third floor smelled much stronger than the first or second. A floral scent mixed with mineral dust, alcohol, something smoking or burning, and a faint trace of citrus lingered in the air. This was most likely the mixture and compound crafting floor, where the tower's alchemists spent their days brewing various concoctions.

Veronica opened the door.

The room beyond was a small research laboratory. Long counters lined the walls, covered with bowls, beakers, sealed jars, polished stones, dried roots, measuring crystals, and several hand-sized cauldrons. Thin glass tubes ran between a few devices, carrying colored liquids from one container to another in slow, measured drops.

Along the left wall stood several narrow glass tanks. One held a pale blue liquid with a small silver leaf floating inside. Another contained black mineral flakes suspended in a clear gel. A third held something that looked unsettlingly like a tiny curled-up lizard, though Veronica hoped it was merely a preserved specimen and not a sleeping familiar.

The room reminded her somewhat of Martin's laboratory, though much smaller and far less dependent on magitech.

At a table near the back, a woman stood with her sleeves rolled up, stirring something inside a small black cauldron no bigger than her hand. She looked to be in her early thirties, with wavy auburn hair tied into a loose knot and several pens tucked through it. Her dark green robe bore the insignia of a certified alchemist.

She stirred the mixture three times, added a pinch of pale powder, then tapped the side of the cauldron with one finger.

The liquid inside flashed bright yellow, briefly illuminating the air above it.

She smiled faintly. “Good. Finally.”

Then she looked over at Veronica.

“You're Veronica Everwells?”

“Yes.”

“Rennia Valore. Alchemist.” She set the cauldron aside and wiped her hands on a cloth. Then she gestured toward a stool near the counter. “The golem said you were asking about a Blue Dryad's Recovery Tonic?”

Veronica took the seat. “That's right.”

“Hmm... not many people ask for that, you know.” Rennia pulled another stool over and sat opposite her at the table, resting an elbow on its surface. “Blue Dryad tonics specialize in repairing damage, specifically cracks, within a mage's mana core. A very rare injury indeed.” She studied Veronica more closely. “Are you purchasing it for yourself?”

“Yes. My mana core has a large crack in it. My tier has dropped because of it. I won't be able to advance until it's repaired.”

“May I ask how it became damaged? Was it due to advancing?”

Veronica shook her head. “No. I was in quite a precarious situation, so I had to burn my mana core to get out alive.”

The moment Veronica mentioned burning her mana core, Rennia's eyes widened.

“Get out alive? I'm surprised you didn't die on the spot!” she said, leaning forward. “That's an extremely dangerous technique. How did you survive the backlash?”

“I have a very special constitution. I only survived because of it.”

Of course, any other mage would have been surprised by what she had said. Burning one's mana core was the mage's equivalent of burning life force, an aura user's last-ditch effort to gain power. In Veronica's case, it had been her exalted form. But because she possessed dual mana cores, the usual consequence of dying from the technique didn’t apply to her.

Rennia pursed her lips, staring at Veronica with clear uncertainty.

“But,” Veronica continued, “because of that, my core has suffered. So I was hoping your mage tower had any Blue Dryad tonics left for me to purchase.”

Rennia straightened on her stool and coughed into her hand. “I see. Well, we don’t have any Blue Dryad tonics at the moment. However, I do have all the ingredients here in my lab to make one. It shouldn’t take more than ten minutes.”

Veronica brightened. “That’s great. How much will it be?”

“12,000 vix. 11,500 for the ingredients, and five hundred for the labor.”

The smile on Veronica’s face fell and died on the floor. That was far more than she expected. The vix she had brought along wouldn’t be enough. Not even close.

But… maybe there was another way she could pay.

“How about a trade?” she asked.

“A trade? What kind of trade?” Rennia asked.

“A recipe. The ingredient and preparation list for Moore’s Reflux.”

Rennia raised a brow, clearly confused. “What is that?”

“It’s a cream mixture. You can apply it to your skin, and it helps repel the surrounding ambient mana from flowing around you.”

“Repel? Why would I ever want to use that?”

Veronica shrugged. “Plenty of reasons, though I’m not saying it’ll be particularly useful in everyday situations. One case would be if someone was undergoing mana deviation, and it became dangerous for any mana to be accidentally re-absorbed into their body. This cream would help reflect the ambient mana while they recover.”

“Oh?” Rennia said, raising her brow even higher. She placed a hand under her chin, one finger touching the middle of her lips. “Mana deviation… a cream like that would certainly help. It’s a rare case, similar to your own, actually. Not many people fall into mana deviation, but having a cream that can alleviate the symptoms would be quite valuable.”

She studied Veronica once more, looking closely. “But why a trade? Is this something you created yourself? Giving me the recipe does not guarantee that you won’t publish it yourself and claim ownership.”

Rennia had a point.

Most alchemical tonics and potions had open recipes. Anyone could visit the mage association and learn the required ingredients for any potion they needed, depending on their standing. That was one of the perks of joining the association.

While a person could distribute their craft locally, distributing it to the wider market, such as other towns, cities, or even nations, was restricted unless the recipe was shared. Only one person could claim ownership of a craft, and any sales of that craft came with a small remuneration to the original owner.

If Veronica wanted to, she could give the recipe to Rennia, then immediately claim ownership of it herself, making the recipe useless to Rennia.

“I understand your concern, but I won’t be claiming ownership of it,” Veronica said.

“Then does someone else have ownership of it? Someone I don’t know, or someone who hasn’t visited the mage association yet?” Rennia asked, still skeptical.

Veronica shook her head. “It was something my teacher came up with. One of their many personal recipes. But they have since passed away, leaving only me with the fruits of their research. No one else knows of this recipe.”

It was a convenient excuse. While all alchemists were mages, not all mages were alchemists.

By relying on a mysterious teacher, she covered her bases for why she knew so many alchemical recipes she could leverage in the future. And of course, she had told Sage to specifically provide one that wouldn’t be claimed for at least two years, giving her plenty of time to use it.

Sorry Moore, or whoever you are, but I’m going to have to borrow your work. It’s all to save the world. I swear.

Rennia hummed, thinking it over. “If what you said is true, and I can get your statement written into a contract, then I suppose that will work.” She crossed her arms, sitting taller. “However, a recipe like that, even with its situational use and niche field, is still an alchemical recipe, correct? How much are you selling it for? I’ll pay the difference, but if it’s too much, you’ll have to wait until I can gather more funds.”

Veronica froze. She did her best to hide her surprise, sitting still and feigning a thoughtful hum.

Sage? Uh, how much should I charge for this? I was expecting a one-for-one trade, but she’s saying the recipe is more valuable than that? Something stupid like repelling ambient mana?

[Every potion has its uses, even if it seems trivial. Considering her words and eagerness to purchase, despite a lack of funds… I recommend stating the price as twenty thousand vix.]

That much?

[Unfortunately, there is not enough data available for me to give you a better evaluation. Individual uses of Moore’s Reflux are not recorded in my database, nor is the given sale price of the mixture. A safe offer would be double the price of the Blue Dryad tonic.]

“How about… let’s say 21,000 vix,” Veronica said after a moment. She did her best to keep her expression neutral.

Rennia stared at her for a moment, right into her eyes. She was looking at them, but she wasn’t focused. It was almost as if she were calculating the numbers in her mind, and Veronica just happened to be in the way.

“All right. I can do that. And how about this, just to keep things even? I’ll make the Blue Dryad’s potion for you and pay you ten thousand vix. A one thousand vix discount off the potion,” she said after a few moments.

Veronica waited, letting the time pass as she pretended to weigh the offer. It was a bit suspicious that Rennia was willing to pay her an extra thousand vix. Perhaps the recipe was more valuable than she realized. However, Veronica didn’t care much. She had many more things on her long list of stolen, or borrowed, recipes.

After a few seconds, she accepted. “I agree to that offer. The recipe for Moore’s Reflux, in exchange for a Blue Dryad potion and ten thousand vix.”

Rennia smiled. “Perfect.” She stood, patting down her lap. “I’ll go draft the contract.”

Back at her inn, Veronica sat in the center of her bed with her legs crossed. She was the first one back; Gwen and Nasha were probably still out exploring the city.

The Blue Dryad’s potion she received sat atop the bedside drawer, cork loosened and bottle emptied. With its contents consumed, Veronica only needed to circulate her mana throughout her body and guide it all back into her mana cores.

Veronica closed her eyes and focused solely on the mana flowing through her body. She let out a deep breath, urging the mana to push outward and fill all of her mana veins. Slowly, she took a larger breath, extending it as long as possible. At the same time, her mana began to flow in reverse, reabsorbing back toward the mana cores in the center of her chest.

As her mana cores slowly accepted the mana, the tension she had felt for the past several weeks began to subside.

[The cracks on your mana cores have been sealed. Please rest for twenty-four hours before using any significant amount of mana to ensure the recovery process completes. I will enter standby mode while I assist in repairing your mana cores. All of my functions will be unavailable for twenty-four hours.]

Veronica opened her eyes. She looked down at her hand and flexed her fingers as she pumped mana through her arm.

“Alright. Finally stable.” She resumed her original pose and closed her eyes once more. “Now to hit the third-tier again.”

Since she had already ascended to the third-tier once, she could reach it again just by meditating, without needing to use a catalyst like kassal oil. It would probably take a few hours, but that was fine with her. Spend the rest of the day ascending, then take it slow tomorrow.

As Veronica sat there, meditating and focusing on her mana cores, a thought came to mind.

Veronica opened her eyes. “Oh, that’s pretty bad.”

She had just realized a problem with Moore’s Reflux.

The recipe given to Rennia was coined under Moore’s name. If the alchemist Moore was already researching such a potion, this would be a blatant declaration that she had stolen it. Calling it Moore’s Reflux, the same as his own name, left almost no way out of being accused if she were ever to meet him.

“Maybe… maybe he discovers it by accident in the future. If that’s the case, then the name wouldn’t be a problem if it existed before he even began researching,” she murmured. “Just a coincidence…”

Hopefully that was the case. But there wasn’t anything she could do about it any longer.

Veronica sighed and closed her eyes once more, focusing on her meditation.
