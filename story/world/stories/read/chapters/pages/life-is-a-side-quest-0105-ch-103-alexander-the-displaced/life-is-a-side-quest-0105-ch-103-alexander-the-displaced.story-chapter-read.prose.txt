Alexander managed to catch his breath as he stretched out his limbs. His arms had gone numb from the intense pressure of the dragon’s grip. He looked around them to see Emily comfortably lying in a soft cushion of moss. She seemed to be mentally taxed from the events. Alexander noticed again the odd contrast between her reactions to events and everyone else’s, as if her mind alone was more anchored in reality.

“Wherever you go from here, magi, we will be watching. If you get out of line, we shall rip a hole into your plane and seek out revenge against you. So tread your feet lightly on the world,” the Queen threatened in a matter-of-fact voice.

He looked the Queen in the eye and responded, “If that is what you feel you must do. I don’t know what you use to measure such actions. I can only do my best.”

“Hmm…” the dragon rumbled, causing even the ground around her to vibrate.

The moss around them started to form a circle. Small roots wound around it like twine, forming a laurel. The moss moved and placed it on Emily’s head. Emily shifted in her sleep, but the laurel held secure. Alexander activated his glasses and saw a weave of several magic types infuse into the vine crown.

“What is that?” Alexander asked.

“A gift for the Dunilith to remember this place. It is closer to her home than she is ever likely to find on your plane.”

“The gateway comes,” the Queen murmured as she shifted her head.

Alexander looked in the same direction as the dragon.

The trees on the outskirts of the glade parted, and a large tree stump glided over the landscape toward them. Alexander bent down and placed his arm on Emily’s shoulder. She opened her eyes and sat up with a yawn.

“I think it’s time to go home,” Alexander told Emily.

“Oh, thank goodness we didn’t have to wander around looking for that. Thank you, Your Majesty.” Emily stood and curtsied.

“You are welcome.”

“What will you do now?” Alexander asked the Queen.

“We believe it is time to seek out the Widow of the Woods. Her actions have been far too brazen as of late,” the Queen rumbled.

“She has one of our companions. Also, I am not sure if Johnny and his dog managed to get out with the others. Could you help them as well?”

“We shall see. Their behavior in these lands will dictate their treatment.”

Alexander debated looking for Johnny, but he needed to get Emily home first.

“I will return for them soon.”

“Noooo, you shall not,” the dragon responded with a thunderous rumble. “Their fate does not concern you at present. Do not overestimate your ability, magi, or you will be torn from your heights like an impetuous pecking raven.”

Alexander didn’t like being told he could not attempt a rescue, but he was forced to accept the authority of this queen or he would have to make an already shaky agreement with the dragon even worse. So he simply agreed and picked up his things. Flick jumped on his head, and he nodded to the dragon slightly.

“Take care, young Dunilith. Keep your mind sharp and grow into someone who can carry your heritage with pride.”

“I will try.” Emily nodded. A noticeably absent confidence shone in her face.

They walked to the tree stump, and Alexander waved his hand for Emily to go first. She crawled into the stump.

“Magi… do not trust the administrators.”

His mind raced back to a conversation he had with Ben. “The badminton players… I think I have been warned about them before. What will they do?”

“They will place strings upon you and puppet you for their amusement. The Dunilith wisely decided to hide from them, though their fate is uncertain. Do not let her be a pawn for them.”

Alexander nodded again and followed Emily. As they approached the familiar tree-stump portal, he began to ask Emily a question but paused. Emily instead asked:

“Alexander, what are we?”

“Apparently, a human wizard and a human with Dunilith heritage,” he answered straightforwardly.

Emily shrugged, as if saying that she should have known.

“But if you are asking what our relationship is, I really couldn’t say. Emily, are you by chance drawn to my magic?”

Emily paused. “I don’t know… but that isn’t necessarily a bad thing. Some girls like big muscles, some like deep pockets, some like a bad boy, and some look for… other qualities. If a relationship is to last, they have to start somewhere.”

Alexander nodded. “Have you heard of Facebook?”

“Yes, Alexander, I have heard of Facebook.”

“You know of the relationship status people put up that say ‘it’s complicated’?”

“Yeah… I think you’re right.” Emily grinned.

“Alexander, would you like to be complicated with me for an undetermined time frame?” she asked, mimicking Alexander’s voice.

Alexander smiled and nodded. “As you wish.”

Emily blushed.

Alexander gestured to the tree stump. “Ladies first.”

Emily looked around one last time, drawing in the scenery, before crawling into the portal. Alexander waited an appropriate amount of time before following.

Pushing his hands to the sides to steady himself, he crawled into the stump, seeing the portal before him. But as he began to pass through, he felt a flash of nausea hit him like a wave. The portal distorted and pulled at him, sucking him and Flick through it like a vacuum. He yelped as he was spit out and sent tumbling on the ground. Steadying himself, he looked up dizzily. For a moment he thought he was experiencing vertigo. His vision blacked out; he rubbed at his eyes. When he was certain he was fine, he focused on his surroundings to see a world of shadows.

“Ahh, shoot.”

Squeak.

“Well, this is not where we were supposed to end up,” Alexander said, standing and seeing odd shadowy shapes of walls and trees—a silhouette outline from the red-hued skies behind them.

Flick spun in a circle before climbing up on Alexander’s head again.

Alexander examined the landscape: a medieval-style rampart in front of him and tall trees surrounding the rest of the area.

“I hope this isn’t another one of those stupid story quests… what do you think, Flick? City or forest?”

Chirp.

“I agree. I am tired of dark forests,” Alexander said before marching toward what he assumed was an old city.

Alexander projected his mana out from him in a cone. He was pleased to see that color and detail returned to the area and at a much greater distance than he had managed before. The contrast in his proficiency projecting mana from his last venture gave him a small measure of satisfaction. Looking left and right, he saw a moat, and just outside of his range was a bridge.

“Hmm. I’m guessing the bridge leads to the city entrance.”

Flick shivered on Alexander’s head.

“I didn’t expect a shadow dragon to be scared of the shadows.”

Squeak! Chirp.

“Oh, of course. You’ve got my six.”

Flick spun a circle on his hat, shoving it over his eyes before settling down again. Alexander pushed the brim of his hat back up and started walking toward the bridge.

Sooner than he expected, he started walking on the old wooden structure. His sense of distance was distorted in this place, just as it was the last time. He felt more confident that he was in the old shadowy world from his first meeting with the warlock.

The bridge reminded him of structures from the early first century. Looking at the piles in the water holding up the bridge, it seemed to be worked by hand rather than with any modern construction techniques. Looking at the tightly woven ropes, he saw that there was a drawbridge. So the moat was more likely a river if boats were able to pass through it.

As he was nearly fifty yards from the city gate on the other side of the bridge, he saw guards, though they were not human. They were short at nearly three feet tall. They had characteristics like those of a mouse made humanoid. They had stern expressions but, despite them, were undoubtedly quite cute. They had small pieces of metal armor and conical metal hats with feathers in them.

As he approached, the mice-men yelled out with soft, squeaky voices.

“Halt your feet there, giant! What business dealings do you have?” they said, lowering their tiny spears.

“Uh, good day.” Alexander was about to introduce himself, but after the past two days’ events he decided to refrain. “I’m looking for directions?” he responded instead.

The mice looked at each other in confusion.

“To where do you seek to go?” one asked suspiciously.

“I think… I’m looking for my planet?”

The mice both looked at each other and simultaneously let out squeaky laughs. One lost his balance and fell over, kicking his feet. “The giant has good jokes!” it squeaked out between bouts of giggles.

Alexander stood nonplussed, watching the little creatures rolling in their amusement. After they regained control, he let one of the soldier mice stand back up and wipe away tears.

“I am sorry. I probably should mention I am not from this… space.”

“You are in good, good company, giant!” the mouse said with amusement.

“I don’t think I understand your meaning.”

The mice looked at each other and grinned.

“Look, giant, we are soldiers meant to protect our place homes. We are not in the information-knowings business. Do you have goods to trade? If not, be you on your ways.”

“I assume you don’t take American dollars.”

“Are they tasting good?”

“…No.”

One of the soldiers grabbed a lantern, approached him, and began looking him over.

“Those are fine buttons on your shirt clothings…”

“Yes, yes, polished and clear,” the other said with a greedy smile.

Alexander thought about it and shuffled in his bag. He was glad he didn’t have to take out his magic resources. From the sounds of it, all of the magically integrated worlds valued those highly. He was bound to stand out in whatever city this was, but they might just heighten his profile beyond what he was comfortable with.

Instead he pulled out a pocket sewing kit. It had five buttons inside: two for pants and three for shirts. He pulled out one of the clear small shirt buttons that matched the one on his hiking shirt and held it up.

“Will this do?” he asked.

“Yes, yes!” The soldier held out his tiny clawed hand.

He handed it over, and the soldier retreated to his guard friend. They looked it over with bright, excited eyes.

They began talking about a cheese monger they would go see tonight. Alexander coughed.

“Would you direct me to someone who might be able to help get me a map?”

They looked up after remembering themselves.

“Yes, yes!” They knocked on the short gate three times.

Alexander heard a board being removed from behind the door, and it swung open.

The guard handed a wooden circle to Alexander. Taking it, he saw writing and holes in it resembling a button.

“No violence, no cats, and no swindling!” one of the guards warned him.

“How about dragons?” Alexander asked.

The mice looked at each other and started laughing again.

“You are much funny, giant! You will do well here home!”

Alexander just gave a pleasant smile and moved to duck under the gateway.
