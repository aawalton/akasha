Ming carried Shuren’s body on his back, walking towards the entrance of the canyon. At the very least, he should hide amongst the forest and recuperate, rather than out in the open.

Once Aerestia brought back up, they would easily be able to find him and Shuren.

So long as it didn’t take too long.

He walked past one of the corpses.

A Silvent man with viridescent hair, who joined the sect to feed his family and children.

“I didn’t expect Shuren of all people to try to save him, even if it meant risking that demon’s wrath. Not once did I ever see him hesitate when it came to killing demonic cultists. His hatred runs deep. And yet…”

Ming wasn’t sure what exactly caused Shuren to change his mind. He couldn’t recall any specific incident that led him to even be lenient just this once.

“My sword was shattered. But the entire blade was cleanly destroyed apart from the hilt. I thought he didn’t use a sword, but it doesn’t seem like it.” Ming talked to himself, thinking back on Shuren’s background.

If his conjecture was right, Liu Shuren was most likely one of the scions of the Liu Clan. If not, one of the descendants of their strongest members.

If so, him being in a sub Immortal sect like the Seven Clouds Sect made no sense what so ever.

His status alone should’ve been enough for him to live a life of comfort and luxury amongst the Immortal realms.

Yet here he was, barely making it by, with an even lower cultivation level than Ming himself.

“Just what is happening in the Liu Clan?”

If he was someone they wanted to get rid of politically, then he was extremely weak right now, and should easily be assassinated.

Or did he have a silent protector in the distance?

Then did the Seven Clouds Sect know of his situation, or were they also unaware?

Ming grit his teeth to bear with the pain, focusing on taking his next step. Even though he stopped the blood flowing from the hole in his body, he could only sustain it for so long. His control of his muscles was strong, and eating a healing pill would increase his regeneration, but with an Iron Talisbody it wouldn’t be enough.

“Whatever. It’ll take time for him to open up. So long as he asks me for help, I’ll do what I must.”

Heading towards the forest, Ming slightly sped up his tracks.

“Don’t die on me now, Liu Shuren!”

Elder Swordfall stood at the peak of the canyon.

He focused his attention on Liu Shuren, who stood standing, unyielding in the face of his enemy.

He only looked with a stoic gaze, unaffected by the disciple’s achievements. The man who stood with a broken sword in his hand, refusing to fall even as he became unconscious. Barely hanging on to life with the amount of blood he had lost.

“You’re walking towards a dead end.”

He watched as Ming carried Shuren on his back, walking towards the entrance of the canyon. Even though his injuries were also grievous, he did everything he could to save his friend. To at least lead them to a place to recuperate.

Elder Swordfall held a severed arm in his hand, but it soon turned to shadows of writhing snakes as it burnt in the sun.

Yet he didn’t continue to chase Lixi. He was only here to check up on the disciples.

He gazed towards the caravan, its contents completely emptied out.

Elder Swordfall appeared in front of Ming. “You’ve done well.”

And seeing Swordfall’s presence, Ming relaxed, finally collapsing from his injuries.

He summoned a grandiose flying nimbus, with a small deck on top. Throwing Ming to one of the beds, Elder Swordfall grabbed Shuren for a moment.

He lifted up his shirt, looking towards his chest.

And with two fingers placed on his heart, he noticed the absence of a pulse.

“A body perfected to be a sword.”

Elder Swordfall looked at the sky.

But what he was looking towards was far beyond it.

“To think in death was your first smile. Not once did you show me this face of yours, Yijun.”

Reizou gazed at the ground, the grass blades swaying with the winds of eve. The hem of his worn, white shirt rippled, and glimpses of his boundless scars surged out to the world. After breaths of silence, he raised his chin and took a deep breath, his dark hair, devoid of all light, gently falling down his shoulders like abyssal waterfalls.

“Her soul has moved on to Genesis. I can’t make use of these materials,” Reizou said with a blunt voice, his hollow, crimson eyes unshaken. Yet a pulse of disappointment came from his heart, one he did not expect.

Was it regret? Or was it sadness? Or was it a wretched form of love, never seeing the kiln of life?

Only with the distant second pulse would he realise the answer.

The sweet nectar of silence was broken.

Wheezes and growls of a ghastly nature bellowed from the distance, akin to a dying puppy. The blades of grass fell, the dirt in the earth erupted, the sludge of the beast festered, bubbling everything in its path to ruin. The man ignored the sounds of the Deviant Beasts, allowing the night air to cleanse his soul.

“How absurd. A being like you, the one most desecrating of death, is capable of praying at a grave. If others knew of this, your image would fall, oh Grand Disciple!” A mocking tone cut through the air, coming from a rock near the small lake.

Lixi leaned with a hand in his robe, whistling a tune from his childhood. Yet, an opaque jade snake at his shoulder hissed, speaking in Lixi’s twisted voice. “Is the air of the outside world sweet? Is the quiet of the night under that purgatorial eye blissful? Is the wasted meaning of a corpse in its barrow beautiful?”

The whimpers and wheezes of the shadows, the long forgotten tune of the north, the slithering sayings of the snake. The soft winds of the night picked up.

“I unearthed an orange, small flower along the way.” Reizou brought out a luminescent blue flower from his pocket. “Without the moisture, it has now died. Yet, under the night sky, its hue has changed.” He leaned down, placing the flower on the unmarked grave.

Yet even as the wind grew faster, the flower did not move.

“There are many possibilities in this world. But in the world beyond death, there are boundless more of these possibilities, undiscovered and unknown. Why can a dead flower still change its colours in the night? Why can a corpse of a woman, bereft of a soul, smile in the grave? Why can the caricature of its eye continue to endow the world with its blessings?”

Facing Lixi, the man pondered out loud. The sight of an amalgamation of deviant wolves sewn to the hem of a dark snake came into his view. The beasts that the caravan had been smuggling to hand over to someone.

The wheezes of the cursed beings transitioned to a guttural howl of madness. Canine limbs grew out of the putrid snake, and with the quietude of Lixi’s whistling, it acted. Within a few breaths, it would easily pounce on Reizou, tearing it to shreds.

The harsh wind picked up even further.

The head of the snake almost bit into Reizou, but slammed into an invisible wall, even as its dozens of canine legs continued to run on the spot. The heads of the deviant wolves roared, spitting out crimson flames towards their enemy. Just the mere presence of these flames charred the surrounding into darkness, yet those flames disintegrated into nothingness the closer it encroached upon his body.

Lixi’s casual demeanour had changed. He stopped relaxing on the rock, standing up to observe his rival.

Reizou stepped forth, and that invisible wall of winds became a veil of dissimilation. With each step, a portion of the beast was wiped into nothingness. The snake was ripped into tiny strips, floating into the man’s chest. The canine legs were shredded into meat, absorbed by Reizou’s body. The dozens of deviant wolves, and the maddening flames of crimson, were wiped from the realm of the living, their pitiful souls falling into the endless space inside of Reizou.

Within a dozen steps, the giant amalgamation over a dozen metres was wiped from the face of the earth. Lixi narrowed his eyes as he watched the commotion. Just the crimson flames of those beasts are capable of melting the bones of a cultivator with even a Gold Grade Talisbody. He’s that confident in that barrier of his?

“I desire only to find the secrets; the truths of that boundary beyond life. And the results of that desire is precisely why I was chosen.” Reizou didn’t even look at Lixi, but rather face the night sky once more. The arrogance of that mere action irritated Lixi.

“You broke through Core Genesis?” Lixi asked in a rare, but serious tone.

“We were meant to deliver a gift to the subsidiary of the Dragon Lotus Pavilion,” Reizou said as a matter of fact.

Lixi grew a mocking smile at Reizou, elated at his ignorance. “That was a ruse. We were simply fanning the flames to smoke out the rat. The gift has long been delivered. Right now, the village where their successors were staying is torn by the storms of chaos. Her right-hand man should be cleaning up the place, getting what we need.”

Placing a hand with his arm, leading the snake back inside, Lixi continued. “The bloodline of that vagrant clan that runs in the Dragon Lotus Pavilion was set to stem from the Immortal who led the second Grand Era, supposedly with the intel the sect found. If it’s true, and the sect leader succeeds, then our sponsors will be very happy.”

Reizou lost interest as Lixi continued. These matters would not relate to him, even as the Grand Disciple of their sect. He walked past Lixi with nonchalance, before parting with some words past his shoulders.

“Your servants have voluntarily decided to become subjects for my work.” Reizou said these words like the light matter it was. The screeching of an eagle came from above, a crimson-feathered shadow landing to his side.

For the first time, Lixi held a deep frown.

But he could not act. Not anymore.

Knowing that at the age of 19, the orphan of the Scum Heap in the distant oceans, Reizou, had become a Rank 3 without support from any elders or factions. A man bereft of noble lineage. Void of a grand inheritance. Barren of the nurture and care of a family.

The rise of a heartless genius.

As Reizou departed, Lixi could only crack his neck with the help of a hand. “Honestly. I met a man with a hypocritical heart, and have to deal with this hollow husk of a Grand Disciple.”

A gourmand fragrance spread in the air, and Lixi took out a wooden ring. “Could you hide any better? Take it. We uphold the deals of your master.”

The ring flew across the prairie, landing behind the rocks near the lake. The fragrance dissipated alongside it.

The irritation of Reizou’s existence diminishing, Lixi’s usual smirk crept up on his face.

His severed arm continued to writhe with snakes, gradually forming into a new limb.

He was lucky. If that elder continued to chase him, he would be dead.

“It seems the truce between the Sect Leader and Aether Ul-Regen is indeed real. Exactly how did she manage that?”

Having now gained info on the spies in their sect, Lixi gazed towards Cloud City.

“Don’t think we can’t play the same game, Seven Clouds.”
