“Don’t look at me like that, Jerzy,” I said. “If you had better dental hygiene you might not have been in this position. And if you hadn’t tried to mind-trap me, you wouldn’t have gotten burned. You need to take accountability for your own mistakes.”

“I like to think they’re still in there somehow, you know. Even if their soul has moved on to burn forever in one of the hells—because they are definitely not getting into The Isles of the Blessed—it gives them a bit more personality. Anyone can chop off a head and hang it from their belt. Not everyone can give their severed heads personality. I think it adds to the coolness factor.”

“What? No. I mean, yes. Since I’m apparently Saint Hecate now, I could sell the ones I don’t need anymore and call them blessings. People see me wearing them. People think I’m super cool. Everyone will want one of their own.”

I rubbed my hands and grinned. “We’re gonna be so rich!”

I leaned in. “Ah, my young student. You are correct. Scarcity will make the value rise. Like I said. We’re going to be rich.”

“That… Yes. But that is also part of my masterplan. They’d be status symbols. And if people can’t afford one, or there simply aren’t enough to go around, they will have to create their own. People will start ambushing inquisitors to collect their heads.”

So now people aren’t just dismembering inquisitors to upgrade their skills, now they’re also chopping them up to make money… Or just because they want to look cool.”

I held up Jerzy’s ugly little head and pretended he was talking in a squeaky voice. “That’s a pretty good idea, Hecate! You’re so smart! I suuuure wish I hadn’t tried to kill you!”

Vasil was staring at me with his mouth open.

Kasia blinked, then shook her head. “You came up with all of this just now?”

I snorted and slapped my thigh. “Get it? Because he’s missing a tooth, so I said denture instead of venture!”

They were both staring at me now. Vasil’s mouth was still open. Neither of them was laughing.

“Oh come on!” I said. “That was a good one!”

Maybe they didn’t get it. Or maybe they simply didn’t hear me. It was possible that one of the birds flying above us had squawked at the exact moment I delivered the punchline, causing both of them to miss it. I mean, I didn’t hear any birds, but that could have been because I was so engrossed in delivering my exceptionally witty joke. Should I repeat it? Damn birds and their squawking. I should have just shot them and then I wouldn’t be in this predicament.

You know what, I’ll just repeat the joke. I leaned a little closer to them in case a bird squawked again. "I’m something of an entrepreneur. A venture capitalist, if you will. Or in Jerzy’s case—a denture capitalist.”

“Yes,” Vasil said. “We heard you the first time.”

I narrowed my eyes. “So why aren’t you laughing?”

“It’s not that it wasn’t funny…” Kasia said, rubbing her arm.

Vasil cleared his throat. “I think I can speak for the both of us when I say that your joke was very clever. The reason we are not laughing is because we were not expecting your plan. It was just…”

“Genius?” I asked.

“Well. Sort of. Yes. It just might be crazy enough to work. Any advantage, big or small, will help us. I think it is a brilliant idea. I just… Sorry, Hecate, but I am at a loss for words. You keep surprising me. The way you got the people on your side. The way you convinced them to offer the inquisitor’s body parts for upgrades, leaving no remains. Now this. The way your mind works is beyond me.”

I brushed some imaginary dust off my sleeve and nodded. “Yeah. Don’t worry, Vasil, stick around and you’ll learn a thing or two. I like to plan things, you know. In my head.”

His expression turned skeptical. “You do?”

I paused, stared at the horizon for a couple of seconds, then looked back at him. “Not really.”

Kasia startled me by making a sound that sounded like air escaping out of a wet waterskin.

"Anyway," I said, ignoring the weird noise Kasia was still making. “I know how much you like talking about plans and walking four steps ahead and stuff, but let’s not get sidetracked. I was about to see what benefits Jerzy over here was planning to provide me, until you distracted me.”

“I was just—” Vasil started.

I held up my finger. “Upupup! You’re doing it again.”

Vasil stared at me with his usual mouth-open stare, but didn’t say anything. I squinted and glared back for a moment, just in case he was planning on interrupting my grandiose reveal a second time. Nothing came, so I cast [Artifact Index] and read the description aloud.
