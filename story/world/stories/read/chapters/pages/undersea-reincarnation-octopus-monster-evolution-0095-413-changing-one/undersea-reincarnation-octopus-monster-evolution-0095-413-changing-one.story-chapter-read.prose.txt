Henry couldn’t tell if he was biting off more than he could chew, but he couldn’t bring himself to be diplomatic right this moment. Only moments ago, he had been worried sick, especially with his pathfinding skill continuously being messed with. He had expected some sort of monster or something dangerous trying to isolate Maurice. The worry had been suffocating.

And then the truth came through, and all that worry had ignited into cold, murderous fury at the sight of his friend in chains.

Henry was twitching, ready to start swinging at the mildest sign of hostility, even if he was still in the process of assessing what he was dealing with. Quickly enough, his Octominds gave him the levels of the two masked individuals, and those two were simply low A-ranks. Inconsequential.

The problem was the one without a mask. And while his first encounter with the Merfolk should have been more pleasant and polite, considering the circumstances, he didn’t mind boosting his Identify to get a more precise reading off of her. If she got offended, so be it.

They were the ones putting collars on people.

[Merfolk (A) - Lvl 37]

Having sensed his Identify, she furrowed her brows at him, but Henry only glared harder, looming closer over them as Lightning danced down his limbs.

She was manageable. Likely difficult. But definitely manageable after his recent upgrade.

“[So. What will it be?]”

At this, the water around Blue Mask grew agitated as he raised his spear. “You will—”

Henry’s limb coiled, ready to hammer down on the pesky nail. But the leader, the unmasked one, firmly grabbed the speaker by the shoulder and wrenched him back, and without looking away from Henry, she spoke.

“Changing One, we did not mean to offend. But this human could be an enemy.”

Henry eyed the speaker. While the two at her back were a mixed bag of hurt-pride and wariness, the leader was all friendliness. And greed. The friendliness, she was projecting at him, trying to appease his anger like she might do to less intelligent beings. But the greed? That was just below the surface.

It wasn’t hard to put two and two together. Her friendliness might be genuine, but it wasn’t coming without strings attached, and frankly? Henry had no time for it.

“[He isn’t. Release him. I will not ask again.]”

Now, he could sense them all bristle, even the unmasked one wasn’t liking an upstart B-rank talking back to her, and maybe that’s why she didn’t try to stop what happened next. Though Henry wondered… With so many of his Avatars around, why did they think they could sneak up on him?

They came at him from above, shedding their invisibility. A team of five, mask-less, but clearly in uniform, and they would be on him in a second. Maybe two.

Plenty of time for him and his Octominds to react and ponder.

Henry fired off five instantaneous Identifies to grasp what he was dealing with, and he was surprised to see the results that his Octominds fed him.

All five hovered between level 18 and 25, which Henry found a little surprising.

There really were a lot of A-ranks on the Reach. It likely still had something to do with how little space there was here compared to the rest of the world, so the levels were even easier to get. Still, he wondered, between the rest of the world and the Reach, which had more non-monstrous A-ranks? The oceans had to win that when it came to non-humanoids. But the rest?

Most of them must all be here, he mused as he channeled Unfettered Echoes and Predation to five different instances of Evershifting Strike and just so he didn’t create any bad blood, he only used the Unimpeded attribute. Henry strongly believed that if he were to use Cutting or Heavy, he might vaporize the people coming down on him. The people in question didn’t have time to react, as by now, they were barely .3 seconds from making contact. But the mask-less Merfolk he’d been speaking to did.

She raised a hand, eyes glowing as power built in her minuscule frame, but then, eight Apostles turned their attention to her, and eight Rupture-cast Telekinesis instances joined to wrap and shove her back. She could have been trying to help them. Or stop them. He couldn’t tell. But he didn’t need her input, and this should clearly display that he could back his threats.

Wide-eyed, the purple nimbus carried her away while at the same time, five skyscraper-sized arms flew up and came into contact with four A-ranks. Not five. One of them had managed to teleport away.

Henry’s arms moved so fast that the water around them was vaporized, and by the time his arm reached the A-ranks, they were wrapped in a layer of burning steam. So he didn’t sense the impact. Not with the difference in size and weight. But with how many eyes he had around him, he could see their forms, and what the impact had done to them.

Clearly enough, all of them were specialists in close-combat, so the four that got hit didn’t all lose consciousness. But two of them hung in the water limply, red blood streaming out of their mouths and whatever other limbs they tried to block the impact with. One was painfully trying to swim away, while one had tanked the hit well enough, but Henry could sense how unwilling that one was to come after him. As for the one who’d teleported away, Henry saw him rush to help those who’d passed out and from the furtive glances he—or she—was throwing at Henry, they were expecting him to continue the onslaught.

Henry wasn’t planning on it, not unless they forced it. But the message had been sent and received. They were all taken aback by how hard the impact had been. Though now, he could feel the mask-less leader push against his Telekinesis, which folded soon after, or rather, Henry let it go instead of fighting against it and earning himself a headache.

Thankfully, while all this had been taking place, a certain crab had dug himself out of his chest cavity, bursting out like some sort of parasitic alien, and with a quick Blink, Maurice latched himself somewhere between Henry’s massive eyes. And with how tiny he was, Henry needed to look at him through one of his Avatars.

“[Hi Henry!]” Maurice sent, waving a claw. “[Couldn’t use my skills because of that collar. But I think I know what we’re missing for the teleport. And uh… we can go if you want?]”

The leader—and the two masked figures—had watched the crab burst out of the teen’s chest with a look of horror, but now that the picture was becoming clearer, they were slowly recovering, and they were reacting.

But Henry had gotten what he had come for.

“Changing One!” called out the tiny leader, raising both arms. “Wait—”

Henry took Maurice and returned to the surface, dismissing all of his Avatars below. He’d recast them. And maybe he’d eventually spare an Avatar to talk to the Merfolk. He was interested to learn more about them, about how they interrupted his divination, and about the title they had called him. But he’d rather do that when no one would get any ideas, and in any case, he wasn’t in the mood right now. He was here to find the orb and potentially get Velistraine back home, not to play diplomat with people he didn’t know, and so, with Maurice in tow, Henry turned himself invisible and began shrinking as he flew toward the lost orb.

“[You’re not gonna tell me off about teleporting away like that?]”

Henry was quiet for a moment, then he let out a sigh. “[I was more worried about you than angry. It was an accident, I’m assuming?]”

“[Yup! Didn’t know it would take me that far. Or that I’d end up right next to their territory. And that they were already there waiting to ambush anyone who’d come near.]”

Shaking his head, Henry flew low, just in case of fliers with sharp eyes. He wondered how much of that was an accident, or if the “random” teleport had somehow keyed itself to the nearest people around or something of that vein. But at the end of the day…

“[Well, no harm done. For us, anyway,]” he simply said, trying to shake off the adrenaline as he watched their surroundings. “[Now though… what were you saying about figuring something out?]”

Hammond watched quietly as their tracker walked slowly around the site. They were back where the eagles had been fought, and this time, the Warden was with them, along with seven other people. All A-ranks, all ready to assess these… new players. To pluck them out early if they were going to become a thorn.

Well, ten A-ranks. There were also five B-ranks that had recently joined the settlements, but those were deadweights. At least until they crossed into A-rank.

Eying the tracker, Hammond watched as the expert crouched and tasted the poisoned dirt, wondering if this was all overkill. He’d been wondering the same in one form or another for a bit, now.

If he and Omar had kept this to themselves, they would be having a grand old time in a tavern right about now, and glancing to the side at his senior, he felt the old man was thinking the same thing, considering the look Omar gave him back.

“I got their scent. Let’s move,” the tracker simply said, and so, they all followed.

Hammond… might take a couple of days to rethink his options once this was all over. A-rank was turning out to be more of a pain in the ass than he had expected.
