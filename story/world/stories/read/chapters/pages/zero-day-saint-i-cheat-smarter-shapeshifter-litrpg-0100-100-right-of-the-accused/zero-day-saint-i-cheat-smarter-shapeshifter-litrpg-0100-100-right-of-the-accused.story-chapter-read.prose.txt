The sudden appearance of what looked very much like an honest-to-god angel did cause the guards to slow down their approach a bit.

Jamie and Zi'rah couldn't help but do a double take either.

But the important part was that their dots remained white. Back when I had frantically healed the hyenas after the battle for what had been the gnoll fortress, I couldn't possibly have anticipated the doors my mercy would open. But that act, healing the Created, had been rewarded by the Blessing.

It had granted me Shepherd's Spirit. A trait that made my efforts to avoid violent conflict with the Created more likely to succeed. I hadn't thought about it much lately, but odds were good that quite a few things I'd pulled off wouldn't have worked without it.

The guards slowed down even more when they got a closer look at Aleria and the scorch marks on her skin.

"Aleria… What happened to you? Did they do this?" His eyes darted between Jamie, Zi'rah, and me, all of us unarmed. Both Jamie and I carried weapons for Zi'rah in our inventories.

"No. They helped me overcome the corruption the Veil Speaker put upon me. It is what caused those wounds. We have a traitor in our midst."

The two guards exchanged glances, realizing they were definitely above their pay grade.

"Weren't you granted a spirit to assist you? To—" As he was about to spell it out, he realized who I was. "Is that the one you were supposed to eliminate?"

Before Aleria could reply, I took a step forward. "I am. Your Veil Speaker claimed I had poisoned Aleria to rot your people from the inside. I demand the Right of the Accused."

Aleria stepped next to me. "And I will be her advocate."

While the elves lacked the same formal system we had, they had their own version of innocent until proven guilty—the Right of the Accused. Anyone accused of wrongdoing could publicly defend themselves before their accuser. And anyone could choose to advocate for them.

It wasn't something outsiders were supposed to make use of. But that was enforced by them never being told about it and instead getting executed with an arrow to the back, even for simple crimes such as trespassing.

Once more, both looked like they'd vastly prefer being somewhere else.

The one who'd done all the talking so far leaned to the side and looked at Jamie and Zi'rah behind us.

"They can't come."

I tried to smile, but probably failed miserably. "But you will detain them and keep them from lurking around out here, will you?"

For the first time, the other elf spoke up. "We will have to look for weapons."

I nodded. My own weapons were rather prominently attached to my fingers, but it was a reasonable precaution for Jamie and Zi'rah, who let the guards pat them down. Zi'rah was the most obviously annoyed by the circumstances.

With that out of the way, we moved toward the entrance. On the way, one of them whistled a signal to the others, causing four more to join us, all with swords in hand.

They were all level 15. If this turned into a fight, I was pretty confident we'd make it out alive, unless they displayed any bullshit magic.

And with every step I took, the desire for this to turn violent grew. The jungle's aura was seeping back in, fostering a craving for violence. At least this shape's instincts were all on finding and fighting the guilty. As far as I knew, those elves weren't guilty of anything worse than being assholes.

As soon as they led us through a hidden entrance, a notification appeared.

[Root Cause (Personal Localized Task)

Objective: The elves have identified you as the source of their corruption. Demonstrate the error. Identify and expose the true source.

Hint: Killing alone is not proof enough.

Reward: ???]

I had anticipated something along those lines, but not for the Blessing to expose the fact that there were indeed infiltrators at work. Up until this point, it had just been a very plausible theory. I also started to suspect that the Blessing thought its hints were hilarious. At least this time the Task objective very clearly matched my own.

What followed beyond the entrance was a small labyrinth of passageways through the overgrown red-brick buildings. There wasn't just a single passable way, but many branching paths. Indubitably leading into dead ends or even traps. If Blessed decided to attack this place, it would make a formidable dungeon. Exactly as the Blessing meant it to be.

I wondered whether the infiltrators were deeply familiar with those paths or preferred to sneak in and out by climbing.

Along the way, we met more elves, going about some business or another in the buildings or leaving the city. None of them hid their surprise at seeing non-elves being led inside. Some at least tried to hide their anger.

Probably something to do with the complete absurdity of stumbling on a stone-carved angel crossing your path.

Eventually we stepped into the yard. It was a breathtaking sight. I had expected dense overgrowth down here, matching the dense cover that shielded the courtyard from onlookers. But that growth began only around seventy feet up, and hanging from it were long vines bearing luminescent leaves that glowed in bright neon colors.

On the ground, dozens, if not hundreds, of elves went about their day. Archery practice. Sword drills. Weaving clothes out of some kind of plant, whose growth they magically accelerated.

But with every step we took, that bustling activity died down. Those elves had sensitive ears, and the murmurs that started around the gate through which we entered the courtyard soon conquered the entire place.

For now, things were in Aleria's hands while I watched for smoke. Literally.

[Eyes of Judgment (Trait)

Sources of corruption, demonic taint, and lies exhibit plumes of smoke to your watchful vision. Effectiveness scales with Heightened Senses Rank.]

That last part was particularly important. Heightened Senses was sitting at an absurd 25, and Apex Seeker's Predator's Perception let those senses pierce supernatural concealment. Eyes of Judgment benefited from both.

Even if the infiltrators had the ability to conceal their presence from supernatural detection, I had the ability to counter that. Whoever they were, I was quite literally their worst nightmare. And they probably didn't know it yet.

Plumes of smoke hung over many people here; even the ground was covered by it. But that was to be expected. This was not a healthy society.

Of course our presence was challenged the moment people became aware of us. But both our escort and Aleria repeated my claim to the Right of the Accused and that Aleria needed to speak with Vaelis, the Veil Speaker.

Jamie and Zi'rah were left at the entrance, with four guards watching them. From here on, if it came to a confrontation, there were only two paths. Either I displayed such violence against one credible threat that every witness thought twice about being the next in line, or Jamie and I would drop the already primed smoke grenades we were carrying in our inventories, and we'd get the hell out of here. The grenades were loot the kobolds had secured from the police station.

Vaelis was tall, even for an elf. He towered at least a head above Aleria, had long blond hair, and wore black and silver robes. And we didn't need to walk long to find him, as he was storming right at us, full of righteous fury. His face would have been pretty if it wasn't contorted in rage. His plume wasn't as tall as I had anticipated. But among the flock of three elves in his wake, one was smoking like a tire fire. I could smell the corruption coming from her. Like the other two, she wore a long robe with a cowl over her head.

"Aleria? Is that… did you bring the abomination into our—" Vaelis began, before Aleria cut him off.

"Be silent." The ice in her voice pretty much froze the elder Veil Speaker in place. "This—this thing you sent me out with. Did you conjure it yourself? Did you feel its touch on your mind as you did? The perverse nature of its essence?"

As Vaelis gathered himself for a retort, the demon behind him made a mistake. She glanced off into the crowd. Following her gaze, I saw her collaborator. He was part of another group of elves who looked like their profession was distinctly magical, though his head was uncovered.

"I was there for it, and I felt it," Vaelis lied. Well, I wasn't entirely sure, but the smoke suggested that, whatever the exact lie was, he was no stranger to deceiving himself or others. "It is a spirit of the hunt. If you felt it didn't align with you, maybe it was because your heart betrayed your feelings on your mission. Instead of rooting out the corruption, you brought it here. Did you intend to doom us, or are you just too short-sighted to see what you are doing?"

I had to applaud Aleria for her self-control. I was very close to attacking the man myself, and I'd only caught strays in his words. The only thing betraying her anger was the creaking sound of leather, coming from her gloves as she balled her hands into fists.

I concentrated on the two infiltrators and used Queen's Voice. It let me choose recipients, so I hoped it reached only those two.

"I have uncovered you. Both of you. End this farce now, and you might survive this. You might be more valuable alive than dead," I said. I kept my eyes locked on Vaelis. I tried to get a read on the one behind him, out of the corner of my eye, but she displayed an excellent poker face. If it weren't for the way her heartbeat suddenly accelerated, I wouldn't have known that my message reached her.

"Your words are betrayed by the evidence of what I experienced. What this thing did to me," Aleria said, raising her arms to display the scorch marks to everyone present. "The only question I have for you is whether you acted out of ignorance or if the enemy has seduced you?"

A murmur went through the crowd. There had been a constant murmur, but this was far more uniform.

Meanwhile, Vaelis' reaction, through a whole host of tiny details, told me that it was both. The way he imperceptibly started to turn his head toward her, then stopped himself. That flicker of doubt, betrayal, and guilt as he saw the marks on Aleria's skin.

Huh. I was usually far worse at this. This shape's perception bonuses did reach deep.

As he replied, far less self-assured than before, and asked how she got those marks, I decided to amp up the pressure on the two infiltrators.

"Do you even know who I am? That it was me who killed Mammon? There wasn't enough left of him for us to loot. I couldn't even eat his heart. Your lies to the elves haven't been entirely wrong. I am violent. The wrath of this jungle feels like a familiar embrace by now. Your hearts I will devour. And I will use your powers to defeat your kin."

This got a reaction. Her eyes shot up under that cowl and met mine. And for just a moment the eyes that met mine weren't elven at all. But neither were they demonic. It had been a flicker of the mantle she'd assumed for the Blessing. And in there? I saw fear.

I tilted my head to look at her and smiled at her. And this shape didn't do nice smiles.

Aleria recounted her experience. How I was willing to risk my life to save her. How the spirit had taken hold in her mind and refused to let go, even as it burned her up. How it had required me, restraining her, to even enable her to fight back. That last part was told with considerable disgust. Because she should never have needed a human's help, and it was Vaelis' fault that she did.

Meanwhile, I had waited for my time to strike. When the infiltrator took a deep breath and half a step forward, my clawed hand shot up and pointed at her.

"She is the one who conjured the spirit. She is about to accuse me of already having corrupted Aleria."

My sudden movement sent a ripple through the crowd. Weapons were drawn, bows aimed, and casters extended hands towards me, ready to blast me to oblivion. But the infiltrator? She stopped cold.

"Was it this one who fed you those lies, Vaelis? Did you hold her as she did it?"

I continued through Queen's Voice, just addressing the two infiltrators. "Last night Nemesis sent assassins to my home. Seven demons. They broke into my home in the dark of night to murder me. Their heads rest on spikes now. Surrender or perish."

At the same time, Vaelis bowed his head. "Yes."

And as every eye rested on her, something twitched under that robe. A tail? At the same time, the elven eyes fully gave way to something different. Fox eyes?

And I wasn't the only one who'd seen her glamour falter. Once more, the crowd reacted. And among the murmurs, I heard 'kitsune,' and I knew that was what we were dealing with. If only I was more of a weeb and understood what that meant.

As everyone's attention was on her, the other infiltrator had vanished from the crowd. I caught only a glimpse of him before he disappeared into one of the buildings surrounding the courtyard. And then another when I spotted an identical copy enter another building. And another.

Ah, yes, illusions. With a beat of my wings, I ascended.

"Subdue her! I will catch her co-conspirator," I shouted.

To my surprise, not a single elf shot at me.
