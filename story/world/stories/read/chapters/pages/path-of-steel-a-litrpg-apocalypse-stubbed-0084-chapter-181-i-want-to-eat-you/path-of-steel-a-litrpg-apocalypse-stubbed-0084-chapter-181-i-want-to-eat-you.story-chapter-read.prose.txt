The strike found Gary unprepared. A burning pain lit up his back, from hip to shoulder, and Gary screamed as he almost fell off the pipe. He slashed around but met only air. Jin was nowhere. A deep stab to the thigh made him fall on one leg.

“Motherfucker,” he muttered. “Song of Rain!”

Droplets of aura splashed everywhere around him. No third attack came, but Jin wasn’t revealed, either. The instant Gary stopped shooting out Songs of Rain, there was a soft wind, and a flash of pain exploded in his shoulderblade. He’d barely moved his neck out of the way—Jin was finally going for the kill.

“Damn it!” Gary roared. He used his good leg to jump backward off the pipe, onto a neighboring one, in hopes of getting a better view. Jin couldn’t be invisible—he was just staying out of Gary’s field of view. Just before he reached the other pipe, he perceived someone standing exactly where he was going to land. He braced himself. A powerful aura strike met his back, impaling him completely and flying out of his belly. A dagger flew out with it to stab into the far-off ceiling.

Gary spat out blood. He lost his breath, as well as the ability to breathe. He landed on the pipe, Jin already gone, and fought to keep himself from collapsing. He could sense the blood pooling inside him. His body failing to function. His eyes shook.

Is this it? he wondered. After everything I’ve done, it ends here? Like this? Was all my struggle pointless? Was my life?

He clenched a fist. No. I refuse.

A dagger sliced the hamstring of his good leg. Gary collapsed, falling off the pipe and tumbling to the floor below. The crash sent a new jolt through his system, but it wasn’t pain anymore. It was a spreading numbness. That scared him.

The pill’s effects were running out, too. The previous wounds and exhaustion returned, leaving him so sore he could barely move. He was done for.

I must live. There is always a way. I must.

He lay face-up. He could see Jin coming, but he couldn’t stop it.

I don’t want to die.

The spear was still in his hand, but his arm refused to move. Something critical had been severed. Even regeneration had its limits. He might survive yet, if Jin suddenly disappeared, but that wasn’t going to happen, and Gary was in no position to fight back.

Unwillingness rose inside him. Hatred. Towards both himself and the world, but especially Jin. He fucking hated Jin. That man wanted to assassinate him. Wanted to waste everything Gary had ever done, invalidate all his effort. Gary was so angry he could die; he was fuming, foaming. He sensed his eyes turn black.

I’ll take him with me, he thought grimly. I will kill him. I will maim him, slaughter him, break him, rip his heart out. I will suck his blood and break his bones until he begs for mercy.

Gary gave vent to the darkness inside him, because there was nothing else he could do. There was no point in being virtuous. He was dying. He kept telling himself all the things he would do to Jin, but none fully encompassed the magnitude of his rage. There was an emotion there, a desire, which he couldn’t find the words for. He didn’t want to just kill Jin. Didn’t want to just make him suffer. Didn’t want to waste him. He wanted something more. Something heavier. Something more complete.

And then, in a flash of dark brilliance, he realized exactly what he wanted to do to Jin.

He wanted to eat him.

His entire being came into alignment—body, core, and soul. That singular desire, that ultimate revenge, spoke to every cell of his body. To his mind. To his spear. Everything came together as one, and as if a veil had been lifted, Gary realized what his core had been whispering since the breakthrough. What it meant to have eternal flesh. Why his starveins felt off.

Jin appeared over Gary. He was no longer hiding himself, since he knew Gary couldn’t move. He was pale and panting—that overwhelming assault had taken a toll on him.

He did not realize the change in Gary’s body.

“Your death won’t make up for Zink, but I pray it will give his soul satisfaction,” Jin said, kneeling over Gary. “I know it will give me.”

He plunged his dagger down. It sank towards Gary’s heart. Pierced his skin. And, suddenly, Gary’s entire body opened.

He became a hole. A pit. An intense suction force erupted from his core, controlled not by tentacles, but by himself. It wouldn’t be enough to affect Jin. As it spread across Gary’s body, however, he sensed his veins squirming. Responding to its call. He realized he’d been holding them back and let go. There were stars in his veins, but between them, there was also void. That void roared in hunger. His veins snapped, digging bloody troughs through his maimed body, ruining the starvein diagram and forming a new one. A crooked one. The stars were no longer blue and sparkling, but dim, shedding black, hollow light.

This wasn’t part of the Starvein Immortal Body Manual, but Gary didn’t give a fuck. He let the hunger warp his body cultivation. Derange it. The starvein diagram was meant to absorb energy from starlight, but Gary’s new diagram could do much more. It could absorb more kinds of energy. All energy.

Fueled by his Void Lightning core, it was so much stronger. Gary roared. He was starving, wholly and desperately, and the most appetizing food was right there.

This all happened in a instant. Jin’s dagger was still descending, just piercing the skin over Gary’s heart. Only now did he sense that something was wrong. He shifted his weight to jump away, but a new aura field spread from Gary’s body, clamping down on Jin. There was a terrible suction. Gary could perceive every gear inside the cyborg’s body, every pocket of aura, the rapidly spinning core hidden in his waist. Gary’s aura latched on to everything, shaped by his veins and powered by his core, and pulled.

Jin gasped. Instead of leaping a hundred feet away, he splashed like a fish. He only moved a foot—not enough to escape Gary’s range. The assassin’s willpower tried to stop him. They warred in their minds, but Jin was in shock, and Gary was fueled by the despair of near-death. There was no comparison. Gary’s willpower held the assassin’s down while his suction did its job.

Jin screamed. Crimson, sucking tendrils had invaded his body, violently ravaging the aura from his bones and flesh. They were milking him dry.

That sinister, rapid aura arrived at Gary’s body and traveled through his veins. The stars ground it down like massive millstones. It ceased vibrating. The aura was reduced to its base components, becoming pure aura which the void, with a cheer, feasted on.

Gary felt himself revitalized. New energy entered his system. His regeneration kicked back in, repairing his broken body. Soon, he could move again. He stood up, invisible tendrils stretching from his veins to the assassin, still slurping him up. Jin was dry like a raisin. His cheeks had sunken in, his body spasmed weakly. Only his gaze held life still, wide with horror as he looked up at Gary.

“Your eyes…” Jin muttered, the voice hoarse and weak. “What are…those eyes… Who are you?”

Gary smiled, leaning close to Jin’s face. “You told me I’d never starved, Jin,” he said. “Well, I have. I just did. And I have to say, you were delicious.”

The assassin could no longer respond. Gary’s Void Drain sucked the last bits of aura from his system, leaving him an empty, broken husk. His eyes lost their light. He died in fear, guilt, and puzzlement.

Gary stood. His draining had stopped, but he wasn’t fully healed. Just enough to walk and fight a little. At least his major wounds had regenerated.

“Um, Gary? I don’t know what you did, and I’m happy you survived, but what you said to him in the end was really creepy. Are you okay?” a voice said from his spear.

Gary gave her a tired smile. “I just wanted to scare him, Goldie. I’m still me.”

“Okay. Because that power felt evil. And your eyes, uh…”

They were interrupted by approaching footsteps. Gary turned towards the corridor and raised his spear. He remained extremely sore and nowhere near full power, but those invisible, sucking tendrils still floated around his body, draining what little they could from the air. Compared to Jin, the air tasted stale and empty.

With horror, Gary realized he’d tasted the man.

A form stepped into the room, and Gary cursed. It was the Slime King, Monom—the green-eyed monster wearing Kyle’s face. He paused at the entrance, glancing between Gary and Jin’s dry corpse. He remained there for a good moment.

“Well?” Gary said.

“I told you we weren’t so different,” Monom said. “Nice eyes, by the way.”

“Are you going to fight me?”

“I was, but you killed my summoner. I’m free now—and I just got a very interesting offer.”

“Is that a no?”

Monom laughed. “Don’t worry, Gary. I won’t fight you. In fact, I belong to neither army, so I think I’ll just make myself scarce. Maybe we’ll meet again.”

“Maybe,” Gary replied.

Monom stared at Gary for a little more, then laughed at a joke only he understood and took off. Gary realized he could no longer inspect the Slime King. He shook his head.

“One mystery at a time,” he muttered.

Cog: Woah! The brothers are fighting the puppeteer. What did you do the formation core?

Gary: The what?

Cog: I told you about it. You’re in the puppeteer’s base, right? Didn’t you break the core?

Cog: Uh, no. They’re trying to stop the puppeteer, who’s suddenly trying to kill Satan, but Satan is also trying to kill them. I don’t understand what’s going on.

Gary: I think I do. Let me know if anyone enters the base.

Cog: Will do!

Ignoring the soreness and fatigue, which made every step a challenge, he made it through the large door which protected the core of the puppeteer’s base—the same door which had stopped them before the base teleported. Inside was sparser than he expected. The place was barren, with the exception of a massive, gray and green crystal in the middle of the room.
