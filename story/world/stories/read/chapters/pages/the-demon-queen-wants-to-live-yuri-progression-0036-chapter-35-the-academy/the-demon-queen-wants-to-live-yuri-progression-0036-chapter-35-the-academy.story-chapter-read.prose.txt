The door kicked open again. The latch I had mended the night before held for precisely two heartbeats longer than it should have, and then a flame-covered boot struck the wood and the frame cracked anew. Ash and I had stirred at the first thud. Our eyes opened by the second. We stared at each other by the third -the one that forced the door open.

"-seriously, what did you DO to this latch, it's even harder than last-"

My eyes opened and closed slowly, but I was not yet fully awake. I had always woken quickly, and yet this slumber had been deep. The room was grey with early light. The egg sat warm against my side, nestled between the pillow and my arm. Ash's arm was across my chest. One of my arms was currently pinned under her side. We both stared at each other for a moment, then turned to look at the door.

Somehow, I had expected this would happen. Perhaps that was why I was slow to react. Ren stood in the doorway. Her red hair was wild, her eyes were wide, and her mouth had frozen in the exact shape of some word I could only guess at. She was staring at us.

The color that rose in Ren's face should not have been possible without some application of her mark. It started at her neck and climbed past her jaw, past her cheeks, all the way to the tips of her ears, until her entire face matched her hair. Her mark flared, and I saw wisps of fire leaking from her ears. She pointed, and as she did, the pointing finger shook. "I- you two- I had no idea you were- oh God. Oh, Divine, forgive me."

I frowned as I cleared my throat. "What? It seems destroying doorframes is an odd hobby of yours after all."

"Sorry! Sorry, sorry, sorry." She was already turning, spinning on her heel so fast she nearly tripped over the ruined door frame. She faced the corridor with her back to us, both hands pressed against either side of the doorway. "I didn't know! Nobody told me! I just- I'm s-supposed to lead you to the chamber in a few hours! I just wanted to make sure you were awake at least! Tour! Uniforms! Breakfast! I'll be outside!"

She spoke fast, until all of the words fought over each other. I could only understand perhaps one in three. She was gone before the last word had finished and her footsteps echoed down the corridor at a pace that suggested her mark also granted [Flight].

I sat up slowly. Ash's arm slid from my chest as I moved while mine pulled out from under her. Between us, the egg pulsed once. I looked at the doorframe. It was cracked open again, in almost the same place I had mended with Bloom. Even now, I could see a faint wisp of smoke curling from the latch.

"She broke it again," I said, using my right arm to wipe at my eyes.

Ash had risen onto one elbow. There was color in her cheeks and she was not meeting my eyes. Her gaze had dropped lower. "She did," Ash agreed carefully.

"What was that about?" I asked. "I do not think she is intelligent, but she was capable of coherent speech last night. What happened with her today?"

Ash's redness only deepened. She sat up fully and looked at the far wall with what I could only call intense focus. "Perhaps…she simply had some strange ideas. That's all."

"That is not an answer," I said.

"It's the answer you're getting."

I blinked and studied her. Those were my words, coming from her lips. They did not sound ill-placed, coming from her. Then there was the color in her cheeks. Mayhaps the room was warm again, though the broken door should have let in a draft.

I would let this go, for now. Either way, it seemed that we needed to get ready. To change into the strange clothes all of these people wore, as that Inker had insisted. The pair of grey uniforms lay folded on the large table between my bed and where Ash's bed had been.

I rose, walked over and inspected the garments. The tunic looked plain and was coarse to the touch, with no sleeves at all. The trousers were a darker grey. One was clearly cut for me, while the other was cut for Ash. I handed Ash the smaller one. She had the nerve to look surprised.

I turned away from Ash and pulled Martha's dress over my head. The morning air bit at my skin. I worked the grey tunic past my left horn -it always caught on the left, for some reason- and then the right. When I turned back, Ash was half changed.

The dress we had come to Koralis in was gone. There were scars across Ash's skin, scars that ran across her back and shoulders. I had noticed some of them before. I noticed more of them now. I saw her muscles. Lean and defined, on a frame that was a Hero's, but not just a Hero's. There was breadth across the shoulders that I had known, but the way it gave way to the line of her back, to the deep curve at her waist-

I stared. I stared for one second, perhaps two before Ash pulled the grey tunic over herself again. She turned, adjusting the collar with one hand. The tunic fit her frame differently than the dress had. Without sleeves, I could see where the scars ended and the muscle began and where the muscle gave way to everything else. She caught me looking. Neither of us spoke at first.

"It suits you," Ash finally said.

I glanced down at my bare arms. Strange, to see Red and Green where Black and White should have been. "This…clothing is not the worst thing I have worn," I said quickly and turned away. It really was warm despite the draft.

After a moment, I reached for the satchel, and then the egg. It sat where I had placed it, cradled in the pillow. It pulsed once beneath my fingers. I placed it in the satchel with care, adjusting the cloth around it. "We will not be long out," I said. "Perhaps you will like to see this Academy yourself." I slung the satchel over my shoulder and moved toward the door. Ash followed behind, yawning. I looked back once, at the room that was now, apparently, ours.

Ren waited outside. She had a bowl of…something in one hand already. Perhaps that was what her earlier flight had been for. She looked up from it at us, and her faintly red face went fully red again. Then she coughed and hurriedly walked down the hall without looking back. It seemed she expected us to follow. When she spoke, her voice was almost composed. Almost. "We're going to the Mess Hall first. It's this way. You guys better keep up or I'm just going to leave you behind."

She rounded a corner, and was still talking. "I'm going to talk and you both are going to listen, cos I'm only gonna say all of this once!" She glanced back at us before looking away. "The mess opens first at sixth bell. You wanna get there before the sixth bell, cos all the good bread is gone in the first five minutes because of Tobias and his fiends. The other food is bleh, but there's a trick to it. Nobody touches the porridge because it tastes bland as hell. But if you're nice and have good manners, Marissa puts a bit of honey in it. She always puts it in mine, because I have excellent manners."

The girl had spoken much, and not taken a single breath as far as I could tell. Ash and I glanced at each other as we walked beside her. Perhaps this was another application of the girl's mark. "Also, you better not sit at the third or the first table. Don't forget this one. Or if you do, don't come crying to me later. Lena's group takes at least one and they will make your life tough if they catch you sitting at their table. Don't sit at the fifth either, though there aren't that many Crests. The second and fourth are fine since you're both Lines." She paused, squinted back at us. Her gaze almost met mine before it darted away again. "You're not writing any of this down."

"I will remember," I said.

"You can't possibly remember all of that!" she said, still walking.

"I have memorized far more in my time, more than you will ever have the chance to learn. I can manage this."

Ren stared back at me, opened her mouth, closed it. She turned to Ash. "Is she always like this?"

"Yes," Ash said, and I caught the trace of warmth in her voice.

Ren led us down two flights of stairs and through a corridor wide enough for four abreast. Our destination must have been nearing, given Ren's faster steps and the growing sound. A few more turns, and we reached it. We reached a massive set of open doors through which an almost endless amount of sound spilled out. The mess hall was a long chamber lined with heavy wooden tables, many of them packed with students. The students that were not sitting instead formed a line in front of a counter at the far end.

Several gazes fixed on us from the moment we entered. Focused on me, specifically. I had been watched before -I had been watched for centuries- though the nature of the watching had certainly changed.

"Half-demon," I heard from somewhere to the left.

"Look at those horns." I heard from further away."…another one? As if she already…" The rest was swallowed by the noise.

Ash stepped in front of me, though she had been trailing one step behind this entire time. Ren led the way inside, and we followed. Their childish gazes were beneath my notice. Ren led us to the line. It took no time at all to understand how this strange custom worked. We had to wait our turn. It seemed the strong were not simply served first.

Ren talked to the woman pouring the strange liquid. It was called porridge, or so Ash had said. We sat at one of the tables. The porridge was warm. There was, as Ren had promised, something very sweet and thick at the bottom. This was honey.

I ate one bowl slowly. I finished it. It was oddly thick on my tongue but not unpleasant, though the stew had been better. I stared at the empty bowl. I glanced at the line that had not thinned at all. Would I need to waste my time for another? I would not. There was a scraping sound. A half-filled bowl appeared in front of me. I glanced at it, then glanced at Ash, then stared at Ash's now-empty place on the table, where her own bowl had been.

"You'll want it more than I," Ash said simply. "I don't feel like it."

It tasted like a lie. This was a tribute, then. One any retainer would give to their Lady. I would claim it, as was my right. I took the bowl. I ate it. It tasted better than the first. Ren watched this exchange with narrowed eyes but said nothing.

After that, we began the 'tour', as Ren called it. The red-haired girl walked at speed, narrating everything. Neither Ash nor I had asked for this. I had explicitly commanded her not to. She had not listened.

She showed us corridors that all looked the same to my eyes, though each apparently had its own significance. The main hall. The eastern wing, where the advanced students trained. The western wing, where Initiates like us were supposed to be housed, if not for our 'special status'. She made that last point rather clear. The central yard, where some lectures were held in the open air. Other classrooms, many of which had been full as we passed by. The heads inside always turned to us as we passed.

As we moved through these corridors, the looks continued as did the whispers. An inker, a thin man in threaded robes, pulled a stack of papers closer to his chest as I passed. An inker woman steered a younger student to the far side of the hall, one hand on the boy's shoulder, her eyes never leaving my horns.

The reaction from the demonbloods was little different from the humans or the rare elves. Their eyes would widen when they saw me, widen again and fix on my horns. Most of them would take the nearest turn that put them into a different hallway than the one I walked. I had not expected them to recognize their Queen -such as I was- but I had not expected them to shy at the sight of me. Ash had drawn closer to me as we walked. I had not requested this.

We passed through a wider corridor near the eastern wing. Most of the students here bore Lines, though I saw the occasional Sigil. Near a tall window, I saw someone who made me pause.

He was a half-demon, with large curved horns that swept back from dark temples. His skin was a deep grey…and he had two heads, each a mirror of the other. Both of those heads were presently in an argument with the other, in a language I did not know. Both sets of eyes widened when they saw me, and then we moved past them.

Ren kept walking, though she did mention the half-demon's name when I asked. It was Malachor.

Eventually, Ren led us to a place she called the 'Beast Hall'. It was vast, bigger than the mess hall had been. The chamber stretched far enough that the far wall was dim.

The space was largely full of reinforced pens that lined both sides, and inside them, creatures stirred and paced. I saw wolves with green pelts, strange birds that left trails of light behind them, reptilian things I had no name for. A spider as tall as a horse crouched in one corner, its many eyes tracking us as we passed. Even Ash's gaze had lingered on most of these creatures. She did not recognize them any more than I.

My hand lingered on the satchel hung at my shoulder. It pulsed against the cloth. Was it truly beating faster, or was that just my imagination?

Ren was explaining beast bonding. This was one of the many explanations she had volunteered today that we had not asked for. "-can't just grab any creature you want. It has to match your mark, your temperament, your whole...vibe, I guess. Some people spend years looking. I still can't find a damn Flame Elemental, and you'd think a fire Sigil would make that simple, but nooooo."

This was one of her few explanations I was actually paying attention to. There was [Beast Taming] in my time too, and it had worked rather simply. There, it was simple. One had the Skill. One beat the creature into subservience, and then one could bind it to oneself.

I spotted the woman in charge before Ren could introduce her. She stood near a larger pen, watching a black snake the width of a tree trunk that had coiled in on itself. Even coiled, it nearly filled the pen. The woman stared at it with her arms folded. She was saying something too low for me to hear. The woman herself was grey-haired and broad-shouldered. On her folded right arm was a single purple Line. She was the first Inker I had seen with a mark that modest.

"Master Hesta," Ren said, trailing behind me. "She runs the Beast Ha- hey, wait, where are you going?"

I was already walking toward her. "I wish to see your finest accommodations," I said, arms folded. I glanced at all of these pens and found them wanting.

The woman looked at me. She regarded me the way one might regard a stone that had suddenly spoken. Her gaze, surprisingly, never went to my horns at all. "Accommodations? Lass, this is a place to keep beasts."

"For a beast of exceptional lineage. I require something appropriate."

Hesta's gaze moved to the satchel at my side, then back to my face. She could not have seen inside. Her expression did not change. "These are pens," she said. "They're all just pens. Some of 'em have a bit more to them, but they're all the same."

"Surely there are quarters befitting something of extraordinary value," I insisted, looking around. "A creature that will one day soon surpass anything currently housed within these walls."

The woman looked at me for a long moment. "We have pens," she said again. "Large ones, small ones, and medium ones. They all have straw."

If the woman was merely lying to me, I could not tell. Whatever hatched from this egg would not live in this. I would find something better. I did not yet know what, but the decision was absolute. I turned and walked away without another word. Ren was staring at me. Ash had her face buried in her hands. Her shoulders shook. It almost made me want to swat her. Gently.

Ren caught up with me outside. "What the hell was that about?"

"Nothing that concerns you."

Ren might have said something further. If she did, I did not hear her, for I was already walking away. I adjusted the satchel on my shoulder, and we moved on.

The bell that heralded lunch rang through the corridors with enough force that I expected it to rattle all of the windows. Ren led us back to the mess hall. The gazes were no less sharp the second time around. How annoying. Did they stare at every half-demon so? Ash had stepped in front of me again, her hand reaching for her hilt. I put my hand on her arm, and she slowly withdrew it.

Then we sat, and I adjusted the satchel on the seat to my left, even as Ash took my right. Of all the gazes, one was more difficult to ignore than the rest. It was Ren's. She had been watching me almost from the start of the day. She did not have the ability to watch anything subtly. "Alright," Ren said, setting her spoon down. "I've been meaning to ask this all damn morning. What the hell is in the bag?"

It was the question I had expected from her. I would not lie, for a Demon Queen does not lie. I met Ren's gaze. "In this satchel is what will one day be the greatest steed in the world."

Ren stared. Perhaps she was waiting for more. I would not give her more. "That's... what does that even mean?" she finally asked.

I returned to my food. Ren looked at Ash for help. Ash shrugged. "She means it."

Ren opened her mouth. She looked at the satchel, then back at me, then at her food. She picked up her spoon and ate with an expression that suggested she was in great pain. Pain from what, even I did not know. A few minutes passed. The porridge was the same as breakfast. I found I did not mind.

It seemed Ren was not done. She glanced at me, then at Ash, then she ended up glancing somewhere in the middle of us. When she spoke, her voice was low. "So uh…been meaning to ask this too…are you two, like…" Her face grew redder and redder by the word, and she made a gesture with her hands. One where she interlocked the fingers of both hands and moved them. The strange motion conveyed nothing to me at all. "Together together? Not that there's anything wrong with that!" she said loudly. Very loudly. Many of the people in the nearest tables were all staring at us now.

I looked to Ash. She was making a strange choking sound, and her entire face had taken the same red color Ren's face had in the morning. It rose all the way from her collar to the tips of her ears. She coughed again and opened her mouth-

"Yes," I said. Ash's mouth closed. "Ash is one of my most prized retainers," I continued. The statement was true. "Naturally we are together, and will continue to be."

Ren blinked. Her brow furrowed. "Retainer? What the hell does that-"

I cut the girl off. "It means she serves at my side. It is a position of considerable importance. One such as you would not understand."

Ren's brow furrowed and her gaze swung to Ash next. If she was waiting for an answer, then Ash did not give her one. The Hero had gone completely still on her own seat. Her cheeks were flushed, deeply so, and she was staring at a fixed point on the table with an absurd amount of intensity. I saw nothing there that deserved it.

Ren slowly turned back to her food. "Half-demons," she muttered under her breath. "Absolutely impossible to understand. Why do I even try?"

After that, our strange tour of the Academy continued. Ren dragged us through more halls and mentioned more names and details. She did it with what I suspected was her usual amount of energy. Which was to say, far too much. She guided us to a wide training yard lined with stone pillars on its sides. Here, students practiced marks against stone targets that cracked and reformed as fast as they could be broken. Even as I watched, a girl used a gust of wind to punch a hole clean through one. Ren spent five minutes explaining how she had once melted three in a single session. This was, apparently, supposed to be very impressive.

The atelier came next, where students formed weapons with their marks alone. How this worked…even Ren did not seem to understand well enough to explain.

There was a yard meant specifically for cultivation. It was a quiet space where students sat in rows with their eyes closed, their marks glowing faintly. A man in dark robes walked between them. The air here was thick. Apparently, this was one of the keys to advancing one's mark. Ren said she spent much of her time here.

Ash talked with Ren through most of the tour. The Hero asked questions and made observations. Whenever Ren said something about a particular hallway or a specific portrait, Ash always had a question. Whether or not she was actually listening to Ren's answers or just being a Hero was impossible to say. I did not listen to any of it.

Eventually, we descended a long staircase. It was almost unremarkable, save that the walls here grew darker the deeper we went. "Alright, this is the last stop," Ren said. The air grew heavier with each step. By the bottom, I could feel it pressing against my skin. We reached a set of massive doors, more massive even than the mess hall's doors had been. The air here almost felt thick enough to taste.

"This is the Level One resonance chamber. At least, that's what some of the stuck-up Inkers like to call it. Everyone else just calls it the Spark Resonance Chamber." Ren gestured towards the door. "You're going to form your Essence Cores here. Took a little longer to get here than I'd like but, well, we're here now so no-one can complain." Ren looked back at us, frowned, and looked back at the doors. "Fair warning though, the mana inside here is…thick. Thicker than you'll be used to. Most people can barely stand their first time inside. Don't be embarrassed if you need to sit down right away. Everyone does."

Then Ren stepped forward and pushed the doors open. The room beyond was strange. It was nothing like the rest of the Academy. The walls were made of dark crystal and they held light in shifting patterns that moved when I did not. Inside were roughly a dozen students. Most were on their knees. The few who moved did so slowly and carefully. Many of them were breathing hard, with sweat running down their brows.

At the center of the room, arms wide, stood Aldric.  "-and there I was, surrounded on all sides. There were ten Sigils, at least. Maybe even more. The leader, a brute of a woman, easily twice my size, but I knew I had to protect the two Consec-"

I stepped through the threshold. The dense mana hit me. It pressed against my skin, filled my lungs. It felt good. I tried drawing in the faintest trace of Mana into the Requiem, and it came fast. The Requiem hummed on my right. The Cradle hummed on my left. Yes, this was a place of power. And this was…level one, was it? I walked, upright and steady. Ash walked beside me, and did not falter either.

Aldric's mouth was still open. "-and then I had to carve out a-" He looked at us. We looked at him. His mouth slowly closed. In the silence, I almost thought I heard him groan.
