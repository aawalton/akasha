Upgrading [Void-step (II)] seemed like the logical thing to do, considering the fact that he planned on using the same skill to take him and Xara to the Krupi’s cave. The only drawback to picking the skill was the twenty intelligence requirement.

To use the upgraded version of [Void-step (II)], he’d have to have twenty points assigned to his intelligence stat, and at the moment, he only had about nine, which meant that he’d have to increase the stat with some of his unused points, which would further affect how many points he could put into his mana stat.

From another perspective, upgrading [Void-step (II)] would considerably reduce the number of void tears he’d have to make, to get to the Krupi’s cave. Levi just had to figure out if throwing all his remaining points into mana was a risk he was willing to take.

Void-step aside, Levi also considered distortion, Invisible wire, and Void-box. At the moment, [Void-box] was leading the race for top pick as it didn’t seem to have a requirement, as well as a mana draw or capacity limit. These were all things that made it seem like it was the prime pick of the bunch, but he couldn’t help but admit that it was useless to him at the moment.

He technically didn’t have anything on his person that required him to use the [void-box], and even if he did, the mage had a storage ring of her own, which meant the skill was as valuable as a grain of sand in his current situation.

And yet, I find myself wondering about the many possibilities that I can achieve with this stone in my hands. Levi remembered how the people back in Gantus had spoken about storage rings like they were the most valuable things in Rinesta, and he couldn’t help but imagine himself with the void-box. It would do him wonders in the future.

In the future. Levi mused. The skill was undoubtedly invaluable, but he had pressing needs that required other skills, such as [Distortion (I)] and [Invisible Wire (I)]. Hell's, even [Analyze(II)] would be of more help to him now that the void-box.

His mind went back to the first time he’d seen Ghost. It seemed like his mind had refused to register the facial features of the rogue that had stood right in front of him.

Right there and then, he wished for a similar skill, and now he had the chance to get it, and it wouldn’t be because he’d seen Ghost use the skill—at least he assumed that it was the same skill based on the description.

[Distortion (I)] to the side, he could see the use of [Invisible Wire(I)]. At the very least, the skill would alert him to intruders—a valuable skill for someone in his situation.

At the moment, [Invisible Wire(I)] was the skill that would allow him to stay here without the risk of a surprise attack or, at the very least, it’d make him confident of spending his time in the Krupi’s cave without having to keep his eyes open the entire time.

He was more than below average against the type of foes that he was going against, and he needed to make sure that he made the most of this opportunity by picking the right skills, or he risked ending up with skills that wouldn’t do much for him in the long run.

[Void-step (III)] and [Distortion (I)] were good options, but they were both ultimately impulse picks. Levi wanted them for what they could do or what he expected them to be able to do. Compared to [Invisible Wire(I)], which He could envision its use in the krupi’s cave, which he and the mage would most likely spend the night at.

The rest of the skills, like [Analyze(II)] and [Analyze-Blocker (I)], wouldn't hold up to the type of people he was competing against, and considering the kind of power that people like Ghost had, it’d do him no good getting skills that wouldn’t help his current situation.

Based on the way things looked, it was fair to assume that he was going to be making a big mistake if he picked skills that had no immediate use.

Hence, the reason why he was dropping the others in favour of [Void-step (III)], [Invisible Wire(I)], and [Void-box]. Those were the three skills he could envision himself using both now and in the near future.

While [Void-box] didn’t fit the immediate use case criteria, it wasn’t enough for him to skip past the skill. He couldn’t take the chance that the skill wouldn’t reappear in the skill list.

I guess that is all there is to it. I choose [Void-box], [Invisible Wire(I)], and [Void-step (III)].

Levi closed his eyes and gritted his teeth as his innards seemed to reknit themselves. The bleeding he’d gotten from when the Krupi’s claws had slashed at him seemed to stop aching, the broken skin closed up just a bit, but it made a world of difference to him. Even his broken ribs received a touch of healing from his influx of health.

While neither of the wounds had healed up significantly, most of the pain that had been affecting him because of them now seemed to have drifted to the back of his mind, and Levi had the feeling that he wasn’t going to have to worry about an infection for a while.

He took a moment to think about what had just happened for a second. If just a single point to his health had him feeling like this, then Levi couldn’t help but wonder what else could happen by just moving a point to another stat.

Maybe if he'd been a bit stronger, then his dagger might’ve been able to pierce through the Krupi’s skin.

Levi knew that his strength had to improve drastically if he had any hope of keeping up with monsters like the krupi. It was a fault of his and one that he hoped to rectify by adding more points to his strength stat.

The difference between him and the Krupi strength-wise had almost been staggering, and Levi was now starting to feel the weight of those differences, but what could he do?

At the moment, his top skills required him to meet minimum stat thresholds, and if he couldn’t meet them, then it meant he’d have to compensate for that by getting more mana, which was technically the only thing he’d been doing for a long time.

He needed to figure out something else to do, or else he was going to have to pay the price for the imbalance in his stats.

Levi shuddered at the thought and then dismissed the blue box, turning his attention to the sleeping mage. She seemed so peaceful there, but he had no time to waste, so Levi got to work rousing her.

“Wake up, we have to leave, now!” Levi said as he tapped her on the shoulders.

“A-Are we under attack?” She asked groggily.

“Not yet, but we will be,” Levi said as he helped the mage get to bed. She might’ve been recovering from her injuries, but it seemed like she still wasn’t up to full strength. Luckily for her, they wouldn’t have to walk far. “Just lean on me, I’ll get us somewhere safe soon.”

A silent nod from the mage was all Levi got, and it was enough for him. he promptly got her standing. Her body pressed against him as they walked towards the side of the room where there’d be a little bit of space he could use to create a void tear.

“[Void-step (III)],” Levi whispered, watching as a purple slit tore into their world. An unnatural chill wafted from the indescribable darkness within. “Let’s go.”

It took them a couple more void tears until they were finally in the Krupi’s cave. He was pretty happy that they escaped without having to kill a single villager. From all sides, that was the best thing that Levi could've done, and he’d pulled it off.

Now they were inside Krupi’s cave, Xara began setting up her tent and his bedroll, which she pulled from her storage ring, while Levi worked on setting up Invisible wires in and around the Cave.

So far, he managed to set one up just outside the cave and added two more in front of the cave in the shape of a cross.

One wire went from the top to the bottom of the cave mouth, and the other went from right to left. It wasn’t much in the grand scheme of things, but Levi put his trust in those three wires for the time being. He was beyond tired after his fight with the Krupi, and his body was practically begging him for some sleep, but before then, they still had to figure out who would keep watch.

Xara couldn’t because of her condition, and neither could he because he couldn’t keep the invisible Wires up until the next day. Or maybe he could.

Levi wasn’t exactly sure how much mana he regenerated per hour, but he assumed that it was around sixty per hour, going by when he’d had to wait for his mana to regenerate before attacking the Krupi for a second time.

That’s right, I should be regenerating about Fifty to sixty mana per hour, and if my rate of regeneration is somehow tied to my intelligence, then I should see a healthy increase in my mana regeneration rate.

Tying his mana regeneration rate to his Intelligence seemed like a reasonable thing to do, especially since he’d experienced mental fatigue when he’d tried to get Xara to the Village. Compared to now, when he’d improved his intelligence, he didn’t feel a little bit of mental fatigue, and it was telling, considering the fact that he covered about the same distance but with almost no break within, apart from when they walked into the next portal.

I’ll have to ask Xara about that. Probably tomorrow. Levi thought as he turned back to stare at the mage's tent. The mage had dispelled the fireball that they’d used to navigate the cave in the dark after getting inside her tent, but his improved Perception made it easy for him to see through the overwhelming darkness in the cave.

He could see the bedroll she’d laid for him just outside her tent. And so Levi took one good look at the placement of the invisible wires, which were only visible to him because of the feedback he was getting from the cables as they were connected to his mana. He let out a grunt of satisfaction and walked to his bedroll and plopped down to sleep.
