Chapter 38: Hunters

When Ambrose woke up for the second time, it was a lot more refreshed, and more than a little hungry. Since it wasn’t like they had any deadlines or plans, he took his time getting dressed and leaving his cabin. They were likely to be stuck together for a while, so he just didn’t feel any particular rush to get going. A rest day sounded good, and so very necessary.

The galley was the same mess it was when he went to bed a second time, but there was still a stale piece of bread or two. He grabbed them and poured himself some water from a carafe, and wandered up to the deck, only to find the ship’s magelights dimmed to nothing, and everyone was already awake, hidden in flickering shadows cast from the expanse.

“What’s going on?” he asked, looking out into the stars. He almost immediately saw two streaks of silver light in the distance, slowly heading their way.

“Shh!” Jessica hissed. She had bags under her wild eyes, and her dark hair was tangled. “Enemy ships, we think.”

SNAC spoke up, “Not enemy ships, Mistress, imperial scouts. But I understand that we are on a stealth mission, and should not be detected, even by allies.”

Ambrose arched a brow, but Luthor shook his head. This was going to be a problem, and it made him want to immediately start picking at it and see what the limits were. He refrained, for the moment.

“We’ve been watching them get closer for about half an hour now,” said Raeva, leaning against one of the metal support pillars of the awning. “Jessica activated stealth mode, and we’re not going anywhere for now. I swear, waiting like this is worse than just getting into a fight.”

Raeleq nodded thoughtfully, but Luthor and Jessica shot her a warning look. The golem said nothing this time, but continued to track the ships with what Ambrose assumed was enhanced sight.

With all the lights out, it was like standing in a dark field at night, with the stars and worlds the only real sources of illumination. Off the port side, one world bathed them in a black and white light, constantly shifting as the images on the surface of the world changed. Among them were visions of mushrooms, bats, spiders, and what appeared to be an active volcano, lava oozing down an obsidian slope. Every so often, an image would let him see more details on his friends’ faces, before they returned to silhouettes.

“So, can they hear us, or did you just shush me because you’re a scared mess?” Ambrose asked, tearing his gaze away from the images.

“Shut up,” Jessica grumbled, pouting.

He wanted to tease her for it, but he refrained from that too. His self control was top notch, given the circumstances. “The expanse seems pretty big, and dark. We’re doing everything we can, right? Might as well relax and get some breakfast. Hop to it, SNAC.”

The golem didn’t move. Ambrose wasn’t sure if it was out of sass or previous orders from Jessica, and decided it was more amusing to not know.

Luthor crossed his arms over his chest, and it was easy to imagine the annoyed frown on his face. “We’re monitoring the situation, and discussing what to do if they come to us. Our secret mission is important to hide, but if we’re far enough away from where we started, there’s always the chance that they won’t know us or care about us enough to bother us. However, that’s obviously a gamble. Do you have any thoughts, or just jokes?”

The ships were nearly big enough to see the crew. They weren’t anywhere near the size of the Wanderer, and looked much faster and more maneuverable. While their airship had cannons and possibly other weapons, Ambrose didn’t see the Wanderer being made for fights. And even if it did, missing patrols got noticed eventually, and they might unthinkingly leave a trail for others to follow.

There was just so much they didn’t know. That was the first step.

“SNAC, what are the nearest worlds to us?” Ambrose asked, looking around at the five or so visible planets within eyesight.

This time, the golem responded. “The worlds closest to us are Karadun, Rihzor, Banadaz, Hinderhea, and Stonskarr.”

Okay, those meant nothing. “Which of those worlds has imperial outposts? Which world is closest to us, and which of them is furthest away?”

The rest of them looked at each other. Jessica said, “Ambrose, where are you going with this?”

He held up a finger and pointed to SNAC.

“Rihzor, Hinderhea, and Stonskarr have imperial outposts. Stonskarr is closest, and Rhizor is the furthest away. Is this information sufficient, Amby-lamb?”

Ambrose froze. Despite their insistence on silence, the rest of them broke out into suppressed laughter, fighting to keep it as quiet as possible. Eventually, even he had to nod and smile. “Everyone else is Master or Mistress, but I am Amby-lamb. A cute little pet name. May it strike fear into my enemy’s hearts.

“Incidentally, this information is sufficient. If you want to sit around with your thumbs up your asses and avoid detection, that’s fine. I, however, have a plan.”

They weren’t impressed.

“You going to share it with us, or wait for us to request that you show off how clever you are?” Raeva scoffed.

“Normally, yes. But today you get a freebie. We’re going to start moving again, and we’re going to intentionally pass them by in this stealth mode. Either they don’t see us, and we make it past them -- “

“Gambling all of our lives in the process,” Jessica said.

“Or we run into them and lie our asses off, and send them on a wild goose chase while we get the fuck out of here. Think about it. Vanderborn wanted me specifically to be a representative out here. He hinted at it multiple times. It’s not just because of my skills, it’s about my attitude. Do you understand yet, or do I need to spell it out?”

The ships got closer. They were heading almost straight their way, with the exception that one was above them, and another below, still flanking on either side. Three people were visible on each one, but that didn’t mean it was all of them. Out of the six visible people, four of them were human, one was tall and had the face of a lion, and the last was a proper demon, unlike his imp.

Lights from both ships cut through the darkness, as if specifically seeking them out. They were still a ways off, like the distance of the campus grounds, give or take.

“Maybe just say it,” Raeleq said nervously, looking around. Fighting while outnumbered again was not what they wanted to do. “Or shut up. One of the two.”

Ambrose bit his tongue for the moment. He did his best to duck under the awning with the rest of them, but it was crowded. After some maneuvering, he ended up pinned between Luthor and Raeleq which, all things considered, was the worst combination possible in the situation.

No one said a word as the ships drifted along, still searching. They held a collective breath as the one above them passed directly overhead. The seconds stretched by, every heartbeat thunderously loud…until the light passed directly over their shelter. A second later, an alarm sounded.

The released their breath as gasps, and were about to panic when Ambrose grabbed SNAC by the shoulder and hissed, “Can we communicate with their ship?”

“Yes, Amby-lamb.”

“Put me through.”

“But,” Luthor protested.

“Do it,” Jessica said, nodding at him.

The sense of trust would’ve made him feel warm, if he wasn’t chilled to the bone with anxiety. They were strong, and maybe they could get away or fight, but they were not prepared for this at all. However, when times got tough, Ambrose had a motto: double down until the end.

SNAC’s eyes changed color, turning a lime green.

He took that to mean he could speak. Putting on the air of an irritable, grizzled old commander, like his uncle had been. “Turn that damned alarm off before we have you court martialed! This is a delicate operation here, and we can’t have you blowing it.”

The alarm continued for another couple of seconds, before it shut off, and a deep, gravelly voice came out of his head, “Who the hell is this? Identify yourself, or prepare to be boarded.”

“As far as you’re concerned, we’re nobody. Just a blip you didn’t actually see or hear, if you value your career. I am trying to do you a favor, friend. Please take me seriously. We’re on the same side.”

Realization dawned on the rest of them, and Raeleq at least had to cover his mouth to stop laughing. The rest shared a look that said it all: this was a stupid gamble, but it would be funny if it worked.

The ship below them stopped and their spotlight joined from below. For several agonizing seconds, they were exposed. All they could do was wait for judgment, breaths held, ready to fight or flee but without agreement on which to do.

“I understand, but there is an alert out there. We need to know where you’re going, so we can report back to the Obelisk on your behalf.”

Ambrose grinned smugly. “Acceptable. We’re on our way to Rihzor with some delicate cargo. That’s all you need to know. You want to confirm, send whichever of you is faster over to your base and talk to the governor. Just understand, if we’re delayed too long, it is your ass on the line. I will not take the fall just because you can’t take a hint.”

Again, silence. Then, before he could feel too proud of himself, SNAC’s eyes flashed green again. “That’s a risk I’m willing to take. Sit tight, and we will go visit the governor of Rihzor. Hope you’ve got good provisions, this could take a few days. Unless you want to be boarded and clear things up with me personally.”

Again, they were unimpressed. Four pairs of angry, accusing eyes drove daggers into him. Raeva mouthed, “I will kill you,” while Luthor just sighed and looked down.

“Fine by me. You wanna fuck up your career that bad, just remember that I told you so. Get your boys out there and take your time, for all I care. Every hour you delay us is a lashing.”

Raeleq put a hand over SNAC’s head and whispered, “what are you doing?”

Their response came through muffled, but with the same, cynical glee, “Sure thing, old timer. Keep your ship where it is and there won’t be a problem.”

The spotlight from below cut out, and that ship turned around and flew right back where they came from, towards a world he hoped was as far as possible, if this ploy was to work. The ship above them stayed, the spotlight shining bright. They weren’t boarding though, and that was important.

Ambrose motioned for SNAC to stop communicating, and the golem’s eyes turned blue once more. “Hear me out here,” he started.

“I guess we have to, don’t we?” Jessica said with a sigh. “Why did you do this? Why did I let you?”

He grinned. “Because it’s easier to escape or crush a single ship. SNAC, how long until the other ship gets out of range of communications?”

“Why would we crush an imperial scout?” the golem asked.

Alarmed, Jessica said, “Our mission is that important. Answer the question.”

“Twenty seconds until the departing ship is too far away to hear a distress call.” The golem was incapable of sounding sad, but there was still a certain melancholy to how softly he said it.

“What’s the plan then, genius?” Raeleq demanded.

“We wait until they’re gone and get the hell out of here. They either follow us, or they run away and we put distance between us and getting caught. If they follow us, we either blow them out of the sky or fight them off with better odds.”

Luthor nodded. “It’s a good plan, but you executed it poorly. Next time, discuss it with us.”

“There wasn’t time,” said Ambrose, “even without my grandstanding. If you trust me, then trust me. I don’t act if I don’t know I can win.”

“I trust you,” Raeva chimed in, to everyone’s surprise. “Mostly. You’re smart, and it makes sense. I for one think we should lure them into a fight. Whittle them down, then blast them out of the sky. Then the others report in with bad info. If they have cards, we take them.”

They weren’t in the best condition, but they were still in the top ten percent for their world. Surely they could take on a couple of people, especially with Ambrose’s new advantage. He smiled and looked for support. Raeva was as ready to fight as he was, and Luthor considered it. It was Jessica he was worried about.

“We have to start somewhere, don’t we?” she said, surprising him. “We can’t let ourselves get caught.”

With that agreed, they waited for a minute, and SNAC connected them again. “We’ve changed our mind. This is too time sensitive to wait. Come on and board us and get this over with.”
