By the time Vivi corrected the fourth girl’s stance for the tenth time, she was beginning to understand why Gael occasionally looked like he wanted to throw her through a window whenever he was the one training her in a spar.

“No, no. Your shoulder has to stay behind the stock,” she said, gently shifting the girl’s elbow with one hand while keeping the rifle pointed towards the line of dummy targets across Miss Umbrage’s clinic atrium. “If you let it sit loosely against your collarbone like that, the recoil will throw the barrel up. In the best case scenario, you’ll miss. In the worst case, you’ll shoot the ceiling, and Jin’s training the boys up there.”

The girl—barely nine, with a fishhook threaded through one ear and two braids tied with scraps of blue netting—stared at her solemnly. “Would shootin’ the boys be bad?”

Vivi stared. “Yes. Extremely.”

“Oh.”

“Please don’t sound so disappointed.”

The girl adjusted her shoulder properly. There. Much better. Across the atrium, nine other girls stood behind a row of makeshift firing lines Vivi had painted onto the floorboards. Several of them used shortened rifles with smaller stocks, and others held compact harpoon guns more suited to Wraithpier’s unique monstrosities, but the youngest among them weren’t trusted with either yet, so they practised aiming wooden replicas at the dummies while the older girls helped correct their posture.

The dummies themselves were old Saint’s Hands training mannequins that Fergal had shipped over from Blightmarch, though the children had improved them immediately by painting giant fish faces across their heads. Vivi supposed motivation mattered, no matter what form it took.

“Remember,” she continued, walking slowly behind the firing line, “try not to stare only at your weapon. Keep your eyes on what’s around the target too. If a Myrmur climbs onto one of your barges, there’ll probably be Saint’s Hands surrounding it already. You don’t want to shoot one of them through the back.”

A girl near the end of the fishing line raised her hand. “What if he’s bein’ annoyin’?”

“You still don’t shoot him.”

Another girl raised her head. “What if he’s really annoyin’?”

“Especially then.”

The girls nodded with varying levels of sincerity, making Vivi sigh. Teaching children to use rifles hadn’t been something she’d expected to add to her life when she still lived in Vharnveil. As a child, she’d briefly envisioned a career of teaching ballroom etiquette, correspondence, household accounting, or genealogy, but she’d never thought about having to teach trigger discipline to a bunch of ten-year-olds who considered harpoon wounds a normal inconvenience. Something was seriously wrong with how Wraithpier locals treated injuries and death. It was like they weren’t scared of pain at all.

Yet here she was, and—strangely enough—she didn’t completely dislike teaching them.

The reason for the training was practical enough. Miss Umbrage’s children were now stationed aboard almost every Saint’s Hands fishing barge operating across Wraithpier, where they acted as guides, navigators, and fishing experts. Most of the Saint’s Hands could protect them perfectly well—Vivi had sailed with several fishing crews herself, and while the gangsters lacked the raw power she’d become accustomed to seeing from Maeve and Jin, they compensated with truly alarming enthusiasm. The men with Jumping Spider Classes fought Myrmurs quickly, violently, and with very little concern for safety, but accidents could always happen. Myrmurs could climb aboard at any time without warning, so the children needed to be able to defend themselves just in case.

Which was why, while the Saint’s Hands handled most of the fishing and Gael and Maeve remained busy interrogating artificial Myrmur Hosts back at the warehouse, Vivi and Jin had volunteered to take over combat training for the children.

As she corrected yet another round of the girls’ posture, another violent thump came from the roof. She flinched and looked up. The clinic ceiling was full of holes from decades of Wraithpier weather and neglect, which meant she could occasionally catch glimpses of what Jin was doing above. A boy’s feet skidded across the roof tiles, and another body flew through her field of view. Then Jin’s voice boomed down through one of the larger holes.

“Stop pulling your punches! If you’re sparring, spar properly! Nobody learns anything by pretending to hit each other!”

A chorus of complaints answered him, making Vivi wince. He really was making the boys learn how to fight properly.

“Again!” Jin shouted. “And if you fall down, get up before the other guy decides to kick you!”

One of the girls beside Vivi stared up. “Boys are stupid.”

Vivi nodded automatically. “Sometimes.”

Then she realized what she’d said and cleared her throat.

“... Right. Rifles up!”

The next volley of bullets cracked through the atrium. Several rounds struck their targets cleanly. Two harpoons punched straight through painted fish heads, but one only clipped the edge of a mannequin’s shoulder, while another struck the wall far enough away that Vivi decided not to ask which girl had fired it.

They were making progress. Just not a lot of it.

After another half-hour of aim training, Vivi finally called for a break before somebody’s shoulders gave out. The transformation was immediate. The girls unloaded their rifles, stacked their harpoon guns by the racks, and very quickly, the disciplined little firing line dissolved into a swarm of children. Most of them sprawled over their mattresses to let out sighs of relief, while others raided the water barrel to quench their thirst. Three girls even pried open one of the loose floorboards and dropped fishing strings into the canal beneath the clinic.

For Vivi’s part, she simply lowered herself onto an overturned crate with a cup of water in both hands, wanting some time to relax herself—but then Nixy sat beside her. Then another girl sat on her other side, and three more gathered behind her. Before long, she was completely surrounded by the girls, and she looked between them suspiciously.

Nixy grinned back.

“Ye fancy Mister Jin, Miss Vivi?” she asked.

Vivi nearly inhaled her water. She coughed hard enough that the girls immediately started laughing, and she quickly lowered her cup before she spilled it all over her dress.

“What?” she muttered. “No. I don’t. I don’t know what gave you the impression, but…”

She didn’t even manage to finish her sentence before she saw the gleeful expressions on their faces, which meant they didn’t believe her in the slightest. Another thump sounded overhead. She glanced up through the holes in the ceiling to see Jin standing under the pale afternoon light with his coat removed and sleeves rolled above his elbows. Sweat darkende his shirt between the shoulders, and he was currently correcting one boy’s stance by moving the child’s fists higher before shoving him lightly back into position.

“Chin down,” Jin instructed. “Don’t stare at the fist. Look at the shoulders and hips. People lie with their hands, but bodies don’t.”

Vivi watched for slightly too long—and maybe with slightly too much interest—so Nixy made a small noise beside her. Vivi looked back down quickly.

“... Well,” she muttered. “Yes, I suppose I do… ‘fancy’ him’. Not that it was a conversation I wanted to have with you girls.”

The girls erupted in excitement then, and Vivi quickly regretted admitting to it.

“I knew it!”

“She likes him!”

“Course she does!”

“He’s huge! And big! And handsome!”

Vivi flushed. “That is not the reason.”

“Is a little reason?”

“No.”

She took another drink of water to give herself something dignified to do, but that didn’t stop the girl from bombarding her with the all-too-familiar questions: where she’d met him, how long she’d known him, and whether or not they’d slept on the same bed. Apparently girls were girls everywhere. Vharnvish noble daughters gossiped during long luncheons and garden gatherings, and Blightmarch girls gossiped while cleaning knives and harvesting herbs from the Fogspire Forests. It was comforting, in a way, to know Wraithpier girls liked gossip as much as anyone else.

So Vivi answered what she could without dying of embarrassment. It wasn’t like she thought of herself as being beyond small talk like this, and if talking about her unsuccessful romantic ventures made the girls just a little bit happier, then no point not to talk about it.

Eventually, though, Nixy tilted her head.

“Wait. Ye said before that yer families already knew each other, right?”

Vivi hesitated. “It’s a little more complicated than that, but I guess so?”

“But wasn’t he th’one ye were… what’s th’word?” Nixy frowned hard, sounding the syllables out carefully. “Be-tro-thed?” Then Nixy brightened when she realized she’d remembered correctly. “Yeah! Betrothed! So why don’t ye just tell him ye were th’girl and get married right away? What are ye even waitin’ for?”

Vivi opened her mouth to answer, but nothing came out immediately.

She supposed it all sounded so simple in a child’s mind. They’d been making even better progress in their relationship recently, after all. He respected her much more now that she could somewhat defend herself against a Myrmur, and that meant he also listened to her a bit more. Sometimes he even smiled around her whenever she managed to reel up a giant Myrmur. To Nixy and the other girls, that meant he definitely liked her now, and she’d succeeded in charming him with her own merits, so surely that meant they could get married right away.

And she would like that. She’d like to leave it all behind right now and go back up to Vharnveil with him if she could, but…

She smiled softly and looked at Nixy, shaking her head.

“... He came down here because the Breeder killed his father, and nobody else was going to do anything about it,” she said. “Everything he’s done since coming to Bharncair has been tied to finding him, one way or another, but even though we’re finally getting close, it’s still far from being ‘over’. There’s still a man behind the Breeder. A Raven. And… I like to think I know Jin well enough to know he won’t be satisfied just taking the Breeder’s head.”

Then she glanced up at the holes in the ceiling, gulping as she saw Jin flipping three boys over while they tried to charge at him.

“Truth is… I think we are close enough now that I could probably tell him about me after we take down the Breeder,” she whispered. And saying that aloud actually frightened her a bit more than she expected. Half a year ago, he probably would’ve stared at her after she told him she was his fiancee, then rejected her proposal and went back to hunting his Myrmurs.

But now?

She didn’t know.

There was a chance, no matter how slight, that he might actually accept her proposal after he took down the Breeder. There was a chance he might be satisfied with just the Breeder’s head and give up on chasing the Raven behind the scenes for her, and somehow, that…

Well, it just didn’t sit right with her.

She looked back down at the floorboards, her soft smile dwindling.

“I’m not like Maeve,” she admitted quietly. “Maeve can do everything at once. She can work as an Exorcist and share a functioning relationship with Gael without compromising on anything. She knows what she wants, he knows what he wants, and they don’t have to choose between each other and whatever they’re working towards. They have the same dream.”

She turned her cup slowly between her hands. “But I still want the things I was raised to want in Vharnveil. I want a comfortable mansion someday. I want quiet mornings. I want to stop waking up wondering whether somebody I care about is going to get eaten by a Myrmur before dinner.” She laughed softly, mostly at herself. “And if I’m being very selfish, I’d like my husband to have eyes only for me. So if I were to propose to him after we take down the Breeder, he might accept it and go back to Vharnveil with me even though I’d know—I’d just know—in the back of his head, he’d regret not going after the Raven as well. What’d I do if he regrets not going after the Raven for the rest of his life? While he sits in Vharnveil playing my husband, what if… he knows he hasn’t really finished the job?”

The girls remained quiet. Vivi felt herself becoming more embarrassed and ashamed with every word, but now that she’d begun, she couldn’t quite stop.

“... So I’ll wait,” she finished. “I’ll wait until the Breeder is dead. I’ll wait until the Raven behind him is dead. Until Jin reaches the end of whatever road his grief has put him on, I can’t—and I won’t—make him choose between me and his path. That’s just… basic common sense, right?” And finally she lifted her head again, giving the girls a flushed smile. “Besides, once all this is over, there’ll be nothing left to pull his attention somewhere else. Then he can look at me, and only me, and I can be as selfish as I want.”

Silence.

Vivi’s smile faltered.

Saintess, now that she played her entire speech one more time in her head, she realized just how horrifying childish she’d sounded. She could practically hear her old etiquette tutor dying somewhere.

“I mean—obviously, that sounded rather possessive, and I don’t literally mean he can’t look at anyone else,” she stammered quickly, feeling heat rushing to her chiefs. “I only meant emotionally speaking, and perhaps I’ve made this considerably stranger than it really sounds. I just meant I’d rather not make him choose between his revenge and—”

Nixy threw both arms around her with a laugh and then two more girls joined in.

“Aww!” Nixy squealed. “Yer cute, Miss Vivi!”

“That’s so cool!”

“Ye wanna monopolize him!”

Vivi’s face burned. “Well, I wouldn’t use that word, but—”

“That’s what it is!” Nixy pulled back enough to grin at her. “Miss Umbrage was like that too. Used t’say if she ever got herself a husband, he’d better come home every night or she’d fish him outta whatever canal he was hidin’ in!”

Another girl laughed. “My mum said the same thing about dad! Said if he stared at another lady too long, she’d feed his best boots to th’eels!”

“Oh, but my sister actually did that!”

“She did? No way!”

That apparently opened the floodgates, because the girls immediately began trading stories of their own. Nixy told her about Miss Umbrage actually a fisherman who’d once tried flirting with her after refusing to pay for treatment. Another girl remembered her mother throwing her father into the canal after he spent three nights drinking away from home, and before long, the conversation had spread to mothers, sisters, aunts, cousins—and Vivi kept listening.

She sat there with her hands folded together between her knees, smiling softly whenever the girls laughed, and somewhere among all their stories, her earlier embarrassment finally began to fade.

Perhaps what she wanted really was selfish. She wanted Jin alive. She wanted him safe. She wanted him to finish this hunt of his and finally reach a point where there wasn’t another monster waiting beyond the next corner, and once they reached that point, she wanted him only for herself. But Gael had told her there was nothing wrong with her selfish wish, so…

She’d wait.

She’d get the Breeder. Then she’d get the Raven behind him as well. And once there were no more enemies in their way, she’d finally tell Jin who she was.

Just wait a little longer.

We’re so close to the finishing line.

I can feel it.

Another ruckus sounded overhead, followed by Jin’s voice ordering one of the boys to stop dropping his left fist whenever he swung. Vivi glanced up through the broken ceiling, gulping. The girls really shouldn’t have mentioned how big and handsome he was, because now it was the only thing she could notice. Every time he folded his arms or reached out to correct one of the boys’ stances, his shirt pulled rather tightly across his chest, and her heart gave an inconvenient little thump.

He really is… pretty…

Her cheeks warmed again, and she refused to finish the thought. She was the one trying to charm him, not the other way around. What was she doing falling for him?

That thought, unfortunately, led directly into a much less pleasant one: what did he think whenever he looked at her? What if she was dramatically overestimating her own ladylike charms?

Actually, what if Jin heard her story, stared at her with that horribly blank expression of his, and simply said ‘no’ to her proposal?

She swallowed, her heart beating a little faster as she stared up through the ceiling.

Does he even see me in a slightly romantic way?

What is he even thinking about these days?
