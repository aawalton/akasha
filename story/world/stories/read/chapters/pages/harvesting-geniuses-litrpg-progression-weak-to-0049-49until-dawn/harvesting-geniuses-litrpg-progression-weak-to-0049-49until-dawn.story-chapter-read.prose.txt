The light and smoke rising as a signal didn’t come from a small campfire as Jinyo had assumed. It was a massive blaze born from magic. In Nephrin’s hand was a 9-inch wand. When she flicked it and chanted an incantation, Planar transformed into fireballs and launched outward.

Cursed Villagers scrambled in a frenzy trying to dodge the fireballs. But compared to her technique in controlling the flames, they were no different from stationary targets. She blew their pumpkin heads to pieces like using watermelons for target practice.

Not a trace of emotional disturbance showed on her beautiful face. Even though they resembled humans, she didn’t hesitate to blast their heads into chunks.

Nephrin had scored first place in the teaching license exam. She had placed third in the practical portion of the Hunter exam. Even though Jinyo had surpassed her in statistics, in terms of overall combat power, Nephrin was visibly superior when handling large numbers of enemies.

Dozens of Cursed Villagers targeted her alone. Nephrin simply stood in place, flicking her wand and casting basic spells to fight them all. Her cultivation rate was quite high and her Planar recovery speed was tremendous. As long as the recovery potions in her bag weren’t empty, she could keep going.

The problem she faced right now was the overwhelming number of enemies that seemed to have no end. During the daytime, she’d had no trouble. But once darkness fell and she built a fire for light—in a forest once shrouded by fog that now had no fog—visibility extended further even in the dark.

Her firelight had lured them in like swarms of zombies. They weren’t terrifyingly strong, but they were infuriating. And the longer time went on, their numbers multiplied exponentially.

“Did I wander into a horror novel or something!” Nephrin swung her wand harder. Where she had been standing still before, she now had to start running. During the early evening, she’d managed comfortably enough. But when the moon began to rise, they suddenly grew more resilient, forcing her to use more power to blow their heads apart.

Nephrin checked her remaining Planar. It was sufficient for now, but there was no way she could last until dawn. Her recovery potions would run out first, and the instant her Planar was completely drained, Nephrin would become nothing but a slab of meat for wolves to tear apart. Even though she had the power to slaughter Level 30 monsters as easily as breathing, once her power ran dry she was just a frail, delicate woman who couldn’t help herself.

I, Nephrin, first-place scorer on the teaching exam, rising star of the education world—how can I possibly die this young? My future is limitlessly bright. No matter how many of you there are, you’re nothing. Nephrin compressed her Planar to maximum density. Using less power for equal effect.

Her fireballs shrank by 60%, but when they struck their targets, they still produced enough explosive force to scatter pumpkin fragments everywhere. She deliberately aimed fireballs at trees to start combustion. Wildfire began spreading, sending smoke billowing skyward. Burning the forest helped reveal her position. Nephrin hoped someone would notice and come to help.

Nephrin didn’t know if this was the right decision. If the fire attracted even more monsters, what would she do? But every investment carried risk. If she didn’t make this choice, she could only lie down and wait for death.

The pumpkin-headed humans dragged farming tools along. Their eyes changed to green. Nephrin’s skin crawled as if she were being watched by a single entity. From the original twenty or thirty, their numbers swelled to forty, fifty—with no sign of decreasing.

That wasn’t all. They were getting stronger too.

“Ganging up on a lone woman like this—you gentlemen are truly shameless.” Nephrin formed ten fireballs at once. Ten was her current limit for simultaneous creation. The pumpkin-headed humans charged in, brandishing their weapons all at once.

Nephrin was pressured into firing. But even if she could blow up ten heads simultaneously, forty still remained.

Could she form more fireballs in time? No—she had to make it work regardless.

“Fire!” Nephrin swung her wand and launched the fireballs. She hurried to create another ten in just seconds. Her technique for controlling Planar quality had an enormous impact on casting speed. She blasted another ten before they closed in on her position. Nephrin tried to run and buy time, but they reached her before she could form her thirtieth fireball onward.

For a mage, the moment they were engaged at close range, it was game over.

Had no one followed the fire signal?

Or had someone followed the flames but decided it was too dangerous, so no one dared extend a hand?

That’s right. No matter when, I always…

Nephrin bit her lips. This wasn’t the time to mope or wail. The red-haired young woman compressed her Planar into a single dense mass. She no longer cared whether she’d be caught in it herself. As long as she survived this—that was enough.

A few seconds before the spell was complete, something astonishing fell from the sky.

“POTATO!!” A bizarre-shaped creature that looked like it had leaped out of a cartoon plunged down among the Cursed Villagers. One unlucky pumpkin-head had its neck broken by the weight of the creature dropping from above under gravity.

“Pota-Potato.” The little potato-person bounced and spun mid-air, making a fierce face and wagging its fingers tauntingly. It resembled a monster but wasn’t one. It was closer to a familiar than anything else. Nephrin stood frozen in shock. Born from her parents’ loins, she had never in her life seen a familiar that looked this idiotic.

It had no weapons. Just a stupid Carrot Sword reduced to a short knife that couldn’t harm anyone. Who was the fool who designed such a pathetic familiar?!

“Ms. Nephrin, get down!” A man’s shout echoed from afar. Nephrin dropped instinctively.

Bang! Bang! Bang! Bang! Bang! Bang! Bang! Bang! Bang! Bang! Bang! Bang! Bang!

Water magic bullets surged from the barrel of a gun, punching clean through the bodies of the pumpkin-headed humans, leaving them riddled with holes. They didn’t die immediately—their bodies tried to regenerate, plugging the bullet wounds to restore themselves. In the next instant, the aim shifted to their oversized pumpkin heads—easy targets. A few dozen shots and the pumpkin heads were blown to smithereens.

Jinyo came running with the watering-can gun, his son still strapped to his back in a vine hammock. He sprayed water bullets until the reservoir was empty. He tossed the gun aside and drew his sword, ready for close combat.

“You! You’re number nine!” Nephrin was relieved to receive help, but couldn’t recall his name immediately.

“No! Number two!” Gretel turned to correct her. Number nine, what? His father had scored second place in the practical exam! One rank above you—you’re number three!

“Ms. Nephrin, eat this first and recover your strength. Luciria and I will hold them off.” Jinyo pulled a thermos from his bag and tossed it to Nephrin. She caught it and unscrewed the lid. The fragrant aroma of rice porridge wafted to her nose. As an experienced mage who had trained in the capital, she could say without hesitation—this was special-grade produce!

“Thank you so much.”

“Luciria!” Jinyo called his disciple’s name. Luciria drew her sword and stood beside her master. She still lacked confidence facing monsters—especially this many.

“From now on, consider this training guidance. You don’t have to fight alone. Stay close to me and support my movements. Understood?”

“Yes, Master.”

Master? Young Lady Luciria has a master now? I had no idea. Nephrin was confused. Jinyo charged into the Cursed Villagers. The fragrance of peach blossoms scattered across the forest being consumed by wildfire. Luciria was nervous but Jinyo led the way. She simply seized the openings Jinyo created and struck each monster one at a time.

[You have begun guidance. Disciple’s stress decreased. Disciple’s comprehension increased.] The training system helped Luciria feel less anxious. Once she successfully slashed a pumpkin-head off, her stress naturally decreased.

What terrified Luciria was how helpless she’d felt before the Locust Demon. Jinyo just needed to show Luciria that she was strong and wasn’t useless. Killing monsters would build her confidence. First priority was giving her more self-assurance.

Nephrin drank the rice porridge. The Immortal Rice broke down instantly into usable energy. Its effect nearly matched the high-grade recovery potions she’d taken during her time in the capital. She was curious how Jinyo had gotten his hands on something like this—but that could wait.

Nephrin conjured fireballs and provided support for Jinyo and Luciria. With two skilled swordsmen in front, Nephrin could focus on aiming without worrying about being rushed at close range anymore.

His Sword Spirit has developed enormously. All of this… because of Master’s guidance? Luciria could feel it. The energy sheathing her blade was sharper. The sensation when the edge cut through muscle and bone was vivid. What was frightening was how rapidly it had improved in just one hour.

Sword Spirit was indeed the most fundamental technique for swordsmen—but those who trained it to a high level were exceedingly rare.

Jinyo’s Sword Spirit proficiency had already surpassed 40. When he used the guidance system, the higher his own proficiency, the faster the student on the receiving end developed too. If Luciria’s Sword Spirit had been around 15 before, it had now risen to 18 with virtually no effort.

That wasn’t all. The experience of watching her master’s movements gave Luciria a learning effect. The more she watched him, the more she felt she could fight better. Jinyo was a good teacher and a reassuring shield—something she had never received from anyone except her birth mother.

Jinyo and Nephrin worked together and killed hundreds of Cursed Villagers, but their numbers barely decreased. They only kept increasing until Jinyo was trapped in a siege just like Nephrin had been.

“I’m sorry, Master Jinyo. I’ve dragged you into this.”

“Don’t worry. This is good training.”

“Huh?”

“Fortunately, this world has a moon.” Jinyo set Gretel—who had been clinging to his back like a baby monkey—down on the ground. The seven-year-old boy was dead weight that restricted his movement. The pressure of facing so many enemies only made the fight more exhilarating. The emerald eyes he fixed on his enemies weren’t the eyes of prey—they were those of a bloodthirsty hunter.

Moonlight poured down upon his white-clad figure. Jinyo radiated as though light emanated from his body—so alluring and captivating that one couldn’t look away. Those resolute eyes made him so commanding that Nephrin couldn’t reconcile him with the quiet, reserved man from the day she’d handed him her business card.

[You have entered Moonlight status.]

“Establish perimeter… Otherworldly Domain.”

A hundred square meters were enveloped by an invisible domain. Intense Spirit Essence permeated the area. When he activated Nature Synthesis Technique within the Otherworldly Domain under Moonlight status, his Planar recovery rate skyrocketed.

“Come on, you ugly pumpkin-heads! Even if it takes all night—let’s see who has more endurance.” Jinyo declared fiercely. The pumpkin-headed humans were provoked into rage. They charged headlong to their own deaths. Jinyo held Sonneteer in one hand and his wooden sword in the other.

The Otherworldly Domain Jinyo deployed this time had a radius of only a hundred square meters—the range within which he could move freely. Luciria brought Nephrin running into the domain. She could immediately feel the Planar concentration she could draw upon increase dramatically.

The Cursed Villagers had no ranged weapons like bows or crossbows. As long as they came charging in on their own, Jinyo had no fear that he couldn’t behead them. No matter how many came.

“POTATO!!” Salt Flavor leaped and detonated itself. The blast shattered their formation. Jinyo gulped down Immortal Rice porridge. Once his Planar recovered, he could resummon Salt Flavor anytime. As time passed, the Cursed Villagers weren’t decreasing in number—but they stopped charging in recklessly.

As dawn approached, they abandoned their dead and retreated. They had realized they couldn’t kill Jinyo without the help of monsters stronger than themselves.

“Phew.” Jinyo and Nephrin collapsed to their knees in exhaustion. Jinyo especially was gasping for air. As the first sunlight of the day drew near, the gray curtain of fog slowly blanketed the forest once again.

Gretel knew he was safe and stopped hiding behind women who weren’t his mother. The observant boy noticed shadowy figures lurking not far away. When the fog began descending thickly once more, those shadows ran out of the forest toward their position.

“Ah! Those jerks!” Gretel pointed his finger. When the boy shouted, the people were too ashamed to look at Nephrin or Jinyo.

These people had followed the fire signal for a long time but seen that Jinyo’s group was fighting fiercely—yet hadn’t come to help. You call yourselves exam candidates? Do you even still want to be Hunters?

“I’m sorry. I thought we’d just be a burden to you, so I stayed back and watched. But if there’s anything you need us to do, just say the word. We’re willing to do anything.” One man spoke up. His expression showed guilt, but he had the courage to face them directly. Nephrin scowled but managed to mold a sweet smile in the blink of an eye.

“It’s perfectly fi—”

“The things you’ve collected in your bags. Can I have them?”

“Huh?”

“Excuse me?” Nephrin and the man turned to stare at Jinyo simultaneously. Jinyo’s gaze was fixed on the man’s backpack.

“The fruit you all picked up. Give it to me instead. Everyone here.”
