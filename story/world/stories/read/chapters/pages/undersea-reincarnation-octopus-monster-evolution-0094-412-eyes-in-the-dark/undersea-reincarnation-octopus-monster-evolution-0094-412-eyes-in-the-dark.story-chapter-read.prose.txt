The trio swam urgently, and they swam fast. Maurice couldn’t see where they were taking him, aside from down. The water was saturated with floating debris, which included algae, organic matter, and a bunch of other stuff he couldn’t identify. You’d think it’d clear out eventually, but as the seconds turned to a minute, they were still diving in nearly opaque water.

At this point, Maurice considered giving them the slip—or at least, trying to—but he could sense some weighty mental signatures around. He didn’t know what the situation was exactly when it came to these people and the local monsters. Whether they figured a way to bypass the beings that lived in the lake or had somehow gotten friendly with them, Maurice couldn’t tell. But he wasn’t sure the peace would be extended to him if he were to leave his escort. So he kept still and continued the charade. After all, these three were running from Henry’s divination. Eventually, his friend would get here, then he’d pull them away toward one of his clones.

Though just in case, he began gingerly snipping an exit out of the human shell. They could keep it.

Around the second minute, the water began clearing out, and slowly, Maurice began seeing deeper and deeper until, after the third minute, the lights became apparent, and a few seconds later, the city was fully visible, and while Maurice had been busy prepping for his escape, he found himself pausing to take the sight in.

It was immediately apparent that this city shared quite a few things with the one that housed the blue lobster, which Henry and Maurice had briefly explored near Thalis. It was at least as large as that abandoned city, potentially larger, and above it, large, multi-colored and powerful-looking fish lazily swam around it, never really getting close to any of the tall buildings they—Maurice assumed—could probably flatten easily enough. They were a mix of white, red, and different colors in between, with long wavy fins, some of them even seemed to be emanating light, but it wasn’t as much as the light coming from the city below. From afar, the lights mostly looked like whitish or blueish dots, but they illuminated the area well enough and all in all, the area seemed pretty lively. But Maurice’s observations were cut short as soon as they swam below the school of large fish, one of the blue dots separated away from the largest tower, and it swam toward them fast.

Right then and there, Maurice felt a twinge of regret at not having attempted his escape sooner. If that oncoming shape happened to be their own Zerathstra, he was in trouble.

“Stay still. We’ll help you with your breathing in a moment,” White Mask said to him, and suddenly, the whistling quality of their voice was gone. Also, they could talk underwater?

Leaving his questions for later, Maurice just nodded, acting a little worried—which he technically was, considering the individual swimming toward them—and within moments, his worries turned out to be warranted as a powerful pulse emanated out of the unmasked figure. He wasn’t sure what the effect had been, as Maurice hadn’t sensed anything different, but he had a bad feeling about it.

His captors dipped their heads to the newcomers, and Maurice gulped. He didn’t have his identify, but he could feel the aura of the gray-eyed being well enough. Long, flowing fins rippled behind her arms and neck, and she only spared him a look before her brows furrowed, looking up past the large roving fish before shooting the captors an annoyed look. Unlike the three he had come with, she didn’t have feet, only a long, sinewy tail with translucent gray fins.

“What have you brought? Its allies are still trying to find this one?”

It was Blue Mask. “Mistress Sel’Veth, this one teleported near our shores, and it was alone. We sensed the divination of its allies, so we brought it here for you. We thought it could be useful as a hostage, to have some of our own—”

As if she hadn’t heard them, Sel’Veth bared her sharp teeth once more, eyes still looking above. “Persistent,” she muttered. Then looking down at Maurice, she opened her mouth to speak, when they all felt it and this time, even Maurice could tell that someone was looking for him. Because all of a sudden, it felt like a multitude of invisible eyes had turned toward them. To them, it might have been unnerving, considering all three masked figures suddenly summoned weapons to their hands, while the mistress stilled, frowning. But to Maurice?

It just felt like home. And maybe a smile had slipped out of him, but he quickly schooled his features while the unnerved captors looked around, their spears in their hands once more. But this… Sel’Veth they all seemed to respect didn’t pull any weapons, instead, she let her own aura roil outward, and for a brief moment, her eyes shone with a harsh, white light, casting away Henry’s presence, but even when her eyes returned to normal, she kept some of her aura out, most likely preventing any further divination.

Maurice hoped Henry would find him soon. Otherwise he might need to eventually improvise.

“You,” Sel’Veth said to White Mask, “Go ring the alarm. Just the first one,” she ordered, and White Mask flitted away toward the city, while the commander looked up, sparing Maurice a look. “Your friends will regret coming down here to save you. We don’t take to your kind’s invasion kindly.”

“Not sure why you’re acting all offended. You’re the one who brought me down here,” he tried to say, but the only thing that came out of his mouth were gurgles and bubbles.

Right. They could speak underwater. But he had imitated Henry’s vocal cords, and those relied on air.

Sel’Veth stared at him, confused for a brief moment before she steeled her features once more. “No matter. We’ll deal with you later.”

Maurice just shrugged, and quietly, he continued snipping at the tendons and bones he had surrounded his real body with. Once Henry got here, he’d burst out of this body, and then they could skedaddle.

Maurice had his plan, and he’d deal with the talking-to he’d get from Henry later. Forcing the teleportation had been risky on his part. He was ready to admit that. Though, funnily enough, it might have been Henry’s fault, with all of his reckless testing. Maurice had learned by his example, after all. So could he really be blamed?

Maurice let the thought simmer for a second, then he was forced to nod.

Yeah, I’m to blame. That’s not good enough of an excuse, I think, especially since Henry’s gotten better with the big risks, he mused to himself as he discreetly eyed the creatures floating next to him.

Were these the Merfolk, then? Maurice had heard of them, but he’d also heard they were difficult to deal with and usually kept to themselves aside from some trading here and there. He hadn’t expected to find them here in the Reach. Though from their behavior, it really did seem like they didn’t like humans very much. But Maurice wasn’t sure how their reaction would turn out if they learned he wasn’t actually human. Though before he could continue that train of thought, the three around him tensed and looked up, and a moment later, Maurice followed suit, sensing what they sensed.

Henry was here.

The first, of course, was the aura. It was as if all the dark crevices and shadows in their surroundings had turned sentient and from within, something had been roused. Then the first glowing kraken swam down from the fog, like a blue falling star. Then the rest came through, dozens, hundreds of avatars, some Lifeblood, and some Arcane until finally, a bit to the left of them, the massive shape of Henry’s real form came down, bursting out of the obfuscating screen like a falling dark mountain. One would think the dark colors would be hard to see, but the inky blackness stood out above the glowing city, and the golden, glowing dots dancing on his skin made him stand out even more.

So it was natural that the placid fish swimming above the city suddenly reacted to the intruder. Like sharks reacting to chum, their movements immediately became frantic, but while any other day the display might have been threatening, Henry was larger than all of them combined. That didn’t discourage them, though, and they all turned to tear the intruder to pieces. The first of them beat its tail and accelerated, its maw gaping wide to take a bite out of Henry’s arms, only for the latter to Blink out of the way and appear right above Maurice and the three Merfolk, his yellow eyes looming over them. Behind him the large guardian fish that had just tried to take a bite seized and twitched, but Maurice saw no lightning. Likely venom, he deduced, though he couldn’t tell when or how Henry had deployed it.

The large fish, noting their target had moved away, turned and converged down toward them, only for the mask-less leader to raise a pacifying arm up toward them, all while maintaining eye contact with Henry, and after a couple of seconds, the fish calmed down and returned to their post. All but the one still twitching every few seconds.

“Will it be fine?” she finally asked, looking up at Henry, and in response, Henry brought one of his arms slowly, until its tip finally stopped a few inches from Maurice’s chest.

“[Release him. Remove the manacles and the collar, and I’ll make sure it lives. Push me, and I’ll make sure none of you do.]”

Maurice gulped. He really might be in trouble.
