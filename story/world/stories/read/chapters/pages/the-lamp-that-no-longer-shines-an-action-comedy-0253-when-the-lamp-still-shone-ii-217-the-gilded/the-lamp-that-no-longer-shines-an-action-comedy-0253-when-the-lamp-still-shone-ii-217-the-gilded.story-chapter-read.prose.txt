[Time]: 1979

The crystal chandeliers of the Casendiara Grand Assembly cast a fractured, prismatic glare across the ballroom, and the light seemed to physically bend and die the moment it brushed against the small figure leaning against a marble pillar near the east wall.

Mephisto held a crystal flute of something expensive and had no intention of drinking it.

She had come because of Selvantara. Under the absolute reign of Josephine Durant, the capital of Casendiara kept two entire avenues of its city centre, running unbroken from the municipal plaza to the gates of Josephine's own estate, as the most opulent and legendarily unbridled red-light district in the known world. The kind of district people planned international travel around.

The banquet was the price of admission. Mephisto was paying it.

The ambient light in her immediate vicinity had made its peace with dimming. Her demonic horns threw spiraling shadows up the marble column, and the spectral tentacles coiling around her calves moved with the lazy, unbothered drift of something that had been waiting a long time and was comfortable continuing to do so.

She was also entirely bored, until the grand double doors at the top of the stairs swept open, and the whispers that had been circulating for a week finally had a face.

The Habsburg anomaly. The convert genius. The air-dropped Countess that an ancient, declining dynasty had broken precedent for.

Mephisto's heavy-lidded gaze drifted toward the entrance. Irene Habsburg descended the staircase with the authority of someone who had never once been uncertain of a room's gravity, and whether it would orient toward her. Her golden eyes moved across the crowd with the detached, appraising quality of an emperor cataloguing a province. A beauty mark rested beneath her right eye, shifting fractionally as she offered the assembled nobility a smile that reached nowhere near her face.

Oh, Mephisto thought, her demonic horns practically humming with sudden interest. She is exquisite.

The formal banquet ran its course. When the diplomatic pleasantries concluded, the lighting shifted to a velvet amber, the music dropped an octave, and the heavy silk curtains at the rear of the room were drawn aside: the standard Witch banquet transition, from polite symposium to the kind of multi-participant exertion that Casendiara was quietly famous for. A senior noble leaned in and murmured something in Irene's ear.

Irene's expression didn't flicker. She offered an apologetic shake of her head and began making her way toward the exit.

Too clean. Too good. Terrified of a little skin, Mephisto thought, and set her untouched champagne on a passing tray.

The terrace was cool, smelling of stone and night-blooming flowers, the neon glow of Casendiara's pleasure district just visible over the estate walls. Mephisto stepped from behind a gargoyle and let her aura surface, a gentle deployment enough to drop the ambient temperature by several degrees, as Irene turned to identify the source.

"Planning an early night, Countess?"

Irene looked at her: tiny girl, spiraling horns, abyssal cold radiating from her frame, smiling in the particular way of someone deploying deliberate eeriness as a social maneuver.

"I prefer a more structured evening," Irene said.

"Structured is a word for boring." Mephisto closed the distance. "You've arrived in the entertainment capital of the Inner Sea on the most celebrated night of Josephine Durant's social calendar, and you're leaving to do what? Read?" She dropped her voice to a conspiratorial purr. "Ditch the carriage. Let me show you what Casendiara is actually famous for."

The oldest playbook in existence. The bad influence offering the sheltered noble a taste of the underworld. Mephisto had run it successfully on at least a dozen pristine, polished honor-student types, and Irene's immaculate posture practically announced that she had never once set foot in an establishment with a bouncer. She would flash the VIP card, cover the first round with theatrical generosity, play the experienced guide, and have the Habsburg genius fascinated with her by sunrise.

Irene looked at her for a moment, a subtle, unreadable light shifting in her golden eyes. "Lead the way," she said.

The Gilded Cage operated in a velvet-lined private wing behind the Crimson Ward's most discreet alley entrance. Mephisto bypassed the line without breaking stride. The manager, a Celestial Witch in robes of moon-pale silk whose smile arrived a precise half-second before the rest of her expression did, recognized her immediately and ushered them into the largest private booth in the VIP wing: crushed silk cushions, sunken velvet lounges, incense that didn't go through the lungs so much as directly into the nervous system.

Mephisto threw herself onto the chaise and waved a hand with magnanimous, theatrical flair. "My guest tonight," she announced. "Bring the premium roster out."

A crystal projector materialized three-dimensional illusions above the table: the club's available elite, rotating slowly. Elves woven from moonlight. Succubi whose pheromones had broken Arch-Witches. Angels with iridescent, half-bound wings. Sirens draped in pearls like they'd been born from the seafloor.

Irene leaned forward, rested her chin on her hand, and studied the projections with the detached precision of a jeweler examining stones under poor light. She tapped a manicured finger against the table. "The blonde elf. The succubus with the twin tails. The harpy. And the siren with the emerald scales."

Mephisto snorted. "Only four? Don't be polite on my account. I said I'd treat."

Irene turned her head. Her face was twelve inches from Mephisto's, expression perfectly, terrifyingly serene. "Send me everyone else," she told the manager.

The manager's smile slipped for a fraction of a second, which for a Celestial Witch was the equivalent of a full collapse. "My Lady, the premium roster has thirty available this evening. Twenty-six, excluding the four you mentioned—"

"The number stands."

Twenty-six it was.

Mephisto's arrogant confidence rebooted instantly. She's bluffing. These were seasoned professionals, veterans who had broken Arch-Witches for a living. A sheltered aristocrat was going to be blushing and incoherent in ten minutes, and Mephisto would dismiss half the roster early, well before the meter ran astronomical.

This plan was dead before the door finished swinging.

The professionals entered expecting prey. They hit the invisible, crushing weight of Irene's sovereign presence and staggered, visibly and audibly, like a procession walking into a headwind. Within five minutes it was no longer a parlor. It was an imperial court, and the professionals understood this immediately and began competing for the emperor's attention with a desperate, frantic earnestness Mephisto had not once witnessed in her extensive career as a patron.

Some girls she only watched: enough to reduce them to conspicuous blushing, performing harder for the mere chance of something more. A breathtaking angel with six-fold shimmering wings knelt on the floor, pouring Irene's wine with visibly trembling hands, eyes tilted upward in wordless pleading. A Siren leaned in and unleashed her innate charm magic at full saturation; Irene's fingers caught her chin before the first note landed, and her thumb moved slowly across the girl's jawline. The Siren's charm magic collapsed on contact. Her voice dissolved into a soft, involuntary gasp as her knees gave out entirely.

A high-ranking succubus, legendary for breaking clients twice her seniority, crawled forward. She pressed her cheek against Irene's knee with the desperate, shivering reverence of a devotee at a shrine, and stared upward with the trembling hope that Irene might, eventually, look down.

Only the absolute pinnacle, the ones who survived the crushing gravity long enough to meet some invisible threshold of Irene's exacting taste, were granted a kiss, or the privilege of extended proximity. The competition for this honor had, by the second hour, quietly ceased being professional.

She was the VIP, the generous patron. Somehow, the management had forgotten to notice.

Instead, Mephisto sat at the far end of the booth with her knees drawn up, a glass of untouched wine warming in her hand, her abyssal aura entirely retracted, staring.

Irene in the center of the sofa. Golden eyes half-lidded. Twenty-six professionals in various states of devotion orbiting her like planets around an inconveniently attractive star. The dim red light catching the silver of her hair, the curve of her jaw, the absolute assurance of her every movement.

Mephisto's brain, which had processed twenty-one years of aquatic horror and abyssal extremity without once requesting a break, quietly stopped working.

She is, Mephisto thought, her demonic horns radiating heat she was absolutely not acknowledging, a monster.

When the manager finally materialized with the bill, the incense had long since burned to ash and the candles had melted down to their last inch.

The scroll unrolled, hit the obsidian table, and continued rolling off the edge to pool on the floor.

Mephisto stared at the number. Her soul temporarily vacated her body.

Her initial deposit of Solars covered approximately twenty percent. She stood up very calmly, excused herself very politely, walked into the hallway, and spent fifteen minutes pressing her forehead against the stone wall while her older sister screamed at her through the communication crystal before the emergency wire transfer cleared.

The morning air of Casendiara was cool and smelled of distant incense and the beginning suggestion of dawn. Mephisto kicked a cobblestone. The spectral tentacles around her calves drooped with depressive solidarity.

Irene stood on the pavement looking immaculate: clothes unwrinkled, expression undisturbed, entirely unaffected by an evening that had just financially detonated her.

"You have a heart of solid stone," Mephisto said. "You sat there the entire night and let me foot an astronomical treasury alone. I had to beg my sister for a wire transfer. I may not financially recover before the decade ends."

Irene looked at her, eyes clear and bright, undimmed by the hour or the evening. "You were the one who said it was your treat," she pointed out.

The words hit Mephisto. She opened her mouth, felt the acute burning awareness of someone who had constructed a flawless predator's strategy and been taken apart from the inside by the intended prey. The experienced guide. The absolute bandit, now standing on a Casendiara cobblestone at dawn having just begged her sister for money.

Before she could formulate a single syllable of response, Irene took a half-step closer, and the careful remove she had maintained all night dissolved. Her eyes softened. The beauty mark shifted as the corners of her mouth curved, and the smile that appeared, quiet, genuinely warm, carrying an intimacy that had nothing to do with performance, landed in Mephisto's chest like a small, precise detonation.

"Thank you, Mephisto," Irene said, her voice dropping into a register that barely carried above the distant city hum. "I had a wonderful time tonight."

Mephisto stood there in the cooling Casendiara dawn, demonic horns flushed with heat she would not acknowledge, staring at that smile.

The financial ruin evaporated. The fifteen minutes of her sister's mocking laughter evaporated.

I made out like an absolute bandit, Mephisto thought.
