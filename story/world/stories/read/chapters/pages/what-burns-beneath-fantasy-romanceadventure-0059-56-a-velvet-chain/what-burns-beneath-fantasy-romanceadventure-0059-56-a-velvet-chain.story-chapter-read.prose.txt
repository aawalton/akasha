Royce sat rigid beside me as I explained the details of his miraculous healing and my subsequent nightmares. His face alternated between shades of bone-white and sickly green.

“So this whole time,” he choked out, sounding as though he could hardly breathe, “just… what? Any moment, I could’ve been ripped open again? No warning?”

I wiped my eyes. “Y-yes. That part’s over, though. He ended it last night. And that’s… well that’s the other important part of my story, I suppose. There were pacted people in this town who’d made a deal with a hag. Baltha.” I shuddered at the name and the memories it threatened to elicit. “They’d been kidnapping travelers and delivering them to her. The three of us wound up trapped in her cave, and then-”

Velidan. My throat closed around the word. Icy pain ripped through my belly and I doubled over, mouth open in a soundless scream.

“Brin!” Teela’s voice was shrill.

“What’s wrong with her?!” Warm hands closed over my shoulders.

Every breath felt like it was fighting its way through a layer of mud. Gravity shifted as Royce lifted me, then lurched towards the door and the silent figure blocking it.

“Move!” my friend shouted. “Or, salt, go get a cleric! She needs help!”

Renner didn’t budge.

“Stop,” I gagged. Clammy hands clutched at Royce’s collar and I fought to right myself. “S-stop. I’m f-fine.”

He was shaking. “You’re fine?! Brin, you’re not!”

“I just f-forgot. I’m alright.”

Very slowly, my feet were lowered.

“New deal, huh?” Renner’s voice was dark.

I sucked in air, rubbing at my throat and chest. At the viscous black within. “Yes. I just… h-haven’t gotten there, yet.”

He rubbed his temples, sighing wearily. “Fine. Get there.”

I turned back to Royce, immediately flinching at the horror in his eyes. “I’m alright,” I murmured again, though my voice nearly broke. “I’m… He came into the cave. The Fae from Fellbrook. He’s the one that killed Baltha.”

“Why?” Royce collapsed back onto the bed. “Why would monsters fight each other?”

I nodded towards the shard. “Because he wanted that. And… well. You asked about the blood, Royce, and that’s the other part of the story. The one everyone here knows, at least. Teela was unconscious, Renner was half-dead, and I… I made another deal. If he healed everyone, I’d give him the shard the next time I slept.”

“And… and what happens after…?” He looked like he was going to be sick.

“I spent hours thinking about that,” I whispered, sniffling. “But I fell asleep earlier, and I kept my promise. I handed it over. Well, I threw it at him. But when I woke up, it was back in my hand.”

“And what else happened?” Renner gave me a hard stare. “What deal did you try to break just now?”

I sank to the bedspread and spent a long time trying to find the right words. “I can’t… tell you exactly. But I know his name, and there’s a way that I could,” my gaze flicked up, rounded in equal parts horror and hope, “use it to kill him.”

Heart-stopping silence descended on the room.

“That was the new pact. I agreed not to, and not to tell anyone exactly how. But,” my fingers curled into the quilt and my teeth clenched, “if there’s a way to get free, to break it or… or if I can find a loophole, maybe-”

“Why are you alive?”

Renner’s murmur crashed through my rambling, and silence fell again in its wake.

The way he looked at me threaded a line between suspicion and fear. “If you know something so dangerous and he had what he wanted… then why in the realms are you alive? Why would he make a deal instead of just killing you?”

‘I could. Truth be told, I should. But you’re so… entertaining.’ Velvet slithered through my mind. I curled my arms around my middle. “Well he… he s-said he w-would. If I didn’t agree.”

“No. Not good enough. That doesn’t make any damn sense. Is there something else he needs from you? Something he asked you to do? Something he wants?” Copper eyes simmered, searching for some clue in my reaction.

‘You weren’t the only one enjoying yourself.’ Iron strength pressing me into stone. Hot breath against the nape of my neck. Bile and something like shame coated my throat, making every word choke as it rose. “No. He… there’s n-nothing he… n-no.”

His voice dropped lower, sharpening at the edges. “Thought you were done lying.”

“Hey, back off.” Royce’s arm curled around my waist. “Show a little-”

“Shut up. Ladyship-”

“I’m not lying.” My nails dug half-moons in my palms. “I’m not. There’s n-nothing he needs me to do, Renner.” I met his eyes firmly even as blood roared through my ears. “I promise.”

He searched my face for a long while, then growled a sigh. “Fine. So who’s staying up to keep an eye on you tonight?”

“No one.”

Teela’s tail snapped at the air. “What do you mean?”

I swiped my tears away. “I don’t want any of you to… to stand vigil over me. I want to sleep alone.”

Her voice rose an octave. “But we can wake you up! If you start to, salt, have your skin ripped off or-”

“No. Look, when I wake up it’s not… s-sudden. It takes a moment. That’s all he needs. A moment. If he decides to kill me, I don’t think there’s any stopping it.”

Royce’s face was ashen. “So, what, you don’t want anyone to even try? That’s not… that’s just… no!”

Teela was shaking her head. “Brin-”

“It’s my decision! There was a… a moment. Earlier. He… threatened me.” My gaze cut towards Renner, who watched in stony silence. Trembling fingers knotted together. “And I knew you w-would see, and would shake me awake, but… Saints, can you imagine? Trying to wake someone up, looking at them, h-holding them, and then the next moment they’re… b-bloody? Torn apart? Dead? No. No. That would be… I can’t think of anything more awful. I could never do that to any of you.”

Three stares bored into me. Shadows danced erratically across each face.

“Brin…” Teela’s voice cracked.

I blinked back the remaining tears. “It’s my decision.”

Royce was shaking his head. His lips were parted, but he said nothing.

He came all this way. He was so happy to see me. And now… My heart ached and I shifted, pressing my forehead against one broad shoulder. “I’m so sorry.”

He practically buried me in an embrace. “What for?”

“Just… I don’t know. You came all this way, you were so happy, and now… I… I’ve ruined it, haven’t I?” I sniffled. “I’m sorry.”

He offered no answer, but the way his hold tightened spoke volumes. His head shifted above mine, jaw settling with heavy weight on the crown of my head.

The back of my neck prickled, making me very aware of our audience. An embarrassed flush urged me to draw back, but I wasn’t quite willing to pull away from this much-needed affection. Not after the past few days. “I’m so glad you’re alright. I really missed you.”

“I missed you, too.” His baritone cracked, and something in me nearly cracked along with it. A moment later he straightened, held me at arms’ length, and offered a weak grin. “So. We’re going to Pinnacle, huh?”
