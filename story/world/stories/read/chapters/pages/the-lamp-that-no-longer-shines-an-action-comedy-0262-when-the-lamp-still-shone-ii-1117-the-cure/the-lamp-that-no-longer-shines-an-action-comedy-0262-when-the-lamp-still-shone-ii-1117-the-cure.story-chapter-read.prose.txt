[Time]: 1981

It is an open secret within the Witch academic community that horror cinema is among the most technically accomplished art forms in the Inner Sea, for an extremely simple and deeply unsettling reason: the aberrations, apparitions, and specimens filling the screen are, in a non-trivial percentage of productions, sourced directly from containment. Premiere theaters quietly maintain standing medical support, primarily to extract viewers whose cardiovascular systems have registered the genuine article in frame three of the third act.

Irene was settled into the center of the Cathedral's sprawling sofa, arms resting loosely along the backrest, waiting for her investment to mature. Catastrophically low tolerance, paired with an unyielding addiction to the thrill: she had worked out the pattern on the first night, and she had been the one bringing the films ever since.

On the massive crystalline screen, something was dragging itself backward down a corridor with too many joints and an enthusiasm for the process that no living thing should possess. Ovelia was staring at it with her enormous black eyes dilated to their maximum aperture, pressed so tightly against Kara that their fur blurred together at the edges. Kara's ears were locked in a rigid 'Y' formation, and her tiny paws clutched her glowing belly with the desperate grip of a creature preparing for catastrophic impact. Close beside Irene, Allison had retained her human form and was maintaining impeccable posture with the focused intensity of someone who was absolutely not frightened.

Ovelia's fur, normally a deep black dusted with faint, slow-pulsing starlight, had come up to full-body illumination; Irene could have read a textbook by her. With every creak of the soundtrack, the glowing sphere drifted a little further from Kara and a little nearer to Irene's knee.

On screen, the creature lurched, and the sound design ruptured the air.

"Meow! Meow! MEOW!"

Ovelia rocketed straight into Irene's lap and buried her glowing face against Irene's stomach, wailing with the full, unguarded abandon of a cat who had temporarily forgotten she was the most terrifying entity in known history. Cat is not frightened, the meowing informed Irene, verbatim. Cat is relocating for tactical reasons.

Allison's composure went in the same instant. With a soft, shattered gasp she folded sideways like a cut ribbon into Irene's chest, buried her face in the crook of Irene's neck, and clutched at her lapel with both hands.

Kara, abruptly bereft of the cat she had been fused to, went after her. Demonstrating the universally acknowledged truth that all Lantern Cats are functionally non-Newtonian fluids, she poured across the gap and down Irene's left side, tucked her glowing belly against Irene's ribs with a series of soft, pitiful whimpers, and began hammering her tail against Irene's thigh with pneumatic urgency.

Irene's arms came down off the backrest, one closing around Allison's trembling shoulders, the other sinking deep into the blazing, star-dusted velvet of Ovelia's back, while her thigh held Kara in place. Three distinct flavors of the First Seat shivered against her, and her golden eyes curved into crescents in the dark. Inner Sea cinema, she thought, truly is the pinnacle of civilization.

"Ovelia," Kara wheezed from beneath Irene's elbow. "Speak—cat—no, speak human."

The luminous mass in Irene's lap gave a full-body shudder. Ovelia extracted her face from Irene's coat, made no move whatsoever to vacate the lap, smoothed her fur with as much dignity as the situation allowed, and lifted her chin in a register of supreme, trembling arrogance that the glow still pulsing against Irene's fingers undermined almost entirely.

"Ch-change the channel!" she declared. "We are watching the duelist draft!"

"The duelist draft doesn't broadcast for another hour," Irene said softly, her fingers working a slow rhythm along the base of Ovelia's ears.

Click.

The blood-soaked hallway vanished, replaced with the brightly lit desk of the municipal news anchor.

Allison had reached out one blind hand and mashed the remote on the glass coffee table, the rest of her still draped across Irene's chest. Only when the cheerful broadcast jingle filled the hall did she peel herself off Irene's lapel, clear her throat, smooth her skirt, and address the television in a tone of gentle, artificially pitched civic responsibility. "The news is also very important. Are the current events of the Witch World not vital to our daily lives? We can simply absorb the news cycle for a while, and then watch the draft..."

With the pressure lifted, Ovelia's ferocity staged an immediate, complete recovery. She puffed out her chest, her fur settled back to its dignified starlight, and she slipped out from under Irene's hand to float a safe six inches above her lap. "Meow-ow," she grumbled. "We might as well watch Catolf."

Irene let her hands come to rest on her knees and looked at the remote on the glass table. Next time, she decided, the remote stays in my pocket.

After several nights of containment specimens on the crystal screen, the genuine, transparent apparition that drifted out of the Cathedral's vaulted stone walls later that evening struck Irene as a budget production.

The ghost was weeping, bobbing aimlessly down the corridor and trailing its misery along the masonry with the specific, worn-down energy of an entity that had lived a cowardly life and died into an even more cowardly afterlife.

Irene stood in the middle of the corridor and waited. The wailing went on, the stone throwing it back echo upon echo, until sheer volume had made it a legitimate inconvenience.

Too loud, she concluded.

She raised one hand. A cage of gold wire closed around the ghost before it had finished its next sob, folded it down to the size of a pearl, and dropped it into a 4,600°C alchemical furnace. Three minutes later a flawless Master-tier shard of [Unmelting Ice] fell into her palm, and she pocketed it.

That disposed of the noise. It did not explain why she had heard it.

She was not a Ghost Witch, and nothing in her eyes had been made for the dead. If she had seen that apparition down to the tracks of its tears and heard every sob of it, then something in her was no longer working the way it had been built to.

The cause was not hard to find. Her curriculum had been punishingly dense, and she had been drinking high-tier potions faster than any physician would have allowed. The residue had built up in her blood, crept past the barrier into the brain, and begun to change what she could perceive.

Is this a problem? She turned the [Unmelting Ice] over in her palm. The toxicity drove every spell she already carried deeper into her spine in half the time, and the [Spell Mastery] feats were arriving far sooner than they had any right to. Against that, new spells took to her at a fraction of their old pace, and at this saturation a single legendary-tier elixir would put her in bed for two or three months. There was no cure. She would pay the price, then.

The following afternoon, in a rare hour off between lessons: "Meow. You have accumulated quite a lot of drug toxicity," Ovelia remarked.

She had been floating near the ceiling, her thick tail mashing the remote control with careless expertise until the screen settled on the tranquil green fairways of a Catolf broadcast, then drifted back down to hover just above the sofa. Irene settled into the cushions and reached up, threading her fingers into the impossibly soft fur between the Lantern Cat's upright ears.

"The academic consensus, as I recall, is that this particular condition has no definitive solution," Irene observed.

Ovelia's tail executed a slow, proprietary swish. "Just because the others haven't researched a cure," she purred, "doesn't mean Cat haven't researched it."

"How would you do it?"

The Lantern Cat nudged her thigh, gesturing for room. Irene slid back against the armrest and watched as Ovelia floated toward the center of the room. The cat's ambient glow spiked, and then the light in the room was seized entirely, torn from every surface, plunging the space into suffocating darkness.

Standing at the center of the room was the Witch of Eternal Slumber. Her raven-black hair framed a face of impossible, gravity-distorting beauty, and her fathomless black eyes carried the slow, turning light of a distant cosmos. She offered a slight, impeccably elegant bow, and began to speak with the unhurried cadence of someone who has rehearsed a particular miracle until it bores her.

"The pharmaceutical toxins within a Witch's body embed themselves at the foundational level of the inheritance factors," Ovelia said. "To cure or purge them, one must operate at exactly that depth. I would need to sever your current factors entirely, disassemble them, forge a clean sequence from scratch, and then inject my own regenerative genes directly into the new architecture."

Irene's brow furrowed. "Regenerative genes?"

Ovelia's gaze dropped, one pale hand rising to rest lightly against her own flat abdomen. "My factors," she said quietly. "It would permanently consume approximately one thousand M-Units of my core mana capacity. In exchange, it would imprint a continuous, deep regenerative ability into your root structure. The regeneration would hold the toxicity at precisely the level where it remains an asset and neutralize anything beyond."

Irene went completely still. At Ovelia's tier of existence, a single M-Unit of her mana outclassed a hundred from a standard Arch-Witch, and she was offering a thousand of them, carved out of herself for good. From the very first potion she had put into Irene's arm, Ovelia had known the toxicity would have to be paid for out of her own body, and she had gone on dosing Irene anyway.

Irene had spent her whole life devouring everything within reach to climb higher. This was the one meal she would not touch.

Her gaze went over Ovelia once, from the slow-turning dark of her eyes to the hand resting flat against her stomach, as though making sure all of it was still there.

"No," Irene said. "Keep it." Her chin lifted a fraction. "I will find my own cure. I have no interest in climbing a mountain that has lowered itself to meet me."

"Lowered?" Ovelia let out a low, breathy chuckle. "Who said anything about lowering?"

She raised her hand. With casual, fluid grace, the sharp edge of her manicured nail grazed the pad of her thumb. A thin, razor-straight red line bloomed across the pale skin. A single perfect drop of blood welled up, detached, and hovered suspended in the air between them.

The moment the blood touched the air, Irene's pupils blew wide until the gold of her irises was a fine, trembling ring. An overwhelming thirst, older than civilization and every pretense of refinement she had spent a lifetime constructing, rose in her throat and simply demanded. Her body wanted to wrap that single ruby bead in mana, draw it past her lips, and taste every atom of it. The color drained from her face.

Ovelia watched her struggle with quiet, satisfied amusement. The drop retracted, sinking back into her skin without a trace. Irene drew a controlled breath, her hands pressing flat against the dragon-hide cushions.

Ovelia smiled, crossed back to the sofa, and sat at the far end of it. Slowly, she raised both hands, gathered the heavy cascade of her black hair, and swept it entirely over one shoulder. She tilted her head to the side, exposing the long, flawless, snow-white line of her neck to the amber glow of the chandeliers.

"Come," Ovelia said quietly.

Somewhere well under the composure Irene's jaw had already made its own arrangements, and she could feel the fangs come down and settle against the inside of her lip.

Ovelia exhaled a faint, fond sigh of impatience. "Stupid girl. Cat is not handing you anything. You have teeth, and Cat has already told you what the universe pays for."

Irene closed the distance along the cushions. The moment she leaned in, the scent hit her: a dense, intoxicating braid of crisp green grapes and the sharp, burning bite of strong shochu.

She lowered her head. Her lips brushed the soft, warm skin of Ovelia's neck. Her fangs pressed into the pale flesh, and she bit down.

The taste was sweeter than any elixir and richer than anything she had consumed: liquid fire the moment it hit her tongue, rushing through her bloodstream and incinerating the heavy, grinding metallic sludge of accumulated toxicity in a single annihilating wave, and under the fire, something that had never belonged to her went down into her marrow and settled there as easily as if the two of them had been made of one substance from the start. The sheer euphoria of it threatened to drag her under entirely. She felt, in one terrifying microsecond, the urge to simply keep drinking until there was nothing left to take.

She broke the seal and pulled back. As she withdrew, the tip of her tongue moved without her permission, dragging lightly across the two small puncture wounds she had left behind.

The pale skin of Ovelia's neck gave a single, involuntary shiver.

The silence in the room was absolute.

Ovelia rose and released her hair, letting the black curtain fall back into place over her neck and everything beneath it, and walked into the shadows of the Cathedral without looking back.

Irene remained alone in the room, the taste of green grapes and starlight still burning on her tongue.
