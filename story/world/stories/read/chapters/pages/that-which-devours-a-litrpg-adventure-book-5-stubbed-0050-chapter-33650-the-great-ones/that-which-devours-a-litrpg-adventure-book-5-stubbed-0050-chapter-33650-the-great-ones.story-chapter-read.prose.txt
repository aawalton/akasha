One second I felt like I was laying on the most comfortable bed I had ever imagined, the next a cold breeze touched my toes and I shot up.

Darkness still surrounded me, but not pitch blackness. Tiny dots of light flickered in the far reaches of my vision. I sat on a slab of stone, floating in the air.

Memories flashed through me, of fighting the glowing creature created by the memories of humanity. Or at least that was what it was sold as. It had become a giant mess of uploaded memories, degrading over time.

I still clutched a pitch black square in my right hand, and I still didn’t know what it was.

My toes were still securely tucked into my boots, and the cold breeze didn’t make sense at all.

“You are awake, good.” Noseen said. He appeared next to the stone bed in a humanoid form.

I didn’t know what to say. My head didn’t hurt anymore, but I knew I had a bunch of notifications stacked up waiting for me.

“I was summoned,” I said carefully.

“Yes, for your reward for hitting 1st place in the great cleansing of Clan Vielether,” said Noseen. Each of his black eyes traced over me, like he could see my soul. They landed on the cube in my hands. “But there are other concerns we need to discuss as well.”

I swallowed hard, but before I could reply, he continued.

“Check your notifications. Time moves differently here.”

I nodded and opened the incandescent dinging in the back of my head. The System’s warnings about being outside of System Space were gone.

[You have survived combat against a level 575, Ouros.]

[You have gained a level.] x 5

[You have collected a high bounty: Gain a Whisper.]

[??????]

[??????]

[You have survived outside known System Space. Title Gained: Cosmos Explorer. One who goes beyond what is known and returns to tell the tale. Provides guidance to the System.]

I really hated question marks at this point. What use were they? Why notify me of them at all?

“Why does it show lots of question marks?” I finally asked.

Noseen sighed. “Because you went outside what is known, and the System is processing the information you brought back. It will let you know when it updates.”

I just stared at him for a second, then shook my head. The motion hurt.

He held my spear in one hand. “You dropped this.”

“How did you get that?” I asked quietly, as I took it in my free hand, then tossed it into my inventory.

Noseen pressed his lips together for a moment. Then nodded. “I guess you might not know. You summoned me for help against Ouros.”

“That’s how he died…”

“Yes. The Blood Emperor had a little temper tantrum,” said a whisper of a voice.

Noseen bowed his head as a being stepped out of nothingness.

[The First.]

It was completely made out of shadows, with wings wrapped tightly around its back. No eyes, mouth, or any other feature was visible on the surface of the darkness. This was the thing I had seen before, in the orb.

“It was justified,” The First added.

Noseen’s head rose, but he said nothing.

I slid off the edge of the stone bed and it vanished, almost throwing me off balance. I tried to put the cube in my inventory, but it wouldn’t go. Instead, it remained in my hand. I couldn’t even upwrap my fingers from it.

Noseen buzzed ever so quietly, and my head snapped up.

The First stared at me. “You solved two hidden quests, and freed me from a prison.”

Noseen's mouth fell open quickly, followed by an easy smile. He added quietly, “Alex is smart.”

“She is.”

“Anyone would do as much as I did, if they could,” I finally said, glancing between the two. Right at this moment, I wanted to know what to do with the cube that wouldn’t come off of my fingers. “It was the right thing to do, and quests need to be finished.”

#

Noseen, in the blackness…

For once, Alex wasn’t acting smart. She had saved a being that we could never touch, and acted like it was nothing. Or maybe she was acting very smart.

The First bowed his head at her, and she gave him a sharp nod.

This child.

I let my shoulders relax. Even with everything, she was still very young. The First knew that even at her level, she was younger than all of the others. The youngest devourer to have ever reached the beginnings of Immortality.

“The Devourers of Light and Knowledge will give you options for your reward for your placement,” he said. Silently, in my head, he continued, “The Creator comes for what is next. Prepare your child.”

For once I had nothing to say, no comment, no thought even.

Then the First vanished.

Alex remained focused on the strange cube she held onto.

I let out an unnecessary breath.

“Alex,” I said carefully. “You are owed much from the Great Ones.”

She shrugged, like it wasn’t a big deal.

“This is serious. Be as smart, and as respectful, as I know you can be,” I added.

She sighed, then straightened her shoulders. “I won the contest without meaning to. What are the options?”

“You should get the planet.” It was the most logical choice for her to make. With her moving up the ranks of Immortality, she’d need a planet if she wanted to make any progress. Very few could keep moving up without one. “We can connect it to the portal system already in place with Lakeside and the Sisters.”

“Sounds like a plan. I can see if Abby will want to create a place on it. Lenna will probably want to help with the survivors.” Her thoughts were unreadable to me.

Even this close, the bond between us still hadn’t reconnected.

“Alex, what happened when you summoned me?” I asked quietly, trying to understand the missing part of all of this, and how the First was involved. I didn’t doubt Alex helping them, they’d said it themselves after all, but none of this made any sense.

While there was a mana blocker where I had appeared, it had been destroyed.

“I fell into the orb which held the First,” she said, as her gaze went off to something I couldn’t see. “Some rewards I’d gotten were removed from my inventory, and then I was back in the council chamber.”

She paused, and she actually looked at me. “I didn’t know I summoned you until you just told me. I assume it was you that shoved me through the tear, then?”

“I destroyed Ouros and went looking for you,” I said carefully, dancing around my temper tantrum, as the First called it. “You weren’t close enough.”

“But you survived,” said Alex.

I chuckled. “I am much higher level than you. You have only taken the first step. I am almost at the final.”

Alex’s face paled. “What does that mean?”

A presence surged into place, and in the distance light flared, taking the shape of what Alex’s people called a black hole.

The space rippled near the edges of the light. I bowed my head gently as a creature stepped out. It was small, furry, and on four legs. The black stripes in its fur made it look harmless.

“The Great Ones greet the Blood Emperor and the Hungry Shadows.”

So this was going to be done as by-the-book as possible. This wasn’t how they normally interacted with Devourers.

“And we greet the Great Ones.”

“The Hungry Shadows took first place in the competition for ownership of the disgraced world. The Great Ones offer ownership to the Hungry Shadows, as long as they respect the native Devourers.”

That had to be from one of the younger Great Ones, who hadn’t heard the entire story. Yet, the first option was for the planet. Something was going on here. Something I didn’t understand.

“Appochas’ young have nothing to fear from me,” said Alex. “They will learn of her, when they are older. My promise to her stands.”

The small creature’s head tilted.

Still, this could not be all they would offer her. Not after she had saved the eggs. For the competition, yes, but not for that.

I almost stepped forward, but resisted as a ripple came from somewhere farther away from here, but closer to where the planet actually was.

“You have restored the potential of the disgraced planet, and I offer knowledge of the planet itself, along with those who remain that do not want to rule. They want to live in balance.”

Before I could give her any guidance, Alex responded. At least she was being somewhat respectful. For her.

Alex nodded. “Sounds good. I know there were some higher leveled beings who did not enter the competition.”

Light shot out and bathed Alex in a soft glow. She tilted a little, and I reached out to steady her, to keep her upright. The light slowly reduced in intensity. Hopefully, her intelligence and willpower was high enough to absorb what she was being given. It should be. Alex was smart like that.

“This concludes the rewards for the contest for the disgraced world. Its details will be updated once the Hungry Shadows selects a new name.”

“Gassamer,” whispered Alex, as she straightened. “I will call it Gassamer.”

“It is done.”

I let go of her arm and gave her a sharp nod. So far, so good, and no surprises. This part should be almost complete. Then I needed to warn Alex about how to act once the Creator arrived.

The small creature turned to go, and vanished. The reflection of the Great One in the distance dimmed.

“That wasn’t so bad,” said Alex, as she rubbed a hand over her face. “My head hurts a little.”

“You need more stats in intelligence and willpower,” I added quietly.

She pressed her lips together and did something quickly.

“We need to speak about what’s next.” I took a breath and steady myself, but she responded first.

“The Creator comes,” she said, not even looking at me. Her eyes were on a fixed point on the horizon.

“Yes. I don’t know if you can survive in their presence,” I tried to explain. I didn’t want her to worry. She was protected. “They are so much more.”

All I knew was rumors, nothing certain. Not one of the eldest I knew had met the Creator. The other question was, why did she need to be owning a planet before the meeting?

That was the only reason I could come up with for this series of events. The Great Ones felt it was important for her ownership to be secured before anything else. Even the First thought she’d have choices, but she hadn’t.

Shaking my head I turned to Alex, and she still stared out into the distance. “Alex?”

“They are here,” she said, then her body vanished.

“Alex?!”

“She is fine,” whispered a quiet voice. “This is one way for them to meet without consequence.”

My shoulders fell, and I let out a sigh. “You mean, they don’t want me to know what happened.”

“Even I don’t know what happened after she left the world,” the First responded.

I couldn’t see him but it was him. I’d know him anywhere. Any of us would.

“It must remain between the Great Ones, and Alex.”

I bowed my head. I wouldn’t push, even though I didn’t like it.

“You have gone far, my son,” he added. “You saw the edges.”

“I had to find her and bring her here.” I kept my voice level. Going out so close to the edges was dangerous. While my time would come, I wasn’t there yet. So much strength was needed to survive there, though there was also so much to gain. A challenge beyond even my current state, it was something to strive for. I needed that.

“She will return to where you found her after this.”

“No. That cannot…”

His presence pressed down on me, stopping the words in my throat. My form shifted into my actual shape, unintentionally. “She will survive. The edge will be far from her.”

Relief buzzed through me, and I hovered there, unsure why I remained if she wasn’t coming back to here.

“You can take her back to that place, when they are done.” He paused, then continued, “You destroyed an abomination, and have rewards of your own coming.”

I waited.

“You should ask what is beyond leveling,” he said. Then he was gone, leaving me puzzled.
