Trevor was already giddy before they had made it to the first booth. Each one was set up with a certain amount of space with their robot in the center, usually on some kind of pedestal. He kept excitedly patting Adabelle’s arm as they walked a little faster than normal.

“You’re quite eager, honey,” the demonkin remarked with a smile. “I have heard the term robots before, but I’m not sure what the differences between them, golems, and constructs are. Could you tell me?”

“I could, but I’m sure we can find someone qualified who could explain it a lot better than I could,” Trevor said as he scratched the back of his neck. “We’d get a more detailed explanation that way.”

“That may be true,” Adabelle agreed, but she shook her head. “However, I want to hear it from you. You’re clearly excited, and I like your passion when you speak about things you like.”

They stopped in front of the first booth and looked past the small crowd in front of it. A pair of demons stood in front of a mostly humanoid copper automaton. Both of its legs split at the knee and had wide feet underneath as if it needed the extra stability. Its chest was open, and one of its creators was pointing out the mechanics inside.

Trevor was aware that his wife wanted him to explain things, but he was collecting his thoughts and waiting for the right moment. He led her down the line, checking out the rest of the booths. Most of them were humanoid in design, but the differences between them were staggering.

The first had clearly been influenced by steampunk, but the next was all smooth curves with white metal and black accents. Its face was an array of lights forming a happy expression. The one after that was shaped like a miniature wracor, and the next looked like a fridge with arms and legs.

That was literal, too; the freezer door was open to reveal a head with a thick knit cap on it for the illusion of warmth. This one was definitely more of an art piece than a functioning robot. It gave the courier a healthy chuckle, but it still wasn’t what he was looking for. It took almost five more booths before he came across the perfect one.

The robot had been made out of pieces of garbage, but it was moving. Rust covered most of the metal, and some pieces had holes through it. The robot couldn’t have been taller than Trevor’s waist, with glowing eyes and a thick battery pack on its back. Frayed wires came out of its joints, sparking slightly, but it seemed like that was a part of its design.

The creator, a lanky demon with filed down horns, stood proudly to the side. A banner spanning across the width of the booth read, “Little Broken is dedicated to the trash tech team from AU. Every piece of this robot came from the dump, just like their ideas. GCC for life!”

“It seems as though someone has a grudge,” Adabelle said after she read the sign, which Trevor snickered at.

The small robot whirred loudly as it moved back and forth. Its actions were very familiar to the courier, dancing with deliberate, halting movements, even going so far as to moonwalk across the platform before spinning. While someone without knowledge from Earth would have guessed it was malfunctioning, Trevor knew what this was. He watched it with a grin as it performed the robot.

Adabelle caught his mirth and tapped his leg with her tail before wrapping it around him. “Will you answer my question now, son of Ander?”

“Yeah, this will be perfect,” Trevor said, gesturing towards Little Broken. “You’ve fought golems before, I’m sure.”

“I have.”

“Golems, like Clay here, are generally solid pieces that move based on magic. Their arms and legs bend where they’re supposed to because that’s what they’re programmed to do. You know, through magic. Their body, just like Clay’s husk, is homogeneous with all other parts of itself.”

The earth elemental nodded as they looked at their arms. They bent it back and forth from the elbow they had made, but then did so halfway up their forearm before looking at Trevor.

“You’re a special case, little buddy,” he chuckled as he reached up and rubbed his familiar’s head. “You don’t need to be programmed, you just do whatever you want with what you’ve got. Golems and constructs, at least when they’re considered monsters, can’t do what you do because you’re so much better than they are.”

Clay stood up a little straighter, puffing out their chest with pride. Their skill at molding earth came easily now that they were a bonded familiar, and their precision was something most wizards would have paid an arm and a leg for.

In fact, there had been a few offers to get the elemental working full time. Clay would usually oblige for a week or two before becoming bored and moving on to new things.

Trevor cleared his throat and continued. “Robots, though, are made up of several interconnected pieces,” he explained before pointing at Little Broken’s arm. “See inside its left elbow? Where the wires are coming out?”

Adabelle leaned forward slightly and squinted. “I do. It looks like there’s something moving inside. Spinning, I think.”

“That’s right. Those are servos, and there should be more all throughout its body,” he said excitedly. “They’re the things that both let it move through a motor and dictate how far it's allowed to go. Just like your own elbow.”

Unconsciously, the demonkin stretched her arm out before flexing it in the way he was talking about. “Okay, I get it. It acts like a joint as well as the muscles, ligaments, and everything else.”

“I… think that’s right?” Trevor asked more than said before offering her an apologetic smile. “It’s been a long, long time since I’ve looked into this, so I could be wrong about many things.”

“If you are, then you are; it won’t be a big deal,” Adabelle replied with a small shrug. “So those servos are all over?”

“Yes,” he confirmed with a nod, turning back to Little Broken. “Each individual piece works to move the whole. The servos requires power to bend, which might come from the battery pack on its back or from bands attached to hydraulics that move its greater arm. Given the range of motion, I’d say it’s probably the former.”

“Because it uses electricity instead of magic, right?” she asked.

“Also yes, though there was mention of magic that had been augmented to work similar to electricity,” he answered with a shrug. “Either way, that’s the big difference between robots and golems as I see it. These ones are a sum of its parts, put together to move a certain way. They also tend to look more techy like most of these are. I’m seeing quite a few different designs that I’m kind of surprised about.”

“Oh?” Adabelle arched an eyebrow. “In what way?”

“Like that first one we saw, the copper one,” he said, looking back that way. “Clearly inspired by a steampunk setting. The staples of those are copper or bronze metals, steam-powered machines, airships, glass, and leather mostly. It was a pretty prolific style of science fiction back on Earth.”

“We did have to take an airship to get here,” the demonkin mused. “I wonder if Sammie the Gamer was a fan of this steampunk style.”

“It seems likely, but there might have been another Summoned who introduced the concept. I think there’d be more if it was something she actively pushed for.”

“That is true,” Adabelle agreed. Her gaze went down the line of booths. “But we’ve only seen a handful of robots, so there might be more in that style. We should keep moving.”

“Sure, I’m with you on that,” Trevor said as he started leading her down the street.

“You seemed fairly well versed in robotics,” the noblewoman stated as she looped her arm around him. “Is it something you studied?”

“Not academically, but you could say that,” he replied as he shook his head. “Nah, I just went through a phase that most boys do. Space, robots, and, of course, cool fights.”

“Cool fights?” Adabelle repeated, her tail tightening around his wrist. “Color me intrigued.”

“There were a couple of television shows and channels on the internet dedicated to robot fights,” Trevor explained. “Some were animated with drawings, or used practical effects, but my favorites were the ones that people actually built for competitions.”

“That’s interesting. Were they like these ones?”

Trevor shook his head again. “No. Well, sometimes. I mean, the animated and movie ones were like that, but not the ones in the competitions. Those ones were small. Maybe around knee height. They’d have buzz saws, and flamethrowers, picks, and flippers.”

“Flippers?” Adabelle asked with a laugh. “Like on a fish.”

“No, I mean like a spatula,” he replied, amused.

She gave him an incredulous look. “How does a spatula help one robot win against another when they have saws and flamethrowers?”

Trevor was about to answer when someone else beat him to it. “If they’re upside down, they can’t fight back unless they have an option to right themselves on their own!”

Both he and Adabelle turned to see a booth with a team of demons and a gnome. They were all looking at one of their animal-eared members, who appeared to have just had an epiphany, as she stared off into space.

“If we put a flipper on Monstrosity, then we can alternate between hammering them apart with brute force trauma with the thick pick and flipping them over!” she exclaimed, pointing at the small black and red robot next to her. It had tank treads and a pickaxe that could alternate between a flat hammer or a piercing weapon. “I can think of maybe three others who can right themselves? Skillz With A Z for sure, Hard Packed is another, and I think the wheels on Super Supper might be big enough to work even upside down.”

One of the other demons rested their face in a hand, and Trevor got the idea that this wasn’t the first time this had happened. “Matty, the purists on the council will never approve such a design. You’ve tried mixing things up before, and you’ve always been shot down since you aren’t following Sammie’s vision.”

“Sod Sammie’s vision! I’ve got a good feeling about this one. It’s so innocuous, how could they possibly get riled up? If it works, we’ll have a whole season of innovation all to ourselves! It’s what Sammie would have wanted!”

“You can’t curse her name and invoke it back to back, Matty,” the gnome sighed.

The demon, a manic look on her face, ignored her team to turn her attention to Trevor. Her green eyes were wide as they locked onto his, and she pointed. “Who are you, good sir?” she asked forcefully.

“Uh, Trevor?” he replied.

“We’re going to win this year’s Fighterbot competition with this new tech for sure,” she exclaimed. Her team was already sighing as they poked and prodded the machine that was, apparently, called Monstrosity as they came up with changes to its chassis. “Do you have any other ideas, Sir Trevor?”

He stared for a moment before finding his voice. “I guess… grabbers?”

“Grabbers! Meke, write that down!” the mechanic shouted. When she turned to address her team, Trevor took the opportunity to lead Adabelle away, walking at a brisk pace. “Wait! I need to pick your brain! Don’t leave!”

They kept going until they reached Hazelwood and turned just as Sharn instructed. Trevor pulled Adabelle into a crowd that had formed around another booth. The courier ducked low enough that he couldn’t be seen with the other people around. His lack of horns made him easy to spot and he wasn’t sure if she’d care enough to give chase.

“I was not expecting to accidentally influence the metagame today by introducing the concept of a wide spatula to something called Fighterbots,” Trevor said with a sigh.

“Sir,” came a polite, refined voice from the booth. “Did you just say Fighterbot with a spatula? Why would you put a… Oh. Oh dear.”

“Noooo,” the courier lamented as he led Adabelle out of the crowd he had escaped into. The man called out to him, but he ignored them.

He didn’t stop until they had passed at least six more booths, turning to check behind them for any pursuers. There weren’t any.

“Not only is my husband exotic, but he’s also in high demand thanks to his intelligence, too,” Adabelle teased as she lightly squeezed his hand. “Although, now that you say it, it seems like a perfectly natural leap to make.”

“It should be, but it sounds like my perspective is wider than theirs,” Trevor sighed as they entered a large plaza. “That first team said something about the purity of Sammie’s ideas, so they probably haven’t innovated a lot. Seems odd considering that she had the Power of literal Innovation, but sometimes people get stuck in an idea and they stagnate, you know? I’m pretty sure that happens a lot everywhere.”

“I suppose it does. That’s good insight, honey.”

He gave her a thin smile before checking behind them again. Now he was sure they weren’t going to be pursued. “This season is going to be pretty interesting, I think.”

“And I think that’s going to be an understatement,” Adabelle said before she nodded towards a statue that had been built in the plaza. “I believe we’ve found our Sammie the Gamer.”

Trevor turned to see what she had gestured to, and they walked over. The plaque underneath it did, in fact, state that the monument was a depiction of the Summoned. Instead of a demon, it depicted what the courier could only guess was a goblin.

Sammie’s head was flat in the front but wide with long, jagged ears. Her eyes were a little too large, and her body slightly too thin. A full mop of curly hair obstructed her vision, which was strange because Trevor was pretty sure goblins were bald.

She had a tool belt around her waist and a wrench in her hand. The monument showed her smacking a car engine with the tool. It was a very precise piece of work that would rival anything Drelik or his late wife, Cindal, could make.

Looking back down at the plaque, he read it aloud. “Sammie the Gamer. The brightest flame of Aletander burned away too soon.”

“Ah, poor woman,” Adabelle whispered, frowning sadly at the statue.

Trevor gave her a curious look. “What do you mean?”

She gestured towards the monument. “Sammie either came here as a goblin or became one. Which isn’t necessarily a bad thing; she obviously did well for herself, but they have a very short life. Even a Summoned goblin would only live for maybe twenty-five years maximum.”

“Oh, dang. That does suck. I can see why she was the brightest flame then.”

Adabelle nodded. “Not only that, but goblins are prone to chaotic bursts of mischief. They can be pointed in the right direction so long as they have enough stimulus to keep them occupied, but it can be rough sometimes.”

“I’ve been there,” Trevor said with a knowing nod.

She gave his hand a gentle squeeze. “The absolute worst part would be reading and writing.”

That made the courier frown. “In my world, we didn’t have goblins but a lot of the stories around them mentioned they were illiterate. Is that something that is generally true?”

“Not by choice,” Adabelle answered. “Goblins, in this world at least, are incapable of learning to read and write.”

“Oh, that’s not good,” he replied with a cringe.

The demonkin nodded. “It’s something done to them at the moment of creation that makes them utterly illiterate, as you said. They are monstrous races, which means they are born from dungeons in a very specific manner. That doesn’t make them evil right out of the gate. Mischievous and chaotic, sure, but not bad. There’s been several studies done trying to break through their mental block through social programs and magical means, though I haven’t heard of any of them going anywhere.”

“Does it bother them?”

“Not usually, no,” Adabelle replied with a shake of her head. “They only try when bribed. While words are out, they do love drawing though. I’m hoping that Sammie the Gamer was different, but I’m not holding my breath. We’ll see when we visit this playground museum Lora teased. If she didn’t dictate to someone to write down her musings, then it might have been down to pictures she drew to pass on her innovations to the country after she died.”

Trevor and Adabelle stared up at the statue before he shrugged. “Well, goblin or not, Sammie the Gamer definitely left an oversized mark on Aletander. She steered this country all the way into the future. I think the only other Summoned who outshines her might be Jackson. Feeling sorry for her because she didn’t live long and couldn’t read probably isn’t the right play here.”

“You’re absolutely right, honey,” the demonkin agreed with a small smile. “The brightest flame that burned away far too soon. An apt description. I’m sure her days were full of joy as she filled the world around her with her inventions.”

Nodding, Trevor turned away from the statue and froze. Slowly, he pointed at one of the booths. “Found AU.”

Adabelle followed his finger to see a solid gold robot in the shape of a demon. It was dancing, but only the lower half. While its feet rhythmically stomped the ground, its two hands were pouring water from one cup into another, and then back again without spilling a drop. Above it was another banner. This one was much shorter than the last.

“Get stomped, GCC nerds! AU rules!”

“Ah, rivalry,” Trevor chuckled. “Truly a wonderful thing to see.”
