The ruin breathed in silence after the collapse. Faint dust hung suspended in the still air, turning the faint glow from her staff into misted light. Elynara moved carefully through the debris, each step stirring small clouds that shimmered before fading. The air had cooled since the fight, leaving a damp chill clinging to her skin.

She lifted a hand, conjuring a small orb of flame. It drifted from her fingers, steady and pale, casting long shadows across the fractured walls. The glow traced the jagged edges of twisted metal and melted panels, the light catching where symbols had once been etched.

The marks were half-buried beneath grime and rust, their shapes warped but still discernible. She brushed one clean with her sleeve, the surface rough beneath her touch.

Curves and angles intertwined in patterns she didn’t recognize.

Her throat tightened with the need to understand.

But at least one thing was clear. If Celestia’s gift held true even here, those patterns were not a language.

Or at least not one of words and meanings.

Elynara pulled her notebook free and crouched beside the wall. Her hand trembled a little from the stress and exhaustion, but she wrote anyway. She copied each line, each break in the symbols. Her pencil fell from her fingers when she got a little too engrossed, and she cursed softly under her breath before steadying herself.

“Not giving up that easy,” she muttered.

Starpuff floated closer, its soft hum filling the silence. It drifted along the wall beside her, faint light slipping through cracks to reveal more signs hidden under the surface.

“Good eye,” she said, smiling faintly. “We’ll call that teamwork.”

The puff trilled in approval and circled her once before settling above her shoulder.

She copied what little remained legible before the rust claimed it completely.

When her pencil finally fell still, she leaned back against the wall. Her legs ached from crouching, her cloak heavy with dust.

The orb of flame floated ahead, steady now, painting the hall in muted gold. For the first time since entering the ruins, she stopped to look properly.

Even broken, the scale of the place was staggering. The walls stretched far beyond her light’s reach. The ceiling was lost to shadow, its supports vanishing upward like the ribs of some giant structure.

Here and there, remnants of walkways clung to the walls, half-collapsed but still hinting at an order that once governed the chaos.

Even her collapsing ceilings onto the bug monster nests didn’t impact the sheer scale of the place much. Still…

“...I probably shouldn’t make this a habit, right? Or there might not end up any ruins to explore. What do you think, my little friend?”

Elynara turned to look at Starpuff, who just bobbed in place, trilling happily at the attention.

She snorted, shook her head at the silly puff, and let her thoughts flow freely again.

“...You know, all this while you have been my companion, and I only considered you a cute mischievous pet. But you’re really growing, aren’t you? Learning, understanding more, and now even casting spells to help?

Elynara had Starpuff’s undivided attention now, it even forgot to bob in place or shift colors, staying perfectly still, hanging onto her every word.

“You’re amazing, you know that? And a great friend. Will you forgive me for not treating you as such until now?”

And now it was changing colors. Rapidly!

Heh, the cute little puff was actually embarrassed. Elynara never expected to see the day!

But instead of tormenting it any longer, she opted for just patting it lightly.

Aaand bribed it with quite a nice chunk of mana in all shapes and sizes she could think of. Including a puff-shaped cloud of mana, which was just enough to shake her spirit companion from the shock.

She smiled at the happy nomming noises, its embarrassment forgotten already, before turning her attention back to the ruins around.

This place was no small ruin. But a half-buried city. One she couldn’t really gauge the size of yet, though Elynara suspected it would easily put Oltenar to shame on what little she had seen so far.

The idea struck heavy in her chest. She closed her eyes for a moment, imagining what it might have looked like alive.

Towers humming with energy. Lights glowing in windows high above. Voices echoing through corridors that now only held the whisper of her own breath.

Her old memories stirred again, faint and unhelpful. Fragments of buildings that weren’t these, machines that thrummed with smoke instead of mana, laughter carried through streets lined with light bulbs.

None of it fit here. The images clashed and broke apart before they could form meaning.

But something familiar tugged at the edge of it all.

The rhythm of design, the confidence in craft. Whoever built this place had known exactly what they were doing and did so with a clear purpose in mind.

Elynara’s eyes opened again. The orb flickered slightly, as if catching her thought.

“...I would have loved to see this place thriving.”

The sound of her voice felt strange against the quiet. She wasn’t sure if she meant it as awe or grief.

A gentle chirp pulled her back. Starpuff had settled on her knee, done with its feast, and in much higher spirits already.

It really didn’t take much to make the little guy happy.

But that was fine.

It made her smile too!

She watched as her flame spell’s light mirrored in the dark shards scattered across the floor. Each fragment caught and reflected the glow, tiny points of red hue among the gray.

Elynara reached down and turned one over with her fingers. The glass was cool, its underside smooth where it had fused to metal. Inside it, faint veins of color still glimmered – blue and gold woven together.

It pulled at her mana, and she let it. Only a tiny bit flowed in before the pull stopped.

And then it did… nothing. The piece just held the mana in, though just the fact it didn’t start dispersing into the air immediately was fascinating.

Was it the material? Or the shapes doing the work?

Who was she kidding, it was probably both.

Her staff hummed beside her, the quiet pulse grounding her in the stillness. She leaned against the wall, exhaustion settling through her limbs.

It really had been a long day.

Her gaze drifted upward again, to the broken archways and the shadows beyond. For all its ruin, the place held an undeniable grace. The precision of its lines. The care in its symmetry. Even half-destroyed, it was beautiful.

She rested her chin on her knees.

“Yet another wonder the world had lost,” she said softly, “no wonder it’s bleeding still.”

The words echoed once, then disappeared into the vastness.

She reached out, touched the nearest wall again, and felt the faint vibration still running beneath the surface. Faint traces of mana still ‘stuck’ in places, refusing to escape.

Elynara let her head rest against the wall and closed her eyes for a few breaths. The light from her staff stayed steady, patient, casting the same warm halo of light.

When she opened her eyes again, the orb drifted higher, lighting the corridor ahead. The ruins stretched on – still, broken, and waiting.

She stood slowly, knees protesting.

“Alright,” she whispered. “Rest’s over.”

The puff darted up to meet her, chirping bright, already ready to move.

Elynara brushed dust from her sleeves and tightened her grip on the staff. The faint hum of power that still lingered here answered in kind.

Whatever the world had been before, pieces of it were still alive.

And she intended to listen until they spoke.

Elynara had lost count of how many times she stopped to rest. Each pause felt shorter than it should, every breath carrying the weight of what might move next.

The ruins no longer felt empty; they felt aware.

Her staff light carved narrow lines through the dark, dust swirling in its wake. Starpuff’s green glow drifted ahead in short bursts, scanning corners before she reached them.

The puff taking its new ‘job’ seriously.

Every sound mattered here. A single drop of grit from the ceiling, a whisper of shifting metal – each one drew her attention like a pulled thread.

In places she had to move slowly, testing each step before committing her weight. The ground was uneven, fractured in ways that spoke of both time and violence.

Her heartbeat never settled, but her pace never faltered either.

“This far in, and still standing,” she whispered. The quiet swallowed the words almost at once.

She kept going.

The air grew thicker, every inhale tasting faintly of dust, ash, and rust. Her boots crunched over layers of old debris – thin sheets of glass, brittle wire, fragments of something too light to be stone.

Once, her light caught movement at the edge of a doorway. Her grip tightened, breath halting, but it was only a strip of metal swinging on a thread of wire, still moving from the breeze she had made passing by.

She let the tension drain from her shoulders and shook her head at how silly that was. She kept watch through mana sensing all the time, and yet a flicker in her vision still caught her heart thumping.

“Jumpier than I thought,” she muttered.

Starpuff hummed quietly, the sound close to a laugh.

“Don’t you start.”

Some corridors she avoided without hesitation. They felt wrong in ways she didn’t have words for – too narrow, too still, their darkness pressing close and heavy. Instinct whispered to leave them alone, and she listened.

Caution was survival, and she would best keep to it.

There was no one here to see her be brave but Starpuff, and the puff was too busy peering into alcoves to judge.

The deeper she went, the stranger the architecture became. The lines of the place lost sense of direction, twisting in slow, deliberate ways. Some walls bent inward as though melted; others were scored with long grooves that glowed faintly when her light touched them.

The remnants of machines – she assumed they were machines – hung half-buried in rubble, their purpose long forgotten.

And then she reached the first hall of figures.

They stood in rows along both sides of the corridor, stretching into the dark. Humanoid, tall, and blurred at the edges, their bodies molded from a dull gray material that might have been metal once.

Each one looked unfinished, or half-erased. Arms cut away, torsos split open, faces worn smooth where detail should have been.

For a moment she forgot to breathe.

Her staff light swept across them, revealing more – dozens, maybe hundreds, fused into the walls or sunk waist-deep in the floor. Some had collapsed entirely, cracked clean in two, their hollow interiors glittering faintly with mineral residue.

She approached the nearest one, stopping just short of touching it. Its proportions were almost human, but not quite. Too symmetrical. Too precise. A faint curve at the joints suggested motion that had been interrupted, frozen in mid-step.

“Statues?” she whispered. “Or something else.”

The thought of them being armor made her chest tighten. The thought of them not being armor made it worse.

Starpuff darted ahead, curiosity overcoming hesitation. It hovered near one of the fallen figures, nudged its surface, and the delicate plink that came back was hollow – an echo that still managed to roll through the corridor, long and distant.

The puff shot backward immediately, hiding behind her shoulder with an indignant chirp.

Elynara exhaled through her nose, forcing calm into her voice.

“You started it.”

It peered out from behind her braid, clearly unconvinced.

Her smile came thin but steady.

“Alright, fair. We’ll call it even.”

The echo from the hollow form lingered longer than it should have, fading only after several breaths. It reminded her of the still air before thunder, the way the world seemed to hold itself still to listen.

She crouched beside the nearest broken figure and brushed her hand across its surface. The material was cold, smoother than stone, with a faint grit that clung to her glove. Beneath the dull gray, she thought she saw veins of something translucent – old conduits, maybe?

A pattern of some sort carved into flesh or forged into armor.

Her pulse quickened.

If these were remnants of the civilization that built the ruins, then this hall might be more than decoration.

A workshop, perhaps.

Or a grave.

She withdrew her hand and stood again. The shapes nearest her leaned forward slightly, their heads tilted in the same direction. Toward the far end of the hall, where the darkness deepened into a narrow doorway.

She swallowed.

“Of course.”

The puff flickered brighter, hovering close to her ear.

“No,” she said quietly, “I don’t like it either.”

Still, her boots moved. Habit, duty, curiosity – it didn’t matter which led her forward. They always ended up meaning the same thing.

Each step past the silent rows pressed louder into her thoughts. The deeper she went, the stronger the faint hum in the walls became, low and rhythmic, following her pace. She half-wondered if the figures could hear it too.

By the time she reached the doorway, the air had grown warmer again. Her fingers tingled where the staff’s light touched the threshold. Beyond it, the corridor sloped downward into something vast.

She turned once, casting her light back over the hall. The blank faces caught the glow in uneven reflections, their forms still and waiting.

Her breath steadied.

“Alright,” she said quietly, to herself, to Starpuff, to the ruins. “Let’s see what else you’re hiding.”

The puff hummed, energetic and ready!

Elynara squared her shoulders, tightened her grip on the staff, and stepped through the doorway.

The silence behind her closed like a held breath.

The corridors narrowed as Elynara moved deeper, the walls closing in until her staff light brushed both sides. The air thickened with the weight of stillness, pressing against her ears until even the quiet began to hum.

At first she thought it was only fatigue. But as she paused to rest her palm against the wall, the hum stayed. It came from the ruin itself – low, constant, and almost beneath hearing.

“Alright,” she said quietly, “either the place is still alive, or I’m imagining things.”

Starpuff floated a few paces ahead, its glow pale in the dark.

It spun once in what she decided was encouragement, though its paling light didn’t inspire much confidence.

The path changed as she went. The shattered beams and fallen rubble that had marked every other hall were gone.

The floor here was whole, and unbroken. Her steps made soft, dull sounds against the smooth surface, absorbed instead of echoed.

She slowed, noticing the shift in texture underfoot. No grit. No debris. Even the dust that had choked the upper floors was gone. The realization landed like a cold hand on her neck.

“Too clean, far too clean.”

No nests, or scratches. Not even any broken shells.

Her light reached the next corner, the corridor – or was it street? – opening up to the evening light, and the air seemed to twist.

The smell of metal thickened, sharper than before, mixed with something dry.

She turned the corner – and froze.

Bones.

They covered the floor in uneven drifts, pale against the dark. Some still held shape, others had shattered into fragments. They weren’t fresh, only dry and brittle, the surface of each one dulled by time.

Most were small, a scattering of limbs and ribs pressed against walls. Others were larger. Too large to be human.

Elynara’s throat tightened. She crouched and brushed her gloved fingers across a femur, the bone crumbling at the lightest touch.

“Old,” she whispered. “Whatever happened here, it’s been years.”

Still, the pattern was wrong.

These weren’t scattered randomly. They had been pushed aside, swept into corners, arranged in lines that led nowhere. She traced the edge of one such path with her staff. It cut clean through the corridor, a narrow trail through the remains where nothing lay at all.

Her stomach turned.

Something had moved through here.

She straightened slowly, the motion deliberate, her pulse steady by will alone. The hum in the walls had faded. In its place came silence so complete it felt alive.

Elynara adjusted her grip on the staff and took another step forward. Her boots brushed bones that cracked underfoot, soft and final.

Every instinct told her to leave. To turn back before she reached whatever waited ahead. But she had come too far to retreat on fear alone.

She looked toward Starpuff.

The puff hovered near the center of the hall, unconcerned, its glow tracing small circles against the ceiling.

“You’re lucky I like you,” she said under her breath. “But if anything breathes wrong, we run. Deal?”

Starpuff trilled a cheerful note, either agreement or mockery – she couldn’t tell which.

The sound loosened something in her chest. She exhaled and pressed forward.

The corridor continued without end, the floor sloping slightly downward. The bones grew thicker near the edges, layered upon themselves until the walls blurred under their pale weight.

Her steps quickened. She didn’t want to look at them anymore.

The corridor widened without warning. The ceiling arched away, fractured metal giving to open air, and Elynara blinked against the sudden brightness.

After so long in the half-light of the ruins, daylight felt intrusive, cutting clean lines through the dust and shadow.

The tunnel ended in what must once have been a courtyard, or a garden of sorts – its walls torn, its roof collapsed. Above, the sky filtered through jagged gaps, the color washed pale by haze. The light fell in narrow beams across a shallow basin of earth and broken panels.

For the first time since she had entered the ruin, the world felt alive again.

Green had returned here, or perhaps had never left?

Grass spilled through cracks in the metal floor, fine and thin but stubborn. Vines crawled across the walls, clinging to the edges of shattered frames. Patches of moss glimmered faintly where the light hit, dew catching on their surfaces.

It wasn’t lush, but it was deliberate.

Elynara knelt near the nearest patch, tracing the curl of a root that had split through steel.

“You shouldn’t even be here,” she said quietly.

The plants didn’t belong to this place. The air still tasted faintly metallic, the mana in it thinner than the outside world. Yet the leaves held a soft sheen, faint but steady, as if drawing on some unseen strength.

She tilted her head, studying their pattern. None of it was random. The growths curved in arcs, looping across the courtyard in spirals that nearly met before breaking away again. The clusters of color formed rhythms – repeated lines that mirrored the geometry of the surrounding walls.

Starpuff floated beside her shoulder, wings humming softly. It drifted lower to investigate a patch of moss, its glow painting the ground in pale green.

“Don’t eat it,” Elynara warned without looking up.

The puff trilled in mock offense and retreated half a pace, spinning slowly.

She smiled faintly.

“Good choice.”

Her attention returned to the plants. The arrangement was too precise to be coincidence. Some of the spirals were incomplete, the outer rings thinning where light failed to reach, but the pattern held.

She brushed the soil near the roots. Beneath her glove, it felt warmer than it should – and felt heat from below, faint but constant.

“Old systems,” she murmured. “This place really was built to last.”

The thought carried a quiet awe.

After centuries of silence, something beneath her feet still pulsed, still fed the fragments of a world that refused to die.

Elynara stood, scanning the basin. In a few places, the air shimmered slightly in mana sight, the floating mana currents crossing there.

She moved toward the faint distortion at the center. Her staff’s tip brushed the ground there, and the shimmer deepened into a ripple.

The vines nearby shifted. Slowly, carefully, their leaves turned toward her light, drawn to it.

She froze, pulse skipping once.

“Responsive flora,” she breathed. “But they shouldn’t have enough energy to…”

She reached out again. The nearest vine quivered, following the motion of her hand. Its surface shimmered faintly, a greenish glow sparking along the stem before fading.

Starpuff fluttered closer, curiosity overpowering caution.

Elynara glanced at it, lowering her hand.

“Don’t even think about it.”

The puff tilted in protest.

She sighed.

“Yes, I know you’re curious. So am I. That’s the problem.”

Still, the warmth beneath her boots tugged at her attention. The soil here wasn’t ordinary. It sat over something artificial – she could feel the faint hum of structured mana under the surface.

“An old garden system,” she said softly, half to herself. “Or part of a reactor? Maybe both.”

Whatever it was, it had adapted. When the ruin fell silent, when the mana thinned, it had shifted from power to preservation, feeding only what it could sustain.

Yet that was still more than enough for those plants, and for centuries they had lived, even if not thrived.

The realization brought a quiet smile to her face.

“Guess you’re stubborn,” she murmured.

She crouched again, brushing her fingers gently across one of the glowing leaves. The surface was smooth, the veins faintly luminous. The leaf trembled under her touch and stilled again.

The puff landed lightly on her shoulder, humming approval.

Elynara’s smile widened.

“Fine. You can touch one. Gently.”

It zipped forward and nudged a vine. The glow brightened for a moment, spilling pale light across the floor. Both of them froze, waiting for a reaction that never came.

When the glow faded again, Elynara exhaled and laughed softly.

“See? Still friendly.”

The sound of her laughter filled the small space, carried upward through the cracks in the ceiling. The ruin answered with its usual stillness, but the plants seemed to move slightly with the air, their small motions steady and content.

She stepped back and looked around again, taking in the scope of it.

The garden – or whatever it had become – covered almost the entire floor of the basin. Between the patches of growth, the panels of old metal reflected the faint light, giving the illusion of a lake.

“This might have been a sanctuary,” she said. “Or a place to rest. Maybe both.”

It made sense, she supposed, to make a space for humans to enjoy among all that metal.

And that an advanced Magitech civilization would have figured out ways to upkeep one without too much supervision.

The puff hummed in agreement, still drifting near the vines.

Elynara turned her gaze upward. Through the broken ceiling, she could see a patch of sky – washed pale, but still beautiful. The beam of sunlight falling into the basin hit her hands, and she felt the warmth spread through her cloak.

For the first time since entering the ruins, the air no longer felt heavy. It felt balanced.

She crouched once more, pressing her palm flat to the soil. She felt the faint mana flowing deep underneath, and smiled.

“You’ve done well.”

The puff landed beside her hand, stilling for just a moment. Together they watched the light catch on the leaves.

For a long moment, Elynara let herself breathe in the silence – the soft hum of living systems, the warmth of an ancient machine that had refused to die.

When she finally rose, the smile remained. “Whatever kept you alive this long, I’ll learn from it,” she said. “That’s a promise.”

The puff trilled once, approving.

Elynara lifted her staff and turned to move deeper in, the garden space still mostly unexplored.

Behind her, the faint glow of the vines shimmered in reply, the pattern shifting just enough to resemble a wave.

She paused, grinning.

“Goodbye to you too.”

Then she walked on, light steady, steps quiet, the air still humming faintly with life.

Elynara noticed the figure before she understood what she was seeing.

At first it looked like another piece of wreckage – another remnant of the ruin caught between collapse and overgrowth. The silhouette leaned against the base of a half-buried structure, its shape sharp against the curve of the broken hill, with a single ancient tree behind it.

She almost walked past it. Then something moved.

A sound rose from the stillness: the dry scrape of metal shifting against itself, followed by a slow mechanical whine. Elynara froze.

The figure turned its head.

Rust cracked and fell in a thin shower of dust. Beneath the corroded plating, dull light pulsed once, then steadied. Two eyes – if they were eyes – glowed faint blue in the shadows of its face.

Elynara’s breath caught.

“That’s… not stone.”

She raised her staff slightly, spreading her stance, though curiosity pulled stronger than fear.

The thing was humanoid in outline but built with inhuman precision. Overlapping plates covered its body, their edges worn smooth from time. The joints were thick, layered in complex shapes that might have once moved with grace. Its right arm ended in a tangle of fused metal, mechanical parts welded together by heat or battle. The left hand rested limply on a fractured knee, palm upward, fingers still curled.

Beside it lay the carcass, previously hidden by the hill.

Whatever it had been, it was huge – six-legged, mostly skeletal now but unmistakably predatory. Its ribcage was crushed inward, the bones blackened where fire had bitten through. The ground beneath it was scarred, the earth fused into glass around the edges.

Elynara crouched without meaning to, her mind cataloging details as if study could steady her pulse.

The machine and the creature had died together. There was no mistaking it.

Elynara’s instincts screamed at her to back away.

The ruin’s silence pressed close again, thick with warning, fear reasserting itself. Every line of her training told her that anything with a pulse of mana – and this machine had one – was dangerous. The faint hum in its chest, the steady flicker of light behind its ribs, were proof enough that it wasn’t dead.

One wrong movement and it could wake. And if it still remembered how to fight, it would crush her before she could lift her staff.

But she didn’t move.

Because something else was here.

The first shimmer reached her vision before she understood what it was. A flicker in the air, faint but deliberate. Her breath caught as the light gathered, folding in on itself until it formed a shape suspended above the machine’s chest.

A spirit.

Its glow was soft but steady, pale silver tinged with warmth. Even Starpuff’s light dulled beside it. The form was clear – taller than her, vaguely human in outline, the details too fluid to settle into anything exact. And not the round fuzzy puff of mana she had expected the spirits to take the shape of, yet unmistakably one still.

And then it moved.

The spirit lifted a hand and waved.

Slowly. Deliberately.

Elynara stared.

“You have got to be kidding me.”

The spirit waved again. This time, the glow around its edges rippled, like laughter caught in light.

Before she could decide whether to run or speak, Starpuff zipped forward in a blur, chirping a delighted trill that echoed through the hollow chamber.

“Wait!” she hissed, reaching out in vain. “Don’t–”

Too late. The little menace was already circling the spirit, spinning in tight, joyful loops. The spirit’s glow brightened in response, its form bending slightly toward the puff as though indulging its antics.

Elynara pressed a palm over her forehead.

“Reckless, glowing–fine! Fine!”

She stepped forward, carefully testing the ground before each stride. The staff stayed angled between her and the motionless machine. Its hum hadn’t changed, steady but low, and the light within its chest remained constant.

The spirit turned toward her approach, its brightness dimming to something gentler. There was no hostility in it – only patience.

By the time she reached the base of the machine, Starpuff had perched near the spirit’s shoulder, trilling small phrases in a tone that sounded suspiciously conversational.

Elynara stopped a few paces away, exhaling slowly. The air here was warmer, alive with faint energy that pulsed between the two entities. The spirit’s glow cast her shadow long across the fractured floor.

“Alright,” she said softly, “let’s see what we’re dealing with.”

The spirit tilted its head, the motion fluid and curious. It didn’t speak, but the air changed. A faint vibration brushed her skin, something halfway between a sound and a memory. The words didn’t form in her ears – they pressed gently into her thoughts.

Hello.

Elynara blinked.

“Oh, that’s new.”

The spirit’s glow pulsed once, and she could swear it smiled.

You can see me. Good.

“I can,” she said, still cautious. “You’re bound to this construct, aren’t you?”

The spirit drifted slightly downward, toward the machine’s chest. The movement was careful, almost protective.

Bound, yes. Not trapped. I chose to stay.

The words came clearer now, calm and measured, carrying neither pride nor regret.

“Why?”

The light dimmed, and the faint hum from the machine deepened. For a moment, both seemed to breathe in rhythm. Then the spirit’s tone softened.

Someone had to remain. It would have been alone.

Elynara’s throat tightened.

“You stayed… to keep it company.”

To remember it, the spirit corrected. To keep its memory intact until someone came who could listen. It is the least I could do for a guardian and a friend.

Her staff lowered slightly.

“And now?”

The light around the spirit brightened again, warm and patient.

Now, perhaps, it can rest.

Starpuff chirped, fluttering up and down in clear excitement. The spirit reached toward it, and for a brief instant, their lights touched – pale silver mingling with soft gold. The air rippled faintly, gentle as a sigh.

Elynara took a slow breath, her earlier tension easing. Whatever this was, it wasn’t dangerous. It was tired.

Her eyes moved to the machine’s chest. Beneath the plates, faint lines of mana pulsed like veins under skin.

“You’re still keeping it alive,” she said.

It keeps itself alive. I only remind it to continue.

The tone held quiet affection, the kind that made her chest ache.

She knelt, resting her hand lightly on the cool metal. The hum thrummed faintly through her palm, rhythmic but fragile.

“It fought hard. I saw the beast outside. It must have been protecting this place.”

The spirit inclined its head.

It protected all that remained. Even after I could no longer help it. And long before I was ever able to.

“And what would you have me do?”

Listen, judge, and help. If you’re able. Remember us if not.

Silence lingered between them, filled by the faint crackle of distant energy. Then Elynara spoke again, her voice low.

“You’ve done well. Both of you.”

The spirit’s glow dimmed further, steadying into something softer, almost peaceful.

You have gentle eyes, traveler. Curious hands. You will carry our story further than we ever could. And perhaps more.

She smiled faintly.

“I don’t know about that, but I’ll try.”

The spirit began to fade. Not vanishing – just easing, its brightness thinning into the air.

Then that is enough. But for now, I must rest.

The hum beneath her hand stilled. The faint light in the machine’s chest flickered once, then settled into quiet rest.

Elynara exhaled slowly, lowering her head.

“Good night, then.”

The air cooled, leaving only the warmth of her own magic and the gentle buzz of Starpuff hovering near her ear.

She looked at the machine at her feet, surveyed the garden around, and considered how tired she already was.

Yes, this was a good point to rest.

And learn what she could of the machine.

Perhaps she would be able to help?

She would very much like that.
