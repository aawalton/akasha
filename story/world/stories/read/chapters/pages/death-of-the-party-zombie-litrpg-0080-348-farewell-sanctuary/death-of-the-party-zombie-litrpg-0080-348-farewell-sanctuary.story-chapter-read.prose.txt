It took another two days to eliminate every last Radoch from the world. While there was a brief back and forth on the edge of the Isles, the main culprit for the duration was those roaches who hid away from their fate. The System put a bounty out for survivors, and once that counter hit zero, the populace celebrated.

Of course, it took almost a full month to resurrect the fallen. Dispatched to high-threat areas, teams sought resurrection scrolls or artefacts to facilitate the process. This gave rise to the first few Adventurers developing an actual ability to bring the dead back to life - and a church known as Sanctuary’s Grace bloomed from these members.

Restructuring and healing the world were still ongoing after three months. Chuck’s power had waned during his war efforts, so much had to be done the old-fashioned way. The Architect didn’t take kindly to certain people finding humor in the fact that he was much like the Observers of the early days of the System. Still, homes were rebuilt, fields and forests replanted, and the wild Seaborn creatures were repopulated.

Sanctuary survived the war. Despite giving up the ability to respawn, in time, the casualties of the brief and deadly attack became zero. The Radochs achieved nothing other than the loss of one of their biggest fleets. Thanks to their arrogance, the world had matured with the STAR+, growing closer to being as functionally real as a planet outside the Sea.

For the Outsiders, it was a grim reminder that despite their power; they were still bound to the rules of the System. It could be exploited against them, just as well as they bent the rules.

Theo led the efforts in finding the most efficient farming spot for resurrection scrolls, and was responsible for a sizable portion of bringing Sanctuary back to life - ironic, given his Aspect.

Ren and Wolf helped get the Forest back in working shape. As one of the areas most devastated by the war, it needed it. Both had a strong connection in their hearts for the woodlands and were pivotal in routing out some of the Radochs who tried hiding out in the many caves and valleys of the area.

After assisting with more pressing tasks, Max led the celebrations across Sanctuary. Experts determined that while his shows were occasionally unorthodox, the increase in morale across the world sped up recovery by a substantial degree. The magician has this report clipping framed on his bedroom wall on the Omen.

Hunter suffered from an undiagnosable malady after redirecting the two powerful divine attacks in short order. After a fortnight of bed rest, he recovered enough to start strength training again. Although he refuses to join the Outsiders, he wishes to tag along for his mission. Several attempts to give him the STAR+ have all failed, as the System seems to just exist within him—until expelled—without affecting him.

Seemingly getting his way (for better or worse), Archie shed some of his latent power and became fused with the moon base and Omen. Similar to the Architect’s tie to Sanctuary, this bond has given the wizard increased control and capabilities with the spaceship - fully committing to their plan of traveling the Sea in search of problems to solve.

For the rest of Sanctuary - the Dangers, the Insiders, the Alchemical Guild, those in the goblin village, the many denizens of Hell, every Adventurer new and old… time moved on. Many learned new abilities in the war, growing stronger. A lot of these were specifically anti-Radoch on some level.

It wasn’t clear if the Radochs knew what had been done to them. Chuck’s sacrifice to provide them with the poisoned STAR+ didn’t have obvious effects, at first. It was near the end of the third month of restructuring that the Architect received a signal from the Sea. The Radochs had unknowingly distributed the trojan System to a smaller world, which had been able to rebuff the aliens.

Now they were becoming a fully fledged world of their own - and had sent word out to Sanctuary directly. A friendly face in the void.

Chuck and Dent’s work never stopped. Expanding the scope of their work, a new group known as Sanctuary’s Light worked on establishing connections to these STAR+ planets. An interplanetary network looked like the possible future.

Which meant more work for the Outsiders.

As for Sally, though? She couldn’t stop smiling. Even when she had to use her zombie army as organic seed planters. Despite Archie making them wear power limiters for their future missions. Through the heartache of seeing the world she loved so ruined by the aliens…

The fight ended in her favor. Sometimes, that was all that mattered. She had torn the brain matter from the antagonist’s head with her own teeth. It was very validating.

So it was no surprise that on this calm, sunny morning, she carried a plate of warm pancakes with a wide grin on her face.

She stepped from the kitchen and into the bare living room to sit beside Theo. All but the essentials had been stripped from the house. They were moving, after all, and the Omen would be their permanent home for the foreseeable future.

The vampire gave her a soft smile before sighing. “End of an era, huh? If these walls could talk…”

“…they’d probably scream.” She gave him a jab with her elbow before digging into her breakfast. “I can’t believe we had to physically drag all our storage items from here to the Omen.”

“The daisy chain of neighborly goblins helped.” He caught a pout developing on her face. “Hey, we can come visit anytime, remember? We’ll not be forgotten.”

“Mfh,” she huffed, mouth full. After wiggling her toes, she gave the vampire another nudge. “You think we’ll meet more oddballs to join our merry group?”

Theo tilted his head, and the sunlight reflected off his round glasses. “Archie says most of our missions won’t need the classic party of five, but I’m not so sure. If he doesn’t count as a participant anymore, we’re short at least one oddball.”

“We need… maybe a mascot. A talking dog?”

“Too cliché.” He rolled his eyes. “I’ll settle for anyone who has earned an Aspect power.”

“Just you watch.” Sally wiggled her fork. “It’ll be some badass blonde or a goofball with messy brown hair. Or worse, both!”

“As long as we can keep doing what makes us happy, together, I don’t mind.”

“Aww. You dork.” Sally wrinkled up her nose and leaned into him. “This is where we time-skip ten years, right? Is it time for a mont- oh!”

She paused and looked up at her messages.

Chuck was requesting the Outsiders presence in the Omen meeting room. Urgently.

Across the vast void of the Sea, a small Radoch fleet sat stationary between travel destinations. The head ship was a sickly ivory white, slimmer and more decorated than most usual Radoch vessels.

A small inhabitant on this ship scurried through well-lit corridors of silver and marble, pushing through into a room that looked less like a control room and more like the interior of a church. He held in his four arms a thick data sheet, pressed to his chest as if it might run away.

Two armored guards beside a wide throne moved their shields to warn him to stay back.

He scowled at them but stayed put. After clicking his mandibles together nervously, he took a cautious bow. “Princess Bobo, I come with news.”

The figure on the throne was a rotund Radoch, as tall as she was wide, decorated with all manner of trophies. Each sparkled like gemstones on her mottled chitinous armor.

“Speak, youngling,” she said. “Have you found anything of interest in the nearby Systems?”

“B-better than that, your highness.”

The small Radoch turned the data slate around, holding it over his head so that the seated leader could see. Dots and lines filled a curved graph. Princess Bobo leaned forward, her eyes widening as the navigator explained plainly.

“We have found a way outside the Sea.”
