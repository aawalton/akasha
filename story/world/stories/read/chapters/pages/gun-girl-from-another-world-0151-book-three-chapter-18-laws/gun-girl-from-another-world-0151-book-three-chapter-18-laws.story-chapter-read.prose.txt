Chapter 18

Laws

Despite the calm, unwavering expression still on her face, Yorin’s grip on my shoulder as she leads me away from the campfire is tight enough to be painful, the universal way of saying, ‘I’m a stiff breeze from bending you over my knee, tanning your hide, and grounding you for the month it’ll take the swelling to go down; don’t be that breeze.’ Still, she goes without saying another word until we’re far enough that the excited chatter back at the fireside is muffled.

“Remmi,” the sacred priestess finally says, her words quiet and tight, “you cannot bring a dungeon monster outside of its dungeon.”

I look back at her in confusion, though. That’s it? What does she mean, I can’t? I obviously just did.

“Why not?” I diplomatically elect to ask instead.

“Because that is not how the rules of existence operate,” she replies. “Dungeon monsters are not truly physical beings. They are masses of arcana. They are manifested and sustained by the dungeon core, and cannot exist separated from it. They would simply collapse and disperse. It is the same reason why more powerful monsters and traps wait deeper within a dungeon, closer to the core. The further away a monster is, the less energy it can receive. Once it crosses the boundary between the dungeon and the outer world, the current is severed entirely.”

She moves her hand between herself and me. “You and I are physical beings. The means by which we operate are contained within us. They must be restocked from time to time, but our operation is not chained to our pantry.” And then she points back to the camp, back to Angel. “A dungeon monster cannot do that. It is powered much as a sustained spell, forever leashed to the core, to its pantry.”

I choose my words carefully. “Clearly, our understanding of the rules of existence is incomplete. The laws of physics have always just been our best guesses that haven’t been disproven yet.” I, too, motion to the bird girl. “She’s right there. We can see her with our own eyes. She is outside of the dungeon, so, obviously, there is a way to accomplish it.”

I cross my arms and shift my weight to one foot. “It came from a Hidden Skill that required negotiating with a dungeon core, so we shouldn’t be surprised that it’s new information we haven’t encountered before. If it’s the how that you’re hung up on, I’m guessing I’m the battery. I provide enough energy for one hour of operation in place of the core, and then she goes back.”

Yorin steps back from me with a deep breath, templing her fingers before her as she begins to struggle to contain herself. “Remmi, these are not matters in which there is room for doubt. They have been established through generations of experience.”

“‘Let it not be said that I do not have more to learn,’” I quote. “Those were your words. Did that change, or is it only true when I don’t have a live bird girl already proving you wrong?”

“You are not experimenting with magnets at your table,” she stresses. “These are fundamental precepts of reality that you are playing with.”

“Yeah,” I confirm with a dramatic bob of my head. “I know. I’m an engineer. That’s my job.”

“You are the Gunslinger Hero,” Yorin replies. “Your job is to shoot the Corruption.”

It’s my turn to take some space to control myself. I rub my cheeks as I do a quick loop away and back to facing her again. “Yorin, I understand that, right now, your role as my friend and your role as Sacred Priestess, Imperial Advisor, and Hero Handler are in conflict. But you once told me not to get lost in what I can do, because the Essence chose me as the Gunslinger Hero for what other people can’t do. It didn’t want another healer because it already had Seina.”

And now, I take a step toward the white-haired elf instead of away. “If you really believe that for even a moment, then you have to accept that how I think is as important a determining factor as what I choose to fight with. I am incapable of thinking in any other manner, but if the System just wanted a sycophant with a gun, I guarantee you that it had plenty of options to choose from. It could have sent you a Marine or Green Beret, elite soldiers that have one job, and they do it the best in the world and without question. But it picked me.”

I put my hand against my chest. “I’m not a soldier. Heck, I’m not even a cop! I solve problems. I look at systems and find problems that need solving. I take situations and determine the systems they need to avoid having problems later. That process is baked into the very way I interact with the world around me. I can no more stop it than you could relax and actually let your ears wiggle.”

She looks a little miffed at that remark, but I motion next in the direction of the Forest Cavern. “You gave me a problem to solve because you couldn’t come up with a solution yourself. Because none of the adventurers you had sent in could come up with one. Maybe you already knew about Angel, maybe you didn’t, but you gave it to me because you knew I would come up with something, even if it was wrong. Because that’s what I do. I. Solve. Problems.”

Finally, I give my holster a slap. “A lot of hubbub is made about this thing, and, yeah, it’s powerful. In fact, it’s even more powerful now than when I first came here and used it to blow apart level thirty golems. But a gun isn’t an identity, no matter what my class may be. If you are to understand anything about my world, make it this: A gun is not a heavenly artifact, a legendary weapon, or a mystical device; it’s just a very specific, very advanced tool. A tool for solving very specific, very stubborn problems, but practically no different from a pair of pliers or a hammer. If you try to make all of me all about my gun, you are butchering my ability to do my job.”

I'm treated to the sight of Yorin’s softening gaze. “You are right in challenging me, Remmi, and I assure you that I hold no intent of sealing away your mind. However, I ask that you understand something in turn: The Heroes were summoned because my world is in danger. Your methods are uniquely yours, but I long to see the threat laid low. I neither know nor share your ways, and in absence of that, I come upon you so often, and find one who seems more interested in playing with my world than saving it. I do not begrudge you your genius, but I confess that I rarely comprehend it. As such, I instead fear the time lost to its indulgence. I know well what I would do, and, in my fear, I grow impatient when I turn about and find you in the other direction.”

Yorin’s humble admission saps my spiciness, too. “I understand, Yorin. Really, I do. It looks like I’m being flippant with the world around me.” I motion back toward Angel. “But this was a side effect of me actually trying to fix things. In fact, I think it’s evidence that we succeeded.”

Impulsively, I lunge forward and grab her hand. “Won’t you come see it with us, Yorin? We’re all so eager to see what happened. Don’t you want to see it, too?”

Yorin almost recoils from my touch, but just as quickly reasserts control over herself. She looks down at my hands, and then, after a moment, smiles back at me as she places her other one over mine. “Yes, you did say that this was intended to help me understand the problem. It seems I brought us terribly off of our intended path.” And she gives my hands a gentle squeeze. “Just, please, Remmi, the next time you intend to casually violate causality, do me the courtesy of a warning?”

I positively beam back. “I’ll do my best to remember. Now, come on! You’re really going to like Angel!”

Ayre doesn't bother to hide his surprise as we come back over to the campsite. “Wow, you're still in one piece,” he marvels.

I blow a raspberry at him and trust the System to translate it for me. “How's our Language Arts student?”

Korrigan bounces like she just learned Christmas is coming early and is bursting to share the news. “She's speaking Imperial Common!”

I turn my wide eyes to the bird girl. “Really? Already?”

Angel nods with no less energy than the oni. “Yeah! With all of that goop you had me drink, I was starting to get it, but after the mists lifted, it just sort of clicked, and everything Ayre and Korrigan had been teaching me made sense!”

“That's wonderful,” I exclaim. “Though, by mists, do you mean the fog that filled the Forest Cavern?”

She bobs her head again, but with a more serious expression this time. “Yes. It filled the cavern right after you all left. I was scared because I didn't know what was going on, but I couldn't run. When the mists hit me, I couldn't even move. And then it all just hung there, and me with it. I couldn't tell you if it was moments or hours. It felt like forever and no time at all! And then it lifted, and everything was different, and then I showed up here!”

I ruffle her feathered hair with my hand, a gesture she leans into. “You were very brave, Angel. Good work. Was it when it lifted that you realized you could speak Imperial Common?”

“No,” she said. “It wasn't until you left me with Miss Ayre and Miss Korrigan and told me to practice. I thought about where our lessons had left off, and realized as I went through the words I had already learned that they were suddenly so much easier!”

Even as I'm smiling and praising her, I'm filing that information away. It's not necessarily evidence that she and the dungeon core share a database. It's possible that the expansion means more processing power, and as it reconstituted the memory files belonging to Angel, the language data was just flipped to on. Bam, all of the linguistic basics she had been told but not yet internalized were just integrated into her language model.

“Excuse me,” Yorin interrupts gently. “Angel, was it?” The girl stiffens and slips partially behind me again, but gives the white-haired elf her attention. “Forgive me, but I must ask. Are you truly a dungeon monster of the Forest Cavern?”

The girl nods, but doesn't come out from behind me. “I am. You don't want to kill me, do you?”

That question surprises me. Until this moment, Angel had never seemed to start with the assumption that anyone meant her harm. She was happy to see us until her surprising us while we were tense had us lining all of our weapons up on her, and then she was very forgiving of it when we apologized. My gaze goes to Yorin to gauge her reaction to this, but she is as calm as always, already having put on her warm personality that she uses when I'm not … well … Remmi'ing all over everything.

“No, dear, I do not believe that I do,” she replies kindly. “But, please, tell me: As a dungeon monster, what is your opinion of Remmi and her friends?”

Angel thinks about that for a moment. “Well, I think they’re really nice? But I don’t know what that has to do with me being from a dungeon. They’re just nice. I think anyone would think so.”

“Yes, I suspect you are correct,” Yorin agrees. “And what do you think of Dabun Village?”

“That’s the place near here, right?” Recalling that takes the bird girl a little longer. “I think I’d like to see it sometime, since I can with Miss Remmi’s help. But I’m looking forward to helping them, too!”

That makes the priestess cock her head. “Help them? How?”

But the harpy can only shrug in answer to that one. “I don’t know. Remmi said she had some ideas. It’s why she wanted me to learn Imperial Common!”

“Did she, now?” Yorin’s eyes go back to me, but I resist the urge to switch places and try to hide behind a preteen girl.

Instead, I put on a bright smile. “Well, we’ll find out how well my ideas panned out soon enough. For now, we all still need to eat and pack up camp! There’s no sense worrying about it before then!”
