Ah. Suddenly, a lot of things made a great deal more sense.

Revenge.

That…made a lot of sense as a driving motivator. I could even attest to just how much it could drive you. There had come a point where the only thing I thought about was what I could do to make sure that Magnus died at my hands. Luckily for me, at the time I’d had more than enough opportunity to make sure that actually happened. Considering what I knew about the personal history of the Great Spirits, I could even take a guess who they would be trying to get revenge for.

“I see,” I said evenly, allowing my eyes to drift for a moment across the pens of still frozen slaves. I felt another pang of sympathy for them. Their situation was bad enough, with how the Korvoks were draining them of their Aether even now. But to be essentially locked in your own body by some mysterious entity?

Hell on Vereden.

Still, that wasn’t the reason I was searching through the crowds. There were some very personal prisoners that the Korvoks had taken, and I wanted to know just where they were. Presumably, they would be down here in the pens with the rest of them. My brief glance didn’t reveal much of anything, however, and I didn’t dare keep my attention away from Neris for long.

The Great Spirit struck me as a very proud existence, always a feather's step away from unspeakable violence.

But if I was going to untangle this knot, I was going to have to prod her a bit.

“And who are the targets of your ire, my Lady?” I asked carefully.

“Hmm…well,” Neris said idly, drifting away from where she had been petting Fade. Said Spirit Wolf did his best to hide the sag of relief he felt at the loss of her touch, but I don’t think Neris either noticed or cared. “There are two, in truth. Two fools who committed affronts that are unforgivable. The first, as you might expect, is that rotten mad Elf sulking in the ruins of the civilization he himself brought to his knees. Long have I desired to see him crushed, only to have that possibility be…out of reach, shall we say. It was a near stroke of serendipity when that one Dwarf approached me. Oh, what was his name again…?”

As Neris’s avatar cupped her chin in thought, my eyebrow rose. “Would it be Anguis, my Lady? Are you saying that he approached you? And, perhaps, Lord Orus as well?”

“Ah! Yes, that was the name,” Neris laughed lightly. “Anguis. The fool introduced himself as ‘The Serpent’. What delightful irony that was. Yes, he approached me. In fact, that was before your little confrontation with the Calamity. It was he who roused me from my long slumber, and I did the same to dear Orus. He did not expect the personal attention of two of the Great Spirits, but he was accepting all the same. I’ve not spoken to him in months, however. With his mutilation of Fynneas’s-”

There was a literal ocean of spite packed behind that hissed name.

“-Mantle, our business was concluded,” Neris continued, calming down.

“And…where is Lord Orus?” I asked carefully. “As this is also his Nexus, I would have expected his presence as well.”

Neris literally waved my words off with one flapping hand. “Oh, brother dear has already fallen back into his deep sleep. He has little care for either the material world or the Spiritual, these days. All he wants is to sleep the eons away, dreaming of better days.” A sudden moroseness rolled over the figure of a woman wrought from black water. She stared off into the distance unseeingly for a moment, and I genuinely wasn’t sure if the Great Spirit’s actual spirit was present anymore to animate her form.

Fade and I exchanged uneasy glances as the silence stretched on for several minutes. Eventually, I cleared my throat delicately, trying to catch her attention. “And the other object of your ire, my lady?”

Neris snapped back to awareness, turning to blink at us for a moment. “Ah? Oh, yes. That. Well, you’ve already met him, I suspect. He’s currently hiding behind the Aetherial skirts of a long-dead god and his throne, up that very corridor.” She waved a dismissive, disdainful hand at the corridor that the still silent Ul-Korveth had led me and my retinue down.

I blinked rapidly as I made the connection. “Nehushtan?” I asked incredulously, only to tense immediately afterwards. Neris had already reacted badly once to the utterance of one name; there was no telling if she wouldn’t again. Still, no surge in my veins happened, so I continued. “Nehushtan is one of your targets? I…almost thought that everyone on Vereden was one of them, to be honest, with how devastating the current phenomenon is.”

That startled a laugh out of the Great Spirit. “Everyone on Vereden? Not even my spite runs that deep, Precursor. I simply have no care for the masses of mortals that call the physical home. Yes, I understand that a great many of your fellows are likely to perish during that Anguis fellow’s grasp for the Mantle, but what does it matter? He assured me this wouldn’t lead to their total extinction. You’ll all breed yourself back up to your previous population numbers in a few centuries, anyway. A small price to pay to settle a debt that has gone unpaid for millennia.”

I tried to hide the fury her utter callousness inspired in me, but I wasn’t sure how successful I was.

That was…something I should have expected, honestly. It was a monstrous thing for Anguis to try and work towards, because he was mortal. He lived in the physical realm with the rest of us. He knew full well just how monumentally bad life was going to get on Vereden, even if he succeeded in stealing Fynneas’s Mantle of Divinity. But for as much as I despised the slaving Dwarf, he was at least striving for a greater purpose. In his arrogance, Anguis truly believed that he was the best person to shepherd Vereden through what he saw as the inevitable collapse of the System.

Neris was just fulfilling a personal grudge, and she didn’t care how many millions of people had to die to see that through. I couldn’t even imagine that level of selfish callousness. The entire planet and everyone on it had been brought to its knees because of it.

I let out a long, slow breath, just to keep myself from exploding at the avatar of said Great Spirit.

Alright. I needed just a bit more information, and then…

Then I was going to see what I could do about that.

“What, exactly, did both Nehushtan and the…other person do to cause such hatred within you, Lady Neris?” I asked, as evenly as I could. Still, I’m not sure I was able to keep all of the disdain I suddenly felt for her from my voice, judging by the raised eye-ridge upon the swirling black surface of the avatar’s face.

I don’t think she cared all that much, though. Instead, she was suddenly very animated in her ire.

“They killed him,” Neris said agitatedly, pacing in front of me in an oddly human gesture. “Before your ancestor’s ancestors were even born, those maggots STOLE HIM FROM US! FROM ME!”

Ah.

“You…” I said slowly. “You’re talking about Vaelis, aren’t y-”

I didn’t even get the chance to finish my sentence. Suddenly, I felt the blood within my throat constrict, cutting off both my airway and my words, not unlike a hand that had suddenly clamped strongly around the breadth of my neck. A strangled, gasping noise escaped me, and my hands shot up to grasp uselessly at me. I think the only reason I didn’t start to black out was because of my Status. It wasn’t much, but with a high enough Vitality, you could hold your breath for longer than expected, because the body simply required less to function.

Still, a creeping greyness began to appear on the edges of my vision.

At the same time…

Neris suddenly appeared in my field of vision, lifting my unconsciously tilting head to stare straight into my eyes from only inches away. She didn’t care a whit about Fade trying to savage her liquid leg, as the furious Spirit Wolf tried to save me from her grasp. “You…” She breathed. “Where did you learn that name, Precursor? It could not have been Anima. My sister still mourns him as much as the rest of us do. None of the Great Spirits even speak of our lost brother to each other, much less one of you fragile, worthless little meat puppets. Speak now, else every drop of your disgusting blood shall boil and erupt from within you.”

The pressure on my throat eased enough that I could breathe again, and I almost immediately started coughing. But a strange heat I felt from within my body almost sent me into an animal panic.

She wasn’t bluffing, was she?

“H-Hermus!” I wheezed out, in between coughs. “I learned of him from Hermus!”

Both the terrifying heat inside of me and the lingering sensation of pressure around my throat vanished. I nearly fell to my knees from the relief of it, only keeping my feet under me because of Fade suddenly appearing at my side to lean against. It took me several seconds to regain my composure, and when I did, it was to find Neris staring at me with an odd, indecipherable look on her avatar’s face from several feet away.

“He…still lingers?”

Eyeing the unstable Spirit warily, I nodded slowly. “For now.”

Surprisingly, Ul-Korveth finally spoke up, taking a step forward, both literally and figuratively, into the conversation. “My Lady, who is the Precursor speaking of? Who is this ‘Herm-”

Without even looking at the Korvok, Neris waved her hand, and I was almost vindictively glad to see him stumble over his words in the same way I had. He gasped and clutched at his skinned throat, slumping to his knees in a way I had managed to avoid.

“To think that after all this time, Death had persisted to even now,” Neris whispered to herself, slowly shaking her head. A slow, ugly sneer rolled over her face then, and the blackened waters of her avatar started to roil at the fury I could sense within her. “Why him? Why Hermus, and not my Vaelis? Why did he have to linger on in some form, when the Lord of the Skies was so thoroughly scattered by Fynneas as that serpentine traitor? You asked why I despise them both so thoroughly, Precursor?” She chuckled bitterly. “I’m sure that both Life and Death have filled your ears about the ancient conflict between the gods and the Spirits. But did they tell you how it ended?”

I blinked slowly.

No, I was just realizing. I had heard about it several times, from both sources that Neris was alluding to. I had just assumed that the Spirits had lost, and I still believed that. But now, I’m guessing that it had to do with the death of…

“Vaelis…” Neris said longingly, staring off into the distance. “In those days, Fynneas bore the command of that abominable relic, moldering in its bay beneath this mountain. We were winning, before that damnable Netherim fool discovered a previously hidden feature of the ship. In an unspeakable violation, it could transfer itself across dimensions from the physical and into the spiritual.”

My eyes widened as I realized what she was talking about. “The Vereden can cross into the Concord? Physically?”

Neris focused back on me and nodded. “Yes. Simply doing so causes immense damage to he fabric of our home, however. Even now, millennia later, the zone where it did so is stained and damaged by physicality to the point we call it the Great Scar. But Fynneas had a target. He aimed the weapons of that abomination at the heart of the nearest Great Spirit to where he had crossed into and fired. With that blast, the weapons of your ‘Vereden’ slew the Lord of Air. You cannot imagine the terror this wrought within all of Spiritkind, Precursor. He threatened to do so again and again and again, until the Great Spirits were all gone, and my people left rudderless. We…surrendered, and as part of surrender, both I and Orus were ordered to a great sleep. My brother was weary and heartsick from our loss, and came to enjoy his sleep. I…” She took a step back towards me. “Did not. I plotted and schemed and dreamed about the day I could finally see justice done against those who had robbed us of the Sky himself. Both Fynneas and Nehushtan, he who pretended to be one of us, and yet acquiesced when it came time for murder most grave. So you see, Precursor…I do not care how many little mayflies need to die, so that I can have my vengeance. The lot of you could perish in the process and it. Would. Be. Worth it.”

I nodded slowly at her words, as I started to understand the depths of Neris’s madness.

She had to die.
