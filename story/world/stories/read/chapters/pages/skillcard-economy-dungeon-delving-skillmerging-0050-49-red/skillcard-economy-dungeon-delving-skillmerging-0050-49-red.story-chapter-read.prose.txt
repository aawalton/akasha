Toby took a step forward.

Palik took a step back.

They circled each other, trying to find an opening. Toby took a quick step forward, slamming his foot into the ground and making a roaring sound. Palik raised his arm, shiv in hand, getting into a stance, ready to defend against Toby’s charge.

But he never came.

Toby kept standing where he was, grinning. He liked how Palik had flinched just from the sound of his foot.

That angered Palik. He roared and rushed at Toby himself. He wasn’t particularly fast, just as fast as a well-trained human. He knew that stabbing Toby somewhere that wasn’t immediately fatal wouldn’t do much, so he went for vital points instead. His heart. His neck. His head. Places where a single stab would kill him. Places where he would bleed enough to die before he could heal himself.

Toby tried blocking. Sometimes he succeeded. Sometimes he didn’t. Palik’s straight jabs changed direction in the air as if his arms were snakes. They wrapped around and stabbed Toby.

Toby grabbed his arm, trying to lock Palik in place. He turned into paper, and his shiv turned with him. He flew past and stabbed again.

His plan had changed. If he couldn’t stab Toby in the vital spots, he would just stab him in enough places that Toby would bleed out before he could heal himself.

I waited.

Toby was getting stabbed, and it hurt to watch, but I had to wait for the perfect moment. The moment he would turn into paper, be close to the ground, and be at just the right angle for me to come out of the shadows and pin him to the ground.

Now.

I emerged in front of Palik.

Before he could register what was happening, his vision went dark as Toby’s coat covered him completely. I pinned the coat, and with it, Palik, to the ground. I covered the coat from all sides with my body, giving him no space to slip by.

He turned back into human form and tried using his strength to wrestle his way out.

That was a mistake.

Toby’s fist crashed into his face. For a second, he went limp, until he started struggling again, spewing curses. Toby punched him again. And again. And kept going.

“Stop!” I had to shout to get his attention. “He will die.”

Toby stood back up straight and stumbled a step. He looked at me, then back at Palik.

“R-remove the coat.”

I did, revealing Palik’s face. His nose was broken, and a tooth had fallen out. His ugly face had grown uglier.

“I don’t think he will be getting up now.”

“Tie him up,” Toby said.

I moved off Palik. The instant I did, he lunged straight up stabbing his shiv into Toby’s neck. He spouted something, but it was incomprehensible through his broken teeth. Then he turned around and ran towards the door.

“Toby!” I caught him before he fell to the ground. “Shit, shit, shit.”

“Don’t... khuughh.” He tried speaking and coughed blood.

“Don’t speak.”

I grabbed a small vial from my waist and poured its contents onto his wound. It glowed golden. The wound started healing, but it didn’t close.

“Fuck. If it was the real thing, this would have healed in a second.”

I would now have to believe in Toby’s regeneration skill.

“I... will... live." Toby managed to speak. “Go... after... him... no... time.”

The five minutes Cerise had given us were almost up. Ten seconds left. Palik was running towards the door. The cameras would come back alive, see him, and the plan would fail. Toby and I might manage to escape, but what about Cerise?

“Don’t you die.” I put Toby on the floor, channeled my Magicka, and focused on Shadowstride. I emerged from Palik’s shadow. Grabbing him by the collar, I tossed him back into the laundry room just before the camera came back alive.

Palik turned into paper, lost his momentum, turned back into solid form, and came to a stop.

He glared at me.

I hopped to his left and punched him in the face.

He should have been able to dodge that using his increased perceptiveness, but he couldn’t because his left eye was swollen. He couldn’t see through it.

He used his flexibility skill on his leg and used it like a whip, striking me in the belly. That seriously hurt.

I grabbed his leg. He turned into paper and escaped.

I need to do something about it.

I searched around for something to use. Washing machine. Detergent bottle. Undergarments. Broken pipe. Water leaking out.

Found it.

Grabbing the broken lid of the washing machine, I hurled it at Palik. He turned into paper and got wrapped around it.

Palik turning into paper was a huge problem because he didn’t seem to take any damage while in that form. Toby had tried punching him hard enough to rupture him, but nothing happened to the paper.

The lid crashed into the wall, and Palik slid out.

Unscathed.

I used Shadowstride to cover the distance in an instant, grabbed the pipe, and forced it out of the wall. Water—a lot of water—came gushing out and bathed Palik and me.

Wet paper is always easier to punch.

“Phucker,” Palik shouted through his broken teeth.

I went for Palik with all my strength. Punching him. He used his flexibility skill to dodge one strike after another. But he couldn’t dodge them all.

I was fast and strong. Palik could either use his perceptiveness skill to see me coming or his flexibility skill to dodge those attacks. He couldn’t do both at the same time.

“Why aren’t you turning into paper now?”

Palik shouted, his blood and saliva flying through the air.

A fist to the face. A kick to the side. Palik was on the ground.

I sat on top of him and bound his hands using his own coat. He did not try to turn into paper because he knew if he did and tried to struggle his way out, he would get ripped apart.

I reached for my holster, but there was no gun there. It had fallen somewhere during the fight. I sensed someone walking towards me.

I looked up and saw Toby. One hand pressed against his wound other held his gun.

“Just shoot him and put him to sleep,” I said.

Toby raised his gun and shot.

There was red.

A pool of red formed beneath Palik, his blood gushing out of the bullet hole in his head.

“Why? Why did you kill him?” I glared at Toby in shock.

“He would have come for us again.” He answered calmly.

“We could have tied him up.”

“For what? For him to turn into paper and escape again?”

“We could have put him to sleep.”

“Come on, Clark. Do you really think you could do this without getting any blood on your hands? Do you think we will only be fighting the Rift monsters? You know, if we were to successfully get out of here and then actually close any Rift, how long do you think we will be able to keep it that way before we are found out and all the top guilds and corporations are on our tails for shutting down their gold mine?”

Toby stared at me.

“What do you think, Clark? Will you be able to survive without getting your hands red?”

“B-but…”

“It will be better the faster you come to terms with it. If you want to do good for the world, you will have to get your hands dirty.”

I couldn’t argue with Toby.

My vision was red from Palik’s blood.

I puked.
