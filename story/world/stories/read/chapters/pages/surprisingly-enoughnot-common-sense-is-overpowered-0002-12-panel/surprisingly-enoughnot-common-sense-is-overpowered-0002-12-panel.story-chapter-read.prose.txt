"...Sixty-six."

Ning slowly pushed himself up from the floor, completing another push-up before sitting back on his heels. He reached for a nearby bucket of water and took several long gulps, wiping the sweat from his forehead with the back of his hand.

Over the past week, he had devoted himself to adjusting to his new life and understanding the world he now inhabited.

The first day had felt so unreal that Ning had mostly taken things as they came. It was only as the days passed that the reality of his situation began to properly sink in.

Part of that time had been spent coming to terms with what being here actually meant: the loss of his old life and the people he'd known, along with the increasingly obvious fact that he probably wasn't going back anytime soon.

So Ning had kept himself busy figuring out his new life. Maybe that was partly his way of coping with everything, but there were worse ways to deal with it.

And there was certainly plenty to figure out.

Although Ji Ning's memories were remarkably complete, he still had to make sense of them.

So he had begun testing everything he could.

He started with his body, which was significantly stronger than the one he'd possessed in his previous life. Every movement carried a sense of effortless power, and his endurance was far beyond anything he remembered possessing before.

Whether this was simply what an ordinary person in a cultivation world was capable of, or the result of Ji Ning's years of martial arts training, Ning couldn't say for certain.

The original Ji Ning had trained diligently under his father ever since childhood, so that certainly had something to do with it.

Whatever the reason, one thing was clear. By the standards of his previous world, this body was already in what Ning would have considered peak human condition.

Of course, the most important discovery he'd made over the past week wasn't his strengthened body.

It was the panel.

Ning closed his eyes and whispered inwardly, "Status."

[Name: Ji Ning.

Spiritual Root: Mid-grade Spiritual Root.

Attributes: Five Elemental Attributes.

Talent: Extreme Space-Time Sensitivity.

Cultivation: Qi Drawing (Consolidating).

Cultivation Technique: Pure Qi Sutra.

Martial Arts: Bone Forging Fist (mortal), Shadow Steps (mortal).]

Ning let out a deep sigh as he studied the translucent panel floating before him.

Having such a detailed overview of himself was certainly useful, but unfortunately, it wasn't nearly as exciting as he had first imagined.

He had discovered the panel almost by accident. With transmigration in play, Ning had jokingly tried all the classics: "System," "Panel," "Properties," and finally, "Status."

He hadn't actually expected any of them to work.

So when a translucent panel suddenly appeared in front of him, Ning had naturally been ecstatic. After all, every transmigrator knew the universal truth: give someone a proper system, and even a pig could eventually ascend to the heavens.

Unfortunately, no matter what Ning tried, the panel remained unchanged. There were no quests or shops, and most tragically, no convenient + button to dump points into his cultivation. All it seemed capable of doing was displaying information about himself and his abilities.

Needless to say, Ning had considered the possibility that he simply hadn't figured out how to use it properly. Perhaps there was some hidden activation condition or a function that would only become available later.

The strange part was that, despite all those possibilities, Ning somehow knew there was nothing else to discover. He couldn't explain where that certainty came from, but whenever he considered the panel, the answer simply felt obvious.

This was all it could do.

And so, Ning's brief dream of hiding inside a cave for a few years, dumping countless free points into his cultivation, and emerging as an invincible immortal had died almost as quickly as it had begun.

Stupid reality. Refusing to bend to my wishes yet again.

Naturally, that was mostly just theatrics. A proper system would've been incredible, but the ones he'd read about in novels were practically reality-warping treasures.

Why would something like that fall into his lap for free?

One thing Ning had learned long ago was that free things usually cost the most. Compared to that, being alive was already a blessing. So, yeah, he was quite chill about it in the end.

Besides, there were still plenty of things on the status panel worth being happy about.

The first thing that caught his attention was his spiritual root. During the sect recruitment, the elders had used a ball-like device to assess his aptitude, but the panel could display the same information without requiring any equipment.

The same was true of his elemental attributes.

Five Elemental Attributes, as the name suggested, meant that Ning possessed affinities with all five elements: Fire, Water, Wood, Metal, and Earth.

The elder who tested him had even lamented that, had Ning possessed a high-grade spiritual root, he would have been considered a great talent.

The reason was simple. While anyone could technically attempt to cultivate techniques outside their natural attributes, doing so was far more difficult and usually produced inferior results. A fire-attributed cultivator trying to cultivate a water technique, for example, would be making things unnecessarily difficult for themselves.

Ning didn't have that problem.

He could choose from all five elements.

It was a widely acknowledged principle that a cultivator's elemental affinity shaped their chosen path, while their aptitude determined how far they could progress along it.

And finally, there was his talent: Extreme Space-Time Sensitivity.

Unlike his spiritual root and elemental attributes, this talent hadn't appeared during the sect's assessment. However, since the panel had accurately displayed the other two, Ning was inclined to trust it on this matter as well.

In fact, he had his own theory about the origin of this extraordinary talent.

He believed it was connected to his transmigration.

More specifically, he suspected that the act of crossing from one world to another might have altered his soul, leaving behind this strange aptitude.

"...But that's just a theory," Ning muttered. "A cultivation theory."

Being trapped inside a cave, no matter how spacious it was, naturally gave Ning plenty of time to think about his situation. He'd spent quite a bit of the past week theory-maxing, partly to keep his mind occupied.

Still, it wasn't all bad. Besides experimenting with his own body and techniques, cultivation itself was surprisingly enjoyable.

Ning sat down cross-legged and turned his awareness inward.

Even after seven days, the sensation remained strange.

He could clearly perceive countless tiny wisps of qi gathered inside his dantian. It was as though an entirely new sense had awakened. In his previous life, he would never have been capable of feeling something so intangible, yet now it seemed almost as natural as breathing.

The Qi Drawing stage was exactly what its name implied.

One continuously drew wisps of qi from heaven and earth, allowing them to slowly nourish the meridians while accumulating within the dantian. It was a gradual process.

Only when enough qi had accumulated to sustain a complete circulation through every meridian could one be said to have reached the First Stage of Qi Condensation.

"Still..." Ning murmured. "It's weird that I somehow know where the qi is supposed to go."

He possessed the original Ji Ning's memories of the meridians, but actually guiding qi through them felt almost instinctive. He somehow knew which pathways to follow and where the flow should slow or accelerate, as though he'd been doing it for years.

It was strange, but after seven days, that strangeness had gradually become familiar. Humans were extremely adaptable creatures, after all.

Ning slowly let out a breath. "Today's the day."

Closing his eyes once more, he guided the accumulated qi out of his dantian.

The stream of qi flowed steadily along the circulation route described in the Pure Qi Sutra.

One breath.

Two breaths.

Three...

The qi passed through one meridian after another without faltering.

It crossed the final pathway and returned to his dantian, completing a full circulation for the first time.

A familiar panel appeared before his eyes.

[Cultivation: First Stage of Qi Condensation (Consolidated)]

At that very moment, a deep rumble echoed through the cave as the stone doors slowly slid open. Brilliant sunlight streamed through the widening gap, washing over Ning's face and filling the cave with warmth.

"I am free!" Ning declared, raising a fist in triumph.

Seeing the sunlight before him, there was only one thing he could possibly say.

"Praise the sun."
