Olethros shifted back and forth between a pulsing shadow and a melting mass of bubbling, dull grey steel.

“Nora, secure Vernie!” I ordered, dropping to my knees before him. “Tetora, help her. Now!”

As they stomped across the wooden floor, I grasped at the dark jewel embedded in his molten forehead. A sharp, burning sensation shot up my arm, but my pink aura flared and pushed it back.

I know you’re still in there somewhere...!

No one answered, but I kept the call going while I searched for him. I felt a vast, empty space, one devoid of his dry amusement and careful restraint.

I need to go further…

Discarding the idea of looking for his positive thoughts, I let myself fall.

Fear.

Fear of losing control, going berserk… hurting me… and becoming his father.

Hmm…. Been here before.

“I see you,” I said as steadily as I could, tightening my grip on the jewel. “But I know you’re feeling way more than just that. I need to trace all of it further to find you. All of you. No judgment, okay?”

Anger pulsed coldly at being struck without warning by an enemy who chose efficiency over honor.

Contempt turned inward, for misjudging the situation.

Guilt sizzled along his edges for recognizing, only too late, that Relias was once again the catalyst of his suffering.

Many more dark strands surfaced and shimmered, their sparking ends swaying and fraying with unmitigated rage.

Aha! Rage!

“There!” I shouted, knowing I had found all of him at that very moment.

I didn’t shatter the jewel because it had worked itself so deeply into his core. Instead, I dissolved it slowly but surely, coaxing his spirit back into the spaces it had taken over. Dark waves washed over his hanging strings of emotion, sealing their ends and weaving them back together.

“I will make him pay for this!” he growled from somewhere beyond.

“Yes!” I agreed fiercely, urging his anger to pull him back together. “Together!”

Surviving out of spite is still surviving.

And I was serious about the together part.

Purple shadow and gold sparkles burst from between my fingertips, and I felt the jewel melt away. I let his core reconnect itself, the restored threads pulsing with life.

When I opened my eyes, he was with us again, splayed out on the cabin floor.

“Third time connecting with your inner self’s a charm,” I murmured, reaching over to pat him on his head. “But if something like this happens again, I’m going to start charging extra, partner.”

With a burning but unfocused gaze, he stared, holding my hand to his crown with both hands.

“You’re taking the rest of the day off, okay? Please rest. I got this.”

With a low yet somewhat accepting snarl, he dissolved into smoke, the slow vapor pouring into the serpentine mark on my sword hilt.

I pocketed the remnants of the jewel’s housing for later study. Then, with a deep breath, I stood and turned, my eyes falling on Vernie.

She laughed weakly from the ground. Lengths of knotty rope covered her from shoulders to ankles. Her pockets had been meticulously emptied, and all the contraband had been tied up in Nora’s knapsack. “Demons caring about demons? Pssh. Absurd.”

Three strides were all I needed to reach her. I hauled her to her feet, grabbing her by a makeshift handle of rope at her front. “What the hell were you thinking when Relias offered you that damn thing?!”

She spat in my face.

So I grabbed her ear and twisted—hard. “I will have the truth from you!”

“Rachel!” Tetora yelled, inserting himself. “Enough!”

“Not enough!” I countered, though I did release her ear to wipe my face. “I want to know the exact moment she became an idiot!” I grabbed Vernie again and shook her several times. “Did you start drinking again before or after Relias tricked you?!”

She started to blink. “Tricked…? Me? No, never… But that thing should’ve been here by now—”

An explosion came from above, ripping across the cabin’s roof.

Nora’s barrier caught the brunt of the timbers collapsing around us. With a swing of her staff, she popped it, using its energy to hurl the rubble outward and away. When the dust cleared, she stood there.

“Bodhil.”

The metal angel’s blast was aimed at Vernie. I pushed her to the ground even as Nora blocked the attack with another barrier.

Tetora and I surged forward together. He reached her first, his claws carving deep, ugly rents into her silver plating.

I struck from the opposite side, Faith swinging cleanly through her guard and forcing her to abandon a second shot at Vernie. I pulled back my shield, noting the small dent it left in her.

We can both do damage now!

Vernie screamed. “She’s trying to kill me?!”

“We know,” I heard Nora yell back from behind.

Bodhil’s dark jewel flashed, and she twisted sharply, her wings thrusting forward to form a phalanx of interlocking steel feathers.

I drove straight into her winged wall anyway, hacking through the outer layer. Feathers sheared off and dissolved into black-red vapor before they could hit the ground. Tetora tore several more of them free on his side, snarling as sparks trailed from his claws.

Once we opened a sizeable hole, she pulled her remaining plumage back and pivoted on one heel. With an open palm to the chest, she slammed Tetora backward across several feet of gravel. Before I could follow up, her other arm unfolded into a serrated blade, slashing at my throat.

I raised Faith, and she caught it with a loud, hissing screech.

“I liked your fans better,” I grunted, pushing her away. “At least they had flair. Your arm-for-a-sword is just lackluster, you know?”

My attempts to provoke her were understandably fruitless, yet I still found her lack of reaction a bit unsettling.

And how exactly am I supposed to get a clean shot at that hunk of eberorite in her forehead anyway... And why does the thought of actually landing it make my stomach twist?

Tetora did not share any of my qualms. He advanced again and slashed at the opposite side as I tested her defenses. He tore off chunks of metal just as fast as they could reform from the dark essence pooling beneath them.

Bodhil twisted away from him, her wings flaring, before her right one faltered. It buckled with a grating screech, folding at a steep angle behind her.

Tetora lunged for the gap, raking at her head from her right side. His claws skidded across the surface of the ereborite, leaving deep gouges down the length of her faceless head but failing to chip the jewel itself.

Bodhil retaliated, snapping her sword arm out to fend him off with a swipe to her right. He struck at it from the inside, forcing her arm to open wider and pull her guard away from her center.

It was my invitation to strike.

I circled in from that angle, raising Will to bash at her left arm as I got in closer. Bringing Faith across the front of my body allowed me to set up a thrust above the shield’s rim.

“Don’t!”

Beigaldi buzzed in just behind her, stabbing Olethros’s blessed stick into an open wound in her right neck. A viscous, oily substance leaked out as she suddenly stiffened, held in place by its pulsing electric blue aura.

“Please!” he begged as he wrapped his arms around her. “Please! Like you did for His Majesty!”

Fucking hell.

“Gaaah!” I screamed, dropping my sword. I clawed at the jewel in Bodhil’s head, shrieking, “You were right, Beigaldi! I really am a foolish oaf!”

Here I was giving an enemy another damn chance.

This always bites me in the ass!

Bodhil was cold and empty; a chasm with no edge in sight. Darkness everywhere, except a tiny lick of flame far, far down.

I jumped in.

Two ghostly children ran by as I fell.

Farther down, they climbed a tall pine tree.

One of them slipped, screaming—

Bodhil appeared, catching her before she hit the ground.

Someone screamed, “Demon!”

A mob gathered behind an angry priest.

Bodhil’s screaming core was confined in the town square by several burning runes.

“Keep stabbing with the blessed daggers. The holy sage won’t be here for several weeks.”

“Open the stores! We’ll celebrate her exorcism properly.”

The sound of raucous cheers.

The child she saved was purposefully chipping away at her core.

Bodhil’s never-ending shrieks of pain filled the night.

The General’s army gathered nearby on the dawning horizon.

Spite.

Broader, deeper, and older than Olethros’s.

Spite for all of humanity, for punishing her for her first and final act of kindness.

“Well, no wonder you’re always so angry.”

The flame at the bottom of her spiritual chasm burned higher and hotter, but it wasn’t enough.

I hesitated, then doubled down on the idea that she was rage-against-Rae-powered. “Well, what about your core-mate? Tell me, how do you feel about Beigaldi for real?”

Bodhil’s jewel melted a split second before I was blasted backward.

“Fuck you for thinking you deserve an answer!” Bodhil bellowed, oil and metal sliding off her like discarded snakeskin.

I guess love really does come in all weird forms.

“Bodhil!” Beigaldi cried, weeping into her back.

“Let go! Let me go!” she shrieked as she struggled to get away from him, her eye sockets reigniting with flames.

“I’m sorry, love, but…” Beigaldi said, tightening his grip. “Choose slaughter, and the General is forever lost to us.”
