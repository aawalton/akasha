By the time Wei Zhusang reached the spectator stands, the second stage was already well underway.

Zhang Feng noticed him first and raised a hand. "Wei Zhusang, over here."

Wei Zhusang made his way through the crowded row and found Zhang Feng sitting with Lang Rulang, Qiao Mei, and another disciple he didn't recognize. Qiao Mei had been laughing at something a moment earlier, though she straightened slightly when she noticed him and shifted aside to leave him a place to sit.

"Zhang Feng, Lang Rulang, Junior Sister Qiao." Wei Zhusang greeted each of them before taking the offered seat.

"Senior Brother Wei," Qiao Mei replied, smoothing her sleeve as she spoke.

Zhang Feng gestured toward the broad young man beside him. "This is Li Langtian, one of my friends. He's also quite close with Ning and came to cheer him on."

Fatty Li immediately cupped his fists. "Senior Brother Wei. You can just call me Fatty Li."

"Brother Li." Wei Zhusang returned the greeting.

He had only just met the man. Using a nickname like "Fatty Li" so casually when they weren't familiar with each other would have felt rather presumptuous.

"Don't underestimate him just because he's still an outer sect disciple," Zhang Feng added casually. "Fatty Li has a spiritual body, and he's already close to breaking through to the Seventh Stage. At this rate, it probably won't be long before he's joining us in the inner sect."

"Brother Zhang, I'm not there yet." Fatty Li looked slightly embarrassed by the praise.

"You'll get there soon enough."

Wei Zhusang gave Fatty Li another look.

A spiritual body, nearly at the Seventh Stage, and someone Zhang Feng trusted enough to bring along. Yet Wei Zhusang couldn't recall hearing of any notable Li cultivation family nearby, nor had he seen Fatty Li in the Outer Sect Competition.

If the young man had reached this stage without powerful family backing or the competition's rewards, then his talent was worth paying attention to. Even Ning had relied heavily on the opportunities he'd earned along the way.

He was someone worth knowing.

"If you're interested, come find me sometime," Wei Zhusang said with a smile. "The Mutual Aid Association is open to anyone in the sect, regardless of cultivation stage. We're always happy to have another friend."

Fatty Li smiled. "Then I'll remember Senior Brother Wei's words."

Zhang Feng laughed. "I introduced you to Fatty Li, and you're already recruiting him. You really haven't changed."

"What did you expect?" Wei Zhusang asked. "You should have invited him yourself."

"I was planning to eventually."

"Of course you were." Wei Zhusang knew that the thought never even crossed this guy's mind.

Qiao Mei lowered her head slightly to hide her smile, while Lang Rulang merely shook her head at the two of them.

Wei Zhusang then looked toward her, and his eyebrows rose slightly. "I'm more surprised to see Rulang here this early."

Lang Rulang gave him a flat look. "Early?"

"It's already the second stage," Zhang Feng said. "You're the one who's late."

"I had some business at the Weapon Refining Hall."

"Then you missed quite a bit." Zhang Feng immediately perked up. "You should've seen Ning's face during the first stage. He looked so confident when they announced the material-processing test, and then halfway through they made everyone exchange their prepared ingredients. The moment he saw what he'd received..."

Zhang Feng stared blankly ahead with his mouth slightly open, doing an exaggerated imitation of Ning's expression.

Qiao Mei laughed, and even Fatty Li joined in despite having missed the original.

"It wasn't that exaggerated," Lang Rulang said, though the corner of her mouth had already begun to rise.

"It absolutely was. He looked like someone had personally offended his ancestors with those ingredients."

Wei Zhusang chuckled. "Now I regret being late."

"You should. You always seem to miss the interesting parts," Zhang Feng replied, sounding a little annoyed.

After all, cheering for his friends had been quite a difficult task. Lang Rulang didn't even try and Qiao Mei was too nervous to cheer properly. That left him and Fatty Li responsible for making enough noise to support both Ning and Su Fan.

Honestly, did these people have no sense of camaraderie?

Wei Zhusang merely smiled at his complaint before glancing toward Lang Rulang again.

"Still, I never expected you to spend half the day watching an alchemy competition."

Lang Rulang gave a quiet hmph. "Ning and Su Fan are both members of the Mutual Aid Association. Someone should be here."

"How responsible of you."

Lang Rulang shot him a sharp glare. And let it not be said that her reputation as an icy beauty was undeserved. There was a reason Zhang Feng loved teasing her with that nickname.

Wei Zhusang wisely turned his attention back to the competition grounds, while Qiao Mei struggled to suppress another laugh beside him.

He quickly located Ning at Station Seventeen and then found Su Fan farther away. "Speaking of which, how is brother Su Fan doing?"

"Pretty well," Zhang Feng said. "He had better luck than Ning in the first stage. The materials he received after the exchange weren't nearly as miserable, so he handled them smoothly enough and finished fifth."

"Fifth?" Wei Zhusang felt it was a good score, overall.

"Ning was second."

That made Wei Zhusang look at Station Seventeen with renewed interest.

Zhang Feng spent the next few minutes catching him up on everything he had missed.

By the time he finished, Wei Zhusang was looking at the competition grounds with a strange expression.

"So, in the first stage, they had to work with ingredients prepared by someone else, and now they're supposed to develop their own pill formulas while fighting over the same supply of medicinal materials?"

"Pretty much," Zhang Feng confirmed.

Wei Zhusang shook his head. "It seems the elders of the Alchemy Hall aren't much different from those in our Weapon Refining Hall."

"Are the Weapon Refining Hall's examinations also this strange?" Qiao Mei asked curiously.

"Of course they are!" Zhang Feng answered before Wei Zhusang could speak, a grin already spreading across his face. "Speaking of which, remember that assessment where they kept exchanging everyone's tools?"

Wei Zhusang sighed. "I was hoping you'd forgotten about that."

Unfortunately for him, Zhang Feng had an excellent memory when it came to other people's embarrassing moments.

He immediately began recounting an assessment in which the contestants had been required to forge weapons while attendants periodically exchanged their tools with those of other participants. According to the elders, the purpose was to determine whether the disciples truly understood the principles of weapon refining or had simply grown accustomed to using their favorite equipment.

Naturally, the assessment had produced quite a few complaints.

"One senior brother spent half the examination insisting that the hammer he'd been given was defective," Zhang Feng explained, barely containing his laughter. "Eventually, Elder Wu got so annoyed that he walked over, snatched the hammer out of his hands, and used it to finish forging the blade himself."

Fatty Li was already chuckling, but Zhang Feng hadn't reached the best part yet.

"Then Elder Wu handed the hammer back and told him, 'I've found the problem. The hammer works perfectly fine, but the fellow holding it seems to be defective.'"

Fatty Li burst into laughter, and even Lang Rulang couldn't quite suppress the twitch at the corner of her lips.

Wei Zhusang, however, rubbed his forehead. "You always leave out the part where the hammer actually was badly balanced."

"Well, Elder Wu managed to use it just fine."

"That's why he is the elder."

Zhang Feng shrugged, completely unbothered. "Still funny."

"Though, I think I understand why the elders designed the competition this way," Wei Zhusang said thoughtfully. "They're judging the contestants' decisions as well. Which ingredients they prioritize, what they're willing to compete for, when they decide something isn't worth the trouble, and how they adapt when someone else takes what they need."

Qiao Mei followed his gaze toward the competition grounds. "Some contestants have already had to change their plans because of that."

"Exactly. If the elders simply handed everyone a random assortment of ingredients, they wouldn't be able to observe any of those decisions."

Lang Rulang glanced toward the scorched section of the ingredient tables. "I doubt Feng Yue destroying the ingredients was among the decisions they intended to test."

Zhang Feng laughed. "Probably not. But you have to admit, it made the competition much more entertaining."

Before Lang Rulang could respond, Fatty Li suddenly leaned forward.

"Brother Ning's being judged!"

Everyone turned toward Station Seventeen.

One of the elders stood before Ning, examining a pale-golden pill between his fingers. Ning seemed to be explaining something, but his voice couldn't reach the stands.

The elder scraped off a tiny portion of the pill and tasted it.

For a while, nothing happened. Then his stern expression relaxed, and he began staring thoughtfully at the jade bottle in his hand.

Zhang Feng leaned forward. "What's he doing?"

The elder turned to the second judge and said something. The two exchanged a few words before the first elder began gesturing at the bottle, looking as serious as though he were discussing some profound mystery of the Dao.

Fatty Li's eyes widened. "Is Brother Ning's pill really that profound?"

Lang Rulang gave him a strange look. "He's been talking about why the bottle is round for the past half a minute."

"Perhaps he's comprehending something."

Zhang Feng burst out laughing, slapping his thigh, while Wei Zhusang struggled to keep a straight face.

Meanwhile, the elder seemed to realize something was wrong. He hurriedly circulated his cultivation, and his expression gradually returned to normal.

The judging took longer than at the other stations, but the nearby projection formation eventually carried enough of the discussion for the spectators to understand the pill's effects.

Apparently, it produced a pleasant feeling of euphoria and encouraged unusual ideas and associations, though it also had a tendency to send one's thoughts wandering in completely useless directions.

Zhang Feng rubbed his chin. "As expected of Ning, he always comes up with strange things. From what the elder said, it seems he made the formula himself?"

"Seems like it," Qiao Mei said.

"It could actually be useful." Wei Zhusang considered it for a moment. "Someone stuck while developing a technique or trying to approach a problem from a different direction might get something from it."

Fatty Li nodded enthusiastically. "Brother Ning is a genius. You never know what he'll do next."

"That part I agree with," Lang Rulang said. "But you're all conveniently forgetting the mumbling, the tipsiness, and everything else that happened to the elder."

Zhang Feng's laughter slowly faded as he considered that. Then his expression became thoughtful. "Isn't that even better?"

Lang Rulang stared at him.

"Think about it. Maybe I could take one before training and finally comprehend the Drunken Fist."

"You use a spear," Lang Rulang reminded him, already familiar with Zhang Feng's occasional brain farts.

"Drunken Spear, then!" Zhang Feng's eyes lit up as though he had just discovered his true calling.

As expected of Ning! Even without taking the pill himself, Zhang Feng was already coming up with all sorts of brilliant ideas.

Lang Rulang closed her eyes and sighed. "That wasn't the problem."

"I see it now! I've been too rigid all these years, limiting myself to conventional spear techniques. You're right! I need to broaden my horizons and break free from the limitations of traditional martial arts!"

Lang Rulang hadn't said anything of the sort.

Ignoring Zhang Feng, who was already imagining himself founding an entirely new school of spear arts, Wei Zhusang turned his attention back to the judges.

The judges became more interested after learning that Ning had lost a crucial ingredient from his original formula and had been forced to rebuild the medicinal balance using what remained.

The pill's purity was surprisingly good, but the ratio of medicinal properties was a complete mess. The euphoric effect was far stronger than necessary, and the wandering thoughts it induced were unpredictable, making it difficult to say whether the pill would actually help someone.

Naturally, Elder Mo Jian was the only one who disagreed with their criticism. He seemed genuinely convinced that Ning had stumbled upon something worth exploring.

"Looks like Brother Su Fan is doing well too," Zhang Feng remarked, drawing everyone's attention toward another station.

The judges evaluated Su Fan's pill next. Although it attracted less curiosity, the refinement was very good, giving them little to criticize beyond several minor imperfections.

Yue Chan's result was better still. She presented her pills, answered the judges' questions calmly, and waited while they examined a refinement whose quality drew approving nods from nearly everyone on the platform. There was none of the spectacle that had accompanied Ning's judging, and she didn't need it.

By the time the last contestant had been evaluated, speculation had spread throughout the stands, only to quiet when the jade screen above the competition grounds shimmered and the rankings began appearing.

First — Yue Chan.

The favourite to win it all.

Second — He Ming.

Wei Zhusang didn't recognize the name immediately, but he quietly committed it to memory. Anyone capable of placing between Yue Chan and the rest of this field was worth remembering.

The third name appeared.

Third — Su Fan.

Zhang Feng immediately clapped. "Brother Su!"

"He has been practicing alchemy since the beginning." Lang Rulang also nodded.

Then came the fourth.

Fourth — Ji Ning.

Fatty Li's face fell. "Only fourth?"

Lang Rulang looked at him. "Fourth out of forty-two after creating a pill halfway through the stage is hardly poor."

"I know, but I thought he might get higher after the elder liked it so much."

Wei Zhusang shook his head with a smile. "Novelty isn't everything. Ning recovered well after losing his original ingredient, but the pill still had plenty of problems. Fourth is already a strong result."

Fatty Li reluctantly nodded.

Wei Zhusang looked over the four names again, lingering briefly on He Ming's. Yue Chan had been expected to perform well, and both Ning and Su Fan had already built reputations of their own, yet there were clearly other talented alchemists in this generation who had no intention of making things easy for them.

"This is a tough competition," he said.

Zhang Feng leaned back in his seat. "Yeah. Ning and Su Fan have their work cut out for them."

...

Ning stared at the rankings for a few moments before looking away.

Fourth place.

Not bad, he supposed. In fact, considering his current skill in alchemy, it was a pretty good result. His gamble with the pill seemed to have paid off, especially since Elder Mo Jian had given it such a favorable evaluation.

More importantly, placing second in the first stage and fourth in the second meant he still had a good chance of fulfilling his promise to Grandmaster Fu.

While the attendants cleared the competition grounds, Fenghuang hopped onto his shoulder and settled into his usual spot.

"Don't get too comfortable. There's still one stage left."

The raven gave a quiet caw.

"I know. I'm tired of this too."

Ning leaned back and waited. The break between stages wasn't particularly long, though it was enough to let the excitement of the previous round settle. Around him, contestants talked among themselves or watched the attendants work, and several were still looking toward the rankings overhead.

A bell rang soon afterward.

The conversations in the stands faded as Fu Xuan stepped forward.

"The third stage will test your refinement across the breadth of First-Tier alchemy."

The jade screen behind her lit up.

"There will be three mandatory portions. You will begin with low-grade First-Tier pills. Those who complete the required refinements may proceed to middle-grade, followed by high-grade. Every refinement you perform throughout the stage will be recorded and included in your final assessment."

Ten pill names appeared beneath the heading Low-Grade First-Tier.

Fasting Pill, Blood Replenishing Pill, Minor Qi Recovery Pill, Wound-Clotting Pill, Clear Breath Pill, Minor Detoxification Pill, Muscle-Soothing Pill, Heat-Clearing Pill, Spirit-Calming Pill, and Minor Meridian-Nourishing Pill.

All common formulas.

"Choose five different formulas from those provided. Seven portions of medicinal materials will be available to each of you, issued according to your requests. Every portion you receive will count toward that limit whether your refinement succeeds or fails."

The screen changed, revealing eight middle-grade formulas.

"Those who advance will choose four of these and receive six portions."

Five high-grade formulas appeared next.

"The final mandatory portion requires two successful refinements from these five formulas. Four portions will be available."

Four attempts for two successful high-grade First-Tier pills sounded generous until one remembered that reliably producing high-grade pills was already beyond many of the contestants below.

Fu Xuan allowed the noise to settle.

"Quality, failed refinements, wasted materials, and the time taken will all be recorded. You may stop and recover whenever you wish, though the allotted time will continue to pass. Recovery pills, spirit-restoring medicines, stimulants, and other external aids are prohibited."

That last part caught Ning's attention.

Five low-grade refinements, four middle-grade, then two high-grade. Eleven successful batches before the mandatory portion was complete, assuming he never failed.

Most of the contestants seemed occupied with choosing formulas.

Ning found himself thinking about what their hands would feel like eight or nine furnaces from now.

A single Fasting Pill was easy. So was a single Minor Qi Recovery Pill. Even the middle-grade formulas weren't especially frightening when approached individually. The difficulty came from repeating that concentration over and over before reaching the pills where a single lapse could ruin an entire batch.

Ah. A hidden test perhaps?

Fu Xuan hadn't needed to mention endurance at all.

"The details of the final portion will be given to those who qualify," she continued. "The remaining rules are unchanged."

Her hand rose.

"Begin."

The bell rang, and requests immediately sounded throughout the grounds as contestants called for their first ingredients.

Station Seventeen remained quiet.

There was no reason to join the rush.

Seven of the ten low-grade formulas were familiar enough that Ning could refine them with reasonable confidence, and after some consideration, he chose the Fasting Pill, Minor Qi Recovery Pill, Blood Replenishing Pill, Heat-Clearing Pill, and Minor Meridian-Nourishing Pill.

More importantly, he decided how he would approach the stage.

Slowly, at least until he had a reason to do otherwise.

Three months hadn't been enough to catch up with someone like Yue Chan in experience, and Ning had stopped pretending otherwise fairly early in his preparation.

Instead, he'd spent that time developing a style of alchemy around the advantage he possessed in ridiculous abundance: perception.

Pure Eyes, spiritual sense, Season-Reading Eye, and even his sensitivity toward space allowed him to notice things most alchemists would have missed. The problem was that seeing more didn't automatically tell him what any of it meant.

So Ning had spent much of those three months making everything he could control as consistent as possible.

Know Thyself had been invaluable there. A normal alchemist could repeat the same hand movement a hundred times and know that some attempts felt better than others. Ning could tell when his wrist had turned differently or when the qi passing through his arm followed a slightly different route.

He applied the same principle to everything else he could reasonably control: furnace temperature, flame intensity, qi input, extraction timing, and the transitions between different stages of refinement. If those remained familiar from one attempt to the next, then unexpected changes in the medicine became much easier to identify.

From there, the principle was simple enough. Observe first, understand what had changed, and interfere only when necessary.

At least, that was the idea.

"Fasting Pill," Ning called.

A sealed ingredient box arrived shortly afterward.

Before touching the furnace, every medicine received a brief inspection. Pure Eyes turned his irises blue as their finer structures came into focus, while spiritual sense supplied another layer of information about the spiritual energy contained within them.

Everything was ordinary, which was exactly what he wanted.

At least Feng Yue can't sabotage me in this round.

Ning just wanted a normal alchemy competition.

Taking a deep breath, Ning used Crimson Flame Control.

After the number of Fasting Pills he'd refined, most of the process had become deeply familiar. His hands moved through practiced motions while the flame followed patterns repeated often enough that he didn't need to consciously think about every adjustment, leaving more of his attention free for the medicine itself.

A faint irregularity appeared along the edge of the first medicinal liquid.

Pure Eyes caught it immediately.

Ning watched for another breath without touching the flame.

The irregularity remained for a moment before beginning to fade on its own.

There was nothing to fix.

Grandmaster Fu Xuan's lesson had taken longer to sink in than Ning cared to admit.

The next ingredient entered.

From there, the process continued smoothly. Two small adjustments to the flame carried the medicinal liquids through integration, after which condensation and Spirit Locking proceeded without trouble.

Four pills rolled into the collection tray.

The best was submitted, and the first mark above Station Seventeen lit up.

Only then did Ning sit back and reach for his cup.

Fenghuang immediately pecked at the rim.

"Get your own."

Caw

"You have a bowl I placed specially for you."

The raven looked toward the small bowl beside the furnace, then back at Ning's cup.

"...You're becoming increasingly difficult."

He tilted the cup anyway.

By the time Fenghuang finished stealing his water, several contestants had already begun their second batches. Yue Chan had submitted her first pill minutes earlier and was well into another refinement.

Ning made no attempt to catch up.

He gave himself a short interval and requested the next ingredients once he was ready.

The Minor Qi Recovery Pill passed quietly.

The Blood Replenishing Pill was more interesting. One medicinal essence extracted several breaths earlier than it usually did, and Pure Eyes immediately revealed the difference.

Ning didn't immediately change the flame.

The temperature was where he expected it to be, his timing hadn't changed, and the medicinal essence itself remained stable. With so many of the variables he controlled already accounted for, forcing the refinement back toward his usual schedule would have introduced another disturbance for no real benefit.

Instead, the next ingredient went in early.

The two medicinal essences met at the stage Ning wanted, and the rest of the refinement continued normally.

His third mark appeared.

By then, several contestants were already approaching their fifth low-grade refinement, and one had begun requesting middle-grade materials.

The Heat-Clearing Pill came next.

This formula could easily have tempted Ning into using the cold qi, yet its temperature requirements were already well within what Crimson Flame could manage. Introducing another energy merely because he possessed it would create another variable for him to control.

A gradual reduction of the flame at the proper stage allowed the medicinal properties to settle naturally, and another qualifying mark followed.

The Minor Meridian-Nourishing Pill finished the low-grade portion sometime later. When the fifth mark appeared, both spare portions remained unused, and Ning had spent less qi than he would have if he'd chased the contestants ahead of him.

The difference in pace was obvious. Yue Chan was already deep into the middle-grade section, with Su Fan and He Ming ahead of him as well.

Ning gave Fenghuang one last pat before turning his attention back to the furnace.

It's time for the mid grade pills.

The increase in difficulty became obvious almost immediately. More ingredients meant more medicinal relationships developing at the same time, and a mistake during extraction could survive through several later steps before becoming obvious enough to cause trouble.

Six of the eight formulas were familiar. After some consideration, Ning selected the Azure Breath Pill, Bone-Mending Pill, Clear Spirit Pill, and Flowing Meridian Pill.

The Azure Breath Pill came first.

Seven ingredients arrived.

Pure Eyes handled their finer structures, though the principal medicinal plants received another layer of scrutiny through Season-Reading Eye. One herb had been harvested slightly later than ideal, its medicinal essence having developed a little further than the version assumed by the standard formula.

A more experienced alchemist might have recognized the difference from subtle changes during extraction. Ning had the advantage of seeing it beforehand, while his experience in the spiritual fields supplied the rest.

The extraction time was shortened accordingly.

Halfway through the refinement, Fenghuang shifted on his perch.

"Not yet," Ning said, and the raven settled again.

White Flame could improve purification, but one of the Azure Breath Pill's medicinal essences reacted poorly to strong yang influence.

Just because it was a cheat code didn't mean that it was applicable to all situations.

Crimson Flame carried the batch through on its own.

The pill qualified, though its quality was merely respectable.

A glance toward the other stations showed Yue Chan submitting a middle-grade pill whose medicinal fragrance remained noticeably richer even from this distance.

Ning could perceive an enormous amount, but seeing a change didn't automatically tell him what that change meant. Yue Chan had spent years watching medicines behave inside a furnace, and there were things she could recognize almost immediately that Ning still had to stop and reason through.

The Bone-Mending Pill gave him a chance to use another side of his method.

Eight ingredients arrived, and Ning examined them one by one. The principal medicine contained several impurities that his Crimson Flame could remove given enough time, but Fenghuang's White Flame was much more effective.

Ning had tested both methods extensively, and depending on the pill being refined, introducing Fenghuang's White Flame could improve its purity by an additional five to ten percent.

That might not sound like much, but a ten-percent increase was nothing to scoff at. After all, there were plenty of situations in life where an extra inch or two could make all the difference.

...Such as measuring the length of a spiritual herb, of course.

Through Beast Heart Communion, Ning conveyed his intentions to Fenghuang. The raven straightened on his shoulder and released a narrow stream of White Flame.

Crimson Flame Control caught it the moment it entered the furnace.

The pale fire curved through the furnace under Ning's guidance while Pure Eyes tracked the impurities separating within the medicinal liquid. One vanished beneath the White Flame, followed by another, and the moment the surrounding medicinal structure began reacting to the flame's powerful yang nature, Ning adjusted his intent through their connection.

Fenghuang reduced his output almost immediately.

By now, their cooperation had reached a point where Ning barely needed to convey his intentions. Through Beast Heart Communion, their awareness was so closely linked that directing Fenghuang's flame felt almost like controlling an extension of his own body.

His power was Fenghuang's to wield, just as Fenghuang's flame was his to command.

The remaining White Flame withdrew, and Crimson Flame resumed the refinement.

The Bone-Mending Pill eventually qualified with better purity than the Azure Breath Pill, though its integration still fell short of the strongest middle-grade pills being produced elsewhere.

Purification was one of the strongest parts of Ning's alchemy. The rest still had to keep up.

By the time he prepared for the third middle-grade refinement, the atmosphere across the competition grounds had begun to change.

The opening had been filled with constant requests for ingredients and flames rising from almost every station as contestants tried to establish an early lead. Now someone nearby was flexing stiff fingers before beginning another batch, while farther away, another contestant remained seated long after his previous pill had been judged, eyes closed as he circulated his qi.

A young alchemist who had raced through the low-grade portion ruined his second middle-grade batch and stared into the blackened furnace for several breaths.

The part of the stage Ning had suspected from the rules was finally becoming obvious: fatigue.

He'd been pacing himself for exactly this reason. Alchemy demanded too much precision for exhaustion to remain harmless, and a hand moving a little slower or concentration slipping for half a breath could be the difference between success and failure.

Ning flexed his fingers and found them steady. Pure Eyes was beginning to leave a little pressure behind his temples after prolonged use, though nothing serious yet, and his qi reserves were sufficient.

This was another benefit of practicing the Sevenfold Origin Art. His endurance was nothing to scoff at.

Taking those breaks had also been worth it.

The Clear Spirit Pill came next.

With nine ingredients, the refinement demanded more sustained concentration. Pure Eyes and spiritual sense remained active through most of the process, while Fenghuang contributed three brief bursts of White Flame during purification.

Near the end, a fluctuation appeared within the medicinal mixture. Ning almost adjusted the flame before noticing that the surrounding essences remained stable. He held back, watching as the disturbance gradually faded on its own.

Three pills eventually emerged, and the best qualified with a quality modestly above the required standard.

Ning studied the result for a moment before handing it over. It seemed taking Grandmaster Fu Xuan's criticism to heart had done him some good.

The Flowing Meridian Pill came after another period of rest.

This one had been chosen deliberately. Its medicinal integration was difficult, though its greatest problem involved several temperature-sensitive essences that needed to remain within a narrow range, which happened to suit Ning particularly well.

The seventh medicinal essence reacted more violently than expected.

Heat spread through the right side of the furnace while another essence was still settling nearby.

Lowering Crimson Flame would have been the obvious response, except Pure Eyes showed him that the rest of the furnace was exactly where he wanted it.

So the flame remained unchanged as cold qi flowed through his meridians instead.

The familiar circulation stirred the Worm Overlord almost immediately. Part of the passing cold energy was consumed and refined into smoother Jade Frost Essence, while the Gu's own power helped the remaining qi maintain its shape against the furnace heat.

After months of practice, the Gu had grown accustomed to these circulation patterns. It couldn't understand alchemy or judge which medicinal essence needed cooling, but its instinctive cooperation meant Ning could devote more attention to the refinement itself.

Pure Eyes identified the unstable medicinal essence, while Extreme Space-Time Sensitivity helped Ning guide the cold qi precisely into the affected region. Crimson Flame continued heating the surrounding medicines without interruption.

Ning reduced the flow slightly.

Several breaths later, the essence returned to the range he wanted, and the cold qi withdrew before it could suppress the reaction too far. The surrounding medicines had barely been disturbed.

Fenghuang remained on his perch throughout. There had been nothing for White Flame to do.

Another quarter-hour passed before condensation finally completed.

Two pills emerged, one bearing a faint line across its surface while the other looked intact.

The better pill went to the assessment formation, and his fourth middle-grade mark appeared.

Ning released a slow breath as he leaned back.

All four refinements had succeeded. With results like these, Ning could finally consider himself a proper alchemist.

More importantly, the style of alchemy he'd developed was holding together under repeated refinement.

Ning looked across the competition grounds.

Yue Chan had completed the middle-grade portion well before him, and her pills maintained a quality Ning couldn't reproduce across so many different formulas. He Ming had already begun preparing for high-grade refinement, while Su Fan had reached that portion ahead of him as well. Luo Xiulan sat beside her furnace recovering before her own attempt, and several contestants Ning only vaguely recognized were still going strong.

Many more furnaces had gone dark. Some contestants had exhausted their available portions, while others had accumulated too many failures to finish within the remaining time, leaving attendants moving among the silent stations as the field continued thinning.

The first real weariness had begun creeping into his body by now. Similarly, Fenghuang had used enough White Flame that Ning would have to be more selective from here onward.

He was tired, though nowhere near enough to stop.

Resting his arms on his knees, Ning studied the five high-grade formulas displayed on the jade screen.

So far, things had gone better than he could reasonably have hoped.

Nine refinements, nine successes.

Unfortunately, Ning knew his limits. He still had enough strength to continue, but high-grade refinement would demand far more precision than anything he'd attempted so far.

Then again, hadn't cultivation always been about pushing beyond one's limits?

Well, there was only one way to find out how far he could go.
