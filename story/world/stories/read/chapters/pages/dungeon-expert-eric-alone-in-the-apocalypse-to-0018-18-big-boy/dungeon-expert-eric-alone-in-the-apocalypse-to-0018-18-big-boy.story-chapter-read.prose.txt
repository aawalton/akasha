The forest shook from the impact of one rolling step after another, the bamboo dropping flat in the distance. Slowly, Eric made out a large, conical shape, rotund around the middle but tapering to a point at its peak. He couldn’t stare too long, too occupied with keeping the gnomes from carpet-bombing his feet, but he knew what was coming even before it breached the edge of his clearing with a final crack-thud of falling bamboo. Already knowing what he’d see, Eric looked up.

A truly massive gnome loomed over him. Its hat stretched nearly to the top of the bamboo, and its red eyes glowed like the headlights on a train: large, round, unstoppable. Its round belly was bound with a length of earthenware cord, and blue porcelain robes draped around it. Two big brown boots with curled toes finished the look. It opened its mouth, revealing flat, dull teeth, and roared loud enough that its belly-button length beard clattered against its stomach. Eric backed up a step, bracing himself against the blast of hot air.

I think I’ve found the boss. Or rather… the boss found me.

Without hesitation, Eric snatched up one of the little gnomes still running his way and chucked it at the big guy. The gnome shrieked as it flew toward the boss gnome and exploded instants before they collided. The boss gnome rocked back onto his heels, but didn’t budge. Not even as much as a crack appeared in his body.

He’s tough. The same explosive technique he’d been using on the bamboo wouldn’t work on this guy. For a split second, Eric considered getting up close enough to set off the big boss, then running off, but the memory of the houses exploding was enough to change his mind. The houses had exploded hard enough that he’d felt them go through the earth. This big guy would probably explode hard enough to kill him even if he ran at top speed. The amount of shrapnel in his body alone would tear Eric to pieces.

If I could set him off, then knock him off a cliff or something, he mused, but unlike the snakemen’s ravine, there was no obvious elevation change in this dungeon. There was just him, the bamboo, and the gnomes.

The giant gnome charged. Eric leaped out of its way and snatched up a little gnome in passing. Rather than chuck it at the big guy, he threw it ahead of him, clearing out more of the bamboo. He needed a bigger arena, and better yet, if he could find another place to fight it—a real clearing, for example, with better footing—that would be even better.

Tearing past him, the giant gnome stomped into the bamboo. Its heavy footsteps flattened the bamboo as it passed, leaving a trail of pressed bamboo behind it. Eric jolted. Right! It walked here. Can I use that path to escape?

There was no time like the present. He whipped around and ran for it, punting a few gnomes out of his way as he went. The big gnome was still in the bamboo, lumbering around as it slowly turned its bulk toward Eric, which gave him the time he needed to explore where it had come from. He sprinted down the flattened bamboo path, lifting his feet high over the tips of the strands. The bamboo was pointed toward him, since the gnome had walked toward him, kicking it forward as it walked, and the tips kept trying to snag his toes and knock him on his face. Still, it was better than standing still in a clearing with bad footing and no clear route to victory, so he charged onward.

Behind him, the gnome roared, its voice reassuringly distant. Less reassuring was the heavy thumping rattling the forest floor that immediately followed, quickly growing louder as it closed in on him. Eric glanced around him, then squeezed through the bamboo. Rather than trying to walk on what little ground there was, he stepped on the stalks, throwing his weight against the plants opposite his feet. The leaves slashed him, drawing blood, but they only managed surface cuts. He pushed on, putting enough distance between him and the flattened bamboo that he could barely see the path anymore. The footsteps grew so loud they drowned out everything else, and the bamboo shook so hard the leaves cut him merely from the force of the steps. He fell still, going completely motionless. The huge gnome stomped past, kicking up strands of bamboo and re-flattening them as it passed.

It vanished deeper into the thicket, and Eric fought his way out of the bamboo and back to the path, gazing after it. It had run on a curved path, arcing away into the forest. From here, he couldn’t see where it had gone nor its back, just a long sweep of flattened bamboo.

He glanced behind him, looked at where the gnome had ran off to, checked behind him again, then shrugged and followed after the gnome. He had a way to dodge it, and his health was still fine. In the spirit of the scouting run, he might as well see this through.

The gnome roared in the distance, and he heard the hammering of its footsteps once more, this time a different direction, racing off to the left. Eric jogged after the gnome, curious where it had come from.

There were fewer little gnomes out this way. He saw a few of them rush by in the forest, glowing red eyes locked on the clearing he’d left behind. He breathed out, relieved. Whatever they were seeking to—heat, sound, the rumor mill—at least they couldn’t track his mana signature through the snakeskin jacket.

Wait. There’s fewer of them, but they’re running from this direction. Doesn’t that mean they spawn somewhere down here? He didn’t know why there were fewer of them closer to the spawn; it seemed like there should be more of them, if anything, or at least the same quantity. Maybe the houses had a spawn limit, he didn’t know. But he could put two and two together to figure out that if their backs were pointed this way, they probably came from this direction.

I can at least take out the little guys, he thought, then: but do I want to? The little guys had become one of his primary weapons and his way to get through the bamboo forest, absent the big gnome. If he no longer had the little guys around, could he even retreat? Or would he have effectively locked himself in here with the big guy and nothing?

Scouting run. Let’s take a look. Once I know, I can make a decision. He ran on, chasing after the trampled path the giant gnome had left behind.

As the path arced further and further, Eric slowed more and more, swapping from a jog to a walk to hear what was going on ahead of him. The big gnome was still rampaging around the field, close enough to keep him on edge but not close enough to be an immediate threat. Sharp, high-pitched sounds came from ahead of him every now and again, and the closer he got, the more they resolved into clattering. At last, he turned the corner and came to the end of the path.

Eric immediately flinched back around the corner, putting bamboo between himself and the gnomes. When he didn’t hear the clatter of tiny feet chasing after him, he adjusted his bandanna, lifting it over his nose once more, and peeked through the gaps in the bamboo.

The path came to a large lake, which stretched as far as the eye could see; this was the barrier the giant gnome had reached that had forced it to divert from its initial path. At the edge of the lake, a series of gnome homes formed an idyllic porcelain lakeside village, just as neat and nice as a stay-at-home mom with access to hobby stores could make it. Between him and the houses, a mass of gnomes milled about aimlessly, waiting for some kind of trigger. Every now and again, one of them would turn, eyes lighting up, and run off into the bamboo, but most of them simply wandered. The houses weren’t producing gnomes as fast as the houses he’d encountered earlier. If there was some sort of limiter on their spawn rate or on how many gnomes they could have active at one time, they’d reached it, and only occasionally did another gnome squeeze out of the improbably small doors of the lakeside villas.

Holy shit, that’s a lot of gnomes, Eric thought, and then: Enough gnomes to blow up the big guy, I bet. Especially with how fiercely the houses blew. If he could lure the big gnome back here, he could hoist it upon its own petard, in a more literal sense than the phrase was usually utilized. It wasn’t often he got to use the phrase about medieval siege explosives to refer to actual explosives, after all.

He eyed the lake. It was an elevation difference, and on top of that, the water would absorb a lot of the force of the explosions. He had a bail-out for after he lured the guy here: just jump in the lake.

Okay. I can do this. Scouting run? What scouting run? He could beat this. There was no reason to hold back; might as well finish the job.

Hidden in the bamboo as he was, out of the sight of the gnomes, he had a moment of peace all to himself. He pulled up his map, considering his options. The map was zoomed out to the general area, but when he clicked on the No Mess dungeon, it zoomed in, showing him the layout of the dungeon. Areas he hadn’t visited yet were blank, but all the parts he’d visited were displayed in detail, including the gnome houses and the clearing he’d blasted in the bamboo. The little gnomes within his visual range were displayed as red dots on the map, but the map didn’t include any gnomes he couldn’t currently see, including the big guy—though the boss was also off on the blank part of the map, well out of the parts Eric had explored, so that wasn’t a surprise.

I could chase him down, but that’s a lot of getting chased. I’m no distance runner. I’ll run out of gas before the gnome does. Luckily, that wasn’t his only option. He knew how else to summon the big gnome; he just didn’t like it. I like it better than running five miles while consistently dodging a giant gnome, he countered himself, and set his eyes on his prize. It was time to go gnome-tossing.

Eric stepped around the edge of the bamboo grove. One or two of the gnomes turned, locked eyes, and rushed at him, but most of them continued to mill around. That was fine. He didn’t need the whole pack drawing aggro yet. He knelt as the gnomes closed in, cracking and glowing as they began to explode. Grabbing one, he chucked it into the heart of the pack, while he punted another directly into the lake. The third one he dodged, leaping to the side as it leaped at him, full body extended like he was a football one foot beyond the touchdown zone. It flew past him and exploded behind him. The force threw him forward, but the shrapnel bounced harmlessly off his coat.

The huge gnome roared in the distance. Its feet thudded on the earth, growing steadily louder as it closed in on Eric’s location.

Yes! He pumped his fist. As expected, explode a few little guys and the big guy came running. Whatever it cued off of, sound clearly attracted it.

The rest of the gnome pack froze. As one, they turned, their eyes all blazing to life in sync, each one locking on to Eric with a tiny clatter. Eric stopped his fist mid pump and took a step back, swallowing nervously. Right. Until he gets here, I’ve got to deal with these guys.

The gnomes lunged, and Eric took off running.
