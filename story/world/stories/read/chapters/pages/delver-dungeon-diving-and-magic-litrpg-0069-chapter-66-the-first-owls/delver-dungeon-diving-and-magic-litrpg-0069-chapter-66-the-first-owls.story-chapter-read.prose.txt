During the worst of the first incursions, humans had only one defense. Before we discovered enchanting, when magic was still in its weakest forms, we relied on the power of Runic Wards. The wards hid us from their sight, from their scouts and their hunters. Simple to make, and with many uses depending on the knowledge of the Runesmith, wards still remain powerful tools for those who dare to venture into the realms of the demons.

The Infernal, By High Priest Storm

Everyone looks to me, the fire illuminating our faces as I tell my story. They lean closer, the words escaping me slowly, each one bringing them deeper.

“The warrior looked over her people, raising her sword high and pointing to the endless hordes that faced them. ‘Do not fear.’ She said, her voice carrying over the hundred warriors that defended their home with her. ‘For I will hold your fear for you. Trust in me, and live only in that trust. I promise you, today, we will not fall.”

I reach down and sip water from my leather flask. The silence stretches on for a long moment, as they wait for my next words.

“Then she signaled forward, and Empress Sala led the last of her empire’s knights into the endless horde. They did not falter, nor fall. Instead, she led her people into an impossible victory. Two of the famed Knights survived the battle against thousands, and they carried back their Empress, laying her to rest among the greatest of heroes in the Gardens of Angel Sol.”

My voice fades, leaving only the crackling of the fire. A distant howl echoes through the forest, echoed by more as a pack of wolves hunts through the Living Forest.

“That’s quite the telling of the Sala Knights.” Libby says quietly.

“You’ve heard the story before?” I ask, leaning back to rest on my hands.

“A long time ago.”

“It was popular with our old mercenary group.” Daron adds, “Martok was particularly fond of it. Kept saying he was descended from those ancient knights.”

“It’s the first time I’ve heard it.” Eyla says, Tanya nodding vigorously beside her.

“Ya tell it well.” Grildor takes a skewered animal off the fire, and blows on it.

“Thank you.” I say.

Libby pulls a pot off the fire, and pours me a cup. I take the tea, and sip at it, enjoying the sharp flavor followed by a chill aftertaste.

I send her a curious look as she pours for the others.

“Peppermint.” She says, handing a cup to Eyla next. “I’ll have to restock soon.” She offers a cup to the wooden golem, who holds his hand up in refusal.

“It’s good.” I take another sip, then let out a content sigh. “Alright folks, since we’re all here, I think we should discuss what we’re doing next.”

That draws their attention. Keve sets down a carving, while Libby pours the last of the tea for herself. The others lean closer, their faces growing serious.

I look at Eyla. “This was your delve, so I’d like to ask you what you think. I’d personally like to save a lot of these items to help recreate the Owls.”

Eyla nods, her eye gaining a chill to them as they narrow. “If the Crows think they can target my family, then they are deeply wrong.” She smiles, the air cooling around us. “If you’re right Leo, then I’m descended from the Owls. If anyone is going to carry on their legacy, that’ll be me.”

“Everyone onboard?” I look around, receiving a series of nods. “Good. Then first thing we’ll need is a secure place to store the items.”

“You can store them with me and the De’Vereth. Although….” She pauses, “I’m not sure you can trust the mages right now.”

“The stronghold.” Keve says. “It is safe.”

I nod my head, then pause. “Yes, but there is someone we’ll have to be careful of.”

Keve tilts his head in confusion.

“Lyla, the Leifen girl.” While she had saved us from the cultists, she’d also been helping them at first. “I don’t know who she’s working with, but she’s not working alone.”

Keve gives a heavy nod, “She will be watched.”

“I’ll need to return home after.” Eyla says quietly, “I’ve already been gone for too long.”

I frown, “That’s dangerous.”

Eyla shakes her head, “My life has always been dangerous.” She lifts her cloak to reveal a dagger of the owls, “But now I have something they’re not expecting.”

I let out a breath as I nod my head, “I guess nowhere is safe…”

“You said you needed a follower of Oathsworn Levis in order to gather the last of the items inside.” Libby says, her eyes meeting mine as she takes a long sip of her tea, “Is there more to that?”

“Yes, and no. A reaper of Yon might work, but I have reason not to trust the church.” Brother Ivan had told me not to at the very least, “Trust matters more than anything right now. The Oathsworn paladins have been staying hidden, and are less likely to be…” I hesitate on saying the words.

“Agents of the Crows.” The wooden training golem speaks for the first time in our conversation. “It is not the first time they have infiltrated the Holy Orders, and it will not be the last.”

I nod to the golem, “That’s my fear at least.”

The group falls into quiet, only broken by Grildor taking a large bite of his roasted meat.

“We can search Stronghold Deragun for the Paladins,” Eyla looks down at her tea, “but I haven’t heard anything.”

“I know of one Oathsworn Order that has revealed themselves.” I say quietly, “Brother Ivan told me that a High Paladin of Angel Mira went to the meeting of the High Priests.”

“So, we drop everything off with the Vishten, then go to Stronghold Deragun and kidnap a Paladin.” Daron leans back, crossing his legs with a shrug. “Sounds like a good plan to me.”

“Ya realize we all migh’ be the first Owls in five hundred years.” Grildor says around a mouthful of food.

We all turn to him in surprise.

He swallows, and wipes his mouth with a rag. “Wha? Jus’ thought I’d mention it.”

“I suppose that is acceptable.” The Training Golem says. “You are wearing our cloaks after all.”

I look around the campfire. He’s right. We are wearing their colors.

Tanya, our Druid, who’s sneaking some pieces of meat to an owl.

Eyla, the Ice Mage, and descendant of the Owls, smiles as I meet her gaze.

Keve, the Vishten warrior who learns the magic of fire, carves another wooden owl.

Libby, the healer who keeps us all moving smoothly, drinks her tea with a knowing smile.

Daron, the scout who’s quick with all too quick with a joke.

And finally Grildor, the man who’ll jump in front of dragon fire for any of us.

A golem flying down, and landing on my shoulder reminds me of the last of our crew. Shar, created by an Archmage long ago.

My gaze returns to the leader of the mercenaries. “Libby, I’d like to talk to you about permanent employment.”

The group breaks into laughter, the serious atmosphere disappearing as laughter fills the night, undercut by the crackling of the fire.

The next morning, I start my day by joining the others in combat drills with the Training Golem, learning about the habits of different undead and demons.

After that, we break for breakfast, the women cleaning in the stream first, while Daron makes us all food. Then we switch out, rinsing ourselves off as best we can.

After we’re cleaned and fed, we split to pursue different projects. Keve, Tanya, and Grildor meditate by the large tree at the center of camp. Daron and Libby join the three of them this time, leaving me and Eyla to work on magic.

I’m fairly confident in basic second circle Novice spells now. I’m working on the banishing spell Eridor’s journal teaches, but he wasn’t kidding about the Desire rune Banishment being complicated. So many twirls and lines for a singular rune is beyond anything I’ve worked on before. During breaks in my own practice, I run Eyla through some of the more basic and useful Desire runes.

This continues for a few hours, until Lunch. Lunch is leftover jerky torn and handed out to all of us to help the aching in our bellies.

I personally can’t wait for a meal at an inn. Bread in particular, sounds amazing.

After lunch, I find Tanya, and work with her to try and recreate basic Circuitry Ink. Thankfully, there’s a book that describes the process well. We’re missing a few ingredients, but Tanya’s experimenting with replacements she thinks might work.

I return to magic practice for a little while before frustration gets the better of me, and I take a break to work on something else.

Today, I decide to learn more about Warding. It seems that there’s quite a bit I don’t understand about it, but if I’m ever able to recreate the effects of the Angel Statuette…. Well, that opens up a whole world of possibilities.

I find the staff with the basic ward preventing scrying. A simple study of it tells me that I should be able to condense the ward enough to fit a second ward on it.

My afternoon is spent this way, eventually succeeding in a second ward that should make us invisible to the senses of weaker undead. The ward needs to be charged every once in a while, but isn’t nearly as bad as the first one I’d made.

By the time I’m finished with that, Dinner is ready. We all gather around the campfire for a real meal, that Tanya cooks up for us. It’s delicious as always, despite the lack of ingredients.

After dinner, I work some more on magic, succeeding a total of once, in my attempt at a Second Circle banishment spell.

Eyla and I chat late into the night, staring up at the stars and staying up way too late. Even so, I find myself reading after our discussion, studying the Blood Rituals Of The Patari, and The Infernal with Sharks help.

Then it’s time for sleep.

The next morning, I wake up slowly, Shar tapping my forehead with her paw.

I stand up and stretch, my light stone falling to the side. The book I’d spent so much time reading goes into my Private Library a moment later.

Heading outside, I find most of the others already awake, packing up camp. Today’s the day we’re supposed to escort the Dralk Lady to the Stronghold to talk with the Vishten.

I grab some breakfast, then break down my own tent. All ten of the Shadow horses are out, and about. Daron and Tanya working together to tie items to them with what extra rope we have.

Packing up camp is a surprisingly quick affair with so many hands. Then it’s just time to wait for the Dralk.

They arrive a few hours later. Lady Kala with the golden mask escorted by a familiar Commander Vakar and his five warriors. The Dralk have a pack of large Elk with them that they ride,

I pick up the Angel Statuette, revealing out people.

A familiar Shadow Mare walks over to me, and I hesitate for a second before climbing on top of her. If they have mounts, it’s best to not slow them down. The better time we make, the more likely we are to avoid danger as well.

Everyone in our party mounts up with Tanya’s help, Grildor sitting atop the largest horse with his armor already attached.

We quickly fall into formation.

I look up into the sky, where Shar flies away, scouting the way.

With a wave of my hand, the large group moves.
