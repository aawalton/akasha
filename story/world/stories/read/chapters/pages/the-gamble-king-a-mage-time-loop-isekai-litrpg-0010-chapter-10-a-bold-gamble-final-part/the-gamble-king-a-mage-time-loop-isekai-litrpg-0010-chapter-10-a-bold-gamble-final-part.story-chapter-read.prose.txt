The glamorous life of a master thief, indeed.

Western equipment cache—spare tents and canvas, perfect kindling that would create maximum smoke. Southern grain stores—not the actual food supplies, but the wooden storage frames and empty sacks that would go up like torches.

The genius wasn't in the fires themselves. Any idiot could start a fire. The genius was in the positioning.

Set one fire, and ten men would rush to put it out while maintaining normal security elsewhere. Set three fires simultaneously at maximum distance from each other, and you'd force the camp to choose—abandon security to fight all three, or let parts of the camp burn while maintaining minimal response teams.

Either way, chaos.

The problem was distance. Even running at full speed, Max couldn't cover the ground between target zones fast enough to light all three before the first was discovered. He'd be caught red-handed with a torch, probably before lighting the second fire.

Unless he wasn't on the ground at all.

"No," Gregory said slowly. "You are not." He looked at the chest in Max's hands, then back at his face. "If the Chronicler truly sent you here, then perhaps you have a purpose."

His hand was no longer on the sword's hilt.

"Take it," he said. "But know that I will be watching. If you fail to live up to the trust I place in you now..."

"You'll stop me," Max finished. "I understand."

Gregory moved to the chest and examined the damaged lock. With a few precise movements of a small knife, he worked the blade into the mechanism. It opened with a soft click.

Inside, nestled in white silk, lay the Dragon Heart.

It was smaller than Max had expected, about the size of a child's fist. It pulsed with a soft red light, and even looking at it made his skin crawl with barely contained power.

"What happens now?" Gregory asked.

Max looked at the pulsing red stone. According to everything he'd been told, this would probably kill him. According to his own hope, it would kill him and he'd come back with the power he needed.

"Now I find out if I'm brave enough to do what needs doing."

He lifted the Dragon Heart to his mouth. The surface was rougher than he'd expected, like biting into an unripe peach. He sank his teeth in.

The taste hit him immediately—metallic, bitter, with an underlying sweetness that made his stomach lurch. It was like chewing raw liver mixed with copper pennies and something that might have been cinnamon if cinnamon could burn your tongue.

One bite. The flesh was dense, almost rubbery. Juice ran down his chin, warm and sticky.

Two bites. His stomach felt impossibly full, as if he'd just eaten a seven-course meal. The sensation was overwhelming—not just fullness but a deep, bone-deep satiation that made no sense given he'd barely consumed a quarter of the thing.

Three bites. He had to force himself to swallow, his body rejecting the foreign substance even as he pushed it down.

Max looked up at Gregory, who was watching with the detached interest of a scholar observing an experiment. The knight's expression gave nothing away.

"Enough," Gregory said quietly, reaching for what remained of the Heart. "Any more and you'll simply waste it."

Max handed over the remnants without protest. His jaw ached from chewing the tough flesh. Gregory placed the partially consumed Heart back in the chest and closed the lid with a soft click.

"Do you feel anything?" Gregory asked.

"Not really," Max started to say, then paused. Something warm was trickling from his nose. He wiped it with the back of his hand and stared at the dark smear across his knuckles.

Blood.

More warmth, this time from his ears. He touched his left ear and his fingers came away red. His vision began to blur at the edges, colors bleeding together like watercolors in rain.

His heart started racing—not the quick flutter of anxiety, but a deep, thundering rhythm that made his ribs ache with each beat.

Gregory watched it all with calm interest.

"This is normal," Max said, his voice already sounding distant to his own ears.

"You are dying."

"Yeah," Max agreed, blood now flowing freely from his nose. "Yeah, I am."

"You seem remarkably unconcerned by this fact."

"Used to it," Max mumbled. The world was starting to tilt sideways. He could feel his body beginning to shut down, systems failing one by one like lights going out in a building.

Damn, that stuff is the real deal...

Through the growing haze, a thought occurred to him. "If I were to go back in time," he said, each word requiring tremendous effort, "and see you again, before all this... what would be the best way to approach you? To tell you about the story?"

Gregory was quiet for a long moment. Max couldn't tell if the knight was thinking or if his own perception of time was warping.

"Do not tell me until you have proven yourself," Gregory said finally. "Manifest Fanga first. Complete the Proving Year, perhaps. Earn my trust. Then come to me, and I will listen."

"Why?" Max's vision was going gray at the edges.

"Because even now, knowing what I know, I have reservations about this choice. Power given too freely is power misused. If you are to carry such knowledge, you must first show you are worthy of it."

Max nodded, or thought he did. It was getting hard to tell if his body was responding to his commands.

"I understand," he whispered.

The darkness was closing in now, warm and welcoming. His heart gave one final, tremendous beat, then stuttered, then stopped.

Max died.
