Yuna stood at the base of the ice mountain, her body humming with anxiety as she impatiently waited for Seojun and the strange woman to return from the top—her mind still reeling from the explosion of power Daruk had unleashed only moments ago.

Daruk had said he wanted to summon his brother, but he had summoned a host of powerful individuals instead—her brother included—and the longer she waited, the tighter the bad feeling in her chest grew.

As happy as she was to finally see her brother again after nearly four days in this cursed realm, that excitement was undercut by trepidation and a near-crippling fear as the oath chains around her core twisted and tightened.

“You’re pacing, Yuna,” Mira said, seated on a hovering platform of wind. Yuna blinked, realizing she had indeed been pacing. She stopped and clasped her hands behind her back to keep them from fidgeting.

This was the worst day of her life.

Mira looked at her anxiously, guilt and shame written plainly on her face. “You have to tell him, Yuna. Brother will know what to do.”

“No,” Yuna refused. “If he comes down now and we leave, I won’t need to tell him anything. It’ll all just go away like a bad dream.”

Mira’s expression softened into sadness. “You know that’s not true. We don’t know what the cultists are going to ask you to do. You could be on the other side of the realm, and they—”

“No, Mira!” Yuna’s voice came out sharper this time, edged with desperation. “I can’t tell anyone. They’ll never trust me if they find out. Not to mention, Brother would be so disappointed. And Father—” she choked. “No, I can’t.”

Mira’s gaze softened, tears pooling in her eyes as she whispered, “I understand. I’m sorry.”

Yuna’s shoulders sagged. “Stop crying, Mira. I’ve told you it isn’t your fault. You have to put on a brave face like we did with Daruk Brystion. Nobody can know.”

“I know, I know.” Mira shook her head, her shoulders shaking with silent sobs. “It’s just if I’d been stronger—”

“Stop it!” Yuna snapped. “This is hard for me to deal with, Mira. I cannot deal with another guilty breakdown right now. I have said times without number, It is not your fault.”

Mira glanced away to wipe her tears, and though the guilt didn’t leave, she relaxed her shoulders as her sister had commanded. With one last sniff, she turned to look at the top of the mountain, intentionally keeping her gaze away from her sister.

If she stared at Yuna, she would burst into another fit of tears.

Silence stretched between them, deafening and uncomfortable. Tense.

Mira couldn’t take it. After a few seconds, she broke it. “Could you have imagined Daruk would be this powerful?” she asked, glancing at Yuna and shaking her head. “I knew he was strong—the way he carried himself—but this strong? I can feel the chill of the ice even from here.”

But the change in topic didn’t serve as the distraction she’d hoped.

Yuna glanced up, taking in the aurora of warped reality at the top of the mountain and the thousands of jagged icicles jutting down from the sky. Even from the base—far from the summit—she could feel the frigid bite in the air, sharp as needles pressing into her skin.

And all she could think of was how much stronger Aodhán Brystion must be. Daruk himself had said it—his brother was stronger. But if Daruk could do this, if he was this powerful… how much more was his brother?

A lance of terror raced down her spine. She shuddered, her expression twisting into a pleading sob. “Brother, come down,” she whispered. “I want to be far from here before that man arrives.”

Mira grimaced, feeling the weight of fear settle back on her shoulders. They had met Aodhán Brystion before, but back then, Yuna hadn’t been a sleeper agent of cultists. And with his legendary senses, only the Ascendants knew what he could or couldn’t perceive.

But Yuna’s fear was deeper than that. She feared the cultists would order her to fight him the moment he arrived—throwing her straight to the wolves.

Sighing heavily, Mira stood to comfort her sister. But the moment she rose, she felt more than saw a lightning bolt flash past. The force of its passage flung her and Yuna several feet backward, their skin prickling with electricity.

Mira’s eyes widened in shock as she slowly picked herself up, afraid that even a sharp movement might draw his attention. “He’s here,” she whispered. “That’s Daruk’s brother.”

She glanced at Yuna, who was completely frozen in shock, her expression ashen with terror. Her gaze darted to Mira, eyes still wide as she slowly picked herself up. “Come on, Mira. We have to go now.”

Mira didn’t hesitate. Their brother would just have to find them again.

Together, they raced away from the mountain, feet slapping against snow and ice with abandon. However, they’d barely crossed a dozen meters when they suddenly slammed face-first into an invisible wall with a loud thunk!

A sound that was completely drowned out by the blare of an alarm so loud, it was like a knife stab into the skull itself. Yuna and Mira staggered backward, eyes widening even further as a crimson screen appeared before them.

THERE ARE TEN GRAND ABYSSAL PINS ON THIS MOUNTAIN. NO ONE IS PERMITTED TO LEAVE THE MOUNTAIN UNTIL ALL TEN PINS BELONG TO ONE PERSON.

FIGHT FOR YOUR OWN SALVATION.

“No,” Yuna breathed as she read the message, dread pooling within her belly. She wasn’t sure which was worse. A direct command or this? And then she glanced back at the top, realizing that she would have to go up to fight for the remaining spikes.

‘By Aeloria!” She cursed. “I told that boy to keep his ascendant-forsaken spikes. How are we to survive up there, not to mention fight? We are only at the evolved class!”

But the cultists weren’t apparently too keen on killing them off, because an instant later, Yuna felt a tug on her storage.

The tug came once. A second time. A third—

The grand abyssal pins flew out of her spatial storage to float in the air. And then began floating skyward, most likely to join the others.

With her mouth opened wide in shock, Yuna watched her only hope of immunity in the coming calamity float away from her. But rather than fear or anger, Yuna only felt relief.

Maybe she couldn’t flee to safety—but at least she wouldn’t have to partake in some sick game of blood and desperation.

Leaning back against the invisible wall, Yuna sighed and resigned herself to wait, praying to Aeloria that Seojun made it out alive. That was the only person who mattered.

The others could kill themselves for all she cared.

As if her words were a trigger, a shrill scream echoed from the mountaintop—and an instant later, Yuna saw the first spray of blood.

The crimson screen dissolved, leaving behind a silence so complete it felt like the realm itself was holding its breath. Scattered across the mountaintop were a total of thirteen people, each person eyeing the others warily, thoughts racing, and hearts pounding.

Yet, no one moved.

They didn’t even glance up at the ten grand abyssal pins floating just out of reach above them. Instead, their gazes were fixed on each other—sizing up, calculating. Thinking.

Tension hummed between them, so tangible Aodhán was certain he could cut it with a claw. The cultist had put them in a moral dilemma, forcing them to choose between their self-preservation and greed.

Oh, how Aodhán hated dancing to their tunes, but this time he couldn’t help himself. Ten grand abyssal runes. One person. This was a chance at gaining full immunity from the Calamity that was about to befall them all.

Despite their deal with Serythion, their survival wasn’t certain yet. But these pins could provide that certainty—even if not for him, then for someone close to him who couldn’t protect themselves.

Six more grand abyssal pins, and he could guarantee the survival of a person for the next twenty-four to forty-eight hours.

He wanted it. Of that there was no doubt. But so did the twelve others scattered across the mountain—and unlike him, more than a few probably needed it more, their chances of survival less than abysmal.

Still, Aodhán wasn’t about to hand it over. He had walked miles to find three of those ten pins. In fact, of everyone here, he had contributed the most.

But he also knew no one here would agree to simply give the pins to one person. He could see his greed reflected in their sharpened gazes. Everyone wanted it. A few, like Monica and Sebastian, seemed even willing to kill for it.

The only way to go about this without much bloodshed was a battle—a sort of tournament where the winner walked out with all ten pins.

But the odds of him coming out victorious were—he grimaced—slim at best. Not with people like Mirith, Baxter, and Monica competing against him.

He grimaced, his gaze darting to the floating pins for a moment before glancing back at the others, expression wary. No one had moved an inch since the crimson screen dissolved—almost as if they all had a silent agreement to do nothing.

But that wasn’t it. Everyone was just scared of the burst of violence and chaos that would erupt the moment anyone moved. In this very moment, with a chance at immunity hanging directly over their heads, moving—even an inch—was a crime punishable by death.

Inevitably, however, after several seconds of tensed silence and stillness, someone moved.

It was the Calodan group leader.

Moving at a speed that would have confused the senses of most people in her tier, she shot into the air, metallic essence exploding from her as she sought to manipulate the floating pins toward herself.

She didn’t even live to grasp the first pin.

Everyone reacted instantly, even Aodhán—a beam of pure electricity surged out of his palms to slam her back to the ground. But where his attack had been restrained to avoid outright killing, the others hadn’t been so restrained.

Beams and constructs of infinity, sound, starlight, and entropy sweeping out to skewer her to pieces. Her body exploded from the clash of energies. Bones cracked, and blood sprayed, splattering everyone in crimson patches.

For a heartbeat, there was silence as the two remaining Calodans stared at the vanishing body of the leader, eyes wide in shock. And then naked fury.

With a roar that seemed to erupt from their very souls, they attacked, blood and light essence exploding out of their bodies in a furious, vengeful wave.

But compared to the array of powerful individuals they now considered enemies, they were weak. And an instant later, Aodhán felt another splatter of blood on his face. Only this time, it was accompanied by a rain of bloody tissues, viscera, and other body fluids.

Aodhán grimaced as their bodies vanished, realizing that the harvest ritual was once more ongoing. Jethro was once again gaining power from their deaths.

His grimace deepened, and he scowled at the ten people remaining on the mountain. “We are feeding the Fated’s power with all these deaths. This is exactly what he wants!”

“I don’t care!” Monica scowled at him. “Nobody’s taking my pin. I suffered greatly for it.”

“Somebody’s going to have to take it eventually.” Sebastian grimaced. “The longer we spend on the mountain, the less time we have to actually prepare for the calamity.”

“This is preparing.” Orin spoke, his entire demeanor tense. “Technically, my chances here are greater than they’ll be in the final battle.”

“You don’t even have a stake here!” Monica scowled at him. “You didn’t contribute a single pin to the ten.”

“That doesn’t mean I’m going to give it up,” Orin sneered. “And if you were in my shoes, you would do the same.”

Aodhán sighed, the thunder in his voice slipping as frustration mounted. “I think it’s clear now that moving is a death sentence. Let’s talk then.”

“Talking isn’t going to get us anywhere.” Monica outrightly refused.

“Neither is fighting, you fucking meathead!” Baxter scowled at her. “If we are going to survive the coming calamity, then we’ll have to be alive and strengthened for it. We cannot afford to kill each other off and waste willpower. Personally, I’d rather we talked.”

“Baxterion is right,” Mirith finally spoke. “We all want the pins. We can either vote for it—”

“Voting is out!” Sebastian quickly interrupted, his stance slightly panicked as he shot a glance at Aodhán. “There are ten of us here. But half are allied to one person.”

His gaze hardened as it swept past Destra, Brennon, Seojun, Daruk, and Baxter. “It wouldn’t be fair to any one of us.”

Aodhán blinked and then glanced around too, realizing the truth in Sebastian’s words. If they went the route of a vote, it was very possible that he would gain a majority of the voters. He hadn’t even realized it.

But now that Sebastian pointed it out, everyone else was suddenly realizing it.

“Well,” Mirith said, her lips thinning into a grimace. “This changes things considerably.”

“Yes, it does.” Sebastian let out a smile of relief. “Mathematically, our best bet—”

“Save your bets and probabilities, Rune!” Monica scowled. “No one is falling for your selfish little probabilities here.”

“Can I just say, I’m not interested in all of this?” Seojun spoke, eyes slightly panicked. “I was earlier, but”—his gaze darted to a puddle of blood and viscera on the floor—“but not anymore. I officially bow out of both conversation and fight. I’ll take my chances against the calamity.”

“Me too,” Brennon spoke quickly. “I’m just an illusionist. I’m not cut out for all this stuff.”

“Wise,” Aodhán nodded, slightly relieved that their numbers had just dropped to eight. He glanced at Destra, whose gaze was instead locked on Daruk until he prompted. “Destra, are you bowing out too?”

Destra glanced at him, glanced at Daruk, and back at him before nodding. “Yeah. I’m bowing out.”

Orin smiled widely. “That brings our numbers down to seven—”

“You fucking swine!” Monica snarled. “You and Baxter should bow out too if you have any shame left.”

“I have none.” Orin snarled back at her.

“I have none, either.” Baxter spoke, his eyes hard. “But I will bow out too. Not because of you, Monica.” He glanced at Aodhán and smiled. “But because of you.”

Aodhán nodded, and then a voice he hadn’t expected spoke too.

“I too will bow out,” Daruk spoke for the first time since the crimson screen appeared, his gaze cold and calculative. He glanced at Aodhán. “There’s no point fighting against each other for something I would use for you if it ever comes down to it. I’m rooting for you, Aodhán.”

Aodhán exhaled deeply, feeling a surge of emotion well within him. With Daruk bowing out, only five of them were left. He, Monica, Sebastian, Mirith, and Orin.

Aodhán scowled and turned to glare at the only odd member remaining among them. Out of the five of them, only Orin hadn’t contributed a single pin to the pile. It was understandable at first, since everyone was fighting selfishly, but with everyone else having bowed out, Orin's insistence to fight them for something he had no right to was annoying.

Unfortunately for Orin, all of them shared the same views—and in that moment, four pairs of eyes turned on Orin with such murderous intensity that he visibly paled, his earlier bravado crumbling as he realized he was now the only one standing between four apex predators and their prize.

Silence stretched between them, heavy with unspoken violence. Then Aodhán spoke.

“Let me break this down for you, Orin. You’re at the 35th tier, only three tiers above me, but I swear to you right now that you’re the weakest person among us. If we were to fight, I would defeat you in under a minute. Your chances of survival aren’t as high as you think they are.”

Aodhán let an animalistic snarl appear on his face, Varéc’s nature pouring into him. “You have two choices here.” Stay and die. Or bow out now.”

Orin glanced at them all, genuinely scared for his life. But his mind was too clouded by greed to see reason.

“I—” his voice cracked. He swallowed hard, and then his gaze hardened. “I’m not bowing out. I’ll fight. I have every right to fight.”

Aodhán grimaced. “Then you leave me no choice.”

In a blur too fast for Orin to react, he teleported, hands clamping onto Orin’s skull. {Absorb Energy} activated instantly, locking the man in place as the energy of his core bled into Aodhán.

Orin’s eyes widened in terror as his essence drained away, and though it only lasted a few seconds, he stumbled the moment Aodhán’s skill finally released him—a wave of dizziness slamming into his mind like a sledgehammer.

“What have you done to me?” He gasped, his body shaking from the sudden loss of power. “You… you—”

“If you take a long nap now, you’ll be fully recovered before the calamity.” Aodhán spoke. “Be thankful I didn’t drain you completely dry. Regardless of what you may think, I just did you a favor.” He glanced at the others, who were all staring at him with troubled expressions. “I doubt they would have been so kind.”

“I wouldn’t.” Mirith smiled lightly. “You truly have been done a favor.”

Orin shuddered and shook his head. “I bow out.”

Aodhán nodded somberly and glanced at his opponents. It seems he would be partaking in a tournament after all. Only this time, it was a lawless, macabre version of a sport he'd once looked forward to with excitement.
