Varéc bared his fangs in a grin as he phased through stone and soil—his form a crackling haze of silver lightning. The earth parted around him like water, offering no resistance to his immaterial passage.

Four hundred meters or a thousand—distance meant little to Varéc. It was simply space to be conquered, and despite Aodhán's warnings to avoid any sort of fighting, Varéc had every intention of testing his dazzling majesty against whatever waited beneath.

Of course, he couldn't conquer the dragon itself, but if it had any spawns, then Varéc wouldn't hesitate to measure himself against one of them. Just to see how he compared to real dragons in terms of power.

Excitement sang through him as he phased deeper, and his grin widened, revealing more of his fangs. This whole expedition reminded him of his solo adventure in the sky dungeon—the primal thrill he had felt, the burning satisfaction that came from ripping prey to pieces—oh, such excitement!

This time, however, Varéc doubted he would be the predator he had been in the dungeon or that it would be as much fun. The lives of nearly a hundred people hung in the balance, and it was up to him to right the scales, or at the very least tilt them a bit.

But most importantly, he was determined not to fail Aodhán, if only to prove to his bond that he wasn't quite as reckless as he thought.

The last of the earth parted, and the chamber's ceiling was soon revealed—smooth concrete covered in an intricate network of purple and black runes. Void essence wafted off the concrete like smoke—growing in intensity as the black null runes flickered, sparked, and dimmed.

The runes were failing.

Varéc gulped at the sight and firmed his resolve. He could do this. The dragon was kin. It would listen to him. It had to listen to him. Aodhán's life was at stake if he failed.

Bracing himself against the static interference snapping and lashing at his lightning form, Varéc phased closer to the chamber, feeling an overpowering aura of void emanating from below.

The aura washed over him, and Varéc tensed, expecting to experience the terror Aodhán had felt only minutes ago. Instead, he felt what he determined to be curiosity.

That was a good start. The dragon wasn't about to kill him on the spot.

In response to the dragon's curiosity, Varéc let himself exude an aura of calm and friendliness. We are the same, he thought to himself. You are a dragon, and I, a fury. We are kin. We are brothers.

As if in response to his thoughts, the dragon's curiosity grew and suddenly turned inviting.

Varéc grinned, accepting the invitation.

Without wasting time, he dove through the stone walls, ready to face the dragon as a brother, but he hadn't considered what effect the smattering of runes would have on his lightning form.

They burned.

Like iron brands, they stabbed into his spirit, ripping the skill apart just as he appeared within the dragon's chamber. His form flickered—phasing between tangible and immaterial for a moment before settling on tangible.

Varéc lost control of his flight immediately, and his body smashed into the concrete floor with a loud boom, scattering dust, bones, and debris in every direction.

"Well, that's an entry." A voice resounded loudly, spoken in a tongue Varéc had never heard before but understood clearly.

Draconian.

He pushed himself to his full height immediately and looked up, only to freeze as his gaze locked with that of a creature more than four times his size. It hung suspended in the air before him, held up by a trio of null chains so large that each link was nearly the size of his head.

But as majestic as the creature was—even when bound—it was its eyes that drew Varéc's attention. Twin slits of inky nothingness that seemed to drink in the light around them.

The dragon was regal and magnificent—exuding a sense of power and authority that Varéc hadn't gained yet.

Without hesitation, Varéc bowed low, his wings folding against his sides. "I greet you, oh great one. My name is Varéc Brystion, and I have come here to speak with you."

The dragon regarded him for a moment, its massive head tilting slightly. "I see you've summoned the courage to come see me. It was you I sensed earlier."

Varéc didn't bother correcting the dragon's assumptions. "What may I call you, oh great dragon?"

The dragon's chest swelled in pride, and it growled. "I am Serythion. The last of the Voidborn Brood. The unbound—" Its words trailed off, tail swishing in embarrassment. "I guess I cannot call myself the unbound anymore."

Serythion unfurled his wings, showing where the chains hooked painfully into his sternum. "For now, I am Serythion the bound." His snout peeled back to reveal sharp fangs as he raised his right wing. "But I won't be that for much longer."

And that was when Varéc noticed it—a gaping hole in Serythion's right sternum where a fourth chain should have been.

"You're breaking free," Varéc noted, excitement and dread warring within him.

"I certainly am," Serythion growled, his jaws parting in a lipless snarl to expose an intimidating number of curved fangs. "Those mindless humans on the surface have finally lost their minds."

And then he laughed—an echoing boom that shook the entire chamber and caused the chains to rattle loudly. Varéc felt a chill spread through him at that, and he realized he had gotten carried away. He had a mission to carry out here. He wasn't just here to make friends with this lonely dragon.

But before he proceeded with his mission, Varéc had one more question. "Where are your spawns?"

Serythion swished his tail to the left, and Varéc followed the motion to see a pile of dragon bones in one corner, some still carrying flecks of meat.

"You've eaten them?" Varéc rumbled in shock.

"I have to eat something." Serythion replied, his words resonating through the air. "It's been weeks since I woke up from my induced slumber. I needed nutrients." His pupils gleamed, and he subtly leaned closer, his snout scrunching into a grimace. "You don't seem very edible, Fury. I cannot smell your blood."

"That is because I am not a being of flesh and blood, like your spawns, Serythion. I am a familiar. A being of chaos and storm bonded to a human."

The mention of the word 'human' triggered Serythion badly, and he reacted with a sharp hiss, his tail lashing out behind him in agitation.

"You are friends with the two-leggeds?" Serythion boomed loudly, void essence rippling out of him in a wave as his pupils narrowed further. "I should have realized it when you descended from the surface. What have you come for, Fury? Have the two-leggeds sent you here to monitor me?"

"No, nothing like that," Varéc growled hastily, shielding himself from the surging void essence with a layer of lightning. "I am not friends with the cultists who did this horrible thing to you. I want those people dead as much as you do."

"And yet, you're bonded to one!" Serythion roared, black-void flames erupting from his body as his rage grew. Space undulated around him, and more spatial tears appeared, their edges crackling with dark energy that bent light in sickening spirals.

"The one I am bonded to is not a cultist!" Varéc roared, his voice resounding like thunder to get Serythion's attention.

Fortunately, it worked. Serythion stilled.

"I am no friend of cultists," Varéc thundered, his scales crackling with energy. "I am a victim, as you are. My bond is a victim. Everyone in this realm—beast and two-legged alike—are all victims. Do you not see? Your wrath favors the very ones who chained you."

"You lie!" Serythion hissed, the words stretching out like a blade dragged across stone. Bloodlust erupted from his body, and heat began to gather as Serythion opened his mouth to burn Varéc to crisps.

Varéc doubted he could escape. So, he did the next best thing.

He attacked—a beam of red lightning erupting out of his mouth to strike Serythion.

The condensed beam struck.

And failed woefully.

So woefully that Serythion burst out in laughter, his broad chest rising and caving as mirth overtook him. More spatial tears appeared in the air, but this time, they weren't quite as terrifying.

"Pitiful," Serythion hissed as his laughter died down. "If you intend to kill me, Fury, you'll have to put your back into it."

Varéc bristled, his scales lighting up in embarrassment. "That's because I didn't intend to hurt you. I simply wanted to get your attention. If you will listen to me, I will explain why we aren't your enemy."

Serythion let out a sharp snort, his tail lashing dismissively. "There's nothing to tell. Your words will not change my mind, little fury."

"What if I tell you that those people on the surface are captives just like you are?" Varéc growled, and for a moment, Serythion's tail paused mid-swing before resuming its agitated motion, his posture shifting to show curiosity.

"What do you mean?"

"That the cultists are playing both sides," Varéc growled low, each word deliberate. "They captured you and the other creatures, imprisoned you here, and then kidnapped dozens of humans in a twisted form of entertainment." Varéc's tone grew agitated. "They want both sides to kill each other for their own amusement."

Serythion hissed, his massive body groaning as he lowered his head to Varéc, the chains digging deeper into his sternum. "Are you trying to tell me that the two-leggeds are deriving joy and pleasure from my bondage?"

"Not all the two-leggeds," Varéc clarified quickly, meeting Serythion's void stare with his own. "Those currently on the surface are victims just like you are—ripped away from their families and loved ones to participate in this twisted harvest for the cultists' entertainment. By killing them, you're just playing into the cultists' hands. Have you not wondered why the two-leggeds are freeing you from your bondage?"

Serythion raised his gigantic head toward the chains holding him up, and he let out a soundless hiss that caused Varéc's form to rattle uncontrollably. "I thought they were mocking me. So drunk on power that they didn't mind releasing me."

"That's not what they are doing. The cultists want you and the other creatures to kill them. They also want them to kill you. If we are to defy them, then we must play a different game."

Serythion was quiet for a long moment before shaking his massive head. "You and your bond cannot convince the others to heed you. The Wolf perhaps, but the Basilisk and the Phoenix have lost their minds. They will never stand by your side."

"We don't need them to," Varéc growled, raising himself to his full height. "We want to survive. And if we can get the two of you on our side, then we stand a chance."

Serythion hummed, fixing Varéc with his piercing gaze. "If all you have said is true, then the hatred I have nursed has been misdirected. But words are wind, little fury. How do I know you're telling the truth?"

Varéc stuttered, his wings folding and unfolding nervously. "I cannot offer you an oath if that's what you want."

"Then you must offer me something else," Serythion spoke, the chamber walls shuddering from the resonance. "Offer me a promise to release me from this prison before the time elapses. If you and your bond can do that, then I'll fight alongside you."

"And how would I know that you are telling the truth?" Varéc asked.

Serythion let out a rumble of laughter, his tail swishing in amusement. "Because, unlike you, little fury, I have no problems swearing an oath. I swear on my core to fight alongside the surface dwellers if you and your bond can set me free in a day. If I'm to face the other creatures, then I will need time to regain my strength and create new spawns."

Varéc growled with renewed respect as he sensed the oath lock in, tightening around Serythion's core like a vise.

"Then I shall speak to my bond, and we will try our hardest to set you free before a day elapses."

Serythion grinned, the corners of his massive snout curling upward to reveal sharp ivory fangs. "Then it is settled. I shall await my freedom, and the moment I am free, I will fight alongside you and your people in rebellion against the ones who have put us here."

Varéc smiled, a deep rumble vibrating in his chest. "I look forward to fighting alongside you. Perhaps, I will learn a thing or two."

Serythion let out another full-bodied laugh, the chains in his sternum rattling loudly. "I look forward to teaching you. Now, go on, little fury. I will not keep my word if I am not free by the end of the day."

Varéc lowered his head in deference. "Thank you for granting me a listening ear. You shall hear from me again when the day is over. By then, your name shall be Serythion the Unbound."

Void essence rippled out in a wave as Serythion laughed loudly. "I look forward to it, little fury. I look forward to it."

Varéc raised himself into the air, and after one final glance at Serythion, he activated {Lightning Body—Silver}, infused the skill with a massive surge of willpower, and phased through the ceiling, runes snapping at his form in a desperate attempt to tear the skill apart.

They failed, and for the second time that day, the earth parted around Varéc without resistance, his heart filled with hope.

He had done it. He had gotten the dragon on their side. Now they just had to find a way to set it free within twenty-four hours. Varéc had no idea how they could possibly do that, but he was sure Aodhán would come up with an answer.

After all, he had done his own part.

Back in the Amphitheatre, a tense silence reigned as elders looked at each other uneasily, their gazes subtly darting to the still empty throne of the Fated.

It had been eighteen hours since the Fated had left for his 'walk,' and since then he had not returned. The handlers had stopped collecting the bodies of the dead, leaving them to rot in Abyssos instead, because the Fated was nowhere to be found.

"Where is he?" Elder Voss whispered to Elder Makin agitatedly. "This is a brutal waste of resources. How can the Fated be so careless?"

Elder Makin shrugged. "He already has many of these skills, so he isn't losing much. And I doubt he'll be pained to lose the thread affinity."

"Still." Elder Voss grimaced. "It is wrong—"

A roar from the screen cut his words off, and everyone leaned toward the projection instinctively, eyes widening as they watched the dragon familiar of the transmigrant appear from the earth, whole and unwounded.

"How is this possible?" Elder Makin breathed in shock. "Serythion should have eaten it alive."

But the familiar's appearance wasn't the only shock they were about to receive, because a moment later, the transmigrant and his group began jumping about in celebration.

It seemed they had managed to convince the dragon to fight on their side.

The elders glanced at each other in panic. Where was the Fated when they needed him?
