Riley sat across from Nikola at the long wooden meeting table inside the tower. Sunlight streamed through the narrow windows and cast warm rectangles across the polished surface. The rest of the command team had already left for their duties, leaving the two of them alone in the quiet room. A faint scent of fresh pine lingered from the newly installed beams overhead.

Riley leaned forward slightly and rested her hands on the table. “We need to go over the build for the next little while.”

Nikola nodded once, his expression calm and precise as always. He folded his hands in his lap before he spoke. “As directed, I have focused on leveling the buildings we have. All buildings except the tower are level three. The tower will reach level three by the end of today. We have also gained another forty level one soldiers.”

Riley allowed a small smile to touch her lips. The numbers felt solid after the round-the-clock effort the two had put in. She drew in a slow breath and continued. “Great. We need to talk about the options for research. Most of the options were already maxed out. Resource yields determine how much troops can collect while gathering per hour. Carry capacity shows how much soldiers can bring back per gathering trip. Resource building production yield controls how much each of the resource buildings can produce per hour.”

She paused for a moment and met Nikola’s steady gaze. “Then there is wall defense. Wall durability only has one more level of research to complete. That measures how much damage a wall can take before it falls. Wall repair speed determines how long it would take to repair a wall after it suffers damage from an enemy siege machine like a catapult. When that happens I will be able to select the section of damaged wall and repair it through the HUD. That repair ties to a timer and once the timer runs out the wall will be like new again. Or at least that is how it worked on all the games I played at home.”

Nikola listened without interruption, his dark eyes reflecting quiet calculation. The afternoon light shifted across the table between them as the settlement continued its steady rhythm outside the tower walls. The distant ring of hammers on anvils and the sharp calls of drill instructors drifted in on the breeze.

Riley continued speaking in a measured tone while she kept her hands flat on the smooth wood of the table. “Soldier training speed has every level maxed out already. That has cut the time it takes to train new level one soldiers down to less than four hours. The next step will be to level up the Academy so we can unlock the next tier of research expansion opportunities.”

“I have seen things like many different wall defense opportunities become available in the past. Archers could defend from arrow slits or loopholes that were narrow vertical openings in walls or even a small tower built into the walls for shooting while staying protected. This type of defense worked well against other ranged type soldiers. Wall spikes were large poles sticking out of the wall sharpened to a spike at one end and proved more effective against cavalry soldiers. Rolling timber would be dropped from the wall and dealt real damage, most effective against infantry soldiers. While all of these defenses would damage all troops, they seemed to deal the most damage to specific troop types. All this being said, we will have to see what becomes available as we level up.”

She leaned back slightly in her chair and let her gaze drift toward the window for a moment before she returned it to Nikola. “Based on my experience level five should usually unlock some exciting new opportunities.”

Nikola gave a small nod of acknowledgment and waited for Riley to go on.

“The last research still running is the final level of wall durability and that will finish today. The combination of leveling up the walls through the building timer and the research together decides how strong our walls really are. It is quite common for players to pursue secondary tiers of research to add even more strength to their walls. Those higher tiers take significantly longer to complete. Lower level players might need only hours or days for a single level while higher level players often need weeks or months. Those advanced tiers also demand many times more resources. That is why higher-level players end up needing millions of units of each resource while lower-level players only require hundreds or thousands to finish their levels.”

Riley paused and let the weight of the explanation settle between them while outside the distant calls of soldiers shouting drill commands drifted through the open windows. She let her fingers trace a faint groove in the tabletop as she spoke again. The wood felt cool and solid beneath her touch. She kept her voice steady even though the realities she described pressed heavily on her mind. “This often meant a higher-level player’s attacks would wipe out the lower-level players sometimes easily. This is the truth we now will have to face.”

She drew a slow breath and met Nikola’s eyes directly. “Level one soldiers were nowhere near as strong as level four soldiers. A novice might assume that it would take four level one soldiers to match one level four soldier. That was never the case. This was a very complex question with a number of factors that needed to be considered. How much soldier research had been completed affected how tough the troops were. Most games called it soldier health which meant how much damage they could take before they ended up in the hospital and needed to be healed. Another factor was how much research had been completed for soldier attack strength which meant how much damage the troops would deal when they attacked. Finally, there was soldier defense which meant how much of the enemy soldiers’ attack the troops could defend and neutralize. That would impact soldier casualty rates.”

Riley paused and let the silence stretch for a moment while the distant clang of training weapons echoed faintly from the courtyard. She shifted in her chair and continued in the same even tone. “Another important note about the hospitals was that once the beds of the hospital were full the soldiers would die. There was no healing them.”

Nikola remained perfectly still, his hands folded neatly in his lap as he absorbed every word without interruption. These were all in game or system factors, she reminded herself. She had wondered since she had started to build out her base here whether she could take a level one soldier and upgrade their armor not through the system but by forging real armor and making them wear it. Would that armor increase the soldier’s health or the amount of damage he could withstand? More specifically would that effectively make a level one soldier equal to a level two soldier because he could endure more punishment? Would the same hold true if she upgraded their weapons as well?

She knew the answer even as the questions formed in her mind. Even if all those improvements worked the way she hoped, they still would not make a level one soldier equal to a level four soldier. The gap remained too wide. The most she could hope for was that if it normally took ten level one soldiers to bring down a single level four soldier, then perhaps she would only need eight after the modifications.

Nikola finally asked, “If the Clawborn have been building their army for several months or years, wouldn’t they be many levels higher than ours? And wouldn’t they have a large number of high level soldiers?”

Riley looked at Nikola as the silence hung between them and the tension grew, gradually drowning out the noise of the world beyond the tower walls.

A firm knock sounded at the heavy wooden door. Riley straightened as the door swung open and a young soldier stepped inside carrying two wooden buckets. He set them down carefully on the floor beside the table. “Riley, here are the samples you asked for.” One bucket held a fine white powder that caught the light like fresh snow. The other contained a coarse brimstone yellow material that gave off a faint earthy scent.

Riley looked at the buckets while all the thoughts of strategy continued to reel through her head. The white powder gleamed softly and the yellow chunks sat heavy and dull in their container. She studied them for several long seconds, letting the weight of her earlier calculations settle.

Then in a single moment she stopped and turned her full attention back to Nikola, who had been waiting patiently for the answer to his simple question and now watched her expectantly. Riley finally said, “Let us talk about this mine.”
