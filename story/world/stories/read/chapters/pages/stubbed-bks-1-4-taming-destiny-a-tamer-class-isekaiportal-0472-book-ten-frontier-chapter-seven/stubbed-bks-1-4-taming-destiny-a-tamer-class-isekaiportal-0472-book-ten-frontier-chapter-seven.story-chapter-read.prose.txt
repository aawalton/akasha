“Are you sure this is a good idea? It’s entirely untested.”

I feel like I should be the one asking the question, but instead it’s Valence voicing his doubt. He’s pulled me to the side of the square for a private word while Sarran, Keshaka, Tiria, Elarion, Master Mirena, and River are all busy preparing the ritual. They each have different skills that will hopefully see us to success.

As it turns out, Elarion has had some experience with rituals before, as has Keshaka. Tiria hasn’t actually conducted any herself, but learning the theory is part of most mages’ training. Master Mirena’s presence is obvious – a ritual as intricate as this one does better with runes to help guide the Energy. Sarran is there to place the Cores – he knows the theory of where they should go from when Nicholas brought me over – and River is there to mix the reagents and paint them where she’s told. Honestly, Sera Kerst is more skillful and might have been a better choice than my companion, but the others all agreed that having one of my Bonded be part of setting up the ritual would have an impact on its success, and Hunter isn’t yet practised enough at runes to take Lizil’s place.

It’s already only a couple of hours before dawn – designing and verifying the ritual theory took the rest of the day yesterday and some of the night. The rest of the time has been spent setting it up – greater stats might mean people can move quicker and with more precision, but we can’t risk any errors. The best case failure scenario would be that the ritual sputters out quietly – and with how close dawn is, even that would have significant consequences. The worst case is that the ritual will detonate with the power of a nuclear bomb.

We considered completing the ritual in a different location – one far from everything we’ve built – but there were too many issues with that and no one could suggest a location that would offer equal chances of success as here. So instead, we’ve taken preventative measures just in case of disaster.

“It’s the only idea we have,” I sigh, finally answering Valence’s quiet question.

While we’ve been working on the ritual, the other mages have been creating thickly-walled, enchanted stone bunkers to shelter our population. On that note…. “Please sound the call for everyone to evacuate,” I ask Valence, doing my best to hide any misgiving. After all, they will all be sheltered, but I’ll be at ground zero.

Valence dips his head.

“Yes, my lord,” he acknowledges. Then, hesitating a moment, he continues. “I am sure it will all go well but…if the worst happens, I wish to say that you have been an inspiration to me. I…You’ve shown me what leadership is and…I promise I’ll do my best to be a better one.”

With that, his ears a little pink, he turns and hurries away. I’m left staring at his back, feeling a mixture of emotions. Pride, for sure – he’s come a long way from the man I once saw assaulting a woman he saw as a mere object. Embarrassment – at the praise that I’m not so sure I’m due. And nerves – he wouldn’t say such things unless he was sure there was a good chance that he won’t be able to say them later.

But I can’t think such things. My thoughts shape reality – far more so here than back on Earth.

It isn’t long before the six preparing the ritual call me forwards. It’s just as well that our time so far on the Lost Continent has been fruitful in terms of Cores – five of the Cores used in the ritual are Tier three. Three of those are Cores that I'm directed to place personally – having me play this final part will apparently increase the chances of the ritual succeeding.

This is a very expensive ritual even before the other Tier two Cores and reagents are taken into account. But when the alternative is a battle against a Tier four, I am willing to pay the cost – even more so when it’s Kalanthia’s mate we’d be fighting.

Finally, I’m directed into the smaller circle of space that sits adjacent to a much bigger circle. Even if I hadn’t been part of the conversation that designed it – a mostly silent, listening part, but present nonetheless – I would understand what the two circles are for. One for me to stand; the other for Kalanthia to appear.

The intricate design curls around us like vines, Cores blooming at the heart of its flowers, their roots forming the circles. Unlike an actual vine, however, instead of nutrients feeding from roots to flowers, this will be the very opposite.

“Now, just to reiterate, before you use your Skill, you must connect into the ritual. Do you recall how to do so?” Elarion asks, his tone brisk, but I don’t think I’m imagining the hint of nerves. This ritual will make history one way or another. Either by being a complete success, or by killing the dimension-travelling heir of House Titanbend.

“I remember – in theory, anyway,” I reply. I’m surprised about how calm I am at the prospect of my death. Perhaps I’ve just faced it too many times by this point for it to make me shake and shiver. Or perhaps even my subconscious knows there is no better choice.

“It’s easy enough,” Elarion dismisses. “We’ve removed all requirements for Words of Power since you’re not familiar with them. Just make sure you place your hand onto the knot of roots between you and the receiving circle and don’t let go,” he emphasises.

I nod stiffly, choosing not to remind him that I’ve heard all of that several times before. I know as well as anyone that ensuring things go to plan is literally a matter of life and death.

“I understand.”

Elarion fidgets uncharacteristically with his robe. Is he fretting? I wonder to myself. And if so, what is he most afraid of – that I will die, or that he will be blamed for it?

The thoughts feel distant. Maybe I’m more affected by fear than I thought.

Elarion nods.

“Lady Luck bless you,” he wishes me, then turns quickly on his heel before I can reply, heading towards the others who are tidying up their tools.

“We must go,” he tells them briskly. On their way to the bunkers, they stop to wish me luck. I notice that Tiria has tears in her eyes as she hurries out at the words, barely able to look at me while Keshaka actually looks troubled. River pauses the longest, indecision written across her face as she looks like she might even step over the thick chalk line that encircles the whole of the ritual design.

I could stay, she offers. Perhaps I could help.

“You already have,” I tell her, shoving my appreciation down the link. “I want you to be safe. Then I’ll be able to concentrate fully on the ritual.”

What if I’ve made a mistake? she frets. My Master would have done a better job.

“Yet would Sera Kerst have taken the amount of care you have over each and every reagent she mixed and painted?”

River is silent.

I smile at her, though can’t help a hint of sadness from entering it.

“It’ll be fine,” I tell her reassuringly. “I promise.”

She and I both know I can’t actually make that promise, but she pretends to believe it.

Nodding jerkily, she wrenches herself away from me, hurrying away without a backwards glance.

I clench my fist to stop myself from reaching out to her, clamping down on my urge to call her back. Everyone else is either in the bunker or outside the settlement completely. Artio and the two deris have gone to keep Lathani company – and to keep an eye on the Tier four nunda.

As soon as Elarion gives me the go-ahead, letting me know that everyone has made it to the bunker and that it’s sealed, I take a deep breath.

Glancing at my watch I see that we’re running out of time – less than a candlemark until dawn.

For a moment, I allow myself to think of my companions, of those I might be leaving behind. And then I forcibly turn my thoughts to Kalanthia and concentrate on bringing her back safely.

It’s go time.

Kneeling down, I reach out and place my right hand on the knot of runes as Elarion told me to.

Closing my eyes, I touch them with my magic. Immediately, I feel the humming network of Energy that flows through them. It’s most similar to the Pure Energy I interacted with in the samuran’s world, but it’s not quite that – filled with colour rather than mostly white, and slightly duller. Though, when both feel like staring into the centre of the sun, the difference is mostly academic. Perhaps my comparison earlier to nuclear power was more apt than I had thought.

And I’m procrastinating. Taking a deep breath, I force myself to do more than just look at the power that waits for me. Instead, I dig deeply into it, connecting myself into the network.

I stiffen, barely managing to keep my hand flat on the knot of runes as power floods into me with all the gentleness of ten thousand volts of electricity.

My channels burn, my nerves shriek, and I’m almost certain I can smell burning flesh – mine.

But this is for a reason. Dialling Sensation Management up to the maximum, I use the clarity its relief from pain brings me to focus on my objective.

Kalanthia, Kalanthia, Kalanthia, I chant in my mind, visualising her, summoning her. With any luck, this will give her a little bit of warning even if I can’t speak to her. I briefly spare a thought to pray that she won’t be too angry with me for interrupting her. And then I trigger my Skill. Bond Teleport.

For a fraction of a second, the world pauses. And then I scream as power is ripped through me.

It’s like I’m a lightning rod and I’ve just been struck by three simultaneous lightning bolts. Bond Teleport demands power and the ritual provides. I’m just the conduit.

Dipping into my Core space, concern grips me as I see just how brightly Bond Teleport is burning. No longer gold, it’s a bright white – and most of my internal matrix is dim in comparison. The only parts that are as bright as my Skill are the lines leading in from my hand, the channels swollen with power.

Time seems to move slower in my Core space than it does when I’m settled in my body, but even so, that doesn’t explain just how long Bond Teleport continues dragging in more and more power.

And my internal matrix can’t handle it. Tension grips me as I observe the channels completing the circuit between the ritual network and my Skill – they’re beginning to fray and fracture.

Zooming closer, I try to figure out how to support them – if they go, all that power will just flood into me – or Bond Teleport will run low on the fuel it needs to work.

But my fear only increases as every attempt I make fails.

The biggest issue is that I can’t feed my own mana down the channels – they’re already full to bursting with Energy. And they’re so swollen that I can’t even feed Energy into the walls of the channels.

If I can’t support the channels directly, what can I do?

That I need to do something is clear – every moment that passes leads to them fragmenting just a little more.

I need to think of something out of the box…or out of the channels.

And then my attention pauses on the luminous blackness that surrounds the swollen conduits of Energy. It looks like a void, but I know it isn’t – it’s my soul infusing my body. Perhaps I can do something with that….

Diving deeper, I resist going into Deep Meditation – I need to keep an eye on my Skill – but still reach into the luminous blackness, confident that I can affect it. It’s my soul and my body, after all – why shouldn’t I be able to access them in whatever state I am?

For a worryingly long moment, reaching for my soul feels like grabbing at mist and grasping at my body feels no easier.

The channels begin to buckle; I feel a sharp jolt of nameless sensation as a spear of Energy escapes and pierces the luminous blackness, making it flicker alarmingly.

Once more I reach for the background of my Core space – I have to do this before my channels disintegrate, or I’m done for.

However, the damage has had one positive consequence – by affecting the luminous blackness, it has shown me a path to manipulating it.

This time, when I reach out, I do it with a grip that is somehow deeper and more real than before. The previously mist-like substance becomes tangible and I grasp it firmly. Then, using my Health energy as fuel, I encase the self-destructing channels with an iron grip.

To my relief, my gamble pays off: the support prevents any more fractures from happening. But I can do nothing about the fractures that have already damaged the channels. The ray of Energy that escaped continues to do so, burning me in a way that would probably be agonising if I were able to feel pain right now.

That’s bad enough, but even my stop-gap solution isn’t a situation that can endure forever – the way I’m using the luminous blackness is not what it’s supposed to do and I feel the energy seeping out of it. I know my health pool must be draining, though I can’t be sure about the rate.

I worry that I will run out of health before Bond Teleport completes its task, but then I notice that Health Share’s mass of channels is throbbing faintly, power leaking out from it and into the luminous blackness directly. That creates a new worry – I don’t want to accidentally kill any of my Bonded either.

Though, if the way Bond Teleport is starting to vibrate alarmingly, I might not need to be concerned – it looks to be a moment away from blowing up and taking me with it.

While still holding tightly to the channels, I reach out to touch Bond Teleport with my mind, wondering if there’s a problem.

Immediately, I’m flooded with the sensations of limit, obstacle, net, chains, veil, strain.

It’s muddled, but I instinctively know what to do.

Visualising a blade – the knife that accompanied me faithfully for so long in the other world – I let the Energy coursing through my channels fill it until it burns as brightly as everything else.

And then I let it go.

It flashes out of sight.

A moment later, all sense of restriction abruptly slackens, and I have the distinct sensation that an overextended rubber band has been suddenly released.

For a moment, the world once more pauses, and I sense that everything is on a knife’s edge between success and complete disaster.

And then Bond Teleport’s glow fades completely as all light escapes it. My grip with the luminous darkness clenches in on itself as the channels shatter into dust. I prepare to deal with the Energy ripping through my system, but that has vanished too.

Pulling myself back into my body, I’m hit by a wave of overwhelming sensation. Every muscle aches like it’s been beaten, every nerve is shrieking its complaints, and it feels like a vampire has drained me of blood. I don’t even want to know how much pain Sensation Management is holding back – this is bad enough.

“Next time, someone else can activate the experimental ritual,” I groan, lifting my head to look at the circle before me.

My heart leaps into my throat as I see a large mass of bloody fur.
