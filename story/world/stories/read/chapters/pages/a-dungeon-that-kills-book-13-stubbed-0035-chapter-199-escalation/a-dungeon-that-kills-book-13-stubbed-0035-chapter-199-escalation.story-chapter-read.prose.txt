What do you think, Celeste? Viktor asked his Dungeon Core over their private mental connection.

[About what, Master?]

What else? The conversation I’m having with Azran right now. I’m sure you’re also listening, despite that mountain of gossip you’re busy sorting through. So, are you the one who is maintaining the boundaries of our dungeon, or is it something else entirely?

[Well... I don’t know. I wish I had a more satisfying answer to give you than that, but I unfortunately do not.]

Viktor frowned. What do you mean, you don’t know? You’re the one who built the damn thing.

[Yes, I did. Being a Dungeon Core, I have access to the skills of the Path of the Dungeon, and it is through those skills that I do everything I do, from building the floors to summoning the minions. But how do those skills work? What is the mechanism behind them? Then I don’t know. Isn’t it the same with your own Path, Master? You craft the Thauma, and you use the Thauma. But you couldn’t explain the how and the why, right?]

Fair enough, Viktor conceded.

So perhaps, what maintained the boundaries was not anyone in particular, but rather the System itself.

And such a thought brought him back to the same questions that had been gnawing at the edges of his mind for a very long time. Who, exactly, had come up with all of this in the first place? Who had sat down and designed all these “paths” and “modules”? And who now stood somewhere unseen, overseeing how it all continued to function?

He had once assumed that the Gods themselves were behind it. But then, he had learned that the Paths were what allowed a mortal to climb to godhood, to become a deity in their own right. And that raised the obvious problem: what possible reason could they have to create a system capable of producing upstarts like Nakhran, who could stand toe to toe with them and openly challenge their rule?

He was still pondering that thought when his eyes wandered the mess hall and landed on a face he knew. Calyssa, the Chief Secretary, had just walked in, making her way toward a table where several employees of the Guild sat.

If Isadora has managed to dig up anything about the Iskora team, Viktor thought, then Calyssa might have heard about it from her.

Well, cosmic secrets and horrors could wait. He had a competition to win and a man to kill, and neither of those goals was going to accomplish themselves while he sat here chasing questions that had no answers.

“I have to go now,” he told Azran.

The bald man simply nodded, unbothered as ever, and Viktor rose from his seat, walking toward the bespectacled woman just as she was about to leave the mess hall.

“Chief Secretary!”

“Ah, Quinn.” She turned around. “You’re here, huh?”

“I came to see if there’s any news.”

Calyssa arched a brow. “What news?”

“About the team Iskora is putting together. You’re looking into it, I believe?”

“Ah, yes. The competition.” She let out a breath as if she had been holding it for ten years. “You have no idea how much trouble you’ve caused us by agreeing to that woman’s proposal.” She leaned closer, whispering in his ear. “Just between us, Isadora came back from the meeting absolutely furious, and things got even worse when Cedric told her his party would be the one representing Daelin. So, if I were you, I wouldn’t go anywhere near her for the time being.”

Mommy is mad, huh? Viktor chuckled to himself. Oh, well. All the more reason to find a way of luring the Daelin team away from the battle, before things had a chance to spiral out of control.

“Sorry about that,” he said, offering nothing more than a shrug.

“I’ve heard about your theory about the dark spirit from Rhea. So it seems you place more faith in him to get your revenge than you do in us to get justice for your sister. I won’t lie, that stings a little. But I can’t really blame you. We have made you wait long enough.”

Not quite, Viktor thought. It’s just that I want to take the matter into my own hands.

“Anyway,” he asked, “do you have any intelligence about the Iskora team?”

“What makes you think we’re looking into that?”

“Of course you are. You want to win, don’t you? So naturally you would want to learn everything you could about your opponent before the competition begins. Moreover, with Cedric joining the fray now, there’s no way the Guildmaster would sit back and stay idle.”

Calyssa nodded. “You’re right. She’s working on it. Not that there’s much to show at the moment, however. From what we’ve gathered, it looks like they’re in the process of selecting three or four adventurers from those on the west side to accompany Xander into the competition. Isadora is trying to learn their names, their ranks, their abilities, their past achievements.” She paused, rubbing her chin thoughtfully. “There’s also a rumor floating around. It’s entirely unconfirmed, and frankly rather hard to believe, so I’m not sure how much value to place on it.”

“What is it?”

“A Bronze-ranked adventurer might be joining their team.”

“Bronze?”

“Yes, absurd, isn’t it? Their team should be Xander and several Gold-ranked adventurers, and maybe Zubayr as well. What could a Bronze even contribute to this contest?”

The thing was, the last time when the dungeon had been invaded, when it had faced a threat serious enough that Celeste could very well have been lost forever were it not for Fianna’s sacrifice, the attack had been carried out by a party of four “Bronze.” So Viktor understood better than anyone that this could be a sign of serious trouble. For all he knew, the so-called Bronze might turn out to be the single most dangerous member of the enemy team.

Then again, this time there was no need for anyone to operate in secret, no reason to fake their ranks the way that earlier group had done. Which meant—

“It could be someone who was not an adventurer until recently,” Viktor said. “And by that I mean they registered just a few days ago, purely for the sake of joining this competition.”

“I see.” Calyssa’s eyes narrowed. “An accomplished warrior or mage brought in from outside, someone they’ve recruited specifically for this purpose. Yes, that’s the sort of thing they could’ve arranged. They’ve got the coin for it.” She nodded. “I’ll make sure to tell Isadora to look into it further.”

“If she finds anything, can you let me know?”

“Yes, but...” The bespectacled woman glanced at him. “What could you even do with that information?”

Viktor gave a shrug. “I can pass it to the dark spirit.”

“What?”

“You know that the dark spirit could be anyone, right? For all we know, he could be sitting in this very hall right now, listening to our conversation. So, if the information is allowed to circulate freely, spreading far and wide, eventually it would reach his ears. That would give him an edge in the upcoming battle, and me a better chance to have my vengeance.”

Calyssa sighed. “You really are all in on that plan of yours, aren’t you? I can’t say I like it, but fine, I’ll help where I can.” Then, unexpectedly, a smile spread across her face as she looked past him toward the entrance of the mess hall. “Someone is waiting for you over there.”

Viktor followed the direction of her gaze and found Rhea standing near the doorway. It seemed she had finally finished whatever remained of her work. Time to head back to the Sanctuary, he supposed.

So he bid farewell to Calyssa and turned to walk toward the girl.

However, he hadn’t taken more than three steps before Celeste’s voice rang in his head.

[Master, I found something.]

Oh?

So the tedious business of picking wheat from a mountain’s worth of chaff had finally turned up a grain worth having. Very well. He could hear about it on the way back to the Sanctuary.

What is it?

[I think you should come here as soon as possible and read it yourself, Master.]

Viktor arched a brow. It’s that important?

[Yes. I believe it was a conversation between Khalid, Zubayr, and Xander.]

What?

There was absolutely no reason for him to waste another second here once he had heard that.

And so, he walked over to Rhea and said, “Sorry. Something has come up. I have to head home right away. I won’t be sleeping at the Sanctuary tonight, so can you look after Claire for me?”

“Is... that so?” Her face fell for just a moment before she caught herself. “...Alright. I’ll take care of her, don’t you worry about a thing.”

He nodded and quickly took his leave.

Rhea seemed thoroughly disappointed despite her best efforts to hide it behind a composed expression. It was understandable, he supposed. Even though a simple walk back together was nothing in and of itself, a promise was still a promise, and he understood well enough what it meant to have someone tell you they would do something only to go back on their word at the last minute. But... this was far more important. He would find a way to make it up to her later. Well, he could buy her a meatwrap tomorrow. That would do. Probably.

He walked the rest of the way back to his house, climbed the stairs to his room, and closed the door behind him before calling his Dungeon Core.

“Celeste, teleport me to the dungeon.”

The world lurched around him in that familiar, stomach-turning way, and an instant later he found himself standing once more in the Core Room.

Glancing around, he spotted a single parchment resting on the table next to him. Was this the one? Without saying a word, he reached out, picked it up, and began to read.

“Our superiors were unhappy at first, but then, they’ve decided to give us one last chance to make it right. So this is your order. Ignore the competition. Go straight to the Core Room and destroy the Dungeon Core once you are there. If you can manage that, and only if you can manage that, you will have the full protection of the organization. No one, not even the Grandmaster of the Conclave himself, will be able to lay so much as a finger on you.”

“Destroy... the Dungeon Core?”

“Yes. Don’t ask why. Just do it.”

“But... what about the rest of the adventurers in our team? How exactly are we supposed to explain it to them?”

“Keep your voice down, Zubayr, damn it. Do you want them to hear us? We want no loose ends left when this is over, obviously, so just use them as fodder to clear a path to the Core Room. And if there happens to be any of them still breathing in the end, you know what needs to be done, right?”

“My pleasure.”

“Yes. That particular attitude of yours is precisely why I brought you into our organization in the first place, all those years ago. It came back to bite me rather badly in the end, but still, in a situation like this one, there’s no one better suited for the job than you.”

“So it’s just me, then, along with four or five of these idiots we’ve dragged along for the ride?”

“One more person is joining you. The organization has dispatched someone. An Apostle.”

“An... Apostle? Isn’t sending someone like that a bit overkill?”

“Our superiors have decided to fully commit this time around, holding nothing back. So watch yourself carefully from here on out, Xander. If you fail, you will be cut down on the spot.”

“Look at you, going right back to acting high and mighty the moment you think you are safe. Fine by me. You already know perfectly well that I’ll never run from a fight. Is that everything, then?”

“One more thing, actually...”

“What is it now? Just spit it out already.”

“...You’re going to meet our superiors tomorrow. They... they’ll teach you the Forbidden Words. In the worst-case scenario, use them.”

The inscription on the parchment stopped there. If Viktor had to guess, it was probably a conversation they had while they were at or near that staircase. Even though the quill only recorded the raw content without marking who said what, once he considered the tone of each exchange, it was not too difficult to figure out which line belonged to which of those three men.

Now, back to the actual substance of what he had just read. It appeared this was going to be a full invasion after all, exactly what he had feared it might become. And their goal this time, startlingly, was not to steal Celeste away as they had attempted before, but to destroy her outright.

Why?

What could they possibly hope to achieve by doing something so drastic?

Viktor shook his head, setting the question aside. He could puzzle over the why later, once there was time to spare for such things. What mattered the most right now was finding a way to prevent them from succeeding at whatever they were pursuing.

“An Apostle, and the Forbidden Words,” he said. “Those are the weapons they will bring against us this time around. All of a sudden, Xander now seems to be the least of our problems. Do you have any idea what either of them actually mean?”

[No, Master. That was exactly why I wanted you to come back here as quickly as possible, so that we could start working out our countermeasures before it is too late.]

“How are we supposed to come up with any solution if we don’t even know what we’re defending against?” Viktor dropped heavily into the chair nearby. “Well, I guess it’s time for our friendship with the Eternal Flame to prove its worth. Send Galatea over to Ekon’s place. Ask him whether that friend of his knows anything about those terms, and if she does, what price she wants in exchange for sharing that information.”

[Understood.]
