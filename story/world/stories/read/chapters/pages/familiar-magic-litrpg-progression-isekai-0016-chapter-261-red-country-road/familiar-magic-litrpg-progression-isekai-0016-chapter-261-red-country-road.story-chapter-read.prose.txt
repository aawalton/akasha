After another two weeks on Potentia, everyone had fallen into their rhythm. Selina monitored the many Rune Circles keeping the whole operation running, while researching anything interesting they found in their travels. Apollo kept up his duty of hovering above the growing army, bringing more and more demons into the fold. He increasingly granted his Familiar Angelos control of his body, as the strain and boredom of his role was taxing him.

Thankfully, the Holy Guardian Beast considered taming Potentia to be an excellent use of its time and skills, and was completely on board with their plan.

Ethan’s days had become far more productive once he began using the Deevees as scouts. Not only were they elusive and hard for predators to catch, but they spotted a viable target from the air, they were already in place for Ethan to use them as rift anchors. He was growing quite proficient at wrangling Twilights, as he thought of it.

Two followed him now, the strange, rhinoceros types he’d first encountered in the Forgotten City, what seemed like a lifetime ago. They were armored, not overly fast, and their heads were so small as to have disappeared into their massive shoulders, but heavy tusks shot forward for ramming. Ethan loved it when Deevee discovered more of their kind, as they were easy to taunt, and would seemingly chase him across the world and back. They were also marvelously stupid, and didn’t seem at all concerned by the fact that they couldn’t catch him while he was in the air.

He’d taken to riding on Deevee’s back, idly tossing daggers down to keep the demons on his trail. It was vastly more mana-efficient than using rifts, and far safer than running, but there was a downside. Despite the hydra’s changes and growth from its evolutions, it still flew like a serpent through the air, which to Ethan felt like riding a rollercoaster, sometimes for hours at a time.

When they finally neared the army, Ethan sat up, looking at how far they’d come. The crusade was nearly ten thousand strong now, and stretched out across the landscape. The demons marched in formation now, and they’d made some helpful discoveries as their collection grew.

The advantage that Twilights brought was much more than just power. They had around twenty of them now, and had learned that–even while controlled–the larger demons had an effect on the smaller. When they weren’t actively trying to kill one another, the Twilights acted like pack-leaders. The smaller ones would follow them naturally, staying close with almost no direct input from Apollo.

That had led to the formations, with a Twilight at the head of hundreds of lesser demons. With this change, the army was more manageable, the strain on Apollo was reduced, and they could travel more easily, trusting that the smaller demons would follow the larger.

It was incredible to see the massive sprawl of thousands of demons arrayed across the landscape, but Ethan’s eyes were still drawn toward the center. His spirit still drank in the corruption of Potentia continuously, like a sponge in the ocean. As it drank, it grew, and the Fable Train grew to contain it. The train had begun at roughly the size of a jumbo jet’s fuselage, but was half-again as large now, and forced to expand almost daily.

Ethan’s spirit still thumped its fist as they moved, a drumbeat pulling in any nearby demons, as well as a kind of heralding of the crusade’s path. Deevee flew directly toward the growing train, which sprouted more weapons and defences each day. Apollo, glowing like a golden sun above the train, fired two spears of light directly into the Twilight demons pursuing Ethan, and in a few minutes the creatures were rising, glowing runes on their tiny foreheads, and as obedient as dogs.

Ethan sent Deevee off to keep scouting while he dropped back down to the train, needing a break after ten hours of hunting Twilights. Most days he didn’t find any that fit the sweet spot he’d discovered. Too weak, or too smart, and they’d run from him. Apparently attacking something, then immediately charging in the other direction was suspicious even to bloodthirsty demons.

On the opposite side, if they were too strong, too close to Master, they weren’t safe for Ethan to draw away. He’d tried to pull in a red dragon, and spent several hours dodging fiery breath before giving up and severing one of its wings so he could escape.

But every few days he found at least one of the perfect candidates, as he had today. Satisfied with a job well done, he waved to Apollo–Angelos, today–then dropped into the Fable Train. He moved to the third floor of the main compartment, where Selina enjoyed working next to the wide windows.

He slumped into a comfortable chair across from her, taking a margarita she slid his way. “Don’t you ever get tired of the scenery?” he asked. “So much red,” he muttered.

Selina looked up, the notably red woman glaring at him. He chuckled. “Why would I want a red planet, when I have the best red right here?” he asked.

She rolled her deep purple eyes. “To answer your question, no, I don’t get tired of it. I’m still in awe of actually being able to see my own world.” She leaned on the table, looking out at one of the horrific, sentient forests. “I keep wondering what it was like, back when my people ruled this place.”

Tomo appeared in the seat next to Ethan. “We never ruled over Potentia,” he told her. “We were merely one of the few species that built permanent homes. Some of the other local life even rivaled us for intelligence, although they were individualistic, and kept to themselves.”

Selina seemed to drink in these insights, and she pestered Tomo with questions each time Ethan came to visit. This time, it was Ethan himself that asked the first. “Did you have those disgusting forests?”

Tomo growled a laugh. “We did not. There was carnivorous plantlife, vines that would capture small animals. But no, our woods were massive and ancient. They sheltered us, and countless beasts, but they stayed where they were, roots running deep.”

“They’re gross now,” Ethan complained. “I hate them.”

“I know this, Ethan Bishop,” Tomo said, rolling his reptilian eyes.

“They bleed yellow. It’s disgusting,” Ethan added.

“I know,” Tomo said again, sharing a tired look with Selina, who laughed.

“You’re one to talk about horrible things,” the Runemistress said. “That spirit of yours looks bigger and angrier every day. Are you sure it won’t get uppity, and start ignoring you again if it gets strong enough?”

Ethan smiled. “It is me,” he said. “The disconnect we felt after I healed Dawn is gone. Yes, it can be a little…challenging, at times, but that’s not because it has a different will.” He paused, thinking. “I guess it’s more like when you have a craving that’s hard to resist, or repeat behavior you know isn’t in your best interest. We all have impulses we struggle to control…mine just happens to be fifty feet tall and wearing a crown.”

Selina shook her head, going back to her notes. “If you say so,” she said.

“I do say so!” He leaned back, taking a sip of his drink. Selina was obsessed with margaritas now, and she made them so sour. “Besides,” he said, “we still need it as strong as it can be. It’s powering Apollo, and the Fable train. Not to mention the other uses we might get out of a giant battery someday.”

Selina conceded the point. “We have more reports of demons going missing around the edges of the army,” she said.

Ethan sighed. “More? I need to get enough Twilights to surround us so this stops happening. Where this time?”

“Western flank,” she said, which was no surprise.

“Ocean is still too close to the east,” he grumbled. “I’ve been scouting along the coast; looks like it’s been pulled way inland. Thankfully, as we continue north the land spreads out. We’re probably a week from Texas.”

“Oh! I have a cowboy hat in my inventory,” she said excitedly.

Ethan blinked. “Very appropriate,” he said. “I guess we’re sort of Potentian cowboys–cowpeople? Whatever, I hope it fits over your horns.”

“Your horn envy is showing,” she said, and Tomo rumbled a laugh. “But never mind your embarrassing shortcomings, look at these.”

She handed him two broken stones, each a little bigger than his head. He turned them in his hands and found… “Runes?” he asked in surprise.

“Yes!” she said excitedly. “Apollo spotted something from the air that didn’t appear natural, and had his minions scoop them up. It turned out to be a destroyed building! Can you believe it? It’s the first sign of my people!” She glanced at Tomo. “Our people!”

“That’s fantastic,” Ethan said, happy for her. “Learn anything?”

“Sort of!” she said. “Rune languages have shifted over time already, but I managed to learn a bit from the free Nator back on Nexum. This definitely follows some old patterns, but I think I’ll be able to understand anything else we might find–with Tomo’s help, if he’s willing.”

“Of course,” Tomo said happily. “I was no runewarden, but I will give what assistance I can provide.”

Ethan looked up suddenly, sensing that Apollo wanted to speak with him. He had Fable open a gap in the roof for his other self to descend through, confronted with the unusual experience of trying to read his own expressions on another person’s face. Excitement?

“Selina?” Apollo said, landing next to them and waving away a margarita. “I believe you asked me to tell you if I found any more stones for you to look at?” he asked.

Selina shot to her feet. “You found more?” she said excitedly. “If I can find another few samples, I think I can begin putting a cipher together. How many did you find?”

“Truthfully? I’m not sure,” he said, and Ethan registered the mischief in his own eyes.

“How can you not be sure?” she asked.

“Well, how many stones do you think there will be in a city?”
