"Sadistic cocksuckers."

Bram didn’t notice his comms were on the all channel when he cursed, and Mathew did not bother to correct the man for it.

He was forty metres back from the front rank with his boots planted in a slurry of gore and blood slicked concrete, yet the noises of the more and more abominations swarming them hadn't really stopped. They continued to throw themselves at the barrier in waves after waves—piling on top of their dead and almost covering the dome all the way to the top—and he could see the strain the second Company was in as they held their arms up and chanted and chanted through the same six words to hold the barrier and somewhere back in the belly of the formation the fourth or the fifth company was already gathering the next water pulse out of the air.

War did not pause to let a man look at something. He thought grimly.

But the head of the formation had seen it.

Mathew gritted his teeth and slowly walked forward through the ranks. People made room for him without being asked, and he wished they wouldn't, because he wasn’t the commander of this army and also because every step forward was a step closer to that thing that had caused deep disgust and…

And then he was at the front, so he looked. He was a leader, and whether he was in the big chair or small, didn’t matter because one thing always remained the same. The burden of command, the consequences of decisions.

The wall stood nine metres high and ran the width of the neck of land, and the brief which Joe had provided had called it made of reinforced stone.

The wall… wasn't just stone; it had faces… human faces… thousands of them, all in varying states of pain and despair.

Mathew bit his lip as he saw the dozens of eyes flicked to them, and he hated how he recognized the emotion in them… hope.

He jerked his head away, only for his eye to land on a woman’s face, and the only reason he knew it… she was a woman was because of long strands of hair that remained in a few patches. She had a mole under her left eye, and Mathew froze.

He knew her.

Then his eyes moved to other, and with each set of faces that his eyes landed on… recognition flared in his eyes.

Mathew's hand had gone to his side where his sword was sheathed. He made it let go.

"Command," Vera said over the net. "Command, I… I have to report. There are minds in that wall."

"Say again, Fourteen."

"Minds. In the wall. Thousands of them, sir, and they're—" A breath, dragged. "They're not dreaming. They're not unconscious. They're awake, and they've been awake, and I can't tell for how long, and I'm asking permission to temporarily stop casting the mental net.”

"Granted," Veric said. "Get out of it."

"Sir."

Down the line, Third Company's second rank had stopped chanting. Mathew heard it happen and heard 1st company leader Bram's roar go down the ranks after it like a dropped hammer.

"THIRD, YOU HOLD YOUR VERSE. Eyes on your own hands. You look at the ground, or you look at me, and you keep counting—"

The weave came back. Ragged, but it came back.

Mathew was distantly grateful for having people who could share the burden of command.

"Bearing on the wall," Veric said. "Boss, give me a read."

"Command, Nineteen." One of Illiana's people, running the sensor rig from the second aid station. "I’m sensing an array. It’s ritualistic in nature. The whole wall.”

“It’s not a defense, Veric.” An icy voice overrode the others on the comms, and Mathew had to double-take to put this on Jin’s face.

“The wall was recently built, and it is meant for us to break down. We would have been breaking down if the barrier, Joe’s spell, and our equipment weren’t providing us mental defense.” Jin said over the comms.

"Yes, sir. We have confirmed mental attacks radiating from the wall. The barrier's eating most of it; the armor is eating the rest. We're reading maybe four percent bleed-through."

"It's a message. Veric,” Jin said. “Somebody built that for us to stand here and look at. The more you look at the faces, the more you’ll recognize them."

Mathew heard it and knew it was true and could not move.

He looked at Marta Halden's mouth hanging open a centimeter and could not find the words.

They're alive.

That was the whole of it. Nothing more complicated than that, and it sat on his chest like a fallen beam.

"Command." Bram, from the front rank. "Sir. Requesting orders."

"Commander," Veric said, and he had switched channels, and it was just the two of them now. "Mathew."

"I hear you."

"The array's climbing in pitch. Whatever it's building toward, it'll be worse in ten minutes than it is now, and we've got a column standing still on open ground with forty thousand things behind us." A pause, and the sound of a lighter that didn't catch. "We take the wall down."

"There are people in it."

"There were."

Mathew closed his eyes.

"Sir, I've read Vera's telemetry the same as you," Veric said, and his tone had gone level and slow, which was the closest the man ever came to gentleness. "Whatever's still burning in that stone isn't a person anymore. It's the part of a person that hurts. They've had days of it. Maybe weeks. And there is no version of today where we get them down off that wall and carry them home."

"I know."

"Then give the order."

Mathew opened his eye.

Two ranks over, a boy from Ninth had gone down on his knees in the ash, not from the fear field but from something older, and the man beside him was hauling him upright by the collar and telling him to breathe. Further along, at the seam where First met Second, Mathew found his son.

Rudy was staring at the wall with his jaw set, and the white fire was crawling up his forearms, and he wasn't calling it. And then Rudy's head turned and found him, and Mathew watched his son's face ask him a question that the boy had never once had to ask before, which was: Is there anything you can do?

"Sir," Bram said again. "Orders."

Mathew opened his mouth.

He did not know, afterwards, what would have come out of it.

The comms clicked.

"All companies, this is Jin Winters."

The whole net went quiet, front to back, twenty-two hundred people, and Mathew felt it happen the way you feel a wind drop.

"Wall is a target. Take it down in one go. We have a war to win."

Nobody spoke.

"I’ll say again for anyone who was hoping to hear something else." The voice had a ruthless edge to it, as if daring someone to challenge. The tone never went up or down, and that was what sent chills down everyone’s neck. "The wall is a cult installation. It's an active hostile array. It is going to come down, right now, on my authority and under my name, and every one of you is going to remember afterwards that I gave the order and none of you did."

"They've been dying since the day the cult took them," Jin said. "The dying's just been slow. We're going to make it fast, and then we're going to go and find the people who did it, and we are going to be very, very thorough about it."

"Boss—" Bram started.

"Ninth through Sixteenth, break your cycle and go to earth. Chorus, catch the spill and braid it low… I want it under the foundation, not on the face. Second, do not drop that barrier. Command, count them in."

Veric's lighter caught. Mathew heard the drag of it on the open channel.

"Ninth through Sixteenth," Veric said. "Earth. Charge."

Eight hundred people raised their hands.

Mathew stayed where he was, forty metres from the wall, and made himself keep his eye on Marta Halden's face.

He had made a career out of understanding what a commander owed. He owed his people the truth when he could afford it and the lie when he couldn't. His old commander, Hobbs, had made sure to teach him how to do all that.

The combined chant for the sorcery went up, and Mathew could feel it going in under his boots, running out ahead of the formation through the packed earth, and it was nothing like the water pulse or the chain.

There were no grand flashes of magic to it at all. Just eight hundred voices dropping onto the same syllable at the same time, and the ground beginning to tremble.

Bram's arm came down. "NOW."

The ground bucked.

It was not an explosion. Mathew had seen a hundred explosions. The land itself shifted, like a big shudder from deep underground, and it traveled the last thirty metres in the time it took to blink and hit the wall's footing from below.

The wall came apart everywhere at once, the hasty mortar giving up first and the stone following after, whole sections folding inward.

And the faces went with it.

Mathew made himself watch that too, and heard the sound they made going, and knew that he would be hearing it in the quiet parts of every night that was left to him.

Then the dust cloud rolled back over the barrier's face, and another wave of abominations had arrived.

And the cracks appeared, spidering out from the impact point and racing back across the crown of the shell, and Mathew heard Second Company's chant fracture in the middle of a word.

"COMMAND, BREACH IN—"

"HOLD."

"Command, we can't hold this, we're at forty-one and falling, we're at—"

"THIRTY-NINE—"

"Sir, the whole front's going—"

Mathew stopped listening.

He had been standing still for a very long time, and he was done with it.

"ETERNAL BASTION"

His mantle erupted.

Ashen-grey aura poured off his shoulders and his back and did not stop pouring until it covered everything and everyone inside the dome of the barrier.

A projection of a massive castle with its impenetrable walls appeared above them as Mathew felt the feedback from every single one of them arrive in him at once. All of them inside his walls, all of them his, and the weight of it should have driven him to his knees, and instead it set his feet.

You are behind my walls.

Nothing behind my walls dies today that I don't allow.

Across the formation, twenty-two hundred people straightened at the same instant.

"—Command, my barrier just—"

"Sixty-two. We're at sixty-two. It's climbing. Command, it's climbing—"

"Second, take the tempo up," Veric said, and he'd raised his voice for the first time all day. "Everybody take it up. Use what you've been given."

And they used it.

Second's chant came back double-time, and the cracks sealed themselves out of the crown of the shell. Fifth loosed a chain into the mass and the working was half again as wide as it had been ten minutes ago, and where the lightning went, things died in their hundreds and every one of those deaths came back up the link and into the grey and out again across all of them, so that Fourth's water pulse rose out of the ground stronger than the one before it, and Seventh's blades went through carapace like it was fruit, and the boy from Ninth who had gone down on his knees was on his feet with both hands out and screaming a verse he had learned just days ago in a converted storage room.

Vienna stopped losing ground, and then it started taking it.

"Command, Starkiller." Salvatore's voice, and he was not bored anymore. "Drop me a hole."

"Sir, if we open the front—"

"Drop it."

"Second, open on the right seam," Veric said. "Three seconds. Not four."

The barrier opened just enough for a person to walk through.

Salvatore Silvers walked out of it, raised the greatsword to shoulder height, and Mathew didn’t even register when the man’s sword moved. It just did.

There was no aura on it… No essence, and yet the air split apart with shockwaves along a line two hundred metres wide.

Everything standing inside that line ceased to be standing. The forward third of the horde went out like a candle flame pinched between two fingers. The remains of the wall—the footings, the collapsed sections, the last two standing pillars at the gate mouth—sheared off flat and clean at ground level and slid apart.

Beyond them, three barrier arrays, which sprung up sensing the attack… broke in sequence, snap, snap, snap, like lamps in a corridor.

The dust took four seconds to fall.

When it fell, the road to Cathedral Ward was open all the way to the precinct gate, and there was nothing alive on it at all.

The comms clicked.

"All companies," Winters said. "That was the easy part. Brace for impact and potential ORDER IVs, for we are now entering the den of evil."

"All companies. Advance to the gate. Good luck."

And then, quieter, and Mathew was fairly sure Jin had not meant to leave the channel open when he said it:

"And good hunting."

The formation moved, and the Cathedral Ward opened out ahead of them. More than two months… since anyone from the city had set foot on the once sacred grounds. Somebody in Eighth started saying the old blessing under his breath and got two lines in before he stopped himself.

It had been the most beautiful district in Vienna. Mathew had proposed to his wife on a bench three hundred metres from where he was standing, and the bench was gone, and so was the plaza it had sat in, and so was every window in the colonnade that had run its northern edge.

"Steady," Bram said on the open net, from somewhere near the front. "Eyes forward. Second, your seams. Fourth, charge on the mark."

The barrier walked with them, and the horde still worked at its flanks.

Then Mathew's senses caught the first one.

"Contact—"

He had it half a second before the sensors and others did. A weight coming down and settling over them like a hand laid flat on the back of a neck.

Then another, off the right, from inside the colonnade's ruin.

Then a third, from above, and a fourth that dropped in behind them, and a fifth and sixth arriving together off the left flank.

Six. Then seven.

The grey held.

Mathew's mantle poured off him and up into the burning ash-fortress overhead, and he felt every one of those two thousand two hundred hearts jolt and steady inside his walls, and he set his jaw and pushed harder, until the towers of it dug into the red sky and the weight came off his people's shoulders and onto his own.

It hurt. He welcomed it.

"AURA EVENT," Vera called, and her voice cracked on the second word. "Command, aura event, multiple, I count seven—no—Command, correction, seven confirmed and two unreadable, I can't get a shape on the last two, they're—"

"Enough. Get out of it." Veric barked. "All companies, halt advance. Second through Sixth, anchor where you stand. Anchor. Do not chase anything."

The army stopped dead in the middle of the processional route.

"Establish a foothold!"

Another chant and Mathew felt the ground hardening.

"You are not taking that gate today," Veric said. "You are not taking one more metre today. You are standing on this stone, and you are holding this stone, and you will still be standing on it in an hour. Aid stations forward to the second marker. Twelve, Thirteen, cycle your sustainers now, don't wait for the drop. Bram, the formation's yours."

"Formation's mine," Bram said. "Understood."

"X will break off from here." A pause on the net, and the drag of a lighter. "Our task is to kill anything we can and hold. Everything you can't handle, you send to us. Everything we can't handle—"

He didn't finish it.

No one needed him to.

Off to the right, Joe stepped out of the barrier… a plain oak door swung shut in front of him as he disappeared into it. Salvatore simply walked forward out of the front rank, alone. Reyana went left with her longsword already drawn, and his son went with her.

Rudy passed him at the barrier, white and crimson flame climbing his arms, the greatsword in one hand, and he did not stop, and he did not slow, and he said— not on the comms, just out loud, the way he used to say it at the door on his way to training—

"Back in a bit, Dad."

Mathew held his mantle and let him go, and that was the hardest thing he had ever done in his life.

"X, moving," Veric said. "Commander, you have the wall. Winters, you have—"

The net went to static as an ORDER IV presence rose… close to them… way close.

“I’ll take it,” Mathew said and stepped out of the barrier.
