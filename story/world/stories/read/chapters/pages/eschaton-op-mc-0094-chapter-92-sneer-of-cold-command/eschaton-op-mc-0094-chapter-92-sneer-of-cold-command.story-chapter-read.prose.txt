I meet Thutmose outside his inn before first light. He shrugs deeper into a coat. The desert is cold at night, and the city is only moderately warmer.

“Must I get up so early?” He grumbles, but we both make our way towards the Colosseum of Horus.

“You did say you wanted to beat the rush.”

“Yes, yes,” he says, clacking his beak.

The city is far more lively at this time than it was the day before. It seems other people had the same idea. I catch waves from other people. A nervous pair of probably teenagers shuffle up. One of them is carrying a crystal like someone on Earth would carry a camera, and she shyly waves at me.

They ask for a picture, and I oblige, with Thutmose taking it for them. I watch them scuttle off into the crowd. We walk by what could only be a newspaper stand, and Thutmose hands a gold coin to a wide eyed merchant who stares at me.

He unravels the newspaper and clacks his beak.

“Ah, it seems you are famous in two worlds now,” Thutmose says. He tilts the page in my direction, and I needlessly glance over at it. My picture is plastered over it. It’s of me at the stadium competing in the qualifier bracket for the math competition. It’s still rather funny to me that there’s a tournament for math, but I can’t exactly throw stones. There’s a league for just about everything back on Earth.

Second Visitor Contender Spotted! the headline reads. Math magi destroys all competition easily!

“I wasn’t a huge fan of being famous in just one,” I grumble.

“Ah, stop whining, yes? You will live.”

I’d roll my eyes if I had any.

The Colosseum comes into sight as we step onto the main road. The monolithic stone stadium casts a shadow in the early dawn light, leaving us still in darkness.

The moment we get close to it two men rush forward towards us. They each are wearing a sleeveless vest—it must be frigid at this time of the morning—with the symbol for “paper” on it.

Thutmose clacks his beak with distaste.

“Ah. Reporters.”

He slaps me on the shoulder and straightens his own coat.

“Straighten up, yes.”

Some things never change.

I stand to my full height. I normally slouch a little as to not loom over people, though that’s not really necessary here in Khetmenet. Instead of being a chest and shoulders taller than everyone on Earth, here I’m just a head taller, sometimes less. Tall enough that someone might comment on it, but not so much as to be a genuine oddity. Or enough to make life difficult.

One of them points what must be a magical camera at the two of us and the rectangular crystal glows for a brief moment. The other pulls out a pad of paper and a charcoal stick.

“Machina I am Reporter Sokari and this is Reporter Heti from the Ninth District Press and we would like to ask you a few questions if you have time,” he says in one rapid fire sentence.

“I… guess?” I’ve never been good at interacting with reporters. There’s a reason Morgan has done most of that stuff. It helps that I’m generally so busy I can just leave. Most of the time they even take my silence for an answer, which is very nice.

Thutmose gently pushes at me, and I keep moving towards the Challenger’s Entrance. A dozen guards keep an open space around it. There are hundreds of people looking for the handful of us who are fighting today. They constantly jockey for a position to see us, and I’m lucky they haven’t noticed us already. Despite how different this world is at times, there are parts that are nearly identical to Earth.

“Fantastic now we would like to know more about you and why are you are fighting what is it for you why it matters and are you concerned about any other challenger in particular.”

Reporter Sokari continues to speak extremely quickly, never taking a single pause and getting everything out in one long breath.

“I’m fighting for access to the Royal Market, there’s something I need being sold there. My world is at stake. As for other challengers? I’m mostly concerned at what Vizier is going to do the others.”

“Ah yes Vizier the Cruel is well known already for breaking his opponents he participated in the early qualifier brackets yesterday we heard you were given a spot without participating yourself due to concern you may be of similar ilk.”

Even as he speaks he writes down what I tell him, charcoal stick blurring across the notepad. I have absolutely no idea what he’s writing, so I assume it’s some short hand.

“You are not concerned about fighting him Vizier is a Visitor like you and is apparently considered the strongest seen in the arena to date.”

“I won’t lie, I am a little nervous about what fighting him will entail. I don’t know anything about him, and we’re both Ascended, some of the most powerful individuals of our world. I don’t know the kind of collateral damage we could cause. We’re lucky that it was Vizier rather than any of the other Imperial Ascended that came through. Thank your deity of choice it wasn’t Khalid ibn al-Walid. No matter how bad you think Vizier is, he would be so, so much worse.”

The crowd finally notices us, and they roar as some of them rush towards us. Both reporters notice the onrush of hundreds of people and beat a hasty retreat with rushed thanks. Thutmose squawks and jumps behind me. I continue to wade forward through the crowd.

They actually move away from me, not unlike how Typicals on Earth tend to avoid close contact with me. I assume it’s an aspect of my Aura.

Maybe the average civilian here isn’t actually the equivalent of a 3-Star in that case. That would certainly make their entry on Earth far less apocalyptic.

After we get into the circle cleared by the guards and step into the much quieter Colosseum’s hallways, Thutmose moves to my left again.

“How do so many people know me already? I’ve been here for two days.”

Thutmose shrugs.

“News travels fast, yes. Karnak is large, but also small. And Vizier the Cruel makes a good story. A true villain, an unknown hero. One who breaks men, one who plays with numbers over violence. One who demands all bow before him, one who tells stories of far-off lands to children. What is not to cheer for?”

Hathorsakmet joins us, still holding her clipboard and satchel at her side.

“Good, you’re early. We have a few things that we need to discuss.”

Thutmose rubs his hands eagerly.

“Ah, yes. Your entrance!”

“I’m not just walking out onto the arena?”

Thutmose laughs, the honking noise a familiar refrain.

“If you wish to be forgettable, yes. All challengers should have a good entry. Maybe the others will not, but I will not have my friend taking part in the finals without the appropriate style such an event demands, yes.”

He seems very firm in his opinion, and there’s a small part of me that wants to show off, just a little. Some of the joy that filled me when I started getting used to my new form returns. It had been buried under the weight of months of carnage and the world on my shoulders. But here in Khetmenet, much of that has fallen away, stripped off by the sheer excitement of exploring a new world.

“Say, I saw a balcony the last time I was here…”

Thutmose rubs his hands again and clacks his beak.

I can hear the announcer hamming it up from where I’m waiting for my match. Vizier’s opponent is actually a Minotaur, though a different one from the one I saw in line while signing up.

“From across the Great Sea he’s come to display his valor and strength! He’s battled giants and leviathans alike! He’s crushed every challenger in the Shattered Islands and now he’s here! He’s here for yooooooooouuuuuuu!”

“Let us welcome Isokrates the Slayer!”

The crowd half a million strong roars loud enough to shake the stone walls as the Minotaur walks out of a gate pumping his arms. In one hand he has a massive battle-axe. He points it at the crowd, slowly swinging it around. The crowd somehow roars even louder. I’m glad the Colosseum has magic reinforcing it. I think the sheer volume would tear it apart otherwise.

Healing magic being supposedly so common here in Khetmenet is a good thing. I bet most of the crowd is going to be half-deaf after this.

The spot I’m waiting for my turn from is about halfway up the four hundred foot tall stadium. I’m hiding in the hallway just inside a balcony used to maintain the massive magical projection screens below them. The stadium is so large and tall that many of the people in nose-bleed seats probably can’t see the action.

The magical jumbotrons TV screens are truly impressive, apparently incorporating recording crystals and ultracomplex glyphic matrices. I have absolutely no idea how they work, but while working with Hathorsakmet and Thutmose on explaining my entrance idea, I managed to catch a couple glimpses of them. With my perfect memory, I’ll be able to recreate them later for Morgan.

Directly opposite of me and about a hundred feet lower is the Royal Box. It’s reserved for the Great King, his family, and his guests. He’s actually in attendance. The ram-headed man wears long robes embroidered with gold, silks, and onyx gems. Black is the color of prosperity here, and he has a lot of it. On his head is a crown that glows so brightly it would hurt anyone else to even look at. I can’t make out any details from this distance with how absolutely blinding it is.

“On the other side is a man you’ve all heard of! A Visitor from another world, come to challenge his might against us! A man of cruelty, of violence! He’s here to put us in our place, or so he claims! Tell me Karnak, how do you feel about Vizier the Cruel!?”

The booing crowd is so loud they can probably hear it across the entire city. I hope Thutmose brought hearing protection. He managed to snag some ring-side seats. He’s got a lot of friends in high places, more than a ‘humble merchant’ would probably have.

Vizier walks into the arena without pomp or circumstance. He wields a quarterstaff. From my position, I can’t see his expression but his body language tells me enough.

He’s going to hurt Isokrates. Badly.

Both Vizier and Isokrates walk into the center of the ring as the announcer continues to speak.

“The only rule here is no killing! The Colosseum has been magicked to both protect my lovely spectators and to lower the risk of death! The match will be fought until incapacitation or one challenger yields!”

Isokrates’ Aura is the equivalent of a 4-Star, and it pours out from him as he rolls his shoulders.

“I dedicate this honorable combat to the Gods,” he intones. “May we both bring honor and glory to our Houses.”

“Only the weak cling to such things as honor and glory,” Vizier rasps. He does not attempt to display his Aura.

“Begi—”

The announcer barely gets the first syllable out before Vizier strikes, leading with his quarterstaff. A brutal jab to Isokrates’ ribs sends him flying and rolling across the sandy Colosseum floor with a sickening crack. Vizier follows up with a flurry of heavy strikes, battering at Isokrates laying on the ground.

The minotaur lashes out with a kick, and Vizier jumps over it lazily. It gives just enough space for Isokrates to come his to feet before Vizier is on him again.

The quarterstaff comes down again and again, hammering on Isokrates’ clearly broken ribs, his joints, his head, even his throat. He goes down to his knees again, choking on air. The battle-axe is long discarded, having been ripped from his grip by a series of lighting-quick strikes to his hands.

I’m not sure if the same standards of power we use on Earth apply here, but the only reason Isokrates is still alive is because of his incredible durability. Even from a mile away and two hundred yards up I hear the rapid fire thump and crunch of every hit landing home.

Even after Isokrates is lying on the ground, just trying to cover his head, Vizier lands in a few more strikes before stepping back. The crowd is dead silent, even the announcer is speechless. What we watched was not a match, or a game, or even a fight. It was a ruthless and brutal beat down perfectly designed to break a person. The entire engagement took less than ten seconds. I’m sure it was nothing but a blur to virtually everyone watching.

I take a step towards the balcony before Hathorsakmet grabs my arm.

“It’s not your turn yet,” she hisses. “You’ll get your chance.”

It takes four people to carry Isokrates off the arena floor. The announcer clears his throat and is clearly uncertain of how to continue, but does his best.

“This time we have a local challenger! You all know him, you all love him, Shotepenra the Sandstorm!”

The gates fling open and a sandstorm enters the arena. A gust sucks up sand from the floor and whirls it around, engulfing most of the arena. The vortex bursts when it reaches the center, sand falling down like rain.

A snake headed man wielding two khopeshes stands in the center of the arena. The crowd cheers for him, shaking the stands. Dust floats down from the ceiling in tendrils and drifts, illuminated through the bright sunlight pouring from the Great King Amun-Ra’s solar crown.

“And for the second time today we have another Visitor! He’s come from a place beyond time to save his world! Yet even with such stakes on the line, he finds the time to tell stories to the young! A man of numbers and of slow, friendly nature!”

The announcer sounds nervous, and Shotepenra is visibly terrified. Even the crowd’s reaction is uncertain. It makes me feel a little self-conscious about my grandiose entrance.

“Let me introduce… Machina the Kind!”

I sprint forward and leap off the balcony, cloak trailing behind me. I spot Thutmose and a few others I recognize standing and cheering. While mid air I rip my cloak off, the quick-release clasps letting go just like Morgan said they would.

The cloak vanished into my storage space, I hit the sandy arena with a boom that echoes in the mile wide bowl of stone and sand. The rocky floor cracks and breaks underneath my impact, and I slide towards the center, sending a rooster tail of dust and gravel behind me. Without the cloak, I once again damage the landscape around me.

The crowd roars at my ballistic entrance, though I’m sure the arena maintenance staff will not be happy with it.

“I’m not here to hurt you,” I tell the snake-headed Shotepenra. Hathorsakmet provided me with a heavily blunted greatsword, but I don’t pull it from my storage space.

“We’ll see,” he says nervously.

“Begin!” The announcer calls.

Shotepenra lunges forward in a blur with the speed of a 4-Star. I bat away his blades and push him to the ground as gently as I can. He’s better than Duelliste, but I’m much better than I was too, and I don’t have any allies behind me to worry about shredding with backblast.

He gasps as I knock the wind out of him. His twin Khopeshes clang against my armor to no avail. The rapid fire attacks sound like a machine-gun in their intensity.

“You should probably yield,” I tell him, and he goes slack.

“Thank you,” he gasps. “I yield.”

I help him up off the sand, and he leaves the field on his own two feet with barely a bruise to show for it.

“Victory goes to Machina by yield! What an astounding showing from the otherworldly warrior! Such power, tempered by mercy!”

I was given a weapon by the Colosseum staff, but I didn’t even bother to use it. I didn’t want to hurt the other guy.

There’s only one person I’m here for.

Vizier enters the arena again, looking bored.

“And now for the Grand Finale, the Visitors face off!”
