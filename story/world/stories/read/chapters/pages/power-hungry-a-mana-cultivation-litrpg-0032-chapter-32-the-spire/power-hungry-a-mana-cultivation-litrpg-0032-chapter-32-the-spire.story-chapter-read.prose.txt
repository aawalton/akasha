Darius kept lecturing the recruits while he doled out pills that helped to deal with the fever and fatigue. It was nothing new, it was just Darius venting. Cale found it was pretty fun listening to others get badgered when he had done the right thing.

But he also knew that could breed resentment so he hid his grin.

They got the payload moving, with Darius at the front. He made sure they understood how much him being here would slow down clearing of the spire and how they were a rotten, useless bunch. So nothing new under the sun.

Cale kept slowly working on his cultivation. He was developing a bit of an addiction to suck just a little bit of mana in from a Nevani crystal and then work and condense it as far as he could. As the hours passed, he slowly and methodically worked through a crystal, with Aura instructing him intermittently.

Soon enough the spire started rising from the horizon until it dominated the horizon. It stood out in the barren wasteland created by the Omnic Manifest. While the structure was crusted with a red and black membrane like everything else, it only had hints of hard and jagged edges. It was smooth and spiraling, with soft contouring lines. It was of an older make and clearly something the Citadel could not fully assimilate and consume.

The spire reached towards the sky like a forlorn tree forgotten on earth by something that had gone up in the skies. Cale craned his neck almost fully back. It was at least a hundred feet in diameter and probably a thousand high. The structure thinned into a pinprick at the top.

Once their group got to its base, there was already a group of Sullied working around it, clearing out the motley patterns of red and black that enveloped everything. Beneath it was a barren land of gray dirt, ashen and lifeless.

“The game here is very simple,” Darius said as he turned back to them. He seemed to have no longer need for his anger, for he addressed them in his usual calm demeanor. “I will go to the top of the structure. It was perfectly designed by the Nevani for a single person to sit atop the spire. Your job is to operate the power room. The floors below the surface are usually crawling with both Praetorians and the… mutated. Your job is to take the payload below into the power room. Charge it and protect it with your damn lives.”

Darius made sure to look each and every one of them hard in the eye, letting the silence hang. The unsaid message was clear. Screw this up and it's game over.

“The Sullied here will watch the ground floor and remove the corruption. You will take care of things downstairs. Once you turn the spire’s power room on, two things will happen. Anyone know what?”

Only Fiara lifted her hand up. Darius gave her a look.

“It’s no fun if you answer it,” he said. “I can tell them myself. All of the energy will be routed and concentrated to the top of the spire, where I will be cultivating. I will pop a series of pills that will allow me to enter a trance for the breakthrough. This means that I cannot stop my cultivation before the breakthrough is finished. It also means that the power room must stay operational until my cultivation is finished. If either one is interrupted… run.”

“Run?” Galvon said. “Will the spire explode?”

“No,” Darius said and looked at his nails. “But I will come for you. Running will give you a few more moments to live.”

The heavy silence must have meant that everyone understood. Darius locked gazes with each of them, including Cale, making sure that he knew he wasn’t off the hook.

“I need to make this clear to you,” Darius said. “My advancement to Core Crystallization is extremely important to the syndicate. My level of cultivation is so valuable, that a hundred of you Body Tempering babies can bite it before the syndicate even starts to do napkin math on ideal outcomes. You are expected to die protecting my advancement.”

Cale nodded to himself. He understood. He didn’t like what he heard one bit. He hadn’t signed up for this. But he understood. He was a pawn. And he would remain a pawn as long as he was too weak to pay attention to. Sure, Darius and Ravia Wren might have seen potential in him. They were expecting him to grow and they would even be willing to invest in him. But he had only grabbed the first rung on the ladder of power. It was a harsh reality, it was an ugly reality.

But it was a reality full of potential. And Cale chose to embrace it as such.

He squeezed his fist into a tight ball and looked at Darius. The man turned to him and cocked an expectant eyebrow. Cale gave him a tight smile and a nod. Darius might have even smiled back. Then Darius turned to his secretary.

“You are in charge of keeping the information flowing. To me, to the recruits, to the Sullied. But most importantly, to me.”

Fiara gave a curt, professional nod. “Yessir.”

“Dismissed,” Darius said and turned towards a stairwell leading to the upper floors of the spire.

*

The floors below the spire were labyrinthine. Sprawling tunnels interconnecting, only illuminated by soft lights, identical to the ones Cale had seen in the ruins he had awakened. Another thing of note; there was no corruption here.

This was of Nevani build. It was everywhere. It was in the seamless smooth lines the walls of solid stone made. There were no cuts. Just smooth stone, as if created from thin air. For all Cale knew, that could have been the case.

Along the floor, ceilings and walls were circular nodes and sigil patterns connected by lines, like a grid made for magic. Which, according to Aura, was exactly what it was.

“I have an understanding of this structure,” Aura said. “Which is interesting. I suppose it is because you will require the operation of one of these later down the line in your cultivation.”

Cale, who was walking in a loose formation with Madia, Sintra and Artan only a few feet away from him, just made a grunt of approval and nodded. Aura continued.

“The architecture of the structure is designed to amplify mana and concentrate it into a singular point, which in this case will be Darius. We must supply the power room with a prerequisite amount of energy.”

Cale nodded again as they passed an intersection. That seemed simple enough. They even knew where the room was, because this was a spire the syndicate had used before and Fiara had a map. The only problem was that they weren’t alone.

The screeches started again.

“Be alert,” Artan hissed.

“Take the payload back,” Fiara whispered. “We can’t leave the intersection behind us.

Madia and Ragnak who were at the front nodded and started pushing the payload back. The screeches got closer. Madia snapped her staff open and Risto’s pistols started whining.

“I hate these things,” Galvon muttered.

“Hah,” Ragnak said. “I don’t mind a good scrap.

Cale gritted his teeth. If there was one thing he could agree with Galvon was that he hated these things.

Three of them came at them, one from every direction of the intersection. They moved in red, screeching blurs, mixing soft squishes and hard clonks as they ran at the recruits.

Madia spun her staff at one of the blurs. It screeched wildly and slammed an overlong, clawed hand at her. She toppled, but Sintra was immediately there to drag her to safety. Another red blur smashed into the ground where Madia had just been.

They didn’t have space to surround the blurs in the tight corridor. So Cale and Sintra were on duty to assist the front liners with what little they could. Risto was shooting a fusillade of golden energy at the blurs at a constant rate.

The screechers darted around and kept moving in wild erratic charges, much like Ragnak was doing, matching them in some violent and strange game.

Ragnak finally managed to wrest the momentum from one of the blurs and smashed it into the wall.

“Now!” he yelled.

Artan moved instantly. He was a flash of gray and white as his silver sword sang when unsheathed. With one clean stroke of white energy he sliced the head off the pinned demon.

Cale looked at the rolling head with disgust. Faintly humanoid, but with black overlarge eyes. No nose, no lips, just teeth. Jagged and twisted. Two horns protruded from a hairy head, black as ink.

Manafiends.

The death of one of them gave the recruits space, and Cale ran towards the front. Madia swung her staff at a manafiend, but it dodged. Before it could counter attack, Cale tackled it, sending it sprawling against a wall. Zavio zipped at it, but it was too fast. It lunged at Cale, a clawed hand extended. Cale had been expecting it. He used Ghost step, letting the claw phase through his ethereal form. When the confused demon looked back at Cale, he smashed it with an elbow. It went sprawling on the floor and Artan finished it with a blade strike. Risto kept a focused fire on the last remaining one to keep it moving and not attacking. Galvon and Ragnak finished it off, smashing into its spindly body, crushing it until it stopped moving.

Cale was kneeling in front of the first fiend down, as if inspecting it. Truthfully he was draining it of mana.

The manafiends did not have a core like the murderbots. Instead their mana was dispersed throughout their body, like Body Tempering cultivators. That made draining it hard. Especially with prying eyes around.

“What are you doing?” Galvon asked over his shoulder.

“Just making sure it's dead,” Cale answered quickly with a planned lie.

Galvon grunted but didn’t say anything to that. It seemed even he wasn’t sure what made these things tick.

Cale didn’t dare to take too much mana. Just a handful. And that was already plenty. The mana inside him was angry. It was much harder to control than the murderbot mana. It thrashed and attacked Cale’s will like a rabid dog. Even small amounts were difficult to get under his control.

“This is an example of mana that is not compatible,” Aura said. “Other cultivators can retrieve murderbot cores and use them later when they can work their mana attuners. But the mana these creatures have… toxic.”

Cale could understand that. The mana caused him literal pain. But this was just something to overcome. Cale was of the firm belief that if he could master mana like this, anything would be easy to manipulate.

They passed the intersection, leaving the manafiend corpses behind. Soon they came to another one.

“This way,” Fiara said, not lifting her eyes from her managlass tablet.

Cale worked through the violent demonic mana just in time for them to hear the screeches again. This time only two attacked and they didn’t have an intersection, which also left less space for the manafiends. They came from different sides. Galvan got a nasty cut from a claw on his shoulder, but other than that they got off unscathed. Cale made sure to suck in just a little bit of the mana, so he could keep cultivating. He was starting to get the hang of moving and manipulating mana simultaneously.

After a tense hour they finally arrived. Double doors made of thick stone stood high under an archway. There was no ceremony. Ragnak pushed the doors inwards and they got the payload through.

The doors groaned as they swung open, revealing the power room.

The air inside was thick, stagnant, old. A dry, metallic tang settled on Cale’s tongue, like the ghost of something burned away centuries ago. The architecture was undeniably Nevani—smooth, seamless stone, lines of mana script carved with impossible precision, curving across the walls and ceiling like veins.

The center of the room was dominated by the power relay. It was a circular platform, seven feet wide, embedded with concentric rings of sigils. Thick cables, encased in a flexible metallic tubing, slithered from the relay into the surrounding walls, disappearing into the structure. In the central sigil were six smaller circles. Aura told Cale that those were seats for cultivators who would function as conduits.

To the side, a row of tall, coffin-like structures stood in a recessed alcove. Some of them were cracked open, their interiors filled with a strange, crumbling dust. Whatever had been inside them was long gone.

Cale took a step forward, his boots echoing hollowly against the floor. The silence was heavy. Too heavy. Even Ragnak, who had been eager to fight moments ago, shifted uncomfortably.

Then Cale saw it.

Something was in the corner.

A mass of dark, shifting limbs was huddled against the far wall, hunched and unmoving. The shape was wrong—too large to be human, too jagged to be natural. A thin layer of red-black filaments stretched across its form, like it had been fused into the wall itself.

A pair of glowing yellow eyes snapped open.

The filaments snapped and peeled away as the thing stirred. A deep, rattling inhale filled the room. Then, slowly, it turned its head toward them.

The last thing Cale saw before it lunged was the shine of razor-sharp bone protruding from its arms.
