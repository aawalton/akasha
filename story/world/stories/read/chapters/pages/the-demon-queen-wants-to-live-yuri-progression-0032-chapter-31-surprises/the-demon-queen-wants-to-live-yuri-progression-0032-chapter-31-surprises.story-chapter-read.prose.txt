"It's not here," I said. My hands were still inside the satchel, fingers pressing into the cloth where the warmth should have been. It was warm there. It should not have been warm without the egg inside it.

Then the Requiem came. I had not called for it, but it came anyway. Wisps of black flame curled around my fingers. The table greyed beneath me. The wood aged in a heartbeat. Cracks spidered across the surface of the table. The people closest to us all went silent, looking around apprehensively. I crushed the flame in my fist, and the black receded.

"What?" Ash said.

"The e-egg." My voice cracked on the second word. "The egg isn't here."

We were both silent. I kept searching the satchel, kept digging through it with my hands, as if the egg would simply appear there if I kept looking.

Ash's gaze followed my hands to the satchel. The room had gone very bright and very loud and very far away all at once. I rose, and then I was rising. And in the next moment I was walking towards the two men and the table they had retreated to. There was a third chair there, one that was empty. I seized the first man -the one who had sat beside me- by the collar and hauled him upright. It took little effort. His cup hit the floor. Wine spread across the wood in a dark stain of red. "Where is it?" I demanded.

"What- what're you-" His eyes were unfocused. His breath was thick.

"The egg. You took it from me. Where is it?"

"I didn't take anything! I don't know what you're-"

I shoved him aside and rounded on the second. He was already backing away, hands raised. "I swear, I swear I don't know, we didn't-"

I yanked him into the air and glared into his eyes. "Where is the egg?" I hissed.

His eyes almost rolled into the back of his head from the fear. He did not have it. Neither of them did. Their fear had too much shock in it. I could see that much. My grip loosened and the second man fell to the ground and did not rise.

The inn around me had gone quiet. How long had it been quiet? I looked, and saw every face in that small space staring right back at me. In the silence, the fire crackled. A man gulped, and the sound of it carried. Then the Inker stood and strode across the room in four strides. He had the decency to look mildly concerned. "What happened?" he said.

"The beast egg was stolen from me. It was in my satchel and someone took it while we sat here," I said.

The Inker's face went hard. Whatever the man was, he was not slow. He turned to the nearest silver soldier who had stood up. "You, seal the doors. Nobody leaves this building until I say otherwise."

The innkeeper started forward, his broad face reddening. "Now see here, you can't just-"

"I can," the Inker said, and the marks on his forearms glowed. The innkeeper opened his mouth to say something and closed it without saying anything at all.

Two of the silver soldiers moved to the exits. Another stepped out of the inn and into the pouring rain. A woman near the hearth stood and protested. A man at the card table demanded to know what was happening.

The Inker met my gaze and held it for a moment. No doubt, he wanted me to see what he was doing for my sake. I ignored him. I was scanning faces and counting heads. I was running through every moment of the evening. The bump against the table. The strange weight that had followed it. I turned back towards the two men who had approached me. I looked at their table. Two men. There had been three, I was sure of it. The third man wasn't there.

Then I looked at the chair beside mine. The chair where Ash had been sitting. It was empty. I looked around the inn, and did not see her. I grabbed the satchel, swung it over my shoulder and then pushed through the crowd and past the two men who now guarded the door. It took them a moment to remember that their orders likely did not extend to me. They stepped aside without comment, though they cast me wary looks.

The rain hit me the moment I stepped outside. It was cold, and it took all of three steps for it to soak through my clothing. The street was dark, the only light leaking from the different buildings' shuttered windows in thin lines or from the feeble lanterns strung about. I glanced around.

What did I expect to find? Ash? I did not see her, and did not know how I could in this darkness. The third man? Doubtless he was already gone. Something stabbed at me from the inside. I put a hand on my left breast, and felt it tremble. I tried to steady it. A Queen’s hands had to be steady. It was the bare minimum. It would not obey.

Then I felt it. Heavier than mana, and denser. It was Essence, and it carried an impression with it. The taste of things being cut from other things. Severance. For half a heartbeat, I did not connect it. I had been so focused on my own powers that I had not considered that Ash might have practiced the same thing I had. I felt the weight again, there and gone in moments. I ran toward it.

I found them behind the inn, in a narrow space between the back wall and a stable. I heard Ash's breathing before I saw her. It was ragged and uneven, and the rain almost swallowed it.

There were signs of a fight, one that had not lasted long. A wooden crate had been split cleanly in two. A gash ran through the stone wall of the shed at shoulder height, smooth as glass. There were marks on the muddy ground.

A man was on the ground. He was the third -he had the most unremarkable face of the three. Or at least, it had been, when it had been whole. He was alive, but the margin was thin. His right arm ended at the elbow. His face was a ruin of bruises, blood and split skin. He was whimpering and quivering in his own blood.

The egg was on the ground, in the mud. Ash stood over the man. She was soaked through, Martha's dress clinging to her shoulders, to the line of her back. I could see through it to the skin beneath. Her sword was sheathed. Her hands hung at her sides and they were shaking.

She had not noticed me. Her breathing was ragged and uneven, and all of her attention was fixed on the man at her feet. "I'll kill you," Ash said. Her voice was low, barely audible over the rain. "I'll kill you for what you did to her."

Something behind my ribs tightened. It felt warm, and sharp all the same. The man was too far gone to have heard. I did not think the words had ever been meant for him. I looked at the scene for some time. The blood was thinning in the rain, and Ash's hands and back were shaking.

Then I looked at the egg. I crossed to it, knelt, and touched it. It was warm beneath my fingers as I gingerly inspected it. The black veins pulsed faintly in the dark. It was whole. I held it for one breath, feeling the rhythm against my palm. It was slow and steady, but it seemed to quicken as I touched it.

I rose with the egg cradled against my chest. It was whole. The warmth pressed into my skin through the cloth. I held it, just for a moment. Then I put the egg in the satchel, and I looked at Ash's hands. They were trembling. I stepped behind her.

“Ash?” I said her name. She flinched and did not turn, though she must surely have heard me. The rain ran down her back and she did not seem to feel it. I almost said it again, more softly this time. I did not know how.

"I couldn't stop," she finally said. Her voice came out strange. "I saw your face. I saw it and I just -I couldn't stop." Her hands flexed at her sides, open and closed, open and closed. "Every time I hit him I got angrier and angrier."

I did not know what to say to that. Perhaps there was nothing I could have said, even if I knew how. Ash stared only at the ground. The rain washed the blood from her hands, slowly. I stood a single step behind her. The distance between us somehow seemed larger and smaller than it ever had before. I knew what I had to do, though I had never considered doing it before. The only arms that had ever held me were Sara's -small and warm in a way I had not earned. That was the only shape I knew for this.

I wrapped my arms around Ash from behind. I did not know how tightly to hold, or where my hands should rest, or whether I was doing this correctly at all. It did not matter. It did not matter at all. My arms settled around her shoulders, pulling the Hero back against me.

The Hero was very still. We stood in the rain together. There was a flash of lightning, and the roar of thunder followed soon after. The rain was cold, and Ash was very warm. When I spoke, my voice was a murmur. Barely louder than the pattering rain. "This is not the kind of thing a Hero should do."

Something inside the Hero loosened at that. Her shoulders dropped first, and I saw her hands slowly unclench. She leaned back against me, just slightly. When she spoke, her voice was quieter than mine had been. I almost did not hear it at all. "I couldn't stop. What kind of Hero does that?" She shook her head. "I was never a Hero at all." Then the rest of her was shaking, just as her voice had done.

My arms tightened around her. I did not say anything immediately. I did not speak for some time. I had said the wrong thing before, and had insulted when I meant to praise. I would not do so here. "You found it, Ash. That is what matters. That is what is important. And-"

I had to force the rest out. Because it was important, and the Hero needed to hear it. "And more than anything else…I am grateful." My voice was small. Smaller than I had ever heard it.

Ash's shaking stopped entirely. Then she let out a sound somewhere between a choked cry and a startled laugh. We stayed like that for a time, over the bleeding man, in the rain. I held her until the shaking in her hands -the last of her that still shook- finally stopped.

I released her eventually and stepped back. I looked down at the man. He was unconscious. His breathing was thin and wet. The arm Ash had taken was gone in one unnaturally clean cut. I saw it now, a few feet away in the mud. The man's bruises were swelling beneath the rain, and his chest rose and fell in a way that looked wrong. The Requiem stirred and this time, I let it.

It took some time to turn mana into Requiem Essence. I gathered it, and let it form in my right hand. I used the mark, and a ball of dark flame bloomed and swirled in front of my palm. I kept drawing in more mana, turning more and more of it into Essence. Before long, the ball of black fire was almost bigger than the man himself. It swallowed the rain around it. It was a darkness that made the night seem bright.

It would be so easy. One touch, and there would be nothing left of this man. The Requiem would not even leave the shape of him in the mud. This man had committed a sin graver than almost any. He had touched what was mine. He had touched something I could not replace. The dark flame swelled, and now the ball of Ruin was larger than the man.

I looked at Ash. The Hero had not so much as paid my ball of Ruin a single glance. Instead, she stared at the ground beneath her. I realized she had not looked anywhere else for some time. I did not understand this. She had killed far worse men for far better reasons, and yet she would not look at her own hands. If I killed him now, this weight would not lift from her. I did not understand why, but I had seen enough of Ash to know that this strange guilt of hers was real.

“Damn it,” I murmured under my breath. The ball of flame died in my hand.

And so I knelt in the mud next to the man. I reached for him with my left, and poured Bloom into him. The brilliant white light of the Cradle shone against the dark. And through this power, I felt impressions. There was pain, confusion, and the sharp taste of regret. There was much regret. Under my power, the man's wounds healed. The worst of his bruising receded. The thin, wet breathing steadied into something less fragile.

Then I rose. The man was still bleeding, and it was unlikely he would walk or even wake for some time. I could have healed him entirely and had chosen not to. He was lucky I did not take another one of his limbs from him.

Without the Cradle, it was dark again. Ash was watching me now. Her eyes were red. "I would not have expected the Demon Queen to heal a man who stole from her," she said.

I smiled, just slightly. "It seems we are both full of surprises."

Something in her face softened. She smiled back. The distance between us had not changed, and yet it felt smaller, somehow.
