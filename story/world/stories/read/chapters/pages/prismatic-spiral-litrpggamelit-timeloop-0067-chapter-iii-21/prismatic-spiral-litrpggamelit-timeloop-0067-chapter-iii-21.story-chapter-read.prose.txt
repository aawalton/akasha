Part IV - Porcelain Island

Chapter II.I (2.1)

The barrier materialized in a blink of an eye, carving the ship’s stern clean away. Unlike her first loop, Mitsuko did not steady herself with her magical sword. Instead, she face planted on the wooden deck. She closed her eyes and with a groan, sat up. A quick cast of Mend soothed the ache on the side of her head.

While sailors and passengers scurried around her, desperate to evacuate the sinking ship, Mitsuko took a moment to stretch her arms and refamiliarize herself with her adult body.

For the last several days she’d been in a younger form of this same body, using it to infiltrate and dismantle an abductor’s camp of brainwashed children. With the start of a new loop, the body reverted to its twenties. The sudden juxtaposition made her body now feel gangly and disproportioned, despite being an average sized adult.

Still, the ship was sinking so Mitsuko decided not to spend more time lolligaging. She stood up, preparing to begin her normal routine. Only…she found herself staring at the severed stern of the ship. The Selcouth Sable’s elementalist desperately fought to keep the water from flooding into the open hull and plunging them all into the sea’s depths. Her power ebbed and flowed like a tide as she pressed back against the sea in a dance of control.

Mitsuko stepped up beside the elementalist. Behind her, she heard the captain bellowing at her to get in a lifeboat. But Mitsuko ignored the man and knelt, pressing a hand against the wooden deck. Then she channeled into the boat, willing her magic to Mend the ship.

The backend of the ship remained stuck in place on the other side of the barrier, trapped in a moment of time and unreachable through her current level of spellcraft. But rather than call on that, Mitsuko willed into existence the ship’s previous state of being.

The Selcouth Sable stopped its descent into the sea.

Mitsuko Mended the stern of the ship back together. Newly formed wooden planks grew out from the back of the ship. An exact replica of what had been lost moments earlier. It took some time, but it pieced itself back together before her eyes.

Her most recent level of Mend had added a new ability to her arsenal. Matter creation. It cost a mage a great deal more blood to create objects rather than manipulate existing ones, so this particular skill had been locked behind the eighth level of her spell.

If Mitsuko had waited a few minutes longer, she doubted she could piece the ship back together. The sheer size of the creation created an additional limit on what her spell could accomplish. Sterling had told her before that there was an extremely complex equation to account for the size, time, distance, and complexity of objects Mended.

Breathing heavily, Mitsuko admired her work. There was still sea water trapped in the hull below, but a negligible amount. The elementalist should be able to remove it.

“What…what did you just do?”

Mitsuko looked up to see the elementalist gaping at her. A glance over her shoulder revealed dozens of other people staring, as if in a dazed trance. Only a couple lifeboats had the time to launch so far. Mitsuko had repaired the ship before anyone else had evacuated. Among the crowd she spotted, her friend Holly’s familiar purple hair.

“I stopped the ship from sinking,” Mitsuko said simply. “Why? Were you keen to go for a swim?”

The ship’s captain stomped up to her. “What sort of magic did you just use? Are you the one who damaged my ship?”

Mitsuko rolled her eyes and opened her mouth to speak.

The water around them burst. Orange tentacles with suckers the size of her head lashed out all around them. One of the first lifeboats to launch toppled over, the occupants falling into the sea. The other lifeboat desperately attempted to row back to The Selcouth Sable.

The ship’s sailors snapped out of their stupor and panicked shouts rang out across the deck.

Mitsuko reached out to cast Mend on the people in the sea. She needed to right their boat and get the people back to safety. But, whether due to her exhaustion from her earlier feat, or the distance between herself and them, her spell didn’t reach them before the tentacles dragged the passengers under the surface and out of sight.

“Dammit!” she cursed. She turned her attention to the other lifeboat. The one that remained afloat with passengers huddled at its center. She cast Mend on it, dragging the lifeboat back towards them. Less than a minute later, it was once again secured on their ship.

Now it was time for defensive tactics. Her new goal was to keep The Selcouth Sable afloat. Outlast the kraken for a few minutes until the ship could safely put some distance between them and the sea monster.

One of the kraken’s tentacles slammed into the side of the ship, bursting apart the gunwales and grasping at the sailors and passengers on board. But the sailors weren’t incompetent. This was likely not their first run in with a sea monster. They fought, wielding spells, blades, and even some enchanted equipment. Still, despite their efforts, Mitsuko witnessed one sailor have his face ripped off while attempting to free himself from a suction cup and another lay sprawled out on the deck after being crushed by the kraken’s might.

“Put wind in the sails,” Mitsuko demanded the elementalist. “The dome has completely cut us off from natural wind.”

She blinked at Mitsuko. Then got to work on the task, shifting her attention from the attacking kraken with shards of ice over to pumping the sails with air.

They were now moving.

Mitsuko flicked her wrist, the artifact ring on her finger formed a blade of ice in her hand. Then she dashed off to join the fight.

A second tentacle burst from the port side of the ship. Mitsuko steadied her stance and sliced in an upwards movement as the tentacle descended. While the blade itself stood no chance of severing all the way through the massive appendage, she still left a nasty gash in the kraken’s flesh and bisected one of its suction cups.

Abandoning the blade buried in the kraken flesh, Mitsuko created a second blade with another flick of her wrist. The tentacle lashed out, slamming into her. But this time her slightly curved blade pierced through it, point first. This technique relied on the beast’s own momentum to damage it, like a vertical caltrop.

While the attack likely pained the kraken, the force of it still flung Mitsuko to the side where her back slammed into one of the ship’s masts. She heard the crunch of her spine right as sudden numbness took over the bottom half of her body. But before she even hit the deck, Mitsuko had already cast Mend on herself, healing the cracked spinal cord.

A passenger whimpered beside her, huddling in the ship’s wreckage. At a glance, Mitsuko diagnosed the man’s broken arm as well as the scrapes that tore through his clothing. He’d likely been thrown to the side earlier, similar to the attack that just hit Mitsuko.

She set a hand on his shoulder and cast Mend, healing not only his wounds, but also stitching his clothes back together.

He blinked and raised his now unbroken arm, aghast.

“You can thank me later. Go below deck where it’s safe,” she said, before throwing herself back into the fight.
