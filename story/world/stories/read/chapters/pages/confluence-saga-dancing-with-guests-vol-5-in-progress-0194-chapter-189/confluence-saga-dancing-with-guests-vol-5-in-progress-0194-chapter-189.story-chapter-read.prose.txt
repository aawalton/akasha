Heirs, Scions, and future rulers

Widepond Holdings seem to mostly operate on two scales. So do businesses and companies, but those large enough to worry about succession are almost always closely associated to a Holding or Family.

Feudal Holdings almost always have direct heirs. Their rulers often do not make it pass the Wall, do not live to see a fourth generation of descendants, let alone a tenth. Their children will one day become rulers themselves, so they name them heir and let things proceed as expected.

Family Holdings, for lack of a more useful term, tend to refer to potential heirs as scions. They are the possible heirs for each generation, the descendants who might one day rise to significance within the core structure. Some become rulers of their own branches, others simply gain titles of importance.

You cannot have dozens, or hundreds of heirs. You can have hundreds of scions, each one a possible ruler, should the responsibility fall on them during a transition. There may even be a single named Heir, the one most likely to ascend. But to state it as a guarantee would be most foolish.

Your mother is asking if you're willing..? Gwen sent.

She's lower level. Her Holding is significantly more established but if it came to it…

She wants to spend time with you? Not to criticize?

"She can. As long as she understands it's a family visit for everyone, not just for her. She won't be Sovereign."

"I'll make sure Tomas knows," Fay said. "Or rather let her know myself, since he may not have found his answer in time."

Anya felt his fingers shift and realized she was clenching her hand on them. It likely wasn't enough to actually hurt but it had to be uncomfortable.

"Sorry," she whispered as she forced her hand to lie flat on the table.

Anya? Gwen sent.

Yes?

I don't know everything, but your mother isn't like Lady Phila. She isn't-

I know! she sent back sharply then winced at the feeling of pain she got back.

I mean I know. She isn't the same. But she…

Anya looked over to spot Seb leaning against the arena wall, watching her.

"Done?" she asked.

"I, myself, wasn't as upset," he said as he pulled her into a hug. "And apparently neither were you."

She turned to glance at the handful of training dummies she had let her frustration out on and then back at him.

"Four isn't upset?"

Rather than answer he turned her towards the far side of the field where Gwen and Cara showed no signs of slowing. It only looked like three or four each but where hers were simply broken theirs were beyond any hope of salvage.

Gwen?

She. I. Why. Break!

Gwen!

She saw Gwen pause and look towards her, visibly shaking.

Anya?

Feel better?

She didn't need the band to recognize Gwen's emotions as she brought her mace down firmly enough to send shrapnel flying from the remnants of a torso. Or to understand why it fell from her hand a moment later.

"She needs Cara."

"I know," Seb answered. "She's stubborn though."

She was about to ask what he meant when his sword flickered into his hand only to go flying across the field to crash into Cara's current target. Even that didn't seem quite enough to get her to stop slashing away, at least until the blade visibly pulsed. A moment later Cara's arms were around Gwen and both were sinking to sit on the arena floor.

Anya felt her connection with Gwen vanish almost violently, her thoughts recoiling and finding Seb's in the same moment.

We should leave them be, he sent.

Cara kicked you out too?

Anya, he sent, his thoughts smoothing away the last of the irritation the dummies hadn't managed. I don't know if you and Gwen were filtering at all. You seemed extremely in sync at times. Cara wasn't, even as she rushed over.

She tried to think back, to figure out how much she had felt from Gwen while they were in the arena. She could feel the rage, the pain, the frustration, but she wasn't entirely sure whose it was.

I don't think we were filtering, not much. And she felt raw at the end.

Cara was lashing out at two things at the same time, he sent. How they treated her, and how they treated Gwen all the years when she couldn't act.

Gwen couldn't act either. But just then it was her mother.

I'm going to need to find some dummies later myself.

I thought you weren't as upset?

She felt his emotions shift and almost asked why he had been filtering. Then she realized he hadn't, not the way she would have meant. He was there, he was in her thoughts, in her awareness, they were nearly as close as when they melded. The emotions he wanted to vent weren't part of that.

Cara wasn't filtering. I was trying to stay stable so at least one of us was.

So that's-

-what bled through. What I tried to keep distant.

She took a step away from him and paused just long enough to let him feel the invitation to follow.

There's the smaller field. Pretty sure no one will interrupt us in there, or surprise us.

We won't have any dummies.

Do you need to break something? Or just-

She laughed as she ducked his overly choreographed blow before repositioning herself at the center of the private field.

No techniques and no weapons, she sent. I bet you won't be able to hit me.

She slipped past his next few strikes before he sent anything resembling an answer, each one more obvious than the last. She could feel him planning something, she knew he fought better than this, even on the rare occasions he did grow truly angry.

Se-?

She stumbled, briefly shocked by the burst of emotions. It wasn't the anger he'd suppressed, even if it held the intensity. She felt herself start to blush even as his hands trapped her arms, pulling her close for a kiss. Before she could return it he was a half step away, a proper punch glancing off her shoulder as she dodged an instant too late.

It wasn't enough to throw her off balance, it was barely enough for her to even realize he'd touched her.

Hit you.

That's cheating!

Only because you didn't think of it first.

She giggled before brushing his next blow aside and stepping into him, close enough that neither could attack. She looked up at him, blew him a kiss as their eyes met then lunged forward. If she'd been any further from him he might have caught her, might have turned it into an embrace. Instead he fell backward, staring up at her from flat on his back as she balanced on her toes and one hand above him.

You're right, she said as she straightened, only to find herself tangled up with him as he rolled into her leg.

They took their time separating. They were alone, they could follow whatever rules they felt like. Including trying to distract one another to get an advantage.
