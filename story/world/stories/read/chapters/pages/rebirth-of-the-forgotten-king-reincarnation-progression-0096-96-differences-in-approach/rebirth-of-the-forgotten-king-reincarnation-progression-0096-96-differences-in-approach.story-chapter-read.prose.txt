Disciplinary Committee Private Office

The space was larger than Adrian had expected, but not in the grand, sprawling way that some of the academy’s halls were. No, this room was designed for function, not opulence.

Dark mahogany bookshelves lined the walls, filled with thick tomes bound in deep reds, blues, and blacks. A long, polished table sat in the center, its surface unmarred, except for a few neatly arranged documents and a single ornate quill resting in an inkpot. The walls bore no windows, only dim lanterns casting elongated shadows.

Adrian’s boots felt heavier against the floor as he stepped further in. So this is where we'll be working, it's… certainly more utilitarian than I expected. The thought gnawed at his nerves. Everything about this place radiated authority, and yet, there was something almost oppressive in its silence.

Zuri took her place without hesitation, moving with the same poised grace she always carried. She stood near the head of the table, hands clasped tightly behind her back as she waited for Myrael to speak.

Liora, on the other hand, slumped against the edge of a bookshelf. Her tail flicked idly, betraying the restlessness in her stance. “So, are we gonna talk about the warm welcome we got out there, or should we pretend that went smoothly?”

Vaelion let out a low chuckle, slipping into one of the chairs. “Oh, come now,” he mused, resting an arm along the back of his seat. “I thought it was rather entertaining. Did you see the looks on their faces? I’d say we made quite the impression.”

Adrian’s fingers curled slightly at his sides. Impression? That was one way to put it. The murmur of disbelief from the crowd still echoed in his head. The skepticism. The doubt.

Myrael, standing by the table, finally let out a slow, indulgent hum. She removed her wide-brimmed hat and placed it carefully on the surface, smoothing down a stray lock of her hair before turning to them. “Oh, darlings, did you expect a standing ovation?"

Liora rolled her eyes. “I would’ve settled for less staring.”

“Ah, but staring is good, it means they don’t know what to make of you yet. And uncertainty, my dear, is a far more powerful weapon than outright fear.”

Zuri’s lips pressed into a thin line. “Uncertainty isn’t respect.”

Myrael arched a brow. “No, but it’s a start.”

Adrian shifted uncomfortably. He wanted to believe that, but doubt was certainly present in his mind. The students don't respect the disciplinary committee yet, that much I know. I wonder if this whole thing will work out?

Myrael’s gaze flicked to him then. She leaned forward just slightly, her voice lowering to something almost conspiratorial. “They are simply testing you. But let me tell you a little secret, dear enforcers, those same people who sneered, who whispered, who doubted? Give them time, and they will be the same ones who bow their heads when you walk by.”

Adrian swallowed. That’s a bold promise.

Vaelion smirked from his seat. “And what if they don’t?”

“Oh, my sweet thing,” Myrael chuckled, reaching out to brush an invisible speck of dust from his uniform sleeve. “Then you make them.”

“Not sure if that was comforting or concerning.” Liora said.

Myrael straightened, amusement dancing in her crimson eyes. “It was both. But you’ll understand soon enough.”

The silence stretched between them, heavy and expectant. Myrael’s words lingered in the air like a trace of perfume, sweet but laced with something sharper beneath. Adrian shifted where he stood, his fingers lightly curling at his sides. Make them? She has some rather grand ambitions, I'll give her that much.

Before anyone could press further, Myrael turned with a graceful sweep of her hand. A faint shimmer flickered in the space above the table, an almost imperceptible ripple in the air, before a scroll materialized out of nothing. It floated for the briefest moment, suspended in a ghostly glow, before dropping soundlessly onto the polished wood.

The parchment was thick, bound with an ornate black seal bearing the insignia of the Disciplinary Committee: a sharp, angular crest resembling an open eye. Oh?

Liora’s tail flicked as she eyed the scroll. “Alright, I’ll bite, what is it?”

“Your first assignment, of course.” Myrael traced a single gloved finger along the table’s edge. “Read the instructions carefully. I trust you will handle it… appropriately.”

“What exactly are we walking into?” Adrian asked.

Myrael let out a soft hum, tilting her head slightly. “Now, now, dear Adrian. That would spoil the fun.”

Adrian stiffened. “It’s not fun if we don’t know what we’re dealing with.”

“Oh, but it is. The unknown is the spice of life, is it not?”

Zuri let out a quiet sigh. “Principal Faesbane. If this is our first assignment, shouldn’t we at least receive guidance?”

Myrael exhaled through her nose, amusement never leaving her expression. “Guidance? You are the Disciplinary Committee. You are the guidance now.”

"That's not a proper answer, and you know that." Adrian said.

But before he could argue further, Myrael snapped her fingers. A plume of violet-black smoke burst outward from where she stood, curling through the air in twisting tendrils. The moment Adrian’s vision cleared, she had vanished without so much as an echo of footsteps.

Liora let out a low whistle. “Dramatic and unhelpful. Love that.”

Zuri exhaled, pinching the bridge of her nose. “No point in wasting time. Let’s read the mission brief.”

She strode toward the table, breaking the wax seal before unfurling the scroll. The parchment unrolled smoothly, revealing lines of elegant script inked in deep indigo.

Adrian stepped closer, scanning the words as Zuri read aloud:

Disciplinary Committee Assignment: 01Classification: Enforcement Order

To the appointed enforcers of Mistral Royal Academy,

You are hereby tasked with investigating and sanctioning the illicit distribution and consumption of Duskrime, a contraband substance circulating within academy grounds. This substance, known for its hallucinogenic properties, is both dangerous and strictly forbidden.

Mandates & Powers:

Persons of Interest:

Failure to address this matter efficiently will be seen as a dereliction of duty. Do not disappoint.

— M.F.

Silence stretched between them as Zuri finished reading the mission brief, the weight of its contents settling over the room like an oppressive fog. Adrian swallowed, his eyes flicking back to the parchment. Duskrime. Illegal, dangerous, and already making its way through the academy. We're expected to deal with the problem, but as expected, our hands are tied. While we can report and investigate, we can't detain anyone. Any use of force, even if technically allowed in certain circumstances, would also likely face heavy scrutiny.

But more importantly, the Scarlet Veil drew Adrian's interest most. The Mourne definitely have activity in the academy, there's a chance that the Scarlet Veil are tied to them, or perhaps are just them using a cover name.

Liora let out a low whistle, breaking the silence first. “Well, that’s certainly one heck of a mission."

Vaelion leaned back in his chair, his expression unreadable. "And a complicated one at that. There’s an entire network here with students, underground dealings, faculty involvement. And we’re expected to dismantle it with nothing but a title and vague authority?”

Dain adjusted his glasses, frowning. “We do have authority. It’s outlined here—search permissions, full reporting access to Myrael herself. We are legally sanctioned to investigate.”

Liora snorted. “Oh, yeah. Because a bunch of third-years are just going to hand over their illicit drugs the moment we flash a badge?”

“That’s not the point. The point is that we need a structured approach. Rushing in without planning will only make things worse.”

“Oh, sure, let’s sit around and strategize while more people get high on that stuff, maybe even overdose. Perfect plan, genius.”

“Reckless action is worse than no action at all. You don’t even know the scale of the problem yet.”

“And we won’t if we don’t move."

Zuri sighed, her fingers pressing against the bridge of her nose. “This is getting us nowhere.”

Adrian exhaled sharply, feeling frustration coil in his chest. His gaze flicked between them: Liora’s glare, Dain’s scowl, Vaelion watching with mild amusement as if waiting to see who would win. The whole thing was ridiculous.

He slammed his palm down on the table, the sharp crack echoing through the room. “Are you all serious right now? We just got our first mission, and you’re all too busy arguing to do a damn thing about it.”

Liora blinked, caught off guard by his outburst. Dain’s frown deepened, but he said nothing. Even Zuri tilted her head slightly, watching him carefully.

Adrian inhaled deeply, forcing himself to steady his breathing. “Look, I get it. This isn’t some minor infraction. But if we can’t even work together for five minutes, how are we supposed to handle this Duskrime situation?”

He gestured toward the brief, his jaw tightening. “We don’t have all the answers. We probably won’t have them for a while. But fighting each other instead of the problem? That’s a great way to fail before we even start.”

Silence followed. But soon, Zuri nodded. “Adrian’s right.”

The quiet confidence in her voice carried weight, more than any of them probably wanted to admit. She turned her piercing gaze toward the others. “We may have different approaches, but at the end of the day, this is our job now. The academy expects us to deal with this. We expect ourselves to deal with this. So instead of arguing, let’s divide the tasks and move forward.”

Liora sighed, rolling her shoulders as if physically shaking off her irritation. “Fine. What’s the plan, Adrian?”

Adrian blinked. He hadn’t expected them to defer to him so quickly, but the moment passed, and he pushed forward before hesitation could set in.

“Alright,” he said, rubbing his temples before straightening. “First, we need to split up the workload. Trying to investigate everything at once would be a mess.”

His eyes flicked to Liora. “Silas Verdane is a third-year. You’re a senior too, and you have a reputation. You’ll be able to get into those circles easier than the rest of us. You take Silas.”

Liora arched a brow, smirking. “You saying I give off criminal vibes, Adrian?”

“I’m saying you’ll get people to talk,” Adrian deadpanned.

Liora chuckled. “Fair enough. I’ll handle it.”

He turned to Vaelion next. “You take Ilya Morcant. He’s a second-year, and the reports mention ‘unusual magical outbursts.’ If he’s really using Duskrime, he could be unpredictable. You’re the best at staying cool in tense situations.”

Vaelion gave a small, almost lazy nod. “Fine by me. I do enjoy a challenge.”

Adrian glanced at Dain. “You and I both know this drug didn’t get into the academy without help,” he said, tapping the parchment where it mentioned faculty involvement. “You take point on looking into potential faculty connections: background checks, suspicious activities, anything that doesn’t add up.”

Dain pursed his lips in thought but nodded. “That’s logical. I can start with faculty members who have access to restricted storage areas: alchemy, medicine, and high-tier magic labs.”

Finally, Adrian turned to Zuri. “You and I will focus on The Scarlet Veil. If this alias is tied to what I think it is, then they’re the key to everything."

Zuri held his gaze for a moment before giving a single nod. “Agreed.”

Adrian exhaled, tension bleeding from his shoulders. “Alright. We have our assignments. We check in daily, report findings, and work together. Got it?”

A round of nods followed. Liora stretched, rolling her neck. “Well, guess it’s time to make some criminals very uncomfortable.”

Vaelion smirked. “Let the games begin.”
