We didn't make it far.

Around halfway around the city we were spotted. Well, we were probably spotted well before that, but the first time they met us was about halfway around the city. Two of them, elves, again a male and female, did they call them men and women? Were there different words? I didn't know, but it wasn't important. They met us in the woods, waiting in the path we had chosen.

It was not the same ones as before. I could tell both by the look of them, and by the differences in their auras, which I was slowly getting the feel for.“Hello,” one said in our language.

That stopped everyone in their tracks. Because the ones who'd come to us hadn't spoken our language at all. They'd spoken... elf? I wasn't honestly sure what they called their language. There might even be multiple languages, things tended to do that in my experience. Regardless, she spoke well enough, and in our language.

“Hello, we are just passing through,” Big Op said.

“This is fine, but we have an offer,” she replied.

“What offer?”“Our (City), we built it, would you like to live in it?” The word she used for city was unfamiliar, but it was also clear what she meant.

“Why?” my mother asked, looking confused.

“We wish to teach, our leader taught us much, and wishes us to spread teaching to others too. We mean no harm to you.”

“You want to teach us?” Big Op said, confused.

“Yes, but first, think about seeing our city, and if you'd like to live there.”

“These are shamans,” Pa said softly from nearby.

“Yes,” I agreed.

Pa looked at me, with wide eyes, blinking, as if I'd said something very deep.

“The boy is right,” the male elf finally said, almost smiling.

That was not helpful, not at all. For both they and my own group were now looking at me, a mix of alarm, surprise, and some amount of joy. Mother held me closer, not wanting me to get far from her now.

“He is correct, he can probably see our (auras), as we can see his,” the male continued. “We are happy to explain, and to train if you wish.”

Our group didn't seem happy about the offer, but also didn't seem to want to say no, not for the least because they were probably able to kill us all. Even if that seemed unlikely, as they'd not done anything aggressive, shamans were viewed as dangerous, and it seemed saying 'no' was frowned on.

“Alright, we will come,” Big Op said.

They led us through the forest, trying to make small talk, but between them seemingly not knowing some of the words for specific things, which I didn't know either, and our little group's general intimidation there wasn't much to say.

As we got closer I got a better and better look at the place. The architecture was strange, here and there they were trying to plant trees that I didn't recognize, but those didn't seem to be working in the way they wanted. The buildings were sort of blocky in shape, simple designs for the most part.

As well everything was made of stone, if what I was seeing was right. There were arches in a few places, and I could see far off one or two elves still building, moving blocks into place with magic. That image seemed to spook my little tribe even more, as there were clearly a lot of shamans here.

“Are all of you shamans?” Big Op finally asked, seeing the others far off.

“Most,” the female replied, not looking back. “As we get older most of us get stronger, but all of us have some small amount of magic in us. Even children can do small things.”

I almost sighed, it was clear she'd meant it as something explanatory, but it was also clear what it was doing to my people. Really, whoever these elves were and wherever they came from they needed to get better at reading the room.

“How... how are you making these stones?” Ro asked as he ran a hand over one of the nearby buildings, the surface smoother than anything he made.

“We cut them from the ground, and move them in place to use. They make good houses, warm at night, cool in the day, strong against wind and rain. Many of your people use wood and leaves, and those work well, but these last longer and stand stronger,” the male explained.

“But you will hunt the area clean soon,” he observed, then looked up at the flying dirigible. “Maybe not though...”“Yes, there is less hunting here, but we will (farm).”“(Farm)?” the older man asked.

“Put plants where we want to make them grow, animals too sometimes, though animals can be harder. Once you have enough in a place, you can always just pull it in, put more out, and grow plenty.”

Now that, that got everyone to stop. There weren't many of us, and I knew for a fact that with enough land any idiot had a good chance at subsistence farming. Back on Earth there were certainly failures, but if you planed enough stuff, and enough variety of it, you could really only fail so many ways. Now they were hearing about it for the first time.

It made me smile, for it seemed I wouldn't need to introduce some of the basic technologies I could remember to my people, like farming, or the wheel, or I don't know, the pleasures of bathing. The last would have been hard without doing metal, and I had no idea how to do that, but it seemed these elves had worked it out already.“And you're willing to teach us this?” Big Op asked.

“Of course, it is why we are here, well part of it,” the male clarified.

“We can also help fix your leg if you need,” the female said to Ro.

“I... what will it cost us?” he asked, for even in this world, there was no such thing as a free lunch.

“There are some rules, but not too much,” she said with a smile. “Let us talk.”
