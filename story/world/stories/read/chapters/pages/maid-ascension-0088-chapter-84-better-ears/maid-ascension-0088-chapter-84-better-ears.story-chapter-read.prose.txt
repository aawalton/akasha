Lira spent the rest of her day off catching up with Maren, feeding her some harmless palace gossip, and enjoying a quiet day where she got to be a normal girl before finally returning to the palace by dusk and going to bed early.

The following morning, she woke well before dawn, long before the first servants began moving through the castle. She headed to the library, then slipped through the hidden entrance into her alcove.

“Candira. Fila. Curad.”

Lira lit a single candle, extracted her father's ledger as well as her notebook, and settled into one of the chairs.

Her success with the soundproofing spell had left her feeling confident about sound magic as a discipline. The speed with which she had imprinted the spell made it feel like she could do anything she wanted, so naturally, she decided to create a new spell, something that could help her gather information, maybe.

Could she improve her hearing the same way she had improved her vision with the Eagle Eye spell?

She dipped her pen into the ink and wrote three words.

Pika. Cipos. Hishar.

Mind. Amplification. Wave.

Lira studied the incantation for several moments. The words were familiar. It was the same incantation as Eagle Eye but with Syneth replaced with Hishar.

"It should work..." she murmured.

Starting simple was always best. That way, it would be easily imprinted.

She closed her eyes.

"Pika. Selan. Tureth."

The world faded away as meditation overtook her thoughts.

Rather than forcing new ideas together, she allowed the concepts to overlap naturally. She thought about everything she had learned over the past week, about her understanding of magic in general and sound as a concept. She focused on the concept of amplification and her practice with wave manipulation to adjust the volume by making tiny vibrations become larger.

She thought about the mind and how adaptable it was, and about her Eagle Eye spell and how it was a completely internal spell that improved her vision. So it should be possible to make a spell that did the same thing but for her hearing without actually making the sound louder, only her perception of it.

Pika. Cipos. Hishar.

Click.

She opened her eyes and smiled to herself.

"That was fast."

She always enjoyed that tiny sensation whenever a spell was imprinted successfully. It felt as though she had taken another step toward a greater understanding of magic.

Now it was time for testing.

"Pika. Cipos. Hishar."

Immediately, she regretted it as she was overwhelmed by noise. The scratching of her sleeve against the desk exploded inside her ears, the candle flame sounded like a roaring wind, her own breathing echoed through her skull, and even her own heartbeat felt like a drum. She was so overwhelmed by nearby sounds that she couldn't even begin to separate the cacophony coming from outside as birds sang, people chatted, and the sounds of early morning labor all mixed together into a confusing headache.

"Ah!"

She instantly dropped the spell and clamped both hands over her ears, squeezing her eyes shut.

For several long seconds, she simply sat there breathing.

"...Ow."

Gradually, her senses returned to normal.

She rubbed one ear before letting out an exhausted sigh.

"That..." She shook her head. "It was awful."

She stood up and dug into one of her food stashes for some candy, the sweet treat soothing her headache.

The spell had technically worked. But technically wasn't good enough. It amplified every sound reaching her equally and without distinction. Nearby noises drowned out distant ones until the entire world blended into one overwhelming wall of noise.

When she had done the same thing with sight, it worked because, when it came to sight, what was nearby was just invisible air.

Hearing sound was different, as sound was everywhere.

She reached for her notebook and wrote beneath the spell.

Failure.

Amplifying everything is useless if I cannot understand any of it.

She leaned back in her chair, tapping the quill against her notebook.

She needed a way to improve the selection of sound rather than just blindly increasing the volume. She needed better-directed hearing rather than louder hearing.

After a few moments of consideration, she decided to add a few words to the spell, making it tier one. The first was Velar, meaning Clarity. It was used in her cleaning spells to determine whether or not something should be eliminated, as well as in her improved perception spell, where it ordered her thoughts. Clarity here could help organize information into something the mind could easily comprehend while dismissing random junk noises.

The second word she decided to add was Kaldris, Retrieval. The word was very versatile. She had used it in divination, knowledge gathering, as well as ink cleanup and book summoning.

Retrieval could help her recover sounds that would otherwise fade before reaching her ears, improving the range of the spell quite a bit while adding a more directed aspect to it.

Pika. Cipos. Hishar. Kaldris. Velar.

Mind. Amplification. Wave. Retrieval. Clarity.

Now this looked promising. This spell felt different before she had even begun meditating.

Rather than amplifying every sound indiscriminately, Kaldris, Retrieval, would search for the sounds she actually wished to perceive. Amplification would strengthen those selected waves, while Clarity would organize the result into something understandable by sifting through the flood of noise.

It was a Focused Hearing spell.

She closed her eyes once more.

"Pika. Selan. Tureth."

Meditation embraced her again.

This time, she began with her previous spells.

She first thought about the most recent spell, how it worked, and why it had failed. She then recalled her enhanced perception spell and how Velar worked as a concept there, highlighting countless tiny details into something useful. Then she remembered Eagle Eye, where Cipos enhanced her own mind rather than doing something external. She focused on her Book Summoning spell, where Kaldris helped her retrieve something physical, yet in her Divine Past spell, the same word worked on knowledge. Surely that meant it could also retrieve sound waves.

She visualized a wave of sound traveling through the air, slowly growing weaker as it collided with other noisy waves. The spell only needed to retrieve it before it disappeared completely. Once caught, Amplification could strengthen it, and Clarity could separate it from every other competing sound.

Pika. Cipos. Hishar. Kaldris. Velar.

Click.

Lira opened her eyes with a grin.

"This should be much, much better."

She rose from her chair and quietly walked out of her alcove, heading toward the nearest window. Once there, she looked down over the laundry courtyard below.

The morning had only just begun.

A few maids were already gathered near the well, some pulling water while others poured it into large basins as others mixed in the soap in preparation for the wave of linen that would soon be brought out by the chambermaids.

The perfect test subjects.

Lira rested one hand against the windowsill.

"Pika. Cipos. Hishar. Kaldris. Velar."

The spell settled over her mind, feeling like a second pair of ears rather than an enhancement of her own.

The world remained surprisingly normal, but when she focused on a point above a group of maids below, she started hearing the chatter of the distant servants as if she were standing among them.

"I swear Old Marga hates me. She keeps making my porridge extra spicy," said a very thin maid.

"That's because you refused her when she gave you some of the noble’s pudding," explained her friend.

"It's not my fault I don't like sweet things."

"Oh, you don't like spicy things, you don't like sweet things. Is your tongue made of wood? Why even eat?"

"So I don't die. If I could skip eating, I would."

Lira blinked. She noticed that she wasn't hearing the splashing of water or the rustling of fabric, only the conversation.

With a bit of focus, she managed to return more of the surrounding noise, tuning out the maids' diction and focusing on the sounds of movement as well as the chirping of birds.

She moved her invisible ear toward the stable, where she heard the horses neighing as they were being brushed by the stable boys. She couldn't see them from where she was, but she could hear them. She focused on tuning out all other noise except the conversation.

"Is Master Berem truly going to sell Thunder?" asked a young voice.

"It's a good stallion," said someone older. "He should go to a breeder rather than spending all day here stuck in the stable."

"But he's our best horse," said the boy.

"Weaver is faster."

"But she's only a mare."

"Yes, but she's still faster."

Lira couldn't help smiling.

This spell is amazing.

She could hear everything, spy everywhere; nothing would be beyond her reach.

Then a thought came to her.

"Pika. Maja. Syneth."

Her mage sight overlapped with her normal vision, allowing her to see mana flows and magical interference, and the first thing she saw was a line of magical disturbance linking her with the point of remote hearing.

Panicked, she immediately deactivated the Focused Hearing spell. The disturbance was thin, and it quickly stabilized back into normal ambient mana after only a couple of minutes. Still, that was dangerous, as it directly pointed her out to anyone watching. More importantly, because the line was so long, it extended outside her mana stabilization and mana obfuscation bookmark that hid her spellcasting.

In theory, she could remove “Kaldris” to eliminate the remote hearing aspect. However, after running a few tests while keeping the listening point within her mana obfuscation field, she discovered that the quiet conversation waves didn't reliably reach her to be amplified from that distance. She would end up with choppy fragments of conversation, or nothing at all.

This meant that using the spell effectively would always carry a significant risk.
