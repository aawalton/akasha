“Oh, go on, then. Tell me how I’m wrong. Tell me that you actually got the control you were looking for over your life. Tell me what you would tell yourself if you really did go back, and sit beside your past self, just before that fateful day.”

Sys-Ethan quaffed another liberal shot of JD after he said this, sure in his final, pointless victory over the Demon Hat. Sure that it knew him better than he’d ever known himself.

But the hat that sat beside him on the couch was cool and collected, even as he could see a miasma of white begin to creep down from the sky outside the apartment.

“I would tell him he should try wanting something else.”

Sys would have simply spat whiskey at his crimson eyeball in response to this, were it not for the fact that the apartment TV had started acting strangely.

Where once the image of a longsword-wielding warrior once stood in stasis, trapped beneath the unassailable might of a -PAUSE- screen, now lines of static blurred across the screen.

Then – images started to appear. Moving images that were accompanied by garbled audio. At first, it seemed like just a glitch in the Core Sphere – nothing but a result of the impending deletion that was slowly swallowing up all the citizens in this fake-Boston outside. But slowly, the images began to resolve. Color bled through the screen. Distinguishable shapes started to form. Shapes that were familiar to Ethan blazed into life on the screen even as the deletion process made it difficult for him to remember them:

A rabbit-mage.

A boisterous catgirl rogue.

A musclebound dog-man monk.

A tiny rabbit-child with a beaming smile.

An old druid with lines of regret across his features.

A young priest with eyes that spoke of newfound certainty.

And at the center of them all: a small blue hat with a red eye.

The sounds began to take on meaning too – sounds of laughter, crying, of valorous war-cries and of solemn defeats. But all sounds that brought a strange, inescapable feeling of joy to his thready heart.

“I’m surprised you never realized it, Sys,” he said quietly, watching the memories of his time in Argwyll play out on screen like episodes of his favorite anime serial. “No matter how much you try to deny it, we’re all connected.”

He watched more of the moving images, cringing at some parts, chuckling at others, all while the old him, piloted by Sys, stared impassively through it all.

“I talked a big game. You are right about that. But control? That wasn’t really what I cared about at all. It was just a placebo for what I really needed. Control means nothing, Sys. The more I reached for it, the more I felt it slipped away from me until, finally, I realized not even Gods can really control everything. Who the hell can really say that they are the master of another’s heart and mind, or of their own for that matter? None of that meant a thing. What did matter – more than anything I ever knew could – was looking through another set of eyes besides my own. That’s what Jun gave me. And that’s what’s led us both to this moment.”

Sys tutted. “Very noble. A nice little lesson. If you think to change my mind in the end, you will find yourself again realizing your folly. I have survived as long as I have by exploiting the weaknesses of mortals like you. And – someday – I will live again, Ethan Hawke. Mark me, I will.”

Ethan could have responded to these words with some kind of heroic verbal backhand. But he found that he was far more preoccupied with what was on the TV.

“I had no idea how…easy life could be,” he muttered. “If I just learned to let go…”

“What you speak of is complacency. Nothing more.”

“What I speak of is community, Sys,” Ethan retorted. “It’s the most basic thing, and so I guess we tend to overlook it. But it’s what we really need. Even you.”

“If you think you know me better than I know myse-“

“Oh, come on, Sys,” Ethan chuckled lightly. “You aren’t telling me that you never felt the slightest tingle of satisfaction whenever the Hybrids welcomed us home to Sanctum. That you never enjoyed a single piece of it? Even you could belong, you know. If you just took one look at the world for what it is, even you could live with it rather than against it.”

Visions of Argwyll’s coast and skyline dominated the TV set. Its emerald forests and dense undergrounds flashed by them both like some kind of fantastical nature documentary.

Sys, however, was unimpressed. But he didn’t voice his dissatisfaction. Perhaps he finally realized just how impossible it was to escape his fate. Or perhaps, by now, he was finally ready to die.

“…why did you come here at all?” he decided to ask, watching as the adjacent apartment disappeared just outside the murky window. “Just to lecture me with platitudes? Or were you really naïve enough to believe you could bring just one more being over to your side, at the end?”

Ethan followed his eyes to the window as the show began to end.

He truly hadn’t known exactly why he needed to come here. And he really was still figuring it out at the moment. All he could offer Sys was what his meandering thoughts were telling him now:

“I think – I thought – that I needed to tell you that I was finally ready to let you go. Because in a way you were right about one thing, Sys – I did see myself as a hero. I did want – desperately – to be something that I never was in life. And I never saw anyone else as anything other than a stepping-stone or a toll to help me. The people I met in Argwyll – the creatures and the Hybrids – I never saw them as three-dimensional entities capable of their own thoughts, with their own dreams independent of my own, that even conflicted with my own. But then, well, I’ve gotta hand it to Jun’Ei. Because either through sheer genius or complete accident, she gave me the form that not only Argwyll needed, but that I needed, too.

“I thought I had to come here to see you one last time. And to see if, in the end, you still harbored nothing but hatred for us all. It amazes me that both of us could look through the same sets of eyes – see the exact same things as all our Hosts did – and still come out of this journey so different.”

“Well, sorry to disappoint you, boy.”

The final image then appeared onscreen and stayed there, flickering slightly like a glitch.

But it was no accident.

Because this image was one of Ethan surrounded by Hybrids and humans – all those whom Sys had dragged into the waters of Argwyll.

At the center was Ethan, snug upon the corrupted angel, with his eye wide open.

“…but the reason for me being here is actually more selfish than you think.”

Within that eye, a spectral play of red and green colors swirled.

“I realized that I had to accept that you’re more than just a part of me.”

A rumbling came from outside – the sound of a mindscape collapsing in on itself. The sound of the sky cracking apart.

But it was a sound that both occupants of apartment 10B didn’t pay much attention to.

“I’d been avoiding it for a while,” Ethan said, his mind beginning to meander. “I always told myself that you were just a piece of me that doubted, or a piece of me that represented the worst parts of myself. But that was just another lie I forced myself to believe. No – you’re not just a part of me. You’re everything that I was. That person who was deeply, unapologetically afraid of the world out there. Because that’s what I really was. I was afraid of opening the door to others – to anyone – because then they’d see what a sad, self-righteous sack of shit I was.”

Sys-Ethan rolled his eyes.

“You’re still a sad, self-righteous sack of shit.”

Ethan rolled right back at him.

“But at least I did something useful in the end, right?”

The image of him and his time in Argwyll finally blanked out, leaving him with little more than a distant memory that he knew was soon to be gone.

But it was enough.

“…even if all it was, was to die for real.”

Sys made to pour himself another shot, realized that his glass and bottle had both disappeared, and then looked to see that the four walls of the apartment were now starting to disintegrate, along with all of Ethan’s old memories of earth.

“There’s always something we’re fighting or running from, isn’t there?” Ethan asked – and within the confines of the slowly dissipating space, it was becoming more and more unclear who’s voice was who’s. “I’ve been running from you, fighting you, and you’ve been fighting back and then you ran here. Where things were familiar. Where they’re comfortable. But I’m here to say that I’m done being afraid. I’m done looking to control the trajectory of my life. Instead…I’d like to be whole again.”

Sys-Ethan sat back, letting a small sigh emanate as he sank into the couch, into himself.

“You are truly unafraid?”

“Maybe it’s hard for a galaxy-devouring entity beyond human comprehension to understand, but yeah, I’m not afraid. Before, death felt like an escape. Now, it’s a sacrifice, sure, but my life is a small price to pay for Argwyll. Fact is, I wasn’t really alive until I arrived there. I owe it – all of them – something. And this is all I’ve got to give. So, yeah, I’m not afraid of dying. The question is: are you?”

Within the confines of the slowly dissipating space, it was becoming more and more unclear who’s voice was who’s. But someone turned as the walls, the TV, the console, the floorboards, and the couch finally gave way to a sea of white.

Then all sound, except for one final sentence, was swallowed just like everything else.

“See you in the next life.”

The spectral Light show that had been playing across Argwyll’s cloud-coated skies finally dimmed.

Below, where once the Northern seas raged and tumbled against the land, only a crater-filled wasteland was left. Covering its entire surface were the inhabitants of Argwyll itself – all those, human, hybrid, and monster alike – whom Sys had commanded to march into his jaws.

Thousands upon thousands had thronged to the very apex of the storm that had coated their world. And now, like dreamers waking from a lucid sleep, they rose to a new day.

Their eyes blinked through the haze that had once dominated their vision. And their minds twisted to resolve reality – the stretch of desolate, burned land that lay before them barely even looked like anything resembling what they would call home.

But it was home. And it was still here.

And one of them, slowly coming around, realized before anyone else who that was thanks to.

“By…by Kea…there – there he is…”

It was the young Langley who rushed through the confused hubbub of the crowd, pointing at the stiff, paralyzed figure that rose at the very center of the throng.

Those who looked on followed his gaze. They saw the dark angel that had come to them in their dreams to steal away their futures with its vile burning green eyes.

Now those eyes were blank and vacant – glazed and grey like the rest of its body.

The angel stood rigid as stone, and only one child was brave enough to tiptoe up to the once-Devourer and attempt an Appraisal.

Mara – the Hopla prodigy – palmed the dark angel’s leg and tried to reach into its mind.

But there was nothing there.

“It…he’s dead.”

The crowd that heard could barely even utter a single word, so preoccupied were they with everything that had transpired. But they all soon came to understand. Because each and every one of them received the last System message they’d ever see in their lives:

System Nullification…Complete.

They felt it – they felt something leave them. A presence that had once been there, nestled within all of them. A little nugget of power that they had each harnessed for themselves, until the time when the locus of that power had been ready to take its due.

Now that locus lay before them, petrified and empty, all its power and finery stripped away with one small exception.

Those at the front of the great crowd pointed at the three hybrids who climbed atop the angel-statue, each of them raising a fist in victory.

And though the wolfman among them spoke words that should have inspired uproarious cheers, his and his companions’ faces were streaked with nothing but melancholy:

“The System…is gone!”

Silence in the crowd. All the eyes of every Argwylian stared at the wolf as though they were children watching the conclusion of a play that beggared disbelief.

It took another shout from the Minxit at his side to wake them up:

“Keadmon’s Law – is gone!”

Tara poured her heart into delivering the statement – the death warrant of the old world issued before the chattering crowd. And only then did they begin to understand. Only then did they fall and weep, clutching those close to them, or grabbing at their own hearts. They clutched and cried for what had been lost, for what had been recovered, and for what possibilities now lay before them that they could scarcely even conceive.

Hybrid and human had both felt Sys’s chains round their necks. Even without truly understanding the significance of the Archon’s Guardians, they understood that they had been saved. For now, that was enough.

Only three people in the crowd openly paid a thought to the one thing nobody else was in this moment. The thing that Fauna the Hopla was holding in her hands, cradling gently like a precious, wounded puppy.

Mara noticed first. She ran to Fauna, shouting her name, and then dropped to her knees when she realized.

Others then started to come around. Those closest to him – the Hybrids of Sanctum, the Druids of the Fifth Column, and young Langley – they had felt his presence leave each of them, too, in the moment the evil had been cleansed from their souls.

The clouds cleared. Light peered through the dark, illuminating what some across the world had dreamed of seeing, and what others could never believe would ever come to pass.

Fauna knelt down to place her head against her star pupils’, letting her tiny hands stroke him gingerly as everyone else crowded round to see what was left of Ethan, the Demon Hat, last Archon of Argwyll.

In the years to come, it was said that the silence that followed that moment could be felt across the entire planet.

***

Nestled within a small corner of Argwyll’s Southern mountains, a door opened.

And from the crater it protected, an ancient being emerged for the first time since the world’s conception.

It brought half the ground with it, its massive, hulking body ripping through the corridors that had been its prison for years, and raised its aged wings fully – their span covering the entire mountain range itself.

The Architect opened its onyx eyes to the outside world, and breathed in the air of Argwyll that it had once forsaken as doomed.

But the air smelled different, now. It smelled fresh. New.

And his fangs glinted as the clouds finally parted to reveal the red sun of a new day, and its eyes saw the thing they had wished to see for untold millennia:

System Nullification…Complete.

“So you kept up your bargain, Archon Ethan,” he said. “And you paid the price.”

He felt flames rise in his scaly gullet. It was something he had almost forgotten he could feel.

“Now, let us see this world you put so much stock in.”
