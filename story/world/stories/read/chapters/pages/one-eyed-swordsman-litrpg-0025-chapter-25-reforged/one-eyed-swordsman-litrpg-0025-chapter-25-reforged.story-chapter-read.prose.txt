The knight's armor, blackened and scorched, had split wide at the chest, torn open where Oswald’s final blow had landed. A pool of dark blood soaked the ground beneath it, thick as tar and glistening in the flickering light. His boots squelched as he stepped closer, the sound cutting sharp through the stillness. Pretty sure you weren’t human. But if not… then what were you?

He crouched beside the fallen knight. The helm was dented and warped, its shape ruined by the barrage of stone Oswald had thrown. What was left barely held together.

Curiosity outweighed caution. He reached for the helm, brushing against cold metal. Slowly, he found the edge and pulled.

A faint shudder rippled through the corpse. Just a twitch, but it jolted Oswald back a step, his breath catching in his throat. To his surprise, the knight didn’t move again, but something else stirred.

A dull red glow bled from between the seams of the armor, no brighter than the ember of a dying campfire. It pulsed weakly at first, a soft heartbeat behind steel. From the jagged gash in the chestplate, thin strands of crimson energy slithered outward, curling into the air like smoke from a dying forge.

Oswald’s eyes widened. His foot slid back across the cracked stone. What's going on?

Ash-like flecks peeled from the body, bright red, shedding from the corpse. The air warped where they passed, shimmering like a heat haze. They floated, spun, and twisted, drawn into a spiraling path toward his right eye.

Oswald couldn't move away in time. The moment they touched him, a deep, electric hum bloomed in his skull. The power surged down into his spine, burning hot but not painful. more like a jolt of energy bursting in his veins.

The crimson particles vanished one by one, until there was nothing left of the knight at all. No bones. No blood. Only hollow, scorched armor resting in a heap. A ghost of the thing that had fought him not too long ago.

Oswald stared at it, silent. That’s… new.

He’d seen plenty die before. But never like that. Never just energy leaking out like steam, like whatever was inside hadn’t been alive to begin with. And the way it all rushed into his eye. That… doesn’t usually happen. Normally, I just get the souls and that's it. Maybe Selene knows what all this means. I’ll ask her once I’m done dealing with this place.

[Congratulations, you have gained 300 souls!]

The message blinked into view. Oswald’s eyes widened again. Three hundred? That was more than he’d ever gotten before.

Yet, He barely had time to savor it. A sharp, high-pitched tone sliced across his hearing, like metal screaming through glass. He straightened at once, scanning the gloom around him.

The sound came again, then another layered over it, followed by another. Screeches and snarls piled on top of one another. Oswald knew where these sounds came from, ghouls. Likely dozens, maybe more. Not good.

Oswald looked to the massive greatsword still lying where the knight had dropped it. A brutal weapon, sure, but too slow and heavy. He wouldn’t get more than one swing off before getting torn apart.

He glanced at his own sword, or what was left of it that is. The hilt rested in its sheath, the upper half snapped off and useless. There was no salvaging that in the middle of a fight. Not a chance.

With little options left, Oswald took off at a sprint to try and escape the incoming ghouls. The screeches behind him grew louder, angrier. He could hear claws scraping over stone, the twisted rhythm of dozens of feet pounding closer. He wasn’t gaining ground. If anything, they were gaining on him.

Inevitably, Oswald had to accept the truth. There was no outrunning this.

Skidding to a stop, he turned sharply and scanned the ground. His eyes caught on a boulder half-buried in rubble, roughly the size of a large backpack and sharp-edged where it had broken off from something bigger. He rushed over and grabbed it with both arms, straining as he hefted the awkward weight. It wasn’t perfect, but it was better than going bare-handed.

He turned to face the tunnel, adjusting his grip on the rock as he braced for what was coming. The noises were closer now, echoing through the narrow stone path like the screams of a pack of starving animals. Then, in the black just ahead, something glowed.

A pair of red eyes flashed in the dark. Then another pair. And another.

In the space of a breath, the tunnel bloomed with ghouls, too many to count. They moved with a fury Oswald hadn’t seen before, claws slashing at the ground, their snarls warped by the echo of the cave. They charged as one, shrieking.

Oswald planted his feet and raised the rock, breath tight in his chest. His arms ached just holding it, but he didn’t let go. His plan was to use the rock to kill one of the ghouls and hope that would be enough to scare the others off. After all, the ghouls weren't that smart.

But as the ghouls came closer, Oswald started to doubt his own plan. But then again, he didn't have much of a choice to begin with.

Yet, just as the first ghoul launched itself from the dark with teeth bared and claws extended, everything slowed. The screech stretched, warping into a drawn-out groan.

The ghoul’s body froze mid-air, suspended in motion, its limbs reaching forward like it had been caught in sap. Every sound dulled, as if wrapped in thick cloth.

Oswald stood still, stone raised in his hands, and looked around. Only he could move normally. This again? Feels just like when I was talking to Zvarah in my Animus.

Around him, time held its breath. Then a voice cut through the silence. “Over here, boy.”

Oswald’s head snapped to the side, eyes narrowing. He scanned the frozen dark, until a shape pulled itself into focus just off to his left, like ink spilling across water.

Almost as if on cue, Zvarah manifested into the world. Her long red hair flowed behind her in slow waves, defying the frozen time around her. She folded her arms, lips curled into that familiar, devil-may-care smirk.

Oswald blinked. “Can you always just do that?”

Zvarah gave a soft chuckle. “Relax. I’m not actually here, at least not in the way you think, yes? You’re the only one who can see me.”

To emphasize her point, the demoness walked straight through a thick stalagmite nearby. Her body passed through the stone like mist without any resistance.

“Okay… what do you want?”

“Must I always have some grand ulterior motive? I’m here to help you, obviously. You were about to die a rather humiliating death at the claws of lesser beasts. I couldn’t allow that."

Oswald snorted, stepping slightly to the side to get a clearer look at her. “How am I supposed to trust you? You were draining my soul for who knows how long before we made that contract.”

Zvarah tilted her head with mock offense. “Normally, you’d be right. If you were just fighting a few run-of-the-mill monsters, I’d have let you handle it, or die trying. But this… this is different."

“Different how?"

"Understand this, I am helping you for my own sake, boy. And now is not the time for trivial questions. Look around you, those abominations still stir, yes?"

Oswald turned his head slowly. The ghouls, though, still caught in that strange stasis, were starting to shift. Not much. But enough.

Fingers twitched. Limbs trembled. The stillness was breaking. Whatever had stopped them was slipping, and the air had begun to stir again. Not much time left.

“Alright, what can you do for me?” Oswald asked, looking at Zvarah.

“I can offer you a skill, yes. But it comes at a price.”

Oswald let out a frustrated breath and dropped the rock he was holding. “Fine, open the shop.”

Zvarah snapped her fingers. The sound cracked loudly.

[Displaying Skill Shop]

Note: Pactbound Skills always start at a minimum of Tier 3!

“Pretty sure I have enough souls, so can you give me the skill?"

“You currently have three hundred and fifty souls, yes. Are you absolutely sure you’d like to part with three hundred of them?”

“I’m sure. Just do it.” Oswald said.

Zvarah placed a hand on her chest, mockingly sincere. “Such impatience. Still, thank you for your payment.”

[Deducting 300 Souls]

[Payment Successful!]

As the message faded, something inside Oswald shifted. A slow pull twisted deep behind his eye, not painful, but sharp enough to make him tense. It felt like a thread being tugged from the inside, unraveling something buried beneath the surface.

Heat flared behind his socket, and a rush of crimson light surged outward in thin, writhing tendrils. The energy spiraled through the air, drawn straight toward Zvarah.

She stepped into its path, breathing it in like incense. A faint glow traced along her skin as the red light sank into her body, lips parting slightly as if savoring the taste.

Her expression softened, eyes half-lidded in quiet satisfaction. “Mmm… it’s been far too long since I’ve feasted on soul energy like this, I’d nearly forgotten how exquisite it is.”

Oswald flexed his fingers, feeling the last of the heat fade from his eye. “You’re welcome. Now, how about giving me what I paid for?”

Zvarah let out a theatrical sigh and rolled her eye as she stepped forward. “Patience, boy. I’m getting to it.”

Zvarah brought her hands together and clapped once. From her palms, a pulse of energy rippled outward, slow and steady, stirring the air between them. In the space where nothing had existed, a sword began to form inch by inch.

The longsword was simple in design but beautifully balanced. The metal held a very faint, crimson sheen, but otherwise looked indistinguishable from polished steel. There were no unnecessary embellishments. Just clean lines, elegant curves, and deadly promise.

Zvarah held it out in one hand, offering it like it weighed nothing. “Here. Enjoy.”

Oswald raised an eyebrow. “Isn't this just a normal sword?”

“There is always more than meets the eye, yes? And if you don’t want it, I’ll happily take it back.”

Oswald didn’t buy it. Not entirely. But he didn’t exactly have options either.

Warily, Oswald reached out, half-expecting his hand to pass right through the blade like it was made of smoke. But instead, his fingers closed around the hilt. The sword was real, and the grip fit his hand like it had been made for him.

“How can I feel this? Is it even real?” Oswald asked.

Zvarah gave a long, dramatic exhale as she turned away, the edges of her form already unraveling. “I’ve done my part. You’ll figure it out. That’s what you’re good at… yes?”

Then she vanished, fading into ribbons of smoke that twisted into the stillness around him and left nothing behind but silence. A flash lit up in front of him, sharp in the dark.

[New Pactbound Skill Acquired: Pact Weapon – Zareth, the Unmaker (Tier 3)]

Fancy name. Oswald looked down at the sword now resting in his hand. It was sleek, unassuming, but not much more impressive than a well-kept longsword.

Still, the ghouls were no longer frozen. Their screeches picked up again like a choir of teeth and claws, growing louder by the second. Oswald tightened his grip on the blade. No time to complain. Let’s see what this thing can do.

A faint crunch echoed beneath his boot as he slid it back over the cavern floor. He lowered his weight, knees bending slightly, sword held low and angled to the side.

Then, the hilt stirred beneath his grip. A low hum throbbed through the metal, steady and slow at first, a pulse that traveled up his arm and settled deep in his chest. The steel lost its shine, dimming into a lifeless, ashen gray, like the color of a corpse's lips.

He barely drew a breath before fire erupted. It roared up the length of the blade, hungry and alive, swallowing the dull metal in a majestic blaze.

Flames danced along its edge with a fierce, fluid grace, their brightness so intense it hurt to look directly at them. The air shimmered. Heat poured off the sword in thick waves, pressing against his face and arms, stinging the skin without quite burning it. Sweat prickled along his brow. Smoke hissed from the blade’s edge, curling in thin spirals toward the cave's ceiling like it was trying to escape.

A grin pulled at his lips, sharp and full of fire. Now we’re talking.

The first ghoul lunged, and Oswald sidestepped the swipe of its claws before countering with a wide slash. His new weapon burned through the creature’s midsection like paper catching fire. Flesh blackened, then turned to ash before it hit the floor.

Another came from the left. Oswald twisted, driving the blade upward through its shoulder and out the other side. The ghoul let out a strangled cry before it, too, crumbled into scorched remains.

But then, six more came at him at once, a blur of snarling mouths and snapping claws. Oswald barely had time to brace.

Claws raked across his arms, shredding fabric and skin. One tore across the side of his shoulder, while another gouged deep along his forearm. But he grit his teeth and kept moving. Pain can wait. Can’t let up now.

Zareth burned in his grip, its fire pulsing with each heartbeat. He swung hard, carving through the air. The blade hissed as it passed, leaving glowing trails behind every arc. When it struck flesh, it burned through instantly, causing skin to blacken as muscle shriveled into ash. One ghoul went down twitching, its body folding in on itself as fire consumed it.

Oswald quickly figured out what he needed to do. Quick slashes for the small ones: neck, chest, gut. Deep strikes for the bulkier bastards, the ones who didn’t drop after the first cut. He kept his footing tight, dancing between their swipes like every inch mattered.

Once he dealt with the ghouls that were on him. Oswald saw a chance to finish the others off.

He raised the blade over his shoulder and threw all his weight into the swing. The sword cut a wide arc through the air. A wave of intense flames exploded outward.

The blast tore through the clustered ghouls in a sweeping crescent. Their screeches were drowned out by the roar of flame as their bodies lit up in an instant, limbs writhing in agony. Flesh peeled, blackened, and fell away in flakes. They collapsed mid-charge, turning into burning piles of smoke and bone.

Oswald stumbled back, nearly slipping on the blood-slick stone. His chest heaved, lungs clawing for breath. A wound burned across his ribs, fresh blood seeping into the side of his cloak, and his thigh throbbed with every step from a deep gash just above the knee. But his grip didn’t falter.

The surviving ghouls were now cautious. They hissed and growled, but none dared come closer. One stepped forward, then quickly retreated, its eyes locked on the smoldering blade.

Soon, they all scattered into the dark, vanishing into the cracks and tunnels, swallowed by shadow. Oswald didn’t chase them. Blood dripped from his fingers. Ash clung to his clothes. Smoke still curled around the fallen.

He raised Zareth once more, then drove it into the stone with a sharp grunt.

The blade hissed. The flames died as light faded from the metal, leaving only the smooth steel behind. Oswald hunched forward, resting his palms on his knees as he caught his breath. Sweat traced a slow path down the side of his face, mixing with blood and grime. His arms ached. His leg throbbed. His whole body hurt.

But he was still standing. Still alive.

He stared at the blade for a moment, still buried in the stone. Zareth, huh…? Not bad. Not bad at all.
