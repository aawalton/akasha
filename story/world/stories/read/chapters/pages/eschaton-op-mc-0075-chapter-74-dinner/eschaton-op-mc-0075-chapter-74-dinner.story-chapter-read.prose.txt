Danny drops his fork on his plate. It clinks against the rather expensive dish. The meal, which would have been seriously expensive, is left half finished. It was definitely outside of his budget before he was Exalted by the Apotheosis. Being a SCUBA instructor did not pay as well as most people thought it did.

“Damn, that was good.”

“What happened to being hungry? You didn’t finish half of that.”

“Deviled eggs happened. I can’t help myself when it comes to them.”

Another 2-Star team attended the party, and they joined the Cerulean Guard at their table for dinner. One of their members laughs.

“Yeah, I love them too.” She sighs. “I wish it was easier to get eggs these days, I had a mean deviled egg recipe. Even better than the ones they served here. I’m not sure if it’s because my taste is a lot sharper, or a decline in the quality of the eggs.”

A quiet falls over the table. As Empowered, they understand the struggle the world faces more than most. They’re on the front line nearly every day. It doesn’t help it’s difficult to see a way out. A plan more than just ‘fight’. One day at a time, one day at a time only works for so long.

“Sharper taste buds, probably. Did you know I have heat vision? All three of us do, actually.” Tiffany says, changing the subject.

Ronnie, the Captain of the other team, whistles. “No, shit? Bet that comes in handy deep under water.”

“Yeah, it’s real dark down there. The Deep can get pretty nasty, but we don’t usually go down there unless we’re really needed. 2-Stars can’t help a whole lot with some of the bigger things. Those Fractures are 3-Stars but they are seriously hardcore.”

Ronnie shivers. “God, I can’t imagine going down there. I’m very happy to keep my boots firmly planted on the ground.”

“It’s not that bad?” John tries, and when he gets dubious looks from Ronnie and his team, he shrugs. “Alright, so it is kinda sucky. But someone’s gotta do it, right?”

“Here, here!” Ronnie cheers, holding up his glass. “To us and our fellow Empowered. It’s sucky, but someone’s gotta do it!”

Everyone else raises their glass and cheers. The other guests studiously ignore their boisterous behavior.

“So,” he says after everyone takes a sip. “How’s the party been treating you guys? I’m sure you’ve been a hot commodity.”

John groans. “Yeah, and commodity is the operative word in that sense. I swear, half the people here treat us like weapon systems to be bought, and the other half see us as demi-gods to be worshipped.”

“The Director was nice,” Danny interjects, and John nods his head. “Yeah, true. Everyone else we talked to made me feel a little bit slimy.”

“It’s all about money with so many of them,” one of Ronnie’s teammates mutters into his glass. “Everything is counted in dollars. Damage to buildings is counted in the repair costs, injuries is counted in medical bills. Infrastructure taken out counted as GDP loss.”

“Every time we clear a Fracture, they count the money they make rather than the lives we saved,” Tiffany says with a sigh. “I wish it were different. But what can we do?”

“I won’t lie, having these mega corps come to us with hat in hand is good for the ego,” Danny chirps.

“That’s got to be their strategy, right? Make us feel important, or something,” John replies.

“The work we do is more important, no matter what slick corpo wants to say otherwise,” Tiffany declares. “At least there’s people like Director Lam and places like the Academy. They get it, I think.”

“So does Machina,” Ronnie says. “Speaking of him, I thought he and Morgan were supposed to be here by now. What time—” Ronnie is cut off by a pair of Auras stronger than any of them have ever felt. One makes him want to sing and dance, to revel in the sheer joy of the world. In the act of creation, in the act of art, in the celebration of the soul.

Isn’t life so wonderful? it asks, tempting them to stand up and party the night away.

The other one, somehow even stronger, makes them all want to jump out of their seats and rush to the coast. It straightens their spines and puts steel in their bones. They feel electrified, and the tiredness of the day swept away by an endless ocean of both vigor and conviction.

Together we hold, it commands. It leaves no room for doubt.

The Auras dance and swirl together, neither conflicting or overpowering the other. As a pair, they sing a song of defiance in the face of nightmares. Of a valiant, righteous war in the defense of all that is beautiful. Power and precision, weight and grace in equal measure.

Look at the magnificence of life. Every loss a tragedy. Every life saved, a heroic legend. Let us write our stories that will persist evermore!

“Whew,” Danny says. “That sure is something.”

“I swear, I could go for a Fracture or six right now. I think I could take on the world with energy to spare,” Ronnie says, sucking in a breath.

“Well, I guess we know where they are.”

“No kidding.”

Morgan leads the pair as they move into the central expo hall now being used as a dining area. She floats in the air, her green dress billowing around her. It sparkles and shines, both with mundane gems and to the Empowereds’ more esoteric senses.

She’s followed by Machina, lumbering at a more sedate pace. He wears a gunmetal gray cloak, matching his armor. The inner side is a vibrant blue that matches the fire that fills his helmet. It’s pulled back from one side, leaving his left arm free. A shawl, or maybe a scarf, covers his gorget. It's the same blue as the cloak and the fire, matching them perfectly. Silver cords hang from it, spanning from shoulder to shoulder. Black embroidery traces armor-like patterns.

His steps are slow and sure, and he bobs from side to side as he carefully picks his way towards the stage. Blue light radiates from his boots, almost like a plasma ball. Despite whatever the cloak is doing, the thoom-thoom-thoom of his slow footfalls still rattles the dishware and echo in the now silent hall.

“Drippy,” Danny notes with appreciation.

“I don’t think that’s the proper term for high fashion, Danny.”

He snorts. “Neither of us are high fashioners, Tiff.”

Morgan eschews the microphone for a minor display of magic, made all the more impressive by Tiffany’s own magical knowledge. Being a 2-Star Hydromancer gives her some innate understanding of magic. Enough to know that’s not how it’s done. A snap of the fingers and a flash of beautiful, abstract light is not enough to cast a spell. The only exception might be a raw, uncontrolled blast of Potentia. Certainly not something structured.

“Welcome, everyone.” Morgan says. Tiffany and those at her table can hear her like she’s standing next to them. Not with the booming quality of a powerful sound system, or the echoing nature of a well designed opera house. But as if she was quite literally standing next to them.

“I’m glad so many of you chose to attend today’s event. In times like these, it can seem odd to spend time gallivanting when there’s work to be done. But refusing to take a moment to breathe and rest will kill you as surely as a monster will. Not only that, it also helps us form the bonds that will get us through this. So, again, I would like to thank all of you for attending.

“I would also like to thank our gracious hosts, the California Academy of Sciences, for this wonderful venue. I’m sure nearly all of you have had the pleasure of talking to Executive Director Doctor Benjamin Lam, but please, Benny, if you wouldn’t mind…?”

Doctor Lam stands up and gives a polite wave, receiving equally polite applause.

“Under Doctor Lam, the Academy has been conducting ground breaking work on monster biology. They’ve been developing new fields and methods of research as fast as they’re able to get their hands on materials. All of it is saving lives all over the globe as we speak. Once all of this is over, I’m sure the Academy will stand as a legend for their tireless efforts for the betterment of all humanity.

“Myself and Machina have recently entrusted them a unique, living specimen of Fracture flora. If you haven’t seen it yet in the one-of-a-kind biodome, I implore you to change that before the night is done. We plan to continue this partnership indefinitely. I hope you all can come to appreciate the professionalism and dedication the world-class research staff here at the Academy shows every single day. Now, let me turn the stage over to my good friend, Machina. He’s very happy to be here, though maybe he doesn’t show it.”

She gets a light smattering of laughter at her parting joke, and she floats backwards. Machina skips the stairs entirely, and steps up to the floating ritual circle that projecting Morgan’s voice to the crowd.

“Hello. I’m glad you all decided to attend. I’ve been told they’ll be offering tours of both the exhibits and the research labs later. I hope you all see the good work the Academy does. I would like to humbly ask you to help them. Materials of the proper grade to conduct experiments with are hard to come by.”

He pauses for a moment, and tilts his head.

“Yes, that’s everything. Thank you.” He then steps back, receiving applause as he exists the stage. Morgan smoothly takes the stage again.

“Well, that’s Machina for you. A man of few words, but full of action. Dessert is next, and after that, tours and a little more general schmoozing. Alright, I won’t keep you any longer!”

Waiters come by to distribute plates of pies and fruits. The smell is intoxicating to the Empowereds’ supernaturally enhanced senses.

“Woah,” Danny says. “Okay, I think I’ve got room for dessert.”

The table chuckles, but the clinking of forks on dishes takes the place of polite conversation.

After dinner, the Cerulean Guard find themselves wandering around the Academy. They check a map, and decide to make their way up to the living roof. They find Machina staring up at the sky, still as a statue. His hands are held behind his back and hidden under his cloak, which ripples in the breeze. The flames that boil inside his helmet give away nothing.

The three of them freeze. Both Danny and John turn to go back down, but Tiffany grabs them both by their matching suit jackets. She nervously swallows, but marches forward, half dragging her teammates behind her.

“M-Machina?” Her voice cracks, the very world warping around the demi-god making her even more nervous.

“Hm?” He intones. He turns towards Tiffany, though he does spare a glance for both Danny and John.

“Oh, the Cerulean Guard. I’ve heard good things about you. Nice work. How can I help you?”

“‘It’s about how we can help you!’, what were you thinking!?” Danny cries, the team now downstairs and failing to drown their embarrassment at the open bar.

“I don’t know, okay! He’s very imposing, I panicked!” Tiffany nearly sobs into a glass. The Typical liquor they serve wouldn’t get any of them drunk if they chugged a gallon of it.

“I’ve seen you stare down sharks the size of whales, Tiff! How did you panic in front of Machina?! He’s supposed to be the nicest guy who ever niced!”

“At least you didn’t stumble over your own feet,” John says demurely. “I don’t know what’s worse: if he’ll remember that forever, or if I will. Actually, I know the answer. Definitely him. I don’t even know how that happened.”

“Oh, I wouldn’t be too worried about that,” a melodious voice says from behind. “The world tends to get a little messy when he’s stuck in his head. I don’t have to worry about tripping, though.”

The team turns around as one to find Morgan le Fay floating behind them. She gives them a brilliant smile.

“Didn’t make as good an impression as you hoped, huh?” She says. “Well, I can promise you that you have made a good impression, social snafus not-withstanding. He’s not the kind of guy who really cares about that stuff. You don’t have to worry. The work you’ve done and assumedly will continue to do speaks volumes about you. He’s more an action-get-stuff-done kinda guy, and you’re an action-get-stuff-done kinda team. You’ll be alright.”

“So what now?” Tiffany asks, and Morgan shrugs.

“Whatever you want, really. This party was an excuse to get people to meet, like I said. You’re adults, and are plenty capable of making your own choices. If you want to know what I think, well, I’d be happy to tell you. But you’re free to make your own way. I won’t proclaim to be the one to tell you what to do.”

“The Academy seems like they do some good work,” John says. “The labs they have are really impressive.”

“They are,” Morgan agrees. “The Academy has done and continues to do important work, both in the past and now.”

“They could do a lot more if they had the opportunity and the resources,” Tiffany says leadingly, and Morgan only smiles.

“My dreams of immense wealth, dashed upon the rocks,” Danny moans half-heartedly. “What will I do without my swimming pool of cash?”

“You’ll manage just fine, I’m sure,” Tiffany says dryly. “You do enough swimming as it is.”
