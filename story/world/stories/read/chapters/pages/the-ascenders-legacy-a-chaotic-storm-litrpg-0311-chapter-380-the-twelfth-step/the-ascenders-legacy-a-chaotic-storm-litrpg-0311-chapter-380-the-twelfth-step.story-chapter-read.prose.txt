Reality split.

There was no explosion. No burst of violence. No screaming storm unleashed across the heavens.

Only a single split.

And the world simply… gave way.

Space rippled around him like glass struck from within. Cracks of pale light spread outward in perfect silence, webbing across the air above the ascension circle like cracks across a frozen lake.

Through those fractures, Aodhán glimpsed something beyond—an endless brilliance too vast for mortal sight, layered with colors that had no names and distances that made no sense.

His mind recoiled at once, blood running warm from one nostril.

He looked away immediately, and his gaze landed on the thousands of Mythics around him, each one jerking violently or clutching their heads as understanding tore through them. Heavy thuds echoed across the plain as many fell from their constructs entirely, their minds overwhelmed by insight.

They weren't the only ones.

Sophran had collapsed beside him, eyes glazed as runes ignited across his skin. Seamus was no different. And the underlings...

Nearly all of them had fainted, Crixis included—though a sharp slap woke him back up. Nymera and Ashscar did the same for their underlings, but they simply fainted right back. The ascension had barely begun, and people were already gaining inspirations.

Even the High Elders wore hardened expressions, eyes gleaming with insight of their own.

And yet Edrick Ghaltris stood untouched at the center of it all.

The armor around him brightened until each plate seemed forged from stars. Lightning streamed from its seams in measured arcs—not wild or chaotic, but disciplined. Every bolt moved with purpose.

Then he turned.

Not upward.

Toward them. Toward the Sect.

Toward the world he was leaving behind.

Aodhán froze when those ancient eyes swept over the gathered masses. There was no arrogance in them. No sorrow either. Only a vast and settled peace.

Edrick raised one gauntleted fist to his chest in salute. The First Seat bowed his head in return. Every High Elder followed. Then thousands upon thousands of Mythics did the same. Aodhán found himself bowing before he consciously chose to.

When he rose again, Edrick was smiling faintly. Then he reached up with one hand and removed his helm.

Silver hair stirred in winds that no longer belonged to this world.

He cast the helm downward. It did not fall.

Instead, it dissolved into a rain of radiant motes before touching the earth, each fragment carrying dense streams of insight. Thousands dropped into meditation instantly. Some shouted in revelation. Crixis collapsed again.

Aodhán's own mind detonated with understanding.

Layering. Compression. Structural intent. The way force could be folded upon itself until impact became fuel. The way defenses could become prisons. The way Create Constructs, Spear Rain, and Ruinous Layering could all be improved.

The way they could all evolve.

His Aspect of Water stirred. His Authority stirred harder. Even Time gave a faint, hungry pulse.

Beside him, Nymera had gone utterly still, her eyes unfocused as enlightenment washed through her in dense waves. Ashscar's eyes burned like miniature suns. Darien was surrounded by spinning bands of force—the awakening of a new aspect. Seamus was muttering equations to himself in equal parts horror and delight.

But Aodhán barely noticed them.

Because above the shattered sky, something had descended.

A stairway.

Not stone. Not light. Not cloud.

Concept.

Each step formed only when looked at, stretching upward into the impossible radiance beyond the broken world.

Elder Ghaltris gave the Sect one final look, his family another. Then he straightened his shoulders and began to climb.

One step. The sky thundered.

Two steps. A mountain range in the distance split from the pressure alone.

Three steps. Aodhán dropped to his knees, teeth clenched as his aura shattered. Energy flooded his spirit. Insight flooded his mind—too much to grasp, too much to hold.

Four steps.

The storm above ceased to be weather.

It became law.

Clouds arranged themselves into vast concentric rings around the ascending figure, each layer rotating at a different speed, every movement carrying harmonies of thunder so deep Aodhán felt them in his bones. Lightning churned, inscribing symbols across the heavens before dissolving into meaning too large for language.

Aodhán stared upward, every instinct in him screaming to look away. But he did not. He could not.

Five steps.

Edrick Ghaltris's body began to shed weight. Not flesh. Not blood. Weight itself. The man seemed less bound by gravity with every motion, less tethered to consequence. His silhouette blurred at the edges, becoming both sharper and harder to define.

Aodhán's core pounded painfully. He could feel his own path reacting again. Ruinous Authority recoiled and leaned forward at the same time, like a beast uncertain whether to flee or devour.

Because what climbed those steps was not escaping ruin. It was surpassing the need to fear it.

Six steps.

Veins burst in Aodhán's eyes. Sight vanished. Yet Aodhán looked.

He looked with core sense because he could not afford to miss this. He could not afford to miss any of it.

Core sense strained against the blinding brilliance of cores and energy around him. It struggled to provide what his sight no longer could.

But it provided enough.

Enough to witness the laws beneath motion. Enough to glimpse the instant before force became action. Enough to understand.

Around him, multiple people screamed—not in pain, but inspiration. Cores blazed. Paths sharpened. Bottlenecks shattered. The Storm Sect was growing stronger by the second, the entire plain erupting in pockets of advancement.

And Aodhán was not left behind.

Inhaling sharply, his mind parsed through the flood of insight rushing into him.

Defense was not merely resistance or hindrance. It was authority over what may pass. It was deciding what held sway over him.

His eyes widened.

It was a gate. A throne. A law. Authority over what enters. What breaks. What is denied.

His Aspect of Time churned as realization struck him like a bolt.

Time was no river beyond command. It ebbed and flowed, ever onward, never pausing. Retrograde Storm sought to force the whole river backward—to deny its current entirely. But he could not do that. All the willpower in the world would not make it so.

What he could do was to be a rock—a boulder that resisted the river's passage within his immediate vicinity.

A boulder that declared: Time shall not pass here.

His path surged violently as the inspiration crystallized, seizing upon the concept at once and twisting it through the lens of ruin.

Defense became resistance.

And resistance became jurisdiction—the very same authority he'd glimpsed moments ago, now sharpened into something he could actually wield.

Something colored in ruin.

Inspiration surged within him at once, a hungering maw rising from the depths of his spirit like ruin itself.

Yet Aodhán denied the urge.

He focused intently on the Calamity, because as great as this revelation was, as powerful as the skill he would no doubt gain from it would be—Aodhán knew there was more. There was greater. If only he could look. If only he could keep looking.

And so Aodhán did. Blind, bleeding, yet refusing—denying—to succumb to the inspirations clawing within him.

Thunder boomed. The energy intensified. Core sense sharpened.

Seven steps.

Light flashed over the broken world—so bright that even his blinded eyes perceived it.

Then, abruptly—far too abruptly—the mounting pressure of Calamity Ghaltris's core vanished.

Something happened in that instant. Something Aodhán was too blind to witness.

A shudder passed through heaven and earth alike. Within him, Varéc roared, terror lancing through their psychic bond. Yet even he saw nothing.

Laughter echoed then.

Not loud. Not near.

But vast. Ancient. Triumphant.

Even the world seemed to hold its breath.

And then… nothing.

Aodhán blanched, left teetering on the edge of something greater than comprehension, close enough to feel it, yet impossibly far from ever touching it.

His sight returned a few seconds later, and when he opened his eyes, he found the plain just as it had been before the ascension. Grass stirred gently. Clouds drifted overhead. The runes had dimmed.

Everything was ordinary.

As though a god had not just stepped beyond the world.

He felt robbed.

As though a priceless gift had been placed in his hands only to be snatched away before he could see it clearly.

Shuddering, Aodhán looked around and swallowed as he took in the sight of thousands of Mythics overtaken by inspiration. Another thousand stood guard, having seemingly appeared from thin air, null chains hanging from their arms—ready to curb the twisted effect before it could take hold.

He glanced at his own friends next. Their eyes were glazed, their seals gleaming as they forged inspirations from the insights they had just received. Then he looked to Crixis, who had missed over half the good part and still somehow gained two new seals already.

Ridiculous.

Finally, Aodhán turned inward, toward the clawing inspirations within himself. He closed his eyes, breathed in deeply, and let them take him.

His core exploded an instant later.

Ascension was a rare occurrence. Even in a continent like Gordon, it did not happen often.

Ever since the formation of the Storm Sect centuries ago, only five among its ranks had ascended before this. The first was Transmigrant Argen Vaelor—the first First Seat and founder of the Storm Sect itself. That scarcity made sense. It was one thing for a path to lead all the way to the Calamity Class, and another thing entirely for that same path to surpass it.

The path of Edrick Ghaltris had done just that—surpassed the Calamity Class, where thousands of others had either failed or reached bottlenecks they would carry to their graves. And thousands of disciples—including Calamities themselves—had reaped the fruit of it.

Aodhán was among those thousands.

After closing his eyes, the inspirations had built quickly, surpassing anything he could have imagined. His core had surged again and again and again, until surging no longer felt like the right word for it. Seals had emblazoned themselves into his chest, and his skills had evolved with every condensation of insight he gathered.

Aodhán understood now why Nymera and Ashscar had been so eager to witness it. Why it was so sought after.

Witnessing an ascension was a cheat—a broken, yet valid, path to power.

He had gained so much from this one event—enough that it put all his talk of rest to shame. Five tiers in five months might be relaxed, but ten tiers most certainly was not.

Exhaling deeply, he opened his eyes, blinking as evening sunlight streamed into them. Nearly an entire day had passed since Calamity Ghaltris ascended—an entire day of meditating, of parsing inspirations. Of growing. And it had been more than worth it.

He blinked again, his vision sharpening just as a figure strayed into his line of sight. It was a Mythic dressed in the same robes as the null guards he had seen the night before.

Her eyes widened the moment her gaze locked with his, a flash of fear crossing them. She latched null chains to his hands and legs before he could even blink, clearly expecting him to go feral at any second.

When he didn't, she stuttered. "Are… are you lucid?"

"I am," Aodhán sighed, realizing he would likely have to explain his immunity to the twisted effect before she would ever let him go. An immunity he still did not understand himself.

"I'm immune to the twisted effect," he said carefully. "I do not experience its side effects."

The Mythic stared at him in confused shock for a moment, then shook her head.

"No, that's not possible. You must be one of those cold-killer types."

"No," Aodhán said at once, remembering Daruk's first twisted effect. "I am certainly not that. I'm immune. You can ask—"

He turned, half-expecting to find his friends. Instead, he found himself alone—one of only a few hundred still left meditating in the clearing. He remembered then that his friends did not share his immunity to the twisted effect. They must have awakened, succumbed to the effect, and been taken to seal chambers to burn it out of their systems.

The Mythic must have mistaken his silence for a symptom of instability, because she called a friend over.

"Sevran, come see this. The Crown of Ruinous Storms doesn't express the imbalance of the twisted effect normally. Have you ever seen a lucid twisted effect?"

Aodhán sighed as footsteps approached from behind him, and a moment later the one called Sevran appeared before him, glaring down curiously.

Aodhán nodded politely. "Good evening, sir. I am fine. I am immune to the twisted effect."

Sevran's eyebrows climbed to his hairline. "He's talking instead of attacking."

"Indeed," the woman grinned down at him as though he were some rare specimen. "After witnessing the ascension of a Seventh-Step Ascendant and gaining—" She frowned. "How many seals did you gain?"

Aodhán, however, had barely heard anything she said after the words Seventh-Step.

"What do you mean, Seventh-Step?" he asked. "What does that mean?"

Sevran exchanged a glance with the female Mythic, then shook his head in disbelief.

"He really is lucid. How is this possible?"

"I'm immune to the twisted effect!" Aodhán snapped. "What do you mean Seventh-Step? What happened on the seventh step?"

"How are you lucid right now?" Sevran countered.

"I'll answer your question if you answer mine."

Sevran glanced at the woman again, as though unsure how to proceed, before awkwardly clearing his throat.

"Eh… you see, Ascendants are not always equal. We believe there are twelve steps to ascension. Calamity Ghaltris climbed seven of them before fully shedding his mortality."

The woman nodded. "For an inheritor, he did outstandingly well. Only the Founder has ever reached the ninth step."

Aodhán blinked, their words striking him like fresh thunder. He had read a hundred books these past few months, and yet he had never known this?

Frowning, he remembered the silence he had heard after the seventh step. And the laughter that had followed. Twelve steps to ascension. Calamity Ghaltris had made it seven. Had anyone ever climbed all twelve? Could anyone? Was the Mountain of Tempest modeled after this?

"Okay, your turn," Sevran said, cutting into his thoughts. "How are you lucid after all this? How are you speaking right now and not deranged?"

"I don't know," Aodhán sighed. "I'm simply... immune."

Sevran and the female Mythic exchanged glances once more, and Aodhán sighed inwardly. Perhaps it was time he informed the High Elders of this. Maybe they would have answers.

For now, though, Aodhán asked,

"Can you remove these chains? I need to view my status screen."

Both Mythics hesitated, but after a moment the woman said,

"You can't go anywhere, though. I have to monitor you."

Aodhán nodded, accepting the terms, and a few seconds later core sense flared back to life—far more vivid than it had ever been before.

Far more vivid than it should have been.

Aodhán did not dwell on it. Checking his screen was more important. The screen appeared an instant later, and his eyebrows climbed to his hairline in shock at the unending list of notifications before him.
