Alex recognized plenty of the logos on the Guilders’ bodysuits; there were clusters of Aegis, and O2, and ArchKnights and Fairfax & Co.—talents belonging to the big players in Stark City—as well as dozens of boutique logos he didn’t recognize.

Except as Alex walked, he also saw a few logos from international Guilds, like the Institute, and X1, and even Templar and Echelon; these clusters were much smaller. Presumably new talents that’d arrived from New York City—Alex swore he saw a few faces he’d seen at the Exchange days earlier. It seems like those Guilders talking about how stacked this would be were right, he reflected. Seems like several of these talents are here specifically for this event...

Alex wasn’t the only one eyeing the crowd critically; everyone seemed to be glancing at everyone else, and the reason was obvious.

It was the tag above everyone’s heads—a second reason this server was special.

Above Alex’s head, there was—

_1arcstyrion_ (49)

Lv. 5, elo 771?

His name wasn’t on display, to his relief.

The ’49’ represented his ranking—a ranking which kind of surprised him.

He’d expected a little higher, if he was honest; since the field was 128, 49 was easily above average, but—it still just feels… decent?

He’d mentally measured himself against potential Aegis Guilders like that Alice and Jason in the Aegis rift; he hadn’t actually seen them in action, but it seemed pretty clear, and this wasn’t just pride speaking, he was a clear step above, in terms of ability. And that was before his new gear was taken into account.

Then again, this is a field of talents, so it’s already selecting for, like, the best of each incoming class, I guess. Still, the fact that 771’s just decent speaks to the strength of the field.

And it’s ranked by initial elo.

Alex was quite sure his 771? wasn’t an accurate reflection of his abilities.

What he wasn’t as certain of was how high, exactly, his rating should be. He wanted to say he’d be at least one or two hundred points higher if he fully went for it.

Or maybe that’s wishful thinking, I can’t know, he reflected. Because as Alex walked around the facility, trying to gauge the field before things were truly underway, he did see a few F-ranks 1000+’s. And almost all were also Level 9’s.

Alex also saw several of the folks in that range half geared-up. He was certain several of the items he saw were instance items.

A shield that was essentially a giant white crystal, a gleaming vest of oily-black scales, this regal, jeweled bow—all very, very bright-feeling to his soul senses. There was even a glass spacesuit helmet; certain instance items were weird like that, and certainly looked a bit funny, but he was also certain the thing wasn’t just cosmetic or harmless. It probably granted some insane ability, or at least insane stats.

As for why a few of these folk were wearing their gear like this, he assumed it was to flex, or to intimidate the rest of the field? At least, if it wasn’t the explicit purpose, it seemed to be doing an excellent job of it; these half-geared-up Guild talents were clearly getting the most attention.

It felt kind of like walking into a high school cafeteria, which Alex wasn’t sure how he felt about. Or like the old swim meets or other such extracurricular youth tournaments he and Bella used to go to; there were these established teams in their cliques, and independents nervously waiting around the edges.

Alex got the sense, as he went around observing, folks were kind of aware who the favorites were.

He also didn’t feel guilty at all for listening in. You really could glean quite a bit from snippets of random conversation.

“How are you supposed to make podium like this?” said a crestfallen short-haired girl, ranked 37—she seemed an independent, and was talking with two other independents. “What the fuck?”

“Forget podium,” said a second independent, a tall, lean guy, ranked 32. He was much more relaxed. “It’s fine. There’s still the talents prizes. Like, Ivy Liu placed 21st last Rising Stars and she still got bought out by Templar, didn’t she? And that’s the true prize anyway.”

The guy gestured at the tinted glass of the observation decks.

“But it wasn’t because of what she did in Rising Stars,” said the girl.

“It was, I have a friend in her class—her talents rank was sky high. You just have to show a little potential.”

“Okay, but let’s add some context,” said the third guy, smiling wanly. “Liu finished 21st because she lost to Hazard, who ended up winning, and she was the only one besides Prince to actually give him a hard fight. It’s not like it’s easy getting that kind of talents eval.”

Alex kept walking, lips pursed. Thirty seconds later—

“Every Rising Stars, you always get a few talents breaking through, right? Like Kang, Prince, Iglesias the last few. And Hazard, of course.”

“It’s kind of crazy to think, like, some of these people will be famous in a few years. Maybe even like, world elite famous,” said a guy ranked 75; he seemed a bit in awe. “Any one of us.”

“Some of these groups are going to be brutal... especially since, you know some of these people are crazy underrated.”

“Yeah, you can’t take anyone in the Top 30 lightly.”

Alex’s impression was, plenty of the people there, especially the lower-ranked folks, were kind of there for the experience.

High above them all, there was a giant digital countdown—5:38 until Group Stage begins.

Alex let out a tight breath; he felt at once calm mentally, but also a little physically amped; it felt like the atmosphere had seeped a little into his body. It was more excitement than nervousness. Listening in to snippets was a fantastic way to get himself primed for the fights ahead. He kind of wanted to shadowbox, get a little release; there was an L-shaped section of empty stations, around the outside of the event area, which had sigils floating over them, saying they were reserved for practice.

128 was quite a few people, and in groups, peoples’ names got a bit jumbled; he’d only seen maybe 3 or 4 of the folks ranked in the top 10.

The remaining time trickled by. Alex briefly talked to an independent, ranked in the 50’s, but the guy seemed so nervous he didn’t have much to say. The guy laughed and apologized a few times. Alex had talked to the guy on a whim, because he was kind of curious about a few of the things he was hearing about, but he wasn’t sure why he expected the guy to be familiar with what was going on.

It was after the conversation, with about a minute and thirty seconds remaining, as Alex was mentally prepping for his upcoming Group Stage matches, that he heard something that piqued his curiosity. From a few Aegis Guilders.

“Who else is here, besides Adrian?”

“Probably a few higher-ups in the big Guilds, I guess. But if there’s anyone else like that, like, world-level, I sure haven’t heard. Rising Stars is a bigger deal this season. If I were one of these Guild people, I’d want to see how good my new class really was.”

“There’s a difference between ‘higher-ups’ and Adrian St. Clair. St. Clair obviously has a reason to be here, but I can’t think of a reason for anyone else at that level...”

“It’d be kind of funny if they were. Like, it’s Daisuke and Adrian up there, watching a Rising Stars Cup,” joked the Guilder.

Adrian St. Clair’s here?

Alex felt a little incredulous.

Sibling of Clarissa St. Clair.

And also one of the best Open Class US Duelists on the circuit right now, if not the best.

That… can’t be real, right?

Alex glanced at the tinted glass, like he was trying to peer through it.

He didn’t care how stacked this Rising Stars Cup was, it was still a very early-stage talents showcase. It was a joke that anyone actually important would pay attention to this.

There was about a minute left, and Alex wanted to mentally get 100% ready to duel; it’d be 3 duels, back to back to back, facing the rest of your group of 4. These were qualification duels, to advance to the 64-man knockout stage.

Only 2 of these 4 people were advancing, so the only way to guarantee progression was to win all 3 fights. 2 wins of 3 was also usually sufficient, but you were leaving a portion of your progression up to chance.

Alex watched as the timer hit 0. After which it changed to one giant announcement overhead, featuring all 128 names in a giant list; an official called, “Groups are officially announced! Please make your way to your assigned section. Speak to a tournament official if you have any questions—action begins in 5 minutes!”

At the same time, giant letters, A-Z, floated over certain areas of the hall—clearly where you were meant to go.

There was quite a lot of noise at first as talents reacted to their group assignments; Alex’s assumption was a few people recognized the usernames they were paired up against.

Alex, of course, didn’t; he simply went over to his assigned section, section Q—he was placed quite far away. When he arrived Alex saw 3 people already standing there, beside an official.

One was a mage girl—lilly_xd, ranked 93, whose face was quite rigid; she was gripping a standard steel staff quite tight. A second was a guy in an O2 Guild jumpsuit—Karnation_, ranked 29. Alex would’ve lingered on him a little more, since his elo was 859?—provisional, Alex noticed; so potentially underrated. He’d have to take this person quite seriously.

Except he, like lilly_xd, was watching the third person—the final person Alex was fighting.

A very pretty, fine-featured guy with a saber at his side; strands of blond hair fell across his eyes. He seemed fairly disinterested in all that was going on.
