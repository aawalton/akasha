Gio tensed.

“Relax. You wouldn’t believe me if I told you, but I’d like you to believe that I really do have your best interests at heart. I just need you to tell me what happened to you four months ago.” The stranger said.

Gio did not relax.

“Sure, just tell me why you need to know,” Gio demanded.

The man tapped his chin.

“No.” The man replied. “I’m afraid that I can’t do that.”

“Then, I can’t imagine why I would tell you anything about myself,” Gio said.

“Well, Giorgio. I already know a bit about you. I know that you are an incredibly smart person. I know that you are resourceful… and I know that you are a fierce protector of your friends.” The stranger mentioned.

“How pleasantly vague,” Gio replied. “You could make a couple of bucks as a carnival psychic with skills like that.”

“I know that you didn’t get to spend a great deal of time with your father growing up because of the long hours that he worked at the Copper Ring Quarry. I know that you’ve always felt pressured to make something of yourself because of how hard your parents work in general. I also know that your mother had an older brother who was put to death for disobeying a representative from the Royal Coven back in Ataraxia, which is why your grandmother sent her surviving three children to the Ringed City. Should I go on?”

Gio stood in stunned silence.

“That’s… My family does not talk about that. I don’t even think my cousins know. I only know that from eavesdropping. So you’re a mind reader. I believe that it is illegal for-”

“NRRGH,” the man yelled, grunting in frustration. “NO! Think, Gio! THINK! Why would I be asking you anything if I could just pluck the answers from your mind? Don’t insult me. Mind readers are rare, for which I am eternally grateful, as I have to avoid them like a plague.”

The man paced around the platform, repeatedly checking his pocketwatch.

Tick, Tick, Tock.

“I don’t have the time for this.” He said, pinching the bridge of his nose. “Look… my specialty is information. I can’t get you money, but I could tell you a hundred ways for you to make more of it than you could imagine.”

“Look,” Gio began to say. “I don’t know what your deal is, but I think you should get away from me.”

“I’d love to. Just tell me the name of whatever power you obtained, or whatever relic you’re holding on to.”

So… he really doesn’t know about Rio. But why is he fishing for information about me? What do I have to do with this?

“How are you so certain that I have what you’re looking for?” Gio asked.

“Okay, how about this-” The man said, disregarding Gio’s question entirely. “Your friend Xerxes is going to die at the end of the year, and I can tell you how to stop it.” The man offered.

“Please don’t hurt my friend.” Gio asserted.

“NOT ME, YOU-” the man clenched his hand in front of his face, cartoonishly taking a deep breath and calming himself.

“I am not interested in killing Xerxes de Lamerie. It is simply a fact that he will die before the end of his second year at the Crystal Ring Academy. I can tell you how to prevent it.”

“You’re really not making a great case about your benevolence, here. Holding the life of a friend of mine hostage?” Gio noted.

“Perhaps a fair point. Fine, for free, I’ll tell you that Xerxes seeks entrance to a vault buried deep in the Scriptorium. This vault contains a puzzle that he lacks the context to solve, which will kill him.”

A vault in the Scriptorium… that sounds plausible enough. I’ll warn him. But what would that even look like? “Hey, this crazy guy I met at a train station told me that you’re going to die soon.”

“Gio… You don’t understand what’s at stake. I’m just trying to get some information out of you that may help me do an incredible amount of good for the world,” the man said.

“I believe that you believe that… and you certainly know things about my family that even my closest friends don’t know. I just don’t understand what you stand to gain from this… or why you can’t tell me anything about yourself. You haven’t even given me your name.”

“As I told you, I am nobody of importance. Telling you any relevant details would involve several variables that I am not willing to gamble on. Trust me when I tell you that I am protecting you from a terrible burden. You need not concern yourself with my task- I just need information.”

“...Information about something that I haven’t spoken to my closest friends about. Information that could -no, would definitely be, life-altering for me, should it leak. You’re asking me to trust you, but you came to me today under false pretenses… approaching me alone. Was that your plan? To see if I just suddenly dropped details about my life to a total stranger?” Gio accused.

“Honestly, you would be shocked at how often it works. Some people are desperate for the chance to talk about themselves.”

“Funny how that works.” Gio deadpanned.

The stranger snapped his fingers.

“AHA!” He cackled. “I know. I know what you want. Honestly, I should have started with this one. Are you ready for it?”

He pointed a finger in Gio’s face. Gio was unimpressed.

“The Book of Sand.” The man triumphantly stated.

Gio didn’t react.

“I know, I know. Hard to believe. But I can tell you how to get your hands on it- which, once you know how, is surprisingly easy… in fact, if you keep at your current pace of research, you yourself will manage to figure it out in, hmm, say 47 or so years. The hard part is figuring out how to use it once you have it, which I will admit that even I don’t know how to-”

“What’s the Book of Sand?” Gio asked.

The pointed finger drooped.

“What?”

“I’ve never heard of it. Is it some kind of relic?” Gio asked.

“Is it some kind- You’re lying. You’re obsessed with finding the Book of Sand. You talk about it constantly.”

Okay, he’s insane. This person is mentally unwell. I need to take some precautionary measures.

Gio discreetly prepared himself to react, just in case this interaction became violent.

“Okay, so where is it?” Gio said, playing along.

“You really don’t know, do you?” The man guffawed.

His posture stiffened, and Gio tensed.

“Are you a shapechanger? Did you do something with the original Giorgio deGloria? Is that you in there, Axarandamaias?” The man asked, peering into Gio’s eyes like he was trying to look inside a glass bottle.

“Wait,” Gio said. “Axarandamaias… that was the name of the dragon. You knew about the undead dragon?”

Gio unclasped his spellbook.

The man smiled.

“I know many things. I can teach you many things, if you’d like.”

“I already have a great teacher.” Gio quipped back.

“Yes… Saint Baphelus... Master of the fabulous Manse. I can see that from the shiny badge you’re toting. Another inconvenient anomaly… as it takes a great bit of effort to get him to teach a first-year student under normal circumstances. I take it your dragonslaying was the catalyst for his mentorship, was it? Tell me, what else did you garner from that day? The Horn of Blood? No… that wouldn’t be it, would it?”

“Why didn’t you warn someone? That dragon could have hurt a lot of people. It almost killed my friends.” Gio asserted.

“Because it would have been an acceptable loss.”

“What?!” Gio sputtered.

“Chandrika is dangerous, and Jean… would have been an unfortunate sacrifice… but you? I can’t say that I’ve grieved you.”

Gio studied the man’s wild stare.

“Chandrika isn’t dangerous,” Gio said in a challenging tone.

“HA! That’s the funniest thing you’ve ever said. Chandrika Chakraverti is one of the most dangerous creatures to stalk this miserable, forsaken world. She’ll need to be dealt with sooner or later.”

“And what gives you the right to say that?” Gio asked.

The stranger tapped the side of his head. “Because I know many things. Many, many, many things.”

Gio stared at him. “...so you keep mentioning.”

“Look, Gio. I can tell that I’m talking in circles here. The truth of the matter is that I don’t need you to tell me how you’re interfering with my plans- because that’s why I’m here. You’re getting in my way with whatever it is that you’ve been up to for the past few months. I’d really appreciate it if you told me, but I do have other options. I’m here as a last-ditch effort to avoid having to resort to… distasteful methods. I’m sorry that it’s come to this, but you’ve proven to be… sticky.”

“You…” Gio said, as realization dawned on him. “You’re-”

A chime sounded in the distance. The train was pulling into the station.

“Ah, the mysterious train…and what a coincidence. Time’s up.”

Everything went black.

Tick, Tick Tock.

In the dark, Gio could only hear a disembodied mechanical clicking.

Tick, Tick, Tock.

Suddenly, light returned to the world.

Gio looked down to see a glass blade buried in his chest. He slumped to the ground with some difficulty, as his voice caught in his chest.

“You’re-”

“This really is a shame.” The man said. “I’ll have to wrap things up by other means. You have no idea the amount of problems you’ve caused me, Gio.”

His burning blue eyes studied Gio. He smiled.

“I hope to never see you again. Goodbye, and good riddance.”

And then he was gone. The glass blade disintegrated into rough shards that dug into Gio’s wounds, leaking an unfamiliar type of mana.

“Sorry I’m late, you wouldn't-”

Chandrika gasped.

“GIO!” Chandrika yelled, rushing over to her friend.

Gio looked up at her.

“Is this blood convincing enough?” Gio asked.

“What happened? Don’t move, I need to set the remaining- wait, what?”

“I think it looks too watery,” Gio said.

He allowed his water-clone to melt, stepping out of the puddle and back into the material world. He curiously watched as the remainder of the glass blade evaporated away, taking a great deal of his conjured water with it.

Chandrika’s face curled into a scowl.

“That’s not cool. You shouldn’t be practicing such awful illusions like that, especially not in public, and especially not on me.” Chandrika spat.

“Well, firstly, I’m thankful to have been practicing discreetly changing places with [Reflecting Pool]. I think it just saved my life. Secondly…Rika… I think that I just met the man who tried to have us assassinated.”

Chandrika wore an incredulous expression. “Can you go maybe five minutes without something impossible happening?”

“I would love… nothing more, I promise you. Maybe we won’t be making it to that party, after all.”

Jean rounded the corner, looking like a fairytale prince.

“Hey, guys! Did I miss anything? You both look like you’re ready to party!” Jean said.

Gio and Chandrika groaned in unison.
