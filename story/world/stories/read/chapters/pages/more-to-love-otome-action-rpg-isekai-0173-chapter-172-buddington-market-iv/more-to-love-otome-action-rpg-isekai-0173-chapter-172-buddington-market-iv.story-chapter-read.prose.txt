Aida and Levi sat uncomfortably in bamboo chairs in the living room as Ernie clattered about quietly in the kitchen. She could feel the old man’s tremors in the next room over as he prepared a pot of tea.

After her macabre pronouncement about the absence of his wife’s soul, Ernie had stood up woodenly and insisted they stay for tea—the least he could do for them after dragging them all the way to his home.

Levi had tried to dissuade Ernie from the pointless social ritual, but the old man had looked silently at Levi, waiting for him to finish speaking before saying just one sentence: “Agnes would never forgive me if I didn’t host properly.”

“It wasn’t just me, right?” Aida asked Levi quietly. “You couldn’t feel any mana either, right?”

“I’m not the best person to ask,” Levi said uncomfortably. “I’m more attuned to plants. I can feel small bursts of life from their small garden outside, but no… I couldn’t feel anything from her either.”

“Does this kind of thing happen regularly? Among old people? Among nonpractitioners?” If Aida were back in the world where magic didn’t exist, she would have guessed Agnes was in some sort of coma and thought nothing more of it. Her body was alive, but her mind… who knew where her mind was.

He shifted uncomfortably at the question. “No. It’s highly irregular. If she were a mana practitioner, I would guess she attempted what Ezra did… but there’s no way a nonpractitioner could do something like that.”

Aida frowned thoughtfully, curling in on herself as she thought.

Levi had confirmed her theory, based on what she had learned about Ascension Meditation thanks to the Ezra ordeal. Ezra had enough ability and knowledge to be a danger to himself, actively loosening the ties of his soul to the mortal realm. As for an old woman like Agnes, who by all accounts was just a regular person living a regular life, she should have been too safe from the kind of risk Ezra exposed himself to.

“Huh. Interesting.” Levi had picked up a brightly-wrapped circular block Aida had been inadvertently staring at while she thought. And now that Levi called out the interesting factor, she couldn’t help but agree with him.

The block was about the size of her hand, and the paper and string tied around it were too colorful and beautiful to look like it belonged among the shabby and faded items in the room. It was simply too elegant to be part of the household.

“What is that?”

“It’s a spirit tea cake.” Levi pulled on the silk string, opening the package. “From Moonglade, it looks like. The most potent spirit tea bushes are grown on the upper reaches of the Spire, and the tea cakes are quite expensive.”

He sniffed delicately at the exposed brick of tea before handing it over to Aida. As she took it, she noticed a small dent in it, as if someone had carefully chipped a small portion out of it. “It smells… fragrant.”

“It is, yet it isn’t. It’s a new batch; if you get a chance to smell older cakes, the aroma is—for lack of a better word—heavenly.”

Their conversation was interrupted as Ernie shuffled in, carrying a tray with a full set of tea on it. He seemed to have gotten ahold of himself; he was much more steady now, and there was no outwardly visible shaking in his hands as he set the tray down.

He barely glanced at the package in Aida’s hands, his eyes dim. Hopeless.

“Ah, I completely forgot that it was there. That was a gift from one of our temple sisters; she had just come back from a pilgrimage to Moonglade, and brought back some souvenirs to share with the rest of the assembly.” He began pouring the tea into four cups, setting the fourth directly in front of the room where Agnes lay. His eyes watered as they lingered on the doorframe, but he swallowed back his emotions. “We shared that tea for the first time the night of the Fire Festival, to celebrate another happy year. I wish I hadn’t been so stingy…if I had known this was going to happen, I would have brewed the entire cake at once…”

He gestured for Aida and Levi to pick up their cups. “Please.”

“Have you ever had spirit tea prior to the Fire Festival?” Levi asked. He lifted his own cup to just below his nose, inhaling the steam curling from the brew. Aida retrieved her own cup, but didn’t go through the same motions as Levi, instead turning the tea cake in her hand.

Before she had started brewing potions for Healer Bokar, she wouldn’t have been so sensitive to individual ingredients in the things she consumed. But after absorbing the information Bokar had imparted to her, her way of thinking had changed: everything she ate or drank came from something else, with its own properties and mana source. As Bokar had mentioned, low doses of poisons were needed to brew medicinal potions; it was only when the concentration of toxins reached a certain threshold did they become dangerous.

In short, everything in moderation.

As a result of working with poisons during her short tenure, Aida had begun cataloging the things she had been putting into her body in the back of her mind—if only to make sure any substances she allowed in didn’t do anything terribly adverse to her health.

It wasn’t as annoying of a task as she would have thought, having shaken her head at the same sort of spiel from the health fanatics at the gym she worked at. She supposed her system made things easy for her, color-coding things that she should be wary of, and updating the colors whenever she began to consume too much of one thing.

It was much easier to practice restraint when all the information she needed was right in front of her.

Aida examined the tea cake more thoroughly while the two men conversed, drawing on the basic knowledge Healers Luk and Bokar had imparted to her about analyzing dried herbs. She carefully set her full cup of tea back down. “I’ll be right back.”

Bringing the tea cake with her, Aida returned to the room where Agnes was laying.

The spirit tea itself was innocuous; she didn’t sense any sort of toxin hidden within the clump of dried leaves, but its inert mana signature was certainly different from everything else she had seen. Even different from the tea Ernie had brewed for them, which was just a basic—though personal—blend of tea and herbs.

She knelt beside the old woman, then hesitated.

To be honest, Aida didn’t know what she was looking for. If Agnes had consumed the tea one week ago, and her body was physically fine, then it made sense that the tea would have already passed through her system.

However, since she had gotten transported into a game, then nothing would ever happen for no reason.

There’s a reason something called a ‘spirit tea’ has made its appearance. The name itself gave it away, especially in a world that was quite honest in its naming schemes.

She leaned over Agnes’s face, carefully observing her lips and nose. The old woman’s lips were shriveled, clearly dehydrated. Her breath was steady.

Hefting the tea cake in her left hand, Aida reached out with her right, placing a finger delicately on Agnes’s lips and closing her eyes.

If she first concentrated on the mana in the tea cake as a reference, she could then pick out a wisp of the same mana in Agnes’s body.

Aida tamped down on the exhilaration that threatened to break the tenuous connection she had made.

Slowly, she mapped out the residual traces of the spirit tea in Agnes’s body. It was almost like a cloud, wrapped gently around her figure.

Wondering if it would even work, Aida activated her Detox skill, dissipating the spirit tea leaves’ mana with a puff. She opened her eyes to look at Agnes, holding her breath.

As the thin, nearly lace-like curtains rippled gently in the breeze, Aida felt disappointment well up in her.

I was wrong.

“Did you do something?” Ernie asked hopefully from beside her. She didn’t know when he had followed her into the room.

“I did, but I don’t think it helped.” Aida rewrapped the spirit tea cake and handed it back to the old man with an apologetic look. “I had a theory, but it didn’t go anywhere. I’m sorry.”

Behind Ernie, Levi was staring at the old woman on the bed, frowning slightly. Then his eyes widened.

“Whatever you were doing, it looks like it worked.”
