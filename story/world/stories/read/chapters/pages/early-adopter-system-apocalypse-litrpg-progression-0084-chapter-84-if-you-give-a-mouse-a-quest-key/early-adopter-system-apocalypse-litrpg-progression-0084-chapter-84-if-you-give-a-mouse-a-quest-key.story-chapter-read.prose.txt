Scarlet never thought she’d ever again enjoy running, not since the accident that had mangled her hip. However, after Integration, she was discovering the joy of pushing her enhanced body. Running was actually quite enjoyable when you could move as fast as a car. And weren’t fleeing for your life.

As fun as the telekinetic platform had been, with her current Stats, it was faster to get there on land.

'I really need to develop that whole mind-reading psychic thing, buddy,' Scarlet sent to Maus as she made her way back to her property.

That meant dealing with the information layer. She was barely reading the most basic flows of energy right now and was still leaning on Empathy to translate her psionic impressions. Unfortunately, Empathy couldn’t help her translate the thought signals she was beginning to read.

Just how much information she was leaving on the table because she couldn’t parse it? No. That simply wouldn't do.

Scarlet circled the perimeter of her property when she arrived. The Mini-Map said the coast was clear, but considering the strange and esoteric skills she’d experienced, it would have felt irresponsible not to do her due diligence.

It didn’t take her long before she was at the Portal. The glowing platform was still there, shooting out iridescent light, though slightly dimmer than it had been the first time she'd come across it.

She remembered the Guide saying that if these things weren't regularly cleared, they could destabilize and cause problems. The best solution to that was administrative access or regular Portal clears, which could be profitable.

But that would mean she’d have to clear it. To Claim it. Going in now would guarantee her a world first.

Scarlet looked up at the sky.

The stars were bright. With a solid third of the population dead, and infrastructure slowly failing, light pollution was on a steep decline.

It was late.

Her body didn't really need the sleep, but her mind was exhausted.

'Boss?'

Maus called when she'd been standing in one place for several silent seconds.

'Boss, are we going home?'

'The bunker? Yeah, I just...'

Her eyes tracked the shifting lights. Her instinctual use of psionic energy was causing an increasingly painful migraine; one she was well aware wouldn't settle until she stopped trying to read the impossible knot of information she just knew was embedded in those patterns.

'Boss.'

'Yes. Right. Rest. It's just... I should clear it,' she sent back to him. 'Just get it over with. It probably wouldn't even take long. A couple hours at our power level. Blitz through, get it done. 'Boss, you should take a nap.'

Scarlet pulled her chin back and tugged out the neckline of her collar, peeking at the little guy nestled against her skin, beneath her shirt.

They stared at each other for a while, her purple-rimmed eyes reflected in the galaxies of his own.

'Yeah, Maus. I should take a nap.'

She dashed towards The Old Lodge.

It was early. The day was just waking up along with its people. A few carriages were on the road. Shops were opening, and a few enterprising vendors already had breakfast stalls in full swing. Further in there appeared to be a market square.

Maus paused in the shadow of the doorway to think.

He needed to secure the prisoner. That meant doing a crime, probably. Crimes could be tricky, and he didn’t want to be caught. So, the first thing he needed to do was his diligence.

Doing one's diligence in this case, he knew, involved reconnaissance. That was quiet work; gathering information, clandestine, Boss had called it. Yes, he would absolutely do his diligence.

He'd gotten increasingly adept at manipulating shadows during his time in the training world, and he used them now, along with a presence-dampening technique he and the boss had been working on, to slip through the crowds unnoticed.

He wasn't the only murine creature out and about, but he was absolutely the best looking, and most certainly the smartest. There was a dullness to the eyes of the other rats, mice, and alley scavengers that caused a strange sense of pity to surge within him. He'd have to ask the boss about it later.

The closer he got to the market square, the stronger the smell of food became. Eyes, ears, and every other sense open, Maus tucked himself in a corner near the central fountain, where he could easily observe, remember, and map.

He’d check the prison first. That meant he needed to know where the prison was and how to get there. He also wanted to understand the capabilities of the people in this city: who was the strongest, how did they move, how did they respond to emergencies, and just how much could be gotten away with.

So, he hunkered down, and watched, and listened, and sent out the occasional Echoes that together created a three-dimensional grayscale map of the city. He'd scamper from one area to another, send out a pulse, stop, observe, remember, map, wait, observe, then scamper and do it all over again, and again, and again until he was sure his diligence was absolutely done.

If he did happen to swipe a snack or two, well, that was simply prudence. Mother had taught him the dangers of decision-making when hungry.

Hungry brains made bad decisions.
