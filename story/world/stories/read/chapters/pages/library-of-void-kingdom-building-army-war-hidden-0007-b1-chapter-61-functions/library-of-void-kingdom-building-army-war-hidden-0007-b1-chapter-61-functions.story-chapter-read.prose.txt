They moved through the narrow alleys of the slums, their footsteps crunching softly against uneven stone and packed earth. The air carried the familiar stench of open drains and damp soil—yet beneath it lingered something else. An undercurrent of stubborn life.

Depression clung to the place like fog.But so did resilience.

Vector walked calmly, hands tucked into his sleeves, posture relaxed. To any onlooker, he would appear completely out of place—his clothes too clean, his bearing too composed—as he crossed the slum alleys peacefully. But beneath that calm exterior, his mind was far from idle.

Since leaving the base, a portion of his mind had been immersed elsewhere.

Void Library System.

Ever since its awakening, it had remained quiet—present, waiting. Unlike the tyrannical systems of fiction that barked commands and forced quests upon their hosts, this one felt… passive. As if it existed solely to be used when its host willed it.

Yet what intrigued him most were its Functions.

In the infirmary, he had deliberately ignored them in his haste. Now, with danger momentarily distant and settling everything, eagerness surfaced. He refocused inward, calling up the interface once more.

Of the three listed Functions, he already possessed information about two—Storage Space and Scanning—knowledge inherited from the inheritance. But the third…

Simulation.

That one had been added as a gift by the Will of the Void.

Though he was curious about it, he restrained himself. First, he wanted to confirm whether the other functions truly matched what his inheritance had described.

As they turned a corner, Vector slowed his pace slightly, his eyes unfocused. Unconsciously, he adjusted his stride to remain half a step behind Sebastian, instinctively following, while his attention sank deeper into the interface.

System, He asked in his mind, what is 'Storage Space'? Is it the space where we can store things?

--- > [System Notification]

[Replying to Host]

[Storage Space is a dimensional pocket where the host can store non-living items.]

[Living beings cannot be stored.]

[Current capacity: 30mX30mX30m.]---

His eyes lit up.

Exactly as mentioned… he thought.

Confirming the fact, the first image that surfaced in his mind was weapons. It was normal for Warrior to carry weapons but the city being newly conquered and in unstable phase it was prohibited for anyone from carrying them.

Weapons he couldn’t carry openly. Weapons he hadn’t dared bring to the Earl’s Estate. Weapons meant for emergencies, for moments when preparation decided life and death.

But now… all of them could now be with him.

Hidden.Untouchable.

What was once a logistical nightmare had just been erased in an instant.

This… is absurdly convenient.

With weapons Sebastian would be able to show his peak combat powers. He had Expert mastery in Sword Art. Like Blood Energy, Martial Arts were also divided in Basic, Intermediate, Advance and Expert mastery.

He made up his mind to store some weapons and other miscellaneous items after having breakfast. Explaining the Storage Space to Sebastian, if there was need, would be addressed when such a situation arose.

His gaze flicked briefly toward Sebastian’s back. The man walked unarmed, careful not garnering any attention, yet Vector knew no one was match for him in the city. His strength spoke for itself, but he still maintained the same natural expression as others.

Officially, there was only two Third Stage Warrior in the city, the City Lord and his butler, Josh.

Unofficially—There were three.

And the Earl’s Estate had no idea.

The scenery changed… They had already left the slums behind and were now walking along broader roads leading toward the commercial district. The buildings grew sturdier, the streets cleaner. Patrol routes became visible—soldiers stationed at corners, black polished armor, eyes alert.

Vector shook himself free of the surrounding distractions and turned inward again, refocusing on the interface. He was simply confirming what he already knew—but the satisfaction it brought was undeniable.

Relief.Happiness.

Proof that this power was real.

Not false.Not illusion.Not a cruel trick of fate.

The Void Library System was real.

His next focus—Scanning. Just as he was about to ask about it… he stopped.

A hand landed on his shoulder. Firm. Halting his forward movement. Vector snapped back to reality instantly.

He turned to Sebastian, searching for the reason. The man now stood beside him rather than ahead, his hand resting lightly yet unmistakably firm.

“Hey—you. Stop right there.” Unknown voice disturbed his attention.

Vector turned his head toward the source of the voice.

A patrolling guard clad in black armor approached them, boots striking the cobbled road with deliberate power. His hand rested on the hilt of his sword, eyes narrowed as they assessed him.

“Show me your identity proof,” the guard continued coldly while looking at him. “Why were you wobbling your head like that?”

Vector immediately understood why Sebastian had stopped him. And the soldier asking for his identity, must be due to his suspicious wobbling.

In Carnatic City, vigilance bordered on paranoia. Every street, every intersection was watched. Homes were inspected regularly. Anyone acting even slightly out of place was questioned.

Except the slums.

The slum was free from the patrol guards all because of its nature—filthy, dirty and full of stench where normal people would have difficulty even breathing. Simply a definition of squalor. But it had become paradise for the persecuted people of the Carnatic City. And the operational base for the Resistance.

The guard stopped a few arms’ length away from him, gaze sharp—and then drifting, briefly, toward Sebastian before focusing on him again.

The guard’s lips curved into a faint smirk.

Vector was intimately familiar with the predatory nature of these Liches. It was an unwavering trait of these patrolling soldiers: at every opportunity, they attempted to extort money from the populace of Novastra—people forced to live here after losing their lands, livelihoods, and futures.

Even the faintest hint of suspicious behavior was enough for them to pounce.

They didn’t merely ask for tribute. They swaggered, pressing their authority down on the weak, constricting their breaths until fear loosened their coin pouches and their greed was satisfied.

“I’m a gardener working at the Earl’s Estate,” Vector said in neutral tone, neither submissive nor confrontational.

He produced a small seal and held it out.

The change was immediate.

The predatory gleam in the guard’s eyes vanished.

The seal bore the mark of the City Lord’s Estatel.

The smirk vanished as if wiped away.

The soldier’s posture straightened. The oppressive weight in his stance evaporated, replaced by practiced, almost courteous neutrality.

“Oh,” the guard said lightly. “Estate personnel.”

Working in the Estate came with its own perks. Not to be oppressed by these liches.

Vector noticed the guard’s eyes flick briefly toward Sebastian—but he paid it no mind. After considering the possibilities the guard would let him leave too. He had seen this exchange countless times. Once the seal was shown, questions usually ended.

The guard turned his face toward him again.

“Be careful,” the guard said, tone no longer hostile. “Don’t act suspicious in public. You were lucky it was me. If someone else had seen that…” He snorted softly. “Hmph. You can go.”

Vector inclined his head slightly. “Thank you.”

They resumed walking, once again. Toward their destination.

He didn’t explain his earlier distraction to Sebastian. There was no need. And he also knew Sebastian would not ask unless he chose to speak—and until then, silence was just fine.

Vector once again shifted his focus back to the System—this time with deliberate caution in his steps. He walked carefully, mindful of his surroundings, ensuring he did not draw the attention of the patrolling Lionheart soldiers again.

As he walked, his gaze drifted toward the second function listed on the interface.

Scanning.

Without hesitation, curiosity bubbling to the surface, he questioned it to the System.

System, what about the “Scanning” function?

Silently in his mind.

---

[System Notification]

[Replying to Host]

[The Scanning Function allows the Host to analyze and scan technologies or devices; Martial Arts, Origin Energy Arts, Formations and similar construct.]

[Once scanned, related knowledge will be added to the Void Library and become accessible to the host.]---

Vector nodded faintly.

It didn’t impress him at first.

The explanation repeated information he already knew from his inheritance. He had half-expected the System to reveal something more—some hidden advantage or deeper mechanic that hadn’t been mentioned before.

If he already possessed books, manuals, or written records, then what exactly was the value of Scanning? Was its only purpose to store that information inside the System’s archives, allowing him to access it whenever he wished?

Hmmm…

At first glance, it seemed underwhelming.

But then another thought surfaced.

Perhaps this function would reveal its true worth in the future—especially if he encountered technologies unfamiliar to him. Technologies that could be scanned and added directly to the Void Library without requiring prior knowledge or years of study.

His thoughts began to connect.

Scanning… and adding technologies… were directly related. And that connection led him to something far more important.

The locked technology trees of Earth.

If he constructed even the simplest setup—magnets revolving around a copper coil—it would generate electricity. Electromagnetic induction. Even the most primitive design of a light bulb would count as an initial-tier technology.

But that wasn’t the real point.

The true value lay in what followed.

Once such a creation was scanned by the System, wouldn’t it unlock the entire technology tree related to that field? Electricity. Power generation. Motors. Energy systems. This function wasn’t merely for convenience.

It was a key.

A key meant to unlock Earth’s sealed technologies, one field at a time.

And not just Earth’s.

If this world possessed technologies superior to those of Earth—though considering its technological age, he thought that unlikely—then those too could be scanned and stored within the Void Library.

One scan.

And all the knowledge related to that product would be at his fingertips.

Not necessarily the complete theoretical foundation—but enough to analyze it, research it, and reconstruct the underlying principles. It would make progress exponentially easier.

Maybe even spaceships… he thought idly.

Hehee…

And this joy of his also began to form externally on his face. The corners of his lips curved upward, forming a wide arc—an expression of restrained excitement.

And… the 'Simulation' function? He asked enthusiastically. The one he didn’t know anything about.
