“All right. You used a Skill to do this, yes? A healing Skill?”

He nodded. “It’s called Overheal. It uses life mana to boost the body’s natural regeneration.”

“And I’m assuming it just keeps pumping in energy until you stop supplying the construct mana?”

“Construct?” he asked.

Scarlet paused.

“Your Skill. Overheal. It’s channeled?”

“Um, yeah.”

“Good.” She nodded towards one of the caged Lillows. “Then I want you to reverse it.”

“Pardon?”

“The healing. Overheal forces the body’s natural regeneration into overdrive. I want you to take the pattern that produces that effect and reverse it.”

He looked at her. She looked at him.

“Go on. I’m currently holding it above ground. Whatever you do, it can’t regenerate.”

“That’s not the problem. The Skill doesn’t come with reversal.”

“I’m not asking you to reverse the Skill. I’m asking you to modify the mana weave. The original pattern should give you the clues for how to reverse the effect.”

“What do you mean? The Skill doesn’t come with reversal,” he repeated.

Scarlet blinked.

“How do you use the Skill?”

“I activate it.”

Scarlet looked at him, dumbfounded.

“What about the mana patterns the Skill uses?”

“The what?”

Scarlet blinked, then looked around the field. People had been eavesdropping on their conversation, so she figured at least one of them would nod in understanding. Raj looked thoughtful. Kain’s frustration had spiked higher, though now some of it was directed at himself. However, there was no recognition amongst the crowd.

No agreement, no acceptance. No sign these people had been doing anything but toggling Skills on and off like buttons in a video game.

That was... curious.

Perhaps because Scarlet had customised the patterns for all but her investigative Skills, she’d never considered what it was like for people who simply acquired Skills and used them. The patterns for her identification Skills were so convoluted and esoteric that interpreting the information they provided was challenge enough.

She’d never considered what it was like for people who acquired Skills, then just used them.

No exploration. No questioning. Just plug and play.

Okay. Perhaps this was a blind spot on her end.

She made a mental note to add a tutorial video on STARNet ASAP, because it was clear that people were leaving potential on the table and that was unacceptable. Especially with someone as talented as Myka. It was clear he had talent for mana manipulation. He’d just not considered applying those same instincts in other areas.

She would rectify that.

“All right,” she said. “Let’s start at the beginning.”

She walked him through the process of feeling out the patterns of mana as he wove it, of understanding his Skill and its function, the way the System assisted him and how he used it.

“The pattern is the shape you form your weave into to create a construct,” she explained.

“Oh, okay.”

“So, what I want you to do is alter the pattern for Overheal to reverse the healing.”

Instead of trying to get him to take off the training wheels right now, she picked apart the weave and tried to work out the problem with him.

“Why don’t we try this?”

She’d begun working on telepathic communication but wasn’t quite comfortable enough to use it while her mind was split in so many directions. So instead, she created an illusion in front of him while blocking it from outside viewers, like a one-way mirror.

In front of them, she began to demonstrate ideas for new patterns.

He startled. “You can–”

His voice cut off as her mana prodded his.

“Right, I’ll try,” he said slowly. His eyes were fixed on the shifting matrices in front of him.

It wasn’t that he didn’t understand what she’d done. The control she took so for granted was simply something he hadn’t acquired yet. He’d thought he was pretty good with the way he manually channelled his mana. Her control made him feel clumsy.

Scarlet quickly slapped down the gratitude she was beginning to feel for the Historian. It wasn’t like the idea had been novel when she’d come to him. He’d just helped her refine her control. It benefited him as well.

Instead, she focused on Myka.

“May I?”

She pressed her hand against his back again. He grunted, frustrated, as she very gently outlined a structure within his body, mapping the pathways she felt were viable.

It was a weave she had no natural affinity for herself. While she could force her mana in that direction, Myka took to it like a duck to water.

Ever so slowly, he cast.

The first try was a spluttering failure. It took him several more attempts to get the hang of which channels he was using and the weave pattern for a Skill he had yet to acquire. Once he finally got the pattern into play, it took a few more tries to find the right combination of intent and execution.

But finally, finally, something clicked.

He focused on the lillow in front of him. It had settled into a petulant sort of slump, only beginning to thrash again when it felt mana coalescing around it.

The battlefield had acquired a steady hum as people tended to the injured, recovered, and warily waited for the fight to be over so they could begin harvesting the first Lillow.

Now it fell silent once more.

The feeling around the area palpably changed.

Decay.

What had been light and soothing became stifling and heavy. Where Overheal had forced tumorous growths, pustules and horrific abscesses to form, the defenders now watched as Myka’s mana swelled and pushed out.

As it did, the Lillow began to decay. Bits and pieces of it sloughed off into a rotting sludge.

The sludge quickly became an almost compost-like mixture that Scarlet happily gathered and compressed into several beads of incredibly mana-dense earth, all the moisture wrung out of it as she shrunk the cage she’d trapped it in.

The final piece of the creature floated over to Myka. Analyse told him what it was before it landed in his hands, where he easily identified the shard of mana.

“A mana shard. Nature attuned.”

He hacked out a laugh that sounded almost like a cough. His knees buckled, and he barely caught himself.

Top-up or not, he was tapped out.

Then his eyes lost focus.

A moment later, he grinned at Scarlet and opened his mouth.

‘New Skill?’ she asked in his head.

He nodded frantically, tipped his head back, and joy – not happiness, pure, unadulterated accomplishment – burbled out of him palpably.

‘I’m guessing you can’t do the next one.’

“I’m tapped out,” he said.

Scarlet turned towards the still aggressive monster banging around in the cage she’d made for it.

With a wave of her gloved hand, blades of wind filled the box and tore the beast to ribbons.

This one held no shard, but there was plenty of biomatter. Scarlet compressed that as well, drew it towards herself, and tucked it into her bag.

Then she turned to face the defenders and nodded once.

As though the floodgates had been lifted, activity returned to the field. People scuttled around, beginning to harvest the first lillow, gathering together and debriefing. More than a few glanced their way as Kain and the rest of his group closed in on Scarlet.

Brother Dearest, of course, immediately began.

“What the hell was that, Scar? Seriously?” he shouted, flinching back when he met her eyes. For a moment, caution became the dominant emotion before indignation smothered it. “Okay, Scarlet. You want to be called Scarlet, right?”

“It is my name,” she said, voice even.

Kain scoffed. “How about I call you irresponsible instead? You bow out at the first sign of danger, then you come back and put on a hero act, only to what, play Obi-Wan Kenobi on a battlefield?”

Scarlet just looked at him. Her face its same unreadable mask.

“How many casualties were there among the residents?”

Kain flinched back slightly. “What?”

“How many were injured, and how many of those were critical? Before and after you arrived on the scene? How many people currently require medical attention?”

What had begun as a single question, with a pause for an answer, became a rapid-fire barrage that only seemed to further agitate Kain.

“What the hell are you talking about? How am I supposed to know?”

“Boss!”

A clear but tiny voice made itself known as Maus flickered onto Scarlet’s shoulder.

“Boss, I did the triage with Ms. Margaret, like you asked. Nobody died!” he cheered.

“That’s really good, buddy.” Scarlet plucked him off her shoulder and held the preening mouse in her hand.

“Some people are still hurt bad, but most ’em are stable. And the mayor said we could move them all over to the clinic. We’re out of poultice, though.”

Maus sent a feeling of apology through the bond.

‘That’s all right. I told you to use it, didn’t I?’ she sent through the bond.

Maus brightened instantly.

“Oh! Boss, I found their nest and beat them all up.”

“The lumberyard?” Scarlet asked. She’d scouted the area with him before heading into town to spectate the fight.

“There were so many,” he said gleefully.
