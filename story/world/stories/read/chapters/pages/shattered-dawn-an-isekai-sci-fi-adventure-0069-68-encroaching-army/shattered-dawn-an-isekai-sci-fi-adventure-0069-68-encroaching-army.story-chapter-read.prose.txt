“See what?” Prator demanded, his voice rising. “Don’t be vague with me, man. Spit it out!”

He gestured for Elion to remain hidden in the tunnel.

“Just lights on the horizon,” the messenger said. “Sorry, I wasn’t trying to be mellow dramatic.”

“Zelian’s halls man,” Prator said. “I thought we were under attack. Be more specific with your language in the future.”

“Yes, sir.”

“Where do I need to go?”

“The front gate,” the messenger said. “Velnar is waiting there for you.”

“Thank you. Go tell Velnar I’m coming.”

“Yes, sir!” Footsteps echoed as the man ran off.

Prator turned to Elion. “Come with me.”

Elion followed Prator up out of the dig site.

Prator tossed him a foreman’s jacket. “Put this on,” he said. Elion took the garment. It was light green, with a yellow cross on the back. It looked a little bit like a high-visibility vest. Elion put it on.

“Pull up the hood,” Prator ordered.

Elion did, and Prator inspected him appraisingly. “It mostly covers your hair. Should keep people from asking questions for now.”

They worked their way back through the ruins, and then out of the crater. The sun had set already, their only light coming from splinters of the Celestial Sphere, reflecting a pink sunset hue across the town.

Prator walked fast, and Elion struggled to keep up. The man wasn’t that much taller than Elion, but he powered forward like a man on a mission. After everything he had heard about Prator he was surprised to find that he liked the man.

Maybe he just reminded him of Zev. This guy is literally enslaving people, Elion reminded himself. He’s imprisoned me against my will, manipulating me with a bondhold.

He couldn’t let that go. Prator wasn’t his friend.

“Are you really a knight?” Elion asked as they hurried down the road.

“Yes,” Prator said.

“You swore the oaths?” Elion asked.

“I did,” Prator said again.

“How are you still a knight then, when you have so many slaves?”

Prator slowed, turning to look at Elion.

“Tell me what the oaths are,” he said.

Elion quoted them. “You will stand as a shield to your people. You will wield your blade in defense of the truth. You will fight to save your friends and your enemies.”

“Which one of those do you think I’m breaking?” Prator asked.

Elion didn’t hesitate. “All of them.”

Prator raised an eyebrow, but Elion pressed on, his heart thundering in his chest.

“Stand as a shield to your people—but you send them down to do the most dangerous work, down in the acid pit. People die, and are seriously injured all the time. Are you protecting them?” Elion grew more passionate as he spoke.

“Wield your blade in defense of the truth—How many people did you lie to? Luring them here, to New Kairn Tol, only to chain them up and force them to work when they realized what you were doing.”

Prator said nothing.

“And the last one,” Elion said. “You will fight to save your friends and your enemies.” He met Prator’s gaze, unflinching. “Who do you think they are? Because right now, it looks to me like you’re fighting to own people, and to make money, not save them.”

Prator’s face darkened.

They continued along. Electricity buzzed in Elion’s chest, fear and excitement filling him.

“You haven’t lived long enough to understand what the Oaths mean,” Prator said.

Elion shook his head. “You either die the hero, or live long enough to see yourself become the villain? I’m not buying it. Maybe I’m new to this, but I could never justify what you’ve done.”

“Oh yeah?” Prator countered. “And who made you into Dawn’s Council? You just decided to take it over after Dorian killed them all? You might claim to be a Starholder but that doesn’t make you the judge. I won’t hear another word from you about this until you’re at least ten years older. Be quiet.”

Elion opened his mouth to protest, but Prator’s command shut him up. He still had Elion’s bondhold. Elion wondered if he’d made a mistake in confronting the man. Prator had offered to help him, after all. It was a bad strategy to antagonize someone who could literally walk him over to Venya and hand him over.

Activity buzzed on the walls over the main gate. As Elion and Prator approached, Elion tried to see through the gate to make out what everyone was looking at. He caught sight of a few lights in the distance, but nothing more.

A few lights flashed in the darkness. People gathered near the gate, some of them peering through it, others seeming dazed, simply sitting on the ground, bland expressions on their faces.

A small group of people gathered around a man lying on the ground, administering first aid as he bled from an injury in his abdomen.

Kendry was there, hands on her hips as she snapped at people. Elion shifted his hood, trying to prevent her from seeing his face.

“Patch him up good!” she demanded. “Owned aren’t cheap!”

Prator grabbed Kendry by the arm.

“Where is Velnar?” he demanded. “What’s going on out there?”

“They killed two of my owned on a scouting patrol, that’s what!” Kendry spat. "Zelian’s halls, they ambushed us! I can’t afford to lose another one.” She looked anxiously at the bleeding man on the ground.

“Where is Velnar?” Prator asked again.

The woman pointed up to the top of the wall. Prator jogged up a set of stairs. Elion watched him go. Did he forget about me?

If Elion slipped away, Prator might not even be noticed until it was too late.

But as Prator moved further up the stairs, Elion realized that he was still bound by Prator’s order; ‘Come with me.’

Elion followed the Knight up the stairs to the top of the wall. There, a gruff man with a scar on his chin issued orders while filling Prator in on what was happening.

“How long ago?” Prator asked. “When did you get here, Velnor?”

“In the last 20 minutes,” Velnor said. “I came as soon as I heard.”

Prator snatched a pair of binoculars from Velnor, turning to stare out over the ruins of the city. Velnor gave Elion a strange look, but didn’t question his presence.

“You’re sure it’s Arnolfo?” Prator asked.

“Not sure,” Velnor said. “But who else travels with a pack of arachnatronics?"

Elion squinted, trying to make out what was happening in the darkness. He wished he had the binoculars.

About a half mile away, down the decrepit street that ran through the ruins of Kairn Tol, shapes and lights moved in the darkness. It didn’t look like fighting, but Elion couldn’t be sure.

“They’re trying to capture his cargo, then,” Prator said. “We’ve got to get out there and help him.”

“It’s a surround,” Velnor said. “They might just be trying to prevent him from getting to us?”

“Either way,” Prator said. “I need Arnolfo’s delivery. I’m not going to let those fools out there deter him.”

Then Prator turned to Elion, shoving the binoculars into his hands. “You want to lecture me about the oaths, boy? Take a look out there and tell me what you see.”

Elion raised the lenses to his eyes, and the distant lights jumped into focus.

“I see people, standing in the street,” Elion said. “Looks like they’re carrying rifles. They’ve got a barricade across the road.”

“And beyond that?” Prator asked.

Elion scanned. A mass of legs and blinking lights stood in an empty lot, arachnatronics hemmed in by more people with rifles. Some of the spider-mechs were the cargo hauling type commonly seen in the acid pit or roving the streets of New Kairn Tol. But most of them had a more aggressive, warlike look to them; with protrusions that were probably weapons on their bulbus bodies.

A few people rode on the arachnatronics. The way for the arachnatronics to retreat was clear and unblocked.

“It looks like they’re talking?” Elion said. A man atop an arachnatronic was gesturing, and another figure on the ground in front of the barricade reciprocated.

“What do you learn about the intentions of that army from this,” Prator asked.

Elion thought about it. He realized what this meant. “They’re planning on attacking New Kairn Tol. They must be setting up blockades, trying to prevent any help from getting in here.”

“Yes,” Prator said. “Good. And they’ll likely rob any shipments we attempt to send out.” Prator grabbed the binoculars back and spoke to Velnar. “This has turned all of our worst case scenerios into realities,” he said.

“Agreed, Sir.”

Prator turned to Elion. “What should we do, then?”

“Me?” Elion asked. “You want me to tell you what to do?”

“Based on the oaths. Should we just leave our friend out there, with his shipment of valuable goods? Dodge a fight? Or should we go help him?”

“How important is his shipment?”

“Every shipment is important,” Prator said. “All the shipments out get us the cends we need to buy the shipments that come in. All the shipments coming in are essential to the people here.”

“Well, then…” Elion thought about his answer. “Stand as a shield to your people. And fight to save your friends. You should go help them?”

“I agree,” Prator said, clapping a hand on Elion’s shoulder.

“We’d better go out there and break that blockade,” Prator said. “Velnar, gather my guard. And get this boy a gun.”

Velnar nodded and strode off.

“You want me to go fight?” Elion asked.

“Yes,” Prator said. “I do.” He glanced up and down the wall, lowering his voice so that the nearest guards couldn’t overhear him. “As an Aurelian, I owe it to you to help you learn. What better way than testing you out in battle?”

“I—I can’t,” Elion protested. “If I use my abilities, then Venya will know where I am!”

“Ah, don’t worry about that,” Prator said. “My guard is extremely loyal. They will not betray us.”

“It’s not a good idea,” Elion said. “Besides, Venya has my… sword.” He didn’t think PRator would understand him if he called it a knife.

“She does? How? Why don't you simply summon it back to yourself? Do you not know how? I can teach you."

“It’s some kind of machine,” Elion said. “Blocking me from summoning the blade. When they activated the device, it ripped the sword from my grasp. It held onto my blade so strongly I couldn’t pull the blade back. Whenever I try to summon it, I can sense it. I can feel it out there, and I know where it is, but it just doesn’t come to me.”

"Interesting," Prator said. "Some kind of technology that she's using to disable or harm Aurelians. I will be careful about my sword in her presence. But your Ascended state works? You have any armor?”

“It does… My armor is… Not impressive.”

“Ah,” Prator said, a twinkle in his eye. “The starting loincloth?”

“Some clothes,” Elion said. “Over it. But that’s all.”

“That’s fine. Any protection will do.”

“But I—”

“You’re going to tell me that you want me to obey the oaths, but you aren’t going to follow them yourself?”

“No, that’s not what I’m saying.”

“Then are you coming with me or not?”

“You can compel me,” Elion said. “I have to do whatever you tell me to do.”

“In this case, I will not order you,” Prator said. “If you choose to stay behind, then I will order you to wait here at the gate for me. But this is your decision.”

Elion took a deep breath. He toed the ground, drawing a circle in the dust.

“I’ll go with you,” he said. “I honor my oaths.”
