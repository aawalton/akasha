Jane went back to work on her noodles, biting and chewing a big mouthful of food before she answered Hattie’s question.

"First, I need information. Which requires access. I’ll have to look at every place you draw water. Probably every last one."

Eustice whistled softly. "That's a lot of stops. There must be three dozen wells in town alone, and another dozen or so out at the mines. Several scattered ones for watering livestock, too.”

"Most of them are clustered, though," Hattie added. "The town wells are all within a few streets of here. It's not as spread out as it sounds."

"That's fine. I don't mind the travel.” Jane glanced toward the door, though Dave was long gone by now. “Speaking of which, can I have the use of your driver? I sort of claimed him already. That was mostly a joke, but he did offer to help, and I'd rather work with someone I've already met than start fresh with a stranger."

The man who was not Eustice or Hattie laughed suddenly, though the source of the humor was a temporary mystery to Jane and Bella.

"Sorry about that. I'm Peter. I don't think I ever properly introduced myself, what with all the eating and explaining." The mayor grinned. "Dave is my son."

"Your son?" Bella looked up from her noodles, surprised. "He didn't mention that."

Peter shook his head. "He wouldn't. He thinks about his own work a lot more than mine. He’s excited about working with you two, though. I could hardly tell him ‘no’, with how little call he gets for breaking out his carriage these days. I’m just hoping he learns something from it.”

“Can he serve as our guide?” Jane asked. “Does he know where all the pumps are, in town and outside?”

“Absolutely,” Peter affirmed.

“Then it’s all settled.” Hattie picked up her glass of wine and lifted it in a toast. “To a thorough investigation.”

Everyone toasted, and then finally got down to serious, uninterrupted eating.

—

“You could have talked a bit during that meeting,” Jane told Bella later that night. “You just ate the whole time.”

“It’s my right,” Bella replied. “If I hadn’t focused on eating, my stomach would have talked through the whole thing.”

They were set up in their room now, a sparsely decorated place with two single beds, a small window, and a barebones wardrobe. The innkeeper had shown up to take their laundry before they were truly settled in, and had promised nervously to have everything cleaned, pressed, and ready for the next day.

“So how do you feel about everything?” Bella asked. “It’s your first real traveling job. How’s it shaping up?”

Jane flopped back on her bed. “Ugh. Don’t ask me that. It’s terrible. I’m glad to be here to help, but I’d be much more glad if I knew everything was going to go well. If I can’t help them, this whole town will fall.”

“Couldn’t another mage take a shot?”

“They could, but… it’s a water problem.” Jane heaved a big enough sigh that it felt overly dramatic, even to her. “I hate to say it, but I’m almost the leading expert on water problems. If I can’t figure it out, there’s not much chance another mage would be able to.”

“Oooh, is that a bit of confidence I hear?” Bella threw her pillow at Jane, who threw it back playfully. “About time. Although it’s just like you to have confidence only when you can beat yourself up with it.”

“Fair enough. Still, I want to do a good job. Which, I think, means starting with a good night’s sleep tonight.” Jane sat up. “But first, give me your feet.”

“My feet?”

“Your feet. I’m going to do weird magic things to them, like a scary witch.”

“Fine.” Bella swung her feet over to Jane’s bed. “Just don’t turn them into frogs.”

Jane took a few minutes soaking both her and Bella’s feet in what meager healing magic she could manage. Only a few mages possessed a knack for healing, and Jane was not one of them. But after her aunt’s run-in with a wild boar several months ago, Jane had been trying to practice. She was slowly getting better at wrestling her magic into performing minor reinforcements of what the body already wanted to do.

Now she took a deep breath and tried to concentrate, only stopping when she had saturated the skin on their feet with as much of that unfocused energy as she could.

Bella poked her right foot gingerly. “Doesn’t seem like it did much.”

“Well, maybe not.” Jane yawned. “We’ll just have to see. Good night, Bella.”

“Good night, Jane.”

—

Jane woke to the sound of birds outside her window. She wondered where they even nested, considering there wasn’t much in the way of trees out here. She vaguely remembered hearing about certain types of birds that nested in the ground, but she couldn’t remember if they were the kind that would live in this environment.

She sat up slowly and found Bella still asleep in the other bed. Her friend was a heavy sleeper, it seemed, impervious to the birds outside. Jane envied Bella that. Her own sleep was usually the kind that the smallest sunbeam could shatter, which sometimes made ‘getting enough rest’ an interesting exercise in planning.

She swung her feet out of bed and was pleased to find them significantly less sore than they had been the night before. The healing magic had done its work. It wasn’t a complete cure, but it was close enough to one that she thought she could face another day of walking without too much complaint.

Her traveling clothes were waiting outside the door, folded neatly and resting on a sheet of paper on the floor. Jane picked them up and brought them inside, surprised to see how clean they were. She had expected improvement, certainly, but she hadn’t quite believed they could ever get truly clean again. These were spotless.

Jane tossed a sock at her friend's head, taken from Bella’s pile of newly cleansed clothing. "Bella, wake up. Our clothes are ready."

"Mmph." Bella pulled her pillow over her face. "Five more minutes."

"No more minutes. We have work to do."

"Work is for people who are awake. I need five more minutes."

Jane threw the other sock.

"Come on. I want to get started early."

It took a few more minutes of cajoling, but eventually, Bella dragged herself upright and began to prepare for the day as Jane wrangled her hair into order. They pulled on their freshly-cleaned traveling clothes and laced up their boots. Jane took a moment to appreciate how much better everything felt when it wasn't stiff with dried sweat and all the dirt they’d accumulated between home and here.

"Ready?" she asked.

Bella yawned. "Ready enough. Lead the way, ma’am."

"Don't call me that. I don’t even know why Dave thought it was a good idea. Are we old?"

"We aren’t. He’s just young and trying very hard. It’s cute."

They made their way downstairs to find the inn's common room empty, except for the innkeeper himself. He was a man in his fifties, and the timid deference with which he treated them put Jane on edge.

He looked up as they descended the stairs. Sure enough, Jane saw the same flash of anxiety cross his face that she had seen the day before.

"Good morning, ma'ams. I hope you slept well?"

"Very well, thank you." Jane began to wonder if the ‘ma’am’ thing was a regional peculiarity. She determined to ask Hattie about it at the next opportunity. "The room was lovely."

"I'm glad to hear it. Very glad." He wiped his hands on his apron, another nervous gesture that made Jane feel a little guilty. "What would you like for breakfast? I can make almost anything, if you give me a bit of time. Whatever you’d like."

Bella stepped forward before Jane could answer,

"Whatever's easiest is fine," Bella said. "We're not picky. But could we have a lot of it? We've got a long day ahead."

The innkeeper blinked, then smiled. This, apparently, was a request he understood. This was his territory.

"No problem at all, miss. No problem at all. You two just sit down, and I'll have something ready in no time."

He disappeared into the kitchen. A moment later, Jane heard the clatter of pans and the sizzle of something hitting hot metal. She and Bella chose a table near the window and settled in to wait.

Jane found herself watching the kitchen doorway, catching glimpses of the innkeeper as he moved around his workspace. He was efficient and economical in his movements, and professional in a way she appreciated. He flowed from one task to the next with the kind of practiced ease that only came from years of doing the same work in the same space.

"Uh-oh," Bella said.

Jane turned. "What?"

"I know that look." Bella was grinning at her. "I was wondering how long it would take."

"How long what would take?"

"Before you got itchy to be back in a kitchen." Bella nodded toward the doorway, where the sounds of cooking continued. "You're watching him like you want to go in there and commandeer his ovens."

“I’m not.”

"Mm-hmm."

“Bella! I’m fine. We’ve only been away for a few days.”

"I believe you." Bella's tone made it obvious that she didn't believe Jane at all. "You have absolutely no desire to go back to your kitchen. That’s clear."

Jane didn't answer, because truthfully, she was thinking about it. She was thinking about it quite a lot.

The last several months had been good. After everything that had happened with the lake, and the water-spirit dragon, and her aunt and the contamination and all the rest of it, she had finally been able to settle into something like a normal life.

Most days lately had developed a certain rhythmic sameness that she adored. While her routine at the academy had been oppressive, the predictability of her life in Glenfall was soothing. She woke up early and baked bread. She experimented with new recipes, tweaking ratios and testing techniques, slowly building out her repertoire one successful test-batch at a time. She sold the bread, chatted with neighbors, and was just normal in a way she had never hoped she could be.

Sometimes, she had even been able to forget that she was an archmage at all. She had to keep up with her research, of course, monitoring the lake's magical signatures to make sure that old problems weren’t rearing their ugly heads again. But those tasks took only a few hours each week, and came and went quickly. The rest of the time, she was just Jane.

She missed it already, and she had only been gone three days.

"Here we are." The innkeeper emerged from the kitchen carrying two plates piled high with food. "Sausage and eggs. There's toast coming too, and I can make more of anything if you're still hungry afterwards."

He set the plates down in front of them with obvious pride. Jane had to admit the sheer mass of food was impressive. It was a mountain of calories, and her body wanted every single one.

"This looks wonderful," Jane said, and meant it.

"Thank you, ma'am. Miss. Thank you, miss." He ducked his head and retreated back to the kitchen, clearly more comfortable there than in conversation with his intimidating guests.

Jane picked up her fork and began to eat. The food was exactly what she needed before a long day of investigation. But as she ate, she couldn’t help noticing that the meal was light on anything resembling fresh produce. No fruit, no vegetables. She thought about the wilting gardens she had seen yesterday and frowned.

I'm going to fix this.

When they had devoured every morsel of food, Jane left a few coins on the table. Then they made their way outside. Dave was already waiting for them by his carriage, looking freshly scrubbed and eager. He had clearly been there for a while, long enough to have zoned out. He had to jerk back to attention when he spotted them approaching.

"Good morning, Miss Jane. Miss Bella. I hope you slept well?"

"Very well," Jane replied. "You're here early."

"Wanted to make sure everything was ready." He patted the side of the carriage. "It’s cleaned and oiled and ready to go. I can take you anywhere within reason."

"I appreciate that. We've got a lot of ground to cover. Though we won’t need the carriage for a bit, since we are starting in town."

“Not a problem. My horse can wait. He’s fed and watered.”

“Good.” Bella smiled as she patted the horse’s snout lightly. “We wouldn’t want him to be uncomfortable.”

The three of them set off on foot towards the closest sources of water Dave knew of.

The investigation was now active.
