He shuffled over one way then the other. He sighed, looking down at his leg. It was acting up again, spilling divine mana all over the place. Just successfully finding a way to sever the leg while keeping its affinity for divine mana had almost taken everything out of his thinking capacity. But he had succeeded.

As for how his body would react to divine mana, that was not going to be a problem. He had his own level of affinity for divine mana. It didn’t matter if the gods did not match or agree, assimilating divine mana would not be a problem. And it was not.

Still, the leg acted up every time. With a single-minded focus, he closed up the leak, superficial as it was, and returned his attention to his latest project.

He hummed a tune to himself as he moved through the room. It was a tune his father used to make when he was a child. Even now, he had no idea what the words were supposed to be. He had no idea if there were even words to the tune.

It was a sad thing to think about. His past had been fun enough, being a child had been good. Then he’d grown up and discovered that he preferred more fun things, like playing with the lives of others. Unfortunately, things like that were frowned upon.

So… here he was, in his own space, a room filled with body parts of every living thing he had ever been able to get his hands on that was of any worth.

Discarding sorrowful thoughts, he reached for the lung hanging from a fishhook attached to the wall. He turned it one way, then the other.

“Still not dried out,” he muttered to himself. “Maybe another day or two.”

He released it and turned to the jar on a table close to him. He bent a little, looking into the jar. Within it was water, plain and simple water. Inside the water was a woman’s head. Without a body, he had found a way to keep her alive. Curious, he had placed her in a jar of water, trapping her in an endless cycle of drowning. It was an interesting thing because despite having no lungs to properly drown with, the water still filled her brains and nostrils and eyes.

Every time he saw the woman, she looked like she was drowning and dying. Quite painfully at that.

With a sigh, he turned away from her. Today was not the day he was to release her. She had drowned over fifty people before he’d found her. It was only fair that she suffered for it. So, here she was, drowning for long. Every decade she drowned counted for one person she drowned. It was fitting. At least he liked to think so.

His interface popped up in front of him, informing him of the destruction of some place somewhere. He discarded it without a thought. It was just his father looking for him as he had been doing for so many years.

It always amazed him how his father always found these places long after he had left them. Yes, he was using the principle of his father’s concealment technique to hide himself. That his father was still finding some of the places that he had been was interesting.

But his father would not find him here, though. He could not.

With the divine mana leaking from his leg stopped and the woman still drowning, he had confirmed that everything remained as they were supposed to. Now, he turned his attention to an interesting notification.

He stroked his jaw in contemplation as he looked at it, studied it. Among all the things he had expected and not expected in this life, this was not one of them.

[Aiden Lacheart has slain The Immortal]

Perhaps it was time to pay this… Aiden Lacheart a visit, whoever he was.
