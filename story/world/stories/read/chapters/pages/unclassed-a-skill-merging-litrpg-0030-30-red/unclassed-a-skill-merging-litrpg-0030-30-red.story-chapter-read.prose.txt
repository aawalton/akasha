Okay… time to figure out what a ‘lab’ was.

[Short for laboratory. A room through which experiments and tests are conducted.]

[Hoard]’s ability to define words I read in translations was definitely helpful. The explanation didn’t really give me a clear picture of what a lab exactly was, but I thought I got the basic idea. All I knew was, there were multiple of them on each floor of the facility, and they seemed to take up quite a bit of floor space, meaning they were clearly important and likely a big part of whatever the Drassians did here.

Thankfully, the other notable areas I’d marked out were easier for me to envision. I was starting by heading directly to one of those. Namely, the storage area on the first floor.

Worst case, there were a few new valuables I could store. I didn’t have a lot of spare room in my [Hoard], and I couldn’t envision dragging a cart around the underground, but there were always lower priority items I could replace, and who knows? Maybe I could make a second trip if I didn’t find space for everything I wanted.

Best case, I found something to either upgrade my fighting power or get me through that door. Either way, I wanted to take stock of what was in there and whether any of it was of use to me before I started moving deeper into what was definitely dangerous territory.

I heard a metallic whir all around me whenever I toggled on [Moonlit Grace]. My heightened senses informed me of the life that existed inside of the walls. Moving gears, pistons, an electric hum, and other sounds i couldn’t begin to comprehend the origin of spoke to the living nature of this building, a construct which had outlasted its architects and continued to pulse and breathe even as I ventured through its solemn halls.

Still I heard the occasional clicking sound, a stab of tension cresting over the otherwise peaceful ambience, but I couldn’t determine if what I was hearing was a distant feral or my mind playing tricks on me.

I attempted to track each sound as I moved, my eyes scanning the halls for any odd movement or signs of activity on the thermal sensor, but for the duration of my travel, I encountered no enemies.

That being said, I also didn’t find the corpse of the feral I’d killed here last time. Which felt… incredibly foreboding. It led me to realise that in spite of all the exploration I’d done already, I never actually found any Drassian bodies. You’d think that if they’d all sealed themselves within this place and died, there’d be some skeletons lying around.

Did the ferals eat everything? Bones and all? Is that what had happened to the body I’d left here?

I forced the thought from my mind as I moved past the control room, hanging a left.

Finding my way to the far side of the ground floor, I continued until the lights above me began to continually flicker, then found myself moving into a wide corridor with a series of thick doors much like those of the entrance, only these ones had thankfully been left open.

That was good. No passcode required to enter this place. I was glad one thing had gotten simpler.

Well, simpler and messier. Along with the still-open doors were rows upon rows of shelves, upon which metallic boxes had once sat…

Not anymore. This room had been ransacked. Shelves had been upturned, their contents scattered and, in some cases, smashed. The floor was lined with broken glass and shattered gemstones, metal instruments I didn’t recognise, and other assorted miscellany.

A glance at the room’s contents was far less interesting than what I saw on the far wall. Another set of doors, these ones sealed, that looked as if they’d been repeatedly battered, smashed, even scorched, in an attempt to prise them open. The label above the door was Drassian, but I understood the word for it clearly from Erason’s notes.

Armory.

The assorted glassware and random instruments in this room were suddenly far less interesting to me than whatever laid in there. That was where the Drassians had kept their weapons, and some of them had clearly desperately wanted in when things went from bad to worse here. The smashed console beside the door told me that the only way I was getting into this room was brute force, but…

The door looked to be on its last legs. Clearly, whoever had attempted to breach this place previously had been pretty close, they’d just given out or given up before reaching their prize.

Soon to be my prize. I’d seen what one Drassian weapon could do already. It’d helped me through an array of tough spots. What laid waiting for me in a whole room full of them?

At the very least, more ammo. Hopefully, bigger guns. Maybe even a melee weapon I could use.

I took another quick scan of the area. No clicks, no heat signatures. Nothing on the other side of the door, either.

That said, if I decided to go to work knocking this door down, it’d make noise. Noise would attract more of those things if any were in earshot. Was I willing to risk that possibility on the chance that I could better arm myself in the aftermath?

I looked around the room I was in. The open double doors were a wide entry point, and the room was rather large, but it quickly dawned on me that if getting in that armory was what I wanted, this might be the ideal place to attract any nearby ferals.

I could drag the shelves towards the open space and reposition the contents of the room to serve as a barricade. I could line the floor by the door with broken glass. I could set up my turret with its remaining ammo to point at the door, knowing it served as the only entry point.

No point just thinking about it. I was putting this plan in motion. I picked up and examined whatever objects I found intact, but I wasn’t yet sure what use I was going to get out of ‘safety goggles’, a ‘petry dish’, or a ‘conical flask’, nor the countless other obscure things this room seemed to be littered in, contents spilt all over the floor, overflowing from upturned containers.

Right now, the main purpose of anything heavy I found was to be shoved in a box and stacked near the door, all part of my forming barrier. Both sides of the door were blocked by shelves and barricaded by boxes, and a small, two foot space that I allowed to remain between them was soon littered with broken glass and sharp metal scraps.

[Trap Making: 8 >> 9.]

I summoned my turret and trained it on the opening of the door. I’d already made a fair bit of noise rearranging the room, and in doing so had cleared a space around the centre that allowed me to move freely. If anything managed to get past the opening, I’d need space to fight it.

I spent a couple of minutes simply breathing once I’d finished setting up, my eyes trained on the narrow opening I’d created.

Despite the fact I knew this was a far safer option than walking around and waiting to be ambushed, confronting the ferals on my own terms was scary enough to make my right hand shake. My whole body was trembling, in fact.

I didn’t want to fight more of these things. I could still feel a phantom sensation on my ankle from where the last one grabbed me, and that only sparked a gnawing recollection of what the first feral had done to me, pulling inches of flesh from my face.

It took time for me to work through my fears. When I slapped against the metal door behind me as loudly as I could, my body was still shaking.

Because I hadn’t conquered my fears. I’d simply decided I wasn’t dying here.

The door creaked as I pounded on it, but that wasn’t what I was listening out for. The familiar sound of clicking was.

It didn’t take long for my ears to detect a suspicious noise. Or maybe it was nothing. Maybe the adrenaline was making me hear things. I listened harder, toggling [Moonlit Grace] to heighten my senses for a few seconds, quickly becoming overwhelmed by the abundance of noise.

I listened and I listened. For a while, nothing. Without my skill’s assistance, silence ruled the dark, and with it, the sounds in this place were far too difficult to parse.

But after a time… I heard it again. It was louder. More distinct.

I quickly toggled my vision skill, attempting to detect a mana signature, but this was hard, too. Clusters of what I considered as energy existed almost everywhere in sight and beyond, differentiating between them was…

Wait. No.

I could see something coming.

The mana around the creature was orange, its movements erratic and wild. It was difficult to pin down, and at times it seemed to almost teleport from one place to another beyond my vision. It moved so quickly, so inorganically that I had to wonder whether this mana belonged to an actual creature.

That said, a part of me knew better. There was something malevolent about the energy in the air. Something voracious. As the motes of colour spiked and shifted, I could feel them beginning to draw closer, to focus into a narrowed point, like an arrow pulled heavy on its drawstring.

I was completely locked onto that energy signature. The moment it surged forwards, the speed of its motion shot through my skull, igniting a stinging headache. My hand clammed up as I murdered its tremors, clutching my dagger and fang and waiting in the long still.

I didn’t wait long.

Clamors and loud thuds echoed around the open space I’d carved, an impromptu battleground separated from unknown horrors only by what flimsy defences I’d made. That same barricade quivered and quaked under a barrage of screaming motion.

Monsters from the depths of delirium sieged the walls, tearing away metal like paperboard, sundering wood like ash. I fought the nausea building inside me, toggling [Moonlit Grace] and attempting to catch a glimpse of their bodies, to see how many I was dealing with. One? Two? Possibly more than that?

Was there a chance I’d summoned a whole hive of these things, and that I’d die in this small room?

I felt the turret whir at my mental command. I reached into my [Hoard], using my mind alone to select the items I wished to remove.

As the inside of my barricade began to give way, I threw an entire carcass of madmaw meat onto the piled glass.

I watched through altered vision as two screaming ferals—only stabs of orange light to my eyes—pushed past the physical blockade and stumbled into the centre of the room. There, the two of them clicked rapidly, seeming unsure of their next actions, and apparently unaffected by the thick pieces of glass repeatedly impaling their feet.

It was impossible to tell which way they were looking, at me or at the meat I had deposited.

I didn’t wait for them to make a choice. I revved up my turret, commanding it to fire.

Bullets pierced the air, striking the leftmost feral and ripping enough flesh from its body that its camouflage became enshrouded in bloody mist. The rightmost lunged at me, and I wasn’t able to change my turret’s position in time. I watched bloody footprints thunder against the floor as I began erecting barriers, slicing my dagger through the air in a flurry, hoping the combination of frantic motion and slowing barriers would be enough for me to catch my assailant with a handful of slashes.

The creature pressed against my barriers even as I struggled to add more, each cut of my weapon another second bought. I felt my dagger cut through flesh, felt hot blood spray against my face. As the pressure built, I pierced the creature somewhere around the shoulder and ripped a chunk of meat from its body, leaving a concave hole of muscle and sinew visible.

It screamed against my assault, muttering and spitting and frothing as after five seconds of struggle, it breached my shields entirely, smashing straight into me and knocking me onto my back.

I rolled with the momentum, scrambling back onto my feet and immediately realising I’d dropped my dagger in the fall. I fell back upon my fists, punching, kicking, and incorporating the spikes built into my suit as a means to brutalise the creature before me, using everything I had at my disposal, even barging into the monster with my lame arm, a spike from my shoulder piercing through its jaw.

The feral scrambled to grab my neck as I attempted to dislodge the spike, its claws sharp and tearing at my suit. The spike ripped from its fixture as I threw myself down to my knees, the only way I was pulling it out, then toggled [Moonlit Grace] for the instant I needed to locate my weapon in the haze of combat.

Claws sliced my shoulders as I grabbed my dagger, and my body was forced to twist against them as I turned and opened up the feral’s belly in a wide arc. Guts spilled from it, and the creature staggered backwards, either pained or exhausted, giving me long enough to raise myself to aching feet and stab it right in the neck.

The creature fell, gurgling, and all the while, I groaned, the pain around my shoulders and upper back hard to ignore even through my adrenaline. It wasn’t life threatening, I imagined, and I could still move, but those slashes fucking stung and my back felt wet and warm.

I commanded my turret to stop firing. I couldn’t think through the noise, my ears were throbbing, and considering the bloody paste that the other feral’s chest had been turned into, I assumed the thing—repeatedly stumbling and falling as it attempted to stand in a sea of glass—was too injured to threaten me anymore.

I slit its throat to be sure.

I stood for a few moments, barely breathing, waiting to see if another would come rushing through, if this was just the beginning. Wondering how I’d cope if and when I ran out of bullets entirely.

For a full minute, I listened. And after not hearing anything of note, even using my senses to their limits, I threw up on the floor, then dropped to my ass.

I didn’t really move for the next ten minutes. Didn’t think either. Just shivered, despite the temperature in the facility feeling far more natural than that of the caves, and repeatedly checked ahead to make sure nothing else was on its way to kill me.

It took me a while to be satisifed that I was safe. Two in the area seemed reasonable… but what if some of them were smart? Waiting for me to drop my guard? What if not every feral had a mana signature I could recognise?

I’d hoped that choosing to fight these creatures myself would conquer my fear of them… it hadn’t. It had only cemented how scared I felt. Even with preparation, these weren’t easy enemies, and I’d die to one all-too-fast if I were to let my guard down.

I actually managed to find use for some things in the room. Lengths of white cloth wrapped around my wounds served as a bandage, and a white salve, which burned then numbed my fresh cuts, was apparently something known as an antiseptic. There were multiple tubs of the stuff on a shelf in the corner of the room, and a quick scan of the contents told me that the substance would help to prevent infections in my injuries.

It didn’t immediately close them, like a health potion might, but I’d heard of shoddy healing leading to infections in the past, so I decided to keep a few tubs.

I was avoiding burning through the last of my healing at all costs. Numbing the pain would have to do. If I exacerbated the injuries to the point that I couldn’t continue otherwise, then I’d use a health potion. Until then, I’d have to cope.

And that meant my shoulders coping as I tried to knock this door down. Which almost made me think twice, but I was glad I kept my conviction. Mainly because the door fell on the first try.

Honestly, that push was half strength. I barely had to touch it. The doors had been laying on a slant for fifteen years, battered and dislodged from their hinges. One good push after all that time had really been all it took.

The fall made a loud bang. Very loud. Something I was extremely aware of as I stepped inside, toggling on a light crystal and placing it in the centre of the dark room.

My light crystal was continually flickering at this point. If the facility didn’t have plenty of light sources readily available, I’d have to worry about being blind soon. Thankfully, the limited charge wasn’t an issue right now, or at least was an issue for future me. The intermittent illumination it provided, plus the light streaming in from the storage room was enough for me to find a console on the other side of the door, still intact. Activating that caused the whole room before me to light up.

It was here that I saw something I hadn’t witnessed once up until now. Skeletons. Two of them. One large and one smaller.

The first was wearing body armour similar to the vest I’d just had torn through, as well as padding around their arms and legs. The second seemed to be wearing a tattered jumpsuit.

These people had died in here. The ferals had never found their way into this room and had never eaten their remains. A morbid question sat in my mind, wondering who these two were and how they’d become trapped.

I ignored it, tearing my gaze away from the grizzly sight and eyeing something far less forlorn, something that in spite of all of my pain and fear, reminded me why I’d chosen to come this way and choose this room as my first destination.

And that was the arsenal of weapons that sat waiting for me. Dozens of guns of various shapes and sizes, some of which I wasn’t sure I could even classify as guns. Various other instruments lined the walls, including knives with serrated edges, and, oddly, several large handles which appeared to be missing blades, which sat at the front of the display of knives.

My curiosity brought me to one of those first. I picked it up, and before I had a chance to store it, I felt the unexpected weight behind the handle.

[Would you like to store Borsian V15 Plasma Cutter? Y/N?]

I accepted, and after storing the item, went to find it in my [Hoard].

[Manufactured on the fourth moon of Arad around 42 years ago, V15 Plasma Cutters are considered to be multi-purpose precision tools. Suitable for carving, masonry, architecture, and mining, the V15 boasts an ionised extendable plama edge which burns at 1800-4000 thousand degrees centigrade. Dials on the side of the handle control the beam’s temperature, thickness, and length.]

[V15 Plasma Cutters require connection to a Bosian 24x4 inch ion battery via an auxiliary cord. Due to the incredible heat and cutting force of the V15, specialised training is encouraged before use.]

[Dimensions: 3 and a half inches by 0.8 inches by 1.4 inches (handle). Weight: 2lbs. Estimated value: high.]

This sounded exciting. I pulled it back out, and after staring at the handle for a time, I found multiple turney things that I assumed were dials. That being said, I couldn’t figure out how to turn it on. No matter how I twisted the three dials, the blade never ignited.

Frowning, I re-read the entry. I read the line about ‘requiring connection’ and my brain started to ache. What was a ‘battery’?

[A battery is a container for cells, through which, chemical energy is converted into electricity.]

Took me a minute to wrap my head around that one. Was that like my core powering my spells and skills?

Mana and electricity were different things. At least, I thought they were. Could I push mana into the handle to the blade and get it to work like that?

I spent a while trying, but the handle remained inert. Hmm. It said it required a pretty large sounding battery, as well as cords, but I couldn’t see anything like that near the display. I pocketed the plasma cutter and moved on, hoping I’d find the battery soon after.

Next was checking out the knives on display. They looked well-made. The handles were light and easy to grip, and the blades were still sharp and unrusted despite how long they’d been gathering dust here.

I picked one up and stored it.

[Ark Carbon Steel Combat Knife, 12 series: Mass produced by Arms Korp for over three hundred years, a tried and consistent weapon that is easy to maintain. Known to chip and break against harder surfaces, but easy to sharpen. Length: 10.2 inches (6 inch blade) x 1 inch x .5 inches (.1 inch blade). Weight: .5lbs. Estimated value: 2gp.]

Huh…

I took the blade out and inspected it, then looked to my mythril amalgam dagger to compare. Neither had an obvious marker of superiority.

I walked back into the storage area, shambling over to the dead feral I’d left in the centre of the room.

I took my usual knife and stabbed it in the breast, using about half of my force. The blade sank in with little resistance.

Next, I took the combat knife and attempted to do the same thing on the opposite side.

This one snagged on its way in, didn’t go as deep, and I struggled and grimaced as I pulled it out.

That confirmed my suspicions pretty easily. I already knew that my mythril blade was worth more than the combat knife, but that could simply be based on the value of the materials, not the actual effectiveness or craftsmanship of the weapon.

Ferals clearly had tough skin. I didn’t doubt that the Ark knife was good, especially considering I was physically weaker than the average person. My usual knife was simply better.

That said, I could use one of these as my offhand, rather than a madmaw fang. Well, assuming my off-hand ever worked again.

Grizzly experiment over, I went back into the armory and shifted my focus over to the guns lining the walls.

There had to be at least fifteen submachine guns like the one I’d picked up on my last visit. On top of that, there were about fifty smaller guns. Picking one of these up revealed it to be a ‘pistol’. Specifically, an Ark-14 9mm.

These took the same ammunition as the submachine gun, it seemed, and after a few minutes of experimentation, I found that loading and unloading them was pretty similar too.

That said, when I attempted to fire a round, the gun came up a lot in my hands. It felt like my body was being pushed back. Not only that, but continually holding the trigger did nothing. I only fired a single round.

I don’t know if it was because the weapon was more compact, but I definitely felt it more in my frame when I fired this gun. I wasn’t really sure where the bullet went in the low light, but more concerning than that was the fact that I didn’t really feel like I knew how to hold this weapon. My hand felt small around it, and a couple more shots, gripping the bottom as hard as I could, led to me struggling to maintain my grip, the top of the pistol kicking back hard with every bullet fired.

Maybe I was meant to use both hands. I couldn’t really do that right now. Even changing a magazine required me to hold the weapon under my left armpit.

I might be better off with the smg. I couldn’t aim it brilliantly with just my right, but the strap on the weapon made it far more convenient to fire from the hip. I decided to strap a new one and store a majority of the others. After storing multiple pistols, I moved on to a third variety of weapon.

The Ark-55b 5.56mm Carbine. Bigger than the Ark-46 SMG, easier to reload, and punchier from what I could tell firing a few shots. Definitely louder. My ears rang for a couple of minutes after firing a few rounds, and I stood paranoid at the edge of the armory door waiting for more ferals to show up.

When none did, I stored all six of these weapons. I had an inkling that this gun might be stronger than the one I was currently using, but I definitely couldn’t aim it with one hand, even with the assistance of a strap. The difference in size and weight simply made it too difficult.

One thing that made me think this gun might be stronger was simply comparing the bullets. There were tons of boxes of ammunition laying all around me, all neatly labelled in a language that I imagined wasn’t Drassian. That said, I could make out the numbers, and the 5.56mm bullets were about twice the length of the 9mm ones while maintaining the same thickness.

Did that alone make them stronger? I was unsure, but if I had the choice between using either, it’d probably be the big ones.

The last thing I discovered on my tour of the armory was a singular, curious weapon. It was sitting alone on a rack that had previously, from what I could tell, been populated by multiple, but now there was only one.

It was a small white gun. I wasn’t even sure I’d label it as a gun. It looked to be made of something other than metal. It was partially see-through, and the inside seemed to contain mirrors. There was also what appeared to be a crystal inside of it.

Beside the weapon were… bullets? They didn’t look like bullets. Things which seemed as if they might go inside. Six of them sat within a small box. I felt a touch of static as I removed one.

Before testing anything, I stored both the weapon and what I assumed to be ammo. Better to know what I was dealing with.

[Radiation Emission Device: A weapon briefly manufactured by Nar’zak Technologies before production of the far more popular ‘Laser Pistol’ began three years later. The RED, as it was known, boasts reasonable stopping power versus soft tissue threats but is less effective against armour.]

[The RED’s single shot capacity is the main shortcoming of its design. One cartridge is loaded into the weapon, fired, and then ejected to be placed within its charging port. Recharging a cartridge takes around six hours.]

Okay. This I had to test.

I loaded what I now knew was a cartridge, which was surprisingly easy. I felt a small hum, as if something in the RED had reacted to the cartridge being placed.

It wasn’t lost on me that the gun was called RED despite being almost wholly white and blue. Seemed strange, but maybe it was a translation thing? I wasn’t really sure.

I took the weapon out and aimed it at one of the dead ferals. The gun was light in my grip, and small enough that I could hold it easily. I found it easy to aim.

I pulled the trigger. Before I knew what had happened, blood and viscera sprayed all around me.

I looked down at the feral that had served as my test dummy. I’d blown about half of its head off. The skin on its face was all but non-existent, and there was a massive portion of its skull that looked to have been cracked and seared off. Pieces of its brain were scattered around it.

I looked down at the weapon in my hands, which had fired almost silently and barely kicked doing so.

Holy shit.

The RED stayed on my person at all times, a cartridge loaded. Further raiding of the armory had led me to finding a belt, holsters, and yet more body armour, including some that actually fit me better, but still nowhere near perfectly. The leg guards were impractical to wear, so I stored them. More food and creature parts were dumped to make space for my new weapons. It seemed incredibly worth it.

I layered it over my spike suit, holstered both the RED on my right side and a pistol on my left, and stored ammo in a space in the belt that seemed to be designed for that. I also found pockets in my armour that seemed to serve a similar purpose. I walked out of that armory storing one half and wearing the other half. The only thing I’d left behind were a few knives.

The only thing I wished I’d found that I hadn’t was a storeroom of potions. Some concoction that could fix whatever was still wrong with my body. I’d check the labs next, but it was dawning on me that I might have to settle for the fact that there was nothing in this facility that was gonna cure my noodle arm.

I tore apart the storage room before leaving, eventually finding something that matched the description of an ionised battery and wires that went with it. That thing was heavy. Forty pounds, maybe?

I had to seriously consider leaving it behind. Almost everything in my [Hoard] right now was guaranteed value, and while the bar had been ticking up greatly ever since I’d found myself in the underground, I was still only two thirds of the way to another level in [Hoard] and I imagined another expansion of my inventory size.

What was I supposed to dump at this point? The plasma cutter was meant to be attached to this battery, which essentially outruled it as a weapon despite its apparently impressive cutting power. It sounded like a very effective tool for learning crafting, which was the main reason I wanted it, but considering it required so much space to store…

Eh, fuck it. I dumped some of my least expensive crystals and a couple of guns to make room. I dared not dump any more food. I was cutting it somewhat thin as it was.

There was a little voice in the back of my head that told me I could go get more meat whenever. There was hardly a shortage of creatures down here, after all.

I didn’t listen to it. What if I got trapped somewhere for a while? What if I broke my leg and had to survive until I could walk again? So many things could happen that could turn my ‘simple easy hunting ground’ into something far out of my reach, so I fought my inner greed.

That didn’t make walking away from even the least valuable part of my haul easy. I almost doubled back at one point, rationalising that I was over-worrying and could dump out at least one more week’s food, or some of the campfire supplies.

I ignored it. The thoughts somehow made it easier to walk around the facility. I was less concerned about a feral jumping out when I was warring with myself over potentially leaving money on the table.

And… what do you know, a feral did jump out at me, not even ten minutes after me leaving the armory.

The interaction was short. I shot it in the chest and it fell to the ground, a big portion of its chest caving in. I walked straight over and stabbed it in the head, before dumping out my spent cartridge and slotting in another.

I was quickly growing very attached to RED. And I was starting to think it was aptly named.

Once again, there was blood fucking everywhere.
