The winter market was wonderful. We were there on official superhero duty, of course, on one of the jobs Vera brought to us a few days ago, but it didn’t mean that we couldn’t enjoy it after my shift was over. It helped a lot that, for once, the whole job went off without a hitch. I arrived after lunch, did my few hours of standing around and looking alive in CRC uniform along with some other hero teams, and then it was over just like that.

The money would arrive soon, but that wasn’t the reason we were happy. We were happy because, as night fell, all those little Christmas lights (it was a bit early for them, but they did make the atmosphere almost magical) came alive, and a light flurry of snow began to fall from the sky, enough to give character to the night but not enough to be a bother. Perhaps it was just me, but seeing the snow still made me feel awe and wonder. It didn’t use to snow as much in the past, before the Onset, and even though now it was a regular thing, it was like my brain still filed it as something special.

When the money arrived, I treated Nadia to a dinner out, just the two of us, a real date. Devil Chicken at the Indian place, with Nadia wondering if they even had devil chicken over there or not.

“Maybe one day we could fly over there and see,” she mused. “You better work on absorbing a flight power soon, before the monsters make travel impossible.”

“It’s just speculation that it will ever happen.” I replied, savoring the meal.

She didn’t seem convinced. “The wilderness is getting more dangerous by the day. Didn’t you see that bounty posted at the market? Someone was desperate to even be able to return to their mountain hut to salvage what they could before they declared the property lost. And they were asking for high-tier heroes to escort them.”

“Yeah, the monsters are becoming a problem. But heroes are getting stronger by the day too,” I said.

“Maybe you can get stronger,” she argued. “Anyone else is stuck. Or are you talking about the awakening numbers?”

“That’s right,” I said.

According to what researchers keep saying, average awakenings keep coming in stronger, making what once were outliers into common powers, and opening the door to stronger and stronger new powers.

“Not to mention,” I added. “The mad scientists are really beginning to make wonders. Throw the AIs into the equation…”

“Ah well, maybe I am being a bit pessimistic,” Nadia admitted. “But you, my prince. You are being awfully optimistic today.”

My temples still remembered the pressure of the Mind Privacy gear from the shift as I chuckled. “Not too optimistic as to become careless, though. I mean, come on, it’s our date, we can afford being a bit silly. Let the world disappoint us tomorrow.”

“Speaking of not disappointing,” she purred. “How about we get back home, toss these fastidious things away, and fool around in front of the fire?”

I grinned. “How about I fire up the heater in our room and we fool around there for once?”

“Ohhh, that’s just what I need. Come on, let’s go.”

She practically dragged me back home, jumping with giddy energy. The reason was that we could finally have some nightly fun in our room, on the big bed, without the both of us shivering in cold. And why was that? Because we finally had money to do things, and we immediately used it to buy a couple wood burning heaters. One went straight into the kitchen, and we already used it and loved it, no more eating cold. The other went in our room, today, with the money from the winter market job.

And soon we were going to test how toasty warm the room would be while I made sweet and then less sweet and more wild love to my little subby girlfriend.

◈◈◈

The rest of the week passed quickly, despite the relative calm job-wise. Nadia got better at using her power to grow and then kill trees for firewood, and she was learning through practice how to time the kill so they seasoned faster after I was done splitting them. I wondered if we were getting into crazy plant-experimentation territory or not, but the result was that our fireplace was back to having real, cozy, nice wood and no longer needed those nasty store-bought logs. We even bought another heater to heat the far side of the living room where the heat of the fire didn’t quite reach.

And with that, the house was one step closer to feeling like a real, cozy nest for the both of us. Sure, there were still two whole floors pretty much unused—save for the single upstairs bedroom we used, but that was compensated by a couple rooms on the ground floor that we never even acknowledged they existed, their dark dusty spaces almost forgotten—but hey, one step at a time.

The garden saw work too. We were beginning to plan our greenhouse for real, making a list of supplies we needed to buy (thank goodness AI was getting better and better at helping us with these errands, and teaching us how to build too! Wouldn’t want to repeat another fiasco like with the cameras.)

“You did blame the AI for that, though,” Nadia snickered. “Or are you now saying that AI will save us all?”

“Oh no,” I replied. “AI will kill us all for sure. I’m just hoping it will help us build our off-grid greenhouse first. Perhaps we should buy some solar panels and go full-off-grid in case Skynet awakens?”

“Hmm. If it happens we are dead anyway. But doing it in case the city stops repairing the grid since we apparently now live in the boonies? Yeah, agreed, great idea Adrian Hale.”

And so the list grew, although the solar panels were a real investment and thus went to the bottom of the list. Vera gave us a call, offering another shift at the winter market (which, we learned, was a weekly thing this year, due to how many people it attracted), and I accepted. She didn’t even need to be asked to give us the afternoon shift, after we told her about how we turned the job into a date, she did everything herself.

Again, it went all smoothly. I could get used to it, honestly. Good work, a bit boring, but work nonetheless and it wasn’t like I was an adrenaline junkie anyway. As I was there, standing by, though… I saw a few powered people passing by. I was bored, and I blame the boredom, but a little thought began to snake into my head.

What if, it seemed to say, what if I just blended into the crowd. Wearing a hood or something. Impossible to track or recognize. I could get close, and all it would take was one single touch. A fleeting moment of contact, they wouldn’t even realize it happened until they tried to use their power and found it gone.

And I’d have a new power. Oh, all the things I could do with new powers…

But I couldn’t. And the reason, I’ll admit, wasn’t because it was wrong to steal powers or anything. That only came later. My number one thought was the thing I still held close to me, swimming together with the nanites. Nadia’s elfin looks. Nadia’s power-mediated mutation. I still wondered, late at night while she snored against my chest, if she missed it. If she missed her old self, now that she could have it without the repercussions. And once or twice I still saw her touch her ear, or look away from a mirror, and I almost asked her, but never did.

The shift ended, and it became another wonderful date. We bought roasted chestnut that burned our frosty fingers and made our breath fog harder than usual. Nadia’s eyes glimmered in the dancing light from the hanging lanterns. And again, I almost asked her.

“What’s up, Adrian?” she asked.

But I simply smiled and kissed her. “I love you, that’s what’s up.”

“Aww.”

The next day, we came back to the city to buy the stuff for the greenhouse, knowing that the coming market money would patch the hole in our finances soon enough.

“You know, one of us would have been more than enough,” Nadia joked as we loaded our truck. “But I really like these little moments together. The errands and the ‘normal’ stuff. Do you?”

“Very,” I said.

“It’s like we are glued together, inseparable.”

“Would that be so bad?”

“It wouldn’t be too bad, no,” she admitted. “I like being stuck to you.”

“And I like having you stuck to me,” I said. “Now, though, please survive as I go pay for all this stuff, okay?”

“I think I can manage,” she said. “After all, it’s a woman’s job to load a truck full of heavy and dirty supplies, is it not?”

I chuckled. “It is when the man pays, and it’s the girl who wants to build the greenhouse.”

“Silly gender standards,” she said. “Then go, my prince. Pay and pamper me while I sweat and pant to load these massive sheets of plexiglass.”

I shook my head, amused by her antics. Crazy how they… never got old. Never in my life had I been with someone with whom I felt so in tune, and yet here she was, Nadia, crashed into my life in the strangest of ways and yet so compatible.

“It’s $173.89,” said the man at the counter.

I dug through my wallet to check if I had enough cash or if I had to pay with card. Fortunately the hardware store was almost empty, so it wasn’t like I was clogging the line. There was just another man inside, inspecting this and that, and when he saw that I got stuck he cut in front of me.

I blinked then shrugged. His business (although, I admit, I had this thought about stopping time and maybe tripping him up or something. Petty, I know.)

“Hey, so,” the man began, talking to the shop owner. “Say, do you know if there are hill properties on sale around here?”

“New to town?” the owner asked.

The man fake-laughed. “You can tell, can’t you? Yes, I am surveying properties. Maybe I’ll even buy one one day. I heard there are some vacant lots up the hills, places with a good view?”

The shop owner shrugged. “Not a real estate agent. If you want, I can give you a number of one, though.”

“Ah, that would be nice, yes.”

Hmmm. There was something about the man…

He continued. “What about the hills over the sea? There was a place, owned by a certain Hale guy?”

Hale? I paused, immediately suspicious. But then my phone rang and distracted me. It was Kimberly, that weirdo mad-scientist we almost arrested a while back. He told me Vera contacted him and asked for details about when he could come and ‘overhaul’ our cameras. His words.

“You sure you won’t, you know, transform my whole house or something?” I asked him.

“Nah, nah,” he said, sounding almost dismissive. “Vera was very precise when she explained it to me. I think she thinks I think somewhat weird and that I take things too literally, but that was never me. That was the other guy. I digress. She made it a point that I should not overhaul your house accidentally. Just the cameras. Although, if you want a bigger overhaul, we can discuss it…”

On and on he went, and by the time I was done, I had the cash in hand and no memory of the strange ‘surveyor’ who wasn’t really who he claimed to be. Not until it was too late, the man was gone, and I was driving the truck back up our hill.

“Fuck,” I cursed. Nadia asked what it was about, and I told her.

“We need those cameras asap,” she said. “Good thing Kimberly’s coming tomorrow. I’ll call Vera. We need to talk to her too.”
