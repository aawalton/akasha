Ferris and Jade returned to Lily House for lunch, plus Jade’s second feeding of straw. Ferris didn’t usually eat the mid-day meal at the house, so he would need to think of a plan to feed Jade the extra food on days he worked or wasn’t able to return. Perhaps he could try just two feedings, or have the second and the third all in the evening. If all else failed, he would spend the extra time walking back to Lily House for lunch. This would be easier with soul storage, but by the time Ferris had access to that, he wanted Jade to be eating monsters from rifts.

After eating, Ferris asked Mother Lily for something he could feed Jade as a snack later. The woman graciously gave him a bag of nuts.

Then, it was off to meet Serena for another research project meeting. On the way to the training center, Ferris started to notice a difference in his body. He’d always considered himself to be in good physical shape; he grew up training to be a delver after all.

But now, the simple act of walking felt easier. Each step he took with his crutch felt lighter than before. It was a subtle difference, but definitely noticeable. The only thing Ferris could think of was that this was a result of purging the impurities. If this was the difference after removing four of the rocks, what would he feel like after hundreds?

As was becoming her usual, Serena had arrived early and was waiting for him.

“Greetings, Serena,” Ferris said as he entered the room. The same table and cot were all present, plus the dagger in her hand.

“Greetings, Ferris, and you as well, Jade,” Serena replied with a wave, “I trust that the experience last time has given you more ideas to test?”

“Yes,” he said, “this hole that appears when Jade is stabbed… I need to understand how it works. I expect we will need to test many different ways the slime can be hurt, eventually chopping off entire sections of the slime.” Ferris grimaced.

Serena pulled out a piece of paper, and they started creating a list of experiments to run. It mostly involved feeding and different kinds of cutting or stabbing, but also specified various locations on Jade’s body. Ferris was secretly hoping to find a pattern in the slimespace.

They wouldn’t be able to try every side, as Ferris needed to be touching Jade, but they could still try a lot. Ferris was also cautious, as he didn’t want to risk hurting the Core Tree—or even worse, the Heartfire—but also didn’t want to directly explain why. He was relieved he had at least removed the two ability seeds.

Coordination for such a large list of tests was complicated by the fact that they needed to communicate through Jade. The slime could mentally speak with Ferris, but not Serena. In fact, Jade couldn’t even see or hear Serena when Ferris was meditating and not sharing his senses over the bond.

Last time Jade used some basic motions to signal Serena, but this time they extended it a bit. Instead of just ‘food’ or ‘cut,’ they made new signals Jade could relay, like repeating a test, skipping the next one, or moving the cuts to a new location. Ferris had to memorize the list of experiments beforehand, but they kept it organized and small enough for him. Meditating to enter the soul could take anywhere from ten to thirty minutes—at least at Ferris’ skill level—so they didn’t want to do it too many times.

This time, both Ferris and Serena had brought food to feed Jade. He had the bag of nuts from Mother Lily, while Serena brought more of the carrots from last time. Jade was overjoyed, although they rated the carrot higher.

Ferris unwrapped the magical chain and tied it to the table for safety—Jade’s most of all—and entered meditation. The last time he had done this in a room with Serena, he had been knocked unconscious by the death of his mental body. It was embarrassing to think about how he had been so helpless, but at least he knew he could trust her not to abandon him.

Once Ferris awoke in his soul, he quickly grabbed his bone spear and entered the slimespace. A quick look around ensured that everything was normal. Happily, the Digest River was still at a higher watermark than average from lunch. Many of the clams already had qi chunks displayed, although nowhere near all of them yet.

After finding a good vantage point atop the Heartfire tower, Ferris asked Jade to signal for the first test. Like last time, they would start with only testing food.

The grey birds flocked above the Consume Pool, next to the waterfall of acid that fed the pool. An enlarged slice of carrot fell into the pool, but Ferris had been watching the walls. He had seen where a section of the dome grew dim in a large circle, off to his left.

Instead of immediately moving towards the wall, Ferris asked Jade to signal for the next test: more food, but given to the slime in a different location. He watched closely for the result. Soon, he saw another part of the dome briefly dim, off to the right of the first. That at least confirmed that different locations in real life translated to different places on the dome, although not how.

Eighteen more tests of food came and went, and Ferris discovered a surprising pattern. Serena had been asked to feed Jade from as many different locations as she could reach, yet every place the dome discolored was slightly above the ground in the slimespace. Different horizontal locations, but the same vertical height. He knew some of these pieces of food were dropped from straight above Jade, but nothing happened at the top of the dome. The light of the dome dimmed only at ground level.

This was a reminder for him that, while the world inside the soul reflected reality, it was not a direct representation. As Uncle Winton had once told him, “The connection is symbolic and metaphorical rather than literal, except when it isn’t.” Ferris had felt the statement wasn’t helpful at the time, but that might have been the point.

The dome represented Jade’s body, but it wasn’t bound to the same rules. Hurting Jade damaged the dome, but apparently not in the same place—unless the tests with cuts rather than food gave different results.

What governs all this, and decides how the real world relates to the spiritual? Ferris wondered. I’ve been told everyone has differences in how things work in the soul, but enough is shared to imply some rules or link… He had no answer, and if anyone knew, they hadn’t told him.

This changed some of the plans he had been starting to make and raised new questions. At some point, he needed to see if there was a repeatable pattern to how real-world injuries mapped to the dome, or if it was random. For now, the only thing to do was continue onto the next set of experiments: different types of injuries.

The next set of tests would be food, followed by a small cut in the same location.

He had asked that all cuts be preceded by a piece of food. Originally, he had wanted to make sure they wouldn’t dump acid directly above the Core Tree or Heartfire, but if the cuts all appeared near ground level, it wouldn’t matter. He couldn’t change the plan now, and Ferris figured it was better to be more cautious. Additionally, Jade would love it.

Last time, Ferris had died because he got too close, watching how the wall of the dome reacted to Jade eating food. Then, the cut Serena had made happened in the same location as the food, which triggered a wave of acid to fall right on top of Ferris.

Ferris asked Jade to signal for food, and then took about five minutes approaching the wall of the dome around where he spotted the discoloration.

To be sure of the location, he had Jade signal for a repeat of food. The pattern of events was the same as last time: the wall’s luminescent glow dimmed, the grey birds circled above the discoloration, and then he heard the giant piece of carrot fall into the Consume Pool off in the distance as the wall returned to its normal glow and the birds dispersed.

Location confirmed, Ferris stood far back from the wall as he hesitantly asked Jade to signal for the next test: a small cut.

A large gash appeared in the wall, and a wave of acid surged in. No representation of the blade appeared; a hole just magically cut itself through the dome.

Almost immediately, dozens of nearby grey birds circled above the hole—staying well clear of the acid itself as it poured in. Somehow, the stream of acid flooding into the dome seemed to lessen, like something was stopping the liquid. Ferris’ eyes narrowed as he watched the grey birds, more and more of them arriving. Serena had shown him that slimes would initially bleed acid, but then the flow would stop—at least until too much damage caused the slime to shrink. Could the birds be involved in that?

Once the flood of acid was reduced to more of a large trickle, Ferris had his next surprise. The grey ants swarmed up out of every nearby yellow pillar, all rushing up to the hole. A stream of them ran up behind him, giving him a fright before he moved out of their path.

While he was now used to seeing the ants around the slimespace, this took it to another level. They swarmed in more numbers than he had ever seen, covering the ground and climbing on top of each other as they raced towards the hole. Still more kept coming out of the yellow pillars.

The ants deftly climbed up the side of the wall and surrounded the hole. At the edge, they would fearlessly dive into the wall of liquid, focused on doing something obscured inside the acid.

As if by magic, the hole in the dome started to shrink. Ferris couldn’t see the exact process due to the layers of ants climbing on top of each other, but clearly, the edges of the hole were growing out to meet each other.

Soon, the gash was gone completely. The birds dispersed, and the ants retreated back into their pillars underground. At least, what was left of the ants… many had died throwing themselves into the acid. There had been a couple of partially dissolved ants, but their comrades had taken them away underground.

Ferris took stock of what he had learned: the birds could be related to stopping the flow of acid, and the ants somehow repaired the dome’s wall.

The repaired wall looked exactly the same as before, luminescent glow and all. He cautiously approached where the hole had been, but he couldn’t tell any difference between old and new.

Ferris took a trip over to where he had dug into the dome to plant—and then uproot—the Grow seed. The same hole still existed in the wall; it hadn’t been repaired at all. Something was different between when Ferris dug it out himself and the cut appearing from an injury. The ants showed interest in the small hole Ferris had dug, but they left it alone after a short inspection each time one passed by.

Returning to the location Serena’s cut appeared in the wall, Ferris asked Jade to signal for the next test. He felt a bit bad for Serena, who had to wait so long without any warning, but it was the best system they had. Jade was as excited as ever, as they knew signaling meant food.

Each cut was preceded by a piece of food, and Ferris watched as Serena worked her way through their list of experiments. The first five cuts were in the same location and identical to each other: a small stab to the depth of a fingernail. Ferris wanted to confirm that consistent placement and size of cut in the real world translated to the slimespace, which they did.

Each time, the birds converged above the cut as the flow went from a pressurized burst down to a contained trickle. The ants would swarm in mass out of the ground, covering everything until they repaired the hole.

Next on the list were five long, shallow slices. Serena would cut through the same location, but she started and ended the wounds further out. Ferris stood far back at first, but approached to see more once he felt it was safe. In the slimespace, the holes in the wall were much longer, and stretched off to the left and right of the first holes. Interestingly, all the longer slices stretched horizontally, never vertically. He hadn’t discussed with Serena the direction of the slice, only that she would be consistent. He would need to add long vertical slices to the test list next time.

Then, Serena tested slowly inserting the dagger shallowly into Jade, and then held it there until Jade signaled to go deeper or remove it. For this test, Ferris was interested in how the Consume ability worked for attacks. Was there any real difference between being stabbed and eating the dagger? From what Ferris could see, a slow ‘attack’ didn’t open a hole in the dome. Instead, it made a discoloration just like the food.

Interestingly, once the dagger was deep enough, its tip appeared in midair above the Consume Pool, with grey birds flying around it. It was as massive as everything else from the real world. Ferris knew that the weapon was immune to Jade’s acid, but most of her cuts were finished before it could even fall down into the Consume Pool.

Ferris spent a few minutes asking Jade to signal Serena to repeat the stab. More of the dagger would magically appear as it was falling. When pulled out, the dagger would shrink until the entire thing disappeared. It would reappear high above the Consume Pool for the next cut.

After that was a series of progressively deeper quick stabs. Serena would stab five times at each depth, continuing deeper until Ferris called it off with the skip signal. Ferris stayed far back until he saw at least one stab at each new depth, then tried to watch from as close as he could.

The holes in the wall got progressively larger with each new depth. It appeared that stabbing deeper translated to a larger hole in all directions. The wave of acid that flooded in grew as well, and soon Ferris had to stay far back to avoid getting caught up in it. The birds just stayed in circling formation as the dagger appeared and disappeared above the Consume Pool with each cut.

The army of ants was legion, forever sending more and more of their numbers up to repair the wall with their lives. This reminded him of the experiment Serena had told him about. The slime that stopped healing. She had found two points where the slime’s healing changed. The first was after a minute, the slime had shrunk to about half the size, and had become unresponsive. Then, she had continuously injured it for over an hour, making new cuts as fast as she could. Eventually, the thing had just stopped being able to heal at all, just existing as a slimy core. She had given the slime a day to recover and then some food, after which it returned to normal.

Ferris watched the ants repairing the walls, dying as they did. He wasn’t sure about the first change, but maybe the second happened when there were no more ants left to repair the wall?

If the ants were all dead, then that would explain why the slime had stopped healing. So, it was likely there was a limit, just not a small one. Added to that, if the slime started healing again the next day, then there must be some way for more ants to appear.

If they were spirit animals, then at least he had a clue. Adept Nyoka had said they could use qi to have larger populations. He still felt her details—sparse as they were—didn’t match up to what Ferris was seeing. The ants had never collected qi from the Digest River, even when it was sitting full for days. She’d also implied Jade’s spirit animals would appear as slimes.

Ferris didn’t know what was required to spawn more ants, but he doubted it would be free. That wasn’t how cultivation worked, although the cost might not be qi. At first, almost everything was powered with qi, because a new Adept didn’t have anything else. He’d heard there were advanced techniques and magical resources from the rifts that could perpetually provide other sources of energy.

Ferris had seen the ants jump into the Consume Pool, but they didn’t take anything out of it, so he doubted they needed the food to reproduce. He still felt it was more likely they were making the acid more powerful by tearing apart what fell into the pool. He would probably have to explore underground to learn more about where the ants came from, and what resources they required.

Watching the grey ants repair the wall again and again, Ferris felt that all these creatures in the slimespace all had a purpose. The clams digested food. The ants broke down food and healed Jade. He suspected the birds were involved in holding the slime’s body inside if the outer shell was damaged. Almost from the beginning, Ferris had thought they were components of Jade’s abilities, but this felt like something… more basic.

It was as if they were more connected to simple life support than magical abilities.

In fact, the birds appeared when Jade ate as well. Thinking back to Jade’s real body, after the food was stuck through the outer shell, it would get pulled into a central location to be digested. Perhaps the birds were related to Jade’s control of the acid inside their body…

The body of a slime was much different than a human or animal. Jade didn’t have muscles or a stomach, but still moved and ate. If this were accomplished through magic rather than mundane physical means, then why wouldn’t it have a representation in the soul? In fact, it would be impossible not to, as far as Ferris knew.

The only creature that hadn’t seemed to have a purpose was the fish that killed Jade. Ferris suspected it was different than the rest; maybe one of Jade’s personal demons. If so, he was sure he would see more.

Ferris and Serena had discussed more and more drastic injuries that they could test, but he decided to call the rest off. These progressively deeper stabs were flooding more and more acid into the area, and Ferris wasn’t able to stand close enough to see anything meaningful. The new idea that the ants were a limited resource that cost resources to replace made him hesitant to waste them.

He was also worried about triggering whatever happened when a slime took enough damage to shrink in size. Ferris suspected the entire dome might shrink, or even flood with greater waves of acid. He didn’t want to lose another mental body by being taken by surprise.

Ferris asked Jade to give the signal to skip the rest of the tests, and then woke up.

“How did it go?” Serena asked as she helped him up from the floor onto the chair. “Can’t have been worse than last time, as you are up and about.”

“Yes,” Ferris replied, “definitely better than last time. I learned a lot, but now I have a ton of new theories, and even more questions than before.”

Serena chuckled, although there was a hunger in her gaze. She wanted more details, but was waiting to ask. Ferris could understand her desire: even a casual comment could be the key to healing her hand.

He did want to help her… but many of the details in the slimespace confused him. He really didn’t know what he needed to keep secret and what he could tell her. He knew from the beginning that he would need to share personal details related to the research project, but he hadn’t expected everything to be so confusingly connected.

Ferris checked on Jade while he considered his words. Thankfully, the slime appeared the same as normal. He hadn’t been willing to test worse damage… but he would need to eventually. He wanted to plan and prepare more first.

“I mentioned the wall, last time,” Ferris began, speaking slowly. “After Jade is injured, a hole appears in this wall in the soul. Something happens at the edges, but I am unable to see it clearly due to the acid, and then the hole shrinks until it is gone.”

Serena listened silently. She likely knew he was leaving a lot out, but he hoped she also knew that pressing for more wouldn’t work.

“Then,” Serena said, with a glint in her eye, “the logical next step would be to observe the process closer. Acid Resistance would let you see what happens from right next to it.”

Ferris nodded his head, trying not to look too happy. Serena wasn’t dumb, and she likely saw immediately that he revealed specific information that directed the research towards helping him. She knew, and probably didn’t care. It was a clear way forward, so she would take it.

“I will focus on purchasing the technical manual for the ability,” Ferris said, doing the math in his head. He had been down to three merit points after dealing with the chain demons, and had worked three days since. That would have put him up to six… if he hadn’t spent one merit point on extra food for Jade. Now he would need to work seven days in a row to get to the twelve merit points necessary. “I request that we skip our two meetings next week. If nothing goes wrong, we can start on the ability the week after.” That would give him ten days to prepare.

“Agreed,” Serena said, and stood to leave.

After saying goodbye, Ferris turned to collect his things.

Jade happily disposed of the leftover food. Ferris couldn’t help but watch with renewed interest as each piece moved around with the slime’s body.

His theories on how the mechanics of Jade’s body were inherently magical kept running through his mind. Most monsters had a more common biology, but not slimes. There was no brain, no heart, no organs; nothing that was typically vital to life. He’d even praised this as a strength, happy that Jade wouldn’t have some of the typical weaknesses of a familiar.

But he kept asking himself, why were slimes different?
