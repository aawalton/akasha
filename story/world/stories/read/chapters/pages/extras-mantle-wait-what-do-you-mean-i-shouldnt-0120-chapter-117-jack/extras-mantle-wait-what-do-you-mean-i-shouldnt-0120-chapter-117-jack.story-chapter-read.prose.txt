Jack ran.

Not the controlled… or the measured retreat of a soldier who knew how to run while keeping his body in peak condition—he'd done plenty of that in his years with the garrison—and this wasn't it.

This was the full-tilt, lungs-screaming, legs-burning sprint of a man carrying someone he couldn't afford to drop while death loomed at every corner.

As if that wasn’t enough, Elenor weighed more than she looked.

Jack had carried wounded before—hell, he was the one who dragged Sir Winters out of the collapsed halls, he had hauled Barsta's unconscious bulk across fifty meters of rubble during the early days of the bastion reclamation—but Elenor Valnar was heavier than either of them had been.

He didn't know if it was the armor—or what was left of it, which wasn't much—or whether Overmortal bodies were simply denser than the regular kind, or he was far too tired of the bullshit situations he found himself in one after another.

Either way, the woman was draped over his right shoulder like a sack of wet cement, and the only sign she was alive at all was the faint, ragged wheeze of her breathing against the side of his neck.

She hadn't twitched since Sir Winters had handed her off.

Jin Winters.

Jack's mind circled back to the young man even as he ran—he was sure Jin wasn’t even twenty years old, but the young man had stood between two Overmortals without a single shred of fear—and one of those cultists was half a step away from lordhood.

Form fucking lordhood!

Jack couldn’t even bring himself to imagine going against one. Before the veil and the cult attacked anyone in Vienna, they would have given an absurd amount just to get glimpses of “Lords.”

There were only two in the damn city with over 100,000 population.

Only two! And only a couple of hundred Overmortals.

And now it’s like overmortals and lord rankers were growing in trees. How in hell did the cult manage to cultivate so many high-ranked individuals?

If cults were always this strong, then why did the churches and the priests dismiss them as hillbillies?

“Fuck those priests! And their damn churches!” Jack cursed loudly as he ran. “All they want is to praise their gods and give time tithes.”

“And yet they are nowhere to be seen the moment the veil dropped. Fucking scoundrels!”

The corridor ahead split into a T-junction—one of the dozens of identical intersections that made the bastion's lower levels a labyrinth even for people who'd been navigating them for weeks—, and he hooked left without thinking. Muscle memory. This route would take him past the cold storage annexes and loop back toward Veric's within four or five minutes if he kept pace.

If.

Big emphasis on that.

Behind him—maybe thirty meters back, maybe less—a rune trap he'd carved into the wall during his initial run triggered.

This was the third one, and by now Jack was sure someone was pursuing him. It wasn’t Sir Winters then, who?…

That woman!

Jack recalled the hatred he had felt from the women towards Elenor.

Fuck! How did she get past Sir Winters… no focus, even if she did, I’m sure she would be heavily wounded.

“No time for caution.” Jack breathed deep and pulled on all the runic traps he had been laying down.

BOOM!

A flash of orange light and searing heat assaulted his back, but Jack kept his pace, putting down more and more traps.

He chanced a look back, and through the dust and the fading glow, he saw her.

A figure, bloodied, burned, and missing her right arm entirely, running through the aftermath of his trap with the mindless determination of a woman who had stopped caring about what happened to her own body a long time ago.

He pulled rapidly on each of the freshly laid traps, and flames engulfed the corridor.

“That should slow—” Jack paused as he watched the cultist jump straight through the flames to continue her mindless pursuit, not even registering that the fire was eating at the charred remnants of her leather armor and the exposed skin beneath.

Their gazes met for one terrible second.

"She killed him!" the woman screamed, and Jack felt a chill crawl up his spine.

He'd known people like this before. Not cultists—just people—men and women who'd lost the thing that tethered them to reason, and what was left was a body moving on autopilot toward the last objective their broken minds could hold on to.

They were the most dangerous kind of enemy, because they couldn't be deterred. Couldn't be reasoned with. Couldn't be scared.

And she's faster and stronger than I am!

Jack detonated the remaining two rune traps simultaneously. The corridor behind him lit up in a double blast of fire and a concussive force, but even as the flames roared, he could hear her coming through them.

Damn it. Damn it all to hell.

His essence flared. He slammed his free palm against the corridor wall without breaking stride, and the stone obeyed—groaning, shifting, bulging outward—as he forced the material into a new shape.

A wall of stone erupted from floor to ceiling behind him, sealing the corridor in a rough barrier that was maybe a foot thick and reinforced with as much essence as he could push into the structure while sprinting with Elenor on his shoulder.

It wouldn't hold her long. He knew that.

But even two seconds was two seconds.

CRACK.

One second.

CRASH.

The wall exploded, and through it all, a whip of green-black essence tore through the barrier's remnants and lashed against the area—he was… had he not jumped—with a hiss that left smoking, acid-eaten furrows in the rock.

The venom ate into stone like water into sugar, leaving behind a discoloration that reeked of decaying, rotten flesh.

Jack didn't look back again; he shifted Elenor higher on his shoulder and threw up another wall behind him.

Thicker this time, nearly two feet.

"GIVE HER TO ME!"

The woman’s voice came through the stone, muffled and distorted but carrying every ounce of unhinged fury.

He heard the wall crack.

Come on. Come on. Veric and others should be close.

The third wall lasted almost three full seconds, and the fourth didn't even finish forming before the whip punched through the half-solidified stone, and the tip of it caught Jack's left calf.

“Argh!” He grunted and staggered. Jack conjured a healthy chunk of essence in his hands and tore the whip’s grip on his calf.

The damn whip would have severed his leg had he not ripped it apart. Blood flowed from the injury, showing his bones.

The area around the injury started to rapidly lose color, and darkened veins crawled up.

“Fuck…” The pain and the sudden numbness from the venom made him stumble. For one terrible, suspended moment, he was going to fall…

Twisting his waist, he managed to turn and fall on his back instead of banging Elenor's head onto the stone floor and off her by his own foolishness.

Pain bloomed across his back. Stars burst behind his eyes.

He blinked the stars away and managed to look up.

The woman stood almost over him.

If the cultist had been frightening before, she was a horror now. Her right arm was gone entirely; the stump was a cauterized mess of frozen crystals that crept closer to her neck and covered her chest. Burns covered the left side of her face and neck where his rune traps had caught her, the skin blistered and peeling, and the leather armor that had once covered her torso hung in charred strips that exposed flesh raw beneath.

Blood ran freely from half a dozen wounds.

And her eyes. Those eyes hadn't changed. Wild, bloodshot, locked onto Elenor's unconscious body.

"YOU WILL DIE! ALONG WITH HER YOU WILL DIE!" she screamed, and she stepped on Jack's left foot—deliberately, grinding her heel down onto the boot with enough force that he felt bone grind—and the pain from his venom-burned calf lanced up through his entire leg in a white-hot spike. "YOU ALL WILL DIE!"

Jack bit down on the scream. Swallowed it. His free hand was already moving—reaching for any spell, any skill, anything—when green light flashed in his vision.

He blinked the flashes away just in time to see a lance of light green essence shriek through the corridor and bury itself in the woman’s left side.

The impact of the attack hit the woman with the force of a battering ram, lifting her off her feet and launching her backward. She flew three meters before she hit the ground, and the lance was still embedded, its green essence flaring bright where it met her flesh.

Jack stared, still dazed from the fall and the pain.

But he knew that spear. Or rather, who wielded such powers.

Of all the people in the bastion—

Jack craned his neck backward, looking up the corridor toward the junction he'd been running for.

Gleeward stood ten meters behind him.

The man looked absolutely terrible, which by Gleeward's standards was still a marginal improvement over his usual presentation. His short, messy green hair was plastered to his forehead with sweat. His garrison armor was scuffed and dented from what had clearly been a rough series of engagements. There was dried blood on his knuckles, a bruise the size of a fist darkening his left cheekbone, and a half-finished cigarette dangling from his lower lip.

A cig? That’s not right?… Oh, fuck it. I don’t care.

Jack let out a laugh and pushed himself up onto his elbows.

"Of all the people who could be here…," he managed, wincing as his burned calf protested, "fate sent you?"

"The absolute screaming fuck is happening down here?" Gleeward asked. He took one last drag off the cigarette, made a face that suggested the tobacco had personally insulted his mother, and flicked it to the stone floor where he ground it out under his boot. "Are you kidnapping people now, Jack? Is that how it is? Finally gone full apeshit on us? Because I've been saying for weeks you had shifty eyes, and nobody—"

"Cultist!" Jack barked, pulling himself upright with a grunt. His calf was on fire, and the venom was spreading a numb tingle up toward his knee. "She's a damn Overmortal, you idiot! Sir Winters was fighting her and a peak Overmortal, and for the sake of every bottle of piss-water you call ale, Gleeward—we need to get Elenor to Maya. Now. So for once in your miserable life, be serious."

"Yeah, I can see the venom, Jack. I've got eyes." Gleeward stepped forward, reaching down to haul Jack to his feet with one rough pull that sent a jolt of pain through his injured leg. "Veric told me these things'd calm my nerves," he added, jerking his chin toward the crushed cigarette. "Lying bastard. I'm more tense than a virgin at a—" He stopped, coughed, waved smoke away from his face. "Never mind. Get going. I'll handle the one-armed screaming banshee."

"She's dangerous, and you don't even have your—"

"I know where my bloody spear is, Jack."

Gleeward extended his right hand toward the corridor behind them. His fingers twitched—a small, sharp motion—and the spear ripped itself free from the woman’s shoulder with a wet, sucking sound and a spray of dark blood. It crossed the distance in a blur and slapped into Gleeward's palm.

Down the corridor, the women were already getting up. One arm gone. A hole in her shoulder was pumping blood. Burns across her face and body. And she was standing, her remaining hand's nails already elongating into those whip-like tendrils that dripped with green venom, her eyes still locked—past Gleeward, past everything—on Elenor.

"YOU DARE!" she hissed.

"Gleeward," Jack started, shifting Elenor's weight on his shoulder with a wince, "she's—"

"Listen to me, you daft twit." Gleeward's voice changed. "You either stand here flapping your jaw and let Elenor die, or you get your battered arse moving. Pick one. I don't have the time, the patience, or the sodding interest in debating it."

Jack looked at him.

Gleeward's eyes were different. Jack couldn't put his finger on it, but the man who stood in front of him wasn't the hungover, foul-mouthed garrison guard who complained about his alcohol supply and scowled at anyone above the rank of corporal.

Jack had always known Gleeward was good with a spear. Veric had said as much. The man himself would never admit it, but the talent was there, buried under a mountain of cheap liquor and self-deprecation.

But this wasn't "good."

This was a man who had finally stopped pretending.

"Go," Gleeward said.

Jack gritted his teeth. He looked at Gleeward one last time and nodded.

"Don't die," he said.

"Don't tell me what to do," Gleeward answered, and the grin was the last thing Jack saw before he turned and ran.

He rounded the corner with Elenor over his shoulder, his burned leg screaming, his back aching, his essence reserves running dry, but his feet were moving, and the woman was breathing, and somewhere behind him, a man Jack had written off as a talented waste was setting his feet against a monster.

He's going to win, Jack thought, and he didn't know where the certainty came from—whether it was instinct or faith or just the desperate need to believe in something while he ran—but it settled into his chest with the quiet weight of fact.

Not because Gleeward was stronger. Not because the odds were in his favor.

Because the foulness had found its way back into the man's mouth.

He would take that as fate’s approval.
