All beings are creatures of energy. All of them. Systems all over the multiverse have altered the natural evolution of humanity, dragonkin, and… all other species out there, but there are secrets locked inside your biology.

Things from the deep past.

The System in your corner of the multiverse is responsible for Earth’s lack of energy—this is something you have already discovered, when you spoke to your Ancestral Soul. Before the System was ever created, Celestial Energy was far more prevalent throughout the entire universe.

Even in universes like yours, around Earth it was still there, it still existed, and it was still detectable. This is the same for where dragonkin evolved. Spiritual senses are powerful, but like all senses they can be blocked, dulled, blinded.

All the senses work best when they work together.

But this isn’t the truest explanation—the truest explanation is that every single sense a being possess is, in a way, the same. It is simply a way that that being’s brain interprets reality. Each sense is merely an attempt of deciphering reality, and what those senses show you is merely a simulation inside your brain.

No being can perceive reality as it actually is. All we can do is detect reality. It is unclear if different species, even different members of the same species, depict reality in the same ways in their minds—even after trillions of years, this isn’t a question Derrida has been able to sufficiently answer.

But the fact that each sense merely detects reality means it can be used to detect everything, if that sense is strengthened and expanded in the correct ways, and as each sense is unique it can detect energy in unique ways—each way is helpful to gaining the whole picture of what energy is.

That is what you must do to turn your sense of sight, smell, hearing, taste, and touch into an energy detector. You must expand each of them. Though it will be difficult to gain this ability, it is more than possible—all your senses were once intended to detect energy, not merely your spiritual sense.

All you must do is tap into that deep, instinctual knowledge.

You have done enough work on strengthening your natural sight, and you were wise to strengthen your natural sight while still having access to your other senses. Now, however, it is time you block every one of your other senses and work on your sight in isolation.

Here is the mechanism you will need to block your other senses:

Something entered his mind.

It was almost like a spell pattern in the way, except… no, this was more like a thought-pattern. A set of established steps that showed him exactly how to block his four other natural senses so only sight would remain.

He hadn’t known the Personal System had been able to give him such things.

He blinked. The Dark Tunnel was silent but for his breathing. Whatever Void Dungeon-created beast lay ahead of him hadn’t made itself known yet. He was alone.

For now…

Reading the description made things slot into place. The senses were just a way to detect reality… he knew that. He’d read about it before the System. He supposed humans on Earth had gained a thorough understanding of that concept, even with the limited senses they had available to them. It had always felt like a very philosophical part of science. He’d never really known what to do with the information, and he hadn’t given it all that much thought. Now, it made him wonder if he saw reality in the same ways as those closest to him… but he didn’t think on that long.

Instead, he thought about how beings must have seen the world back then, before the System, when energy had been abundant everywhere. The System in his universe sucked the energy out of basically everywhere not under its domain as it expanded—that was how it expanded—meaning the energy around his planet had always been scarce until it was integrated.

But before there had ever been a System, would humans simply look around them and see the energy flowing through the air, long some sort of aurora borealis? Could they close their eyes and smell when a spell had been cast? Could they feel the touch of energy on their skin as it entered through their pores and made their way into their energy channels? Could they hear it, like a song in the wind?

Could they taste it on the tip of their tongue, when they ate energy-enriched foods?

Was that what he heard, when he advanced—the song of the universe, the rune song—was that pure energy he was hearing? While he advanced, sometimes he’d been able to sense things he hadn’t been able to sense normally. Perhaps it allowed him to tap into another way of being…

Xavier sighed. A lot of new information was entering his mind since he’d come to this place. It felt good, but it also felt like a heavy weight—or maybe that was just the intense gravity. Though he supposed he was adjusting to the feel of the gravity by now.

He’d thought he’d known exactly what path he needed to take, and here he was finding out there was far more to know than he’d imagined…

And that’s a good thing, he reminded himself. He was excited for this new chapter of his training, even if it wasn’t exactly how he imagined it. Then again, he hardly thought his time in this Void Dungeon was the only thing Derrida had in mind.

Something tells me she’ll pit me against other Wanderers or some interdimensional beings at some point. Maybe there’s an arena out here, somewhere in the multiverse. A Void Arena… well, that prospect sounds terrifying.

And more than a little exciting…

He released a long breath, once again contemplating whatever beast was watching him. But it hadn’t attacked him yet. He’d been standing there for a long time now as he’d strengthened his natural sight, and nothing had come to attack him. He’d felt blind when he’d switched off his Immortal Soul lens and his spiritual sense, but now that he’d gained the thought pattern from the Personal System and was about to shut off every sense but sight…

Well, this felt far more dangerous. What if something teleported behind him, and he couldn’t hear it or sense it in any way? It wasn’t as though he could see through the back of his head. Not without a spell, anyway.

And The All-Seeing Eye doesn’t use my natural sight, so it’s not like I can use that while I do this.

He could push through the dungeon, clear every beast from this part of it, from the entire first level if he had to. That might give him a sense of safety.

But he didn’t think that would be necessary.

Instead, he simply walked back through the Dark Tunnel to where he’d taken out the first five Gorgotlin. Nothing should attack him there. It was practically a safe zone now.

He sat, cross-legged on the ground, facing down the Dark Tunnel, taking a few deep breaths, drinking in the feeling of… everything.

Then, he followed through with the thought pattern.

The thought pattern showed him exactly how to shut off different parts of his brain. The parts that processed hearing, smell, touch, and taste. The first thing he shut off was taste. He figured it would be the easiest one to adjust to. The moment he did, everything felt off. He hadn’t eaten anything for a long while, and yet suddenly he realised he’d never contemplated the taste of his own mouth. Now that it was gone, he missed it.

What an odd feeling.

His tongue flicked out. He licked his lips. He could feel the wet sensation of his saliva making them slick, but he couldn’t taste anything.

Weird, but not too disorientating.

Next was his sense of smell. This wasn’t such a bad one, considering this part of the tunnel still smelled rather deeply of the putrid stench of the Gorgotlins’ blood. But it was still strange breathing deeply through his nose and having nothing lighting up that sense. There was so much more than the Gorgotlins’ blood that he must have been smelling. Perhaps he’d been smelling whatever other beasts lay farther down through the tunnel. Maybe he smelled the fabric his clothes were made from. Maybe he smelled the hard stone floor. He’d never focused on his sense of smell enough to differentiate a great deal of it, despite how powerful it was.

I guess that’s something I’ll be doing soon.

The next sense he shut off was hearing.

This was a big one, perhaps the most disorientating yet. The world didn’t simply go quiet, it went silent. Like it never had before. Even when he was inside a mental space he could still hear. A part of his mind was always hearing something, no matter how aware his main consciousness was of that.

The Dark Tunnel had been silent.

At least, Xavier had thought it was silent until he realised what true silence “sounded” like. This wasn’t like when one put earplugs in or wore noise-cancelling headphones.

This was complete and utter deafness.

He couldn’t hear his heartbeat. He couldn’t hear his breath. He couldn’t hear the blood rushing around his body. He couldn’t hear the brush of fabric as he moved. And there was something else he couldn’t hear. Something that was missing that he hadn’t even been aware of. Something he couldn’t put his finger on.

Was I able to hear the sound of a beast, or several beasts, down the Dark Tunnel, and didn’t even realise it?

Xavier took some deep breaths—truly silent breaths, at least to him—and tried to acclimate to how this felt, being so… dulled to the world. But he knew what those breaths were doing.

They were merely him procrastinating, because the next thing he was about to shut off was touch. And something told him that would be more disorientating than anything else he’d done thus far. Back on Earth, he hadn’t heard of anyone ever losing their sense of touch. He couldn’t imagine what kind of life that would be, not to be able to feel anything at all.

Very soon he wouldn’t have to imagine it.

In that moment, many parts of his mind were telling him this was a bad idea. This wasn’t necessary. His Immortal Soul lens was powerful enough, and he could keep strengthening it. He didn’t need to walk down this path.

Some of those parts weren’t merely telling him, they were screaming at him.

He blocked them out, just as he’d blocked his other senses, then did the final step of the thought pattern the Personal System had given him—he shut off his sense of touch.

Everything became… muted. He suddenly felt more disconnected from his body than he ever had in his life, than he ever knew he could be. The feeling was incredibly disorientating, at least it would have been if he could orient himself at all.

He wondered if this was what spirits felt like. Ghosts. Is this how Rhaalir felt? Is this what the elf had gone through? What Volkarin had gone through? The Spirit of Time?

Xavier took a deep breath. His brain had trouble with that, as it felt as though he wasn’t breathing at all. It felt as though his entire body had seized up. Like his heart wasn’t beating. Like his chest wasn’t expanding. His lungs weren’t filling. Hell, he couldn’t feel his mouth opening.

Nothing.

If he couldn’t see, if he couldn’t think, if he hadn’t known how this had happened, he might have thought he was dead. Or maybe just a brain floating in a jar with nothing but eyes. Or perhaps his mind had been downloaded into a computer…

Xavier looked down. His chest was expanding. He brought his hand up—raising his arm, it was like his entire body was asleep—and brought it to his chest to feel his heart. But of course, that was an impossibility in his current state.

Still, at least he could see himself breathing. Angling his sight downward, he saw his mouth open. When he breathed through his nose, he could see it flare. He sat like that for a long time, just watching himself breathe. Then he turned his eyesight toward other parts of him. Toward his hand, moving his index finger.

It was so strange merely seeing his finger move but not feeling it move. What was stranger still was that as he watched, he almost felt as though he did feel it move. As though by simply seeing it his brain interpreted the movement as how it should feel. Brains were more than capable of adapting to a situation, but that felt very strange to him. He supposed what the Personal System had told him was true—that each sense was, in a way, the same.

Even if that was a hard pill to swallow.

He moved more of his body as he watched it. He trailed his vision down, tensing his muscles, seeing where his legs sat on the hard stone. He placed a finger on that stone. It should have been cold. Smooth. Solid. He felt nothing but yet he knew these things. Knew how it should have felt. It wasn’t the same.

He didn’t dare stand up. Besides, he didn’t really think he would need to.

This is weird, he thought, and he still heard his words inside his head.

Releasing a long, unfelt breath, he focused on his sight.

It was the only thing he had left.
