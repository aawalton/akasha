In the single, quick look I allowed myself around the corner, I saw them. That was all I allowed myself before I slipped back around the edge of my hiding spot.

My prey…and their captive.

If I had lingered in the open for even a second more, it was entirely possible I could have been spotted before I was ready. But the glimpse I received was more than enough to realize just what was happening. These were the Elves I was looking for, yes. The rough leather of their breeches and vests, combined with the snarling wolf-faced masks they bore, was sufficient for me to identify them. Even with the time since they had captured me, oh so long ago, I still recognized them. That was more than enough to get my blood running. But there was more to the situation than just them having a captive, a shadowed figure about Dwarf height lying in the shadows of a shoddily constructed cage.

There was another Dwarf down here with the Elves, entirely free from the bondage of the other. And it wasn’t exactly hard for me to figure out just who they were, either. They were literally wearing their allegiance around their shoulders.

A green and gold cloak. The colors…of House Savoy.

I took a deep, silent breath as I heard the Elves continue conversing around the corner from me. That wasn’t all, of course. The silent figure of the Dwarf had been lying on a cot, appearing to be catching a nap. But within reach of him had been a gnarled wooden staff, with a foul magenta crystal held within a basket at the apex of it.

The exact same kind that had been given to Olag, to protect him and his company from the Skyfall.

The situation down here was now more complicated than I had wanted it to be.

I had been expecting six Elves, from the pulses of my blood sense. Four was manageable, I suppose. But now I both felt bound to rescue their captive, who I was guessing might just be the missing gravemender…

And I now had to capture the Savoy Dwarf. It would be a massive waste of an opportunity not to. I’m guessing that this flunky, whoever he was, was both the saboteur's minder, as well as their method of escape. From what I’d been told, these staff-wielding Dwarves were capable of casting full protection spells against the corruption of the Skyfall, much the way that Alveron could. But with the catch of it coming from a foul crystal that looked like it came from the very frozen lightning above. The frozen mantle of Divinity of Fynneas himself.

Who knows what they could do in a fight? It was a tricky situation. I was honestly a bit tempted to back out right now and retreat. In the face of unexpected difficulties, it would make total sense for me to link back up with my subordinates and assault the room that appeared to be the gravemender's home. Large and sectioned off with multiple different walls, it almost looked like a stone apartment. The Elves were all gathered around a lit fireplace on one side of the wall, conversing in low tones while their minder snoozed away in one of the cots next to it. The cage was further into the living space, situated next to what looked to me like an extension of the staircase I had just left, leading further downwards into Vereden.

It would be logical to leave now. This was a hard place to assault all on my own. The confines were tight, and I would be outnumbered in a fight. I’m not sure I even had enough space here to transform to my full, monstrous height if I needed to. The ceiling was too low for that. But…

But.

I didn’t want to retreat. This was personal to me, deeply so.

I had to be the one to kill these Elves. If I had let this opportunity slip through my fingers, I would regret it for the rest of my life.

So.

I was just going to have to adjust, now wasn’t I?

I considered the matter from several mental angles for a few moments.

Should I ambush them from afar with my bow? I could probably take out at least one if I did, possibly two. I discarded that, though, when I realized I would probably wake the sleeping Dwarf if I did. I might end up killing him in the fight if and when he intervened. You never knew what could happen in combat.

Should I try and sneak up on them as they were, maybe slit some throats? No, I discarded that almost immediately. That was just as foolish.

I eventually decided…to wait.

The essence of stealth, as Hook had taught me back in the heyday of the Nocturne Division…was patience. Patience, and the judgment to act on an opportunity when it presented itself.

The situation would change, and I’d have my chance for revenge.

I just had to be patient.

I settled in for observation, almost meditating on my blood sense to keep track of their location.

……………………………………..

About an hour and a half of overwatch later, my chance came from an unexpected source.

From around the corner, on the other side of the gravemender's home, I heard a low groan of discomfort echo within it. The quiet Elven conversation stopped, and I heard the knife-ears shift. I believe one of them even stood up from the stone floor, judging from the sound of stone on stone I could hear, as what sounded like a pebble underfoot ground against the floor. When next I heard one of the saboteurs speak, there was a scornful, exasperated note to his voice. I heard him bark what seemed to be a series of orders to the others, and they also stood up with a series of grumbles.

That must be the leader.

I’d save him for last.

However, I nearly had a damned heart-attack when I felt one of the Elves get up and start walking towards the corner.

Where I was hiding.

I calmed myself, though. It’s not like this was the first time I had nearly been caught in the act, mid-stealth. And I had more tools at my disposal now than I had back during the bad old days.

I had enough in the tank for this, at least.

I re-attached Starfire Veiling to Thorn Cloak, and faded into invisibility just in time. The Elven raider crested the corner in the next second, striding around it like he owned the place. I remained absolutely still as the spear-wielding, wolf-masked Elf passed within feet of me. I didn’t breathe, I didn’t blink. I didn’t even move my eyes as he passed my position. It was only when he had moved further down the hall that I took a slight breath, and almost gagged when I did. He had left an almost palpable stink in the air where he had passed. The leather-clad Elf smelled like a wild animal, in a disgusting mirror of the beast his mask reflected.

No matter. The fool had separated himself from the group.

Easy prey.

I peeled away from the wall and slowly, ever so slowly, drew the dagger I kept at the small of my back. The Oninite blade whispered from the supple leather sheath that I still kept well-oiled, just in case. Stepping carefully, I crept back down the hallway I had done the same only an hour previous.

Straight towards the crouching form of the Elf. It looked like he might be inspecting one of the traps I had swung right over on the way here, one hand held over the inscribed runes and concentrating. As I grew silently closer to him, I saw the Elf pause and reach down closer to the ground and grab something.

When his hand came up, I could see the lines of confusion in his body when he produced a few tiny, chipped pieces of stone.

Residue from when my Skill had punctured the roof above.

Before the Elf could make the connection, I reached him. In a single smooth motion, I reached out and clamped down hard over his mouth with my free hand and stabbed upwards through the bottom of his jaw.

Straight into his brainpan. The Elf was dead before he even knew what had happened to him, all without making a single noise.

Wait, no. That wasn’t correct. A strange mix of a gurgle and a whine exited the deceased Elf, but it was muffled by the sound of my leather-clad, Primordium hand over his mouth. Carefully, I lowered the Elven meat down to the stone of the tunnel and completely disregarded him. He didn’t matter anymore, and I had to move fast.

This was on, now. I had no way of knowing just when the other Elves were expecting my victim back. I couldn’t have them coming to find his corpse.

Instead, I had to make them corpses now.

Well, for all of them but the Dwarves.

One down, four to go.

I hurried as quietly as I could back over to the corner I’d been peering over, and did it once again. I felt a little thrill run through me at what I’d found.

The three other Elves had all broken up to other tasks, while I’d been at mine. One of them seemed to be trying to feed and water the groaning, dazed gravemender they had taken captive, but in a deliberately inefficient, cruel manner. He was spooning slop from an iron pot that had been resting in the fireplace into the cage.

Not into a bowl, no. Instead, he was spooning it onto the black robes of the Dwarf. It must have been scalding hot, but the presumed gravemender didn’t react at all.

Next victim, then.

As for the other two Elves…

According to my blood sense, both of them were within one of the side rooms of the gravemender's home. That room was blocked off by a door, but I think I could just barely hear them speaking through it. But the important thing was…

They had left their comrade alone. How considerate of them.

For me.

One thing first, though. I crept into the chamber proper, the other two Elves not within my sight. I couldn’t even hear them, wherever they were. That didn’t matter, though.

Instead, I approached the thankfully still snoozing form of the Savoy Dwarf, grabbing a bottle of something special from my pouch as I did. I gave it a quick shake to activate it and removed the stopper…

And pouring it into the open, snoring mouth of the sleeping Dwarf.

He immediately tried to choke on it, his eyes flying open in a sudden panic from the impromptu drink. I’m sure the noise would have drawn the attention of the Elf just across the room from me…if I hadn’t clamped a hand down over his mouth. Partly to force the liquid down his throat, and partly to silence him.

I couldn't have you ruining my set-up here, could I, little slave-monger?

Our eyes met, and I swear I saw a spark of recognition in his eyes…

Before they fluttered closed, as the sleeping potion took effect. The snoring immediately resumed, only far deeper now. This guy wasn’t waking up for anything in the next…2? 3 Hours? Hard to tell. Potions could be unpredictable, sometimes.

Two down, three to go.

I craned my neck around to find that the Elf tormenting the gravemender hadn’t even noticed the interruption in snores. He was now trying to dribble the cooling stew into the open, groaning mouth of the Dwarf and laughing obnoxiously as he did.

Hmm.

How to handle this one? Oh, I know. It was a pretty short distance between us, and I had a clear sightline to the asshole.

Crouching, I reached over my right shoulder and withdrew the collapsed shortbow from its combination sheath/quiver combo. With a little shake, the Oninite bow fell open, the string snapping into place with a deliberately engineered, quiet twang. I drew one of the short arrows I’d fletched myself and slotted it against the string. Almost casually, I raised the bow and sighted it at the Elf, or more accurately…

The back of his neck. Such a shot was easy for me, with my level of weapon Skill. I held the bow completely still, lining up the shot…and loosed it on an exhale. Immediately, I knocked another arrow, but I needn’t have bothered.

One was enough.

The arrow might as well have traveled at light speed, from how quickly it crossed the distance. After all, the string itself was more heavily enchanted than the bow. I would know.

I’d done it.

The Elf choked on a sudden mouthful of blood, suddenly weak hands scrabbling for the barbed arrowhead, suddenly thrust through his neck. His hands fell, dropping both the ladle and the pot of stew to clatter loudly onto the stone below, followed shortly by his knees. He didn’t even have the chance to contemplate his death before his head banged onto the bars of the cage with a loud clang.

I blinked. That was…a bit louder than I had been expecting. Probably should have remembered the whole, you know. Ladle and pot thing.

Oh well.

Three down, two to go.

My head snapped over to my left when I heard the door off to the side creak open. I watched with bated breath as the head of one of the missing Elves poked through it, craning for the noise. I saw it when he sighted the corpse of his fellow, slumped against the bars of the ramshackle cage. Immediately snapping his masked face around, he traced the probable path of the shot with quick eyes, over to where I crouched over the body of the sleeping Dwarf. I don’t know what he saw, exactly. Thorn Cloak blurred my form quite a bit, even in direct light. Here in the low, fireplace-lit stone of the gravemenders home…it was probably pretty intimidating.

His eyes bulged in their sockets, visible behind his mask.

I loosed the arrow I held, aimed right at his head.
