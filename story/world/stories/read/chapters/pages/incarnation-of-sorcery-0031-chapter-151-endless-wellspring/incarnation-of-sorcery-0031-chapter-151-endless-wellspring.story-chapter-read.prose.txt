Ryland didn’t follow High Academ Zevo to the old temple at first. After all, [Omniscience] hadn’t revealed the location of the students. Since Ryland tended to be pretty thorough when it came to such searches, the fact that he couldn’t locate the students suggested they either weren’t present or were hidden with far stronger magic than he was anticipating.

Nevertheless, despite Ryland’s results, Zevo was not about to leave any stone unturned. To do so, he was personally leading a search party to the site of the temple to scour it for any sign of the missing students.

Ryland left him to it. While he didn’t discount the possibility that there might indeed be students there despite his searching, he intended to check a few other things first.

Namely, the woman who had first led him there. Wyla.

“We can’t come, can we, sir?” Viren asked before he was about to set off.

“I’m afraid there is no Excursion Club here to authorize that,” Ryland said. He tried for a reassuring smile, especially towards Tezar. “But don’t worry. We will retrieve your friends in no time at all. They will be fine.”

Viren and Acrypha nodded. Tezar was still looking fearful and afraid, despite High Academ Zevo’s departure.

“Listen,” Ryland said, stepping before the Rhellian youth. He made sure his voice was as kindly and calm as possible. “When this is all over, I’ll be happy to help you. I understand you’re going through… difficulties, but you’re not alone. We can work together to solve the issue. Okay?”

Tezar had his eyes closed tightly like Ryland had been about to reveal something that would ruin his life. When he hadn’t, the boy slowly relaxed. Then, after a while, he nodded.

Ryland smiled and nodded back. Viren and Acrypha were looking on, nonplussed but clearly interested. They wouldn’t know the intricacies of how the Glyph of Unity could cause problems for certain people. Of course, they probably weren’t aware of the Glyph at all.

He didn’t feel good about keeping them in the dark, but he also understood the need for caution about revealing such a thing.

Yet another thing to add to his plate for when this current situation was dealt with. Ryland would need to speak with the others, especially people like High Academ Tyra, about where Arcoryx stood on the issue of how the Demiurge had constructed his society through the use of his Soul Glyph of Unity.

For now, he focused on finding Wyla. It took a little bit of time before Ryland could locate her. Just looking around with [Omniscience] wouldn’t be the most effective way to go about it, so he made some enquiries and determined the neighbourhood of the city she lived in.

Once he had discovered her location, he had quickly [Translocated] over to find her at her home.

“Oh, sub-Academ Ryland,” she said at the doorway. She was very flustered at his sudden appearance. “Please, come inside.”

Ryland declined the invitation gracefully. The neighbourhood was a well-to-do one. Everything appeared calm, peaceful, well-organized, and clean. Wyla’s home was just as polished and pristine. One would never suspect she was involved in a conspiracy.

“I’m only here to ask about the missing students,” Ryland said.

Her face paled. “The… the missing students?”

“There is no point in denial. Please, just tell me what happened. It’s the easier option.”

She closed her eyes. Ryland was alert just in case something unexpected occurred, but thankfully, Wyla saw reason.

The whole story took longer than Ryland would have preferred. He had students to find, after all, especially since the priestess didn’t know where exactly they were. But Ryland’s suspicion that she had something to do with the disappearance was proven correct in short order.

Essentially, much of the hullaballoo about the Rhellians’ own outcry regarding the institute’s inability to keep their students safe was merely a front. What they had really been after was to keep the institute distracted while one of their own infiltrated into the grounds and “infected” the students with that curse.

“Why?” Ryland asked. “Speak plainly, please. Since you seem to not know where exactly the missing students are, then I must continue my search elsewhere once I’m done here.”

Wyla sighed. “We wished for freedom. We wished to reclaim the right to determine our own fates through our own choices, regardless of how unideal they may be.” She looked at him imploringly. “Is that such a crime? Can’t we have the same basic rights everyone everywhere else enjoys?”

Ryland inspected the woman intently. He had caught hints of such a wish back when they had first met, but this was the first time she was being plain about it all.

There were a lot of grounding facts about the issue that Ryland needed to deliberate on. In general, the underlying first fact was that the Demiurge, Mavar Val Arona, had his Soul Glyph of Unity branded onto the manasoul of every single Rhellian in existence. One of the key functions of Inscribing a Soul Glyph straight onto a manasoul was surveillance.

Any thoughts, any feelings, anything processed by the subject’s brain and soul, would all be seen by the Demiurge as well. It was one of the most powerful forms of control that existed.

However, that didn’t mean the Demiurge enforced the strictest level of control possible. There were benefits in allowing dissidents to exist. There were advantages to making sure Rhel didn’t become an utterly perfect utopia for every single Rhellian.

One of the ways that was ensured was by relaxing the level of control the Demiurge exerted on certain people. This gave them a taste for freedom.

In rare cases, this allowed them to become rebellious.

The Hands of Freedom would have been wiped off the surface of Rhel entirely if Mavar ever decided to tackle them seriously. Letting them fester and exist allowed Mavar to exert a very different sort of control. A political one that needed the scapegoats and boogeymen that the Hand represented.

So, it boiled down to wondering just how much Mavar was allowing this.

“I am afraid I’m not here to debate on the rightness and wrongness of your actions,” Ryland said. He wasn’t trying to be harsh, though he figured that was how his voice was coming out. “What I intend to do is ensure the safety of the students implicated in the matter.”

“Even if they are willingly giving themselves to the movement?” Wyla asked. “Even if they’re determined to make the most of the first taste of freedom they have attained?”

“Enough, please. My Glyph of Truth indicates that you really don’t know where the students are or what they intend to do. However, you must know something. You must have an inkling of what exact plans are afoot. Otherwise, you yourself wouldn’t be involved. You started all this with one goal in mind—to keep the next generation safe and happy. Or was that a lie?”

“That was no lie!”

“Then why do you hesitate now? You tread on dangerous ground, where things can spiral out of control with no warning whatsoever. Why are you being selfish?”

She didn’t answer immediately. Ryland was honestly unsure why he was even pressing the point. He was doubtful he’d receive the answer he was seeking here, and his time would be better spent brute-forcing [Omniscience] across all of Rhel to locate his quarry.

But then Wyla sighed mightily. “We are trying to protect them. Rhel won’t be able to take them away from us any longer.”

“Those words are dangerous,” Ryland said. “Even aside from how nefarious it sounds, for I know you would never deign to harm the children, how exactly do you intend to stop Rhel itself?”

“The Recreator will help us.”

Ryland frowned. “I’ve not agreed to any such thing.”

“You don’t have to, Marionys Ryland.” Wyla’s voice turned almost ethereal, fulfilling her role as priestess in truth. “At the temple, at the hallowed grounds that hold your name higher than anyone else’s, your inspiration drives forward our mission. Your power gives us hope. For in time, your magic will breathe renewed life back into this decayed world.”

Ryland deliberated for a moment. He wasn’t keen on dismissing her words as mad ramblings or anything of the sort. Not after his last brush with the Oracle of Androvia. Her prophesying was faulty, but her prophecies were not.

Regardless, Ryland had received the information he required from her. The temple. She hadn’t mentioned it off-hand. Even if the students weren’t there, he’d find the truth of the matter.

“Thank you,” Ryland said. “Your ideals aren’t misplaced. However, I suggest that you be more careful about how you follow those ideals. The danger won’t affect you alone.”

If she had a reply to that, Ryland was too impatient to listen in at the moment. He just cast [Translocate] yet again and disappeared. In less than a moment, he had reappeared right in the middle of the chaos in the centre of the temple supposedly honouring him.

The sight that greeted him was a bit horrific. Powerful mages kneeling all around, frightful magic erupting from far beneath the earth to keep them trapped. Their manasouls were being drained at a violent rate. Mana churned out of them like faucets set to their maximum opening, geysers of magic running underground into the trapping Inscriptions and redirecting all that magic elsewhere.

Right into the man he was standing before.

“Excellent,” he said with a mad light. “Excellent. This is a dream come true!”

Ryland frowned in recognition. So the Goodman of the Recreator cult was, if not behind all this, then at least a significant part of it all. Had he always been this powerful, or was it just the powerful Inscription feeding him stolen magic that was turning him into a fake archmage?

Needles of hostile mana were trying to interfere with Ryland’s manasoul as well. He could sense a certain level of restraint, despite the fact that Goodman Harker seemed eager to drink in Ryland’s power just as he was doing with the others. But besides his minimum control, there was also the fact that Ryland’s manasoul was far better protected than anyone else’s.

“Oh, where are my manners,” the Goodman said. “Recreator! I beg that you leave. Otherwise, the Inscriptions will suck out your very soul. You wouldn’t want that, would you?”

Ryland stared the man down. To pretend to some sort of civility in his current circumstances, to act as though the two of them could ever be considered to be on the same side, was ridiculous. “My soul, is it? Alright, go on then. Have your fill. But know that no matter what you do, your fate is forfeit. For now, you face me.”

The naked threat riled Harker up. He was happy to drop his pretend civility, his eyes gleaming with mad avarice. His hoe clattered to the ground. “Alright, then. It seems you’re not willing to comply with my kindness. Don’t say I didn’t give you the chance.”

“You seem confident despite standing against your so-called Recreator.”

“Why wouldn’t I be?” He raised his arms high, waterfalls of mana rushing off his limbs. “I have the power of incredible mages at my disposal. I have power that frightened even the Demiurge from standing before me. I have everything I could ever need to claim all that I should have had from the very beginning! I have nothing to fear!”

The needles that had been poking at Ryland’s manasoul all this while now turned into an avalanche of mana. Irrepressible magic assaulted the chains and leashes he used to keep his manasoul suppressed, battering away at the restraints he himself had placed upon his magic.

True to his word, Ryland let him do it. In fact, he even allowed the man a proper drink.

A snap of one restraint allowed a bit of his manasoul’s liquid magic to violently jet out. Underground, the Inscription turned incandescent like they were standing atop the surface of the sun. Frothing, surging, cataclysmic power now made the air thrum endlessly, pressure churning atop their shoulders and forcing the captured mages even further towards the ground.

“Yes!” Goodman Harker cried. His face had turned to alternating shades of ivory and blue, mana rioting across his form like he was turning into a Pure Manifestation. “I have supped from the feast of eternity. I have drunk from the wellspring of endlessness. O Great Gods, O Incarnations, O Everlasting, witness my ascension!”

His voice was changing just as he was. At the end there, his words had come out almost as a shriek. His soul itself was moulting into a new form.

An ascended form.

All thanks to the seemingly endless bounty of mana he was drinking from Ryland, of the ocean he had tapped into to sip on eternally.

All the while, Ryland just stood there. It was a matter of principle, really. He had told the Goodman that he could use the Inscription on Ryland to his heart’s content. And he was. Ryland not only allowed it, he encouraged an even stronger draw. Snapping a few more of the restraints allowed the Goodman access to even more of his manasoul.

Harker screamed in pained ecstasy. His clothes were crystallizing into mana as well now. If he had been standing under a waterfall of Ryland’s untameable mana before, he was now drowning in the ocean of it that had been upended on him.

And Harker was clearly enraptured by it.

“This is madness!” he cried. “This is insanity! This is heaven!”

Thundering bolts of mana crackled off him like solar flares, carving their way through the world. He was growing far too volatile. It wasn’t an unintended consequence, but Ryland did need to care about the others, so he went about casting [Eternal Bastion] on the other mages, including the Guardian, the High Academ, and Mage Knight Ophelia.

He also unleashed even more of his manasoul, so that there was now merely a few small restraints preventing it from reclaiming its true form.

One might think that would be difficult, what with the Inscriptions trying to pull away his manasoul’s vitality and tug it out of shape. But in truth, it was little more than a tickle for Ryland. That was why he hadn’t reacted thus far.

“Ah!” Harker cried. The air around him was sizzling now, the ground rupturing at his every amazed step. “I am become invincible! I am now unstoppable! None can withstand my—argh!”

Since Ryland was growing tired of the man’s spiel, he cast off nearly all the restraints on his manasoul. All except one, so that he didn’t turn into a true Incarnation just yet.

But that was finally too much.

The balance between ecstasy and agony had been shifting ever since he had started drinking from Ryland’s manasoul, and now, it jumped rapidly towards the latter. Towards uncontrolled aggression that someone like Harker wasn’t at all prepared to handle. Though, he was refusing to admit anything of the sort.

“I can control this!” he cried out. “I will hold onto my power. My power. My every—argh. Argh!”

He continued screaming as he drew on even more power. The Inscription wasn’t stopping, either due to Harker’s own hubris, or, as was more likely, because it didn’t have a fail-safe. There were no Subglyphs to stop the Inscription in case of a fatal development.

And for Harker, who had pridefully sought to drink from the sun, it had gotten to be far too much.

Power was beaming and shooting out of his body, his Manifestation now collapsing in on itself. His whole form was tearing itself apart. The pressure of convulsing magic grew so great, it felt as though a mountain had landed upon them all, crushing the entire area as the earth cracked and the temple was ground to dust.

Ryland and his allies had [Eternal Bastion] to rely on, but just in case, he cast [Translocate] to take them all away from the location.

Just in time too. The moment they arrived a few hundred handspans away from the temple, Harker exploded. A geyser of rioting mana shot into the sky, letting off an earthquake and shockwave, churning the air into a cyclone around the magic pillaring into the sky.

Ryland nodded his head, finally impressed. He was slightly regretting there hadn’t been an achievement from the Grand Frame for overloading someone with his own magic, but at least he was successful in his main endeavour here. Everyone who had gotten caught in the trap was now safe, though they were going to take some time to recover after that level of mana drain.

“What… is—” the Guardian began haltingly, but Ryland raised his hand all of a sudden to cut the man off.

The massive geyser was still shooting off into the sky, but that didn’t mean the situation had righted itself. He now had proof why the Grand Frame hadn’t awarded him.

With a crackle of world-rupturing magic, Goodman Harker slammed in with the eradicating force of a meteor.
