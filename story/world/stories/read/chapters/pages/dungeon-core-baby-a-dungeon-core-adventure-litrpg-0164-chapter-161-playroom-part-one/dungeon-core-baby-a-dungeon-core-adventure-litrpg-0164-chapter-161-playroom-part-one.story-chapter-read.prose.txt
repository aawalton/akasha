Merlin

“A game?” Obie blinked.

Merlin nodded. “Yes, a game. It’s a game I like to call seven—” He looked around the room, scanning for something, anything, to inspire him. He was, after all, making this all up as he went.

“Seven? How does the game work?” Obie asked. Merlin had him hooked. The problem was, he wasn’t quite prepared to reel in the fish he’d caught. That was a work in progress. His eyes landed on a toy sword on top of one of Obie’s toy chests. The pieces fell into place in an instant. “Seven swords! The game is called Seven Swords! Now, listen closely. The rules are very simple, but important.”

Obie plopped forward on the ground, his fall cushioned by the bizarre beard that wrapped around his body. He rested his head in his hands and kicked his feet while he listened.

Merlin cleared his throat at the sight. “Over four rounds, each of us will choose a sword —”

Obie raised a hand. “Can I borrow one from Sir Thespin?”

“No!” Merlin’s eyes lit up. “Seven Swords is a game of imagination. We don’t use actual swords, we use…magic swords. Kind of like how people sometimes spar with wooden swords. It’s like that. You have to get creative for this game. It’s very important.” Merlin paced. Blood flow helped the mind, and he needed his in peak condition for this con. Obie might have the mentality of a child, but if he decided to back out of the game and fight the usual way, Merlin was screwed. His friends were relying on him, and he couldn’t let them down at the first stage of this insane challenge. “Anyway, we’ll each choose a sword, and then we’ll declare the abilities of our swords to one another. Afterwards, the winner is decided based on which sword comes out on top. The winner of the game is the person who uses their imagination best. The first person to win three rounds wins.”

Obie nodded thoughtfully. “I bet my imagination is better.”

Merlin wagged a finger. “I didn’t say the winner will be the one with the best imagination. I said it will be the person who uses their imagination best. There’s a difference, Obie. You’ll see soon enough. It’s probably best that we just get started. It will make more sense once we play a round. Whoever wins this game will win the duel.”

Obie nodded. “Okay, fine! Let’s try it!” He paused for a minute. “Wait, why is it called Seven Swords? If someone wins the first three rounds, then there will only be six swords. And if you don’t, then there are more than seven swords.”

Merlin’s brows furrowed at that. Obie was right. “Technically, the number of swords can vary. But Seven Swords sounds cooler than Varied Swords.”

Obie shrugged. It was hard to argue with logic like that. “I’m ready!” he said.

Merlin took a breath. It was time to win this game he’d made up on the spot. All he needed to do was ensure his performances were entertaining enough to keep Obie’s focus on the game until he could win. “Would you like to start, Obie?” he asked.

Obie’s eyes lit up. He nodded. “I’ll use the…ocean sword! It has the power of the ocean!” Obie’s excitement was the excitement of someone who thought he’d had an amazing idea that no one in the world had ever thought of.

Merlin smiled. He began to weave an illusion together with several of his fake hexes, all working in tandem. An elemental hex to incorporate the water element. A hazel hex for the sake of adding in special effects and a few others for good measure.

A sword of water manifested in the air in front of Obie. The boy’s eyes lit up with excitement. It was the exact kind of reaction Merlin wanted. The only problem was…he hadn’t finished conjuring the illusionary sword yet.

“Obie, you aren’t supposed to use your own magic. It’s dangerous to—”

Obie frowned. “Huh? I’m not!”

“Then what…” Merlin paused, looking past Obie at the wall behind him. What was once the rear wall of a simple playroom in the tower was now a beach. The roof over their heads was that of a beachfront hut. The ground had turned to thick sand that felt no different from the floor he’d been standing on a moment before. An entire ocean stretched to the horizon. The sudden change of scenery was concerning, but that concern was trivial to the giant wave surging forward in the distance.

It was heading straight toward them.

“Obie...run!” Merlin turned to run. What was going on? How had they been transported onto a beach in the middle of nowhere? He paused when he saw it. The door he’d entered with Charlie and Vetica not that long ago. It stood where it had always been. Except for the fact that it was no longer embedded within a wall. Instead, it simply seemed to float there among the sand. This wasn’t the time to hesitate. Any moment now he’d be crushed by a giant wave. He shot forward, grabbed the handle, and twisted.

“Exiting the arena will result in forfeit.”

Merlin stopped in his tracks. “Forfeit?” Was this…was this all part of the game somehow? The sound of a crashing wave echoed throughout the room the closer it got. Merlin turned to see it pass over Obie, swallowing any glimpse of the young boy in an old man’s body. Obie hadn’t looked concerned in the slightest, but he was a Hex Knight. One that specialized in water magic. Merlin wasn’t even a true magician. What would happen if that wave hit him?

He shook his head. What would happen to Charlie and Vetica if he lost this duel? A game he’d created.

He set his feet and stared at the wave. There had to be something he could do. In mere seconds, he’d be swallowed up by a wave strong enough to smash his body against the wall behind him.

The voice he heard when he tried to open the door was the same voice they’d heard when they’d accepted the trial of the tower. If that was the case then maybe…

It hit him then what was going on. There was no time to test his theory or second-guess it. He held his hands above his head and activated his hexes. He wasn’t sure that he needed to, but he did it instinctively all the same. An emerald elemental hex of fire. “Anti-water sword steam blade!” The blade that appeared in his hands was glowing hot, though he felt no heat. It looked as if it had been pulled freshly from a blacksmith’s forge in hell. The design he had in mind would’ve been much simpler, and that alone told him his theory was correct. The fact that he could actually feel the weight of the blade in his hands was a bit of a shock, though. When the wave was just five feet away, he swung his blade downward with all the skill of an inexperienced swordsman. So inexperienced that it slipped from his hand and plopped harmlessly into the wave.

“Well, that sucks.”

The wave crashed over Merlin, engulfing his half of the room. He closed his eyes and covered his face to protect himself as best he could. It seemed he was destined to be crushed against the wall of this tower after all.

But that didn’t happen.

When he peeked from behind his hands, he saw the water had crashed against the rear wall and settled. The water level was lowering, as if there were some kind of secret drain hidden amongst the sand. He checked his clothes and realized he wasn’t wet at all. He was completely dry, in fact. The water flowed around him but didn’t affect him at all. In the moments before he chose his sword for this round, he’d put his hope in the fact that this was all an illusion. But hoping and knowing for sure were two completely different beasts when a giant wave threatened to crush you any moment. Still, this confirmed his theory.

There was a lot more to Obie’s playroom than he’d realized. Merlin let out a breath. “Just an illusion then.”

Across from him, Obie sighed. “Yeah…Gregory said I had to start using my imagination, so I didn’t explode the tower again…or get it struck by lightning…or cause more earthquakes…or…”

Merlin blinked. “Do you mean this room is capable of all that?” That meant at some point in the past, suggesting this game might very well have gotten him killed. He tried his best to push that thought from his mind. What mattered now was that it seemed the voice behind the tower’s trial had usurped control of Obie’s playroom.

Obie tilted his head. “Yeah! So who won?” he asked.

“The first round goes to the Challenger.”

“Ugh! What?” Obie asked. “But I made a giant wave—” he trailed off as the two of them realized what was causing the water to drain from the room. The water was down to their waists now. A hissing noise filled the air as steam rose. A molten-hot blade glowed beneath the water’s surface. His steam blade was turning the water into, well, steam.

“I won...” Merlin’s voice came out much higher pitched than he meant to. He cleared his throat, remembering that he needed to put on a convincing show. “I won,” he said with much more confidence. “The thing about Seven Swords, Obie, is that things can play out in unpredictable ways.”

Obie stroked his beard and nodded. “I see. Okay! I’ll try harder next round!”

Merlin nodded. But deep down, he was worried. His plan was to create a convincing performance of his own. Let Obie win a round or two to make sure he was having enough fun not to quit, and then finish out so Charlie and Vetica could move on with the rest of the duels. But now…well, now he wasn’t in control. Not only had this strange room taken control of the game’s special effects, but it was also calling the rounds.

That meant the outcome was no longer something Merlin was fully in control of. He’d have to actually play Seven Swords, a game he’d made up all of a few minutes ago. He had to play, and he had to win.
