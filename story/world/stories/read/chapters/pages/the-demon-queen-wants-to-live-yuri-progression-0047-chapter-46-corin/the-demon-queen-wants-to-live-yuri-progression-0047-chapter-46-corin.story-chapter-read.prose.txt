"Ash, I agreed to see one child. Yet now there are three before me." The room was not built for five people. It barely managed two.

The bed, the table, the ruins of the second bed, and the narrow gap between them. All of it looked much smaller with five instead of two. Edda, Ren and the boy named Corin stood before me. The boy had not looked up from the ground. Ash stood to my side. For an instant, it felt like being a Queen again, watching supplicants kneel and make their requests of me.

It was a good impression. One that lasted only for a moment. "Who the hell are you calling a child?!" Ren yelled.  She yanked her sleeves up, or tried to yank them, from what I could tell of the strange gesture. The Academy uniforms did not have sleeves. Ren seemed to remember this mid-motion, and then she kept doing it. I had seen Zera patiently regard Hellhounds who chased their own tails, endlessly. Perhaps this was what she had felt then.

She took a step towards me. What she meant to do, only the Gods could say. Edda grabbed her shoulder and yanked her back. "Ren. We are here to ask for help. Remember?"

Ren deflated slowly. Then she folded her arms. Most of her glare was gone. Most of it. "Yeah right. What help? This ain't something anyone can just help with-" She stopped and looked at the boy beside her. Her jaw tightened.

I studied the boy. Corin had not looked up from the ground since the three of them had entered the room. This Corin was, in every single way I could think of, unremarkable. He was short, with dark hair that covered his eyes. The nubs at his temples were so faint they might have been shadows. He was a Demonblood. Which meant he shared some of my blood, some small trace of it, at the very least. It was difficult to believe, even when his nubs were right there for me to see.

In my time, even the lowest of imps had carried something. Some trace that they were who they were, and proud of it. They might have all quaked in front of me, but I knew they all stood tall and walked proudly when I was not there. I suspected this Corin did not walk proudly anywhere. It was no wonder I had forgotten his face in the corridor. He was the kind of person even the System's gaze slid over.

Ash stood beside me. She looked between Edda and me, then spoke. "Edda. Tell Lys what you told me."

Edda glanced at us. The girl was perfectly stoic and composed. Whatever girl Ash had told me of in her story, she wasn't here. "It's hard to believe you both really don't know," she said.

Ash shook her head. I frowned. I did not enjoy being reminded of the gaps in my knowledge, especially by children.

"For humans, elves, beastkin…really just about everyone else," Edda began, "sometimes the Ink doesn't take. Everyone knows this. It's uncommon, but it happens." She paused. "For demonbloods or half-demons, it's different. The Ink always takes. Always. Nobody fully understands why. But it always answers."

I frowned. This seemed odd. That Inker had been rather full of himself at our Inking. "Then what is the issue?"

Ren cut in. "Just because it bloody takes doesn't mean it takes right." She turned to Corin and nudged him, gently. The gentleness was so unlike her usual manner that I noticed it before I noticed the gesture itself. "Can you show them?"

Corin held out his arm without looking up. I leaned forward to see. There was a mark there. Technically. I had to stare, squint, and lean closer. The Spark was so faint it was barely an impression. No, perhaps impression was too strong of a word. I could not tell its color apart from his skin. I had seen dozens if not hundreds of marks by now and every single one of them had glowed brighter than this.

"What is this?" I asked.

Edda hesitated. She looked at Corin. "The Ink always works for us," she said. "But sometimes -more often than it should- it works…wrong. The mark forms, and the Inkers all say it worked…but the mark forms like that. It doesn't do anything. You can't use it to do anything. It's almost worse than not getting any mark at all." She looked at the floor. "These people are called Hollows. It's one of the...very many reasons demonbloods distrust the Ink."

Hollow. It was clearly a kind of insult, one given to the weak by the strong. This Corin did not so much as flinch at the word. Perhaps this was what the Inker had hoped would happen to me when I had taken the Ink.

"Then how is this boy here at all?" I asked. He had a mark that did not work, in an Academy that seemed to value them a great deal. It made little sense for him to be here at all.

All three of them looked at each other. Even Corin. It was the first time he had looked at anything that was not the ground. "He got his mark here. At the Academy, I mean. After he followed us," Edda said.

"Followed whom?" I asked.

Ren scratched the back of her head. "All three of us come…we come from the same village. Eryn. It's far out west from here. Far out west, enough that an Inker only comes around once every few years. It's a tiny place. You wouldn't have heard of it." She said this as if she expected me to argue. I did not. "I got my mark first. There was a traveling Inker who came through and I got lucky." She gestured at her Sigil without looking at it. "My mark was always strong, even against other ones that did the same damn thing. The Inker told my parents I had potential. He told them about the Academy. It took a while to convince them, but I came here. And that's about it."

"That is not about it," Edda said dryly. "She wrote letters," Edda said. Her voice was flat, but something beneath it was not. "Many letters."

"I wrote like three letters!"

"You wrote two hundred and forty-seven letters. Sometimes we burned them when we didn't have enough wood."

"Hey, that's…that's rude!" Ren protested.

"So is sending two hundred and forty seven letters we can't even answer," Edda said. Her face hadn't shifted from the boredom, but I thought I saw her lips twitch. Just slightly.

Ren's face reddened. She opened her mouth, closed it, and turned to me so fast it was a wonder her neck didn't snap from the motion. "Anyway! Edda came here a few weeks ago. She got her mark from the same Inker when he came back through our village. Though her mark is…."

Edda picked up. "Ren told me more about this place than anyone should know about anything. Between all of her ramblings, I thought maybe it wouldn't be a terrible idea to come here myself." She paused. "I wanted to learn. So I came." Something about the explanation did not fit, though it was certainly close to fitting. It did not matter.

"And you?" Ash asked, quietly. She was looking at Corin. "You followed them here, didn't you?" Corin nodded. The motion was so small I might have missed it had I not been looking as intently as I was.

"Why didn't you get the mark earlier?" Ash pressed. Her voice was gentle, as if she had chosen each word very carefully. "You must have had chances. The first time when Ren got her mark. Then the second time when Edda got hers. Why didn't you take it then?"

It took some time for the boy to speak. I did not even think he would. Surprisingly, even Ren did not break this silence.

"I was scared. I was the first time when Ren got hers and even more the second time when Edda went away." His voice grew lower and lower by the word. "It happened to my mother too," Corin said. His voice was barely above a whisper now. "The same thing happened to her with her mark, I mean. Her mark doesn't work either. Half the time most people don't even see it when they're looking right at it. The other half, well, they give her that look." What look the boy meant, I did not know. He swallowed and continued. "And her mother had it too. And her mother before that." He was looking at his own hands. "Everyone…everyone in the village thinks it's something in our blood. So I knew. I always knew what would happen if I got one."

Ren had stepped closer to the boy at this telling. Her face had gone red. I almost thought I could hear the grinding of her teeth.

"Then why did you take it at all?" I asked. The question came out sharper than I had intended. "If you knew with certainty."

Corin shook his head, slowly. He didn't speak for several heartbeats. I wondered if I had, yet again, said the wrong thing.

He finally spoke up. "They both left, I guess." He said it as if those simple words explained everything. "I didn't want to be the one who stayed behind."

Ren's jaw tightened. It was already tight, but now it strained with the effort of it. Edda put a hand on Corin's shoulder. I was not entirely sure I understood this story, but I understood far more of it than I might have once had.

Edda was watching Corin with an expression that was tender. Very tender. And this girl, this Edda, stood half a step closer to Ash than anyone else in the room, and she spoke Ash's name with familiarity. I felt something sharp, though I did not know what. Something unbecoming of a Queen, I was sure. Damned Hero.

"What the hell is that?" Ren was pointing past me, toward the pillow. I frowned and followed where her finger led. She was pointing at the egg. It sat half-visible against the bedding, its gold shell catching the lantern light, black veins threading through it. It pulsed once, as if aware of the attention.

Perhaps I should have kept it in the satchel, but the three had invaded my morning quite early. At least they had knocked, rather than kick down the door. "That," I said, "is the most powerful steed in the world." I paused. "In the future. You should look at it well, while you can. One day, there will be many songs told about the creature who sleeps within that shell."

Ren stared. Edda tilted her head, studying the egg with more interest than she had shown in anything. "Can I touch it?" Ren asked.

"You really shouldn't ask that," Ash said, before I could respond. "Or Lys might do something...reckless."

They both nodded slowly. "It sure as hell looks cool though," Ren said. "With those black lines, I mean. Never seen one like that."

"Of course you haven't," I said proudly.

Ren stared at me. I ignored her. The egg pulsed again. Perhaps it approved of this…strange compliment. I reached for it and adjusted the pillow around it without looking at anyone. The moment passed. I looked at Corin. He still had not looked up. "Very well," I said. "I will see what I can do."

Ren looked at me, and the skepticism was plain. "Can you really do something, though? You're just at Line. Whatever healing mark you think you have isn't going to do much. We even had a few Crests look at him."

I regarded her. "It is beneath a mere serving girl to question a Queen. I will look at him. That is all you need to know."

Ren choked. "Serv-serving girl?!" Ash laughed. Edda held Ren back with one hand. The struggle seemed fake. She could have moved past the blue-haired girl if she wished. Corin, slowly, stepped forward.

I placed my left palm on his forearm and used the Cradle. White light bled from my skin into his.

With Bloom, I searched inside the boy. There was nothing physically wrong with him, save for a small cut somewhere on his left shoulder. I turned my attention towards his mark, and the channels around it. I found them at once. Something about them was simply wrong. Mana channels were supposed to be meandering but flowing things. These were tangled, sometimes looping back on themselves. In other places, they were constricted so tightly nothing could move through them.

Through Bloom, came impressions, as they always did. I had grown used to ignoring them, but these ones were stronger than most. Strong enough that I could not ignore them. Closer to visions than vague feelings.

I saw a long road, stretching away from a tiny village that I had never seen. Two figures walked along that road, far into the distance and growing smaller by the second. The viewer watched them go. Some part of him had expected this day, even as most of him was filled with dread. The vision changed. An Inking. The boy knelt in front of an Inker who did not look that different from Aldric. He held out his arm, even as a part of him knew what would happen. Had always known what would happen. Still, he knelt and held out his arm. Because even a hellhound might dare to jump off a cliff and fly, if it longed to chase a dragon.

Something in my chest pulled tight. I pushed past it.

Deeper still was the thing that made me press harder than I should have. It was his want to stand between his friends and the things that might hurt them. To be the shield instead of the thing being shielded. It burned in him. It burned even now, when he had nothing to give to the flame but himself. These impressions were bright. I had not known these impressions could be this bright. Was this because of my growing power, or something else?

I took a breath and pushed Bloom into one of the tangled channels. Gently. The channel grew. It thickened and strengthened and extended. And the pulsing wrongness grew with it. The knot tightened. The tangle deepened. Everything grew, even what I wished would not. I tried to separate them. I could not.

Corin made a small sound. No more than a groan he tried to swallow. I stopped. I shifted to my right hand. I pressed it to the same arm, just below where the Cradle had touched. Ruin seeped into the boy's channels.

The Requiem searched differently. It found impressions too. They were different than the ones the Cradle had shown, but no less vivid.

I felt the boy's horror when the Ink touched him and nothing happened. Everyone around him glowed, fresh marks on their arms in a dozen different colors. His had lit up, just for a second, and then gone dim. It had never so much as flickered after that. The boy had understood at once that the thing he had feared his whole life had happened, and happened exactly as he had always known it would.

I felt the boy's pain. A pain that had little to do with the mark itself. He had always wanted to protect his friends, and now he would spend his life being protected by them instead.

Next, I felt the boy's physical pain, and there was much of it. Something inside of him ached and throbbed and would never stop aching and throbbing. It was a pain the boy had gotten used to hiding, even if he knew he would never get used to the pain itself. I knew it well. I too had a thing inside me that hummed and would not stop humming.

Finally, the boy had a question. One that looked like an open door, beyond which was nothing. The question was simple. Whether stepping through that door, and into that nothing was better than what came before it. I knew what it meant. Any fool would.

Marin had had this same question, though he was just a child. One who did not understand what he was asking. This boy understood it perfectly well.

I tried the Requiem. With threads of Ruin, I tried to find the wrongness and make it cease, like I had with Marin. I tried to find a seam, some point where the wrongness inside this child was different from the child himself.

I could not find it. The channels were the wrongness just as the wrongness was the channels. They were the same thing, fused at every point, indistinguishable. Almost, I tried anyway. Almost. I pulled back. My hands left his skin. The Ember Core hummed louder.

The boy was standing very still. He must have been in great pain. Perhaps more pain than I. No, I knew he was in more pain than I. He had not shown it, not in any measure. He had not shown it, because it was the last way he could protect his two friends now. The last thing he could shield them from.

His head was still bowed. This Corin must be a warrior too, I thought. For not quaking at the thing inside him.

I looked at the three of them. There had been hope on Ren's face, when I had searched. Perhaps my method had been different from others she had seen, and so she had hoped. She had been trying to hide it. The careful stillness on Edda's face was now too still even for her. Even Corin had lifted his head, just barely. Just enough to look at me.

"I cannot fix this," I said slowly. My voice was very quiet.

The hope the three of them carried died. I saw it die for them, one by one. It left Ren first. Her hands balled into fists and a wisp of flame shot up her arm and died. It left Edda second. Her shoulders sagged. It left Corin last. It was not more than some spark going out of his eyes. I did not know it had been there at all. I knew it now, thanks to its absence. "Sorry," Corin mumbled.

This was the second time now that someone had apologized to me for their own pain. As if a failed healing were a failing on the one to be healed, and not the healer.

Ash's hand touched my shoulder. I had not asked for it. I did not remove it.

The three left after that. Ren's hand was on Corin's shoulder. Edda walked on his other side. The door closed behind them. The room was quiet. Ash looked at me. The question was in her face.

"I did not want to say it in front of them," I said slowly. "The boy's pain should be his own." I paused. "The channels the mark gave him grew wrong. The Cradle cannot fix what was never right -it only restores what was, or what could be. The Requiem cannot find a seam between the wrongness and the boy. Perhaps I am wrong, and I could have tried anyway. It would have done more harm than good, I think."

Ash listened. When I finished, she let out a breath that seemed to carry more than air. "Thank you," she said. "For trying. It was maybe too much to ask with…the pain you're keeping to yourself." She looked at me. "Just...thank you for trying."

Perhaps she both understood me too well, and not well enough. I stared at her. Genuinely confused. "Why are you thanking me?"

"Because you tried."

I held her gaze for one moment. Then I moved back to the bed. I took the egg and placed it in the satchel. I stood, and took hold of Ash's arm. She startled but did not resist as I pulled her toward the door. "To thank me for trying," I said, "implies that I have failed. I have never failed. Not once."

Ash was slow to start walking. "Lys, if they're the same thing then how are you supposed to do anything? You said it yourself, the Requiem is too risky."

I looked back at her. Something in my expression must have shifted, because her stride faltered.

"Did I say anything about the Requiem?"
