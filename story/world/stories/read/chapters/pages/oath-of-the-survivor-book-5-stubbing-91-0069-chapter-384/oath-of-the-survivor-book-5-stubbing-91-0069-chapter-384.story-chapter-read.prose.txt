As the party got busier, Kyle and C.H.A.D.D. excused themselves from the kitchen and settled into a corner to give others space to come give Frank their warm wishes.  He saw a few familiar faces from Earth, but most people gave him little more than a wary nod before getting back to socializing with one another.  Not that he could entirely blame them.  If his reputation and aura weren’t scary enough, the thick dark lines of his Ferric Augmentation ran across his face and neck, with the outer edge of his scar just visible above the relaxed collar of his shirt.  Coupled with his left hand being entirely wrapped in bandages, Kyle looked like a hundred miles of hard road.  Sometimes he felt like it, too.

Could he have tried to suppress the Ferric Augmentation, maybe wore a higher-collared shirt to try and present himself in a less intimidating way?  Sure.  Was he going to do anything about it?  Not beyond keeping his aura restrained to polite levels.  The reality was that their looks just didn’t bother him.  Despite a shared origin, they weren’t his people anymore.  His people were the ones who’d been in his corner since arriving at the Hub, including folks like Frank.  The ones that really mattered to him weren’t bothered by the physical changes he went through, nor were they intimidated by his status or power.  They cared about Kyle for who he was, and that was enough.

That’s not to say that he was left entirely alone.  Joseph and Mary Ellen approached him after some parting words to Frank, and the taller man let out a slow whistle as they sat down near Kyle.

“I always knew you were powerful, but damn,” Joseph said with a smile.

Mary Ellen punched him lightly on the shoulder.  “Joseph!  That’s rude.”

“What?  It’s true, isn’t it?  I got an identification skill when I reached D Grade, and I never realized just how wide the gap was.”

“I had some lucky encounters.  Or unlucky ones, depending on how you want to look at it,” Kyle replied with a smile.  There was no malice or bravado in the words, joseph was just that blunt and straightforward.  Something Kyle appreciated about the young man.

Mary Ellen shot one more exasperated look at Joseph before snuggling up underneath his broad arm and looking at Kyle.  “I just can’t believe how quickly the forests around here have been growing.  I worked with a few of the Nature Mages between the jobs Corthian Mining had for us, and it was all they could do to keep the fields from losing quality and keep up crop production.  Nothing at all on this scale.”

“I got a nice variant class offering when I evolved, and being in C Grade gave me a lot of oomph to put behind those skills.  If it wasn’t for those factors, there’s no way I could have accomplished all this in such a short time.”  Knowledge of a C Grade’s second class wasn’t widely shared, but Kyle saw little harm in sharing these little pieces.  From what he could tell, Joseph was ready to retire from the adventuring life, and it looked like Mary Ellen was going to push for D Grade and do the same.

While Garth was the official leader of the refugees from Earth, he’d left Joseph in charge as the most powerful non-Kyle Earthling on the planet.  The paperwork got a little funky, but it basically set a governance standard that any Earthling other than Kyle and Marcus was welcome, but disputes were subject to the discretion of the most powerful awakened members, which would then fall to Garth or Joseph.  They planned to amend it when the group of settlers was large enough to require a more traditional governmental structure, but this kept things nimble and moving along.  With the first wave of settlers being some of the finest workers among the Earthlings, their disputes were few and far between.

Joseph gave a wistful look out the window at the forest beyond.  “I still can’t believe that this is the same place.  That’s why Johannes hasn’t come yet, you know.  There are a lot of hard memories here.”

“It’s definitely like a dream sometimes,” Mary Ellen agreed.  “I never expected that we would be back, or that this place could look so much like home.”

They all sat in silence for a moment, then she continued, her voice softer.  “So much of life lately feels like it’s been running from disaster to disaster.  I’m almost scared to relax and call this home.”

Kyle nodded.  “There’s nothing wrong with feeling that way.  I truly hope that this offers an opportunity for a quiet and peaceful life for those that want it.”

“Peaceful?  Certainly.  But quiet?  Do you have any idea how many awakened teens we have running around here now?”  Joseph asked with a grin.

“I know of at least one,” Kyle said, giving a pointed look toward where Amalia and some of her friends were all looking at a shared screen.

“Trust me, she’s the best behaved,” Mary Ellen said as she readjusted on the couch.  “It’s the boys that are really getting themselves into trouble.  They all seem to think that they’re invincible.  And there’s just so many of them.”

Kyle remembered the early days after his awakening.  He was shipped off to a special school, constantly reminded of his responsibility to society and the Central Authority, and generally felt like he was stepping in as a cog in a wheel of a utopian society.  There were no aspirations of becoming a D Grade, no questions about what he would do or how he would fit in.  Just duty waiting to be completed.  Now, that structure and rigidity was entirely shattered.

Enrollment in one of the many academies across the Gray Conglomerate was a possibility, but that cost resources and raised questions that he and Marcus still needed to wrestle with.  The kids had some good mentors they could learn from right now, but at some point a longer-term solution would need to come together.

“I’m glad that the two of you will be helping to guide them,” Kyle said simply.

“I just hope Garth and Arianna get back soon,” Joseph added.  “We’ll need a few more D Grades for once the kids start getting a handful of skills.”

“Arianna made it to D Grade?” Kyle asked, his eyebrow raised.  He hadn’t seen the young woman since they parted ways on Kertan Six, and despite her rapid advancement she was still a good distance away.

Mary Ellen nodded.  “She did.  Took nearly every assignment Corthian Mining had to offer, but she made it.”

“That’s incredible.  She has to be one of the youngest D Grades I’ve ever heard of.”

Joseph let out a breath.  “Arianna’s something else, no doubt about it.  I just wish she’d take a break once in a while.  All that training and fighting has got to take a toll.”

As if summoned, Kyle felt the sudden appearance of two powerful auras approaching from the direction of the teleporter; both D Grade.  He felt the grin as he looked at Joseph.  “Well, it looks like you got your wish.”

Joseph looked confused for a moment, and then his head popped up when he could finally feel their presences.  Soon, he heard Frank’s cry of excitement from the other room as Garth and Arianna both came inside, then moved to join Kyle, Joseph, and Mary Ellen.  The broad grin on Kyle’s face faded somewhat as he saw their condition.

Garth was boasting some new scars, one of which ran across the left side of his head from his temple all the way to his jaw.  His gear looked to be worse for the wear, with several deep scratches across his sword’s scabbard  as well.  Arianna, while physically unharmed, looked completely exhausted.  Dark shadows were visible under her eyes, and her dark hair had lost much of its luster.  Moreover, now that she was closer he could feel the instability in her aura.  It was the same kind of feeling Kyle got when he pushed Ignition too far; a strong indicator that she’d taken some damage to her mana pathways.

Fortunately, Joseph stood and spoke first, giving Kyle a chance to compose himself.  “Boss, Arianna, I was almost convinced that you weren’t going to make it!”

Garth shook Joseph’s hand as he smiled.  “Wouldn’t have missed it.  Besides, there’s some important work coming up here that I wanted to be present for.”

“Of course I’m not going to miss my grandfather’s celebration,” Arianna replied at the same time, folding her arms.

Kyle shot Garth a look, which he returned.  The grizzled man’s expression confirmed everything Kyle suspected, that she was pushing too hard.  That would be an important conversation, but not for today.  Instead, he turned his attention to the other matter at hand.

“So Garth, I take it those scars are from Phase Three?  How did it go?”

“Hard,” he said with a snort.  “I probably shouldn’t have pushed as hard as I did, but made it through.”

“What option did you choose?” Kyle asked.

“Defend Position.  Made it through enough of the D Grade waves to warrant a unique enemy as the capstone.  The difficulty spiked a lot more than I expected, and it left me with some nasty souvenirs.”  He punctuated the point by gesturing to the scar on his face.

“I’m glad you made it out safely,” Kyle replied.  “And I’m assuming you got a good evaluation?”

“I got better than a forty-seven, if that’s what you’re asking.  Scored a ninety-two.  And even got some job offers.”

“Ouch,” Kyle said with a mock wince.  “You didn’t have to rub my terrible score in.”

“Well somebody had to work to repair Earth’s terrible reputation after your poor showing,” Garth shot back, a smile on his face.

Despite the scars, Kyle couldn’t remember the last time his friend looked so carefree.  A burden had been lifted from his shoulders as the resettlement plan began, and he could finally think about what he wanted.  Speaking of…

“I don’t see Stephanie and Gwen, how are they doing?”

“Home sick,” Garth said.  “Which means I won’t be staying too long, but I wanted to catch you while I was here.  I accepted an offer with the Mercenary’s Guild, and heard through some unofficial channels that a special job will be coming through shortly.  I know that your friend Tillienne will be invited, and I expect you’ll be hearing soon, too.”

“It’s finally time, huh?” Kyle asked.

“It is.  Chester and a couple of the stronger captains from Corthian Mining will be joining us, too.  More to play support than anything else, but it’ll be their last paid job before the company shutters its doors.”

“Will Angela be coming along, too?”

“No idea,” Garth said with a shrug.  “The moon he’s on doesn’t appear to have multiple C Grades, but we missed them last time, too.  DeRosa has had years to fortify his position, I wouldn’t expect that this will be a quick or quiet operation.”

Kyle did his best to suppress the anger burning in his chest.  When he spoke, his voice was barely above a whisper, but it quieted all the conversations around them.  “I hope he doesn’t go down easily.  He committed so many atrocities.  I’m going to make sure he answers for them all.”

There was silence for a moment as Joseph, Arianna, and Mary Ellen took in Kyle’s words.  Joseph was the first to recover.

“Wait, is it really time?”  He asked.

“I’m going too,” Arianna said a fraction of a second later, though she somehow looked even more haggard than before.

“Neither of you are going, and neither of you need to worry about it,” Garth said with a snap.

“We were there, too,” Arianna said, meeting Garth’s eyes with an icy stare.  “We have every right to go, too.”

Judging by the look between Mary Ellen and Joseph, Kyle was confident that Joseph had no intentions to go, but didn’t want to burst Arianna’s bubble.

“I’m at least as strong as the captains,” Arianna continued, still looking at Garth.

“This isn’t just about power.  You’re still missing years of combat experience.  There’s a reason Angela is trying to keep this smaller,” Garth said.

“I’m not a child anymore!  I know the risks, and I want to go!”

Garth looked about to respond, when a tired voice came from the direction of the kitchen.  “Arianna…”
