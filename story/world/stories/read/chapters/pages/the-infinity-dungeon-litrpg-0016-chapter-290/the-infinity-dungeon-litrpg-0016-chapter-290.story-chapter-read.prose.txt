Chapter 290

“And now that we have control of this new energy, which we are tentatively calling Adherence, we officially have nothing more to fear from Faith users,” Michael said, addressing the room. “The only thing that stands in our way, bar the usual threats, is PAC energy. It corrupts and refuses to be assimilated by the Origin System.”

As Michael’s recounting of the events came to an end, silence befell the Silver Room. He sat back down. Icarus had been tightly wrapped around his arm all the time he had spoken, and now simply lowered their body and materialized a chair hovering in the air next to him, all of it without letting go even once.

Behind the two, the view through the windows was of clear skies and lone clouds, seen through the faint hexagon shapes of the mostly transparent shield. More huge windows surrounded them in all four directions, unobstructed by pillars or support beams thanks to a clever use of structural metamaterials no thicker than a pencil. Site 00 and its mountains, valleys and forests extended in all directions, and only small patches of land were not yet owned by Unity Corporation–hamlets and homesteads, or brutalist constructions left behind by some utility company that would soon become their subsidiary.

“So this Adherence,” David began, brows furrowed as if he was inspecting a particularly nasty piece of rotten food. “Is it actually Faith?”

“Allow me to explain,” Johanne said, and all heads turned toward her. “The preliminary analysis of the energy signature flowing into the Origin System from pope Damasus suggests that Adherence and Faith are closely linked, but not the same things. Adherence is a more primal form of Faith, its raw constituent–so to speak. Faith is a processed version of Adherence, specific to the Christian Church.”

“There are many kinds of Adherence,” Michael continued from where she left off. “Now that we know what to look for, we have found that even this company generates a small amount of Adherence.”

“How?” Travis asked, dumbfounded. “It’s a company. It’s not a religion.”

“But people believe in it,” David said, his voice booming with laughter. “Don’t they?”

Michael nodded.

David scoffed, still half-laughing. “And we can tap into any kind of Adherence? Even if it’s not directed at us? It’s nuts!”

“Only as long as it is within reach of the Origin System, but yeah. While Faith is specific to the Church and its priests, Adherence is the raw form of the energy. Anyone can intercept and use it.”

Travis’s face morphed as his mind made some connections and drew conclusions. “Oh,” he said. “This gives our PR campaign a whole other meaning. If people believe in our company, in the good it can do for the world…”

“It’ll give us even more power,” David said. “Water in the Desert…” he muttered. “Was it part of the plan from the very beginning?”

Michael said nothing.

“David, you don’t understand!” Travis said, fire in his voice. “All this is barely the beginning of it. Think about what’s going to happen when the Origin System spreads to the wider public. People will believe in it because it will give them power and a means to overcome adversity, to rise in status, to fight the monsters that lurk everywhere! But the System is made by Unity Corporation. Can you imagine the Adherence such a feedback loop will bring?”

“I can imagine it alright,” David said. With a nod, he mentally manipulated the screens so that they displayed the quests Michael had given to the pope.

Travis almost choked on a laugh. “You are turning the Origin System into a religion!” he said, disbelief turning into zeal. “You plan to hijack the whole Faith to generate more Adherence for us!”

Michael remained stoic. “There will be a flavor of Origin for everyone, Travis. Everywhere. Every little action, every exchange of wealth and power. What’s money when you have mana coins and XP you can trade, and whose value is guaranteed by the System?”

The implications of the last sentence hung in the air, just as the text of the papal quests remained in the holo at the center of the table.

“Even you David,” Michael said. “The way you manipulated the holos. You couldn’t do that without the System.”

“And the privileged access,” he replied.

Michael shrugged. “A version of which can be bought, piece by piece, spending XP. Again, feedback loop.”

“That is all in the future,” David coughed and said. “Let’s talk about the good spanking you gave to the pope instead. Dare I say, I enjoyed it immensely. I would have slapped him once or twice, though, and I’m quite pissy that I can’t slap him anymore now that he’s our puppet. If everyone turns into our puppets, who are we going to slap then?”

“Devils, for now,” Travis said. “And I am pretty sure that the threat we got coming from space will also be a source of–”

“Not sure about it. Is space energy slappable, Travis?” David interrupted him.

“How do you know it’s not?” the man replied. “Could be aliens. Slappable aliens.”

Michael watched the two banter for a few moments, feeling the soft warmth of Icarus’s body around his right arm. His arm was almost as thick as their torso, and the way they tried to cling as close to him as possible, to the point they were almost falling from the chair, was cute.

“Anyway,” Travis said, putting an end to the show. “I can see in the quest text that we are assuming there are multiple shards around.”

“Shouldn’t it be one?” David asked. “Given the size of the missing hole in the mirror, and the seven that we have. Makes 399 in total, doesn’t it?”

Travis shook his head. “Shards can be broken into smaller pieces. The Don gave me a sliver of a shard when he wanted me to betray Michael.”

“Right,” he said.

“What we know for sure is that there is at least one shard, wielded by a Faith user–a priest, presumably–who can conceal it,” Michael said. “In fact, we can most probably rule out the existence of shards not in possession of Faith users. The PAC energy would be detectable and we would be able to see them with our satellite network.”

“Rogue priests, then.” Travis said. “And each time they use a shard, PAC energy gets released, and we get a local outbreak of devils while the veil weakens all over the world, doesn’t it?”

Michael nodded.

Travis addressed the rest of the room, which also included Trevor, Jennifer and a few other key figures that had been rising to prominence within Unity. None of them had spoken, and it did not look like they would be speaking any time soon.

“We need to come up with a plan and quickly. We can’t just hope they won’t use the shards.”

“They will use the shards,” David said. “You heard what Michael said. Even when not active, the shards slowly corrupt the wielders. They will make them want to use it, first to get more artifacts and grow their power, later–”

“The whispers will get to them,” Travis said. “I remember the voices from the sliver of a shard I touched for the briefest of times. Who knows what they will tell their wielders.”

“Funny,” Michael interjected. “They are like the One Ring.”

Everyone looked at him.

“Ehm, do go on.”

“Right,” Travis said, shaking his head. “As Michael showed us when he made the pope open a rift to the dungeon, the shards–even though they are corrupted by PAC energy–are not limited to only opening portals to the Netherworld.”

“Remember, Tavis,” Michael said. “All portals release PAC energy though, no matter the destination. That is the worst problem of the corruption. Spacetime magic, be it shard or us or another source, if near PAC results in a devil outbreak.”

“Fucking Don,” David muttered. “This shit is all his fault. Michael, what happens when the wielder gets thoroughly corrupted by PAC energy?”

“They become a devil, I’d guess? Similar to what happened to the Don, although I assume it varies depending on what devil manages to lure a priest with a shard into making a deal with them.”

“Do we have any idea how long it takes for a shard to corrupt someone?” David asked.

“If my lord agrees,” Johanne replied. “We can set up some experiments, forcing priests of various levels of power to hold and use shards in a controlled environment to assess the progression of their corruption.”

Michael felt a tug on his arm as Icarus stirred. “I do not agree, Johanne,” he said. “It wouldn't be much use anyway because with Faith there are simply too many confounding variables. I have seen the pope’s Faith actively try to suppress some of the PAC possession, so the timeline might be long, with escalating events.”

“And,” Travis added. “We can’t use the Don as a benchmark either. I talked to him, and I came to the conclusion that the devil possessing him only started to affect him toward the end. Before, the Don had always been careful not to let anyone else touch a shard; he knew that it would corrupt them and attract more devils, to the point the situation would have eventually escaped his grasp. He barely even used the Renegade, remember? He preferred to leave the man starved of magic rather than risking betrayal.”

“For good reason,” Icarus spat, squeezing Michael’s arm and showing sharp and longer than usual canines. “Good thing my Michael killed that cockroach.”

The sudden outburst stunned the room. Seeing Icarus all worked up, and knowing what the AI could do, was scary enough to make even the bravest or most foolish break into cold sweat. Fortunately Travis managed to recover rather quickly, being among the ones who knew Icarus best.

“The Don was also trying to gather all the shards to himself, as the quest says. He was not planning on giving them to anyone. The priests didn’t need them either, they had their Faith artifacts. Things only changed with the release of PAC corruption and the sudden loss of their only source of more artifacts.”

“What was he doing with the shards?” David said. “Rebuilding the mirror?”

“Presumably,” Travis said.

Michael nodded. “Then, during our final confrontation, before he died, the devil that took over his body altered the shard to make sure I couldn’t touch them. I confirmed it with the pope: the moment I withdrew my Domain, the shard calmed down, and the Domain is an extension of me. I know that we can’t leave the shards unprotected, but with the PAC energy increasing each time a new rift breaks we can’t keep them here either. Now, given what I just told you, I have an idea how to dispose of the shards. Anyone wants to try and guess?”

He looked specifically at the newcomers. There were a few from the science division, or the PR branch, but most of them were military heads from Candle Light and former Vanguard. Either rejuvenated, or poached from organizations all over the globe.

Michael looked at them and smiled.

“You all have the Origin System, don’t you?” he asked. They nodded. “Good. So, since you haven’t talked much at all, to incentivize proper brainstorming I’m giving ten thousand System XP to whoever guesses what I want to do with the shards, and along with it they will also receive the Quest to handle the project themselves together with the top brass. Any takers?”
