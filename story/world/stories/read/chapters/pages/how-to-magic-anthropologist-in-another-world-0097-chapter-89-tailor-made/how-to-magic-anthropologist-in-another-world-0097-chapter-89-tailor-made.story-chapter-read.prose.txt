I woke up cuddled against Nina on the rooftop of the observatory tower in the Sunless Reach.

For a while, I didn't move.

The stars filled the sky above us, scattered across the endless darkness. I watched them in comfortable silence while enjoying the warmth of Nina's wings wrapped loosely around us like a blanket. Somewhere overhead, a Shooting Star crossed the heavens, filling me with Star Mana. I felt a strange sensation of being overcharged as the Star Mana slowly converted into regular mana, even though I was already topped off. It was a bit excessive, like finishing a big meal long after I was already full.

As the next Shooting Star appeared, I tried to halt the Star Mana and prevent it from converting. I managed to hold it longer than usual, but it took all my concentration to do it. It wasn't something that could be useful in combat, but still, progress was progress.

Eventually, I turned my head toward Nina.

Her sapphire eyes were already open.

"You're awake." I smiled.

"Yeah."

"We should probably get going."

"Yeah."

She glanced toward the massive telescope standing silently beside us.

"Would it bother you if I started working on the microphone before I tackled the repairs on that?"

I followed her gaze.

"Not at all," I said, following her gaze. "I haven't officially claimed this place from the guild yet anyway. There's no rush."

Her wings twitched slightly.

"Thanks. I really want to try the paper cup experiment first, then see how much I can improve it."

"I figured you would." I laughed.

She grinned without denying it.

"Okay then," I said, climbing to my feet and stretching away the stiffness from sleeping outdoors.

Nina stood a moment later, her pigeon wings stretching wide open. We folded the large futon together before I stuffed it into my Bag of Holding, where it vanished alongside the rest of my worldly possessions.

"Want to grab breakfast before I take you home?" I asked.

"I'd rather take a bath first." She absentmindedly twirled a strand of dark hair around one finger.

"A bath, huh?" I looked around the barren landscape stretching beyond the observatory. "I wonder how I could install something like that here. This place is incredibly dry since it never even rains."

"You could use a water summoning enchantment," Nina suggested.

"Those exist?"

She nodded.

"The water is temporary. After an hour or so, it dissolves back into ambient mana, so you can't drink it, but it's perfect for washing clothes or taking a bath."

"Huh." That actually sounded useful. Then another idea occurred to me.

"...Or."

Nina immediately recognized the expression on my face.

"Oh no."

"Oh yes."

Before she could protest, I slipped one arm around her waist.

She looked up at me suspiciously.

"What are you planning?"

I smiled toward the stars as a Shooting Star crossed the heavens.

"I wish we were teleported to the nearest safe, empty natural hot spring."

The world vanished, and the solid ground beneath our feet was replaced with crunchy snow.

A blast of icy mountain air greeted us.

Nina instinctively pressed closer against me as she looked around.

"...Where are we?"

A small pond rested only a few steps away, surrounded by snow-covered rocks. The water overflowed slightly into a thin stream that cascaded down the mountain as wisps of steam drifted lazily into the crisp morning air.

The sky had changed as well. The endless night of the Sunless Reach had been replaced by a blue sky and a yellow sun.

"We're somewhere in the Kurco Range," I guessed.

I slowly turned until I recognized a familiar mountain.

"There." I pointed toward two distant cliffs standing side by side as though a mountain had been split by an enormous sword strike. "I think that's the mountain pass."

Nina followed my finger before looking around again at the vast expanse of flawless white, unmarked by footsteps.

"This place is beautiful..." A cold breeze swept across the mountains, making us shiver. "...and freezing."

"That's mountains for you."

Snow covered everything beyond the spring itself. Pine trees clung stubbornly to the rocky slopes while distant peaks disappeared into the clouds.

"It's secluded, though," I said. "I doubt much lives up here besides the occasional Ice Elemental."

"That wasn't reassuring..." Nina stared at me.

I laughed.

"No, we'll be fine. The heat from the springs should keep them away."

"Should?"

"I've never been here before."

"Alice..."

"Oh, don't worry." I summoned my spear. "I'll keep you safe."

"Fine." She sighed.

"Okay," I said as the cold quickly began creeping through my clothes. "Enough sightseeing."

I started pulling off my blouse.

"I'm getting in."

Nina, being considerably more sensible than me, crouched beside the water first and dipped a single finger into the steaming pool.

A few ripples spread across the surface.

"The temperature's perfect!"

"Good enough for me."

I hurried through the rest of my clothes before the cold could make me reconsider. The instant I was free of them, I took two quick steps forward and cannonballed straight into the spring.

Warm water engulfed me.

"Ohhh..."

The contrast between the freezing mountain air and the comforting heat was incredible. Every muscle in my body relaxed immediately as I surfaced with a satisfied sigh.

"Now this was one of my smartest wishes."

Nina shook her head, smiling despite herself.

"You really are impossible."

She neatly folded her sarong dress and placed her sandals on top of it so they wouldn't be blown away by the wind before easing herself into the spring far more gracefully than I had.

A second later, she let out a quiet sigh of contentment.

"...Okay."

She leaned back against the smooth stone.

"I admit it. This was a good idea."

After a few minutes of soaking, I shared my cosmetics with Nina and helped her wash her wings.

Once we were done, she stood up and shook them, sending droplets flying along with a few loose feathers. I handed her some towels from my Bag of Holding, and when she was about to put her old clothes back on, since she hadn't brought spare clothes, I handed her a sarong dress similar to her own.

"I didn't know you wore one of those."

"I don't. I bought it while shopping with Nakera and Kuru, but I never wore it."

"Why not? It would suit you."

"I don't know. I'm not comfortable showing this much skin."

"You're literally naked right now."

"Bah! It's not the same."

I dressed in comfortable city clothes rather than my armor before I held Nina with one arm, raised my spear with the other, and tapped into the Star Mana stored inside it. One wish later, we both vanished.

After making one short jump back to the observatory rooftop to recharge my Star Mana and bank another return wish for later that evening, Nina and I teleported next to Louis' new pizzeria.

The smell of fresh bread, tomato sauce, and melted cheese reached us before we even stepped through the door.

And... coffee. I could smell coffee as well.

Louis looked up from behind the counter and smiled.

"Alice! You're back, and Nina too!"

She immediately reached for two oversized mugs.

"You've got to try today's batch."

She set them in front of us before either of us had a chance to speak.

I took a cautious sip, then another.

"...Oh."

I looked down at the mug.

"This tastes far better than last time."

Louis grinned proudly.

"Ja'e turned a few of the apprentice bakers we hired into a dedicated roasting team. They've been having fun experimenting."

"Good for them!"

The bitterness had mellowed considerably. There was still a rich roasted flavor, but now it carried a pleasant sweetness underneath it.

"If they're still experimenting," I said after another sip, "tell them to ferment the beans after starting with fresh fruit. It should improve the flavor profile."

Louis immediately reached for the little notebook she kept tucked into her apron.

"Ferment them?"

"Yep. It changes the chemistry inside the bean before roasting. You can get a less sour brew that way."

"Huh..."

She quickly scribbled the suggestion down with a graphite stick.

"I'll tell them."

"By the way," I said, "I met a few bards in the Great Dream who fancy themselves food critics."

"Oh?” Louis raised an eyebrow. “Which ones?"

"Vargo, Yura, and Wabi."

"I hate Wabi." She groaned dramatically.

"You know him?"

"He insulted Mama Dima's cooking."

She disappeared into the kitchen before returning a moment later carrying a plate stacked with fluffy golden cakes. They looked vaguely like pancakes, except thicker, lighter, and almost sponge-like.

"Try these."

Nina and I immediately dug in.

"They're really good."

"I know." She smiled smugly before continuing. "Vargo's a decent guy. He's a fan of Silnar's kanta."

She hesitated.

"I don't think I know any Yura, though."

"Yura's a Kitsune Dreamer."

"Doesn't ring a bell."

I nodded.

"I also met Lord Mick Ira."

Louis froze.

"...You what?"

"He'll be coming to the grand opening next week."

Her eyes widened.

"Lord Mick Ira is coming?"

"Yep."

She slowly looked around the restaurant.

"What should I do? We really need to impress him."

"Oh, don't worry," I said. "Serve him a pizza, a good cup of coffee, and finish with tiramisu for dessert."

Louis blinked.

"You haven't taught us how to make the dessert yet."

"...Right." I rubbed the back of my neck. "I completely forgot."

"You know what?” I looked toward Nina. “We can invite Louis into the Dream tonight."

Nina nodded.

"I still have a few potions that help people enter the Dream." She looked toward Louis. "I doubt a Holy girl like her would be comfortable sleeping under my wings."

Louis blinked.

"Sleeping under your wings?"

"It's less weird than it sounds," I assured her. "It's how Valkyren mothers help their kids enter the Dream."

“Oh!”

"Free up your evening," I added. "I'll come get you, and we'll spend the night at Nina's house. I bet I can teach you lots of recipes."

"I guess." She looked between us. "But why do that in a dream instead of here?"

"Because I barely know how to make tiramisu myself," I admitted with a grin. "It's easier if I can just pull up a video."

"I don't really understand what you mean by that, but I should learn more about Dreamer magic," said Louis. "Silnar is half Dreamer, so my children would be a quarter Dreamer."

"Oh right... I completely forgot about that. Bring him with you." I glanced at Nina. "If you don't mind."

"It's your sanctuary.” She shrugged. “Your secret to keep."

"I wasn't worried about that." I laughed. "I was asking if you were okay with me inviting a man into your room."

Nina shrugged again.

"That room's only for sleeping. My personal things are downstairs."

She finished the last bite before standing up.

"I guess I'll see you tonight, then," I said.

"Long night or short night?" she asked.

"Long night," I said.

"Perfect!"

As she was leaving through the front door, someone else entered at the same time.

A young dark-skinned woman stepped inside.

"Lera," I smiled. "You're here."

She spotted me immediately, and her face brightened.

"Oh! Miss Alice!"

Turning toward Louis, I couldn't help smiling.

"So you hired her like I suggested."

Louis nodded.

"Yeah. Ja'e did. She's been delivering messages, running errands, carrying supplies, and helping wherever we need an extra pair of hands."

Lera slid onto the bench beside me.

"It were you what got me this job."

She rubbed the back of her neck sheepishly.

"So... thank you."

"I remember you being a good runner," I replied. "Back when we were running from the Spider Matriarch in Weavershall."

She snorted.

"Callin' me a coward now, are you?"

"I call it having good survival instincts."

That earned a laugh from everyone around the table.

Now that she was sitting next to me, I had a chance to properly look at her.

She was fit without looking intimidating. Years of running had given her powerful legs coupled with a curvy, feminine figure. Unlike her legs, though, her arms weren't particularly muscular, but she looked healthy and energetic, exactly the sort of person customers would feel comfortable approaching.

More importantly, as a freelancer trained under Yon, she was still capable of throwing out the occasional drunk or creep.

The clothes, however... had to go.

She still wore the same practical traveling outfit I had first seen her wear in Weavershall. Dark cotton clothes reinforced with thin wooden plates over the forearms and shins. Cheap and functional, it looked more like a ninja outfit without the mask than something suitable for a customer-facing role.

"How are you handling the uniforms?" I asked Louis.

She blinked.

"Uniforms?"

"Clothes for the staff."

"Right, I remember you talking about it." She scratched her cheek. "We... haven't actually gotten that far yet."

"I see."

I leaned back in my chair.

"I think you're underestimating how much appearances matter."

Lera looked down at herself.

"What's wrong with my clothes?"

"Nothing."

I shook my head.

"They're good traveling clothes."

She nodded.

"But they don't tell your customers what kind of place this is."

Both girls looked confused.

"We aren't just making a restaurant," I explained. "This is a brand new way to serve food, and we need uniforms that reflect that."

Louis frowned thoughtfully.

"I guess."

"I don't rightly understand it either." Lera tilted her head.

"You don't have to." I smiled. "You just have to look good wearing them."

"Fair enough." She laughed.

"Did you at least settle on a logo?" I asked.

"Yes," said Louis. "The crest for the shop. Ja'e hired someone to carve it."

She showed me a wooden sign. It was a circular constellation that looked somewhat similar to a laurel wreath, except each star was a heptagram rather than the pentagram popular on Earth. The lowest star was bigger than the rest.

"Perfect," I said.

"I'll be borrowing this." I picked up the sign and dropped it into my Bag of Holding.

"I'll also be borrowing Lera for a short while."

"...Borrow me?"

"Where are you going?" asked Louis.

"To a tailor I know," I said. "I'll handle the uniforms."

Petra's tailoring workshop wasn't far from Louis' pizzeria, both being in the guild district.

By the time we arrived, the little shop was anything but quiet.

We stepped inside to find the young seamstress working up a storm.

Three dresses floated in the air before her, each held perfectly still by telekinesis while six needles danced around them with practiced precision. Every needle carried a different colored thread, embroidering elaborate phoenix motifs across the backs of the dresses simultaneously.

The first dress was crimson with a sapphire phoenix.

The second was sapphire with a crimson phoenix.

The third was elegant black with a brilliant white phoenix spreading its wings across the fabric, the sharp contrast making the embroidery stand out dramatically.

The cut immediately caught my attention. Gone were the stiff corsets and heavy layered skirts that dominated noble fashion. Instead, the dresses were much closer to modern cocktail dresses, fitted around the waist while allowing enough freedom to move comfortably. They still looked elegant enough for noble banquets, but no longer resembled painful torture devices.

Petra noticed us immediately.

"Alice!"

The dresses stopped sewing themselves as she hurried over.

"Petra." I smiled. "How are you doing?"

"I'm doing great!" Her smile somehow became even wider. "Thanks to you."

I looked back toward the floating dresses.

"I see you've got plenty of work, unlike last time."

"Oh, you have no idea." She laughed. "After the Winterbloom Ball, the nobles practically stormed my atelier asking for dresses just like yours. For over two weeks, I barely slept."

"That bad, huh?"

She held up both hands dramatically.

"I had to finish three dresses every single day."

"That's... a lot." Lera whistled.

"It was."

Petra sighed before smiling again.

"Eventually, the larger tailoring houses copied the design and started stealing my customers."

"That's unfortunate," I said.

"Damn nobles always takin' good people's work," Lera muttered.

"I don't mind." Petra simply shrugged.

"You don't?"

"No." She gestured around the workshop. "Before that, hardly anyone knew my name. Now I'm actually competing with the biggest tailoring houses in Hano."

She couldn't stop smiling.

"I even managed to keep Lady Kerissa as one of my regular customers." She pointed proudly toward the three dresses. "Can you believe it? Captain Kerissa is wearing my dresses."

"That's amazing," Lera said immediately. "Kerissa's the best."

"I know!" Petra beamed with pride. "I have more customers than I can comfortably handle, a recognizable style, and my dresses are being worn at noble events."

"I can't thank you enough." She bowed her head slightly.

"You can thank me by helping with my next project." I waved the compliment away.

Her eyes sparkled.

"Anything!" She clasped her hands together. "My workload has finally started slowing down this past week, and you know how fast I work."

"That's exactly why I came." I nodded. "The design itself is simple, but the order is going to be enormous."

Her smile somehow grew even wider.

"I like enormous orders."

"We're opening a restaurant."

"A... restaurant?"

"I want uniforms for the entire staff."

Petra blinked.

"Like military uniforms or butlers' attire?"

"No, just some simple matching outfits."

She looked intrigued.

"For every employee?"

"Exactly."

I thought for a moment about how to explain polo shirts before reaching into my Bag of Holding and pulling out my phone. After opening the image gallery, I scrolled through several folders before finding one labeled Cosplay.

I scrolled down past a few JRPG cosplays, before I found exactly what I was looking for. A cosplay of a pizza delivery outfit from a popular MOBA game. The cosplayer was a former pro player cross-dressing as one of the female champions.

Don't ask me why I have that picture on my phone, because I don't know.

I handed her the phone.

"The neckline should look something like this."

Petra studied the image carefully.

"It looks formal if you ignore the exposed stomach..."

"Yeah, let's not make it that short."

Lera leaned over her shoulder.

"I wouldn't mind showin' my tummy. The weather's warm enough."

I laughed.

"Maybe you wouldn't." I shook my head. "But Louis wouldn't be caught dead wearing something like that. And every employee should wear the same basic uniform."

Lera scratched her cheek.

"Fair point."

"So..." Petra pointed at the image. "A longer shirt?"

"Long enough to reach the hips and cover the stomach."

"I'm guessing no shorts either?"

"Yeah, trousers instead."

She nodded while mentally taking notes.

"What about this part?"

She pointed toward the vest hanging around the model's waist.

"That's actually a half-vest attached around the hips."

She tilted her head.

"...Interesting."

"It should be wearable on colder days, but judging by Hano's weather, it'll act more like an accessory."

"I can do that."

"The shirt should be bright red instead of white, and the trousers should be black."

"And add this crest to the shirt." I pulled the wooden plaque out of my Bag of Holding.

"Embroider it here." I tapped the left side of my own chest. "Right over the heart."

Petra nodded.

"So... a formal collared shirt with embroidery, black trousers, and a matching half-vest."

"Exactly."

"And the hat?"

"If possible."

"Can you make it bigger?" she asked.

I zoomed in on the red baseball cap.

"...How is this part made?" she asked, pointing at the brim.

"The inside is cardboard covered with fabric."

Petra blinked.

"That's surprisingly simple."

"It really is."

"I should be able to make that."

"Good." Then I smiled. "Now comes the difficult part."

She raised an eyebrow.

"The order." I grinned. "We'll need a lot of uniforms."

"How many?"

"Six sets for every employee."

"Six?" Her eyes widened slightly.

"Restaurants get messy."

She slowly nodded.

"Fair enough."

"There should also be both masculine and feminine cuts."

She looked from me to Lera.

"How many employees are we talking about?"

"I honestly have no idea." I shrugged, then pointed toward Lera. "I brought her so you can make the first prototype. We'll show it to Ja'e, and she'll place the order."

"That makes sense." Petra nodded immediately.

She reached out with her power, and a measuring ribbon floated over from somewhere in the workshop.

"Now strip," said Petra.

"What?"

"Your armor is too bulky."

Lera sighed dramatically before starting to unfasten her shirt.

"I reckon I should've seen that comin'."
