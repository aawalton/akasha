Ash’s project helped tremendously with garrison troops. With surprisingly minor help from Tashome, she’d fashioned a ‘control band’ out of the handle of the undead champion’s whip, as well as material from bits of his armor. She’d donned it herself and, immediately, the undead hordes they’d sequestered behind fences to mill about aimlessly went into lockstep. She had total psychic control of something in the ballpark of sixty thousand undead troops.

She’d spent her ensuing days in the grueling process of structuring their independent orders into garrison groups, as needed, whether operating as simple reserves in wait, or as continual sentinels, like living scarecrows keeping an eye on the horizons, whether on tower tops, watchtowers, or city walls. They weren’t terribly smart, basically only as capable as they were ‘programmed,’ but Ash got better and better, through trial and error.

At first, they were kept on the backlines, everyone dubious about their effectiveness and ‘temperament’ of programming, but Ash soon won Dax over on their efficiency and effectiveness as sentries, as well as their baked-in docility to perceived allies. They began getting placed further and further afield until they were a part of the regular army, even if in small numbers, and deliberately cloaked to be less visually disturbing.

They did need a lot of ‘smell mitigation’ precautions, too.

Ash, despite being thrilled at proving ‘positive use’ for necromancy, suffered mightily in the sleep department, because she was permanently worried over some mistake happening in her programming and someone getting killed over it.

Once Samantha caught wind of this, she ordered alternates found so someone could at least work the (ahem) graveyard shift, while she rested — two or three at a minimum. At first, Ash was resistant and paranoid about the arrangement, but when word got around, suddenly they had multiple practicing necromancers step forward, encouraged by what they’d seen from the use of the zombies.

These came from amongst Southlanders, primarily. There were a few human witches, an elf or two, some Naugites, and a very sizable number of Frogmen. As it turned out, a few of their offensive specialists were using disguised necromantic attacks and had even been ‘undoing’ the control of zombies during the battle of Matrazere under the guise of spirit- or mind-based attacks.

They were now spoiled for choices and had a bunch of necromancers asking to begin practicing openly. Samantha wasn’t opposed, with restrictions.

Five alternates were chosen by Ash, and she seemed to have far fewer nerves than before. Four of them were highly-skilled Frogmen, the fifth an elven witch who claimed to also be able to help with monitoring ‘improper behavior.’

And so Samantha had a ‘necromancer core.’ Never would she have thought she’d have anything of the sort, but she’d claimed to be a pragmatist, after all. The copious numbers of regeneration potions floating around her army, including those so potent they were nearly priceless artifacts a month ago — now in the hands of her elites — was evidence enough of that particular fact. She’d only obtained that through the sanctioning of very grisly, secret arts.

She’d do it again, though. It had saved thousands of precious lives at what cost? None, in her mind.

In any case, raising the dead was a slightly different matter. Her own opinion did go the way of Ash’s — bodies were left-behind rotting flesh sacks — but it was a political question, as beliefs and desires for those rotting flesh sacks differed. Some still considered them sacred. The more active necromancy was done, and the more openly it was done, the more chances of disagreeing parties. Despite the occasional heretical elf, most elves abhorred necromancy and might object to doing it actively, even if they turned a blind eye to the unusual situation of utilizing existing, ‘stranded’ undead.

At the bare minimum, she did like having such experts on tap, though. Many extraneous benefits and spell specialties were left in the dark, now potentially brought to the light. She planned on treating this group with respect, bestowing guarantees, protections, and official authorizations on them, and otherwise making use of their talents, regardless of their position of actively raising the dead.

Azure was busy, too, because they finally had the number of ‘bedrock points’ mentioned by the Consolidator as necessary to forge the key they needed to obtain the First Page, Glae’s precious ingredient. Azure led multiple synchronized projects with teams at each location to excavate down to the foundations at her direction. She was more and more certain about what she was doing as she went along, even more so when she cut her first sliver from Itha Ranon’s founding stone and messaged Samantha excitedly, “I’ve got it! I did it! I can do this!”

And similar was communicated from the next one, at the ‘Sun Temple’ in the jungle near Yura, along the new canal that went through Redberry’s territory.

She began knocking the Transition Points out in sequence…

With four locations complete — tiny slivers cut from huge, buried enchanted stone blocks — Azure walked up from the coast of the Bay of Mengior, headed for the fifth Transition Point, underneath a place she truly reviled.

Here again. Likely for the last time.

Her eyes flickered down to the coast, where she knew the cave was, and she grimaced.

It wasn’t real. What is, lies above… and below.

The rocky cliff she climbed wouldn’t need much foundation, as the temple grounds could’ve just been carved right out of it — after all, these shores had endured the battering of countless storms over history. Weaker material had been scoured away.

They hadn’t done any mining here; instead, a mercenary earth elementalist had felt around at her direction and almost certainly found exactly what they were looking for. At that point, he’d moved the ground temporarily to get at the stone. Azure came simply to confirm all was correct, get a feel of things directly, and give the final okay to cut the sliver.

She crested the top of the pathway, as she had long ago when she’d first given herself into the clutches of the despicable creatures known as the Changers.

Unusual movement caught her attention, by a crop of stone a good distance from the ruins. She couldn’t tell what it was, but it was clearly good cover to hide in.

She was also aware of a couple of somewhat suspicious seagulls flying around above. One of them looked right at her, and she felt a communication in her mind from him — obviously a Faerie Dragon scout. “A couple of kids hiding there, trying not to be seen as they spy the goings on in the ruins. They haven’t made nuisances of themselves, so we left them alone.”

Azure grinned faintly at the news. My childhood friend and I weren’t a far stretch, were we, Goldie? “That’s all fine and dandy,” was all she replied to the scout as she continued.

There was a deep pit in the ruins, with a bunch of stacked up, precision-cut stone off to the side, currently surrounded by a few guards and helpers waiting for her. Azure cast a flight spell.

Before she flew down, she shifted her gaze so that the outcrop cover where the kids were hiding would be just seen in her peripheral vision. Once more, she caught a subtle movement as someone ducked down.

Smiling to herself and shaking her head, she then lowered herself down into the pit of scarce sediment and mostly uniform gray stone, the area lit by a simple ‘orb’ light cantrip. The mercenary earth elementalist, a cloaked man wearing a metal mask, nodded to her as she gently landed, her eyes quickly noticing the singular sectioned off portion of stone he was standing next to, as large as a small room.

The stone all around held minor preservational and hardening enchantments, likely difficult to detect from the surface. Instead, over long, long periods, the magic bled into the common stone, making it seem ‘naturally’ harder and pulling mana toward the area. The Changers had likely built there because of that, and then pulled and focused the mana more by their own designs. What had been there before them wasn’t at all clear. Certainly, there were no catacombs, but that was hardly surprising on a stormy coast in the Land of Naug.

“Found it easy enough?” Azure asked idly as she approached the founding stone, now about 99% sure it was the correct spot.

“One of the easier jobs I’ve had in my life, honestly,” the man replied in a thick Southlander accent. “My Lady. I appreciate your dutifully exact instructions, by the way.”

Azure paused just before touching the stone, cocking her head at his accent. “Are you from Zek Sephel?”

“Mmn. Good ear. Hehe.” He chuckled a bit nervously. “Don’t tell anyone, would you?” It had ironic tones, probably realizing Servitors were listening in. “I’m not ‘of’ that land, if you fancy a difference.”

Somewhat amused, Azure nodded in understanding. “You can’t help where you’re from. An outcast, perhaps?”

“If the Goddess razed that overgrown hellscape back where it belongs, I would shed no tears. I might even throw a prayer her way.”

“How generous. I could put in a word for permanent recruitment, if you like. I might be something of an insider, you could say.”

“I’m aware.” He paused and shifted uncomfortably. “I have an aversion to contracts, I’m afraid.”

“I know the feeling. Consider staying on, though. We’ll work with you, whatever your situation and predelections. The pay is unmatched, as I’m sure you’re aware.” Shrugging in dismissal of the matter, Azure finally reached her hand out to the founding stone. As soon as she touched it, though, a surprising and weird vibe shot through her, making her hiss and pull her hand back, as if she had touched a hot stove.

What the-?!
