Luke’s eyes narrowed as he took in the state of Lyon Marxx, second prince of the Marxx Kingdom. His hair was matted with mud, his face bruised, and his once-regal robes reduced to tattered rags. Were it not for the fact that Luke had been expecting him, he might not have even recognized the man.

The once difficult, irascible noble was now reduced to a broken shell.

But what truly shocked Luke was what came next—Lyon staggered forward and threw his arms around him in a desperate embrace. His robes were damp and filthy, and the stench clinging to him was nearly unbearable. It was as if he hadn’t bathed in months.

The sensory assault was immediate. Luke could feel the frail, quivering body pressed against his own, Lyon’s chest rising and falling in ragged sobs.

“Thank you, Luke…” Lyon whispered between gasps.

Just three words—but the pain behind them told an entire story. For a man who had likely never known true suffering until now, this moment would leave a lasting scar.

Rather than pull away or scold him, Luke lifted a hesitant hand and patted the prince’s back. The gesture was awkward, but genuine.

“You did well to stay alive,” he said softly, letting the truth in his voice speak for itself.

He could have offered comforting words, false promises that everything would be fine. But that would’ve been a lie. The mission had only just begun, and time was already slipping through their fingers.

Luke gently stepped back, steadying Lyon as the man nearly collapsed from exhaustion.

“Prince Lyon. Richard. We need to talk,” he said calmly.

There were urgent matters to address—questions that couldn’t wait. Without waiting for their response, he launched into the first one, a knot forming in his stomach.

“Who’s in charge of the camp? Is it Selis or Lhair?”

Richard scratched the back of his head, his expression sheepish. “I’m sorry, General. We don’t know. The soldiers are all wearing different types of armor—like a patchwork militia.”

Luke’s brow furrowed.

Are they disguising themselves to throw off scouts? But if that were the case, why not just wear Lhair’s colors?

Then it hit him. If these troops were seen wearing Lhair armor deep in Qin territory, someone would’ve sounded the alarm.

“And what about the commander?” Luke asked, unease creeping into his voice.

“He wears a beard that looks like it’s been dyed in blood,” Richard said, his voice dropping. “And his helmet’s iron, with a cross-shaped slit. I never saw his face—but something about him gave me a terrible feeling…”

Luke’s breath caught in his throat.

“Holy Knight,” he muttered, swearing under his breath. His entire body tensed.

This is bad.

If it was anything like the Holy Knight’s he’d encountered in Clayton City, their chances had just plummeted. His entire force was unarmed. If that man attacked, they’d be slaughtered like livestock.

Tristan might stand a chance, Luke thought grimly. But I’m not sure how skilled he really is with weapons...

“General… I don’t think we have to worry about the Holy Knight,” Richard said after a pause. “He didn’t seem that strong.”

Luke didn’t answer. His hand reached for his knapsack, brushing against the two thunder bombs he’d yet to use. If he could catch the knight by surprise and kill him in one blow, that would be ideal.

But the downpour complicated things. Lighting the rope in this rain would be nearly impossible.

“Take the prince west until you reach our camp,” Luke ordered at last, his tone firm. “Neither of you are in any condition to help in the fight.”

While having Richard on the battlefield would’ve been a boon, the man was clearly running on fumes—and more importantly, he was without that monstrous greatsword. As he was now, he’d be far more valuable protecting Lyon.

Thankfully, Richard didn’t argue. Lyon looked as if he wanted to, but the sight of the hulking man’s hand clamping firmly onto his shoulder quickly silenced him.

The two began trudging westward, leaving Luke behind.

He exhaled slowly, closing his eyes.

“Eagle Eye,” he muttered.

His consciousness soared above the canopy, rising through the rain and into the sky. From this bird’s-eye view, the forest stretched endlessly below, cloaked in shadow. The campfires had long since died out in the rain, leaving the entire area shrouded in gloom.

Visibility was poor—but not impossible.

Luke narrowed his gaze, sweeping over the camp’s center. That was usually where command tents were placed.

Sure enough, he spotted it. A much larger canvas tent stood amidst the others—taller, wider, and sturdy enough to house at least four men comfortably.

“Be ready to sound the retreat on my command,” he said aloud, voice low but resolute, his attention still locked on the distant image from above.

If the Holy Knight emerged, their window of opportunity would vanish in an instant. Luke could only hope the man remained asleep long enough for them to do their work.

That was when it hit him—a shiver up his spine, instinctive and visceral.

This wasn’t from the cold.

A deep, primal warning surged through him, and his eyes widened in alarm. Something was… wrong.

From a small opening in the command tent, an inky black substance began to spill out, thick and slow. More of it followed, pooling at first before swelling in size, coalescing like an oil slick come to life.

His instincts screamed at him to run. Every fiber of his being urged him to flee.

And for a moment—just a moment—he nearly gave in.

If he didn’t have the Dragon’s Heart skill, he might’ve run.

But the skill steadied him, dulling the edge of panic, and he held his ground.

The black substance writhed unnaturally, untouched by the falling rain. Slowly, impossibly, it began to take shape—black scales forming one after another, gleaming like polished obsidian as they slotted into place like armor.

A serpentine body coiled into being, winding in on itself.

Then came the eyes—two glowing red slits that blazed to life at the head of the forming creature. A long, flickering tongue uncurled from its maw, tasting the air.

Luke staggered back, breath caught in his throat.

A snake!?

The thought was immediate—instinctual. But this… this thing wasn’t just a snake. No ordinary creature gave off this kind of pressure. This was something else entirely. Something wrong.

Unexpectedly, a deep loathing surged within Luke as he stared at the creature. It was visceral, rising unbidden from somewhere deep in his soul. The instinct that had once told him to flee... now urged him to fight.

Then—a familiar chime echoed in his mind, sharp and sudden.

Before he could even register it, the snake's massive head whipped upward. Its glowing red eyes locked onto something far above—onto him.

What!? It can see me?

A jolt of panic shot through Luke. He had always believed Eagle Eye projected only his awareness, not his physical presence. So how had he been discovered?

The inky-black serpent remained still, tongue flicking intermittently as its glowing gaze bored into the sky.

For a few tense seconds, Luke dared to hope. Maybe it’s just sensing something—maybe it doesn’t truly see me.

The knot in his stomach loosened slightly.

But the illusion was short-lived.

Without warning, the coiled beast lifted off the ground. It floated into the air with unnatural grace, gliding upward as if the very sky were solid ground beneath it. The sight shattered Luke’s sense of normalcy. His mind faltered, struggling to comprehend what he was witnessing.

As it approached, the loathing within him intensified. It wasn’t fear now—it was something older. Something primal. An urge to rip the creature apart took root in his chest, growing stronger with each passing moment. His heart beat faster, the compulsion almost overwhelming.

He clenched his jaw, forcing himself to remain still. The snake was coming closer. He could feel it.

But oddly, it didn’t seem poised to strike.

Then, a voice slithered through the air—sickly and hollow, each word dripping with malice.

“What are you doing here?” the creature asked, stopping midair twenty feet from Luke’s ethereal position. “Did the Lord send you?”

The Lord? Who is it talking about? Luke’s thoughts raced.

His mind shifted gears. Scholarly Pursuit activated on instinct, urging him to seize the opportunity. If this thing thought he was someone else—he could use that.

Luke opened his mouth, and what came out was not his voice.

“The Lord wishes to know your progress. He is tired of waiting…”

Even as he spoke, he almost flinched. The voice that echoed from his lips was deep and ancient, imbued with a weight that made his own words sound foreign. It rumbled like distant thunder—imperious and cold.

It was the kind of voice he imagined a dragon would use to address ants.

The snake recoiled. Its massive form shuddered, coiling tighter in midair. A loud hiss escaped it, laced with fear and alarm.

“I gave my report just a few days ago! Did something happen to Victor? I knew that damned idiot would get himself killed!”

It cursed violently, body writhing in agitation. Then, more cautiously, it began to slither around him in a slow, circling motion. Luke stayed still, heart pounding in his chest even though he knew this wasn’t his physical body.

The serpent’s red eyes scanned him with eerie curiosity.

“I haven’t seen you around before,” it said in a more measured tone. “Are you one of the Lord’s new subordinates?” It paused, studying him closer. “To think… your soul form is that of a dragon. Your powers must be quite impressive.”

Luke froze.

Soul form? A dragon?

This must be some kind of ability he received from that ‘Lord’ he mentioned…Could it be the Bishop of Lhair?The same one the system mentioned during the battle against Lhair?

Luke’s thoughts spun in the background, analyzing the implications.

If the Holy Knights could communicate like this—through some kind of soul projection or spiritual channel—it meant they could relay information instantly.

How far did this ability extend? Across cities? Kingdoms?

If so, the threat they posed was far greater than he’d realized.

“They are satisfactory enough for the Lord,” Luke said, his deep, thunderous voice reverberating across the firmament.

The snake recoiled again, visibly unsettled. It wasn’t just fear—it was submission. As if some ingrained hierarchy compelled it to obey.

Luke straightened his stance.

“Now, tell me of your progress,” he commanded, letting his tone sharpen. “The Lord’s patience is vast—but not infinite.”

He tried to sound natural, authoritative. Each word calculated. His goal wasn’t just to delay—it was to distract. To keep the Holy Knight off balance long enough for the infiltration to succeed.

Still, he knew he had to walk a razor’s edge. Too much deviation from expectation, and the ruse would crumble.

The snake let out a long, raspy hiss that teetered between laughter and rot. Its sickly voice returned, more at ease now—its suspicion seemingly quelled.

“We’re a day’s march from the Wu River. The main force is set to meet us upstream in a week—provided nothing’s gone wrong.”It tilted its grotesque head, tongue flicking thoughtfully.“They are still coming, right?”

Luke felt his heart drop.

If he’d been in his body, his knees might’ve buckled. This was it—confirmation.Lhair was planning to march on Ralis City.

His instincts had been right all along.

And his rage—real and righteous—bled into his performance.

“You dare question the Lord’s plan, you fool!?” he bellowed.

The sheer force of his voice shook the very air, thunderous and wrathful. Whether by instinct or panic, the snake coiled tight, cowering beneath his spiritual pressure.

“N-No! That’s not what I meant!”

“I… I only wanted to confirm the plan hadn’t changed. Please—don’t punish me on the Lord’s behalf, General…”The snake’s voice trembled with desperation.

Luke didn’t reply. Not yet.

His mind was working fast, connecting pieces. Ralis was under threat. The attack wasn’t just possible—it was imminent.

He glanced down—his Eagle Eye still active—and caught sight of his own men, moving silently through the camp. They now wielded weapons, moving among the tents.

Then he noticed it: the snake’s head began to tilt… downward.

Shit. He’s about to spot them!

“You insolent worm!” Luke roared, channeling every ounce of fury he could muster.

The serpent froze in place, paralyzed by the sheer dominance in his voice.

“Were it not for our Lord’s magnanimity, I would tear your soul to ribbons and cast it into the abyss. You shame his power with your impertinent drivel!”

His eyes flicked downward again—and there, just outside the large command tent, was Tristan.

The man was moving with precision, blade in hand, nearing the tent’s entrance like a ghost through the mist.

Come on... almost there.

The snake trembled, completely subjugated.

“Th-thank you for your mercy, General,” it stammered, curling tighter.

Luke’s breath hitched—he was on the cusp of relief. Just a little longer and—

But then it happened.

The moment Tristan stepped through the tent’s entrance, the serpent’s entire body went rigid. Its enormous head jerked to the side, snapping toward the command tent.
