I didn’t dare look at the people on my sides. I stared straight ahead, feeling the eyes land on me from all around. It made sense. These people wanted no part in a war between Denarla and Eschal! Many of them had friends from the country! Our last count was nearly six thousand souls behind this wall, and the northern and eastern trenches. All of them could be bought with just one life.

I gulped.

Would I feel heavy hands on my shoulders? I didn’t think any of the townsfolk would do it… Except the miller. The miller probably would. But would they have a choice? Neogon would–!

“Chett!” came a booming roar from behind me, and I turned to see General Nadir shouting down over the battlements. “Show the lady what we think about ransoms here in Tacuria!”

“Y-Yes sir!” came a reply a small way down the line. The man held up his hand and appeared to strain.

I jumped. I’d been actively expecting something to happen, but still I jumped, horrified as sound crashed through my eardrums.

I was partially blinded, seeing spots. I almost missed what happened. I wasn’t alone. Everyone had jumped. Even Nadir, who’d commanded the order.

It was a natural reaction.

Chett had called down fucking lightning.

“How!?” I wondered, staring at the bald soldier in awe. He looked half dead. His face was grim, and his palms were shaking. Unfortunately, he seemed to be in worse shape than his target.

A circle of burning grass surrounded her where the lightning struck, but within Freyna remained pristine.

It was too much to hope that she might die so easily.

“Ah… I see. You had a gift for me after all,” the voice sounded in my ears, muffled by the ringing.

The woman was staring at the wall. It should’ve been hard to tell from that distance, but I could see what she was looking at anyway. Chett. She knew exactly where the lightning had come from.

The man was gasping, trembling. I shuddered as I saw his eyes, unnatural light burned within, making them glow in the evening light. Living lightning, caged like a pet, danced between his fingertips.

“How!?” Nadir asked. “How could she have blocked lightning?”

“Best to assume just about anything is possible from that woman,” I said. “She’s able to fly, has wind magic that can strike from hundreds of yards away, and her voice can burst eardrums. The only reason she isn’t doing that is because I don’t think she can target us without hitting her own people. I also think she can control the rift monsters… somehow. Who knows what else she can do.”

The general turned to me, eyes narrowed in suspicion, but he merely nodded.

“By dawn, your town will be nothing but rubble, and I’ll have two new pets. Just a little more now. One more loss. One more moment of doubt, and you’ll be mine,” the woman said. It was unnerving, hearing her voice even though she was so far away it was difficult to see her.

I had no idea what she was talking about. She was speaking nonsense. Riddles. One more moment of doubt? We all had plenty of doubts. What was one more, at this point? Who was she even talking to?

Why was she talking at all?

“You hate it so. I know you do. You hate what you’ve become. Hate how you’ve been forced to use it. Don’t be afraid. I care for the lost. But I believe in fair play. A gift for a gift, and yours is a mighty one,” she said.

Suddenly, the floating hostages began to accelerate. None of them moved or woke, but they flew all the same. As they grew closer, it became clear that an inexplicable updraft of wind was the cause, their hair and clothes floating upward as if buffeted by a massive fan from below.

I ducked as the bodies rocketed over the wall, and then abruptly stopped, just behind it. Still thirty feet in the air, hovering there.

“Your lost comrades. What few of them remain,” came the dark words, stilted with sudden strain. She was using wind magic to hold all of the hostages aloft, but even she couldn’t keep it up for long.

I immediately slammed 100 points into the Jumping skill before dashing to the edge of the wall. Other soldiers, even quicker than I was, had already realized what was about to happen, and many of them leaped before I did.

Not enough, though.

The wind stopped, and the bodies fell.

I caught a man who was the size of a walrus mid fall. Jumping was a well known skill and high values in it naturally allowed one to land without injury. It wasn’t a magic buffer protecting knees and hips from collapse, but just a general knowledge of the best way to set feet to minimize impact. Of course, all skills felt a little bit like magic when they got past a certain point.

I grasped this instinctively and still screamed in pain when I landed in a roll on a downwards slope. My legs didn’t snap, and hard as we landed, I thought I avoided the worst of the fall.

Anyone who’d been quick enough to leap for their fellow soldiers had managed to save them.

Some hadn’t been so lucky.

Legs burning, but thankfully not broken, I rolled the hulking soldier off of me and knelt over him. I was about to place two fingers to his neck before I spotted the soft rise and fall of his chest. He was alive. Cold and pale, but alive. He had to be drugged with something. No one could’ve slept through all of that without something keeping them under.

A healer rushed up beside me moments later, screaming something in my ear. It took a moment to parse the words, the bolt of lightning still leaving a ringing noise.

Thankfully, the woman hadn’t used her sound skill to wreck my eardrums. Terrifying as her wind magic, and poison was, the sound attack was the one that scared me the most.

“Get out of the way!” the healer cried, gently pushing me off the man before he lifted his hand and a green light burst forth.

I scrambled away and got back to my aching feet, as realization crawled up my spine.

A line of men and women spaced evenly apart had formed around each of the fallen hostages.

Some of them hadn’t been as lucky as this man. The girl three paces to my right had landed on her neck. Sightless eyes stared up at the evening sky.

Gods.

I turned to look further down the line. I thought I’d spotted Berkenem three or four men over, and he was surrounded by two people. Part of me wanted to go see if he was okay. A larger part didn’t want to know.

I turned back to the wall. It no longer seemed quite so high.

I leaped, and stood once more on the rampart. To my surprise, Freyna had retreated back into the tree line and was no longer visible.

‘By dawn, huh?’ I thought. ‘We’ll see.’

“Lady Elmerina,” came Nadir’s voice, and I made my way back to his side quickly.

“Why?” I asked, with no preamble. “Why turn her down? Much… much as I don’t want to be given to her as a captive, it could’ve saved the lives of everyone here! Everyone I care about!”

‘Everyone you truly care about is gone, already.’ The thought came unbidden and I felt guilty for it. There were many people I loved here, but that was true. Everyone I loved the most was already well away from here. Mom, Dad, Reid, Hadra, Oxana? All of them were probably far safer than I was at the moment.

I didn’t want to die, and I certainly didn’t want to become a captive of that woman. But the general hadn’t even entertained the idea!

“Don’t get the wrong idea. I don’t agree with most of your opinions. I don’t even think you should be at our meetings to voice them. You’re a child, and war is no place for a child. However… I am not blind to the love this town holds for you. You’re their beating heart, and if we surrendered you, we would likely face a mutiny, even in the face of certain death. Giving you up was never an option.”

His face remained sour through the entire speech. The hysterical image of him as a pickle in human form entered my mind. Helmet, breastplate, and all, sour as anyone I’d ever met.

“Pure pragmatism then,” I said.

“Aye.”

“Well, thank the gods for pragmatism.”

He nodded. “Well done with the jump. I didn’t realize you were also a dragoon.”

I flushed. Until a few minutes ago, I hadn’t been.

“Did the soldier live?” he asked.

“Uh… y-yes,” I said, suddenly on the back foot.

“Good.”

The Lieutenant General turned and looked back out over the clearing as the sun began to fall beneath the horizon. There didn’t appear to be any mustering, but Freyna’s threat remained heavy in the air.

The town would be rubble by dawn.

They would attack tonight. Tonight, we’d fight for our lives, and this time, I didn’t think we’d escape unscathed.

What could I do? Was there any way I could help us? We needed better fortifications. I couldn’t help but think that if the wall stretched all the way around the town, then even Freyna wouldn’t feel nearly so confident.

An inkling of an idea began to form, but before it could, I caught sight of Chett further down the line.

He was staring down at the fallen soldiers behind the wall. One in particular seemed to have drawn his eye. A girl. My age. She’d been caught and rescued from the fall, but it had been for naught.

Her throat was cut. No longer bleeding. She was dead before she’d been sent over the wall.

Chett was trembling.

A sick feeling began to form in the pit of my stomach.

Freyna had acted like she’d only just discovered the lightning mage, but she’d clearly prepared for him. She’d expected an attack from him. Encouraged it, even.

Now someone he cared for was confirmed dead.

Lightning rippled along his uniform. Tiny sparks briefly lit his hollow, broken eyes.

I watched him run toward the ladders and climb down them. Instead of running to the girl, though, he fled desperately into the town.

“Chett! Soldier! Get back here!” Cried a sergeant.

The man didn’t stop.

“Gods be damned, of all the times… Private Umeldrei, go help him!” the sergeant ordered. “Get him a bed and some soup. I’ve got a feeling we’re going to need him tonight.”

“Yes, sir,” responded an older private before she took off after the fleeing lightning mage.

I had a very bad feeling about this.
