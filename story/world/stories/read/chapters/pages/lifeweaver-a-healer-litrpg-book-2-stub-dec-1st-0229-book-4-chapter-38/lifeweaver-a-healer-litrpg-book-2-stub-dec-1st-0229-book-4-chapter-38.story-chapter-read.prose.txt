Feeling a bit bad about killing the Singer party's mage, Luke decided against closing the portal and trapping them in the dungeon. Having seen what it did to Priyah, how she was still keeping close to... Nash. Damn.

He pushed the thought aside and focused, gathering the weave around him, on the lookout for more Singer threads. When he found none, he pushed outward, leaving the dungeon weave behind. Accessing The Greater Weave took some doing from this place, but in the end, it appeared before his inner eye. Luke half expected to see uncountable flashes of light blue, pale light, and red streaking in every direction, but the whole thing was nothing more than a dark, cohesive pattern where nothing stood out, like skills he had yet to unlock in a game. Perhaps that was it. Perhaps he hadn't grown strong enough or knowledgeable enough to extend his perception of other Integrated beyond a local system. What a pain.

Echo threads were still available, but that wasn't what he was after, not this time. Instead, he stepped through the portal and emerged into what appeared to be a square, tent-like structure, except with rigid walls, erected over and around the portal. Singers guarded the entrance, and even more of them had formed a line past the entrance, like they were waiting for their turn to enter. A hush fell over the crowd as Luke emerged, and their attention made him feel like they were waiting for him to speak. Fresh out of speeches, he instead stepped through the weave to a random Singer.

This one wore what looked like ceremonial robes and sat at an oval table far up in a building where the windows overlooked a sprawling metropolis. Eleven more Singers wearing identical clothing stopped what they were doing as all turned to face him, eyes going wide.

"You are all Level one," Luke said, glancing through a window into the corridor beyond, and the office-looking space beyond that. "Is it so you can get food and water from the Integrated Shop?"

Rather than answer his question in a polite manner, they all stood and screamed, jabbering in a language he didn't understand as they fled deeper into the building. Rude. Then again, perhaps it was a bit rich to expect every single Singer to be speaking English. This was their world, after all. Not that he needed their answer. Luke was already pretty sure he had the right of it. Why else would he be appearing in front of a bunch of Singer Integrated who hadn't gained a single level? They were doing the same thing Earth was: securing a never-ending supply of food for everyone.

The news of his arrival in the building seemed to spread because the screams multiplied, and the sound of people running took a while to quiet down. After maybe five minutes, the place was quiet as the grave. This was just what he'd been looking for, and Luke took the Singer's chair and swiveled around a few times before stopping to face the window, taking in the view. The city was huge, with wide streets and tall buildings as far as the eye could see. Small dots of movement down below showed Singers out and about, and all of them seemed to be walking. Maybe they didn't have cars. A couple of trains moved on a raised rail system that saw them drive through tunnels in buildings with incredible speed.

"Neat," Luke said to himself before closing his eyes. He wasn't here for sightseeing, and there was work to be done.

As he delved into the Singer system weave, the whole thing lit up like a Christmas tree with an uncountable number of Integrated. Luke found he could use his will to choose whether to highlight different types of threads. Once the pale lights of Integrated no longer blocked his metaphysical vision, the portals stood out, much easier to see. Frowning, he tried his best to get an approximation of their number despite knowing that perceiving all of them at the same time was beyond his skill. Even so, there were hundreds of them. Far too many.

"This isn't right."

Even so, it was his job to close them. The sound of Singers running echoed through the empty corridor. This time, they were approaching him rather than fleeing in the other direction. Guards? Maybe. Luke wasn't about to stick around to find out. Reaching out, he picked a portal thread and weavestepped. Another tent-like structure appeared. Not a platform. The ground was covered in moss, and several tree stumps surrounded the portal. Singers on guard again. This time, Luke needed mana, so he took theirs. It wasn't much of a fight, but his T-shirt got covered in blood, and it was starting to fall into tatters, having been pierced a couple of times.

"Need time for enchantments," Luke told a harvested meta-heart before putting it away. "Also, my robe."

Stepping through the portal again, he appeared at yet another dungeon portal. Only then did he realize his mistake. A dungeon portal was not the same as a Singer-made one leading to Earth. Of course, it wasn't. Sighing, he kept an eye on the Singers who, to their credit, were backing away from him.

Luke (Guild): "Anyone near a portal back to Earth right now?"

Hannah (Guild): "We've moved away from the platform."

Milla (Guild): "Three teams have been forced to retreat back to Earth. The remaining teams have pushed deeper but are being told to keep the portal in sight. Unsurprisingly, a lot of orders are going ignored. Yay."

Milla (Guild): "Also, Raphael is still here waiting for you."

Lemira (Guild): "We are still guarding the portal from our side. You could come here and go through."

Luke (Guild): "Right. Good idea."

Luke (Guild): "I won't be needing Raphael. I think."

Said and done, Luke reached for Lemira's echo thread and stepped through the weave, enjoying the fact that the pain from touching a thread had been turned down to what amounted to little more than an annoyance at that point. Progress, if there was ever an indication for it.

The Earth side of the portal was an oasis of calm. Integrated had spread out across the immediate area, and Luke saw scouts positioned farther out. Lemira dangled her legs off the edge of a crate as she hummed a tune that sounded a lot like Twinkle, Twinkle Little Star with a faraway look on her face.

"Hey," Luke said. "All well over here?"

Lemira's eyes regained focus, and she looked at him with a smile. "Greetings, stranger. The Singers mounted an initial assault that failed. They have not returned since. I have a few companions tracking a group moving away from here." She pursed her lips. "I think they are retreating to a different portal."

"If they're going to blow us up, it makes sense to get their own people the hell away," Luke said with a thoughtful nod. "That's bad news for us."

"That would be my interpretation as well."

They shared a couple of seconds of silence before Luke broke away and pointed toward the portal. "I've got to go. Sorry, I haven't been around much, but you know what we've been dealing with. Once the Singers are gone, we'll catch up, yeah?"

Lemira nodded. "I will hold you to that."

Walking through the portal and back into the Singer system, Luke found another human on the other side. Sam stood on the edge of the platform, keeping an eye on the ramp. At first, she didn't notice him approach, and when she did notice and turn around to face him, a weight settled in his stomach. While her face was as expressionless as ever, the swollen, red eyes and flushed cheeks betrayed the emotion beneath her stoic facade.

"Sam," Luke said.

"Luke."

"I'm sorry about your brother."

She nodded and turned back toward the ramp. When she spoke, her voice trembled. "I miss him." Then, as if anticipating what Luke was thinking, she added, "It wasn't your fault."

"Thank you."

"I'd like to be alone now."

"Yes, of course," Luke said as he retreated back to the portal. "Be careful."

Focusing on the task at hand proved difficult, but in the end, he managed to separate the portal's thread and identify it as different from the dungeon portal ones. Another color appeared. This time, it was a light purple.

Ray: "I think I've found something."

Luke: "Relian?"

Ray: "Hell no."

Ray: "I'm talking about the weapon. It's some chick. A Singer. They ordered her to make one, I think. It was hard to get close enough. They've brought her to a hangar-type place. Haven't found a way in without being spotted just yet, but guess who's also around and just followed her inside?"

Luke: "Who?"

Ray: "That Lifeweaver guy! Luke, he's wearing your robe."

Luke: "You're shitting me?"

Ray: "I am a thousand percent serious. Dude is walking around like he owns the place."

Ray: "I really want to charge in and kill him."

Ray: "Can I? Pretty please."

Luke: "Don't. Stay close, but out of sight. If you can get in and scope the place out, great. If not, just don't get spotted. We'll come to you."

Ray: "Cool. The Lifeweaver had better not get away, though. He has my dagger, too. I'm sure of it."

Ray: "Anyway. I think we still have a little time since she just went inside. Give me like thirty minutes to find a way in."

Luke grinned. Thirty minutes would be plenty of time to finish testing the new purple threads and then gather the muscle needed for a serious assault against whatever they were up to in that hangar. With a little luck, they might even get a glimpse of how the Singers intended to fire a weapon from their system and hit Earth. Things were coming to a head, and despite it all, Luke found himself excited. It was finally time to end the Singer invasion once and for all and show them Earth would not be stepped on. Not by anybody.
