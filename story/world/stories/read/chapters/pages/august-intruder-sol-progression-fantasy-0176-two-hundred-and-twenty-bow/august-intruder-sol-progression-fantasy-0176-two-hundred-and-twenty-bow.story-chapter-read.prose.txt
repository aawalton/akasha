“You have got to be fucking kidding me,” Inevitability swore under his breath, snatching his keys from his office table.

He made his way out of his office, slightly fuming. As he walked, he dialed another number on his cell phone and put it to his ear. He waited as it rang. There was no answer. When it finished ringing out, he called again.

“Come on,” he growled. “Pick up.”

The familiar clicking sound of someone picking up was like music to his ears.

“Nkechi,” he said, trying for a soft and gentle voice to hide his frustration. “How has it been?”

He really hated small talk.

“I’ve been good,” she answered from the other end. “To what do I owe the pleasure, Chetam.”

“I need a teleport.”

“You have a private jet, Chetam,” she said with a sigh. “Use it.”

“If a private jet was as fast as a teleport, I would’ve used it already. I need to get to a destination in the blink of an eye. What I need is a teleport.”

“Can’t help you,” she said simply.

Inevitability bit down on his irritation. “I know, Nkechi. I need you to help me talk to Vanor. He’ll listen. He’s not picking my calls.”

“That’s because he’s currently on a mission in Zanzibar. He’s clearing out the fire bat’s nest.” There was a sound of shuffling. It sounded like papers being shuffled. “Once he’s done there, there are rumors that a tiger looking monster has begun, somehow, growing a pride of tigers. Who would’ve thought that was possible?”

“It’s called an ambush or streak of tigers, Nkechi,” he muttered, only barely keeping his voice under his breath.

“I’m talking about a pride, Chetam,” Nkechi said. “An ambush or streak is a group. I’m talking about a team of tigers moving around together. Anyway, Vanor is busy, from Zanzibar he’s going to Malaysia. In case you didn’t know, teleporters are kind of hot cakes these days.”

“I know, Nkechi,” he groaned. “Do you have anyone else in mind? Another valid teleporter?”

He got into the elevator. It was his and his alone in the office. He moved to press the button for the ground floor and hesitated. He looked at the button to the roof and wondered if the pilot was around. Going to the helipad would definitely be faster for his plans.

“I don’t have one,” she answered. “Maybe you can use another one of your many contacts, Chetam.”

Inevitability sighed when Nkechi hung up. Nkechi had been his teammate once upon a time, in the earlier days of his life as a Delver. They had been… close. Inevitability shook his head as he pressed the button for the roof.

Helipad it was.

As the elevator rose, he turned his head to the side, stared at the numbers on the interface waiting patiently for him.

[12813km]

How long was the [Visitor] going to wait? Was the [Visitor] here to stay? What did the [Visitor] want? There were far too many questions in his head.

But more importantly, it was safer to assume that the [August Intruder] was there with the [Visitor].

Shitty time to not have a teleporter on hand.

In truth, teleporters were not very easy to come by. And there weren’t that many that could teleport across great distances. He could probably count how many he knew on one hand.

Three. He knew three.

He dialed another number as the elevator came to a stop and put it to his ear. The person on the other end picked after one ring.

“Deoti,” he greeted. “I need your help.”

“Kind of busy right now,” she answered to a backdrop of cheers and grunts. “Who’s this?”

Inevitability rubbed his eyes in exasperation. “It’s Inevitability.”

“Does Madness know you’re talking to me?”

“Madness does not—”

“Bye.”

She hung up on him, and Inevitability did his best to hold down his annoyance. This was the problem with talking to those who worked under Madness, in their own ways they were almost just as mad.

It was his fault for taking a roundabout method when… he looked at his contact list and remembered why he had not just called Fendor directly. He did not have the man’s phone number.

He stepped out of the elevator and looked at the helicopter waiting for him there. There was no pilot sitting in place. There was no pilot anywhere.

His day could not get any worse.

Grumbling to himself, he dialed another number as he walked over to the helicopter. He really hoped his jet was ready for take off. Climbing into the helicopter. He strapped himself into the pilot seat.

If you want something done, sometimes you’ve got to do it yourself.

But the rule only applied to some instances. God knew there was nothing he would be able to do about it if he got to the airport and his plane wasn’t ready for takeoff.

Twelve thousand eight hundred and twelve kilometers, he thought. He’d already checked the map. Madness had not been lying, not that he had thought the Oath was lying to him, though. As the chopper rose, he switched apps on his phone while the call he’d placed to the airport continued to ring. He pulled up the map and fixed the exact location.

Tatelat, he thought, already aware of it. Zooming in with two fingers as he steered the helicopter, he looked at the precise location and frowned. This part, he hadn’t known.

Fallen High.

It just had to be a school.

“Let’s hope this visitor is friendly,” he muttered to himself.
