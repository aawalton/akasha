The announcement brought on a round of resistance from the entire Arcoryx Academy contingent. Ryland was actually taken aback by the sheer widespread vociferous protests from his fellow Vyrdians.

“What is the meaning of this?” Mage Knight Ophelia said, stepping forward. “Why are you singling out a specific member of our group?”

Her feelings were echoed by a great deal of others. Sint and Linsby had joined in too, as had several of the students, most of them first-years. Ryland spotted Viren loudly yelling about unfair treatment, while Acrypha was shooting scathing looks at all the Rhellians.

Even the Academs from Arcoryx Academy had stepped forward, raising their voices against the idea of taking away Ryland on his own.

He was actually touched by their response. It left a warm feeling in his chest.

Credit to Yulkaviel, he was clearly trying to contain his shock—and anger too, if Ryland was reading the hints of his posture correctly—at the turn of events. “Please, calm down. I am certain there is a very good reason why we’re taking the actions we deem necessary for the continued safety and security of everyone involved.”

“This is preposterous,” High Academ Tyra said, her bushy hair shaking in growing anger. “Singling out any specific mage is a grave indictment of your process, Your Worship. You do understand how this reflects on Rhel’s efforts to enter into the Accords, yes? If this is how any mage is expected to be treated, then this creates a terrible conundrum!”

“Please, High Academ,” Yulkaviel said. “Perhaps we can all discuss what the issue is.”

He turned very meaningfully to stare at the guards. They all looked like they couldn’t care less about everyone else’s affront at their actions, but clearly, they couldn’t disobey the Second High Adjutant of all of Rhel itself.

“We were ordered to apprehend Marionys Ryland,” the dragonkin said, his words echoing a little from within his helm. “The First High Adjutant is in charge of record-keeping across Vyrd. There are certain, high-profile records that she maintains, and the record of Marionys Ryland is one that requires deeper verification. As such, he needs to come with us.”

“And what is this record that you speak of?” Tyra asked. She was slowly approaching their location, as though she was about to physically place herself between Ryland and the Rhellian guards.

That was a very sweet gesture, especially considering Ryland was the last person to need anyone to stand before him for any sort of protection.

Only the dragonkin’s eyes were visible through the slit in the helm, and they were entirely unamused by developments. “The record is one of debt.”

That made everyone pause. It even made Ryland pause.

“Debt?” he asked, terribly nonplussed about the situation. “Do you mean metaphorically, or…?”

“No, Ryland. I mean literally. Actual, monetary debt.” The dragonkin sighed and pulled out a page from a pouch at his waist. “Look at this, if you still remember how to read Rhellian. See how much you’ve accrued since you were last here.”

Ryland accepted the paper. The amount stated there was certainly large enough for one of the most powerful figures in the Realm to get involved.

Then he looked at the source of the debt noted at the very top.

“I can’t believe you kept track of the time I said I would pay back for the flagon of ale I took,” Ryland said, shaking his head. “Over three centuries ago. Even the tavernkeeper said it was fine.”

“Verbal promises are entirely unreliable, Ryland. He clearly kept a note of it and posted it to the treasury for eventual collection.”

Eventual collection. Even if it took a few centuries for the loanee to return so the debt-collectors could get their hands on him. Ryland was tempted to call the whole thing insane, but he understood this was a front. They clearly had other intentions. And the longer he spent delaying it, the more he was causing trouble for the others.

This First High Adjutant was clearly nothing like Yulkaviel, who was doing his level best not to appear mortified by developments he had been desperately seeking to avoid.

“Alright,” Ryland said. “I will go with you and repay this debt in full.”

That had the rest of his group all protesting yet again. Though this time, the protests were directed at him. He weathered all their shocked and disappointed looks, before raising a hand and stepping forward resolutely, leaving a parting smile for Tyra.

“Don’t worry,” Ryland said. “I will return shortly.”

High Academ Tyra didn’t bother voicing her opinion of that decision because she was busy stealing a glance at the paper the guards had handed him. “Ryland, that debt…”

Ryland offered her a comforting wink. “Shh, High Academ.”

She just returned him a mortified look. To be fair, she had good reason to be shocked. The interest accrued on any debt that had lasted over three centuries was an enormous amount. Especially when it was compound interest.

So, with a reassuring glance at Viren and Acrypha, Ryland followed his apprehenders to where they wished to handle his business.

The secure location Ryland was being taken wasn’t at the institute. He was rather certain it wasn’t going to be a jail, but he figured it might be a fortified location. They tended to have a lot of those all over Rhel. A holdover from their past that they had stubbornly carried into their present.

His prediction turned out to be true, more or less. The guards took him to a guardhouse that probably would have sufficed as a small redoubt during the Realmbreaker Wars.

Ryland frowned. The familiarity suggested it had been a redoubt once. Hmm.

He got a lot of furtive looks and sidelong stares inside the fortified building. He could ignore it without difficulty because he was refamiliarizing himself with it all. The stuffy air, the Inscriptions he had seen both on the exterior and the interior of the building, the style of the armour everyone wore even in broad daylight and with no sign of danger anywhere.

Oh, yes. Rhel was certainly determined to hold onto its tradition. An understandable action.

When a world was ruled by the exact same entity for centuries on end, this was the natural end result. Stagnation of values.

Ryland didn’t stop until he reached the very top office of the building, where a wide chamber held a human man clearly awaiting his arrival. He was dressed much the same as the guards, except his helm had a golden sheen instead of the grey iron of the rest of his subordinates. The pin on his chest confirmed he was the captain of this unit of guards.

It appeared Ryland’s memory of the military ranks on Rhel was still intact.

“You finally came,” he said, deep voice muffled by his helmet. “I was starting to suspect there might be some struggle or something of the sort.”

Ryland didn’t entertain any such notions. After all, he wasn’t in any real danger here. “What do you want? I doubt the legitimacy of this debt of yours.”

“And yet, you yourself decided to come here.”

“Indeed. I didn’t wish to cause trouble for my friends, so here I am. Where I can deal with the issue without causing anyone any nuisance.”

“You’re certainly causing me a good deal of nuisance.”

Ryland was tempted to point out that this whole rigmarole was their idea, not his, but it might not actually be their idea either. At least, if they considered themselves an entity separate from the First High Adjutant.

“But since you’ve so kindly decided to accede to our request,” the guard captain said. “I hope you will not protest the… compromise we’ve all come to favour.”

Ryland recalled well his compromises when he had first entered Arcoryx. He still had all the suppressions he maintained to ensure no real alarms went off. And perhaps that was something affecting him here. Maybe it wasn’t just his history. The wards were even tighter on Rhel than they were in Arcoryx, so they were even more alarmed than the authorities there.

That didn’t mean Ryland was open to reducing his magic even more, however. Especially when he was barely using his spells. He certainly wasn’t letting his manasoul out.

At some point, it behoved the people around to grant him just a tiny bit of trust, especially when his track record spoke for him. The few times he had allowed something close to his true magical potential to emerge, it had been to help others. To deal with threats and dangers no one else present could.

And it wasn’t as if his presence alone was what had caused those threats and dangers to rise in the first place. The Order would have acted with or without him there.

“Go on, then,” Ryland said. “Let’s hear this compromise, at least.”

The captain frowned, apparently already hearing in Ryland’s tone that he wasn’t exactly inclined to the compromise. “You might be familiar with it already. A Glyphic suppression, using the Star Shackles.”

“You… really think that will work?”

The captain and the other guards all stared at Ryland. They exchanged quick looks before the captain turned a hard little smile to Ryland. He couldn’t see it through the metal helm but he could certainly hear it in the man’s words.

“You will be surprised at the advances we’ve made in all our various Glyphtech since you were last present,” the captain said. “Bring it in!”

The other two guards obeyed their superior in short order, carrying in what looked like a fancy necklace made of glimmering orbs strung together. Ryland frowned just a bit. The Star Shackles did seem different from when he had last seen them. They weren’t prevalent Glyphtech in the rest of the Realms as far better means of forcefully suppressing magic had been developed.

Although, it seemed the authorities on Rhel were confident that their new and improved version could stand toe to toe with any other similar technology out there.

“Stand still now,” the captain warned. “We’ll be putting this on you to keep your mana contained.”

“It won’t work,” Ryland said simply. “None of the other Glyphtech measures have ever worked, even recent ones, and I have severe doubts this will either.”

The captain frowned at that. “It can even stop Guardians now. As long as you don’t make this a hassle, then there’s no reason for it to not work. Now, stand still.”

Ryland sighed, then let them get on with it.

The captain and the guards quickly secured the glinting chain around Ryland’s wrist. His skin prickled uncomfortably just a tiny bit at their closeness. As soon as it was secure, the chain dulled. The glimmer around every orb started diminishing with incredible quickness.

Ryland felt the tiniest of tugs on his mana. A nearly imperceptible sliver of something slivering under his stomach. An odd sensation, as always, but lasting merely a second or two.

From what he recalled, the Star Shackles attacked a target’s manasoul directly by attempting to draw out the vital essence that powered them. Essentially, it ate away at its target’s mana, the magic stored within the Inscribed orbs forming a conduit for its absorption powers.

But in this case, the magic wasn’t working. It was like an ordinary pail trying to dump out an ocean’s worth of mana. No wonder it failed almost instantly.

The shackle’s rapid reaction alarmed the guards.

“What the—”

“Sir—”

“Don’t panic,” the captain said. He shot the quickest of glares at Ryland before martialling his subordinates with looks just as stern. “We’ll need to reinforce it with our magic. Join with me now. [Reinforced Shackle].”

“I would advise against doing that,” Ryland said.

They ignored him. The other two guards echoed their superior’s spell, sending their mana into the chain. It started glowing yet again, shimmering and glinting once more. Not as much as it had before being cinched around Ryland’s wrist, but it certainly wasn’t dull any longer.

Then they began to feel the drain. The captain’s eyes widened within his helm as the shackle drank in more and more of his magic.

“What… is this?” he growled.

The other two guards had begun to shake where they stood, desperately trying not to pull their hands away from where they needed them to be.

Ryland scratched the corner of his mouth as he idly observed the chain in action. “How intriguing. You’ve added amplification Inscriptions to cover up the glaring weakness of the chain when it comes to dealing with powerful targets. Sadly, even then, it’s not enough to handle me.”

“Preposterous!” the captain said, his voice growing in volume. “We will not fail in our holy duties granted to us by Kindly Mavar himself!”

Somehow, the mention of the ruler of Rhel brought on a new surge of strength within the guards. The magic surged. Their power doubled, then tripled, growing as rapidly as the chain’s magic had died in its attempt to secure Ryland. Very intriguing how that occurred. Ryland was almost tempted to investigate the phenomenon right then and there.

Kindly Mavar’s magic was a fascinating thing to behold. He knew it from experience.

But even with the sudden boost in the guards’ manasouls, it wasn’t enough. There was the faintest hints of a tickle, but that was about it.

“This is impossible,” one of the junior guards muttered.

“Sir!” The other one looked almost imploringly at their superior. “We can’t cont—”

“We will!” the captain yelled. “Divert power from the local Inscriptions. Immediately.”

Both subordinate guards looked terribly alarmed at that.

“But sir,” said the first one, bravely voicing his protest despite the increasingly unhinged behaviour from his superior. “If we do that, then the local area will lose their protection. Even if it’s temporary. We can’t do that!”

“We can and we will.” He shot a death-glare at the fellow. “Don’t argue, obey!”

There was that same intriguing sensation again. Even more intense this time. Ryland’s lips curled in slight disgust. He had never really liked the sort of enforcement that the predominant style of magic on Rhel preferred to use.

But the guards were cowed by their superior and quickly did as they were told. The Inscriptions on the walls glowed, threads of mana connecting to their manasouls to amplify their reinforcement of the shackles. Ryland was almost impressed by their determination to make this work, regardless of the hopelessness of their attempt.

For even then, even after gathering all the power stored within those Inscriptions that had lasted centuries, the shackle only made Ryland’s skin prickle a tiny bit more.

He was fine, really.

Unfortunately, the same couldn’t be said for the guards. Especially not when their fears about the reduced power of the Inscriptions manifested.

A crack in reality shattered open, and in no time at all, a glowing, spiky arm emerged and grabbed one of the guards before he could scream.
