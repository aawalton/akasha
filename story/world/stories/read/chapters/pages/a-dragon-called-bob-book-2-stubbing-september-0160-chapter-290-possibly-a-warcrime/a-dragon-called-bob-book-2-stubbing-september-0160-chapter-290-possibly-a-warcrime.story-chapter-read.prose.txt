“I don’t mean to pry, but why did you happen to have that much baby lotion in your storage space?” Nolixyl asked quietly.

We had slipped down the side of the Cornmarket as instructed and now stood with the toxic river sludge on one side, and a shadowy corner of masonry on the other.

“I’ve got lots of stuff in there,” I replied defensively.  “It’s just kind of built up.”  Esme had some exotic tastes that occasionally required a lot of lubrication, but I saw no reason to provide any more details than were strictly necessary.

“Uh huh,” Glump whispered.  “Least you didn’t just happen to have vials of human blood on hand.”

“I normally just eat mammals,” I growled.  “I don’t even have any human corpses in there anymore!”

“Anymore?” Nolixyl asked, raising one invisible eyebrow and causing her right eye to appear larger than the left.

“I gave them back for proper burials,” I snapped.  I was using some twine to tie a chicken feather to a stout stick as we talked.  “You guys lick your eyeballs.  I view most mammals as ambulatory meals; maybe don’t piss me off?”

“Amphibikin are way better than mammals,” Glump muttered.  “Stupid hairy things.”

“Frog legs are a delicacy in some countries on my home world.”

The pair gasped.  “Savages,” Nolixyl hissed.  “I trust it wasn’t your home country?”

“Technically, my country had spent most of a thousand years at war with them.  On and off.  Not because of their diet, though.”

“Noble.” Glump nodded in approval.  “Lizards and amphibians are closely related, you know.  Some of our salamanders are as powerful as dragons.  Mammals don’t have anything like that.”

“Nothing compares to me,” I sang, paraphrasing an old song in a slightly narcissistic way.  Vanity had recovered from his beating and was determined to reestablish his authority.

“Abalaxis the Abhorrent was twice the size of your dragon form.  She could spit fire, but her real strength was her flaming aura.  Anything that got close burst into flames or melted,” Nolixyl said.

“I could have taken her,” I bragged.

“She could fly as well.  No wings, it was like a snake swimming in the air,” Glump replied.

“Meh.  I’ve got some other tricks up my scaly sleeve.”  The stash of weapons I had borrowed from Bulb was going to see some use in the near future.

“Not enough to handle Abalaxis.  When she died—”

“If she died, she obviously wasn’t all that.  Let me guess… some idiot with a magic sword and a class that made him immune to fire damage put her down?”

“It was a spear,” Nolixyl muttered.  “Ah, here he is.”

Blurp’s eyes were descending the wall above us.  He released his sucker toes and dropped the last ten feet to land with a thump next to me.  “As requested.”

“That is a finger,” I observed.

“Didn’t have any vials.”  I suspected the invisible newtman shrugged, but couldn’t be sure.

“Whose finger is it?” I asked tiredly.

“I didn’t ask for their name, Bob.  Just you know…”  his mouth spread wide and snapped shut with a clop.

“Where did you acquire this anonymous digit?”

“Six streets that way,” Glump replied.

I blinked and focused on my thermal vision.  Nope, the amphibians were room temperature and almost invisible on that spectrum as well.  “Your arm is invisible,” I observed, unable to see where he gestured.   “Never mind.  As long as the poor bastard running around crying about invisible frog mouths taking off fingers is well away from here, I guess it doesn’t matter.”  On some level it did bother me.  That was one taxpayer who would be less capable of earning a salary and paying me a percentage on everything they bought.

“It will serve as a distraction and lead any pursuit away from us,” Glump said.

I held the finger, a pinky, over the bucket of baby lotion and squeezed until a single drop fell into the oil.  “We didn’t have any pursuit.  No one knew we were here until someone decided to chomp on strangers' hands,” I grumbled.

“Well now they’re looking on the streets while we will be inside,” Nolixyl replied.  “You did fine Blurp.”

The feather was dipped, and bricks were slapped, all in the order Alad had explained.  I glared at the wall as nothing changed.  “Should I do it ag—”

A vortex flashed into existence in front of me and sucked all three of us in.  I stumbled as we reappeared, kicking over the baby lotion which had also been brought with us.  Mops, brooms, cleaning powders and products.  Yep, this was a storage closet.

“Ooh, that’s nice,” said Glump.  The spilled lotion swirled as he rubbed his feet around in it.

“Sweep and clear.  Find the entrance to the granary proper,” Nolixyl ordered, suddenly all business again.

We moved down the corridors cautiously.  It was late, and the place was closed, but guards would be hanging about somewhere.  The polished wood of the walls and floors gave way to marble as we entered the public areas.  Rows of booths, sacks of grain stacked behind them for the morning trading, lined the floor below us.

“It will be back there,” Nolixyl said.

“Behind the counters?” I guessed.

“Yes.  Glump, Blurp, move ahead,” she ordered.

The blue eyes swarmed over the balcony and disappeared along the ceiling of the floor below us.  I prepared to hop down, never one to miss the opportunity for a superhero landing, when I paused and went still.

Warm shapes, a pair of them, moved into sight below me.  Their armour was cheap, but the blades on their hips looked highly functional.  How to do this quietly…  I did not want to bring any other patrols down on our position.  I ducked behind the fancy balustrade and waited for my moment.

“They can’t sail against the fleet, Jan.  They’ll melt our ships!” one said.

“Mages, Bill.  We have mages and those hairy bastards don’t.”

“They’ve got magitech though!  Didn’t you pay any attention to the briefing?” Jan snapped.

“That cobbled-together crap can’t stand up to a few cadre of Mages Guild pros.  They should just fly out and burn them.  Jan?”

Bill turned to find his colleague had misplaced his head.  A pair of floating daggers were flashing in and out of his chest as orange eyes glared at Bill from where his friend’s cranium had recently been.

A pained scream emerged as Nolixtyl withdrew her mouth, which had covered poor Jan’s head, and her tongue flicked out, sticking to Bill’s face, smothering his yell.  I had moved as soon as I saw the axolotl make her attack and thudded down onto the marble with one knee bent.

“And no one to witness it,” I muttered.  Annoyed at having wasted my snazzy landing.

Invisible fronds reached out, only noticeable because they made parts of Bill’s head and shoulders vanish, to draw him close enough for the knives to go to work.

“He won’t be worrying about dandruff anymore,” I grumbled.

Hurrying over, I scooped the pair of corpses into my pocket dimension and glared at the splatters of blood on the floor.  “You’ve made a mess.”

“You’ve got human corpses in your storage again,” she replied quietly.  “We don’t need much longer.  There will be a little while before the next patrol comes round.  We have the window we need to do what must be done.”

I didn’t bother arguing as we headed after the pair of newtmen.  The space beyond the wide doorway at the rear of the tradehall opened onto a vast space, with six equally impressive portals spread out on the far wall.  Beyond them lay piles and piles of grain.

Glump and Blurp were waiting for us.  I began handing out suspiciously cartoonish-looking bombs.  Long pointy cylinders made of brassy chrome.  In addition to impact triggers, Jace had insisted on including timers on his latest creations.

“These will go boom in ten minutes.  We need to spread as many of them out as possible.  How do we make the fire spread?”  A flaw had emerged in my plan.  Blowing up the entrances might work, but magic would let the workers clear the rubble in minutes.  We needed to make sure the food all burned.

“You don’t know much about grain storage, do you?” Nolixtyl asked as she accepted a bomb.

“I prefer my meals to be more… breathing.”

“Grain powder is highly flammable. There's always dust, and dust goes boom.”  I was treated to a Cheshire axolotyl grin.

Five minutes later we had distributed thirty bombs, which I assumed would be enough to level the entire building even if the grain didn’t catch fire.

“Use this as well. We’ve got some time to make sure we do the job right.”

“Lamp oil?” Glump asked, peering at the jars I’d given him.

“Yep.  Let’s make sure as much of this stuff will burn as possible.”

This wasn’t the time for worrying about karma.  I fully intended to make sure as many taxpayers—I meant citizens—survived, but sometimes a dragon’s got to do what a dragon’s got to do.  And burning shit down was comfortably within my area of expertise.

I built a pile of jars of oil and headed for the silo on the far left, which had already been primed with bombs and incendiary substances.  Wood and stone reached up above me for a hundred feet and stretched away in a gentle arch on either side.  This much food…it would have taken us years to starve them out.

A blast of orange-green flame spewed out of my nostrils and coated the barley that was exposed at the base of the silo.  It caught with a merry blaze, quickly spreading out.  Then the glass jars cracked in the heat and thick smoke spread as the heat intensified.

I nodded in satisfaction and backed away quickly to repeat the feat.  The pile of jars was gone, and I could see Slime Team Six’s eyes floating in the distance.  “You all good?”

“Yeah, we’re just going to guard the door over here,” Nolixtyl muttered.

“You’re scared of fire?”

“It dries us out,” Clump said unhappily.

I was on the fourth silo when the notification came in and all my plans went out of the window.  Portal stones appeared in my hand without me realising I’d called them out.  Wrath and Greed subsumed my sanity, but I fought for control and broke into a superhuman sprint to hose down the other silos.

Claim staked.

The Unnamed Dungeon is contested.  Two factions vie for control of the stingiest dungeon in the world.

Emperor Lozenge Madson has dispatched three elite kill teams, and four looting squads.

The Pretender Bob has local forces available.

The spoils will go to the victor!

“Motherfuckers.”  I finished dousing the last silo and ran back to the amphibikin.

“Bit rude—”

I opened portals beneath them and dropped them into the sea near the fleet, then vanished into one of my own.  My mammal suit melted away as I fell, and I slammed down onto my hoard in full draconic majesty.  My snout swept around, purple sparks falling from my eyes.  They were going for my hoard.  That simply wasn’t cricket, and I was going to make the bastards prey with an E, as well as pay with blood and lives.

“You alright boss?”  asked one of the deathkinigits standing eternally by the final entrance to my treasure.

I ignored him and looked up.  I had buried the dungeon core in the ceiling above my hoard, and the replacement stone was unmarked.  They hadn’t gotten this far.  All they would have to do is touch the core to take my dungeon away from me.

“Someone is invading the dungeon.  Get all your lot up to the portal floor.  I’ll hold position here and…coordinate.  Get me Kat as well!”
