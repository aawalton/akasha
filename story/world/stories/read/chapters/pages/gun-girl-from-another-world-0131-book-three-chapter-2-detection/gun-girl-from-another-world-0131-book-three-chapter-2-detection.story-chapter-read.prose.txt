Chapter 2

Detection

The sound of the shattering crystals hangs in our ears a disproportionately long time, even as the plains outside the walls of Lurei seem to disassemble around us and rebuild themselves into the familiar yard of my estate in the woods outside of Dabun.

As soon as I’m able, I turn around on the spot, inhaling deeply. “Home! Oh, how I missed it!”

“I am gladdened that it has taken root so deeply in your heart.”

The voice causes me to stop on the spot, my spine stiff, and I turn my neck with surprising difficulty toward the source: The smiling, patient face of a white-haired elf in white robes with red sashes, who looks entirely too young to have mastery over such a motherly expression.

“Yorin!” I greet her, my voice slightly shrill beyond my control. “What are you doing here?”

“I would think that should be apparent,” she replies far more easily. “I was awaiting your arrival.”

It’s actually Ayre that manages to verbalize the issue first. “You haven’t been standing there ever since you sent for us, have you, Sacred Yorin?”

Her smile turns into practiced amusement as she places a hand against her cheek. “Why, no, of course not! I sent that letter a week ago! You would have me grow roots of my own, standing in one place for so long!”

I turn fully to face her. “Then how did you know when to be waiting for us?”

Her smile never falters. “Oh, surely nothing more than an educated guess.”

I give a quick, bemused shake of my head. “Not beating those allegations of divination magic, Yorin.”

“Divination?” she repeats, her face shifting into one of confusion as she works through what I mean. But then she gets it and her smile returns, again in amusement, precluded by a little laugh.

"My, spells to know the very future of events? Your people are truly imaginative, Remmi, but if such spells existed, surely they would be so overused as to render themselves all but useless! Just imagine, everyone using magic to predict everyone else using magic to predict everyone else! An endless chain where nothing would be accomplished for tactical insecurity and none of the answers would be valid for everyone constantly changing them!"

“Lost art, got it,” I decipher, and I think her expression might have tightened up just a little at that. That clearly isn’t what she wanted me to take away from her mini-rant, but, hey, play games, get prizes. I don’t dwell on it or rub it in her face, though, and instead cross my arms. “So, hypothetically, if you were going to set something up to know when someone was going to arrive, how would you do it?”

“Hypothetically?” the priestess asks, and tilts her head as she thinks it over. “Well, there are detection spells used by important places that are triggered when someone attempts to enter the area via teleportation. You should probably invest in such an enchantment for your own property, in fact. I shall see it added to the requirements for the renovations you wanted to do.”

Ah, is she trying to distract me by changing the topic? “I appreciate it,” I play into going along with it. “I can do the designs for systems, but I have little idea what all I need for security and the like. Is a system like that what the Imperial Grounds and High Temple use?”

But Yorin shakes her head. “No, there is an even greater level of security there. Those grounds are completely warded against teleportation outright. Entering them via such a manner is simply impossible. However, you want to be able to teleport back to your home, so you would be better served by the alarm.”

I nod along with the explanation. “And it more neatly matches the question I asked, too. But is there any way to make use of that information? It’s all well and good to know someone’s coming in, but is there some way to respond to it if you’re not there?”

“There is a higher-ranked teleport spell,” she provides after a moment of thought, “that you can set to activate automatically upon a certain condition being met. Theoretically, one could combine these two spells, with one as the condition for the other, to teleport to or away from the receiving location as needs must.”

Yorin raises her hand to shake her finger at me. “Of course, these are both spells of great advancement and complexity. It would require a mage of significant power to achieve this combination. Hero Benarou Sendai might one day accomplish such a feat.”

Ayre frowns, tipping his head to one side as his ears tilt with him. “Does this mean these aren’t things that a Priest can do, then?”

Yorin gives the archer her smile again, though it’s a bit of a sad one this time. “Oh, it is not even within the domain of a Priest’s magic, and a grand shame it is. We could know the moment survivors of vicious battle arrive, and assemble directly at their location to begin treating the wounded! But no, I know of such things through erudition, not through my duties to the Temple.”

I’m left once more wondering about Yorin’s mysterious background. All of these things are impossible for Priests, but are they beyond the White Witch? I keep that thought to myself, as Yorin has never outright spoken of her past, and I don’t want to be inconsiderate of my friend, but it spins about in my mind all the same.

Korrigan takes that opportunity to step around from behind Ayre and myself, peering up at the priestess. “So you’re the Sacred Priestess that Hero Remmi is so afraid of?”

I stiffen visibly at that statement. It’s a reminder that Korrigan really is a child, despite her high intelligence and magical ability. It’s the kind of misrepresentation and lack of tact that can only come from one who has yet to learn to equip a proper filter or know about reading the room.

I suddenly find the sky very interesting as I do a very poor attempt at innocent whistling. Little surprise it doesn’t work.

“Remmi.”

Lowering my head feels like I’m having to ratchet it down. “... Yes, Her Excellency Sacred Yorin?”

“Abduction of children is a violation of Imperial law. Your teleportation field seems to have committed a grievous sin, indeed.”

Oh, good, she’s hung up on that and not reading anything into what Korrigan actually said. I let out a sigh I didn’t realize I was holding as I bend down to clap my hands on the oni girl’s shoulders while grinning up at the elf.

“Oh, she’s not abducted! This is our party’s new mage! I assure you, she’s up to the punch required to keep up with Ayre and myself! Her spell diversity is a little low, but we’ve been working on that! Yorin, meet Korrigan! Korrigan, this is Sacred Yorin, my friend and advisor and absolutely not someone I should be described as afraid of!”

“A mage?” Yorin questions, her attention focusing back on me instead of on the oni girl. “Not a shieldbearer, not a priest to cast barriers and manage the status of the group, but another ranged combatant that will need to be defended against attacking monsters?”

I take one hand off of Korrigan to scratch at the back of my head with a nervous laugh. “Ahah, yeah, I know our front line is, ah …”

“Nonexistent?” Ayre offers helpfully.

I shoot him a scowl, anyway. “I was going to say it’s wanting.”

“You should have said it faster, then.”

I pretend to ignore the fashionable elf and turn my attention back to the ancient one. “What I’m trying to say is that we’re kinda assembling a team as we come across the right candidates. It’s not that we’re not looking for a good tank, we just haven’t found the right one yet.” I stand back to my feet as I put on all of my charm. “As for a priest, I’ve already got the best candidate imaginable picked out! I’ve just got to convince her to give up working a temple to come along with us!”

Yorin gives a deep sigh, but then chuckles with a shake of her head. “But that I could, Remmi. Well, no, I could, a Hero has the authority to recruit even a servant of the High Temple.”

I give a lop-sided grin. “I don’t meet the level requirement for this conversation option yet, huh?”

“No, sorry,” she replies apologetically, and I believe her. “Until you are stronger, my presence would only serve to stifle your own development.”

“Yeah, from the stories I’ve heard,” I agree, “even with Xuhi, you pretty much only advised and let her handle every fight she could without your interference.”

“Which would make me an excellent mentor, but a poor teammate, and you require the latter.” Yorin takes a deep breath to indicate a change of topic. “But, since you have raised the topic, how has your cultivation progressed?”

I beam a wide smile, one hand propped against my hip as I thumb toward myself with the other. “I’m level thirty!”

“My, already?” she puts on that motherly visage of being impressed with her child’s bragging. “Might I see?”

“Sure!” I don’t hesitate to open the window and flip it about to share it with her.

She reads it over slowly, nodding along. “You are keeping a steady spread, good. Keeping your Intellect high will ensure both your spells and your magical rounds remain powerful. And you are not neglecting your physical attributes. You are remarkably hardy anyway, but when one is as prone to harm as you are, more health can only be a positive.”

“Can I take Xuhi yet?”

Yorin’s gaze goes from my status window to my face, and I immediately recognize the scolding expression on her own. “Do not forget the parables of those who thought that numbers alone were enough to win a fight.”

My nervous chuckle makes a comeback, as does the itch on the back of my head. “Still that one-sided, huh?”

The priestess gives me a speculative stare for a moment, then sighs again. “It is not that she is a poor specimen against which to measure yourself. Your skills are similar, so she is actually a good metric, if I am to speak honestly. The obstacle you would face in sparring against her is not in how your abilities measure up, where you are certainly closing the gap. It would not even be in the diversity of your spells, though hers is far greater and more specialized. Do not permit me to forget to ask after how yours are progressing later, though.”

I nod. “I get it, it’s not a gap I can close by buying things through the System. So, what is it, then?”

Again, she hesitates to answer, looking me in the eyes like she’s considering whether what she’s about to tell me is going to hurt. “Remmi, you are a very … honest combatant. I do not in any way wish to portray that as a bad thing, for it is certainly not. You enter battle with your intent on full display and, on your honor, refuse to deviate from it. It is an admirable trait to possess, a Heroic one even. But it is also why you would lose to my precious Xuhi every time you faced her, even if you exceeded her in every other category.”

I take that in and process it with a second round of nodding. “She fights dirty, and I fight too cleanly to keep up.”

Yorin doesn’t answer immediately. Instead, she steps forward and takes my hands in hers. She gives them a squeeze and looks me in the eyes again. “Remmi, by all means, compare your performance to hers. She will prove excellent for this a while longer yet. But do not measure yourself by whether or not you can defeat her in combat. You were each raised for very different battlefields.”

She waits for my signal of understanding, gives my hands another squeeze with a smile, then turns her attention to Ayre and Korrigan. “And your friends, am I to assume they have made similar gains?”

They each beam with pride and nod, and with pride in my own voice, I support them. “They’re keeping up excellently, what with how we’ve been pumping through dungeons. In fact, even with just the three of us, we’re easily clearing Silver-rank dungeons now. Their levels are higher than mine, of course, but their performance is comparable.”

Yorin’s proud, motherly smile returns to her face once more. “What excellent news! I have no doubt the Guildmaster will want you tested for Gold while you are here!”

That immediately makes Ayre’s face fall, followed by his collapsing shoulders and ears. “Not again …!”
