“What?” Lexie’s heart immediately plunged into her stomach, her hand gripping the table. “What do you mean? What happened to Stella?”

Aiden focused on stirring the defrosting soup. “A battle began on the day you disappeared. The Emperor dropped a multitude of bombs, and Stella was one of the heroes called to deal with the situation. Unfortunately, she sustained a direct hit from a mana bomb, and she got seriously hurt."

“No,” Lexie whispered, bringing her hand up to her mouth, a wave of nausea robbing her of any appetite. Stella wasn’t supposed to get hurt. She was too powerful, too durable. She wasn’t supposed to get injured, much less land in a coma.

In this world, Lexie knew how dangerous comas could be. A coma essentially meant that a plethora of healers had attended to you and couldn’t entirely fix what had been done to your body. Considering how powerful healers were, if they couldn’t help you, it was pretty much a death sentence. The longer the coma went on, the more certain it would lead to death, like for Lucy Frank, who had been in a coma for years now.

Only in rare cases did people wake up from comas on Earth 9. A mere eight percent of the time, if Lexie’s memory served correctly.

Stella had to be part of the eight. She couldn’t die. Not like this.

Except that she’d died in the initial story.

Just like Aiden had become a villain who took down the association.

Was that what was going on? Was Fate trying to take it back to the original ending?

“I want to see her,” Lexie said.

“After you eat, I’ll take you to her.”

“How come no one mentioned it?” Lexie asked. “I hung out with Dewie for most of the afternoon, and he didn’t tell me.”

“I doubt he knows. They’re trying to keep her condition a secret for now. The Firebringers have a lot of enemies, and knowing that they’re without a matriarch will bring those people out of the shadows.”

"How can they keep it a secret when so many people saw her getting hit by a bomb? Did none of the PHORBS catch it?"

"No. It was pretty chaotic."

Lexie pushed back from the table. “Take me to her. I’m not hungry anymore.”

“Lexie…”

“Please, Dad.” The desperation in her voice likely also reflected in her gaze as she stared at her father.

Aiden sighed and lowered the heat on the soup. “Fine. We can go see her while the food simmers."

"Thanks," Lexie said. The Lightlarks were already gone at this point. Even if they weren't, it wouldn't matter, because Lexie and her father teleported right from their home into the hallways of a serene building with white walls and quiet corridors that seemed to swallow up footsteps to prevent excessive clatter.

It was obviously a healing center, and the fact that Aiden had brought them here meant he'd been here already by himself.

Lexie spotted Aiofe and Nathan standing at the end of the hall, and they looked over when Lexie rushed to them.

“How is she?”

Aoife and Nathan reacted in surprise. “Lexie…how did you–”

“My dad told me,” she said. “Please, can I see her? Please?”

“I’m not sure.” As their gazes switched to Aiden, they said, “No one but family is supposed to know she’s here.”

“I’m her goddaughter. That’s like family, isn’t it?”

Aoife bit her lip and then sighed. “Give me a second.” She opened the door, and Lexie only got a glimpse of Mane’s back before Aiofe entered and closed it again.

“How are you guys holding up?” Aiden asked Nathan, who gave a weak shrug. Despite his idle nature, he had deep-set eye bags underneath his eyes and new lines in his forehead.

“Luther is holding the fort well enough, I suppose,” he said. “Though the rest of the family is demanding to see Stella, to know the true extent of her injuries. We’ll have to release a statement soon, and that’s when the bloodbath will probably begin. Every single asshole is about to crawl out of the woodwork and attempt to take a swipe at us. They'll want to be the ones to wipe out the Firebringers once and for all. Stella’s strength was a huge deterrent.”

“But Theo and Thor are powerful, too. Not to mention Luther.”

“Yes, but…” Nathan shrugged. “Everything was much easier with Stella.”

“Don’t say 'was' like she’s gone,” Lexie said sharply. “She’s not gone.”

“Of course not. I’m sure she’ll wake up any second now, right as rain.” Even he didn’t look like he believed that. But Lexie had faith. She wouldn’t let Stella die, even if she had to defy her destiny and drag Stella’s soul back herself.

The door opened again with Aiofe making her reappearance. She waved at them. “You can come in.”

She stepped aside to allow Lexie and Aiden to enter. The private room was vast, overlooking both a forest and the ocean in the distance. Lexie’s gaze fell on Mane first, who had his back turned to her. It was the first time she’d ever seen him slouched over, his shoulders weighed down from his grief. He didn’t give any reaction when they walked up beside him, but Lexie didn’t blame him.

Especially when she saw Stella.

She almost didn’t recognize her. Her godmother’s entire body had been bandaged, down to the hand that Mane held with his. A variety of tubes ran through her, and she was still, so still, with not even a steady rise and fall of her chest. The only sign of life was the consistent beeping of the monitors beside her.

Lexie let out a choked sound, slapping her hand over her mouth. That finally got Mane’s attention. His features were drawn, like they’d aged at least a decade since she last saw him. His hair was perched in a messy bun and looked like a bird’s nest, having lost its luster. He was clearly going through the depths of hell right now, but he still attempted a smile and took her hand, squeezing gently.

“She’s okay,” he said. “She’ll be okay.”

“I’m so sorry.” Lexie bit her lip as tears pushed the back of her eyes. It had been so long since she’d cried. It felt almost strange to do it, like part of her body was wondering what was going on while the other part sank beneath the waves of devastation. “I wasn’t here. I could have stopped it if I were here.”

Mane’s smile faltered, turning watery. “That’s exactly the type of thing Stella would have said. Don’t let her hero’s guilt rub off on you now.”

“I’m not a hero. Not like her.” Stella had sacrificed herself, given up everything for the role. And what had it gotten her? No time with her family, and her entire body broken on a hospital bed.

“You’re more of a hero than you realize, Lexie,” Mane said. “Although I’m not sure that’s such a good thing anymore.”

Lexie nodded slowly as she stared at her godmother. Suddenly, she was hit with a flood of rage, so strong it made her teeth chatter.

Her fingers clenched into fists. Every muscle tensed with overpowering bloodlust. Madness tinged the corner of her mind, her heart pounding in her ears.

She wanted the Emperor dead.

She wished she could teleport right now and just shoot him in the head as she'd done to Vulcan.

But his situation was somehow more complex than Vulcan's. She had to be patient.

She would kill him at the right time.

She took deep breaths to calm herself.

Aiden walked up to Mane and laid a hand on his shoulder. He didn't say anything, but when Mane’s eyes met his, there was a deep understanding in them, a commiseration of soul that not even Lexie could comprehend.

She knew that if anyone understood what Mane was going through right now, it would be Aiden.

Mane's hand went up, folding over Aiden's.

Whatever Aiden's expression communicated had Mane's face rippling before he inhaled and spread a hand over it.

“I should go,” Mane said. “The twins will be home by now, and I don’t want them to miss me too much. It’s bad enough that their mother is in this state. I don’t want them to have to worry about me, too.”

"Do they know?” Lexie asked.

“I’m not sure. We’ve been attempting to keep the news from them, but I think they probably suspect something is wrong. Especially Thor. He’s more sensitive than we give him credit for, and he's been a little nightmare since the day of the accident."

“Can I see them?” Lexie asked.

“Of course. Luther should be there as well, and so should Theo and Torin. We can all have dinner together like old times.” He attempted another smile, but didn't quite make it all the way there. His voice cracked on the last word as well. They all knew that it wouldn’t be like old times. In the old dinners, Stella was absent because she was out saving the world. Now she would be absent because she’s in a bed fighting for her life.

“Can we?” Lexie asked her father.

He nodded. “Yes. Let me just go turn off the stove, and I’ll be back.”

“Okay.”

Mane and Lexie were alone for a few seconds while Aiden did that, and typically, Mane would take the chance to make small talk, ask Lexie how she was doing, and all that.

Today, he couldn't seem to find it in him.

Although when Aiden returned, Mane said, “I heard what happened to the two of you. Heard that you’d been ambushed.” He shook his head. “I just want to let you know that I don’t agree with what the association did at all. Neither does Stella.”

“Of course. Stella had my back.” Lexie ventured with the next words carefully. “Did she tell you about me?” Though Lexie had trusted Stella with her secret, she wouldn’t have been surprised if Stella had shared it with Mane and asked him not to disclose it to anyone. Somehow, Lexie knew that Mane would have kept the secret, not necessarily for Lexie’s sake, but because he would rather die than break Stella’s confidence.

Mane cocked his head. “In what way?”

Lexie didn’t know how to define it, so she simply said, “Never mind. But...is it okay for Stella to be here? Alone? Just guarded by Aiofe and Nathan?"

"Yes," Mane said. "It's the safest healing center there is, where a lot of heroes come to recuperate. It's a little isolated, well guarded, and only approved guests can teleport onto the facilities." He gave Lexie and Aiden a wry look. "I'm not going to ask the two of you how you got around that, but no one should be able to get into this room without prior approval."

"Okay," Lexie still wasn't satisfied with that, though. She would craft new security cards just in case.

Mane called Aiofe and Nathan into the room to keep an eye on Stella while they were gone.

Then they disappeared and appeared in abject chaos.

Lexie didn’t know what to focus on first: the sound of plates smashing, the hysterical screaming and sobbing, the angry voice of a man yelling, “Thor, if you don’t get down from there, I swear on everything I have, I’ll paddle your butt so hard .”

“You’ll have to catch me first,” Thor retorted, followed by another round of plate smashing. Mane sighed and hurried into the kitchen with Lexie and Aiden following.

They found Thor wedged into one of the high cabinets, throwing plates at the cooks who had huddled to one side behind Luther Firebringer. Luther looked different. He’d gained weight, a husky layer of fat and muscle softening the previously harsh lines of his face and body. He’d also grown out his beard, and if not for the familiar scowl on his face, Lexie might not have recognized him.

“Luther,” Mane said. “What’s going on?”

“What do you mean? What’s going on?” He pointed at Thor. “Don’t you see what your demon spawn is doing?”

“Well, get him down from there.”

“Gee. I wish I had thought of that first. Here’s what happens when we get close.”

Luther took a few steps towards the boy, and the temperature in the room rose several degrees. A blaze of heat so uncomfortable that Lexie wanted to rip off her clothes hit them.

Mane’s eyes popped out of their sockets.

“Did he preawaken?” he asked. ”When?”

“Today, I guess,” he said. “And he’s been a terror ever since. I'm concerned he might accidentally set himself on fire."

"But he's only seven. Torin was the youngest preawakener at eight."

"Well, Thor just broke his record."

The next plate throw, Lexie stopped in the air. “That’s enough of that.”

Lexie held out her hand and encased the child in a forcefield and brought him out of the cabinet, lowering him to the floor.

Thor really didn’t like that. He screamed and banged his fists on the containment, but she didn’t release as his father walked over.

“What brought all this on?”

“He wants to see his mom,” Luther stared at Mane. “Are you sure we shouldn’t just tell him where she is?”

“No. That would make things worse.”

“I’m not sure how it can.”

Mane shook his head as the crying got closer and closer. They turned around to see Theo coming in while carrying a sobbing Tamsin, both of them looking at the end of their ropes.

Lexie walked to Tamsin and patted her hair. "Hi, Tam."

Tamsin quieted and held out her hands for Lexie. Theo allowed Lexie to carry her.

Somehow, between the five of them, they managed to calm the children down and clean up the mess. Mane spent a long time apologizing to the staff for Thor's mess and also scolding his son, but his heart was clearly not in it. Even Luther didn't have the heart to yell once it came down to it. They all sat somberly at the kitchen table, staring down at their meal.

"Good to see you back, Luther," Aiden said to break the silence.

He shrugged. "Can't say I'm happy to be back, but duty calls."

It was strange. Luther's antagonism towards Aiden wasn't present anymore in his speech. Lexie would have thought that, if anything, it would have increased with all the rumors swirling about her.

Then again, Luther probably had too much on his plate to care about that. Or maybe he was just as disillusioned with the association as his sister had been.

"Where's Torin?" Mane asked.

"He was in his room pacing and talking to himself," Theo said. "I left Sir Hoppington to keep an eye on him."

"Sir Hoppington?"

Theo nodded. He retrieved the card that would activate the frog and enable him to see what he saw.

Suddenly, Theo uttered a loud gasp.

He bolted out of his seat.

"Theo! What's wrong?"

"He's not in his room," he said. "He's...damn it, Tor."

"Where is he?" Mane asked.

"I..." He choked. "I'm pretty sure he's at the Emperor's castle."
