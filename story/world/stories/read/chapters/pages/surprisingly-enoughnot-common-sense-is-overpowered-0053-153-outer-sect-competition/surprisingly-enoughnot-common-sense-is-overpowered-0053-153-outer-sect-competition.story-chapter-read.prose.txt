During cultivation, one's perception of time often faded, with days and months slipping by almost unnoticed. Before Ning realized it, another year had passed.

In the quiet clearing outside his hut, Ning moved through his routine with practiced precision. His breathing remained steady as he shifted from one stance to the next, muscles and tendons working together under the strain.

Compared to qi refining, body refinement demanded something more fundamental: patience with one's own flesh.

Ning had read that body refinement was a path that did not seek power from the heavens, but rather from within oneself. It emphasized opening the so-called gates of immortality hidden inside the human body, gradually unleashing its potential until the microcosm of the human body could coexist with the macrocosm of heaven and earth.

At its theoretical peak, the cultivator would achieve equality with that macrocosm, existing within it while simultaneously transcending it, eventually tearing through the void and stepping beyond the universe itself.

At least, that was how the manuals described it.

Ning understood it in much simpler terms.

If qi cultivation was like borrowing energy from the environment, body refinement was like upgrading the hardware. The stronger the hardware became, the more power it could withstand, and eventually the body itself would become a source of tremendous power.

Simply put, you became Saitama without losing your hair.

The beginning of that very long journey was reaching the innate state of the body, which roughly corresponded to the Foundation Establishment realm.

When his routine ended, Ning took a deep breath and deliberately relaxed his muscles, allowing the accumulated tension to slowly drain from his body. He knew better than to push recklessly and injure himself when there was no reason to rush.

A faint glow appeared across his vision.

[Jade Skin and Icy Meridians: Skin Refining (200/200)]

After four years of uninterrupted practice, he had finally completed this stage of body refinement.

His progress was faster than average. Compared to Qi Condensation, body refinement demanded far greater resources, but once those requirements were met, advancement was considerably steadier. Fortunately, Ning had planned ahead and planted Frozen Breath Grain in sufficient quantities from the beginning, giving him a stable supply of resources throughout his refinement.

Ning slowly clenched his fist before adjusting his stance and striking a nearby tree with a controlled punch.

Thud.

The trunk shuddered beneath the impact. Bark split and a shallow indentation appeared in the wood, yet when Ning pulled his hand back, there wasn't even a mark on his skin.

His physical capabilities had improved tremendously.

Fully refined Jade Skin allowed external force to spread across the surface of his body rather than penetrate inward. Even without circulating qi, ordinary blades and blunt weapons would have considerable difficulty injuring him.

Ning then focused inward and accelerated the circulation of qi through his meridians.

The flow remained smooth despite the sudden increase in speed, without the tearing sensation or instability that would once have accompanied such reckless circulation. Years of refinement had strengthened his meridians enough to withstand a much greater throughput of qi, allowing him to unleash techniques faster while reducing the risk of injuring himself in the process.

In the martial manuals, this stage was sometimes described as the body "entering the vessel phase," becoming capable of properly containing and wielding its own power.

Ning preferred the simpler explanation.

He could hit harder, endure more, and use his qi more aggressively without breaking himself.

Against an ordinary cultivator of the same realm, that alone was already a considerable advantage.

Still, Skin Refining was only the beginning. The next transformation lay deeper within the body, where he would begin strengthening the bones and marrow themselves.

And Ning had already prepared for it.

He took out a jade bottle and tipped a single pill into his palm. The pill was pale blue, with faint traces of frost drifting across its surface, yet beneath that cold aura Ning could sense a subdued warmth.

Preparing it had taken weeks. Ning had even asked Su Fan to commission the pill through the Alchemy Hall, and despite supplying the ingredients himself, the refinement fee alone had cost him fifty spirit stones.

Dragon Qi Grass provided vitality and explosive potential, while Frozen Breath Rice tempered and stabilized the meridians. Several auxiliary herbs had also been added to control the medicinal energy and reduce the risk of backlash.

The result was a pill specifically tailored to assist his breakthrough in body refinement.

Ning swallowed it without hesitation.

Cold spread through his body first, flowing outward from his stomach and seeping into his limbs. The heat followed soon afterward, slower but much heavier, rolling through his core as the medicinal energy began interacting with his circulating qi.

Ning immediately guided both forces through his body.

Pain followed.

His muscles tightened as the medicinal energy penetrated deeper, while his skin repeatedly contracted and relaxed as impurities were gradually forced out. A faint jade sheen appeared across his arms, becoming more noticeable as the pill continued to dissolve.

Ning didn't remain seated.

He rose and began moving through a sequence of body-refining forms, carefully coordinating each motion with his breathing. When he punched, medicinal energy was driven through his arms and into the bones. A turn of the waist carried it along his spine, while lowering his stance forced the accumulated pressure deeper into his legs.

This was something Ning had gradually discovered through his own practice.

Body refinement worked far better when the body was actually moving.

Simply sitting and enduring the medicinal energy allowed too much of it to gather unevenly or dissipate before being properly absorbed. Controlled movement kept it circulating through the muscles, tendons, bones, and marrow, while his breathing allowed him to vent excess heat whenever the pressure became too great.

Ning continued through the forms, adjusting his pace whenever the medicinal energy became unstable.

The cold protected his body from the worst of the strain while the heat continued driving the refinement forward. With every circulation, the medicinal energy penetrated a little deeper, and Ning could feel far less of it escaping uselessly into the surroundings.

The pain remained, but it was manageable.

More importantly, it was working.

His movements gradually slowed as the pill's medicinal energy dwindled. Each form became heavier than the last, his bones aching under the accumulated pressure while a deep warmth settled into his marrow.

Eventually, the grinding sensation began to fade.

Ning completed the final movement and slowly straightened.

A familiar glow appeared before his eyes.

[Jade Skin and Icy Meridians: Skin Refining → Marrow Strengthening]

Ning remained still for several moments, carefully sensing the changes taking place inside his body.

"So this is Marrow Strengthening..."

Unlike Skin Refining, this stage did not primarily improve his external defense or qi circulation. It targeted something deeper: the source from which the body's vitality and physical strength were sustained.

In countless martial manuals, marrow was described as the root of vitality. Blood was born from it, physical strength depended upon it, and even endurance and recovery were closely tied to its condition.

Ning naturally had an easier comparison.

If his skin was the armor and his meridians were the pathways carrying power throughout the body, then his marrow was part of the engine underneath everything.

Unlike completing Skin Refining, however, merely entering Marrow Strengthening didn't immediately give him a tremendous increase in strength. This stage was a gradual transformation whose benefits would become increasingly obvious as the refinement progressed.

Ning glanced at the empty jade bottle in his hand and sighed.

"Alas. Frozen Breath Grain's efficiency is going to drop again."

He had expected this. Frozen Breath Grain had carried him through much of his earlier body refinement, but Marrow Strengthening demanded more varied and potent resources. That was precisely why he had commissioned the pill rather than attempting the breakthrough with Frozen Breath Grain alone.

Which meant his cultivation expenses were about to increase again.

Ning stared at the empty bottle for a moment before putting it away.

Well, that was a problem for future Ning.

For now, he flexed his fingers and felt the subtle changes beginning deep within his body.

"At least I have one more trump card for the competition."

The Outer Sect Competition was held once every five years.

Not only did the top-ranked disciples gain honor and recognition, but they were also rewarded generously by the sect itself. This time, however, the competition was particularly special. All of the participants were newly admitted outer sect disciples, making it essentially a newcomers' competition.

"It seems everyone is here," Wei Zhusang said calmly. "Let's go register."

For an event of this scale, the members of the Mutual Aid Association had agreed to meet up first and register together, partly out of camaraderie and partly to boost morale. Ning found the idea reasonable, which was why he had gotten up early to join them.

As they moved through the crowd, a familiar voice rang out.

"Ning!"

Zhang Feng strode over with an enthusiastic grin. "It's really been a while! How much have you trained?"

"Quite a lot," Ning answered honestly.

He had never been the type to exaggerate or downplay things unnecessarily.

In exams, there were always different kinds of people: those who claimed they hadn't touched a book yet somehow aced the test, those who truly hadn't studied and failed miserably, and those who prepared steadily and said exactly that.

Ning had always belonged to the last group.

"I hope so." Zhang Feng laughed. "I've been training like crazy myself. Look at this!"

He flexed his arm, muscles tightening visibly beneath his robes. "Today, I'll show everyone the power of youth!"

"Sure," Ning said with a small nod. "I'll be cheering for you."

As he spoke, Ning quietly observed the others. They might be companions, but today, they were also competitors.

Zhang Feng's aura had indeed grown stronger, denser, and more stable than before. The same was true for Lang Rulang and Wei Zhusang, who walked at the front of the group. Both radiated a restrained sharpness that hadn't been there previously.

Their seclusion within their clans for the sake of this competition had clearly paid off.

They soon reached the registration area, where several long stone tables had been set up. Elders and inner sect assistants sat behind them, recording names and issuing identity tokens. Every participant was required to register in person.

The process was efficient, requiring little more than a name, hall affiliation, and cultivation stage.

Ning placed his token down, received a numbered jade slip, and stepped aside without comment.

Once everyone from the Mutual Aid Association finished registering, they regrouped near the edge of the plaza.

Wei Zhusang spoke again.

"The first round has already been announced," he said. "It will be a free-for-all. Ten participants per arena. The last one standing advances to the next round."

Several members stiffened at the words.

"A free-for-all..." one of them muttered. "That's brutal."

Ning, however, remained calm.

Connections really are useful.

Without inside information, many disciples would only learn the rules shortly before being thrown into the arena. Knowing the format in advance already gave them an edge.

Wei Zhusang then took out several jade slips and handed one to each of them.

"These contain information on strong competitors," he said. "I bought them from informants. Someone has already listed some of the top contenders, so this should be useful."

Ning accepted his slip. He wasn't particularly surprised that someone had already ranked the newcomers. After all, without a clear sense of the strength hierarchy, protagonist-style face-slapping wouldn't be nearly as intuitive.

He quickly read through the contents.

[Qiu Han: High-grade spiritual root disciple. Already at the peak of the sixth stage of Qi Condensation. His qi foundation is extremely stable, and his combat experience is rumored to be far above average. Suspected to have learned an Earth-grade martial art, possibly from a hidden inheritance. Possesses the supernatural ability Quick, allowing him to explosively accelerate his movements for short bursts, making him exceptionally dangerous in close combat.]

[Jin Shu: High-grade spiritual root disciple at the sixth stage of Qi Condensation. A beast tamer whose contracted companion is a gray wolf at mid-stage Qi Condensation. The wolf possesses keen senses and strong coordination, making Jin Su particularly dangerous in prolonged battles and group engagements.]

[Zhou Sheng: High-grade spiritual root disciple at the sixth stage of Qi Condensation. Member of the Zhou Clan. Suspected of having learned an Earth-grade martial art passed down through the clan. Known for his steady temperament and methodical fighting style, rarely revealing his full strength unless forced.]

[Xiao Hong: High-grade spiritual root disciple at the sixth stage of Qi Condensation. From the Xiao Clan. Suspected of possessing an Earth-grade martial art inheritance. Aggressive and decisive in battle, favoring overwhelming offense and swift resolution.]

[Wei Zhusang: Mid-grade spiritual root disciple from the Wei Family. His cultivation is slightly lower than that of the top contenders, but he is rumored to have mastered the Wei Clan's inherited martial art.]

…

[Ji Ning: Fifth stage of Qi Condensation. One of the only newcomers to obtain a spiritual certification for a secondary profession, a Spiritual Farmer. Has demonstrated notable talent in magical spells.]

Ning was genuinely surprised to see his name listed in tenth position.

After all, unlike most of the others, who hailed from prominent clans or possessed high-grade spiritual roots, his background was fairly ordinary.

Still, the research seemed thorough. Even his certification as a spiritual farmer had been uncovered.

While none of his achievements were truly hidden, he had never gone out of his way to flaunt them either. The fact that this information had still been compiled spoke to the accuracy and reach of the informants.

Ning closed the jade slip, his expression thoughtful.

"Looks like there are quite a few strong opponents this time," he muttered before sighing lightly. "Well... with the protagonist around, it'd be strange if a bunch of geniuses didn't show up."

There was a saying that whenever a great trend arose, all sorts of demons and ghosts would emerge alongside it. Naturally, Ning felt the same principle applied to the rise of a protagonist, which always seemed to coincide with the sudden appearance of all sorts of supposedly rare and never-before-seen geniuses.

Fortunately, Ning didn't need to concern himself with any of that. All he had to do was avoid triggering any death flags and go with the flow.

It was as simple as that.
