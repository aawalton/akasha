System Warden.

Wait, System Warden?

Like, being in charge of a jail? Was Earth the prison in this metaphor? No, he got the strong impression, in the same way he'd known about the Prism Illusion skill, that the System meant warden in the traditional way: one who guards.

Thomas stared at the scroll, then looked up and around as if for help. Of course, his team wasn't here for input. That was by design, so his opinion would not be influenced. This was his decision and his alone.

While he was extremely tempted, he tried to be the guy who looked before he leaped. Plus, he had questions.

Top of his mind: What exactly were the duties of a System Warden? Would this get in the way of his dungeon diving? Could he use a warden's power to get the word out so people unlocked their Gift before the System integration? Also, how could he really help? He was the loot guy and the only one on the team who didn't have an combat skill.

This was all important stuff, and he thought he knew how to get the answers to most of it without firing off endless questions to the System.

Thomas cleared his throat. "Would it be possible to see these classes before I make a decision?"

There was no reply at first, and Thomas felt a little stupid talking to a scroll. Then the golden font started sketching itself on the scroll. He unrolled more of it to give the System room to work.

Warden of Defense:

Become the Unyielding Guardian.

Starting active class skills: Decree of Bulwark, Rejection of All Undue Influence.

Starting passive class skills: Escalating Defensive Power, Allies Under My Protection.

Four skills per level.

Healer Warden:

Stand firm as the keeper of life.

Starting active class skills: Emergency Restoration, Take Wounds Unto Self, Ten-Minute Reprieve Over Death.

Starting passive class skills: Warden's Protective Healing Aura.

Four skills per level.

Prosperity Warden:

Ensure the bounty of the System is not wasted.

Starting active class skills: (None)

Starting passive class skills: Warden's Ultimate Bounty, The Golden Age of Prosperity, None Fighting Shall Go Wanting, Prosperous Domain.

Four skills per level.

Warden Purifier:

Eliminate all corruption, inward and out.

Starting active class skills: Corrective Purification, Identify All Which Is Unclean, Ultimate Warden's Quarantine, The Scouring Wash.

Starting passive skills: None.

Four skills per level.

Okay, big yikes on the Purifier class. Thomas was in no way a fan of the ball of Corruption mana in his core and had the feeling that would be just the class to wipe it out. He also got the funny feeling that if he took it, he'd eventually find himself dressed in stark black with, like, an old-time Pilgrim's hat on screaming "Burn the witch!" It seemed to be an intense class. In fact, there was no passivity to it at all, considering the lack of skills that wouldn't need his direct attention to activate.

It would have made his choice a lot easier if the remaining three also sucked, but they didn't. They were tempting in their own ways.

The Guardian class was the most straightforward. It was a basic tank role, which he naturally fell into while in team combat. Only it seemed to be a defensive tank on steroids.

A trickle of understanding entered his mind as he looked at the skills. Decree of Bulwark would let him stand practically unmovable between an aggressor and what he wanted to protect. Rejection of Undue Influence would give him strong defenses against mind or emotional manipulation. Though because that was an active skill, he had to actively identify the threat before he put mental walls up against it. Still, that was tempting after dealing with Tiny and Raveth's attacks. Escalating Defensive Power would make him stronger, harder to move, and generally more stubborn the longer he stood and defended. Cool. Allies Under My Protection was an aura skill that transferred a portion of Thomas' defensive prowess to them.

He liked the idea of defending his friends. That spoke to him.

Unfortunately, the theme of the whole class seemed to be 'Stand there and take the pounding.'

Thomas did fight back when he wasn't risking soul-damaging backlash. Sometimes, even if he was. He could use some of the other (gasp) four skills per level to help mitigate that, he supposed, but the core of the class would be to become a wall between people he wanted to protect and the attackers.

That brought him neatly to Healer Warden.

It dovetailed into what he was already doing with his skills, though he wished there was more of a combat aspect built in. Emergency Restoration would link with Living Architecture and increase the speed of healing, which was always valuable. Take Wound Unto Self meant he could transfer the damage from a teammate or an innocent onto his own body. That wasn't exactly fun, but between Living Architecture and the Wellspring skill, he had amazing self-repair capabilities. The Ten-Minute Reprieve Over Death skill was awesome. In case of true emergency or an instantly fatal blow, he could basically put a 'pause' on death for ten minutes, which was plenty of time to fix what was wrong. On top of all that, he'd also get a protective healing aura skill that would further keep his teammates and any allies close by safe.

Yeah, it was a solid class if he wanted to go full healer.

Again, he had the four (four!!!) skill slots per level to keep himself fighting fit, but this would be a big step towards embracing Healer Thomas.

He could live with that. Was it really what he wanted, though?

The last class, Prosperity Warden, was a little... odd. To start with, every class skill was passive, and the names were certainly esoteric enough to mean they may be powerful. As the basic knowledge of the skills was revealed, he found himself intrigued. All of them were aura skills, a lot like what Raveth had used, only they weren't for domination. They were for... the good of others.

Warden's Ultimate Bounty made it so his and any allies' fields were more fruitful, their livestock more fertile, healthy, and nutritious. The Golden Age of Prosperity increased his and allies' luck in all financial matters, which he assumed included mana, as well as added an increased rate of vitality and health. Prosperous Domain was another luck aura skill which tipped the scales to make his personal households and land function more smoothly in a thousand tiny ways. Appliances wouldn't break down, roof damage wouldn't be so likely during a winter storm, people inside his walls would be more likely to succeed in a task or increase their rate of learning.

None Fighting Shall Go Wanting would increase loot drops of himself and allies by 25%, with a 10% higher likelihood of that drop directly benefiting them based on class and need.

Holy crap, that was basically a multiplier of his Gift.

Standing there in a silent room, facing a choice that was his alone, Thomas had to admit to himself that the theme of Prosperity Warden deeply spoke to him.

There was a reason why he had chosen healing and kept on with it, despite the drawbacks and Burden. He liked having the magic power to help himself and others. He'd come to think of his Gift as something greedy, based on the content of his soul. But maybe, just maybe, it was easier to embrace cynicism than face the cornball realization that when System Integration was announced on Earth, Thomas felt a thrill of undiscovered opportunity.

Yes, there were monsters and aliens and maybe System gods, but for the first time, he had the chance to have magic and use it to better himself, his friends, and his family.

That was the core of it. He wanted a prosperous life, and he also wanted to share his abundance with others.

The drawback was that with the exception of the loot multiplier, none of these passive skills would help him fight enemies or dive dungeons. How was he supposed to be a warden defending the integrity of the Integration or whatever by making his allies' fields more fertile?

The answer was Thomas would be making them stronger just by being around them. The whole world was about to be thrown into chaos. People would be ripped away from their families and communities. Widespread famine and disease was almost a certainty. The Light elves would surely be pulling more tricks to divide whatever was left of functioning society so they could pick off the weakest groups.

Not his people, though. They would be happier, healthier, and more likely to succeed in anything they tried. Their loot drops would be better, their luck would be greater.

Plus, he had four other skill slots help out with the rest. He could still tank and still heal. The System had lent him a hand and hinted at his next most viable direction with his new illusion skill.

Really, there was no decision at all. A part of him had known he would accept being a Warden the moment he asked about classes, and no other class got him on this deep level.

Thomas took a breath, let it out, and said, "I choose Prosperity Warden."

It was as if a warm summer breeze flowed through him. For a moment, he smelled wheat ready to harvest under a blue, untroubled sky, felt the contentment of a good meal with good friends, and heard the unbridled laughter of happy children.

Then the moment faded and Thomas found he had one more scroll waiting for him in the chest. This was, thankfully, a short scroll.

You have been granted title: System Warden

Effects:

Significantly increased likelihood of being assigned a System quest

Ability to hide your Warden class from identification skills. (This does not extend to Level 5 classes)

System Favor.

Go forth, Warden, and ensure the prosperity of your people.

"Thanks," Thomas said. "I'll do my best."

After tucking all three scrolls back in the chest and closing the lid, he walked out the door. He had taken his time and was sure his team would be worried about the fact he'd just disappeared.

The door led directly out of the dungeon as usual, but he found only Zach outside. He was sitting cross-legged in his usual meditation pose. That wasn't unusual, but the air of calmness and peace that seemed to surround him definitely was.

Hearing him, Zach opened an eye, saw Thomas, then grinned. "Welcome back, my dude. The others haven't made their way out yet."

Realization washed over him. "We were all separated?"

"I think so." Still grinning, Zach rolled to his feet in a graceful motion. He wasn't wearing shoes. "Let me guess: The System had stuff to say to you, too?"
