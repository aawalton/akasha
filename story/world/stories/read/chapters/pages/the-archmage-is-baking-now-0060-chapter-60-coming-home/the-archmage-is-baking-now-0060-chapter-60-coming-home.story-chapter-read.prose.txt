The next morning came too early and too bright. Jane woke to the sound of the door opening and groaned, pulling her pillow over her head to block out the light streaming in from the windows.

"Wake up, sleepyhead." Bella's voice was entirely too cheerful for whatever hour it was. "I have news."

“Not right now. I had too much ale last night to deal with whatever it is.”

"This news can't wait." Bella crossed the room and yanked the pillow away, ignoring Jane's noise of protest. "It's confirmed. The water levels are completely back to normal. All the pumps are running like they haven't run in months. The town council is talking about throwing you another party."

Jane sat up slowly, blinking against the light. Her head really did ache from the ale, but the news cut through the discomfort like a joyful knife.

"Completely back to normal?"

"Completely.” Bella was grinning. “Eustice came by this morning to tell us. He said his mother wanted you to know before you left. I think she's hoping you'll stick around for another celebration."

"Absolutely not." Jane swung her legs out of bed and stood up. “One celebration was more than enough. I want to go home."

Bella tossed a clean dress onto the bed. "I figured you'd say that. Get dressed. The innkeeper has made us breakfast, and I think he's gone completely overboard."

She wasn't wrong. When they came downstairs, the common room was empty except for the innkeeper himself, who had apparently decided that the best way to express his gratitude was through food. The table nearest the kitchen was covered with dishes, piled high with what were probably the entire contents of his kitchen.

"This is too much," Jane said, staring at the spread. "We can't possibly eat all of this."

"You can try." The innkeeper beamed at them, clearly pleased with himself. "After what you did for us, it's the least I can do. Please, sit. Eat. Take your time."

Jane and Bella exchanged glances, then sat. There was nothing else to be done. The man had clearly put a lot of effort into this meal, and refusing it would have been rude.

Besides, it was delicious.

They both ate until they were stuffed, working their way through the eggs and sausages and making serious inroads on the toast before finally leaning back in an almost uncomfortable state of fullness.

"I'm going to have to roll myself back to Glenfall," Bella groaned. "I don't think I can walk anymore."

"You won't have to walk." Jane pushed back from the table and stood, feeling the weight of the meal settling in her stomach. "We have a ride, remember?"

Bella brightened. "Oh, right. Dave! I forgot about that."

"Speaking of which, we should go find him." Jane looked around the common room, but there was no sign of their young driver. "Do you know where he is?"

Bella stood up as well, wobbling slightly. "His father's house, I guess. Should we go there?"

"Let's." Jane turned to the innkeeper, who was watching them with a satisfied expression. "Thank you for the meal. It was wonderful. All of it."

"You're welcome, Miss Jane." He ducked his head with that same nervous deference she had seen when they first arrived, though it seemed warmer now. "Safe travels. And thank you. For everything."

They both gave him a warm smile before returning to their room to pack. Jane took one last look around the small space that had been her home for the past few days, then shouldered her bag and headed for the door.

The walk to Peter's house was short, like every distance in Trailor. They found Dave outside, loading a trunk onto the back of his carriage with his father's help. He looked up as they approached and smiled.

"Miss Jane. Miss Bella." Wiping his hands on his trousers, he came to meet them. "I was just about to come find you. Are you ready to go?"

"Almost." Jane looked at the trunk and carriage. "Are you sure about this, Dave? Leaving your family, your town? It's a big step."

Dave glanced back at his father, who nodded him forward.

"I'm sure,” he said, turning back to Jane. "This week has been the best of my life. I want more of it. I want to see more places, meet more people, do more things. I can't do that here."

"Then let's get going." Jane smiled and offered him her hand to shake. "Welcome to the team, Dave. Such as it is."

He shook her hand, his smile widening into a grin. "Thank you, Miss Jane. You won't regret this."

"I know I won't." She released his hand and turned to climb into the carriage. "Now come on. We have a long road ahead of us, and I want to sleep in my own bed soon."

—

The journey home was far easier than the outgoing journey had been. Having a dedicated horse and carriage changed everything.

Dave drove them with the same careful attention he had shown in Trailor, keeping the pace steady and the ride smooth. He seemed to know instinctively when to push, when to rest, when the horse needed water, and when the road ahead was likely to be rough. Jane found herself wondering where he had learned all of it, then decided it didn't matter. Some people just had a knack for certain kinds of work, and Dave was the type who would use every opportunity to improve on his own natural talent.

Through careful planning, they managed to stay at an inn every night of the journey. It required some adjustments to their route. They had to take a longer path through some sections to hit a town before dark, but it was worth it. Jane loved the chance to sleep in a real bed every night, eat hot food, and start every morning clean and fresh. Besides, they were still moving faster than they could have on foot.

The days passed quickly, each one bringing them closer to home. Jane watched as the dry scrubland around Trailor gave way to greener hills, and eventually to the familiar pine forests of the higher altitudes. The air grew cooler and cleaner, carrying the scent of the trees to her waiting nose. She was used to that now, she discovered, and liked it just fine.

It was late afternoon on the fifth day when Glenfall finally came into view.

Jane's heart hitched up into her throat at the sight of it. The town sat on an eroded plateau cut into the side of the mountain, like a meal set out on a table. The lake dominated the center, with buildings clustered around the water's edge and then spreading out to form a disk.

Glenfall was visibly busy. Even from this distance, Jane could see people moving through the streets as they went about their work. Despite the warmth of the day, smoke rose from chimneys, probably from bakeries and kitchens preparing the evening meal. Life was happening there, ordinary life. Jane realized that however much she thought she had missed it, she had underestimated.

"There it is." Bella leaned out the carriage window beside her, grinning. "Home sweet home."

"It really is. I didn't think I'd miss it this much."

"You always miss home more than you expect to. That's how you know it's really home."

Dave guided the carriage along the winding road and into the town proper. They passed suddenly familiar shops and streets, digging deeper and closer to the lake. Jane drank it all in, feeling more and more at ease with each passing second.

They made it a little way into town before Bella tapped on the roof of the carriage. Dave pulled the horse to a stop in front of a building Jane recognized as Frank's house.

"This is the end of the road." Bella hopped down from the carriage and turned to look up at Jane. "I'll show Dave where to stable his horse, then get him set up with temporary housing. Frank and Mary have been moping ever since Cecelia left for that job up north. They'll be thrilled to have a boarder again."

Jane climbed down as well, looking between Bella and Dave. She had assumed she would be part of the settling-in process for their young friend. But looking at Bella's face, Jane realized she wasn't needed here. Bella had things well in hand.

"I might just head somewhere else, then," Jane said. "If you've got this covered."

Bella's expression shifted, a knowing smirk tugging at the corner of her mouth. "I bet I know where."

“Maybe."

"Go on, then." Bella waved her off. "I'll find you later. Go do whatever it is you're definitely not thinking about doing."

—

The streets of Glenfall welcomed Jane back like an old friend. She had lived here for less than a year, but it felt like longer.

Everyone knew her now. That still felt strange, even after all these months. She got nods and smiles everywhere she went, from shopkeepers and craftsmen whose names she didn’t even know. Some of them called out greetings. Others just acknowledged her with a tip of the head or a raised hand. All of them knew she was Jane the baker-archmage, who could scare away dragons and, perhaps even more importantly, made good bread. Both were appreciated things, in Glenfall.

She made her way quickly across town. Her destination was halfway to the far side, where the lake's water gathered and flowed out through a river that tumbled down the mountainside in a huge waterfall. It was one of her favorite spots in Glenfall, and the scenery was far from the only reason.

A sturdy stone bridge arched over the outgoing river. Jane crossed it in a hurry, heading to the far side as quickly as her feet would carry her. Then she turned towards the familiar staircase that led down to the riverbank below.

The steps were worn smooth by years of foot traffic from generations of Glenfall residents. Jane descended carefully, her bag bumping against her hip with each step. At the bottom, she stopped. The river flowed past her, clear and cold and inviting.

In that moment, she found that even her hurry took a momentary back seat to her curiosity. She couldn't help herself. Kneeling at the edge, she dipped a finger into the water.

Clean.

The sensation was immediate and overwhelming. The water was clean, so purely and completely clean that she almost couldn't believe it. Whatever contamination had been there in the past, whatever poison had seeped into the lake and threatened to destroy everything, it was gone now. She couldn't sense even the slightest trace of it.

This was probably courtesy of a dragon who seemed to have made it his life’s purpose to gather anything remotely unnatural from the lake he called home.

Jane had talked this dragon into purging the water, which had saved this town. It was easy to forget sometimes, in the day-to-day routine of baking bread and living a normal life. But moments like this brought it back. She had made a difference here, despite all her misgivings about her own capabilities.

And now, she had added another town to her list of good deeds done. It felt nice to know.

Jane rose slowly. She was still standing there, staring into the water, when she felt arms wrap around her from behind.

Strong arms. Familiar arms. She knew them at once, knew the feel of them and the warmth of the body they belonged to. She didn't startle or pull away. She just smiled, a smile that came from somewhere deep inside her, and turned around.

"Allen."

Her favorite man in the world was exactly as she remembered him. He was tall and broad-shouldered, his hands calloused from frequent contact with tools.

He was also wearing absolutely filthy work clothes. If either he or Jane cared about that, neither of them gave any sign of it as they locked into an embrace that took several minutes to come out of.

"How was the trip?" Allen asked eventually. "Did you fix their problem?"

"I think so. The water was coming back when we left, and they're changing their practices, so it shouldn't happen again." Jane looked up at him. "It was good work, I think.”

"It sure sounds like it." He pressed a kiss to the top of her head. "I missed you."

"I missed you too. More than I expected to."

"Is that so? You thought you’d just forget about me, huh?"

"No.." She reached up and touched his face. "I thought about you the whole time, if you must know."

Allen's smile widened. "That sounds boring. I’m not that much to think about."

“You do just fine.”

The air took on a sudden chill as the nighttime wind came down off the mountain. Now tucked in a warm set of arms, Jane began to realize how worn-out from travel she still was.

“You know what I want now?” she asked.

"Dinner?"

"Dinner. And then sleep. In my own bed."

"Sounds perfect." Allen took her hand. He was reliably good at that kind of accommodation. If Jane needed food and rest, he would give her just that, and wait however long it took until she was ready for anything else. “Let’s go.”

They walked back through the town together, hand in hand, towards a warm meal and a real reconnection. From that moment, until the moment Allen kissed her goodbye at her door later that night, that was all Jane cared about.
