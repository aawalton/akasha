With the ball acquired, I moved to the foot of my bed, taking a seat right in front of its edge on the floor. The floor was freezing—cold enough that I could feel it through my pants.

I exhaled, several faint huffs leaving my mouth, as I placed the ball on the ground and stood back up, taking support from the bed. Even the bedsheet was cold.

Now, while I enjoyed freezing temperatures, I preferred them when I had a blanket available, not when I was in summer clothes. Wasting no time, I walked to the Icelure, grabbed the longer white thread that ended with a translucent bluish crystal, and pulled it, turning the Icelure one level down.

I pulled it two times more, and once satisfied, walked back to my seat, on the floor that was still freaking cold!

My eyes turned to the ball as I reached for it. Its surface was warm, and the heat it radiated soothed my chilled skin. It felt as if I had a sun of my own in my hand—one that wasn’t as hot or bright, just… a ball. And now it was cold.

“Idiot ball,” I clicked my tongue, placing the leather ball carefully a step away from me on the ground.

I shifted slightly, moving closer to the ball, and after some careful calculation, which was basically positioning both my index fingers in opposite directions, I paused. Then, exhaling one last time, I struck the ball with all my might.

“Ahh!” I yelped. The ball was like a stone—hard and unmoving. It didn’t roll; it just vibrated.

Removing my left hand, I continued the experiment. When I pushed the ball with just my right hand, it moved easily.

My eyes narrowed slightly. I had gotten the basic gist of it. When two forces collide while moving in opposite directions, they basically kill each other. I clapped my hands, observing the same phenomenon. My hand, which would have otherwise moved forward, stopped the moment the other came in contact.

Looking to the ball, I brought both hands behind it and flicked it at the same time. The ball moved faster than ever.

Meaning when both forces act in the same direction, the power increases drastically.

“Whoa!” My eyes widened in amazement. It felt like I had made a brilliant discovery. Though my amazement was short-lived—obviously, a scholar or a traveler must’ve thought of it before me, I murmured.

I mean, the Otherworlders were much more intelligent than everyone else.

“Haah,” I let out a long, tired sigh, placing the back of my head on the bed. My eyes fixed on the ceiling above. “How do I implement it?” I wondered.

The ceiling didn’t respond.

Well, of course it didn’t; it probably didn’t want to get scrutinized for giving the wrong answer. I mean, I could definitely bully a nonliving thing, right?

“Am I losing my mind!” I yelled out loud, sitting up straighter than usual, my spine probably as stiff as a wand.

“Luminous,” I mumbled. The lights in my room turned off. My eyes were now fixed aimlessly on the cupboard as my vision blurred. I closed them, picturing the scene from the day before yesterday.

Violet-dark mana poured into the staff, its condensed energy stealing mana from the surroundings. After that, the mana stream split at the end of the staff, forming an uneven quadrilateral. “How did he split it, though?” I wondered.

My eyes fluttered open as I leaned back against the wooden bed frame.

“Move the mana sideways. The core pulls on mana by itself, nothing to gain there.” Instructor Mirayel’s words echoed in my mind. “Move the mana sideways,” I repeated. “Sideways?” My eyes widened as I crawled toward the ball and set it before me.

The pull felt by the mana particles was constant, always toward the core. I looked up at the wooden bedpost, imagining it as the anchor.

I drew my right hand toward the post, feeling for the attractive force. Now sideways—I brought my other hand out, forming a ninety-degree angle with the right arm.

“If it works, Instructor Mirayel dies,” I decided, swallowing. My eyes narrowed as I pulled both index fingers back, making sure to apply the same force in the strike. Then, I struck the ball.

The ball moved.

It moved neither straight nor to the right, instead. It rolled diagonally, somewhere between the two forces. Meaning if I were to focus solely on spreading the mana sideways near my hand, the mana would instead move diagonally, since the attractive force was always present.

“Really?” I stood from the floor and walked to the bed, picking up the rough notebook in my hand. I began doodling over the second diagram this time, drawing a quadrilateral pathway for the mana flow, similar to the one Instructor Mirayel had shown me.

After completing the mana pathway, I glanced at the first diagram again. I hadn’t yet added the body’s range to it.

“How does a hand’s mana range work, though?” I wondered. Unsure of the answer, I drew another circle. It was smaller than the one formed by the mana stone, though it still enclosed most of the staff.

With the two diagrams made, it was easy to correlate how mana flow worked. For the range influenced by both the body and the embedded mana stone, the resultant attraction was a diagonal pull—neither straight toward the mana stone nor fully in the direction I pushed it.

And as soon as the body’s mana range ended, near the end of the wand, the mana moved straight toward the core, forming a sharp angle.

“Okay…” I murmured, tilting my neck to the side to ease the tension. My eyes flicked to the clock—10:10. The mess hall was about to close. If I wanted to eat, now was the time.

Though… eating right after making a big discovery?

“What kind of man would I be if I did that?”

Turning around, I walked to the study table and placed the rough notebook safely above the monster biology book. Then, I strode to the cupboard. I was certain I had bought some fruit not too long ago.

They shouldn’t have spoiled yet, right? I asked myself, unsure, as I opened the cupboard. The smell of banana filled my nose, overpowering the faint scent of apple… then again, do apples even have a smell?

I crouched, removing the small wooden basket from the bottom compartment. There was still an apple and two bananas left—enough for dinner, I think.

“Luminous,” I murmured, my eyes narrowing as I inspected the apple. It was in perfect condition, definitely not spoiled. The bananas, though—one was fine, while the other had blackened a bit, still not spoiled though.

Shutting the cupboard, I moved back to the bed and took my seat at its edge. I peeled both bananas, leaving their ends unpeeled for grip. After that, I ate, or more like inhaled, the fruit in four bites. They were sweet, just the way I liked.

With the bananas finished, I barely chewed them properly, I turned my attention to the apple in hand and began eating it.

And… It was freaking sweet! I probably blushed while eating it; it was that sweet!

With the fruit’s noble sacrifice, I subdued some of my hunger, and I placed the basket with the leftovers on the study table before moving back to the bed.

Climbing onto the bed, I sat up straight, resting my back against the headboard. The wood was cold; I could feel it seeping through the thin fabric of my shirt, though it helped me stay awake.

Grabbing the blanket, I placed it over my legs, tucking it loosely around my waist. The fabric was cold at first, but it quickly grew warm, trapping my body heat.

I grabbed the wand placed on my right—and the pillow too, though I didn’t need it right now, so I tossed it to the far end of the bed, not wanting it in my line of sight.

Letting out a long exhale, I calmed myself. “Relax, Arnos,” I murmured. Just spread the mana to the side… to the right? Yeah, that should do it, I added in my mind as I closed my eyes.
