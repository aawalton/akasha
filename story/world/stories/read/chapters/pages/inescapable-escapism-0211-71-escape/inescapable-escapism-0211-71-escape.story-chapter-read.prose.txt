I was frozen, trapped in the hallway just beyond my bedroom, and I couldn’t even breathe as words floated up the stairs towards me.

“There’s no need to hammer away on the door like that,” Mom snapped, her words slightly slurred and her tone sharp. “I was on my way.”

She’d been drinking. The realisation sent a wave of rising panic through me, and my chest clenched in fear. I didn’t know who the people knocking on the door were or what they wanted from us. I wasn’t sure whether they were the same group that had stormed the Academy, murdering everyone in sight, but if they were, I knew Mom wouldn’t be able to stand against them. Even if she were completely sober, they would still easily be able to overpower her.

But it couldn’t be them. It couldn’t be the same people banging on the door in both worlds. They shouldn’t have been able to find me in my reality. There was no way to track me between the worlds. I hadn’t physically entered any of them, and it wasn’t like I was leaving a trail or anything. I couldn’t have been leaving anything behind, so they wouldn’t have anything to use to follow me. It was fine. It had to be fine.

It was probably just a coincidence. I was almost sure that was all it was, I tried to tell myself, but my reassurances felt weak. Even I could hear the hysterical edge that my thoughts had, and I could feel my heart speeding up once more.

Another soft growl came from the door seated on my bed, and I glanced around at him, trying not to let his reaction fuel my fear. It wasn’t helping, though. I hadn’t had him for particularly long. It had only been a couple of weeks, but that was enough for me to know Rex didn’t normally react like that to someone knocking on the door. In fact, it might have been the first time I’d ever heard him growl in my reality.

Perhaps it was just how violently they’d done it. I had barely been in the world, but I’d heard the bangs echoing through the house. They must have smashed their fist against the wood so hard that it had hurt them and probably left bruises on their knuckles.

My eyes met Rex’s, and I lifted a finger to my lips to shush him, silently praying that he would understand me. His lip continued to twitch. His teeth were still bared, but I was relieved to notice that no more sound came from him.

I looked away from Rex again, and my gaze drifted back towards the stairs before panic gripped me. I couldn’t help but worry that they’d already broken into the house and were simply using the person at the front door as a distraction. They could have found another way in and already be in the process of surrounding me, their guns at the ready, before I even realised what was happening, and if so, it was working. I hadn’t even thought to check the windows or even look over my shoulder until that moment, but my head snapped around, and I scanned the corridor.

But it was empty. No one was there, and nothing appeared out of place, but that didn’t help my racing heart. I rose up on my toes, peering through the window at the end of the hall, but nothing seemed suspicious in the garden either.

Movement in the field beyond caught my eye, and my breath caught in my chest, but it was just the sheep. They milled about aimlessly, nuzzling the grass in search of food, and I watched them for a moment, trying to spot the people I expected to see darting between the cover of the sheep, but there was no one there.

“Ah, I’m terribly sorry. I thought perhaps you might be in the garden when I first knocked,” someone replied to my mom.

Her tone was polite. She sounded appropriately apologetic, but something about her voice made the hairs on the back of my neck slowly begin to stand on end. It was just unsettling, and I couldn’t quite pinpoint why. Just something about her made me uncomfortable.

“We have a doorbell, you know? It’s right there,” my mom continued. “Most people use that to get someone’s attention.”

I couldn’t help but wince at how rude she was acting towards that person, and it terrified me. I was almost tempted to run downstairs and shout that my mom didn’t mean it, that she was joking. I wanted to tell her not to antagonise them because I was scared of how they might retaliate, what they might do to her, but I couldn’t.

My feet were too heavy. They refused to move. My presence could make things worse. That was my worry. It could push them over the edge and cause them to hurt my mom, and I couldn’t let that happen. Instead, all I could do was wait anxiously for the woman’s response.

The agonising silence stretched out, and my ears straining to pick up any sound, any hint that they were preparing to attack or hurt my mom, but no sound came from downstairs. Still, I was so worried that I could barely breathe, and my heart was pounding. It seemed to be ricocheting painfully off my ribs with each beat.

“Of course,” the woman replied, a few seconds later.

Her tone was unchanged, and I wasn’t sure whether to be relieved or concerned about it, but I could feel anxiety and restlessness growing within me. I glanced over my shoulder once more, checking to ensure the corridor was still clear, before taking a step towards the stairs.

An almost silent thump came from behind me, and I leapt into the air. My muscles tensed in preparation for the fight, but it was just Rex jumping down from my bed. He hurried across the carpet, his steps barely audible as he came to join me, and I realised that his hackles were still raised and his lips still pulled back away from his teeth.

“Well? What do you want?” Mom demanded, continuing before the woman had a chance to reply. “Are you trying to sell me something? Or are you one of those scammers I heard about? Think you can swindle some money out of me, huh? Go on then. Which charity are you pretending to be with? Save the dogs? NSPC—whatever? You’re all a bunch of frauds, and I’m not interested. You can take your deceptions elsewhere!”

My expression twisted into one of despair, and the urge to cry out, to beg my mom to stop before it was too late, pulled at me, but the words refused to leave my throat. I was stuck, listening on in horror and certain I was about to hear the click of a gun being cocked.

A creak came from the front door as Mom started to close it on the woman, and I stumbled forward before freezing as an unexpectedly light and musical laugh reached up towards me.

“Ah, don’t worry. I’m not here to try and sell you anything or get money out of you,” she said. “But I applaud your shrewdness. I read a report the other day that almost eighty percent of door to door charity collectors do not actually represent the charity they claim to be supporting.”

That didn’t sound right. It seemed far too high, but Mom didn’t question it. She just scoffed loudly.

“I knew it,” she replied, the sneer audible in her voice. “Well. What do you want then?”

There was another slight pause, just long enough that panic tightened around my chest once more, before the woman spoke again.

“I’m actually here to speak to you about your daughter.”

I felt my heart drop into my stomach, and Rex huddled closer to me. His body pressed against my legs, seemingly trying to push me backwards into my room, but I couldn’t move as horror built within me. I was right. They were looking for me.

“My daughter,” Mom repeated. She sounded so taken aback by the mention of me that some of the anger left her voice for a moment, but it immediately flooded back in. “What’s she done now?”

My stomach clenched. I had no idea what woman would say to her, but another pleasant laugh drifted towards me.

“No, no. She’s not in trouble. Don’t worry,” the woman reassured my mom, pausing before adding, “You are Ms Barker, yes? Grace’s mother?”

“It’s Mrs Barker,” Mom corrected her. “And yes.”

She sounded cautious, almost reluctant to admit that, and her voice was at odds with the woman’s. Everything about her seemed perfectly professional and poised. It was as if she’d said the words a hundred times before, or perhaps she’d just rehearsed them in the mirror, practising again and again until they flowed out almost naturally. I could even hear a polite smile in the woman’s voice.

I didn’t quite know what to make of that, but the fact that she knew my name, that she knew my mom’s name, filled me with fear and another emotion. It was a less expected one, but it felt almost as strong.

Deja vu.

Something about the situation felt familiar. I couldn’t quite place it, couldn’t work out what exactly reminded me of what was happening. Perhaps it had been another world, one that I visited long ago and had mostly forgotten. That would make sense. I assumed they must have hunted me down before. They’d probably figured out where I lived at least once in the past, but I wasn’t sure when or where that had happened.

Was it the last world I’d been in? I could vaguely remember it happening. Masked figures had arrived in my quarters, and I was pretty sure I hadn’t welcomed them in. Or perhaps I had. The memory was hazy, but perhaps that was what I was thinking.

“Excellent,” the woman said, sounding genuinely happy. “In that case, may I come in?”

I almost expected my mom to say no. Even though I wasn’t foolish enough to believe that would deter them, I still hoped she would. Then, they’d have to either convince her or force their way in, and the facade would be over. I would know the true reason they wanted me.

Plus, Mom didn’t like to let people into her home, especially not when they just showed up unannounced. She normally preferred to have time to prepare, to begin tidying before screaming at my dad to finish, so she could give the illusion of a perfect household, and that woman had yet to actually explain what she wanted. She’d just said she was looking for me, so perhaps Mom would just turn her away.

However, as much as my mom didn’t like to allow anyone to see beyond the image of our family that she had carefully crafted, she also didn’t like to be the centre of gossip, I realised. Especially not when the gossip could be about something bad, and the woman could cause that. I didn’t know who she was, what she looked like, but I assumed her appearance matched her voice. She was probably wearing a suit or something smart, and if the neighbours saw Mom talking to someone who looked like that, rumours would spread.

I wasn’t sure what they’d say, but I knew they would assume the worst. They probably speculate that she was a police officer, even if she was not in uniform. Or perhaps they’d guess that she was a social worker, that she had received a report that my mom was abusing me or something. It didn’t matter whether it was true or not. People would still talk about it, and by the end of the day, the entire village would know.

Despite my fear, I felt myself wince. Duncan and his parents lived in the village too. They didn’t seem like the type to indulge in gossip, but they might still hear it, and I didn’t want that to happen. I didn’t want Duncan to worry about me, for his parents to judge me or think less of me. I just wanted them to like me.

“Alright then,” my mom decided, but her reluctance was clear.

The front door hinge squealed again as Mom opened it wider to let the person in, and Rex pushed back against me again. I could feel a growl rumbling through him, but thankfully, he made no sound.

“You have a lovely home,” the woman remarked.

Mom didn’t reply. She just let out a grunt.

I didn’t move. My breathing was shallow as I stood in the hallway, and my gaze was fixed on the wall at the top of the stairs, but my attention was elsewhere. I focused on the soft footsteps below, trying to determine whether the woman was alone. I was pretty sure I could only hear two sets of steps, my mom and her, but it was hard to know for certain.

It seemed unlikely that she’d come alone, if she was with the group who’d overpowered the Academy. That was too dangerous. The chances of me being able to get away from one person was much higher than if there were more.

But maybe only she had entered the house. The others could be waiting outside. They may be lining the drive or sneaking in through the gate at the side, preparing a trap, should I try to escape.

Panic rose within me again, and I couldn’t help but glance down at Rex as it turned into helplessness. I didn’t know what to do, whether I should stay there and wait to see what happened, but the temptation to run thrashed in my heart.

I couldn’t, though. I knew that. If I ran, my mom would be stuck with the woman, and I wouldn’t be able to get her out as well. If they were somehow the people I feared, I had no doubt that they would use her to try to coax me back. She would bear the brunt of their misplaced anger, and I would not be able to live with that. The knowledge would haunt me, and I would be forced to face them once more, to turn myself in.

And they may have set a trap, a small voice in the back of my head volunteered. If I tried to flee, they could still catch me, and they might hurt Mom to punish me. I would be forced to watch as they cut the flesh from her limbs, ripped her fingernails from their beds, and pulled her teeth, all the while telling me that it was my fault. I was to blame for the pain they would inflict on my mom.

The lounge door closed with a snap, and I jolted. My breathing hitched, and I glanced between Rex and the stairs, trying to come up with a plan, but I had nothing. I couldn’t stay in the hall, though. It felt too dangerous, too open. There were too many windows that someone could force their way through, and my room was better. It was safer. I only had one window, and I could watch it carefully, wait for someone to appear.

My knees shook as I turned slowly and walked the few steps back to my room with Rex following behind me like a shadow. If it weren’t for his reassuring presence bumping against my leg, I wouldn’t have known he was there. His movements were too quiet, but I was glad. Someone might have been listening in, and I didn’t want them to know I’d heard the woman downstairs.

I stopped not far into my room and looked around, scanning the room quickly before dropping into a crouch in front of Rex.

“What do we do?” I whispered, my voice barely above a breath.

Rex looked back at me, his dark brown eyes staring deeply into my own, but he did not reply. I knew he wouldn’t, but I was still disappointed.

“I really wish you could talk,” was all I was able to say before a sob caught in my throat and cut me off.

It seemed like he felt the same way too, though. Rex’s intelligent gaze was too steady, as if he were trying to communicate something to me, but I wasn’t sure what. Perhaps I was just desperately grasping at straws, though. I wished so strongly that I had someone I could talk to about what was happening, someone who could tell me whether I had lost my mind or was right to be scared. I just wanted someone to tell me what to do because I was so terrified, and I didn’t know.

The urge to flee tightened around me. I longed to run, to hide from what was happening so that I never had to face it, and I couldn’t help but glance at the window, but the sight just sent a wave of disappointment through me.

It was not an option. My bedroom window was too high up, and the ledge on the outside was too thin. It wasn’t wide enough for me to balance on, and even if I somehow managed, there was nothing but smooth brick below. I was a good climber. I always had been, but my time at the Academy had improved that further still, but it was still impossible. I’d have to cling to the windowsill as best I could before dropping to the paving stones below, and an impact from that height would hurt. If I slipped from the sill, it could kill me.

Would that be better than facing the woman seated in the living room with my mom?

My gaze drifted towards the window again, but I snapped it back to Rex, shaking my head in an attempt to push the thought away. That was not an option. I was not willing to kill myself to get away from them. There had to be another way.

But there wasn’t. The only other routes were either through the front door, the back door, or the French windows, and they all involved going downstairs. If I did that, the woman might hear me. She might come out before I had a chance to slip away, and then I’d be trapped.

I was already trapped, though. I was stuck in my room with no way out, and I didn’t know what to do. There wasn’t anything I could do, and I was worried about my mom. She was already with the woman, and I had no idea what was happening down there, but it scared me.

The woman could be anyone, though, I tried to tell myself. There was every possibility that I was just being ridiculous, and she wasn’t even dangerous. Perhaps she was a completely normal person, someone utterly harmless.

Or maybe Mom was already dead.

I straightened suddenly, my heart thudding in my ears. I wasn’t sure if I was even breathing as I turned and left my room again. I didn’t even check to make sure there was no one waiting in the corridor outside. Panic was propelling me forward, and it felt like I was floating along above my body, watching as it walked silently towards the stairs, but I didn’t want to reach them. The sooner I did, the sooner I would get to the lounge, and I was terrified of what I would find there.

The house was quiet, I realised as I neared the stairs. Abnormally so. No voices came from downstairs, no noise from the television, and that felt wrong. There was always noise, always something. Even with the door closed, I should have still been able to hear the quiet murmur of voices. It shouldn’t have been so quiet. Not unless something was wrong. Horribly wrong.

Rex came to a stop behind me as I reached the top of the stairs and grabbed at the bannister, gripping it tightly for support as I let out a shaky exhale. I wasn’t sure my legs would actually keep me upright; they felt as though they were about to give way, but I needed to get down there. I had to find out what had happened to my mom.

My foot had just left the carpet when a sound came from below. The slight squeeze of a door handle being twisted split the otherwise silent house, and my eyes widened. I spun around and sprinted back towards my room with silent, well-practised steps. I threw myself through the door just as a shout erupted from downstairs.
