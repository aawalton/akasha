Aysha watched the trails of cora around Hayden. Unlike normal sight, coran sight viewed the world through the density and fluctuation in cora. The trails told people how their opponent was moving in three-dimensional space. It captured everything: trajectory, acceleration, and relative distance. In blindfold boxing, the boxer not only followed those trails—but they intentionally disrupted them to blind or confuse other boxers. They even manipulated cora to appear as limbs—a concept known as an illusion. But she wouldn’t use those. It was bluntly unfair.

That said, she did question whether she should. Hayden’s body told her nothing. He was deathly still, barely moving to breathe. It was eerie.

How do you even stand that still? she wondered, feeling nervous. Fine. Jab it is.

Aysha struck without warning or indication. Her fist was like a bullet, sharp and clean, intending to land before he could react. Hayden dodged. She saw that and corrected the punch mid-motion, striking horizontally with a hammer fist. She could already hear people screaming, Is that legal? in her mind, but Hayden’s sign clearly defined a punch by Blindfold Boxing standards—so she used it.

Not that it made a difference. Hayden leaned back, and her fist missed.

What? Aysha was stunned when her elbow fully extended, ending the match—in failure.

The audience was dead silent. Then, a few brave voices rose over the eerie atmosphere.

“Wait, is it over?”

“Yeah, I think so.”

“And Hayden won?”

“Yeah. I’m pretty sure.”

“No way…”

“Yeah way, look at her face! Hayden just wiped the floor with a world champion!”

The dumbfounded voices picked up speed, moving until people were practically screaming at each other. Once it hit a fever pitch, the crowd erupted in overwhelming applause.

“Shut up!” Aysha yelled. “It’s not over! Have some respect.”

The students tried to suppress their excitement, but it was unquenchable. So Aysha would have to block during their deafening cheering. But that was okay. She was used to that. She got into position.

“Are you ready?” Hayden asked.

Aysha read the waves when he spoke. She saw the way the vibrations fluctuated in the cora. She could feel it. She was part of it. She said, “Yes.”

Hayden’s attack was immediate—and shocking. He simply punched without aura, illusions, disruptions, or extension. It was a simple jab—but it was at an impossible speed. She had never seen a punch that fast without aura—and she competed throughout the Golden Belt!

Aysha only determined that after the fact because Hayden hit her in the face so hard she felt his knuckles bruise her cheekbone as she stumbled backward. Her world became befuddled—disoriented—as if she were underwater. Half the crowd was screaming bloody murder, celebrating her downfall, and the other half was saying things like, “Was that actually a punch?”

Aysha sat up slowly, head dizzy as the audience cheered. He really didn’t hold back… She looked up and saw Hayden offering her a hand. She accepted his hand to stand, then stared at him before walking away.

“Well that answers that,” someone said. “Damn. Who’s going to box him now? He just crushed the best!”

Aysha’s heart burned with competitive spirit, and she whirled around. “Double or nothing—I’ll box you again, but this time, I’m using illusions and the equalizer.”

An explosive murmur spread through the crowd. Hayden’s current sign read, “Current pool size: 382,000.” If he accepted and won, he would be up to 764,000 credits.

“Bro, if he turns twenty credit hugs into a million creds in two days, I’ll…”

“You’ll what?”

“Nothing, actually. If he doesn’t, I’ll be shocked.”

Laughter rocked the area as Hayden locked eyes with Aysha.

“That’s a half a million credit bill, all said and told,” Hayden said. “As much as I need the money, I would feel predatory to exploit you like this.”

Aysha opened her mouth to laugh, but closed it. He had just beaten her twice despite her using extension rules. It was clear he was a master. And while she did have the money, she wasn’t as confident as she needed to be to lose it so frivolously.

“I will accept another match for one thousand credits, though,” Hayden said. “But you’ll have to abide by the standard rules, aside from the equalizer. That means no illusions. Those are only permissible if you double it.”

He was being reasonable, so Aysha nodded. “Okay, I’ll—”

“Don’t you dare play it safe,” her teammate—Dae—said. She was on the blindfold boxing team, and she was furious. “It doesn’t matter if it’s five hundred thousand or five hundred million—we need to do this. For all of us. So don’t worry about it.” Dae walked up to the pay pad and tapped her bracelet. “I got it.”

“Thanks, Dae—I won’t lose.” Aysha’s eyes flashed with determination. This wasn’t about her anymore. It was about her entire team. “You better be an actual blindfold boxer because I'm not holding back.”

Hayden regarded her emotionlessly. “I'm not asking you to.”

Anticipation was on blast off, heating the sunny weather to the heat of a nuclear core. Her coach and team eyed her, nodding in approval. The majority of students seemed to want her to lose for the story, but unfortunately for them, she would please the elitists and salty past competitors praying for Hayden’s downfall.

There was no choice: she was using illusions.

Illusions were manifestations of cora that gave the impression of being a punch when they really weren't. These decoys were both the strength and weakness of blindfold boxing, and taught people how to disrupt a riftwalker's instincts and senses within rifts.

“What do you want to do first?” Hayden asked.

“Attack.”

It was stupid to give her a choice. Hit defenders had to attack dazed.

Obviously.

“Okay,” Hayden said. “On you.”

Aysha didn't hesitate. She cocked her fist back and summoned a blunt cylinder of raw cora over the top of her arm. She then sent the cylinder at his face, giving the impression she was punching head-on. At the same time, she prepared a hook.

Hayden would dodge the fake strike, then she would clock him.

Things went perfectly. He dodged—she hit him head-on.

Or not.

To her bewilderment, her punch hit dead air. What just happened? she wondered. I clearly hit him… didn’t I?

-

The casual observers were equally perplexed. To them, Hayden stood perfectly still, and she punched at dead air to his left. One student said, “Did that first punch break her or something?” Their friend replied, “Maybe… She just hit… nothing.”

-

Aysha turned to her team in confusion. She thought she botched it, but her coach and teammates were equally stunned. Tiasha had her eyes fixed on Hayden, but the rest said unique variations of, “She totally hit him. Right?”

Dae turned to Tiasha. “What the hell just happened?”

Tiasha studied Hayden carefully. “We’ll review after the match. It’s still going on.”

A lingering dread loomed over Aysha like a mountain’s shadow when she said that. She looked at Hayden like a supernatural beast. He was standing without the slightest hint of emotion—an impassable wall. She gulped and got into position with a flapping heartbeat. Never before had she felt so much trepidation—no, fear. Horror, even. She winced as she got into position.

“Are you ready?” Hayden asked.

Aysha felt sweat drip down her chin. “Yes.”

“Okay. Get ready. I'll attack in three, two, one…”

Hayden threw a simple hook. It was good, but not good enou—

A fist crashed into her face with the force of a falling shuttle, and she hit the ground and rolled a full rotation before coming to a halt. She had absolutely no idea what had happened.

The spectators were also perplexed—and they let the world know it. Chaotic cheers rang out like a flock of startled birds, and the students circled Hayden like vultures. Nearby students said things like, “He actually broke her,” and “No joke. First dead air, then dodging for no reason. What the hell’s going on?”

The enraged blindfold boxing team turned to Tiasha. “He’s cheating! What the hell’d he do?”

Tiasha didn’t answer. She just walked up to Hayden, making the audience fall silent again. Once she stood before him, she said something that instantly sent ripples through Junolyx.
