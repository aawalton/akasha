After the quick meeting to welcome her and make sure Aida didn’t have any pressing issues to discuss—but unfortunately didn’t have time to reveal more information about the epidemic they were seeing (“Let’s get together at lunch to catch up”)—Bokar immediately assigned her to the public clinic to help lower their case load.

“All of the other clinics have been overrun and can’t treat those who need more minor treatment. Please help us clear the queue.”

That was how Aida found herself standing underneath a tent outside of the pharmacy-clinic as part of the triage team, using her mana to Heal or Detox those who sustained minor work injuries and didn’t need the full might of the Clinic’s resources.

It was boring work; the injuries were all things like pulled or strained muscles, or even some minor poisonings from children who ingested things they shouldn’t have. But at least time passed quickly, and people were appreciative.

The two other Clinic employees at the tent with her weren’t practitioners; they worked with the blunt efficiency of jaded nurses who had seen their share of those at their worst, bringing patients with broken bones and deep gashes (those with these kinds of injuries looked like Adventurers) in their flesh into the clinic. Aida had offered to heal them, but they had stopped her.

“No offense, but since you’re new it’s better to have our more experienced Healers deal with them.”

Well, their loss, I guess. Even though she was certain she could have handled the injuries, she was still gaining a significant amount of experience for Heal based on sheer volume; she had already reached Heal (Lv30), and despite nonstop Healing for the past two hours her mana recovery was easily keeping pace with her output.

“Are there normally so many of these… minor injuries?”

Joann, the more senior nurse, clicked her tongue. “We’re not treating only residents of Burston City. Several of the neighboring villages are finally making their way into town for treatment now that the monster attacks have subsided.”

She nodded towards the line of mostly Adventurers, who had been given diluted sleep potions to numb their pain as they waited to see a more “experienced” Healer. “And of course, the number of Adventures injured on patrols has been increasing.”

“Some of us have come from as far as the Grasslands,” the mother of the queasy child Aida was examining at the moment offered just then. She shivered. “It… things haven’t been good there.”

“What’s going on?” The child had no traces of poison in his body, and his body was a healthy green according to the Heal overlay. She frowned, squatting down so that she was looking into his eyes. He met her gaze hesitantly. “Other than your headache, do you have discomfort anywhere else?”

He shook his head. “When was the last time you ate?”

“On Moon Day,” his mother answered for him, wringing her hands. “He’s only been able to drink broths, he can’t eat any solids. He throws it all up.”

“That’s one star cycle ago,” Aida said, startled. “Did you bring any of the food he can’t eat?”

“I didn’t… but I don’t believe it’s poisoned. My husband and I have been able to eat the same things with no issues…” Mom shook her head in despair. “He can’t even eat the food here.”

“This may be a more complicated issue,” Joann said, frowning. “Let me go see if we can fit your boy into our clinic for treatment.”

“How do you feel when you eat?” Aida asked the child quietly as Joann departed.

“Bad,” he said dully. He swallowed. “I feel like there’s something stopping me from eating.”

Frowning, Aida had him open his mouth. No blockages, no irritated flesh in his mouth or back of his throat.

“What about when you drink?” He shivered at the question.

“It tries to stop me, but…” he swallowed again and became even paler. “But I force it through. Because I’m so hungry.”

‘It’ tries to stop him? Feeling dread build in the pit of her stomach, Aida turned to the mother. “May I speak with you in private?”

Leaving the boy with a cup of pure water, the two women stepped aside, with the mother glancing worriedly at her boy as he sipped at the cup, coughing.

“Can you tell me more about what your son was like before he could no longer eat, what’s been happening in the Grasslands, and anything else you might think is important?”

“Thank you so much for working on your first day here,” Bokar said gratefully as the two of them sat down in the dormitory cafeteria. “I was planning for you to explore Burston City a bit, get used to the place during the rest days so you could be fresh and ready to go on Spark Day. I greatly appreciate your flexibility.”

“Not a problem,” Aida said. “I’m happy to help.”

“And your help is invaluable!” Bokar dug into his plate, sighing in contentment as he swallowed. “The majority of our Healers pick up some mana senses over the course of their career, but it can’t compete with your natural ability. Having you on staff will go a long way towards bolstering the amount of work we can take on.”

His spoon paused in the air as he suddenly recalled something. “That’s right, Professor Bruce had mentioned something about you having to take on additional obligations…”

Moment of truth. Will he let me go to Moonglade for Meditation training?

Keeping her expression carefully neutral, Aida waited with bated breath for his verdict.

“You were supposed to travel to Moonglade for special training, if I recall correctly—but fortunately for you, your would-be instructor decided to come here, instead!” Bokar beamed at Aida, as if he had given her a treat.

“That’s great!” Aida exclaimed, matching his energy. “So—he’s here already?”

“Yes, yes, he says he found accommodations at Paw’s Rest nearby; I’ll give you his details and you can go find him later.” Bokar fixed a stern, though not unkind, eye on her. “Now, I trust my employees to balance their work and life obligations appropriately, but I must give you this warning regardless: this morning’s work notwithstanding, I won’t demand you use your free time to attend to Clinic duties. However, I do expect your devoted attention and effort during your regular work hours.”

“Absolutely.” He beamed at her response.

“Excellent! Now, tell me how your morning went. I know Charlotte woke you up quite early…”

Later that afternoon, Aida stood outside of a shabby inn. There didn’t seem to be much patronage of the establishment: a goat with a single spiral horn watching her warily with intelligent eyes from a small barn, and one mildly rotund gentleman wearing a weathered straw hat sitting cross-legged on a similarly weathered straw mat beside the inn’s door.

He lifted his head as she approached, revealing a pleasantly plump face that crinkled into a smile as he saw her approach.

“Greetings! The day is young yet for earning merit. Spare some alms to protect your soul?” He lifted an empty bowl towards her.

“Um… Sorry, I don’t have any baen on me. Are you…” Aida glanced at the scrap of paper in her hand. “Are you from the Clear Mind Temple?”

“Indeed I am! My name is Senior Brother Wonathan, but you may call me Brother Won.” The monk heaved himself to his feet with the aid of a bamboo pole that had been laying beside him. He was only slightly taller than Aida, but the way he smiled kindly at her made her feel as if he was looming over her like a child. “May I help you?”

“I’m Aida Loreh, and I was told that you would teach me meditation…” Aida dug through her bag and drew out her graduation badge, holding it out for him to inspect. The gleaming mana-laden jewels glowed even in the late afternoon sun.

“Oho!” Brother Won pushed his rectangular spectacles up his nose as he examined her face. “I’m glad you came to find me; I was going to head to the clinic on Spark Day to see if you had arrived yet. I’m very pleased to meet you, Miss Aida.”

He bowed to her with a reverence she didn’t feel she deserved. When he straightened, she suddenly felt even smaller, younger, than before. It was as if he was looking into the innermost depths of her soul, trying to figure out who she was.

The worry she had expressed about getting found out when Professor Bruce had first broached the subject about getting her Meditation training—and Levi had so easily dismissed—resurfaced as she saw herself reflected in Brother Won’s glasses.

“Why don’t you come on inside, Miss? I’d like to get to know you better.”
