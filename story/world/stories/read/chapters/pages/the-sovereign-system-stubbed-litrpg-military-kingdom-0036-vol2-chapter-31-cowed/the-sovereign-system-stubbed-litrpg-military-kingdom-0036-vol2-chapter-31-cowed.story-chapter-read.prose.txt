After bidding farewell to the crown prince, Luke signaled to Tristan and made his way toward the second prince’s group. He watched with barely concealed distaste as Richard struggled to coax the drunk prince into the carriage.

Like a tantrum-throwing child, Prince Lyon kicked and thrashed, refusing to cooperate. It wasn’t until one of his servants arrived with a full cup of wine that he finally relented, stumbling into the carriage of his own accord.

Maintaining an impassive expression, Luke turned to the head librarian.

“Can you ride? I think we could use the fresh air,” he asked.

“Yes. The weather is lovely this time of year in the capital,” Tristan replied with a knowing nod, the two men coming to a wordless agreement.

Luke approached a tall gray gelding, extending his hand. The horse nuzzled it gently.

“Good boy,” he murmured, patting the animal’s neck.

The gesture reminded him of his cheeky mare, the one he’d been forced to leave behind in Clayton City. A quiet sigh escaped his lips—half-lament, half-memory.

I really wish I could have taken her with me…

“General Drakon, aren’t you supposed to ride in the carriage with the second prince?” one of the soldiers—one of Richard’s men—asked, clearly confused.

“I prefer to ride, soldier. You’re more than welcome to take my seat and accompany His Highness,” Luke replied curtly, his tone brooking no argument.

Babysit that bastard? Fat chance.

Without waiting for a response, he mounted the gelding in one fluid motion and took hold of the reins. Tristan followed suit, climbing onto a nearby mare with the grace of someone long familiar with the saddle—despite his age.

“Y-you too, Deputy General?” the soldier stammered, eyes wide. “We won’t have enough horses if both of you choose to ride…”

Luke waved a dismissive hand. “Nonsense. What kind of general rides in a carriage while his men brave the elements? Not I, and certainly not Tristan Gale.”

A heavy silence followed. Luke could feel the weight of multiple stares on him. Shifting in his saddle, he cleared his throat and gestured for the soldier to hurry and secure two more horses.

Fortunately, a nearby stable had a pair ready for sale. Within ten minutes, the group was assembled, and Luke gave the command to move out.

Just as he did, a court official rushed into the path of his horse, his face flushed with panic.

“G-General! You haven’t even been briefed on the mission yet!”

The high-pitched, almost effeminate squeal in the man’s voice made Luke shudder. It reminded him far too much of Minister Chao—the king’s delegate in Clayton City, and a man Luke would’ve preferred to forget.

Suppressing the unpleasant memory, Luke focused on the official. He’d indeed been hasty—but with good reason. He wanted to avoid Hayden Hart at all costs.

“There’s no time to delay. Tell me the details on the way,” he said, gesturing to the back of his horse.

The minister’s panic only grew.

“B-but General… the carriage?” he stammered, casting a hopeful glance toward it—clearly where he had expected to ride for the duration of the journey.

Luke’s expression darkened.

“Every moment you delay, you endanger our soldiers. I wonder how the king will react if he learns we lost valuable men because you couldn’t follow simple orders.”

The color drained from the minister’s face, his horror plain for all to see.

In the next instant, the man vaulted onto the back of Luke’s horse with surprising agility—drawing stunned gasps from the onlookers. Even Luke raised an eyebrow in disbelief.

“Move out!” he barked, pointing down the road.

“E-er… General,” the official said meekly, leaning in from behind, “we’re actually headed southeast.” He pointed awkwardly toward the opposite side of the road.

“…Southeast!” Luke shouted without missing a beat, pulling on the reins and guiding his gelding in the correct direction.

With that, the column began its march. Fifty riders and a horse-drawn carriage set off along the dirt road, hooves kicking up dust in their wake.

Luke rode at the front, spurring his gelding into a canter and setting the pace. Shouts and curses rose behind him—soon swallowed by the rhythm of pounding hooves.

“G-General,” the official began nervously, “if we keep this pace, the second prince will—”

“BLURGHHHH!”

A wet retching sound echoed from the carriage.

Luke’s lips curved into a grin. Prince Lyon had finally succumbed to motion sickness, and a rare sense of satisfaction bloomed in his chest. He listened to the curses that followed for the next fifteen minutes, thoroughly enjoying every moment—until they faded away at last.

An hour passed. The high walls of Ralis City were long gone from view, swallowed by the rolling hills behind them. Finally, Luke slowed his horse and brought the column to a halt.

He had wanted to put as much distance as possible between himself and the capital—to avoid one man in particular.

Hayden Hart.

The prince’s misery had simply been an added bonus.

“Someone check on the second prince,” Luke ordered, swinging down from his horse with practiced ease.

The court official moved immediately—but Luke stopped him with a sharp look.

“Not you.”

The man froze mid-step, his face twisted in worry.

Tristan dismounted beside Luke, giving a slight nod. With the group finally settled and away from the city, it was time for the mission briefing.

“Now,” Luke said, “tell me what we’re dealing with.”

The official gathered his thoughts and launched into the explanation.

The king had deployed nearly fifty thousand soldiers from the capital to Yunxi, a fortified city along the kingdom’s southeastern border. The army had laid siege to Qin forces entrenched there. After a prolonged campaign of attrition, the defenders had finally fallen.

But before surrendering, the Qin troops poisoned the city’s wells and burned all remaining food stores—leaving the victors with nothing but their own dwindling supplies.

A request for additional provisions had been sent to Ralis nearly a month ago. The king approved it. But since then, not a single message had arrived. No confirmation. No reports.

Just silence.

As Luke listened, a chill crept up his spine.

No word in a month…

It would take ten days on horseback to reach Yunxi. But now, he wasn’t even sure there would be an army left to evacuate.

“Were they ordered to advance further into the Qin Empire after conquering Yunxi?” Luke asked, his brow still furrowed in thought.

“This was always the plan, General. The king wished to strike the Empire hard and fast on multiple fronts,” the official explained.

“And what was their next target?”

“That would be the city of Xiandong, General.”

Luke inhaled sharply, a cold breath catching in his chest as a chill settled over him.

Beside him, Tristan reacted similarly—though the quartermaster hid it far better.

“W-what is it?” the official asked, clearly unsettled. Watching both the commanding general and his second-in-command turn grim at the same moment was hardly reassuring.

But Luke didn’t answer. His thoughts were already racing.

Xiandong.

A fortified city nestled in the southwestern-most region of the Qin Empire—bordering not only the Marxx Kingdom, but also the Principality of Selis. And more alarmingly… Lhair.

Strategically, Xiandong was a linchpin. The city commanded a crucial area overlooking all three neighboring nations. Its fortress, perched on the edge of a deep valley, made any southern assault a logistical nightmare. The elevation, the terrain, and the supply routes—all favored the defenders.

Unless…

Unless the attackers came from the north. If someone struck from within the Empire’s own territory, the fortress would be far more vulnerable. Its rear defenses were notoriously underdeveloped.

Luke clenched his jaw. From a purely tactical standpoint, the king’s plan to capture Xiandong made sense. But in light of Lhair’s secret aggression, it had become a reckless gamble.

He visualized the maps he’d memorized through countless books and briefings. Routes spread across his mental landscape—lines, curves, choke points, and open plains. A worrying possibility began to emerge.

If Lhair bypasses the valley using our own territory, they could flank our forces as they march south from Xiandong…

Even if it sounded unlikely, Luke had a gnawing feeling in his gut. It was a risk they couldn’t afford to ignore.

He would need to proceed with caution.

“Where is the bastard!?”

A high-pitched shriek shattered his concentration.

Luke’s head turned sharply. From the carriage stumbled Prince Lyon, regally dressed but in pitiful condition, supported by a beleaguered soldier.

The poor man assigned to check on him was pinching his nose and looking visibly traumatized.

The prince’s once-elegant robes were soaked in a rancid blend of wine and vomit, the acrid stench of alcohol wafting through the air. Judging by the stains and his disheveled state, the prince had passed out after vomiting and then rolled in it throughout the bumpy ride.

“Where is the bastard who did this to me!?” he bellowed again, louder this time, scanning the group with wild eyes.

Their gazes locked.

For a brief second, the world seemed to pause.

Then rage contorted the prince’s face. He stormed toward Luke, fists clenched, eyes blazing with fury and humiliation.

Luke sighed quietly and braced himself.

Though he had been named a general by royal decree, Prince Lyon’s presence created a precarious situation. He had been sent to earn merit—not to command. In theory, he held no actual authority over military decisions.

This operation was Luke’s to lead.

And Luke intended to make sure everyone, including the prince, understood that.

“YOU!” the prince thundered, storming toward Luke with murder in his eyes.

What happened next took place in the blink of an eye.

Before anyone could intervene, Luke stepped forward, pivoted, and drove his heel into Prince Lyon’s sternum with a powerful push kick. The blow landed squarely, sending the prince flying backward like a sack of grain. He crashed to the ground with a solid thud, his limbs sprawled out in disarray.

A pitiful groan escaped Lyon’s lips as he struggled to breathe, the air knocked clean from his lungs.

Luke resisted the urge to follow up with a few hammer fists. Instead, he calmly adjusted his robe and watched the man writhe on the ground, his face flushed crimson beneath a mop of disheveled golden hair.

“SEIZE HIM! KILL THAT MOTHERFUCKER!” the prince shrieked, pointing a trembling, accusatory finger at Luke. His voice cracked into a near-hysterical screech.

But no one moved.

The fifty men present—including Tristan, Richard, and the court official—stood frozen in place. Some looked down in silence, others shifted awkwardly. Their expressions ranged from barely-concealed pity to tightly restrained anxiety.

Seeing no response, the second prince began to seethe.

“What are you waiting for? He assaulted me! I’m the second prince! How dare you disobey my orders?!”

“Prince Lyon…” Richard began cautiously, stepping forward, “that’s the general. We can’t just—”

“I don’t give a shit who he is!” the prince shouted, cutting him off mid-sentence. “He must be punished!”

He stomped his foot like a spoiled child throwing a tantrum.

Luke began walking toward him, slow and deliberate, his expression unreadable. The moment he activated his Domineering Air skill, the very air around them shifted. It thickened like fog—heavy, pressing, and suffocating.

The second prince visibly recoiled, his bravado wilting as he stumbled back a step.

“S-stay back! Don’t come any closer!” he stammered, raising one hand defensively.

Luke didn’t stop. His eyes glinted with restrained fury as he closed the distance, radiating an overwhelming presence that made the prince shrink further with every step. The foul stench of bile and half-digested wine hung in the air, but Luke ignored it.

He stopped a mere foot away.

“Listen here, Your Highness,” Luke said coldly, the last two words dripping with disdain. “I don’t give a fuck who you think you are. Prince, bastard, soldier, noble, official—it doesn’t matter.”

His voice didn’t rise. He didn’t need to shout. Every syllable landed like a hammer striking steel.

“While you’re under my command, you will obey my orders. My orders—and mine alone. Do I make myself clear?”

The second prince’s expression twisted. Confusion, fear, and disbelief fought for dominance on his face. It was painfully obvious he had never been spoken to like this in his life.

“I—I…” he faltered.

Luke slowly reached a hand toward his waist, resting it upon his weapon.

The prince flinched as if struck, his eyes wide with panic.

“Yes, General!” he cried, raising both hands in surrender. “Y-yes! I understand!”
