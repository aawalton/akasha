“Let me guess,” Richard said, giving me a sidelong smirk as we took the elevator down. “Either you had a really nice night, or…”

Aerion rolled her eyes. “I wish. He was out like a log.... and gallivanting around on the rooftop for hours testing out his new toy,” she unhelpfully added.

“Hey!” I retorted. “It’s not a toy, okay? And besides, you made good progress on your front too, didn’t you?”

“Galia’s development is a critical need for the entire party,” she said defensively.

“Uh-huh, and mine isn’t?”

Aerion narrowed her eyes but said nothing, instead frowning and pressing her lips together.

“Yeah, that’s what I thought,” I said with a smirk.

Galia had visibly grown from her feeding frenzy earlier, having ranked up and leveled to E-5, and not only had her powers all been upgraded significantly, [Incinerate], which was stuck at F-MAX, could now rank up to Emergence. She'd also gained an ability called [Aerial Strike] which allowed her to launch a force blast with her wings.

That might not have sounded like much given all we'd fed her, but even at F-0, the ability could push cars around, and had a massive area of effect. I was almost salivating at what she'd be able to do once it leveled.

Plus Galia now looked almost large enough for Aerion to ride. I was still out of the question, though, and likely would be for a while given our lack of soul crystals.

The sudden upgrade had her preening like a queen, and I could only pray it didn't worsen her already inflated ego. We seriously spoiled her way too much.

“Well, I hope you won’t keep the secret from us for too long,” Richard said, entirely oblivious to my musings. “We’re all eager to see what sort of contraption you’ve cooked up.”

“Oh,” I replied, my grin growing even wider. “You’ll see. You’ll see.”

It would not be an overstatement to say that, even including the full might of my Siege Bolts—which, while destructive, not every situation called for killing everything and everyone up to and including myself—this new weapon I had created was in another realm entirely.

It multiplied my offensive ability rather than simply adding to it. And the deluge of stats I gained from having such a stupid number of soul crystals was just the icing on top. The thing was everything I’d hoped it would be and more… and I could not wait to test it out on some juicy undead.

As Aerion had said, the previous night was actually quite relaxing, with everyone sharing a nice meal that Yashas had cooked up, generally just relaxing and enjoying the last bit of luxury that we’d likely have over the next few days. Yashas had scouted out potential locations we might use as safe houses that were near the enemy complex, but without seeing them for ourselves, we wouldn’t know if they’d be viable.

As for why we hadn’t set out the previous night, well, we wanted to maximize the amount of time we had with the zombies in their dormant state.

Yes, it meant waiting another several hours. Hours where people could, and very likely would, die on the outside, but being well rested genuinely could mean the difference between making a fatal mistake in the middle of combat and surviving.

And also, the time advantage we had within the dungeon reduced the urgency just a bit. Yashas had spent about a year inside, though only a few months outside had passed.

Even still, every minute the dungeon existed meant more monsters spewed out, assaulting the military forces who had gathered to stop it, to say nothing of the assaults on cities and other places.

I had to admit, I struggled to come to terms with the fact that my existence was so critical to the livelihood of Axius. That even my inaction meant lives lost. It was honestly terrifying and more than a little nauseating, thinking of my life that way, where every single move I made meant the life and death of so many.

The only way I had learned to cope with it was to ignore that fact. Thinking about it created such a crushing pressure that I wasn’t sure I would be able to go on. It had been the topic of last night’s discussion around dinner, and I was glad to learn that I wasn’t the only one who felt that way. I supposed anyone in our situation would have similar thoughts.

Yashas fired up the Humvee and we made our way down the streets, avoiding zombies whenever we could and crushing them when we couldn’t. While we did gain somewhat of a crowd following us, my newly Soulwoven shards came in clutch here, launching out of my inventory and piercing heads one after another before returning and relaunching.

Thanks to their limited awareness doing the heavy lifting, instead of merely holding off the horde, this time I actually thinned them out to the point where there were only a handful of stragglers following us by the time we got on the highway.

“I don’t know what’s scarier,” Arianna said. “Your ability to seemingly upgrade your abilities like that overnight, or the brain capable of devising such clever solutions.”

She had her eyes on the snake-like chain that was currently coiled against my armor when she said that.

I shrugged. “We’re all on the same team. Just trying to do my part.”

The Brazilian woman nodded. “You are certainly succeeding at that,” she said, biting her lip.

Was that jealousy in her tone?

That was ridiculous, considering how instrumental she’d been so far. Even if all she ever did was keep Richard alive, that in and of itself was a massive boon to the party. And her insights and constitution allowed her to do a hell of a lot more than that.

Rather, I felt like the imposter, relying so much on my gear to give me an advantage. Which was, of course, ridiculous considering that was my power and one that had proven a massive advantage over the other Champions’ more limited power sets.

We got off the highway only a few minutes later and puttered down some residential streets until we arrived at an unobtrusive four-story building from the 90s that I would never have known was the nerve center of Tokyo’s vast Metro Network.

“I stumbled upon this quite by accident,” Yashas said. “As I went through every building in the area, this one, strangely didn't have a single shambler within it. In fact, it was so free of hostiles that I considered making it my location for a safe house. Unfortunately, it was a bit too far from the center of things to serve that purpose well.”

“Uh-huh,” I muttered, noting that we had actually traveled several miles south, away from where we needed to go in order to get here. Which did mean fewer enemies, but it also meant more of a distance to travel going back.

Yashas punched a combination on a security door—one that I had no idea how he’d obtained—which clicked happily, allowing us to enter into a stairwell that led down to a basement and another door, which required a separate combination that the Champion also unlocked.

I had to wonder if he was getting some of this from that information broker he said he had access to through his power. It seemed incredibly unlikely he’d found it any other way.

The door opened up into a vast, windowless room filled with rows of long desks and even more monitors.

It reminded me a bit of NASA’s Command Center for rocket launches, with a vast array of panels set up on the far wall, each showing a different camera on the Metro System. A map of the whole system with LEDs lighting up each station was in the very center, though every station on the network currently showed red, with more red Japanese characters flashing ominously above them.

“What I'd give for google translate right now,” I muttered, then cracked my fingers. “Guess we figure this out the hard way.”

After about a half hour of digging around on the various computers and trying to understand the system, or really more like trying to fumble around computers set up in a language none of us knew how to read, it became obvious why everything was red.

The system had gone into an emergency lockdown mode upon detecting the zombies, most likely having done so after a train crashed into one or more on the tracks, which shut down the whole system.

Getting it back up and running was as simple as clearing the lockdown from one of the terminals once we’d found the control.

That lit up most stations in green, though a handful were still red. I could only imagine it was because the train there had gotten derailed or because there were shamblers still on the tracks.

Clearing the lockdown, however, only allowed the possibility of train travel to start. It didn’t actually restart any of the trains, which was good because we wanted to be very selective about which ones we re-enabled.

The fact that there were cameras literally everywhere, with multiple in each station and even junctions, gave us a lot more options than I’d initially considered. I honestly couldn’t believe it was still working a year later without anyone to tend to it. Japanese meticulousness really saved our butts.

“You can see everything from here,” Aerion muttered as her fingers flew over the keyboard, changing from one camera to another.

Of course, it was mostly her who had figured out how the system worked, despite the technology coming from my world. That was my mechanical genius. She’d have been such a successful engineer back on Earth. I could almost imagine her huddled up on an office chair in front of a monitor with glasses and an oversized T-shirt and shorts, whipping up some monstrous invention in CAD at 2 AM. The image was almost too perfect.

“It seems that we can remotely activate trains from here,” Aerion announced. “Well, most of them, anyway. Some lines have not been equipped with automation. Those will require us to manually attend the trains.”

“All right,” I said, rubbing my hands as I stared up at the dizzyingly complex map. “Option one is to just pilot a train all the way north, dealing with whatever zombies come into our path.”

“That would be quite a lot of them,” Yashas said.

“Right,” I said. “Which means it would most likely cause the train to derail at some point. I figure we can probably ram into a handful without much trouble, but a horde…”

Yashas nodded. “I would not count on it.”

“Right, which means we need to be a little smarter about this,” I said, an idea coming to mind. A crazy idea, but when were Greg ideas anything but?

And if it worked, it wouldn’t be so crazy now, would it? Didn’t someone say that the line between genius and insanity was measured only by success?

“So hear me out,” I said. “I have another idea that might get us there without even having to deal with a single zombie at all.”
