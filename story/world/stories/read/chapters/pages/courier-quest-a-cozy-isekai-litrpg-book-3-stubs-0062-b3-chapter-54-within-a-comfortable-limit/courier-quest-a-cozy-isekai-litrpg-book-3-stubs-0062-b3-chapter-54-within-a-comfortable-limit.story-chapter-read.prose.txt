The meteor storm had lasted almost three hours, dazzling not just the newlyweds but the entire island’s population. Whatever magic the ritualists were working on lasted until about a half an hour after the show was over, but most of the viewers had gone home by then.

Trevor and Adabelle wandered through the cold streets of Rabinar side by side on the way back to the hotel, still occasionally looking up at the sky. An errant meteoroid would streak by every so often, but not nearly in such prolific numbers as they had before.

They rested easily that night, though they didn’t have a chance to sleep in the next morning.

Woken up by an enthusiastic knock on their suite’s door, Trevor groggily extricated himself from Adabelle’s limbs and used quick change to get into his pajamas. When he opened it, he was met with a grinning Lora.

“Wakey wakey, eggs and bakey!” she all but sang as she saw her fellow Summoned. “A very good morning to you, Mr. Anderson. I hope last night found you well and that you had plenty of chances to wish upon the stars.”

“It did, thank you, though we could have gone without Lord Gertabare’s presence,” Trevor yawned, making it perfectly clear what he thought of the minotaur’s title.

Lora hummed and tapped her chin before turning down the hallway. “That would explain these.”

He leaned out to see a cart with a truly gigantic bouquet of fresh roses on it. There was a letter addressed to Mr. and Mrs. Anderson-Katine leaning on its side. The courier frowned and narrowed his eyes. “Where did they even get so many flowers?”

“I think you underestimate rich people,” she replied as she shrugged. “No one was supposed to be there other than the observatory’s caretaker, though. I’m sorry this so-called lord tried to use his station to get in your way.”

“Not your fault. Plus, I got to see Adabelle give some rich dude the business,” Trevor said, sighing wistfully as he remembered it. “Guy thought I was her butler, not husband, and offered to accompany her for the night despite having some other noblewoman there.”

“No!”

“Yup. I’m not sure what he thought his game was, but my wife is awesome.”

“As much as I agree with you, I’m going to ask you to wake her up because we’ve got to hit the road soon,” Lora told him with a smile. “You’ve got enough time to get ready and eat and stuff, but I’d like to get you to the warehouse and to our next destination.”

“And will you tell us where we’re going?” Trevor asked. “Or where these eggs and bacon you mentioned are?”

“No, and also, right here,” she said before ducking behind the cart with the flowers. The teleporter picked up a tray with two plates of breakfast food and two glasses of orange juice. She handed them to Trevor before saluting. “I’ll be back in a bit!”

The courier chuckled as she disappeared from sight. “Yeah, seems like she’s recovered nicely,” he joked to himself before closing the door, leaving the flowers out in the hallway for whoever might want them.

Rousing Adabelle was an easy task with a Full Night’s Rest, which was an item of dwindling stock. He was sure that he had enough to last until the end of the journey, at least. There were only one or two more destinations to go, anyway.

They ate and prepared for the day. While Adabelle didn’t wear the earrings like she had the night before, she did wear the bracelet and diamond necklace.

“I especially like how the etching matches this,” she had told Trevor, stopping him after his shower to trace the tattoo on his forearm. “Clay did a great job capturing it.”

The earth elemental decided it was time to give Adabelle’s shoulder a try even though she was slender compared to their usual mode of transportation. She didn’t mind it at all.

When Lora came back, she was still all smiles. “Yo! You three good?”

“Despite being woken up early, yes,” Trevor grumbled without any heat.

“Excellent. Sorry, timetable. Gotta get moving before someone decides we’re not worth waiting for,” she said before shaking her head. “And also, no, I won’t elaborate until we get there. Also also, nice jewelry!”

“We’ve come to expect nothing less, and thank you. Clay made them,” Adabelle said with a smile, reaching up to touch her necklace in a way that also showed off her bracelet. “Shall we head to the warehouse?”

“That’s okay, I can teleport us there,” Lora replied. “Time is of the essence!”

Trevor pulled Clay’s husk and the other magical items they wore into his inventory so they could be teleported, and the next moment they were in a well-lit warehouse. At this point, the courier was a dab hand at what to expect.

“Boxes labeled five?” he asked.

“You got it,” Lora answered. “And these are the new ones.”

With a nod, he started retrieving the cargo from his inventory. There were quite a lot of them now, but still not as many as he had left on the Isle of Horns. That made sense to him. Of course Klar would want the biggest shipment to go to his homeland. He might not have wanted to live there anymore, but he still cared greatly for it.

The Quest Master didn’t give him his reward until he had picked up all of the boxes labeled six and seven. It was a doozy.

[[This screen will have a little bit of everything.

Quest complete! The Floating Isles cargo has been dropped off.

You might not know this, but this island is a nifty little trade hub. Klar can reach at least seven different countries using ships, and they all need things like talismans, medicine, strange hats, and whatever else he’s had you bring.

It is also exactly what you need to put you over the brink. I’ll get to that later.

New Quest! It’s more of the same, more of the same.

Destinations six and seven now have even more stuff in your inventory. You’re on the home stretch now, kid! You’ve almost made it!

And here’s the big one.

Level up! You have gained 1 stat point. It has been added to your bank, which has 1 stat point left.

Notice! You may learn a new Skill! This will be your level 25 skill, and I can get that done for you whenever you like.

Also, let’s be real, you’re going to put that point in Stamina and bump it up to 10. I can’t make that decision for you, but we both know it’s going to happen. Prepare to have two Skills to choose from.]]

Trevor smiled as he dismissed the text box. “Finally level 25,” he announced. “And you’re right, Quest Master, please put my point into Stamina.”

[[Stat increase complete. Stamina is now 10. You have no banked points remaining.

One of your stats has reached 10! You may choose a skill for this stat.]]

“Great work, honey,” Adabelle praised, kissing his cheek. “That’s a big milestone. Congrats.”

“Thanks. It’s taken a while to get here, but I’m happy it’s finally happened. Shall I—”

“Prepare for teleport, please,” Lora said, smiling apologetically. “Sorry. Time. It keeps slipping no matter how much we try to stop it. Except inside of your inventory a little bit, I guess. Still.”

“Heard. Alright, I’ll put it off for later,” he agreed.

“Cool, thanks. Hands, please,” she said, holding out her own. The moment everyone was touching her, she scrunched up her face, took a deep breath, and activated her Power. It seemed like the world pulsed around them for a moment before the warehouse disappeared.

They were now inside of a warm and cozy log cabin. It was circular in nature, reminding the courier of a wizard’s tower, though he wasn’t sure how tall it was. There was a fire burning in a stone hearth nearby, and a monstrously large bear skin rug laid between two plush couches. The smell of coffee was strong in the air, and a white-haired dwarf watched them from a table.

She had a long enough beard that she had it tucked to the side and wrapped around her like a belt. Her skin was not wrinkled, but she still had a look of age to her. Piercings of stone and metal adorned her nose and ears, and her clothing was light in both color and substance.

“Sorry if we’re late, Shaman Brown!” Lora announced, waving at the woman.

“I have only been waiting for a short while. You are not egregiously behind schedule, so this is fine,” she replied, her voice rattling slightly. Her steely gray eyes turned to them. “These are the two who, like you, are in need of my power?”

“That’s right, please and thank you,” the teleporter answered with a nod. “This is Lady Adabelle, and Trevor, and Clay, though they’re just a little marble right now. You three, this is Shaman Brown. She’ll be protecting us while we’re here.”

“We’ll need protection?” Trevor asked as he pulled Clay’s husk out of his inventory. The marble was absorbed into the stone, and they immediately jumped onto his shoulder to keep an eye out for anything dangerous.

“Protecting us from what?” the demonkin asked, though there was an undercurrent of excitement to the question.

“The white death,” Shaman Brown declared ominously. “For centuries, this land has been uninhabitable to all but the hardiest of souls, sapping the strength even from Summoned. Without my power, you would not make it an hour outside without curling up in a feeble attempt to nurture your last remaining cinders of life. It would not work as your heat would be ripped out into the devilish cold only to disappear without a trace not long after.”

Trevor and Adabelle stared at the dwarf.

She sighed and pinched the bridge of her nose. “The Froslan Peaks have a supernatural cold about them. It comes from the dungeon. Without magic, not even a Summoned as powerful as you would survive, Lady Adabelle. Or so I’ve heard. I came up here as a courtesy to Klarkartar and Asalorana because I was told that Trevor would be bringing medicine and goods for my people, but I really must be returning to my village so we should get this over with.”

“Sounds good to me,” the demonkin said, clapping her hands. “I was hoping we’d come to this one.”

“That’s my wife. She loves dungeons,” Trevor chuckled.

“That’s good, because we’ve had to take some… interesting measures to keep our resident Summoned out of it so that you would be able to partake,” Shaman Brown told them. “That being said, expect him to challenge you to a competition, and don’t expect him to be very bright.”

“I’m just surprised that there’s a Summoned this far out in the middle of nowhere,” the courier remarked. Now that they knew the danger was outside, Clay moved over to the demonkin’s shoulder. She began to scratch their chin. “Kind of reminds me of Vilderchtif, actually.”

“Where there’s dungeons, there’s someone to take care of them. Sometimes that’s a guard contingent, but other times it has to be a Summoned,” Adabelle explained before turning back to the dwarf. “We’re ready to receive your magic. We won’t want to keep you.”

“Good,” Shaman Brown said as she stood up. Her knees and back cracked, but she ignored the noise as she gestured the four of them onto the bear skin rug. “I’m going to assume you’re all familiar with wizardry and get on with it.”

“Yup. Whenever you’re ready,” Lora confirmed as she stood behind the newlyweds, who nodded at her words.

The dwarf cleared her throat before raising her hands. “O great mountain! Cold stone! Frozen waste! Stinging air!” she proclaimed loudly in the cadence of spellcasting. “Let the unforgiving winds falter upon the countenance of these individuals! Allow their warmth to stay within their skin and keep their souls aflame!

“Within a comfortable limit,” Shaman Brown whispered, low enough that the courier wasn’t sure if he heard it at all.

“O great mountain of frost, ice, and death!” she continued. “That which has taken the lives of countless men, beasts, and monsters! Hear my words and know that you will be denied! Together, we deny you! We refuse to be sapped of strength, of heat, of life, for it does not belong to you! All of our warmth shall stay where it belongs, inside of the body!

“Within a comfortable limit,” she whispered again. This time, Trevor was sure he wasn’t hearing things.

“For as long as they step foot on the many dangerous facets of your great and terrible peaks, they shall deny you with every breath they take, for their heat belongs to themselves and themselves alone! Cold stone shall remain cold! Frozen wastes remain frozen! Stinging air shall not touch them for as long as my magic courses through their veins, collecting and spreading the heat that they produce within themselves!

“Within a comfortable limit.”

Shaman Brown cleared her throat again. “Let these four become one with the great mountain, and yet still separate from it! Allow them to survive, live, and thrive, and see thou wonderful bounties and greatest dangers for themselves. Begone, cold, and welcome the warmth of these new bodies upon your slopes of snow!”

Trevor felt a heat descend on him. For a moment it flashed hot enough that he thought he would begin sweating, but it only lasted a moment. He didn’t feel any different after that.

“That will keep you safe on the mountains for at least three days, just in case something goes wrong,” Shaman Brown told them as she headed for the door. “I won’t tell you to leave, but feel free to stay as long as you like so long as you don’t go upstairs. Lora knows where the tools of your descent are. There’s also hot cocoa in the next room if you need it, and coffee by the fire, but I’d be careful of how much warmth you intake while here.”

“Will it last after we leave the Froslan Peaks?” Adabelle asked curiously.

“No. Once you leave the seat of my power, the magic will be gone from you. That means don’t stray too far away, either,” she responded before leaving without saying goodbye.

“She’s a little odd, but all wizards are,” Lora said apologetically. “Shall we go to the village? Or do you want some hot cocoa first?”

“I think I’m good to go,” Trevor said, turning to his wife and familiar.

“We are, too,” she confirmed. “How are we going to get to the village?”

“By skiing, of course,” Lora answered before running to the door. “Come on! We’ve got a longer trek than you think ahead of us.”

She left it open, expecting them to follow her, and Trevor grinned. “I haven’t been skiing before. This should be interesting.”

“It’s fun. I’ll show you the ropes, but you shouldn’t have any problems with your Dexterity,” Adabelle said with a smile.

She led him out into the frozen wonderland. There was no telling how high they were considering they were surrounded by mountainous peaks, some of which ascended above the clouds.

In the far distance, down the slope, he could see Shaman Brown moving at high speeds with a pair of icicles strapped to her feet. She was heading towards a forested area with plenty of smoke coming from it. Each stream was segmented and thin, likely coming from chimneys, and marked their destination.

Lora was already preparing their skis, which were thankfully normal, and Trevor went to help her. It was then that he realized he was outside without a jacket, and he felt just fine.

“Man, magic is so cool,” he remarked.
