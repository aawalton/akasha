Saturday November, 5

Everything would be okay. Everything would be okay. This became my mantra all day long. Kate just needed space and whomever Greg had poking around in their security systems wouldn’t find anything, because I wasn’t hacking in any traditional sense.

Greg was a monster and I’d done the right thing. I’d exposed a monster and no amount of PR and blame shifting would take that from me. I took to spying on Greg in my spare time. It was kind of like reality TV. I streamed it all day to my television. Any activity on his phone or computer went straight to my screen where I sat waiting and hoping for a slip up.

When Kate came back, she wouldn’t be mad at me watching. I wasn’t actually doing anything.

Me: I know you’re upset. I’m here when you’re ready to talk

Message undelivered.

Remember, remember the fifth of November… I started imagining myself as a vigilante hero. Someone who could take down the powerful corrupt, maybe I could do this date some justice.

Then I remembered the guy the poem was about tried to blow up parliament. I certainly didn’t want to blow anything up. Not literally anyway.

Sunday November, 6

I tried calling Kate. Once an hour. The last call of the evening I left a voicemail.

“Kate, I’m so sorry. If you could please call me back I can explain everything. It’s not what you think and I’d really like the opportunity to talk to you. There are a lot of things at play and I wasn’t sure how to talk to you about any of them. But, that’s not an excuse. At the end of the day I was a coward who took advantage of your trust.”

She never called back.

Monday November, 7

The apartment looked amazing.

That was the only thing I was clinging to in the days following Kate no longer returning my calls or messages. The apartment looked amazing. The new walls, curtains, tv, artwork, everything hell, even the stupidly ugly and impossibly comfortable couch looked as though it fit the vibe.

This was the apartment where a happy, well adjusted, successful person lived. Or at least that’s what it looked like.

Was it ironic that Kate spent years wanting my apartment to look like this, and now that it finally did, she wanted nothing to do with me? I glanced toward my apartment door. Sitting beside it was a bag, glittery tissue paper peeking out of the top. Inside, the cashmere scarf I’d bought for Kate hid, still wrapped, still waiting for a bar date with my best friend that would never come.

The roses died. I’d picked them up Thursday evening to continue our joke. I kept hoping that I’d give them to her when she came around, but three days later and they were wilted on my counter. Now, I didn’t have the heart to throw them away.

I looked at the dying roses and picked up one of the petals off of the counter. I could change the color, the temperature, the size but I could not reattach it to the rose or make it grow again.

I needed to fix this. I got in my car and drove to Kate’s. I sat there in the parking lot working up my courage for a full twenty minutes before I walked to the door. The doorman, Mr. Rocks, looked at me and shook his head.

“Sorry, ma'am. Ms. Frank asked for no visitors.”

“Can you tell her I want to talk to her?”

He nodded. The pity in his eyes, told me that he knew that I’d done fucked up and that he didn’t think I’d be un-fucking it any time soon.

I didn’t protest, I just turned around and walked solemnly to my car.

What would I have even said anyway. ‘Kate, I’m sorry I lied to you. I didn’t think you would believe that the universe is a simulation and I can change pretty much anything within it with my mind. I used these reality altering skills to make my wine glasses larger and to try to destroy our boss, who may or may not be a serial killer.

I didn’t bother going to the coffee shop. I watched Greg’s internet life and I wallowed.

The rest of the week kind of blurs together from here. I know at one point I texted Scott and called off our date. I hadn’t really been responding to his texts since Friday anyway. I’m sure he was expecting it.

One day I ordered groceries even though I had no appetite. I shoved them all in the fridge. Each day, every few hours, I would open the fridge, look inside and decide that nothing sounded good before closing it again.

I applied half heartedly to jobs I didn’t bother checking the legitimacy of and didn’t want any way. If they reached back out, I even set up an interview. At least I could say I was trying.

The code shimmered around me constantly trying to lure me out of my funk. And on one hand I considered it. I had essentially unlimited power. Well, I had limited power that caused bloody noses, headaches and vomiting but aside from that…

On the other hand, what good was power when you were just sitting at home with nothing and no one to share it with?

My revenge plans did become borderline obsession then. I spent morning and night tracking Greg around. I’d begun messing with him in small bursts. Any time he texted, called, or wrote something on his computer denying his actions I would cause the lights to flicker in that room. If he was on a phone call, I disconnected it.

I pushed myself and my limits as far as I could just so that I still felt something. I could keep an eye on multiple devices at once, and even monitor security cameras without passing out now.

I’d found out who Greg was working with, some family friend lawyer who was used to dealing with high profile cases and making unfavorable information go away. I deleted Greg’s case off of his computer. I queued up Drop it Like It's Hot to play every time he tried to use his music app.

He was beginning to get spooked,

Attorney Light: I know I agreed to this case, but I’m starting to think it may be a little larger than I can handle.

Mrs Light: Should I be worried about the kids?

Attorney Light: No, just let me know if anything weird starts happening.

Mrs. Light: Weird how?

I hadn’t gotten access to their home network given I spent most of my time monitoring Greg, but I’d started to come up with plans. Plans that I could enact, to encourage them to drop the case.

One night, I found myself staring at Kate’s contact. My thumb hovered over the call button. I could see the code and follow it through the network. I could see the code that was preventing my texts and calls from going through. It would be so easy to bypass, to force my call to go through. Or maybe I could force a text to go through containing the perfect thing that would make her forgive me.

I put my phone down and didn’t bother checking it again.

Even my audiobook didn’t hold my attention. I’d pause it mid sentence and realize hours had passed, Aurora had made her choice. She’d gone with Malikai, left behind everything she’d ever known. She embraced becoming something new, terrifying and powerful.

“I don’t know what I am anymore.” Aurora whispered. ‘Am I human? Fae? Something else entirely?”

Malikai traced a finger along her cheekbone. Faint silver markings had begun to appear on her skin. His deep voice rumbled into her neck. “You are becoming what you were always meant to be. The question…”

I turned it off. The book no longer felt like escapist fantasy.

I thought about calling my mom. But I couldn’t tell her. We didn’t share secrets. I couldn’t tell her about the code, I couldn’t tell her about Kate. I couldn’t tell her I was having a breakdown or she would find a way to make it about herself. Neither option seemed like how I wanted to spend my time.

I thought about Sarah. We hadn’t really talked in years. I had her phone number and address. We exchanged Christmas cards, but that was the extent. Would she even remember me? Would she care?

I thought about Scott from the coffee shop. The way he smiled, warm and genuine. The way Kate had teased me about getting his number. How excited she had been. I didn’t even know his last name and after bailing on lunch I doubted he wanted to get to know me. He hadn’t texted again anyway.

Outside the sun was setting. Orange and pinks streaming in through my windows past my deep emerald curtains and dancing on the leaves of my mostly dead house plants.

My apartment was beautiful. Daniel was destroyed. Greg was exposed, even if he was fighting it. I had money, power, abilities that defied explanation. The code shimmered around me, patient and eternal just waiting for me to decide what to do next.

I had won, hadn’t I?
