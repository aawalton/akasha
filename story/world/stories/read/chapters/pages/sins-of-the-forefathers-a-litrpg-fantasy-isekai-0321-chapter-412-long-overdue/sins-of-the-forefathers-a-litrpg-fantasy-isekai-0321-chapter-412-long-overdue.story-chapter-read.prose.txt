The Elf ducked, of course. Why wouldn’t he have? It had been fairly obvious just what was about to happen, considering the state of his comrade. And I hadn’t acted quickly enough at the sight of his peeking head to catch him with another arrow.

And so, the Elf ducked, slamming the door that he had just been looking through behind him. Credit to my strength, these days, but the arrow didn’t just lodge itself into the wooden surface of the door.

No, it instead drilled right through it, disappearing from my sight.

I clucked my tongue as I heard the Elf curse within the room.

Ah, well. No point to stealth anymore, was there? I knew both of them were in there; they knew I was out here…but there was a difference between our positions.

They were trapped in there, while I had a bow trained upon the exit. They were going to have to come out sometime. They couldn’t stay in there forever, now.

And I had all the time in the world to wait for them.

Well. Most of the time in the world. I’d have to meet up with the rest of the expedition eventually. If this took too long, I’d just drop the bow and barge through that door in my full transformed state. I’d be bent over like an aged old woman doing it, but it was theoretically possible. I could probably rip the two of them apart with my bare claws if I also went in there with Might of the Wyrdwood cranked up as high as I could manage.

I could move pretty fast for such a huge being when I combined those two Skills.

That was a last resort, though. Preferably, I could just take the two of them out the easy way first. Time to settle in for the wait, I’d say. I reached out a hand and pulled up one of the stools that the Elves had been sitting on before I’d arrived, never taking my eyes off the door they were hiding behind. All the while, I could hear a fierce, whispered conversation erupt in their accursed language within the room.

These pointed ears were good for something, after all, and stone wasn’t exactly good at dampening sound.

I sat down on my new stool, nice, comfy, and with my bow trained on the door while the debate raged within.

I didn’t have to wait long before something changed. But…not quite where I was expecting it to.

Off to my side, I heard a sputtering as the captive Dwarven gravemender coughed up the thin soup that his now deceased tormenter had been ladling into his mouth. I heard him groan with effort as he leveraged himself into a sitting position, and then a rattling thump as he leaned against the bars of his cage.

And then he yelped in surprise.

I spared a single instant’s attention to glance over at the gravemender to see if he was alright, before focusing back on the door. That was enough, however, to both show me that he was fine and get a better look at him in the process.

The gravemender was very…plain, I could say. Slate grey eyes, remarkably tanned looking compared to most Dwarves I’d met, and…totally bald. Bald face, bald eyes, Hell. I don’t even think he had eyebrows. That left the gravemender looking awfully egg-like, to me.

A short, shocked-looking tan egg, gaping at the scene in front of him.

He had just caught sight of the dead body of the Elf that was slumped up against the bars of his cell with an arrow sticking through its neck. I could understand the reaction.

“What…” I heard him breathe from across the room. “What in all the hells…”

I smiled slightly to myself, eyes never budging from the door across from me. The Elves had gone awfully quiet. No doubt they were scheming in there, but that didn’t matter to me. The Dwarf did.

“Good morrow, master gravemender,” I called over to him pleasantly. I heard him jump slightly at the sound of my voice, the movement shaking the bars of the cage. “I hope you’re doing well. Might I ask your name?”

There was silence for a moment. “Ah…” He stuttered out. “I’m…Humphrey. Humphrey Verclay, and as you say, I’m the gravemender around these parts. That is, er…milord?”

I was in such a good mood that I’d killed two of the Elves already, that I nearly had a conniption fit. I tried to restrain a manic giggle, but wasn’t able to before it burst from my widely smiling lips.

I…don’t think that helped put ol’ Humphrey at ease, but I didn’t care.

Humphrey? Really? The universe really did have a sense of humor sometimes.

“A pleasure to meet you, Humptie,” I managed to get out between my giggles. “How are you feeling?”

I could practically feel the Dwarf’s confusion across the room. “Well, milord-”

I cut him off. “Sir or Marshall will do, Humptie. I’m not a noble.”

“…Sir, then,” ‘Humptie’ replied, sounding more uneasy than ever. “I’m…well. Only, Sir, my name isn’t Humptie. It’s Humphrey. Might I ask what Sir is doing here…and what his name is? Purely so I can thank him, of course,” He hastily added. “I’m truly grateful you’ve saved me from the Elves, Sir.”

I calmed down a tad, recriminations from my Core Lattice ringing in my ears. Yes, yes. I understand. The adrenaline high was just getting to me, that’s all. “Sir Nathaniel Hart, of the Order of the Polaris Reach,” I said, in a much calmer tone. “I came here hunting these Elves, in fact. That I can save you in the process is all the better…Humphrey. Only…” I raised my voice to cut off the Dwarf when I heard him start to speak up. “Your ordeal isn’t quite over, master gravemender. I’m afraid two of your assailants still live. I’ve trapped them in that room over there…haven’t I, knife-ears?”

“What?” I heard Humphrey sputter. “That’s my gods be damned bedro-”

Poor ol’ Humphrey was cut off once more. Only…not by me.

The Elves in the room seemed to have finished their hurried conversation. “Negotiate!” I heard a gruffy, animalistic voice call out from within the room. “We…negotiate!”

I felt a small, mirthless smile crawl over my lips as I heard Humphrey start from across the room. “Y-You animals!” He blurted out. “You torment me for weeks, and now you want to negotiate?! You can’t, Sir Hart! They-”

“Negotiate!” The same Elven voice cut him off, which only seemed to piss off the Dwarf more.

I shook my head, which seemed to be enough to stifle the gravemender’s complaints. “Negotiate what, exactly?” I called back.

“We…free!” The Elf called back. “You…take stunties…we leave. Never…see again. Agree?”

A low, distinctly malicious chuckle emerged from my throat. Even I was surprised at the depths of disdain I could hear within it. It certainly seemed to unnerve both Humphrey and the Elves. “Is that so? There’s a problem with that, you see,” I said casually. “This isn’t just a matter of rooting out a rats nest, nestling in the heart of the Florens. No…this is personal.”

Silence fell once again, as my hissed word reached the Elf on the other side of the doorway. “What you want?”

“What do all people want?” I shrugged, never once allowing my draw bow to waver from its position trained on the door. When I didn’t receive an answer, I laughed in a deceptively light manner. “That wasn’t a rhetorical question, by the by. I want an answer from you.”

“…food,” The bestial Elf grunted. “Females. Power.”

That last was spoken with a particular hunger in it, and I didn’t fail to notice.

I tsked. “You’re so simple in the end, aren’t you?” I shook my head in a…strange mix of disappointment and relief. “Base needs, for a base creature. Fynneas…the Mad God…he’s rendered you nothing more than an animal with faint delusions of grandeur. I had all these impressions of you and your band, after all this time, that I had built you up in my mind. After all, those first few days here on this planet were so fraught for me that something deep inside of me quailed at the thought of you. The big, bad, scary Elves who captured and sold me, looming larger than life with a bonfire at your back in my dreams. How sad you are, in the end.”

“…Uh, Sir Hart?” I heard Humphrey say hesitantly. “You should really…”

“You. Meat?” The Elf said in a confused tone, drowning out what the gravemender was trying to say.

“Even now, you’re focusing on the wrong thing,” I sighed, shaking my head once more. “It’s as if you’re incapable of realizing the depths of the situation you’re in. All you heard was that I was once a slave you sold, and that really does confuse you, doesn’t it? How can a mere piece of meat get the better of you like this? But that doesn’t matter, does it? You can’t understand, and that means…there really wasn’t a point to all of this, was there?” I laughed, feeling a knot of tension unwind from somewhere deep within myself. “No point to me going rogue and hunting you in this manner. No point in even monologuing like this to you, is there? You’re an animal, after all. Something little better than a beast. It’s like…posturing to a squirrel, in the end. The squirrel thinks of nothing more than food, and females, and posturing. It doesn’t understand the concept of catharsis, so it’s just…confused. This…all of this…it’s just for myself. I really will have to apologize, won’t I?”

“Sir Hart, you need to look-!”

“I beast?” The Elf growled hatefully as he pressed himself up against the door he was hiding behind. His animalistic, yellow-green eye peered out at me from the arrow hole I had drilled through it with an animal. A bestial note of triumph snuck through his fury, to spark in that orb. “Beasts bite!”

“Oh, spare me,” I said scornfully…

And shifted my bow upwards, loosing the drawn arrow in the same instant…right at the Elf creeping along the ceiling.

In an entirely unintended mirror of my last kill, this arrow also struck the Elf cloaked in a weak camouflage Skill right in the throat. He barely had a moment to choke on his own blood, his Skill flickering away, before he dropped to the stone ground, stone dead.

I’ll say this, at least. It was some pretty impressive grip strength to grasp the seams between the stone blocks above us and slink across them. He’d even gotten pretty close, too. A few more inches and I bet he would have had a great vantage point to lunge at my own throat, with the dagger he’d held clasped between his teeth. Sadly for him, it didn’t matter whatever secondary exit from the room there was. I had felt it with my blood sense the moment he had come out, even if I had deliberately not reacted.

After all, why complain when the quarry came to you?

The whole ‘negotiation’ thing had just been a trick from the leader, while his underling shimmied his way towards me. Oh well.

Four down, one to go.

“Oh,” Humphrey said weakly, having spotted the Elf through its poor illusion. “You…saw it.”

The eye of the last remaining Elf, peering out at me from the door, narrowed. Still, I could see the apprehension in that eye, and for all of my own realizations about the nature of feral, maddened Elves…

It still gave me a sense of satisfaction.

I made a show of standing up from my stool, collapsing my bow, and storing it in the quiver on my back. “Well? Come on, then. It’s time to settle this; no negotiations needed. I won’t even use my bow, if that makes you feel better.” I drew my dagger from the small of my back and, in a move that really surprised Humphrey, judging by his yelp, extended it to the full spear form. “I’ll even meet you on your own playing field. You and yours were spearelves, weren’t you? I remember your pike-work, back in the day. At the time, it was impressive to poor, Earth raised me. I’m interested in seeing if my memories are just exaggerating your ‘prowess’.”

That did the trick. I saw the eye bulge in its socket as I questioned its ‘power’, and with a distinctly animalistic roar, the Elf shoulder checked the door, lost to his fury. To his credit, the hardwood of it immediately shattered into a million shards as he rocketed through the hail straight at me with a raised spear.

And then stumbled, the second he was clear from the room. The leader of the Elven raiders stared at me in stunned, incomprehensible confusion before looking down at his chest.

The handle of a single, immaculately forged Oninite throwing dagger stared up at him from where it was buried in his heart.

As the Elf collapsed to the stone floor below, I stared at his twitching form, as blood started to foam at his mouth. “You don’t fight animals. You lay traps for them.”

Easy to miss a single, underhand-thrown dagger through a storm of splinters, wasn’t it?

I stepped forward to look down at the bloodshot eyes of the dying raider, considering him for half a second. “And once in the jaws of it, we grant animals mercy.”

I flipped the spear I still held in my hands around till it was oriented point down.

Down the blade, and then up again.

I sighed, rolling my shoulders as the last bit of tension in them eased. “I didn’t even need Skills for you lot, did I? What a long, strange day it’s been…”

Five down…none remain.
