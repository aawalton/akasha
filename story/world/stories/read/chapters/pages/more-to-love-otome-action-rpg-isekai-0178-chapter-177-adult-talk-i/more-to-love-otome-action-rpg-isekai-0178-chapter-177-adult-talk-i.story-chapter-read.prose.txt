Sixth Moon, Shoots Day

The Gullbeaks Headquarters had changed drastically since the last time Aida had visited, which she was shocked to realize was five moons ago.

The original tavern portion of the building had been entirely cleared of its papers and maps, and now looked just like a typical eatery that was closed for business. Even Kawa’s paperwork station at the bar was pristine, only carrying sensible dishware.

“What happened?” Aida asked, aghast. The area looked so clean and unused, she didn’t know what to make of it.

“Oh, you don’t know! The Gullbeaks expanded!” Sue laughed as she pulled Aida through the doorway. “Tyda and Yoon decided it was time to hire help on the logistical side—community members to handle the reports and compiling information, some staff members to help with keeping supplies stocked and such—especially with how taxing the patrols were getting.”

Sue waved at all the equipment that was hanging and stacked along the walls. “So this original building is now the place where all of us active members stay. They have a second building down the street that’s the more community-facing branch, so that’s where all the planning and information takes place. This is a place for us to relax.”

“Is this your equipment?” Aida asked, catching sight of what looked like a set of brand-new light leather armor. There were pinkish-red accents on the edges of the armor set, which gave off a very Sue-vibe.

“Yep! I don’t have a weapon yet, but Arryn is going to start me on training to find what I work best with.” Sue pointed at various weapons that were hanging with the other Gullbeaks’ gear before giving Aida a cheeky grin. “He said using the right weapon will unlock my potential tenfold!”

Aida nodded mutely as she examined the rest of the armor sets that were hanging along the walls. It seemed like the Gullbeaks had converted the first floor entirely to something of an armory, giving each person their own personal space to gear up.

“Are the Talonwings staying here, too?” she asked, counting the number of armor stations spaced throughout the room. Much more than the seven she would have expected for the number of Gullbeaks members, though she did notice that a few stations were empty.

“Yeah, that was part of the reason why Tyda said we needed to expand. They used to keep all their equipment in their own rooms, but it was already getting too crowded down here with all the information collecting.” Sue looked fondly at her brand new armor before clapping her hands. “Oh! Come with me to the training yard. I want your opinion on something.”

The two of them crossed the eerily empty tavern floor and exited to the training yard, which had also been cleaned up. Just like the interior, it looked completely new, as if freshly installed.

Sue made her way to a rack of weapons that were in a corner and grabbed a rapier in one hand and a greataxe in the other.

“Which one do I look better with?” She stamped the axe into the ground, posing with the rapier hidden behind her back so that only the greataxe’s silhouette could be seen. “I want to go for a heroic, awe-inspiring look!”

Sue hefted the greataxe with one hand and rested it on her shoulder for a second pose, giving Aida a threatening look.

Aida struggled to keep her face straight. “That looks a little too unwieldy for you.”

“I thought so,” Sue sighed. She stabbed the axe into the ground so that it remained upright and whipped out the rapier, giving a few experimental swishes in the air as she moved rapidly away from the axe. Her dancer’s grace suited the slim blade more, capitalizing on her dexterity.

Aida clapped politely. “That looks way better than the axe.”

“Really?” Sue gave another flourish, smiling. “I like the idea of a sword, but I also like how wham, bam, kablam a greataxe is.”

“We can keep that as a runner-up.”

The two of them turned to the speaker, who turned out to be Tyda. He smiled at Aida. “Good morning. I didn’t know you were coming, Aida.”

“Oh, I brought her because she had something important to talk to you about!” Sue gathered up the two weapons before depositing them back into the weapons rack. She bounded over to nudge Aida eagerly, whose throat had suddenly become quite dry.

Tyda quirked an eyebrow. “Is that so? Come on in, then.”

The Gullbeaks leader led the two of them back inside and up the stairs, where he opened a door to a sparsely furnished room. “This room isn’t occupied yet, so we can have some privacy.”

Aida sat down on the stiff mattress beside Sue, who was completely at ease. Tyda sat backwards in the chair that was placed at the desk, gazing at her kindly, giving her his full attention. He had always been kind and honest with her, but it was the honesty she was afraid of. She steeled herself.

“I know this is a bit of an outlandish…request,” she started. Sue’s arm slipped around Aida’s shoulders, and that was when she realized she had been twisting her fingers tightly.

Stop acting so pathetic. She forced her hands still.

“I don’t have proof, but my gut is saying there’s something going around causing people to fall into the same mysterious sleep Ezra fell into.” Tyda’s gaze sharpened at her words.

“I don’t know what the mechanism is,” she continued, gratified that he hadn’t dismissed her yet. “All I know is that I met someone who also fell asleep for no particular reason…”

Tyda let Aida talk—ramble, more like—for nearly thirty minutes, asking clarifying questions here and there. When she finally fell into silence, he was looking thoughtful.

“And what are you asking us to do? We’re an Adventuring group, primarily experienced in slaying monsters and protecting our community. This sounds like something that would fall under the Healing clinics’ jurisdiction.” Aida’s heart fell at the discouraging tone.

“I was hoping you might have more information, or heard talk from among your information network about an unexplained illness with these kinds of symptoms,” Aida said falteringly, trying to recall every other trope that was used in every plotline she had ever come across. Now that Tyda said it so directly, she felt silly for not considering going to a clinic instead.

Tyda gazed at her with an inscrutable expression before he leaned back against the desk, thinking.

“Let me talk to Yoon,” he said finally. “She might have heard something already. Give me a moment.”

Aida nodded as Tyda left the room, feeling the tension leave her shoulders. He didn’t outright say no.

“Nice job,” Sue said encouragingly. “You explained everything really well.”

Did I? He just said it didn’t make sense for them to be responsible for this…

“Thanks for the support,” Aida said wearily. “Do you think Yoon will be more sympathetic?”

“I think so!” Sue nodded vigorously. “Especially since Tyda already seems like he’ll follow up on this.”

“Really?” Sue’s confidence ironically made Aida even more pessimistic about her odds of getting help.

“Yeah, if he didn’t think it was worth it he’d just say so outright. I’ve seen him say no to a lot of other people, and he’s not shy about it,” Sue said confidently. Just then, the door opened and the brunette stepped through, smiling.

“Aida! Good morning.” Yoon leaned down to give Aida a hug before she leaned against the wall. “Tyda said you had a request. Can I hear it again?”

Aida repeated her story and plea again, before waiting with bated breath for the newcomer’s verdict.

“It’s certainly an interesting theory,” Yoon said slowly. “I can say for now, we haven’t heard anything remotely similar occurring, but there could be any number of reasons for that. People only report monster attacks and sightings to us, for one thing; perhaps the Healer clinics would be the ones who might know. I believe we have a contact in one of the major clinics we can follow up with…”

Aida glanced over at Tyda, who was watching Yoon think impassively. He caught her questioning gaze and smiled.

“Assuming our activities continue as they are now… do you think we’ll have the bandwidth to start canvassing for this information?”

“Of course,” Yoon said immediately, making Aida’s shoulders slump in relief. However, the next words stiffened her up again. “Unfortunately, effort may not translate to results, so if monster activity ramps up again, we will have to reprioritize protecting the town from tangible threats.”

“Wait, ‘monster activity ramps up again?’“ Aida repeated blankly. “What—?”

“Oh.” Yoon pressed her fingers to her lips as she looked at Tyda sheepishly. “I’m sorry. I forgot you weren’t part of us…”

“That’s all right,” Tyda said as he gave Yoon a small, albeit exasperated, smile before turning to Aida. “You’ll likely hear about this eventually. But we have noticed over the course of the past star cycle that monster activity has decreased to a worrying level.”

“’Worrying level?’” Aida’s brain felt stupid and sluggish as she kept repeating things the Gullbeaks said.

“Yes. A decrease in monster activity is good and couldn’t have come at a better time, because Adventurers and Strongholders have been getting fatigued trying to keep them in check. However, it’s worrying because we don’t know why the monsters have suddenly decided to slow down.”

Tyda gave Aida a moment to absorb his words.

“One theory we have is that monsters may be preparing for a large-scale attack.”
