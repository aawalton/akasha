Risha watched the last of the spark-touched fall to the ground, his blood spreading through the murky swamp. Darkness retreated from the surroundings, returning to the void, as Nyx’s power lessened.

With the power of the Spark retreating from the swamps, her attention turned to the fighting warriors on both sides. Spirit gathered to her, and she once again cast “[Bond Breaker]”

The skill spread through the scaled warriors, turning their scales pale as they lost their connection to the Spark that controlled them.

Their chief, a female, was the first to call the surrender, and from there, it was a wave of lowered weapons and lowered heads.

***

Risha cleaned her blades with a cloth rag, wiping the blood away with careful swipes. She sat on a stump that kept her above the waters and gave her a view of the defeated clan. Glo had achieved a victory on his side, and soon they would continue into the hills where the Drakes roosted, the center of their power.

Two kobolds walked up to her, their faces covered by masks, and hooded cloaks covering their features.

They bowed to her.

“Grand Mother, I bring news.”

Risha paused, and lowered her sword. She recognized the voice that spoke to her as belonging to Mist-Prowler, the Coyote.

She looked up to the two of them, examining their spirits to recognize Mist-Prowler and Wind-Whisperer, the Jackal that had protected her during the assassination attempt.

She returned to wiping her blade, “What news?”

Mist-Prowler lowered her voice as she spoke, “Your fears about what lies in the depths of their territory… The Spark controls them.”

Dragons. That’s what Mist-Prowler spoke of. Risha had asked Elder Kulos to investigate, but she hadn’t expected to hear back so soon. Grand Mother Iris had wanted proof, but Risha didn’t have any, and she trusted the words of her daughter. “How many?”

Mist-Prowler lowered herself into the murky water, “Forgive me Grand Mother, but I couldn’t get close enough to say.”

Risha looked to the sky, the mountains standing tall in the distance. There wasn’t much to be done. She would inform the other Grand Mother, and her help would come. She would inform her own people to be ready.

Maybe they could stop their march? No. Something told Risha that if she gave the spark more time, its power would only grow. Plus, she was on a clock herself. The spiders would need to move to their warrens for winter, and Risha did not wish to push them too far in the cold.

“Inform my Spirits, and the Draconic Riders.”

The two Kobolds bowed to Risha, then they ran through the swamps.

Risha sheathed her sword. They’d managed to fight for control of the skies thanks to the power of her Draconic Riders, but if they faced creatures stronger than Drakes… There were only two of them.

For now. There were only two of them for now. It was time to make another offer to the scaled warriors who fought for her.

“Kliks, carry me to the Scaled camp.”

The snoozing spider woke up at her words, then jumped off of her shoulder onto a nearby web between the trees. With the vibration of the web, the defences of Risha’s camp were briefly visible, spanning between all the trees in traps large and complex trails.

Risha stood on top of her tree stump, then pulled herself onto her spiderling.

Kliks took off through the webs and the trees, bringing Risha over the army that accompanied her.

Many fires were lit, mourning the fallen, or celebrating their own life. Victory came with a cost, and every warrior here knew it, as did Risha.

The Scaled Warriors that had chosen to follow Risha rested in their own camp. Risha was glad to see them mingling with goblins and orcs, even the rare kobold. The latest battle had proven their loyalty, and brought her people closer together.

Four drakes had chosen to follow her and take on the purple scales. More were captured, chained and watched in prisoner camps until Risha had time to offer them a new leader.

The scaled warriors quieted as she approached, lowering their heads in reverence.

Kliks dropped down in front of the drakes, all of whom placed their heads against the swampy waters in subservience.

Risha stood on top of Kliks, and looked at them for a long moment.

“Not too long ago, Risha made you an offer to be hers. Since then, you have proven your loyalty, fighting for the freedom of your people from the Spark that controls them. Once again, Risha makes you an offer.” Risha took a deep breath, “Join me as my sons and daughters.”

The drakes looked at each other, until one, the youngest of them, stepped forward. It placed its head before Risha, its golden eyes meeting hers before it closed them. “We follow the Mother Of Monsters. And I call myself Risha's loyal daughter.”

Risha smiled wide, then placed her hand on the Drake’s forehead.

Spirit rushed through her and into the Drake.

“What do you want to become, daughter of mine?”

“I want to remind the Spark of fear.” Anger filled the drake’s words, and Risha wondered what history sparked such anger. Perhaps, someday the monster would share the past with Risha. She would listen with care.

[Motherly Advice]

Draconic Terror

Risha spoke to the Drake, and it changed with her words. Two legs sprouted from its front, and it grew in size. Her scales thickened, turning black as night, with tiny patterns of purple barely visible. Her head lengthened as her teeth sharpened, and her wings grew in length, their tips turning shadowy as the creature blended into the night.

The creature smiled with its sharp teeth, then lifted its head to the sky and ROARED.

The roar made its way through the camp, cowing the other drakes, and inflicting fear on even Risha. Risha couldn’t help but smile with the power of the sound, her blood racing with the desire for blood.

The Draconic Terror then locked eyes with Risha, and bowed its head once more. “They will know to fear the Grand Mother.”

“And you will know that you don’t need to be afraid.” Risha said.

The words hit the Draconic like a blow to the chest, its eyes widening. Risha turned to the next Drake, surprised and embarrassed at how much of an effect the simple words had on the Draconic. They’d come out without Risha thinking about them.

The next Drake evolved into a Draconic Storm, lightning flickering between its wings, the last two both evolved into Draconic Frost, their scales blue.

Risha was surprisingly exhausted after the evolutions, her spirit low from evolving the great beasts.

***

Grand Mother Iris stared at her hands.

Risha watched the older mother, wondering what thoughts went through her mind.

Iris didn’t look up from her hands for a long time, but Risha waited patiently.

“I will come-” Iris started.

“You don’t have to-”

They both paused, and Iris motioned for Risha to speak.

“We will win, even if you can’t move.” Risha said.

Iris looked into the distance, “I would be there now, if I could. Something else requires my attention.”

Risha tilted her head, “Something else?”

“They disturb the skies above Hwahwei’s domain. If they are what I suspect them to be, you will know soon enough.”

Risha waited for more, but Iris just looked into the distance, distracted by her own thoughts.

“We will win on our own,” Risha repeated to the other Grand Mother, “And I will save as many of the dragons as I can.”

Iris turned to her, a sad look in her eyes. “I know you will, dear, but if you must slay them to free them of the Spark’s control, then you have my approval and forgiveness.”

Risha nodded slowly, “Are you alright?”

Iris smiled, “Old memories come to the surface, young Mother. Do not worry overly much for me. Focus on your children.”

Risha took a deep breath, then bowed her head to the other Mother. “Risha wishes you well.”

“You as well, Risha.”

The world dissolved around Risha, and she reappeared in her tent, Kliks snoozing on her shoulder.

The conversation didn’t sit well with her, but there wasn’t much she could do except focus on the coming battles.

***

Risha and Glo’s forces reunited as the mountains loomed ever closer. As they marched, Risha could feel the power of the Spark growing, filling the very ground under them.

Surprisingly few battles slowed their march, but they did not grow complacent.

On the second night of the march, two figures entered Risha’s tent.

Mystic Klu walked in with the same powerful presence he always carried, a smaller figure following close behind.

Risha looked up from the arcane symbols she’d been practicing, and looked up at the two of them.

The second figure slowly lowered her hood, revealing two horns that had been sheared off, leaving only nubs. Her eyes were a light red, and orange veins glowed under the dark skin of her face.

The woman kneeled before Risha, shaking slightly. It took Risha only a second to remember the woman as the first demon she’d met trapped in that cursed room.

Risha stood up, and walked over to her. She lowered herself to the woman, and helped her stand to her feet.

A flurry of emotions crossed the woman’s eyes, impossible for Risha to parse.

“Klu, what brings the two of you here? I thought you were staying in the city.” Risha looked to the orc, who bowed his head in greeting.

“Grand Mother,” Klu greeted, “The spirits guided me to join you here and guard the latest caravan. It’s a good thing I did. There was an attack.”

“They slipped past us?” Risha growled. It was unfortunate, but monitoring the entire swamp for movement was simply impossible.

Klu nodded.

Risha looked at the woman, “What is your name?”

The woman bowed her head, “Grand Mother Risha, my name is Siyah.”

“She helped defend the caravan.” Klu spoke up, “And I judged it wise to have you meet her.”

“It is good to see you doing so well,” Risha said, noticing how the previously emaciated woman now looked some semblance of healthy.

“Thank you.” The woman paused, “Grand Mother, I wanted to see you to show my loyalty as your daughter, and to… ask for your help.”

Risha looked the woman over, then guided her to the cushions she’d been sitting on earlier, “What do you need?”

The woman took a deep breath, then lifted her head, meeting Risha’s eyes with an inner strength that made Risha smile. “I need your protection. I am Siyah, Inheritor of Freera.”
