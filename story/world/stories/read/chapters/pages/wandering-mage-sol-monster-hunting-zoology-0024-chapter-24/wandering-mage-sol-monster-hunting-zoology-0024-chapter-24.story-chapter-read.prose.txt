“Ugh, I wish I had a horse!” Tressa said later that day as she collapsed down into a pile of leaves.

“Get up, would you?” Briadon said.

“My feet hurt,” Tressa complained, her arms and legs spread out. They had been walking for more than half the day, and the sun was long past noon, moving deep into the lower parts of the sky. They had made it past the dark woods and to a place where they no longer needed the torchlight, so they had extinguished them and put them away.

“Tressa,” Lisha sighed. “Come on, stand up.”

“No, my feet hurt,” Tressa said as she looked at them from the ground.

She noticed a look cross Lisha’s face, and Tressa yelped as Lisha pulled off her boot and sock and found that Tressa’s foot was swollen and bleeding.

“Why did you not say anything sooner?”

Tressa sat up, pulling her foot from Lisha’s grasp.

“I couldn’t find a shoemaker!”

“This has been going on for a while hasn’t it? Don’t you know ignoring things just makes them worse?” Lisha said admonishingly, looking at the blister. “How have you even been keeping up as you have?”

Tressa twiddled her fingers. “I use magic to float my hurt foot suntimes.”

“Hmmph,” Briadon said, folding his arms. “You’re too squishy.”

Tressa shot him a glare, grabbed her boot, and threw it as hard as she could, hitting him square in the chest. The boot fell to the ground with a soft thud as they all looked at it in silence before Lisha burst out laughing.

“Shut up, bullhead!”

“Geez, Tressa, you know you need to throw it a lot harder than that to do any real damage to him and his ego.”

The side of her mouth rose as she sneered, still not taking her eyes off Briadon.

“Then he shouldn’t call me squishy. Next time I’ll use my magic and throw him off his hooves, and then he will see to call me squishy!”

Briadon barked out a laugh.

“You? Throw me off my feet?”

Tressa’s glare sharpened as she put up a palm and let a little wind magic dance around her, tussling her hair.

“I can.”

“Oh, I’m sure you can try, little elf.”

Tressa’s eyes narrowed, wondering if she really could do it. Admittedly, if he braced himself first, it would be hard, even with pushing a lot of mana into it.

Lisha couldn’t help but laugh as Tressa looked ready to prove it right then and there.

“But no, in all seriousness, Tressa, your feet are a mess,” she said, looking at the blisters and the dried blood on the socks. “Why didn’t you say anything sooner? We have been walking for a while. You should have at least said something before we left.”

Tressa looked away, a bit guilty. Her boots were far beyond worn down; they were nearly falling apart. Her feet had started to hurt a bit before they had taken on the job for the scorpion eggs. Yet she had pushed it aside, thinking that her boots would last a bit longer, but now that they had been on the road for a time of nonstop walking, she was starting to realize her mistake.

“By that look, you thought you would be okay, didn’t you?”

Tressa nodded.

Lisha let out a sigh as she grabbed her own rucksack and dug through it, searching for the medical supplies. She pulled out the small container of medicine that Tressa had made for them. Opening the tin lid, she scooped out some of the ointment and spread it around on the wound.

“You know I can do that myself, right?”

“Yes, but it’s fine. I already started. I don’t mind.”

Tressa watched her work for a moment. There was something familiar about the way Lisha fussed over the injury, careful but firm. It reminded her of home. It brought up a particular memory of when she’d fallen ill. The healers had treated her, but her mother stayed by her side at all times until she was better.

“You have done this before?” Tressa asked.

Lisha glanced up at her. “A few times.”

“For someone close?”

A small smile crossed Lisha’s face. “My three younger siblings. I used to look after them quite a bit.”

Tressa’s expression brightened. “You have younger and older?”

“Three younger, three the same age, and four older,” Lisha explained. “Most of my income I send home through the Guild. I try my best. I hope I get to see everyone again soon.”

“That’s so cool! What are they like?” Tressa said excitedly.

“Haha, well, they are as scaly as I am. Though my younger brother is a deep grey, which is very rare for our kind, and my two younger sisters are dark coral. They are also rare for our kind,” Lisha said with pride. “My sisters were born as twins under a new moon. Among the Stitiki, that is considered a great blessing.”

“Really?”

Lisha nodded. “Yes. Being born beneath a full moon or a new moon is considered lucky. My people believe those children are born with the moon’s favor. My twin sisters being identical meant my people believed it even more.”

“That is so cool!”

She chuckled. “Yes, but I tried to give my little brother extra attention when he was growing up. Since the twins were considered so special, I worried he might feel overlooked.”

Tressa’s expression softened. “That was kind of you.”

“He never complained,” Lisha said. “But that doesn’t mean he never felt it.” She got a look in her eye and continued, “As a result, he and I had become quite close. Next time I make it home, I’m excited to see him most of all.”

“Do you write letters?” Tressa asked.

“It is not really in my people’s culture to do that. We have a written language but it is… hmmm well, it is not so conversational as the common tongue can be in writing. If that makes sense? But if something grave were to happen, they would get word to the guild that I should come home, but that’s about it.”

“I see…” Tressa said, pondering over the different culture.

Silence fell over the group while Lisha took off Tressa’s left shoe and found it was at least in better shape than the other. She still bound her ankle with a linen bandage to give it extra support, since she knew Tressa would be favoring her bad foot.

“There. How does that feel?”

Tressa let out a soft sigh. “Thank you. I’m sorry I didn’t say anything sooner. I figured once we got back, I’d be able to get a new set of boots, but I’m starting to see that I should have done so before the eggs.”

“Tressa, you must remember these things, or else it will slow us all down. And I don’t mean that in a bad way. I just mean that it will do more harm to you, not to mention making things more dangerous in a fight. What if you’re not able to get out of the way of something fast enough?

Tressa made a face. “I… didn’t think of that.”

“Well, just do be more careful. And don’t forget to speak. But here, it is all done,” Lisha said as she stood and looked down at Tressa, who looked at her own bandaged feet and slid her socks and boots back on.

“Briadon, can I see the map?”

“Yeah, sure. One second,” he said, reaching into his bag and pulling out the map. He handed it over to Lisha, who opened it and spread it out on the ground.

“Can I ask what you are looking at?” he asked, leaning over her to see it himself as Tressa pulled on her boots and laced them up with a leather cord.

“Trying to determine how far we are from Sarif,” she said, using her claws to measure the distance. Maybe it’s better to detour farther northwest to Kaloid,” she said, pointing at the map. “It is much closer…”

“But Kaloid is the human farm town, is it not?”

“It is, so there is a worse chance of us being able to get Tressa some new shoes. That is why I’m thinking Sarif.”

Briadon rubbed his beard and looked at it. “I see your point.”

“I’m sorry, what are we discussing?” Tressa asked as she looked at the map. She didn’t really know the areas too well, not like Lisha and Briadon, as they seemed to be more well-versed in the area.

“Trying to decide which town would be closer, Sarif or Kaloid,” Lisha explained. “It would be a waste of time to turn around and head back to Ligis to get you the shoes. Sarif is very populated, and they sell good stuff.”

“My foot is okay,” Tressa said. “Now that you’ve wrapped it.” She felt a little guilty they were getting off course because of her, though also excited she would get to see Sarif after all. She had only read of kobolds and had little idea what they looked like. Would they be similar to Lisha? Did they also lay eggs? Tressa wanted to know.

A low, giddy chuckle slipped into Tressa’s voice as she sheepishly played with a braid, imagining what the kobolds must be like. “But as you said, my foot may become troublesome… It seems we have no choice but to stop in Sarif after all. Heheh.”

“Tressa…” Lisha said dryly.

“What?” Tressa put on her best innocent smile.

“Whatever thought you are thinking of, dispel it now.”

“I don’t know what you are talking about.” She looked away as if she had no clue what Lisha was talking about, but knew she wouldn’t be able to get it past her.

“As I said, no. You are not going to study the Kobolds, and it would be rude of you to do so. So no. Dispel any plan you had to do so.”

Tressa stuck out her bottom lip as she folded her arms. “I’m surprised you would think so little of me.”

Lisha flicked Tressa on the forehead, and she yelped in pain. “No, I do know you, and you were one hundred percent thinking of it.”

She let out a sigh. “Fine. But I still want to meet them. It seems so cool. Are they like Stitiki?”

Lisha tilted her head, thinking. “Well, a little bit. We are both lizard types, and we don’t breathe fire, nor are my people skilled in magic. They can, though. They are supposedly descended from dragons.”

Tressa’s eyes widened. “Dragons?”

“Don’t get your hopes up, kiddo,” Briadon said.

“Why not?”

“Dragons haven’t been seen in hundreds, if not thousands, of years. They are told of in stories and are gone. The last one was seen back in the Age of Myth.”

Tressa sighed. “I had hoped our texts had lied. Or that they were somehow wrong.” Glormect was technically a dragon himself, but he was of a very different type than the kind that supposedly once flew through their own world.

“Sorry, Tressa, but they haven’t been seen for a really long time. Humans are always searching for them. Maybe they moved to another continent across the sea where humans couldn’t hunt them.”

“Humans hunt dragons?” she asked, a bit of anger coming into her voice.

“Yes. Well, they have tried. But as I said, dragons haven’t been around in a very, very long time,” Lisha said sadly. “Trust me, I’d love to see one as well. They sound like amazing creatures.”

Tressa looked away thinking of the statue of the dragon around the goddess Emera. “I would fight humans if they hurt dragons.”

Lisha looked at Briadon as they shared a look. She shook her head, telling him now wasn’t the time and maybe later.

He let out a huff as he agreed. They didn’t have to speak to each other to communicate, as they had been friends for nearly five years and understood each other very well.

“I understand humans have their faults, as do any race, but hunting a dragon…” She sighed. “It’s just wrong for them to try and harm them.”

Briadon tilted his head. “Why do you say that?”

Tressa looked up at him as she slowly stood. “Well, my people… Well, we worship the dragons, as they are… Well, how do I say it in the common tongue?” She paused, trying to think. “I think the word is child. They are child of the goddess. As are we.”

“Goddess?” Lisha and Briadon said together as they shared a look.

“Yes. The goddess Emera,” Tressa said. “She is the creator of our world and gave us the dragons. Then came the elves, then humans, and all other creatures. She made this world. Though the first goddess was Analeah. I heard she created the dragons first. And the dragons of this world were the children of Emera.”

Lisha and Briadon looked at each other, then at Tressa.

“Never heard of her,” Briadon said as he shook his head.

Lisha nodded.

“Well, I figured that the knowledge was lost among the others, as even our texts are older than time. Well, there used to be more until the Mad King burned the archives,” she said.

“The Mad King?” Briadon grunted. “That bastard keeps coming up.”

“He has caused great harm to we elves,” Tressa said.

Lisha coughed. “Well, Tressa, do you think that you can walk to Sarif? It’s about a day and a half.”

Tressa nodded reluctantly. “Yes, I think so. Thanks for the help.”

“No problem. That is what teams do for each other, as well as friends.”

Tressa smiled, as she had finally made friends, even though she had figured that out the day she had rejoined them. But just hearing it made her so happy.

“Now that you’re ready, let’s head out," Briadon said. "I guess we are headed to Sarif.”
