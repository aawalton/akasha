Jade bolted into the spiral stairway that ringed the inside of the collapsing tower, urging her companions to go faster as pieces of masonry showered down on them from above. As soon as she found room to do so Jade leapt from the stairway and into the open space in the middle of the quaking building, spreading her wings and slowly descending while her companions sprinted circles around her, running along the stairs that spiraled down to the door.

“Almost there!” she called. “Come on!”

As soon as they were out of this tower, they’d be face to face with a horde of newly awakened monsters. Maybe she could slip outside and deal with a few firs-

Something large and hard smashed her in the shoulder, shattering that section of her protective ward. The stone fragment bounced away, tumbling to the floor with another shower of debris. She grunted in pain, clutching her shoulder and trying to regain control of her flight as the impact sent her tumbling towards a wall.

“Aah!” Naomi’s frightened cry caught Jade’s attention. With her magical senses active she felt more than saw a large piece of the tower's ceiling impact the step between Naomi and Aki. The Earth Warden nearly lost her footing as the stairs behind her sheared off the wall, but she flung herself forward. Luis grabbed her, pulling them both against the still-shaking wall as more masonry rained down around them. Unfortunately, Naomi wasn't so lucky.

"Naomi!"

Jade kicked off the wall, launching herself back into the open central column of the crumbling tower. She slammed into her plummeting friend, more tackling than catching her, and flared her wings to stabilize their wild descent.

"Hang on!"

Both of their protective wards sparked and crackled as more debris pelted them. Then Jade heard a new sound. It came from the tower itself, an alarming series of loud cracks, like rapid-fire lightning strikes. It was a noise no building should ever make, and Jade redoubled her efforts. She strained her wings, trying to arrest their fall as best she could. Wings of Night had a strict weight limit, and holding the disoriented bard had Jade well past that threshold. The ground rushed up to meet them with frightening speed, and she braced-

SLAM

The pair hit hard, tumbling onto the hard cobblestone floor. Jade grunted as pain flared through her legs and side. Everything was fuzzy. Was Naomi okay? Where was everyone else? She couldn't seem to focus her gaze on anything. Jade tried to stand, only to fall back to her knees as the world spun around her. A distant voice called out nearby, accompanied by a surge of warmth. The worst of the pain faded, the world snapping back into focus as Naomi's healing spell took effect.

"Come on!" She tugged Jade to her feet, hands still glowing with lingering magic. "Hurry!"

Naomi tugged Jade towards the door just as the rest of her companions reached the bottom of the stairs, rushing outside. More loud crashes rang out behind them as the tower began to truly collapse, a wave of dust blasting out the door behind them and leaving them coughing and disoriented.

"Form up!" Parker shouted nearby. "They're almost on us!"

Monsters!

In the chaotic escape, Jade had nearly forgotten about the swarm of dark knights and other ghoulish creatures that were pulling themselves up from the cathedral grounds. She joined Parker at the front of the group, calling for the rest of her party to get ready. This wasn't good. They were injured and caught off-guard. Everyone was recovering quickly, the training had done its job, but the monsters had a head start on them. A large group of them resolved out of the darkness and lingering dust. Dozens of figures, some in dark armor and others draped in long cloaks, approached at a run, brandishing wicked blades. Jade had just raised her hand to cast a spell when the receiver in her ear crackled to life with Tara's voice.

"Incoming! Duck!"

She barely had time to process the words before the entire front line of approaching monsters exploded in a plume of scorching fire.

Tara ignored her fleeing companions, opting to remain atop the now-unstable structure as it shuddered and quaked beneath her feet. Her suit had a slow-fall function, so long as the tower didn't fall on top of her, a drop from this height would be manageable. What wouldn't be was if they scrambled out of the collapsing building in a disorganized mess right as a mass of high-level monsters reached them.

"Suit. Track target trajectories."

Tara hopped atop the battlements, balancing precariously on the uncertain footing to get a better field of view. Her suit intelligence highlighted each monster in her visor, adding vectors that showed their probable locations in one second increments. The information shifted constantly, and only Tara's extensive training with the Expeditionary Taskforce Mark IV combat suit allowed her to make sense of the jumble of data filling her vision. Drawing her wand, she began channeling one of her Labyrinth imprinted elementalist spells. The Fireball spell was a personal favorite, especially with the ability she possessed that could boost it all the way to B rank.

The destructive spell had two major downsides, however. Its lengthy casting time, and a relatively slow travel velocity. These issues combined to temper the spell's destructive potential, as it didn't matter how strong your magic was if you failed to hit anything with it. It was this aspect of high rank magic that led most of her people to the conclusion that magitech was strictly superior. The ability to streamline complicated spells into instantaneous effects through carefully designed technology was an easy sell, especially considering that the resulting tools took far less training to use effectively. And yet, cutting the caster out of the spell formation process exacted a toll on the end result. Magitech weapons were rigid and unyielding, incapable of producing anything but the exact effects built into their substrate.

Tara did not share that weakness.

She continued channeling her spell, keeping her eye on the approaching enemy formation. The power built up rapidly, forming a swirling ball of orange fire that hovered on the tip of her wand, growing in size with each passing moment. Using her suit's projections as a guide, Tara aimed at the point the group of monsters were converging on. It wasn't ideal, the monsters hadn't spawned in a tight cluster and would be more dispersed than she liked. Right before she released the spell, Tara made a small adjustment to the stream of mana flowing down her arm. The ball of flame pulsed, growing larger as she increased the area the spell would affect. The modification would reduce the overall potency of the attack, but she judged that to be a worthwhile tradeoff for hitting the majority of the enemies. After making sure the resulting explosion would stop short of the tower entrance and not wash over her friends as they poured out of the tower, she released the fireball.

She barely had time to shout a warning over her earpiece before the floor dropped out from under her, cutting her off and sending her tumbling towards the ground far below.

The blast killed less than a quarter of the monsters, but it was more than enough. The remainder were severely burned, disoriented, and flung to the ground in the smoldering crater Tara's spell left in the garden. Jade was quick to move in, dispatching enemies with efficient slashes to their necks, chests, and wherever else her Vital Sight talent highlighted as a weak point. The rest of the team followed a moment later, swarming the stunned monstrosities before they could regain the initiative. The system labeled the larger, armored creatures as Templars and the hooded ones as Corrupted Spirits. Despite the name, none of the foes were intangible and Jade's blades easily cut them down one after the other.

There were too many of them for their group to dispatch before some were able to fight back, but by the time the creatures recovered they had lost the advantage of surprise. The teamwork Jade and Parker had drilled into their teams proved its worth once again as the tanks moved forward to intercept attacks, trusting the healers to support them while damage rained in from afar. The battle lasted less than a minute, with no one suffering more than minor wounds as they efficiently cut down the last of the newly spawned enemies.

"Tara." Jade said with a sigh, sheathing her daggers as they regrouped after the fight. "Would you give us a little more warning next time?"

The tek'kalla was still atop the pile of rubble that had once been the tower, which she had apparently ridden to the ground. Her quick thinking had turned the tide of the battle, but that explosion had practically been on top of them.

"I'll do my best, but there wasn't a lot of time." The tek'kalla shrugged. "I targeted my fireball conservatively. I knew it wouldn't hit any of you."

"That was conservative?" Naomi asked with a slow blink.

"Of course. At that range, my spell targeting has around a fifteen-foot margin of error. To be safe, I left at least twenty."

"I think you and I have a different definition of conservative."

"It's probably a translation error." Tara offered helpfully.

"Sometimes you have to seize an opportunity before it slips by." Parker said, still surveying the area for further enemies. "Those guys were tough. Taking them head-on would have been dangerous, given the state we were in."

"Good point." Jade nodded. "Tara, good job. That was quick thinking."

The tek'kalla mage shuffled her feet awkwardly and glanced aside.

"Of course." She sniffed. "That's why I'm here."

"Still, I did not expect the tower to fall out from under us." Aki said, stowing her mace but keeping her shield handy. "We must remain vigilant."

As if on cue, a nearby scraping sound brought them all back to alert. It wasn't hard to find the source. Nearby, another of the armored Templars was pulling itself from the ground, rising from the dirt like a zombie in a horror movie. Siora summoned a pair of vines that wrapped around its arms, stopping it from getting to its feet long enough for Luis and Parker to pummel the monster to death. Just as they finished, another Corrupted Spirit started doing the same a short distance away.

After a few minutes of dealing with this, it became apparent that the monsters weren't going to stop spawning. They weren't rising in a massive swarm like when the crystal atop the tower was destroyed. Instead, it was in ones and twos. They were vulnerable before getting fully free from the ground, fortunately, making them easy to defeat. However, the unending nature of their spawning posed a new challenge.

"We can't leave them here." Aylin said, blasting a half-risen templar to pieces with a focused bolt of magical energy. "They'll get out of control before we finish with the other towers. And then we have to deal with the boss."

"Maybe we should farm them for experience." Luis suggested, looking around for the next one. "These guys give a lot, and the loot isn't bad."

"This heightened spawn rate is likely another manifestation of the Core's instability. I've never heard of Labyrinth-born monsters being created at this frantic a pace. We should stabilize it as soon as possible."

"Sorry, Luis." Jade grunted as she threw Phantom into the face of a Corrupted Spirit as it emerged from the gloom. "I don't want to be responsible for-"

She blinked to the monster, wrenching Phantom free and spinning to avoid its clawed hands. Summoning her aura blades, Jade cut it down with a double slash to the neck.

"-Antarctica being blown up." She continued, only slightly out of breath. "Or whatever will happen when the Core goes into meltdown."

"Not to mention we're in here." Siora added. "I don't think that would be good for us either."

"You've got a point." Luis conceded. "Alright then, we should leave a couple of people here to stop the monsters from spawning while the rest of us go to the next tower."

Some brief experimentation showed that any two of them, except perhaps two healers, were capable of covering the spawn area. While the monsters here were high level, they seemed to take increased damage while stuck in the process of pulling themselves from the ground. That made it all the more important that they were stopped before reaching full strength. Eita and Aki volunteered to cover the first zone while the rest of the group went to the next. They had a lot of experience working together and could stay in contact with the radio earpieces L-ReF was outfitting all outgoing Wayfarer parties with.

"We need to hurry." Jade said, jogging at the head of the group. "If the other towers work the same, we'll be spending a lot of mana stopping the monsters from spawning."

"It's a good thing it regenerates faster here." Tara said. "I'm already almost back to full."

"Me too, but everyone we leave to guard our backs won't have time to recover it. And something tells me we don't want to fight the boss in corespace flooded with monsters that never stop coming."

"Well, shit. That's a pleasant thought."
