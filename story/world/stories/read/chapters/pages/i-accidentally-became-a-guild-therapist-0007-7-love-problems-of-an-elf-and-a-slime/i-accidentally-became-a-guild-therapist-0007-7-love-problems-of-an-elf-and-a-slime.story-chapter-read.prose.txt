ARC I: THE ACADEMIC EXILE

SYNOPSIS: Phina and Glimmer are official now—the whole guild knows it, Glimmer glows every time she’s near. But when Phina’s old mentor arrives at camp with an ultimatum from the Arcane Conclave, Livia learns that the hardest part of loving someone isn’t confessing it. It’s defending it. And her new Empathic Insight is about to activate for the first time—on someone who very much does not want to be read.

Livia Marcelline Quinn, Mental Supporter Lv. 2 and reluctant server mascot, was starting to think her Therapist’s Log had a new problem: entries that didn’t resolve, just quietly evolved.

Its pages were a warzone:

Bron: Plushie mania. Provisional resolution.

Nyx: Dark phobia, awkward crush. Significant progress. Ten seconds. Do not think about the smile.

Alaric: Betting addiction. Unresolved. Currently betting on his own recovery.

Phina: Slime romance—resolved? Glimmer confirmed official. Status: happy, I think. Hasn’t been herself since Tuesday. Follow up.

The Bloodbath & Beyond base camp was its usual circus—glowing runes flickering at irregular intervals, enchanted swords abandoned at angles that suggested their owners had left in a hurry, and Glimmer, now a fixture rather than a visitor, attempting what could generously be called a cartwheel near the east tent.

They're official. It's been four days. The slime still hasn't landed a single cartwheel.

I have never seen anyone so happy to keep failing at something.

Livia perched on her crate, silver-adjacent robe still one upgrade away from silver, and watched the failed cartwheel with real affection.

[Achievement Unlocked: Chronic Therapist Fatigue. +10 Endurance Points (Barely Enough)]

“Tell me about it,” Livia muttered, swatting the notification. Her progress bar read Mental Supporter Lv. 2: 1400/2000 EXP. Six hundred to go. She had not seen Phina all morning, which was unusual. Phina generally announced her presence from at least forty feet away.

She was still turning that over when Phina arrived. From the wrong direction, at the wrong speed, with the wrong posture entirely.

Phina did not glide.

She walked—ordinary, human-paced, shoulders drawn in—and sat down on the edge of Livia’s crate without asking, which was its own kind of alarm. Glimmer trailed behind her, subdued, its usual golden shimmer dimmed to something closer to candlelight.

Something's wrong. She didn't announce herself. She didn't recite anything. She just... sat.

“Phina?”

Phina held out a folded parchment, sealed with a wax stamp shaped like an open eye. Her hand was steady. Her voice was not.

“It arrived Tuesday,” she said. “I have read it eleven times. I was hoping that if I did not mention it, it would resolve itself. This has not happened.”

Livia took the parchment and unfolded it. The handwriting was precise, old-fashioned, the kind of penmanship that took itself very seriously.

By order of the Conclave of the Arcane, Seraphina Duskveil is summoned to formal review. Cause: conduct unbecoming an Archmage of recognized lineage. A determination will be rendered in person. Attendance is not optional.

— Magister Verren, Conclave Overseer, Duskveil line, ret.

“Conduct unbecoming,” Livia read aloud. “What conduct?”

Phina looked at Glimmer.

Livia looked at Glimmer.

Oh.

“They heard,” Phina said, very quietly. “Of course they heard. I told half the server. I composed odes. I was not exactly discreet.” She said the word like it tasted bad. “Magister Verren trained me for six years. He recommended me for my rank. My family’s entire standing in the Conclave runs through his goodwill.”

“And he’s coming here.”

“He is already three portals into the journey,” Phina said. “I received a follow-up pigeon. He is, apparently, very disappointed, and wished me to have advance notice of the disappointment so that I might, quote, ‘arrive at the appropriate feeling before he does.’”

[Patient: Phina. Surface: Composed. Beneath: Terror—not of losing Glimmer. Of losing everything else if she keeps him.]

That's the actual issue. Not whether the relationship is real. Whether she gets to keep her whole life if she chooses it anyway.

“Can I ask you something,” Livia said, carefully. “Not about Glimmer. About you.”

Phina nodded, tightly.

“If Verren wasn’t coming—if there was no letter, no review, nobody watching—would you still choose this?”

The answer came fast. Too fast to be anything but true. “Yes.”

“Then the problem isn’t the relationship,” Livia said. “It’s that you’ve spent your whole life being someone people approved of, and you don’t know what happens to you if that stops.”

Phina went very still, the way people go still when something lands exactly.

Glimmer, sensing the stillness, oozed closer and pressed itself gently against her ankle. Not performing. Just present.

Magister Verren arrived at midday, precisely on schedule, through a portal so formal it had its own sound effect—a low, dignified chime, entirely too pleased with itself.

He was tall, silver-robed, with the kind of beard that had clearly been groomed by someone who considered beard maintenance a moral discipline. He surveyed the camp with the expression of a man auditing a crime scene.

“Seraphina.”

“Magister.” Phina stood. Her spine was very straight.

Verren’s gaze traveled—the runed tents, the mana puddle nobody had explained, Bron’s wicker man leaning at a structurally concerning angle, and finally, at ankle height, Glimmer, who jiggled once, uncertainly, under the attention.

“I did not cross three portals,” Verren said, “to find the last Archmage of the Duskveil line consorting with vermin.”

The camp—which had been listening, because the camp always listened—went very quiet.

“It is not vermin,” Phina said. Even. Controlled.

“It is an ooze,” Verren said. “A creature with the cognitive architecture of digestive fluid. I have reviewed the reports. Poetry. Public ceremony. A confession, apparently witnessed. Your grandmother held a Conclave seat for forty years, Seraphina. This is not conduct befitting that inheritance.”

Glimmer’s jiggling stopped.

Its surface, for a moment, went very still—and then, faint but unmistakable, it darkened at the edges. Not glowing. The opposite of glowing. A slime, Livia realized, could look hurt.

[Patient: Glimmer. Surface: unreadable, technically. Beneath: also technically unreadable. And yet.]

It understood that. Every word of it. Whatever Glimmer is, it is not too simple to know when it's being called worthless.

Livia stepped forward before she’d fully decided to.

“Magister Verren,” she said. “Livia Quinn. Guild therapist.”

Verren looked at her the way one looks at a footnote. “This does not concern you.”

“It does, actually,” Livia said. “You just told a room full of people that something is too simple to be loved. I’d like to point out that it flinched when you said it. Digestive fluid doesn’t flinch.”

A ripple went through the camp. Bron made a sound that was trying very hard not to be a laugh.

Verren’s jaw tightened. “A reflex.”

“Sure,” Livia said. “Want to test that theory? Say something nice to it next.”

Verren did not say something nice to it.

“Seraphina,” Verren said, turning back to her, ignoring Livia with the specific effort of a man choosing to. “The Conclave has offered a resolution. Discretion. End the—” he gestured at Glimmer without looking at it, “—arrangement, quietly, and your rank recommendation proceeds as planned. Continue this, and I cannot protect your standing. I will not pretend otherwise.”

The camp had gone fully silent now. Even Alaric, who had already produced a scrap of paper for odds, had stopped writing.

Phina looked at Glimmer.

Glimmer looked back, its surface still dim, still darkened at the edges, waiting—not hopefully, not desperately, just waiting, the way something waits when it has already decided to accept whatever answer comes.

“I have spent six years,” Phina said, slowly, “being exactly what the Conclave wanted. Precise. Correct. A credit to my line.”

“As you should be,” Verren said.

“I do not love Glimmer because the Conclave permits it,” Phina said, and her voice did not shake once. “I love Glimmer because I do. It was never going to be a vote, Magister. I only let you think it was one because I was afraid of what happens to me if I stop being correct.”

She knelt—not for ceremony this time, just to be level with him—and Glimmer surged up against her palm, its color returning in a slow gold bloom, brighter than Livia had seen it since before the letter arrived.

“I’m not ending it,” Phina said. “If that costs me the recommendation, it costs me the recommendation.”

Verren stared at her for a long moment.

Then at the slime, glowing, wrapped around her hand like it had never once considered letting go.

“Your grandmother,” he said, finally, “was insufferably stubborn. I see it skipped no generations.”

It was not an apology. It was not approval, either. But something in his posture had shifted—the rigid formality softening into something closer to weary respect.

“The review stands,” he said. “I make no promises about its outcome.” He turned to leave, then paused, and—almost against his own will, it seemed—glanced down at Glimmer one more time. “...It flinched.”

“It flinched,” Livia confirmed.

Verren said nothing else. The portal took him with the same self-satisfied chime it had arrived with.

[You helped an archmage choose authenticity over approval, in front of the one person whose approval mattered most. +1000 EXP]

The camp exhaled as one organism.

Bron let out a whoop and immediately started explaining to three confused players what had just happened. Alaric crossed out an entire column of odds and started a new one, muttering about “unprecedented variables.” Nyx, from his crate, said “that was… brave,” which from Nyx counted as a standing ovation.

Phina was still kneeling, Glimmer glowing gold against her palm, and for once she wasn’t composing an ode about it. She was just there. Present. On purpose.

Livia’s HUD flared before she’d even finished writing the session notes.

[LEVEL UP! Mental Supporter Lv. 3: 400/3000 EXP]

[New Passive: Empathic Insight Lv. 1 — detects subtle patient emotions beneath the surface.]

[Robe upgrade: silver trim. You have earned this trim.]

Livia stared at her own sleeve, where a thin line of silver had, in fact, appeared along the hem.

New skill. Immediately terrifying. Let's not test it on anyone important.

Miles chose that exact moment to walk past, arms crossed, watching the last of the portal-shimmer fade with an unreadable expression.

“Not bad, rookie,” he said. “You told off a Conclave Overseer. That’s a first, even for this guild.”

“He called Glimmer digestive fluid,” Livia said. “I have professional standards.”

Miles almost smiled—a real one, brief, there and gone—and turned to leave.

Livia, without deciding to, pointed her brand-new skill at his back.

[Empathic Insight: Active]

[Patient: Miles Vexley]

[Surface Emotion: Approval. Genuine.]

[Beneath it: Something that looked, briefly, like longing. For what, the skill could not determine. Duration: 1.4 seconds. Then: gone.]

Livia stared at the notification.

Then at Miles’ back.

There it is again.

Whatever that is.

She wrote it in the margin of her notepad, small: Miles. Same flicker as before. Not a fluke. File for later. Much later.

She underlined later three times.

Phina found her before the day was over, Glimmer riding contentedly on her shoulder, both of them glowing in the low gold light of early evening.

“Thank you,” Phina said. “For the part about the flinching.”

“It was true,” Livia said. “I just said it out loud.”

Phina considered this. “That seems to be your entire methodology.”

“It’s not much of a methodology.”

“It is the only one that has ever worked on me,” Phina said, simply, and glided off—properly gliding now, composure restored, already murmuring the first line of what was unmistakably a new ode.

[Achievement Unlocked: Slime Whisperer]

[System Note: No psychology textbook has a chapter for this. You are writing it.]

Livia closed her notepad.

“I talked back to an interdimensional wizard on behalf of a slime’s dignity,” she muttered. “This is my legacy.”

She had helped Phina choose herself over approval.

She had a new skill she didn't fully trust yet.

And something about Miles kept flickering under the surface, over and over, patient and unreadable, waiting for her to be ready to look at it properly.

Not today.

But soon.
