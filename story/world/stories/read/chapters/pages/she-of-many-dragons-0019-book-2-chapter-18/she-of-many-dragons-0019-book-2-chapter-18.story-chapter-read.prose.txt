Oliver led Alice through the hallways of the sprawling Protectorate building. Though she could not find her way back to her room if she were paid for it, Alice still had a vague sense of the direction they were heading.

As they walked, she focused on each hallway to try to memorize the layout. It wasn't too different from the sprawling noble estates where she had lived as a General Laborer.

Honestly, some of those estates had been much larger — she had to stop letting herself be intimidated by the presence of high rankers. She was no longer a nobody. She had a valuable class too.

Alice focused on that and firmly ignored the fact that her shoulder felt cold and empty. Since she hatched, Prim had almost always been with her, and it felt odd for her to be training somewhere else

But Alice was not alone. She had Bella, Natura, and Numi.

With a little start, Alice realized that she had never truly been alone since she had become She of Many Dragons.

Oliver suddenly turned a sharp corner, and they came to a set of double doors that opened to a courtyard.

Alice hadn't been expecting it, as they were not on ground level but on a low rooftop. This provided them a magnificent view of the city, once they looked past the potted plants.

Natura stepped forward after Alice and paused at the sight of the plants, the pathways with several benches set out for people to rest, and fountains with playful water features. She said, “Ohh, these poor dears,” and immediately rushed to the nearest potted fern. “The balance of the soil is all wrong for you, isn't it?” Natura muttered to herself, looking quite fretful.

Alice turned to Oliver. “What is this place?”

“People normally sit here and eat lunch in good weather,” he replied. “Samantha was truly joshing you earlier. The servants do clean up, but I don't think any of them are plant magicians—come to think of it, I don't believe there are any plant magicians within the Guild, or at least not assigned here.” He trailed off, his face going a bit red as if he realized he had been rambling. “In any case, I think this is mostly for decoration.”

At a glance, none of the plants seemed suitable for food, so it felt like a bit of a waste to Alice, but of course, she would never be so rude as to say so.

Natura continued cooing over the fern, which was already looking a bit perkier.

“Well, she seems to have found the place to her liking,” Oliver said with a light laugh. “And what are these two?” He smiled at Numi and Bella.

“Bella is a shadow dragon,” Alice said, thinking up the lie on the spot. “I'm certain all these shadows cast by the plants will be perfect for her training.”

She gave her dragon a significant look, and Bella nodded, “Yes, I do love... shadows,” she said in her smoky voice and then jumped up to glide into a particularly thick patch of plants.

“And Numi…” Alice was lost for an excuse for a second, but Numi was quicker than most and grabbed Oliver's attention by scuttling around in tight circles in front of him.

“I will find things! I'm a great finder,” she said, then immediately darted away.

“Well, that's that it then,” Oliver said, and then hesitated, looking a little... was he in pain? “Would you perhaps like to take lunch with me?”

Alice felt a little tempted, but… “I should stay to monitor them,” she said and then leaned close as if to impart a secret. “Sometimes Numi doesn’t really understand what is lost and what someone had just put down for a moment. I would hate for there to be any unhappy accidents.”

Oliver chortled, and something in the glint of his eyes somehow made his expression seem more open than usual. Alice felt like she could trust him… which was a dangerous thought. He was a nobleman’s son—and not just any nobleman, but one that she had worked for.

I can not trust him, she reminded herself. I can only trust my dragons.

“Then at least allow me to fetch you something,” Oliver said. “Would a sandwich do?”

“That would be lovely, thank you.”

He took his leave, and Alice watched him go before she glanced around to make absolutely sure no one else was in the courtyard. It seemed that even though lunch was being served, no one had decided to eat out here. It was rather warm, and the sunshine could be intense with the courtyard stone baking underfoot.

“So,” Numi said as she scuttled back out of the foliage, “What are we actually doing?”

“Why, training of course. Natura, please come join us. You too, Bella.”

Bella was more than happy to creep out from under the plants. “I do not have mere shadow powers,” she snarked.

“It is only a cover story, dear,” Alice said before she looked at Natura. “Bella requires something she can Sacrifice in order to eat. Are there any dead plants or vermin she could use?”

Natura reeled back. “Even the vermin have a place in here, Alice. They have made this little refuge their homes, made their nests—”

“And since they are vermin, I’m certain their population will not suffer if a few go missing,” Alice said firmly. “Bella will not eradicate all of them.” She gave her little dark dragon a hard look. “Will you?”

“I may eradicate a nest or two,” Bella said. “Will anyone but Natura miss them?”

“Oh!” Numi bounced on her toes. “Pack rats often have treasures that I can find and Appraise.” She must have seen the look of disgust on Natura’s face because she turned to her and said quite reasonably, “I know you want this area to be as healthy as possible, and you have seen the consequences in the forest when one type of creature grows out of control. Bella will help with that.”

“I… suppose,” she said with reluctance. “But I do not like the thought of anything dying.”

“I will not make it painful,” Bella said with all the air of someone making a grand concession. “It will be like pruning a sickly branch from an otherwise healthy tree.”

“Bella is the newest hatched of all of you, and she needs the most to allow her to grow,” Alice reminded her.

Natura sighed. “I suppose. But do you mind if I also enrich the soil here? It seems no one with a proper Gardener class has looked over these plants at all.”

“No, do whatever you feel is best to help the plants,” Alice allowed.

Natura perked up immediately.

Alice took a seat on a bench, and the three dragons set off together, Natura pointing to a corner where a gutter had been laid to take away the worst of the excess water during a heavy rainstorm. She sensed there were several rat nests down there.

Immediately, Numi skuttled down, her lizard-like body making it easy.

In contrast, Bella was still quite small, but she was shaped flat and squat with a shovel-like head. She had a much more difficult time finding a way into the hole.

That was fine for Numi, who, even though she would rather appraise objects and find shiny treasures, was still a dragon. She had claws, and the vermin were only rodents. She brought dead rats up to Bella.

With a dark chuckle, Bella took them and slunk off to a shadowed corner, where she started her Sacrifice skill. She immediately gained a level.

Soon, even Natura decided to help and reluctantly presented a handful of very fat green caterpillars to Bella. “There are more than enough of these on the plants to feed the next generation of butterflies, and they are sadly chewing through the leaves.”

Bella took them happily, and moments later they were ash.

She moved to another, more obscured part of the courtyard where she could not be seen just before Oliver returned. He had with him two sandwiches wrapped in white parchment paper as well as glasses of fizzy drink that Alice had never tasted before.

Alice sat on a bench beside him and had a lovely meal, ignoring the fact that several dozen feet away, Bella was sacrificing different animals to her green fire, and Numi was probably elbow-deep in rat nests.

Natura moved from plant to plant, renewing each of them. Soon, several were blooming before their eyes.

“That is extraordinary,” Oliver said. “I wonder how useful she would be during a drought.”

Alice smiled, proud of her dragon. “She is a marvel with plants, but it is not as if she can alter the weather.”

Natura gave her a look that clearly said: Not yet.

Oliver shook his head. “Even helping to heal a field would be a boon for a town. My uncle has renters who had drought-stricken lands. It got quite dire for his tenants until the rains came. Bring this up to the leadership, and they may send you on a mission of mercy.”

“Truly?” she asked, startled. She had thought that Oliver was making idle conversation.

He nodded. “You could make a real difference.”

With a smile, Alice bit into her sandwich.
