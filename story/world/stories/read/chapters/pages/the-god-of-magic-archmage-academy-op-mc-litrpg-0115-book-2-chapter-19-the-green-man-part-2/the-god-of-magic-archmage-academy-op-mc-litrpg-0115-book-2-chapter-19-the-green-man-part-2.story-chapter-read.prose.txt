The Green Man

He’d swept across the Isle of Magic, first as flocks of birds, then like a rolling wave sweeping away those he’d been sent to destroy. The power of the plague bearer embedded within him was no mere contagion spell, nor was it limited in the way normal sickness and diseases were. The power within him tailored itself not to a species, but to the victim itself. If his plague found its way into a room of nine slavers, each of those nine could die in a different way. It mutated in between each victim, targeting their weaknesses, searching to cause maximum harm and suffering.

Tonight, the Green Man was both surgeon and reaper, and none would escape the purpose he’d been given.

Seventeen locations. He’d been given seventeen locations to cut away from society, sparing only those who had nothing to do with the oppression of the dreadbeasts. His pathogens weren’t so limited that they needed to attack blindly. They could search for the tether spells that created bonds between masters and slaves. They could search for general memories, glimpsing whether a person knew about the slavery occurring in their town, or the work their employer was doing. How they felt about it, whether they would change it if they could—these were factors in deciding whether someone would be infected or simply serve as a carrier. He worked across seventeen locations at once, but with each passing moment his job became easier. The plague finished its work, and he felt it as those sicknesses ceased to exist, leaving behind a trail of corpses and freed dreadbeasts in their wake.

When the humans discovered the bodies, they would know it was sickness that had taken them. It wasn’t the work of other humans, mana beasts, or dreadbeasts. No, it was sickness, and that wasn’t something freed slaves could be blamed for. This was clearly the work of a high-level mage. The type of mage no one would want to get on the wrong side of.

Only three locations remained. Copies of himself worked now to ambush lord-level mages who were either involved, protected, or oversaw the slaves of one of his locations. As soon as he’d identified them, he’d allocated more of his power. He was fortunate no one above the level of lord was present, so the dreadbeast’s intelligence reports had been correct. That was good; it meant his master would not need to dirty his hands with such work.

The first lord fell without a problem. A simple ambush in his sleep. The second he ambushed in the streets of his town when the moment was right. The last location, the Halls of House Dulmont, he’d attended to personally. His birds had sensed not one, but three powerful lords here. The first he’d already dealt with. He’d found an effective strategy that had worked against all of his lord-level opponents so far. Granted, he’d had the element of surprise in those cases. Now he would need to test himself in an actual fight.

He suddenly felt the urge to introduce himself. After all, these would be his final moments. His work was almost complete. Even if they wouldn’t be around to remember him, he wanted someone to hear it.

His name.

“I am the Green Man, and today you’ll be cut away from the society you have poisoned. Rejoice.”

Koré

She eyed The Green Man for a moment before her eyes cut away towards Tetra. “What’s wrong, Tetra?”

Tetra grimaced. She pulled her hand away from her chest, where she’d been clutching it. Green ooze fell out of a wound. “He caught me off guard. Punched a hole in me. I haven’t felt right since.” Tetra gritted her teeth as another wave of pain washed over her.

“Alader, keep an eye on him,” Koré instructed.

Alader nodded, his eyes never leaving their opponent. The Green Man tilted his head as he watched Koré approach Tetra. He was odd. It was as if he were studying them, hoping to learn something. She ran her mana senses over Tetra, and her eyes widened.

She knew Tetra’s body inside and out. Tetra trusted no one else to heal her after fighting, and Koré had seen her through grievous wounds, sicknesses, and even assassination attempts. She knew the rhythm of her sister’s heartbeat. She was wounded and possibly afraid; her heartbeat should be elevated. Instead, it was slow, slow to the point that Koré knew instantly what was wrong.

“Tetra…”

Her sister shouted in pain and keeled over.

She was having a heart attack.

There wasn’t anytime to waste. She focused her mana and cast. “Healing Cleanse!”

A pulse of pure blue mana washed over Tetra the same way it had a hundred times. It flooded her body, forcing its way through her veins, rejuvenating her cells and hunting down impurities.

However, for the first time since Koré had ascended to the rank of Lord, her powers failed her. Whatever this monster had done to her wasn’t affected by her purification spell. She shook her head. “No...”

Tetra gritted her teeth and held a hand towards the Green Man. “Just die already!”

“Tetra, wait!” Koré called out. In her state, using magic was far too risky. Her body was struggling to stay upright as was; if not for sheer willpower and the supporting energy of her mana, Tetra wouldn’t even be able to stay up on her knees. Despite that, she pressed forward.

“Corruptive Flood Rot!” Tetra shouted. A burst of purple mana exploded forward from her hand and shot towards the Green Man.

It cut through his viscous, liquid body and continued flying. The attack smacked against the exterior walls of House Dulmont and tore a hole through them. But the Green Man reformed as if there’d been no effect.

Tetra’s eyes widened.

Alader grunted. “If magic won’t work, then I’ll end this myself.” He charged forward, and once again, Koré’s warning went unheeded.

The thin line representing a mouth on the Green Man’s face fell into a frown. He’d said something earlier about wanting to set Alader free. Perhaps he really didn’t want to fight the dreadbeast. But despite that, he reacted this time. The Green Man held out a hand. “Flesh Sculpt Maxim.”

A liquid mallet the size of a house shot forward from the creature’s arm and swatted Alader away like a child. The dreadbeast slammed into the ground and skidded several feet away, leaving a trail where he’d dug through the dirt. The Green Man sighed. “Total Restoration Maxim.” He immediately healed Alader, but the dreadbeast did not get up. His consciousness had slipped away during the attack. Alader was powerful. A proud dreadbeast from a line of dreadbeast Khans. She knew he would one day reach the rank himself. But before this creature, he was swatted aside as if it was nothing.

Despite her warnings to them, Tetra and Alader weren’t wrong. When healing magic didn’t work to stop a debilitating spell, the next best solution was to destroy the caster. If he wouldn’t release Tetra, and Koré couldn’t heal her, she only had one option.

She had to kill him.

She might not be able to cleanse the impurity within Tetra, but she had a more powerful technique. One that could work offensively. She was hesitant to use it here, considering the risk, but her sister didn’t have much time. Already she’d likely sustained permanent damage to her heart. If Koré didn’t end this now, she’d lose more than a father tonight.

She’d be all alone in this world.

“Cleansing Field Purify!” A rush of mana poured out of her like an ocean from a cup. A huge sphere made of translucent white light surrounded their makeshift battlefield. This green man accused them of poisoning the world, but they’d see who was cleansed and who would remain afterwards. Koré would protect her sister at all costs. And the spell did come with a cost.

No barriers, no wards, no protective runes. Normally she wouldn’t cast this spell without a regiment of her father’s men protecting her. But right now her life wasn’t of any importance. Alader and Tetra were in danger, and she’d gladly die for either of them.

Even if it killed her, the cleansing field would close.

She would wipe him out of existence like a germ.

The Green Man

The dreadbeast was incapacitated. The human woman, Tetra, wasn’t long for this world. Flesh Sculpt had proven an incredibly powerful ability in his hands. He’d been instructed not to infect Lord-class mages with the plague-bearer spell. But his unique form made that unnecessary. He’d pierced a hole in her body and flooded it with the liquid that made him. Even now, portions of him swam through her bloodstream, obstructing the flow to her heart. She hadn’t even realized what he’d done until it was too late. Subtle, shallow strikes that she thought she could brush off had become fatal.

Now he just needed to finish his last opponent. He stared up at the white dome around them; it was closing, slowly but surely. He could feel the caster’s authority growing within this space. Perhaps that was all it did: give an edge to her other techniques. But his instincts told him there was something more going on. Something more sinister. His assignment was almost finished. The plague should finish its work in the two locations where he’d killed the other lords soon. Once he finished with the girl, he would ensure this location was scoured as well for any remnants and survivors. But this cleansing field admittedly worried him on an instinctual level. He thought it best to at least provide his master with an update, just in case things went awry.

He lifted a hand, and a green bird formed on it, in the form of a vulture. “Go and update the master.”

The bird spread its wings and soared forward toward the top of the dome. A piece of himself. The field had temporarily cut him off from mentally connecting with his copies and the other birds of his flock, but this should allow him to bypass that telepathic block.

The vulture reached the roof of the dome and shot through.

It dissolved instantly. With it, a piece of him died.

He coughed up thick liquid that splattered on the ground before him, leaving it hissing as the liquid ate through the ground. Slowly it returned to his body like little slugs leaving trails as they moved. He realized then the danger he was in. That cleansing field had instantly eradicated a portion of himself that should’ve broken through easily. How strong was this girl? Did she somehow surpass the others? He’d handled them fairly easily, albeit by surprising them, but still. Could she really have a technique powerful enough to end him?

No.

She was exposed; he could feel it. Her defenses were down. This overwhelming spell was taking too much of her focus. He’d simply strike her down.

He held a hand up to her. “Fireball Maxim!” he shouted. A flaming sphere of green flew towards her, intensifying the heat all around them.

She didn’t panic or attempt to dodge. She stared him down in defiance.

Her sister had fallen on her side in pain. Tetra reached out to warn her sister.

Too late. It was far too —

Something blurred past in the moment before his spell struck her, and the grooves of his eyes stretched wide.

Alader, the dreadbeast, had roused himself somehow. He shouldn’t even be awake. But he’d managed to get up in time to protect her.

“Why are you protecting her?” the Green Man demanded.

How had he protected her?

That blow should’ve been more than enough to kill him. They were both fortunate it hadn’t.

“Alader! Are you okay?” the human woman asked.

He glared at the Green Man, but his words softened when he responded. “Fifty-eight percent. It seems our first field test was successful. Now, let’s put an end to this,” he said.

She smiled at that. “Right.” Her eyes locked on the Green Man’s, and she closed her fist.

The sphere closed more rapidly. Shrinking to encompass them all.

Moments. He had mere moments before it closed in on him.

This was it.

She was going to kill him.

Time seemed to slow down around him as his thoughts sped up.

What could he do?

His work wasn’t done yet.

The plague still needed more time in the other towns. And he’d all but liberated this area, but he couldn’t be sure he’d completed his work. He’d failed to warn his master in time. Now, if this woman escaped…if the other towns survived the plague, then the plan would fail.

He’d been given life and purpose by the only master worth serving on this corrupt, crooked planet. The Green Man existed for one purpose: to serve. Failure wasn’t an option.

He’d been given specific instructions.

The dreadbeasts must live.

The slavers must die.

Lord-class mages must be dealt with personally.

No one Lord-class or above can become infected by the plague.

But now, those conditions were no longer all possible.

If he failed, there was no guarantee that the dreadbeasts would all escape. Some slavers might survive and hunt them down. It was of the utmost importance that they all escaped tonight; the master had been clear on that.

His death meant that his ultimate purpose would go unfulfilled.

He had two options then. Die and hope for the best, or break the last rule. Infect this woman with the plague personally, ensure she dies, and the spell is broken.

It was risky, but it was the only way he could guarantee her death. There wasn’t enough time to do this any other way.

Fireball and Flesh Sculpt were powerful, but he was a plague creation. When everything was on the line, his instinct was to rely on his true nature.

He focused on her. “Paragons—”

No, she wasn’t someone who met the conditions of the spell his master had cast. He instinctively didn’t want to fight her. He’d decided to because she was protecting one of his other targets. But now she threatened everything. The Paragon’s Plague wouldn’t work here. So he resorted to the original version.

“Plague Bearer Maxim.”

Power exploded through him. His body exploded, sending droplets into the barrier that dissolved upon contact. He ignored the brutal sting of portions of his soul being wiped away. Only one thing mattered. Stopping her. Freeing himself and finishing his work. He had to win. The Green Man slithered through the air like a liquid serpent.

The cleansing field reacted immediately, triggered by his sudden explosion of power. It closed after him like a predator hunting prey.

Alader tried to cut off his path, holding his arms wide and stepping in front of the girl protectively.

The Green Man’s liquid form split and traversed around the dreadbeast. He struck the girl and infected her through touch, entwining himself with her as thoroughly as he could. He was no longer just a host for the plague; he was the plague itself, and he’d infected her.

The cleansing field caught up to him at that same moment.

It sought to erase him, and it was successful.

Almost.

Koré

The cleansing field closed around her, and her entire body screamed.

Black lines crawled up and down her arms. Her heart pounded in her chest.

Alader grabbed her shoulders. He looked incredibly distraught. She wanted to comfort him, but the pain was overwhelming. She saw her own distorted reflection in his eyes.

Blood poured from her eyes, her nose, her mouth, and her ears.

The cleansing field was supposed to wipe away impurities.

So why was it targeting her?

“—me!” Alader shouted.

She couldn’t understand him.

“Share it with me!” he screamed again.

Nothing made sense. She just wanted the pain to stop. A few moments ago she might have refused, but now she just wanted it to stop.

She did it; some small part of her thought perhaps he’d be immune. Fifty-eight percent was surely enough, right?

He could ease her burden.

“Transfer Impurity!” She tried to say. The words were garbled, and she bit her tongue, but the general intention was there. A portion of the plague spell destroying her from the inside transferred to Alader.

[Status Update - twenty percent of the infection has been transferred.]

Just twenty percent?

Alader’s eyes rolled into the back of his head, and he fell to his knees.

She regretted it almost instantly. That small portion helped her tremendously, but look what she’d done to a close friend to get it. No, she needed to get control. It was the only way to save all three of them.

She couldn’t let this beat her.

Koré screamed, and the cleansing field exploded like shattered glass. She couldn’t cleanse the plague inside her. Her only hope was to control it. So she embraced her family’s technique. One she’d always rebelled against.

Her intentions came out in a single word.

The Maximizer her father had used to terrorize their enemies.

The word her sister, Tetra, used to torture prisoners.

A power she despised.

“Rot!”

As she embraced it, she felt the plague give way, and power flooded her body. This wasn’t just control; this was integration. Maybe that was why her system interface updated her on her body’s new guest as well.

Garvhadda

He stared at the sealed mountain before him with crossed arms and a heavy heart. He didn’t turn away when he heard familiar footsteps.

Retter. It was Retter.

“We’ve received reports from all over. It’s done.”

That made him look back. He glanced back at his old friend, daring to pry his eyes away from the power upon which his people’s fate would rely. “Casualties?”

He hadn’t expected Retter to smile. “Some were injured in the fighting. But…no deaths, Garvhadda. Our people made it.”

He frowned and shook his head. “Those Drussan could save. But how many did we abandon to make that possible? I promised our people freedom, but when it came time, how many will wake up tomorrow in chains because of my decision?”

He’d sent out the signal before Drussan and their other agents were ready. He’d abandoned a few for the needs of the many. That was never the kind of leader Garvhadda had aspired to be.

Retter stepped beside him and stared up at the mountain. “I don’t think you understand. I’m not sure I fully do. But…no one was left behind. I don’t know how he did it, but Drussan found a way to free all of them.”

Garvhadda’s eyes widened. “What?”

Retter smiled. “I was once disappointed you chose Drussan’s path instead of mine. I thought war was the way forward. But I see now, Lord Garvhadda, I see now that you made the right decision. Your promise was fulfilled tonight, brother.” Retter grabbed him by the shoulder and grinned.
