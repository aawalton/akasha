The oiled rag squeaked across oxidized steel. Dalgor rubbed harder, his knuckles white, stripping away three years of cellar grime to reveal the roaring silver lion embossed on the breastplate.

Mara stood in the doorway. Her breathing hitched, a sharp, wet sound that cut through the drafty air of their small, mud-brick home.

"Dalgor, don’t be stupid." She gripped the wooden doorframe, her knuckles turning white. "Think of our unborn child. You are heading to your death. Have you not heard the disparity? Cinderfall marches with fifty million men. Please. See reason."

Dalgor didn't stop polishing. He held up a matching pauldron, checking the leather straps for rot.

"The numbers are growing," Mara pressed, stepping into the room. She reached out and grabbed his wrist, her nails digging into his skin. "The whole military might of the Hegemony is moving toward that valley. The young Lord Alarion has barely three million troops. Dark Elves and metal golems. It will be a slaughter."

She dropped her head against his shoulder. Her tears soaked through his rough tunic. "I know our history. But we are not nobles. No one will know if you stay. We can hide."

Dalgor set the pauldron down with a heavy clatter. He turned and cupped her face, his calloused thumbs wiping the moisture from her cheeks.

"My love," he said, his voice dropping to a gravelly whisper. "I will not have my child grow up knowing their father is a coward. We owe our lives to House Wight. Have you forgotten the vow we made?"

Mara squeezed her eyes shut.

"Have you forgotten the Gray Flood?" Dalgor demanded, his grip tightening slightly. "When the river banks burst and washed away half this village. The capital did nothing. The Lady of House Wight came herself. She stood ankle-deep in the mud, filling ladles of soup for the starving. The Lord brought his own guards to repair the dam and rebuild the collapsed roofs. When our crops rotted beneath the sludge, he distributed seeds from his own private stores for the next harvest."

He stepped back, picking up the heavy breastplate. He hauled it over his head, the metal settling onto his shoulders with a familiar, comforting weight.

"We would be dead," Dalgor stated, staring at his reflection in a cracked mirror. "The winter would have taken us long ago if not for them. And now? Alarion is married to Lady Nyxia. He unites the Sword and the Shadow. He is the rightful King of Aerthos. He is our first true King."

Mara bit her lower lip, tasting blood.

"That boy," Dalgor continued, strapping a worn scabbard to his waist, "holds the very best of them. We were conquered. We paid taxes to the enemy. We kept our heads down. But when we starved, he still fed us. He rained bread from the sky while Theron took our last sacks of grain. I refuse to turn my back on him."

Mara didn't argue anymore. She closed the distance and hugged him, burying her face in the hard steel of the lion crest. She squeezed him tight. She knew this was the last time she would smell the woodsmoke in his hair. He was marching to his death.

The mud sucked at the boots of the Cinderfall scouts.

They crested the final ridge overlooking the valley. The freezing wind whipped the grey ash into their eyes. They raised their spyglasses, expecting to see the massed, glittering ranks of the enemy army. They expected banners fluttering in the wind, knights sitting atop armored warhorses, and the imposing, lumbering wooden frames of magical siege towers.

They saw none of that.

The valley floor was an ugly, scarred expanse of churned dirt. The enemy had dug deep, ugly trenches into the earth, reinforcing the mud walls with stamped metal plating. Instead of spear-walls, strange, cylindrical metal structures poked out from behind berms of dirt. Massive, snub-nosed domes sat squat on solid bases. Thick coils of razor wire glistened with cruel, jagged barbs, forming a metallic briar patch across the open plains.

No soldiers stood in the open. The entire valley looked deserted, save for the silent, unmoving machines.

The scouts swept their spyglasses further back, toward the shoreline. They blinked, rubbing their eyes and looking again.

Two massive, skeletal structures were rising from the earth. The framework stretched for miles, a chaotic tangle of steel scaffolding reaching toward the sky. It looked like the enemy was trying to build two separate fortresses, side-by-side, in the middle of a warzone.

Worse yet, the scouts saw the massive, black hulls of two enemy ships resting firmly on the shore. They were beached. Destroyed.

The scouts reported back to the vanguard commanders.

A heavy-set Cinderfall general laughed, a booming, arrogant sound that echoed through the command tent. He tossed the scout's sketch of the trenches and the beached ships onto a wooden table.

"They dig holes like rats," the general scoffed. "Instead of facing us in a glorious head-on battle, these people choose to bury themselves in the dirt. They hide."

Another commander, polishing his ornate golden gauntlet, smirked. "And look at their ships. They crashed their own navy into the rocks. They are desperate. They are cannibalizing their own vessels to build fortresses right next to each other. What kind of tactical blunder is that? A single concentrated barrage from our mages will wipe out both structures at once."

A younger captain chimed in. "This region experiences sudden, violent rainfall. Perhaps they intend to drown in their own hiding holes. It will save us the trouble of executing them."

The tent erupted in derisive laughter. They were an army of fifty million. They possessed the Mother Phoenix. A few metal tubes, some beached wrecks, and dirt walls were a pathetic joke.

King Theron Flavius sat at the head of the table. He did not laugh.

He stared at the crude sketch of the razor wire, the squat, domed turrets, and the massive skeletal structures rising on the shore. The gnawing dread in his gut twisted tighter, a physical pain that made his jaw ache.

The boy who built ships that flew did not crash his own navy by accident. The boy who annihilated a coastline in minutes did not dig holes to hide.

Theron looked at the oblivious, arrogant faces of his commanders. They saw cowardice. Theron saw a slaughterhouse. The kid wasn't stupid.

They were stepping directly into a trap.
