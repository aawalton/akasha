Ash was already gone when I woke.

Light found me past the unshuttered windows. I rose slowly, and my body felt rested. It felt strong -my mana did not. More of this world's thin supply had filtered into my core overnight, and now my mana core was leaking like a damaged vessel. There were ways to fix that, ones I had never needed to use. My stomach growled.

My armor wasn't there -the Darkness Binding was gone. Something sharp rose in my chest. Who would dare-

The door swung open. "You're up, dear? Ash said you might sleep in late. Come now, you must be starving." Martha said, peering in through the door.

"Where is my armor?" I demanded.

"Oh, that old thing. I set it aside for a wash. Starting to smell, it was." She gestured to a corner. "Left you something else. That fancy thing ain't going nowhere."

She wasn't lying, I could tell in her face now. The Darkness Binding had never needed cleaning before, but it was less than it had been. I stifled the emotion that came. "What payment do you require for this service?"

"None at all, dear." Then she chuckled and turned away. "Come now. You must be starving."

My stomach answered before I could. Even I had no choice but to follow, after I got dressed.

It was a simple dress -brown and faded. Next to it, there was a pair of worn leather shoes. This was what the woman expected me to wear? I held the dress up. It was too short by a hand's width, at least.

The fabric was coarse under my fingers, which told me enough about its make. I put it on. Or rather, I attempted to.

The neckline caught on my left horn. I tried to tug at it and force the horn through, but the cloth would not obey. I tried to tug harder, and felt the moment the cloth almost tore entirely. I stopped, and tried again. This time, I put the fabric on far more carefully than I was used to. The left horn finally went through after I tilted my head sideways. Then I attempted the right horn. It caught in almost the exact same way, because of course it did. By the time the dress fell past my shoulders, my hands were almost shaking.

But I was wearing the strange clothing now. This 'dress' did not sit right on me. It was far too wide at the waist, while being too tight across the shoulders. And it was much, much too tight across the chest. I inspected my reflection in the window glass. A stranger stared back at me. I did not look long before I left the room.

The stew was already waiting for me at the table. I sat at its head, as was proper. The first bowl was gone before Martha had finished sitting down. She gave me the second before I asked. I ate four bowls and each tasted less extraordinary than the first, but each was still among the finest things I had ever tasted.

"Dear," Martha said gently. "That's your fourth."

I did not respond. The woman hovered, and she touched my shoulder when she passed behind my chair, and she refilled my water without being asked. I did not know what to do with any of it.

"Where is Ash?"

"Oh, your friend's out helping around. She sees something that needs fixing and throws herself at it." Martha shook her head, smiling. "Somewhere close, I'd wager."

Friend?

All I had to do was join the small crowd that had formed in one corner of the village. I found the Hero.

Ash was dressed in a similar rag to myself, hers more pink in color. As I watched, she brought down a small axe over a circular piece of wood lying on a tree trunk. The axe cut through the wood cleanly, splitting it into two equal halves. Ash was splitting logs, using one strike to split each. The pile beside her was already taller than the child handing her the next piece.

The crowd whispered. I caught only small fragments of their whispering.

'…doesn't even see her mark…'

'…must be a Sigil at least.'

I took note of those words. Then I took note of the way Ash had smiled at the child, and at some of the people watching. As if splitting wood was something worth smiling about. Ash rose and wiped sweat from her brow. She scanned the crowd and seemed to hunch in on herself as she did. Then, her eyes met mine.

"You're awake." She waved.

I moved through the crowd. "Do tell me this isn't what I believe it is."

"Huh?" She looked at me, then down at the log. "Oh, I'm just helping out a bit." Then her head tilted. I got the impression she was scanning my dress. It was no more shabby than her own.

"You should try it." Ash held out a second axe from where it rested against the trunk. "The work goes faster with two."

"You think I would use these hands to split wood?" I turned and walked past the crowd, to the edge of the village.

There was a patch of grass near the wall where I could see most of Hamel and where nobody seemed to go. Perfect.

There were things these hands were made for. I intended to prove they still could. I did not trust those meager walls, especially not against the kinds of beasts I had encountered in the forest. I sat down on a patch of grass and crossed my legs. It was undignified to do this on anything less than a proper dais, but I doubted I would find one of those in these savage lands.

I closed my eyes and reached outward with my mana. A thousand invisible fingers, touching the world all at once.

The mana here was thin, as thin as it had been in the forest, but thinness was not absence. It could be compressed and refined. I had techniques for this -twenty breathing methods. Every single such ability the System had, some so rare only a handful of people had ever found them. Well, before I had found them and forced out the knowledge, at any rate.

If the Hero could make fire without her skills, why couldn't I do this? Other races had fancy names for what I was doing. Some called it cultivation. Some called it purification. Some called it cycling. It depended on what their System told them.

Some fools thought themselves poets. Zarvok had said it was clashing darkness against itself, till from its clash sprang blessed light.

I had not minded that eccentricity of his.

I began with the simplest method and drew the ambient mana in through my skin, even as I tried to compress it against itself within my core. The mana refused to take form. It did not work.

"Excuse me...miss?"

My concentration shattered, and I opened my eyes. A man stood a few paces away -he was young, holding a rake. He had the look of someone who had drawn the shortest straw. "We're clearing the north path. Could use another pair of-"

"No."

He blinked. "Right. Sorry to-"

"Leave."

He left, and I closed my eyes again. The mana I had gathered had already dispersed, not that my attempt had been working. I started over.

The second technique was harsher. It churned the mana instead of compressing it. This technique didn't take -the mana swirled and then went still. The third technique -threading- did not work either. I tried the fourth, layering, and had no more luck with that than I had with anything else.

I opened my eyes and drove my fist into the ground. The earth gave way easily, and my knuckles did not hurt. At least that much still worked. I closed my eyes again.

A while later, there was laughter -shrill and getting closer.

I opened my eyes to find three children chasing each other past me. My gaze only lingered on the one in front. He ran on tiny platforms of silver light. The platforms wobbled beneath him, yet he ran anyway. Another one behind him sprayed a thin trickle of water from his palm. The marks on their arms glowed.

They were mere children. And they were using power I did not have.

The third child stopped and stared at me. She had dirty black hair and a runny nose. "Why are you sitting like that?" she asked. "All alone? Are you ok?"

"I am doing something beyond your comprehension, child." I said, keeping my tone as neutral as I could manage.

She considered this. "You look angry."

"Leave."

The girl ran off after the others, and I watched her go. The silver platforms all winked out one after another. Even children could use this power, while I sat here and failed.

I took a deep breath and regained my focus. I attempted the ninth technique. The tenth. The eleventh. The twelfth. My execution of all of them was perfect, and yet they all failed. In the exact same way. The mana simply refused to move as I wished. There was a small gap there, somewhere. A small gap between my understanding and my ability to do what I wished. It was all the more infuriating for how small it was.

I heard footsteps again, and braced myself for yet another interruption.

"Thought you might want something, dear." The woman hesitated briefly, and then lowered herself. She set down a bowl beside me. I did not need to look to know what it was. It was more stew.

"I did not ask for this."

"Suppose ya didn't, but you don't have to ask to eat," She straightened, brushed off her knees. She paused at my side. Perhaps she had finally noticed the three holes in the ground. "Eat when you're ready." She left.

I refused to look at the bowl, or touch it. For ten minutes, I did neither of those things. Then I looked, and then I ate. I ate standing, making sure that no one would see. The food was still warm.

I had only one technique left. I had saved this technique because it brought immense pain. Each breath felt like driving needles into one's own lungs. I did it anyway. The mana twisted and I felt it almost catch. Then it slipped, as all of the other techniques had. My hands were shaking, and there were five holes in the ground now.

I, Lysanthia, the Strongest-

I stopped and did not finish the thought.

There might not be chains in this world, but there would be dangers. Dangers I could not defeat even now, let alone when more of my mana weakened. To not be powerful was the same as being helpless.

"And…" I raised my hand and looked at it.

I tried a spell. It was not a weapon. Even calling it a technique was generous. The spell was a petty thing that made golden patterns of light that twisted in the air. I had watched them sometimes, when nobody was looking. It should have come at a thought. My hand closed around empty air.

I had no power, and there was now only one way to get it. I had not wanted to look upon it, but it had been impossible to miss each time I'd opened my eyes.

Those marks.

Everywhere I looked, their marks glowed with power I could not touch. How infuriating it was to sit here and see power with no way to access it. It was a pitiful display compared to what I had once wielded, and yet it was far more than what I had now.

I did not yet know if they were just another kind of chain. I only knew that I needed this First Ink ceremony. That was what that blathering oaf had called it, was it not?

"There you are."

I paused and turned. I had not seen Ash. She approached me from the side, a gaggle of kids behind her. One of them was climbing on top of her. The black-haired girl from earlier hung at the back, halfhidden.

"You have taken strays?"

Ash frowned and sighed. "Go on. You can do this," Ash said.

I frowned. Ash stepped aside, and from behind her stepped forward the girl from before. I looked at her more discerningly this time. She had dirty black hair, puffy cheeks, and a runny nose. She was holding her arms behind her. The girl looked down at the ground as I stared at her. Ash nudged her back with her leg.

She held out a vase. That was generous -it was a lump of dried mud, with a crack along one side filled in with more mud.

"She made it a little while ago," Ash said quietly. "For you."

"Why?"

The girl's feet made a small circle on the ground. "P-pretty."

The word stirred something. She was complimenting my beauty, which even in this form was something worth complimenting...yet never had I heard it said so.

I reached for it. My hands -the same hands that could no longer even hold onto mana- closed around the rough mud. It was light as I turned it over and examined it. It was the ugliest thing I had ever held. The base was uneven, and the proportions were wrong. These were facts.

"This is adequate," I said.

The girl's face scrunched, and she looked up at Ash. "She means she likes it," Ash translated.

"That is not what I said."

"Lily-"

"It is adequate. That is what I said, and that is what I meant. It is not poorly made, for something constructed by a child from dirt."

The girl's face was crumbling in what was clearly disappointment. Why? I had accepted her offering. I had accepted it just as I had accepted so many others. I kept talking, for the silence had always been uncomfortable. Perhaps she wanted a more accurate assessment of what she had given me. "The structural integrity could certainly be improved. See here? It is uneven, but for a first attempt-"

A tear slid down the girl's right eye. I still did not understand. I had given an honest assessment. No, that was not the half of it. My assessment had been generous. There had been no insult. In fact, I had not even mentioned the lopsided rim, or the fact that one could not actually put water in it without it leaking through the base.

"-it is fine," I amended, though I did not know what I was amending. "I will keep it."

The girl sniffled and rubbed her nose with her sleeve. She looked at Ash again.

"See?" Ash said gently. "She's keeping it. That means she-"

"Why are you crying?" I demanded, leaning forward. "I accepted your gift. What more do you want from me?"

The girl looked at me and took a step back. Her eyes were wide. My grip shifted, and I felt it slip. The mud was rough against skin still slick from the earth I'd been punching all afternoon.

I reached for it. I caught it. I almost caught it. The vase hit the ground and broke. The sound was so small, barely a crack, hardly anything at all. The girl stared at the pieces, and her lip trembled.

"It was already cracked," I said quickly. "It was-"

She turned and ran, shoving past Ash's legs, past the other children. The girl was crying. Her wailing could not have echoed, and yet I heard it do just that all the same. I stared at the broken pieces on the ground.

"I did not-"

"Don't." Ash's voice was quiet.

I looked up. The other children had fled, and now there was nobody watching. "She spent all morning making that," Ash said. "She asked three times if it was good enough." There was fury in her gaze, almost as much fury as she'd had in that throne room, not so long ago. "Her name is Sara."

Ash turned and walked after the girl. I watched her go.

The fury came first. It always did. I had tried. I had taken the thing. I had called it adequate. From my lips, that was-

That was what, exactly?

I looked down at my hands. There was mud on them, from the vase or from the holes I'd beaten into the ground. I could not tell which.

I could not tell which.

The fury rose, and rose. And as quickly as it rose, it cracked, somewhere right down the middle. What rose through it was sharper, and it sat behind my ribs and would not move. I knelt and reached for the broken pieces.

My hands tried to join them. They tried to remake the vase. These hands had once been enough to wipe out kingdoms. A race. Even two Gods. They could not bring these pieces together. They, and I, did not know how.

"Damn it."
