Redberry watched in amazement, enraptured by what she was seeing. The giant oaf, Zadkiel, shifted into a short, small Naugite, blue-skinned, etched flagrantly with authentic red tattoos of some demonkin homeland or another. He had long pointed ears and was almost feral-looking… in a cute way.

Incredible… he is like an imp.

Slowly, Redberry rose, inspecting the new form judiciously in fascination. Even more satisfying than this humble revelation, Zadkiel had his eyes adorably downcast, embarrassed in the extreme by all the things he was admitting, reluctant to expose his greatest vulnerability.

But he chooses to submit it to me.

Powerful impulses lit inside her with burning intensity. Seize; strike! But she kept her cool… let it stew. She walked close, standing over him now, and exhaled with a note of amusement. “All this time, you were hiding this deep within. It certainly explains your gremlin-like tendencies — and your spellcasting acumen. You were a wizard?”

Zadkiel nodded, his expression subdued, and still not meeting her eyes. “It is difficult not to be when born into the spellblood. The natural blue skin.” He looked down and held up his arm, exposing more of it, and the curving tattoos. “You know it's natural by the pink undertones. Compared to the likes of Azure, for example. The tattoos were paid for in my youth, to reinforce and protect me in my vulnerability.”

Redberry listened intently, fascinated by his changed voice, now light and smooth and simply stating things plainly without a constant ‘puffed-out chest’ element. It was obvious what the giant form was to him: the ideal Demon King, huge and strong and larger than life. He had to keep that face forward toward the world, to display and to enforce his will as a deity. She understood that well.

She reached out and took his arm, to which he adorably tensed and stiffened. But when she began running her hands over the tattoos, he relaxed, even shivered at her touch, watching them with wide eyes. “I see scars, too. I feel… anguish paid out in this form.”

Zadkiel swallowed hard. “In my homeland, Kalfaust, they persecuted those of the spellblood at the time I was born. I should’ve been killed as an infant, but my mother refused to do so. She hid me away and eventually arranged for me to be dyed red.”

“Mmn… your mother truly loved you, and so you are here. Is it still this way in Kalfaust and other lands?”

“Some places in the south, perhaps. Not in Kalfaust. When I came into power, I conquered it and had the old priests torn limb from limb, their temples razed to the ground, and all literature so much as hinting at old, despicable superstitions annihilated. In lands under my control, they are officially celebrated as rare, talented people worthy of more than ostracization. The public is still adjusting, and bias remains in most places.”

Redberry listened, intrigued. Zadkiel was still looking off, so she moved her hand under his chin, directing him to meet her eyes. Reluctantly, he did so, and she smiled warmly at him. “I’m pleased by this. By all of it. Thank you for sharing this with me, Zadkiel. Rest assured, I understand the power over you this gives me, and I will keep the secrets of your origin solemnly.”

He nodded and swallowed hard again. “Wh-what do you thi-”

“I like it,” she interrupted, knowing she was answering his question. “I like this form, I mean.” She brushed a finger over his cheek, then trailed it over his ear. It twitched, and he flinched in obvious sensitivity. She laughed. He responds just like an elf! “It explains a great deal to me that was off, that was mysterious and unexplained about you… Such an exposure took guts, to put it plainly, in a way I find especially appealing. It intrigues me, and I’d like to explore more of these hidden depths you’ve teased.”

The impish figure beamed brightly in surprise and elation. “Really?! I’d be happy to-”

“Shh-shh-shh,” Redberry interrupted, putting her finger directly to his lips to quiet him. His wide-eyed reaction was priceless. “Later, little imp.” Her other hand undid his robe’s belt in a swift, practiced motion. “I have other designs, first, on new, supple prey at my fangs.” She pushed Zadkiel down onto the couch — this particular form gave way easily, as he sputtered helplessly and plopped backward onto his rear. “But it won’t be supple for long, now, will it?”

This makes me feel young again.

Grinning briefly, devilishly, as she loomed over her prey, Redberry promptly pounced on him.

Shortly after the wedding, the business of the Samanthian realm went back to full tilt and intensity. The warfront was a tedious, plodding affair of slowly expanding and securing territory that the Dominion went ‘scorched earth’ on, doing their damnedest to deprive the oncoming, hungry monster of food. It didn’t really work, but it slowed things down, and it made the whole affair much more miserable for the populace. Naturally, the Dominion laid all the blame squarely on the Witch Queen and her ‘demonic’ allies. From a certain point of view, that was true. Nothing could erase the fact that they were the invaders.

Regardless, the Dominion forces were pulling all of their resources and people continuously back toward the capital, leaving behind relative annoyances in skeleton crews, spells, traps, and saboteurs. Increasingly, they were making use of things like animals, summons, undead, and cheap constructs for everything from bomb delivery to actual defense and occupation.

They were great at terrorism. It was more and more difficult to move forward without tiptoeing around their functional landmines hidden in every pile. They unavoidably caused petty attrition and casualties daily, and no territory could be taken without exhausting resources, ensuring it was safe. They were punished for their dedication to the sanctity of life, essentially.

Samantha spent more of her time helping generically, here, now unafraid of the Dominion’s threat to her person. Whatever special contrivances they might have brewing, feasible or not, they would save them all for the final siege, she was sure. The small fraction of effort and resources they put into slowing down the advance pointed to this. They were still cowards, content to play the survival game, afraid to risk more at this or that fortress or city.

In truth, she couldn’t blame them — they’d just lose again. If Matrazere didn’t rebuff her, no fortress between there and the Capital would.

Primarily, Samantha helped by analyzing and predicting trouble, and sometimes physically being present to detect and/or disable hidden traps, which she was among the best in the very thinly-stretched army at. She didn’t need to tell herself this, either — Dax constantly logged requests to Jeeves in this regard, and ‘mysteriously’ more requests piled up every time Samantha got the latest batch finished.

The consequences of the Dominion’s strategy over the long-term were heinous. It was not a small empire, and hundreds of thousands, perhaps millions, were flushed out of their lands and homes. They forced the hungry into conscription as the only way to survive personally or for their family, and then a portion were squirreled away to the equivalent of medieval black sites to secretly turn them into more durable undead. With the lockdown of propaganda and a populace well-indoctrinated against trusting any form of supernatural entities, the majority could do little more than accept the dark fate handed to them.

Try as the Dominion might, shrewd, lucky, and capable people slipped continuously through the cracks. Some hid themselves away in the wilderness, effectively ‘heading for the hills,’ some managed to flee all the way to Burnstire, some fled right into the Alliance army’s arms, begging for mercy with the apparent expectation of none, and some came with sober protestations of allegiance and pledged themselves to the war effort on the spot. These weren’t the majority, however…

Soul-crushing as it was, the alliance kept pushing forward on the march, taking territory between Matrazere and the Capital, something like a big crescent curling toward the former. They took the spookily-abandoned great city of Govend, halfway to the Capital, fighting continuously and laboriously through it, dispelling or triggering traps, summons, undead, common clay golems, exploding birds, exploding buildings… The list went on and on, but they secured it after an entire day of effort, and blew up the fortress rather than risk whatever madness was inside. They would simply create a new one, on a better foundation.

So it went. Samantha rather looked forward to the coming day when they would take the city of Eltridge, the place she’d staged the daring escape of Ash out of remotely, via mirror shenanigans. It was only a couple of days' travel from the Capital, and it was more due south from the Capital than the ‘High Road’ that marked the direct route from Matrazere, so it would likely be a while. Things would only get more difficult and grueling after Govend — that was the consensus of the Brain Trust.

Meanwhile, they were constantly losing forward troops, energy, and resources in keeping their territories south, constantly under terrorist threat and attack. Regardless of whether they ‘needed’ to take the less responsible position of leaving them to their own chaos, they could not allow the Dominion a foothold like that, because they’d absolutely take advantage and cause even more damage, namely to supply lines. It just was what it was.
