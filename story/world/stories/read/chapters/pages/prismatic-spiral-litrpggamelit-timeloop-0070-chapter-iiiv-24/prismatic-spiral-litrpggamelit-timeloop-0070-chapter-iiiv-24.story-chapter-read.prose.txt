Chapter II.IV (2.4)

“Mitsuko!” Holly burst into their small cabin aboard The Selcouth Sable, the sudden interruption jerking Mitsuko’s attention up from her current book.

“What’s wrong?” She snapped the book closed and prepared to enter a fight. But then her gnome friend just grinned at her.

“I found one of the residents of Porcelain Island. And you’ll never guess what! They’re not bone people at all like I first thought.”

“You assumed they were… bone people.” Mitsuko eyed her friend.

“Well, yeah. I heard they weren’t human but also not undead. I thought maybe they were some sort of humanoid who wore their skeletons on their exterior. But they’re nothing like that!”

“Well, what are they like?”

“Follow me and I’ll show you!”

Mitsuko set her book to the side, next to Holly’s piles of maps and cartography supplies. She’d been reading up more about the history of the archipelago in hopes to uncover more information about the sages and guardians. So far, Mitsuko hadn’t found anything particularly useful. Most of what she’d uncovered at the docks had been copies of ancient shipping records. Not history tomes detailing the nuances of the relations between island civilizations. Though trading records did still serve a use. Understanding the imports and exports certainly helped her establish a bit of foundational knowledge about the islands and their trading partners.

Ashen Island exported a unique charcoal that burned for hours and the lumber there was known to be the sturdiest in the archipelago. Mauve Island had orchards and gardens. A lot of unique fruits and vegetables that came in shades of purple, as well as just colored normal fruit. It seemed that something about the island turned everything grown there a violet shade, regardless of its origins. Verdant Island had cocoa beans, green bananas, and a special type of algae exported worldwide. All of those islands Mitsuko already had a decent understanding of. Nothing about those shipping records shocked her. Though there was an odd amount of fishing exported from Verdant Island for the harbor size. But there might very well be off-shore boats moored off the coast.

The other two islands were different though. Amber Island was known as the wizard island and it had weird exports. They changed dramatically over time. One year they’d be shipping out massive amounts of enchanted golden chains and then the next it would be all about artificially grown beef. And it was never just one thing either, instead always a scattershot of random exports. As far as Mitsuko could tell, it really just depended on the whims of the wizards in charge at the time.

Porcelain Island exported very little. A few fish and seal pelts. The most valuable thing to come from the island was the occasional juhyo pelt. The little snow monsters were famous for their ability to keep themselves at a temperature below freezing. While normal freezing enchantments on ice boxes took care of most people’s needs, juhyo pelts could be formed in bags easier. A niche, but valuable product.

The book’s information helped her build a foundation of knowledge about the archipelago. There was genuinely valuable information buried in the dry ledgers for anyone willing to dig for it.

But, of course, she found no records of the fabled Azure Island. When she asked locals about it, all she got were blank stares or cagey deflections.

Holly led Mitsuko off The Selcouth Sable and onto Crocus City. She gabbed as she walked. Mostly about the date she’d been on earlier that day. Mitsuko had heard a variation of this date half a dozen times by this point.

“And the gnome community stretches all the way down to the docks!” she exclaimed, entering into new territory for the conversation. “So I thought, why not ask him about Mitsuko’s problems. See if any of the local gnomes know about the other islands. Any sort of network beyond Mauve.”

“You uncovered something?” Mitsuko asked.

“He told me about this pub!” Holly gestured her arms forward at a stairway down into the basement of a sagging building.

“You’re certain this is a pub?” Mitsuko craned her neck around, searching for some sort of signage.

As she glanced behind her, she spotted Juniper on a post by the docks, preening her feathers. She knew it was Juniper, not a random seagull, because her eyes glinted an eerie brown in the sunlight. Surprisingly though, the sage was mostly keeping to herself so far this loop. Likely attempting to salvage their relationship and not come off as too overbearing after her confrontation with Sterling last loop.

“This is definitely the right place,” Holly said. She trotted down the steps and threw open the heavy metal door.

A gust of frigid wind erupted out of the room below. Mitsuko took a step backwards, caught off guard by the chilly assault on an otherwise warm, sunny day.

Mitsuko cast Mend on herself to reset her temperature, but the cold air only hit her again, just as strong as the first time. Unless she somehow figured out to put herself in a perpetual state of the moment before opening the door, it would only make the experience worse as the cold juxtaposed with her warmth, not allowing her to get used to the chill.

Actually…she knew of a spell that could accomplish exactly that effect, keeping something perpetually frozen in time. The only temporal spell she really knew of before getting thrown into the Prismatic Spiral nonsense. Stasis. It could be created using potions using magical materials, but the resources required were both expensive and rare. Would she be able to cast the spell naturally? Was that one of her future spells? Something to consider.

For now, Mitsuko stepped through the doorway and snow crunched under her boots.

A dull blue glow emitted from the torches on the walls. Unlike natural fire, the closer Mitsuko drew to the light, the colder the temperature dropped. Icicles hung from the ceiling above, reflecting the blue light across the bar.

A few of the patrons looked up and gave her and Holly quizzical looks as they strolled into the pub. And that was only natural, since they looked utterly out of place amongst the bar’s clientele.

Every person in the tavern was made of ice and snow. They were structured the same as Mitsuko, albeit half a meter taller, but not one of them had a single bit of flesh and blood on them. Even their hair was crafted, solid pieces of snow shaded differently from their heads rather than individual strands like a human. They all wore clothing crafted from a white fur that fluffed like snow. Mitsuko had never seen a juhyo pelt in person, but she was willing to bet that was the material. Something to help keep them chilled even while outdoors in the streets of a city in the tropics.

At first, Mitsuko thought they might be golems like her friend Wan. But after a moment of consideration, she struck that off as a possibility. Wan was a unique artifact of the World Dungeon. Every other golem she’d encountered in her travels was incredibly limited in its mannerisms and none of them, not even Wan, ever bothered with something as trivial as drinking at a tavern.

“Welcome,” the bartender greeted them. His voice was surprisingly warm for a man made of snow. Though his eyes were glassy shards of ice as he examined them. “Please, take a seat. Not often I get a pair of fleshies. Name’s Nick.”

“Appreciate the kind welcome.” Mitsuko helped boost Holly onto a barstool before sitting beside her friend.

“Think nothing of it. Now, can I get you something to drink? We have snacks as well. Have you ever tasted shattered raspberry ice before?”

“I haven’t. Is there an alcohol version? Maybe something like a wine variety?”

Nick, the bartender grinned, showing off a set of perfect, oddly human, pearly white teeth. “A woman after my own heart. I’ll get you a plate.”

“Mmm—Mmm—Mitsu—tsuko,” Holly said through chattering teeth. “I—I th—think th—this was a mmm—mmm—mmmistake.”

Holly’s gnome body was not built for the cold. Its small size lost heat a great deal swifter than a human’s and she didn’t have very many layers on. Of fat or fabric.

“You can go wait outside,” Mitsuko suggested. “I’ll ask around and gather info. You won’t be able to say much as you are right now anyway.”

“Ask around?” The bartender returned, a platter of shattered ice in hand. He set it in front of her. “Does this mean you didn’t come down here dressed in summer garb just for my company?”

“Unfortunately not,” Mitsuko admitted. She picked up a shard of ice and bit it. It was delightfully sweet as it melted in her mouth. Holly would love it, if she wasn’t freezing to death. “I wanted to ask about portage to Porcelain Island. I don’t suppose you know anything about how to chart a course there?”

“It’s hardly a secret.” Nick gestured at the pub’s other patrons. “Everyone here is a sailor who travels between here and there. They make the trip every day.”

“Well, since they’re all down here, I couldn’t find any out there.” Mitsuko pointed her thumb over her shoulder at the pub’s entrance.

Nick laughed. “A good enough point I suppose. We sculpts don’t mingle too often out in the open sun. Even with the proper gear, it’s as uncomfortable for us with you as it is with you here.”

Sculpts. Mitsuko had heard the term before. In fact, she’d even seen one of these people, now that she thought back on it. Captain Alina on The Terror had tortured and killed one of these species on her deck using a heating stone and Mitsuko had witnessed the act through Retrospection. Probably best to not bring that up.

“So it won’t cost much to get a map over to your island?”

“Not much.”

“Mmm—mmmap. P—p—please,” Holly requested. Her teeth were threatening to crack with how aggressively they were chattering together.

Nick leaned forward, an arm against the bar. “Let’s jump to this quickly before frostbite takes your pal’s nips. You have money?”

Mitsuko paid Nick using the doubloons the archipelago used as currency. The sculpt slipped them into his coinpurse without even a second glance.

“One sec. I’ll go see what I can scavenge up from the back.”

Mitsuko felt a pit of dread form in her stomach. This man was going to run off with her money as soon as he was out of sight, wasn’t he?

But no, to her utter shock, Nick returned a minute later with a piece of weathered parchment in hand.

“Here,” he passed it to her. It was barely more than a scrap, but it did have the outlines of islands with a compass at the bottom depicting north. “These lines here show the iceflow. Careful around them. Lots of ice just beneath the surface and those bergs can rip through a ship’s hull. What you see above water is nothing compared to what lies beneath.”

“For icebergs, it’s ten percent above, right?” Mitsuko recalled the statistic from her studies in Hon.

“Depends on how much rock and silt is weighing down the ice. Sometimes you see nothing at all. Which is why you should go around the ice flows. We’ve got some wicked currents flowing nearby.”

“Got it. Thanks.” Mitsuko folded the parchment and stuck it in a pocket. “Any other advice you’d be willing to part with?”

Nick picked up one of her wine shards and tossed it in his mouth. It crunched as he chewed, considering.

“Sculpts aren’t used to fleshies stopping by our island. There’s only one inn with your kind in mind. So dress warmly.” Then his friendly face hardened. “Finally, we have a single taboo on Porcelain Island. Break it and die.”

“And that is?” Mitsuko asked.

“No fire.”
