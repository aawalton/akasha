Levi cursed his luck because it was an unspoken rule, even in his old town gantus, to avoid using skills during tavern fights as the damage that could be done to the surrounding buildings and people was unpredictable and Levi knew that if he decided to use his void tier, it would definitely raise some eyebrows and put a few collectors on alert for him, as he was the only one he knew with a diamond-shaped portal as all the others he’d heard of were circular.

On the other hand, while the Abyss ball wouldn't cause any physical damage, it was still rather unique, and Levi would rather keep that trick up his sleeve in case he couldn't handle the mob headed for them.

His eyes quickly scanned the area, taking note of the two sailors that blocked the exit out of the tavern with three more that were charging towards him, and the captain himself, who was almost within touching distance of Levi's face.

Luckily for Levi and Xara, they were the only ones sober who were going to be involved in this fight, as the captain and his men were all obviously drunk on ale. And if there was anything Levi was most proud of, with or without the system, it was his footwork and hand-to-eye coordination, which he believed to be elite in his little town, and maybe even here.

Levi dodged the wild haymaker thrown at his face by the captain, his mind already whirring as he began to put together a plan to get both him and the mage out of the tavern while avoiding using their skills in order not to attract the guards of Planthrith into what was supposed to be a regular tavern fight.

Although that seemed like it would be a lot harder than it had to be, as Levi feared the mage would be much of a liability in this fight, because if she did decide to use a fireball here, it could burn the place down. Or even worse, she could throw a fire disc or shoot her Xistol at any of the drunk patrons, which would certainly kill or badly injure them in ways his daggers could not.

So far, he hadn’t seen the mage in hand-to-hand combat, and he highly doubted that she was competent in that aspect, which meant that the mage was going to fight the only way she knew how to, and Levi wasn’t comfortable with that, as all of Xara’s attacks could kill with the slightest contact.

And if the mage used her magic in this cramped space, then they were sure to go from visitors of Planthrith into fugitives, with the possibility that the knowledge of Xara’s Xistol would spread if she used the weapon, which meant Levi had a limited amount of time to handle the situation before it blew up in proportions that he wasn't quite sure he could handle.

This is going to be a sticky one. Levi thought, as he spun around and elbowed the already off-balanced captain in the forehead, knocking him out cold, his focus already switching to the other three headed towards him before the captain's body dropped to the floor, unmoving.

“Gentlemen, I am sure you all want to fight, but your captain, as you can see, will no longer be participating in this fight. And with that in mind, I’d like to offer you all a free gold to share. It should be enough to buy enough rounds of drinks that will have you all pissing ale by the end of the night.” Levi rolled the coin effortlessly across his knuckles. “Now I'm willing to give you this coin in exchange for letting me and my partner leave here unharmed. No blood has to be shed, and you can take care of your captain.”

The men seemed to stop in their tracks, each sharing a look with the others before they burst out laughing. “Ye hear that, lads? The kid says he has a gold coin for us, and we'll be pissing ale by the end of the night. What does breaker Amon think?”

The sailor who had spoken turned to a huge brute who had been sitting alone at a table behind the three men, his table cluttered with an unhealthy amount of empty ale, his eyes normal yet devoid of any emotion as he stared at the rogue.

Silence regained its hold over the tavern as the Breaker, Amon, stood up from his chair. His gait was surprisingly steady for someone who had emptied all those mugs of ale that Levi had seen on the table.

“I don't care about any gold coin. I just want to massage my knuckles with your face and hear you scream until the only thing you can do is wheeze in pain,” the breaker said as he forced the three sailors to make way as he made his way to Levi. “No amount of coin you have is worth missing out on a valid chance to let out all my pain and anger. And best believe, boy, I have a lot of anger to let out.”

“Uh, Levi, I think we should run,” Xara whispered.

“Agreed,” Levi whispered back.

He knew who breakers were, because he had seen a few of them around Killian’s, and he knew for a fact that breakers were usually the most depraved of men and women who enjoyed torturing people.

One look at the hulking brute with bulging muscles was enough to help Levi come to the sane conclusion that there was no way he was going to win this fight without using his skills. And if he did that, he risked lighting the fuse that would lead every other person to do the same.

Which meant they would probably bring down the rickety building that was the Sailors' Maiden, which, in turn, guaranteed they would become fugitives. It seemed like their options here were between fighting and becoming fugitives, possibly dying here, or somehow managing to escape with their limbs and ribs intact. Levi knew which of the three he preferred.

“Okay, it's not about the money, is it?” Levi said as he scanned around, looking for anything that could either help them stay and fight or escape the tavern.

His eyes drifted from patron to patron, and all of those who were not affiliated with the sailors seemed to be minding their drinks, with their eyes occasionally drifting to the fight about to begin.

It was clear that a blanket of overwhelming silence covered the crowd as they waited with bated breath for what was sure to come. Levi pushed the observation aside as his eyes continued to scan the crowd until he spotted the pickpockets from earlier.

The male pickpocket signaled to the door, which was blocked by two of the Captain's men. All right, it seems like we might have a way.

“I might’ve just made some friends that might help us get out of here,” Levi briefly tuned and whispered to the mage. “When I give you the signal, run through the exit of the tavern and do not stop for anything, not even me.”

Xara seemed ready to protest his words, but Levi had already refocused on the breaker, with his rogues bared in the brute's direction, who had been staring at Levi with a menacing smile on his face.”Let's get it on!”

The breaker moved a lot faster than Levi would have expected for a man of his size and sobriety level as the breaker's closed fist came down towards Levi's face like a flash of lightning. But the rogue was faster. Levi ducked under the blow and slashed the side of the breaker's ribs, his attack eliciting a barely audible grunt as the attack drew blood.

Fortunately for the breaker, Levi's attack barely did any damage as the rogue had only intended for it to be a warning strike to the breaker.

“Now,” Levi said as he went past the breaker, drawing all the attention to him, which created the space for Xara to sneak past them.

The pickpockets seemed to have caught on to his plan as they were now both headed for the door, which meant that Levi had to play his part by being bait, and he could only hope that he would escape the tavern with all his bits intact.

“Get over here!” The breaker yelled as he caught onto the back of Levi’s cloak, hoisting the rogue backward and throwing him into the bartender's counter with a solid thud!

Levi cursed as he looked up from where he had landed. The bartender seemed unperturbed by the violence, as he picked up another mug and started cleaning, paying zero attention to the rogue that had just been ragdolled into his counter.

That is going to leave a bruise. Levi thought as he stood up, his hands going up to confirm his hood was in place. A relieved sigh left his lips once he confirmed that it was. His current situation was hard enough as it was, and he couldn’t risk worsening it by having anyone here recognize him as the obsidian rogue.

He stylishly glanced at the door where the captain's men now lay on the floor unconscious, with the door wide open and the pickpockets and Xara rushing out.

“That’s my cue,” Levi mumbled, and then grimaced as the taste of iron saturated his mouth with blood covering his open palm as he succumbed to the cough. I'm definitely going to feel that tomorrow morning.

“Alright, boys, I'd love to stay a little bit longer and have the old chitchat and beat down, but I'm afraid I have places to be, so please pardon my manners," Levi said as he reached into his void box and pulled out three smoke pellets and struck them against the floor.

Levi was already moving towards the door before the smoke began to spread, causing mass panic within the tavern. And within eight long strides, Levi had crossed the entire distance between his former position and the exit of the tavern before the first of the captains figured out his plan.

“Stop him! “ one of the Captain's men screamed, but it was too late, as the smoke easily obstructed the view of everyone in the tavern while the rogue slipped away and into the open, where the mage and the pickpockets stood awaiting at the mouth of an alley. They beckoned him over, and Levi quickly closed the distance between him and them as he burst into the alley and caught up to the mage and pickpockets who’d resumed running.

“Where are we—” Levi started, but was quickly interrupted by the female rogue.

“Save your energy. If you can talk, it means you're not running hard enough. The sailors will soon be on our heels. Which means we have limited time to escape them.”

Levi simply pressed his lips in annoyance as he briefly considered holding back the mage and trying their luck by running in a different direction from the pickpockets. But he decided not to, as the two pickpockets were probably their best chance of getting away unseen, as they seemed to be more familiar with the city than he or Xara were, which meant their best chance of a successful getaway lay with the pickpockets. Unless this was a trap, and even then, Levi didn't expect it f to be much of a problem for him and the Mage to handle.

As it was, Levi had no qualms using his skills if he felt beyond threatened, with no way to escape. And he knew for a fact that the mage had been working on her Xistol. He didn't think it was quite finished, as she had spent a few hours on Symon's wagon, complaining about the

Either way, he felt pretty confident about him and the mage's chances against the pickpockets, so he kept his mouth sealed for the meantime as he continued to run following the pickpockets that led him and the mage through the different alleyways of the city. The movements of the pickpockets running were much more coordinated and precise than their pickpocketing skills, and Levi had to give them credit there.

And even when he was, he knew all the alleyways of Gantus like the back of his hand, which was enough to let him escape, and he supposed it was the same for the pickpockets, judging by the way they seamlessly led the way through alleyway after alleyway, and not once did they pause in their stride to check over their shoulder or look to the side, which made the rogue a bit more confident in the in their probabilities of successfully getting away.

By the looks of things, the pair were leading them away from the port. But to a shadier part of the city, by the looks of things. Still, he didn't caution them. In fact, Levi was starting to feel more and more at home the longer they ran and the more rubbish he saw littering the street.
