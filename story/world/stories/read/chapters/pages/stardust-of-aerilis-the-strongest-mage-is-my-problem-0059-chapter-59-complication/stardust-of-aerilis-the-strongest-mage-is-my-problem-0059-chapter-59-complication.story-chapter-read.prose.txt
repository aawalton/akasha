Virelle didn't wait for the cyclops to find its rhythm. With a sharp flick of her wrist, she manifested a massive sphere around the creature, intending to repeat the erasure she had performed on the first monster guardian. She clenched her hand, the mana crushing inward with a localized gravity warp.

CRACK.

The sphere didn't hold. With a single, brutal swing of its bronzed arm, the Cyclops shattered the glass like it was nothing more than a soap bubble. The divine radiance from its six wings flared, neutralizing the spatial pressure before it could settle.

The Cyclops' eye narrowed. A beam of white-hot celestial energy shrieked through the air. Virelle threw her hand up, conjuring a circular mana shield. The beam impacted the lavender surface, scattering sparks across the marble, but the impact forced Virelle back a few inches—her boots skidding in the air.

Before the beam even faded, the giant was already moving. It blurred forward, taking advantage of the visual cover to close the distance. As its massive forearm pulled back for a crushing swing, Virelle’s prismatic orb hummed with a desperate, high-pitched screech.

She pointed a finger. A needle-thin, hyper-condensed mana beam lanced out, piercing the giant’s shoulder. The Cyclops let out a divine howl of pain, its momentum faltering as it writhed mid-air, the bronze skin smoking from the burn.

However, it was relentless. Before Aiven’s eyes, the smoke dissipated, and the wound knit itself back together in a blur of golden light. The creature roared, its six wings flapping in a violent, synchronized rhythm that sent a dozen crescent-shaped mana slashes flying toward Virelle.

Virelle snapped her fingers, manifesting a layered aegis. The slashes slammed into the shield—one, two, three—and on the fourth, a sickening spiderweb of cracks appeared on the lavender mana.

Aiven watched from the crater below, his heart sinking. He saw the way Virelle’s shoulders slumped slightly, the way the translucent sleeves were trembling. The bloodstains from her previous battles—the red of the chimera, the green of the hydra—stood out starkly against her pale skin. She was exhausted.

Virelle noticed his gaze. Even with the blood on her face and her mana flickering, she managed a small, tired smirk. "Master, don't look so tragic," she called out, her voice carrying that familiar, theatrical sass despite her heavy breathing. "I’m just giving the ugly one-eyed beast a false sense of security before I turn it into garden gravel."

She lunged forward, her hair trailing behind her like a comet's tail. She swiped her arms, sending a barrage of fast-moving mana slashes at the giant. The Cyclops didn't even dodge; it simply clapped its massive hands together.

BOOM.

A physical shockwave erupted from the impact, shattering the mana slashes instantly and throwing Virelle off balance. She spun through the air, her prismatic orb wobbling.

"Virelle!" Aiven shouted, reaching out with his right hand as if he could catch her from across the hall.

She regained her balance, but the Sentinel was already on top of her. It fired another golden laser. Virelle managed to manifest a final, thin shield, but the moment the beam touched the mana, the violet barrier shattered into a thousand useless shards.

The cyclops closed the distance in a heartbeat. It swung its massive fist in a wide arc. Virelle tried to dodge, but her fatigue made the movement sluggish. The blow connected, sending her hurtling across the cathedral like a ragdoll.

She slammed into the marble wall with a sickening thud, the stone cracking behind her. She began to fall, but inches from the floor, she flared her mana, stopping her descent and flying back into the air with a defiant snarl.

"How... persistently... annoying," she hissed.

She raised both hands, summoning a forest of icicle spears that glinted with a violet frost. She launched them with a scream of effort. The Cyclops clapped its hands again, the shockwave shattering the ice into a fine mist—but it had fallen for the feint.

Hidden within the cloud of ice shards, a single, massive mana spear—larger than a cathedral pillar and glowing with the concentrated remains of Virelle’s power—was already plummeting toward the giant’s back.

The spear struck with a sound like a mountain splitting open. It drove through the Sentinel’s bronze hide, impaling it through the center of its wings and dragging the legendary beast violently down to the marble floor.

The Cyclops let out a choked, divine shriek, writhing in pain as it was pinned to the stone. In its death throes, the guardian’s golden eye began to fire beams frantically in random directions, lancing through the air in a desperate, final tantrum.

Virelle reacted instantly. "Master!" she cried out. With a swift flick of her fingers, she cast a shimmering violet dome over Aiven, shielding him from the stray lasers that carved smoking lines into the floor around him. She dodged several beams herself, her movements a blur of silver and lavender.

Nearby, Pelka, who had been "playing dead" amongst a pile of rubble and holding her breath, let out a high-pitched scream. A stray beam nearly took off her horn, forcing her to scramble out of the wreckage and run blindly for better cover.

Virelle didn't give the beast another second. Her expression turned cold and decisive. She flicked her wrist, summoning a massive, horizontal mana slash.

Being immobilized by the heavy spear, the Sentinel couldn't move. The blade of light lanced through the air, cutting the ten-foot giant perfectly in half.

The shrieking stopped. The golden eye dimmed, and the entire creature began to dissolve into a cascade of brilliant, warm golden light. Within moments, there was nothing left but a fine, shimmering dust.

Virelle immediately unmade the dome surrounding Aiven. She took a deep breath, and before she even reached him, she performed a quick cleaning spell. The multicolored monster blood stained on her outfit vanished in a sparkle of mana, restoring her to her pristine, porcelain glory.

She collided with Aiven in a sudden, fierce hug.

Aiven almost lost his balance from the impact, stumbling back a step. Virelle tightened her hold, burying her face against his shoulder. "I'm so sorry, Master," she whispered, her voice uncharacteristically small and trembling. "I should never have let you survive on your own for so long. To think I allowed such an ugly creature to lay a hand on you... it is a stain on my pride I shall never forgive."

Aiven stood frozen for a heartbeat before slowly reaching up and hugging her back. "Virelle, it’s not your fault," he said softly. "You were fighting the whole dungeon by yourself. If you hadn't arrived exactly when you did, I'd be dead. I’m the one who should be thanking you."

Virelle started to look up, her eyes searching his, likely preparing a theatrical rebuttal to his thanks.

"KYAAAAAAAH!"

Pelka’s scream tore through the silence of the cathedral, echoing off the high-vaulted ceiling.

Virelle let go of the hug, her face twisting into a look of profound, murderous frustration. "Honestly," she muttered, her fingers twitching. "Does that snack-creature have no sense of timing? Interrupting our sweet reunion like a creature desperate to be made into skewers."

As Aiven and Virelle made their way toward the far end of the hall, they saw Vane rising slowly from the rubble near the wall, coughing and wiping dust from his suit. They reached the obsidian pedestal where Pelka was standing, her bangs wild and her eyes fixed on the empty space.

Pelka turned toward them, her face pale with absolute horror.

"It's gone," she whispered, her voice shaking. "The Loom-Breaker... it's gone!"
