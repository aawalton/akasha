Naerabel hovered above the mantis carcasses, shadows flickering around her form like a thin, flame-wrought halo. To Henry and Maurice’s surprise, she took their explanation of why they were here and what was going on calmly and dare he say, with an open mind. When he mentioned his proof, she asked to see them, and now, it had been nearly ten seconds of silence as her eyes roved from one corpse to the next. She floated lower to one as a shadowy tendril extended out of her arms, then she stilled, glancing up at him with her inky eyes. “May I?”

Henry blinked, then dipped his head. “…of course,” he managed after a beat, and under their gaze, the tendril widened at the tip for a brief moment before a gleaming black beak snapped shut around a stalker’s head. A flicker later, it retracted within her body, as if nothing had happened, while the kraken closed her eyes.

This whole encounter, so far, was nothing like Henry had expected. Getting down here had been unnerving, especially when it became clear that this was currently the home of one of the most powerful creatures Henry had encountered within this world. It was a possibility he had considered before coming here, which was why he’d left it for last, expecting a difficult fight. But for one called the “Doom of Nymara”, the kraken was… polite and willing to talk. Somewhat friendly, even, if a little aloof. She kept her aura and anger in check, and seemed to be very willing to take them at their word.

Tilting her head, her pitch-black eyes opened and narrowed at the stalker. Long, dark arms sprouted from behind her shoulders, which she used to lift one of the corpses up before her gaze flicked back to Henry. “I’ve sensed these things. Before today.”

Henry stilled, and both he and Maurice shared a look before he focused back on the kraken. “Did they show up here? When?”

Then, recalling the difference in status, he continued, with a little less urgency. “If you recall, of course.”

Still, Henry’s mind churned. This could be a problem. If somehow, the mantises had made their way through, it was only a matter of time before things got dicey. But the kraken shook her head.

“No… they didn’t show up here. Not exactly. And while I did lose track of time lately, I’d say it’s been years. Long before either of you were born.”

Naerabel grew quiet for a second, then noticing their puzzled faces, she beckoned with her Merfolk arm, a cloud of darkness flitted out from the edge of the space and lazily, it was shaped into a sphere.

“Shadows and darkness are part of me. And I’m part of them. Especially when I’m meditating. If I wanted to, I could reach out much further than my domain. To the shade of a tree. To the darkness that dwells within beings much larger than… this,” she said, pointing to the space around her while the orb of shadows changed from one scene to the next. “I can even send my senses all the way up, when it’s night, and it’s much easier on moonless nights. That’s when I sensed them. At the edge of the System’s manicured gardens. Like vermin, crawling, searching for a crack to slither through. I tried to snatch one of them back then, but, our gardener stopped me.”

Henry had so many questions as he eyed the shadowy arm wrapped around an insectile form. But Maurice did as well. “The System?” the crab asked.

“Yes,” she said, and the shadows became two dark featureless figures, one that looked just like her, and one that took the form of a slightly taller Merfolk holding on to a sickle, with a bushel of dancing algae under its arm. “The System claimed pulling one of them in would attract much more of them than this world could handle. So I refrained,” she muttered, almost as if she was still annoyed about it. As for Henry, he was still digesting it all. Especially the bit about the kraken sensing things from all the way up to outer space. Henry could sense and see things from afar, but that’s because he had Avatars right there to see for him. Naerabel could just connect to shadows and perceive through them? Just like that? If that was a skill or an Aspect, it must be pretty damn powerful. In fact…

“Did that have something to do with what happened earlier?”

The words flew out before he could make them make sense, and naturally, the kraken, and Maurice, just stared at him. Feeling a little foolish, Henry straightened himself, shoving his discomfort to the side. This was too important to let some little embarrassment get in the way of learning something valuable. “I meant, your connection to shadows. When you were speaking of it earlier, it almost felt as if everything around you had trembled. Somehow.”

Henry had some other questions, such as how she could tell they were young. But he opted to ask about that later, if they got the chance. For now, this was important. As for Naerabel, there was a risk she’d take his question badly, but again, this felt worthwhile. But she didn’t.

“Oh,” she simply said, then nodded. “Yes. My connection to shadows and darkness is beyond what this world can handle, so had said the System. But allowing my soul to take that step would mean I’d have to leave my home to a place it wouldn’t tell me about. So I refused it, and I refused to let my soul take the next step until I’m ready and willing.”

“Are you ready now?” Maurice asked.

The kraken stared down at him, then her gaze flicked to Henry, while the arms behind her back danced leisurely, then she looked around.

“Maybe, little crab. Maybe. Do you feel ready?” she asked him, but Maurice didn’t even give it more than a second before he waved the question away. “I don’t know about ready. Sometimes things happen and you just gotta hug something to death or blow it up, you know. Being ready is nice, but we don’t always get to be.”

At that, Naerabel smiled, showing her sharp, shark-like teeth for the first time. “Well said. The night often comes when expected. But shadows can engulf you at any time. Be it from a tree, a whale, or even the moon itself. One must be always ready. To some degree. And I guess with these tidings that you two have brought, I must put a stop to my meditation and leave my home for a while. To make sure there is a peaceful home once the work is done.”

Henry’s eyes widened, and once more, he was a little taken aback. The kraken was nothing like he had expected.

“That’s great!” Maurice exclaimed, likely feeling the relief of having someone as powerful in their camp. “You know, you’re a lot less scary than we thought you’d be. The Merfolk that ruled this place must have been real stupid-brained if they made you that mad at them.”

And just like that, the good, pleasant mood was shunted aside. Then again, as the dreadful darkness around them suddenly loomed heavier and closer, Henry decided that testing the emotional restraint of their new potential ally might be a good idea. He wouldn’t have personally gone for the poke-the-boil method right away, as he personally had some filters, but this should still be a good test.

Naerabel’s aura, while heavy and sharp, was still decently restrained, even though the shadow-arms behind her back were now significantly more agitated. They had been smoothly shifting left and right but now they were lashing with sudden, jerky movements every so often. Still, while she didn’t point her grievances at them, they were still being affected by the splash.

“You two are strangers, and while I can sense no malice from either of you, I do not appreciate the attempts at dredging my past.”

Henry, still wary of anything sudden, dipped his head. “We understand, and we apologize. We meant no insult.”

As for Maurice, his eyes were downcast. “I’m sorry, miss Naerabel. I won’t do it again.”

The kraken stared down at them for a few more seconds, then the weight around them lifted, while the darkness that had encroached on the city was once more banished.

“I take no offense,” she said after a bit. “But it’s a long story. One that I do not wish to speak of at this time. Suffice it to say, they did a great friend of mine an unforgivable injustice, and when they had the chance to make amends, they doubled down. So I cast their precious city to the depths, and let them scurry away like the cowardly bottom-feeders that they are. You two are young. And though you seem well traveled, you should never underestimate the lengths to which the petty would go in the so-called intelligent species.”

The two took their chastisement readily, and Henry was glad that the kraken was proving herself to be well in control of anger. This boded well for their future collaboration. Still, one question was bothering Henry, and now he had to ask it. Especially considering it shouldn’t offend at all, and would do well as a distraction.

“How can you tell that we’re young?”

Naerabel glanced at him, then frowned. “Well, he is obviously young,” she began, pointing a dark arm toward Maurice, “considering the enthusiasm. I remember ten-year-old Merfolk with less whimsy than your crab friend. Also, I can vaguely feel how many day and night cycles he’d experienced. There’s naturally a difference between him and the shell, but that’s normal. As for you, there’s the day-night cycle as I mentioned, and there’s also the fact that we’re both krakens.”

She said the last bit as if it should have been obvious, but then, when it obviously didn’t click for Henry, she tilted her head, and the aura of menace slithered back around. Not as overt as it had been when bad memories had been brought up, but still, it was right around the corner.

“Even if you grew up by yourself, with no one to care for you, it should be obvious. It’s in our flesh. In the luster of the eyes. In the movement of our arms. In our skin, our beaks, in how we move.”

Henry, for a moment, considered some way of hiding it, but this might be the first, old, and powerful kraken he had ever met. Even Rocky was young and somewhat inexperienced. He couldn’t lie to Naerabel. And there was no point to. She saw the truth, as clear as day. There was no obfuscating it. There was no way around this situation but to get through it.

“You aren’t a real kraken,” she declared, and Henry could only dip his head.

“I am not,” he said, and he sensed the truth in his own words echoing through his spirit. No longer human, but not fully a kraken either. Not to any of those species, anyway. But was he okay with it?

Henry thought he was. Probably. Then he told her his origin, and he hoped she wasn’t particularly pissed at humans too. One thing for sure, though. He really did distract her away from Maurice’s sensitive question.
