As Nigel continued his walk, a strange numbness began to creep over him. He was a prisoner of war being escorted deep into enemy territory. He expected damp stone, the smell of rust, and the familiar, oppressive gloom of a military base.

Instead, he felt like he was walking through the immaculate hallways of the royal palace.

Everything was polished. The floor beneath his boots was a seamless expanse of dark, reflective material so clean he could see his own exhausted face looking back at him. It made him acutely aware of the dirt still clinging to his skin and the fabric of his borrowed clothes. A strong, almost irrational urge washed over him to take off his boots. He wanted to feel the pristine surface against his bare feet. It seemed almost disrespectful to tread upon it with battlefield grime.

He noticed the architecture. There were grates lining the edges of the corridor, designed to pull excess moisture and water away. It was a stark contrast to the Cinderfall trenches, which were perpetually flooded, a miserable reality that constantly gnawed at the morale of his troops.

A heavy, metallic thud drew his attention. An armored soldier walked past, heading in the opposite direction. His boots were caked in mud, leaving dark, ugly marks on the perfect floor.

It appears people like that exist everywhere, Nigel scoffed internally. Men who don't care about the cleanliness of their environment.

His quiet judgment was immediately interrupted by a bizarre sight. Small, disk-like metallic creatures slid out from hidden compartments in the walls. They scurried over to the muddy footprints, humming softly. In seconds, the mud was gone, the floor spotless again. The creatures vanished back into the walls as quickly as they had appeared.

Nigel’s eye twitched.

These soldiers live in absolute luxury. The thought was a bitter pill. He wanted to scream at the top of his lungs. Why? Isn't this a bit excessive for a military encampment?

As they walked, Nigel started to realize there was a system to the madness. Whenever there was a singular, clunky-looking metal man, it was a golem. But if the armor was sleek, with narrow slits for visors or glass faceplates, they were Dark Elves utilizing the strange power armor.

He had managed to sweet-talk the morning shift guards into bragging about those specific suits. It was another thing Nigel found himself intensely jealous of. Apparently, the armor augmented their physical strength, functioning similarly to the baseline enhancements provided by a magical contract. An ordinary foot soldier wearing one of those suits could fight toe-to-toe with a Tier 5 knight.

If I only had this too, I could finally even the playing field, he thought, a familiar ache settling in his chest. But he was just a prisoner, envious of his enemy's toys.

Through the wide, reinforced viewports lining the corridor, Nigel caught glimpses of the outside world. He saw the colossal structures being built on the horizon. The sight sent a cold shiver down his spine. The enemy was already nearly impenetrable in their current state. What would happen if those fortresses with legs were actually finished? The speed at which those structures were being built was absurd.

The deeper the group moved into the complex, the tighter the security became. Nigel saw automated turrets tracking their movement, watchtowers looming overhead, and heavily armed patrols that increased in frequency the further they went.

He had originally thought the layers of security to exit the holding cells were excessive. This took that to an entirely different level. It was significantly harder to get into this central area than it was to escape from the prison.

They finally arrived in front of a heavy blast door. From the outside, the building looked much larger than the holding cell complex. But Nigel had a gut feeling that appearances here were often unassuming.

He had grown numb to the bureaucracy of the place. One security check after another, one clearance verification after the next. He still didn't even know who had invited him to breakfast.

It was well past the designated breakfast time at the holding cells. Nigel's stomach rumbled loudly, protesting the delay and anticipating the exquisite food he was currently missing out on.

He stood before the large blast door. Is this it? he wondered. Or will there be another layer of checks and clearances waiting for me?

The door hissed open.

The sight inside made Nigel's breath catch in his throat.

It was a dining room, featuring a large, long table. That in itself wasn't extraordinary. The shocking element was located at the far end of the room.

Behind the head chair, a starlit dragon was taking a nap.

It was ginormous. Its massive, scaled body took up a significant portion of the room, leaving Nigel to wonder how they had even managed to bring it inside. The beast couldn't even lift its head fully if it wanted to without hitting the ceiling.

Most people in this world were unaware that space dragons could alter their size at will. They rarely saw them in their smaller, more manageable forms because the dragons only did so around people they trusted completely. Unless you were actively contracted to one, seeing a space dragon in a cat-sized form was practically impossible. They could even shrink to the size of an insect if they sensed a threat to their lives, making them incredibly difficult to track or kill.

Nigel didn't know he was standing face-to-face with a blue-platinum dragon. He only saw a monster.

The only logical conclusion Nigel could reach, the only thought ringing clearly in his mind as he stared at the beast, was simple and terrifying.

I am breakfast.

The guard beside Nigel nudged him forward, instructing him to take a seat. Nigel obeyed mechanically, his eyes never leaving the sleeping dragon. He sat stiffly on the edge of the chair.

A moment later, a door in the far left corner of the room slid open. A man stepped in.

The starlit dragon opened one eye, puffing its nostrils. The man walked over, gently caressing the dragon's massive head, and took a seat at the head of the table.

Nigel knew this man. He had seen this face broadcast across the world when the boy declared that Cinderfall and Sylvanheim were doomed. He was the one who had come to Aerthos to rescue the King of the Dragons and his sister.

It was the Lord of House White.

...

I saw Nigel look at the spread of food on the table, then back at me, his face pale as he tried to process the situation. I had called him here to discuss the status of the negotiations regarding his release, but the man seemed completely intimidated.

My idea of talking over a meal didn't seem to be working. I glanced back at Kaelus. It was probably his fault. The dragon was agitated, puffing up his body and acting passive-aggressive. He didn't like strangers, and having one here made him hiss like a cat puffing out its fur. The more time passed, the more he acted like a common house cat.

I looked back at Nigel. He was hesitant to even touch his utensils, staring at me as if I were a ghost.

"Is the food not to your liking? Shall I have something different brought for you?" I asked, keeping my tone polite.

Nigel jumped slightly, acting like a scared pup. He quickly picked up his fork and began taking small, skeptical bites. His chewing slowed, then sped up, the skepticism replaced by genuine appreciation. The more he ate, the more intense his focus became.

He finally looked up from his half-empty plate, a touch of color returning to his face. "I thought the prison food was the best thing I had truly lived to taste. I thought nothing could top it. It looks like I was wrong."

Yes, Patricia indeed makes the best meals. I always thought I was partial to her cooking because she was my nanny, and I grew up eating the food cooked by my mother and her. I would never say it aloud, but her cooking might actually be better than my mother's. That fact should never be taken lightly. For a son to even think the food is better than his own mother's cooking requires an absolute, undeniable level of culinary skill.

But to my mother's credit, she was the one who had taught Patricia how to cook.

Before I knew it, all the food on Nigel's plate was gone. The man was reaching for a full loaf of bread, thoroughly enjoying his meal.

I almost felt bad for the hypothetical bucket of ice water I needed to drop on him. I set down my fork and leaned forward.

"We have received a reply to your ransom order through Dragon Valley," I began.

That made the man put down the bread. The look of a wounded animal instantly returned to his face.

"I thought this could be handled amicably. I thought King Theron would at least agree to a bare minimum settlement to save face. But he continues to astonish me with his bullheadedness." I sighed, shaking my head. "He said we have permission to have you executed. He refuses to pay a single coin for you or your knights I have taken alive as prisoners, stating you are useless, and Cinderfall won't pay for hostages unless they are Phoenix Knights."

It appeared Nigel was expecting that exact outcome. He didn't look shocked or betrayed. He looked at me with a quiet, grim resolution. For the first time since he entered the room, he spoke directly to me, not as a prisoner, but as a knight.

"I have no qualms about such an end. If that is the will of Cinderfall, I am willing to lay my life down."

"I wish things were as simple as that," I replied.

"To make matters even more complicated, we have received formal correspondence from your estate. They are willing to pay for your safe release. That further complicates things, as the amount they can muster would be a grave insult to the Crown."

I could see Nigel was already misunderstanding the situation, his eyes narrowing as he thought I wanted to exploit his people for more gold. I lifted a hand to stop him.

"Don't get me wrong. I have no qualms about releasing you for a small ransom. But the problem lies with the fate of your territory. To offer such a small ransom and have it accepted would be deeply humiliating for Cinderfall. The King would likely retaliate against your people for making him look weak."

I tapped the glass table. "There is also the fact that I have over thirty thousand of your knights as hostages. Cinderfall doesn't know that. They think it's just a few hundred that we captured. If all of you were to suddenly return from the dead and walk back, no doubt they would be treated as traitors who surrendered. The only reason your knights are so well-behaved in the prison right now is that you are alive. In fact, I had you placed in the cell block with them to both prevent you from taking your own life, or them from doing anything rash."

I rested my chin on my hand, studying him. "What to do..."

I looked across the table. "I can't also have you surrender your territory, effectively making your knights my subjects under the rules of war I set forth. Your territory is right next to the capital of Cinderfall."

I could see Nigel was also weighing his options. That was good. He was using his head. That was better than ninety percent of the other Cinderfall commanders I had observed.

Which meant he might be open to my suggestion.

"From where I stand, the most effective way to resolve this is to execute you and your thirty thousand retainers who wish for the same fate as you," I stated flatly.

I could see in Nigel's eyes he wasn't bothered by his own life ending, but his knights were a different story.

"OR..." I dragged the word out. "I could figuratively kill you. You see, I do hate unnecessary killings. Cinderfall may see them as loyal knights. But what I see is thirty thousand farmers."

That sentence made Nigel chuckle. A dry, rasping sound.

He didn't realize so many of his knights had survived the charge.

His laughter didn't sit well with Kaelus. The dragon opened his eye and snorted, a sharp exhalation that made the air pressure drop significantly in the room.

Nigel realized where he was and immediately stopped himself, his posture going rigid.

"Might I ask what is so funny?" I asked mildly.

"I mean no offense," Nigel replied, his voice tight. "But I find it ironic. The whole reason Cinderfall invaded the lands of Aerthos is because Cinderfall is barren land where growing food is almost impossible. We are forced to sell raw material in exchange, arming our enemies for the bare minimum for survival. Making our enemies stronger in exchange for food. That is why we coveted the lands of Aerthos."

I looked at him, shaking my head. "That right there is why I find Cinderfall's outlook on the world so backward. Why not sign a mutual defense pact? Why not, instead of thinking of your neighbors as enemies, work together for the betterment of both?"

Nigel frowned. "Who in their right mind would agree to something like that? And how would it even work?"

I explained my agreement with the Dwarves. I detailed the economic zone plan, the integration of their craftsmen into our logistics, the mutual protection. Nigel looked at me as if I had grown a second head.

"Young Lord, you never cease to surprise me," Nigel finally replied, leaning back in his chair. "I wish there was still time, or possibility, for Cinderfall and you to achieve such an agreement. But we have come too far. Honestly, I don't blame Cinderfall. Who would even think this kind of agreement was even possible? But let's get back to the main point. What do you need from me so that I may save my knights?"

"Simple," I said. "I need you all to put down your arms and accept your death. For all intents and purposes, I need you to convince your knights to accept the surrender terms. Leave the battlefield and become civilians. Tilling the fields and starting new lives. I need you to put down the blade and forsake your knighthood."

I held his gaze. "I know it's a tall order to ask of you. But think of it this way. You already finished your death charge. You failed. You died on that battlefield. This is your afterlife. They can join the five thousand who have already surrendered and start a new life. For now, they need to forsake their families back home and their kingdom. Maybe one day, when this war ends, they can reunite. But for now, they all need to die."

Nigel fell into deep thought. He stared at the half-eaten loaf of bread on his plate for a long time.

"That is truly a magnanimous offer," he asked, his voice low. "I wouldn't wish to be rude, but may I have some time to think it over? Even though my King has turned his back to me, to forsake my morals is a tall order to ask of me."

"Take your time," I replied, leaning back. "You have until the end of this war to decide. But do keep in mind, whatever you choose, most of your knights will follow you in that."

"Can I ask you a question?"

I nodded.

"I get why you want to spare my knights. But why would you be kind to me? A man who had all intentions to strike you down?"

Did he just say 'had' or did I mishear him? Things were getting interesting. The man's loyalties were actually shaking. So, I dropped some bait.

"Well, I have a personal interest in your family," I said smoothly.

Nigel frowned, clearly trying to work out the angle. "It is due to my royal blood? The fact that the Acards are a sub-branch of the Flavius family?"

"I don't care about the Cinderfall royal family one bit," I replied, my voice turning cold. "In fact, having them all wiped out would be best."

"You see, I have a personal interest in the Vex family. After all, you are their last living descendant."
