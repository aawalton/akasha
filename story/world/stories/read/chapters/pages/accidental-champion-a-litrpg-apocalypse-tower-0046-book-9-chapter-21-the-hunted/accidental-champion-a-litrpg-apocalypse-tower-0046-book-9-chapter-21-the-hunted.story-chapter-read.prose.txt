“We summoned a hundred Champion parties,” a stern-looking elven mage said beside Queen Aecia.

A hundred Champion parties…

Xavier snapped the time dilation field back around himself and looked through the notifications he’d received. Nowhere in the notifications had it mentioned there would be other Champion parties here. It wasn’t entirely unusual to encounter other Champions from around his universe on a floor. In fact, it was a regular enough occurrence.

Those didn’t tend to be his favourite floors—except, perhaps, for the hundredth floor.

He’d liked that floor.

But usually, whenever he arrived on a floor, such a thing was mentioned in the notification.

He looked at the ceiling. “You’re holding out on me,” he muttered. Xavier scanned the barren moon with his Immortal Soul lens. When he’d pushed his awareness out through the moon the last time, he hadn’t encountered a single other soul—and with the Immortal Soul lens it was much harder for people to hide behind their veils, though not impossible.

Another scan came up with the same issue, until he looked at the barren moon with The All-Seeing Eye and noticed something rather interesting.

White lights.

In ninety-nine different spots all around the moon were patches of white lights—usually four, though sometimes there were only two or three of them.

Not once was there only one.

Within the white lights he could see the outlines of people.

The other parties… they’re still mid teleport. Still looking at the ceiling, he frowned. I was teleported here while inside a time dilation field… so that means I arrived faster than all the others?

On the other floors he’d shared with other Champions, he’d never really checked that. He reviewed his memories. It didn’t take him long to confirm that this wasn’t the only time he’d arrived first.

In the past, he’d always had to shift his time dilation field to allow the other Champions to step out of their safe zones, to choose an option in a notification, or enter the fight. So he hadn’t even noticed this little quirk…

This floor was beginning to get even more interesting than he’d thought it would be.

Xavier stretched the time dilation field back out. He looked at the projection in the middle of the room and thought he best make his own one. He manifested a spell pattern, and to his left a smaller projection appeared, showing the white lights he’d seen on the barren moon.

The queen frowned at the projection. “What am I looking at?”

He made the projection flick from that set of lights to all the others he’d found. “These are the other parties. They haven’t quite arrived yet.”

“Your time dilation field must be a strong one.” She eyed his sword. “And you’re a solo Champion? I’ve never known a time mage to be able to solo a tower floor…”

“I’m not exactly a time mage,” Xavier replied.

An elven woman about a head taller than him wearing golden, full-plate armour stepped forward. “Let them come. We need them.”

“I’m not sure that’s a good idea.” Xavier shared with them the notification he’d received, the one that gave him a choice to either fight for the planet Galadsea or to capture the queen for the Empire of Servitude.

“The System has betrayed us!” Queen Aecia hissed. She put a hand to her mouth, eyes widening, and glanced at the ceiling as though expecting the System to smite her there and then. When it didn’t, she scowled.

The tall elven woman drew her sword and pointed it at Xavier. “Which did you choose.”

“To fight for Galadsea. That’s why I’m here.”

Queen Aecia narrowed her eyes. “Before you knew of the other parties?”

He nodded.

“You must be a powerful A Grade,” said the mage who’d spoken before.

“Something like that…” Xavier looked at the projection showing the millions of ships. “The Empire of Servitude—have they tried to contact you?”

Queen Aecia’s face soured. “They sent an emissary, about a thousand years ago, with an ultimatum.”

“Let me guess, serve or die?”

“Exactly that.” Queen Aecia straightened. “For two million years I have ruled this world. The Empire of Servitude isn’t the first threat to our freedom we have faced. We have pushed back every single one.” She opened her arms. “The Queendom of Serrilia is a sanctuary. There are no contracts on this world. People do not serve me unless they wish to. They do not fight unless they wish to.”

Xavier blinked.

The queen cocked her head. “You must think me foolish for running my world this way.”

“Foolish? No. I find it admirable,” Xavier replied. It was exactly the way he ruled his own domain. And she’d managed it on a single planet for two million years…

“We have been fighting the System’s wish for conflict this entire time,” the elven mage said. “For the past million years we have managed to hide the location of our planet.”

“Then that emissary arrived,” the tall elf said, sword still in hand.

He was beginning to think he should have asked for introductions.

“They’d found you?” Xavier asked.

Queen Aecia shook her head. “No. The System sent them here on a quest. They still didn’t know the location. But the System…”

“It forced a conflict?”

Queen Aecia nodded. “It gave us a thousand years to prepare, then provided our enemies with our location. I thought it would be enough, but…” Her gaze shifted to the projection. “You must allow the other Champions to arrive. The planet’s shield will stop the beam weapons, but the System is going to allow the Denizens to teleport onto the planet. I received the notification a few seconds before you arrived.”

The notification was shared with Xavier.

You have acquired an enforced quest.

Defend the Queendom of Serrilia from the Empire of Servitude’s armada.

Serrilia’s shield is strong, but strength does not come from shielding oneself from the universe. Every ten minutes, the crew of one of the Empire of Servitudes’ ships will be allowed to teleport to the surface of your world.

The timer will start when the Champion parties arrive.

Xavier wrapped his fingers around Bones’s hilt. The grip creaked under his strength.

Bastard System.

This was very much reminding him of the Endless Horde. Xavier wondered, for a moment, if this was the floor Adranial had wanted to mention to him. But with how powerful the enemies were, he doubted that.

She’d probably think I was crazy for jumping forward this far through the floors. Maybe the floor she wanted me to visit was five-hundred-and-six, and I was only one away from reaching it…

Xavier wings started opening and closing.

“Champion?” Queen Aecia prompted. “Will you do as I command?”

Xavier smiled. “First, call me Xavier. Xavier Collins. Second, this may be your queendom, and I may have chosen to serve you, but don’t for one moment think you command me.”

Queen Aecia’s eyes widened so much they looked like they would pop out of her head. The tall elven woman’s sword rose. The queen put a steadying hand on the woman’s forearm.

“Fine, Xavier Collins. You are right—you are not under my command. But you have decided to help our cause, have you not? Will you do as I wish?”

“Yes and no.” He looked at the ceiling. “I don’t trust those other Champions to decide to fight for you without a little convincing. I have a chance to do that, but something tells me I won’t be able to teleport out of your force field without your permission.”

She turned to face one of her entourage. “Ilyana?”

The woman inclined her head. “Won’t take but a moment.”

Xavier watched with interest as the woman altered runes that were in the very throne room’s floor. He could have done it himself, given enough time, but as he’d never encountered a force field strong enough to contain an entire planet, he thought it best to watch someone else do it first—the last thing he wanted was to accidentally let the entire armada through.

While he watched, he split his mind and pushed his Immortal Soul lens out to examine the force field itself. Initially, he hadn’t focused much on it when he’d arrived. Now, he couldn’t help but notice just how much energy it contained.

It was only then that he realised exactly where that energy was coming from.

With the rest of the inhabitants of Galadsea all but frozen outside his time dilation field, it had been difficult to notice. Now it was incredibly obvious. Tiny lines of energy rose from every Denizen of this world, all of them offering up a small amount of their power to the force field.

So that’s how it’s so strong…

It reminded him, heavily, of when he’d borrowed power from his own people.

Roughly twenty billion Denizens, all powering the same force field. No wonder the armada couldn’t get through without the help of the System.

Once again, he cursed the bastard System.

When Ilyana was done, Xavier teleported away, leaving the queen outside his time dilation field, and materialised on the barren moon in front of four glowing white lights. He expanded the field to encompass them.

The Champions’ own teleportation finished instantly after he’d extended the field. They blinked, staring at Xavier, then all their weapons were in their hands.

Xavier had his hands raised, expecting something like this. “Peace.”

The woman who must have been their party leader stepped forward. Blue tattoos swirled over her face, moving as though they were alive. She had the horns of a demonkin, but there was something fae about her. She held a small, dainty looking wand, and she was quite short.

Though his Immortal Soul lens told him she was anything but a pushover. Here was an A Grade stronger than Arrack—the whole party was.

Tread carefully.

The pixie demon woman sniffed and looked about. “Time distortion.” She pointed her wand at him. “What is the meaning of this, stranger?”

“I’m gathering the troops.”

“Explain. This is not a part of this floor. We’ve inspected the memories of Champions who’ve been through it. You must be another Champion. Tell me, why do you intercept us?”

“When the notification pops up, I want you to choose to fight for Galadsea.”

The pixie demon woman glanced at her party, then threw her head back and laughed. “What in Auron’s name are you talking about, dragonkin? No one chooses to fight for Galadsea.”

“Anyone who does, dies,” a man said behind her. His horns were longer. He stood tall, proud. He looked like he belonged on the cover of a romantasy book—something Liana would read.

“Well, that’s about to change. Trust me on this. Besides, if no one has done it before, think of the titles we’ll get.”

The man’s forehead creased. “I know you…” His eyes widened. “It’s him. Xavier Collins! The Hunted!”

Desire entered the pixie demon’s eyes. “The reward!”

Energy crackled around the four Champions. Spells powering up from every single one of them. Xavier saw them readying those spells—saw what happened inside their body as they did. He snapped his time dilation field away from them, flinging himself backward at the same time.

But he wasn’t fast enough.

A bolt of lightning from the leader’s wand managed to make it to him first. It slammed into his chest, shattering his Force Armour. His entire body shook and convulsed. All his nerve ending fired. His skin sizzled and if he could breathe, he would have smelled burnt flesh.

Xavier teleported back to the throne room. He flopped onto the shining silver floor, unable to control his limbs. His time dilation field didn’t falter the entire time.

Eventually, after perhaps five seconds had passed, the convulsions stopped. His skin no longer sizzled. Though he could smell that burnt flesh now.

Slowly, he pushed himself into a sitting position.

“And this is why I work alone,” he muttered.

The others in the room were still frozen outside his time dilation field. He made his way back to his feet and took a moment to breathe. Looking down at the skin of his hand, it didn’t take long to heal.

He was far stronger than he had been when he’d faced Arrack. He’d enhanced his body significantly. And yet whatever spell that pixie demon had cast on him had taken him down.

Then again… had it taken him down? He’d still been able to cast and manifest spells while suffering from the effects. It hadn’t interrupted his active spells, either. And now here he was, already fully healed, his enemies several million miles away back on that barren moon.

But he imagined what it would be like to face a hundred parties of Champions, every Denizen among them as powerful as they’d been…

He couldn’t face them how he used to. When he’d fought against the Endless Horde, he hadn’t had the power to stop time. He’d brute forced his way through them, soul apparitions withering their ranks, reaping souls left and right…

Xavier’s wings unfurled. In his mind’s eye, he saw all the different glowing white lights on the moon.

They’re not going to join me. And even if I could convince some of them, could I really trust them?

The man had called him “the Hunted,” the woman had mentioned a reward. They’d known him by name.

Chances were they wouldn’t be the only ones.

His was a name that would soon be known by just about everyone across the universe, if it wasn’t already.

Ah, the price of fame…

Right now, it wasn’t working in his favour. One day, however, he could see how it would. Entire sectors would be coming to him first, asking to be joined with his empire.

The universe will want to unite.

But that was a long way away.

Xavier returned his focus to the problem at hand. Namely, the three million ships in orbit. He tapped his foot on the silver floor, the pain that had covered every inch of him from that bolt of lightning a mere memory.

He recalled how he’d dealt with the Endless Horde in the end. He hadn’t waited for every single wave to come to him.

He’d gone straight to the source.
