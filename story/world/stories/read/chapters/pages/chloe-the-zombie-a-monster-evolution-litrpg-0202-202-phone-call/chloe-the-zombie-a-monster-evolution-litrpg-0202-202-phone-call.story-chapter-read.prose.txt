Ishgria had long since stopped thinking of the room as a command center. A command center implied order and some reasonable expectation that the people inside actually knew what was happening. This place had none of those things.

It was a cramped disaster room crowded with maps, hastily written reports, and scraps of paper covered in names and numbers, while the stale air carried the mingled scents of sweat, dust, ink, and overheated machinery. Several cheap lights had been positioned around the room, their flickering light illuminating clusters of exhausted angels as they hurried from one table to another, carrying messages, updating maps, scribbling down instructions, or quietly arguing over which routes remained safe. Some had blood staining their clothes, others had makeshift bandages wrapped around their arms or heads, and more than a few looked as though they were running purely on caffeine.

Ishgria was at the center of this whole mess, seated behind a battered wooden table that had long since been buried under layers of maps and reports. Her eyes moved steadily across the information in front of her, absorbing every detail while her fingers occasionally shifted a marker or moved a piece representing one of her teams.

A radio sat beside her, its speaker crackling constantly with fragments of conversation from scattered teams throughout Tartarus. "Team Seven, report."

Static answered. Ishgria waited. Nothing. She made a small mark on the map. "Team Three, respond."

More static crackled through the radio, followed by another mark on the map as Ishgria carefully recorded the latest status, her expression remaining composed even as the list beside her grew steadily more concerning, because at this point every lost signal had become a fifty-fifty proposition: either the team had been wiped out, or they had simply moved beyond the range of their communications network, and while she preferred the latter explanation, experience had taught her not to confuse what she hoped was happening with what the available data actually suggested. "Team Twelve, status?"

The radio crackled for several seconds before a broken voice finally emerged through the interference, barely audible beneath the constant hum of activity around the room.

"Team Twelve... we've lost the... eastern corridor, we're pulling back toward..."

The transmission dissolved into static before the sentence could be completed, leaving Ishgria staring at the radio for a moment longer before calmly reaching for her pen and marking the team's last known position on the map, deliberately avoiding the word lost because there had been no confirmation of casualties and, until there was, she would not make operational decisions based on assumptions. However, the situation was becoming increasingly difficult to interpret as anything other than a rapidly deteriorating operation, and as Ishgria's gaze traveled across the sprawling map in front of her, she could see just how many markers had already gone dark, with some teams having reported casualties before their signals disappeared while others had simply stopped responding altogether.

The teams that remained active were hardly faring better, with reports coming in about blocked routes, dwindling supplies, damaged equipment, and increasingly aggressive opposition from Divinity Inc. That forced Ishgria to revise plans almost as quickly as her people could carry them out.

She leaned back in her chair and slowly exhaled as another burst of static filled the room, reminding her that she had anticipated casualties, and even accounted for the possibility that several teams would be forced to retreat. But, she had not anticipated losing this many people in such a short period of time.

The room had settled into another stretch of tense silence when a pair of footsteps approached from behind Ishgria, and she looked up from the map just as a female angel carefully made her way through the crowded command room until she finally stopped beside Ishgria’s chair with a small flip phone held out in both hands. “Boss, you have a phone call.”

Ishgria stared at the phone for a moment before looking up at the angel, whose white wings were slightly dusty. “Fiona, did you check the number properly? I want you to make absolutely certain that it isn't connected to Divinity Inc., one of their subsidiaries, or anyone currently operating under their authority, because if someone from that organization has somehow obtained this number, I want to know before I answer.”

Fiona nodded immediately. “Yes, Boss, I checked it twice before coming to you, and the number doesn't match anything in our records or anything we've previously associated with Divinity Inc., although we don't have any idea who the caller actually is.”

Ishgria finally took the phone, turning it over in her hand as she studied the unfamiliar device for a moment, her expression thoughtful as she weighed the possibilities, because an unknown caller reaching her at a time like this could mean almost anything from a desperate request for assistance to a serious security breach. There was, however, only one way to find out.

She flipped the phone open, pressed the answer button, and brought it to her ear. “Hello?”

For a brief moment, there was nothing but faint static on the other end, followed by the unmistakable sound of someone breathing before a familiar male voice finally came through. “Boss?”

Ishgria straightened in her chair. “Greg?”

A short laugh came through the phone. “Yeah, it's me, Boss, although I have to admit I wasn't entirely sure this number would still work after all this time."

"It's good to know you're alive, Greg. Things haven't… been going very well as of late."

"Yeah, I figured. Anyway, don't worry about me, Boss. I'm alive and I'm safe for the moment," Greg said. "But, considering everything that's happened, I guess 'safe' is a bit of a generous way to put it."

A faint smile touched Ishgria's lips despite herself, but it disappeared as quickly as it came when her instincts as a leader took over again. "Greg, start from the beginning and tell me exactly where you are, who you're with, and how you managed to get access to a phone," she instructed, already reaching for a fresh sheet of paper and preparing to record every useful detail he could provide.

There was a pause on the other end, followed by a quiet chuckle. "Well, Boss, that's actually a pretty long story, and if I start explaining everything that's happened since I got here, we're probably going to be talking until morning."

"That's fine, we have time, and I want the complete version rather than a rushed summary, so start wherever you think the story actually begins."

"See, that's not really the problem, Boss."

"Then what is?"

"There's someone here who really wants to talk to you."

"Who?"

Greg didn't answer. For several seconds, there was only silence on the line, and Ishgria's fingers tightened slightly around the phone as she waited, wondering whether the connection had been interrupted.

Then she heard movement on the other end. and then came a voice. "Mom?"

Everything inside Ishgria seemed to stop for a second. It was hard to believe her own ears.

"Mom? Hellooooo?"

Finally, Ishgria answered. "Chloe?"

There was a tiny pause, and then the girl's voice burst through the speaker with all the warmth and excitement Ishgria remembered so painfully well. "Oh my gosh, Mom! Are you okay? I missed you so much!"

Ishgria didn't know what to think. For all the reports she had read, all the names she had crossed from one list to another, all the contingency plans she had constructed and all the losses she had forced herself to accept because there had been no other choice, she had never allowed herself to seriously consider the possibility that she would hear her daughter's voice again so soon, but there it was.

Ishgria swallowed against the sudden tightness in her throat before forcing herself to focus on the most important question, because no matter how relieved she was to hear Chloe's voice, relief meant very little if her daughter was still in danger. "Are you all right, sweetie? Are you hurt anywhere, and are you somewhere safe?"

"I'm fine, Mom, really!" Chloe replied. "I'm safe now, and I've got a lot of friends with me, so you don't have to worry about me being alone or anything like that."

Ishgria let out a quiet breath as she closed her eyes for a moment and allowed herself to simply listen to her daughter's voice. "That's good to hear."

"But where are you, Mom? I want to come see you."

Ishgria opened her eyes again, immediately understanding that there was considerably more determination behind those words than Chloe's cheerful tone might have suggested. "Chloe, I'm in a dangerous place right now, and I don't want you coming here. Whatever you've been through to find me, you've already done enough, sweetie, so stay with your friends and keep yourself safe until this situation is under control."

"But, Mom..."

"Please understand, Chloe, I want you to be safe."

"I've already come this far," Chloe insisted, and Ishgria could practically picture the expression she was making on the other end of the phone, probably the same frown she had worn countless times whenever someone tried to tell her she couldn't do something. "I've been searching for you for way too long to just stop now that I finally know you're alive, and besides, I'm not going to leave you here by yourself."

"I know," Ishgria said, a small smile finally appearing on her face. "I know you're not going to give this up."

"Duh, obviously."

"All right, then. I'm on the lowest floor of Tartarus, so if you're determined to come looking for me, then come here."

There was a brief silence on the other end before Chloe's voice brightened even further. "Really?"

"Yes, really, and I'll know when you arrive, Chloe, so you don't need to worry about finding me yourself. Just make sure you stay with your friends and don't take unnecessary risks getting here, because I would much rather have you arrive safely than have to organize a rescue mission for my own daughter."

"Okay, Mom! I'll be careful, I promise."

Ishgria shook her head faintly, already knowing that Chloe's definition of careful and hers had never been particularly compatible.

Chloe stared down at the little flip phone in her hands as though it had somehow become the most important thing in the entire world, her fingers curled carefully around it while the last few words her mother had spoken continued to replay inside her head. Chloe...

She could still hear Mom's voice. Even though the call was over, it was still there, almost as if Mom were standing right beside her instead of being somewhere impossibly far away at the bottom of Tartarus. Chloe swallowed hard, blinking several times as she tried to keep her eyes from watering again, but it was difficult when her brain had apparently decided that now was the perfect time to replay every little thing Mom had said.

Chloe squeezed the phone a little tighter. For so long, she had wondered what was happening to Mom. And now she finally knew that Mom was alive. She was really, actually alive.

Chloe's lips slowly curled into a tiny smile as that thought settled in, and the tight feeling in her chest began to change into something warmer, something that almost made her want to laugh and cry at the same time.

For the first time since everything had gone wrong, Mom wasn't some distant mystery. She was somewhere down there, waiting for her, and Chloe finally knew exactly where she needed to go. That meant they were closer than ever. And somehow, that made everything feel a little less scary.

Her thoughts drifted toward all the memories she had of Mom. There had been little trips, meals together, stupid arguments that seemed incredibly important at the time, and all those tiny moments Chloe had never thought she'd need to remember because she'd always assumed there would be thousands more waiting for them.

Her smile grew wider as Chloe knew there would be more of those memories soon enough. There had to be more.

Chloe looked down at the phone again, her eyes becoming a little misty as she gently ran her thumb across its surface. "Mom..."

She said it so quietly that she barely heard herself. Then a hand suddenly patted her on the back.

Chloe jumped slightly and looked over her shoulder to find Z standing beside her, watching her with a calm expression. "Are you all right?"

Chloe quickly rubbed at the corner of one eye with her sleeve, then gave Z a small smile and nodded. "Yeah… I'm fine. I'm just really happy to hear from Mom again after so long."

Z nodded. "Then let's go find her, we're getting closer than ever."
