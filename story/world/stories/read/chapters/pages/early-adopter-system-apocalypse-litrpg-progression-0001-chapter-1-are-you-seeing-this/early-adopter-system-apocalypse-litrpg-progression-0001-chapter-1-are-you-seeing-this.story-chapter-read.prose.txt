Watching the scene from her hiding spot on the balcony, Scarlet refocused the camera and took another picture. She moved her camera slightly so that the lens wouldn’t reflect any light through the small gap in the curtain. As she continued to observe, she briefly considered whether she needed to get a short recording as well. Posterity, and all.

Considering the compromising position of the two individuals, a video would feel somewhat voyeuristic.

The woman was sprawled over the man’s chest, both of them only half-covered by the mussed bedsheets. For married people, they were sharing some remarkably tender pillow talk. Too bad neither of their spouses was there to appreciate it.

Now if the woman could just reposition a bit.

Scarlet tilted her head slightly as the couple shifted, and… There. Oh, dear. Scarlet wondered if you could still classify the permanent ink on the woman’s body as ‘art’. Scarlet almost pitied her.

Well, her job was to confirm the tattoo existed and collect proof of identity, not to judge other people’s choices in permanent body alteration. She’d absolutely do it, but that unpaid labour was hers alone.

A small, familiar weight shifted under her collar. Its tiny paws pressed briefly against her skin before settling again.

“Yes, yes, I know. The human mind is truly unfathomable,” she agreed quietly, her words barely a breath in the afternoon air.

The weight shifted once more.

“Almost done,” she added more softly.

For the amount of money Scarlet’s client had paid, the more evidence, the better. She adjusted the angle of her lens, refocused, then took another silent shot just as the two began to stir. When he was only centimetres from the woman’s face he froze.

Something flickered right into the middle of Scarlet’s field of view.

She went still.

Somehow it didn’t block her sight, but it was there. Fixed, clear and impossible to ignore.

[Congratulations!]

[EARTH has reached planetary threshold for System integration]

[Commencing Initialization]

[Time until full integration: 71:59:58]

[Welcome & Good Luck]

She blinked once, blinked again, then peeked inside her shirt collar only to see a pair of beady black eyes blinking back at her.

“…ah.” So she could clearly see through the messages. They were just sort of in the way.

With the resignation of someone consistently inconvenienced by reality, Scarlet lifted the camera again, shifted her focus back to the room, and took one final silent shot before packing her camera away. Only then did she rub briefly at her forehead with two fingers, like she could press the message out of existence. Sure, she could see through it, but she wanted it to go away.

It seemed like that thought did something, because in the next moment the message disappeared. It was still there, somewhere, she could feel it, like a lingering sneeze. Present but not quite. She was somehow sure that if she wanted to, she could recall the words.

On a whim, she tried to bring only the countdown timer back into view.

[71:57:22]

To her surprise, it worked. Now in the corner of her vision were a faint series of numbers inexorably counting down to… something. ‘Initialization’. Whatever that meant.

Across the room the man had unfrozen. His expression was now shifting in a kaleidoscope of emotions Scarlet couldn’t be bothered to decipher.

The woman was slapping at his bare chest, her lips parted, and her expression the facial equivalent of a question mark.

“Ow, stop it.” He grabbed her hand, but the woman wasn’t paying attention.

“Do you see this?”

“You see it too?” he shot back immediately. “Do you know what it is?” he whispered, like loud noise might disturb the numbers somehow.

The woman ignored him, waving her free hand in front of his eyes like that would do something.

Scarlet watched them for a few seconds longer, glanced back at the floating numbers, and blinked.

“Yeah. I’ve done my due diligence,” she murmured. “This feels like an appropriate time for my departure.” She slung her camera bag over her shoulder and opened the sliding balcony door. Something told her that right now, the stranger in the room would be the least of their worries. No use wasting an efficient exit.

Both of the people in the bed turned as she appeared from behind the curtains.

The woman blinked rapidly at her, clearly having difficulty comprehending that a person had just strode in. The man followed her gaze a moment later, his expression going strange.

“Was she there before…?” the woman asked.

“The maid? Is she a part of whatever this is? A hallucination?” the man said, looking between Scarlet and the empty air in front of him. “Is she- Hey, are you real?”

Scarlet did not respond as she made her way to the exit. Neither of them moved to stop her. The man’s gaze snapped toward a half-empty pitcher on the bedside table, and the empty glass beside it. He eyed the water suspiciously.

Neither of them interrupted Scarlet on her way out.

When Scarlet finally stepped into the hallway, the shift was immediate.

What had been a quiet corridor only an hour or so ago was now filled with noise. Doors were opening and shutting, voices overlapping as people spoke over each other. Phones lit up in almost every hand.

“Wait, man. You see it–”

“No, I’m telling you, it’s on the news, watch–”

“Mom? Mom, can you hear me?”

Scarlet adjusted her grip on the strap of her bag and started walking, her pace steady despite the slight hitch in her step and the surge of movement around her. The small, warm weight shifted again beneath her shirt.

“I know, buddy,” she murmured. “Bit of a bumpy ride. Don’t mind the turbulence.”

She glanced down at her phone as she moved. Her thumb flicked across the screen with practised efficiency, opening and dismissing one app after another as she walked. Notifications were stacking faster than they could be read. One enterprising news site had already begun live reporting. Headlines, blogs, stories and social media were updating in real time, and despite nothing actually happening, they were somehow failing to keep up with whatever this was.

Twitter (X), WeChat, Line, VK, WhatsApp. Every feed, every app, everywhere.

Scarlet looked at the innocuous little countdown in the corner of her field of view and frowned internally. A fuzzy little head poked out of her collar, and Scarlet put her phone away then stroked the silky fur.

“You’re right, buddy,” she said. “If we can’t beat it, we outthink it, outrun it, outlast it.”

Her pace didn’t change. And while her gait wasn’t quite even, it didn’t slow her at all. She wove through the chaos of the hallway with precise, deliberate movements. Somehow she managed to always be at least a step ahead of distress or disaster.

She slipped between two people, one backing into the hall without looking, the other trying to make a call while lacing up their boots. Scarlet adjusted automatically, pivoting around them on reflex.

Eventually she made it to the access hallway. In just a few steps she was inside the storage room, closing and locking the door behind her.

In front of her, the cleaner she’d borrowed the uniform from was a Statue.

She was dressed in a long robe, still sitting where Scarlet had left her a couple hours earlier, phone clutched in both hands, eyes locked on her phone. Instead of the exhausted friendliness she usually displayed, now she was just staring dazedly at the screen like it owed her money.

Thinking of which, Scarlet slipped a folded $100 into her free hand and watched as her fingers reflexively curled around it. When no further action seemed imminent, she simply let go and got to business.

She slipped out of the cleaning uniform in smooth, practised motions, folding it once before placing it on a box near the other woman.

Then Scarlet turned, already reaching into her bag.

Shirt first, then pants. Next the vest. Then jacket over shoulders. Her hands moved through the motions without hesitation. Buttons aligned cleanly, cuffs were adjusted. Glasses slid onto her face, and a tiny friend was transferred securely into place.

“Slow is smooth, smooth is fast,” Scarlet mumbled, and Scarlet was smooth.

Her tiny companion nosed at her collarbone in agreement.

Looking back at the cleaner, who had eventually stood up, though her eyes remained on her phone, Scarlet leaned in just enough to brush two quick air kisses against either side of her face before stepping back. It was as much habit as ritual at this point. Maybe that’s what finally snapped her out of it enough to respond.

The woman blinked at Scarlet, her expression tight. “Did you see…” She waved her hand in front of her face.

“Once more into the fray,” she murmured. Her tiny companion shifted slightly as she stepped into the hallway.

The noise hit harder in the hotel proper. What had been mass confusion a few minutes ago was starting to solidify into much more distinct reactions. Voices overlapped as people spoke over each other. Shouts, whispers, and exclamations created a disorienting cacophony of sound. Beneath it all, the overlapping uncertainty of the crowd pressed against Scarlet's awareness, an uncomfortable pressure distinct from the noise.

As always, she did her best to ignore it.

Scarlet made her way through the throng. Her gait rhythmic as the cane touched down in quiet, measured beats. Where some hesitated or collided, she adjusted, angling past them with small shifts she made seem effortless to those paying attention.

Scarlet always paid attention.

Under her collar, tiny paws pressed briefly against the side of her neck.

“It’s loud, I know,” she placated. The weight pressed closer. “People are strange, and today is stranger.” She shrugged. A cold snout pressed against her skin and Scarlet huffed an amused laugh. “Yes, yes. They can’t all be you.”

Scarlet stepped up to the balcony that overlooked the main hall.

The convention floor spread out below her in full color and motion. A dense sprawl of costumes, booths, and people painted a bizarre moving picture below her. Some were filming, some laughing, and some arguing. Curiously, there were very few scenes of blatant panic or fear.

Scarlet supposed this was a fair reaction from a crowd of people attending a fantasy convention.

Her gaze moved across the floor as she sorted through the spectacle. Her mind was already on her next steps. What was useful, what to avoid. Where were the gaps, and the blind spots. She needed to prepare.

Her focus flicked briefly back to the numbers in her vision.

Unfortunately, what Scarlet wanted was often immaterial.

She let out a slow breath, looked at the queue for the elevators, looked at the chaos near the fire exits, and finally turned to face the escalators. Eugh. It was the best of bad options she supposed.

“Into the fray indeed…” She adjusted her grip on the cane and started forward.

So, seventy-one hours. What could she reasonably do with seventy-one hours?

Prepare for the worst felt like a prudent place to start.
