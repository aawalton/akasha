Chapter 120: Manufacturing Hope

The battering of the fortress wall by Chime was a long-period pulse immediately succeeded by another pulse.

“What the hell?!” Highfive exclaimed in the team gestalt.

“He could already do that,” Inkblot said calmly, “if you remember the tests. The sim likely didn’t actually do it due to the lack of efficiency. Though it also seems he’s a bit stronger than anticipated.”

The second pulse may have cracked the wall a bit. It was going to take a while.

“Don’t think he’s going for the whole fortress,” Bo said.

“Right, why would he get this exact, perfect sight profile, blowing out the rubble?” Jack asked. “I think he’s aiming to break down a similarly-sized hole for them to go through. Avoid spending time inside the walls without being inside the fortress, and dodge the obvious chokepoint of the gate.”

Unable to keep sitting back and doing nothing, Jack pulled some ammo already ready well outside the fortress from his prior attack, and rained them down, fairly blind but in the general area he knew the enemy was in. In the same timeframe, he pulled some waiting sheet metal from up top and filled the gap in the wall completely, blocking sight through it. He continued raining down to make things difficult, though he wasn’t going to bother making it anything more than an annoyance to wear them down and gain some psychological attrition.

From the look of the sky in various feeds, Délight’s aura bloomed again — no doubt on account of her figuring Jack would get no additional benefit from knowing her general area. Indeed, in addition to the less effective views via camera, she was deeper down in the terrain, making it more of a problem to actually pinpoint her.

After a brief pause, a new, concentrated distortion wave came from their hiding spot. The wave burst through the metal like a damned shaped charge, pushing it wide open. The camera took some disruption from the air concussion caused, flickering briefly, but remained functional.

Jack strongly considered playing the game of remaking the wall over and over. The camera might die, but it would anyway if the fortress wall broke. It was fairly significant energy, too, but his main hesitation was that he still had the power of misinformation. The more he adapted correctly now, with both of them in their bunkers, the more Chime — a Blaster whose role was creatively blowing through shit — the more Chime would adjust to his best methods.

He’d just waste more time. Chime would inevitably adjust to his adjustment. It was better for Jack to save his tricks for when they finally moved forward.

So he left the remains of the stopgap wall, making it seem as if he couldn’t repair or transmute it quickly. He dropped another volley of darts down in their area, but immediately following this, another fortress-rocker slammed home. The others focused on inspecting the wall while Jack worked on caused distractions. He put down a new sheet metal wall for Chime to blow through — for keeping up appearances and adding more metal in the vicinity. He wasn’t planning on doing it much more.

“The one section has most of the damage,” Inkblot reported.

“Confirmed,” Bo said on his heels. “Spiderweb cracks and pulverized stone on the outside, right through the hole in the wall.”

Another blast blew through the metal wall. Jack realized something else this time — it was heavily displacing his particulate fields in the immediate fallout zone. In fact, even the blasts hitting the fortress were passing through and warping the fields slightly. He winced. There was nothing he could risk doing. As soon as he started moving them around and screwing with them, Chime might suddenly become aware of the field. From the looks of it, he couldn’t clear it out with the current blasts, likely because they had an existing focus.

It was what it was. But it added another reason not to keep adding the barrier back.

“There’s no point in blocking them until they get close,” Jack said to his team. “If they want a clean sprint path before coming out, so be it.”

“Does it make sense scenario-wise, though?” Highfive asked. “Protecting the base and all.”

Jack was impressed that he even asked. “Good question. The thing is, we never had a chance at protecting this antiquated place from serious breach. Too much enemy capability, not enough people to cover the fortifications. Hell, with Délight out there, an army might be worse than useless. Also, when it comes to walls, I can fill the holes with metal in mere moments. All that matters is defeating the invader, I think.”

“Yeah, good points, bro.”

Jack focused on nothing more than annoying the enemy and wearing them down as Chime continued the laborious process of breaking down a section of the fortress wall. Darts rained down on the enemy; existing darts and their remains turned into chaotic storms of metal that Chime was forced to blast away and gradually pulverize to make Jack lose his sensitivity to the material. The others could only help by grabbing metal and hurling it, seemingly. Jack didn’t have enough sensitivity to capitalize on such maneuvers in time, though it was likely just Hiccup and Defiant doing it, to which things would be dubious to work regardless.

Chime was not stopped by this, just slowed and frustrated. Jack could only imagine the stress and panic going on in their miniature hell pit, under constant fire from above and all around. He was almost certain that when that wall broke, they’d burst out for all they were worth. The plan might’ve been to pause, catch their breath, and send Defiant to scout, but those odds seemed negligible after all this.

From the previous battle of the PACCs, Jack had gotten significantly better with ranged operations, indirect fire, and multitasking. Predicting that he could do this would’ve been unlikely, so they would’ve figured this plan to have a smoother path.

Nonetheless, the fortress wall was getting cracked and busted, more and more pieces falling from it. Particularly damaged was the ground-level portion immediately forward of the wall breach.

Jack split his time between harrying the enemy and preparing for the inevitable breach points. Three stages, as he saw it: inside the walls, moving through the fortress hole and inside, and then the debut team ambush. It was critical that they had an advantage when they clashed. He’d maybe narrowed it slightly, but not enough. A success from the remaining traps was all but required.

Finally, after minutes of grueling, erratic work, the wall broke apart and collapsed with a resounding crash and great cascades of dust. The fortress was made of sturdy and stable composite stuff, though — it held itself above, suspended, effectively just leaving a hole breaking out to the daylight. The floor and ground were full of loose rubble pieces of varying sizes. The camera feed died, of course.

One more blast came, scattering the loose rubble to make a better hole.

Jack occupied himself by moving one of two backup cameras up from over the lip on the roof to look down on their enemy’s approach. A risk, if Chime noticed, but he figured they would be quite occupied in their rush to take advantage of their new ‘daylight,’ and there was tons of dust and chaos as cover.

A small sandstorm approached, and shadows just barely seen running inside it. Chime seemed to be ringing a cowbell, but the sound barely reached the fortress. Its music was battering the ground all around them. Jack almost laughed.

Clever, though this did frag his chances of seeing me move the camera up. Chime is impressive, though.

Jack sent obligatory waves of darts from the roof as they ran across the divide, mostly intended as distractions. The shadows moved swiftly within, and they only slowed down briefly.

For the walls, they came…

The aura from Délight could be seen exposed in all its glory as she neared. A constant multicolor pulse that pooled around her in a smaller, thick radius, then exploded outward every second — unlike before, it seemed like she was varying it, able to delay or speed it up slightly here or there. Every time it did, the whole team moved slightly faster. He had a feeling it had some sort of ‘perception of time’ component, and the lent efficiency was what made them faster and more fluid in motion.

Tricky. Very tricky.

His immediate act as they were passing through the walls, finally into his particulate field zones, was to close a waiting, quite thick steel panel into the fortress hole. This was bait, mostly — Chime would have to choose between the sand cover and blowing through it. There weren’t many other solutions. Defiant pushing through, maybe, but that would be slow and dubious. On top of that, the sand outside was not nearly as loose and plentiful immediately inside the walls. Figures were quickly becoming more discernible as silhouettes.

Jack still had the advantages of timing and calm, safe multitasking operation. His output at the maximum, as soon as the enemy team was rushing into a proper domain of his control with his particle fields pinpointing them, Jack ripped the deadly, sharp wires under the sand up and into their midst. He pulled what material was closest right up from below, and meanwhile ripped sections going to either side of them up and collapsing toward the enemy into a nasty, interlacing web, ripe to use their momentum against them…

Unfortunately for him, something out of his control made the timing of their arrival far from perfect, because it was right as one of those damned bursts came from Délight.

The team had little choice but to stop dead in their tracks and react to the threat. The sandstorm dissipated rapidly as Chime reacted, making all of their silhouettes visible enough to discern who was who. Predictably, Hiccup evaded them with ease. Equally predictable, Defiant enveloped Délight like a glove, diving and rolling with her. He avoided some wires, and others wrapped him, but he was blindingly quick, and as they mutually popped up, he was ripping the wires off of him en masse. Délight rolled away, scot-free.

Chime blasted wires immediately in front of him, preventing most of the mass attack, more or less intended for him.

But not all — Jack had wire free behind him, and was doing a wrap around his calf just as Délight’s precious buff was running its course and falling off. Digging in on one side, slicing fabric. Superficial wound. Mere moments from honing in, delivering a nastier wound by tightening up. Focusing on it.

He saw, and moreover, in the field, he felt Hiccup moving rapidly for her beau. He’d felt this exact occasion many times in simulation — enough that it was familiar. He’d been countered by this many times. She’d grab his shit, phase with it and toss it away, ‘magically’ undoing any sort of entanglement.

It honestly kinda pissed him off — just a little bit.

This time, Jack lashed out for her hand a split-second before she’d grab it, running the sharp material for her palm or fingers, whatever he might get.

Everything happened in a flash. She did her little displacement trick immediately and flung the wire away, as usual. But he felt something else. Her hand jerked back toward her protectively, in reflex. Her fingers curling, maybe? Was that a bodily shudder? Blood?! And he could tell there was a simulated effect of some sort…

He put it all together in his head: he would’ve just sliced off her fingers by his interruption of her timing! But allowing severed digits was a bridge too far. The sim chamber was hitting her with pain and locking the joints inward, probably very uncomfortably. Her right hand. Her dominant hand.

Jack spared no time for celebrations. She was far from neutralized as a martial artist, and he had to keep pushing. Unfortunately, it was unlikely he could take advantage to wound her further, with her current favorable position. Chime was spinning around, blasting away more wire, breaking it apart.

Nonetheless, Jack focused hard on Chime to either get a lick or force Defiant away from Délight. He mostly threw wire at him haphazardly, to utilize for follow-up, but added an almost simultaneous layer of needles firing up out of the sand.

Indeed, Defiant couldn’t ignore this — or Délight wouldn’t let him. He zipped across space and provided a screen.

Délight was smart in this situation. She fell back hard toward the injured Hiccup, knowing that the other teammate would recover and stand a good chance of protecting her.

Jack noticed she was ‘holding’ that pulse in, now, almost certainly with growing strain. Likely, it cost her fatigue.

Damn.

He didn’t go for precision, here. He pelted them with masses of ammo, his ‘feelers’ out for follow-ups. Small spheres, here and there, but he didn’t branch from them. He was analyzing, scanning, and looking for the right timing. For the moment, anyway, they were in his killing box, but they were good at reacting as a team to his singular focus. He had to look for the right opening, the perfect timing.

With each passing moment, visibility improved as the billowing sand cleared. One thing he noticed from the improved visibility was that everyone, aside from Defian,t had some extra neckguards around their neck.

How smart of them.

Around this time, Inkblot was let loose from his sound-cage to go wherever he thought would be advantageous, per his expertise. A risk, if Chime located him, but that would be hard to do in the midst of everything else he had to focus on. Likely, any sort of cover out of line-of-sight would work. A calculated risk, in any case.

Délight finally had to use the pulse to avoid the wires closing around her, while Chime was simultaneously back to back with Defiant, both of them fending off blasts of ammo.

Making damn sure I can’t get Chime, now. Smart again. But I have to punish them for it. Does she know that, too? Is she ready to respond, in turn?

On the ‘down-pulse’ of her effect, Jack pushed himself to an overcharge in output, fully intending on focusing hard for Délight, but ready to pivot to Chime if Defiant lost his nerve.

Jack hit her with needles, forcing her to dodge and evade, and Hiccup to screen, getting hit but displacing soon after to drop the needles away almost harmlessly. Superficial wounds, that’s what they were ensuring with these tactics, and with a strong carefulness for follow-ups. They knew what he could do once he had metal on someone for a few moments. Less than that, actually, if he wasn’t working indirectly.

With both of them forced on their back foot, and Defiant staying put on what was almost certainly ‘orders,’ Jack sent a mental command to Bo, who was waiting for it. “Disrupt Hiccup’s body.”

Hiccup was one of the least relevant people to possibly do this to, as she could recover instantly. But during the actual disruption, she was on the fritz — in that split moment, she couldn’t do shit. If she were defending herself, it might not matter. The timing was too difficult. But defending someone else across space was a different story.

A sphere, basically just a tossed bullet, hit the ground near Délight’s ankle, ready to bounce and roll like so many others. She wasn’t even paying attention to it, because how many had been doing the same? Flung and missed. Perhaps they wondered why he was even bothering with them as opposed to darts.

The sphere was tweaked to roll right behind her, out of her field of view, at which point all of Jack’s focus went into it, exploding into a tendril going for her ankle.

Nothing fancy at all. It was simply what he’d been practicing, to counter everyone: wrap and twist quickly. Crack something.

The timing was close. From Bo’s disruption, Hiccup had gone to the dirt twitching, but she’d twisted toward Délight with her last sliver of ridiculously quick body control. She entirely regained control and resisted the disruption mid-fall, and suddenly displaced into a spring, immediately bursting from it in a sprint to help Délight, not even knowing what was coming.

Délight’s instinct was to go to ground preemptively as the tendril closed around her ankle and pivoted one way in a practiced maneuver to dislocate joints. Jack had not really expected the dive, so his twist was a little spoiled, or he might’ve broken it.

Of course, this was right when her pulse exploded out again. Hiccup inevitably grabbed the tendril, displaced, and flung it away before he could blink.

But Délight had to be helped up, and though she didn’t need Hiccup to move, she was not moving well because of her ankle. A sprain. Score!

At the same time, Chime had taken advantage of the focus on Délight to blow through the metal panel, ringing his cowbell continuously and emphatically to burst forward, Defiant screening him. Chime seemed to be straining, even letting out a war cry to push himself for intense output enough to hold the opening.

“About as much as we can expect, here, I think,” Inkblot said. “But I noticed something: Defiant has a slight injury to the foot, too. Almost a limp. He’s hiding it well, and it took me a minute to confirm, but I see it when he plants. A slight favor.”

“What?!” Bo exclaimed. “When? How?!”

“Not sure.”

Huh. From a random dart? No. From shit flying around outside the walls? From tweaking it during a roll?

For some reason, Jack’s mind flashed to when a cup spilled on Defiant from the table in the mess hall. It was incredibly dumb — it wasn’t an attack, but it came to mind anyway. As it did, his mind leapt to the possibility. What if you splashed it in his face, comparatively? Well, it wasn’t clear, but still…

“Accidents?!” Jack asked incredulously. It could well be just that. Or specifically self-inflicted damage, or maybe just indirect damage. It explained his improved armor, as it would protect him from environmental damage, while not mattering much against more directed harm, considering he’d resist it anyway.

There wasn’t much time to discuss it, though. The enemy was coming.

There was a wonderful consequence of the injury to Délight. Chime and Defiant, in their perhaps adrenaline-fueled surge and scramble to make a way through the hole, left a more perfect gap than he could have hoped for, as Délight could not keep up, and Hiccup had little choice but to keep near her by necessity.

This necessary sacrifice? I’m afraid it may have left a... fatal flaw.

Jack had been waiting rather than striking, and he was singularly focused on his next task. With Transmute: Quick-change, he reformed the blown-out panel wall right into the gap they left, blocking out Hiccup and Délight. Jack had set up this assumption. He’d given false hints that he couldn’t reform that fast with the prior wall. They’d executed this last push with hopes of taking advantage of that, making a trap for themselves.

Give your enemy enough rope to hang themselves.

Hiccup looked close to trying to displace through anyway, but she thought better of it at the last moment and hesitated, either for fear of Délight being isolated or Jack’s ability to form the metal with her not quite close enough. Maybe that was dangerous intrinsically for her? Regardless, she was shut out.

Chime and Defiant. Not ideal, but we’ll take it.

Jack sent the signal to the rest of the team and opened the door to their room. The time for caution was over; the full-on ‘offense of the defense’ was about to be unleashed on an already terrorized enemy.

“Time to destroy them,” Jack declared in the gestalt.
