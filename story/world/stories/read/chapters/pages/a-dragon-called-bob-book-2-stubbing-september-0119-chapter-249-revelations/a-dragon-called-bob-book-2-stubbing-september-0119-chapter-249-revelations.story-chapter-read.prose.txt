The former kitchen in the Swinging Cod had been transformed into something of a meeting room merged with a busy passageway.  Serving staff bustled back and forth, studiously ignoring the gathering of bosses who sat around the table that was now shoved against a wall to one side.

Fortunately, it was a large table, as we now had quite the gathering.  Breville, Neville, Geville, Elkville all sat at one end.  I am sure I was getting at least one of their names wrong, but I was in that awkward position of not wanting to look like I’d forgotten their names… Dude was such a useful word.

Kat sat in the middle, her back against the wall, with a tiny desk and chair set up for her to take notes.  She had a pair of miniature pince-nez spectacles perched on her nose, one side of which was useless due to the pirate’s eyepatch the system demanded she wear.

At the other end, Phillpot in human form, and Lady-the-Colonel Heartflash were having a spirited conversation about animal noises.  I suspected Philpott was just replying in kind and didn’t realise that Heartflash was certifiable.

Next to them, Armand and Pratnip sipped silently from glasses of scotch.  Both men wore finery rather than armour.  Pratnip’s ostentatious red and gold frock coat looked out of place next to Armand’s grey drabbery.

I sat in the middle with Esme on one side and Mrs Sanderson on the other.  I felt like the Spartans at Thermopylae.  Stony looks surrounded me.

“We can redirect some of my dungeon forces to support your main estates,” I offered to the nobility on my right.

“What about our bloody pubs?” snapped… I wanted to say Beville?  I was pretty sure he ran the Longhorn in Ankmapak and would be the one landlord most affected by my fall from grace within the empire.

“I can’t station a dragon on your roof in the middle of the bloody city.”

“But the others all get dragons?”

“I didn’t,” Elkville grumbled quietly.

“You’re way out in the sticks.  You’ve got a swarm of bunnyborgs to man the walls.  Whatever happened with that lumberjack guy?”

“He’s your new mayor of the town,” Mrs Sanderson said, looking up briefly from her notebook to glare at me.

“Kat’s taking notes as well.  You don’t both need to do it.”

“I’m writing the next screenplay, Bob.  I’m also very carefully not killing you for fucking up my premiere.”

“How was I supposed to know Vik was going to play me like that?  Besides, he kind of hinted that you and the gods had been fucking up the whole  manipulating me thing, and the lords of Hell had decided to step in?”

I left the question hanging.  She didn’t flinch, but she slowly put her pen down and removed her glasses to give me a one-eyed glare.

“You have a destiny to fulfil.  Within ten seconds of meeting you, I knew if I tried to push you, you’d only fight against it.  This is on you for being an ass, Bob.”

“Setting that aside…”  My voice was cold, my mental menagerie was cowering away from an incandescent Wrath.  “What exactly is this destiny?”

“Well, you already got the girl, so I guess all that’s left is to save the world?”

“I’m not the girl,” Esme hissed.

“No offence, Esme.  You’re a nice lady.  Much better than scale-brain deserves,” Kat replied.

The others had all gone quiet, the bickering and meowing turning to dumbfounded looks.

“Well, thanks.  I think,” Esme replied.

“What destiny?”  Mrs Sanderson’s voice cut off half a dozen other people who had opened their mouths.

Kat looked uncomfortable, shifting awkwardly in her chair in a way that would set hearts racing if she weren’t ten inches tall.  “There is an Unravelling… look, the Overgods don’t give two shits about pretty much anything.  I’ve never spoken to them; people who do talk to them tend to go insane unless they’re part of the WOO.  If a million worlds get unmade, they barely even notice.  But the WOO, my bosses, do notice.  Abarama is hardly what I’d call a caring deity, but he takes his job seriously.”

“Abarama?” I asked.

“My boss.  The guy in the WOO who sends me on these shitty assignments to play babysitter to random assholes.  No offence.”

“I’m struggling to think of a reason not to take offence,” I replied.

“Try harder.  Something is coming on Heltstat.  Shut up with the sniggering!  An event, an entity, we don’t know what, something is going to happen that will unmake, Unravel, this universe.  And that means a shit load of paperwork for my boss, so I was sent to help you stop it.”

“We’re all going to die?” asked Elkville quietly.  “Beville, I’m sorry I said you were a duck-nosed little bitch who couldn’t tell a good ale from swill.”

“When did you say that?” Beville asked in confusion.

“Oh.  I might not have said it to your face.  Anyway, I’m really sorry.”

“Who the fuck did you say that to?” he demanded.

“Well, there was mum, and Nekville, and—”

“Nekville?  He went rogue!”

“Fruit-based beers.  I know, he’s a barbarian,” she replied.

“She said the same to me,” offered possibly-Greville.

“You too?  You’re barely compe—”

“ENOUGH!”  They fell silent at my outburst.  “Kat, this is a little late in the game to be dropping something like this on me.  What do I need to do to stop this Unraveling?”

Helstat, for all its oddities, was home now.  I liked the place, and most of the people, barring ninjas, annoying guards, the Armaments Guild, the Hunters Guild, a handful of the gods, and ninety percent of the population.  That was still a huge improvement from Earth in my books.

I was also invested—emotionally and financially—in a number of people and places in this world.  My hand settled on Esme’s knee, and she gripped the back of it tightly.

“It’s not clear.  It’s not like we get given a roadmap, Bob.  Can you imagine how many moving parts there are in a world that can influence the way things play out?”

“I’m just a humble scale-brain, Kat.  You’re the one who hobnobs with the Big Gods.”

“It’s not like that,” she said quietly.  “I’ve done… forty-three of these missions.  I know the signs when the divine intervention isn’t going to work.  This world is doomed.”

I leaned back, ignoring the angry gasps from around the table.  Shinies and private time with Esme, those were all that mattered.  Lust and Greed tag-teamed Wrath, so that I could think clearly for a minute.

“How many of your missions have ended up in world-deaths?” I asked.

“Nine.”

“I don’t like those numbers, meow,” Heartflash whispered.

“So why are you obsessing about the premiere if the world is going to explode?” I wondered.

“It might not explode.  Sometimes, a power takes hold and runs the world into the ground, destroying it gradually.  Or it might be some Void Entity that swallows the universe in one gulp.”  She shook her head.  “But if the hero is a fuck up, it’s going to happen one way or another.”

“Perhaps if you had made this clear early on, we could be in a better position to deal with whatever it is,” I said in an acid tone.

“It can’t be some great evil thing.  What use, no disrespect, would a dragon be against something that eats universes?” Armand pointed out.

“I could kick its ass?” I snapped.

“Love.  Let’s be realistic, sweetie.”  Esme patted the hand that was resting on her thigh.  I hadn’t noticed it sliding up her leg.

“It must be some kind of worm in the apple.  Something that will rot the whole edifice from the inside out.”  Mrs Sanderson didn’t look up from her notebook as she spoke.

“That’s a big reach.  It could be bloody anything,” I groused.

“It makes more sense than some impossible woss-name come to devour the universe,” Pratnip pointed out.

Pinching the bridge of my mammal-nose with one hand, I held the other up.  “We don’t know, and we probably shouldn’t waste our time guessing.  “What was I supposed to do to prevent it, Kat?”

“Found the mmmph mmph.”

“The what?”

“The Primal Empire.”

“But that was already founded thousands of years ago.”

“I’m aware of that, Bob.”

“So re-found?  Make another one?  It ties up with the whole Hardprick thing.  Hang on, isn’t he supposed to re-found it?”

“Just the human part of it,” Kat said.

“So I need to get all the other races involved?  We’ve got the skuldrak, dwarves, dragons, some of the humans, some of the orlics… shit.  How many others are there?”

“Pixies, elves, salamanders, giants, and dwelvers,” Mrs Sanderson replied.

Jesus Christ.

“And how long do I have to get this all sorted?” I asked wearily.

“A couple of years?  Give or take,” Kat replied.

“When discussing the destruction of the universe, vagaries are about as useful as a chocolate fireguard.  In case you haven’t noticed, none of us will just blip back to our boss when we die.”

“Neither will I, this time.  I’ll be done with it all forever.”

The fatalism in her voice took me aback.  “How long have you been doing this?”

“Four thousand years, give or take.  It kind of blurs together a bit.”

“Hence the obsession with the movies?”  It really didn’t make sense to me.

“That was just to fill the time.  You get good at wasting years in my line of work.”

“Miss Kat, woof, you shouldn’t abandon hope yet,” Heartflash said.

“I’m not going quietly into the night,” growled Armand.

“All I had to work with was him.”  She waved a hand in my direction.  “This verse was doomed from the get-go.”

“Setting aside my personal feelings about that comment, you pixie asshole, now that I know, maybe there is something I can do with the information?”

“You’re too stingy with your gold, too obsessed with yourself.  You won’t make a good emperor,” she said sadly.  “Sorry, Bob, but this one is on you.”

A wave of power washed out around me.  The draconic aura that I’d never been able to control, or understood, flooded the room, pushing people back into their chairs.

“I guess I need to make some more friends and get the ones I’ve already got ready to face whatever is coming.  Armand, Pratnip, gird thyselves for war.”

“Gird? Cheep!” Heartflash added to their decisive nods.

“I am not.  Or I won’t be cheap anymore.  Another auction is in order, I think.  We’ll keep half a dozen Immortality Injections behind, and you lot are all getting the jab, like it or not.  The dungeon has… just shy of two billion gold.  I want the dungeon turned into a fortress and a retreat.  We need to be self-sufficient for food, water, bacon, everything.  And we should have the capacity to take in refugees.”

“It won’t help unless you bring all the species together,” Kat said.

“Then I’ll bloody well do that too.”  I met Esme’s eyes for a moment.  “This world isn’t dying on my watch.”

The weird territoriality that stemmed from my lizard-brain had taken over.  If I were responsible for this entire damn world, and possibly also the universe, that made it mine.  We all know how I feel about things being taken away from me.

Kat looked up, her unpatched eye brimming with tears.  “I didn’t think you had it in you.”

“I’ll start on the elves.  I’ve already got an in with them.  Although… shit, they’re going to want my entire bloody life story the next time I visit them.”

The pixie grinned, the imminent tears evaporating in an instant.  Had she just played me yet again?

“I knew I could count on you not to fuck this up.  You, beer merchants, we’re gonna need to drive traffic through your places, and a bunch of it is going to be disguised warriors.  You’ll get protection, don’t worry.  Milords and Lady, I’m going to send Tim and Simeon to your people.  We can make them better, and it won’t cost six million dollars.  Esme…”

“Yes?”

“Go bang Bob’s brains out.  He’s going to be rather busy for the foreseeable.”

Now that was a plan I could wholeheartedly get behind.  Esme threw her glass at Kat, who dodged it and flew through a portal, cackling.

“Well… It’s not a bad idea?” I offered.  I probably deserved the slap I received.
