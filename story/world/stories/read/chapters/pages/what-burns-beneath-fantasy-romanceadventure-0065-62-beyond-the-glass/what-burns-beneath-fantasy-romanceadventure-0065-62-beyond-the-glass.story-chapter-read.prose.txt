I froze.

The scream echoed faintly from within the manor, then abruptly died out. Chilling quiet settled around me.

I stared at the looming doors, eyes wide and knees locked into place. My fingers were iron-tight around the pail.

Ashes, I knew something bad was… Is he… fighting something? Hurting something? It sounded scared, whatever it was. Was it him? Another Fae? It couldn’t be a human, could it?

The awful thought drew me towards the dais. I paused after each step, breath held tightly, waiting for another scream to ring out.

Nothing. Nothing except the rustling of lush leaves and velvet petals.

The ivory felt cold beneath my feet. I drifted up, heart hammering, and set the pail down with a too-loud clink. Silence snaked around as I flattened one palm against the grand double doors.

They didn’t budge.

I tried again. Harder, with both hands, bracing the balls of my feet against smooth stone and grunting with the effort.

Nothing.

Did he lock me out? Does he not realize I’m here?

My knuckles rapped against the door. “Hello?” My uncertain call echoed off the stones.

There was no answer.

I wrung my cloak for a moment, then pattered back down the steps and towards a great, gleaming window. The manor’s foundation was raised, but I was just tall enough to peer inside.

Clear moonlight illuminated the familiar, vast foyer. Rich blue carpet with pearly thread lining its sides, a looming alabaster staircase swallowed by shadows at the top, and a central chandelier dripping with glittering white diamonds.

But no figures or movement. Just a still, empty space lit by silver light.

My gaze trailed down, eyes growing wide as they fixed on a trail of dark, spattered something along the floor further down the hall. Barely discernible amidst the shadows and flickering glow, but…

Salt, is that blood? What in the realms happened? Is it his?

I stared for a while, knuckles growing white against the stone. After more silence I crept along the outer wall, pausing at each window to rise on my tiptoes and peek inside.

Every view was more stunning than the last. I glimpsed lush parlors, decadent sitting rooms, and a massive library. Each was decorated with paintings of landscapes both beautiful and terrifying and sculptures formed in the graceful and hideous shapes of bizarre creatures I’d never seen. One parlor held shelves of sparkling bottles filled with colors so vivid I gasped in awe, unable to tear my eyes from the mesmerizing hues. At one point I came across an enormous space set only with polished black tile and a crimson, carpeted dais in the center. The shadows in that room seemed particularly deep.

One room held my attention for quite some time. The space was lit by opaque, glowing globes of lavender light that dangled from the ceiling. It almost resembled Brother Clem’s workspace, though on a much grander scale; it was filled with stone work tables, beakers and bottles, heavy leather-bound books, and shelves lined with pouches, plants, and oddly-colored jars.

Is this a… a Fae version of an apothecary workshop? What does he need it all for?

I drew back, giving my head a little shake. I suppose I could come up with thousands of questions about this place. And who knows, perhaps I’ll even live long enough to have some of them answered.

The next pane of glass revealed a room larger than my entire home. I peeked in and gasped. The space was composed almost entirely of a deep, black stone basin filled with steaming, sapphire-hued water. Crisp white rose petals drifted carelessly along the surface. I lingered at that window for quite a while. Peered in, biting my lower lip, wondering with a shiver what bathing in such luxury would feel like.

I wonder if this is how nobles in the great cities live. Huge rooms filled with all kinds of things that are beautiful, instead of practical. Wine bottles stacked to the ceiling, grand dance floors, baths you could swim in… it must be an incredible way to live.

Hold on. Is that why he always smells like roses?

My gaze traced a petal as it trailed through the vibrant water. I pressed my fingers against cool glass, imagining instead the feel of endlessly heated water lapping against my skin. After some time I sighed and reluctantly moved on.

The next window was extremely broad and tall, covering the manor’s back end and flooding the space within in silver light. I gripped the sill, rising up onto the balls of my feet. Ahead of me lay another grand staircase beyond a foyer set with burgundy carpet and lavish, dark chaises. Long hallways stretched out ahead and to both sides.

I’d scarcely peeked in when a door just down the main hall opened and a familiar figure stepped out. Velidan. Tall, broad-shouldered, skin aglow with moonlight and shoulder-length black hair swept back. A face of carved grace and arrogant beauty. He wore a navy vest and dark trousers, both of which shimmered like water beneath starlight.

The Fae shut the door behind him. Then, as if he could feel my gaze, he turned and looked directly at me.

I fell back from the window. My heart leapt into my throat as I stumbled over thick shrubs and tangling flowers.

Velidan stalked towards me. Mouth twisted downwards, hands clasped behind his back. He paused in the foyer, examining me through the glass as I regained my footing.

Then he just… watched me.

I tried, and failed, not to tremble beneath the attention. The pane of glass in between us was nowhere near enough. My instincts screamed for me to flee even as my knees turned to jelly.

And he just… stood there. Unmoving. Unreadable.

Ashes, I almost wish he’d… I don’t know. Burst through the glass and say something. Do something. What’s the point of just standing there?

The leaves around me rustled in a fresh breeze and a sweet smell hit my nostrils. I glanced to one side, where a head-height little tree swayed beneath the starlight. Its trunk was a thin, twisted mass of knobby black bark and its leaves were broad, flat, and hued like overripe eggplants. And beneath each leaf dangled a familiar little fruit.

I stood shivering for a while, cutting my gaze all around and then back to the figure looming ahead. But… nothing. He just watched, frowning thoughtfully.

Perhaps he’s trying to frighten me. The thought hit like a stone, bringing a swell of indignation with it. I cast a wary glance upwards as my brows knit together. Maybe the whole point is to intimidate me. Maybe he thinks it’s funny.

“Don’t you have anything better to do?” I breathed, one hand clenching into a fist.

There was, of course, no response. He continued to frown, and I began to frown back.

Well, if he’s just going to be all ominous and silent… saints, I can’t take this. I can’t very well get inside from here, but…

I crept to the tree, glancing over my shoulder a few times to see if he’d… I didn’t even know. Shatter the window and snarl for me to stay away from his precious plants.

He did not.

I plucked a fruit, rolled it uncertainly between trembling fingertips, then slowly paced back to the manor. One nail pierced thin, supple flesh as I reached the glass. I rubbed the juice along my fingers and, after a moment’s hesitation, reached up.

Velidan cocked his head to the side, the sneer fading in favor of a curiously arched brow. I tried not to look at him as I traced. Crystal-clear glass squeaked beneath my touch.

‘Can you’

I paused, lowering my hand. My teeth sank into my bottom lip.

What if he says yes? Then what? Will I spend the rest of my life being angry, hating myself for not thinking of it earlier? Should I even ask?

But… how can I not?

He stepped closer, nearly flush with the pane, and my heart stuttered. A quick glance up showed that he was scowling again.

Probably doesn’t like me making a mess on his fancy house. Well… too bad.

I squared my shoulders, dug my fingers into the fruit again, and continued to write. Scarlet smeared and squeaked, staining the glass.

‘Undo death?’

Perhaps there was a more eloquent way to phrase the question, but the little fruit was nearly bled dry. I let it fall to the grass and, holding my breath, peered up.

The Fae’s carved features remained cold as he read the words. If he was surprised by the question, he gave no indication. Eventually those fathomless eyes flicked to mine, narrowing ever so slightly, and he gave his head a slow shake.

I lowered my lashes. Right. Of course not. No one can be that powerful.

The silence stretched and pressed and, after a while spent observing the ground, I whispered, “What did I expect, anyways? That I’d run in and beg, try to offer him something for Terrence to be… saints. Dug up from his grave? Pieced back together? Just so he could go home to his mom and with all the memories of… of…” my voice hitched.

Of being dragged through the woods by people he thought were his friends? Brought face to face with a monster? Torn apart?

The leaves and blooms around me blurred. I lifted my clean hand, brushing away fresh tears.

And now he gets to watch me weep again. I drew the cloak tight, as if I could hide beneath it. Warm, soft fabric shrouded my shoulders, but a chill swept through me nonetheless. He probably enjoys it. Probably thinks it’s… entertaining.

Another scream rang out.

The sound jolted my spine straight. The cry was shrill and muffled, going on and on, and my pulse thrummed louder with every breath. I turned wide eyes towards the Fae.

Veldian’s glower returned. Red lips curled and too-sharp teeth glinted at me through the glass. He cocked his head to one side, night-black hair swaying down and dark brows drawing tightly together, as if listening with distaste. The wail continued, and his look of annoyance grew.

Then he met my eyes, waved one hand absently- almost as if he was shooing me- and stalked back to the dark door. He yanked it open and the sound grew slightly louder. It sounded vaguely feminine.

I staggered back, fully at a loss of what to do, as my captor disappeared inside the doorway and shut it behind him. A few breathless moments later, silence reigned.

I was left staring up in helpless, frightened isolation, kept company only by the murmuring of gentle wind through leaves.

What in the realms is going on?! Are there other Fae in the house? Some kind of… I don’t know, fight or argument? He said the High Fae meet, sometimes, and there’s been dissent among them… is that happening now?

I envisioned all the impossibly beautiful, cruel faces that had leered and laughed while I’d lain bound, bruised, bleeding on the balcony. Were some of them in the manor now?

The next thought came like a breath of fresh air.

He didn’t look angry. Well, not murderously angry. He didn’t rush out and kill me.

I scarcely had time to savor my relief before the sky began to swirl. Cool soil sifted beneath my feet, warm air breathed along my skin, and my haunting dream shivered into memory.
