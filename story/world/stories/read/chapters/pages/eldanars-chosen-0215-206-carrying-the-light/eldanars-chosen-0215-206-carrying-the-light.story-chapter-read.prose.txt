The sprint through the corrupted forest was a masterclass in pure, unadulterated panic.

They were no longer a methodical, heavily armoured rescue squad carving a careful path through the dark. They were a desperately fleeing column, running a horrific race against the encroaching, tireless tide of the undead horde. The freezing black mud, which had previously been a miserable nuisance, was now a deadly obstacle, actively trying to drag them down into the rotting earth with every single step.

Ronald and the lithe rogues took the lead, acting as the sharp point of the spear. The feline beastkin did not bother with stealth. He tore through the dense, thorny briars and dead undergrowth like a muted green blur, his twin hunting daggers flashing in the gloom. Whenever a wandering, corrupted forest stag or a stray skeletal hound stumbled into their path, the skirmishers did not break their stride. They simply bypassed the creatures' guards, slicing hamstrings and severing necrotic tethers before sprinting past, leaving the crippled monsters to be trampled by the heavy fighters bringing up the rear.

Elara ran right beside Ronald. The injured skirmisher was completely silent, her face a mask of chalky white determination. She held her steel hunting knife tightly in her left hand, whilst trying to ignore the burning agony radiating from her mangled, magically sealed right shoulder.

Behind the skirmishers came the mages and the wounded, forming the fragile, vulnerable centre of the column.

And they were failing to keep up.

The initial burst of adrenaline that had carried them up the burning rubble ramp was rapidly evaporating, violently replaced by the crushing, biological reality of severe magical and physical exhaustion. Robes designed for static defence were incredibly ill-suited for a dead sprint through a freezing swamp.

Kaelen, the broad-shouldered dwarven Stoneweaver, was practically using her massive granite staff as a crutch, vaulting herself forward with heavy, wheezing grunts. The human geomancer and the blue-robed mage were stumbling wildly over hidden tree roots, their boots entirely caked in thick, heavy layers of black sludge.

"Keep your knees up!" Brett ordered between ragged, desperate gasps.

The elemental mage was fighting his own brutal internal war. His pale face was covered in a sheen of cold sweat, his breath whistling harshly through his teeth. However, Brett possessed a deep, stubborn reservoir of willpower that often surprised the more physically inclined fighters. He kept his hands tucked close to his chest, forcing his long, elegant legs to keep pumping, refusing to let the heavy mud pull him down.

But the true concern was Carcan.

The elven healer had poured a terrifying amount of raw, concentrated holy magic into the wounded over the past few hours. She had purged the black sludge from Bhel’s chest, sealed Elara’s severed arteries, knit the lacerations on Josh’s face, and repaired the ruined flesh of the Iron Gauntlet tank.

Without the humming, latent energy of her magic to sustain her physical form, the exhaustion was crashing down upon her all at once. Her pristine pale green and white travel robes were now soaked heavily with freezing mud up to her knees. Her silver eyes were completely glassy, and she was swaying dangerously with every step.

"To our rear!" Perberos shouted from the back of the column.

The elven ranger was running backward, his liquid agility allowing him to effortlessly navigate the treacherous terrain without looking. He smoothly drew his bowstring back and loosed a heavy, steel-tipped broadhead into the suffocating darkness behind them. A loud, wet thud and a hollow shriek confirmed the kill.

The other archers mirrored his movements, firing blind volleys into the gloom to slow the pursuing horde.

Josh ran at the very back of the formation, shoulder to shoulder with the Iron Gauntlet tank. They were acting as the physical wall between the fragile casters and the snapping jaws of the dead. Josh swung his longsword in a wide, desperate arc, cleanly decapitating a wildly leaping Crypt Ghoul that had managed to slip past the arrow volley.

He kicked the crumbling corpse aside, his lungs burning like they were filled with crushed glass. He looked ahead at the struggling column.

The pace was dropping rapidly. What had started as a frantic sprint had degraded into a clumsy, staggering jog, and it was threatening to slow into a walk. The mages were simply out of breath.

Josh turned his head slightly, listening to the cacophony of noise echoing from the forest directly behind them. The snapping of dead branches, the squelching of mud, and the terrifying, unified clicking of a thousand skeletal jaws were growing steadily louder. The horde was not slowing down. They were closing the distance.

If they maintained this current pace, they were going to be overrun before they ever saw the watchfires of the camp.

Josh looked forward again, his storm-grey eyes locking onto Carcan. The elven healer stumbled heavily over a thick, moss-covered root. She let out a soft cry, dropping her amber-topped staff and falling hard onto her hands and knees in the freezing black sludge. She tried to push herself back up, her arms trembling violently under her own weight, but she simply did not have the strength left.

The decision was instantaneous.

Josh did not stop to consult or debate the tactical merits of the manoeuvre. He simply acted.

He reached over his shoulder and slammed his heavy steel shield onto the locking clasp attached to his backplate. The massive piece of metal secured itself with a loud, resounding clack, instantly freeing his left arm. He kept his electrified longsword gripped tightly in his right gauntlet.

He surged forward, his boots churning the mud, and closed the distance to the fallen healer in three massive strides.

"Josh, wait," Carcan gasped weakly, looking up at him with wide, exhausted silver eyes as he loomed over her. She reached a trembling hand out for her fallen staff. "I just need a second to catch my breath."

"We do not have a second," Josh replied, his voice a rough, uncompromising bark.

He did not wait for her permission. He stooped low, sweeping his left arm firmly behind her knees and behind her back, and scooped the elven healer smoothly out of the freezing mud.

Carcan let out a sharp, startled yelp as her feet left the ground. She was incredibly light, her slender elven frame weighing almost nothing compared to the dense, iron-clad bulk of the dwarf he had carried earlier. Josh smoothly shifted her weight, pulling her securely against his chest piece, his left arm locked tightly around her waist and legs.

"Hold on," Josh ordered, adjusting his grip on his longsword.

He immediately broke back into a dead sprint. He channelled all of his energy into his legs, heavily relying on the passive physical enhancements of his Juggernaut's Tread skill to carry the extra burden.

Carcan was completely mortified. A bright, furious blush rapidly spread across her pale, aristocratic cheeks, completely contrasting with her usual aura of profound serenity.

"Put me down, Josh!" Carcan protested, pushing weakly against his heavy steel breastplate with her muddy hands. "This is entirely unnecessary. I can run. I am a seasoned adventurer, not a delicate noblewoman in need of a rescue."

"You were crawling, Carcan," Josh corrected bluntly, not breaking his punishing stride. He easily caught up to Brett, matching the mage's exhausted pace. "And I am not leaving you in the mud for those monsters. So you can either hold still, or I will throw you over my shoulder. Your choice."

Carcan opened her mouth to deliver a sharp rebuke, but the words died in her throat as a rusted spear flew out of the darkness, burying itself deep into the trunk of a pine tree just inches from Josh's helmet.

She looked past his broad, steel-clad shoulder, staring back into the suffocating gloom of the forest. She could see the glowing green eyes of the pursuing horde, a terrifying, endless sea of monsters crashing through the undergrowth just thirty yards behind them.

The blush of embarrassment remained, but the protest instantly faded.

She was pressed tightly against the cold, unyielding steel of his breastplate, but beneath the thick metal and the padded gambeson, she could feel the steady, incredibly powerful thudding of his heart. She could feel the raw, rhythmic power of his massive chest rising and falling with every exhausted breath. He felt like an immovable mountain, completely solid and unbreakable amidst the chaotic, terrifying storm of the rotting forest.

Slowly, almost tentatively, Carcan stopped pushing against his armour. She let her tense muscles completely relax, sliding her muddy arms up to wrap them securely around his thick neck. She rested her cheek against the cold steel of his shoulder guard, closing her silver eyes and sinking entirely into his massive frame. She felt profoundly, undeniably safe.

"Well," Brett wheezed, looking over at Josh as the heavy fighter matched his pace while carrying a full-grown elven woman. "That is certainly one way to solve the logistical bottleneck."

The other heavy fighters at the back of the column instantly realised what Josh had done. They saw the necessity of the manoeuvre.

The massive Iron Gauntlet tank let out a loud roar, stowing his ruined rectangular shield. He practically lunged forward, grabbing the stumbling human geomancer by the thick collar of his robes and throwing the screaming man unceremoniously over his heavily armoured shoulder like a sack of grain.

A scarred mercenary to his right grabbed the blue-robed mage, hauling the caster off his feet and tucking him under his arm. Even Kaelen, the stubborn dwarven Stoneweaver, allowed a towering heavy infantryman to scoop her up, though she muttered dark, colourful dwarven curses the entire time.

The effect on the retreating column was immediate and completely transformative.

With the exhausted casters entirely removed from the physical equation, the pace of the group skyrocketed. They transitioned from a clumsy, staggering jog back into a terrifying, unified sprint. The heavy fighters, possessing vast reserves of physical stamina and high strength statistics, simply absorbed the extra weight and powered through the clinging mud.

"Pick up the pace!" Ronald roared from the front, glancing over his shoulder and noticing the sudden surge in their speed. The beastkin grinned a feral, fanged smile. "The treeline is less than half a mile away! Don’t slow down!"

They tore through the final stretch of the corrupted forest like a runaway freight train.

The rangers remained at the very back of the formation, running and firing in perfect, mechanical unison. Perberos was a silent instrument of death, his heavy arrows finding the glowing green eye sockets of the fastest skeletal hounds that tried to close the gap. The other rangers covered the flanks, keeping the scuttling Crypt Ghouls at bay with a constant rain of steel.

Josh focused entirely on his breathing. His left arm ached with a dull, persistent fire, but Carcan’s presence was a steady, grounding weight against his chest. He tightened his grip around her, his heavy boots churning the black sludge, refusing to yield a single fraction of an inch to the fatigue burning in his blood.

Slowly, the suffocating, pitch-black darkness of the dead canopy began to break.

The thick, thorny briars thinned out, and the ground beneath their boots began to slowly slope upward. The cloying, oily stench of rotting meat and stagnant water was suddenly cut by a sharp, freezing blast of clean winter wind.

"We’re clear!" Elara screamed from the front, her voice cracking wildly with absolute, profound relief.

The skirmishers burst violently through the final line of dead pine trees, stumbling out onto the wide, frost-covered dirt path that led directly to the forward operating camp.

Josh broke through the treeline a few seconds later. The sudden transition from absolute darkness to the ambient twilight of the open plains was blinding. He kept running for another fifty yards, putting solid, uncontested ground between them and the dark, silent forest, before finally allowing his heavy boots to slow to a halt.

He stood in the middle of the dirt road, his chest heaving with massive, ragged gasps. He lowered his electrified longsword, resting the heavy steel tip in the dirt.

The rest of the rescue squad spilled out of the forest behind him. The heavy fighters unceremoniously dumped their exhausted magical cargo onto the freezing grass. The archers maintained a loose defensive line facing the trees, their bows drawn, but the undead horde did not immediately pursue them out into the open. The monsters seemed perfectly content to remain lurking just within the shadows of the rotting canopy, their glowing green eyes watching the humans with silent, terrifying malevolence.

Josh looked down at Carcan. She was still holding tightly to his neck, her silver eyes closed, her breathing finally slowing to a normal rhythm.

"You can open your eyes now, Carcan," Josh murmured softly, his hoarse voice rumbling deep within his chest. "We are out. We made it back."

Carcan slowly opened her luminous silver eyes. She looked around at the open twilight sky, the frost-covered road, and the distant, glowing watchfires of the massive camp. She blinked, rapidly processing that the horrific sprint was actually over.

Then, she realised exactly how tightly she was clinging to Josh's neck, and how entirely she had relaxed into his arms.

The furious, brilliant blush instantly returned, turning the tips of her pointed elven ears a bright, vibrant crimson. She scrambled wildly against his chest piece, suddenly desperate to find the ground.

"Right. Yes. Thank you, Josh," Carcan stammered uncharacteristically, completely losing her serene composure as she practically jumped out of his left arm. Her boots hit the solid dirt road, and she took a rapid, clumsy step backwards, frantically trying to smooth out her ruined, mud-caked travel robes with trembling hands. She refused to meet his storm-grey eyes, staring intensely at a small patch of frozen grass near his steel boots. "Your assistance was... highly efficient. I appreciate the intervention."

Josh stood up straight, stretching his aching left arm. He could not help the small, genuine smile that tugged at the corner of his mouth, completely amused by her sudden, flustered embarrassment.

"Anytime, Carcan," Josh replied smoothly, keeping his tone perfectly neutral.

Before the quiet, slightly awkward tension between the two of them could settle, a loud, dramatic coughing fit interrupted them.

Brett stumbled up the road, coming to a halt beside Josh. The mage was bent double, resting his hands on his knees as he fought to drag freezing air into his burning lungs. He looked like he was about to collapse into the dirt, his pale face flushed with severe exertion.

Brett slowly turned his head, looking up at Josh through his messy, unkempt blonde hair.

"Yeah, thanks for the lift, mate," Brett gasped sarcastically, forcing a weak, incredibly witty smirk onto his face. "My legs were feeling entirely neglected anyway. I really appreciated the opportunity to build some character while you carried the healer."

Brett offered a perfectly timed, highly exaggerated wink, then immediately turned and began limping away towards the safety of the camp watchfires, leaving Josh to stare after him in exasperated disbelief.

Josh shook his head, a genuine laugh finally breaking through his exhaustion. He turned back to Carcan, opening his mouth to offer an apology for his friend's terrible timing, but the words were cut short as Perberos joined them on the dirt path. The elven scout did not look relieved. He looked deeply, profoundly troubled.

Perberos pointed silently back towards the edge of the forest.

Josh followed the ranger's gaze, and his brief moment of levity completely vanished.

The dark, unnatural corruption of the forest was not static. The thick, foul-smelling black mud, the dead, grey moss, and the suffocating necrotic fog were physically creeping out from beneath the canopy. The corruption was actively eating the healthy, frost-covered grass of the plains, spreading like a slow, terrifying infection toward the dirt road and the camp beyond. The ambient dark magic of the horde was literally rotting the world as they marched.

"The basin is overflowing," Perberos stated quietly, his silver eyes fixed on the creeping black sludge. "My bet is that the boss monster is moving his army."

As if summoned by the grim reality of the situation, the heavy, rhythmic thud of a marching column echoed from the camp.

Caistina Tharistrar emerged from the safety of the perimeter watchfires, flanked by a dozen heavily armed Ashenfall city guards. The elven mage trainer looked completely composed, her sapphire-blue robes immaculate, but her amethyst eyes were hard and entirely devoid of warmth. She carried her pristine white staff firmly in her grip.

She walked quickly down the dirt path, assessing the battered, bloody state of the rescue squad with a single, sweeping glance. She noted the massive, creeping stain of corruption bleeding out from the treeline, her expression tightening significantly.

"I see you found the missing party," Caistina stated, her melodic voice carrying an uncompromising, sharp authority. She did not offer praise or relief. "I am glad you are all breathing. But we do not have the time to celebrate your survival."

Caistina turned and pointed her staff back towards the massive, bustling encampment.

"The wagons are fully loaded, as are the wounded. The civilian labourers have already begun the march back to the city," Caistina announced, delivering the final, terrifying verdict. "The camp is officially abandoned. Gather your remaining gear and fall in with the rearguard. We begin the retreat to Ashenfall in exactly ten minutes."
