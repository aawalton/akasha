I’d never seen such a delicious-looking meal.

There were roasted, braised, and charred meats; a gilded bowl full of dew-fresh fruits in all the shades of a sunset; steaming bronze bread that smelled like a campfire; cheese the color of a rising sun; glittering amber wine in a crystal glass.

All of it sat upon a table carved from thin, marbled porcelain. Four silver-wrought chairs gleamed around it, but thankfully the Fae was the room’s only occupant. He sat with a book propped open beside a plate of half-eaten food.

I spent an awestruck moment taking in the feast, vivid tapestries, plush emerald rug, and shining chandelier before rushing forwards.

“I need your help!”

He seemed to be chewing a mouthful of food. One eyebrow rose, speaking volumes.

“Please, I mean. Look, I’m… you told me to run. Save myself. Well, I didn’t. And now I need something that can help me kill a Fae.” I squared my shoulders, somehow managing to keep my voice even. “Or you’ll never see the shard.”

Endless eyes narrowed. He stared at me for several heartbeats.

“If I get devoured or torn apart, you’ll never know where to-”

He took a sip of wine, then pointedly resumed reading.

My mouth went dry. This had gone much better in my head. “Didn’t you hear me? If you don’t help-”

“I heard you.” He turned a page.

“Well… don’t you care?”

“It’s not a matter of caring. Tools to kill Fae are rare. Precious. If it’s a lesser creature, some witchwood should suffice. Go find a tree.”

“I did,” I snapped, hands twisting at my borrowed cloak. The fabric was sturdy and thick, but coarse. “It’s been torn apart! Killed! And the plants around it are all rotted and… I don’t think it’ll be of any use.”

“How unfortunate. I have no aid to offer.” The Fae leaned back, gesturing towards a nearby chair. “So we can be done with the extortion attempt. Sit.”

I remained standing, blood thrumming through my ears. “No aid that you can offer, or no aid that you're willing to offer?”

“Both.” He rose, lips pressed into a thin line and tone thick with displeasure. “I’ve already helped you. Several times. Yet I’ve received nothing in return. My weapon remains missing, and you continue to feign ignorance.”

“Feign…” What is he talking about? Our time earlier, in his garden… it was nice. Pleasant. What changed? I sank into an ornate chair. Metal pressed against my skin, chilly even through my fresh clothes. “I just thought… I’d hoped… Look, someone’s life is at stake! I can’t hand over your weapon right now, but if there’s things you want to ask, I’ll answer. Honestly. I promise.” I peered up, teeth sinking into my lower lip, ready to beg if that’s what was needed. “Just… please. Please. Teela needs help, and I don’t even know where to begin.”

“Tell me, then. Who is helping you?” he breathed, searching my face hungrily.

“What?”

“Perhaps you haven’t been given a name. That’s no surprise. But tell me what they look like.”

My nose scrunched. “Do you mean Renner? He left this morning, and ‘helping’ is a pretty generous-”

“Brin, what Fae is helping you?”

The statement reminded me of our very first encounter, when he’d seemed to think I’d already made a pact with one of his kind. I shook my head in baffled, weary frustration. “Well, clearly not you.”

He did not seem amused.

“You already asked about this. About me and other… I’ve only ever made a bargain with you.”

“Liar.” The word came out with bladelike sharpness.

Heat bolted up my throat. “I’m not lying! Why would I?”

He began pacing. Cloth rustled, but besides that the large room was nearly silent for a long while. When he finally spoke every word was low and hurried, as if he was speaking more to himself than to me. “I only suspected it during our first encounter. When you withdrew from me… and had the gall to feign confusion.”

“It’s not feigned,” I growled back, “I’m actually confused.”

“Brin. I’m not a fool.”

“I didn’t say you were.”

“Then I’m to believe you resisted the glamour of your own power, then?” He scoffed, looking me up and down with all the regard one would give an insect.

Withdrew, resisted… It took me a long moment to parcel out his meaning. The memory of falling endlessly into those beautiful eyes, of the heady scent and gentle dark of him all around me…

Then pulling away. Waking, as if from a dream.

“Maybe you’re just not as powerful as you think you are,” I huffed, crossing my arms.

He fixed me with a withering stare.

Before I realized quite what was happening I scowled upwards and added tartly, “My lord.”

Full, red lips parted. Black brows rose.

For a moment, I wondered if I’d gone too far. I held my breath, muscles going rigid, and looked up nervously.

But it was something very different than anger dancing across the Fae’s features. A thousand pinpoints of silver glittered down as he took a slow, deliberate step forwards. “Are all humans this insolent?”

“Probably not.” My pulse gave an unsteady thrum.

“Mm. My good fortune, then.” The Fae’s mouth twitched. Dark hair hissed along silk as he tilted his head, gaze narrowing. “And I suppose next you’ll say it was mere chance that someone tried to rip you away after your ill-conceived attempt on my life?”

I blinked, repeating the bizarre question in my head several times. “Chance that… someone… what?”

“Don’t be coy, Brin. It’s only charming for so long.”

My cheeks burned. “I’m not being coy! I’m… saints, I said I’d give you answers, and I’m trying to! But I don’t understand what you’re asking about!”

“You’re such an earnest thing.” He stepped closer, searching my face with furrowed brows. His expression was almost hungry. “I could almost believe you.”

I raked both hands through my already-tousled hair. “What’s the point of asking me questions if you don’t believe anything I say?”

The question earned a scowl. He turned away, bracing both palms against the table. Rather than answer he breathed a little growl, muttering almost to himself, “It’s my own fault. I underestimated you. I thought you were nothing more than a frightened mortal desperate to save her friend.”

“That… no, that was correct. You correctly, um, estimated me.”

“Oh, your fear was genuine. You reeked of it, and I never dreamed you’d be brazen enough to lie. Not well, at least.”

Cold metal dug into my spine as I sagged against the chair. “Back to this again? Look, you haven’t asked anything worth lying about. Mostly you’ve been… I don’t even know. Asking me what I’m doing, I suppose.”

“Well, you keep doing strange things.” The ghost of a smile graced his lips, but the expression quickly hardened. “But do you really expect me to believe that there’s no one aiding you? And powerful, I’d wager, since they very nearly tore you away.”

“You said that before.” I rubbed my temples. “I still don’t understand what you mean.”

Cloth rasped as the Fae rose to his full height, moving to practically tower over me. “I mean, Brin, that after you stabbed me, another Fae tried to pull you back to the mortal realm.” He watched me intently, doubtlessly waiting for my reaction to give something away.

All he got was a baffled shake of my head. “What? You mean when everything went sideways? The world spinning? I thought that was you.”

Full, red lips thinned. “It wasn’t.”

“Look, besides you, all the other Fae I’ve met tried to kill me. Or didn’t care that I existed.” Round, golden eyes gleamed from the back of my mind. I shuddered. “Or were… too polite to eat me.”

He was silent for a very long moment. Then, in a tone so icy it made my shoulders curl, “You promised honesty only moments ago, Brin.”

“I am being honest! Look, I… there’s…” I hesitated, flicking my gaze downwards. I need his help. I need him to trust me. He doesn’t need to know about the runebook, but perhaps I can give him something… “There’s… another possibility.”

“Oh?”

“Yes. There’s another explanation for… for all this. Why your… glamour, or whatever it was, didn’t work on me. Why something tried to wake me up last night. If that was someone or something trying to help me, then…” I rose, shoulders squared, and said evenly, “I believe it was one of the gods.”

He didn’t bat an eyelash. “One of the gods.”

“Yes. I’m… a runekeeper.” I waited, heart hammering, for his reaction.

He looked unimpressed. Silence crawled into the room.

“It’s someone who’s been blessed. Gifted with divine magic from-”

“I’m aware of what a runekeeper is, Brin. I’m also aware that they are… exceedingly rare. Practically extinct.”

Rather than be dissuaded by his obvious doubt, I lifted my chin. “Maybe that’s true, but here I am.”

The Fae’s broad chest swelled and he looked up, as if searching for patience. “Surely you can think of a more convincing falsehood. Runekeeper.” He scoffed. “Perhaps next you’ll claim to be a fierce warrior, or a member of some glamorous human nobility.”

Blood rushed to my face as I glared at him, arms folded.

He took in my stance with a mocking sneer. “You seem upset, mortal. Did you want to continue this particular lie? Fine. Show me your magic, runekeeper.”

“It’s not a lie! And I… I can’t.”

“Of course you can’t. Perhaps your next falsehood will be more-”

“It doesn’t work here!” I stamped one boot onto the carpet, feeling both incredibly childish and wildly indignant. “But I… I can make fire! And little rain showers! And I can change how food tastes. I’ve been doing that since I was twelve. I can make light, too- that’s how I frightened your dog. That’s what made him let go of Royce.”

With every word, the obvious scorn faded from the Fae’s handsome face. Uncertainty rose in its place. “You… can’t.” He actually took a step back, looking me up and down.

“Look, you wanted some kind of explanation, and that’s all I’ve got! And even if you don’t believe any of it, I think it makes sense. And… and do you know…” I lifted a hand to my mouth, the tide of indignation swallowed by sudden thoughtfulness. “That happened twice. That feeling, like everything was spinning and I was being pulled away… It happened in the cave, too.”

“The cave?” He watched my face intently. Mouth tight, fingers drumming on the tabletop.

“Yes, the night I ran into the mist. First I fell asleep, and I was here. In your garden. Well, mostly. I crawled into the woods, and then I found your house, and then… it was like something yanked me awake. Was it because I was in danger?” I paced, nearly breathless with sudden excitement. It felt like the pieces of a puzzle were falling into place, creating a most spectacular image. “Maybe it’s some… remnant of divine power? Trying to help me. Trying to protect me.”

The fallen gods. Fallen, but unyielding. Vigilant.

The Grace that flowed unending. The sun that rose every morning in a blaze of guarding light.

The silver trees that knew me.

My heart pounded with every eager thought. “And last night, don’t you see? It wasn’t because I stabbed you! I heard things, just a few moments later. When I was on the floor. Awful things… fingers against the window, and something stepping on the floorboards. There was a Fae outside our room! That’s why something tried to wake me up!”

I stopped pacing and whirled, beaming up at the Fae. “It’s some… some echo of their power, trying to bring me home! And I bet that’s why the runes… why they’re just there, sometimes! Just behind my eyes! Why I think of them when I don’t even know what they do, but somehow it’s always right. They call up water when I’m tending to plants, or fire when I’m in danger. It’s because there’s… there’s… intent behind them!”

My smile was so wide it hurt.

He gazed down, seemingly lost in thought. Silence crawled around us until he frowned, giving his head a little shake, and murmured again, “Such an earnest thing.”

The response muffled my enthusiasm. Not in full, but enough for me to let my chin fall a bit. “Right. You don’t believe me.” I heaved a sigh, scrubbing at my face. “So where does this leave us? You’re convinced I’m lying and just going to… not help me? At all?”

Silence.

My throat constricted. “Great. So… so all of this was for nothing. I’m a fool and I’ve wasted time and I’ve probably hurt myself because I dared to hope that maybe you were telling the truth this whole time! That maybe you’re actually good and kind and all the salt, but-”

“Brin.” His voice was soft. Satin and rose petals again.

“What?!” I slammed a palm down onto the table, rattling the silverware. “Salt, I’m such a fool! And I… I need to go back. Teela needs me. Who knows what’s happening while I’m here trying to… Saints! Wake up, wake up-!” I clawed at my arms. Hot pain blossomed on my skin and my nails left red, angry streaks, but it did nothing to jolt me awake.

“Stop.”

Under different circumstances, the sharpness of that tone might have made me freeze. Now, though…

“NoI” I raked at my scalp, close to screaming. “No one else is even looking for her! Renner left! He said he’d tie me up and I had to give him-”

“Stop.” Impossibly strong hands seized my wrists, drawing them between us. “You’ll wake in your own time.”

I jerked back, trying to tear free. My hips hit the table, making silverware clatter once more, but his hold didn’t relent. “That’s not good enough! Don’t you understand?! She’s alone, she’s all alone, and no one even knows… Whatever this monster-”

“It sounds like a hag.”

I stopped struggling. “What?”

“A hag. Their presence withers life away.”

Frenzied panic gave way to a desperate need for clarity and the sudden, fierce hope that he was going to help. “A hag. A hag. Okay, what does that mean?”

Seemingly satisfied that I was done trying to claw my skin off, he released me with a little shove. “It means your friend is likely alive, though whether she wishes to be is an entirely different matter.”

The blood drained from my face. “Gil said it was going to eat her.”

“This ‘Gil’ was correct. But hags are not powerful enough creatures to prey on a whim, and they need flesh for sustenance, not mere indulgence. She’ll take her time.”

I stared up at him, hope blossoming like warmth in my chest. “Does… does this mean you’re going to help me?”

Carved features twisted into a frown. “I’d rather you not die until you’ve returned my weapon.”

A little laugh choked its way up my throat. “I’d rather not die at all.”

“Then abandon your-”

“No.”

He chuckled.

“Can you…” I held my breath. Wrung my hands, pleading up with wide eyes. “Please, please, if you give me a weapon, something to hurt her with, a way to find her, anything, I’ll return it. I promise.”

“This is a home built for luxury, Brin. A place where I am the only weapon needed.”

A pit opened in my stomach. “So there’s nothing you can do?”

He spent a long time studying my expression. Then, hesitantly, “If it were nighttime, perhaps-”

“I can’t wait that long. Teela might not have that long.”

A crease formed between the Fae’s brow. “You’re not making this easy, mortal. But,” his voice sharpened alongside his gaze, “if what you claim about having magic is true, why not defend yourself with that?”

I sagged against the table with a groan. “I’ll try. Of course I’ll try, but I’ve already done so much today! The runes hurt and I don’t-”

“Magic hurts you?” Uncharacteristic confusion flashed across his features.

I spread my hands, fighting the rising tide of helplessness. “Sometimes. Sometimes it just makes me sick. That’s why I’m here. I used a rune, a new one, on the witchwood, because I wanted to ask you for help. I fainted.”

The mystified expression grew with every word. The Fae searched my face, looking as though he couldn’t believe what I was saying. “What…” he sounded like he couldn’t believe what he was saying, either. “What was the… rune?”

I hesitated, but only for a moment. Teela’s life was worth far more than my uncertainty. “Luridel. I don’t know what it does.”

The Fae’s jaw dropped. “Mortal, what-” The room shivered around us. He gave his head a frustrated shake and stepped closer, so that we were practically touching. “Fine. Tell her, then. Hags are cowards. Warn her of your magic. Threaten her.”

The ceiling started to spin. “I’ll try, but-”

“And if that doesn’t work…” Startled heat licked at my insides as he reached up, threading strong fingers into my hair. His voice lowered to a purr, to silk and smoke and the petals of a black rose, and the glint in his eyes was positively wicked. “Then tell her you belong to a High Fae. One who takes vengeance in blood.”
