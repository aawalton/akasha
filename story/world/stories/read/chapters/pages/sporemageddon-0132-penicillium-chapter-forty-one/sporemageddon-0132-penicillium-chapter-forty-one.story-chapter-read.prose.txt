Chapter Forty-One

The next week or two were busy. For me, they were hell. I kept upping the dosages on the poisons I was using while tending to my farm. It meant that on most days I was sick with something. Sweats, fever, the shakes, too often digestive issues or strange audible or visual hallucinations. Sometimes breathing issues, though I tried to avoid anything that caused those. My lungs were fragile enough as it was.Meanwhile, things in City Nineteen heated up and then calmed down.The factories that had been sold off were rebought. Industry slowly filed back into the city, taking advantage of people who were desperate for work, regardless of the cost.A goblin pen had been set aflame at some point, and from what I heard, a few hundred died in the fires. It meant that some of the cheap labour that they had brought was gone and now there was more room for human workers.Sickening, but not unexpected.I watched it all from my little corner while raising new, more potent mushrooms, while tentatively dipping my toes into alchemy by mixing poisons together to see if their combined effects would stack, and while working hard to grow my skills.It took all that time before I finally started to approach my goal. I even gained a level in [Knitting].The situation outside calmed itself bit by bit. The riots settled down, in part because the previous riots didn't help, and because it was hard to stay angry on an empty stomach. The news that I could get, journals and newspapers from the nicer parts of the city that I paid Bet to grab for me, didn't report on the casualties, just on the loss of income.I wished someone would take a picture of the streets lined with open doors, bodies piled up on the sides for the Brothers of Dearil to pick up.In the end, it felt like none of it really mattered. The wheels of industry chugged on.And so did I.
