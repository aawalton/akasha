Clack! The sound of bones breaking and meat being grinded could be heard all over the mountain as the wind gave sound to death. I can't think about it. Harry thought to himself. It's not a graveyard, it's merely a... it's merely a... But he couldn't think of anything else. For this was a cemetery. And each step he took proved that once more.

The image of the dead kid still lingered in his mind like a shark circling a raft. The Locksmith shook the thought away but it came back. Sometimes even stronger.

Swoosh! A rock hit his ankle with such force he almost fell into the sea of Metas that preserved the bodies of the prisoners. Harry knelt down and conjured a couple doors to protect himself while he took care of the wound. The young thief ripped off his tie and tied it into his ankle. It seemed to have dulled the pain as he continued, constantly looking for his companions.

What had happened to Goldman? Or even Petrikov? Were they alive? The Locksmith didn't know.

All of a sudden, however, he felt a blow to the back of his head. Sliding through the snow, he felt another blow, this time more sharp; a kick. Fuckin' hell! He added inwardly, but kept being thrown into the ground.

"I'm sorry, Harry." A raspy female voice could be heard. "But I can't let you keep fucking with my plans. I'll be the one to reach the top and my father will be saved, okay? You know only one of us gets a happy ending. "

Prism!? But the Locksmith was too injured to properly acknowledge her. It was her voice, however. One filled with despair, anger, and somehow sadness all over like an executioner lamenting the death of a condemned man.

He had killed before. Never for personal revenge, never out of desire. But he knew the feeling. It didn't please him, and he could live without it. A part of him felt disgusted by this feeling, but the other part, however, was so enraged that it didn't care about its moral values.

"You can't stay invisible forever." Harry said in a weakened voice, breaking Prism's monologue. The woman, before she could answer, dropped her body onto the snow as she fled before anyone saw them.

And then he rested against the snow.

"...Harry..." He heard a distant voice calling his name. Familiar, the voice aimed to bring him back and delay his passage to the other side.

He was no stranger to this feeling of confusion, of impaired mental faculties. He forced his eyes open, but remained staring into the darkness. His body no longer responded. He could still feel the porous texture of the snow that surrounded him. The wind that had once cut his cheeks now felt like a light breeze. Was he losing himself in the process, or was this a wake-up call? Freedom, at last.

"...Wake up!" The voice once again shook his body as he seemed to regain control. He remembered his brother. He remembered how he left Matthews to fend for himself when they were younger. He had to make this worthwhile; he couldn't give up. The Locksmith then grabbed his soul and shook it. Oh, actually no. That was just Petrikov pushing him.

The first thing he saw in his blurry, narrow vision was possibly the most beautiful sight he had ever seen. Entwined in the sky like vines under a wall, a pattern was taking shape. One that was about to collapse upon him. What was the motivation behind all this? Suddenly he felt a curiosity about the entire structure of the tower. About its floors, about the Metas. About the Emblems. He knew the Astronomer had something to do with it all, and he imagined that everything was connected.

"Harry! You're awake!" He heard the familiar voice again and opened his eyes. He sat up with a groan and put a hand behind his head. Ouch.

He glanced to the side and saw a small man with a mustache that seemed out of place with his face. Or perhaps it was too much of a match. "...Petrikov?" He whispered as he approached.

"Yes." The man was sitting in an awkward position. "I thought you were dead. By the gods. You really are a survivor, young man!"

"What are you doing here? Why are you doing this? Go away! Go to the next floor!" Although his head was still spinning, he realized his condition wasn't as severe as it seemed. Not compared to after his fight against Dracno. He was dizzy and his head ached, but he was alive. That's what mattered.

"Why am I here?" The professor replied, almost offended. "What a silly question, boy. Don't worry about me. Come on, let us go upstairs, we have to help you get up there!"

Harry then stood up, wondering why Petrikov was holding Vela's candelabrum and why Vela had disappeared. Weren't they together? Where is everyone? I don't see anyone... Everywhere he looked, he saw only a mass of stones, snow, and rocks. He looked a little further ahead, but the mist quickly obscured his vision; the artificial snow of the Metas.

"Where's Vela?" Harry asked, now standing. "And why are you holding his candelabrum?"

"Look, Harry..." Petrikov said as he avoided the young thief's gaze, his voice now trembling. "We separated. The mist is too thick and... I lost my track. The rocks don't seem to be raining so much now, perhaps because the bigger ones are already below. I thought I saw you falling down and quickly came to your rescue. What happened? Were you hit by something?"

"You could put it like that..." The Locksmith decided not to add any more drama into the conversation. "You said they aren't a problem?"

"Y-Yes!" Petrikov continued. "It's been a few minutes already. I think what was going to fall has already fallen. That's enough of that, Harry. We have to get to Goldman. I'm afraid we have a few minutes before the damned mountain causes more damage."

"Wait..." He grabbed Petrikov's shoulder, who looked at him confused. "Why are you doing this? Why did you come back to help me?"

As if he didn't know what empathy and compassion meant, Harry looked curiously at Petrikov's face. Why not save himself? He wondered. He had known the man for no more than a cycle and a half, and yet, he had come to help him while the others didn't even notice his disappearance, probably due to the chaos. Is this what trust really means? He thought about the word, truly. One he had forgotten a long time ago.

In the boxed-up recesses of his mind, he remembered a time when he was loved. These memories came with the smell of home-cooked food and tender laughter. He had never forgotten. He had only chosen to ignore it.

Since arriving at the tower, he had found a group of people ready to defend him. People who wouldn't take advantage of his weaknesses. There was Prism, of course, who had manipulated him in so many ways that just remembering the invisible woman made him tremble with rage.

For some reason, however, this betrayal made this moment even more important. A reminder that for a shadow to exist, there must be a light. That's what Harry chose to believe, at least. It was naive, but necessary.

"We're together, aren't we?" He said simply, a smile on his face. Then he turned and continued on his way, each step a battle. Petrikov, after all, was a warrior.

Even while helping the professor, they felt they were getting further and further away. The snow, though soft, was dense and seemed to slow their progress. From time to time they had to stop so Petrikov could catch his breath. I know, friend. I know how it is. Harry gave him an apologetic smile as he patiently waited for Petrikov. The mountain drew closer.

Near a berm, the two took refuge while Petrikov carefully sat down in the snow.

"Damn it..." he gasped, maintaining his smile. "You know the worst part of all this? No matter how much exercise I did... I'd never be in shape. Like they came from hell, the fat would just go straight back to my belly. Hahaha..."

He continued to laugh wearily, his spirit as fragile as his body. Harry, feeling the oppressive air closing in on them, wondered if they would make it in time. It didn't matter if he helped Petrikov; it seemed they were never fast enough. He took a deep breath and sat down next to the man.

He tried to use his doors, but because the view ahead was so low, he couldn't throw them very far, maybe just a few meters in front of him. This, combined with the fact that he still had to help Petrikov, quickly tired the Locksmith.

"Do you know why I call them Meta?" Petrikov asked.

"Um..." He was speechless for a second. "Of course. Meta. Metamorphosis."

"Metanoia." He said, as if that explained everything. "It represents almost an ontological change. Essential. A transformation, you know?"

Before Harry could add anything, he continued.

"They think, young Harry. The Metas." He said, his voice intense. "They pretend not to hear. They pretend not to understand, but I've seen it, I know that deep down there's something. Something that transforms and molds itself. A shape that soon takes the form of consciousness. They learn, and they remember. They're childlike, you know? Almost as if they've only recently realized what they are."

"Petrikov, I—"

"That's why I stayed." A tear colder than snow fell near his smile. "It's for them. I can't get out of here, I've already accepted that. Those children? Now I know they were brutally murdered. What kind of place is this that allows this kind of death? And to think they were so close to me... For a few seconds, each cycle, I could lie to myself and say that the Metas had a piece of them. That I could teach them the same way I did out there."

"...What do you mean by all this, Petri?" Harry frowned now, holding Petrikov's hand tightly. "Stop talking that shit. You're going to get out of here. We are going to get out of here. That way, you'll go back to Drumond and you'll get through this like a warrior. You remember love, and that's what matters. Now come on, get up and—"

"Don't you understand?" He pushed Harry's hand away and stood up without the help of the candelabrum. "There's no way! I can't keep up the same pace! Vela almost died trying to help me. I'm not going to make the same mistake with you, Harry. You're young, you have a chance to fix things."

"What are you talking about?" Harry stood up and started shouting. "You're young too! Surviving here means nothing! A life isn't counted by its days, but by its moments. Damn it, Petrikov! Don't pretend that—"

"No, Harry!" He took a step back. "It's no use. We both know we won't get there if you stay with me. Look at me, boy... Don't you understand I don't want to live anymore? I spent years inside that damned cave, telling a lie I never believed. They put me inside this Tower, and that's where I'm going to die. But not alone. I'm going to die knowing I had you as my companion. I'm going to die on my own terms."

The young man, at that moment, looked to the side while his face contorted. No... No. No! And he thought about the possibilities. He knew he was telling the truth; they would never reach the top with Petrikov in this state.

"I..." Harry felt his eyes welling up. "Don't do this, man. He'll never forgive you."

"I know..." he said with a weak smile. "But at least he has you and Daniel, doesn't he?"

Harry chuckled as he hugged the little professor. He felt his pain. He understood his loss. When he let go, he took one last look at his face and, for a moment, realized that his mustache suited his features. It gave him tenderness.

The young thief then turned and continued on his way, ignoring the cold inside and outside his body. Did he have a chance? Could this not have happened? He thought about the possibilities. He thought about how he fell for Prism's trick and how things could have turned out if he had realized her true nature sooner. It was obvious, wasn't it? And perhaps it always had been. But she had introduced him to the concept of hope. It was a lie, of course. The only connection they had was through their journal.

Minutes passed as his melancholy then transformed into fury. The ice turned to fire. In an explosion of emotions, he felt something inside him reignite. He looked to his side and realized he wouldn't let it get in the way. I'll carry him on my back, even if I die trying.

A determined smile spread across his face as he looked back and ran back to where Petrikov had gone. Despite still feeling his head throbbing, he jumped and created more passages. Each jump brought him closer.

"Petrikov!" And when he arrived at the spot, evident from the marks in the snow where they had sat, he saw nothing.

No. No. No... The voice inside his head screamed as he frantically searched for the man. He jumped and picked up speed. He threw himself between the rocks that still dared to fall from the ever-closer mountain. Then he saw a yellow figure. He fell to the spot, searching for the man, but saw only a snow-covered candelabrum.

He turned around and saw nothing but a white sea. A monochromatic environment that seemed to devour empathy just by looking at it.

If he had tried harder, they would both be free and safe. He blamed himself for the death of a man. A warrior.

Before returning to the top, Harry wept silently amidst the snow.
