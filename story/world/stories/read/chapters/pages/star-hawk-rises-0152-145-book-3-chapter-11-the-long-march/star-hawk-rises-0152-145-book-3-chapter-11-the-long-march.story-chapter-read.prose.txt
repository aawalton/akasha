Zeo looked around as Brendan and Travis shuffled around to stand between me and the armoured men who were slowly walking towards us. It felt a little intimidating, but I was relaxed and comfortable in the knowledge that if these three wanted me dead, then I would not have seen them before bullets started flying.

“They are” Brendan began.

“Not ours” Travis finished as they both slowly slid laser pistols out of their jackets as their eyes darted around, watching the armoured guards.

“Then whose are they?” I asked.

“They are my guards” a voice replied from far to my right, causing all of us to turn our heads, a dwarf I recognised was standing nearby glaring at me.

An image flashed across my mind of the same dwarf standing next to me outside a shuttle as Brasdrec walked into the mountain. My gaze shifted from him to the guards that were still slowly approaching. I could now recognise they were all dwarves.

“Why are they encircling us, then? Your father only just went into the mountain, and you are doing whatever this is instead of getting your house in order?” I glanced back at Brasdrec's son.

“A ghost visited my father and guided him into the mountain, it was an honour for him.” the young-looking dwarf started before his voice heated. “However, the ghost is still here, instead of returning from whence it came. The elders believe that the ghost must be encouraged to move along before we can get the house back in order. I agreed, you have already taken our oldest pillar and yet, you remain… who else have you come to take from us?” He was shouting by the end.

“Uh… no one, as I told your father I was here to meet a friend, I did not come for anyone else.” I replied, looking at him, feeling a little confused.

“Liar! You took my father, and there is no telling who else you have come for, you will leave, now!” He screamed as he pulled an axe from over his shoulder. “Or I will make you!”

I stood up slowly as I glanced at the armoured dwarves still approaching, it was then I realised that they were approaching from one side because they had wanted to herd us out of this beautiful room.

“This is a beautiful promenade, peaceful, relaxing. I could spend days here just soaking up the experience, it would be a shame to leave so soon.” I began before I suddenly had an idea and a grin spread across my face. “Ah, that is an idea, I think I will do it.” I waved at the trio to follow me as I started backing away from the guards.

“Do what!” Brasdrec’s son demanded as he brandished his axe.

“Hmm? Oh, nothing really, don't worry about it.” I replied, trying not to grin too much.

I knew I was goading him, but I had also sped up my steps and was moving at an almost quick walk towards the exit, my mind already ripping my ship apart mentally as I tried to figure out where I could place my own promenade aboard it.

“Are you threatening me!” He screamed and the look in his eyes had gone wild. I glanced at the guards, noticing the minute movements as they checked each other to see if they were really going to follow the kid into a battle they clearly didn’t want to be a part of.

“No, I’m pondering ideas, you just happen to be hearing the musings. However, I would tone down the theatrics before your guards start wondering whether they serve a lord or a tantrum”

I saw the words hit him like I had physically slapped him. His face darkened beneath his beard, a flush creeping up from his collar. His fingers tightened end around the haft of his axe until the leather wrapping creaked.

“You dare,” he snarled, stepping forward, boots striking the ground harder than necessary, “to speak of tantrums in my father’s hall?”

I caught the guard's shift out of the corner of my eye, it wasn't forward towards me, it was more nervous energy, that they were not sure where they should be placing it. I also saw his gaze flicker, a glance between two of the older warriors. A hesitation, then his jaw clenched.

“You think I do not see it?” He said suddenly, voice dropping lower, rougher. “You think I do not know what they whisper? That I am untested. That I am too young. That I bark before I bite.”

His axe lowered a fraction, it didn’t appear to be a surrender, but like the weight of it had suddenly become real in his hands. Before his gaze firmed and he looked at me again.

“You took my father,” he continued, the fury cracking around the edges, something raw bleeding through. “He walked into the mountain with honour. With calm. With certainty.” His glare sharpened, wounded pride flaring back to life. “I told you I would tell his story, that was your cue to leave. However, you now stand here, smiling, mocking me in front of my own warriors.”

I saw one of the older looking guards shift and heard him quietly murmur, “My Lord…” it didn’t sound defiant, nor agreement, it sounded like a warning. That small sound struck harder than my words ever could.

The young dwarf straightened abruptly, spine rigid, dragging himself back into the shape of authority. His gaze firmed, and he looked like he was reclaiming himself from his grief.

“You will leave,” he said, no longer shouting, the tone of his voice still intense. “Not because I scream. Not because I threaten like some beard long fresh from the forge.” His grip steadied. “But because this is my father's station, his home that he defended and refused to leave for over two thousand years. And until I march into the mountain myself, it will not be made a spectacle of.” his eyes burned now, not wild anymore, but blazing with something steadier. Grief. Pride. Determination.

“Mock me if you wish, ghost,” he finished, lifting his chin. “But do not mistake restraint for weakness. I am my father’s son.”

I saw the guards straightened at that and this time, they were ready to move. A small smile appeared on my face and I bowed my head slightly to the son as I pushed the trio out of the promenade, blocking the door with my body.

“There it is,” I whispered as I gave him a slight nod. “May you rule as wisely as your father.” I finished before vanishing through the door and running down the hallway, feeling a strong urge to vanish into thin air and complete the spectacle of ghost vanishes.

Brasdrec walked with a smile on his face, when the darkness swallowed him, he glanced back, seeing the one he knew had to be a ghost and his son watching him vanish into the tunnel. He marched on, feeling pride in each step, he was being sent to his final moment with such honour, that he was sure that if he didn’t keep a lid on it, it would see him skipping down the tunnel.

He walked for what felt like hours before his mind slowly turned to what Lady Kenna had said to him, he played it over and over again in his mind. Each time it brought comfort to him and seemed to strengthen his stride.

He followed the tunnel he knew would lead to the deepest point of the entire mine and made sure he had the best chance of reaching the farthest point. He knew he could not rest now that he had started. Each time he walked past a resting station, seeing the dwarves staring at him. He gave them a polite nod of the head and a warm smile.

After he walked past the third station he could see it in their eyes, the previous stations, no doubt, sent word ahead that he had passed the station and these clansmen knew he was on his final march.

He watched them all stand and slam their fists against their chest as he passed. He knew they would stay like that until he vanished from sight, a last salute to a warrior. He knew they didn’t know the verdict of his last ear, but they had pride and respect in him that they were willing to display that openly to him as he passed.

He didn’t speak, he didn’t tell them the verdict, he faced forwards and marched on, his chest swelling with pride. Taking comfort in the embrace of his clansmen, sending him off with such high honour that it felt as though their strength marched beside him.

He could hear the rhythm of their fists against their armour echoing down the tunnel long after he had passed, a steady thunder that settled into his bones. It was not the sound of his people mourning, but of respect. He smiled, they were not saying farewell to the fallen, but giving tribute to one who walked willingly to his end.

It wasn't until he passed the next rest station and found it deserted that he frowned, confused. It did not slow his pace, but his mind did begin to wonder where everyone was.

He felt the air grow colder as he descended, the stone darker, the torches fewer. The deepest point of the mine awaited him. He turned his mind away from the missing dwarves. However, he soon understood where they had gone as a mine cart rushed past him, a single dwarf riding the full cart at break neck speeds towards the surface. Only for another to rush back with a dwarf pushing it to go faster.

A huge smile spread across his face, and a deep respect and honour settled into him when he found the end of the tunnel was not where it was meant to be. His clan men were deepening the tunnel as fast as they could to give him more distance to walk — so that his final march would not be a handful of solemn steps into the dark, but a legend measured in unrelenting steps on miles of stone, carved by loving hands that refused to let the clans' leader journey end too soon.
