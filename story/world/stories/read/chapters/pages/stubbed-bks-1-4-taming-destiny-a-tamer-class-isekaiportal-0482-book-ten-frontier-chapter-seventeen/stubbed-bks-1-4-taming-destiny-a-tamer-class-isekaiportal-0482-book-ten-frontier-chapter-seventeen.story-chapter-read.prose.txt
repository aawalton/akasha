I spend some time thinking things over, making and discarding plans, considering the potential implications of some initiatives I’d like to bring in. As if they had just been waiting for me to give them time, ideas bombard me, some good, some less so. I actually end up taking out a piece of parchment and noting down some of the ones I think would be worth raising at the meeting today with the other settlement leaders.

At some point, I realise that my belly is rumbling – I haven’t eaten anything since breakfast, and by now it’s past noon. Given how long the days are here, I’m surprised I haven’t felt the need to eat sooner. Perhaps my belly still thinks food is limited.

I pull out some of my emergency rations and chew them while continuing to write. I’ll eat a good dinner tonight to make up for the poor lunch.

I’ve just finished my meal when I hear the sound of chattering and clicks coming down the slope behind me. I stand up and twist around, spotting a whole gang of samuran hatchlings being led by the five adults, accompanied by the three Tier one raptorcats.

Catch spots me and nudges Iandee who passes on the message to the other samuran adults. They immediately adjust course. It takes the samuran hatchlings a moment to notice, but when they do, all but the stragglers quickly follow their elders. Even Falls-into-a-hole eventually realises. I wonder briefly whether maybe he has an issue with his sight the way Catch used to.

Perhaps that’s another reason to have a Bond with the hatchlings – it would enable me to check that everything is growing as it should. Which then raises the question – which Bond.

Given the way it affects the soul, I’m not keen on using Dominate on creatures who haven’t even reached adulthood yet, and certainly haven’t fully developed their personalities, but Tame just doesn’t offer all the options Dominate does. With Dominate, I would be able to heal pretty much any injury to their physical form and guide them on healing any issues in their internal matrices that might have already developed. And then, if they got in a dangerous situation again, I’d be able to do something about it. With Bond Teleport currently offline, Tame would only allow me to communicate with them at a distance – if I ordered them and they chose to disobey, I wouldn’t be able to do anything about it.

Then again, should I intercede if they’re intentionally doing something stupid? I want to – given how much we’ve gone through to keep those hatchlings alive, losing even one is a blow. But on the other hand, natural selection is a thing – if they are stupid or reckless enough to seek out danger when we’ve given them every warning we can of what not to do, maybe we don’t want their genes continuing. I recall the Darwin awards with a flicker of wry amusement.

As I step forward with a smile and rub cheeks with each of them – first adults, then hatchlings – I decide to speak to them about it.

I think you should Bind them, River says immediately after I’ve shared my thoughts. You helped so many of our village to Evolve. She gestures towards the other two Pathwalkers and Catch. With such a small village now, we need as many as possible to survive and Evolve as possible.

“I was able to help those in the village because they were all adults who had, for the most part, already established their internal matrices and just needed some guidance in fixing the bits that were holding them back,” I point out. “It’s not the same as Binding those who are barely more than babies.”

They are old enough to make their own choices, Catch points out. Before you came, they would have been hunting by themselves for moons now. These have developed differently, but they are almost adults.

Hunter nods firmly – she must have picked up that human gesture from Lizil or Alyna since she spends the most amount of time with them.

They think more deeply than hatchlings would normally. They think of more than just food and sleep. They can make the choice.

That’s an interesting point that Hunter makes. It’s true that the hatchlings have had a lot of influence that they wouldn’t have had – the school if nothing else. I have to admit that I haven’t been paying the hatchlings much attention recently, but I trust Hunter’s assessment.

“Alright, thanks,” I tell them, then turn to the hatchlings. “Gather around, everyone,” I instruct, leaning on Animal Empathy to make myself easily understood. The samuran hatchlings are pretty much bilingual at this point, but their carers have indicated that they respond better to their ancestral language than Moriaxan – it may even be something they learned in the egg.

I explain about the three different options and then ask if there are any questions.

The hatchlings look between each other and I hear a few murmuring snaps and clicks.

Finally, Leads-the-charge steps forward – predictably.

“Do we have to make the decision now?”

“Not necessarily,” I shrug. “But if you choose Dominate, the sooner I’m able to check your physical bodies and Core spaces, the earlier I’ll be able to help you address issues. Or find lingering parasites.”

Questions-everything is another predictable speaker.

“Do we have to keep whatever Bond we agree to now?”

“No. You can’t break a Dominate Bond by yourselves, but you can just ask me to break it if you decide you don’t like it.”

I give them a bit of time to discuss it amongst themselves. They seem a bit uncertain, several mentioning the Bonds that their elders have, others misliking the perceived limit to their freedom.

Finally, a samuran steps forward. This one is more of a surprise than the previous two. Studies-quietly is known as an introvert, someone who listens far more than he speaks.

“I will take a Dominate Bond. I wish to be like my elders – that is how they started, is it not?”

It is, Catch agrees.

I smile and pull Studies-quietly into the Battle of Wills. Within moments, a Bond has settled into place.

After that, the rest of the samurans step forward one at a time – or two, in the case of Chases-impatiently. Most choose Dominate; some choose Tame. Three choose no Bond at all. I accept that, but check them over with the others, just to look for any parasites. I can’t be sure that they are free of the wretched things, but with its weaker stealth abilities, I’m fairly confident that they aren’t there. I find six infections in the samurans and deal with them quickly.

By the time I’m finished, the sun has moved noticeably in the sky and I see the adult samurans fidgeting.

“Where are you off to?” I ask, eyeing the weapons that all of them bear, including the three Pathwalkers.

A hunt, Hunter tells me with anticipation.

“All of you?” I ask in some surprise.

Hunter waves her tail from side to side in a shrug.

We have remained behind too much recently. Our claws begin to get blunt.

Given that the Pathwalkers traditionally stayed in the village almost entirely, only leaving it to go to the festival or, in the case of Herbalist, to collect ingredients that she didn’t trust to others, I’m a little surprised at her words.

Still, I’m not going to try to stop them. At least the forest is probably emptier now than normally.

“Alright, well, good hunting,” I wish them, sending waves of encouragement down the Bonds to the newest additions to the network.

And then they go, in a chattering horde once more. Pulls-a-tail and Falls-overboard – yes, the names stuck – wave at me cheerfully, responding to my encouragement with an incorrigible excitement. They, at least, learned something from their naming experiences and weren’t among the young samurans who charged onto the battlefield.

Once they’re gone, the quiet seems almost oppressive. Perhaps I should go back to the settlement now. Since there’s no urgent demand on my time, I’d like to focus on checking out Bond Teleport. I’m able to – slowly – deal with the damage to the channels without diving into my Core space, thanks to the upgrade to Meditation, but the damage to my Skill is another question, and it’s risky to sit out here without any of my companions around. If Aingeal were with me, it would be fine, but he’s gone to play in the fires of the smithy. And Sirocco’s out with the hunting group.

But I’ve been trapped inside too much recently, either because of the manastorm or because of responsibilities. It would be nice to spend some time outside. Perhaps I should just concentrate on repairing my Energy channels while keeping an awareness of my surroundings.

Since that will probably be necessary before touching Bond Teleport anyway, I make my decision.

I’ve already started to make progress on the damage to my channels – I couldn’t spare the concentration during the heat of the moment with Tilathis and Kalanthia, but there were several periods of time during the Alliance discussion where I was grateful for something to distract my attention a little so I didn’t try to stab him and set everything off again. Being able to concentrate fully on it, even if I keep my ears open and pulse Inspect from time to time, allows me to make much quicker progress.

Even in my somewhat aware state, I feel my senses drawn towards Bond Teleport. Though I know I need to fix my Energy channels, I feel a sort of…pull that intrigues me.

Glancing around I see no signs of any companion who might be able to guard me. But maybe I have another option.

Concentrating, I summon a large ball of flames, pouring in mana until it becomes white hot.

Then, treating the flames like putty, I form a guardian. While initially thinking about creating a dragon, I find the form becoming something different. A large stocky body about my height. A big head full of teeth. A heavy, spiked tail.

I smile as I look at it. Fair enough, I tell my subconscious. Fenrir has been protecting me all this time – stands to reason he would become my flame guardian too.

“Can you warn me if something approaches? And protect me if something tries to attack?” I ask the flame guardian hopefully. I’m not sure how much awareness it will have. Then again, the small dragon on the ship played with Aingeal….

The flame guardian looks back at me, stoic, but I sense that it’s understood at least something.

I resolve not to get too deep into my meditation, but decide to trust it. Worse came to worst, I could call for help from Elarion – he’s not far away.

Closing my eyes, I dip into my Core space. While continuing to direct mana to regrow the channels that disintegrated, I approach the blackened Skill.

Curious. As I noticed before, it almost feels like it isn’t part of me any longer. I stop working on my channels, dedicating all my focus to figuring this out. Zooming closer, I inspect the blackened lines, looking for any that look particularly bad – or better.

It all seems pretty uniform to my ‘eyes’. However, I’m not daunted. I’ve dealt with major problems with my Core space before. I have faith I can figure it out this time. My next step is to try to pull mana into the Skill. As before, the mana stops at the edge where gold transforms into black. Interesting. Getting in close, I try to push the mana harder, trying to force my way past the barrier.

No success. If anything, it feels like I’m more likely to rupture the intact channel than force my mana into the blackened one.

Well, maybe it’s not the right type of Energy. I learned that before – I used pure Energy to fix my Core, then mana to fix my channels, and then stamina to thicken them and make them more robust, then health to fix my soul.

I’m about to try pulling on stamina when I feel a nudge from something outside me. Alert, I quickly pull out of my Core space, then open my eyes.

The first thing I see is a wall of flame. I blink, tensing for a moment before I recall the flame guardian I made for myself. Fire Fenrir is standing with me behind him, facing something. I push myself to my feet and look beyond to what has alerted him.

I blink again. Elarion’s standing on a massive column of ice, looking down at me. And from what I can see, he’s grinning.

Elarion?

We’ve done it.

It takes me a moment to process exactly what he’s talking about. And then a massive smile spreads across my face to match his.

I’m coming.
