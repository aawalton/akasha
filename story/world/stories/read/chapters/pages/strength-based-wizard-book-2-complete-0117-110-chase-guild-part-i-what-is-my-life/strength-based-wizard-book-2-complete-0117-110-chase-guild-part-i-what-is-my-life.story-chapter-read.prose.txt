Chapter 110

Chase Guild, Part I (What Is My Life?)

The portal spits us out like a burp. My vision is drowned in white for a moment, and my other senses are the first thing return. Summer humidity that hits my skin like a familiar slap, coupled with the scent of cut grass and charcoal. My ears prickle at the cricket song and the echo of distant traffic. Someone’s dog barking a few houses over. My fingers are tingling.

Then, my vision returns. We’re back in my parents’ backyard.

I blink, slow, as the patio light buzzes overhead and the garage floodlight paints everything in a harsh yellow cone. My heart is still racing, processing everything my body had been through on the other side of that Gate.

Liv stumbles onto the grass beside me, eyes wide, hair wild, cloak slipping off her shoulders. Jelly Boy is still tucked against me. He vibrates and leaps out of the crook of my elbow, leaping onto the ground and landing with a couple soft bounces.

I take in the group of us. We look like absolute shit. Sweaty, filth-covered and absolutely exhausted. I sigh, stepping closer to the others and scrunching my facial muscles, casting [Clean].

The spell washes over us like a soft white wave. Dirt, blood, soot, all gone. The sticky swamp smell mixed with the stench of stress-induced sweat evaporates. Liv’s hair untangles, falling around her shoulders as though she applied a heavy helping of dry shampoo. Jelly Boy gleams like a freshly waxed car.

A wet, excited slapping sound fills the early summer air.

Tom, Jax, and Other Tom come barreling around the corner like three anthropomorphic water balloons with legs. They’re in a frenzy, jiggling so hard they’re practically vibrating into a new plane of existence. Jelly Boy vibrates in response, matching the tone and frequency. I wonder what information they’re trading through the vibration, or if the vibrating was even necessary when they’re all a part of a shared hive mind.

My mom is right behind them, stepping carefully, cardigan on to ward off the nighttime chill, eyes wide and alarmed like she just followed three sentient puddings to an active crime scene (which she essentially has).

“Oh thank God,” she says, clutching her chest. “The boys went into an absolute frenzy! They bolted from the couch. Wasn’t even during an ad. So, I know something was wrong. Ran out here and—! Well—! I thought—I thought…” She looks between me and Liv. “What… happened?”

Liv and I glance at each other. She turns towards our concerned mother, who looks on the verge of a nervous breakdown.

“We did leave you a note,” she says.

“What she means to say is, we’re sorry for worrying you, but we’re OK,” I add.

Mom just rushes in and hugs us, pulling us in to a hug so suffocating I’m surprised she’s not a System user.

“Mom!” exclaims Liv.

I lean into the embrace. To my surprise, I feel tears building behind my eyes. God, I needed this. I guess I just hadn’t known how badly.

The three slime brothers swarm Jelly Boy.

They huddle around him like loyal subjects greeting their returning king. Tom flops on the grass before Jelly Boy, prostrating himself. Jax bends himself in half in what I think is some sort of bow. Other Tom wiggles in place, oozing pure joy.

Jelly Boy’s eyes are beaming with pure joy and delight. The surface on the top of his head simmers like a pot of water, and he bounces in place. The little guy’s probably happy to be back in the limelight, like he deserves.

My dad appears at the back door, spatula in hand, eyebrows drawn tight in that “I’m not mad, I’m disappointed and also deeply confused” expression he’s mastered over the course of our childhood. He strides across the yard, a little hitch in his step.

Mom relinquishes her hold on us and, as soon as Dad’s at her side, the questions start flying.

Liv turns instantly into diplomat mode, voice calm and fast. She starts juggling questions and concern like she’s on a press tour.

“Mom, Dad, we’re okay. We had to… attend to something. It was Guild-related.”

My dad’s eyes narrow. “You left a note.”

Liv nods, wincing. “A short note.”

“A short note with no specifics,” my mom says, hands on hips now. “You can’t just vanish for days and leave a sticky note like you’re going to the grocery store.”

My stomach drops.

“Days?” I repeat, and I pull my phone from my Inventory, as though she has reason to lie to us. I unlock it, ignoring the hundreds of notifications flooding my screen, and stare at the date.

Four days. Four days.

“Holy shit,” I whisper. “Four days.”

When I had spent days inside the Bronze Gate, only a few hours had passed back here on Earth. It seems certain Gates have more time compression than others. Which made sense. The Gates I entered for Municipal Guild jobs had compressed very little time, but were relatively low-ranked Gates. Bronze Gates, which granted Users their Classes, had to be more powerful than run-of-the-mill Gates. I tuck this information away for later.

Liv’s mouth opens slightly. “That— that shouldn’t—”

“Gate time,” I say hollowly. “It makes sense.” Or as much sense as anything relating to the System does, at least.

My dad steps closer, eyes flicking over me. I’m shirtless, practically sparkling thanks to the help of the [Clean] spell, and probably still have a manic look in my eyes.

He opens his mouth like he’s about to lecture me into the ground.

I don’t let him.

I step forward and hug him.

He freezes for a half-second… then hugs me back, tight and real and grounding in a way no spell ever has been. This time the tears well in my eyes.

And because I’m still me, because I’m still Joseph Sullivan, because my brain handles trauma by grabbing onto the most normal thing it can find, I mumble into his shoulder, “I’m so hungry.”

My dad’s sternness cracks. A smile tugs at his mouth.

“I figured,” he says, voice softer. “I grilled a few extra steaks. Kept to the habit, not knowing when you guys would be back.”

I close my eyes. Home, I think. God dammit, it’s good to be home. No matter how many Gates I face in the future, nothing will ever change how good this feels.

After housing two steaks, four baked sweet potatoes, and a hefty scoop of my mom’s homemade broccoli salad, I’m laying in bed, deciding to retire early for the night after a long four days.

I’m not exhausted, exactly. Not the way I should be after everything we’d just been through. It’s more like I’m wired, vibrating with aftershock adrenaline and the strange quiet thrill of being back in my own bed.

And there’s my phone. Missed texts. Missed calls. Voicemails stacked in my inbox like a tower of guilt.

I groan, out loud, to nobody in particular (even though Jelly Boy is seated on my desk, using my laptop to catch up on his shows). “Ugh…!”

I’m about to unlock my phone when I remember the minimized System Messages. In the frenzy of everything going on, I must have subconsciously suppressed them. They’re blinking softly in the corner of my vision. Focusing on the blinking icon, I pull up several windows into my vision.

“Alright,” I mutter.

The first two are a couple of level-up notifications. I’ve gone up from Level 27 to Level 29. I’m almost to Level 30, which—while arbitrary—feels like a significant milestone.

I blink, then smile despite myself. “Yeah. Okay. That tracks.”

I assign my points without hesitation, dumping the four points into Strength. Because I’ve learned my lesson. If the world insists on throwing me into physical hellscapes, I’m going to be strong enough to punch the hellscape back. One spectral fist at a time.

I close out of these first two windows and move onto the next one.

The next message is makes my heartbeat stumble.

QUEST UPDATE: Echoes of a Dead World!

You have successfully completed a Bonus Objective: Successfully access and remove the System World Seed fragment from this Realm.

Reward: Legendary Spellcaster’s Chest (x1)

I sit up.

“No way.” I forgot about this Objective, to be honest.

I withdraw my reward.

A chest appears on my bed, heavy and ornate, carved with glowing runes that shift when I stare at them too long.

I open it by focusing my intention on the command.

The lid lifts, and the whole box dissolves into glittering ash, and in its place is a tiny scroll. Well, it’s more like… a cigarette-sized roll of something dense and etched, about the length of my finger.

I examine it. Triggering another System message.

Item Identified: [Augmentation Array]

Description: A compact spell formation. When properly used, with appropriate materials and methodology, may permanently augment another spell’s formation into an item.

Note: Improper use may result in unstable item formation, spell decay, or catastrophic mana disruption.

“Cool,” I whisper. “And good thing I don’t have any mana.”

So, this thing can install a spell’s properties into an item of mine? That’s immensely useful. I roll the spellcraft-cigarette between my fingers. It feels important. Like the kind of thing people in the Discussion Channels write three thousand-word posts about, arguing in the comments for twelve pages about the best use-case. It’s definitely something I should consider carefully.

I grimace, already imagining the rabbit hole of searching for useful information in the System Discussion Channels. I’d have to dig through threads without a user-friendly search or aggregation function.

Then, I recall. The Great Lakes Spellcaster Society. They’re meeting in Detroit on July 1st. “I need to go,” I say aloud. “I absolutely need to go.”

I grab my phone, open it, and register immediately. I add it to my calendar with a giant reminder and multiple ticklers, so I don’t forget it again.

Great Lakes Spellcaster Society — July 1. Detroit. Find nerdy ass wizard who can help me!!

There. Done. No excuses now. If there’s anywhere I can learn what the hell an Augmentation Array is and how to use it, it’s there. And I’d have time consider which item and spell I want to combine. Maybe someone there can just do the thing for me! Man, that’d be nice.

I sink back into my bed. Pulling out my phone again, I begin working through all my unread messages.

Clyde. Veronica. Unknown numbers. A couple of people from Harvest Guild, including Labonte himself. People asking if I’m alive. People telling me to call them back. People leaving voicemails that probably include the words “Where the hell were you, dude?”

I rub my face.

“Alright,” I mutter, scrolling. “Let’s see how much trouble I’m in on Earth.”

The Next Day. Ace Boxing Gym.

It’s morning. Technically. My mind knows it’s morning. But my body is moving like I should be tucking back into bed. Interdimensional jet lag is an absolute bitch! The fluorescent lights overhead buzz in that familiar gym way.

Jordan has me working the bags first—footwork, slips, pivots, jab-cross-hook, then back to cardio like she’s personally offended by the concept of resting.

Veronica is here too, because I dragged her into this. Having a lot to catch up on from the past four days, I decided to combine as many of my appointments as possible. I think back to my exchange with Veronica from last night.

>Joe: Hey!

[Veronica reacted to your message]

>Joe: Free tomorrow at 8:30a? Wanna hit up a boxing class with me?

[You’ve received one voice memo from Veronica]

“Seriously!... You ignore all my texts and calls and then hit me up and it’s… Argh!... You know what? Sure, why the hell not. Send the deets.”

Now, she’s wrapped in hand wraps, has hair tied back, eyes bright and eager. She’s also already mad as hell.

“This is bullshit,” she pants between rounds, wiping sweat from her brow with the back of her glove. “You didn’t tell me this would be so… hard. Or… so much… cardio!” Despite her complaining, Veronica is a natural and has far better mechanics than I did when I first wandered into this gym.

“That’s what boxing is,” I say, circling on the balls of my feet. “Pain and breathing and… Well, punishment.”

“I thought it was punching,” she snaps, then immediately smacks the heavy bag with a clean, crisp combo that makes Jordan’s eyebrows lift.

“Good, good,” Jordan calls. “Again! Keep it up!”

After the bags, Jordan finally waves us over.

“Alright,” she says, pulling her mouthguard in. “Sparring. Joe—let’s see what you’ve got today.”

I nod, fit my mouthguard, and step into the ring.

Jordan moves in with that practiced, calm aggression—tight guard, quick head movement, feet light as two feathers. She’s good. I had forgotten how good.

I realize my [Perception] has improved since our last lesson together, and I notice so many more details in her movement, technique, and strategy. Despite being barely perceptible before, I now notice slight tells before she comes at me in certain ways. A slight twitch of her quad when she’s fainting high and preparing to come in low.

I may not be as technically sound, but I outclass her. My speed is unfair. My strength is ridiculous. My reflexes are sharper than they should be, like my body’s been tuned by a cruel god of bloodsport. My additional levels and combat experience against actual monsters has helped me reach an entirely new level.

I slip her jab without thinking. Counter with a touch—light, controlled—then pivot away before she can punish it. She tries to press. I step out. She throws a hook. I duck under and tap her shoulder again, just enough to make the point.

Jordan stops after the round, breathing hard, staring at me like I just did a magic trick.

“Okay,” she says slowly. “That’s… not normal.”

I pull my mouthguard out and grin. “Thanks?”

“No,” she says, pointing at me with her glove. “I mean it. That’s not normal. How did you change so much in… Less than a week?”

Veronica leans against the ropes, still catching her breath, and gives me a look. I have a lot to catch her up on.

Jordan nods toward the corner where one of the big Guild-standard reinforced bags hangs. It’s thicker, stitched with heavy seams and faintly runed along the base with magical enhancements. The kind of bag meant to survive enhanced strikes and magical attacks.

“Hit that,” she says. “As hard as you can. And I mean it. Give it everything you’ve got.”

I blink. “Jordan—”

“As hard as you can,” she repeats. “I want to see something.”

I walk over, set my feet, and stare at the bag. “Yes ma’am!”

I breath in through my nose. I exhale. Then, I punch, focusing all of my Strength into that single blow.

The impact is a cannon shot. The bag doesn’t swing when my fist strikes its surface. No. It splits. Straight down the middle, seam bursting like a zipper giving up on life. The stuffing inside erupts in a cloud, and the two halves sag apart.

Silence.

Veronica’s mouth falls open.

Jordan just stares.

“…Holy shit,” she says finally.

I flex my hand, wincing slightly. “Yeah. I—uh. I might have overdone that.”

Jordan walks up, pokes the destroyed bag with her glove like she’s confirming it’s real.

Then she looks at me again. “Why haven’t you joined a Guild yet?”

I feel my expression tighten. Not because I’m offended—because it’s a good question. It’s just… complicated. Harvest Guild. Labonte. It all felt like a monumental decision. And I’d sold my soul to a company before. I wasn’t eager to make the same mistake.

“I’m… considering it,” I say carefully. “I’ve been busy. And, uh…” I shrug. “I’m broke as hell.”

Jordan snorts. “Yeah, that tracks. Even the smaller Guilds pay decent starting rates, though.”

Veronica hops down from the ropes, towel around her neck now. She’s watching me with that look she gets when she’s decided something and is about to drag me into it.

“Come with me,” she says.

“To where?” I ask, already suspicious.

“Chase Guild,” she says. “Open assessment in Columbus tomorrow. I’m driving down. You’re coming.”

Oh yeah… Clyde had mentioned something about that to us.

My first instinct is to say no. Then I think about Harvest Guild. About Labonte’s weirdness. His eagerness to recruit me. I should have something to compare them too.

And it would be nice to spend time with Veronica and Clyde, who has been a training maniac ever since we’ve returned from the Bronze Gate.

I sigh.

“Fine,” I say. “I’ll go.”

Veronica grins. “Good.”

I nod, mostly to myself. “It’ll be a good comparison point. To the offer I have already. And Clyde will be excited, I’m sure.”

Jordan shakes her head, still staring at the ruined bag. “Just… don’t punch anyone in the assessment like that.”

“No promises,” Veronica says immediately, and I groan.

The Next Day. Somewhere in Central Ohio.

I’m behind the wheel, heading down I-71 toward Columbus, and the sky is that washed-out Ohio gray. The road hums under the tires and trucks pass us like slow-moving buildings. My coffee sits in the cupholder, already lukewarm because I forgot it existed for five minutes and that’s apparently enough time for the watered down bean juice to give up on me.

Veronica’s in the passenger seat, legs tucked up, hoodie on, scrolling through her phone.

I clear my throat. “So… what else is new?”

She doesn’t look up right away. “I’m talking to a guy.”

That gets my attention. “Oh?”

“Well, a few guys. But there’s one that definitely seems to be my type. Damien,” she says, and there’s a tiny smile there—subtle, but real. “Just moved to Cleveland. But he travels out of town a lot for work. Consultant. We’ve gotten coffee before, but still waiting on a more… exciting date.”

“Consultant,” I say. “Gross.”

“Don’t ruin this,” she says, but her smile widens. She playfully punches me in the arm.

“Hey! I’m driving!”

“I’m definitely interested. In Damien, I mean. I’ve been on a couple other dates since joining that app—total duds. Like, one guy spent the entire dinner explaining his Skill tree to me like it was foreplay.” She mimics gagging. “I get it. We’re System Users, but can you try to have an actual personality?”

I wince. “That’s criminal.”

“It should be punishable by law,” she says. “Damien’s… different. He seems like… I don’t know. A fully-realized person?”

I nod, watching the road. “Good. I’m glad. Sounds like an interesting guy. He likes coffee.”

Veronica snorts.

She shifts, glancing at me. “So. You gonna tell me about the Gate you disappeared into?”

I sigh. “Brief version?”

“Brief,” she agrees, then adds, “but don’t skimp on the juicy details.”

I give her the sanitized version. A portal in my backyard. Walter, Preston, Grush. A realm with a Serpentine Lord who dresses like a medical-themed drag deity. A World Seed fragment going unstable. A fight. A lot of fights, actually. A ridiculous amount of fighting. Then we fixed the problem and got out. Bingo, bongo!

Veronica listens quietly, eyes forward now, expression shifting from amused to attentive.

Then, I mention the name.

“Chernobog. Apparently someone named Chernobog had to do with all of those issues that world was dealing with.”

The air in the car changes like I cracked a window in winter. Veronica’s shoulders stiffen. Her mouth tightens. The smile disappears completely.

“…Yeah,” she says softly. “Don’t love that.”

“I don’t either,” I say, and my fingers tighten on the steering wheel. “It seems like bad news.”

She swallows, gaze fixed on the road ahead. “Joe—”

“I’m serious,” I cut in, gentler than my tone suggests. “Be careful, Veronica. And… if you want help? If you want to get rid of that Trait? I’ll try. I don’t know how yet, but I’ll try my best to help you.”

Veronica exhales through her nose, a quiet laugh that isn’t funny. “Thanks.”

Then she shakes her head. “I don’t want to eliminate other people from a Game I’m not even sure I want to participate in anymore.”

That lands heavier than it should.

I keep driving.

“Why did you decide to join the God Game in the first place?” I ask, and the question comes out before I can stop it.

Veronica is quiet for a beat.

Then she frowns, like she’s genuinely searching her memory. “I… can’t remember.”

I glance at her. “What do you mean you can’t remember?”

“I mean,” she says slowly, “I know I did it. I know I chose it. But the reason? The moment? It’s just… blank. Too distant.”

She looks at me now. “Why did you?”

I open my mouth. Close it.

My brain reaches for an answer and finds a fog bank.

“I…” I say, then shrug. “I can’t remember either.”

The words taste wrong.

“I was in a dark place,” I add quickly, because that part I do remember. Not the details. Not the decision, or what specifically compelled me to accept the invitation to the System. Just the weight. The darkness. The numbness. “I’d rather not think about it.”

Veronica nods, slowly, like she understands too well.

And then the car goes quiet. We drive like that for ten minutes, engine humming, tires whispering on asphalt, both of us staring forward while something unseen sits between us in the cabin. I occasionally take sips of my increasingly colder coffee.

Finally, Veronica clears her throat and points ahead.

“There,” she says. “That’s it.”

I blink, and sure enough. Chase Guild headquarters rises in the distance, clean and tall and corporate against the gray Columbus sky. A new, towering building of spell-reinforced glass.

“We’re here,” she says, voice back to normal, but not quite.

I swallow.

“Alright,” I mutter. “Let’s go see what the biggest Guild in Ohio thinks of us.”
