Interlude: Council of Accountability

In his long life, Charles Vanderborn had faced all manner of danger, violence, and trouble. Very little of it compared to the headache that was his students’ betrayal.

He should’ve already been gone by now, but their fight in the garden had attracted a lot of notice. Rentan Academy was no stranger to conflict, given their yearly tournaments and the handful of times people had been stupid enough to assault the school to get at him, but it had been years since there had been any real hiccups. Their fight was loud, flashy, and ended with cannon fire and them speeding off fast enough to demolish entire storm clouds in their wake.

Getting a whole half hour to himself had been the best he could hope for. The rest of the night he spent putting out fires and positioning his people to be ready in case of retaliation or retribution. It wasn’t just five missing students, it was five important students.

“Professor Vanderborn! They’re waiting for you, sir.” His aide, Margot said as she met him outside the Amaric Forum . She was a heavyset, middle-aged woman. with curly red hair and a pleasant, disarming demeanor. Now, she looked as grim as he felt.

Charles grunted, and she fell in step beside him. Their destination was the meeting place of the Great Houses, where all the noble families and colonial ambassadors would gather to discuss problems and vote on potential solutions. It was a place where people lied, argued, and plotted against each other endlessly, usually to his benefit.

The building itself was a crystalline structure, glittering spires jutting out angles above, catching the sun and serving as a beacon for miles around. The Forum sat alone in a field of grass and flowers, four narrow paths dividing the garden into quadrants, decorated and designed based on the four corners of Amaria, almost completely conquered at this point.

Almost.

“How upset are they?” he asked, not bothering to hide his irritation or impatience.

“Well, as far as I can tell, only one of them wants to kill you,” said Margot cheerfully. “But I’m pretty sure he wanted to kill you before this, and you already knew that. But General Graves is keeping her opinions to herself, and Aloysius Adams is all empty bluster.”

His gut clenched. “And Elaine?” he asked.

Margot took a deep breath. “You know her. She’s quiet at the best of times, until she’s not. I have no clue what she’s thinking, but if I were her, I’d be pretty upset at losing my daughter. Even if I didn’t blame you directly.”

Charles shot her a dirty look, but Margot just hummed to herself as they went.

Two guards stood in front of the wide, foggy glass entrance. Once they were close enough, the guards stepped out of the way and opened the doors for them. He paid them no mind, too focused on the coming battle.

The parents of his students had wanted to meet in his office, but Charles had insisted on the Forum. It was neutral territory, and it would be a reminder to them that his power didn’t lay in being an educator, but as this world’s power broker. After so much relative peace, perhaps they’d forgotten.

The entire building was made out of shaped stone, glass, and crystal. Both he and the assembled people saw each other through murky, semi-transparent windows overlooking stadium seating and a podium, where one of his puppets would run things when the Forum was in session.

Now, it was just four disappointed parents, sitting in rich, plush seats in the center, near that empty podium. The rest of the workers for the building were nowhere to be found, giving them relative privacy, though Charles knew from experience that everyone involved used recorder cards to show others later. He made his way down the sloped path and took the podium, while Margot stood nearby, holding a small lockbox he’d asked her to bring.

“Good afternoon, and thank you for coming this quickly,” he started.

“Quickly? Our children are missing and you’re fucking late, Vanderborn,” King Rhondo Ransa challenged immediately, standing. He was a powerfully built, vibrant man, just starting to dim with age. His head was shaved smooth, but his beard had more silver than black. He wore his famous scorpion-scale armor, like he was expecting a fight to break out. “We demand an explanation.”

Charles wanted nothing more than to throw caution to the wind and blast him with a bolt of lightning, but that would be politically inconvenient. Everything about the Bedamin was inconvenient, he found. When he ruled over this region, he’d do something about that permanently.

Clearing his throat, he said, “You want an explanation? Your children robbed me and tried to kill me.”

You could’ve heard a pin drop from the silence. He met his daughter’s blue eyes with his own. They were full of disbelief, and the same smoldering anger as everyone else in the room.

“Explain,” said General Graves, a sturdy woman in her forties, and an up and coming player in Amaric politics. She’d accomplished what most powerful people believed they did: earned her place. “My son is neither a thief, nor someone to go off half-cocked like that.”

The good thing about the story he’d chosen, was that it was very nearly the truth. Out of them, only Elaine had a card capable of telling when he was lying, but then, he’d have a special talk with her later, telling her what really happened. He owed her that much.

“You’re right, he’s not,” he said. “But Ambrose Adams is. He led them to my office, breaking in with an illegal [Skeleton Key] and stealing the tournament prizes, as well as two personal cards that were priceless. I caught them red-handed, and tried to talk to them. But Adams attacked me, and his fellow students joined in while I attempted to apprehend them.”

All attention went to Aloysius Adams. He was the spitting image of Ambrose’s future, a lanky, oily, weasel of a man. Their family was on the brink of ruin, and he did his best to try to deny it, but anyone with a shred of perception could see his clothes were threadbare and repaired in several places. His eyes darted around.

“Ambrose did this?” he asked, shifting uncomfortably in his seat. “Look, I’m not going to say he’s not capable of it, but I was under the impression that he was doing well in the tournament, and likely to win. What reason would he have for trying to rob you?”

On the surface, it sounded reasonable enough. He was good at deflecting and wheedling his way through problems. But not today.

“He wanted the Wildcard he knew I had,” Vanderborn said, leaning forward against the podium. Scowling, he didn’t have to fake the irritation when he continued. “I challenged him to make number one in his class, and promised him the Wildcard if he succeeded. He decided he wanted it early, and the others backed him up.”

“Even Jessica?” Elaine asked. They were just two words, but they came out crystalline, perfectly innocent and accusatory at the same time.

“Even Jessica,” he echoed, nodding at his daughter. She had the same long face, dark hair, and bright eyes, full of the same canny cynicism. “When the time came, she chose her friends over her family, despite my giving her a chance to do the right thing.”

“This is nonsense,” said King Ransa, still standing with his fists clenched in barely restrained anger. “What reason would my kids have for joining the Adams boy in stealing? He’s never been a friend to me and mine. Raeva used to write home about what an unpleasant little bastard Ambrose is.”

Aloysius opened his mouth to argue, but conceded the point with a nod. General Graves said, “So, we have your answer on what happened. Where are our children now? What did you do?” Her voice was like cold steel, firm, unyielding, but also measured and in control.

Charles paused for a second, then sighed. “I tried to subdue them nonlethally, and because of it, they were able to slip away, escaping on my ship. I pursued as far as I could, but they were heading east. By the time they outflew me, they reached the coast. I believe they are heading for Riddolsta.”

Unlike the previous silence, everyone began speaking all at once.

“Riddolsta? What do you take us for?” King Ransa scoffed.

“Wow, if you’re going to make a run for it, I guess…” Aloysius muttered.

“I don’t believe any of this,” said General Graves. “Not a word. If they go to Riddolsta, they’d be killed on sight.”

Only Elaine remained silent, but the fire in her eyes said enough. She knew Riddolsta was nearly empty, save for lingering storm enchantments. The engineered plague had done its job. Charles let the chatter go on long enough before he slammed his fist down on the podium. The impact echoed throughout the empty forum, hushing them for now.

“Please, I understand. I consider this among my greatest failures. I’ve already arranged for Professor Dietrich to cover the rest of the tournament. I’ve got a second ship that’s nearly ready for flight, and I am going to go after them. To Riddolsta. And I’m going to bring them back, at any cost. But before I do, I ask for your grace and patience. I need time to get there, and time to get them back.

“More than anything, I need you all to work with me, and not turn this into an opportunity to fight or squabble, as we’re all so fond of doing.”

In a full session, it would’ve gotten a few good laughs. Now, it just pissed people off.

“If anything happens to Raeva or Raeleq, you will answer to me,” said Rhondo, pointing at him. “I don’t care what it takes, I will burn your legacy to the ground if they are not returned home safely.”

“I am inclined to agree,” said General Graves, crossing her arms over her chest. She looked like she tasted something sour. “This doesn’t add up. I want to hear the story from Luthor himself. I will accompany you to Riddolsta.”

“No,” Charles said firmly. “With me being gone from the school and the continent, Amaria will be vulnerable. I will need you all to help me maintain order and control while -- “

“Why should we?” Rhondo stepped away from his seat and approached the podium with murder in his eyes. “Martha is right. This doesn’t add up. I don’t like this one bit, and I’ve no reason to play along or help you out. Get my children back, or I will come down on you with all the fury of the Bedamin.”

Without another word, he stormed off. The heavy thuds of his boots lingered, long after he was out of sight. Charles sighed and said, “Anyone else?”

General Graves stood as well. “You’ve got a serious problem, Charles. I will refrain from causing trouble for the moment, but you need to understand: you are on thin ice. I can keep Rhondo from exploding for a little while, but if anything happens to any of our kids…” She gave a polite but pointed nod and left as well.

Then it was just three of them. Aloysius Adams looked like he wanted to be anywhere but there, but was too scared to directly threaten him the way the others had. “What will you do to Ambrose when you catch him?”

Meaning: what will you do to my family?

“I haven’t decided yet,” Charles lied, and this time Elaine stirred, favoring him with an unimpressed glare. “But I am open to leniency, assuming you’re willing to work with me.”

“Of course,” Aloysius said smoothly, brushing off his suit jacket. “You know that I am a practical man, Professor Vanderborn. I will do whatever it takes to keep my family’s heads above water.”

“Good. Margot?”

“Yes sir.” she said, stepping forward and handing the lockbox she’d been carrying to the Adams patriarch. With a bow, she retreated once more.

“What’s this?” Aloysius asked.

“A bunch of bribes,” said Elaine, sighing. “Isn’t that right, Dad? You want him to spread the money around to a few key people while you’re away, to keep the Great Houses too tangled to move in any one direction.”

Charles smiled, but said nothing. She was his favorite for a reason. Even when upset and worried, they were on the same page more often than not.

“Right. Well, I can do that. Let me guess,” Aloysius laughed, “you want me to stir up some of Rhondo’s enemies to keep him busy for a bit. I can do that.”

“Then do so. The payees are written inside. Waste no time, and take no more than a reasonable ten percent of the total for yourself. If you fail me, then all of your children will pay the price. Am I understood?”

“You are,” said Aloysius. He stood and tucked the lockbox under his arm. “If there’s nothing else, I’ll do my part. Please…just don’t hurt me or my family.”

After he left, Elaine tore into him. “What the hell happened to Jessica, Dad?”

Charles deflated, and stepped down from the podium. “Meet me outside, Margot.” His aide bowed and left. He waited nearly a minute before continuing. “Basically everything I said before is true. Ambrose did try to rob me, but Jessica and the others tried to stop him. When I caught them, they overheard me speaking to Turhan, so I gave them a choice.”

Elaine groaned and tugged on her hair. “The old join or die routine? You didn’t try to lie, deflect, or buy their silence? You went straight for threats towards the stars of your third year class? Of course you did. You’re getting to be more and more of a petty tyrant in your old age.”

She took a deep, steadying breath and held her hands out. “Okay, fine. So you decided to pressure them all. I’m assuming a fight did break out, or we wouldn’t be here.”

By now, the fight itself was a rumor spreading almost as fast as his ship could fly. You couldn’t walk around Rentan Academy without hearing the whispers or feeling the loss of five star students.

“It did,” said Charles. “When I attempted to subdue them, I’d nearly succeeded when Jessica broke apart and used the Wanderer’s cannons on me. They escaped and left the world behind. They could be anywhere in the Aetheric Expanse. And I need to find them before they do something stupid, and Turhan finds out.”

Elaine thought about it for a moment and nodded. “Okay. We’ll go get her, and bring her and her friends back. Safely, in one piece, and not as bound cards.”

“There is no we,” he started.

“Dad, do not test me on this!” his daughter snapped. She rarely, if ever raised her voice. When she did, people paid attention. “I’m not letting you screw this up again. Not when Jessica is involved. You will either take me along or I will go with Rhondo and destroy every bit of the legacy you’ve built here. Tell me I’m bluffing. Go ahead.”

Charles looked away, sighing. “I taught you to never bluff, and to always be willing to carry out your threats. Fine. But we need to leave now. Once we’re out in the Expanse, I should be able to track the Wanderer, or at least its trail. We’ll bring Jessica home, but Ambrose and the rest are at my mercy. This is not negotiable.”

Elaine inhaled sharply, closing her eyes. “They are not my concern. She is. So, lead the way, old man.”

In spite of himself, he smiled. Out of all of his brood, no one else had the grace, poise, and spine she did. No one else could get away with challenging him like this. As angry as he was at Jessica, he could live with sparing her. That didn’t mean she wouldn’t go unpunished, of course.

“Okay,” he said. “Let’s go.” He offered his daughter his arm. She took it, and together, they left the Amaric Forum.
