Getting into the house itself wasn't that hard, after I knew the trick—just covering myself with the tarp and crawling to the front door was enough. I took great care opening up the front door, wincing as it creaked open.

I waited one second.

Two.

Nothing happened. I breathed a sigh of relief, put the tarp in front of me, and stepped inside. That's when I realized that I really was dealing with a madman. Before I even noted doors or thresholds, I saw mirrors. They were strewn randomly on the walls and ceilings at odd angles, the vision bouncing between them in unpredictable patterns. I winced. No doubt, there was a monster somewhere in the house, staring at a mirror that reflected dozens of others that acted as a security system.

I crept into the house, carefully keeping the tarp forward.

The pace was agonizing. It would be a better descriptor to say I shuffled rather than walked.

Each time I slowed in the slightest, I would move back, locate the mirror, and shatter it with my crowbar. I couldn't reach all of them, particularly the ones on the ceiling, but I could somewhat trace which of their brethren were "connected" by vision and attack those instead.

No plant monsters were rushing me, and the work wasn't dangerous, but it was time-consuming, and I couldn't lose focus. I thought I had finally gotten through the worst of it, but in all honesty, I should have known better.

Thump.

A heavy weight from upstairs, as if something had fallen. My body stiffened, my foot ached, and I cried out in frustration, but readied itself to burst into action. Bum-dum—my heartbeat in my chest, a Godsend as it gave me an anchor for the passing of time despite the adrenaline. The floorboards upstairs sobbed as the noise revealed itself to be footsteps.

Bud.

Raise.

Dum.

Step.

Bud.

Raise.

Dum.

Step.

Perfectly in rhythm with my heart.

I could trace the position of the movement by the bowing of the ceiling as it drifted straight toward the stairs leading down. Whatever that thing was, it wasn't human, and it sure as hell wasn't one of those plant monsters.

I took stock of my surroundings. Long, wide hall---stairs taking up half of it. Seemed to be a shotgun house, so the other half had a door leading out. There were no regular openings. Everything seemed to have a door. Two on the left side, one on the right. The closest was on my left.

I shifted fast, grabbed the door handle, and stepped through.

It led to a kitchen. It was bare bones. There was an oven, a microwave, and all the essentials. There were two additional doors. One of them was made of mesh. It pointed towards the barn. Wait. What?

I did a double-take. The barn wasn't supposed to be in that direction.

The groans of wood came closer, and instead of coming from the door I had just entered, they sounded from further into the house.

I recognized something was happening, but I didn't have time to process it.

I tiptoed to the door leading outside, taking care not to rustle the tarp too much. I opened it and stepped through, ready to lead this monster on a chase through the cornfields before looping back.

Instead, I found myself inside the house, as if I had just walked through the front door again. Broken fragments of mirrors littered the ground, and as my eyes traveled from the floor to the stairs, I locked eyes with something monstrous descending.

I struggled to process what I was seeing. A werewolf? One destroyed, broken, and rebuilt. It stood upright, blood and saliva dripping from its shattered opening of four flailing flaps—more blooming wound than mouth or maw. The creature had more skin than fur, with patches of thick, mid-length human hair sprouting in uneven tufts across its body. It hunched in the stairway, its too-long spine curving and twisting against its gaunt, sagging skin. The eyes of the beast were pristine, a deep blue color boring straight into me. Noise cascaded from its mouth, crawling along the floor before tearing up my legs and into my ears, a hiccupping human cackle.

I could see it, and it could see me. I was frozen. I couldn't move. My breath came in heavy gasps. I couldn't think. I had no plan. I couldn't move. I was doomed. My breathing rose faster and faster, and I realized the creature didn't have [Line of Sight]—my breathing would've slowed if it did.

No. I was just afraid.

I didn't know what that thing was. I didn't care to know. I needed to die. I needed to die right now before it got to me, but my body was still frozen as we stared each other down.

It took a step forward.

I bolted for the door on my left.

As soon as I moved, the house shook. The sounds of creaking wood became splintering and breaking. Anything loosely attached to the walls–mirrors, paintings, portraits–crashed to the ground. A side table was upended into the air, flung straight at me. I dove into the room, slamming the door behind me.

Back in the kitchen. Only one other option. I sprinted through the only remaining door.

Bed. Closet. One door, the one I entered. Chair by the window, something pressed up against it that I couldn't see. Ropes were tied around it at chest height. I was on the second floor.

I rushed forward, raising my crowbar high, and brought it down on the invisible plant creature tied to the chair. Tossing the chair aside, a chill shot down my back as parts of the door splintered out behind me. A ripple in space, in waves, washed over me, and I found it hard to tell where anything was. As soon as the wave came, it faded.

I didn't even bother opening the window. I just brought the tarp up in one hand and crashed through. It protected me from the glass, but the fall still landed me on my side.

Dazed, I tried to stand on my feet, my shoulder resisting my movement.

I gave up on that arm. Switched to the other.

Shed the backpack.

A canteen toppled out. I grabbed it.

No time to pick up the tarp.

I was outside now, but I abandoned the idea of running through the fields; the thing was too fast, and the other windows---and monsters within would see me.

I stumbled, hugging the house's edge, and caught sight of the kitchen side door.

From behind me, a large thud resounded as a mass of flesh landed on dirt.

I stepped inside and found myself back at the front door.

The hallway was decimated. No time to pause.

I ran for the stairs. If that thing was on the second story originally, that meant there was something important up there worth guarding. I could find it.

I was more crawling than stepping up the stairs as half of them had been caved in by the monster's weight. I turned the corner of the staircase as the front door creaked open again. This time, the beast didn't kick it open with force. Just a soft opening, like someone coming home.

The beast laughed. Its short, choking hiccups trailing behind me.

I passed the half-landing of the staircase, moving up a few more stairs before I felt myself slow. I caught the mirror at the end of the hall, then another, and another. The mirrors. The fucking mirrors, and I didn't have the tarp. Could I loop around to get it? Not likely. I took the canteen and chucked it at the nearest mirror.

It let me walk a few more steps, reaching the next door before I was slowed again. Behind me, I could hear the massive footsteps ascending the stairs. But I also listened to them crack and burst underneath heavy footfalls. It would buy me time. Not much.

I needed to do something now.

I could steal a door? Use it as a shield.

I opened the nearest room and looked within to a laundry room. Dead end. From the hall, I shoved Peeler in the space between the wall and the hinges and pulled.

The house shook. The disorienting ripple of energy washed over me once more.

I frowned, my heart quickening as I considered the possibility of another, scarier monster—one big enough to make a house shake—waking up.

I pushed my crowbar further in and heaved. The space that took up the threshold writhed, deforming and lashing out in great bursts.

A strand of space punctured out, hitting me. My entire chest split in two places, existing in both simultaneously. The world corrected the discontinuity and launched me to the opposite wall of the hall with a force that broke bones.

My vision left me. I smelt blood. Light came back to my eyes as my sight slowly returned. There was a flickering of space at the threshold between the hallway and the room. Two existences, one that led to the laundry room and the other to a bedroom. My eyes widened. The spatial effects could be broken.

My joy didn't last long. I heard the monsters laugh. I saw its maw peeking over the top of the banister. It smiled, twisting its head as if curious. Its horribly serpentine spine slowly began to straighten, the muscles in its neck stretched, and the smile rose and scraped the ceiling.

Then the head burst forward. Its jaws were closing on my forearm before I noticed, just above my wrist. My body reacted, and I jerked back. My mind still hadn't caught up. Shark-rowed teeth shredded my receding arm from four angles. Blood cascaded out, and the mouth closed around my hand on all but my pointer finger and thumb.

I wasn't sure if I was relieved that the monster's teeth were so sharp they pierced through flesh completely. Perhaps it would have gained enough purchase to drag me if they were duller. In the moment all I could do was scream.

My brain had finally caught up.

I held the bloody hole that was my hand. My two remaining fingers twitched but were unresponsive. Torrents of blood flowed freely from me. Tears fell, and I scrambled for the one thing I trusted. Peeler, which was still wedged between the doorframe.

A thought hit my rattled, delirious mind.

Ha. Hahaha! Yeah. That would work.

I stepped forward and moved the half-broken door back into place; the crazed energy settled down as I did. I stepped through, placing my crowbar towards the bottom as I did so. I lay on the floor, my feet coiled like springs. Now, it was just timing. I was good at timing.

I leaned on the crowbar, putting enough force to create tension without breaking the door. The stomps of the monster grew closer, and with it, the horrifying laugh that filled me with equal parts rage and fear.

I blinked. My only warning was the fast pattering of its feet that exerted themselves in thunderous footfalls.

Its arm was wrapped around my left leg, jaw halfway down into a bite, when I kicked Peeler. Hard. Harder than I've ever kicked anything in my life.

Thunder. Pain. My ears were ringing, my bones aching, and my arms didn't rise easily—fighting any semblance of movement as my nerves danced. What they danced to, I wasn't sure, but it must've been pain. It was all numb now.

I opened my eyes. The beast's jaws were pierced into my leg. One of its limbs lay a few feet away. Its torso was attached to its elongated neck, but its legs were nowhere to be seen. I should've been in the kitchen, but instead, I was in one of the bedrooms.

The rest of the room was in disarray. The force from the rupturing was enough to push everything a few feet back or knock them over. Glass shards were scattered around the floor. At least I wouldn't have to keep dealing with that. But that thought was cold comfort.

I wanted to stay on the floor. To close my eyes and sleep. I mean, did I really care about the monsters anyway? Did I really hate Maggie, Nate, and the Instance Holder enough to go through with this?

Yeah. I did.

I tried to move. I was sluggish. Unnaturally so. I waited for my eyes to open and saw a fallen chair with a rope facing me. To my surprise, no vague self-deprecating remarks about luck came to mind. I was simply too tired for snark.

It was a bitter realization to know that the only arm that worked at the moment was the one with two fingers. Practically one—the thumb—as the pointer was only barely holding on after the explosion. It was enough to creep to my feet and stumble to the chair to bash the plant monster's brains in.

Shaking heavily, exhausted, and on the verge of passing out, I felt at the unresponsive arm. Dislocated. With great effort, I managed to brace the arm straight against the wall. I took deep breaths. I remember reading somewhere that humans screamed before doing something scary because it tricked their bodies into producing more adrenaline. I don't think my adrenaline had stopped since entering the house, but it was worth a shot. I let out a scream that sounded dull and shoved my body into my shoulder. I felt the joint join once again, and I crumpled to the ground. Everything was overwhelmingly numb; I could feel where the agony wanted to be as light electric zaps convulsed my arm, but there was no actual feeling—like pressure on a sedated limb.

I stumbled out of the room, heading to the staircase, until a sudden panic shook the fatigue from both mind and body.

Peeler. Where was Peeler! A chill washed over me as I imagined it shattered into pieces. I almost didn't look, standing still, frozen in indecision. It took blood pooling from my mouth to the point it overflowed and dripped out of my lips to draw me back out of my stupor.

I looked around the hallway, and to my infinite relief, it had just embedded itself into the wall, still as pristine as when I found it. I limped with great speed, retrieved it, and recovered a small modicum of safety. I looked from the staircase to the remaining doors.

I sighed and limped further down the hallway. I needed to kill the other monsters tied up in the other rooms. I froze again. No. That was tertiary to everything else. I wasn't in any shape to be picking fights, even against incapacitated opponents.

I limped downstairs. I took note of the paintings and pictures. The house was decorated well, and if not for the monsters, nicely designed. It gave the image of a loving family, but I swore to the Gods, if the scientist gave me some sob story, I'd put the crowbar through his eye.

Finding the basement was easy since the spatial magic was gone. The only two places the lab could be were the attic and the basement. I bet on the basement.

I sat at the bottom of the stairs. The sound of a generator running filled my ears as I stared at the scene. Cages and dried blood splattered the floor. Books were scattered, pages open and bookmarked on tables, on the tops of shelves, and all over the floor. The walls were a strange mix of dirt and stone. The only clean part was the operating table in the center of the room.

The cages were full. Various monsters were shoved and twisted inside. Some creatures loosely resembled the dog monster from upstairs, others more similar to the plant creatures. They reacted poorly to my presence, whipping themselves into a frenzy, but even that was pathetic. Their bodies were twisted, contorted to fit into the small space, and it briefly dawned on me that perhaps when that werewolf monster was first created, its spine hadn't curved like that.

I moved to some of the books and saw medical diagrams that I had no way to decipher, but it was simple to get the gist of it. The entire thing was a Chimera farm. There were some passages talking about the importance of blood and the virtues hidden inside of it as an explanation for their obsession with it. He believed that during his integration process, he balanced the humors and virtues between the human and animal/plant parts, but they didn't wish to live in an equilibrium. The thought that only one could exist, and so when met with human or animal blood, they went into a frenzy trying to break the balance.

The process was surprisingly simple. Apparently, he had a needle shot that, when injected into someone, forced any object or creature they touched to integrate into organic matter. It was noted that cutting his Visitor subjects open wasn't strictly necessary, but when certain parts were removed and selectively put together, the results were vastly improved.

An insane man.

I made my way to where I heard the generator. It was behind a locked door, but I had a crowbar.

My mind flickered, and my vision was consumed in darkness. I felt my body hit the floor. After a moment, my vision returned. Where was the enemy? I rolled onto my back, my eyes darting weekly, but I saw nothing and no one. My eyes wanted to close. I felt tired.

Oh. I was dying. No one had attacked me.

I needed to live. I needed to live so I could win this. This was the mad scientist's lair. There had to be something, integration, humors—it all meant nothing to me. I got to my feet like a zombie and threw myself against his work table at the back. Jars, metal instruments, and glass made dull noises.

Glass. Two syringes full of some strange liquid. I saw these before. I took them. Weakly, I began limping towards the cages. Maybe I could integrate with one of them. Maybe it would save my life. I took another step. No. That was dumb. Even now, in such a desperate situation, I knew it was dumb. So, I moved past the cages upstairs.

I wanted to make it to the second story, but as I looked at the broken stairs, I knew I didn't have it in me. So I went to the kitchen instead, where two legs lay near an open door leading to a laundry room. I injected the synergine into my dribbling hand and reached out to the limb.

My hand turned soft like hot wax or moist clay. It deformed against the wolf's leg, covered in human hair. Agony. I felt the bones churn in my hand. I felt the ghost pangs of a torso that wasn't mine. I felt my leg twitch, except the feeling came from my hand. Pain and agony, even through the fog and adrenaline.

I had never been so thankful to pass out.

When I came to, I instantly checked the time as I allowed static to take my sight. Only eight minutes had passed. Good. Good. Hungry. I was so hungry. I smelt something. Delicious. My mouth salivated. It was all around me. So–

I opened my eyes. I wanted to want to throw up, but I just grew hungrier at the sight of all the blood. I tore my eyes away. I felt my previously injured hand drag. My two fingers were still there, but the spot where my three fingers used to be was thicker. Much thicker than my hand or forearm. An entire leg was melded there, hanging low and dragging against the floor. I could feel the bare toes against the wooden planks.

I focused on anything else, like how I had stopped bleeding from my hand and mouth. I was still aching all over, but I wasn't dead. A vast improvement. I took a step, and the balls of the grotesque foot dragged across the wood, causing all fifteen of my toes to curl.

I jerked and grabbed at the arm, hefting the heavy appendage off the ground. Later. Always later. Deal with it later.

I made my way back to the basement and finished opening the door to the generator with Peeler one-handed. Inside were several switches that looked like they were responsible for routing power. I raised every single one of them. I looked at three gas canisters in the corner of the small room. I felt a horrid grin creep across my face, and I couldn't help but let out a long, drawn-out laugh.

It was almost time for the final act.
