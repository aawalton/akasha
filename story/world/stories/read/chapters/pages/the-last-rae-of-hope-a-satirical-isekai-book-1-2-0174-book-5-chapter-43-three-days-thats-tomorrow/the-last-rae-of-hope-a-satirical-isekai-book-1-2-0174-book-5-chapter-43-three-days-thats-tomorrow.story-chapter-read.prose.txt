Olethros was the one who pulled me off stage through the portal mirror. As soon as I was out of the public eye and into the war room, I showed my true self.

“There was supposed to be more time!” I shouted at the ceiling, flinging my arms wide. “Tomorrow? Tomorrow! That’s only a day away!”

Nora bit her lip, choking back a poorly-timed laugh. “Not even. It’s technically only a few hours away, but that’s not something to be worried about—”

“I have every right to be worried! We don’t know who we’re going to encounter outside of Chairo, what traps have been set, or hell, what’s the weather like?!”

“Marching isn’t the same as arriving,” Olethros noted carefully, reaching for my hand.

I pulled it out of reach. “Explain.”

For some reason, he looked rather guilty. “My brother wishes to put his troops through their paces. I must admit, his plan was a bit rough in the beginning, but after prying through his pride, his rationale makes sense.”

“Only when you’ve heard it,” I grumbled, folding my arms.

Olethros took the hint and put his hands behind his back. “He’s making them work together again. But beyond that, he’s also rooting out spies and traitors. Marching for demons isn’t exhausting—it’s a good way for us to gather ambient animus. At least, when they’re marching on the ground.”

His oddly contrite tone cut through my anxiety, poorly disguised as rage, and I found myself curious. “Uh, where else would a demon army march?”

He grinned weakly. “Why, through this Bastion. Galenus has several rooms simulating the landscape of their trek.”

I glanced at Nora.

“Olethros said he was going to tell you!” she protested. “Again, I’m not keeping secrets!”

Even before my head stopped swiveling between them, Olethros replied quickly, “I was going to talk him out of it, but it had more merit than I realized. She’s right; I did promise to tell you. However, negotiations with my brother went on longer than I intended. This one is… What is the phrase? My bad.”

I grumbled for a few moments. “He called you ‘Your Majesty’. I bet that negotiation point took the longest.”

A dark shadow flickered across his face, his eyes snapping shut before they shared their secrets. “Indeed...”

Nora cleared her throat. “It’s not just demons marching with him, either. Father Irijah’s with them, along with Captain Armand, Jessamy, and ex-captain Alban. Oh! And a sizeable part of the Order of Blue. Captain Armand did a bit of recruiting.”

“They’re all going to be together?”

“Ehh… Sort of. Once they start, we’ll keep a bit of distance between groups but do some small stuff together. At night, everyone will take a rest, with the night watch sparse. Bodhil and Beigaldi will be watching for any fights—and setting up traps for anyone wanting to use the downtime to communicate with Relias.”

“There are troops who want to do that?”

“Almost always so,” Nora replied. “We’re looking for anyone stupid enough to try to report back to Relias about what we’re bringing with us. We started with the white lie ‘marching on chairo tomorrow’ as the first one to ice their kickers.”

Olethros finally cracked an eye open. “I beg your pardon?”

Nora grinned. “Isn’t it obvious?”

“In no way, shape, or form!”

I slapped my hands together. “To disrupt and exhaust the frontlines! Relias knows we have mass translocation. He’ll think that means we’ll be arriving on the battlefield tomorrow—as I did. But we’re not showing up when he thinks we will, and he’s going to default to being the petty bastard he is, making the frontlines work hard to figure out where and what we’re up to.” I arched an eyebrow at Nora. “However, I think that’s a real stretch of the idiom.”

She shrugged. “That’s what he gets for almost getting me in trouble.”

His Dark Majesty glowered at her for a moment, but it had no effect. “This also gives time for the Skreethi to complete their swim upstream into the sewage lines.”

“The… sewage lines…” I echoed softly with a shudder. “We’re going to owe them so much.”

“Every hanging skreethi pelt in Turri,” Nora agreed. “That’s the going rate for this service.”

“So, when do we actually plan on the army arriving?” I asked.

“The full moon is in three days,” Olethros noted. “Against instinct, we’ll want its light to showcase our planned distraction.”

This was the part I was worried about.

“Do you think your brother can really pull it off?”

“His confidence insists upon it,” Olethros replied with a hiss. “I’ve heard stories of the size he can reach, but I find many tales of his conquests suspect. Ruling the southern wastelands is one thing. Taking on the human capital is another.”

I took a deep breath. “Your father was worried enough that he sent you to stop him. Here’s to hoping that wasn’t just delusion.”

Galenus appeared with a loud buzz, holding out a parchment. “The current weather in Chairo, Captain.”

“Huh?”

He tapped his glasses. “You asked about it earlier.”

“I did?”

“Yes. When you were temper-tantruming.”

“Ah.” I glanced at the lengthy report, summarizing it in one word. “Snow?”

He nodded, even as I saw Nora take a small step back.

“Shouldn’t your blizzard be long gone by now?”

Nora tittered nervously. “You’d think, right?”

“How much is on the ground?”

Galenus shook his head. “It just started. However, the cloud causing it has been hovering there for a long time.”

“Aina?” I asked. “What’s she doing with it? Wouldn’t holding a giant cloud in place be hard?”

Nora nodded. “I’ll take care of it—” She paused, seeing my face contort. “After I think about it for a bit, of course!”

“I don’t want to be struck by lightning, or blown away by a tornado, you hear me?” I warned.

“Picky picky…” I heard her mutter under her breath.

The next three days blurred into war games and worst-case scenarios. Most of them started off well enough, but on the last day, I found myself asking things like, “What if Relias drops Paradise on our heads?” and Olethros reminded me that while there’s no such thing as no risk, some risks are more likely than others.

The cliffside on the outskirts of Chairo was bitterly cold when Aurum and I arrived. Tetora, Vernie, and Nora waited in near-silence, their breath melting the occasional snowflake. Above and all around us, dominating the night sky, hung a giant cloud.

Olethros phased in at my flank, his closed eyes already on it.

Then the portals appeared. Dozens were spreading across the plateau below in rings, all a few shades deeper than the surrounding night. Priests in simple robes walked out of them, side-by-side with masses of demons and armored hybrids. Boots and hooves alike stamped across half-frozen ground, their equipment clinking softly as sounds seemed muffled by both distance and soft snowfall.

Three days hadn’t been enough to drive the recruits to idle chatter with their neighbor, and their shared disgust with the opposition kept them silent but trained on their shared enemy.

Ragnerus assured me he issued the proper rules of engagement... and Olethros confirmed it. I trust them.

Captain Armand and his mount landed next on our side, flanked by Jessamy and Alban. They scanned the terrain and pointed—

A brilliant spark of silver shot into the air from the opposite side of the battlefield, lighting up the opposing army hiding in the shadow of the city. For a brief moment, we could see them: enormous winged angels on the forefront, and full regiments of the Order of White behind them. Here and there in the back were smaller, wingless angels—our first target.

“Nora!”

Before the silver ray could reach its zenith, she snuffed out its light with a contemptuous wave of her staff. The opposing trumpets still sounded, but we had countered the spell that would have disrupted the forms of the demons. Several more desperate silver flares shot skyward, but the Tower of Dark Mages made short work of them.

“Good job,” I commended Nora as more portaling demons flooded our lines. “They’ll probably start shooting blessed bolts at us next.”

“Father Irijah and company can make short work of the blessings on them, and the demons can shield…” She trailed off, her eyes still looking at the giant cloud above the city proper. “But that’s the real problem, I think.”

“I don’t disagree, but I’m not sure I quite understand it,” I admitted.

“I think… Well. I’ve said this before, but air is a terrible conductor,” Nora stated. “Yet that cloud is in between Chairo and Paradise. Ice crystals, even suspended in a cloud… Hmm…”

I shivered for the first time, but not because of the cold. “Something to help transfer magical energy down to Chairo?”

“Or up to Paradise,” Nora replied. “Whatever we’re doing here, we’d better make it quick.”
