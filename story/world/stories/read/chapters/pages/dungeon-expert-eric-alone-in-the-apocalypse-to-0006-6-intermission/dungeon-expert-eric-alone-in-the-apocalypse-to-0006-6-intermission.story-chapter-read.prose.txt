Back at the gas station, Eric sunk to the floor, back to the wall, and just breathed out, enjoying the A/C and safety alike. He’d done it. He’d entered a dungeon and survived. The few snakes he’d encountered on the way back didn’t even seem that dangerous anymore.

He didn’t sit still for long. He had work to do, even if he wasn’t going to clear the dungeon today, and he had to keep moving. Had to. Otherwise the thoughts would slip in. Thoughts like how alone he was. How he’d killed things. Real living things. Things with thoughts, that reacted as though they had feelings. Killed them with no hesitation or remorse, and just kept moving. Killed them so they wouldn’t kill him, but it didn’t make the guilt weigh less heavily on his shoulders. He kept running the images in his head: the snakemen’s lifeless bodies. The blood spilling over the dusty ground. The elation he’d felt at being alive, at killing. At winning. If he sat still, all of it looped in his head, ready to be examined and reexamined and stared at until he didn’t know who he was anymore, and with no one to talk to, there was nothing to quiet the questions in his head. So instead, he moved.

He started a routine. Every morning, he got up and cleared the clearing of any threatening monsters, then ran a hundred laps around its perimeter, carrying a full load of weapons. Anything that got in his way also met death. When he was too exhausted to move anymore, he retreated to the gas station. Pushups, situps, even pullups, using the toilet stall crossbar as a pullup bar, then exercises he half-remembered from his swim team days: flutterkicks, bicycle kicks, weighted squats (using beer kegs as weights) and kettle bell swings (same). For ordinary weightlifting, bicep curls and the like, he used gallon bottles of water. After all that, he practiced bat swings and his best guess at what knife fighting looked like. His phone was nothing but a pile of dust in the pocket of his ruined khakis, so that was the best he could do.

When his arms and legs felt like limp noodles and he couldn’t press on any further, he sat down in the center of the gas station and practiced Regeneration. Over and over, he practiced snapping his mana into the right channels and circulating it in the right shape for Regeneration. The more he circulated it in the right shape, the more it remembered the shape, and the easier it was to snap it into place. It got to the point that he could almost do it without thinking, and still he kept practicing, but now he mixed it in with the other exercises. He practiced Regeneration while he ran, while he lifted weights, while he did floor exercises, until he could snap into it at any moment, no matter how tired he was.

Not only that, but he noticed another side effect of using Regeneration. Fundamentally, working out was about breaking down muscle fibers so they could grow back stronger. When he ran Regeneration alongside his workouts, not only could he work out for longer, but he could also build muscle faster. The better he got at Regeneration, the better he got at working out, and the stronger he grew. By the time two weeks had passed, even his stats reflected the change:

He hadn’t gained any levels, but he’d gained stats anyways as the sheet updated to reflect his efforts. It was more than a single level, even. Still, he had the distinct feeling that these gains were only possible because he’d started so weak, and moreover, that there was a ceiling to them. Using Regeneration pushed that ceiling back, but it wouldn’t push it back forever. There was an upper limit to the strength a human could hit naturally, and by the end of the week, while he hadn’t hit that ceiling—far from it—he could tell that he’d already hit the point of diminishing gains. He’d quickly climbed from 4 to 5 STR over the first two days, and 5 to 6 had only taken another three or four days, but it had taken him almost an entire week to reach 7, and he was currently stalled there. STR was just one example; it was the same across all his stats.

For all that, he didn’t feel at all that it was wasted effort. He was stronger and more fit than he’d been in a long time, and he knew what his body was capable of much more clearly than he had before. On top of that, he’d picked a class with low STR growth and hadn’t gotten a single STR on his first level. Eventually, it would become irrelevant, but at these low levels, STR was just as important for him as it was for everyone else.

He was cooling down after his workout, sucking down a post-workout protein shake, when something thumped down behind the counter. Eric whirled, startled, to find a pimple-faced young woman hunched behind the counter.

“Uh… hi?” he tried, a bit lost.

“Hey, kid. Damn, I could use some sugar right now. You got any of that highly processed corn syrup stuff lying around?”

Eric gestured at the entire store around him. “Take your pick. And, um… Joe, right?”

The young woman glanced at her nametag. “I’m Angela now, I think. But you can keep calling me Joe if it makes you feel better.” She hopped nimbly over the counter and beelined for the soda fountain, filling up a big cup, then wandered over to the candy section and pulled a dozen packets of gummies off the rack. Walking back over to the counter with her treats, she hopped up onto the scanning area and cracked open the first bag, tossing a whole mouthful into her mouth at once. She chewed loudly, her mouth half-open, eyes half-shut in delight.

“Damn,” Eric muttered.

“What? I’m really craving sugar. Specifically corn syrup. I can’t help it. That, and why not? It’s not like this is a real body. I can indulge in all of its vices without any of the downsides, so why should I hold back?” Joe—Eric decided to keep calling him Joe—tossed his hair in an imperious manner that Eric highly doubted the cringing, pimply young woman had, and threw back another handful of pure sugar.

“Did you come here to say something, or just to go on a sugar binge?” Eric asked, stretching. His original polo and khakis had gotten ruined in his first run at the dungeon, and he wore the CAT shirt with the yellow tire mark and the matching yellow shorts now. He’d taken to wearing a shirt and shorts until they got too crusty to keep wearing, then tossing the dirty clothes in a corner and taking a new set off the rack. The old clothes vanished overnight, and new ones appeared on the rack the next morning. For a while, he’d tried changing his clothes every day, but the shirts had slowly become thinner and thinner until they were barely shirts at all. When he gave it a few days to regenerate in between each set, they came back at full thickness instead.

“Both. First off, the first episode is airing tonight. You should watch it.”

“I can watch it?” Eric asked, shocked.

“Sure. It’ll be on the TV.” He thumbed over his shoulder at the cash register’s monitor.

“That’s not a TV,” Eric said.

“It’s good enough for us.”

Eric shrugged. Joe was the expert there. “Is it a biweekly show, or…?”

“No. It’ll be nightly after this. The two-week lag is just to give them time to decide the main characters for the first couple of episodes and to get enough hits on film during the most boring portion of the game—the opening days of the Tutorial—to be worth primetime. Of course, some of the premium viewers have been watching since day one, but they’re real addicts who don’t mind weeks of filler for one minute of killer. Most of those already have their favorites. They might even be sending them gifts already. You’ll have to watch out for those—the spoiled brats will have overpowered tools and an attitude to match. They’re my least favorite types… but there’s some whales out there that love nothing more than spoiling up a big fat brat for primetime.”

“Oh,” Eric said, feeling like he’d just been blasted with information. Most conversations with Joe felt that way, to be fair. He was starting to get used to it.

“The point is, watch the show tonight. You’ll get a feel for what plays and what doesn’t. It isn’t that different from your reality TV, but there’s some nuance to watch out for. That aside. When are you going back to finish off that dungeon?”

“Soon,” Eric said, and meant it. He was almost strong enough. He might even be strong enough. He just wanted to be sure before he moved.

“Today. Don’t put it off any longer. Trust me.”

Eric looked at Joe, but Joe didn’t explain any further. Eric nodded, a little lost. Still, so far, Joe hadn’t led him wrong, so he made a note to head out after their conversation.

Joe tossed back the bag of gummies, sucking down the sugar at the bottom of the bag, and Eric finally asked the question he’d been holding onto since the start. “Why are you a girl now?”

“Huh?” Joe looked down at himself, then grabbed his chest and stared, as if he’d just realized. “Huh, mammaries. I guess it is female. I didn’t notice! Hey, don’t get it twisted, kid. I barely know what your females look like. Or your males. Whichever you’re into.” He squinted. “You… boy…?”

“I’m male, yes.”

“Oh, good. I wasn’t sure. Gendering weird hairless monkeys isn’t my specialty.”

“Apes,” Eric corrected him. Humans were apes, not monkeys.

“Potato, po tah toe.” He gestured at the sun. “It’s a different day, a different time. Remember, this is just a mimetic body I’ve generated to appear before you; this isn’t me. If you killed this ‘me,’ it wouldn’t harm a hair on my real head. Mimetic bodies powerful and flexible, capable of taking almost any shape and possessing power even greater than that of the caster, but in return, they are tied to the memories of the place. If I’m someone else in the same place, it means that the place remembers someone else there at this time, on this day. Joe had a different shift from Angela. Last time, I was here on Joe’s shift. Now, I’m here on Angela’s. The place remembers, and the place gives shape to the body. If I show up at night or on Tuesday instead of Monday, I’ll be someone else. Don’t worry about it; it doesn’t mean anything.”

Eric nodded even though he didn’t understand anything. He squinted at Joe. “What are you?”

“Regional System Admin. A skrub. Totally worthless to the corporation and primed for demotion.”

“No, I mean… physically. What are you?”

Joe waggled his finger and smirked, an expression that had made more sense on Joe’s grizzled face than it did on Angela’s pimpled babyface. “Dangerous question, kid. I can answer it, but you don’t want me to.”

“Why not?”

“Let’s just say ignorance is bliss. Anyways, I didn’t come here to talk about me. I came here to talk about you.”

Eric folded his arms. “Watch the show, run the dungeon today. What else?”

Joe frowned, a deeply somber expression somewhat undermined by the sugar clinging to his face. “Once the show starts, you’re going to get offers. Even the little guys do, at first, so it’s worth warning you. There’s two types of offers. Sponsorships and Partnerships. Sponsorships are fine. Those are like… like your ad reads on your primitive streaming services. You have to put on a good face while they’re sponsoring you, but when it’s over, it’s over, and back to your usual hijinks. Partnerships, though—that’s what you have to avoid at all costs.”

“They’re not the same?”

“Not at all. They’re more like… more like your favorite streamer’s channel getting bought out by a big media company. Someone else greenlights everything you do. Someone else has veto power. They’ll replace your manager with one of their own men and control your story. When they want you to hit hard, you’d better hit hard. And when they decide they’re done with your plotline… well.” Joe grimaced and shrugged. “That’s nighty-night for you.”

“Don’t like that,” Eric said.

“Yeah. Especially you, who wants creative control so badly—don’t sign up for a Partnership. Well… I imagine you’re already against ‘em after what I’ve said. Still. I’d tell anyone the same, even someone who was totally malleable and willing to do whatever they wanted, because it’s not just creative control they want. They want everything. They want you.” He opened his mouth, but no sound came out. After a moment, he smirked. “Guess I can’t tell you that yet, but trust me. Partnerships are bad news.”

Eric nodded. “Got it. No Partnership, but Sponsorship okay.”

“Yeah. Sponsorships can still be dangerous, so don’t go signing up for them willy-nilly. Let me read the language first and make sure the contract isn’t predatory. But even the worst Sponsorship is head and shoulders better than the best Partnership.” He lowered his head, looking Eric in the eye. “Remember, kid. Sponsorships end.”

“Yeah, I get it.” He had the feeling that Joe was trying to tell him something else, but he didn’t know what. There was something in the way he stressed the words that implied something, but without context he sorely needed, he couldn’t figure out what that something was. Still, he got the basics, and that was good enough for now.

“Sure ya do.” Joe sighed. “Oh well. You’re a smart kid. You’ll figure it out eventually. But yeah. Get out there. Clear the dungeon today if at all possible. Watch the show tonight. That’s it.” Joe ripped open another bag of sour worm gummies, downed them in one, and, chewing in open-mouthed ecstasy, vanished off the counter.

Tiny sugar and sour crystals rained down on the counter where he’d sat. Eric grimaced. Gross.

He turned toward the door. Finish the dungeon today. He still had daylight to burn, and Joe didn’t hand out advice lightly. If he was telling Eric to do something, there was a good reason to listen, even if Eric didn’t know what it was.

I’m stronger now. I’m better equipped to take on that challenge. Strong enough? Equipped enough? He took a deep breath. He could always turn back. Life was more important than whatever Joe wasn’t telling him.

I’m going to survive this. No matter what. Resolve in his step, he strode outside, back into the fight.
