The village craftsmen had also been hard at work as usual. Cedoron had sent someone in the morning to tell him that the second steel-making furnace had been baked fully and had cooled down, so the blacksmith planned to put in a fresh batch of iron rods in it today. Apart from that, the third furnace for steel-making had also been shaped from clay, and that would also be put to start baking now. It would take around a week for it to be baked fully and to cool down, and then they could put in a third batch of iron to make steel in that one.

The best news was that the first furnace was already cooling down the steel, And in around 10 days, the bars of iron would be ready to take out as the first batch of Made in Tiranat steel. Then the blacksmith would start to make scorpion parts again—with steel this time. On the other hand, Darora had been busy making more and more crossbows until yesterday morning to give to the guards, but now he had returned to his usual task of crafting the wooden parts for the second scorpion. He would only assemble them once he had all the steel components available, but it would make it easier to make multiple scorpions when the steel was ready.

The coke-making oven had also cooled down for the first batch, and the blacksmith had started using it for his usual tasks to improve efficiency, and had also put a fresh batch of coal in it to bake again. Of course, the major improvement from coke would come when they started smelting iron ore here and made a blast furnace, but that was for the future. The single beehive oven for making coke would be enough for the blacksmith’s usual tasks for now.

Taniok had also been working to make the first triphammer for the last week, and this morning he had reported that it was finally done. Cedoron had taken over the triphammer's operations, and would use it for forging iron and steel. It would free up a lot of manual beating of the hot iron done by his apprentices, which would speed up the tasks throughout the production chain of everything built of iron or steel.

Taniok’s apprentices had also supervised the construction of the third longhouse properly and had ensured it got ready as soon as possible. Fitting the two layers of bunks on each of the floors had been done rapidly this time, with the sawmills producing the planks at a breakneck pace.

With the third longhouse block now ready, the apprentices had started stocking up timber to make the losuvil processing encampment in the eastern hills. The sawmills were producing plenty of timber these days, although a shortage of log-movers was still severely limiting their output from what they could produce at full capacity. Still, it was quite enough for their current uses, and for the next few days needed to gather enough timber for the encampment, Taniok would work on the second triphammer, which was planned to be used for making paper at a larger scale. The old carpenter expected that with all the required parts for the second triphammer already stocked up, he just had to assemble it, and it should be done by the time he had to leave for making the encampment. After that they could shift the full process of making paper to the eastern bank of the stream at the dam.

The plan was for him to leave when they had around half of the construction material ready, and the guards had returned from the counter raid. The transport wagons would then come back to the village for taking another batch of construction materials to the encampment site. After that, all the timber from the sawmills would be used for making the fourth longhouse block. that would also be double story and taniok's apprentices would oversee the construction again.

The trench surrounding the village walls had also been completed recently, and the recent rains had filled it with water, making it look like a proper moat. Now they just needed to put some alligators in it.... He chuckled, thinking about whether those animals were even present in this world, but if they were, or if there was something even remotely similar, he would try to put them in the moat—just a nice surprise for any would-be raiders.

I think there will still be a few dozen people who’ll have to sleep on the floor and the number will increase every week, but that’s still far less crowded than having hundreds of people sleeping down there."

Kivamus laughed. “How did you read my mind? Actually, I’ve been thinking of something.”

“Of course you have...” Duvas shook his head with a smile. “What is it this time?”

The guards walked in a box formation around them as usual as they walked north towards the longhouse blocks, with the gravel the workers had covered the road in front of the longhouses crunching under their feet.

Kivamus looked at the majordomo and lowered his voice. “I think it is time we started making soap here.”

Kivamus grinned, since Duvas also knew about his origin, apart from his former teacher Gorsazo. “Well, if you know, you know.”

But when the guards return from the west, we’ll have a full 100 of them now. So we can send even more hunting groups after that, by including women in the teams, since we are now slowly getting to the stage where we have enough crossbows for them. That’ll ensure us having a steady supply of tallow. We aren’t burning any wood for heating in this weather, but we are still making lye at a small scale from the stored ash for making paper. We already have some workers trained in it, so we just need to expand its production to start making soap here.”

Once we have the whole setup working properly, I also have some ideas about how to make a better quality luxury soap and maybe even a liquid soap which we can sell for a high price to nobles. We can sell it in Tiranat-made wooden containers as well.”

“That’ll net us a good profit,” Duvas grinned. “We can also sell the surplus from the cheaper soap version to visiting merchants once we are making enough for the village, which will also reduce our expenditure by itself. Or rather, we can start selling it to the villagers instead of providing them a soap bar every fortnight along with their rations, since you plan to start paying them in coins soon. Either way, it will be profitable for us." He snorted. "I guess that’s why the guilds of Plumron keep the process a secret even if it’s so easy.”

Kivamus frowned. “They’ll cause us trouble for this, won’t they?”

Duvas shrugged. “It won’t matter if we are only using the soap locally, but they won’t like it once we start selling outside Tiranat.”

“Hmm... don’t worry. We’ll deal with them once we have to.”

Kivamus looked at the double-story block ahead of them. Time to give a speech.
