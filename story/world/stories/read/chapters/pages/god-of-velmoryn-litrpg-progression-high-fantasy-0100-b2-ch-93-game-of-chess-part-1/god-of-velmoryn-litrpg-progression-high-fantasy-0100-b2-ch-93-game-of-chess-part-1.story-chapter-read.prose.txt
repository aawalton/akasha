I looked at the golden window, unimpressed

[Congratulations, Verde!]

One of your Authorities has reached the required minimum of 100,000 Divinity Points. You may now initiate Rank ascension.

However, if you wish to preserve the Radiance of both your divinities, their growth must remain balanced.

A deity cannot safely ascend while one divinity significantly surpasses another. Each source of divinity should meet the minimum required threshold before ascension. Failure to maintain this balance may result in severe consequences.

Guidance: Accumulate at least 100,000 Divinity Points of both divinities before ascending.

I already knew that much.

The fact that my status window now showed two values for my Divinity Points instead of one was proof enough that after gaining a new Authority, the minimum requirement for raising my Rank had doubled.

I had no intention of ascending and risking slumber now.

Actually, even if I did, I would not have been able to. I had over 150,000 Divinity Points, and with the conversion rate of sixty percent, I would need more than 270,000 Divinity Points to balance the golden and crimson energies and ascend.

I was not even close.

But that was not a bad thing. Not when I still did not know for certain whether I would automatically ascend once I reached the required threshold.

Does Noah not know I have two Authorities?

If I trusted the God of Beggars’ words, then the Night God’s plan had been to force me into slumber and slaughter my believers while I could offer them no divine opposition.

I had to admit, that sounded like something Noah would do.

Yet, it felt simple. Far too simple.

How was it possible that a god who had managed to deceive so many deities, even the Goddess herself, would make such foolish mistakes against me?

Had I overestimated the Night God?

Was I overthinking and becoming a victim of my own paranoia?

No.

I did not think so. Expecting more from a god who had built his power by outsmarting other deities was not a mistake, especially when he seemed to deceive divine beings more often than a village charlatan sold miracle potions to desperate peasants.

I felt trapped in a chess game where I could not even see my opponent’s moves, his pieces hidden behind a thick veil. And whatever slipped past that veil seemed so simple and foolish that it only made me doubt his true motives even more.

Let’s check on Aurelion and the Velmoryns first. I’ll let these thoughts settle, then properly consider every option I have.

Realizing I had spent a little too much time focused on what was happening inside my realm, I removed the layer of crimson energy I had created to isolate the God of Beggars from my divine realm.

His sphere once again hovered before me, dull and unresponsive.

I pressed on it, making sure I had not missed anything and that the God of Beggars had truly perished. After wasting a few moments and sensing absolutely nothing alive within, I briefly considered channeling my divinity into the core.

It was empty. The presence of a god was completely gone, and I was more or less confident it was safe. But I still decided against experimenting with it. This was not the right time. Once the war was over, I would have more than enough time to test it properly.

Adding several layers of divinity around the god’s sphere, making sure none of them touched its surface, I smiled. Satisfied.

There was no such thing as too much caution after all.

Then I flew toward the Window and checked the rest of the notifications I had ignored before.

Almost all of them contained information about things I already knew: the Divinity Points I had obtained, the impressive feat I had accomplished by killing a god, how Aurelion had started using my divinity to heal himself…

All of them felt unimportant.

Except two.

[The Pantheon has decided to pay close attention to your rapid growth.]

…

Fuck…

I knew that sooner or later, I would be forced to meet whoever ruled the Pantheon. Those powerful bastards, who in my mind were so absurdly strong that they bordered on omnipotent, would definitely come into contact with me someday.

But not now. Not while I was not strong enough. Not when I needed to dedicate all my attention to the war against Noah.

I stopped my thoughts before they could spiral out of control.

I loved this talent of mine. Silencing thoughts was something I had tried far too many times as a human, more than I was willing to admit. But back then, it had obviously been impossible. Even the thought of silencing thoughts was still a thought.

But as Verde, I could do it.

That brief silence always brought me peace and clarity, and taking a pause helped me see things from a different perspective.

Even now, as I shut down the threads of my mind and allowed quiet to wash over me, I felt… free. All the worries, doubts, and fears disappeared for a moment.

Good. There is no bad news. Only obstacles I have to overcome.

If the word “attention” had frightened me at first, now I could also see it as a dangerous opportunity. After all, any change and widening of one’s reach always brought new possibilities with it, and once I was ready, I would welcome them.

For now, the Pantheon was not standing at my door and knocking, so wasting more time on it would have been foolish.

I moved to the next notification.

The one that required my immediate attention.

[New Authority!]

You may claim a new Authority, Verde. The Authority of the God of Beggars is yours to inherit.

Ordinarily, stealing another deity's Authority requires seizing a portion of their domain or an extension of their divine presence. The alternative, reserved for the mightiest of gods, is to slay the deity outright.

The Authority of the God of Beggars has 1,245,721 followers, all of them human.

The specifics of this domain will be revealed upon claiming it.

Note: You may instead choose to destroy the Authority. It cannot be bestowed upon one of your believers, as none currently meet the requirements to inherit it.

There was nothing to consider, really.

I already had two Authorities, and even maintaining those two felt absurdly difficult.

When I had gained the velmoryn domain and realized just how powerful its effects were, I assumed that gaining more Authorities could only be a good thing. But the God of Many Faces had been an eye-opener. Even as a god, nothing was free. Gaining more Authorities meant I would need that much more divinity to ascend, and worse, I would have to purify more divinities to improve my Radiance.

That was what bothered me most.

By now, I had confirmed several times that my Radiance was what truly made me powerful. Athena, the God of Beggars, even Noah… my divinity was far stronger than theirs.

But even though these lower deities that troubled me now possessed divinity less pure than mine, there was no guarantee the Pantheon, or even just high-Ranked gods in general, would be the same.

The time would definitely come when I faced a god with the same Radiance as me, perhaps even higher.

No, new Authorities be damned, I needed to raise my rank and improve my Radiance above all else.

Making up my mind, I focused inward and searched for this new Authority that must have tethered itself to me. I needed to sever that link, to crush the bond before it grew too strong to cut away.

As I submerged in darkness, my crimson divinity appeared, roaring through my realm, swollen and overwhelming after I had absorbed the God of Beggars’ power. Then I felt pure gold, a thin but brilliant current moving elegantly within the scarlet ocean. Despite one being so much greater than the other, the two existed in harmony.

“Where are you…” I whispered, trying to ignore both gold and crimson as I searched for a different kind of energy.

It took me a moment to shut my senses against the very power that made me, the very power that was part of me, perhaps even was me.

The golden color disappeared first, like a sun swallowed by layers of cloud. The crimson followed, darkening until it turned black.

Then I felt it.

A wisp.

No, weaker than that.

A speck. A single particle of foreign energy that felt wrong.

It was green, but not vibrant and alive like grass or leaves in early spring. It was dull and murky, like the surface of a stagnant swamp. Its presence unsettled me, rubbing against something inside me in every wrong way.

“Disgusting…” The word escaped my mouth involuntarily.

I did not know why I felt that way. Green was one of my favorite colors, after all, and other than its appearance, I sensed almost nothing from it. It did not feel dominant like my crimson or elegant like my gold… it felt like nothing.

It was simply there. A dull, unwanted stain.

Reaching for it, I felt pressure, as if my nonexistent heart had begun to ache.

I endured.

I focused on the speck and pressed down on it, forcing it out. It was not needed. My realm would never become its dwelling place.

Even with my eyes closed and my mind fully focused on that tiny fragment, I felt my divine realm tremble. The mist began to boil, swirling up and down, inward and outward, as though the very space around me resisted what I was doing.

The green speck flickered.

Then vanished.

My realm settled, and another window flashed before me.

[You successfully destroyed the Authority of the God of Beggars!]
