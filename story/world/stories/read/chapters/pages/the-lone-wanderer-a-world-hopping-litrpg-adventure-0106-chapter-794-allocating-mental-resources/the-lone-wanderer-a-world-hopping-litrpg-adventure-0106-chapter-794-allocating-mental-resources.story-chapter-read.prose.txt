Percy had to admit that Machaon’s latest move had rendered the alliance that he had painstakingly built practically useless.

In terms of personal strength, his upgraded physique and expanded mental capacity were more than enough for him to feel confident in challenging even a demigod with Micky’s help. By the time his third core caught up with the other two and the patriarchs of the Great Houses mastered the Dance, the Lone Wanderer might be strong enough to take all six of them on.

Unfortunately, the same couldn’t be said for his subordinates. With the alliance’s expansion now stunted, there was no amount of second cores, composite affinities, nor reinforced bones that could strengthen the twelve families on his side enough to matter. Not if they would have to fight against every noble House on the planet, which was exactly the situation they were heading towards.

The hive was the only reason Machaon’s army hadn’t invaded yet.

The Blue wasps alone were more than a match for Remior’s nobility, as there were over half a million of them hiding within Percy’s underground kingdom. Nine out of ten were admittedly mere Starry Breeders, which were flightless, largely immobile, and terrible combatants.

Still, fifty thousand Starry Commanders were a force to be reckoned with. Had Percy been able to deploy them freely, he would have won a long time ago.

The problem was that beasts at that level burned a lot of stamina just to stay alive. Even with a second core and a boosting art bolstering their reserves, the creatures wouldn’t last long if they left their special habitat and exerted themselves on an extended mission.

Flying to Machaon’s stronghold would take several days at a minimum, by which time the Starry Commanders would be too weak to fight. If their opponents weren’t complete idiots, they wouldn’t wait for the wasps to catch them. They would flee around the continent until the creatures starved themselves to death or grew weak enough to slaughter with ease.

All things considered, the Great Houses were currently unable to invade the Fungal Spire, and it was debatable whether their side would ever be powerful enough to take Percy’s army of monsters head on, though they might be forced to take that risk if their plans for the artificial hive failed or once their side stopped growing.

Meanwhile, Percy didn’t have a way to bring his enemies down either. The Guild’s Aurora Dew output would probably be enough to meet his side’s cleansing needs for the foreseeable future, but they would also remain cut off from the rest of the planet until he figured out a way to win.

More importantly, Percy understood better than anyone how precious their time was. The sooner this war ended, the sooner the people of Remior would begin preparing for their next challenge.

‘Weaponizing the hive might be my only path to victory,’ he realized.

He’d tried to win without using the wasps as anything more than a deterrent, but there was no denying that they were his most powerful asset by far. If he could somehow harness their strength without restrictions, he would be able to bring down pretty much anyone who wasn’t a god.

He knew that there was an answer hidden somewhere between his mind affinity and his familiar’s mutations. Maybe the Command trait as well. He just had to keep digging until he found it…

One of his ethereal cords vibrated as he was pondering over the problem, indicating that his soul clone was finally on the way back from Tenefyre. Percy no longer thought that his or Micky’s personal strength would be the decisive factor in the war against the Great Houses, but the idea of his friend acquiring another blessing still excited him.

He decided to take a short break from his other problems to sort the clone’s gains out.

The wisp fused into his soul a few minutes later, passing him his piece of the spatial seal which hopefully contained their prize. Assimilating his counterpart’s memories, Percy quickly understood that they had successfully collected over sixteen hundred droplets of augmented Clear mana from the ocean of magma.

It might have been overkill, because that was supposedly more than the Violet ice users on the greater spring needed to internalize the blessing. Since Micky’s second core was two grades lower than the Tenefyrians’, he might only need to absorb a fraction of the mana. That said, Percy hadn’t wanted to risk wasting their efforts. If they ended up needing fewer droplets, they could always give the rest to other ice users in the alliance.

Studying the greater spring’s method of extracting and storing the conceptual energy within a Ring of Sacrilege was also something that Percy wanted to do one day, but it wasn’t a priority right now.

‘How am I meant to use it?’ Micky asked after discarding the upper half of the oversized geode and examining the midnight blue liquid that swirled within the bottom half. ‘I don’t think it’s safe to drink.’

‘Well… your body is more disposable than most, though I agree that drawing Clear mana directly into a Green core would be dangerous,’ Percy replied.

He hadn’t been as concerned about consuming the life mana during the ritual, because that affinity had a natural tendency to permeate a person’s body and mend their wounds. The worst that could happen would be to waste the precious resource on a healthy body that didn’t need it.

Other elements were far more volatile, however.

‘I have no idea how the natives of that planet typically absorb the droplets, but I suggest that you use the external part of your boosting art to mix them with yours and slowly pull the diluted mana to your core,’ Zoris said.

Micky nodded, already following the disembodied god’s instructions.

‘Do you have any idea what concept they contain?’ Percy asked.

‘You’ll have to describe what they look and feel like,’ the former deity replied.

‘Right…’ Percy kept forgetting that Zoris couldn’t perceive the world like a regular person. After millennia of imprisonment inside his ring, he had learned to interact with others quite well, using nothing but Soul Vision and the pitiful morsel of control he could exert upon his rigid body. Sadly, he couldn’t see colours, hear sounds, nor feel how hot or cold something was.

Percy proceeded to describe the strangely-coloured liquid, as well as the way in which it had interacted with its scorching surroundings.

‘Must be the Blessing of Frost,’ Zoris concluded.

‘Frost?’ Percy raised an eyebrow. ‘So what, is it just colder than regular ice mana?’

‘More or less. Didn’t you mention that one of your other friends has the Blessing of Heat? It’s pretty much the exact opposite of that, though the Blessing of Frost is the auxiliary concept of a composite affinity rather than a common one.’

‘Does that make a difference?’

‘Other than the fact that it’s rarer, no.’

Percy had honestly expected a less straightforward blessing. Then again, he’d known that not every auxiliary concept was as obscure as the two he had internalized. Either way, this explained everything he had seen on Tenefyre, and might not be a bad choice for Micky. It would help the Huehuan lean harder onto his existing strengths without having to change his fighting style.

Percy was also thankful once again that he had managed to strengthen his mutation, otherwise he would have surely struggled to handle his friend’s augmented mana.

Oblivious to his thoughts, Micky had already absorbed about two percent of the first droplet. The frigid substance had scattered across the blizzard raging around the Huehuan’s crystalline body, painting both his mana and Micky’s flesh in its unique colour. The trickle that reached the elemental’s abdomen appeared to deposit something inside his core, altering it at a fundamental level.

Micky had started somewhat cautiously, but he’d kept accelerating the absorption until he began feeling uncomfortable. At this rate, he would likely need about a year to consume every single droplet, though that only made Percy more confident that a fraction of the stash would be enough.

After all, Percy had also spent about a year internalizing his Blessing of Permanence, despite circulating mana that had been several grades less dense than Micky’s.

Leaving the Huehuan alone, he tasked two of his mental pockets with mastering his boosting art: one to perfect Circulation and another to complete the external flow based on the wasps’ insights. Seven of Percy’s mind clones would practice mindset creation under Nephthys, as accelerating his thinking speed as soon as possible would help with his other projects.

Finally, his last mind would work exclusively on affinity fusion.

Percy had never managed to combine soul mana with another rare element, so he didn’t know what to expect from this project. However, he had a hunch that his time wouldn’t be wasted.

As for his plans to weaponize the Starry Wasps… they would have to wait until the next round of improvements. Once he got all his minds thinking several times faster, he hoped that it would be easier to come up with a solution.
