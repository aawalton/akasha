As much as Asher wanted to work on figuring out the puzzle that was the mysterious control nodes he and Groln’s team had discovered, there was work to be done. While the zombies were no longer moving like one directed force into the heart of Whikoga and destroying everything they came upon, there were still thousands of undead roaming the streets and preying on those who were trapped in their miraculously still-standing homes or stuck under rubble. So it was that despite the fact Asher wanted nothing more than to Recall back to his palace room and pass out for the next week, instead, he got to work.

Asher’s gravekeeper class was an excellent tool for hunting down undead, as it highlighted any wandering corpses within a few dozen feet of him. Using that, he ran through back alleys and between collapsed buildings, dispatching zombies everywhere he went. The Shard Detection skill he still had on him thanks to Permanence allowed him to pick out survivors who were trapped under the rubble of destroyed buildings, and a combination of Astral Dip to get to them and Recall to get them out allowed him to save life after life while he traversed the first ring. Most of the other warriors had fallen back to the second ring, so there was no end to the number of zombies waiting to be killed.

While he went about cleaning up the city, he briefly checked in on the others through his Eternal Mark. Samantha and Sarah appeared to have repelled the last of the sea serpents, and Samantha had joined Moxy up on the surface as they worked together to heal some of the wounded warriors. Likewise, Rulfar was using his nose to help point out civilians who were trapped under piles of fallen rock. While Groln’s team had managed to keep the colossal golem on the city outskirts, the golem had still hurled a number of rocks into the city, causing a significant amount of damage. Amanda was at Rulfar’s side, carving through stone with her glowing sword as she retrieved the wounded and healed them enough that they could get back on their feet.

Is it really over? Asher frowned as he sliced through another zombie before trying yet again to call Donvath, only for the High Prince not to pick up. Part of him was tempted to simply Recall back to the royal palace and find the man himself, but he had a feeling Donvath wasn’t exactly conscious at the moment. The High Prince had looked to be in horrific shape after leaping into the past to help put down Ploryn, and if the recovery from straining his skill so much was anything like that for tritessence debt, he didn’t want to ask Donvath to use his skills to peer into the future.

Even so, he couldn’t shake the feeling that this wasn’t over. Part of him wondered if Lethinal and the other remaining lich had simply decided to cut their losses with the Council of Death and had already gone back into hiding. But after all these months of first hiding from and then hunting down the Council, he felt like he had a pretty decent understanding of how they did things, and ‘giving up’ wasn’t really in their vocabulary. The only question in that case…

Was when would the other shoe drop?

As much as he wanted to devote everything he had to running around and saving people, Asher jogged through Whikoga at a leisurely pace, making sure he was gaining more energy than he was expending with his skills. Something didn’t feel right, and he wanted to be ready to leap into action whenever Lethinal’s attack was sprung. Yet as he kept clearing the streets of zombies…

Nothing happened.

Asher fought for another hour, eventually making his way all the way over to the wall separating the first and second rings. Unsurprisingly, it was there he discovered Bordan and Clare once again, the truthseeker and his apprentice looking as though they hadn’t taken so much as a single break since the very start of the fight. Clare looked haggard and exhausted, leaning against the main gate in her black armor as she fought to catch her breath. Bordan, on the other hand, stood tall and powerful as ever, with his greatsword held at the ready to wipe out any undead that dared to approach the main gate.

“Reaper,” Bordan nodded, slowly sheathing his greatsword as he watched Asher jog up to them. “I take it the first ring is largely cleared then?”

“Yeah. I must have gone through a few hundred undead on my own just getting here,” Asher nodded, tossing a small smile in Clare’s direction. “Clare.”

“Yo,” she said, giving him a lazy wave before forcing herself back upright and strolling over. “Haven’t seen you in a hot minute. Bordan tells me you’re officially one of the good guys now. It’s a lot less sexy, I have to say.”

“Clare…” Bordan said, closing his eyes as he let out a weary sigh. Just like that, Clare had exhausted the man more than a few hours of fighting against an endless horde of zombies ever could. Shaking his head, Bordan seemed to choose to ignore the comment, turning away from his apprentice as he looked at Asher. “Thank you for your assistance. Has the lich been taken care of?”

“That’s the thing. The lich wasn’t here,” Asher explained. Pulling the inert control node from his rift, he showed it to Bordan. “I found this instead. Another team from the coalition found a second one in the heart of a giant golem attacking Juhstain. I’m not sure why only those two liches had these things and not the rest.”

“Curious,” Bordan grunted, taking the control node from him and staring intently at it. His eyes seemed to shine, and after a moment, Bordan looked at him. “Can you bring me the other one?”

“Hm? Sure, wait a second.” Placing a mark on the ground, Asher Recalled over to Rulfar, causing the bloodhound to jump when he popped into place beside him. Rulfar was in the middle of getting a drink, and he lowered his canteen, giving him a tired look.

“What is it?”

“Can I have that other control node you guys found?”

“Sure,” Rulfar said, pulling it out of a pack on his back. Asher wasn’t surprised that they’d decided to make Rulfar hold the giant stone, seeing as he was the largest of them all by far. As soon as he had the stone in hand, he thanked Rulfar before Recalling back to Bordan.

“Here,” he said, handing it over. Now that the two control nodes were side by side, it was incredibly obvious that they were identical. Other than the gouge missing in his from where he’d stabbed it with his void dagger, and what looked like a series of claw marks scratched out of the other that he assumed was done courtesy of Groln and his demonic form, there was no way to tell the stones apart. Bordan stared at the two stones for a moment, his powerful Truth element revealing to him secrets that nobody else could possibly know. After a few tense seconds, Bordan revealed his groundbreaking findings.

“They’re the same.”

“No, really?” Asher drawled, his shoulders slumping as he let his hopes fall. “I could have told you that.”

“You don’t understand. They’re the same,” Bordan repeated, handing them back to him. “Not just in structure or design, but even the tritessence flooding through them. That’s the truth.”

“What? How is that possible?” Asher asked. Looking between the identical stones, he frowned. “We figured the one in control of the giant golem was a lich called Krotao because he’s a golem fanatic, but our intel didn’t say anything about him having the ability to create an army of plant zombies. Regardless, why would one of the remaining two liches stretch themselves to attack two different cities at the same time? And if they did that, what the hell is Lethinal doing during all this? He’s supposed to be the leader!”

“Perhaps there aren’t two liches remaining in the first place,” Bordan suggested. “I can’t say I know much about the Council of Death, but on any council, having guaranteed votes is only ever a good thing. Is there a chance this lich you’re after could have created an entire fake persona without the others knowing to better ensure things went the way they wanted them to?”

“I mean, maybe?” Asher admitted, frowning as he thought about it. “Like I said, Lethinal is the founder of the Council. I suppose if anyone could do it…”

“The one who created the Council in the first place would be in the best position for such a stunt,” Bordan nodded. “The more members the Council has, the more dangerous it appears to the rest of the continent as well. It was a clever trick.”

“Okay. Assuming your theory is right and Krotao doesn’t actually exist, which is still a big assumption, seeing as Lethinal would have had to create an entire fake phylactery convincing enough to fool the others to pull this off in the first place… Then where the hell is he? Why use two control nodes when he could have shown up in the rotting flesh at one city and only faked being in one other one?”

“I mean, we smoked their asses in both, right?” Clare snorted. “Self-preservation? Seems like plenty of reason to me.”

“Clare, language,” Bordan said, frowning at his apprentice. “Still, she’s not wrong. It might be as simple as that.”

“I don’t buy it,” Asher decided. Shaking his head, he placed the inert control nodes back into his rift. “Lethinal is the ruler of the Council of Death. I just can’t see him taking the coalition destroying everything he’d worked toward over the past few millennia without getting his revenge. He’s probably the one who enacted the whole ‘mess with us and we’ll destroy your whole country’ rule that kept everyone so afraid of going against them all these years in the first place. Something is coming, I just don’t know what yet.”

“Well, until that day arrives, we’ve got our work cut out for us here in the present,” Bordan said, turning and motioning for Clare to follow him. “We’re off to go help clear out the second ring. If you’re not too tired, we could always use your assistance.”

“It was way more fun when we were trying to kill him,” Clare grumbled under her breath before sighing as she fell into step beside the truthseeker.

“Thanks, but I think I’m going to go gather up the coalition and head back to the palace,” Asher admitted, stretching as he looked forward to the thought of sitting down after all the running around he’d done in one day. “Looks like the others have pretty much finished taking care of the wounded, and I’m sort of everyone’s ride home. If I need anything, I’ll let—”

A glowing, purple chain burst out of the ground before him, shooting straight for his chest without warning. In a strange sense of déjà vu, Asher flickered through the astral by reflex, dodging Clare’s Binding Chains skill as the chain shot directly through him. More confused than anything, he moved a few feet to the left and popped back into the material. It seemed he wasn’t the only confused one, because Bordan was looking at his apprentice like she was insane.

“Clare! I told you he was working on our side now!”

“Yeah, yeah, you know I actually do listen to you every now and again,” Clare said, shoving a finger in her ear as she picked it clean.

“Then why did you try to attack him?” Bordan asked, his frown deepening.

“I think you might be losing your touch with age, old man,” Clare said. Flicking away some earwax, she grinned and nodded toward her chain. “I wasn’t trying to attack him. I was going after his tagalong.”

Asher shared a confused look with Bordan, before their gazes followed Clare’s glowing chain. Asher had thought the end was just lying on the ground behind him, but that wasn’t the case at all. His breath caught when he realized Clare had caught something. Perhaps even someone, depending on one’s personal philosophy regarding skills and sentience.

There, lying on the ground and bound from head to toe in glowing chains, was a figure made from shifting shadows. He had no face and thus no eyes, but somehow, Asher knew the being’s attention was fully on him. His bow made from the same shadows as the rest of him was slung over his back, but there was no quiver of arrows to be seen. As Clare directed her chains to lift the man so that he was standing, Asher found himself face-to-face with someone he hadn’t spoken to in months.

“Reaper,” Owl said, his voice seeming to emanate from all the shadows within a dozen feet of them. “I need your help.”
