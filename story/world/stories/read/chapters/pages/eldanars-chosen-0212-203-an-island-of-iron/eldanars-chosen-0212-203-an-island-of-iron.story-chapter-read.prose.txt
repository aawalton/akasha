The reality of their situation at the bottom of the sinkhole settled over the heavy fighters like a suffocating, physical blanket.

Josh stood shoulder to shoulder with the exhausted tank of the Iron Gauntlet party. Their heavy steel shields were locked firmly together, creating a solid wall of metal that formed the absolute centre of the defensive semicircle. The sheer limestone wall of the gorge pressed cold and hard against the group's backs. There was nowhere left to retreat. There was no more ground to give.

"Brace!" Josh roared, his voice cracking slightly from the freezing, foul-tasting fog coating his throat.

The undead horde did not hesitate. They surged forward as a single, mindless wave of rotting meat, jagged bone, and rusted iron. The sea of glowing green eyes illuminated the thick, swirling mist at the bottom of the pit, creating a terrifying, chaotic strobe effect in the darkness.

The impact was absolutely staggering.

Dozens of skeletal hounds and disfigured, rotting ghouls threw their bodies violently against the raised steel shields. The concussive shockwave of the assault reverberated directly up Josh’s arm, painfully rattling his teeth inside his skull. He bent his knees deeply, driving his heavy steel boots inches down into the freezing black mud, using every single ounce of his enhanced strength just to maintain his position.

To his left, Ronald was a blur of muted green motion. The beastkin could not rely on a heavy shield to absorb the crushing weight of the horde. He survived purely on impossible, liquid agility. Ronald ducked under a thrusting spear wielded by a skeletal warrior, using his twin hunting daggers to cleanly sever the creature's exposed spine. Before the skeleton even hit the mud, the beastkin had already vaulted sideways to avoid the snapping jaws of a corrupted dire wolf.

"We are boxed in, Josh!" Ronald hissed, his ears flattening tightly against his skull as he kicked a crawling ghoul back into the throng. "They are going to crush us through sheer bodily mass! We need an exit strategy right now!"

Josh knew the beastkin was absolutely correct. The rescue charge had been a stunning, explosive success in terms of reaching the trapped party, but strategically, it was a dead end.

He glanced briefly over his shoulder. The rock face behind them was completely sheer, rising thirty feet straight up into the smoky darkness. It was slick with freezing mud and dark, foul-smelling slime. It was physically impossible to climb, especially for heavily armoured fighters actively defending themselves against a relentless siege.

They were an island of living iron completely surrounded by an ocean of rot.

"Keep the perimeter tight!" Josh commanded, refusing to let the creeping panic infect his voice. He activated his Lodestone Taunt, sending a pulsing, magnetic wave of magical energy outward from his breastplate to forcefully drag a charging group of skeletal orcs away from the severely injured Iron Gauntlet cleric. "Do not look at the wall! Look at the enemy!"

Josh thrust his electrified longsword into the chest cavity of a rusted skeletal guard. The raw blue lightning violently scrambled the dark magic holding the dungeon spawn together. The creature collapsed into a pile of brittle bone and instantly dissolved into a shimmering pile of golden dust, completely vanishing into the thick mud.

But for every single monster that Josh destroyed, three more poured into the depression from the surrounding corrupted forest. The edges of the massive sinkhole were practically overflowing with fresh horrors. The ambient necrotic energy of the gorge was drawing every undead beast within miles.

The pressure against the shield wall reached a critical, breaking point.

Beside Josh, the towering tank of the Iron Gauntlet party finally reached the absolute limit of his human endurance. The massive man let out a ragged, breathless groan. His knees buckled under the relentless, crushing weight of three heavily armoured skeletal knights pressing simultaneously against his dented rectangular shield. His heavy boots slid backward in the freezing sludge, breaking the solid defensive line.

"I cannot hold it!" the tank gasped, dropping to one knee.

Instantly, the largest of the skeletal knights raised a massive, rusted iron warhammer high above its skull, aiming a devastating, executing strike directly down at the exposed, unprotected back of the fallen man's neck.

Josh did not have the physical leverage or the time to swing his sword to parry the blow. He dropped his stance, planting his boots firmly, and triggered his aura skill that he had held in reserve.

A brilliant, pulsing sphere of dense, translucent silver energy erupted violently from Josh's breastplate, rapidly expanding to encapsulate the fallen tank beside him. The rusted iron warhammer crashed down with the force of a falling boulder, striking the silver magical dome just inches above the injured man's head.

The kinetic force did not shatter the dome. Instead, the terrifying, physical shockwave of the blow was instantly and violently transferred directly into Josh's own body.

Josh let out a sharp, agonised roar. It felt as though he had been kicked in the chest by a stampeding draft horse. The concussive transfer of kinetic energy cracked two of his ribs and forced all the air violently from his lungs. He tasted hot, fresh blood pooling in the back of his throat, but he did not yield a single inch of ground.

"Get up!" Josh coughed, spitting a mouthful of crimson onto the freezing mud as he reached down with his shield arm, grabbing the heavy leather straps of the tank's breastplate and forcefully hauling the massive man back to his feet. "You do not get to die in the mud today! Lock your shield!"

The tank, eyes wide with terror and profound gratitude, slammed his rectangular shield back into place beside Josh, desperately sealing the breach before the undead horde could pour through. They were surviving, but the bloody cost of maintaining the line was rising with every passing second.

High above the claustrophobic nightmare of the melee, Brett was operating on the very edge of his magical endurance.

The mage stood near the lip of the gorge, looking down into the swirling, chaotic pit. His dark red drake-scale gloves were practically smoking, radiating an intense, searing white heat. His chest heaved with ragged, exhausted breaths. The volume of mana required to maintain a continuous, highly compressed barrage of living plasma was astronomical.

Brett unleashed another concentrated bolt of white-hot fire directly into a cluster of Crypt Weavers attempting to scale the opposite wall. The elemental heat struck the massive, bone-plated spiders with terrifying precision, shattering their fused skulls and sending their massive bodies plummeting down into the mud below.

But the victory offered no relief. Brett looked down at the tiny, heavily armoured pocket of survivors pressed against the limestone wall.

They were completely bogged down. He watched Josh brutally deflect a massive, rotting bear paw with his shield, only to be immediately shoved backward by a surging wave of skeletal infantry. The heavy fighters were taking immense punishment just to maintain their footing in the freezing sludge.

"This is impossible," Brett murmured to himself, his sharp, analytical mind rapidly calculating the horrific variables of the battlefield.

He could provide covering fire for another ten minutes before his mana pool completely ran dry. Perberos and the other rangers were rapidly depleting their quivers. Once the ranged support from the ridge stopped, the undead horde would simply wash over the defensive circle below and bury them alive.

They did not need more firepower. They needed a way out.

Brett immediately stepped back from the treacherous edge of the sinkhole, dropping his Focus Mind skill. The sudden return of his peripheral awareness brought a rush of chaotic noise and freezing wind, but he ignored the sensory overload. He spun around to face the loose line of magical casters maintaining the bombardment.

"Cease fire!" Brett roared, his voice cracking with urgent, desperate authority. "Mages, fall back from the edge! Now!"

Several of the casters looked at him in total confusion, their staffs glowing with half-formed spells, but the sheer, uncompromising panic in Brett's pale face made them hesitate. They slowly stepped back from the rim, lowering their weapons.

"We are wasting our mana on a tactical dead end," Brett explained rapidly, walking down the line of gathered adventurers. He pushed his imaginary spectacles up his nose, his eyes darting between the different robes and magical focuses of the casters. "We have successfully reinforced the Iron Gauntlet party, but they are entirely trapped against a sheer rock face. They cannot climb out, and they cannot push back through the centre of that horde."

"If we stop providing covering fire, they will be slaughtered in seconds," a human mage wearing deep blue robes argued, pointing a glowing wand towards the pit.

"If we simply stand here and exhaust our reserves, they will be slaughtered in ten minutes anyway," Brett countered sharply. He looked directly at the assembled casters. "We need to change the physical parameters of the battlefield. We need to build them an exit. Who here possesses high-tier geomancy? I need earth shapers immediately."

A sturdy, broad-shouldered dwarven woman stepped forward from the back of the group. She wore heavy, earth-brown robes over a thick layer of chainmail, and she carried a massive, unpolished staff carved from solid granite.

"I’m a Stoneweaver," the dwarven woman grunted, her dark eyes looking at Brett with serious intent. "My name is Kaelen. I can shift bedrock, but this entire gorge is heavily saturated with dark mana. The earth here is corrupted and actively resistant to natural magic. It will require a massive, sustained effort to mold."

"I possess high-tier geomancy spells as well," a tall, slender human man added, stepping up beside Kaelen. He carried a pair of heavy iron gauntlets etched with glowing green terrestrial runes. "But she is right. Shaping that much solid limestone in a hostile, necrotic environment will take everything we have. We cannot do it from up here. The range is too far. We need to be directly above them to channel that much physical mass."

Brett nodded rapidly, his mind instantly formulating the new tactical approach.

"Then we move," Brett decided, pointing down the long, curving rim of the massive sinkhole. "We need to travel along the ridge until we are positioned on the cliff edge immediately above Josh and the trapped party. We will clear the ground, and you two will shatter the sheer wall. You will force the limestone to collapse outward, creating a ramp of rubble and earth that they can walk up."

Kaelen the dwarf looked over the edge of the pit, tracing the distance to the trapped fighters with a critical, experienced eye. "It will not be a gentle staircase, lad. It will be a steep, unstable slope of jagged rock and freezing mud."

"A steep slope is infinitely preferable to a vertical tomb," Brett stated coldly.

"There is another problem," Perberos noted, stepping out from the shadows near the edge of the trees. The elven scout had his longbow lowered, his silver eyes fixed on the dark, rotting forest surrounding their position on the ridge.

"The horde is not just pouring into the pit," Perberos warned, his voice entirely devoid of emotion. "They are actively flanking us. The Crypt Weavers are directing groups of corrupted native beasts up the sides of the gorge to secure the high ground. If we break our defensive line here and attempt to march around the rim, we will be entirely exposed to intercepting forces."

"Then we fight for every single inch of the ridge," Brett declared, his hands glowing with a fresh, surging wave of white-hot thermal energy. He looked at the archers and the remaining elemental mages. "The heavy fighters are buying us the time with their blood. We provide the exit. Form a protective ring around the geomancers. Nobody stops moving until we reach the drop zone."

The ranged squad immediately shifted their formation. Perberos and a half-dozen veteran archers took the outermost positions, their arrows nocked and ready. Brett and the offensive mages formed the middle ring, while Kaelen and the human geomancer were heavily protected in the very centre.

They began the treacherous, rapid march along the slippery lip of the massive sinkhole.

It was an incredibly dangerous manoeuvre. The ground beneath their boots was unstable, completely slick with freezing, foul-smelling mud and thick patches of dead moss. A single misplaced step would send them plummeting thirty feet down into the writhing sea of undead below.

They had not moved fifty yards before the forest actively retaliated against their new strategy.

Bursting violently from the dark treeline to their right came a pack of heavily corrupted, rotting forest wolves. They were missing huge patches of fur, their exposed ribs glowing with a faint, sickly green necrotic light. They did not howl. They simply charged the moving formation with terrifying, silent speed.

"Hold the line!" Perberos commanded.

The elven ranger did not break his stride. He smoothly drew his bowstring back to his ear and loosed a heavy, steel-tipped broadhead in a single, fluid motion. The arrow struck the leading wolf directly in the centre of its skull, shattering the bone and dropping the massive beast instantly into the mud.

The other archers fired a coordinated volley, pinning two more wolves to the rotting earth, but the remaining beasts closed the distance too quickly.

"Stand back," Brett warned.

He stepped slightly outside the protective ring of archers. He did not bother with a massive, mana-intensive stream of plasma. He simply raised his right hand and snapped his fingers. A highly compressed, perfectly spherical orb of roaring white fire materialised in the freezing air. Brett flicked his wrist, sending the orb hurtling directly into the centre of the leaping wolf pack.

The explosion was deafening. The thermal shockwave instantly incinerated the rotting flesh of the beasts, turning them into a cloud of foul-smelling ash that washed over the edge of the sinkhole.

"Do not slow down!" Brett yelled, coughing as the thick smoke coated the back of his throat. "Keep moving towards the mark!"

They pushed forward, fighting a desperate, running skirmish along the edge of the cliff. They encountered groups of skeletal hounds, rotting orcs, and a terrifyingly fast Crypt Ghoul that managed to scale the gorge wall right in front of them. The archers and mages operated with ruthless efficiency, blasting the horrors back down into the pit or leaving their ruined bodies in the mud of the forest floor.
