Kayline watched Hayden stride away in a state of awe. He didn’t express worry, excitement, or passion. He just spoke the way her father did when he regarded people who made poor political decisions. She couldn’t help but remember the last time she heard that tone. She was thirteen, and her father was watching a large transport shuttle taking off. It’s amazing that some people can build galactic empires over the course of a century, only to destroy them in thirty minutes, her father had said. Remember the name Coltena Industries. That way, you never forget the importance of diplomacy.

The next year, Coltena Industries disappeared as a major brand name. She couldn't even remember what they sold. That’s how thoroughly her father destroyed them.

Hayden sounded just like that.

It gave her chills and an inextricable mixture of excitement and dismay.

Hayden just declared war on one of the most powerful women in the universe, and while it would make Kayline sound insane to speak it aloud, she believed—if only for that moment—that he could win.

Loricks didn’t think Hayden could win, so he was drowning in despondency. Why’d I let him come here? he wondered. He knew from the second Hayden’s personality changed that he would get himself in serious trouble—but Loricks didn't invalidate the tournament. Part of him believed that Hayden was wiser—but that was wishful thinking. The entrance exams weren't even over, and Hayden was already on the chopping block.

Never before had Loricks felt such regret.

A series of thunderous knocks, followed by a flurry of doorbell presses, broke him out of his stupor. That’s a bad sign… Loricks thought. He let out a groan as he stood, “Alright, let’s get this over with,” he muttered aloud.

Loricks pressed a button to open the door. It hadn’t even opened a foot when Mayle pushed herself inside sideways. She then strode into the room, scouring every inch of it.

“I’m not hiding him in here,” Loricks said sarcastically, knowing damned well what she was looking for.

“I’m not looking for him,” Mayle declared. “I’m looking for the booze. I sent some here—where is it?”

“I gave up drinking when I got married.”

“It wasn’t for you,” she chastised, looking around and grunting. “And just because you can't… whatever. Let’s talk about Hayden. Ha ha. You win. I rejected Vestra Alicibria—and gave Hayden protection. Speak.”

Loricks’ eyes widened. “You rejected him? I told you to protect Hayden, not wage war!”

“Oh, I tried. I offered to let Vestra stay, but I’d have to give up Hayden. And you know what that means.”

Loricks rubbed his face with both hands as though he was washing it. “I shouldn’t have let him come here.”

“Yeah, you shouldn’t’ve. But you did, so let’s fix this. What’s special about Hayden? I know he's a time-traveler with universe-shattering technology, so you can save your breath on that. I want to know about his personality. I'm meeting him tonight, and I barely even know him.”

Loricks’ face twitched. “Uh… He told you all of that?”

“Yes. He clearly knows how to suppress and relay information. Now tell me—is that the same person you knew? Or is he possessed?”

He paused. “Hayden's completely different, but… I think so. I'm not sure I can give you anything else. I was just going to convey that he's definitely not the Hayden that worked for me, but that I didn't think he was being controlled by a spirit. That's all. It sounds like you know more.”

“What about his personality? Does he have age dysphoria?”

Age dysphoria syndromes were the single most damaging ailment of those living extended lives. Classic age dysphoria was when someone couldn't handle that their visual age didn't match their internal personality. It often made people feel inadequate or looked down upon when people treated them as young, or as though they were trapped in someone else's body. There was also Social Age Dysphoria, whereby people felt too old to interact with younger people, and too “young” to empathize with the plight and struggles of the older generations. That often led to social isolation, internal corruption, or double lives that spiraled into dissociative personality disorder.

It was a serious syndrome that drove many to insanity or simply warped them in terrible ways. Those who escaped it had strict behavioral codes or systems. Stella Ingsley, the Golden Belt's presider, famously required all hosts to remove mirrors when she was visiting. She also used blurring software to hide her face on online videos and pictures.

Not all people had to use such extreme measures, but everyone had to have a system or mindset to counter it. Mayle was a “goddess,” meaning that she accepted her eternal youth and had standards for how young she would accept partners. Eternals like Belia often treated all people as insignificant, and therefore, their age didn’t matter. Most simply had a code for how they acted with everyone, regardless of age.

Hayden’s inflexibility and confidence in his decisions led her to think he had a strict code. That said, he was walking around in an acne-stricken teenager's body. If he developed dysphoria due to people treating him like a kid, he could quickly spiral off the deep end.

“I don't think we have to worry about that,” Loricks said. “Hayden treated students like children, and adults like adults at Galma. He also has no trouble speaking down to adults.”

Mayle chuckled ominously. “Yes, he certainly doesn't have a problem with that.” She paused. “Well, it's relieving to know he's stable. That said, if his code's rigid, it's going to be like talking to a wall.”

“I get that vibe, too.”

Mayle sighed. The more stable a person was, the less they were willing to change. That's why he was so confident and decisive, but it also created an unnecessary disaster.

“Do you think he's competent?” she asked.

“Well, he did come back in time ‘universe-shattering technology,’ so I'm sure he is. That said, I don't think he realizes how powerful Belia Alicibria is. We need to get him into Maric. If he's there—”

“He won't,” Mayle interrupted. Maric was their university, one of the most powerful and influential in the universe. He could get in—but it wasn’t even worth asking in Hayden’s case. “He’s waiting for the rift to open to make his move,” she explained. “We have no choice but to wait it out. That, and…”

He frowned. “And what?”

“He wants a contact in the auctions industry, and the only one that could challenge Belia…”

Loricks sat on his bed and groaned.

“You need to convince her,” Mayle said.

“She’s not going to get involved unless she has something concrete. So get something, and…” He groaned again.

“You reap what you sow,” Mayle said.

“I don’t want to hear that from you.”

She smiled weakly. “You wanna drink?”

“I’m married. Miserable. And sick of politics. The last thing I need is to drink.”

“You’re incorrigible.” Mayle opened the door and looked back. “I’m being serious, Loricks. Hayden’s giving a demonstration of universe-moving technology tonight. If he comes through—you’re groveling. That, or he's dying. So prepare yourself accordingly.”

The second she left, Loricks sighed. “I can’t believe she’s a centur.”

Mayle looked thirty, had the body of a twenty-five-year-old, and acted like she was forty—but she was actually over a century. Loricks was the same age, was also young at heart, and had a poor track record with women—that’s why he refused to drink with single women. That said, he did drink with himself. He unzipped his travel case, revealing the bottles of wine and fine liquor she sent to his room.

“Thank God she sent so much,” he muttered. “This is gonna suck.”

Mayle thought the same. That’s why she visited the Sunset Bar in the South Tower before her meeting with Hayden, drinking skinny cocktails until her superior body couldn’t keep up. It took a full fifth of brandy, but she was able to enjoy a pleasant buzz for three minutes before her negative emotions returned like a hurricane.

I should’ve just let him die, she thought. I’m so screwed….

She picked a fight with Belia Alicibria of all people. Belia. Ali-ci-bri-a. Junolyx was spiraling toward destruction, and it was all because Mayle blindly trusted someone who showed her a few magic tricks.

Hayden’s face flashed into her mind. What’s he even doing here? she wondered. If it’s to save the world, they sent the wrong person. She laughed bitterly and checked the clock. The time made her groan. I hate this…

Mayle was worried that she would get to Hayden’s suite and he would show her some dinky magic trick. Then, it would all have been for nothing. She didn’t even want to go, but she had to, so she forced herself up and pointed at another bottle of brandy.

“Give me that.”

The bartender hesitated, but Mayle owned the school, so he handed it to her regardless. Then, she walked to the elevator, took a deep breath, checked to make sure she had Hayden's supplies, and hit the up button.

Five minutes later, Hayden’s doorbell rang five times in rapid succession. He opened the suite’s door and found Mayle Haya in the hallway, cheeks flushed, shaking a bottle of brandy. His guards weren't pleased.

“This is a surprise,” Hayden said dryly.

“It shouldn't be,” Mayle said. “Do you even know how many lawsuits and criminal complaints Belia has filed on Junolyx? Seven. Seven, and it’s only…” She checked the clock on the wall. “8:15. It hasn’t even been five hours.” She pushed herself into his room. “Now stop talking. You owe me a demonstration, but you owe me a drink first. Because I refuse to let you shame me by making me drink alone.”

Hayden glanced at her open bottle. “Only if it’s in a glass.”

“Of course it will be.” Mayle strode to the room’s bar and filled a rocks glass with brandy. “You look like someone who drinks it neat.” She handed it to him. “You can frost it yourself.”

Hayden took that as a challenge. He accepted the glass and frosted it upon contact.

Mayle plopped sloppily on his bed, staring at the glass and then back at Hayden. “That’s quite a bit of skill you got there. Sure it took serious time to learn it.”

“If you want an answer, ask a question,” he said.

“Okay…” Mayle eyed him up and down. “How old are you, really?”
