I had all of a half second to process what I was seeing before the situation demanded action.

Two bone-white clones stood between me and freedom. Eskil's giant form was easily ten feet tall even in its skeletal form, and it wielded a greatax that looked like it could bisect a tank, but Ohana's clone wasn’t exactly any less sinister.

Sure, she looked like a child next to him, but between knowing she was Grace’s champion and the dozen throwing knives she had in her hand told me they’d make an absolutely devastating combination.

As for why these two were here fighting me and not the real Eskil and Ohana? Well, if the explosions outside were any indication, they seemed to be occupied just fine by the armada of artillery and grunts the enemy was throwing their way.

Ultimately, my choices were few, which made things simple. Fight or flee. It was the age-old question, except both options were equally shitty in this case.

Fleeing meant retreating back inside the subterranean compound toward the reactor room, where my clone and the others were probably recovering from the explosion. I'd be boxed in, with enemies on both sides, and not even [Spacefold] would save me then. The clones would corner me and turn me into paste.

Fighting was just as bad. Sure, I could hot swap between Grace to Vigor once, tank some hits and dodge others, but I had no delusions about out-gracing Grace's actual Champion. Not with my current suit, anyway. And Eskil's clone? The real Eskil was already an unstoppable juggernaut. A supercharged bone version sounded like a nightmare I wanted no part of. Even if I could tank his hits with my Legendary suit, which was a big if, I saw no way to actually win.

What I wouldn't give to fight Cyrus again. That undead ice lord and his frozen minions seemed downright quaint in comparison to this bullshit.

Still, standing here wasn't an option, either. As much as I hated my chances, I’d have to fight. Not to win, but to at least link up with the real Ohana and Eskil. At least then, we’d be united. Then we might have more options. Maybe.

I ran through everything I had. Conventional weapons wouldn’t do the trick here. To win, I had to get crafty, and while I couldn’t know if the clones knew about the Champions stored in my inventory, it was my biggest advantage right now.

Yashas would be useless in a fight like this. Worse, actually, since he'd be a liability I'd have to protect. Arianna and Aerion could help, but neither had any way to end the fight quickly. That meant more time for the enemy to land killing blows, but also more time for the other Champion clones to catch up.

Richard, though...

I’d already sprung him on surprised skeletons earlier in this very room. Of course, our enemies would be far stronger, and I doubted they’d go down that easily. At the very least, it might distract them long enough for me to [Spacefold] past them.

The clones moved first.

I pulled siege bolt cores from my inventory and fired a handful with [Remote Launch], aiming for maximum chaos. The projectiles appeared right behind them, flying at speeds that should have been impossible to track.

For a moment there, I actually got excited despite myself. I didn’t know how strong Eskil was, but there was little chance Ohana’s clone would survive a Siege Bolt explosion.

But her clone didn't even flinch. Her hand blurred and the throwing knives in her hand disappeared. It was only after I saw the cores fall harmlessly to the ground that I realized each knife had intercepted a core mid-flight, cutting them in half harmlessly.

“You've gotta be fucking kidding me,” I muttered, already activating [Spacefold].

That wasn’t just fast. That was cheating! Her Grace had to be thousands of points higher than mine for her to move that fast.

Space twisted and I appeared twenty feet to the left, angling for a gap between the clones that would let me break for the exit.

Bone-Eskil was there before I finished materializing. Because of course he was.

The greatax moved far faster than it had any right to, and there was no doubt in my head that the force would've split me in half if my instincts hadn’t forced me to throw myself sideways when they did.

The blade carved a foot-deep gouge in the stone tile floor, and the shockwave of the impact rattled my bones even through my armor.

I [Spacefolded] again, appearing behind Ohana's clone, but she was already turning, preparing to throw those knives of hers. Three of them left her hand in a spread pattern that covered every escape route I had, and I just didn’t have enough information to know if they were weak enough to be ignored or if they’d be the last thing I ever saw.

So I brought my tower shield out and caught all three knives on its face. The impacts rang like bells, and I saw hairline cracks appear on the Mythril shield’s face.

Three knives. Just three hits, and nearly 700 points of condition gone. Two thirds of everything it had.

These were clearly anything but normal throwing knives, and I didn’t know what sort of cheat power Ohana had, but anyone who thought all she had going for her was speed probably hadn’t lived long at all.

I fired off every bullet in my arsenal and added my shards for good measure, but even at point blank range, they were about as useless as I thought they’d be.

As for the flying swords that went after her… well, it’d be comical at how lost they were if it wasn’t my life on the line.

Ohana's clone didn’t move so much as she flickered in and out of existence, leaving afterimages in her wake from all the micro teleports. Her movements somehow coming across as fluid and, well, graceful despite the teleports.

Nor were they just for show. She didn’t just avoid my attacks with those. Each and every movement of hers was designed to force me into Eskil's cutting arc, and were it not for my own cheat teleportation ability, I’d have died to his ax in no time.

Even as it was, I [Spacefolded] desperately, trying to create distance, but they worked together like they'd been fighting as a team for years.

This was choreographed. They were herding me, and their synergy was honestly downright terrifying. Did their real counterparts harmonize this well, or was this a Dungeon boss perk they all got?

One of these superpowered Champions would be bad enough, but now we had 6 of these to deal with…

Eskil's ax came at me once again with a side swing that had every ounce of his augmented power behind it.

I dodged, but Ohana's knives forced me back. Right in time to receive Eskil’s follow up. The giant hadn’t even killed his momentum, instead spinning around with his ax and adding even more force to the strike.

I knew at a glance there was no dodging that, and [Spacefold] was the only thing that stopped me from being eviscerated. I was running out of options and they knew it.

Which was why it was good that between Eskil’s mega swipe and Ohana’s freshly thrown knives, I had a brief respite before their next volley.

Just a couple of seconds to launch my Hail Mary.

I could only pray it was enough.

I pulled out the only weapon I had that couldn’t miss, no matter how fast the enemy.

The Brit materialized mid-dodge, his combat instincts kicking in immediately. He saw the clones, saw me pointing frantically, and by the time I managed to voice my request that he implode the fuckers before they killed us, he’d already fired off his ability.

His hand came up, his eyes closed, and… yeah. Bone-Eskil didn't even slow down.

The giant's ax came around in a horizontal slash that forced me to grab Richard and [Spacefold] away.

I braced for Ohana's follow-up, but it never came.

I glanced back to find her body frozen, stopped mid-movement, one hand raised with a throwing knife that never got released. The skeletal form stood that way for a half-second longer, then collapsed, its bones clattering across the concrete with a sound that would never get old.

“Holy shit, it worked!” I shouted, still not quite believing our luck.

“Bloody hell better,” Richard said, panting. “That one took a lot out of me.”

I patted the Champion on the back, keeping a keen eye on Bone-Eskil.

Despite the win, I couldn’t help but despair at the monumental task that lay ahead. She’d been the clone of the Champion of Grace. Not Vigor or Dominion. I knew first hand just how squishy I was in my Grace suit, and I had the benefit of stat rings that she most definitely wouldn’t.

And then the dying clone's hand moved, throwing the knife it’d been clutching. Never mind that its owner was dead.

The projectile streaked toward Richard's chest with the same impossible precision I'd seen before.

I just barely [Spacefolded] between them in time, pulling Richard into my inventory in the same motion. The poor guy was going to be so confused later.

Assuming, of course, that there would be a later.

The knife hit my tower shield, the impact widening the cracks of the already damaged surface.

The thing was barely holding together, with just under 100 points of condition. I stowed it before it could get any worse. Not like it had a prayer of blocking anything the Viking sent my way.

Whether from outrage over killing his sister? Ally? Or simply because he was pissed we weren’t dead, Bone-Eskil roared an inhuman sound that made my skin crawl, and charged us.

I didn't wait around to see what a pissed-off ten-foot skeleton giant could do. I triggered [Spacefold] toward the exit Ohana's clone had been guarding, and without her speed to block me, I ran right through, spamming the ability the instant its mercifully brief cooldown ended. Even that felt like an eternity, and I was half-convinced I’d have an ax ripping out my back any second.

The ax never came and I was suddenly out into the compound's main courtyard.

Where, apparently, an apocalypse had just occurred.

The real Eskil stood in a flaming crater of magma and ash, and I could’ve sworn I’d somehow been teleported to a distant volcano, had it not been for the charred remains of the top of the iconic temple pagoda.

Because that was all that remained of the compound. Everything else was a scorched battlefield.

Which only served to make Eskil look even more amazing, standing proud in the middle of all that, as though more than a hundred artillery guns weren’t pointed his way.

His golden Viking armor blazed in the sunlight, and nordic runes looked to have been etched into every surface. By the way they glowed, I guessed they weren’t just for looks, though they certainly made the guy look like a living god.

The giant wielded his equally oversized greatax like it weighed nothing, and by the way each swing cleaved through skeleton soldiers and heavy tank armor with equal ease, I guessed it weighed literal tons.

A tank tried to bring its main gun around, but Eskil's ax lopped the barrel in half, before following through to split the entire tank vertically. The two halves fell away, and Eskil was already moving to his next target, laughing some Valhallan chant while he did it.

I was suddenly so grateful for my spidey sense telling me not to fight his bone clone. If the Viking had gotten this strong, how much stronger was that thing?

But as impressive as Eskil was, his moves weren’t anything I hadn’t seen before. Upgraded, yes, and terrifying, but just more of the same hulk smash goodness I’d come to expect from the guy.

Ohana, though? Well, it was obvious where the bone clone’s inspiration had come from.

She micro-teleported around in a manner I’d never seen, her flowing kimono somehow never getting in the way as she quite literally danced her way between enemies. At this point, I was convinced that the dance was part of her ability set, because it was far too intricate to do for the hell of it, no matter how dedicated she might’ve been.

Throwing knives appeared in her hands on demand, each one detonating on impact with enough force to turn skeleton soldiers into bone dust. She'd leap from an exploding gunship to a falling piece of debris to the top of a command tent, her movements so fluid and graceful it looked like I was watching a highly choreographed martial arts movie.

If there was such a thing as a real art of war, I was watching it.

A helicopter gunship tried to strafe her position, but Ohana threw three knives in quick succession. The rotor assembly, tail boom, and cockpit all went up in small explosions, which quickly became a big explosion as the fuel tank ruptured.

The gunship corkscrewed out of the sky and crashed into a group of skeleton soldiers, the impact sending shrapnel raining across the courtyard.

Ohana could also defy gravity, apparently, because she landed on the wreckage like a gymnast after a high jump, already throwing a knife at her next target.

They were holding their own against the small army of skeleton soldiers, brain commanders, gunships, and tanks, and despite how much I disliked Eskil, I had to admit I was impressed.

Looking at them fight like this, I could see how these Champions might save the world.

But their advantage was about to disappear. Familiar figures emerged from the Senso-ji’s gates, starting with my clone, followed by the clones of Aerion, Arianna, and Richard. And right behind them was Bone-Eskil, his roar alerting his Champion counterpart and Ohana to his presence.

Five Bone Clones against six Champions and one who was one in everything but name.

The skeleton army seemed to sense the shift too, because their tactics changed. They stopped trying to overwhelm Eskil and Ohana with numbers and started pulling back, creating space to form a perimeter.

They were, it seemed, content to have their superweapons to do their dirty work.

I cracked my knuckles and stepped forward, pulling my chain whip from inventory as well as all the other Champions. With this, we had every Champion assembled except for Wisdom’s, and despite the absolutely insane odds, despite knowing we were probably all going to die here, I couldn't help but feel a little smug.

If Eskil and Ohana were that strong on their own, what did the combined might of all of us look like?

I couldn’t wait to find out.
