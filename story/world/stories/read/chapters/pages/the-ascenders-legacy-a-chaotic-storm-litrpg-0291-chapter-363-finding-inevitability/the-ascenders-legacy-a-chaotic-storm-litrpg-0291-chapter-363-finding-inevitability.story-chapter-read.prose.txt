Time slows down when you're in danger.

That was something Crixis had heard many people say—that the world wanted you to register every single detail of those last few seconds.

He'd never believed it. Never thought it was possible—until now, as he watched the world slow to a crawl around him, time stretching thin as dozens of Skitterlings raced toward him, their chitinous bodies gleaming like embers beneath the pale glow of a sun shrouded by smoke.

Crixis had never fought this many opponents before. Let alone ones three tiers above him.

He was out of his depth.

He wanted to run—to flee this battle as fast as his legs could carry him. But his fear of disappointing Aodhán was louder than his fear of the monsters barreling toward him.

Aodhán was watching.

He couldn't afford to run. He had to fight. Even if he failed, he had to fight.

His gaze hardened.

And that split-second decision couldn't have been more timely.

A Skitterling descended on him in that moment, gleaming limbs and razor-sharp mandibles aimed straight at him. For a moment it loomed—imposing, terrifying.

And in the next, its entire abdomen burst apart in a shower of gore and ash, the pale sky visible through the hole a blistering beam of lightning had just punched clean through it.

The Skitterling crashed into the ash like a sack of wet stones, and for a heartbeat, the others stilled, shock rippling through their ranks.

But they weren't the only ones in shock.

Crixis stared at his hands—the same hands now crackling wildly with red lightning—unable to believe what he'd just done.

In that single instant of decision, that exact moment he'd chosen to fight despite his fear, he'd accidentally activated two entirely different skills at once.

Lightning Beam and Maxim.

And the result was… he couldn't believe the result.

Maxim was a skill he'd inherited from his father. A physical buff anchored on the path of superiority. It had no business interfering with his other skills. No business at all.

Yet in that moment it had merged with Lightning Beam, turning a perfectly dangerous arc of crackling electricity into something five times faster and five times larger.

He glanced back at the dead Skitterling, still unable to believe his eyes. Then at the others.

They flinched, mandibles and claws clicking in fear. All because of one attack.

Crixis looked back at his hands. And then he smiled, his body settling into a stance of quiet confidence.

The Skitterlings skittered anxiously, antennae waving as their fear mounted. They gathered themselves, instinctively rallying behind a new leader—and then, just like before, they lunged, fury blazing in their crimson gazes.

This time, however, Crixis was ready.

He stretched his palms toward them and activated the same accidental combination again.

Lightning Beam—Maxim.

Twin beams of crackling red electricity erupted from his palms, so fast he could barely track them. They slammed into the Skitterlings and simply punched through—ripping hardened carapace like paper.

The creatures crashed to the ground like broken toys, blood and viscera pooling beneath them to stain the ash red.

The entire battle had taken less than two seconds, and as the last Skitterling hit the ground, Crixis let out an excited whoop, a wide grin splitting his face.

"Did you see that?" He nearly squealed, his entire body buzzing with excitement. "I did it. I created a new skill and killed them all."

Silence.

He frowned, eyes searching the empty space. "Aodhán? Are you there?"

Once again, only silence answered him.

A sense of trepidation stirred in his chest, but before he could truly panic, another crack split the air, the ash shifting as a fresh wave of Skitterlings clawed their way to the surface.

There were dozens of them this time, each one bigger than the last.

But Crixis wasn't worried. He summoned a storm, and when they lunged, he whispered.

Lightning Spears—Maxim.

The Skitterlings barely lasted a second, their bodies skewered to pieces by crackling spears of red electricity. Power roared through him and he grinned.

Maxim was intoxicating, and for a while Crixis forgot about Aodhán entirely—energy singing through him as he braced for the next wave.

There were over a hundred this time. Yet he cut through them in minutes, blistering spears of electricity ripping through carapace to nail Skitterlings to the ground before detonating in showers of ash and gore.

Half an hour passed in this manner.

Each victory lent Crixis more confidence. More certainty in his own strength. But each victory also meant more opponents. Stronger opponents.

And by the time he realized he'd grown overconfident, it was already too late.

There were hundreds of them now, with more clawing free from the earth every second. They surrounded him—Skitterlings whose size dwarfed him, whose carapace were hard as stone and limbs sharp as butcher's hooks.

He swallowed and activated Lightning Pillar—Maxim with a surge of energy and willpower. The storm above thundered, and from it a dozen pillars of condensed lightning struck down, slamming into the Skitterlings with enough force to crater the earth.

Many fell. More were injured. Yet hundreds remained untouched, their bodies shielded by the haze of ashen energy cloaking their forms. Some were even covered in ashen armor.

And still more were pouring from the ground.

His gaze met their crimson ones, and his earlier fear returned as the new wave either tanked or outright ignored the pillars raining down on them.

Maxim had reached its limit.

He had no choice now but to be inevitable.

Sadly, he still didn't know how.

***

Aodhán watched Crixis curiously, wondering what he would do now.

The last half hour had been impressive enough that Aodhán had every intention of learning Crixis's enhancement skill for himself. But he hadn't brought him here to show off his superiority skills.

He'd brought him here to learn how to be inevitable. And it seemed Crixis had finally run out of alternatives.

"How will you proceed now?" Aodhán murmured, his gaze intent as creatures six tiers above Crixis barreled toward him with bloody intent. There were hundreds of them, with more erupting from the earth every second.

Crixis couldn't kill them all before they struck. He was overpowered and outnumbered. There was no winning this unless he reached into the power of his path.

But that didn't seem like it would be happening anytime soon.

Aodhán sighed.

He considered intervening—thinning the horde without Crixis noticing—but quickly discarded the idea. Maxim had been impressive. Powerful. Efficient. But it wasn't inevitable. It was just stronger. And stepping in now wouldn't help Crixis learn any faster.

It wasn't entirely his fault. The path of superiority was a self-centered path—it demanded precision. Aimed attacks. Calculated trajectories. Guiding each beam, spear, or pillar to its target. Proving, always proving, how far above one's opponents one stood.

Inevitability was different. It didn't calculate. It didn't guide.

It simply was.

And Crixis needed to realize that.

A scream tore from Crixis moments later as the Skitterlings descended, claws and mandibles finding flesh. He fought back hard, his control sharpening with each strike—but it wasn't enough.

If he didn't find inevitability soon, he would fail.

He wasn't quite close enough to death yet, though. When that moment came, Aodhán would step in.

Until then, he folded his arms and settled in to watch Crixis try and fail to walk his path, a quiet question forming at the back of his mind.

Was the path of superiority too ingrained in his bones?

***

Crixis stumbled, hissing as a clawed limb pierced his shoulder and another drove into his thigh, ripping through flesh and muscle like paper.

Gritting his teeth, he directed several pillars of lightning at the Skitterling and everything around it. The pillars slammed down, punching through carapace with the force of falling meteors. Limbs cracked. Abdomens burst. But for every Skitterling that fell, dozens rushed in to take its place.

Crixis fought like a madman—hands sweeping as he directed arcing waves of lightning at the monsters closing in. Lightning crackled. Thunder boomed. And yet he was no closer to touching the essence of inevitability.

He wasn't even thinking about it. Not with his life in the balance. He was simply fighting to survive.

And he was drowning in the numbers.

With each passing second, his attacks did less damage. His injuries mounted. His willpower bled out with every strike as he poured more and more into each skill.

A clawed limb swiped at his face, nearly taking his eyes. He countered with a beam. Then another. Then another as the Skitterling refused to die.

A limb stabbed into his foot, and pain exploded through his mind like a spike driven straight into his skull. And just when he thought—finally, Aodhán will save me now—a claw drove into his stomach.

Another pierced his back. A third his thigh.

Pain blinded him, white-hot, as the attacks came all at once.

"Elder brother," he shouted, Chains of Fate erupting from his body to beat back the closest creatures. "Please—save me."

But he received no response. Aodhán didn’t appear.

"Aodhán," his voice cracked. "Let's try again next time. I'm too injured to think."

Still, no response came. Not even the faint breeze of movement.

And that was when Crixis understood. He was alone.

Aodhán had said he would be invisible, but it was obvious now—he had left him here to fend for himself. If he were here, surely he would have intervened by now.

No one was coming to save him.

"No," he gasped, energy surging desperately within him. "No. I can't die here. I'm inevitable."

But was he?

He had chosen the path, but hadn't begun threading it at all. And until he did, he couldn't claim it—couldn't draw on its power.

But how? How??

The thought was nearly a scream, frustration and pain carved deep into his expression as he slammed another pillar of lightning into the horde. A beam followed. Then chains.

He was giving everything he had.

And still it wasn't enough.

A Skitterling broke through the chaos then—far larger than any he had faced before. And before he could so much as blink, it lunged, bladed claws aimed for his throat.

The limb split the air at a speed he couldn't follow. And Crixis knew, with cold certainty, that he could not dodge this.

This was the end.

And just as the world does for all those near the end—time slowed once again. Sound vanished, colors glowed, and every detail sharpened with crystalline clarity.

Wide-eyed, Crixis watched the bladed limb arc toward his throat with helpless abandon, feeling the inevitability of the strike. He would never dodge in time. He couldn't avoid this fate.

It was inevitable.

Or was it?

Something strange moved through him in that moment—a quiet tingle, deep and certain, as a glimmer of inspiration shot into his mind.

A glowing fragment of insight.

And he realized that despite how inevitable the blade seemed, it was far from so. If Aodhán intervened right now, he might survive this attack. He might dodge it.

That wasn't true inevitability.

Inevitability meant that not even a higher power could stop the attack from landing. That spoke of a control far beyond anything he possessed. Far beyond anything any mortal could possess.

It was divine.

And in that moment, Crixis finally understood what Aodhán had meant about surrendering control. Inevitability wasn't a technique. It was a mandate—one no force in the world could alter save for him, the bearer of said inevitability.

He alone could change the course of his attacks. But his control, no matter how refined it became, would never be precise enough to override fate. It could never be as precise as the divine.

He had to let go. His will. His calculations. His need to guide every strike.

He had to surrender it all to the storm—and only then would he be truly inevitable.

The blade grazed his neck.

Crixis held the Skitterling's gaze. And in that moment, he stopped trying to control everything. He released his grip on attacking, on defending, on guiding.

And simply locked eyes with his target. His attention absolute. Unwavering.

The blade bit in deep.

Crixis held his gaze, and with perfect clarity, he whispered.

Thunder Strike You.

An instant later, light split the world in two and shook the earth to its core.
