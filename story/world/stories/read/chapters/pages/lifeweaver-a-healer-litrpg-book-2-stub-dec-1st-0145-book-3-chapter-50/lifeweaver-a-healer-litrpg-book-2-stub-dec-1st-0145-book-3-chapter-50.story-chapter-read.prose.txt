Such a powerful ability, being able to use Weavestep to travel through the system. If only it didn't come with such a massive disadvantage. Almost dying each time he used it was more than a minor inconvenience. It was bullshit.

Luke got to his feet. "Same as always. Get stronger."

"At least one more. It's barely lunchtime yet."

Hannah groaned, then shook her head and stood with a sound of protest. Grumbling, she glared at him. "Fine."

"First, though," Luke said. "Hand over your equipment so I can enchant it."

It was something he should've done a long time ago, but it kept slipping his mind. The bonuses from an enchanted piece of gear weren't much, but once you got everything together, it amounted to a significant boost in attributes and abilities, even at Luke's low rank.

"Guess I should share some too," Ray said, before handing out thin vials of what looked like orange juice.

"Alchemy?" Luke asked.

Ray nodded. "You bet. The good stuff, straight from my loving hands."

"You make that sound dirty," Hannah said with a shudder.

He winked at her. "I can make anything sound dirty."

Elijah cleared his throat. "I don't like where this is going."

"Me neither," Curtis said, pointing down a street to their left. "There's a portal over there. You go. I'll stay here in case they return."

"That's stupid," Ray noted.

Curtis glared at him.

"It is," Luke agreed, shaking his head as he headed toward the portal Curtis had indicated. "Just don't attack them on your own again if they do appear."

With that, they were down to four people. Lemira had, once she stabilized her pets, whatever that meant, decided to join another group tackling a dungeon, so she wasn't available to join. With so few of them, they decided Luke should not be first to enter, since that'd up the difficulty to a point they might not be able to handle.

Ray stepped through the portal, and they found themselves with another assassination quest. It was almost too simple. Forty minutes later, they left the dungeon without Luke's experience bar having moved much at all. At least he'd spent the entire time enchanting whatever he could get his hands on, all the while pushing the limit of Threads of Mana, forcing it to reach just a little farther.

"I'm taking a break," Hannah said once they were out.

Ray's gaze didn't leave his dagger. It had fed on a good number of souls in that last dungeon, but it was as if the weapon was some sort of enigma that Ray couldn't puzzle out. Even so, he nodded. "Same. Need a breather."

Elijah agreed, and the trio headed back to guild headquarters, leaving Luke alone. With everything that was going on, it wouldn't feel right to just sit around and rest.

Stepping through the closest portal, they found dead monsters, a lot of dead monsters. It was a field of green grass under a blue sky. Almost like an ocean of green since it spread as far as the eye could see in all directions. The grass wasn't all that green, either. The dead monsters formed a carpet over the ground, their blood staining everything red.

Curtis hunched down to inspect a corpse. "Goblins."

"We'd better not run into more Singers in here," Luke said with a sigh, pointing off into the distance. "Whoever did this is over there."

Goblins surged like waves against what looked like a small group of people, threatening to overwhelm them. Even from this distance, Luke got an inkling of who they might be.

"You think this is the same world as last time?" Curtis asked as they set off toward the group of Integrated far off in the distance.

"Could be."

"Is that?" Curtis asked once they drew closer.

The voice of Nash carried over even the goblin shrieks and the clash of metal against metal. "Take that! And that! Feel the rhythm in my blood!"

"Loud," Curtis said, coming to a stop.

Luke looked over his shoulder without stopping. "What are you doing? Let's go. They'll need our help!"

"I'm not so sure," Curtis said, shielding his eyes from the sun by forming Ray-Ban sunglasses out of darkness. "You go. I'm going to try something."

"Experimenting?" Luke asked.

He grinned, showing an expression so rare it was almost a little disconcerting. "Experimenting."

With that, Luke went on by himself, closing in on his fellow guild members. The goblin horde was like a ceaseless, raging river washing up against the impressive bulwark that was Nash's shield and Savannah's ghost-like armor. Coming closer, he saw large swathes of monsters standing still, frozen mid-stride and acting like walls to funnel the party's enemies toward the tanks. It was an impressive strategy, and it showed how far Priyah had come.

The main damage dealers seemed to be Sam and Mas. Red lines sprang from both of them, latching onto the closest goblins before multiplying and cascading out across the battlefield. It reminded Luke of his own skill, Cascading Threads. A good way to get some area of effect damage going, to be sure.

At first, he'd set off to help them, but it soon became clear they were able to hold their own well enough, even if it didn't seem like they were making much of a dent in the still-growing number of goblins rushing over a piece of elevated grassland in the distance. They just kept coming and coming with no end in sight.
