At first, barely anyone paid attention as our carriage rolled through the city. But the deeper we went into the city proper, the more heads turned, recognition dawning as people caught sight of the sigil painted along the sides of the half-dozen carriages.

Those who didn’t recognize the crescent moon as the duchess’ mark turned anyway at the sight of literal knights grunting and shouting as they hauled the carriages like beasts of burden.

Less than half an hour in, a crowd had gathered along the edges of the central road. Some cheered. Others only stared. I caught a few bowing or offering salutes. I returned none of it. Not out of disrespect, but exhaustion. A deep, creeping weariness that had been growing worse with each passing day.

Physically, I was fine. Mentally, it felt as though I were long overdue for rest. Unfortunately, circumstances rarely allowed such indulgences. They never had.

The carriage finally rolled to a stop at the base of the recently reconstructed ducal Silver Spires. The Silver Keep, though no longer as elegant as it once had been, was still unmistakably regal. Its windows and pale paint gleamed in crisp silver, reflecting the sunlight with ease.

Attendants—young squires alongside older aides in sharply pressed uniforms—hurried forward. Doors were pulled open, hands politely offered as Nida and I were guided down.

Dralos vaulted from the opposite side of the carriage and immediately began barking orders to the knights flanking Raphael’s escort.

“Take him to the Iron Wing,” I said, referring to the steel-clad clinic hidden in the eastern shadows of the third spire. “Three guards outside at all times. A warding stone planted at the threshold. He doesn’t leave until I say so, and no one who treats him leaves either. I will meet with him later."

“And if he wakes before then?” Dralos asked.

I turned toward the Spire doors, already climbing the small mountain of steps. “Then he waits.”

Inside, the air was cooler. The familiar scent of stone, oil, and faint lavender followed us through the corridors toward the main hall. The interior was as cavernous as ever. If anything, the vaulted ceilings felt higher, the great pillars of white and obsidian marble brighter and more solid than before.

I ignored the people milling about the main floor and headed straight for the central staircase spiraling upward through the heart of the Keep.

“Marcus, get me Benedict,” I ordered, sending the knight-captain off. He relayed the command to another knight without hesitation. “Nida, find Ethan and meet me in the study later tonight,” I added.

She nodded and disappeared back the way we’d come. Ethan likely wasn’t far behind.

I continued on and when I had at last settled into the leather chair of my study, the door slammed open hard enough to rattle the frame revealing a half-crazed Benedict. His usually slick black hair stuck out in every direction, as if he’d been tugging at it for weeks. His eyes were narrowed to near slits, black irises barely visible under swollen eyelids. Clothes that were normally immaculate were smeared with dried red and brown, permeating a stench of piss and blood that was enough to make my eyes water.

“Oh, for fuck’s sake, Benedict,” I shouted, recoiling and nearly tipping out of my chair in surprise. “Go clean yourself. You smell like someone tried to drown you in a bucket of shit and it's to the point I'm wishing they'd succeeded."

He only laughed. “I have found the answer to your question, Lilliana,” he said, once again omitting my title.

“Which one?” I snapped, irritation at my continuing exposure to the disgusting smell layering into my words. “I have many.”

He pointed to his eyes. “The golden eyes.”

I was on my feet instantly. Every ounce of exhaustion vanished. “Tell me.”

The man, in his familiar arrogance, shook his head. “You must come with me. I will show you. It is… I am not sure how to describe it.” Judging the situation by the crazed look in his eyes and his even more unkempt appearance, I followed. We left the office, and I noticed the child soldier guarding my door keeled over gagging from Benedict’s stench. I lifted the boy—he couldn’t have been more than fifteen—off the ground and set him back on his feet.

“When Nida and Ethan arrive, tell them I may be much later than I'd anticipated.” The boy nodded. I turned to leave, then paused and faced him again, stopping him midrun. “On second thought, inform them we’ll meet tomorrow at lunch.” I rubbed at my increasingly heavy eyelids. I needed sleep. The paragons could wait.

Though my heart and mana cores kept me physically primed, my mind still needed rest. Rest I had denied it for far too long.

The ex-marquess led me back down the spiral staircase I had just climbed, and we moved through the throng of workers preparing to head home. They shoved one another aside, clearing a path for us in both awe and terror, though whether it was terror of me or of Benedict's wild apperance, I couldn't say. Once past them, I followed Benedict beyond the staff kitchen and down a hallway that ended at a heavy steel door. He knocked twice. It swung open with a piercing screech, as if it hadn’t been oiled in an eternity.

I thought I knew where we were headed, though I had never been there in this particular spire.

The dungeons.

That assumption proved iinstantly wrong as we continued down the stairwell past them, descending even deeper beneath the Silver Keep. Two of my Paragon stood sentry at either side of the passage, though I couldn't remember either of them.

“I didn’t know this place existed,” I muttered after we were out of earshot. “How did I not?”

Benedict chuckled darkly. “I had it created while you were gone. There were too many… unpleasant things that needed doing.” My silence was pointed enough that he caught the hint and hurried on. “Mainly dissections. The Hydra, for one. And the golden-eyed knights,” he added quickly. “But only as you commanded. Nothing more.”

“And?” I pressed, lifting a brow. "What answers did you find?"

He scratched the back of his unruly hair. “I didn’t know where to begin with the golden eyes, so I had to… interrogate some of the soldiers who were still alive.”

I nodded once, eyes narrowed. “Has any of this leaked to the public?”

“No,” he said, shaking his head, a bead of sweat forming on his left temple. “Only the paragons and myself have been allowed beyond the dungeon and the cells. That has been strictly enforced."

“Good. Let’s keep it that way.” The last thing I wanted was word spreading that the foreign noble I had empowered in Elyndor had been torturing Elyndorian soldiers under my authority.

Minutes passed in silence as we descended to the bottom of the stairwell, tens of feet beneath the Silver Keep. The air grew damp and stale, thick with the scent of alchemical preservatives and blood so old it had turned to copper dust in the stone. When we finally reached the end and stepped into the chamber beyond, an overwhelming stench assaulted me—one that made Benedict smell like flowers by comparison.

It was as if someone had slammed my face into a heap of grotesque scents, putting even the Sanctum in Silverwater to shame. Shit, blood, vomit, piss, and rot flooded my nostrils. For a moment, even I gagged uncontrollably.

“You get used to it,” Benedict said, stuffing two small bundles of cloth into his nostrils. He offered me a pair.

I shoved his hand away. I wasn’t so weak.

The corridor stretched ahead, lit only by mana lanterns embedded in slick, wet stone. Their flickering blue light stained everything the color of old bruises.

“Took quite a few earth-attributed paragons to carve this out,” Benedict said his voice nasaly, stopping before another door. This one was polished obsidian, its frame carved with runes arranged in a pattern I didn’t recognize. He pressed his palm against the stone and whispered something too quietly for me to hear.

Like a spark catching dry tinder, the runes flared for a split second before dimming again. Metal and stone shifted as the door unlatched, opening with a deep, grinding groan.

A wave of cold slammed into us, one bitter and bone-deep.

I stepped forward, and aside from the sound of my own footfall, the chamber greeted us with silence.

The room was circular, its walls lined with slabs of enchanted steel and stone. At its center rose a tall glass cylinder set into a platform of bronze and silver. Within it floated the corpse of a man. Or, at least, what had once been a man.

His skin was pale, blue veins stretched thin over muscle and bone. But it was his eyes that seized my attention.

Still open.

Still gold.

Even in death, they shimmered with that same unnatural hue. Tears of luminous liquid gold streaked down his face, dripping from the bottom of his chin and pooling beneath him. Each drop sent out a faint pulse of mana that I could feel in my bones.

“He died three days ago,” Benedict said, circling the cylinder like a mad professor. “Arrested for desertion during the Hydra attack, so I took some… liberties. After death, the body began to decompose normally. Skin sloughed. Veins collapsed.” He pointed sharply at the man’s face. “But the eyes didn’t fade. Didn’t dim. It was as if the power within them was sourced from an entirely different life. So I placed the corpse in stasis.”

I approached and laid a hand against the glass. It was ice-cold. The golden eyes stared back at me unblinking, unmoving, and yet somehow aware.

“They aren’t enchanted?”

“With what?” Benedict scoffed. “Divine magic?” He shook his head. “No enchantments. I verified it myself. For an enchantment of that magnitude to remain hidden from me, it would need to be well into the fifth realm, which hasn’t been reached in…” He shrugged. “Centuries. If ever. There’s no residual energy either. No glamour. No illusion.”

“Then where is the color coming from?” I asked.

“That,” he said, “I’m not entirely certain of yet. The gold isn’t paint or pigment. It’s part of the ocular tissue, and it goes far deeper than the surface.” He crossed to a nearby desk, opened a drawer, and retrieved a large, heavy jar. Inside floated two severed eyes, their optic nerves trailing behind them.

Gold.

He held the jar out to me, and I took it for a closer look.

“Can you tell if they’re still active?” I asked.

Benedict laughed. “Try feeding them some mana.”

My heart core ignited, and I pushed a thin sliver of energy toward the eyes. It vanished instantly, swallowed whole.

Not a trace remained.

“Fascinating,” I murmured, handing the jar back. “How is that possible? Are they artifacts?”

“I don’t believe so,” Benedict replied. “I think the eyes act as conduits, or some sort of connector, to another power source.”

I tilted my head slightly. “As if someone is spying through them?”

Benedict nodded. “That, and more. The optic nerves connect directly to the brain. If that gold liquid,” he gestured toward the puddle beneath the corpse, “extends beyond the eyes and into the brain, then whoever controls it could access every function of the contaminated individual.”

I clicked my tongue. “Weaknesses?”

This time, Benedict’s smile was wide enough to expose the rawness of his gums. “Dragon fire.”

“That would destroy the individual, not just the gold,” I countered.

He returned to his desk, rummaged for a few seconds, and came back holding a small tube of red-gold fluid. Heat radiated from it violently. The closer he brought it to me, the hotter the air became.

“We pour liquid dragon fire into the eyes, burn out the gold, and then heal the eyes afterward,” the Caelian traitor said. “Easy.”

I doubted it would be anywhere near that simple. More likely, the liquid dragon fire would burn straight through the entire body of whoever received it. Still, I didn’t press the issue. Not until I knew more.

“What are the chances Duke Alistar was behind the golden eyes?” I asked. It seemed the most obvious conclusion.

“I believe he was involved to some extent,” Benedict said, chewing the inside of his lip. He gestured toward my storage ring and tossed the ducal heir’s House Coin he had borrowed back to me. “The House Coin is connected to the eyes, after all. It gives a degree of control over the golden-eyed knights, though it’s far from absolute. Still…” He hesitated. “I doubt someone as righteous as Collin Alistar would knowingly manipulate free will.”

I clicked my tongue, thinking. “I’ll discuss some of this with Lady Eliza.” I raised a hand as Benedict opened his mouth to argue. “Not the details. But perhaps there’s a ritual performed on new soldiers that grants them the golden eyes. If so, we may be dealing with an ancient spell left behind by the duchy’s ancestors.”

Benedict snorted. “I’m not certain what the connection is, but I’m confident no Alistar would ever condone mental manipulation.”

I shrugged. “What if they didn’t know that’s what the spell did?”

He had no answer.

"Have you found any clear connection between the golden eyes and the Pandorian Empire yet?"

Again, I was met by only silence.

“Keep looking into it,” I said, stifling a yawn. “And see if we can produce our own version.”
