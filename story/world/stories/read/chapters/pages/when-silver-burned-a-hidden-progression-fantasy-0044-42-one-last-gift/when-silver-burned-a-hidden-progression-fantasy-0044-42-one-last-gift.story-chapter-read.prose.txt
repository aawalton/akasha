Kalsin quietly pulled the door shut behind him, cutting off the streak of sunlight from streaming in. His steps were soft on the wooden floor. It didn’t matter, though. She was already up.

Ringtail sat on the bed, sheet pulled up to her chest, blinking blearily at him with hair sticking up in every direction. Her bare toes stuck out from beneath the covers, wiggling like the antennae of a slighted bug.

"...You're not wearing a shirt," she noted, rubbing her eyes.

“You drool when you sleep.”

She pulled the blanket up to her chin and glared at him. “I do not.” There was some prodding underneath as she covertly tried to wipe the corners of her mouth with the fabric.

Kalsin didn’t respond. He just watched her in silence.

Ringtail matched his stare, until she looked away and let the sheet fall back down to her shoulders. “You were gone when I woke up.” Ringtail’s voice was quieter now.

“I was just outside.”

A pause.

“Oh,” she breathed. “Well… good. That is, um. Good.”

Kalsin leaned down to grab Ringtail’s boots from beside the door and walked over to the bed. He placed them beside the bottom rail before sitting down. Ringtail quickly scooted over to give him some room, the bed creaking under their combined weight.

And then, as things would have it, silence crept back again.

“Sooooo…” Ringtail said, looking at him sideways. She held onto that blanket like it was a shield against the Veiled. “Did the fuck was good?”

Kalsin raised a single brow.

“What I, uuuh—ahem—what I mean to say is, did you sleep well last night?”

“I don’t think either of us slept a whole lot.”

Two weeks ago Kalsin would have never guessed Ringtail was even capable of blushing. “Cheeky bastard,” she muttered a bit more under her breath, but didn’t dispute the truth of his statement. Kalsin could still feel the bitemarks on his neck from last night. Things had gotten quite… vigorous.

Ringtail stretched, this time letting the sheet drop and pool around her waist. She stretched out her arms above her head—theatrically so—and then draped them over Kalsin’s shoulder to lean into him like a human pillow.

“Fuck. I’m beat,” she said.

“A lot happened yesterday,” Kalsin agreed absently. He felt her finger draw little circles on the small of his back.

“Mmh.”

“Say; how much of this was planned, and how much was pure impulse?”

Her head resting on his shoulder turned toward him a bit. “Whatddaya mean.”

“Condier it for my perspective. I’ve got a girl hounding me, just lightly at first, but then reaching a point where we can’t exchange a single duel without it devolving into a shouting match. Then we make up—somewhat—and fight together, and grieve together, and then argue some more.” He poked her arm wrapped around his chest. “Jumping my bones is not the next logical step in that equation.”

“Mate. Man, buddy.” Her voice was somehow pleased and exasperated at the same time. “For someone so intelligent, Ghost, you can be really dense sometimes.”

“Spell it out for me, then. Ringtail.”

She snorted and ignored his request. A tiny woman with enough pride to supply a village. “It’s Rin, by the way.”

Kalsin paused. “Your name?”

“Mhm. Rin Origáte.”

“Interesting. Kalsin.”

They both remained like that, Kalsin staring at the morning sun lighting up the room and Ringtail still in bed, using him as a climbing pole.

“I think I prefer Ringtail. Suits a little muppet like you more.”

“I think I’ll be calling you Ghost. Kalsin is too refined for a brickhead like ya.”

He shook his head. “You never surrender a fight, do you?”

“Oh, sorry. I didn’t know you liked your women all meek and powerless. Why, the moment we go outside I shall discard my sword and shield, pick up the nearest needle, and—”

One moment she was running her mouth, and the next she gave a yelp as Kalsin hooked an arm around her waist and twisted, pulling her down flat against the bed with a soft whump. Her legs flailed once, blanket twisting with her like a poorly caught sail, before she landed square on her back with Kalsin above her.

Ringtail blinked up at him, hair sprawled across the pillow in a tangled mess.

Kalsin kept one hand beside her head, the other flat against her stomach to keep her still. “Gods,” he muttered with a low voice. “You’re exhausting.”

Her grin came slow, teeth bared like she was about to say something stupid again. “Oh, I definitely exhausted you last night. Saw the way you were limping just now, trying to spare your poor soldier. Was I bouncing too hard, eh? Eeeh?”

Through the wrapping around her stomach, he could feel the rough outline of her scar. It immediately sent an uneasy shiver across Kalsin, and he almost pulled his hand away. Almost.

Kalsin slowly leaned back away from Ringtail. But his touch remained on her slowly rising and falling belly.

It took effort. It took a damn lot of effort. Holding a white-hot branding iron would have been easier.

Ringtail’s grin slowly disappeared. Her eyes dropped down to where he was touching her, then back up to him. “I…” She swallowed. “Ghost. Kalsin. I don’t know how else we could have— I just didn’t want to make you uncomfortable, and, you know, maybe if I helped you forget about my scar, there was a chance we—”

“Shush.”

“No, I’m serious. I don’t want to make you feel, I dunno, uncomfortable with—”

“That’s enough, Ringtail. I understand,” Kalsin said. “I do. You were being more thoughtful than I deserved. Thank you for that.”

They watched each other for a moment. Then both their eyes slipped back to the place where Kalsin's hand was resting.

“Uh. Yeah, um… Guess I’m kinda spectacular like that, huh?” Ringtail tried to joke. But she couldn’t hide the tension in her body as she saw Kalsin’s hand slowly move toward the edge of the wrapping. “Hey. What are you…” she started, grabbing his wrist.

Kalsin gave her a look.

She bit her lip. “You don’t have to, you know… prove anything to me. You don’t like scars—that’s fine in my book. We can just pretend mine doesn’t exist, if you want. Whenever we want to do,” she vaguely gestured around the room, “this, more.”

Kalsin would have liked that, he realized. He would have liked to spend another night with Ringtail.

But he was leaving tonight. Away from the Old; from his mates, from the army, and from the retribution that would follow the moment the Civil House’s agents realized that he had made his decision.

It was a sweet fantasy, to delude himself into thinking that he could ever live a normal life. The moment he revealed his power—whether intentionally, or by accident—he would have a horde of people descending upon him to try and take him out. The Clenn recruits camping nearby. Those who followed Hadúr’s and Tűzmother’s teachings. Scarrd higher-ups who would want to keep the status quo.

The ability to heal Scarrd did not guarantee for those same people to join him. He needed a headstart. He needed to disappear with Jack and start out slow, start out smart, establishing himself one step at a time at a place people wouldn’t think to look for him.

Jack was right. He needed to act. To move.

But before that—though it was foolish and stupid and all the heroically idealistic things he had scorned his entire life… before that, he wanted to help one girl that, for a single night, had made him feel alive.

Softly, his fingers moved across her fluttering belly. They gently hooked under the edge of the wrappings that covered her bare skin. He tugged them out of the way,, and she slightly lifted herself, bracing her arms against the bed to help him do it.

Of all people, he never would have guessed it would be Ringtail he first cured by his own will.

Her stomach was just as captivating as the first time he’d seen it. Bathed in the soft light of morning, with the room still steeped in the warmth of last night’s closeness, the small scar just left of her bellybutton seemed like a trivial thing. Barely worth noticing.

And yet, to the Republic, it was everything. Based on the values his childhood had indoctrinated into Kalsin, that scar was a one-way ticket to hell.

At least, that was how it was supposed to work. But today, he was offering a rare refund—personally chauffeured by him, with the Clennlands as the final stop.

His hand hovered over her scar, hesitating once more. He looked away, down at his own bare chest, burning the image of unblemished skin into his memory. To cherish it one final time before it was gone.

He let his hand fall. The pain was quick to follow.

But this time, he was ready to peer back into the past.
