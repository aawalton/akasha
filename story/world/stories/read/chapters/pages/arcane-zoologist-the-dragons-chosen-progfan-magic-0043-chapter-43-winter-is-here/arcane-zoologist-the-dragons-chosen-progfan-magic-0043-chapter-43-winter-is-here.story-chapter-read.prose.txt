Despite Callie's warning, it did little to help the village. The first frost came the very same night she discovered frost caterpillars, sending the villagers into a mad frenzy the next morning.

While frost wasn't too bad, and most of it had already melted by late morning, it was far too early for frost. Barely anyone has finished harvesting their crops. Nobody had begun proper winter preparations, Callie's family included. They had some firewood prepared for winter, but nowhere near enough.

The coop and stables also needed to be checked and any holes sealed for the winter. They still needed hay for Gale and chicken feed that would last through the winter. They had barely prepared any preserves.

So the moment morning came, both of their parents sprang into action. Mom instantly prepared for a hunt, together with other hunters from the village.

"This is going to be a long winter. We need to get as much food as possible before the real cold sets in," Mom explained to Callie before she left in a hurry.

"There are a lot of things that need to be prepared for winter. I'm going to have to work the forge the whole day. I'll drop by Granny Ethel and Blackthorn to explain that you two will be busy with the preparations. The crops remain in your hands," Dad said and hurried away before even washing his breakfast dishes.

Callie and Callen shared a nervous look.

"D-Do we have to prepare all the preserves by ourselves?" Callie asked her brother. While they had helped out each year, it was never something that they had to do solely on their own.

"Mom and Dad are busy. We're going to have to make do on our own," Callen said, rubbing the back of his neck.

Callie felt a bit nervous. If they didn't do things right and messed up the preserves, they could starve during winter. But thankfully, she wasn't alone. Together with her brother, Callie tackled the task head-on.

First, they harvested everything that wasn't yet harvested. Vegetables that needed some time to dry were left in the sunniest places in the yard they could find. Cabbage was shredded, salted, and put into a barrel to be fermented into sauerkraut. Tomatoes and cucumbers were stuffed into jars with marinade to be made into pickles. Apples and pears were made into jam.

For a whole week, this was all that Callen and Callie did, from early morning to late evening. Callie had washed so many jars that she was seeing them in her sleep. She also occasionally had to run to the neighbors to get some of the stuff they were missing, like jar lids and salt.

Sadly, everyone was missing salt, and the only place to get it was from the merchant Silas. Callen, being busy with boiling marinade in a giant pot, sent Callie to Silas. His stall was busier than ever, with all the villagers rushing over to buy things they would need for winter.

"Oh, hello, Callie! Running some errands for your family?" The merchant greeted her with a warm smile.

For some reason, she still felt uneasy around him. Perhaps it was because he was an outsider.

"I-I need some salt…" Callie muttered, seeing that there were already others in the line behind her, looking very impatient while she stammered in front of the stall.

"Sure thing, little miss," he said, and produced a small packet for her. He then leaned over the counter and whispered. "I'm giving your family a discount since your brother has helped me out so much."

He winked at her and took half of the necessary coins, pressing the rest into Callie's palm, so others wouldn't see. The gesture was kind, but she was slightly startled by his cold hands. Then again, with how rapidly it was getting colder, it wasn't that surprising.

"Th-Thank you…" Callie muttered and hurried back home.

Throwing a glance back, she saw that the merchant was soon swarmed with customers. It had been kind of him to give them a discount, but Callie just couldn't shake the unease she got around the man. Part of her still suspected that he might have been the one behind the theft of the glauxis egg, even if he appeared completely harmless.

Then again, she also suspected Mister Blackthorn, who outwardly definitely appeared more suspicious than Silas.

I don't have time to think about this. I got work to do!

With each day that passed, it got colder and colder. Callie saw more and more frost caterpillars each morning. Dad came home only late at night, long after Callie went to sleep. Mom returned only for one night. The hunters had returned with a deer and three rabbits, which were being prepared and smoked by the other adults.

The chief was overseeing the process and making sure that meat was distributed fairly among families. Families of hunters received the largest share, and the rest was split equally. Even so, Callie's family didn't get all that much.

Two weeks after the first frost, the first snowflakes began to fall. Callie was put in charge of the preserves, while Callen had to chop firewood. Dad rushed home for a few hours each day, running around and repairing what he could—preparing the stables and shed for winter, as well as checking all the doors and windows of their house.

There was only one saving grace in the middle of all of this madness. Callie's magic. Since Dad was at the forge, the task of lighting the stove went to Callie. The first few times were… a bit explosive.

She had a hard time gauging her power. Unlike the first few times, when she had to try really hard to get her magic going, now it responded much more quickly. In fact, it seemed quite eager to do things.

It was a good thing she had directed all of her magic into the stove. The smoke cloud was quite impressive and took several hours to air out of the house. But she had done it. Every consecutive time, Callie was able to control her magic a bit better.

While using fire magic, she focused on restraint. Fire was quite eager to burst out and show its power, so keeping it restrained was key. Her attempts were still far from graceful, and where they were restricted to the stove, Callie had no doubt she would have long since burned the village down. But it was valuable practice.

Three weeks after the first frost, a small layer of snow had settled over the village and the nearby forest. The Harvest Festival had been cancelled entirely since everyone was far too busy to even consider the idea of a festival.

Mom had finally returned home from two more hunts, having brought back two boards and several peasants alongside other hunters. But even with their larger share, it still felt like scraps. It wouldn't be enough to last the winter at all.

Not to mention that they had another problem. Their clothes. Namely, Callie's and Callen's clothes. They have both grown. Callie hadn't grown that much, but enough that her previous winter coat and boots had become too small. Even last winter, they felt too small, so they could no longer be worn.

But it wasn't the end of the world. Mom would trade her smaller coat away to one of the smaller village children and try to get a used one from one of the older ones.

It wasn't so easy with Callen. He has had a real growth spurt over the summer, so his old coat would just be torn apart if he tried to put it on. Not to mention that there weren't any boys slightly older than him from whom Mom could get a used coat. They would have to get him a new one.

Except they didn't have any money left. Everything they had, they had already spent on buying other things they needed for the winter.

"I'm going to go and ask around. See what I can find," Mom said and left with Callie's old coat and boots in tow.

Once she returned, her expression was grim, although she did have a bundle with her.

"I managed to get a used coat for Callie and some used boots, too. They are not the best, but if we line the inside with some of the fur I got from the hunt, it should last the winter," Mom said, showing the coat and boots to Callie.

They looked rather shabby and clearly worn for more than a single winter. But beggars couldn't be choosers. She would have to make do. Callen didn't have even that much. Mom had managed to convince the shoemaker to prepare a pair of boots for Callen in exchange for some of the boar meat their family had.

It was a pricey trade-off, but the shoemaker wouldn't settle for anything less, having received barely any meat since he was compensated with leather instead.

But the coat… well, there wasn't any. Nobody just had a spare coat in Callen's size. The ones passed around between kids were too small for Callen, and adults in general had one coat they wore for sometimes decades.

"It's okay. I'll borrow one of the wool cloaks from Teacher Blackthorn," Callen said.

Callie scrunched up her nose. "Brother, those cloaks are old and patchy. No way that will be enough to keep you warm!"

"Callie's right. You need a proper coat," Dad said firmly.

Mom pressed a thumb to her lips, deep in thought. After a while, she seemed to have come up with something, but she glanced at Callie with a forlorn expression.

"We're going to harvest the frost moth cocoons and try to sell them to Silas," Mom announced.

"What?!" Callie gasped, staring at Mom. Harvesting cocoons now would mean certain death for the moths.

It was actually common for people to gather frostmoth cocoons in the middle of winter. But it was done after the moths emerged so as not to kill them. It was a bit odd that people of this village, who usually hated arcane beasts, would go out of their way to preserve their lives.

But frostmoths were actually a source of joy for many people. Bugs persistent enough to live through even the coldest of winters. Not to mention that their emergence from the cocoons signified that the spring was not too far off.

"Mom, you can't! Not many will have even made cocoons this early. Harvesting them now would mean that they would die!" Callie objected.

"I'm sorry, dear. I don't really want to do this either, but we don't have a choice. We have no money, and Callen needs a new coat. You wouldn't want your brother to freeze."

"Of course not! But the moths… surely we can do something else to get the money…"

Mom shook her head. "I wouldn't have suggested it if there was something else we could do. We can't sell any of the food anymore, since I don't even think we have enough to last us through the winter. Selling chickens is also out of the question, and we'll need Gale during winter, especially a winter as harsh as this one."

"But the other villagers—"

"Most of them are even worse off than we are. I'm sorry, dear. I wish we didn't have to do this. You know how much everyone looks forward to seeing frostmoths emerge from their cocoons. If there was a better solution, I would have suggested it," Mom said, patting Callie's head.

Callie's eyes filled with tears. Small though they were, frostmoths were still arcane beasts. Callie had wanted to study them, understand them. After all, the fact that they seemed to eat nothing but the winter frost was amazing in its own right.

Yet to think that they would have to resort to killing them. It likely wouldn't just be Mom. The whole village was desperate, and frostmoth silk was valuable, even Callie knew that. It was actually one of the reasons Greyfen could even exist and why Gerda's family was the richest in the village.

She would collect the incredibly warm silk and make winter clothes out of it. Not for the villagers, but rather for the wealthier people in town. As ironic as it was, nobody in the village wore the clothes from frostmoth silk, because it was simply that much more valuable to be sold.

"Wait, Mom. Let me talk to Silas first. He's been drowning in customers for days. Maybe I can offer to help him in exchange for money or a coat," Callen said, and hurried out of the house before anyone could object.

Callie felt hopeful. She and her parents waited for Callen's return with bated breath. Not that they were standing around. They were still moving about and doing as much work as they could.

But after about half an hour, Callen returned with a large grin on his face. "I managed to strike a deal with Silas. It won't be easy, but I think Callie and I could do it."

Mom raised an eyebrow. "Callie too?"

"Yeah. Don't worry, it's nothing crazy, but I'll need Callie's help too."

"I'll help!" Callie shot her hands up. "Whatever Brother needs, I'll help."

As long as it would protect the frostmoths, Callie would do it. Mom, on the other hand, looked skeptical. "What is it exactly that he needs?"
