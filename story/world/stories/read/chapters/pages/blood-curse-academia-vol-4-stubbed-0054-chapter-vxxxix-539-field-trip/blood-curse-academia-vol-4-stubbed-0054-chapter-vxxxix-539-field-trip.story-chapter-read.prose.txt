Chapter V.XXXIX (5.39) - Field Trip

The weekend after all the drama with Emilia, Kizu stood on the porch outside Anata’s schoolhouse in an uncomfortable gray outfit with two highlighted yellow stripes across his chest. Taroe had dumped this on him along with the responsibility that came with it. He was to be a chaperone on Anata’s class’s field trip.

Kon sat at his feet, eyes darting about and examining every passerby with suspicion. Inside the schoolhouse, the students were getting a lecture from their teacher, Veronica Reads, about the rules they needed to follow while out as a group. Kizu didn’t really understand why the chaperones had been asked to remain outside, but he didn’t mind. The weather was nice and the air fresh.

The other two chaperones stood to his side, also wearing the garish outfits. He vaguely recognized them from when Taroe had dragged him to Anata’s parent teacher meeting a few months ago. Both of them were mothers to children in the schoolhouse. One of them, a woman with her brown hair up in a meticulously complex bun, had been screaming at Ms. Reads during the meeting. The other wore large emerald green spectacles that kept slipping down her long nose.

Neither of them acknowledged him beyond a distasteful sniff and turn of their heads. They chatted quietly to one another and Kizu decided to practice enhancing his hearing to eavesdrop on their conversation. It was a spell he’d been wanting to get better at. Using potions for a similar effect was easy, but required resources.

“What if the over-sized white rat attacks?” the woman with the glasses asked. A furtive glance at Kizu’s feet revealed who she was talking about.

“If they try anything on this trip, I personally will guarantee their expulsion from the school,” Ms. Harrow, the angry mother, replied. “I’ve set up precautions to protect our children from these horrible gremlins.”

To keep himself from snapping at the horrible ladies, Kizu instead wondered about gremlins. He’d heard mention of the creatures in the past, but only in the context of an insult for someone small. He’d never actually seen one. He wondered if Ione had any experience with the creatures. What could they be used for in a brew? He felt like the crone might have referenced them in the past. The memory proved elusive in a normal, not locked away in a far corner of his mind, kind of way.

While he deeply disliked the two ladies joining him as chaperones, Kizu decided he’d had enough drama for the week and would treat them with a civility they didn’t deserve. He wouldn’t start anything with them and would do his best to keep Anata, Mae, Kon, and Rui in line. However, if this woman had some grand plan to get the children in trouble, he’d already decided to stomp on that immediately.

The woman with the glasses pushed them up her nose and glanced again in Kizu’s direction. Her eyes flicked to the top of his head, where Mort rested on his green hair. “That boy over there—”

“Is a child. Not even worth consideration,” Harrow replied. She looked to be about to say more, but the schoolhouse front door opened, revealing a line of children. They all wore rucksacks on their backs, stuffed with blankets and boxed lunches.

“Remember!” Ms. Reads called out. “Two-by-two! You’re a class of wonderful students, not an uncivilized mob!” In a quieter voice, the teacher addressed the chaperones. “I’m going to lead. I need two of you in the center and one taking up the rear.”

Kizu opened his mouth to volunteer for the role in the back, but Harrow beat him to it.

“I’ll watch any stragglers in the back. I won’t permit any lollygagging.”

Kizu’s heart dropped when he spotted who the final two students were in the line. Mae and Rui. At least Anata had been mixed up in the front center area. Small blessings.

Kneeling down, Kizu met Kon’s eyes. “Listen, I’m going to leave Mort with you. If that lady tries anything, let Mort respond before you rip out her calcaneal tendon.”

“Calcan…” Kon shook his head and growled. “What?”

“The back of her leg.” Kizu sighed. He’d been studying too many medical textbooks recently. “My point is, do not hurt the mean lady.”

“No promises,” Kon growled. Kizu opened his mouth to retort but Kon continued speaking. “I will try not to bite her.” He then snapped the air as if to punctuate his statement.

That was the best he could hope for. Mort stretched and yawned. The monkey hopped onto the back of the Kitsune boy as the white fox trotted over to join his sister at the back of the line.

Kizu took his place in the center, near Anata. With everyone in place, their march to the park commenced.

All of the children held hands with their ‘buddy’ as they walked. Some looked happier with the arrangement than others. Anata looked as if she was holding hands with an Ooze. Only her index finger was grasped by her companion.

The girl had pigtails and, judging by the chatter Kizu overheard, was a bit of a know-it-all. She was insisting that the birds flying overhead were hatched in palm trees. It just so happened that Ione had rambled about these particular birds to him once, so Kizu knew for a fact she was wrong. The boy behind her also, correctly, thought the birds were born in holes in the desert over in Edgeland; however, he lacked the girl’s confidence and was quickly dismissed by her.

They exited the town and hiked along a boardwalk. Occasionally, the stopped and Ms. Reads explained the ecosystem and different animals that lived nearby. They even got lucky and spotted a silt sloth. An evasive magical creature Ione had been trying to locate ever since arriving on the island. It peeked up from the mud and smiled at them as the students pointed and excitedly chattered. It lifted a clawed hand, as if in a wave, then burrowed back under the mud. The mud bubbled for a few seconds after its departure before stilling.

Ms. Reads called for a five minute water break for all while they watched the mud bubbles. The students unslung their rucksacks and began gulping down bottles and flasks of water.

“Are you actually Anata’s father?” a boy asked beside him. He was smaller than most of the others. He held hands with a familiar, bigger boy, Kizu recognized as Dean, the son of the angry mother.

“No! He’s just her father’s errand boy!” Dean snapped at the boy.

“Dean is correct, I am not Anata’s father,” Kizu replied to the boy who’d asked the question.

“He shouldn’t be allowed here at all. He’s not even an adult!”

Kizu stared at Dean. He remembered how the boy had hid behind his mother’s skirts and whined during the parent teacher meeting. For whatever reason, he now showed no fear or respect when speaking to Kizu. Likely due to the lack of Taroe’s presence.

“I turned seventeen a few months ago,” Kizu said. “According to Hon law, I’m an adult.”

“Not a real adult.”

“Hm.” This boy was exhausting.

“Yesterday, Anata switched out one of Dean’s pencils with a big worm,” the first boy whispered loudly. “He’s really angry today. He doesn’t like bugs.”

Kizu recalled Anata and Mae proudly carrying a jar of dirt around Taroe’s house the other day. They’d said they’d captured a ‘mega earthworm.’ That jar had been absent last night when he’d stopped by for dinner.

“Do you have any evidence it was her?” Kizu asked.

“Of course it was her!” Dean loudly snapped. “No one else is disgusting enough to play with worms.”

Several students around them turned to look at the angry student. Anata and her companion were among them. Despite the disrespect, Anata looked near bursting with laughter.

“Do you want to rephrase that statement, Dean?” Kizu asked.

“What? No! She’s horrible. Everyone knows it.”

Kizu considered his options. He could punt the brat into the mud under the boardwalk. That might get him in a bit of trouble though. In fact, most acts of disciple would probably be frowned upon. Tenfold with the angry mother in tow. He could also inform Ms. Reads and get her to discipline the boy. But again, nothing would happen so long as the mother was present. They boy clearly felt untouchable.

Anata clearly didn’t care and even found the accusation amusing. The smart thing to do was let it go. But Kizu was coming fresh from dealing with Emilia’s tyrannical behavior. He didn’t particularly feel like allowing his niece to get insulted.

“Have you ever heard the legend of Fredrick?” Kizu asked.

“Legend of… what?” Dean sneered at him. “Are you stupid?”

“I haven’t,” the other boy said. “What did Fredrick do?”

Their break ended and they were back to walking along the boardwalk. Kizu noted that under the boardwalk had shifted from mud over to a yellowish water that reeked of sulfur. Likely due to the volcanic activity under the island.

“It’s an old witch’s tale,” Kizu said. “Legend has it that once there was a boy named Fredrick and he was despised by everyone around him. He couldn’t comprehend why other children disliked him so much. He was always doing the right thing and exposing their sins to adults in power. Sometimes he even took it upon himself to punish his sinful peers.”

“What kind of sins?” the boy asked. “Were they, like, killing people? Was this a lawless land full of evil bandits?”

“Not quite. It was a schoolhouse in a small village. The sins were things like sitting in a seat improperly during class and putting elbows on the table and, worst of all, sometimes they would hide storybooks behind their textbooks.”

“Oh. Those don’t really sound like sins. Just… mistakes. It doesn’t even sound like it really bothers anyone.”

“I’m pretty sure that’s how rational people also saw it. Unfortunately for them, Fredrick wasn’t known for being rational.”

“So… what’s his legend? Where’s the witch?”

“Ugh,” Dean rolled his eyes. “Hinata, why are you listening to this? It’s stupid.”

“That’s all only the set up,” Kizu continued, deciding to ignore Dean. “One day Fredrick skipped home, feeling particularly good after successfully tattling on a girl in his class. She’d swapped out his boots with old lady slippers but he’d caught her in the act. Justice had been swift after he screeched for their teacher.”

Anata perked up and Kizu had the suspicion she was filing away that idea for later use. He decided not to note her change in posture, continuing on with the story as they walked

“On the way home, an old lady stood in his path. Fredrick tried to step past her but she seemed to be in his way no matter how he moved. He snarled up at her, demanding she step aside.

“‘I’m waiting for my granddaughter,’ the old lady said. ‘Boy, be a dear and fetch her for me.’

“Fredrick had no intention of helping the lady. He hadn’t done anything wrong and she bared his path. He told her to get lost and tried to shove past her. In his haste, he tripped. As he fell, he saw the same shoes as earlier on the lady’s feet.

“In a flash, Fredrick stood back in the schoolhouse. The girl he’d tattled on was there. She was with her friend and they talked about Fredrick. They gossiped about how detestable he was. They spoke about how he took insult to even the smallest mistake, while he was more slovenly as the rest of them put together. Fredrick tried to shout at them and wave down the teacher. They were being mean and gossiping! But no words came out. Then, in another flash, he stood next to a boy as he used a crude drawing of Fredrick as chamber pot paper. Flash again, and Fredrick witnessed his teacher venting to her husband about what a parasite he was. On and on, he saw what people thought of him, unable to open his mouth.”

Dean snorted loudly. “Let me guess, he woke up and changed his ways. He let all the people in the wrong walk all over him and was happy about it.”

Kizu kept his face completely placid as he faced the boy. “Not at all. He remained comatose, trapped as his mind replayed every horrible thing said about him for the rest of eternity.”

“What…” Dean’s hands clenched into fists and he clenched his teeth. He took a step out of line to stand in front of Kizu.

“The moral of the story is, nobody likes a self-obsessed brat,” Kizu finished.

Dean punched him.
