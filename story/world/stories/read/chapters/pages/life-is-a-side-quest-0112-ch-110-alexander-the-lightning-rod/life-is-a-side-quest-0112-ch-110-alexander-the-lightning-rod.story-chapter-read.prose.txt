“Three, two, one, GO!” Min Min screamed as the sound of lightning became deafening.

They darted to the next pillar close by. They had made it about halfway through, sometimes running for a distant pillar instead. It was stressful work and Alexander was once again amazed by the little mouse’s daring. One hit would certainly knock Min Min out. The Prince, as someone that had a class, likely had more constitution than the tinker.

“Fifteen seconds.” Min Min called as they caught their breath.

“This is quite fun entertaining.” The Prince laughed.

“I would have to disagree.” Alexander panted after the many short sprints.

“As the giant says.” Min Min agreed, breathing heavily.

“Live your life in a stuffy safe castle and you to would take joy in any adventure you can.”

“Says the Prince who enjoys fight killing Dowcats for leisure time… three, two, one, go!”

They darted to a larger pillar before Min Min immediately pointed out the next one and said, “Go!”

As they approached the pillar, Wensley pulled his sword and shouted, “Storm Wraith! Watch out!”

Coming around the corner was a glowing vortex shaped like a tornado with arms. Alexander almost blasted it with a piercing wind out of reflex, but thought better of it as it would likely draw lightning to them. He instead made a sign and stretched out his hand to unleash a volley of piercing firebolts that passed right through the tornado body, leaving it mostly unaffected.

“Divine Wall!” The Prince shouted as a bolt of lightning streaked from the monster. A glowing form of a spectral gold wall sprang up to block the bolt.

“That way! Now!” Min Min shouted.

As one, they changed direction and ran behind another pillar.

Min Min quickly recalibrated her path. “We must double retrace back and go around!”

They darted back and took a path further left from where they started. They continued following Min Min’s lead until they spotted another larger figure in the distance.

“Curses, the spirits are busy active today.” Min Min despaired. “We may require need to go back and wait for another time.”

Alexander scanned the area for more lightning spirits when he noticed a section devoid of the prevailing trails of wind mana. “What’s that?” Alexander asked, pointing at a small square structure in the distance.

“I’ve not seen discovered that yet!” Min Min exclaimed.

“There is less wind mana from over there. Maybe check it out?”

“Yes! Go!” She shouted before taking off at an angle.

They darted around to three more pillars before reaching the object. As they approached, they noticed it was reflecting light off of it.

“No lightnings?” She asked in awe.

Alexander approached knowing it was safe after understanding the mana movements Min Min was looking for. He cautiously placed his hand near the surface, feeling out any buzz of power. There was none. He glided his hand across, feeling a perfectly smooth surface. Flick took the opportunity to jump onto the top, slipping on the slick surface.

Squeak.

“I agree, I think it’s made up of glass. Maybe the lightning changed the structure as it was initially made up of of a sand composite?”

There was a small thirty-foot gap surrounding it, leaving space between rock spires. They began to walk around the structure to examine it. It was about five feet wide. When they reached the other side of the cube, they found a perfectly round hole in the center of the cube’s face. Alexander pulled out a maglight and clicked it on, shining it directly into the center of the hole. An orb filling the space sat floating in the center.

“Is it treasure?” Min Min asked, excitedly.

Alexander looked at the powerful pale blue mana swirling around it.

“No… I don’t think so.”

“Poke it with a rod stick!” Wensley suggested.

“So you recommend the scientific method?” Alexander asked, causing Min Min to giggle at the Prince.

“The ‘check if its new fresh’ method. When tracking your prey you don’t just stick you finger in the poop droppings!” The Prince laughed.

“Colorful.” Alexander smiled.

“Only if they snacked ate some mushrooms.” He grinned.

“Man boys are gross.” Min Min sniffed.

They looked about for anything else of significance, but didn’t find anything.

Alexander examined the orb a bit longer before shrugging and pulling out a walking stick. “If the prince says so, we can poke it… for science.”

“My wisdom prevails.”

“Your death wish prevails.” Min Min huffed.

“Flick, can you shoot some lighting at that side of the wall?” Alexander asked, pointing at the side of the structure.

Chirp. Flick nodded.

Alexander placed his hand on the adjacent side.

“Go ahead.”

Flick touched the wall and electrified his body. Alexander braced himself but felt nothing.

“Non-conductive.”

“For the record, I am mostly against this coarse of action.” Alexander said pointedly. “If it didn’t seem this place had relevance to the surrounding environment I’d say we just make camp here and try and figure out the behavior of the Storm Wraiths.”

“Yes, yes. If it turns into a dumb bad idea I will take full credit for it.” The Prince waved, nonchalantly.

“Ok, here I go.” Alexander said, reaching out the stick and poking the orb. His stick pushed into the orb and it behaved elastically like a thin rubber. It stretched inwards, without piercing, and when he pulled the stick back the whole orb rippled for a few seconds.

“Huh, odd.” He watched the pale blue mana start to shift and grow before a liquid tendril shot forward, stabbing him in the shoulder. A shield pin flared on and blocked the tendril, but the momentum and power of the strike still pushed Alexander backwards off his feet, shattering the temporary protection.

“Enemy attack!” Wensley yelled, jumping in front of Alexander with sword raised.

The ball morphed and flowed slowly out of the structure, and began to shape itself as a liquid ten-foot tall golemoid.

“Another spirit!?” Min Min exclaimed.

The liquid golem finished forming before its entire body began rippling.

“That doesn’t look good.” Alexander said, pushing himself up.

“Divine Wall!” Wensley shouted as half a dozen of the piercing tendrils shot forward.

The tendrils pushed the impressive wall backwards, but it held.

“Are we fighting or retreating?” Alexander asked.

In response Min Min pulled two strings on her shoulder. “We are fighting!”

Armor snapped out from under her cloak, snapping itself around her chest and arms. A small barrel protruded from her right wrist. Pointing it around the transparent wall at the golem, she snapped her hand shut, and a shot fired, punching a three-inch hole in the liquid body. The golem’s body rippled from the hit, but the hole slowly closed up and it started forward to attack.

The Prince darted around his own wall, leaving it to cover Alexander, and started cutting into the golem’s leg rapidly. Liquid splashed about as the monster swung an arm down to smash at the Prince, but he darted out of the way.

“It is slow sluggish!” We can wear it down!”

Alexander signed for a floating disc under one leg and lifted it rapidly, causing it to fall sideways.

Flick took the opportunity to get close and release a lightning breath that caused the body of the golem to quiver in waves before it broke apart into a liquid puddle.

“Great job mighty dragon!” Wensley called.

The puddle undulated and started forming again, and the party regrouped.

“Uh oh.” Min Min muttered to the group.

“What?”

“Many lots of Storm Wraiths.”

As they looked up, they saw a dozen forms making their way towards them.

“We have to get out of here fast quick!” Wensley called. He said, taking out the box with the earth crystal. He wound the crank three times and pressed it into the box with a click, then threw it at the two closest wraiths. It hit the ground and began to hum before sharp jagged rocks exploded into existence, engulfing the wraiths within.

“That work great wonderful!” Min Min shouted.

“Its not going to save rescue us for long!” The Prince responded.

“Everyone get behind the Divine Wall.” Alexander called. They immediately moved.

Alexander jumped on his floating disc and waited for the golem to stand after regrouping. He formed a shield in one hand and flew right under the body of the golem. Then he put his shield above his body and laid down, curling into a ball.

He gathered every drop of will and split the attention three ways. Holding both his floating disc and shield firmly in mind, he shouted full of adrenaline, “Piercing Wind!”

His glasses lit up with waves of colors as his head began to pound. He watched as a wind blade tore upwards through the golem. Then, as predicted, several lances of lightning shot down to consume the wind mana. The golem exploded around him, some of which landed on the disc and began shocking him. He cried out in pain, but his health only dropped by a third. He felt a thunk as something landed on his shield and rolled off.
