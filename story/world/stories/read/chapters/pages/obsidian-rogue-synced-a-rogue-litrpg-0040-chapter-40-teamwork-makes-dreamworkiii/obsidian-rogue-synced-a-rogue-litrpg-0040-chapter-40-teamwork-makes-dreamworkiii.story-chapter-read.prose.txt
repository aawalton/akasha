Levi turned to stare at the molten slag at the other end of the room, then at the smoky ruin that was the ogre's neck, and finally at Xara. The mage was still looking at him, and he could tell from the expression on her face that his next words would matter to her.

“You made that,” he said, his voice filled with confusion.

That much power in that little piece of metal was a bit hard for Levi to understand.

He had needed repeated strikes with his dagger to do damage to the ogres, and even then, the damage inflicted by his dagger was mild compared to the damage that the Xistol had inflicted.

Never in his wildest dreams had he expected something like this to exist, or even, at the very least, have it save his life. Levi could tell that the weapon itself was very valuable from the brief demonstration that the mage had just shown.

Levi would have thought it impossible, but somehow the mage's cheeks grew redder at his question, and she seemed almost nervous to answer. So it was no surprise that her answer eventually came out as a stutter.

“I–I did,” she said.

“How?”

“Uh, it's nothing. I just managed to figure out how to create a blueprint from the feasibility study I got from my family's vault.”

Levi looked at the mage in disbelief. Feasibility study? Blueprints?

The Xistol seemed like a weapon that would fetch an incredible sum on the black market. Heck, any market at all. And if it was something that Xara's family had created, he expected to get word about it even in the small town he’d grown up in. A weapon that could deliver such devastating levels of damage up close would surely be the talk of the black market or the underworld, but the fact that he hadn't heard a single whisper about a weapon such as this sent a chill down his spine.

“You made that from the feasibility study you got from your family's vault.”

“Well, I actually stole it from my family,” Xara winced. “It was supposed to be thrown away because the synced researchers who worked for my family could not find a way to create a stable version of it that could be mass-produced, so it was discarded, but I found it in the family vault. It seemed like a brilliant idea, and I hoped that if I could create a stable version of the Xistol, it’d be enough to maybe help me get one over on my brother. But that was not the case.”

“Doesn’t matter, you're a genius,” Levi said.

“I wish,” she chuckled. “The first iteration of the Xistol that I tried to build on my own exploded in my face. If not for the fact that I'm a mage with some skill with fire manipulation, I could have been burned badly.”

“But still you didn't give up, and you ended up making that.”

“Yes, I did. But that is nothing. At least that's what my father said.” Xara sighed. “In the end, my little side project was not enough to improve my standing within the family. Neither was it enough to cover my lack of talent compared to my brother.”

“Talent?” Levi said in disbelief. His eyes momentarily drifted to the molten slag before returning to Xara. "If that doesn't qualify as talent, I would hate to meet your brother."

“I like that you think that my work means something,” She smiled warmly. “ Unfortunately, my brother is tipped to become one of the greatest mages of our generation, and this stupid molten slag isn't enough to get me in those kinds of conversations. But it was a lifeline for a bit. And I've invested so much time and effort into making it work that I cannot even fathom abandoning the Xistol, despite how unstable it is.”

“That’s why I keep it on me, and I’ll continue to make more and play with different runes till I succeed.” Xara sighed as she briefly turned her gaze to the molten slag. “I’m beginning to understand why my family's synced researchers marked the project as unfeasible.”

“And why is that?” Levi asked. Surely coins could not be the only reason they had abandoned such a project. The Houses, as he knew them, were wealthy beyond what he could even comprehend.

“For one, the high-level synced mages wouldn't need this tool to take out any foe or opponents that they come across. In fact, I doubt the Xistol in its current form would be able to take out any foe that would give them problems.” Xara said. “Secondly, the weapons require a significant sum to create because the parts for the best results are expensive. That aside, the Xistol still needs a few runes for stability. Of course, those runes themselves are not in my family's library, and I know for a fact that they aren't anywhere else in Rinesta… Well, maybe it could be in the forgotten library.”

Once again, Levi's eyes unconsciously drifted to the molten slag, and he couldn't help but fixate his gaze on the destroyed weapon.

The Forgotten Library was what the mage had just mentioned, a library that no one knew, or to put it more aptly, no one had concrete information on. It seemed that every record of the known location of the hidden library had disappeared.

There were no traces, records, directions, maps, or scrolls with anything tangible about the Forgotten Library. But according to what he’d heard in the taverns of Gantus, it was a place where a vast amount of knowledge had been hidden away for the greater good of Rinesta. Levi disagreed.

It was impossible for such a building to exist. Levi believed it was just a way for some synced researchers who had been unable to get their hands on a project of their own to feel that if they dedicated their lives to finding a magical building that somehow magically disappeared, they would be able to leave a mark in the world or achieve something worthwhile.

Even from Gantus, he had heard that a lot of money had been funneled to synced researchers to locate the Forgotten Library. To Levi, it seemed like the Forgotten Library was just a way for synced researchers to make good coin without actually doing any work, which was probably why the myth still hadn't been disproved.

He turned his gaze to the mage. “Your family might not recognize what you have achieved, but the head of the ogre certainly does, and so do I,” Levi said with a smile, which was hidden beneath his hood.

What wasn't hidden by his hood was the face of the mage, which had gone redder from his Words.

“Thank you.” The words were the softest he’d heard from the mage's lips, and it seemed like she was holding back a tear or two. Rather than let the budding awkwardness that was starting to build creep in, Levi decided to move the conversation along.

“Do you think you're in more trouble since you stole this from your family's vault?”

“Maybe.” Xara's said. “The feasibility study for the Xistol wasn't the only thing I took from my family, but I don't think they are going to miss any of the other things. They are basically just feasibility studies that were rejected. I think I might be able to implement in the correct environment, or at the very least get close enough,” she paused, an apologetic look crossing her face as she turned to Levi. “I apologize, I hadn't really thought about being hunted for the feasibility studies, but we should be fine. House Noir has a lot of things in the works. I don't think they'll chase me all the way here for a bunch of feasibility studies that were rejected.”

“I hope so,” He said, skeptical.

Levi didn't really care, but if those feasibility studies that the mage had stolen from her family's vaults were anything remotely as close to the Xistol, the mage would probably become a person of interest, or at least would be chased down by her family once they got word of it being used in the open. Of course, the fact that it had only been used in the dungeon meant that they were safe for now, but he didn't know how much longer it would remain that way if the mage were to create and use another Xistol on an opponent in public.”

He would just have to take her word that her family didn't care about the feasibility studies, because he wasn't quite keen on fighting for survival on multiple fronts.

Levi leaned forward. “You said your family has a vault. If you don't mind me asking, what exactly goes in there?”

“Well, just a few things that were passed down by my ancestors, down the family line,” she said, then paused, “a few spell tomes, artifacts, equipment, those kinds of things.”

They are already five steps ahead of the rest of us from the moment they are born.

“Really, your ancestors left stuff behind for you guys?”

“Yes. When you say it like that, it sounds silly, but there's actually a good reason behind it,” Xara said after spotting Levi's raised eyebrow. “Have you ever wondered why synced of the same house usually go with the same class?”

“No, not really.”

“It's because it is easier to pass down information, artifacts, equipment, skill tomes, and basically any other thing that will improve the chances of the next generation of the house climbing higher in the social hierarchy, or at least being able to defend the position of the family in the social hierarchy if all members of the house are of the same class. In our case, our father is the Duke, which is the highest position, second only to the Monarchy,” she said, and paused to make sure Levi was listening. “ If the children or the heir of the house, in this case, my brother and I, are both talentless or average, we will have the chance to bridge the gap between the other houses' heirs and us by making use of the artifacts kept for us by our ancestors to ascend further faster.”

“Now, these artifacts, equipment, and skill tomes come with restrictions of their own that are usually level-based. So we might not be able to use most of them until we have ascended a bit on our own. I believe the lowest artifacts and equipment I have seen with the lowest restriction in our family vault is at level 41, and I am still a far ways away from reaching that, so is my brother, but he's a lot closer to it, which is I wouldn't put it past him soon becoming able to use the equipment for himself to improve his standing and go on better dungeon runs. Which is mostly the point.”

“Oh, that makes sense,” Levi said.

The act of getting better weapons, equipment, skill tomes, and, in fact, every other thing from their ancestors that the mage had just listed made Levi understand why power was usually retained within a small circle of Rinesta.

While some of those within the Rinesta's high circles needed to work for their power, it was very hard for Levi to believe that they were working a lot harder than the rest of them at the bottom.

The weapons and artifacts would obviously make things a lot easier for them, just as they would for him. In fact, the items that Xara had bought him had already started to show their importance, and he could not deny that the improved cloak, which basically glued the hood to his face, was definitely a big improvement on his starter pack that he had received for free upon syncing. His new daggers were also a lot better than his first.

All in all, the improved equipment he had gotten had significantly improved both his fighting capabilities and his chances of survival in one go. Levi could only imagine what the ancestors of the strongest families in Rinesta had left behind for the generations after them.

In fact, just the Xistol, a feasibility study that had been abandoned, was enough to blow both Levi's mind and the ogre's head, which was enough to raise the question of what else they had marked as feasible? What else did they have readily made and prepared?

It was obvious that anyone in the high circles of Rinesta’s leading families who was smart enough to put in a bit more effort than the rest of the so-called heirs and children of the other higher houses would be able to get a major boost compared to the other heirs and to those at the bottom like him who had had to shed sweat, tears, and blood to get where they were.

That is the game, I suppose, never on even footing. Levi thought.

It was never fair. Even Xara herself, who was from a high-class family, still felt cheated by the advantages given to the heirs of prestigious families.

According to her own words, she had witnessed her brother receive special privileges which improved his ranking over time.

It is what it is. Levi reluctantly pushed the thoughts of how the high-class family held on to power to the back of his mind.

He couldn't dwell too much on the fortunes of the already established families of Rinesta, but he did wish he could do the same for the generations that would come from him.

Levi knew it was possible because he had heard of a few families in the high circles of Rinesta that had emerged over the years and grown in influence. And if he were lucky, then maybe he'd be the one to change things for the rest of his lineage, and maybe even beyond that.

All it takes is one person to pave the way. I can be that one person.

More than that, though, was the recurring theme of always having to be close to his enemies. Of course, that was the theme of being a rogue, so he couldn't really complain. But his current predicament made his needs a bit different from the average rogue. He wasn't just a thief or assassin at this point.

He was a mix of both and even more.

More in the sense that he had to fight a lot more monsters head-on. And if things were going the way that he predicted, he would have to fight Ghost head-on, which meant there would be no sneaking around, no backstabbing.

He needed range, and it seemed like the synergy between the portalist subclass and his rogue class had once again come through by providing him with the Abyss Ball. His synergy, Voidstepper, gave him access to skills like Abyss Ball, which a normal rogue wouldn't have access to. The description of the Abyss Ball itself gave Levi a bit of joy and relief. The skill would improve his chances of survival, and it added to his unpredictability, just as the Xistol had done for the mage.

I wish I had a Xistol. Levi thought.

It was a reasonable wish, but at the moment, he would have to settle for the abyss ball, which wasn't so bad if the description was anything to go by.

“Yeah, besides, it's not like I have a choice in the matter,” Levi said with a smile.

Of course, with the way his lips were hidden behind the darkness of his hood, he was sure she hadn't seen the expression on his face, but that didn't matter as he pushed off the ground and onto his feet, with the mage following suit.

He pressed his hand to the side of his ribs, and the pain caused him to wince internally, but he kept his face stoic and his mouth shut.

There was no point in complaining. They still had to get through the next door of the dungeon if they wanted to get out of the dungeon alive. “Do you have any health potions?”

“No, but I'll be sure to get some when we're out of here.”

“All right,” Levi said.

It was a bit of a reach to ask for a health potion because the mage herself had been injured a while back. And if she had had any, she surely would have used it on herself at the time. But there was no harm in asking.

With all the words needed to be said spoken, Levi reached for the door handle before a hand wrapped around his biceps.

“You are injured. Let me go in first this time,” Xara said.

Levi hesitated for a moment, and he almost had half a mind to refuse her, but from the look on her face, it was certain that she wouldn't take no for an answer. Besides, if there was any surprise on the other side of the door, Levi didn't think he was in the best shape to evade it, and it was obvious that she had a better chance of evading a surprise attack than he did, so he let her go first.
