Chapter V.LXVI (5.66) - Doppelgänger

Kizu’s eyes flickered open, revealing a dank cage. For a moment, he wondered if Ione’s final comment earlier had held some measure of truth, as this certainly wasn’t a mushroom chamber. Perhaps he’d materialized in yet another dream. This time the nightmare of a stranger. The cage certainly matched the motif of terrible tragedy and fears that they’d encountered so far.

Unfortunately, the person smiling at him from the other side of the bars put that theory in the ground. Kizu doubted some random stranger would know this face. After all, it was his own.

“Hello, me,” his doppelgänger said cheerily. He wore nicer clothing than his disheveled long hair and mad golden eyes warranted. “How are you this fine morning?”

“Where am I?” Kizu asked.

“What’s this? Where are my pleasantries? I demand an exchange! Have I diverged so far in the last half a year as to lose my manners?”

“We never had manners. We were raised by the crone. Answer the question.”

The doppelgänger buckled over in uncontained chortles as his remark. “You’re right! Manners? Where did I get that notion from? Ha!”

“Where am I?” Kizu repeated, unamused by his mad-self’s reaction. “And where are my friends?”

“Can you be specific about which friends you speak of?”

Kizu’s frown deepened. He wasn’t certain how much information to give away freely. It now occurred to him that him waking up here alone might be an indicator that they hadn’t found the others, only him. If so, he might be able to use this as an opportunity to mislead them into believing inaccurate information.

His bond with Mort let him know that his familiar wasn’t far away. That could mean he was also captured and held hostage nearby. Or, it could mean Mort was leading his friends to his location in an effort to rescue him.

“Just kidding!” his doppelgänger declared. “Ione and Basil are in cages over there in the torture room.” He tossed a thumb over his shoulder. “Mort’s in the next wall over. I found you all slumbering among the mushrooms like sleepy kittens. So, I scooped you up and carried you off. Don’t worry, though. Not a hair has been harmed on them! Yet.”

Kizu was slowly regaining his bearings. They were in a bad situation. His spatial ring and ice sword ring were both absent on his hands. His atlas and bell were likewise gone. All that remained was his uniform and his necklace. That necklace’s existence still with him raised a few questions.

“How did I find you?” the other Kizu asked. “Don’t make that face. You’re groping around at your neck. I know what that fashion accessory does as well as you do. It’s obvious what you’re thinking.”

“Fine. Yes. I’m curious about how you located us.”

“I was just sitting down here, with my dear foxy friend, when I felt you stroll down into my territory. ‘That’s odd,’ I thought to myself. ‘I haven’t been down here in a while. I wonder what I’m getting up to.’”

Kizu crossed his arms and gave his other self a flat expression. There was only so much irritation he could display while trapped in a cramped cage barely a meter high, but he must have successfully gotten his message across because the other version of him giggled and waved him away.

“When Otochi reconstructed a body for the soul sliver you so callously discarded, he rearranged more than just my bum leg. He might have tinkered a bit with my soul. Did you know he was once buddies with a powerful necromancer? One of the most powerful in history. He can do a lot with souls, should he choose to.”

“I suspected as much,” Kizu replied. He didn’t go into detail, but he’d had reason to believe the gnome who once owned Owl’s Respite had come here to seek out the Blood Lord.

“Well, Otochi borrowed plenty of blood from his old pal. More than enough to reconstruct my soul to let my familiar bond run one-way.”

Kizu’s breath caught. He should have assumed as much from the reaction Mort went through when attempting to contact the sliver a few months ago. However, instead he defaulted to the idiotic thinking that it would be the same in the opposite direction. He had thought the reason Mort lacked a clear connection with his mentally mutilated version was because Kizu held the greatest portion of the soul, and therefore the connection defaulted to him. Never once did he stop to consider the pain might be a safeguard set up by Otochi. He should have pressed Aoi for information. No, more than that, he should have asked Stina and her coven for information about familiar bonds. In hindsight, it was so foolish for him not to ever consider the nuances of his soul connection. He defaulted to believing he was the acting authority and most knowledgeable about a subject he was woefully ignorant about.

If this version of him had access to Mort in the same manner Kizu did then that was bad. More than bad. It was genuinely catastrophic. He’d been feeding Otochi information for the last half a year while spying on him whenever he pleased. How often had Kizu left Anata in Mort’s care? This version of him might very well know far more than Kizu about some aspects of Anata’s life. That notion terrified him.

“Oh, relax,” his doppelgänger said. “Nobody’s in any real danger right now.”

“What you just said….”

“Yes, yes. I know you’re very clever. Already figured it out? Only natural I suppose. How many hours did the crone force us into spending staring into divinations of the village? Not that different of an experience. Somehow, I found it even more tedious than watching those idiots in the village. You know how often our familiar spends sleeping?”

“Mort is my familiar,” Kizu said darkly. “Not ours.”

His doppelgänger waggled a finger at him. “Our bond sure begs to differ.”

Kizu opted to remain silent. Logically, he should press this opportunity for information while his doppelgänger remained in a cheerfully chatty mood. But he felt violated and beyond irritable. Kizu didn’t trust himself not to lash out and ruin everything.

The other version of him simply stared, head tilted slightly with a wonky smile frozen on his face. They stayed like that, staring into one another’s golden eyes, for countless minutes.

“You know, I have your other jewelry,” his doppelgänger said, breaking their silence. He held out his hands, showing the two rings, and wiggled his fingers. “Your face. Your clothes. Your accessories. I bet that I could go up above and take your place. Nobody would know any better. I even know about what’s going on above ground, thanks to our little monkey friend. And trust me, I’d love to finish up those last few fights. The tournament looks so fun! I have a few tricks up my sleeve that nobody would ever see coming. I know a hex that transforms feet into slugs! And I’ve got loads of neat potion ingredients down here.”

“But you won’t,” Kizu concluded. “You know you’d get caught by the headmaster the moment you step foot in the academy.” He said the words with far more conviction and certainty than he felt. If this version of him chose to go up there, he could cause irreparable damage to Kizu’s name with barely any effort. One wrong word spoken could send cascading bad harm that could potentially last for eternity. Let alone if he decided to hex someone or destroy something. Best to keep him wary of entering Kizu’s life.

His doppelgänger blew a long strand of green hair out of his eyes. “You’re no fun. Yes. The headmaster is a problem. For now.”

“What does that mean?” Kizu had a bad feeling in his gut about that statement’s implication.

“Oh nothing.” The other Kizu raised his hand and admired the spatial ring there. His body was gaunter than Kizu’s, so he wore the ring on his middle finger. “I’ve been pulling things out of this, but it’s almost all just useless junk. Substance fillers, sleeping draughts, addiction replacements. Where’s all your fun stuff at? I didn’t see a single liquidization potion or zombification serum. I know you know how, cause I know how. That wouldn’t be something the crone would block from your memories. You have free reign over all potion knowledge in, what I imagine must be an attempt to keep you somewhat competent in something.”

“Why would I need to liquidize something?” Kizu said, though his mind raced at the mention of a different brew. His addictive replacement for Anata’s blood. He glanced down at his hand. Had his finger been twitching like that ever since waking up? Or was it brought on by the comment? Regardless, he needed that medicine. But he also needed to insure that his doppelgänger didn’t know he needed the medicine. Also, there were far more dangerous potions stored in that ring as well, best to not let him know. If he only knew what Mort could visibly see, then he was likely unaware of Kizu’s sorting system in the spatial ring and was simply yanking things out at random. As an Anata safety precaution, Kizu designed his sorting so the easier to access bottles were harmless brews. It would likely take him some time to pull anything dangerous out.

“Liquidizing people often isn’t a matter of need,” his other self scolded. “It’s a simple matter of want. And why wouldn’t you want to liquidize? It’s an artform! I’ve turned at least half a dozen different bloodspawn into puddles these last few months. And that’s to say nothing for the humans I encountered in Hon. So many fresh test subjects over there. I miss it. I only have unlimited access to one down here, and I have to be careful. Otochi will be pissed if I accidently kill her.”

“Her?” Kizu said.

“Yes, yes. It’s the Kitsune Harbinger. No bother keeping that a secret. Have you met her? She’s a dour creature. Never smiles.”

“She’s alive?” The question was stupid. Through context, Kizu knew she must be. But it was all he could think to ask.

“Mostly.” The doppelgänger’s manic grin grew. “You want to see her?”

Without waiting for a response, he strolled across the jail hallway and around the corner, just out of sight. Kizu heard him rip off a cloth sheet and saw it flutter to the floor.

“Wakey, wakey! Little fox, you have a visitor.” His words were punctuated by a thump followed by piteous noise, not quite a gasp but also not a yelp. “Oh, he can’t see you from over here, can he? Hm. Well, I suppose I can take you out. Not like you’re going to make a run for it.”

Chills went down Kizu’s spine as he heard his own voice cackling at the statement. It was somewhere between his natural laugh and the crone’s mad cackle. The one she used when she was feeling particularly sadistic.

A large orange and brown figure collapsed in front of his cage. It twitched and an animalistic head shifted in his direction, though it lacked eyes to see him with. It took Kizu several seconds to truly process what this was. Kumiho in her fox form. Though, it hardly looked like a fox now. Her limbs had been sliced off, leaving her legs as short nubs. Her triangular ears were likewise gone, along with her eyes. The brown mixed into her patchy fur was dried blood.

“Kizu?” she said, her voice a wheeze. As she opened her mouth, she revealed very few remaining teeth. Maybe five or six in total. And of those, all were twisted in unnatural angles.

“I’m here,” Kizu said, his voice cold. “The real one.”

“Hurtful!” His doppelgänger rounded the corner back into sight and clutched at his chest, dramatically. “I am equally as real.”

“My children?” Kumiho wheezed.

“Safe. Above ground.”

“Yes, yes. Little Mae and Kon are doing quite well,” his doppelgänger said. “Did I not tell you? I’ve been checking in on them these last few months. Very cute pair of foxes. The girl just started dating a human boy a few weeks back.” His face lit up and he snapped his fingers. “You don’t know! I just realized, I’ve been sitting on this juicy tidbit for ages.”

Kizu’s heart sank as he quickly realized what was about to be revealed. He opened his mouth to stop it, but his doppelgänger swished a finger through the air, sealing his lips together into smooth flesh. The hex took hold of Kizu, stopping any attempt at speech.

“Get this, Kizu sold your daughter out to save his ass! And, get this, it’s the best part, the person he handed her over to was a coven of witches! Ha! Is that not hilarious? You trusted him with her and, first sign of danger, he tossed her over to witches!”

Kizu struggled, grasping at his now lipless face. There was a great deal of context missing from that statement.

“I don’t… believe you,” Kumiho said, between gasps of breath. “You said… seeing human.”

“Oh? You think I contradicted myself? Fair enough. Though our past guest said the same, you remember that? Wait, hm, that’s right. We concussed you a few times as a game. Your memory might be a bit jumbled. Oh well! But know this, my Kitsune friend, I don’t lie. There’s no contradiction there. Though, thinking back to my days in the jungle, I doubt my coven would have allowed their girls to date around. I think it happened once and both parties were eaten! Hope little Mae avoids that fate. Though, is it truly even cannibalism if you’re only one fourth the same species?”

The other Kizu’s voice lowered and his eyes glazed over as he muttered, debating with himself about the definition of cannibalism. After pacing back and forth for a minute, he stomped away without so much as a ‘goodbye.’

A few minutes later, his hex on Kizu faded and his mouth reformed. He flexed his lips and popped his jaw to the side, relishing the motions.

“I didn’t sell her out,” Kizu said softly. “And she’s fine. Please, trust me. My friend is keeping her safe. No harm will come to her. You have my word.”

“A… witch friend?”

“Yes,” Kizu admitted. “But she’s a witch from Tross, not Hon. They have completely different cultures and how they treat their apprentices is night and day different.”

“I know… them. Trust… you.”

Kizu blinked. He supposed that it made sense she had dealings with the Tross coven. Kumiho was older than her normal human appearance let on and she’d have traveled far and wide across Tross in her journeys. Encountering witches during her travels was likely inevitable. If she trusted him, then her experiences must not have been too terrible.

“Good. Then you can trust me that I’ll get us out of here as well.”

Kizu had escaped Ototchi’s lair half a year earlier. Now, with all the experience and spells he’d learned, he should be more than capable of repeating the feat.
