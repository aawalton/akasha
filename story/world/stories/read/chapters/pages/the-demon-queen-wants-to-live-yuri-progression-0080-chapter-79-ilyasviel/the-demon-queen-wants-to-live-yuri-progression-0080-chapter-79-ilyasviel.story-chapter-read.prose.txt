We walked in darkness. Mostly darkness. The lanterns still burned in their long warm lines above the streets. The music grew dimmer with each turn we took. There were no children now. The few people who remained walked unsteadily and with their heads down. One slouched against a wall. One climbed through the window of a building, rather than take the open door just beside it.

"Do you know where we are going, Hero?" I asked.

"Yep." Ash walked beside me, her hand in mine. "I know the way."

I had been following the Hero's lead since we left the stream. I had followed it in the Academy, to reach our classes. I had not questioned it then. I questioned it now, because I was curious. "And just how do you know the way," I said, "when we have only come this way once?"

"Hmm?" Ash turned to me. "It's not that interesting. I was just memorizing all the turns we took since we left the Academy. So I can just reverse all of them and end up back there." She shrugged. "Besides. I bet we'll see it once we get close enough anyway."

I considered her answer. I had thought it was some secret skill the Hero had been keeping from me, the same way she usually slept before I did. It turned out the answer was somehow more simple and more impressive than I had imagined. She had been building a map in her mind all this while. "That is a very useful skill, Ash."

"They all used to say the same thing," Ash said softly. "You need at least someone in a party who knows directions or things get out of hand very fast. The rest of them were hopeless. Beyond hopeless, even. We somehow got lost even with spells meant to tell the direction. I got tired of always getting lost inside dungeons."

I said nothing to that. We walked in silence, and the loudest sound around us was the sound of our own feet. Solara was sleeping between my horns soundly. I had not felt her shift even once. "Since we're on the subject," Ash said after a time, "I'm curious how you can walk and not drop her." She looked up at my horns. "Since she's asleep, I mean."

"It is no great skill." I kept my stride even, as I had been keeping it. "One has only to keep their back straight and their steps measured. I have had many centuries to practice this skill, Hero."

My race was one that valued strength. From their Queen most of all. One who could not put on a proper performance would never last long.

"I thought that was just who you were," Ash said.

"It is who I am now." I looked ahead at the emptying street. "A Queen who stumbles is not a Queen who inspires confidence."

We turned a corner, stepping beside a human who was asleep in the center of the road. Ash only spoke several steps later.  "If you ever do want to stumble a little, you can lean against me. If you ever feel like it. I'm pretty sturdy, you know. I won't mind," the Hero said brightly, as if it were a simple offer to make.

Several objections came to me. I voiced none of them. "I will remember that, Ash."

Eventually, the low hum of the music disappeared entirely. We turned several corners, and did not see anyone at all. The crowds had certainly gotten thinner as we had walked, but here there were no people at all. Not a single soul.

"Ash," I said.

"I know."

Ash took a step away from me. I surveyed the street ahead and the road behind. We were alone-

There was a certain weight that prickled at my back. The exact kind of weight I had felt before this night. Back then, I had looked around and found nothing. Solara let out a small chirp from my head. From her, I felt the same note of danger I had before.

"Oh? So you did notice. A little faster than I had guessed you would." The voice came from above us, and it was soft, and smooth, and very silky. "Perhaps the two of you are not complete fools after all."

We both looked up, and saw her seated at the edge of a rooftop. The figure was red-skinned, with horns that pointed straight up toward the sky. I knew who she was at a glance.

Ilyasviel rose. She stepped off the edge of the roof and landed on the ground with hardly a sound.

Ash stepped in front of me. Sol woke fully and loosed a mighty chirp, her four wings flaring wide above my horns.

"So," Ilyasviel said. Her gaze crawled over me. "You are the one who calls herself Lysanthia."

My gaze went from her to her arm. In the Arena, I had only managed to see its color. I saw more now. The Crest was in the form of a large snake, with several heads. A hydra. I counted its heads, though some part of me already knew what the counting would find. Twelve. There were twelve heads. Something stirred in my chest. I crushed it. Now was not the time.

"I am," I said. "And you are Ilyasviel. Some name you the strongest in this Academy. Here I had thought the strongest would walk openly and proudly. Not slink across rooftops to watch from a distance."

Her expression scarcely changed. There was the smallest twitch at the corner of her mouth, there and gone. "I had not believed it," she said. She began to walk toward us. "I admit, I was curious. A half-demon who would openly call herself that name. Who would take up the name of the great mother, and wear it in the light."

The great mother. No one had ever called me that. In my age there had been no such thing to call me, for the Class had not permitted the concept at all. It seemed a thousand years was long enough for a myth to grow in strange directions.

"Even I would not dare take that name." She stopped a few paces off. She looked at my forearms, at the black running down the right and the white running down the left. "Then I saw your marks. You somehow have two Sovereigns. And you were hiding them. No doubt with help from Valtheris himself." Ilyasviel folded her arms across her chest. The gesture was familiar. "So the luck has gone to your head. And now you believe you can call yourself that." Her own head tilted. She looked from me to Ash then back at me. "Are you going to keep hiding behind your woman?"

I knew a challenge when I saw one. "Ash," I said. "Step aside."

Ash looked back at me. "But-"

"Ash." My voice was firmer. She looked into my eyes and, slowly, she nodded. She held out her hands, and I lifted Sol from my horns and she twisted in my grip, and through the bond came a spike of protest so sharp it needed no words at all. Ash looked at me and took Sol in both hands. Sol's chirping turned furious, and Ash's eyes did not leave mine.

"Be careful," Ash murmured, and stepped aside. That left the two of us facing one another, a half dozen feet apart.

"So why have you come here?" I asked. "You have come a long way down from your roof. State your purpose."

"My purpose." Ilyasviel tilted her head. The white Crest caught the light. "I came here to take away your name, and the pride that lets you walk around freely with it."

We were of the same height, or near enough that it made no difference. The half-demon's eyes were a red brighter than her skin. Dimmer than mine. I folded my hands and looked into those red eyes. "It is my name," I said. "And I will not part with it."

"Then let us see if you can actually bear to stand under the weight of it."

Ilyasviel took a breath, and Essence filled the air and flooded the square. My legs buckled and I went down to one knee. I heard Ash gasp. I turned enough to see her on both knees with Sol pressed flat against her golden head.

I had been forced to bend like this before. The Archon had done it with the power of his Sovereign mark, one that related to gravity. Right now I was bending under only the weight of Essence, so much of it that it felt like a hand pressed against my back. It was akin to being inside a resonance chamber. One far harsher than the one I had been given to prepare for the duel.

In this world, all Essence had a taste. My Requiem tasted of Endings. My Cradle tasted of Beginning. What I felt from this woman tasted like a thing that had been, was, and always would be. A thing that could be burnt, torn, shattered, or slashed, and would only return stronger than before.

"This is just my Essence. Your legs cannot even carry you under this," Ilyasviel said. She had not moved. "And yet you would carry a name that once carried the world." She tilted her head, slowly. "They say all of us half-demons have something wrong with us. That rare is the one who knows her place. Even so. I have never heard of one fool enough to walk about wearing the great mother's name. Perhaps the demonbloods you walk around with say nothing. I will."

The indignity of this was considerable. If the half-demon thought that this would be enough to keep me off my feet, then she was wrong. I gritted my teeth and reached into the Ember Core. The reaching brought with it pain, but also Essence, and I forced all of that Essence into my limbs. I stood again, staggering forward a half-step. I stayed standing.

"You speak of the weight of that name as if you knew just how heavy it was. You do not know what you claim to, girl," I said.

"I see. So you are that far gone," she whispered. The Essence flooding the square retreated. Behind me Ash drew several deep breaths and pushed herself unsteadily to her feet. "Very well, then," Ilyasviel said. "It seems a simple warning will be insufficient."

Her mark glowed. The half-demon extended her right hand toward me. The hand grew and swelled, until it was twice as large as it had been. Then it split with a wet sound, even as the flesh turned to white scale. Where once had been an arm were three great hydra heads, each the size of a wolf.

"Lys-" Ash said.

"Do not, Ash," I said, turning to see her. The Hero's hands were both clenched tight. Her left arm was half risen, as if she meant to use Parting here and now. She slowly forced it back down. Solara's four wings were flaring, and her beak was half-open. From the bond, I felt the tiny phoenix's fury. Ash's right arm gently closed around Sol, and they both stayed where they were.

The three heads rushed for me together. I darted back from the first as it smashed into the ground where I had been. The second followed my leap back. I extended my right hand and hurled Ruin at it. The entire head was swallowed in the black flame. It kept moving for one half-second, and became nothing in the next. The third head struck me from my blind spot. It drove me into a wall so hard I might have gone through it, and I felt one of my ribs give.

I pushed myself off the stone. And as I watched, the head I had unmade hissed and reformed until it was exactly as it had been before. I stared at it.

This was the second time something had met my Requiem and survived. The first time, the corruption had pushed through the Ruin to try and reach me. That had been a surprise, but it was easier to accept than what I saw now. My Ruin had brought this head an ending. It had simply come back from it, somehow.

This Ilyasviel was a hydra in more than just the form of her mark.

If I could not use the Requiem, then I had few options. Only one that was worth considering, in truth. Turn came to mind. If Ilyasviel's power could withstand being unmade, then it would be wiser to turn it into something else instead. It was a comforting thought. One I abandoned immediately.

Turn was far harder to use than either Bloom or Ruin. I had to use precise quantities of both marks, with complete focus. Even under the most ideal of conditions I had managed it one firing in three. And even when I did succeed, Turn required a level of thought that Ruin did not. Yes, a weapon I did not know would only serve to get me killed. Ruin was the weapon my hands knew. And Ilyasviel stood exactly where she had begun.

I leapt. As I rose I loosed the Ruin down at her, and three heads rose to block it and ceased. They returned from the stumps before I had finished falling.

"You cannot dodge mid-jump." Her voice was almost bored. "I would hope someone calling herself Lysanthia would know that much at least."

She was right. Once, I had never needed to land at all. [Flight] had gone the way of the System, and I had not missed it until precisely that moment.

The heads were getting closer. I had only seconds before they reached me in the air. I took a deep breath and made my choice. I fired more Ruin, but not in a blast, and not aimed at any of the heads. This time, I used a single coiling whip of black, sending it between the three heads. I aimed along a line drawn in my mind rather than with my eyes. Then one of the heads struck me and knocked me from the sky. The hydra's head filled my vision. Its eyes were an endless white.

I fell back down to the ground in a heap. Pain came, and I ignored it. I rose quickly, ready to dodge an attack that would surely come. None did.

I raised my head. Ilyasviel stood with one hand pressed to her face, and where the whip of Ruin had passed it had opened her right cheek clean to the bone. Blood ran down the side of her jaw. "How odd," she said. "I actually felt it."

The half-demon said the words strangely, with genuine wonder. I recognized it, for I had heard it once before, from a general who wielded four greatswords at the same time. It was the voice of one who has not been reached in a very long time.

I had made my calculation to take the woman's head with my Requiem, not just scar her face. How strange. If I had missed, then from the angle of my attack, I should have missed her entirely, rather than merely scar her. Ah. So that was it.

She was not standing where she had been. She had taken one single step to dodge, and so a blow that might have done far more had only scarred her.

Blood dripped from her cheek and onto the cobblestones. I had expected the wound to close immediately, just as those hydra heads had reformed instantly after my Ruin. It did not. Ilyasviel tilted her head, and more blood fell. Then she stabbed her nails into her own wound, grunted once, and tore the ruined flesh away from her face and cast it aside. Only then, in a single instant, was she whole again, the cheek smooth and red as though nothing had ever touched it.

I had already guessed from the heads themselves, but now I was sure. Her strange mark granted her the power of restoration. A strange restoration that was not like the Beginning, though perhaps it was its cousin.

"What manner of power is that?" I demanded.

"I should ask you the same." The three heads sank back into their length, and soon her arm was only an arm again. She looked at me, and something in the look had changed. "You are not terrible," she said. "Better than any Line has a right to be. I can see now why that fool girl lost."

It was faint praise, meant to sound like anything else. I had given compliments like that often.

The half-demon whirled and took several steps. She almost reached the corner before she paused and looked back at me over her shoulder. "But not terrible is all you are. There is only one person in this world who deserves to carry the name Lysanthia. And it is not you."

I had heard my name said before in many different ways. Sometimes in reverence. Often in fear. I had rarely heard it said like this.

Ilyasviel turned a corner, and she was gone. And as she went the sound of the world came flooding back in her wake. Ash rushed to my side. I reached up to my ribs with the Cradle, and the broken ones ground back into place. The pain made me wince. "Lys! Are you okay?" she said.

"I am well enough, Hero."

I was already standing well enough on my own, but the Hero steadied me with her arms anyway. I let her. If I leaned forward against her just slightly, then it was a betrayal of the body, nothing more. Solara let out a large chirp from atop Ash's head and flew to mine with only a small sway.  I looked up at her. Through the bond came her hot anger and it was oddly pointed at herself. She was furious that she had not done more. Even as a bird smaller than a soup bowl.

"You held yourself back," I told her. "You are clever, when you choose to be. It is no failing of yours to not charge into a hopeless fight." She squawked at me. I smiled.

We both looked at the corner Ilyasviel had gone around. "It seems that my name means a great many things to a great many people," I said.

"I guess so," Ash said, reaching for my hand. "Let's go back. Before something else happens." Her voice was firm. I did not know why. I had no intention of doing anything else.

"Indeed." I took it, and we began to walk. "You know, Ash," I said, almost absently.
