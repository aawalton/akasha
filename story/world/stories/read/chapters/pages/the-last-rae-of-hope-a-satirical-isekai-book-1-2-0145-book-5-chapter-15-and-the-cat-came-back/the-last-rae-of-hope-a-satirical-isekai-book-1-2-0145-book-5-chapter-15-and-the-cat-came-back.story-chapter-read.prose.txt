With an unstable flicker, Lieutenant Beigaldi—or perhaps just Beigaldi now—stared unnervingly at me.

“Partner…? Even after what her church has condoned?!”

“Not my church,” I snapped, gingerly pressing at my nose only to be rewarded with a worse headache. “I disavow any relationship declared or implied!”

“So then you’re here to kill her, is that it?!”

“Don’t bother answering him,” Nora advised as she came over. “Sit down and let me fix your face first.”

“How long do you think we have until she comes back?” I asked as I sat, still feeling the demon’s accusatory glare burn into me from behind.

“Galenus calculates about two hours, but we’re not sure of her maximum flight speed,” she replied. “Ugh. Your nose is out of alignment. Didn’t I tell you to be careful?!”

“She wasn’t reacting to me… until she did,” I objected.

Nora stared at me long enough for the stupidity of my statement to sink in.

“Now hold still and pray you don’t need rhinoplasty.”

“Wait—what are you—”

She grabbed my nose with an iridescently glowing hand and pulled.

“Gaaaah!” I shouted, then gasped as my sinuses suddenly cleared. “What…?”

“Magically enhanced manual realignment and healing,” she explained after the fact, utterly unapologetic. “Not too shabby, if I do say so myself.”

I rubbed at my face, carefully at first. “It… feels like normal!”

“Just what were you expecting? Also, don’t let it happen again,” she muttered.

“Yes, ma’am,” I replied as I stood up. “Now, back to your question, Beigaldi. We weren’t here on a mission to kill Bodhil. We’re here looking for clues to where Mistress Aziza is being held. Do you have any leads?”

Beigaldi folded his arms.

“Answer her,” Olethros said as a dark aura formed around him.

He flinched, then stared openly at each of us. “You mean… You don’t know? She’s been imprisoned in Chairo!”

“You’re certain of this?!” Ambrose demanded.

Beigaldi looked at him with undisguised repugnance. “I was there when Landon issued the orders, human.”

“Just as we feared…” Galenus said with an exhale.

Olethros’s eyes opened slightly as he straightened, looking up at the sky. “We can discuss this more once we’re back at base.”

I glanced up at Paradise as well.

Did Relias purposefully send Bodhil here to test us?

What if he’s watching us at this very moment?

The thought filled me with an icy fear, and for a fleeting moment, I felt a sharp pain shoot through my neck.

It’s not real.

I’m still alive.

So if you are watching…

“Fuck you, Relias!”

The single gesture wasn’t enough. I flipped the double deuce skyward with frantic waves of my arms. “Fuck everything about you!”

No one, not even Beigaldi, felt the need to tell me I was being ridiculous. I’ll admit, even in my weakened state, I could see wisps of animus dissolve from my hands as I continued my cathartic release.

It ain’t much, but it’s honest work.

Even the sun seemed to share my sentiment, glowing a bit brighter afterward.

“Okay,” I said, composing myself. “I imagine we’re all done here, then? The original animus we sensed was him, right? So there’s nothing else—huh?”

The sun was expanding rapidly in the sky. Its golden edges flared before the orb abruptly deviated, revealing itself to be separate from the celestial body I’d mistaken it for. We all spread out instinctively as it locked onto me.

Oh, fuck you too, karma!

The golden ball slowed on its final approach, suggesting at the last second that it wasn’t the attack from on high I’d feared. It hovered, a tiny silhouette encased within, before popping like a bubble.

“Chester?!” Nora shrieked as a package-laden cat landed neatly on its feet.

“Chester…?” I echoed, dumbfounded.

Nora and I approached slowly.

“Mrow?” he asked, dropping his small box at Nora’s feet.

That’s Chester’s call, all right.

“No, we don’t have any snacks!” Nora scolded. “How could you even think we would—how is it that I understand you?!”

“Because he always wants snacks… but why’d they make you come here…” I murmured as I scooped him up. “You poor thing… You deserve so much better.”

His unfortunate fate did not preclude me from burying my face in his fur.

Olethros reformed beside me. “Is that your name now? Chester?”

I clutched him protectively. “What are you—No!”

Chester nudged my chin insistently. Horrified, I held him out at arm’s length.

“Say it isn’t so, Chester!”

He flicked his tail, clearly offended by the lack of proper support.

I shook him once. “You’re just a cat, right?”

“Mrow.”

Olethros’s eyes widened as an errant drop of sweat trickled from his brow. “I suppose one could make that argument…”

“What argument?”

“Allow me to translate,” he said before clearing his throat. “If a sentient being not only realizes but permanently achieves its perfect form—both spiritually and physically—can it not be called a cat? However, I respectfully object to your usage of the word ‘just.’ It has long been my conclusion that cats were blessed with perfection from the moment of their creation. I came to accept this as fact during my early experiments under Aziza’s command, and I would ask that, as my assistant, you not only recognize my discovery but support it in all of our future endeavors together.”

I brought Chester up to eye level. “Perfect form… Assistant? You said all that with a single mrow?!”

Chester turned away toward Nora. “Mew.”

Nora swallowed hard and reached for the ribboned box. “Yes… I’ll be sure to read the note inside.”

I stared at her, then at Olethros, then at the cat in my hands. “You mean… He’s really…”

No one around me is who I thought they were.

I set Chester down and turned away before I could say anything incriminating. “Let’s just go back.”

While everyone else, demons and humans alike, understood that I wasn’t in the mood for company, Chester followed me through several random mirror portals, determined that he needed to trot between each step of my feet.

I stopped in one of the wallpapered hallways to strip out of my armor. “Don’t you want to go explore your old haunt or something?”

He sat on my right foot and stared up at me.

“I know it’s me with the problem,” I admitted quietly. “But you’re going to have to give me some time, alright?”

He rubbed his face against my leg several times. Then he stood up, stretched, and walked toward another mirror at the end of the hallway. Before he stepped through, however, he sat and stared expectantly.

“Mrrow.”

“Alright, alright, but I’m warning you, if you’re just leading me to some magical pantry…”

Actually, would that be so bad?

I shrugged and followed him through before I could overthink it.

It was my old bedroom.

Or more accurately, a more-than-reasonable facsimile. The wood paneling was still unevenly spaced, their squiggly knots in all the right places. My books were still in uneven stacks on my nightstand, and there, in the center of my bed, was my soft pink faux-mink blanket, swirled up invitingly.

Chester hopped up into its curl, kneaded at it twice, then circled and flopped down with a satisfied purr.

“Alright… I’ll hear you out.”

I sat beside him, then leaned back to stare at the drop ceiling.

“Huh. You even got the glow stickers right. I thought for sure some of them would have fallen by now, though.”

Without announcement, he climbed onto my stomach, twisted into a warm, vibrating loaf, and closed his eyes.

His same weight and warmth were really all I needed.

“Okay, point taken,” I murmured, running my fingers through his fur under his left ear. “You’re still Chester.”

He purred louder.

“And I don’t care if you understand me more than I can understand you. I don’t care that you could talk—you don’t have to. Honestly, I’m not sure that I want you to.”

Chester’s tail flicked.

“Well, I’m sure humans seem even more ridiculous to cats—I just don’t need to be reminded of that, right? So, you can be a cat, and I remain blissfully unaware. That’s the deal, right?”

He shifted and stretched before settling again, all the while radiating his smug approval.

“Oh, but there’s one thing, though.”

He stiffened slightly.

“You knew Olethros… before me, right?”

His ears flattened.

“What do you think of him? I mean… generally speaking. I’m just—”

Chester abruptly launched himself off my chest, tail poofing as he ran out the bedroom door.

“Oh, come on!” I groaned, scrambling up. “It was just one question!”

With a sigh, I went through the door after him.

Beigaldi was there in the next room, throwing his arms wide toward Olethros. “Your Majesty, she cannot be trusted!”

Olethros turned his head slightly, then smiled.

“Uh…” I started, taking a step back. “Looks like I was interrupting something... I’ll just—?”

He moved forward without a word, lowering his head.

“…You want me to—”

Slowly, I lifted my hand and patted Olethros on the head exactly three times.

Beigaldi’s color drained as he flickered. “I… This… You’re insane! She could have—!”

He fled without finishing his thought.

“My goodness,” I muttered in bafflement. “You would have thought I was holding your hand or something.”

Olethros suddenly grinned. “Head-pats are a traditional sign of absolute trust among demons. Allowing one is… not done lightly. It means I trust you not to absorb or harm my core.”

I frowned. “Not all demon cores are in the head, though, right?”

He straightened up, running his fingers through his hair. “The more skilled ones, like me, move them around as a defense, but that does complicate matters. Following the form of vertebrates is usually the most efficient, especially given the trouble eyes cause us in particular.”

“Huh, the more you know…”

“I would not dare return the gesture,” he continued smoothly, “as I suspect you might interpret it as diminutive.” His gaze lingered, and I was suddenly filled with the urge to cover my face. “However, should you desire a physical affirmation of the trust I place in you, you need only to ask.”

“Uh…”

He leaned forward. “For example—”

“Stop whatever you’re doing!” Nora shouted as she burst through a side mirror, waving a cellphone. “It’s time to get the party back together for real!”

If looks could kill, there might not have been anyone left to tell the rest of the story.
