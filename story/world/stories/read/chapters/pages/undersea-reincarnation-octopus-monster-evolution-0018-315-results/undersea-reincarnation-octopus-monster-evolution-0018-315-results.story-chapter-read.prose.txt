Looking at the reduced list, Henry nodded appreciatively. He still had plenty he could combine and fuse, but it could wait. What remained didn’t block him from moving on to the next rank, both in his levels and in his Aspects, and he could chip at them as time went on, but for now, he was pretty happy with what he’d achieved.

He’d ranked up and leveled plenty of his skills through the constant battle over the last few days. He’d fused Draining Touch and Lifebane Touch into Siphoning Touch. Invisibility was now part of Trickster’s Camouflage, which should make it not as easy to see through, especially considering Trickster’s Camouflage hid his mana and presence as well. He had merged Control Water into Trickster’s Domain, and managed to organize most of his telepathic capabilities under Mind Weaver’s Tongue, with Mind Shield included. Even Arcane Arm and Echo Strike had become something new. He pulled the definition for that one and re-read it.

Echoing Arm (D):

Summon an arcane limb that can be used for both precise manipulation and powerful strikes. When willed during an attack, the limb, just like your other arms, can release a delayed echo, an afterimage made of force to extend your reach and impact.

“Should be pretty useful,” he thought as he finally arrived at the arena, dismissing his interface and looking up at the re-modeled grounds of the festive venue. Once more, the bleachers and the massive branches that held them had been pushed further away, while the central area had been massively stretched to be nearly as wide and long as to cover the whole forest that carried the city. It must have taken Zerathstra a great deal of effort, Henry thought as he glanced down at his feet. The wooden grounds seemed pretty stable, which made sense, considering the massive branches supporting the platform he’d noted on the way here.

Good thing he’d included some rampart, Henry noted with a smile as he took in the mood of the celebration. He could already see a couple of folks with flushed faces and drink in hand. No one seemed to be too bothered about the delayed B-rank tournament. With the wave having come earlier than expected, the event had been officially pushed back earlier. Though at this point, Henry didn’t think he cared much for it.

Sure, he’d be passing up on a couple of prizes, but unless he limited himself severely, he didn’t see how he could lose and how anyone would wager anything of import against him. Especially once he officially stepped into B-rank. It’d be a waste of his time. After all, his strengths were built around his own skills and capabilities, and to improve those, nothing was as good and efficient as heading out in the ocean and finding a few cool opponents. And he just knew the one he wanted to start with, as soon as the wave was passed.

Once the wave was passed, Henry was planning on going to say hi to that blue lobster and see if it’s interested in a little spar. His first serious fight against an A-rank. Though he better be ready to run. While he felt confident against most B-rank, the A-ranks still spooked him a little. The gap between B and A-rank was a little wider than that between C and B.

It might be worth asking Velistraine for a spar soon, Henry mused as he scanned the crowd. See how I stack against her…

Quickly enough, Henry found his quarry, and he headed toward them. Now he just needed to find a drink.

In a bubble of saltwater floating a few feet off the ground, Maurice, perched on top of Rocky’s head, felt the kraken glance around for a moment before he focused back on the scene in front of them. Both of them watched, fascinated, as the bartender mixed the drinks for Henry and the others and by now, Maurice was feeling he could maybe come up with something cool like what the bartender was whipping up.

Her name was Xeri, Maurice believed he had heard. She was wearing black, sleek and sleeveless robes that matched her short hair and right now, she was placing pieces of cut pineapple in a jar-like thing before she covered it with a lid and channeled her mana through it for a couple of seconds. There was a soft hum, and Maurice was almost sure he could feel some air-concepts emanating out of the jar. When the process was done, she removed the lid and titled out the jar, and out came pouring a smooth liquid that she guided into one of the four cups, which she then mixed with the white, milky-looking liquor already within with a long wooden spoon. She did the same for the remaining cups, and once they were full to the rim, she lodged a little slice of pineapple on the edge of the nicely carved cups.

Maurice eyed the jar she used to turn the fruits into juice. He had to get himself one of those, he thought, then as they carried the cups to the table with a deft application of Telekinesis, Maurice clicked his claws, glancing back at the bar. “I could do it,” he said once Ash and Fabian joined them with the drinks the rest of the crew had requested.

“No, you can’t,” stubbornly repeated Ash. “You don’t even know the first thing about mixing drinks.”

Maurice waited until all the drinks were served and until they were seated once more to turn and face the girl.

“What?”

Maurice just rubbed his pincers, amused. “I. Can. You’re just mad you two still can’t beat me.”

Ash pursed her lips then shot Fabian a glare at the snort he’d just let out, while Henry was too busy downing the drink he’d just gotten and before Maurice could rub it in, a spray of water hit him on the back of the shell and made him flinch, followed by a severe click. “Don’t be a bad sport, Maurice,” chided Stormsong, her voice echoing out of the amulet tightly wrapped around her right flipper.

A minute later, they all got up and went to see more of the celebration, leaving the ‘adults’ as Fabian had called them to their talks and drinks. There was much to see and to sample in this little festival, and Maurice wasn’t learning much new. He’d already heard the stories of Henry’s old world, and he didn’t need to sit through Velistraine’s interrogation to hear the same stuff all over again. Though he still wanted to listen, just in case something he didn’t know got brought up. But he didn’t need to stay. After all, his personal kraken-clone was transmitting him the conversation, which allowed Maurice to do other stuff while he listened.

When Maurice had asked for that, Henry had called it a pod-cast or something like it. Maurice didn’t know what those were, but it seemed like something he’d like.

Maybe one day we’ll be able to make the trip. I’d love to see some of the stuff Henry had talked about, Maurice thought to himself for a moment before he turned his attention back to their argument. “I still think I can mix drinks like the nice lady can! Maybe not as good as her, but I can do it!”

Ash crossed her arms and gave him a challenging sneer, coming to a stop in front of a grilled squid stand that both Stormsong and Fabian showed interest in. “Prove it, then. Me and you. The best drink wins.”

Maurice was about to retort when an arm tapped him on the shell. Looking down, he found Rocky staring up at him. The kraken wringed his two frontal arms and glanced past them at the bar that was only a few yards away. “[I want to mix the drinks, too.]”

Maurice chuckled and tapped the kraken on the head before he pointed a challenging claw at Ash. “I’m in! Rocky’s participating too! Let’s see who’s the best drink mixer of us three. It’s going to be fun. But what do we wager?”

Maurice had been enjoying these competitions. It kept him motivated.

Ash frowned and pulled her satchel in front of her before she started digging into it. Maurice did the same, mentally eying the content of Hermit’s Hoard. He was sure he’d find something worth the wager, though, to be honest, he cared more about the win in itself than the prize.

A few minutes later, each one had their wager up on the free table they managed to sequester away. Maurice had a small pile of teal-colored scales he’d cut away from a B-rank drake, Rocky had a little pile of gold-veined rocks and other interesting looking minerals, and Ash, a little flustered, had put up a book about known and documented monsters which mightily interested him and with word of this improvised competition having spread, they now had a small crowd.

“You have fifteen minutes,” said Stormsong as Fabian put down a single bottle of whitish liquor on the table. Both had decided to be the referees, and Fabian spoke next. “You are all to use this liquor to make the best tasting drink possible. You can go fetch whatever ingredients you want, but this is the base. Ready? Go!”

Still on Rocky’s head, both of them sprang forth to the cheer of the small crowd of mostly teenagers, making Maurice wonder for a second why so many of them were volunteering to be tasters.

It must be a human thing, he decided after a moment before he shelved the matter away. He had a competition to win!
