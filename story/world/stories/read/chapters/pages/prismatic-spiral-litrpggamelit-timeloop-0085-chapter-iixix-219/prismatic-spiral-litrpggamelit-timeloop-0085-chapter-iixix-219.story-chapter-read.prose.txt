Chapter II.XIX (2.19)

“This spell advancement will allow me to reset my memories to a different point of time?” Mitsuko asked.

Juniper, back in the body of a gull, nodded her head at the assessment. “This is not my spell and I will not pretend to know the nuances of its full capabilities. However, my understanding is that, yes, this will let you rewind your mind to a different point. It is a very niche ability and I never saw Escara use it beyond the practice fields.”

“You never had to deal with mind mages,” Mitsuko surmised. To her, this aspect of the spell seemed absolutely invaluable. In fact, she believed this was the most useful part of Retrospection so far. It allowed her to worry a great deal less about mental tamperings. “How do I activate it?”

“Look out at the sea and cast it now,” Juniper instructed.

Mitsuko did as commanded. A blink of her eye the waves completely randomized in front of her and the distant islands shifted on the horizon. Even Juniper’s position on the gunwales moved over to the left.

“Did I just…leap forward in time?” Mitsuko asked, checking over her shoulders. The sailors of The Selcouth Sable continued to work. Perhaps if she’d been paying attention she would have noticed a difference in their positions.

“You set up a moment and then your memories reverted to it. You can either activate that manually, as you did just now, or it will automatically send you back if your mind gets tampered with.”

“Like a short version of what the Prismatic Spiral does if my mind becomes compromised,” Mitsuko realized.

“Exactly so, Poppet!” Juniper spread her wings and nodded her head.

Mitsuko pondered on that for a little while staring out at the sea. “Are aspects of your spells incorporated into the Prismatic Spiral?”

A pause followed.

“That was meant to be one of my questions,” Mitsuko clarified.

Juniper perked up. “Yes. Aspects of all the spells exist within the Prismatic Spiral. Our combined power, knowledge, and souls are what made this all possible, so incorporating our individual talents was not a difficult hurdle. However, using those abilities outside of their intended purposes is impossible. Our souls are bound by specific pacts to make this all a reality. While you likely see the Prismatic Spiral as a marvel of magic second to none, it actually is far greater than you comprehend. It required decades of planning and arrangements. I am afraid if you want the exact minutiae of how it functions, I am not the right sage to ask. I forfeited my power and soul into the mold but did not personally construct the mold.”

That implied that a different sage had constructed the mold. Mitsuko didn’t think it was Sterling, if for no other reason than his dismissal of other spells and his lack of knowledge on the soul magic that doubtlessly had been required. Escara also seemed extremely unlikely. Which meant it was likely one of the sages she’d yet to encounter.

“Explain Deteriorate Self to me,” Mitsuko said, shifting the subject from the Prismatic Spiral over to Juniper’s specific spell.

“What do you believe it does?” Juniper asked.

“The opposite of Mend Self. It makes my body experience time rapidly. But how does it actually work? If I stand here, does it take fate into account and make me experience what I hypothetically might have? Or does it take the very moment of existence and exacerbate it?”

“Usually the latter. However, your will does factor into the equation. Why don’t you cast it right now?”

Mitsuko did. Over the next few seconds, she felt her hunger rapidly grow, thirst drying out her mouth, and her body go weak with weariness. Slumped against the gunwales, Mitsuko forced her eyes to stay open. She felt pathetic. Not even able to hold herself together. The yawning abyss of sleep called for her, beckoning her to embarrass the nothingness beyond consciousness.

Mitsuko cast Mend Self and her body stiffened back into alert wakefulness.

“That was unpleasant,” Mitsuko commented. She stretched her arms over her head, as if waking from a long nap. “I don’t have the faintest idea how to use that effectively to my advantage.”

“None at all?”

Mitsuko considered the question. “If I’m jogging and I cast the spell, will it exacerbate that action? Will I gain further muscle mass from it?”

“Most likely, yes!” Juniper chirped. “You’re quite clever. Muscle is one shortcut to be gained through my spell. Though be warned that the hunger and weariness also persist with this method of usage. As too does slight aging.”

And the only ways to recover from that were to either sleep, which would waste precious time in the loop, or Mend her body, which would revert her back to where she’d been before exercising.

“This is really not a very helpful ability.”

“It also can help quickly stanch bleeding and assist recovery times after taking wounds,” Juniper added defensively.

“Which Mend makes obsolete.”

The bird hopped off the gunwales and waddled away in a huff, muttering to itself.

“All these sages sure are defensive about their spells,” Mitsuko commented to no one in particular.

“What? Are you talking to me?” Hideo, the useless father, pointed a finger at himself. Apparently he’d been lurking about within eavesdropping distance.

“Why would I be talking to you?”

“I don’t know,” he replied defensively. “It’s not like there’s anyone else around.”

Mitsuko shook her head in exasperation then walked away. Everyone else on the ship kept away from her. After her display of magic and power against the kraken at the start of the loop, the other passengers remained extremely wary of her. Hideo was either too dumb to comprehend or incredibly bold to approach her. Judging by his pathetic fathering skills, Mitsuko believed the former over the latter to be applicable.

Once they arrived on Mauve, Mitsuko needed to decide whether or not to take the direct route to Porcelain Island or the indirect route. One gambled time, the other offered safety.

She watched the sailors for a minute. A direct course to the island would risk their lives as well. Was she really so callous as to put their lives on the line? She doubted the captain would be convinced to depart on the short route if he knew of the existence of the abomination that was that eye whale. So she would be forced to keep it to herself.

The answer was yes. It made her feel guilty and she liked to believe that if she wasn’t in the loop she would choose the safe option, but as it was, she’d risk their lives if it meant getting her to the island faster.

With a heavy heart, Mitsuko made up her mind. Getting to the island before it all melted was essential if she intended to make any meaningful progress. Tomorrow she would face off against the sea monster and the crusts.

The worst part was that this decision meant keeping this detail of the loop from Holly. It felt like a betrayal of trust, but Holly would push back if it meant the lives of innocent sailors.

“Am I a terrible person?” she muttered to herself. Rationally, risking these temporary lives would only serve to assist in saving the permanent ones at the end of the Prismatic Spiral. And yet… it still felt super shitty.
