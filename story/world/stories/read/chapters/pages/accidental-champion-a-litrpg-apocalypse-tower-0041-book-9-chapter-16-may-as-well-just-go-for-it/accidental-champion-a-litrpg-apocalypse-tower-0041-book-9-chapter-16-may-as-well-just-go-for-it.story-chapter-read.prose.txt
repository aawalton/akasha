Xavier returned to Earth.

A lot of things had changed since he’d left.

For the universe at large, barely three hours had passed. For him, a couple of years had gone by inside his time dilation field, when he’d finally had access to it again, in Arrack’s base.

But for the worlds under his rule, and for the people closest to him, decades had gone by.

He’d never anticipated what it would feel like to be on the other side of this. Usually, far more time would pass for him than anyone else. He might spend a decade in a time dilation field while barely a few moments passed for his found family and friends.

Now, the tables had turned.

All the things he needed to do burned inside him. His advancement to B Grade was a long time coming. Jhanku’s threat felt more imminent than ever. His sectors needed to continue to expand.

But what ached inside him more was how much he missed those closest to him. Though he was used to time away, after Palini’s death, that lack of connection hit harder than ever.

Adranial was the first person he’d contacted—even before Xavier Prime—after he’d escaped Arrack’s base. A quick message to say, I’m safe, and I’ll see you soon.

Now, he finally had a chance to catch up with her in the flesh.

He didn’t return to Collinsville—the city had expanded even more through the ancient forest it was housed within while he was gone, one day he wondered if it would become the larger of the two—instead, he arrived atop the mountain where he and the others had last trained together.

Xavier summoned his Roving Seed Base. He touched the grey standing stone, running his hand along its rough surface, before he pushed in and found himself standing on the grass.

This place, more than anywhere else ever had, felt like home to him.

Adranial arrived less than a minute later. She didn’t pause to speak. Didn’t stop to look him up and down. She simply ran forward and threw her arms around him, then held him by the shoulders. “You’re skinny. When was the last time you ate?”

He chuckled. “It’s been a long time.”

They spent a full month inside that base, under the strongest time dilation field Xavier could manage to hold. The time they shared was almost everything he’d thought about when he’d stood inside Larona’s bookshop.

He didn’t want to leave.

After that first month, he invited the others and soon found more things had changed than he’d imagined.

His friends had not remained idle while he was gone. Not in the least.

They hadn’t expanded Xavier’s empire—not without him—but they had pushed forward the development of every Denizen on every world as best they could.

The Ventorin sector had become as fully integrated as the Silver River sector. The next time Xavier needed to pull off the same stunt he’d used against the World Destroyer, he would have twice the power to pull from.

All those years, they hadn’t stopped training. Hadn’t stopped mastering the arts that he’d taught them.

And they hadn’t stopped living their lives, either.

Some of them had married.

Justin and Siobhan had children—adult children, no less. Not with one another, mind. That had full families and full lives.

Michael had gotten married. So had Rebecca. Howard and Kelly had been bugging the both of them for grandchildren.

So much had changed.

Xavier relished every minute of finding out about their lives. This was what he wanted for them—for everyone under his rule. He wanted to give them time. Wanted to give them freedom. Those under his rule had responsibilities—they needed to become stronger, to teach others, to govern and keep the peace. They needed to be there when he called upon their power as he had against the World Destroyer.

But the benefit of the time dilation fields wasn’t a singular one—it gave everyone an opportunity to keep living even with all that needed to be done.

It made him happy, meeting their partners, meeting their children, even if Xavier had never been much of a social animal.

It also made him feel more removed from them—in a blink of an eye, his found family had expanded, encompassing people he didn’t even know.

He had to fight his instinct to pull back and become more insular, to think they’ve drifted away, and so should I.

Instead, he made himself be as present as possible. And the whole time, he couldn’t help but look at Adranial and wonder what their future together might look like.

We still have so much time to figure all that out.

But he knew thoughts like that were only putting things on hold.

Seeing the children of his friends made him think of the spirit from the Otherworld he’d met. The man who was, in many universes, Xavier’s son.

Will he be again?

When it came time for him to tear himself away from this interval, this quiet escape from the pressure bearing down on him, he made a promise to himself that his empire and his grade weren’t the only things he would push forward.

The others all left, departing back to their own lives, assuring him they were always there when he needed to call upon them. Howard, Siobhan, and Justin lingered.

Howard clapped him on the back. “I’m sorry about Palini.”

They’d talked about what happened at the base. He’d held off on teaching them how to use the Immortal Soul lens. With all the powerful beings having their sights on Xavier, they agreed it was wise to keep a few of his unique powers to himself. While he would hate to imagine one of his friends being snatched, he couldn’t deny how possible it was.

And they wouldn’t have a mental construct to protect their memories from being stolen.

“Thank you, Howard,” Xavier replied.

Howard walked off, out of the field.

Siobhan came up to him next. She glanced back at Xavier’s house inside the Roving Seed Base—Adranial was still waiting inside. “She’s not going to wait forever, you know?”

Xavier smiled. “Oh, I know.”

“And you’re going to do something about that, right?” The redhead raised an eyebrow at him.

Xavier nodded. “Soon.”

Siobhan gave him a hug before stepping through the base’s barrier.

Justin sighed. “So, how many years until we see you next?”

Xavier ran a hand through his hair, thinking of what needed to be done. “Honestly? I couldn’t tell you.” He smiled. “Say hi to your grandkids for me.”

Justin’s eyes widened. “Grandkids. God. I haven’t got any of those yet. How old do you think I am?”

“Well, you aren’t a teenager anymore, that’s for sure.”

Justin chuckled. “No.” His expression shifted, becoming more serious. “We can all see it, you know.”

Xavier blinked. “What? This isn’t about Adranial again, is it?”

“No. Though Siobhan’s right, you better get a move on there, you know having kids is a wonderful thing, and she won’t wait forever—”

“Justin.”

“Right.” Justin’s lips formed a line. “That worry behind your eyes. It almost looks like…” He sighed. “Like you’re afraid.”

Xavier paused. “I am afraid.”

Justin shook his head. “Look, I don’t blame you. Who wouldn’t be? But you have to let this go.”

“What? Palini—”

Justin raised a hand. “I’m not talking about that. Learn from that, but don’t forget how you got where you are. You’re Xavier Fricking Collins, man. You’re insane. You’re driven. A bit of fear’s a healthy thing, but it’s Jhanku who should be shaking in his boots.”

Xavier looked at his boots. “I’m not shaking.”

Justin laughed, put a hand on his shoulder. “Just, remember what I said.”

After his old party left, Xavier stood alone on the grass, trying to remember the drive he’d had what wasn’t so long ago, wondering how to get it back.

He thought of the people he’d just spent time with, the lives and families they’d built while he’d been gone.

For a moment, he imagined all that ripped away by men like Jhanku.

His fingers curled into fists, bones creaking as he clenched.

You will pay for what you did. I’ll make sure of that.

Xavier walked back to his house. For several years now, whenever Adranial was inside the Roving Seed Base with him, he’d shared the house with her—but he still thought of it as his house.

The thing Siobhan and Justin were prodding him about, making their relationship more official, pushing it forward… he was still having trouble imagining. He couldn’t bear the thought of bringing a new life into a universe that felt so chaotic.

Not when there was so much left to do.

There will always be so much left to do. I can’t wait until everyone’s saved to life my own life, can I?

Still, he wasn’t ready—not yet.

But there was something he could do. Something he hoped Adranial would appreciate, as what Justin had said—she won’t wait forever—had been popping into his mind more than once over the years.

She’s already waited decades for me to return. Longer than half her life…

He stopped outside his house. “Adranial,” he called.

“The others said their goodbyes, then?” Adranial said, walking out.

Xavier took a moment to take the woman in.

She was roughly a head shorter than him, and her pale skin and hair looked almost exactly as it had when they’d first met. Her hair was down, curls bobbing as she walked up to him.

The woman wasn’t wearing her mage robes. Instead, she was wearing clothes she must have picked up from living on Earth. Blue jeans, a white top. A silver necklace—the same silver as her eyes, a trait they shared. Very casual, and yet she was the most stunning woman he’d ever seen.

Adranial raised an eyebrow as she reached him. “You’re staring.”

“Just wondering how I became the luckiest man in the multiverse.”

Her eyebrow went farther up her forehead. “The luckiest man in the multiverse? Why, because of all those powers you have?”

Xavier shook his head. “No, because out of the trillions upon trillions of people out there, you’re here with me and no one else.”

She slapped him on the chest, then cozied up to him, running a hand down to his stomach. “So, you can be a charmer when you want to be.”

Xavier pulled her in, kissing her deeply for a good long while before looking into her eyes and smiling.

Adranial blinked up at him. “What’s gotten into you?” She smiled. “Not that I don’t like it.”

Xavier cocked his head. “Just… been thinking about how much has changed since I last saw you. With…” He waved a hand. “Everyone’s lives.”

Adranial nodded, curls bobbing. “You know I don’t expect any of that, right? Marriage, children…”

“Actually, I have no idea what you expect. We haven’t really talked about it.”

Her fingers rested on his chest. “I’m not sure I’m ready to talk about it.”

Xavier released a sigh. “Honestly? I’m not sure I’m ready either.”

She smiled. “Oh, I know you’re not.”

“You do?”

“I may not have known you as long as the others, but I do know you, Xavier Collins. You’re driven. Obsessed. And I mean that as a compliment, mind you. The things you’ve accomplished—they go beyond what I imagined possible.” She ran her thumb along his brow. “I know how full your head is.”

“Heavy lays the crown,” Xavier muttered.

“Indeed. And you have your sights set on far more.”

Adranial looked away, toward the barrier where the others had left.

The sun was high in the sky. They could see for many, many miles all around. Ice-capped peaks, snow-laden forests, and the deep woods beyond. It was one of the most beautiful places Xavier had ever seen, and he didn’t even need to leave Earth to see it.

“But I also see something else in you,” Adranial said. “It’s not only power that drew me to you, Xavier. The way you enjoy life in these snippets, when you escape into your time dilation field, when you switch off from the pressure pushing down on you—it’s that man I fell in love with.” She smirked. “Though the power, that definitely was the hook.”

Xavier chuckled.

“I’m not ready for…” She waved a hand, as he’d waved his just before. “All that yet. But when I am, I don’t think you’ll be all that far behind.”

Xavier bit his lip, feeling a little apprehensive about what he was going to do next. He shook his head. May as well just go for it. “Come on, there’s something I want to show you.”

Adranial had a dubious look on her face. He took her hand and walked her out of the Roving Seed Base. Touching the grey stone, he returned it to his Storage Ring and stared around the peak of the mountain.

“Do you like this spot?” Xavier asked.

“Well enough,” she said slowly. “Why?”

“Well enough to… live here?”

She folded her arms across her chest. “Xavier Collins, do you mean to leave me at the top of this ice-cold mountain in the middle of nowhere?”

“Like you can’t teleport wherever you want.” He laughed. “Well…” He rubbed the back of his neck. “The Roving Seed Base, it’s felt like home to me for a while now, but it’s a home I always take with me. And, most recently, what’s felt even more like home is…”

“Is… what? What exactly are you procrastinating saying?” Adranial’s voice sounded stern, but there was a little smirk playing on her lips.

“Spending time with you,” he uttered. “So, I thought, maybe we should build a new home. Something that’s both of ours.”

“Here?” Adranial looked around the mountain’s peak.

Xavier shifted his weight from one foot to the other. “Well, it doesn’t have to be—”

“No, no.” A brilliant smile brightened her face. “That actually sounds perfect.”

“Actually? As though you’re surprised?”

“It’s not that I’m surprised, it’s just—you haven’t been one to make gestures like this in the past.”

Xavier inclined his head. “I know. But I think it’s long overdue. When I’m out there, when we’re both out there, I want there to be somewhere we want to return to. I want a spot that feels like us. The Roving Seed Base has been that, to a degree—”

She squeezed his hand. “But this is better.”

Xavier smiled. “I’m glad you agree. And, in the future, when…” He waved a hand. “Things are different, this might be a good place for our life to expand.”

“So, there is more going on in that head of yours than the fate of the universe,” Adranial said with a small smile. “Will you stay, build the house with me? We can design it like you designed the Roving Seed Base. We certainly have enough spirit coins to make something nice. Only, you were just about to leave.”

“I think I can stay a little longer for this.” He tapped a finger to his chin. “So, what should it look like?”
