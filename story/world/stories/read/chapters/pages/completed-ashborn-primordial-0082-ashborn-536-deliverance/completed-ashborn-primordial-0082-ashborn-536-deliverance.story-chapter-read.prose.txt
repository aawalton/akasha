Maiya pinched herself to make sure she wasn’t dreaming.

Mejai of all ranks worked alongside demonic Thaumaturges from several clans, adjusting crystal arrays and prepping the contraption in a display of cooperation the realm had never seen.

Meanwhile Maiya paced the length of the broadcast chamber, just trying not to get in their way.

Ira watched her from beside the door with an amused expression. An expression that Maiya caught redhanded.

“Relax,” Ira said, clearly resisting the urge to roll her eyes. “This isn’t your first speech. Nor even your hundredth, I think.”

Easy for her to say. She wasn't the one about to address half the realm.

Not that Maiya was especially dreading this one. If anything, her anxiety came from excitement rather than fear.

Every other time she'd stood in front of one of Saunak's crystals, she'd been delivering casualty counts and Ash Tear warnings. Either that, or emergency evacuation orders. She'd gotten quite good at delivering bad news over the years, and she'd hated every moment of it.

This was different. This was, finally, good news. The best possible news, even. The sort anyone would be proud to deliver.

Thankfully, they had just the tool to do it. The broadcast network had been Saunak's solution to a problem that had sprung up on them almost overnight.

The Altani had long used communications orbs that conveyed images and voice across distances, and even the Kin’jal had their version of the Thaumaturgy. Maiya had used it to chat with Vir across realms for years, after all.

Saunak had taken that principle and scaled it well past anything its human Thaumaturges could have imagined. Now, in every major city across the human realm, an enormous crystal stood in the central square, with larger cities having several.

When Maiya activated the broadcast, her image appeared in all of them simultaneously. Where a hundred thousand might have crowded into Sonam's largest square to hear her speak, now millions could.

As for why it was her and not Ira delivering this speech? The reason was both simple and rather convoluted. Ira was the Imperatrix of a nation that had grown in economic power to rival or exceed even Rani and the Altani. By any formal measure, she outranked Maiya considerably.

But Maiya was the one the realm looked to. Not as a queen or a ruler, but as an advisor, and these days, apparently an oracle.

These days, they called her the Saint of humanity. An overly lofty title she didn’t feel she deserved.

And yet, as the strongest human alive by a not insignificant margin and bearing the rank of Prime Mejai, to say nothing of her association with a being largely heralded as a living god, when Maiya spoke, people listened in a way they didn't to anyone else.

She'd never get used to that. The very idea of her leading a whole realm even now, years later, still made her skin crawl.

The lead Thaumaturge looked up from the array. “We are, my Lady.”

Maiya stopped pacing and nodded to the demon, walking to the center of the room where the primary crystal awaited, glowing with prana.

She took a deep breath and began.

“People of the realm!”

Her voice came out thankfully steady, and she knew the very sight of her would set a million minds at ease, as ludicrous as the idea was.

“You must have every question in the world, and I intend to allay your fears, but first, I owe you an apology. I have been absent for longer than I had expected, and in my absence, you have faced the apocalyptic threat alone. Not with fear, but a level of courage and endurance that has defined this realm through every crisis it has ever known. I am sorry I was not here. I hope what I am about to tell you goes some way toward explaining why.”

She drew in a breath and put on a wry smile.

“As you can see, the Akh Nara and I have returned.”

She waited a moment for the chuckles and sighs of relief to ripple through the crowd.

That was the most difficult part of all this—she lacked a live audience to gauge reactions and alter her speech on the fly as all master orators did. And while the several projections arrayed before her displayed many of the city squares from across the realm for her benefit, it was hardly a substitute for a live crowd.

“We set out to find a solution to the Ash Tears. A permanent solution. As you all know, the Ashen Realm’s encroachment has plagued us since long before any of us were born, while the the Ash Tears have wrought upon our realms for just the past years. Our journey was long and dangerous, and there were moments when I genuinely did not believe we would return. But we did. We had to. And I am proud to say we did not return empty-handed.”

She waited again as cheers rang out on the array of projections before her.

“As you have no doubt noticed, the prana level of our realm has noticeably increased. This is by design. It will take some time to grow accustomed to for some of you, especially those in far-flung regions to the west. You may feel lightheaded. The young and the elderly may feel the pressure more acutely than others. Yet I promise you this poses no danger. In fact, you will soon be healthier and fuller of life than ever before.”

The energy of the crowds’ enthusiasm came through more clearly than ever, though it was especially pronounced in the Hiranyan cities and Western Rani, where prana was forever in short supply.

“The three realms are now one,” she continued, glossing over the details of exactly how that came to be. That fact would cause more harm than good, and the last thing everyone needed was another reason to panic. “I am proud to say that as of this morning, the Ashen Realm no longer exists. There are no Ash boundaries, and all Ash Beasts have been wiped from the face of the map. No longer will a single Ash Tear plague us! The nightmare that has defined our lives, that has claimed so many of our loved ones and cost us so very dearly, has finally come to an end.”

She had to wait a good minute for the uproarious cheers to die down this time, a proud smile plastered upon her face. It was only half a lie. The Ash was gone, that much was certain. But whether the Fateweavers would find a way to weaken the fabric of reality again, no one could say.

She looked around to find that all of her staff in the room had also stopped moving. Even the ones who'd already been briefed looked as though on the verge of breaking into applause. Humans and demons alike.

Once the applause died down, her smile fell and she adopted a more somber tone.

“I know how much has been lost. I know that no amount of joyous news will undo the grief of what this realm has endured. I am not asking you to set that grief aside. We will never forget all those we lost. To honor their sacrifice and our victory over this cataclysm, I hereby declare a full month of celebration across the realm. That we may remember their sacrifice and look to the future their noble sacrifice has bought!”

Maiya thought of Janak. Of the machine and what it had cost them. Of the memory of a face she could no longer quite recall.

“What comes next will not be simple. The joining of the realms has created untold work for us, more than any one generation has faced before. There are new maps to draw, both geographically and in prana. The new, equal distribution of magic will impact economies and upset trade, to say nothing of the Demon Realm, now reachable via nothing more than an airship, and all the open land in between. I’ll not lie. It will take a great deal of work and a great deal of patience. But we know how to work and we know patience. For the first time in an Age, we will build a world without Ash.”

Had she been there in person, Maiya suspected the cheers would have deafened her, and once again, she was forced to wait until wave after wave of cheers finally died down.

“I am asking the rulers of all nations across the realm to convene at Sonam Castle. There is much to discuss, and it is best discussed together, face to face as the single united realm we now are. Details will follow through official channels. I look forward to seeing you all. Go, celebrate now, and be ready to build this beautiful world to come.”

She stepped back from the crystal, not waiting for the shouts and claps to die down yet again. She wasn’t quite sure she could take any more of smiling like a statue…

The Thaumaturge thankfully deactivated the array and the glow faded soon after. The room was quiet for a moment before someone started clapping. It spread quickly, and soon the whole room was cheering for her.

Ira walked up to her and took both her hands in hers.

“Well done,” she congratulated, beaming.

Maiya smiled back at her.

It was a conflicted smile, and she couldn't fully hide it. The room was full of people clapping, the realm had just heard that the worst was over, and yet Vir was out there carrying within him a prana core that grew by the hour. A literal bomb that threatened to go off and wipe everyone from the face of the realm in an instant.

And behind all of it, unknown to everyone in the whole realm, a force that manipulated Fate was undoubtedly sowing seeds to trigger it.

She wondered how the millions watching the broadcast would react if they knew.

She shook off the thought.

“Come,” she said, squeezing Ira's hands. “There is little time and much work to be done.”
