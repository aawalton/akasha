Chapter 181 / B3-26: High Dive

A second before slamming into the rocky floor after plummeting ten stories from the top of the cliff, Joe activated his [Flutterfall] charm. All of his inertia magically vanished, allowing him to glide smoothly into the throng of wights as they emerged from the tunnel.

He went to town with [Haybreaker] and the dirty maneuvers supplied by [Brawler]. Joe kicked the legs out from under the wight on his left, broke the kneecaps of those on his right, used [Heavy Hand] to launch a wargish deader flying into the rock wall, while dragging his claws across the glowing eye sockets of the ghast in front of him.

A buzzing whirl of wings joined the fray a second later. Yuk wasted no time calling forth his newest trick. Green spectral rats burst into existence and began devouring the squad of undead. Joe found himself embroiled in a swarm of tiny lime-hued feet, whiskers, and teeth, yet none of the semi-solid rodents took even the slightest nibble on him. They saved all their ferocity for the twenty or so dirge wights around Joe and Margen. The deaders shifted their focus off the [Halefire] enshrouded sword-master to the life-abundant healer and teeming throng of ghost rats that had just landed in the middle of their pack.

Joe usually didn’t let himself get surrounded this badly, but there had been no good way to aim his landing from a hundred feet up. Even so, he was not having nearly as rough a time as he expected. The rodents were drawing many of the attacks, allowing Joe to [Sidestep] his way to a better position. He didn't have enough time to use his curses, but with having another front-line ally and [Brawler’s] more aggressive fighting style, the wights were not overwhelming him. Joe took several painful slashes, but he was certain he wouldn’t be dragged off his feet as he had been when saving Wen.

Margen stuck with his surgical strikes, now that he was safe from being mobbed.

He could hear Myllo’s muffled side of the conversation taking place at the base camp through the stud. The guilder must have had the earring covered, so only a murmur trickled out of the bauble. [Crystal Mind] gave Joe plenty of focus to block out that minor distraction until the last wight hit the ground. The fight had been furious yet, thankfully, over quickly.

“Ok. Good news. I’m in contact with the base camp,” Joe huffed to his teammates, while wincing at a deep cut across his back. Joe eased off the gambeson and found that the undead claws had hit hard enough to cut through his [Bonemail] and the reinforced material of the armored coat. Joe would need to mend his trusty gambeson once again.

“Do you want to …” he started to say before the founder made an emphatic cutting gesture. Clamping his hand over the communication item, Joe gave Margen an exaggeratedly confused look.

“You were telling me during the healing how the town just had a huge festival around us founders,” the big man began. “How they have performances about me and my being lost.”

“And?”

“Sounds like Fort Coral has tossed me and the others up on a pretty high pedestal. That kind of reverence can lead to poor tactical decisions. A good leader can’t ignore sentiment, but he doesn’t let emotions form strategies. If you go and tell them you found me, they are going to stop focusing on the task at hand and switch to a rescue mission. I don’t need to be rescued. We need to bring down the Erlking, break the loom, and fix the wards you said were busted.”

“Ok. I can see where you’re going now. But what happens if they jump in here during a fight? They won’t be expecting a hulking semi-rotting stranger. If you get offed by friendly-fire, that’s it. That last century of toughing through the rot would have been for nothing.”

“Good argument,” the commander complimented. “Alright. Let’s meet in the middle. If my existence would alter their plans, we speak up. Otherwise, we keep a lid on it until we’ve finished this thing, one way or another.”

“Ok. As long as we tell them about you before we get face-to-face with anyone.”

“Done. Now, one more thing. I want the same commitment from you. You don’t try to save me from dying by the undead. I can do another stint in Dooms. I’ll be back in a few months. If it comes down to completing one of our objectives and saving me, you let me go. Swear,” the founder demanded, bearing a stern look which brooked no objections.

“I get it. Ok. I swear.”

‘Us too,’ the echoey voice in Joe’s head chimed in.

“Just make sure you grab Dooms if I go back inside.”

“You have my word there, too, Margen,” Joe answered earnestly.

‘Us, too!’

‘How the heck are you going to carry that big-ass sword, bud?’

‘We’ll figure it out. That’s friggin’ DoomHerald. Who wouldn’t want to carry it?’

Joe couldn’t help but smile at the ragamuffin's excitement. Before he could reply, Myllo’s voice spoke through the earring.

“Everything ok down there? Sounds like you’re not fighting anymore.”

Joe realized that Myllo couldn’t hear the battle, but he could hear Joe’s breath and sounds during the brawl..

“All good,” Joe answered. “Just making repairs, both items and skin.”

“Alright. As we were saying, we have two plans. The first one is an immediate extraction. Vex can use his [Drifting Rift] again to drop you a beacon, and we’ll open a door to you now.”

“Okaaaay. That seems pretty simple. Now, I’m curious as hell to hear what could possibly be Plan B.”

“This is from Nara,” Myllo quickly specified, which did absolutely nothing to alleviate Joe’s building trepidation. “The Hellions reached a series of rooms that looked almost identical to the loom room that Mortalius had scryed. These three rooms were dedicated to one of the other four fey queens and king. There was also a stairway heading upwards, which we assume will lead to the Spring King’s hall. Our divinations are being meddled with by fey magic, but we are almost certain that’s where the Eternal Loom and the Erlking are.”

“Eternal Loom? You figured out its name?”

“We did. Though the Hellions were unable to lay their eyes on it. They found a pile of treasure being offered to the Lady of Winter, Queen Morrigu. Someone,” Myllo growled, with such a heavy emphasis, Joe was sure the guild-leader was staring at someone overhead, “couldn’t keep their hot hands off the loot and grabbed an axe … I don’t care if it was an epic weapon, idiot.” Myllo’s voice had shifted as if he were talking to someone beside him

“Sorry, Joe,” the guild captain resumed, his focus back on the conversation with Joe. “The long and the short of it, touching that treasure pile set off alarms. A whole pack of them mind-bending wights and bear mummies swarmed into the room.”

“Mournful thralls,” Joe supplied.

“Yeah, those. Level-wise, it wasn’t much of a match. The team tore apart a big chunk of the thralls, but then the bastards got into Nara's mind and made her turn on the team. Morty scrubbed it, but by then, they had Reven, too. When they were close to overwhelming Mortalius, the Hellions had to bail. They just didn’t have high enough mental resistance for that many dominator-types.”

Joe thought the sinister-seeming team had tons of determination. He had learned that personal grit helped mental defenses, but he conceded it was not the same as true resistances.

“Turns out you do,” the guild strategist conferred. “Kenda says you have major resistance to three of the four mind categories and a decently-leveled skill for {Stun}.”

“So here is the Plan,” Myllo continued. “Do you think you can get to the third floor? We can [Drift Rift] a care package down to you. Whatever you need, plus a wayfinder keyed to the room where the Hellions were pulled out from. We will also include the base of a gatestone. Vex is finishing that up for you. You’ll just need to add an identifying symbol and infuse it. That will give us an anchored gate inside and a solid footing to fight from.”

“Vex can’t open a gate to the same place he grabbed the Hellions out of?”

“He could explain it better,” Myllo sighed, “But the gist is one way gates are more fragile than anchored gates. If we were to throw something volatile through an unanchored gate, there is a very good chance it wouldn't go through. You have no idea how many stories we have about mages blowing themselves up trying to shove big magic through portals. Thank the One. If deadly spells could be thrown through any old gate, no place would be safe. He is muttering something next to me about how the rarity of the magic has to be at least two ranks lower than the portal.”

“Alright. Let me discuss with Yuk. Mind if I mute you for a minute?” Joe asked before adding, “And how do I do that?”

“Just think ‘silent’ at the stud.”

“Okay. I should have gotten that. One minute,” Joe said before silencing the earring. While cleansing the blademaster again, he explained the options to both of them before asking for their opinions. He wished Yuk would come clean, or Margen would. These phonetag / half-conversations were getting cumbersome.

“Tactically, the gate is a sound plan,” the founder stated. “There are a number of short-term mental buffs that would not last the trip from the edge of the valley all the way back into the Erlking’s chamber. You, your Yuk, and I have a far better likelihood of reaching the Honor Halls of Summer, Fall, and Winter, especially if we are provided a map.”

‘We know you are going to think we are just agreeing with Margen, but we liked Plan B before he spoke up. We’re doing really well. We know you have the worst of it, though, Joe. You’re taking all the hits, so if you’ve had enough, we are happy with just bailing and trying something else.’

‘I’m ok. Love the rats! They were a great pick.’ Out loud, he then stated. “Okay. Plan B it is.”

Joe unmuted the communication item. “Ok, let's go with Naragash’s plan. For the care packet, we’re going to want potions for sure. Stamina and Mana. Oh, and mending charms. I’m going to have to spend my last one in a second to fix my armor. Food too. Something with some good buffs in it. It’s cold, and we’re beat. We could use the pick-me-up. Send more than you think we’d need. Imagine somehow we had Nard with us.” Even though Joe could keep patching up Margen, that one meal had seriously enhanced the failing founder’s overall health.

On that train of thought, Joe added, “The mournful thralls have a mummy rot attack,” he made up. According to the big man, his condition had been inflicted by the Erlking himself, but Joe couldn’t very well explain that with the restriction on what he could and couldn’t say. “Does Mortalius or the Guild have any super cure-alls at their disposal?”

“Sorry, kid,” Myllo consoled, after a brief conference topside. “Puqmup says we used all the [Panaceas] we had after Cauldrakon. We have alchemists brewing more, but they take weeks to craft. Are you infected?”

Joe covered the stud, before stating, “This is a pain in the ass, sir.” before uncovering it and dissembling, “I have it under control. Alright, I’m about 20 yards from where I caught that last [Drift Rift] and about 35 yards further down. Do you want me to head back to where I was?”

“No, stay put. We have a lock on you. Give us a minute, and we’ll send you a [Paired Pouch].”

“Easy for him to say,” Joe grumbled, as he eased out another stinking trickle of mummy rot from the legendary general.
