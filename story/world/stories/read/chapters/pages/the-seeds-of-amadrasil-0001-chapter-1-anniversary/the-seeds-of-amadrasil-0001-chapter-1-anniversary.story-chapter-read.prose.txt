Chapter 1: Anniversary

Every portal anchor shattered is a catastrophe prevented. Every portal allowed to mature risks becoming another San Alvero.

I know the difference. I stood on the edge of the breach five years ago and watched a world of fungal darkness pour into our own. I watched friends die holding the line against an enemy that never tired.

Worldshield exists so that no one will ever have to stand where we stood.

- General Carrigan Blackwell, Director of Worldshield Operations, Veritania. Press conference on 19 August, 30 FPE, the thirtieth anniversary of the First Portal Event

Bzzzt.

Kal’s eyes snapped open as his phone vibrated violently against his sternum, where he must have dropped it when he fell asleep. His bedroom was still dark, with the morning light barely beginning to brighten the gaps between the window blinds, and yet his dream lingered well beyond the point of lucidity. The image of that vast, strangely majestic golden crystal tree loomed somewhere beyond the walls of his small room, visible somehow, as if he were peering into another realm. While there was no frame of reference, it felt immense beyond imagination, looming over worlds. If he squinted, he would have been unsurprised to find the entire blue-green world of Valmyra dangling there like an apple from a glowing branch.

It called to him in ways he could barely articulate, even to himself, like it was tugging gently, insistently at the essence of his being, urging him closer. To listen to some incomprehensibly vast song, hovering just beyond the grasp of comprehension. A message of urgency, a call to action: but to what? The meaning eluded him, as always.

He knew what came next.

The sight of the hungry black flames dancing up the trunk to wreathe the branches and leaves left him hesitant, breathless, as if he were experiencing not fear itself, but the echo of it—a fragmented, haunting memory devoid of any recollection of where he had acquired it.

He had always had the dreams, ever since he could remember, but they had never been so vivid or real until he’d foolishly thrown himself into his mother’s corrosive mana aura at the hospital in a desperate attempt to reach her. Nothing had been the same since.

He shook his head to clear the persistent dream, groaning as his sudden movement left him dizzy and weak, and with a throbbing pain that shot through his skull as a reminder that he was still not fully recovered from the mana sickness from his last job.

“Ugh.” He rolled over and forced himself to sit up, flicking a finger across the front of his phone to see who had woken him up.

Erin Vega: Fresh portal opened in Ravenna Mercado. Interested?

Kal sighed. He needed another day or two to recover from his latest bout of mana sickness before he’d be allowed to take another portal job. Still, he was several months behind on Mira’s university tuition, and he’d just received the final warning.

Kaldon Wolfe: Do you know what time it is?

His phone buzzed no more than three seconds later.

Erin Vega: Sorry :(

Erin Vega: It’s urgent.

Erin Vega: I secured the first harvesting position on the condition that I can assemble my team on site by 8 am. They’re offering double the rate in hazard pay to enter alongside the hunters.

Erin Vega: Kal, the scanners are predicting a Goblin world. You know what that means.

Kal paused, staring at the light blue, glowing text for a good thirty seconds. He definitely knew what that meant. A goblin portal world meant human-compatible mana seeds. He’d seen E-rank fire magic mana seeds fetch more than six hundred thousand credits on auction. With his cut from such a job, he could easily cover Mira’s outstanding tuition bills and prepay her final year, not to mention catching up on rent and even refilling the refrigerator. There was only one problem:

Kaldon Wolfe: I still have mana sickness.

Erin Vega: Fuck.

Erin Vega: How bad?

Kaldon Wolfe: Officially, two more days before I’m cleared for portal duty.

Erin Vega: Hmm… you could still drag the cart and collect herbs and crystals, right? I don’t want to make you do something… well, something you don’t want to, but… is there any way you can make it work?

Kal considered it. He really considered it. The word Erin was skirting was ‘illegal.’ Seedless, baseline humans like himself were allowed to work E-rank portal worlds without an implanted mana seed to protect them, but Worldshield maintained strict regulations around managing mana sickness. If he were caught, he’d be fined heavily and probably have his license revoked for a few months. It was a bit of a moot point, though.

Kaldon Wolfe: My mana exposure meter is still dark. They wouldn’t let me in.

Erin Vega: You’re friendly with Rigel, right? He’s onsite and can take care of that for you. Sorry to be so pushy, but I need you on this job, Kal.

Technically, he would be breaking the rules, but she was right. As uncomfortable as it would be, he was probably strong enough to do the work—those new articulated ATV harvest carts basically pushed themselves—and this was not the kind of job that just landed in one’s lap every day. These kinds of portals were jealously hoarded, fought over. The fact that Erin had managed to swing the first harvester position for her team over one of the guild groups meant someone, somewhere, had fucked up and dropped the ball.

He sighed and willed his thumbs to move.

Kaldon Wolfe: I’m in.

He would regret it afterward when the mana sickness left him bedridden and shaking for most of next week, but getting the debt collectors off his back and ensuring Mira would not be kicked out in her final year of veterinary school would be worth it.

Erin Vega: I owe you big time! See you at eight!

***

Kal wiped the moisture from the mirror and grimaced at the bags under the dark brown eyes staring back at him, and the mana sickness pallor in his cheeks. I need to get the blood flowing. There’s time to hit the gym first. He rubbed the towel over his short black hair and called it good.

Kal emerged freshly showered from his room, wearing sweats and settling his favorite, well-worn cream hoodie over his shoulders. He still had a couple of hours before he had to check in at the portal, time enough to hit the gym first and warm up.

More like, figure out if my mana sickness will get in the way.

“Morning, Kiki,” Mira said, using the childhood nickname she had made up for him back when they were both unholy terrors and small enough to trip over.

“Hi, Mira,” he said, glancing at the couch where his sister sat with Abby curled up and purring in her lap while watching a stream on her tablet. He caught the distinctive sound of General Carrigan Blackwell giving a speech to commemorate the thirtieth anniversary of the First Portal Event. A pompous affair put on by Worldshield, filled with empty promises to push back the monsters, restore San Alvero, and ensure public safety. For the good of Veritania. It was all a PR stunt; they had lost the strength to achieve anything meaningful five years ago.

“I bought some incense,” Mira said, her voice heavy. Knowing her, she hadn’t slept all night, and she sounded like she’d been crying.

“Thank you,” he said, walking over to the mantle and staring for several minutes at the pictures that rested there.

Mom. Dad. I… I miss you. I wish you were still here. Blinking back the wetness welling in his eyes, he carefully lit two sticks of incense and placed them on the stand beside the photos of his parents. It was hard to believe it had been five years already.

Kal’s eyes slid to the side, coming to rest on the heavy, matte-black pistol set beside his father’s portrait. “Dad, you’ll be happy to know I haven’t given up the training,” he whispered.

His dream, since he had been old enough to understand, had been to follow in his father’s footsteps by earning a warrior seed and exploring portal worlds. To test himself against the monsters and grow stronger. Some of the older generation, those who had lived in the Valmyra before the portals came, found magic and mana seeds to be fearful, frightening. Not him. He had always dreamed of wielding magic through a sword or with his fists, of exploring the strange fragments of worlds behind those blue gates and learning their secrets. His parents had shared his passion and encouraged him at every opportunity, and his father had even trained him to fight, insisting that fundamental mastery of martial arts was the only way to break through the threshold and into S-rank.

Training with Lucien was never quite the same. The old man was unequivocally a master, but he wasn’t Gareth Wolfe. When Carrigan Blackwell—she’d still been a Major back then—had come bearing the awful news and an armful of his father’s effects, Kal had borne it stoically, refusing to cry like Mira, but when she had offered him his father’s pistol, he had finally lost it. Somehow, the sight of the weapon, which rarely left his father’s side, had made the nightmare real in a way the words had not.

It had been the awful power of his mother’s shattered S-rank seed, the black flames run amok and disintegrating the hospital around her while Carrigan held him back from throwing himself into oblivion, that had truly broken him. He had screamed at Carrigan until she had left. Even knowing it was not rational, he still blamed his parents’ best friend for getting them killed. He had never returned any of her calls after that, nor had he allowed her to speak to him at the funeral.

It had taken months for him to claw his way back from the precipice, and when he had, he had found himself and Mira alone, staggering under crushing debt. The once powerful Silver Wolf guild had been disbanded by the state, and all its resources redistributed among the remaining guilds ‘for the good of the country,’ leaving him and Mira without any prospects to earn the mana seeds his parents had wanted for them.

“We’ll get our seeds, Dad. I’ll get stronger, and then I’ll finish what you started,” he said. It was a vow he made every year on this anniversary. It was not a promise of vengeance. Instead, it was what his father would have wanted. “The Wolfe family always finishes what they start.”

The job was not done.

His hand settled on the pistol’s heavy stock, and he picked it up, turning it over in his hands. His eyes traced out the lines of the ‘Chekhov Custom .50’ in bold lettering engraved on the barrel. It was a stupid joke; one his father had loved dearly. So much so that he had carried one of the most useless weapons in existence to an S-ranked raid the day he died. And yet, it reminded him of the person his father had been.

He sighed and belted it to his waist, under his hoodie. “Dad, I hope you don’t mind. I’m going to borrow this today.”

“What are you going to do with that, Kal?” Mira asked, coming to stand shoulder-to-shoulder with him.

“I got a job,” he said.

“Already? You haven’t recovered yet.”

“I know. But Erin said we will get first dibs. And it’s a goblin world.”

Mira’s eyes widened, but then she frowned. “I don’t like you risking yourself all the time in portals. You’re pushing yourself too hard.”

“We really need the money,” he said. “Tuition is ten thousand credits, due at the end of the week.” They were worlds away from the promise he’d just made, but the first step on that daunting journey was to get Mira her degree. With the money she could earn, he could return to college and finish his degree. Then they could begin to save for a common warrior seed to get them to the actual road forward. He swallowed.

I have to. There’s no other choice.

“Can’t you call and ask them to delay it?” Mira asked, turning to look at him. “I can pull some overtime at the restaurant; we can make it work.”

“This is the third time already. They already sent the final notice,” Kal said, nodding at the pink page lying neatly folded on the kitchen counter. The problem was that the tuition notice was only one of many. “Besides, if this job is as lucrative as Erin thinks, I should be able to pay off the rest of your tuition in one go. That means I shouldn’t have to do more portals. Maybe it’ll even be enough for me to start applying to go back to school.”

He hung his head, wishing she would not argue too hard this time. He could never stand to see what was in her eyes every time he told her he was going into another portal.

She nodded slowly, biting her lip. “Just be careful, ok?”

“I will.”

“Promise?”

“Gaah, you’re killing me with those puppy-dog eyes, Miri,” he said, using his old childhood nickname for her.

But when they hugged, he felt her trying to stop shaking, and that was what stuck in his heart like a red-hot poker. Without a shadow of a doubt, he would have lost himself over that dark precipice had she not been there to hold his hand. He could not imagine losing her. Never. Nor would he ever fail her.

***

Lucien Cross cut an imposing figure, standing opposite Kal in the sparring ring. His graying hair was worn cropped short and tight, and his light-tan, weathered skin contrasted with piercing light-brown eyes that missed nothing and glinted with amber whenever the light caught them just right. He wore sweatpants and a plain black T-shirt that seemed to be straining to contain the layers of wiry, lean muscle packed onto his six-foot-two frame, but Kal knew from long experience that the slightly taller man’s weight would do nothing to slow him down. On the contrary…

“Are you sure you’re up for this, kid? You don’t look so hot,” Lucien said mildly as he brushed his foot idly across a recently mopped-up bloodstain on the floor. Like his dad, Lucien did not shy away from pushing trainees hard enough to spill a little blood. If anything, Lucien was stricter and more brutal in his methods, and yet, never once had their training sessions even so much as wobbled his iron control. Nobody was getting seriously injured in his ring, and that was because he would never allow it.

“Try me, old man,” Kal said, raising his bokken, the wooden practice sword having definitely seen better days, but still meticulously maintained. While Lucien might be wearing nothing more than a light robe, Kal was decked out in a proper padded gambeson and wearing protective headgear. He knew the drill.

Today was the day. Today, my sword will finally find him!

Kal could still recall the first day he’d found Lucien’s training gym. It had been several months after his parents had died, and he’d just dropped out of his engineering degree at the University of Ashmont to reduce their costs and take a job to cover rent and Mira’s tuition. In hindsight, he realized he had been struggling with depression and grief. His father had taught him advanced martial arts and sword skills, and so, he had not thought much of Lucien when he’d offered to spar that day. The gym—or dojo, really—was tucked away in a dingy alley, and all the equipment inside was old and well-worn, and Kal’s snap impression the moment he’d walked in had been less than kind.

Instead of reacting to Kal’s brusque, arrogant attitude—what he later came to realize had been papering over anger, frustration, and grief—Lucien had shown him hitherto unimagined levels of sword mastery, and he’d gone home nursing several nasty bruises with a firm vow to never return.

A few months later, under Lucien’s ‘delicate’ coaching, Kal had entered his first regional competition, placing third in the non-seed open division.

Faint creases briefly appeared around Lucien’s eyes, and he said, “I see that my last lesson about respecting your elders has just about worn off already.”

“Maybe you failed to teach it properly?” Kal said, bowing the proper depth to indicate he was ready for instruction.

“Looking for a few fresh bruises today, I see,” Lucien said.

“Learning is a gift. Even when your teacher is a… I mean… when pain is your teacher,” Kal quipped, wielding one of Lucien’s favorite quotes like an opening thrust.

Lucien coughed eloquently and bowed, raising a matching bokken.

Kal did not require more invitation than that, darting forward in a fencing stance to minimize his exposure to a counterattack, and thrusting low, just above hip level. He kept his gaze fixed around the middle of Lucien’s chest with a wide focus as he’d been taught. Ready for anything.

Lucien’s battle-scarred bokken blurred as he took a single step back and rotated his torso slightly. A loud crack filled the dojo as Kal’s thrust was deflected, passing less than an inch away from Lucien’s abdomen.

Kal followed up with shifting steps and two quick, slashing strikes, but with barely a twitch, Lucien deflected one and sidestepped the second. Kal frowned as the sidestep had been initiated before his first attack. Am I that predictable?

“Where is your power?” Lucien chided. “You trying to give me a massage?”

Kal darted backward to recover, guarding against the riposte that didn’t materialize and grimacing at the sudden tremble deep in his hamstrings. Aiming to cover his weakness, he flowed into another attack, this time unleashing an overhead strike aimed at his teacher’s unprotected head, but the sword in Lucien’s hand moved as if it had foreknowledge of the strike, deflecting it and drawing him forward. Kal shifted quickly to avoid overextending as he caught his balance.

“You need to do better than that if you want to place at the Ashmont Grand Melee next week,” Lucien chided. “And you need to place if you want to qualify for nationals again this year.”

“I know,” Kal answered, his teeth clenched together against the slowly mounting throbbing in his head. And Lucien hasn’t even attacked yet. I need to end this quickly. One hit, just one.

Kal pushed forward, but Lucien suddenly went on the offensive with an impossible flurry of whipping strikes that made audible swishing noises as the wooden sword sliced the air, leaving Kal retreating as he threw up several hasty blocks that were far sloppier than he liked. He shook his head to try to shift some of the sweat off his brow under the mask, but that proved to be a poor choice given his mounting headache. Worried about his stamina now, Kal darted forward again, aiming at his sword arm, but Lucien shifted ever so slightly and tapped his ankle with an effortless foot sweep, sending him sprawling across the ground.

“How did you… You moved before I did,” Kal grumbled as he rolled back up onto his feet. In a real fight, he would be dead.

“Your balance shifted before you attacked,” Lucien answered gently.

Kal frowned. It definitely did not. But instead of dwelling on it, he launched a fresh attack, a balanced and well-timed thrust, but Lucien sidestepped as if he had minutes, not milliseconds. How is he reading me so easily? That was perfect…

“Don’t lose focus,” Lucien said, interrupting with a pair of back-to-back slashes that had Kal hopping backward a fraction of an inch from getting skewered. The sword master didn’t give him a moment to breathe, advancing with precision and overwhelming dominance.

Kal felt himself being pushed around the ring like a novice. He gritted his teeth and focused on the flow, the rhythm of attacks, throwing out desperate blocks like birdseed to eager pigeons. He took a deep breath, struggling to get his mind into the zone.

It came to him like a cloak of settling silence. His hearing heightened, and his surroundings dimmed as his focus narrowed on his opponent. For one crystal-clear moment, it was just him and Lucien, and the rest of the world faded away.

There! In the heart of that moment, he saw it. The pattern emerged as clear as the sun breaking through the clouds, and he leapt at it. Using the very counterattack Lucien had just used to knock him down, he struck. It was perfect. Driving up through his core from a solid base of well-trained footwork, he twisted, accelerating his blade through the tiniest of gaps. Those piercing eyes widened almost imperceptibly as Kal’s sword gently grazed the underside of his guard on the way to his ribs.

A dangerous glint flickered like molten steel in his eyes, and a sudden, intangible weight bore down on Kal like an anvil upon his shoulders. Lucien shifted instantly into a stance Kal had never seen before. His blade vanished to the sound of a loud whistle and a deafening crack. Two devastating attacks landed in the blink of an eye, knocking him sprawling to the ground. Kal groaned as he found himself staring up at Lucien’s wooden blade pressed lightly against his throat.

“What…” Kal gasped, struggling to recover his breath. “What the hell was that?”

Lucien took one step backward and withdrew his blade, looking faintly embarrassed. “Congratulations, Kal,” he said, reaching out a hand to help him up. “That was the first time you bypassed my guard.”

“Fat lot of good it did,” Kal grumbled, but he reached out and took his teacher’s offered hand and stood. He couldn’t help but notice Lucien wasn’t even breathing hard.

“You should be proud of that attack,” Lucien said. “It was excellent.”

Despite the thorough trouncing, Kal relaxed and stooped to collect the remains of his own practice sword, now snapped cleanly in two. Lucien’s praise was hard to win. Instead, he asked, “So, what was that technique you used to counter it?”

Lucien’s voice grew wistful as he relaxed his stance, “This form isn’t taught anymore. Some say it was never taught.”

“How come you never showed it to me?”

Lucien hesitated; his eyes briefly focused somewhere beyond the mundane walls of the dojo. “It’s not part of any style taught on… around here,” he said quietly, offering Kal a faint, cryptic smile. “Some things you carry with you from places you’d rather leave behind.”

“I’m… sorry,” Kal said, meeting Lucien’s eyes as he instantly resonated with his expression of painful memories of the past.

“Old history, kid,” Lucien said, brushing it off like it was nothing. “I think you earned a reward for that attack you pulled off, though. How about I teach you some of the footwork drills required to understand that technique, and we’ll see how long it takes you to master them? I warn you, though, it’s a demanding style.”

“Thank you,” Kal said, bowing to him. The opportunity to learn something new was always welcome, and judging by what he’d just witnessed, it was a technique Lucien had etched into his bones until it had become part of himself. Either way, it promised to be something special, and Kal’s blood was already pumping at the prospect.

Lucien returned the bow, and as they exited the ring, he turned to place a hand on Kal’s shoulder. “How’re you holding up? It’s the anniversary today.”

He ought to be ok, it had been five years already. It was just a day, like any other, and it would pass. Crying over it again would not bring them back. He swallowed against the lump that suddenly formed in his throat. “I’m ok,” he managed.

“Good,” Lucien said, squeezing his shoulder and letting go. “Let me know if you need anything. And don’t be alone today if you can help it.”

“I have a job,” he admitted.

“In your condition?” Lucien said, raising his eyebrows.

“Is it that obvious?” Kal said, wincing at the brightness of the light streaming into the lobby windows.

Lucien twisted his lips into a wry smile, “I thought you were being a masochist, fighting with mana sickness.”

“I thought the exercise would help expel the mana faster,” Kal admitted.

“You know it doesn’t work like that, right?” Lucien said.

“I guess I do, now,” Kal said, frowning. The best resources on the internet were divided on the subject, and yet… Kal glanced at Lucien. How is he so certain?
