I wish I could tell you that the moment the Order of White saw what happened to Captain Sonea, they revolted as one—turned against the angels in their ranks, dissolved their front lines, and joined us in the assault on the gates of Chairo.

Yet even in a fantasy world, we couldn’t be so lucky.

First, let us remember, even if it curdles my blood, fear of those in power is a strong motivator. Chairo’s army had no means to fight the angels embedded; only the ability to be jeweled and stripped of their will, just like their captain had been.

However, we managed to do something.

Their attacks were bereft of Conviction. Defenses lost coordination. Their collective Faith and Trust wavered, replaced by their own ambient animus.

And that would have to be enough for us.

“There is sufficient animus density behind enemy lines for our next phase,” Galenus’s voice echoed from Nora’s staff. “With the additional amity support from the priests here in the Bastion, I should be able to sustain multiple simultaneous portals within range of the South Gate.” He paused. “I may very well be able to translocate to the portcullis directly.”

“Too risky,” I replied, watching some of the White’s holy knights swarming toward our suggested destination. “Let’s zip around and see if we can’t throw them off a little.”

Being shredded up against the gate was not in my plans for the day.

“Use some of those secondaries to yeet any aggressors that get too close to us,” Nora added. “We’ll just ride straight on—you put us on the right angle through.”

“Understood,” Galenus replied firmly. “Though bear in mind that beings who do not want to be translocated cost us more energy.”

Nora nodded absently. “I’ll try to shove others away, too.”

Tetora’s ears flicked twice, then shot fully forward. “That holy crystal flickered again.”

Nora and I stared—finally catching it.

“Pull me up!” Nora demanded. “Now!”

I reached down, and she grabbed my arm. I swung her into the saddle behind me, a bit surprised by how easy it was to toss her.

"Gentler next time!" she complained, more for ambiance than any true irritation.

There won't be a next time. We either breach the gates or we die trying.

"Yeah, yeah," I muttered instead, adjusting myself in the saddle better. "Get ready, everyone."

Tetora grunted, getting ready to run. Olethros summoned a crooked gold staff, and the moment his hand closed around it, a scythe blade materialized at its top—wickedly curved, crackling with purple animus that ran the length of the edge like dancing bolts of lightning.

He caught my stare. "A proxy to channel everything I can gather into one strike," he muttered darkly. "And I swear, I will get the real other half of me back." His grip tightened on the weapon. "One way or another."

I nodded fiercely, only half-understanding exactly what he meant now.

Nora tapped her staff and cleared her throat. “And… Break!”

General Ragnerus, still a writhing mass of limbs and tentacles far off to the side, burst apart, with hundreds of demons shedding from his monstrous host and charging. Their paths were wavy, meant to look chaotic, but they knew their targets. Instead of engaging knights, they rushed past them when possible, hell-bent on hurling Nora’s blessed gemstones at any angel they could get in range of.

We also surged forward. Tetora was faster than I had ever seen, though the electric blue glow his boots gave off answered the obvious questions. Aurum’s horseshoes also bore the same coloring, melding a bit into the pink aura we shared. Olethros simply kept his form more of a mist, stripping away bulk for speed.

And so we descended into the battlefield—roar and all.

A thousand voices screaming orders, battle cries, death rattles. Steel rang out in discordant pulses. Swords and claws clashing, shields shredding. Blessed bolts buzzed through the air.

Aurum's hooves pounded the semi-frozen ground in steady rhythm. Tetora's claws sizzled and sparked as he ran. Somewhere to our left, a demon shrieked as a blessed bolt found its mark, the sound cutting off just as suddenly as it came.

Try as we might, nothing we planned could prevent their deaths.

Mounted knights pursued demons that passed them by. A core burned out with a hollow whoomph, the demon thankfully banished to the Bastion. Another was not so lucky—a blessed bolt hit, and a demon's shriek turned to static, sparkling dust scattering on the wind as they were erased entirely. NAUGHT no longer complained about storage restrictions for their souls, and I could only hope Relias hadn't figured out a way to bypass that protocol.

Angels jeweled demons and fellow soldiers alike with sickening efficiency. I heard the wet thunk of a sword driving through armor, followed by a choking gasp. Metal scraped metal somewhere, and someone screamed desperately for their captain. The ambient roar below the shouts never stopped, but it seemed to swallow some of the more pointed, angry shouts.

A winged angel descended before us. Tetora lunged beyond the barrier’s safety. His claws met its chest with a metallic screech, sparks flying. When he pulled back, the gemstone hehad driven through its hull gleamed, then burned. Electric blue arced across the angel, stilling it and knocking it onto its back.

"Focus!" Tetora roared to Nora as she scrambled to cover him. A blessed bolt whistled past, scratching at the side of her barrier and dimming it some. "Worry about yourself first!"

"I've got worry to spare for your antics, too!" she retorted through grit teeth. "Grraaah!"

The barrier surrounding us thickened with a resonant hum.

"Try and cancel me out, huh?!" she shouted over the din. "Well, you can't—huh?"

Everything turned dark for a moment as we moved through a portal. We came out the other side, the roar of battle shifting around us.

"Flurries?" she said, looking up at the doom-foreshadowing cloud.

They melted when they hit Nora's magic shield, dripping tiny tears along its edges.

"No effect though," I heard her mutter just behind me. "But why now?"

And why wasn't the cloud zapping our magic? Are we too far away on the ground?

The sound of hoofbeats, heavy but coordinated, thundered toward us. A squad of mounted knights that had been at the portcullis broke off to engage. Three dark portals opened before they closed half the distance. Two units vanished mid-stride with a bit of a struggle. The third managed several steps before a flurry of vines dragged rider and horse backward toward its maw.

Our next portal came sluggish, the hum taking too long to build. When it finally opened, we stumbled through just as blessed bolts detonated behind us. Another volley shrieked toward us, too numerous to count. They were from different angles, their whistles overlapping. They hit Nora's barrier in rapid succession, each loud crack reverberating.

"They're catching on," Tetora roared over the cacophony. "Coordinated spreads will bury us!"

He was right. The blessed bolts weren't random anymore. Nora's barrier flickered under the barrage, dimming with each impact.

"I'm ready," Olethros hissed from my right. "I can't collect any more animus with this gilded toy!"

Far be it from me to chide him about having a favorite yet inaccessible weapon.

"The crystal's going out like a dying light bulb!" I shouted, drawing my sword. "Let's get this over with!"

How many knights was I going to have to cut down...

"Stop the line. Slight detour!" Nora shouted sharply. At first, I thought she started cursing under her breath, but then I caught the rapid, intentional clicking of her teeth.

Is she speaking Demonic?

Her staff flared. A portal tore open directly in front of us like it was summoned in an emergency.

We plunged through, finding the translocation rough.

When we came out, Aurum skidded slightly, hooves scraping a bit as he righted himself. We were near Chairo's gate, but not the South one we'd been aiming for.

"Change of plans," Nora grunted, breathing hard. "East gate. No swarming knights, out of bolt range."

I looked around. She was right. The East Gate stood nearly undefended, as most of the Order's forces had concentrated at the South Gate, expecting us to stay there.

"Nora!" I said in full adulation, before squeaking. "I hope this isn't a trap..."

Nora raised her staff in warning—both to quiet me and to get us ready.

Right. Sorry. Doesn't matter now.

I boosted my aura even as Nora dropped her barrier for the Demon King. As Tetora and Aurum slowed, Olethros shot forward, planted his forming feet, then swung his gold scythe in a wide arc, a crackling wave of purple energy trailing as he poured his animus into it.

The blast’s ultrasonic crescendo shot above the human threshold, taking Tetora to his knees and even knocking out any angry complaints he might have made had he been able to speak. Even as the arcing energy zeroed in on the gate, Kaikias’s eye, now the closest to us, went dark.

Boom!

Nora raised her barrier again as Olethros’s blast hit, rubble flying everywhere.

Unrelated, but synchronized with the attack, the shine of the holy barrier behind the gates trickled out as each crystal's light was snuffed.

The sulfurous stench pervaded Nora's barrier before we could calculate the damage, watering our eyes. Aurum snorted with considerable irritation. Even Olethros wavered, switching back to mist so as not to have a nose.

Enemy screams from behind, however, forced us forward almost blindly.

We needn't have worried. Olethros's blast had obliterated the wall holding the gate, leaving a gaping black hole easily wide enough for an entire herd of horses to charge through—if any of them had been brave enough to approach the still-smoking crater.

Too bad the Order of Silver wasn’t in any condition to manage such a mounted retreat.

Some were on their knees. Some were flat on the ground.

Several were demonstrating exactly what happens when you spend too much time in close, unventilated quarters with angry Skreethi. It turns out that an extreme olfactory assault is incompatible with holding on to any kind of military bearing. A few were even in the middle of regurgitating their last rations, while the Skreethi politely let them finish before spraying them down once more to herd them out.

Welcome to Chairo, second cesspool of hell.
