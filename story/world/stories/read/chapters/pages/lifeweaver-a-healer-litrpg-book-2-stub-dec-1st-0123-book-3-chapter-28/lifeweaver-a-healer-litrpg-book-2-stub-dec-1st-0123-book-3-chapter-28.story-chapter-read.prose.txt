Nodding, Luke wiped his hands on his robe and straightened his back. Even then, he had to look up into her eyes. "Those Singers I met."

"Their report was an interesting read. Quite popular in our entire system for a few days. You are famous. Your two friends as well, if to a lesser degree. I haven't seen the fourth from your group. Perhaps has fallen?"

Luke didn't bother to give a reply. Not that he knew what was going on with Hiroki, who'd been the one to accompany himself, Curtis, and Hannah into that dungeon, where they fought for their lives against a group of Singers intent on killing them. Instead, he raised the hood of his cloak, activating its effect.

"Do you think that little trick will work on me?" Leminal asked, waving the two vials back and forth, threatening him with their contents.

"My friends would kill you the moment you return without me," Luke said. It wasn't a threat, just a stated fact.

"Doubtful," she scoffed. "Unlike the demons, I can survive in your system without issue. If my diplomacy isn't enough to satisfy your rulers, I'll use force instead. Taking over and preparing you for submission once the joining takes place will be a simple enough task."

"Thank you," Luke said.

That seemed to take her aback, and she waved a little. "Whatever could you mean, human?"

"I knew I would have to kill you. Taking you back with us was never an option. Not with that," he said, pointing at the vials, then at her head. "Or your knowledge of how to create more. This way, I won't have to feel bad about doing it. Self-defense, you know?"

"Bah!" she said, throwing the vials. Both bounced off the fabric of Luke's robe, some of the liquid death energy spilling out before they crashed onto the floor. Whatever was left in them pooled at his feet, and their effect was immediate.

The death energy ate into the fabric, breaking it down faster than the robe could regenerate. Even before getting through, it was already eating at Luke's skin and insides as well, not to speak of his face and hands. The pain was immense. If he hadn't practiced withstanding getting hurt without flinching, it might even have been enough to make him pass out. Now, he could bear it for the moment it took to numb the worst of the pain. Removing all of it was impossible without taking away his ability to move, and that wouldn't end well.

As it was, he'd given himself a window of opportunity in which to act. Leminal was withdrawing more vials from her satchel when the door slammed open, and her minions began pouring into the room. For a moment, Luke had thought his friends were coming to his rescue, and he breathed a sigh of relief when he saw it wasn't so. They would not live long if they stepped inside the small chamber. As it was, the healing mana in Luke's Meta-Heart had to work hard to heal his body faster than the death energy could eat through it, and with so much of it around, it was a close fight. Time was not on Luke's side either, because soon the healing mana would run out. He needed to act before it was too late.

Undead with raised weapons swarmed toward Luke while Leminal withdrew, stepping back, to the side, and out of the way so her skeletons, zombies, and liches would be able to reach Luke. A mistake, not leaving the room right away. Blades flashed, digging into Luke. Compared to the suffering the death energy put him through, it was nothing. Ignoring the multitude of wounds opening up, Luke weavestepped past the wall of monsters, ending up in front of a lich in the process of creating a spellweave.

With a flick of a thread, Luke sliced the spell apart and stepped past the monster, ignoring its grunt of surprise as he put a palm against its chest and pushed. It was light for its size and couldn't keep its footing. Stumbling backward, it crashed into Leminal, who was pulling something from her satchel. Another vial, perhaps. It didn't matter, since she wouldn't get a chance to use it.

Like with most non-combat classes who'd stood against him, she wasn't ready for Luke's use of brute force.

Snap.

Crunch.

Leminal screamed, holding up the now useless arm she'd been using to root through her satchel. Something struck Luke through the back, and the tip of a spear appeared thrust out of his chest, but Luke ignored it. Out of healing mana in his Meta-Heart, he stepped forward and away from the spear tip. Weaver's Renewal took care of the wound as he got down on one knee right in front of the still screaming Leminal.

"It always comes to this," he said, reaching into her with threads of mana.

Spellweaves formed in front of her forehead and right in the middle of his chest, but Luke cut through them, neutralizing her with little effort. Just as he was about to claim her Meta-Heart, a gray and green-tinted smoke rose up and out of her shift. Luke coughed once, and blood came up. So much blood. More death energy. Inhaled, it wreaked havoc on his lungs and the rest of his insides.

He slammed his fist into her now snarling face. Leminal's eyes rolled into the back of her head, but she somehow kept conscious, still flailing and creating spellweaves, trying to reach into her satchel. More weapons sliced into him from behind. Time was running out.

Luke used Needle of Life with practiced ease, slicing through the Singer's main mana channel, pulling on her Meta-Heart even while he drained it of mana, using that to fuel the healing of his extensive wounds, both internal and not. Even now, the death energy built inside him, eating away at mana channels and flesh alike.

Leminal died.

Her minions died with her.

Luke did not level up.

Rather than fight to stand, Luke sank to the floor, feeling the cool stone against his cheek. Working with a sort of detached, out-of-body experience, he pieced himself back together. The wounds were simple enough. A few injuries were due to having been hit with some spells. Those took a little more time to deal with, especially since he needed to keep the death energy at bay at the same time. What a pain.

The main source of his trouble took considerable effort to deal with. Cleansing himself was an arduous process of finding every last scrap of death energy and eliminating it before it could spread again. In the end, it was a lot like dealing with the radiation damage from the nuke, only worse. Not worse than the blast itself, but the lingering radiation in his body even after he'd brought himself back to life several times over. Turns out, all kinds of radiation are a bitch to cure, and the excruciating pain, even with most of it numbed, didn't help.

Unsure how much time he'd spent on the floor, Luke reached over and closed the flap to the satchel, then took it into his inventory. Ezezal's corpse hadn't spread the death energy while in his interface, but the satchel did, making Luke lose HP at a slow but constant rate.

Perhaps that was how Leminal had become immune to it? Luke could carry it around for a few hundred years, and maybe he'd be able to shrug off its effect, too?

Hannah: "What's going on, Luke? A bunch of Leminal's skeletons just fell apart."

Luke sat up against the wall, panting. The room was empty. Other than Ezezal's head, the pool of blood it rested in, and Leminal's corpse, it was empty. The undead were gone. Not just dead, but no trace of them remained.

Hannah: "You killed her, didn't you?"

Luke: "Self-defense. She tried to kill me. Turns out, the Singers we met spread the word of our little fight."

Luke: "I'll be there in a minute."

Hannah: "What about the death energy thing?"

Luke: "I have it."

Hannah: "What are you going to do with it?"

Hannah: "It's like the aftermath of a nuke in virus form."

Luke: "Not pleasant. Ask me how I know."

Ray: "I can see it on Hannah's face that she's telling you to get rid of the nuke-poison. Don't."

Luke sent the same message to both of them.

Luke: "Haven't decided what I'm going to do with Leminal's... stuff yet."

Checking the time remaining, he struggled to his feet and took Leminal's corpse into his inventory. Time was running out, so he'd have to check her for loot as he went. That done, he nodded goodbye to Ezezal's head and took his leave. The corridor was littered with cultist corpses. Conscious of needing credits, he reached out with threads of mana, touching each of them so he could put them into his inventory. This time, more than ever before, it felt a little gruesome to go around collecting the dead with the intention of selling them off to The Greater System. Still, he continued as he walked back to the others.

Even though they needed credits to start a guild, Luke selected the other option for his quest reward. Five attribute points were just too good a reward to pass up, and he put them all into Willpower, along with the ones from his level up. Ten points. It was a significant bump, but he didn't feel any different afterward. Not that he had expected to. The changes brought on by mental attributes weren't as straightforward as that, and often made themselves known only during a crisis.

The others waited for him right back where he'd left them. Even after having scoured his body for any trace of death energy several times, he did it again, seeing the others. None remained. None, except for what he carried in his inventory.

"Hey," Nick said.

Luke gave him a nod as he looked around. "Where is she?"

"THE DEMON, YOU MEAN?" Clementine shouted. "THE SPAWN OF EVIL?"

Too tired to deal with her, Luke just nodded. "Yeah."

"She's waiting by the portal," Nick said. "I've told the others what the deal is.""WE DENY HER ENTRY TO THE LAND OF OUR LORD," Clementine shouted, raising her hands into the air.

"Is she always like that?" Ray asked.

Clementine glared at Ray, but Nick ignored her. "We haven't seen demons before. Undead, yeah, but not demons."

"I don't care about what she is, but my apartment is kind of full," Curtis said.

"The Conqueror is dead. We could just kill her," Ariel suggested.

Luke shook his head. "I gave my word. Besides, I have a sneaking suspicion demons don't die just because you cut their heads off and loot their bodies."

Ray snickered. "Did you get any good loot?"

"We should get going," Nick said, waving for them to descend the stairs. He was right, too, because the portal would close in ten minutes. Getting stuck in there, like Leminal, would suck.

As they walked, Luke checked Ezezal's corpse and found that it held something. He brought it out to take a closer look.

Pendant of Memories.

There was no description to it, and no use that Luke could see other than ornamental. The pendant was shaped like an infernal core, intricate enough to look like energy swirled around it.

"Here," Hannah said, reaching over. She did something to it, and the pendant opened up.

"What's inside?" Curtis asked.

"A picture," Luke said, holding it up for the others to see. It was a girl, maybe three years old, with the smallest of horns jutting out of her head. "It's her. Lemira, I guess."

"You should give it to her," Hannah said.

He nodded in agreement and put it back into his inventory. Ezezal must've held real love for his daughter. Of course he did. Why else would he have sacrificed his life to save her? While he was rummaging through the remains, he checked Leminal's and the cultists', finding nothing. The satchel was like an itching, open sore even while closed and in his inventory, but at least it didn't affect the others. Luke still wasn't sure what he should do with it when they made it to the main entrance hall, the walls lined with huge demons regarding them. They stared but didn't move, following their now-dead leader's orders.

"Eerie," Nick said, keeping a firm grip around Clementine's arm, as if afraid she might engage them all in a fight.

The portal came into view as soon as they left the keep. Luke looked back over his shoulder, thinking about how the dungeon didn't play out like he had expected, at least not after beating the initial onslaught of undead and the demon that followed after. Ezezal mentioned how The Greater System had made a mistake in sending them against him, but Luke wasn't so sure. It didn't feel probable that a portal would open up and take them to a dungeon with an impossible challenge. Beating The Conqueror in a straight-up fight might not have worked, but they had finished the quest in the end, just in a roundabout way, and would now be leaving with a half-demon, half-Lilineese. This meant that the quest had not, by definition, been impossible. It also suggested the dungeons weren't random, in a more intelligent-design sort of way. Not handled by some deity, perhaps, but by someone or something. The Greater System being sentient was Ezezal's own theory. That might be it, but Luke's gut told him that wasn't it.

Both Leminal and Ezezal had also alluded to events that would be coming. Systems brought together, or perhaps portals between systems? If they were to stand and fight together against the void, it made sense to foster alliances. Even so, it made Luke feel uneasy, as if he wasn't in control. He knew he wasn't, but even the illusion of control was slipping through his fingers.

Dungeon closing in 6 minutes.

Lemira waited for them right by the portal, just as her father had ordered. She still wore the cultist robe with the hood up, but pulled it back when Luke and the others approached.

"Hey," Luke said.

She gave him a searching look. "My father is dead."

"He is," Luke confirmed, holding out the pendant that turned out to be a locket.

Lemira's fingers trembled a little as she accepted it, and she nodded, not saying anything.

Dungeon closing in 4 minutes.

"Everyone ready?" Nick asked.

Lemira looked up at the tall keep, her face neutral, then turned to Luke. "I am ready to leave. What of the Singer?"

"She won't be coming."

"I see," Lemira said, not giving away her feelings on the matter, if she had any.

Hannah stepped up next to Luke. "Luke, about the... stuff."

"Right," Luke said, turning to the rest of the group. "Why don't you guys get out? I have something I need to do. Clementine, can you heal Lemira until I return? It'll only be a few minutes."

"No," Clementine said. "I would rather spend eternity in hell than heal the demon spawn."

Dungeon closing in 3 minutes.

"There," he said, stepping back. "That should do it."

"I'm glad you decided to get rid of it," Hannah said.

After a couple of seconds, Leminal turned into floating motes of light. A few more and there was no trace of her. Lemira gave him a thoughtful look, but did not comment.

Dungeon closing in 2 minutes.

"Let's get out of here," Luke said, and the three stepped through the portal and out of the dungeon, leaving it behind. Hopefully, Luke wouldn't have to visit a third time.
