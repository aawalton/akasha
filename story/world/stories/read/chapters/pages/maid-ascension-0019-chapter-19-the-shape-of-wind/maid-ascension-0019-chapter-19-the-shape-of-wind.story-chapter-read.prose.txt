Lira did not sleep much that night. She lay on her narrow bed, staring up at the dark ceiling, her mind turning over the same thought again and again.

The words alone did nothing. Knowing the language was not enough, not exactly.

There was always something else, something deeper. The primer, the focus, the trance, and then the moment where everything aligned: the click.

She turned onto her side and pulled the thin blanket tighter around herself. If the primer was the key, then the spell was not the words alone.

The words were only the outer shape of it.

The real question lingered in her mind long after her eyes closed.

Could she make her own key?

The next morning, Lira woke before the bell. The dormitory was still half asleep, filled with slow breathing and the faint rustle of shifting bodies. She slipped out quietly, careful not to wake Tessa in the next bed, and made her way into the corridor.

The castle felt different at this hour, quieter, almost hollow. She moved quickly through the familiar servant paths, head lowered out of habit. She did not want to be seen, not today, not when she felt so close to something important.

She returned to the library tower and slipped inside her alcove. The hidden space was just as she had left it, untouched and forgotten by the rest of the castle. Lira pulled the tapestry back into place, sealing herself in, and sat down. She laid out the ledger, the fire primer, and the water primer in front of her with careful precision.

Her eyes lingered on the water primer as she opened it again, scanning the same lines she had read the day before.

“If the trance led to the learning of the spell, then the prayer created the trance,” she murmured to herself. “That means I need to enter that same state again.”

The idea felt solid, but incomplete. She leaned back slightly, thinking. How could she reach that state without a primer? Or maybe… could she build one herself?

The thought came suddenly.

She stood, exiting the alcove, and moved toward the shelves, scanning the scattered books until she found what she needed. The first was a thin collection of poetry about nature and the seasons; she had remembered stacking them when she was working with Tessa. She brought it back to her alcove and set it down, then took an empty scroll and began copying lines, selecting only those that spoke of wind. Between each passage, she carefully wrote the incantation she had constructed.

Pika. Riad. Frushum.; Mind. wind. Manipulation.

When she finished, she sat back down and began to read.

She tried to lose herself in it, to let the words carry her into that same quiet place the primers had brought her before. The poems spoke of breeze and storm, of leaves dancing in the air, of unseen currents brushing against the skin, of lightness and flight. She read slowly, focusing on the feeling behind the words, rather than the meaning.

Nothing happened.

She reached the end of the scroll and lowered it slightly, her expression tightening. The trance did not come. The stillness remained just out of reach.

Lira frowned. Maybe it was not enough. Maybe the words themselves mattered more than she thought, or the way they were written, or the intent behind them. She considered the materials, the ink, or the age of the primers. Perhaps there was something she was missing.

She set the scroll aside and stood again.

This time, she searched for something different. A book of natural philosophy caught her attention, its pages filled with theories about air and motion. She skimmed through it, then began copying passages onto another scroll, choosing sections that described how wind moved, how pressure shifted, how currents formed and flowed.

Once she finished, she sat down again and began to read.

The words were more rigid this time, less poetic, and more precise. They spoke of cause and effect, of invisible forces shaping the movement of air. She focused on them, trying to understand, trying to feel the concept behind them.

Still nothing.

The silence pressed in around her.

This seemed like a step backward, if she was honest. Both primers never worked when she focused on meaning rather than feeling, but still her gut feeling told her that she wouldn't be able to master the wind if she didn’t understand it.

Lira exhaled slowly, frustration building. She set the scroll aside and rubbed her eyes.

Her gaze drifted across the alcove until it landed on another book, one she had barely skimmed through when she first discovered the alcove and was happy to read anything and everything. A manual on martial arts, worn and faded, with drawings of offensive and defensive movement. She flipped through it until she found a section on breathing and meditation.

She followed the instructions carefully, emptying her mind, slowing her breathing, letting her thoughts drift away until there was nothing left but stillness. When she felt ready, she picked up her makeshift primers again and tried to read, first the philosophy, then the poetry.

Nothing changed.

The trance remained out of reach.

Lira lowered the scroll and stared at it, her jaw tightening. She was missing something.

She tried appreciating the wind and understanding the wind. Maybe she should feel the wind.

She left the alcove behind. The library's main room was on the second floor of the tower, its windows shut tight. Despite that, the air was always drafty, the wind seeping in through seams in the walls, stirring up dust and making the space unbearably cold. She crossed the room and began opening the windows one by one.

The draft increased tenfold.

Air rushed through the space, a strong current cutting from one side to the other. Dust and debris lifted from the floor, swirling into the air. Her hair stirred around her face as the still room came alive with motion. Loose paper flew upward, almost touching the high ceiling.

Lira stepped into the center of it and sat down, crossing her legs.

The cold bit into her skin, sharp and uncomfortable. For a moment, she considered casting the warmth spell, then dismissed the thought. That would defeat the purpose.

She closed her eyes.

Starting with meditation, she emptied her mind of distraction, ignoring the feeling of cold and the loud sound of paper and books getting shuffled around. She focused on the wind itself, on the way it moved around her, the way it pressed and shifted, and the way it carried everything in its path. The philosophical text she read guided her understanding that the library space had been closed for so long, and now that it was finally opened to nature, the air inside was finally moving from west to east, just like the air outside. She felt the wind against her skin, listened to its movement, and thought about how she felt about it. Guided by poetry, she remembered being on her father's shoulders, the wind pushing her hair. She remembered the wind hitting her ragged shelter as she slept outside after her father’s death. She remembered the library draft messing up her work after she dusted on the first day. Giddiness, fear, frustration.

Her breathing slowed.

Her thoughts quieted.

She did not think about the spell, not at first. She thought about the wind, felt the wind, loved the wind, hated it, understood it.

Gradually, something aligned.

“Pika. Riad. Frushum,” she whispered.

Click.

The sensation was immediate. The same quiet certainty settled into her mind, deeper than logic. She knew… she just knew that the spell took shape.

Her eyes opened.

She raised her hand and spoke the incantation again.

“Pika. Riad. Frushum,”

Her will followed the feeling she had just grasped. She imagined the wind inside this open space, the dust in the air gathering, moving, flowing as the wind carried it.

The air responded to her. All the loose pieces of paper rose to the ceiling, while the swirling dust shifted, drawn toward the easternmost window in a single, controlled current. A cloud of debris rushed outward and vanished into the open sky.

Lira stared at it, her breath catching.

She had done it. She had controlled the wind.

Then the world tilted.

A sharp pain struck behind her eyes, and her vision blurred. The strength left her body all at once, and she collapsed onto the floor before she could catch herself.

Darkness followed.
