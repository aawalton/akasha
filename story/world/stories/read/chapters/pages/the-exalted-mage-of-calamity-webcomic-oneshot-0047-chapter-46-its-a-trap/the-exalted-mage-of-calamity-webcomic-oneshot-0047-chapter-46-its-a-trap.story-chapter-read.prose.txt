Veronica followed a trail of blood and goblin corpses. She slowed when she saw figures break through the treeline. Gwen caught up with her a moment later. They both stared at the scene ahead, carefully hidden behind a large tree.

“What’s going on?” Gwen whispered.

Veronica peered forward, unsure what to make of it. For now, she waited and listened, focusing her hearing and drowning out any unnecessary noise.

“Don’t make this any harder than it needs to be. The poison is already working through your system. It doesn’t matter if you kill us. We don’t have the antidote.”

The voice came from one of a pair: a male voice.

They wore gray cloaks, daggers in hand. The blades were wet; the metal was covered in a mix of blood and something else. Green-colored, if her eyes were to be believed. She could only assume they were bandits.

“We wouldn’t be stupid enough to stab ourselves, so we obviously didn’t bring one,” said the other, also male.

They had their backs to Veronica from her angle; she could only judge by their voices. The third person, however, was clearly not with them.

A woman in her early twenties stood with one knee bent, one hand clutching her side. Her other arm held a bow, ready to nock an arrow at a moment’s notice. She had short red hair that barely brushed her shoulders. She looked like a mercenary or assassin, dressed in dark leather gear with thin metal plates—greaves and small shoulder guards.

Her skin was pale. But her face—it was far worse than pale.

“Is… did they stab her?” Gwen whispered.

“Looks like it,” Veronica replied. She still wasn’t sure what was happening. Were they all trying to steal her commission? Had one of them arrived first, and now a dispute had broken out?

The corner of Veronica’s mouth fell.

Those merchants really had to yell commission out loud, huh? she thought, seething.

All three of them might be after her vix and Hartivel vines.

The red-haired woman spotted her, eyes snapping in her direction.

“Hey! You’re finally here!” she called out.

Huh?

Both cloaked men turned to face Veronica.

“You—argh!” one of them shouted.

In that brief moment of distraction, the woman reached behind her and threw a short dagger. It struck one of them in the abdomen.

The other gasped, eyes darting wildly between their injured partner, the woman, and Veronica and Gwen. The wounded man gripped the dagger’s handle, ready to pull it out, but his partner grabbed his wrist.

“Idiot! Don’t pull that out! You’ll bleed out!” he shouted.

The red-haired woman moved immediately. She nocked an arrow from the quiver at her back and fired.

Both cloaked figures looked up just in time to see it approaching. The uninjured one leapt aside, but the stabbed man faltered, pain crippling him as he tried to move.

The arrow struck him square in the chest. His body fell backwards, landing on the ground, unmoving.

Gwen leaned closer. “Do you know her?” she whispered.

“No—she just used me as a distraction,” Veronica said flatly.

“Damn it!” the remaining man shouted as his partner fell. He turned, gripping his dagger tightly, and rushed toward the red-haired woman.

She nocked another arrow and fired. The man sidestepped it cleanly, closing the distance. Before she could string another shot, he lunged, dagger thrust forward.

“Die!”

With Veronica’s enhanced senses, she saw what happened next: an impressive, quick maneuver.

The woman lifted her bow, catching the blade along its crooked edge and knocking the weapon from the man’s hand. He stumbled, tripping over his own momentum. He fell into her, but she twisted sharply, grabbed him, and spun him onto the ground before dropping to her knees on top of him.

The man groaned as his back slammed into the dirt, his head spinning from the sudden disorientation. When he opened his eyes and realized what had happened, the last thing he saw was the woman’s face, and a crooked smile.

No words left his throat as she pulled an arrow from her quiver and drove it into the side of his neck.

She huffed a few breaths before standing and looking over at Veronica and Gwen.

“Looks like we’re up,” Veronica said, stepping out of the treeline into the open. Gwen followed, staying just behind her.

They stared at each other for a long moment.

“So—” Veronica began, “are you here to steal my commission too?”

Several emotions flickered across the woman’s face before settling into exhaustion and frustration.

“Damn. Just my luck,” she muttered.

“What was that?” Veronica asked.

“Nothing. Keep your commission.” She turned and began walking away slowly.

Veronica placed her hands on her hips, baffled. “What’s her deal?” she murmured.

Gwen stepped beside her, eyes strangely focused. Veronica noticed her expression.

“What? Something wrong?” she asked.

“She… she looks a bit familiar,” Gwen replied.

Veronica glanced at the retreating woman. She was still clutching her stomach, walking with a slight limp.

When Veronica looked back, Gwen was staring at the two dead bodies.

“Hmm… maybe this is a good time for a lesson,” Veronica said. “Hey!” she called out.

The red-haired woman paused and turned slightly.

“I see you’re still injured. Want a free heal? I’m trying to teach my student a thing or two about magic. How about it?”

The woman narrowed her eyes. “How do I know you won’t attack me like those two did?”

Veronica’s gaze drifted to the corpses. Her lips twisted to the side. “I guess you can’t. But I would’ve attacked you already if I wanted to.”

Silence stretched between them. The woman lifted her hand from her side and stared at her palm for a moment. Finally, she shifted her position to face them directly.

“Nothing’s free. What’s your price?”

“Your time,” Veronica replied. “I’m teaching her, so the healing might take longer than usual—if you’re fine with that.”

The woman’s eyes shifted to Gwen, still in her waitress uniform. The odd choice of outfit, so far into the woods, might’ve actually worked in their favor.

“Fine,” she said at last. “Beats paying the healers in Ronswick.”

Veronica looked at Gwen. “Alright. Time to show me your healing magic.”

“Alright,” Veronica said. “Show me the understructure.”

Gwen obeyed at once. Her spell circle widened, its surface peeling back as the layered equations that supported it unfolded into view.

It was only moments prior that Veronica understood the girl’s second path.

Gwen followed the Path of Blooming… and the Path of Longevity.

“You’re using the Tier-2 spell Antidote,” Veronica observed. “With Longevity as your second path, your immediate healing output is lower—but the spell becomes far more efficient over time.”

The red-haired woman rested against the trunk of a tree, propped upright by its rough bark. Her dark leather top had been unfastened, exposing the injury across her abdomen.

A narrow stab wound marked her skin, the surface already closed, but beneath it, a sickly green tint lingered, the poison festering just out of sight. Gwen held her hands above the wound, the full geometry of her spell matrix glowing between her palms.

Mages who followed Gwen's path combination had weaker upfront healing but significantly prolonged effects. Most Blooming mages could mend wounds quickly, but Longevity allowed for continuous healing through spell layering. With enough overlapping spells, they could keep someone alive through nearly anything—so long as it wasn’t immediately fatal.

In battle, they excelled in wars of attrition. Their magic could sustain allies, endure environmental strain, and outlast drawn-out conflicts. Aside from imbued artifacts, such mages were essential for travel through the Ranavaul Empire to the east, where relentless ice and snow demanded constant warming spells just to survive.

“These here,” Veronica said, pointing to the outer edges of the spell frame, “are inefficient. You’re using Tier-1 logic to construct them. Learned it from a basic guide, right?”

Gwen nodded. “Yeah. Um… The Blooming Mage’s Guide to Healing.”

“Volume 8?” Veronica asked.

Gwen shook her head. “No. Volume 6—the latest one. I don’t think they’ve made an eighth edition yet.”

Ah. Right.

“Look here,” Veronica continued, indicating a cluster of sigils near the perimeter. “Your objective with Antidote is to guide your mana so it seeks out and neutralizes toxins in the patient’s system. This configuration works, but it’s still structured like a Tier-1 pathway, not Tier-2. You’re expending a burst of mana on a process that should unfold gradually.”

Gwen’s lips thinned as she studied the flaw.

“When you advanced to Tier-2, you gained access to additional variables, correct?”

She nodded.

“Then break this section apart and weave in the new variables. Let them stabilize together. Once they settle, replace this entire segment with the refined structure.”

Minutes passed in quiet concentration as Gwen carefully reworked the equation under Veronica’s watchful eye.

The woman against the tree remained silent despite the poison still coursing through her. Either the toxin was mild… or she possessed a far higher tolerance for pain than her appearance suggested.

Every so often, her sharp gaze drifted toward Veronica.

There was something unsettling about her eyes. It wasn’t hostile. Nothing that simple to explain. Instead, it was more like being examined, as though the woman could see past skin and bone and into whatever lay beneath. Veronica found herself glancing away almost reflexively.

At last, Gwen spoke.

“Okay… I think I found a matching equation. You want me to replace this section with the revised variables?” she asked.

“Go ahead,” Veronica said. “Then cast Antidote.”

Gwen made the adjustment and activated the spell. The circle flared softly.

Her eyes widened almost immediately.

“Whoa,” she breathed. “It’s using the same amount of mana, but… it feels stronger.”

“That’s because it is,” Veronica replied. “With your pathway, the spell will operate more efficiently and persist longer. It’ll continue removing residual traces even after most of the poison has already been neutralized.”

This wasn’t a specialized insight unique to Veronica. It was advanced foundational theory; the kind taught in higher academies to refine lower-tier spellwork. But Gwen had never received that kind of education.

So they would start with the basics.

Strengthen her foundation first. Only then could she stand on equal footing with the so-called prodigies.

Of course, there were slight inefficiencies and drawbacks with the method she explained, but fixing them could come later. It was advanced theory for a reason.

“Go ahead and cast the healing spell again,” Veronica said, “and replace every instance of the old equation.”

She watched closely as Gwen wove the magic. The sickly green discoloration beneath the woman’s skin continued to recede, shrinking inward as the poison was gradually forced out. With the refined healing layered over it, the wound sealed more cleanly, the skin knitting together with renewed steadiness as its natural color returned.

Only then did Veronica properly notice how toned the woman was. There was strength in the lines of her arms and abdomen; clear evidence of long training and a body honed for combat. She wasn’t muscular by any means, but she definitely had an admirable figure.

Hopefully, Veronica thought, she could reach something similar in time. Especially with Sage’s continuous upgrades, like the Ascended Body spell it had granted her.

“There,” Gwen said at last, lowering her hands. “You’ll be fine. It should finish healing on its own in a few minutes.”

They all rose.

The woman glanced down at her stomach, flexing slightly as she examined the fading injury. She gave a quiet hum of approval. “Thanks.”

“Thanks for being the test subject,” Veronica replied lightly. “Now—mind telling us what happened here? And who those two are?”

Their attention shifted to the pair of bodies lying nearby in the grass.

The woman pulled her shirt down and zipped up her leather tunic. “I heard rumors early this morning about a commission. Something about clearing goblins for a thousand vix and some rare alchemy ingredient. Figured I’d handle it before whoever officially took it arrived.”

She turned away, slinging her bow across her back. “Looks like I heard the rumor too late, though. Since you showed up.” Her eyes flicked briefly to the corpses. “Those two were hiding inside the wagon. Not sure how the goblins missed them. They’d rigged it with paralysis powder. Stabbed me before I could get distance.”

“Paralysis…” Gwen murmured.

“Luckily, I didn’t inhale much. I could move again by the time you two arrived.”

Veronica frowned.

So it had been a trap after all. Those merchants would face heavy consequences once she returned to town—especially if she dragged back the bodies of their accomplices.

“Even though I killed the goblins, you can still take your commission. I won’t make a fuss. Guild rules,” the woman said. She turned toward Veronica. “Give me your hand.”

“What?”

She simply stared at her.

Veronica relented under that gaze and extended her hand.

The woman took it, then lifted her leg and reached into her boot, pulling out a small pen.

She wrote something on Veronica’s palm.

When she finished, she tucked the pen away and released her. “I don’t like owing debts. That’s payment for the healing.”

She turned east without another word. “Name’s Nasha.”

“Veronica,” she replied. “And this is Gwen.”

Nasha nodded. And just like that, she broke into a run, disappearing through the trees within moments—as if she’d never been injured at all.

“What did she write?” Gwen asked.

Veronica looked down at her palm.

“Friday. Crimson Court. 9 PM. —Nasha.”

Gwen suddenly raised both hands to her mouth, as if she’d just been handed a billion vix.

“What?”

“No wonder I recognized her…” Gwen said, her voice muffled behind her palms.

Veronica raised a brow. “Who?”
