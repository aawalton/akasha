Another explosion blew dust and stone debris down from the summon wall, covering Hunter. As dark energy refilled the open revolver cylinder, he glanced at the wizard.

“Should we go find Sally? Feels wrong holding this mountain without her.”

Archie winced at the sound of the distant mortar firing again. “As long as she keeps a calm and clear mind, she’ll be fine. No sense in getting ourselves killed moving from our position.”

The shadowy man grunted, giving a half-shrug. “We’re sitting ducks, anyway.”

“Well, unless you can use that decaying beam again.”

“I can’t.” Hunter clicked the cylinder back into place. “Even if I could, I can’t see where they are firing from. Can’t you just create somethin’?”

Drumming his fingers against the wall, Archie furrowed his brow. Much like their new ally, he was still only a part of the whole. With his full memories and power, he would make quick work of this Radoch threat. Right now, he just felt tired and amateurish.

They both ducked as the mortal shell struck the mountain behind them, killing two of the skeletal dwarves. Clouds of debris ran down through where they were crouched, obscuring their vision.

Archie could sense the approaching Radochs, to some degree. It was more accurate to assume he was able to feel an echo of the data sent from the Omen. He wasn’t a part of the same System just yet, but he could almost subconsciously interact with it.

All this power, and for what? To cower away? Sally had been correct, he thought. He was an Outsider now, regardless of past lives or guilt. All those years traveling alongside the group, he should have learned something…

“Their flank has encircled Sally’s position,” he ventured. “Rather than surround her, they are pushing to keep us in place, leaving her to face their strongest alone.”

“Peachy.” Hunter pulled back the hammer on his gun. “So, what’s the plan?”

Archie held out his hand, and a small wireframe square of blue light appeared.

“I don’t know,” he replied. “But it’s going to make someone’s day a lot worse.”

Things could be better. Theo smiled to himself as he rose from the bloodied ground. It was easy to forget that some of his powers were unavailable. Muscle memory occasionally kicked in, and he poured his energy toward… something that wasn’t there.

Rather embarrassing to run right into the swinging club of the large Jawgore, but thankfully the others were more focused on dealing with their own monsters.

Massacring the smaller fish-creatures had boosted the stats of these giant three by almost thirty times their natural state. On this world, that not only meant increased speed, damage, and health, but appeared to also improve damage reduction and resistance thresholds.

Adjusting his grip on the hammer, he darted forward. “I too buck diminishing returns.”

Pink light engulfed his weapon as he struck the bipedal shark. Although it didn’t even bruise the creature, the hit allowed a follow-up strike to come out. The club came down and cracked the courtyard stone, but the vampire had already moved away. A trail of pink blurred through the air as hits mounted up, and his attack speed increased.

A white dove fluttered in the air beside the western monster, circling around its head. In an instant, it switched places with Ren. The elf held the rifle in mid-air and pulled the trigger, almost point-blank to the Jawgore.

The creature staggered, but lashed back out. A puff of feathers and blood burst from the crushing grip as Ren switched back with the summoned bird. The Jawgore seemed only irritated by the shot, briefly confused as to why there was a dead animal in its grip and not the suited magician instead.

Back at a distance, Ren racked the bolt of the rifle and fired an entangling shot. Roots grew up from between flagstones and gripped at the monster, holding it in place. She lowered the weapon and then held out her hand.

“How tiresome,” she muttered. Above the courtyard in the muted sky, several dark clouds began to form.

Max rolled away as splinters of the struck chair burst around him. Back up on his feet, he dusted off his suit while grinning at the Jawgore. “Eager to see one of my greatest tricks? You’ll have to be a little patient.”

The monster growled and brought the club back up for a second hit.

With a gesture, the magician stepped to the side and held out a velvet curtain. After a brief flourish, Max swept away the fabric to reveal a demonic hound - which immediately leaped toward the Jawgore.

As the summoned demon stole the attention of the large opponent, Max withdrew a purple card and held it between two fingers. He hummed a showtune under his breath as the energy pooling into the small rectangle grew brighter.

Just a little longer, and he’d win this with the flick of his wrist.

Sally stumbled forward into a tree, dropping her dagger again as she bent her wrist from the impact.

“Sssstop running,” she hissed at the escaping weapon. After stooping over to retrieve it, she wiped the excessive drool from her mouth.

Her brain was turning to mush.

For someone as experienced as Sally Danger, it was nowhere near the first time this had happened. She was aware of the signs that madness brought. That didn’t mean she was any more capable of keeping herself together, but she was at least a conscious participant in what her current plan was.

Which was eating the alluring brain.

“These arms aren’t fun,” she complained to a mostly quiet forest. After killing the fast Radoch, there had been no further interruptions to her journey down the mountain.

A sure sign that this was certainly a trap. If it weren’t for the occasional distant explosion or crack of falling trees, it almost seemed as though this was just a normal day, without an alien threat waiting to kill or capture her.

Not that capture particularly worried her. She stopped for a moment to rest against a tree, and beads of sweat dripped onto dry leaves.

Theo had once been captured, but had escaped. How difficult could it be? Maybe she could learn some Radoch secrets, destroy more of their giant spaceships, or snack on a few neat brains along the way. She certainly had the appetite for it.

Then again, that wasn’t the current plan - which she had to stick to.

Her feet took her forward again, meandering around the bushy undergrowth and ragged rocks. The arcane tome still floated behind her, but it lagged in the air, drooping or sweeping left and right just as much as she did.

Eventually, either by luck or guided by her stomach, she stumbled her way into a flat clearing.

She blinked away the blur in her eyes to see that someone had been waiting for her here. No - several people. In fact, as she turned her head, the outer perimeter of the clearing was thick with Radochs. Tall ones. Well-armored ones. All different shapes and sizes, differing from the rank and file. Elites and special forces.

But the one in front of her was unlike any other she had seen. He sat upon a throne carried by several other cockroaches. Despite having the same dark eyes and gnashing mandibles as the rest, this leader of the bugs somehow had an oppressively smug aura around him.

“Blech.” Sally stopped in place. “Have you come to parley, space pirates?” Her eyes wanted to close, but she fought it.

Graken clacked his fingers together in glee. “See how pitiful these System cattle are? This rotten maggot is supposedly one of their great heroes.”

Sally twirled the dagger in her hand. “Hell is littered with those who underestimated me, so this is nothing novel. Where you really messed up is by making me hungry.”

“A simple siren-song.” The large Radoch gestured dismissively. “Few of your kind can detach themselves from their base desires.”

“Too much talky.” She gave the circle of elites a dull glance. “I guess you wanted to watch me pry the skull open of your chosen champion? Pick who goes first.”

Graken leaned forward in his chair, briefly putting pressure on the front load bearers. “I’ve already lost plenty thanks to you and your ilk.” He raised his upper right arm and clicked sharp fingers together.

One of the lackeys in the crowd tapped at a held device. Before Sally had the chance to question what shenanigans they were up to, a wave of energy pulsed around the clearing. She clenched her teeth and looked up.

“A force-field,” she muttered. “To contain me.”

“Not quite.”

Sally felt a fresh wave of exhaustion run through her body. As she sighed by reflex, her two borrowed arms fell from her torso. After shooting them a forlorn look, she narrowed her eyes back at the alien leader.

“Power negation.” She chuckled. “You think I need powers to kill you all?”

“Yes?”

“That’s the difference between you and I,” she mumbled, stepping forward to keep her balance. “Other than a now greater disparity of working limbs. I believe there’s always a chance, and I do whatever is necessary to win.”

Graken stared blankly at the zombie before shrugging. “Believe your delusions. I had planned to use you as a hostage to contain the others, but I might store you away if you continue to spout nonsense.”

Sally grinned to herself. The mania had tapered away as soon as they turned off the lure. Anything remaining was just her usual goofball persona, plus the irritation of losing her arms. It put her in quite an awkward situation.

Fighting against twenty elite or specialized Radochs at once was a big ask. Without her powers… still possible, but unlikely. Lacking arms, the chances were extremely slim. She wasn’t trained in kicking, and her feet weren’t dexterous enough to wield her dagger that way. Knowledge born of many lost toes in the past.

But here she was. Fire burning in her soul, and an enemy that needed to be vanquished before her.

The one thing on her side was that the Radochs didn’t know about her new, unsettled System. It didn’t allow her to break their containment, but her fingers were feeling at the edges, trying to tear their way through.

“Stow me where?” she said. “You have no ships and no way off this planet.”

“Hah.” Graken shook his head. “Backup has been called, and should be here any-”

A heavy thrum burst through the air above them, blowing trees around and clearing the sky of smoke. The atmosphere itself crackled as a Radoch spaceship popped into existence. Darkness covered the clearing, along with half of the mountainside.

The Radoch leader clicked his fingers at one of his underlings. “Hail the vessel and find out who answered the distress call. Arrange collection.”

Sally ran her tongue across dry lips. Red fire flickered around her eyes, illuminating the darkness. Exhaling, she slid her right foot back into the dirt, getting into a martial pose.

“Archie, forgive me.” She grinned. “But I’ll have to go all-out, just this once.”
