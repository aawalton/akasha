In the early morning hours of another day, Ace stared into the small fire he had built for the team.

Firelight played across everyone’s faces, casting more shadows than warmth. The small blaze they'd built in their secluded clearing within Gloomvale Woods did little to dispel the icy silence that had settled over the group—a chill that had nothing to do with the temperature and everything to do with what the System had told them.

Ace sat with his back against a gnarled oak, one knee drawn up, his eyes constantly scanning the darkness beyond their small circle of light. His Phantom Step Resonance Boots hummed with sensory input, mapping the ground around them through vibrations too subtle for normal perception. The forest breathed around them—animals skittering through the underbrush, night insects buzzing in the canopy, the occasional distant howl that reminded them they weren't alone in these woods.

Moonfall lay across his lap, its silver surface catching the firelight in ways that made the blade appear almost liquid. Now that they had a moment of respite after the slugs, he could celebrate hitting Level 25.

And with that new Level came new gear.

As the silence weighed on the group, he accessed his inventory through the system screens and tapped on the gauntlets he had acquired a few days ago but hadn't been able to use due to level restrictions. To his delight, the gear notification had changed since he'd last checked it. The red text barring him from using the gear had now changed into a shiny new “Equip” button.

He tapped it. The Dominator's Gauntlets materialized on his hands, and goddamn were they beautiful.

The gear gleamed in the moonlight, their darkened steel absorbing more light than they reflected. Small runes covered the steel plates, barely visible even to his enhanced vision. The metal felt cool against his skin, but quickly warmed as it responded to his touch. When he flexed his fingers, the gauntlet moved with perfect fluidity, the joints bending without resistance.

Ace examined the gauntlets, getting a feel for their surprising lightness despite their sturdy build. They looked like they belonged to another era—not quite medieval, not quite modern, but something between, as if designed by someone who had witnessed centuries of warfare and distilled the most effective elements into a single design.

A system screen flashed in front of him, warning him that the gear required blood to attune, as well as 150 HP.

Of course.

Ace slid his left hand out of the second gauntlet and ran Moonfall across his palm. Pain lanced through his hand and up his forearm as the blood pooled in the grooves of his palm. Without pausing, he poured the slow trickle of blood onto each of the gauntlets.

The gear absorbed the blood instantly. It flowed into the metal, awakening something that had lain dormant.

A sharp jolt shot through him as it took the HP it needed to attune, and he gritted his teeth from the unexpected blow. The sensation wasn't just physical—it resonated on a deeper level, as if the gauntlets were absorbing more than just his blood.

After a few moments, a subtle crimson light traced the runes etched into the gauntlets’ surface. It ebbed and flowed, glowing with a surreal shine as the lingering pain faded. He flexed his fingers again, and this time, power hummed through the metal and resonated with something primal in his soul.

"Jesus," he said under his breath.

This wasn’t just gear.

They were hungry.

He glanced up to find the others huddled around the fire, completely oblivious to his new gauntlets. No one spoke, and the silence between them all stretched on.

As the intensity of fusing with his new gauntlets faded, Ace’s fingers absently traced the runes etched along Moonfall’s blade. The System's words about the Evolutionary Keystone replayed in his mind on a loop. Her offer hung between him and his team like a physical presence—one of them could either surrender the Keystone for guaranteed rewards or risk using it themselves.

The unspoken part remained loudest: only one of them could claim it.

Tara sat cross-legged opposite him, her hands busy sorting through her recently acquired healing supplies. Marcus poked at the fire with a stick, his face drawn and haunted in the flickering light. Olivia stood at the edge of their circle, almost more shadow than substance, her eyes reflecting the flames with unnerving intensity.

None of them had spoken for nearly an hour.

Ace cleared his throat, the sound startling in the quiet. "We need to address the elephant in the room."

Three pairs of eyes shifted to him.

Nobody responded.

"The Keystone." He set Moonfall aside and leaned forward. "You heard what she said. Only one person can use it."

Marcus dropped his stick and met Ace's gaze. "Do you think she was telling the truth about what it does?"

"Evolutionary control?" Ace shrugged. "It's possible. But remember who told us. The System lies."

"Not always," Olivia said, her voice unsettlingly calm. "Sometimes the truth hurts more than lies."

As she spoke, however, something peculiar happened—something that had nothing to do with what she had said or how she had said it.

Ace felt a cold ripple—like water sliding down his spine—but the sensation wasn't physical. It wasn't his own emotion either. It felt... external yet intimately connected to him, carrying impressions of calculated, detached interest.

He blinked, momentarily distracted.

"We should at least consider her offer," Marcus said, rubbing his hands together. "The Veil Key and Twilight Circlet sound valuable. And that title would boost everyone's stats. No matter who gets the Keystone, there’s enough loot there to trade between the lot of us."

As Marcus spoke, Ace experienced another foreign sensation—a flutter of anxiety tinged with hope, like standing at the edge of a cliff and looking down. The feeling vanished as quickly as it had come, leaving Ace momentarily confused.

"Does no one wonder why she wants that?" Tara finally joined the conversation, setting aside her supplies. "The System can’t evolve. What on Earth is she going to do with it? Do we really believe the System will just reward us and let us continue on our way?"

Ace felt her uncertainty—not through her expression or tone, but as a direct impression in his mind. Concern. Wariness. Trust and doubt swirling together until they were indistinguishable.

“Good…” He faltered mid-breath, the strange sensations momentarily overwhelming him. “That is, uh… yeah, good point.”

"Ace?" Tara's voice cut through his confusion. "Are you all right?"

Three pairs of eyes fixed on him with varying degrees of concern.

"Fine," he said, rubbing his temple. "Just... tired. The slug battle took more out of me than I realized."

"You did pretty much die for a moment there," Marcus pointed out.

"Thanks for the reminder."

Marcus grinned, apparently unable to resist making a joke despite the circumstances. “Anytime, boss.”

"We all need rest," Tara interjected. "Maybe we should table this for now."

“Maybe you’re right.” Ace shook his head, trying to push the strange sensations aside. “What’s the point of making plans when we’re not even in the Crucible yet?”

“Because we have no idea what’s in there,” Olivia answered, her cold eyes focusing on Ace. “Or who might get greedy once we’re inside.”

A ribbon of fear snaked through the air from her like a firework, and a small muscle twitched at the corner of her eye.

Interesting.

“Alright,” Ace said with an exhausted sigh. “Look, here's the reality of the situation—we don’t have enough information to make any decisions right now. We don’t know what’s waiting for us, what traps there might be, or who can even get to the Keystone. Who knows where it’s hidden? What traps are in the way? What we’ll need to do as a team,” he emphasized the word with a quick glance toward Olivia, “once we’re in there.”

“So?” Marcus pressed. “It’s still an important thing to discuss.”

“Sure it is,” Ace agreed with a shrug. “But we'll deal with the Keystone when we find it—if we find it. The Crucible itself is the immediate challenge.”

Everyone glanced down at the fire, and for a time, no one spoke.

He, however, had a pretty good idea of what was on everyone’s mind.

“Then there’s the question of us,” he pointed out. “I know you’ve all thought of it at least once. The thing no one wants to bring up, but which I’m sure everyone has considered at some point.”

“And that is?” Tara asked.

“Do we stay together as a team?” Ace replied. “Knowing only one of us can ultimately get the Keystone?"

His question hung in the air, and no one replied. Ace studied their faces, but now he paid closer attention to the odd impressions that seemed to be seeping into his consciousness.

The sensations didn't come through his eyes or ears—they bypassed his physical senses entirely, flowing directly into his mind like water finding cracks in a stone. Each impression carried a distinct signature, as unique as a fingerprint but composed of emotional residue rather than whorls and lines.

From Marcus came a tangled knot of conflicting impulses. The predominant thread was loyalty—not born from devotion but from the desperate need to belong. Beneath that ran a current of fear, raw and primal, but what disturbed Ace most was the undercurrent of temptation. Marcus's thoughts kept circling back to the System's offer, like a tongue probing a sore tooth. It didn’t seem like he could help himself.

Tara's presence in his mind felt warmest—her emotions ran deep and clear, like a river with powerful currents beneath a smooth surface. He sensed both her iron-clad determination and her doubt. Most surprisingly, he felt her concern for him specifically—not just for his physical recovery, but something more. Something he couldn’t quite name, but which felt heavy and ominous.

What the actual hell is happening to me?

The thought rang through his head even as his attention shifted toward Olivia. The woman's internal landscape felt vastly different—barren and cold, with emotions that seemed increasingly abstract rather than raw and real. Whatever sensations were rolling off of her reminded Ace of a chess player calculating moves, except the pieces were other people’s lives, and the stakes went far beyond a casual game. The most unsettling aspect wasn't what he sensed from her, but what he didn't—compassion.

She felt nothing.

Instead, he sensed a surreal appreciation for violence and suffering, and that left him unsettled. Olivia seemed to be losing her grasp on reality more and more by the day.

The experience left Ace on edge. These weren't simple hunches or intuitive leaps—the impressions carried too much specificity, too much texture, and too much intuition to be his own thoughts. They flowed through channels that shouldn't exist, creating connections where there should be boundaries.

His throat tightened as a new thought emerged—maybe they could sense him, too. Maybe they could sense his doubts, his rage, his growing comfort with bloodshed and brutality.

The idea left him feeling vulnerable in ways that physical danger never had. As a soldier, he'd mastered compartmentalization, keeping his darker impulses sealed away from his conscious mind.

Now, however, those mental walls weakened by the day. He wasn't sure if the System was breaking him down to the bone or building him into something new entirely.

Maybe both.

Bit by bit, hour by hour, his grip on his old life was fading, and this brutal new world was taking over.

And, if he was being honest with himself, he kind of liked it.
