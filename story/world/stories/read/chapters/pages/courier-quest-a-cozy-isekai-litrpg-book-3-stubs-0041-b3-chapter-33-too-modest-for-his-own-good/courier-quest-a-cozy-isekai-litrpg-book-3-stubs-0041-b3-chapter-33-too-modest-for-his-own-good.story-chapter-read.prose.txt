Parasailing had been exactly what they had wanted, even Clay. Trailing from behind a massive airship hundreds of feet in the air while attached by two ropes had been a thrilling experience that didn’t overstay its welcome. Afterwards, Trevor and Adabelle had returned to their room to come down from the adrenaline rush.

“And you had rides like that everywhere in your world?” she had asked once she had fallen onto the bed.

“I wouldn’t say everywhere,” he answered. “Not unless you count cars, which I don’t, but there are amusement parks in every state. Even if there weren’t any nearby, there were usually county fairs or traveling events with smaller rides. They weren’t as fun as the big stuff, but they were a good way to spend an evening.”

“All those thrills and you were scared of ghosts?” Adabelle teased.

Trevor grumbled, but didn’t reply. His wife laughed, pulled him onto the bed, and kissed him.

The newlyweds were presentable by the time Horatial had come to retrieve them for their dinner with Captain Braidley. With Clay back in their stoat form, the four of them headed towards the main deck and then even higher to the bridge. They could see a myriad of instruments through one of the portholes and three members of the crew keeping an eye on things.

To Trevor, it almost looked like it had been modeled off of some spaceship from television with a steampunk twist. He wasn’t sure how much of that was a stylistic choice and how much was necessary, but he found himself enjoying the view nonetheless.

Directly above the bridge was the captain’s quarters. Horatial knocked on the door.

“Come in!” Braidley called.

Opening the door for them, Horatial bowed and gestured for them to go inside. They did so.

The captain’s quarters were large. His living room was at least the size of the newlywed’s whole suite, and there were two other doors that were closed, so they couldn’t see any further. They both looked around.

Their food was already on a nearby table. The platters were covered to keep them heated, but the smell of seared meat permeated the room. A desk covered in papers sat opposite of it, and there were a few comfortable chairs in the middle that were clearly meant for entertaining guests.

Trophies from both races and monsters lined the walls. The former started with a few bronzes and silvers, but slowly morphed to more and more golds in a visual tale that seemed to show Braidley’s growth at the sport. Those took up a wall and a half, leaving the rest for trophies taken from monsters.

Horns were the most prevalent, each one mounted on a piece of wood that complimented their natural color. One side had them arranged in descending order while the other had them ascending instead. The juxtaposition was a nice touch.

Clay tapped on Trevor’s head and pointed behind them. He turned to see an absolutely massive pair of black antlers that took up the whole wall above where they had entered, and it was set up so that its several tips pointed downwards. They were so large that he had a hard time believing they could bring it through the door. He wasn’t sure it could fit inside any extradimensional space that wasn’t his own.

Adabelle turned when her husband stopped, and she also looked upon the antlers. Her expression was more pensive than Trevor’s obvious wonder.

“Are those the horns of an Imblex?” she asked.

“Good eye, Lady Adabelle,” Braidley praised with a laugh. “Yes, I hunted it myself about three months ago. We actually had to remove some of the wall in order to get them to fit.”

“I believe it,” the noblewoman replied. She caught Trevor’s questioning glance, and explained. “The Imblex is a large monster, which you could tell from the antlers, but the dungeon it belongs to only spawns one at a time. It’s mean, powerful, and can fire energy beams from any of those tips that are nearly touching the floor. They’re such deadly foes that defeating one can instantly elevate the standing of the successful Summoned in the eyes of their peers.”

The captain nodded as she finished. “That’s exactly right. Traveling between continents so often gives me a lot of opportunities to try new things, and I’m always down for fighting huge monsters. Figured I’d give it a shot, and I managed to put it down before the town needed to start firing.”

“Why would the town need to start firing?” Trevor asked, both confused and a little concerned.

“We can get to that while we eat,” Braidley said with a clap of his hands. “Please, why don’t we sit, have some dinner, and swap some stories, eh? Horatial, you’re excused. If we need anything, I’ll call.”

“Aye aye, captain,” he replied before closing the door, leaving them alone.

Braidley pointed out their seats, but waited for them to sit. Clay slid down onto Trevor’s lap to curl up, prompting him to reach down and pet them with one hand. While they were settling, the captain unveiled their food. A bacon cheeseburger with all the toppings, french fries, corn on the cob, coleslaw, and a pickle sat on their plates.

“Not what I was expecting, but I’m always down for a good burger,” the courier said.

“I took a shot in the dark, one Earthling to another,” Braidley replied with a grin before sitting down across from them. “And, of course, if you don’t want it, we’ve got a big menu. I won’t be upset in the slightest.”

“It smells delicious,” Adabelle praised. “One of the other Summoned in Tosa, where we’re from, is a Texan who tends to spend as much time at his grill as he can. I can certainly appreciate a good burger.”

“Good, I’m glad that I didn’t just make a huge fool out of myself,” he laughed before removing the lid off of his own plate, revealing the same thing. The demon took it all in before going for the corn first. “So, Lady Adabelle, Klar told me you used to be a pretty active adventurer out of Eskretet. Did you ever go out that way? Towards the Imblex, I mean.”

“My group and I did, yes,” Adabelle answered as she passed her pickle to Trevor. He happily traded it for his coleslaw. “We had planned to take turns fighting it solo. Unfortunately, there was an incident that took place before we had a chance to do so and the dungeon was nearly destroyed in the process. This was before they installed the safety measures.”

“Which, I assumed, were the things the town was firing?” Trevor asked.

“That’s right. See, the dungeon of the Imblex is surrounded by the town of Imbeldin,” Braidley answered, gesturing towards the courier with a french fry in his hand. “Because it’s lucrative and there’s usually Summoned to handle it. I’m sure you’ve seen towns like that before.”

“Locker comes to mind,” he said with a nod. “They have a ghost pirate dungeon pretty close by.”

“Yeah, probably a lot like that,” the captain confirmed. “Well, a few years back, the Imblex broke through the barricade while chasing a pair of Summoned out of its hole. Right, it lives in a hole. Kind of like a rock quarry, you know? One of those ones that you kind of spiral your way down into.”

“I have a vague understanding of what you’re talking about.”

“Alright, cool. So this thing nearly breaks out completely because it followed the two bozos who were fleeing it, who were obviously not thinking. Then, it takes its first steps out of the dungeon for the first time in… centuries, I think.”

“Two hundred and seven years at that time,” Adabelle stated.

“Yeah, yeah, yeah. That sounds right,” Braidley said with a nod. “Obviously, they can’t have that happen again, so they bring in the big guns. Literally, I mean. They got magic guns pointed at the spiral in case the Imblex breaks free like that again. Pretty expensive, but if it saves the town then they gotta do it.”

“Makes sense to me,” Trevor replied before taking a big bite of his burger. Taste flooded into his mouth, and he nodded appreciatively at the food.

“The townsfolk don’t play anymore, so the moment it gets too close they start blasting. So when I say that I took it out before they needed to fire, that means it didn’t get very far at all,” Braidley bragged. “Didn’t even get hit, either. My Power lets me do a lot from a distance, see, and it was too slow to catch up.”

“It sounds like you managed to breeze through the fight, huh?” the courier asked. Braidley snorted and his wife gave him a flat look. He was confused at first before he realized what he had said. “Pun unintended.”

“Hey, I thought it was funny,” the captain chuckled. “But yeah, I was a bad match for it. Sometimes Powers are just like that, hard counters for even the toughest of monsters. Terrible that you didn’t get a chance to fight it though, Lady Adabelle.”

“Oh, I was able to fight it, I just wasn’t able to get my turn alone,” she replied as she dabbed a napkin at her mouth.

Braidley leaned forward a little bit. “You were there, huh?”

“I was,” Adabelle confirmed. “My group was the reason why it didn’t get any farther than the entrance to its dungeon. Instead of fighting for bragging rights, we fought to protect, so we worked together.”

“Do you think you could have soloed it?” Trevor asked curiously.

The demonkin thought about it for a moment. “It would have been difficult since I wouldn’t have used my Power, but I believe I would have come out successful in the end with only my sword. The Imblex is a large creature, sure, but something that big can still bleed out. Time would have been on my side.”

“Whoa, you think you could have beaten it without your Power?” Braidley asked, not sure if he was believing what he was hearing.

“My Power is ill-suited for fighting a monster like that,” she answered. “So, all I would have to rely on were my skills and my sword.”

Leaning back, the captain blew air out of his mouth as he looked at the noblewoman carefully. “You didn’t even bust it out when it was fleeing?”

Adabelle had been about to bite into her burger when he asked, and she lowered it from her mouth. “If I had needed my Power, then Imbeldin wouldn’t have existed for the Imblex to destroy,” she said, her tone cold. The look she gave him was full of confidence to her husband, but sent a chill down Braidley’s spine.

“Dang,” the captain replied with a nervous laugh before glancing at Trevor. “Quite the lady you married, huh?”

“Yeah,” he said with a proud smile. “Adabelle’s the best.”

“Thank you, honey,” she replied before tilting her head in his direction. “Honestly, though, my adventures are pretty standard considering we were guild affiliated. Trevor here has had a few that go against what most Summoned are used to.”

“No kidding?” Braidley asked, shifting to look at him. “You have any big stories for me?”

“Big stories? Gosh no,” Trevor replied with a laugh. “I’ve got small stories. Personable stories. I’m a courier, not an adventurer.”

“And yet you managed to defeat your first dungeon all by yourself, and collect no less than sixty-two perfect armors in that ghost pirate dungeon,” Adabelle bragged in her husband’s place.

“No. I mean, yeah, those sentences are technically accurate, but that’s skipping a lot of nuance,” he argued.

“Then hit me with the nuance. This is exactly why I invited you two up here,” Braidley said, brandishing half a pickle at the courier. “Networking, sure, but to swap tales and eat good food, all in great company. You got nuance? I want to hear it.”

Trevor hesitated, so Adabelle spoke up. “He’s too modest for his own good. Trevor’s first dungeon belonged to earth elementals that were proliferating faster than usual.”

“Ah, yeah, those buggers can do that if you happen to miss any of them,” the captain replied with a knowing nod.

Clay uncoiled on Trevor’s lap and raised their head above the table. The shine from their sapphire eyes got the demon’s attention, and the familiar held up a paw. Obsidian claws appeared from within, sharp and deadly, before they made an “I’m watching you” gesture. When Braidley arched an eyebrow, they lowered themselves back into their master’s lap.

Nodding as he crossed his arms, the captain thought about this. “Your familiar was a part of that dungeon, weren’t they?”

“Yup. Black core elder earth elemental,” Trevor replied as he pat Clay. A surge of happiness crossed their bond at the attention. “They’re a good little buddy. Helped me wrestle a tegrunaman that tried to stab me once.”

“Honey,” Adabelle sighed.

“What’s up?”

“That’s a whole other story,” she affectionately chastised. “This is why I tell the stories of your fights for you. You gloss over all the really good parts, boiling it down to the basics.”

“You just like to brag on me,” he replied, pouting.

“Yes, I do, but we’re also dining with another Summoned,” Adabelle pointed out, tilting her head towards Braidley. “We’re supposed to embellish, brag, and make ourselves look better. That’s how this goes.”

“She’s right,” the captain agreed. “Earth elemental dungeon, though. That’s cool. That’s something my Power would have trouble with. What’s yours?”

“Inventory,” Trevor answered without hesitation. “I’ve got an extradimensional space attached to me. Anything that touches my skin can be sent there so long as it’s not, you know, living. Or too big.”

“No wonder Klar wants to treat you so well,” Braidley laughed. “That’s exactly the kind of thing that’s up his alley.”

“Oh, for sure. I’m on some courier quests for him now, actually, with Adabelle as my escort. Going to level up a few times before we return home, I think.”

“Very nice, very nice. But, I still want to hear about this earth elemental dungeon where you gained your familiar,” he replied. “Because, between you and me, something tells me that your Power isn’t really Inventory.”

Trevor frowned. “But… it is?”

“Are you sure?” Braidley asked, leaning back in his chair with a roguish smile. “From what little I know of you, I would say the Quest Master gave you something a little extra.”

“I’m not quite sure what you’re getting at.”

“I’m talking about your ability to pick up monsters. Snagging a black core elemental as your familiar is one thing, but do you see the way your wife looks at you?” he replied with a barked laugh. Then, he glanced at Adabelle. “I do mean monster in an absolutely respectful manner, of course.”

“Oh, I know exactly what you mean,” the noblewoman replied with a small smile. “And it’s not just myself and Clay, but there’s a shark back home who would do anything to protect her Uncle Trevor. She very much fits the bill.”

“Rashie is a menace,” Trevor chuckled. “But, no, just the Power of Inventory. Everything else is me just stumbling through life and pulling way, way above my weight class.”

“I hear you, brother,” Braidley replied with a laugh. “Enough praise, though. Tell me more about this earth elemental dungeon. I gotta know how a simple courier gets through it alone.”

“And mostly unharmed,” Adabelle added. “Only his palm got injured, and that was after his first combat.”

“See, that? Inject those kind of details directly into my veins. Get to bragging, Trevor.”

The courier looked at the two before rolling his eyes, letting out a dramatic sigh as if they were putting him on the spot, and finally smiled. “Alright, so I was out in this little mountain village…”
