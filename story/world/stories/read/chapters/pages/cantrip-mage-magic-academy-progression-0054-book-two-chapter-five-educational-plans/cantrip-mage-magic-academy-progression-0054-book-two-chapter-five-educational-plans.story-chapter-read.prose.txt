The final class of the day was Eola’s apprenticeship with Instructor Tarik.

Not that it was typically much of a class. Aside from the impromptu unit on historical duels of honor last term, she, Colin, Patrice, and Roth had mostly treated their time with the professor as an independent study, and he’d treated it as something similar. He’d been happy to help Eola, Roth, and Colin with their magic in whatever ways he could, but he’d also been content to leave Patrice to her napping. They’d used it more aggressively while preparing for the Library Exam, of course. That time together had been critical to their dubious success.

One look at the scribbled notes on the blackboard, though, and Eola knew that the terms of their apprenticeship were about to change—and change dramatically.

Syllabus for Second-Term Apprenticeships

Each of you will submit a detailed plan for your second-term education, including steps for attaining Journeymage rank or the equivalent. You will take into account your current class schedule, extracurricular learning, and unique attunements in this regard. Have a rough idea presented by the end of this class on Fifth-Day.

- Tarik

Eola got to work quickly. She’d been thinking about nothing but this since the end of the first term, after all, so it wasn’t hard to start putting it into written form. She hunkered down at a desk in the corner and got to work.

First, the Cutting mark. She could keep working on it in private with her friends, and she could use it in the library, but until she knew what Maestro Yarrowbloom’s half of Spellsword Tactics was like, she couldn’t be sure she’d get the opportunity to use it against her classmates. That was fine; she needed other combat skills as well. But the Cutting mark would be a core part of her fighting style. It might even be more important than her swordplay skills.

Second, there was Eddarn. She and her friends had beaten the Scholar’s Cultist and his magical book monster of a familiar during the Library Exam, but he’d gotten away. He was still a threat, and once she found a partner for Patrol Duty, she’d start trying to hunt him down.

Third, she wanted to keep digging into Varin’s apprenticehood journals. The word-play, use of extinct languages, and codes were all but overwhelming, but that was what Extinct Languages was for, right? If she could start to understand the greatest magical mind in the last thousand years, maybe she could learn a few things from him—for one thing, the strange, unusable modifying mark he’d put on the single page he hadn’t encoded in some way.

And finally—and most important for reaching Journeymage and getting closer to saving her parents—Eola needed to handle her Mana problem.

She drew for a while, seemingly nothing but doodles in pen. The runes she knew, she drew in a quick, messy shorthand, while the ones she didn’t, she left as blank circles. Clean arrows pointed to each section of the magical blueprint, while descriptions of what, specifically, she hoped to accomplish covered the margins. After nearly twenty minutes, she finished.

Colin was buried in his own notebook, furiously jotting down and scribbling out lines. Roth had his eyes closed, his tell-tale glare plastered on his face. And Patrice…Patrice’s orange hair cascaded over her head as it rested on her desk. Was she sleeping again? Eola couldn’t be sure.

“Instructor Tarik?” she asked.

The professor looked up from Magic and Power in Pearl Bay. “Miss Lemiene? Can I help you?”

“I’d like to discuss an ensorcellment project and how it relates to my goals, sir,” Eola said quickly.

“Let’s hear it, then.”

Eola walked to the desk at the front of the classroom and flattened out a piece of parchment. “This is my rough idea. Obviously, it needs revision and an expert’s eye, but this is one of the core goals for my second term. I’d like to finish it before the midterm exams.”

“Interesting,” Instructor Tarik said. He peered over it and added a few marks of his own—mostly corrections to specific runes along with one annotation to Eola’s margin notes. Then he nodded slowly. “I’m no expert, especially compared to Lannith. It’s an interesting design, though, and I’m sure your Ensorcellment instructor will be eager to see you complete it.

“A word of warning, though. It will expect you to create this wand from scratch. That means melting and reforging the silver and chromite, cutting the branch yourself, and either harvesting the reagents yourself or, if they can’t be found nearby, buying their least refined form and doing the work yourself. It’s very particular about its class.”

“I understand,” Eola said.

Instructor Tarik shook his head. “No, you don’t. If I were you, I’d strongly consider joining the Spine Mountain patrols rather than the library ones.”

Eola started to protest.

“Hold off on complaining until you talk to Lannith about this. It might be able to give you more perspective on how complex your project is. Lady Clerk, are you working or sleeping?”

Patrice’s head snapped up, hair flying. “I’m thinking, sir!”

“Good. Keep thinking, but consider writing down some of what you’re thinking about. Miss Lemiene, I tentatively approve this design. Work on your written plan.”

The professor’s book went up, and Eola returned to her desk.

Roth’s eyes followed her as she walked, staring at her intently.

Colin slipped his hand around hers as they left Instructor Tarik’s apprenticeship.

Eola squeezed back. It was…Y’aer, it was nice. They walked the long way back to the dorms—partially because Eola needed to stretch her legs after Spellsword Tactics, but mostly to get some time alone, without Patrice and Roth. The hedge maze was green, with only a few leaves starting to turn, and the sound of boot soles on cobblestones followed them instead of the crunch of snow.

When they got back, Eola finally let Colin’s hand go. The door was still shut, and she looked left, then right, making sure the coast was clear. Then, closing her eyes, she planted a quick kiss on Colin’s cheek. “Thank you for the walk.”

“Of…of course,” Colin said, blushing. “Dinner tonight?”

“Cafeteria. I need to get back to work.”

“Ugh.” Colin’s expression drove the point home, but he nodded. “Half an hour?”

“Sounds wonderful,” Eola said. She opened the door, expecting a clear path to her room.

Instead, she ran into a wall of tangled orange hair and robes. “Hello, Eola,” Patrice said. She’d pulled off her cloak and made herself comfortable up against the door frame. “I’ve been waiting. Can we talk?”

“Of course.”

Patrice pointed to her room. “In here.”

Eola followed, a little bemused. She’d been in Patrice’s room once before, but that was before she’d moved into this suite. She’d also been a bad combination of drunk on wine and hung over on that particular morning—not to mention embarrassed from waking in Patrice’s bed—and hadn’t bothered getting much of a look around.

This time, she made sure to correct that oversight. Patrice’s room was the largest in the suite. No windows or lofts, but she’d obviously been one of the students bringing furniture last year, judging by the finely-carved wardrobe and the life-sized wooden mannequin with a dust-covered dress mounted on it. It was the picture of a noble girl’s room, with two obvious exceptions. The first was the second life-sized mannequin, this one wearing silvered steel armor that’d been taken care of much better than the frilly pink dress. And the second was the too-long pole-hammer on a rack against the wall.

Patrice ignored the armor, dress, and unmade tangle of bedding. She pointed at a letter on her desk. “I have no idea what to do with this.”

Remedial Ideograms - Instructor TarikRemedial Mana Studies - Madame ReyannaBeginner’s Battle Magic - Lieutenant-Librarian Fringe and Professor RosewindPatrol Duty - Lieutenant-Librarian FringeApprenticeship - Instructor Tarik

Eola skimmed over the list. After a moment, she shook her head. “I don’t understand what you need from me.”

Patrice groaned. “My older brother—the one who’s betrothed to Catrine—told me all about the remedials. They’re supposed to be a nightmare. Simple spells, lots of practical work, and frequent exams.”

“Oh,” Eola said. Then she thought for a moment. “Oh.”

“Oh is right. They’re going to make me cast. You know what that looks like—I can’t do it. And Tarik’s assignment on top of it? A study plan? Me? I need theory classes, not practice. I have no idea why they put me in the battle mage track. I could probably pass Spellsword Tactics—“

“I don’t know…Fringe is a madman.”

“—but battle mage? Ludicrous. I can’t cast!”

Eola touched Patrice’s hand. “I don’t think you need to hide from your attunement, Patrice. We need to start getting a handle on it, that’s all.”

“Y’aer’s curse, I’ve been trying to do that my whole life!” Patrice snapped. “I was supposed to be betrothed four years ago. I accidentally set him on fire. Guess how long that lasted? Now I’ve got a new betrothal, but that’ll only last until I have to use my magic. Everyone’s better off if I don’t cast at all!”

“I…well, you saved all our lives against Eddarn, right?”

“I had no choice. Swinging my hammer around like a Y’aer-cursed trog wasn’t working.”

Eola looked around the room again, then settled on what was missing. Every student had a silvered cage for their familiar. Even Eola did, technically. Atta never used it, and it was gathering dust, but it existed. Patrice didn’t. No familiar, no control over her magic, and no idea how to fix any of it.

“I’m sorry. It must be frustrating,” she said after a minute.

“You have no idea.”

Eola stared at Patrice, one eyebrow raised.

“Okay, fine, you have more of an idea than anyone outside our suite. How about that?” Patrice sat down heavily in her desk chair.

Eola waited. When the redhead didn’t say anything else, she cleared her throat. “You need help with your plan, right? I’ve got a lot going on this semester, Patrice, but I’ll do what I can. I can ask Tarik to talk to Reyanna and Rosewind. I doubt it’ll do much, but…”

“It’s something. Thank you,” Patrice said. “I also need to get to the third floor again. There have to be answers there. I just need the time to figure out what I’m looking for first.”

“Alright. Don’t lose hope. We’ll get through this.” Eola walked to the door, opened it, and paused. “Try to get some sleep, Patrice.”

The tall girl snorted, then laughed. “I can always sleep. Thanks, though.”

When Eola finally made it back to her room and locked the door behind you, Atta was waiting on the desk.

“Don’t say anything,” Eola said. She flopped onto her bed.

“I wasn’t going to,” the cat said.

“You think I’m overcommitting.”

“Well, yes. But you’re far too much of a silly girl to listen to what I think, and besides—“

“I don’t care,” Eola interrupted. She stared at the picture on the wall.  Her mom’s brilliant golden hair caught the sun in the portrait—and the reflected light from Eola’s Bright Ball—and her dad’s smile shone almost as brightly. The three of them had posed for it five years ago, near Greenarbor’s dueling grounds. She’d been thirteen then. Small, but that hadn’t changed. Scrawny, though, with the thin, coltish limbs of a girl about to grow into herself. Each parent had a hand on one of her shoulders. She missed their touches. “They need my help.”

“Patrice? Absolutely. I was going to say that I agree with you. You are overextending, but if you want to push deeper into the library, you’re going to need her help. No, the boy with the yellow snake-cat’s the one you’re wasting time with.”

Eola said nothing, but she did close her eyes, replacing the portrait of her with her parents with the long, comforting embrace Colin had held her in last term. Her arms wrapped around her own shoulders. It was ridiculously unfair. Mom and Dad needed her, yes. She was working hard to learn enough magic for Truemage—she’d discovered a whole new way of doing magic, after all. But they’d also survived in Lord Card’s dungeon for years, and the dungeon below Greenarbor wasn’t something she could just waltz through. She needed to make sure she was ready before trying it.

In the meantime, she was almost nineteen. Nineteen, and she’d never had a proper courting before. She hadn’t had friends since fifteen, for Y’aer’s sake. Not until Colin, Patrice, and Roth. Her eyes opened again, and she met her parents’ gaze—and a pair of yellow cat’s eyes, too. “You’re going to have to be patient, but I’m not giving up.”

“You’d better not be,” Atta said. She hopped from the desk to the bed and licked Eola’s nose. Eola shivered, but the cat kept talking. “I’ve done too much wo—“

Another hacking fit interrupted her, and by the time it was finished, Eola was good and awake; the thought that a cat might throw up on her face had gotten her up and out of bed. “You’ve done too much work?”

“I can’t say, and you clearly know it. We agreed not to push on this, Eola.”

“Fine.”

“Since you’re not tired, let’s work on Varin’s Notebook for a while.” Atta bounced back across the narrow gap between bed and desk, then pawed the stack of papers.

Eola pulled the first page off the pile and smoothed it out carefully. She’d gotten it—or more accurately, been gifted it—after her first third-floor library trip. She closed her eyes, pushed Colin’s face and comforting arms out of her thoughts, and tried to focus on the work.

This being the diary of me, Varin, apprentice to Archmage Frelanne, I record my thoughts for my future self here.

I have been apprenticed to Frelanne for two months, and she’s encouraged me to begin documenting my runes and marks in this book. Frelanne’s a great spellcrafter. Her runes and marks are leagues better than what I can hope to achieve. However, she’s a harsh mistress to learn from. I don’t have the strength of soul to do what she’s asking of me, so most of our training time is focused on strengthening it. Honestly, I doubt anyone has the strength of soul to keep up with her. She’s been training for two hundred years or something.

One thing, though. I’ve noticed she always, always uses the same mark when she casts. I’ve been spying watching when she casts, and I think I’ve got the mark itself figured out. I just wish I knew what it did.

Eola had studied this first page to death, but it was the only one—besides the very last—that didn’t require translation. It also painted a pair of pictures of the Great Mage Varin. He was desperately curious. Inquisitive enough to watch Frelanne cast, and detail-oriented enough to note the unique modifying mark. An ideal apprentice, for any master. If Eola flattered herself, she was somewhat like him in those regards.

And she was like him in a second manner, too.

His soul was inadequate for the magic Frelanne was asking him to do, just like her attunement was useless for Ordered Magic.

With some effort, she set aside the first, simple page and set the second in its place. He’d switched languages to an archaic Emerald Cursive, but she had the first sentence more or less figured out.

That’s the mark Mistress Frelanne uses. It doesn’t match anything in Alemic or Orantic, which are the usual languages of marks.

That tracked with what Eola knew. It also gave her an angle to study—and would quickly put her at odds with Instructor Tarik’s recommendation to only study one extinct language at a time. He wouldn’t cover Orantic Dictation until the third fifth of the year; she couldn’t wait that long to learn about the mysterious mark.

“Keep going,” Atta said. She butted her head against Eola’s elbow. Eola sighed, readied a fresh sheet of parchment, and began the process of copying Emerald Script so she could start translating it in earnest. It was going to be a long night.
