Chapter 16: First Blood

Green eyes rolled in dramatic fashion as Kari turned toward the source of the mocking voice, her expression shifting from post-sparring exhaustion to weary annoyance. The change was so swift and practiced that I suspected this wasn't the first time she had dealt with whatever was coming our way.

"Is this what we are on today, Molech?" she said, her voice carrying a note of exasperated familiarity. "Listen, I know you have this whole 'I'm the next big Nyxen Lord on the block' vibe going on, but can you just try to be original for once? The constant belittling is getting really old, and frankly, it wasn't that creative to begin with."

Molech was a draxi, but unlike the yellow-scaled servants I had grown accustomed to seeing around Vash's mansion, this one was clearly something different entirely. His scales ranged from every shade of black one could name, each individual plate touched with just a slight hint of silver and blue that caught the crimson light filtering through the training hall's windows. The effect gave him the appearance of a being born from the very night sky itself, as if someone had captured a piece of the void between stars and given it reptilian form.

He was larger than the servant draxi as well, standing nearly as tall as I was with a broader, more muscular build that spoke of serious combat training. His movements had a predatory grace that reminded me uncomfortably of the way Vash moved when she was preparing to demonstrate a particularly painful lesson. This was not a creature accustomed to servitude or deference.

His eyes were the most striking feature, pools of liquid mercury that seemed to shift and flow even as I watched. The pupils were like captured sunlight, bright and piercing against the metallic backdrop. Those alien eyes narrowed as he focused his attention on Kari, and he hissed at her with genuine menace.

"Your humor does nothing to mask your pathetic display," he said, his voice carrying the distinctive sibilant quality of his species but with an educated articulation that marked him as far more than a simple servant. "Humans truly are weak creatures. It is no wonder your species required rescue from its own incompetence."

My eyes narrowed at the casual insult, and I felt something cold and sharp unfurling in my chest. Once upon a time, I wouldn't have responded to such provocation. After all, what did I care about what some alien creature thought about me or my species? All I had cared about then was what my family thought about me, their opinions and approval the only currency that mattered in my small, comfortable world.

But things were not so simple now. Everything had changed with the Awakening, with loss, with the brutal education I had received at Vash's hands. I was no longer the man who could afford to ignore insults and provocations, who could walk away from confrontation secure in the knowledge that his loved ones were safe at home.

All around us were other initiates, some of whom had stopped their own training to glance toward our growing confrontation. I noticed several figures in the distinctive black and crimson garb of higher-ranking Nyxen officials standing near the edges of the training hall. Maybe not lords or ladies, but certainly individuals of significantly higher rank than mere initiates like myself.

Weakness was quickly identified and ruthlessly eliminated among the Nyxen. I had learned that lesson through observation and bitter experience. I could not afford to appear weak before the games, or at any other time for that matter. Such a reputation would follow me like a shadow, marking me as prey for anyone looking to advance their own position through violence.

It would endanger me. More importantly, it would endanger Elisa.

Or so I would tell myself in moments like that one, when the darkness inside me whispered that violence was not just justified but necessary.

My resonance blade ignited with its familiar hum as I tapped into the cold wellspring of the Flux within me, the white light touched with crimson casting dancing shadows across the arena floor. I pointed the weapon directly at the draxi's chest, my stance shifting automatically into the ready position Vash had beaten into my muscle memory.

"Careful who you call weak, lizard," I said, my voice carrying a edge that surprised me with its coldness. "I'll defeat you just as easily as I defeated her."

The words hung in the air between us like a challenge made manifest, and I could feel the attention of every initiate and observer in the hall focusing on our confrontation. The background noise of training and combat seemed to fade, replaced by a tense silence that made every heartbeat audible.

Molech's mercury eyes widened slightly, whether from surprise at my audacity or anticipation of violence, I couldn't tell. His body tensed, muscles coiling beneath his dark scales as he prepared to respond to my challenge. His own hands moved toward the weapon harness across his chest, where I could see the distinctive grip of what appeared to be a resonance weapon of his own design.

But before either of us could escalate the confrontation into actual combat, a new presence made itself known. A man appeared at the edge of our arena with the kind of fluid movement that suggested he had been watching our exchange for some time before choosing to intervene.

His skin was dark ebony, polished to an almost unnatural perfection that reminded me of carved obsidian. His eyes were the most unsettling feature, reminding me of fresh blood scattered across pristine snow, bright crimson against stark white. They held an intelligence that felt ancient and calculating, as if he had seen countless confrontations like this one and found them all equally tedious.

He was dressed in the familiar black and crimson garb I had seen Vash wear, but his outfit was more elaborate, with additional ornamentation and symbols that clearly marked him as someone of significant rank within the Nyxen hierarchy. A cloak of some material that seemed to absorb light flowed from his shoulders, and when he moved, it created the illusion that pieces of the shadow were following in his wake.

Kari immediately bowed her head a fraction when his gaze swept over us, her earlier defiance replaced by the kind of careful deference that meant survival in this place. The man's eyes lingered on me for several long seconds, his lips twisting in what might have been displeasure or simple assessment. I hadn't bowed my head, hadn't shown the automatic submission that seemed expected.

I met his gaze directly, neither challenging nor deferential, simply acknowledging his presence without conceding authority. It was a calculated risk, but something in my gut told me that showing weakness now would be more dangerous than appearing defiant.

"Molech," the man said, his voice rich and smooth like aged whiskey, carrying a combination of that distinctive Nyxen accent touched with something more cultured and refined. "We are leaving. You may prove your dominance in the games tomorrow, where such displays actually matter."

The words were spoken calmly, but there was an undertone of command that brooked no argument. This was clearly not a suggestion but an order from someone accustomed to absolute obedience.

Molech's mercury eyes flicked between me and his apparent patron, and I could see the conflict playing out across his reptilian features. The desire to continue our confrontation warred with the obvious necessity of obeying his superior. Finally, with visible reluctance, he took a step back from our arena.

"This is not finished, human," he hissed at me, his voice carrying a promise of future violence. "Tomorrow, in the games, I will show you what true strength looks like."

With that ominous declaration, he turned and followed the dark-skinned man toward the exit of the training hall, his movements still carrying that predatory grace but now directed away from our immediate confrontation.

I watched them go until they disappeared through the massive doors, my blade still active and ready despite the apparent end to the immediate threat. Only when they were completely out of sight did I allow the weapon to deactivate, the white light fading to leave only the simple crystal focus in my hand.

"Who was that?" I asked Kari, nodding toward the exit where the two figures had vanished.

She watched the doors for several more seconds before sighing deeply, her shoulders sagging slightly as the tension drained from her posture. When she turned back to me, her expression was a mixture of concern and dark amusement.

"That was Lord Drayven Vox and his chosen initiate, Molech," she said, her voice carrying a note of weary familiarity. "Pleasant individuals, don't you think? Lord Vox has a reputation for collecting particularly aggressive initiates and encouraging their worst impulses. He believes that cruelty and ruthlessness are the highest virtues, and he trains his people accordingly."

She paused, running a hand through her short hair in what I was beginning to recognize as a nervous habit. "Molech is his current favorite, though from what I hear, Lord Vox tends to go through initiates fairly quickly. Either they advance to become his apprentices, or they die in training. There's not usually much middle ground."

The casual way she discussed the possibility of death in training sent another chill down my spine. I was still adjusting to the Nyxen approach to education, where pain and potential death were considered normal parts of the learning process.

Kari didn't wait for me to respond to this sobering information. Instead, she turned to face me directly, her green eyes bright with curiosity and something that might have been concern.

"Well, it was nice to meet you..." she began, then paused with an expression of sudden realization. "Oh, you know what? I never did catch your name. We've been sparring and threatening draxi together, and I don't even know what to call you."

I hesitated, that familiar suspicion raising its head once more. In the weeks since the Awakening, I had learned to be careful about sharing personal information. Names had power in this new reality, and the wrong person knowing the right details could lead to complications I wasn't prepared to handle.

But Kari had shared her own story freely, had opened herself up in a way that suggested genuine trust rather than manipulation. And if we were both going to be participating in the games tomorrow, there was a good chance our paths would cross again whether I shared my name or not.

Finally, after a moment of internal debate, I said, "Varus. Varus Thorne."

She grinned at my obvious reluctance, the expression transforming her face from merely attractive to genuinely beautiful. "I can see the Nyxen paranoia is already seeping into you," she said with a laugh that held no mockery, only understanding. "Careful you don't lose yourself completely to it, eh Varus? The darkness in this place has a way of getting inside people and changing them in ways they don't expect."

There was something in her tone, a note of personal experience or perhaps warning, that made me look at her more closely. But before I could ask what she meant, she was already stepping back and preparing to leave.

"Anyway, I better get going," she continued, shouldering a training bag I hadn't noticed before. "Big day tomorrow and all that. Try to get some rest, and maybe avoid picking fights with any more draxi before the games start. You'll have plenty of opportunities for violence soon enough."

She waved goodbye with the same casual friendliness she had shown throughout our encounter, then turned and walked toward the exit with the easy confidence of someone comfortable in her own skin. I watched her go, noting the way other initiates stepped aside to let her pass and the respectful nods she received from several of the more experienced trainees.

As the training hall gradually returned to its normal rhythm of combat and instruction, I found myself alone in the arena where our confrontation had taken place. The rope barriers hummed softly around me, and the scorch marks on the floor told the story of countless practice sessions and occasional accidents.

Kari Smith was right about one thing, I reflected as I began to make my own preparations to leave.

It would indeed be a big day tomorrow.

The question was whether any of us would survive it to see the day after.
