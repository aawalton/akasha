Chapter 138: Big Time Selection

As Jack went down the elevator of Central Processing, he sighed and tried not to roll his eyes at Bermuda’s response about his dad. He asked as politely as he could, “May I request your assessment on probable likelihoods in regards to my father, based on what you know, Agent Bermuda?”

Bermuda was amused at his tone. “You can show a little professional tact? Who knew? Very well — I can’t pass up the opportunity to reward good behavior. He’ll be quarantined, obviously, but his case is rather light, despite his voluntary fanaticism. He needs psychiatric care, and that’s probably the best way to get anything out of him. He’s going to resist, but separation from his supporting environment and conspirators will do some work with time. I don’t think any extreme measures will be taken. We’ve got others more deserving who know as much or more — we think. Anyway, that’s my assessment. Can’t promise anything.”

Jack nodded slowly, somewhat feeling better in his anxiety about his dad. He didn’t think Bermuda would mince words with him at this point, so he could hope for what she said, at least. “Is the soft touch for my sake at all?”

“Perhaps a little. But it isn’t much of a stretch, Agent. He is a sound target for careful rehabilitation, even in his mental history, which is of a pretty grounded, salient bent. There is a tremendous incentive to gain people who legitimately believed this tripe, to turn that around and willingly cooperate with us, for reasons I’m sure you can figure out yourself.”

“Mmn. Thank you. Should I be used at all, to help?”

“No. For various reasons, you’re a raw, open wound to him. Right now, he’s just going to keep refusing to believe you’re you, no matter what you do or say. He’s deeply paranoid, and you clearly demonstrated ANP superpowers. That’s categorically impossible to him.” Bermuda translated a conclusive shrug.

“Yeah. Makes sense.” The existential dread of his own father not believing in him was quite the knife in his gut, but he couldn’t help feeling like he deserved it. He’d betrayed his dad, after all. Sure, it was to literally save his mind and his life, not to mention fight humanity’s natural enemies, but — painfully — his dad had thought he was in a great place where he’d been, and had it blown straight to hell. Jack had been the trigger of that.

It felt awful, and he couldn’t shake the guilt.

Bermuda continued, “You’ll see him again eventually. Hopefully, Psych can set him straight, and you can come in on the other side of things, and maybe that’ll even be familiar territory for you. Post-rehab contrition and so forth. Take note: he’s your father, and contractually, we allow mothers and fathers to ‘know,’ so you’ll be able to explain things to him to some degree. But you’ll need to be patient.”

“I understand. I’m not sure he’ll forgive me, anyway.”

“Don’t be ridiculous, Jack: of course he will. How many times have you forgiven him for his screw-ups? Yours isn’t even a screw-up, just an unsavory clean-up.”

Jack smiled faintly at her lapse in using his first name. “I hope you’re right. Thanks, Bermuda. Are you my handler, now, by the way?”

Her response came with sass. “Now? I’ve been exactly that from the beginning. One of them, that is. Over and out, Agent. Get some rest. Your ceremony is still on.” She disconnected.

Jack felt a combination of excitement and annoyance at that reminder. Obviously, he wanted the official stamp on his record and the honor of the jacket. At the same time, he could sleep for at least half a day after what he’d just been through.

Oh well.

The elevator opened, and he shuffled into Operations to get his beloved abs back. Even more than that, he looked forward to his bed… he couldn’t decide whether he would manage to eat before passing out, though.

🌑 🌒 🌓 🌔 🌕

Jack was briefly semi-aware of being caught in a vivid nightmare, surrounded by a crowd of people screaming and begging for mercy as they burned and their faces melted off, as meanwhile the fire didn’t even touch Jack. He tried to help them, but he couldn’t reach them. He screamed with them…

A sudden, cooling presence enveloped him, and the fire went out. The people disappeared, and darkness took him again. Despite the intrinsic human fear of the dark, Jack felt none of it, knowing it was someone he loved wrapping him up. Cool and soothing, yet somehow insulating, too. Warmth within the cold. Calmness.

He felt her purr. He fell into a deep, restful sleep very quickly and forgot most of it had even happened.

When he awoke to a set alarm, bleary-eyed and wanting nothing of the world deep in his very soul, it was with a strange feeling that someone ought to be there in the bed with him. He looked for her, and didn’t find her, and then was shaking his head at the audacity to think that, embarrassed.

Getting a bit ahead of myself, aren’t I?

Despite the lack of sleep it entailed, Jack had given himself plenty of time to get ready before the ceremony. He got up quickly, though, getting some coffee and a bite to eat. A dozen subjects danced in his head from his day and his night. He didn’t think any day had been as packed as that one had. At least he didn’t wake up to sirens and alarms and calls to action for a humanity-wide threat or the like. Apparently, Central had decided to continue the ‘cold war at home’ for a while longer.

He more or less agreed. He was worried, but also glad things wouldn’t get that insane so soon. If nothing else, even if the Dreamer knew it could mess with humanity by flagrantly sowing fear through public exposure, it would want to keep being insidious for no other reason than to entrench itself deeper before the shit really hit the fan. Both sides might be gambling equally there. As they waged the cold war, the Mems could better prepare for the eventuality of a hotter war in the meantime.

As he ate, Jack also couldn’t shake the feeling that Neex had visited him. He remembered purring. Did he just dream it?

No. I think she… must’ve comforted me. My head is full of shit right now, so I’m sure it was when I was asleep.

He poked at her mentally. Seeing she was awake and not super busy, he sent her a heart sign. “Thanks, Neex.”

Neex sent the heart back, along with a hug — which Jack saw and felt in his mind. “What for, specifically, if I may ask?”

“Well… I think you did your ‘psychic envelope’ maneuver when I was sleeping. I assume that helped me sleep.”

“Oh!” She seemed a bit embarrassed as she smiled sheepishly. “I hope it’s okay… You were having a nightmare, and I understood why. You didn’t deserve that.” He could feel her resoluteness in this regard. “Those people dying wasn’t your fault, and I wasn’t about to let you be tormented about it.”

Jack smiled at her explanation. “I appreciate it. That’s always okay, Neex. I don’t see why not, if you want to.”

“Is it? In that case, w-we could make that a routine. When you’re going to sleep, I mean.”

“You want to protect me from even starting to have nightmares in the first place, don’t you?”

Only the briefest of anxious pauses blipped before she admitted, “Yes, Boyfriend.”

Jack chuckled at the word use, as if it were a title. “I see, I see…”

A little more anxiousness. “Do you agree?”

“Yes, Girlfriend. I do, in fact, consent to it.” It was faintly ironic because in his mind, only a complete idiot would say otherwise. “As long as it won’t interrupt your sleep.”

Warm pleasure translated across the bond. “You called me Girlfriend! We’re Girlfriend and Boyfriend!” She squeed. “Ahhh! I gotta go, now.” She made a heart sign and awkwardly, shyly blew a kiss before quickly retreating.

Jack burst out in laughter, suddenly glad she disconnected, to free him to do so. He shook his head in incredulity. He was almost certain she was going straight to Bo to ‘brag.’

His mood drastically improved, Jack moved over to the couch and lay down. He was pretty confident that he was awake enough to do Calibration that way without falling asleep.

Not long after falling into a meditative state, he shifted into that familiar conceptual realm, like the black void of space.

The angel form of Memoria was facing away from him this time, staring over at three massive, complex bundles of energy pulsating and shifting in front of her. She had her arms crossed, tapping one finger on her lip, in an expression of consternated contemplation. “Hmm.”

Jack chuckled as he floated over. “Just ‘hmm,’ that’s all?”

“Yes.”

Rolling his eyes, Jack took a look at the options.

Memory Metal

Transmute +1.0

You can 'program' metal to automatically reform into its original shape if deformed, or add a simple condition to take a new shape. Some material loss may occur if damaged in deformation, and the effect isn't instantaneous. This function operates as if at 75% of your total output, though it requires none.

Hot From the Forge

Augment +1.0

You can superheat metal for additional heat-based damage and other effects. Determine the maximum Magnitude of the effect as 70% of your total Magnitude, but this requires minimal output. If triggered passively, heat ramps up by 10% per second until achieving the max in 7 seconds. Active focus improves this.

You and your metal have Heat Resistance equal to your Level+20, which is far above your superheating effect. Unless exceeded, you have no difficulty working with heated metal.

Head in the Cloud

Interpret +1.0

Your sensitivity while within a metal particulate cloud, through that cloud, is greatly enhanced. Extrasensory information is as good as your primary sense throughout the zone. Add 75% of your Interpret stat to Evasion, Parry, Accuracy, and any other task strongly dependent on perception and positioning.

This does not stack with any benefits gained from existing 'natural' senses. Exceptional circumstances that interfere with the cloud will also interfere with the benefits.

Jack laughed as he read Hot From the Forge — it felt like a bit of a pun after what he’d just been through. Though clearly an influence, it couldn’t be just that creating the mutation, as he’d tested and faced that particular weakness of his metal before.

But as he looked at them all, Jack once again found himself wincing, shaking his head, and finally sighing regretfully. “Damn.”

Memoria also sighed and nodded. “Yeah.”

“They’re all very desirable.”

“Indeed, they are.”

“One of these gives me an alternate damage type.”

“That is quite rare, especially this early. On top of the absurd resistance to a common hazard.”

Jack kept wearing the mindscape equivalent of a pained wince. “Another, Memory Metal, has a ton of tricksome, flexible potential. This might’ve been influenced by all my trap-making. I can think of numerous lethal applications in addition to the defensive benefits.”

“Mmn. Re-growing walls, shields, armor. You can do it yourself, but action economy is a thing, no?”

“Yeah. Maybe that’s slightly less relevant later on. Wait — can I develop something like this myself at some point?”

Memoria tapped her lip some more, pondering it. “It’s possible. Need higher Transmute, certainly. Perhaps a later, more versatile mutation would make it easier.”

“You’re saying something might come at higher levels that basically just includes this potential? What about the other two?”

Star-Angel Memoria smiled faintly. “Thinking long-term, eh? I can’t account for every variable. High stats and raw levels can accomplish a lot eventually. Head in the Cloud provides stat boosts. On the one hand, any given value is eventually obtainable, but the boost is an additional multiplier. Hot From the Forge is very particular. Heat Resistance is not unique, but adding heat yourself is. One way or another, a mutation is needed for that.”

Jack nodded, absorbing the fact that they were both very valuable in their own ways. Meanwhile, he was still gambling that he could get the effects of Memory Metal later, if he passed on it now.

Head in the Cloud also had some danger of overreliance on a hypothetically more ‘foolable’ sense that required special setup and conditions (such as reasonably calm winds). Once again, great if pre-prepared or with at least enough time to drop the field. In addition, Jack was already specialized and enhanced in his primary sense, vision. The boost probably wasn’t quite as big for him, though he didn’t have the data on it.

That being said, when he was out of line of sight and needing his fields, and he could be within that field, the mutation would be absolutely golden. It was tough to pass on. If it were just any field, rather than a contiguous one he was inside, it might’ve put it completely over the hump.

But Hot From the Forge was fantastic, too. Hot, even. The alt damage source tugged his FOMO of the selection something hardcore. Would that turn up again? It was a unique concept barely tied to his powers. And the idea of walking through lava and fire and shrugging it off was especially tempting for the sheer ‘coolness factor.’

Being able to handle unusual environmental conditions is rather appropriate for the one called ‘Ferronaut.’

He did still have some questions. Turning to Memoria, he asked, “Do any of our enemies use heat as a common damage type?”

Memoria nodded. “In addition to being a kind of common talent you’re likely to encounter out there, a group commonly and rather insufficiently known as the Mutants often favors heat rays as one of their most efficient widescale technological implementations abroad. You’ll be briefed more on them very soon.”

“Huh. So, I’m likely to face them in the near future or something?”

Memoria grimaced. “I certainly hope not. I’d sooner avoid it a while longer.” She sighed. “It’s probably inevitable at some point, and a danger out toward the tip of South America and the remnants of islands around it. That’s the border of their territory. They came close to permanently entrenching in our island territories in the early days, but competitors just inhibited them enough for our expansion to root them all out. These days, they mostly try excursions in the Graham Crescent or Palmer. They’re strongly kept in check, and most of that area is a wasteland.”

“That’s close to the Calm, where Neex’s home is.”

“Yes. You’ll be hitting our hard point base on Palmer before submerging. Anyway… that’s a briefing for tomorrow.”

Jack felt his heart race a bit, in a combination of excitement and trepidation. “Really?!”

“Considering how bad the situation is getting here, I need more Q-Loth, ah, ‘summoners’ so to speak, and ASAP. I don’t relish using you for that.” She threw her hands up in frustration. “If this crazy ‘bondathon’ plot is even feasible! Hell if I know. But we have to go there to find out. I’d like to send a larger group, too, but it might send the wrong message.”

“After the recent discoveries, I was kinda wondering if I'd need to stick around here longer.”

“By no means can I wait any longer. And I know you’re worried about your dad, but he’ll be okay. He’s safer than he was. With the public wartime exception option off the table for now, your mission is the priority to help improve this infiltration situation, Jack. I’d rather you be at the max training level, Level 9, but it just isn’t in the cards. It’ll take too bloody long. Kinda like your selection here is taking.”

“Alright, alright…” Jack took a deep breath and stared at the options. “Okay, one more question. Do you think there’s some synergy to others heating up my metal to the limits of my resistance?”

“Yes. You can obtain much higher Magnitude effects from the applied intensity of others. Heat will dissipate with time, but it’ll still be extra deadly for a while.”

“Okay, that’ll do it.” Jack punched in his selection for Hot From the Forge and watched the energetic nova explode and branch out through his little universe. Soon thereafter, he made his selection for his stat choice: Control.

Star-Angel Memoria clapped her hands, floated upward with a splay of her wings, and smiled grandly. “Selections are made! Prepare for cementation, a return to meatspace, and… jacket tiiiiime!”

The depth of expansion was nothing new, but the oddity of the unique secondary mutation was very different. He felt the branches dig deeper into his physicality, touching every fiber of him and making an uncountable number of hyper-stable extradimensional nodules. 'Heat sinks' were what he figured they were like, but he could tell most of the heat would be inaccessible if absorbed by his body, to keep it from burning his tender flesh.

His metal would be a different story.

Jack awoke on the couch, blinking up at the ceiling. His first thought was a peculiarity noticed in the stat gains.

Toughness went up by 0.2 from a level?

Mini chimed in response. <At four levels over from Stitcher’s mod, you’d normally be at 10.0 to have that. Something to do with the System evening out gains at that level, and continuing to be 0.1 per Access Level beyond it.>

Jack shrugged. “Sure thing…”

Sitting up, he checked the time and muttered a curse. He was dangerously close to being late for his promotion ceremony!

He rushed to his bedroom at super speed to get ready.
