Elion gasped for breath, running hard as he carried Liora down the hall. Tuft ran alongside him in Snickers form, patting along on the elegant marble floors. Isalyn, Keyla, and Selna turned occasionally, sending shots backwards with their laser arms to discourage any pursuit.

"We're not gonna be able to stop Dorian," Elion shouted. As if in response, the ground rumbled and shook. "He's too strong."

“What should we do?” Isalyn shouted back.

“I might be able to stall him,” Elion said. “I’m the only one strong enough. We need to heal Liora and get her back to the throne room to stop all this.”

The stone of the walls cracked, crevices  glowing with Zelian power. Elion ran harder, hoping to outrun the spell. Behind him, Selna cried out and tripped.

Small, finger-like tendrils of violet Zelian magic reached out of the ground snagged at Elion’s feet. Beside him Keyla was caught and went down hard.

Elion ripped his feet free from the grasping spell, but they grew stronger. Each step was harder, and then his back foot didn’t break through. He fell, face first, but rolled at the last second to prevent himself from landing on his sister.

The wind was knocked out of him as he collided with the ground. A thousand Zelian spell hands grabbed his hair, clothes, and limbs, pinning him tightly to the ground.

Elion struggled, but found himself entirely immobilized. He jolted his head, and a clump of hair tore free before he was yanked back down.

A booming voice echoed down the halls. "Don't run, Elion. This is your fate, and it always has been."

Dorian floated toward them, hovering a few feet off the ground. He glowed with a violent aura of Zelian power, which cast everything with a purple hue. Dorian laughed loudly as he approached Elion and his friends, all incapacitated on the ground.

“Ants,” he said.

Elion continued to struggle against his bonds. He thought he felt the spell weakening. He’d broken free from Zelian attacks before, but Dorian was so much more powerful than any warlock Elion had faced before.

Elion cursed. He was out of tricks.

Dorian laughed again, his voice thunderous. His white hair stood on end, crackling with power. His eyes burned.

In each hand he held a warlock staff. He raised these, crossing them over his head. His aura brightened, and a burning point of light—brighter than an arc welder—formed where his staffs crossed. Power shook the air and vibrated the stones with a deep bass tone. Dorian's ability charged up.

"No!" Elion screamed. He felt helpless. Overcome in his final moments.

"I finally claim what is mine,” Dorian smirked. “My sister thought she could stop me, but in the end, she could only delay me. I will be the most powerful being the galaxy has ever seen."

The pinprick of light brightened, growing like a molten sun, and Dorian laughed once more.

"Goodbye," he said.

Elion fought against the binding spell, struggling with all his might to resist. The power of the spell weakened. Dorian poured his energy into his finishing attack, and Elion ripped one arm free.   Then he tore the other from the ground, pushing himself to rise. At least he would meet his fate on his feet.

A lance of light leapt out, aimed at Elion’s chest.

It never struck.

Elion caught a glimpse of something rippling down the hallway behind Dorian.

Like an iridescent bubble, the wave of Tephalian distortion ripped through the air. It moved so fast that it overtook Dorian’s spell, a moment before it struck Elion in the chest. It tore through the palace, obliterating any trace of Praxian magic. This wave was the strongest Elion had ever seen.

Elion’s armor vanished. Dorian’s spell evaporated. Dorian fell to the ground, his warlock staves smoking.

The Artefin techniques holding the laser guns together faded, and component parts clattered to the ground. Tuft was instantly transformed back into a man.

Elion picked Liora back up and started running again. Dorian shouted angrily as Elion's friends joined him. They could probably outrun a powerless, middle-aged man.

"The effects of the distortion field won't last forever," Elion shouted, pivoting back to his original plan. "I'm the only one who stands a chance against Dorian in his powered-up state. Someone take Liora and heal her—she's been hurt badly by trying to control the throne. If you can wake her up, she might be able to stop this."

He handed his sister to Isalyn.

"Can you help her, Tuft?"

The man shook his head. "Not inside this distortion field, but outside of it—maybe. She doesn't seem to be physically injured. I think the damage might be more mental, than physical.”

“That’s not great,” Elion said.

The distortion field faded out, and Elion's armor returned to him. He stopped and prepared to turn to face Dorian and protect his friends.

A massive granite block fell to the ground, smashing into the marble tiles where Elion had just run. More debris followed, as a hole opened in the ceiling. A brilliant ray of golden sunlight pierced through, illuminating the ornate artwork along the wall, and Zev descended through the oculus like an angel from heaven.

He spotted Elion immediately, and his face darkened with anger.

"What are you doing out here?" he spat. "You're supposed to be in the throne room. You're the only one who can stop this."

"But Liora—" Elion began.

"Liora betrayed you," Zev snapped. "She's helping Dorian."

"She's unconscious," Elion protested. "She needs medical attention. I can't use the throne unless she abdicates—"

"Abdicates or dies," Zev said.

Elion and Zev stared at each other for a tense moment.

Then the walls of the palace shook and Zelian lightning crackled along them again—the same spell that had bound Elion earlier. Zev gestured, and the Zelian power was met by a barrier of Aurelian light, blocking it.

"I'm doing my best to stall Dorian so that you can stop this," Zev said. "Give me a boost. Use Empower.” Then get to the throne room and do whatever it takes to control the throne. Do you hear me? I don't care how you have to do it. If you don't take control of that throne, this whole planet dies."

Elion took a deep breath. He reached out his hand toward Zev.

> << Empower >>

A cord of light connected him and his uncle. For a moment, he felt all the stress and uncertainty weighing on Zev. The burden of raising Elion and Liora after their parents died. The difficulty of fighting his own brother. Elion understood that Zev was willing to do whatever he had to to protect his planet.

Strength and energy flowing from Elion into Zev. Zev breathed deeply and his eyes brightened. Elion felt slightly drained, but strangely also strengthened by the deeper knowledge of his uncle’s mind.

“Thank you,” Zev said, in a calmer voice. Then he continued issuing orders. "You need to get to the throne, and you need to reassemble the Celestial Sphere. We are running out of time to stop the Tephalian craft. Reports show it is closing in rapidly. Their warriors are advancing on the palace, and if we don't get the sphere back in place before their warship enters the atmosphere, this planet is doomed and everyone on it."

He tensed his jaw. “Look Elion, I’d rather not kill my brother. But I’ll do it if I have to.”

Then he turned and soared down the hall toward his warlock brother.

Elion caught back up with Isalyn and the others. Tuft’s healing magic glowed around them, as he worked on Liora.

“Tuft, we just need to wake her up enough to abdicate the throne. Zev believes I can stop everything then.”

“There is a lot of internal damage here,” Tuft said. “I’m doing the best I can.”

"Does anyone know how to get back to the throne room?" Elion asked.

"I do," Isalyn said.

He took his sister from the woman’s arms and let her lead the way.

As they jogged through the halls of the palace, the distant rumblings of Zev and Dorian's epic fight shook and rattled everything. Tuft continued trying to use his healing magic on Liora as they moved, but from his disconcerted facial expression, Elion was not hopeful.

"Can't you do something to wake her up?" Elion said. "I just need her to wake up and abdicate the throne."

Tuft shook his head. "There doesn’t seem to be much more that I can heal."

“Keep trying,” Elion said.

They reached the throne room. It lay in ruins, the wreckage of Dorian’s and Zev’s fighting marring the once beautiful space.

Elion carried Liora back to the dias.

“It’s going to be okay,” he muttered. “You had to find a healer for her. You had to help her.”

Keyla stepped up beside him, placing a hand on his shoulder. “I’m sorry about Yaret,” she said.

“I can’t think about that right now,” Elion said. “We have work to do.”

“Ah hah!” Tuft exclaimed, grinning.

Liora blinked her eyes open and took a sharp breath of air. She looked dazed, then focused in on Elion’s face. Her brows narrowed, her face slowly shifting into a snarl.

“You!” she spat.
