The tree turned out to be more stubborn than Sakura had expected. She thought about that while sitting knee-deep in wet soil by its roots and clutching a narrow trowel in her fingers. Her fingers already hurt, her back ached, and at some point it began to feel as if everything hurt, even the parts of her body she had never thought about before.

But the old, tall tree with its dark, crooked trunk still stood. It grew on the roadside outside the city and had long ago started leaning toward the road. Sakura had chosen it not only because it was tall and perfect for her plan, but because the ground under one side of the roots had sunk after the rains, while on the other side the roots were almost exposed.

Sakura could not fell a healthy tree even with Pampli. But she could spend the whole night digging under weak roots while Pampli tore at the earth with his paws.

"Quiet," Sakura suddenly whispered.

Pampli froze, raised his muzzle, and snorted unhappily. She froze too and listened. The night was quiet. Only somewhere below, closer to the city, a badly fastened shutter creaked. No one was traveling on the road.

Sakura drove the trowel back into the ground and kept digging with all her strength. She knew why she was doing this. For Gilbert and for Lir. She had to carry out the plan exactly at the appointed time.

She knew the schedule: the wagon from the Castle was supposed to pass in the morning. Dirty laundry was usually delivered to the workshop before the start of the day shift, so they would have time to sort it, wash it, and send it on for work. Dirty laundry from the prison was not hauled out every day. That would have been too expensive and stupid. They collected it, sorted it, and sent it out once every three days.

If Gilbert had managed to leave a sign, it had to be there. If he had not, then she had nothing. Sakura did not want to think about the second option.

She pulled out another clump of wet dirt and threw it aside. Pampli dug. His claws went into the earth much better than her tool, but even he was getting tired. Sometimes the beast stopped, listened, and looked at her as if asking why they were torturing a tree in the middle of the night.

"Because we’re small and very clever," Sakura said quietly.

Pampli blinked.

"Fine. You’re not small."

He snorted again and struck a root with his paw.

By dawn, the ground around the tree had turned into a muddy pit. Sakura stood beside it, trembling from cold and exhaustion, but she could not wait any longer. If the wagon appeared before the tree fell, the whole plan would become nothing more than a stupid night walk with digging.

Sakura tied a thick, strong rope around the trunk and fastened the other end around Pampli.

"Not right away," she warned. "When I say."

Pampli crouched, and Sakura braced her shoulder against the trunk, but the tree did not even move. Sakura felt very small. But she knew that together with Pampli, they would manage.

"Now."

Pampli lunged. At first, nothing happened. Then something cracked dully in the ground. One of the roots split with a wet sound. The tree swayed, froze, and began slowly falling in the direction Sakura had spent all night persuading it to go.

"Back!"

She jumped aside, slipped, almost fell, and managed to grab a bush. The trunk crashed across the road with such force that the birds in the nearby trees immediately burst from the branches.

Sakura froze, breathing hard. She had just blocked the road with a large, heavy tree, which meant everything was going exactly according to plan. For now.

Sakura led Pampli into the bushes beyond the ditch and made him lie down. Pampli clearly believed that after such work his place was beside her, not in a wet hiding place, but he could not go into the wagon. If the drivers saw a strange obsidian beast near a prison wagon, the plan would end at once.

"Wait here," Sakura said. "If I don’t come back, don’t play hero. Wait. If anyone comes near you, go into the forest," she instructed him.

Pampli looked at her with such intelligent eyes that Sakura almost did not doubt he had understood everything.

The wagon appeared when the sun had just risen above the distant city roofs. It was covered, rough, and marked with the dark symbols of the Castle service on its sides. Several strong, good horses pulled it.

The drivers saw the tree and stopped almost at once.

"Let the damn thing rot," one said.

"It already is," the second answered. "See the roots?"

"Is that supposed to make me feel better?"

Both climbed down. One went to the trunk, while the second opened something under the seat and began rummaging there. Sakura could not see what exactly he was taking out. The wagon blocked her view, and looking too long was dangerous anyway. The important thing was something else: both drivers were distracted by the tree. The first was already standing by the trunk, kicking branches with his boot and swearing, while the second was saying something to him, also irritated.

At that moment, the countdown began. While the men were busy with the tree, Sakura had time to carry out the plan. Exactly the way Gilbert had instructed her.

Sakura slipped out of the ditch. Every step seemed too loud. Mud squelched under her boots, and her heart beat so hard that the whole city could probably hear it. She approached the back of the wagon and crouched by the door.

Gilbert had managed to teach her this not long before his arrest. She was not supposed to pick the lock. It was complicated, and it would be checked first. Hinges were checked later, if they were checked at all. Work wagons that did not carry prisoners were often repaired carelessly, and the rear door was held on two outer hinges with pins. If the pins were knocked out with a thin wedge, the door could be removed without leaving a broken lock.

Sakura took out a small set of tools. The first pin did not come out right away. Her fingers slipped with sweat, and she nearly dropped the wedge. The drivers’ voices came from the front of the wagon.

"You won’t move it that easily."

"Am I arguing?"

"And what are we supposed to do with this?"

They were swearing, and that was good, because they were so busy with the obstacle that they were not even thinking of checking the rear of the wagon right now. Sakura pressed harder on the wedge, and the pin finally shifted. She pulled it out and moved to the second. That one came easier, and the door moved slightly away from the frame. Sakura caught it with her shoulder so it would not hit the side, lifted it, squeezed inside, and pulled the door back.

The door sat crookedly in place, but from the outside it should look closed. Sakura put the pins back in, but not all the way. Just enough for them to hold. If she was lucky, she would push them out later and leave the same way.

Inside, it was dark and cramped, and it smelled of wet cloth, sweat, blood, and something else. The sacks lay in two rows, tied with coarse rope.

Sakura took out a small, dim lantern with a narrow lens and a dark metal rim. Under the glass, tiny glowing moths shifted like little sparks.

Sakura still did not know where exactly Gilbert had gotten such a thing. To her, the lantern was still a small miracle. But the most miraculous thing was something else: only its light could reveal the secret invisible ink.

Sakura clenched the lantern between her teeth, untied the first sack, and began pulling out cloth. There was a lot of fabric, and it was all different. Sometimes she found thick, good-quality bandages, then towels, shirts, and a little protective cloth. She lit the items one by one, but found no secret messages.

Then a sharp sound came from outside, followed by another. Sakura froze and listened. It sounded like an axe.

"Chop lower!" one of the drivers shouted.

"You chop lower if you’re so smart!"

Sakura slowly exhaled and forced herself to keep going. The fact that those men had an axe was unpleasant, but not too unexpected, though of course she had wanted to believe they were not that prepared. Gilbert had said the drivers would most likely tie the tree to the horses and try to drag it away that way. But they were doing it differently. Still, a tree like that could not be cleared in a couple of minutes. The trunk was heavy, the roots were still clinging to the earth, and the branches lay across the road. Even if they started chopping, they would have to struggle with it.

That meant there was still time. Less than she wanted, but there was time.

She shoved the first cloth back in any way she could and tore open the second sack. Her fingers were shaking, but Sakura forced herself to breathe more evenly. She knew panic would not make her faster. On the contrary, it would only make her clumsier.

Items flashed under the lantern light. A shirt, a cloak, a blanket, more bandages. Sakura sorted through them quickly, trying to shine the light over every side. Knowing Gilbert, the message could very well be hidden somewhere extremely unobvious. But no matter how much she searched and shone the light, there was still no message.

Outside, the tree cracked again, then something heavy dragged across the ground. A rope creaked as it tightened.

"Come on!" one of the drivers shouted. "Again!"

The horses snorted. The wagon rocked slightly, though it was still standing still. Apparently, the drivers had now put the horses to work. Sakura did not know exactly what was happening outside, but she guessed they were cutting off branches and dragging the trunk away to the roadside.

Sakura shoved everything back in as it was and tore open the next sack. The lantern slid over cloth. Pale light fell across stains, seams, other people’s sleeves and collars. There were no invisible letters. Panic began rolling in after all, without asking permission.

Outside, someone shouted again, and the rope out there tightened with such a creak that Sakura wanted to cover her ears. Judging by the sound, the tree had moved. Sakura could hear its branches scrape the road, as if resisting the shift.

No, no, no.

She had to make it in time.

According to the plan, the tree was supposed to delay them longer. Long enough for her to find the right sack, check the cloth, get out, and disappear into the ditch. According to the plan, Pampli was supposed to wait for her behind the bushes, not be left somewhere behind while the wagon carried her toward the city.

The plan, clearly, had no interest in what it was supposed to do. The wagon rocked, and Sakura hit her shoulder against a sack. The wheels jerked, creaked, and began to roll.

They were moving.

For several seconds, Sakura simply froze, unable to believe it had happened. Then the wagon bounced over the first stone, and that returned her ability to think. She still had to find the message. She had not checked everything. If she never found Gilbert’s message, then their plan with the prison would collapse completely. She would not be able to save Lir or Gilbert.

So, no matter what, she had to keep searching. Sakura turned over the next sack, and the first thing that caught her eye was a dark, well-made healer’s robe with a torn edge by the pocket. Sakura ran the lantern along its outer side and found nothing. She turned the robe over and now shone the light on the inside. Then thin lines appeared in the pale glow.

17 GR

18 BL

Under the first line were two short strokes.

It was definitely a message from Gilbert. Sakura looked at the letters for only a moment, but it was enough to understand the code. On the seventeenth, there would be green mist here, and on the eighteenth, blue. They had to aim for the seventeenth, but not forget that if something went wrong, the eighteenth would be their Plan B. That meant everything had to be prepared.

From relief, Sakura’s hands weakened for a second, and she let out a deep, heavy breath. Then she remembered that she was still riding in a wagon toward the city and the workshop, and that was not very good. Sakura pulled herself together, took out a small scrap of paper, and quickly copied down the code. Then she hid the paper behind the inner seam of her sleeve, not fully trusting Inventory. She shoved the robe back into the sack and tightened the rope as well as she could.

The wagon jumped sharply. Sakura struck her side against the wall. Behind her, something cracked dryly, then there was a slap, and gray morning light struck into the wagon.

The rear door had come off.

For several seconds, Sakura stared at the open hole and the road running away under the wheels behind her, and there was not a single decent thought in her head.

Outside, the driver shouted:

"Stop! The door came off! The door!"

That was an exit. Jump out now and... And almost immediately behind the wagon, a rider turned onto the road from a side path. Sakura saw him only for an instant: a dark figure in the saddle. The rider saw the wagon, which meant he saw the open rear too. And if a girl in dirty clothes fell out from inside right now, he would definitely see that as well.

Sakura clenched her teeth. The exit immediately stopped being an exit. Feeling the wagon slow, Sakura did the only logical thing in that situation: she threw herself into the sacks. She buried herself in the dirty cloth as deeply as she could. A wet shirt fell over her face. The smell almost made her retch, but she forced herself not to move. She could not cough, or breathe too loudly, or think about who had recently died in that shirt.

Judging by the sounds, the rider had already rushed past, but the drivers’ footsteps took his place. They were talking to each other.

"I told you the rear door was loose," one said.

"It was holding fine this morning."

"The sacks in place?"

A pause. Sakura felt someone tug the nearest sack. The fabric over her face shifted, and Sakura pressed herself deeper, feeling the dirty laundry push against her chest.

"In place."

They both fell silent. Then one spat.

"Let’s put it back. We need to go, and make sure the rest of this doesn’t fly out."

The door was lifted, and it scraped against the frame. Then one pin went in too deep. The second was struck with something heavy. Then again.

Sakura closed her eyes. Now she definitely would not pull them out quickly from the inside.

The drivers swore a little more, checked the fastening, and returned to the front. The wagon moved again. Sakura lay in the sacks until the wheels settled into a steady rhythm. Only then did she carefully crawl out, spitting out dust and other people’s cloth.

The door stood firmly in place. Sakura crawled to it and tried to hook the pin with the same tool, but this time she failed. The drivers had done their job well. She would have to pick at it for a long time. Did she have time for that?

Sakura leaned her forehead against the wood and let out a doomed sigh. Outside, the sound of the road changed. The wheels were no longer going over soft earth. Instead, they rattled over stone. Around the wagon came human voices and distant dog barking. From that, Sakura quickly understood what was happening: the wagon had entered the city. Greymost. And that meant she could no longer get out of here so easily, even if she managed to remove the door again.

And now Sakura was locked inside a wagon that had already entered Greymost and was inevitably approaching a workshop packed full of workers.
