The day of the march has finally arrived.

There was an unusual sense of urgency amidst the recruits as they went to devour their early breakfast. Words were dull if spoken, and Kalsin made it a guessing game to see who would be the next person to break the monotony of clinking cutlery amidst the tightly packed dining hall. He wasn't typically a morning person, but this morning was far from typical.

Kalsin raised another mouthful of badly cooked beef as he observed the others. Crawl, sitting on his left, poked at the meat with a faraway look that could've easily been mistaken for a halfway state out of sleep. That wasn't it though, since Kalsin knew his food rarely went uneaten in the mornings.

Littleguy caught Crawl's eyes as he leaned in from Kalsin's left, receiving a small shake of head when he pointed at Crawl’s plate. Kalsin pushed back in his seat to avoid the large hand questing for fresh food, scooping up Crawl's plate to set it before himself. At least Littleguy retained his usual appetite.

"Twenty-three days," Uggo said from across the table. Other than Kalsin, he was the only person in their company with a scar on his face. A large-ass scar that started at his forehead, stretching down along his nose and curling over to the other end of his mouth like a fish hook. The black line pulled on his muscles into a lopsided sneer whenever he spoke.

"Twenty-six days over here," Dweller muttered from two seats down the row. "But I doubt even another month of training would make a real difference."

"Please. Fresh air, open skies, and the eventual whiff of Veiled corpses decomposing after a good night's work. What's not to love?" Ringtail said with an oddly upbeat tone, almost knocking over a water jug as she lightly bumped Dweller with one shoulder.

Kalsin wondered if she really meant that. Though most had committed crimes to get here, there was a long gap between minor infractions such as thievery, and acts like murder. Even if said murder was necessary to defend one's homeland. Part of him wanted to ask, but he and Ringtail were uneasy acquaintances at best. She'd never give a straight answer.

Instead, he simply said, "It'll take a while until we reach the border, so. I'm sure we'll get our fair share of training in the meantime."

"Tell that to my feet," Crawl said. "Gods, I can already feel their protests and we haven't even started out."

Littleguy didn't look up from his feast. "Putting those legs to work, there's no better way to build endurance. Gotta harden up, Crawl. Been telling you."

"I know you have. And I will, but it's the principle of it, you see. Sometimes, you just need to sit down in peace and get all the bitching out of your system, lay it all out before you're neck deep in shit."

"Truth," Kalsin said around a half-chewed piece of bread, going along with a general murmur of agreement around the table.

"Rum? Anyone's got rum left?" Youthere's voice rose, followed by a hurled bottle meeting a tragic end against a wall suspiciously close to his head. Apparently, that was a negative.

Other than a few disappointed glares, nobody paid much attention to the unfolding argument between Batshit and Youthere, whose daily verbal clashes could almost be considered tradition at this point.

"Mhm, Ghost," Crawl said with visible effort to shake off the last dregs of sleep. "I saw your bed empty at dawn, and thought, righto, that's a first. Made me wonder where our resident creature of the night slipped off to."

"Maybe he found another bed to haunt?" Littleguy said with a wooden expression, wagging his brows at Ringtail. She gave him a middle finger.

"You remember the people I came to join up with?" Kalsin asked. He'd painted them a rough picture about how he ended up joining the army—leaving out names and replacing his crimes with a severe case of tax evasion instead of the whole Civil House tomfuckery. And he never mentioned Jack and her cleansing, of course. That was a pile of bones he had enough issues dealing with in his own head. But he did tell them about his injured traveling companions. "I wanted to try and check on them one last time."

A sympathetic expression surfaced on Crawl's face. "Let me guess. Request denied?"

Kalsin nodded. "But one of the guys watching the gate was nice enough to run over to the medical ward. He returned awful quick. Said neither of them were in good enough condition to make it through a single day of training, let alone march."

He wasn't lying, but he wasn't telling the whole truth either. The guard told him Erazza still hadn't woken, the doctors uncertain about her condition. Áron was healing up nicely, but that was about it. The azTél brother would be bedridden for at least another two weeks. Which meant that, save from Cinder and Jack somewhere in the midst of the Clenn ranks, Kalsin was being whisked away toward the eastern border on his own.

"My cousin tried dodging a boar hunt, once,” Dweller said. “He was a peaceful sort, never touched a bow, but his name was pulled from the hat and so he had to go along. You know what he did? He put a hammer to his finger." She shook her head. "Everyone thought him a moron, at first—him included. Coward of the village. Then the hunting party returned with two people mauled to death by game they failed to catch. And then? Nobody was laughing then. Who knows. He could’ve been one of those folk. A broken finger might've saved his life."

"Good for him. Here though, circumstances are a little different here," Crawl said. "Ghost's friends are in the army now. Medics are gonna do their best to patch them up, then bam, send their asses right into the fray. If they’re not coming with our batch of recruits, then they're going along with the next one."

Kalsin figured the same, though he hoped the azTél siblings would take their sweet time recovering. No offense to them, but confronting prime fanatics of this seb hordozó thing, well, that was a bridge better left uncrossed.

By the time they finished up, Lieutenant Brownweather was waiting for them just outside the barracks with several gray uniformed men in his company, including the stablemaster, a stout man Kalsin had only seen a handful of times when visiting Sap. The horses had been herded together with large packs tied to their backs. The recruits had been warned the journey would be spent on foot. For one, only about half the men owned a mount. For two, unless the recruits wanted to haul a pile of dead weight that was their armor, halberds and shields all the way to the east, they had to make peace with small sacrifices, such as the luxuries of horseback.

Understandable, that. At least for someone as versed in traveling as Kalsin. With his pack hoisted over his shoulder and sword sheathed in his underarm sling, adding a five foot long metal pole on top would’ve proven problematic.

The recruits' gray attire was a simplified variant of the Wounded Division's outfits, missing the black accents around the neck and at the sleeves. It was their first time wearing the uniforms, the clear distinction between soldiers and soldiers-to-be made all the more pronounced by the entourage waiting for them. They fell in the practiced line in front of the lieutenant.

"This year, Lostpoint Station is sending you off as the second group of miscreants unfortunate enough to lend aid to our Republic," he said, tone just a hair more lively than usual. Didn't bring much color to his dead voice. But at least Kalsin had the pleasure to discover what it felt to be promoted from manure on the roadside, to puke stain in a fancy ballroom. "Had you gotten Lieutenant Lemming or Cogmann, you'd be listening to a different sort of speech right now. Instead, you got me. Fucked lucked, you lot are."

He said something to the stablemaster, dismissing him from his side, then turned back to pace down the line of recruits. "See these four-legged mules behind me? You owned them before you joined up, now I own you, which means I now own whatever the hell you owned beforehand. I can aaalready hear it. 'But sir, it cost me two halves of my family home and my auntie's overgrown spine to buy a horse!'" He stopped, leaning into Joe's face. "Too. Bad. The holiday resort spots are rented out to the most valuable assets present, and since every last one of you is a worthless sack of rancid sock, it's gonna be foodstuffs, tents, and heavy weaponry taking the saddle."

To Joe's credit, he didn't as much as flinch in the face of the spittle storm. Brownweather stepped back and continued. "Now, that might sound unnecessarily harsh, so let me share a snippet of good news, then. I'm not a discriminatory man. Nobody," he said, eyes sweeping over Littleguy, "will get special treatment. Everyone has a pair of army-grade boots, well, you'll get to use them over the coming weeks. Everyone's got a sword and shield to their name, you'll continue that monkey parade you call training every time we stop and rest, and before we break camp. If you're a big boy with the sword and figure there's nothing else anyone could teach you, you'll be pushed double at the halberd. Mastered hiding behind your shield, I'll smack you to train without one. Think you're good at something, good fucking job, it's still nothing I haven't seen a Veiled extinguish in a second. Think of that before you think of getting lazy with your training."

He turned, signaling to the gathered soldiers. They started filing out the gates, some leading horses, others doing last minute checks of equipment. Over his shoulder, Brownweather called, "These men, don't bother them. Good and proven soldiers. They're our escorts to the Outer Line. You got something to say, pretend they're me and otherwise stuff it."

Kalsin watched a balding man tighten a harness across Sap's back amidst the bustling crowd. Save for a small flick of his gills, his bahten tolerated the man's administrations without issue. Kalsin thought of Hollow, and immediately pitied the poor soul who would try and turn Jack's warlander bahten into a mere pack animal.

"Still nothing about surge," Crawl said. Kalsin didn't need to look at him to hear the frown in his voice. "Weapon training, building endurance, hammering us to follow orders, that's all good and well. Basics of soldering. But I thought by now the lieutenant would've at least asked the extent of our surges. Why's that, you think?"

Sap would be fine. He was used to Kalsin's absence, and he had better things to worry about. Anyone treats Sap gently, the only thing you need to look out for is keeping your hair from turning to munched grass.

"Ghost?"

"We're Scarrd, Crawl. No one gives a toss about our surge. You're either a dirt, a rot, or a hive, none of which makes a ton of difference on the battlefield."

"Hive does. You saw Brownweather's power. Felt it, all of us did."

Kalsin glanced at him. "Apologies, correction then. Unless you're hive, your surge won't make a difference. If you are one, you might look forward to a potential leading position in the Scarrd Division somewhere down the line." He shrugged. "Which still means fuck-all for most of us."

By now Crawl has probably picked up on his annoyance, but if so, he chose to ignore it. "What are you?"

"Rot. With my dirt surge so weak it might not even exist." The practiced lie rolled smoothly from Kalsin's tongue.

"I'm a dirt. Mediocre one at that, too. Not much, honest, but it's a tool I will use when fighting for my life. So, I dunno—it just comes off as strange that Brownweather wouldn't let us train with it." Crawl pulled on his pack, shifting the weight on his back higher. He appeared more collected than before, not fidgeting quite as much as recruits got ready to move out around them.

"We train to improve," Kalsin said. "You swing a sword, eventually you get better at it. Doing the same thing over and over again and expecting different results ain't insanity. It's practice. That's why our sweet-and-sadistic lieutenant wakes us at the crack of each morning, telling us again, again, again, using nothing but the muscles of our body. When we spar, the only factor in play is the knowledge and skill we have accumulated with sweat and tears. And the surge? Adding surge to that mix would be like spitting in the face of our growth. There's no respect in beating Littleguy simply because the surge you were born with is just a little faster than his. No lessons to draw from it. Just pure, meaningless disrespect."

Kalsin took a deep, calming breath. He said his piece, and if Crawl wasn't open to his views being opposed, then that was that.

Thankfully, Crawl remained silent for a while, as if he really did mull over his words. Far as Kalsin was concerned, that was a rare quality in people. "I just don't think it's helpful to act as if the surge doesn’t exist. Maybe… an extra hour a day to test our limits, setting aside some time for it? Dunno. But I see your point. We are what we are. As Scarrd, we’re never gonna rain fire and flames down on the enemy lines."

Kalsin nodded. "An extra hour a day wouldn’t help you much, I’m afraid. The only way you improve your surge is by advancing, which isn't something you can train."

Not to mention, Crawl's suggestion would make keeping his secret—his complete lack of surge—much harder.

His secret? One of his secrets, more like. No surge, scars sticking to him, keeping Clenn as company, and the small possibility of being haunted by ghosts...

At the rate he was piling those secrets up, he'd be lucky if he wasn’t buried under ten feet of bullshit by the end of the year.
