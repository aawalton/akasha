Chapter 41: Gathering Power

Molech had claimed an old fort as his stronghold, a structure that looked like a smaller, broken version of the Cathedral that dominated the dungeon's center. The walls were built from the same black stone as everything else in this place, but time or violence had taken chunks out of them, leaving gaps that would make defense difficult. Faded dark orange symbols decorated what remained of the walls, carved deep into the stone in patterns that hurt to look at directly.

The symbols were different from the ones I'd seen elsewhere in the dungeon, suggesting this fort had been built for a different purpose than the passages and chambers I'd explored. The lines were more angular, more aggressive, speaking of a culture that valued conquest over subtlety. Whatever civilization had originally constructed this place, they hadn't been concerned with beauty or harmony.

More than forty initiates prowled around the fort like ants around a disturbed hill. Groups were constantly coming and going, bringing back dead Flux beasts slung over their shoulders and other materials I couldn't identify from this distance. There was some organization to it, but it looked loose, more like people following basic orders than any kind of real military discipline.

I was standing far enough away that I hoped not to be spotted, crouched behind a cluster of twisted trees whose alien bark provided decent camouflage. The distance made it difficult to get detailed intelligence, but I could see enough to understand that this wasn't just random chaos. There was some structure here, though it looked fragile.

My plan to observe undetected lasted about ten minutes before a lookout spotted me. The initiate was posted on what remained of the fort's highest tower, probably more by luck than systematic planning. He pointed directly at my position and yelled something I couldn't make out from this distance, but his meaning was clear enough.

A group of initiates immediately broke away from their activities and ran into the forest toward me, moving with more purpose than I'd expected but lacking real coordination. These people had some training, but they weren't soldiers.

I decided to run rather than stand and fight.

It wasn't out of fear, though any sane person would have been afraid of facing ten opponents simultaneously. It was tactical thinking. Leading them deeper into the forest would prevent the commotion from drawing additional enemies, and the terrain would give me advantages that open ground wouldn't provide. Fighting smart was better than fighting hard.

Tree leaves blurred past me as I sprinted through the alien undergrowth, my mechanical breathing only slightly faster as my feet pounded against the uneven ground. The respirator's filters worked efficiently even during exertion, maintaining steady airflow that kept my thinking clear. My enhanced physical attributes from weeks of Flux advancement made the pace sustainable even while carrying the backpack.

I could feel the pursuing initiates through the Flux, their excitement building as they closed the distance. Their aspects surged within them, power flowing in preparation for violence. Ten of them, all moving with confidence that came from being part of a larger group. They expected to overwhelm me through numbers, though their approach lacked real tactical thinking.

As I ran, I reached out through the Flux and began calling the local wildlife to my aid. The creatures were there, waiting in the shadows between the twisted trees. Wolves with eyes like burning coals, boars whose metallic hide clinked as they moved, and even a few strange birds whose wings moved in patterns that defied normal aerodynamics.

The beasts responded to my summons with eager hunger, abandoning whatever they had been doing to converge on the pursuing initiates. I heard the first contact begin behind me, roars and shouts mixing with the distinctive hum of resonance weapons being activated. The sound was music to my ears, the chaos I needed to even the odds.

I turned to engage as the battle erupted in full, my own blade igniting with its familiar white light touched by crimson. The illumination cut through the forest's eternal twilight, marking my position for anyone who cared to look. But stealth was no longer an option, and I preferred honest violence to skulking in shadows.

Two initiates broke away from the larger fight to engage me directly, aliens with bark-like skin and ashen eyes that reminded me of Zoey. They attacked with resonance weapons that manifested as curved daggers, glowing with sickly yellow light. The weapons suited their nature, but their short reach put them at a severe disadvantage against my longer blade. It was trivially easy to stay outside their effective range while striking at will, even without the advantage of sensing their aspects through my bloodline's authority. Their intentions were as clear to me as if they'd announced their attacks verbally.

I froze one of the tree-like opponents with my Command Presence while engaging the other, easily dodging her clumsy strikes and responding with a thrust through what I assumed was her midsection. The resonance energy burned through her bark-like flesh like a hot knife through butter, and she collapsed in a shower of smoldering fragments.

Nothing more than burnt kindling now, as far as I was concerned.

I wasted no time dispatching the second alien, my blade removing her head before my hold on her aspects could weaken. Power flooded into me as she died, familiar and sweet, adding to the growing collection of energy I'd accumulated through violence. Each kill made the next one slightly easier, advancement building on itself in an endless spiral.

The Flux beasts were dying just as easily under the initiates' weapons, which was fine with me. All I needed was for them to provide distraction and divide attention, not to actually win the fight for me. They were tools, expendable resources to be used and discarded as tactical necessity demanded.

I crushed the throats of several more initiates one by one through the Flux, their corpses falling to the forest floor as invisible hands closed around their windpipes. It was efficient work, requiring minimal effort while producing maximum results. My enhanced Resonance made such techniques almost casual, barely worth the energy required to maintain them.

One initiate stood out from the others, a black-haired man with striking blue eyes who wielded a massive claymore. He swung the weapon as easily as if it weighed nothing at all, the resonance energy eliminating physical mass while maintaining all the destructive potential. His technique was competent, suggesting real training rather than improvised desperation.

His attributes were low compared to mine, however. I could sense his limitations through the Flux, the gaps in his development that marked him as someone who had focused on weapon skill over raw power advancement. The imbalance would be his downfall.

I reached out and crushed his weapon through concentrated force, applying pressure until the crystal matrix that contained its energy collapsed into useless fragments. His eyes widened in shock as the claymore crumpled like foil, but he had no time to process what had happened before my blade removed his head in a spray of arterial crimson.

His blue eyes died like embers of a long-abandoned fire, the light fading as his consciousness fled whatever remained of his body. His corpse became just another cooling sack of flesh as I stepped over it to engage the remaining enemies.

The other initiates died in similar fashion, their resistance crumbling as the tactical situation became clear. One even tried to run when he realized how outmatched they were, but I made a fist and crushed his knee through the Flux before he could escape. Bone and cartilage compressed under invisible pressure, producing a wet cracking sound that echoed through the forest.

He cried out in agony as he collapsed to the ground, his resonance blade flickering out of existence as his concentration shattered. The weapon's hilt clattered across the forest floor, useless metal without the will to animate it. He lay there writhing, clutching at his ruined leg while tears of pain streamed down his face.

I stood over him as the sounds of battle faded around us, his companions dead or dying among the twisted trees. He was blonde with pointed ears and features that were more beautiful than handsome, possessing an alien elegance that marked him as something other than human. His eyes held intelligence despite the pain, the kind of awareness that suggested he understood exactly how precarious his situation had become.

Interesting. Apparently elves existed in this multiverse, or at least something close enough to the mythological ideal to make no difference. I filed the observation away for future reference, another piece of data about the scope of reality beyond Earth's narrow boundaries.

My voice emerged from the respirator's speakers, more digital than human, processed through filters that stripped away every trace of warmth or mercy.

"Let's talk, you and I, about Molech."

The elf's eyes widened further as he realized he might actually survive this encounter if he provided useful information. Fear and hope warred across his elegant features while pain from his crushed knee provided a constant reminder of what happened to those who disappointed me.

It was time to learn everything I could about my final opponent before the real battle began. Knowledge was just another form of power, and I intended to gather as much as possible before walking into Molech's stronghold.

The elf opened his mouth to speak, and I leaned closer to listen. What he told me would determine both his fate and my approach to the final confrontation that would decide the outcome of these games.

One way or another, I would have that fourth key before this was over.
