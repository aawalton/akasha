‘Interesting,’ Nephthys mused after listening to Percy. ‘Did your mind clones benefit from Enhanced Cognition?’

Percy raised an eyebrow. ‘I think so?’

The truth was that he hadn’t considered this earlier, but each of his clones seemed to have retained his increased thinking speed. One of them would have certainly noticed if his thoughts suddenly slowed down.

‘Good. That means that your bloodline will likely amplify the effects of your mindsets – at least in some cases,’ Nephthys explained.

‘So… are we still going to prioritize mindset creation?’ Percy asked.

‘We are, albeit with a slight change,’ the Ruby Princess said. ‘We’ll start with a mindset that lets you harness the cognitive power of your clones more efficiently. It would be a waste not to take advantage of them as soon as possible, and this is going to be a relatively simple mindset anyway.’

‘How so?’

‘I have no idea how to create something as complicated as a mind clone, but your bloodline does all the heavy lifting for you. We just have to figure out how to link your minds in a more practical way. I bet that your Blessing of Connection and experience with soul clones will completely trivialize the challenge. In fact, this mindset should be somewhat similar to the one that Phoebe helped you create, so it shouldn’t take more than a week to design.’

Percy’s heart skipped a couple of beats as he realized once more how uniquely compatible he was with the mind affinity. Without considering what else he might be able to do with it, its interaction with his bloodline alone would basically accelerate his training by a factor of ten.

Perhaps he ought to thank Baphomet for forcing his hand.

He was about to ask Nephthys to start teaching him, when Micky interjected. ‘Percy… do you think I should activate the Void Decree as well? I know that we decided to hold on until we found a way to extend our lifespans, but that’s a bit of a moot point now, don’t you think?’

Percy fell silent for a minute, pondering over his friend’s words. Awakening Micky’s third core wouldn’t be entirely risk-free, since they didn’t know their exact lifespans. The Huehuan might end up with even less time than Percy, and whatever solution they found might not work for both of them.

Then again, their limited lifespan was no longer the only – nor even the most pressing – problem. They only had a few thousand years to grow strong enough to protect Remior from Baphomet. If Micky’s third affinity proved half as useful as Percy’s, it would be better to start cleansing and practicing with it sooner rather than later.

‘Alright. I’ll start granting second cores to the Starry Knights once I have some free time,’ Percy reluctantly said.

They’d obviously go for a rare affinity again, and they’d have to prepare the sacrifices by themselves this time. Empowering the Green wasps would take years, though it was something that they’d have to do regardless of the Huehuan’s new core, as it would increase both the hive’s military strength and nectar output. The delay would also give them plenty of time to pick the best affinity for the Huehuan.

That said, Percy would rather complete the initial exploration of his mind affinity before embarking on that project.

With another long-term goal added to his to-do list, Percy began studying the basics of mindset creation under his familiar’s guidance.

Nephthys taught him how to shape his mind mana into various types of permanent structures inside his head: bridges that could link mental processes more efficiently, blocks that would sever those connections entirely, gates that he could open or close at will, funnels that could slow signals down, and even a type of magical grease that could accelerate his thoughts.

According to the reincarnated goddess, these were the universal building blocks that most mindsets made use of. Some specialized mindsets like Ludwick’s Compass required more advanced techniques, though that thankfully didn’t apply to what Percy was currently trying to do.

This wasn’t his first time hearing this information, though he didn’t mind reinforcing and expanding his understanding now that he no longer had Phoebe to provide him with mana or hold his hand.

‘Remember that the mental structures you implement don’t have to be exact. Real world physics do not apply to your mindscape, so your intentions ultimately matter more than specific shapes or material properties,’ the Ruby Princess explained.

Percy nodded. He had to constantly alternate between the real world – where his body was sitting cross-legged on the rough floor of his familiar’s cavern – and the starry sky that existed solely within his and Micky’s heads.

Whenever he exited his mindscape, he drew a trickle of mind mana to his brain to introduce a magical link between his mind and that of a clone. After returning to the ocean of stars, he had to locate the new object and shape it into something more deliberate.

Contrary to his expectations, Nephthys explained that his bloodline wouldn’t allow him to think ten times faster.

Not exactly.

It would allow him to think ten times as many thoughts in the same amount of time – which sounded similar but was slightly different.

According to his tutor, there was no way to stack his minds on a single line of thought, because each had to complete a thought before the others could build upon it. The best that he could do would be to connect his clones into a shared pool of memories and a central decision-making mind.

That way, each clone would be aware of what the rest had thought as soon as they had thought it, allowing them to cooperate without arguments, redundancies, or unnecessary friction. However, his minds would still be working on multiple tasks that might or might not be related.

Apparently, there were ways to directly accelerate one’s thoughts, and Percy’s Enhanced Cognition was one of them, though the mass-produced mindset was rather poor at it. Percy and Nephthys still intended to upgrade that mindset later, in the hopes that they could combine its serial processing boost with the parallel processing effect of his bloodline.

Alas, it was best to focus on a single problem at a time.

Two giant constellations had originally floated within Percy’s mindscape. The first consisted of pale stars arranged in the shape of a giant crow. The second was made of grey stars arranged in Percy’s image.

The pair of enormous structures were soon joined by ten smaller ones. Each was also shaped like Percy, though nine were made of brand-new stars that shone with the orange light of his augmented mind mana. The tenth was made of a mixture of pale and grey stars that Percy had pulled out of the larger constellations.

While the orange shapes represented his mind clones, the tenth represented the soul clone that had been consumed during his fusion with Micky. The clone’s cognitive prowess hadn’t been lost – it had simply been absorbed by their main minds. Since Percy was going through the trouble of defining and linking himself to the resources provided by his mind clones, he decided to carve out a dedicated avatar for the soul clone’s resources as well.

If one examined the new shapes carefully, they would notice lots of thin threads linking them to one another, the giant constellations, and even the sea of colourful stars that shone in the background.

Connecting everything took a couple of days, because there were several more minds involved this time than just his and Micky’s. Even though Percy’s bloodline hadn’t directly affected his fellow aspect, he wanted to have the option of lending him the excess brainpower if necessary, so he had to ensure that the new mindset was flexible enough to allow that.

It was with great anticipation that he pulled the final thread in place. After double- and triple-checking his work with Nephthys’ help, he willed the intricate tapestry of stars to activate.

The constellations lit up one by one, causing the contents of Percy’s skull to shift in strange ways. It felt a bit like linking himself to his mind clones, but the feeling was simultaneously more intense and less disorienting.

There were no stray thoughts or annoying whispers littering his mind. Instead, he felt ten new cognitive “pockets” appear inside his head. He instinctively knew that he could fill each of them with various tasks, without affecting his sensory domain nor his ability to control his body.

In some ways, the mental pockets reminded him of how Micky’s mind was lingering at the edge of his consciousness, except that these minds didn’t possess personalities of their own.

Or rather… it might be more accurate to say that there was a single personality in control of them all.

Between the soul clone, the mind clones, and the original minds, Percy and Micky had access to twelve mental pockets in total. Percy still had no idea how to connect himself to the rest of his soul clones – assuming that such a thing was even possible – but he already considered this a resounding success.

‘Micky? Can you try borrowing a couple of minds from me?’ Percy offered.

Realizing that most of his mental pockets were busy clearing and tempering channels, he promptly freed up a couple for his friend. A few seconds later, he felt a gentle tug drag a pair of minds across the barrier, causing their constellations to rearrange themselves into orange crows. The mind clones still technically existed inside Percy’s brain, but Micky was now the one in control.
