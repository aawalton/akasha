Rowan found himself sitting awkwardly at a long table with only three other people. Three other bird people.

At the head of the table was Helja’s father, dressed immaculately with feathers of whites and light greys, staring at Rowan with significantly less hostility than the day before. To his right was another avian whose feathers were coloured in shades of golden white just like Helja, who sat to her fathers left. Rowan was next to her, hands clasped in his lap as he looked nervously between Helja’s father and who he assumed to be her mother.

“T-thank you for having me, I greatly appreciate it.”

Rowan felt delirious, like he was in a trance out of control of his own body as the world around him got stranger and stranger. The feeling only deepened as Helja’s mother smiled at him.

“Oh don’t worry dear, we’re happy to have you.”

Without even a good morning, Helja’s father followed up with a question.

“What is that essence you have?” he asked, nodding his head towards the essence to Rowan’s left.

“Father!” Helja admonished; a seemingly common occurrence. Helja’s mother also held a long-suffering expression as the man broke what were, to their minds, important social rules.

He could have left it behind in the bedroom, but it was currently one of his only possessions. On top of that, there was that itching at the back of his mind that said it was important. When he had touched the sphere, the blue box had told him it was a Reality Essence with a second tag that said Fabled. That probably meant it was valuable, right?

“I don’t know,” Rowan lied, “It was next to me when I woke up yesterday.”

Rowan didn’t quite trust the avian family, though they hadn’t given him any reason not to. The butler had jumped into danger for him, Helja had saved him and even suggested the night before that he absorb the essence as quickly as possible, whatever that entailed.

A stream of servants came from a door different to the one Rowan had entered through, delivering plates of steaming food, water and what looked like a cup of wine for Helja’s father.Quite dissimilar to Rowan’s usual breakfast of cereal, here they had placed large plates in the centre of the table containing a wide range of plant-based foods. A servant waited next to each person at the table and Rowan watched as the members of Helja’s family pointed out different platters. The servants judiciously plated up the selected foods before placing them delicately in front of each person. Rowan turned to look up at the well dressed avian behind him, the large person towering over his seated position. He then turned to Helja.

“Uh, I haven’t really seen any of these things before. Could you recommend anything?”

He got another strange look from Helja before she pointed out a few items to the avian behind Rowan. A plate was placed in front of him, in addition to a utensil in the form of a skewer.

“So, have you considered whether you’re going to absorb your essence then?” Helja’s father asked.

Rowan looked up, frowning.

“I have thought about it but I’m unsure. What does it do?”

Apparently past admonishing her father, Helja turned to him.

“Of course you should! I mean, it’s very unlikely you’ll come across another in your lifetime if you do not become an essence user yourself, so why not? You could sell it if you need the money, naturally, and we would be happy to identify what type of essence it is for you if you would like.”

“Sell it? Is it… worth much?”

Helja politely swallowed her food before answering, looking to her parents for confirmation.

“Well… if it’s of the common variety, a good few gold coins or, if you’re selling to another essence user, one or two Tier three spheres? Each rarity up from that would multiply its value by two or three times, depending on how rare it actually is.”

Rowan’s eyes widened in surprise. Gold coins? Well, if they were real gold, depending on how much they weighed, they could help pay off his university debt quite nicely. His mum would be so happy to hear that… if he ever spoke to her again. He didn’t even have his phone to call her.

He had no idea what a Tier two sphere was, though.

“You would need to get it somewhere you can sell it without having it stolen from you though,” Helja added.

A harrumph escaped her father’s lips.

“Scoundrels and ruffians everywhere these days. Back when I was your a-”

Helja cut him off quickly.

“But, depending on what it is, we might be interested in buying it, wouldn’t we father?”

Some of Helja’s father’s increased politeness this morning compared to the night before started to make a little more sense.

“It is a possibility. We would need to identify it first, however.”

Rowan swallowed a strange vegetable that had tasted like a tomato, only sweeter, that had been cooked in a way that gave it a crispy outer shell with a softer inside.

“And, um, what would absorbing it actually do?”

“Oh, that depends on what power and element affinities you have from your innate trait,” Helja said simply.

Rowan felt a strange headache building.

“My… affinity?”

As he said it, a little blue box appeared but this time, unattached to any object.

Race: Displaced Soul

Innate Trait - World Guide

You can interact with items and people to learn basic information about them.

You have access to a personal map.

Power Affinity: Utility and Affliction.

Element Affinity: Astral.

Huh?

Head swimming, he read through the box.

“An affinity for the Astral?”

All around the table, brows creased as Rowan felt everyone in the room focus on him.

“An affinity for the Astral as a human? That is very rare,” Helja’s mother murmured, looking thoughtful.

Rowan looked at the other two lines under what was apparently his innate trait. The first was obvious, something he realised he had been using since waking up. The little blue boxes must have been a result of that. Looking down at the line about a map, another box appeared that showed a map with the house and its surroundings, though the edges were greyed out.

With excitement, he thought about zooming out and the map obliged. It moved further and further out, showing his path through the forest with everything else greyed out, the only exception being major geographical boundaries such as rivers, shown with simple lines. Going far enough, he began to see the edges of the continent until the whole world was there before him. It… wasn’t his world.

Similar to Earth, there were the north and south poles, each surrounded by polar ice caps. Rather than the continents he was used to, however, there were only three major land masses other than the polar ice caps.

Excitement turned to confusion and worry, the map closed as his focus left it. He didn’t think university debt was his biggest problem anymore.

“S… so what would absorbing the essence give me?”

The events of the day before were beginning to really crash down on him, the odd blue boxes, the strange runes that created fire or adjusted his clothes. The next thing Helja’s mother said was at the top of the growing list of absurdities, however.

“Well, with an Astral affinity, you would probably gain some kind of portal power eventually, maybe a personal storage power. Other than that, it will be very dependent on the essence. What is your power affinity?”

Rowan realised he wasn’t breathing and took a moment to centre himself.

“Utility and Affliction?”

Helja nodded, though she looked surprised.

“Very high chance of portal and personal storage powers then. Probably some kind of soul based afflictions like curses as well I’d think, with Astral and Affliction. To have two power affinities is incredibly rare, just so you know.”

Helja’s mother nodded in agreement, while her father kept an unerringly intense gaze on him.

“The rest will depend on what type of essence that is and what type of stones of power you use.”

It hadn’t escaped Rowan’s attention that the blue box had said his race was something called a Displaced Soul. With that and his map showing him a completely different planet, the idea that he had somehow been moved from Earth to some other place was growing. Ignoring the theories of a coma or a really messed up dream, that meant he was on a planet that was not his own. He had a feeling that Helja and her family were catching onto that too, his next question not helping in the matter.

“Stones of power? What are those?”

Helja’s father put down his cup and intertwined his hands.

“You’re not from this world, are you? Everyone knows what essences are, what stones of power are. It’s like you were born yesterday.”

Helja’s mother put her face in her hand, while Helja turned an incredulous look on her father.

“Have you lost all decorum? You can’t just ask him things like that!”

He looked like he was going to argue the point, but it was Rowan that spoke.

“No, I don’t think so.”

“Ha! I knew it.”

Helja’s father went back to drinking his wine with a pleased look on his face, while Helja looked at Rowan with increasing interest.

“Really? Oh my, how exciting!”

She turned to her mother.

“To think we have the privilege of meeting a displaced one, and so recently brought here! Isn’t it exciting, mother?”

Her mother nodded emphatically.

“Oh quite! Rowan, you should really absorb that essence. If you’re really new to the world, you’ll need all the power you can get.”

Rowan looked from one to the other.

“Displaced one? What? Why? Am I in danger?”

Helja’s mother laughed, waving a hand genially in his direction.

“Oh darling no, you’re not in danger. Not from us, anyway. Displaced ones are quite rare certainly, but not unheard of. It’s just that… well, other cases of displaced ones have had a tendency of getting themselves into quite some trouble.”

Rowan paused for only a moment.

“So… should I do that now?”

He felt more and more unsettled as the conversation continued. The realisation that he wasn’t in his own world any longer was making him feel off kilter, the headache now fully settled into his brain not helping with the disparity between his view on reality and the world currently around him.

Helja put a comforting hand on his arm, the feathered limb not quite helping fight the building confidence that he had lost his mind.

“Not now, don’t be silly. We’ll do it after breakfast!”

She said it with a radiant smile, before turning back to her parents.

“Last night, Rowan informed me that he felt a tremor, it could be another emergence.”

That got both the parents' attention focused intently on Rowan.

“A tremor you say. After which that creature followed you?” Helja’s father asked.

Rowan frowned.

“Mmm, no. It was a few hours earlier, unless the giant ant followed me for that long I wouldn’t say so. Why do you ask, are they linked in some way? You mentioned an emergence, what is that?”

Helja’s mother dabbed at her lips with a clean white cloth before answering.

“An emergence is the release of magic from the core of the planet, which often forms as magical and quite aggressive creatures, like the one that was chasing you yesterday. They have been getting worse and worse as of late, more frequent than I can remember.”

They sounded dangerous to Rowan, especially if that tremor was some kind of precursor to the emergence like they seemed to be suggesting. His attention wavered and wandered away as he focused on enjoying the food in front of him, which was excellently made and the first thing he had eaten since the night he had fallen asleep in his apartment. That felt like so long ago, despite being only a full day prior.

From that point, conversation turned to lighter topics as Helja and her family realised that Rowan needed some time to stew with the new information and attempt to accept this new reality. They spoke to each other of upcoming events with adjacent political allies and enemies, as well as about the general state of the capital city, Lyel. They even gossipped about the most recent scandal, the oddly familiar conversation helping Rowan feel a little less out of place. Gossipping about some celebrity scandal or the other was something Rowan’s own mother loved to do very much.

He ate robotically, his mind moving from the food to the fact that he might not see his own family ever again.

Before long, he found his plate empty, with Helja and her family having finished only minutes earlier.

Servants began cleaning up as Helja’s father stood.

“I apologise, Rowan, as I realise we never introduced ourselves. I am William and this is my wife, Esmerelda. Please, bring your essence and we shall move to the ritual pavilion.”

The butler, Hoster, who had been standing in the corner of the room gestured to a servant who quickly left.

Rowan quickly stood as William did.

“That’s quite alr-”

He had accidentally knocked into a servant that was collecting his plate. Apologising profusely, he picked up the plate and held it out for the avian. They looked at him in a slightly confused manner before taking it.

“Quite alright,” he finished, “Is there anything I need to worry about doing this, uh… ritual?”

Helja gave a light laugh at the nervousness in his voice as Rowan picked up his essence.

“Not at all,” William said, leaving the dining hall and heading back towards the entrance hall.

Helja and Esmerelda followed suit as they left the house and took a path to the right. The group passed through the peaceful garden around the side of the mansion where a beautiful pavilion crafted from the same redwood of the house stood. It was obvious to Rowan that the same expert who had made the avian mural in the entrance hall of the mansion had also crafted this pavilion.

Throughout the decorative handrails of the pavilion there were different glyphs carved into the wood, flowing transitions leading from one to the next. The stone that the structure was built into had the same two circle ritual pattern with ten equidistant lines leading from the outer to the inner that he had seen in his mind after touching the Reality Essence. The servant that the butler Hoster had sent earlier was pouring salt into the engraved lining in the stone.

Rowan stood nervously, surrounded by the giant avians.

“So, how do I do this exactly?”

Esmerelda gestured to Helja and she stepped up into the pavilion.

“You stand in the centre circle holding the essence and repeat these words. ‘Soul to Mind to Body, I accept this essence to be the strength of the Body, the sharpness of the Mind, the moral of the Soul.”

Rowan scratched the back of his head.

“Um, this thing isn’t going to like, change me, right?”

Helja cocked her head.

“Change you? A little. You’ll be able to live off solidified mana rather than food for one. Depending on what it is, it might also alter your appearance ever so slightly as you rank up through the tiers. If you rank up.”

Rowan shook his head.

“No, no, I mean me, it won’t change who I am, will it?”

Helja scoffed.

“Oh, no, of course not.”

Feeling at least a little mollified, he looked down at the essence. The Reality Essence. If this really was all real and he wasn’t the subject of the most elaborate prank to exist, maybe it could help him get back home? Astral, which he could only assume was some type of plane of existence that he could go to, or perhaps space, in addition to reality. Maybe those two combined could give him some type of power to go home?

“Alright, I’m ready.”

Helja tapped her chin as she observed Rowan.

“Are you entirely sure you don’t want us to identify it first? If it is a rarer one, we might be able to trade you for something else you may prefer.”

Rowan looked up.

“You have more of these?”

She nodded.

“Of course! We can even get different ones from Lyel, if you were willing to wait some time.”

“What kinds of essences are there?”

Helja pointed at her father.

“Well, he has the Strength essence, which combined with his air and physical attack affinity, provides all kinds of strong attacks that mix with high mobility. Mother there has the Sly essence, though keep that one to yourself, which combined with her nobility and utility affinities gives her quite a combination for social manoeuvring.”

Helja leaned in closer to whisper.

“Father married into nobility, you see. That’s why he is a little less… socially adept.”

She leaned back and held a hand to her own chest.

“And I have the Magic essence, with air and utility affinities. It provides me with flight and other powers related to controlling the air and using it for magical attacks.”

Rowan frowned.

“So, what exactly is the Astral? Why would an affinity with it give me teleportation and personal storage powers?”

“Ah, you do not have the Astral where you are from?”

Rowan shook his head.

“Not that I know of?”

“Well, it is not fully understood, I must admit. There are others who are more knowledgeable in its workings than me but the way it has been described to me is it being both the fundamental power that all magic is made from and a place beyond our world. As such, an affinity with it doesn’t impart specificity like the air affinity does, simply an affinity for the fundamentals of magic.”

Head swimming with all the new information, Rowan stepped into the inner circle of the ritual holding his essence, now that the servant had finished pouring the salt. A little blue box appeared.

Ritual Valid.

Initiation Phrase - “Soul to Mind to Body, I accept this essence to be the strength of the Body, the sharpness of the Mind, the moral of the Soul.”

“Well, here goes I guess.”

He repeated the phrase given to him by Helja and again by his innate trait. Nothing happened for a moment, before the essence in his hand floated up above his head as it began to glow with an increasingly bright light.
