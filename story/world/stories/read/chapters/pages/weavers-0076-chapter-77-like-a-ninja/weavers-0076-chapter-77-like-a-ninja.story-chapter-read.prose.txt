Chapter 77 – Like a Ninja

The academy halls were quiet.

Kaelin walked them alone that morning, well before anyone else might be awake. Her footsteps were muffled by the damp chill that clung to the air. The storm outside raged on, slanting rain streaking down the tall windows, thunder rolling in the distance like the growl of some large predator.

Each corridor echoed strangely, not quite in rhythm with her steps. The patter of rain above seemed louder than it should have been, beating a steady, intrusive rhythm into the silence that filled the halls. The marble beneath her boots gleamed, polished and cold, reflecting flickers of the orange fire that illuminated the academy from the inside of lanterns.

The academy had never felt so unfamiliar.

Tomorrow was the final test—an open-arena combat test to rank the top students at midyear. It should have been everything she’d trained for. Everything they’d warned her about. But all she could feel was the static weight in her chest, the bone-deep exhaustion that hadn’t left her in days.

And the quiet.

She hadn't slept. Not really. Whenever she closed her eyes, she’d hear something darting around in the dark, opening her eyes just to see the dark void that was her room at night. A memory, or some kind of vision, would claw its way into the forefront of her thoughts. The ouroboros. Visions of a childhood which wasn’t her own. They were Marcel’s memories. The weight of a dagger in her hand. The sense of ruin, and of fate coiling like smoke.

None of these memories were hers. Not entirely, no matter how much they felt like it.

But they lived inside her now.

She pulled her coat tighter, the threads in the lining warming slightly against the damp chill. The storm seemed to push against the academy’s high glass ceiling, trying to press its way in. She stared upward for a moment, eyes tracing the rivers of rainwater carving pathways across the panes.

They shimmered red for a heartbeat.

She blinked. It was only the light.

***

She found Lena in the garden courtyard, beneath one of the cracked arches. Despite the storm and the early hour, Lena had settled on a stone bench under the overhang, weaving a wreath like shape out of thorns. Nature threads danced gently around her fingers, glowing faintly.

“You look like shit,” Lena said as Kaelin approached.

Kaelin gave a half-smile and sat beside her, the chill of the stone bench soaking through her coat. “Thanks.”

Lena looked up, brushing raindrops from her fringe. “You been up all night again?”

“Hard to sleep when your own brain’s trying to keep you awake.”

Lena stared for a moment before placing the thorn crown atop Kaelin’s head. “You want to talk about it?”

“I wouldn’t even know where to start. You’d probably just think I was crazy.” Kaelin exhaled slowly, watching her breath mist. “It's awful. Imagine you have a whole ‘nother life sitting in your head, someone else’s memories, intertwined with your own.”

“Visions?”

“No. Not quite. More like pieces. Names I don’t know. Faces I can’t place. But I feel like I should. And then there are the ones I do know.”

Kaelin stopped for a second, debating whether to tell Lena about her run in with Aric.

Lena spoke up before she could decide, “You’re probably just stressed about the last exam, I know how much you’re wanting to win. Take a rest, you’ll feel better in no time.”

Without giving Kaelin much of a chance to think, it seemed like Lena had decided for her.

Kaelin’s throat tightened as she mumbled, “No surprise there. Shouldn’t’ve bothered you anyways.”

Lena looked over at Kaelin, “Say something?”

Kaelin dropped her eyes. “Nah, I was just talking to myself, don’t worry about it.”

***

Later that day, the upper floors of the East Tower creaked with the wind. The windows overlooking the cliffs bent slightly with the pressure of the storm, casting distorted shadows over the wood-panelled walls.

Aria was waiting for Kaelin in her own bedroom, the room they used for their club, as chosen by Aria. Kana was slouched in a chair, flipping through some fantasy book as though she hadn’t a care in the world.

“You look like you’ve seen a ghost,” Aria said without turning.

Kaelin managed a dry smile. “More like I got one in me”

Kana looked up with narrowed eyes. “You hearing voices or something? Maybe you need to see a doctor.”

Kaelin didn’t answer right away. She walked slowly to the nearest window, her hand trailing over the cold glass. The winds were whistling through tall pine trees below, causing them to flex like a fishing rod in some mad struggle.

“I had a dream,” she said eventually. “I saw Aric in the courtyard, next to the outdoor gym, and ran after him. Then, the next second he had put me in a place I’ve never been to. This city, it was like it was waaay in the future. They had these pieces of glass which they could poke, and it would change this painting, making it show something else. I don’t know how it worked, but it was kinda cool.”

Kana stared at Kaelin like the madwoman she seemed to be, while Aria kept sorting her bookshelf, without batting an eye.

Kaelin continued, “There was so much stuff I’d never seen before, then I saw Erundal.” She turned, her voice quieter. “I saw it from above, like I was in the body of a bird. And there was this guy, he called himself Ezra, but he both looked and sounded exactly like Aric. I’m telling you, he showed me his past, or something like that. I don’t know. It was all so confusing.”

Aria met her eyes. “And you think this means something.”

“I know it does.”

Kana shut her book with a snap. “People see weird stuff in dreams all the time.”

“It wasn’t even a dream, Aric put me there, he was showing me it on purpose.” Kaelin said sharply.

Kana rolled her eyes. “So what, you think you’re some kind of prophet now?”

“I didn’t say that— Are you even listening? I said I was in the body of Aric when he was younger, it’s not like I’m seeing the future.”

Aria raised a hand, silencing them both. “Kaelin, I trust you, whatever it is, you came to the right person. No matter what, I’ll be with you ‘till the end. What do you think made Aric show it to you though?”

“I don’t know. None of it makes sense to me. He mentioned something about a time barrier, blocking entry from the outside. As for how he got in, I’m not sure. Though he did say that he thought his high affinity for time might help.”

Kana frowned. “Just don’t go running off tomorrow, getting yourself disqualified.”

Kaelin snorted. “Course I won't. I’m fighting, no matter what.”

“Good.” Kana leaned back. “I have some questions I think we need to ask Aric, and tomorrow might just be the perfect time to corner him.”

***

As the sun began to set, Kaelin made her way to the main dormitory building. The storm hadn’t let up. Wind whipped through the upper walkways, making the lamps gutter. Thunder cracked over the academy like a war drum.

Inside the common room, Zephyr sat by the hearth, his legs propped on a table, a book open in his lap. He looked up as she entered, his gaze wary.

“You look—”

“I know,” Kaelin interrupted. “Like shit.”

He closed the book, setting it aside. “You’re not okay.”

Kaelin let out a small chuckle, “Hah, surprised you noticed, and I thought I had hidden it quite well.”

Zephyr stood, crossing the space between them. “Kaelin, sit down, this isn’t like you.”

Kaelin laughed softly. “You don’t know me that well.”

He moved forwards with a demanding step. “You’re scaring people. Professor Aric told me to make sure you get straight to sleep, you clearly need it.”

“Fuck off! You don’t know what I need.”

“You're acting crazy, just calm down and talk to me.”

“Oh you too? Think I’m crazy, eh? I never even told you what happened!” she snapped, then caught herself, turning towards the stairs sighing. “Sorry.”

Zephyr didn’t flinch. “Whatever you saw, or think you saw... it doesn’t matter—”

Kaelin interrupted him, “Did Lena tell you then?” Her threatening gaze bored a hole through Zephyr’s focus.

'Can’t let up, just gotta remember what Aric told me. This is all for Lena.' Zephyr thought to himself.

“Kaelin, she’s worried about you, you’re stressed about tomorrow, and I get it. Just go get some rest... please.”

They stood in silence for a moment. The fire crackled between them. Kaelin looked at the flames, trying not to picture something ancient staring back.

“You still gonna fight tomorrow?” Zephyr asked.

“Yes.”

“You gonna win?”

“I have to.”

And then she turned and left up the stairs.

'Ahhh fuck, it’s happening tomorrow. Aric was right, he said he could stop her though... Can he? Argh, just gotta trust him, not much else I can do.' Zephyr’s final thoughts echoed in his mind, bouncing around uncontrollably, his fear was just too great.

***

That night, the storm cracked the sky wide open. Kaelin sat at the window of her dorm, watching the rain streak down the panes. Thunder shook the walls. Her fingers twisted absently in her sheets.

The ouroboros burned behind her eyes.

The ruins. The names. The twin daggers. Aric.

She fell asleep after an hour of struggling against herself, as if God was allowing her some rest, knowing what was to come.

***

The arena’s stone floor shimmered with early morning heat, though the sky above remained cloudy. The stands were packed—students, professors, staff, even nobles with little else to do, were seated beneath glowing thread-lights that hung like lanterns in the air. Everything about the Battle Royale was dramatic, excessive and unnecessary, especially since it wasn’t grading the students, only first place mattered, the rest were useless.

It was a performance. A tradition. One last spectacle before the academy returned to normalcy in the next school year.

Kaelin stood near the edge of the sand-ring, arms folded, coat unbuttoned and flapping slightly in the breeze. Her expression was unreadable. Beneath her boots, fine dust stirred and clung, turning the black leather grey.

Kana approached, casual as ever, though her uniform had been cinched tighter and her sleeves rolled to her elbows. Mind threads shimmered faintly around her fingers, more out of habit than anything. She stopped beside Kaelin, scanning the arena with a faint look of disinterest.

“They’re actually doing seating by betting odds,” Kana muttered. “It’s like a theatre show with less blood.”

Kaelin didn’t smile. Her eyes stayed fixed on the other side of the arena, where the gates were just beginning to creak open.

Kana leaned closer, her voice dropping. “You sure you’ve got this?”

“I wouldn’t have asked for your help if I didn’t.”

“You didn’t ask, you just said ‘stand back and watch.’” Kana raised an eyebrow. “That doesn’t count as asking.”

Kaelin didn’t answer. Her jaw clenched slightly.

Kana studied her for a moment longer, then sighed. “Alright. But if you die mid-plan, I’m not cleaning up the mess.”

Kaelin’s lips twitched, almost a smile. “Noted.”

A bell rang, a sharp, crystalline note that echoed through the coliseum, silencing the crowd for a single beat.

“This is the moment,” Kana said under her breath. “Whatever you’re planning, make it count.”

Kaelin finally turned her head, meeting Kana’s eyes. “It will.”
