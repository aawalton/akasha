The harsh rasp of metal sliding across stone cut steadily through the camp as Korr drew his blade along the whetstone in slow, practiced strokes.

Shhk.

Shhk.

Shhk.

The rhythm was familiar.

Grounding.

A counterpoint to the chaos behind him.

The air carried a thick, metallic scent. Sharp, iron-laced, and unmistakable.

Blood.

At the center of the camp lay the source: the carcass of an oversized boar, sprawled across packed dirt, already half reduced to usable cuts by the bandits working around it. Knives flashed, laughter rang out, and the occasional shouted argument over portions broke through the steady hum of activity.

A hard-earned prize.

The creature had been young, barely more than a child according to the System.

Level thirty-five. Only one level above Korr.

Earth-aspected and stubborn. It had fought with a surprising degree of instinct, manipulating the ground beneath them with clumsy but dangerous bursts of power.

Not enough.

Now it fed the entire camp.

Korr allowed himself a small smile as another cheer rose up behind him. Someone had clearly claimed a particularly desirable cut.

They were a strange group.

Loud, unrefined, and extremely prone to questionable decision-making.

But good people.

That was what mattered.

Korr, a Wolfkin, had not always lived like this.

Once, he had been a guard. A proper one, sworn to protect a modest but thriving town at the edge of a lesser kingdom. The land had been fertile, the people steady, their lives predictable in the best way.

Then the fields began to rot.

Not all at once. Quietly.

Crops failed. Soil turned sour. Something unseen spread through the land, consuming its lifeblood without explanation.

What followed was not a battle. It was an exodus.

Families left in waves, carrying what little they could. Those who stayed simply… faded.

In the end, there was nothing left to protect.

No town. No people. No purpose.

And so, Korr had walked.

Far enough to cross into another kingdom.

Far enough to become something else.

Now, he led a band of outlaws who survived on small robberies, wild game, and whatever edible substances the forest chose not to immediately punish them for consuming.

It was not the life he had envisioned, but it was a life.

And for now—

It was a good one.

The sound of footsteps reached Korr before the figures themselves did. Loud ones. Completely unconcerned with who might be listening.

They moved through the forest with the kind of careless confidence that only came from familiarity, or a complete lack of self-preservation.

Korr did not look up immediately.

He finished a stroke along the whetstone, adjusted his grip, and only then let his attention shift toward the treeline.

The robbery party he sent out earlier.

It had to be.

A voice carried through the undergrowth before the group even broke into view.

“Yo! Smelly Pete! Got another one for ya! Make sure ya thoroughly bore her with yer damned story, since she ain’t got no coin!”

Korr’s brow twitched.

Smelly Pete, seated near the firepit with a half-carved chunk of meat in hand, snapped his head up in immediate offense.

“Stop callin’ me smelly or I swear I’ll pop ya upside the head! An’ my stories ain’t borin’, ya damn mutt!”

Laughter rippled through the camp.

A few bandits chimed in with poorly disguised agreement. Others simply enjoyed the exchange for what it was.

Korr snorted under his breath.

Pete wasn’t smelly, that much was true.

But his stories…

Those were a trial. Not of patience. Of endurance.

The brush parted as the group finally emerged, several of them idly brushing leaves and stray branches from their hair and fur as they stepped into the clearing.

And behind them—

Korr’s gaze sharpened.

A Foxkin. Small and white-haired.

She followed just a step behind the others, her presence oddly… quiet against the noise of the group. Pale skin caught the scattered sunlight filtering through the canopy, almost porcelain in its smoothness, while her hair and fur shimmered faintly with each shift of light.

Her face—too refined, too precise—was drawn into an expression of unease, fear carefully arranged across delicate features. Her hands were held in front of her chest, fingers laced together in a nervous, fidgeting motion.

Convincing. At a glance.

Korr watched her more closely.

The way she walked.

Not heel-first, like most. The weight rested on the pads of her feet, steps light, controlled.

Her posture held tension. Not the rigidity of fear, but the coiled readiness of something prepared to move at a moment’s notice.

A mask of weakness, poorly hidden. Perhaps deliberately so.

Korr rose in a single, fluid motion.

The whetstone fell silent.

Steel flashed as he brought his blade up, leveling it toward her without hesitation.

His stance settled immediately. Balanced, grounded, and ready to strike or defend in the same breath.

Whatever she was—

She was not just a lost child.

Korr’s gaze narrowed as he inspected her.

…Farmer.

Level thirteen.

Korr did not lower his blade.

In front of him, the Wolfkin who had led the group—Dax—flinched at the sudden shift in atmosphere, shoulders tensing before he realized the sword was not meant for him.

“...Boss man?” Dax said, confusion bleeding into his tone. “What’s wrong? Why ya pointin’ that at the little girl we caught?”

Korr did not answer. His attention never left the Foxkin.

“You an adventurer?”

The girl blinked.

Her head tilted, just slightly.

Dax frowned, glancing between them.

“Boss man, look at her leve—”

“Quiet.”

The word cut cleanly through the air.

Dax snapped his mouth shut. Understanding followed a heartbeat later.

Something was wrong.

Around them, the camp shifted. The laughter died. Conversations fell silent.

Hands drifted—slowly, subtely—toward weapons.

Eyes locked onto the scene.

Waiting. Watching.

The Foxkin spoke.

Her voice trembled.

“...I—I’m not… I’m not an… adventurer…”

Several weapons lowered, tension easing at the edges.

Korr’s did not.

“You aren’t good at pretending to be weak,” he said evenly. “Farm work builds callouses. You don’t have any. Your clothes are too clean, too well made. And your posture—” his eyes flicked briefly to her stance, then back to her face, “—reads like you’re about to move at any moment.”

A pause.

“No reason to lie to me.”

She blinked again.

Her ears lifted and her tail gave a small, almost appreciative sway.

And then—

Everything changed.

Her posture straightened.

The tension vanished. The fear dissolved from her expression as if it had never been there to begin with.

Replaced with an unreadable stillness.

A soft smile settled across her face, pleasant, composed, and utterly devoid of warmth.

Korr felt a faint chill run down his spine.

Around him, the robbery party stiffened. Several of them took half a step back without realizing it.

“Impressive,” she said, her voice now steady, clear. “I did not account for those variables.”

A slight tilt of her head.

“Although, for your information, I did not lie. I am not an adventurer.”

Korr swallowed, throat suddenly dry, before forcing himself to speak again.

“Why are you here, then?”

“The village chief, Arlo, assigned me a quest.”

Korr’s grip on his sword loosened slightly, though he did not lower it.

His gaze dipped, thoughts turning.

“I see… so the village has finally decided to stop tolerating us.”

It had always been a possibility. Inevitable, even.

The Foxkin tilted her head a fraction further.

“Quite the opposite. I was sent here to convince you to settle in the village as new citizens.”

“…Huh?”

The word left him before he could stop it.

For a moment, Korr simply stared at her, the meaning of what she had said struggling to take hold.

“He wants us to… join his village?”

She nodded.

“Correct.”

“Why?”

The question came out slower. More cautious.

She gave a small shrug.

“I am unsure. However, I believe it serves both his interests and your own. From my observations, you are all… decent people, despite your current occupation.”

Korr’s eyes widened as the pieces slid into place. Understanding struck with the certainty of a solved puzzle.

“I see,” he said, voice gaining firmness. “You were testing us. Acting weak to see how we would behave.”

The conclusion felt solid. Certain.

She paused, just for a moment.

Her ears angled slightly to the side.

“…Yes. That is correct. Had you behaved poorly, this conversation would have been significantly more violent.”

Korr blinked.

There was… something off about that answer.

A faint hesitation. A trace of something that almost resembled embarrassment.

Why?

Had that not been her intention?

…

He decided not to dwell on it. Some things were better left unquestioned.

“May I discuss this with my—”

“Ain’t no need, boss man! Let’s accept!”

“Hear, hear!”

“Hell yeah, let’s all go live in the village!”

“Finally! Real food! Not just meat and suspicious mushrooms!”

…

Korr’s tail thumped once against the ground in exasperation.

Any hope of a measured discussion vanished instantly.

The Foxkin’s tail swayed lightly, betraying a quiet amusement as she observed the chaos.

“I take it you have reached a consensus?”

The response was immediate.

Unified.

Loud enough to startle birds from the surrounding trees into a panicked burst of wings.

“HELL YEAH!”

She blinked.

Korr realized, a moment too late, that he had yet to properly meet her gaze. He looked at her eyes—

And found his focus sliding, uncooperative, to her cheek instead.

…That felt important.

Korr decided it wasn’t.

He let out a short, resigned chuckle.

“I suppose that settles it. Would you mind leading the way?”

She nodded.

A brief flash of golden light shimmered across her palm, and a single silver coin materialized in her hand.

She studied it. Suspiciously.

Then, with a small shrug, it vanished as quickly as it had appeared.

Spatial storage.

Korr’s grip tightened ever so slightly.

Expensive.

Very expensive.

He looked at her again.

This girl—

Was a mystery.

As she moved to lead them back toward the village, she stopped abruptly.

Her ears snapped toward the treeline, locking onto something far beyond what any of them could hear.

She tilted her head, listening.

Then turned slightly—

Not toward the forest. Toward the boar carcass.

“Ah,” she said lightly. “That would explain it.”

Korr frowned.

“What? Is something wrong?”

She nodded.

Her tail flicked once. Sharply.

…Excited.

“It would appear,” she continued, almost pleasantly, “that you have angered the mother of that boar.”

Then Korr heard it. A distant, thunderous pounding.

Heavy. Relentless.

Trees cracked. Underbrush tore.

Something large was coming, forcing its way through the forest with no regard for the terrain in its path.

Korr’s blood ran cold.

If the child had pushed him and his group to their limits—

Then the mother—

He did not have to finish the thought.

The forest parted, and it arrived.

A massive boar burst into the clearing, easily ten meters at the shoulder, its body reinforced with thick, stone-like plating that shifted and flexed with each movement. Tusks like jagged spears jutted forward, long enough to skewer a Direfang clean through.

It radiated weight.

Power.

Korr inspected it without thinking.

They could not allow that.

Around him, the bandits followed suit. Weapons drawn. Stances set.

No hesitation.

They would not win, but they could buy time.

The boar’s gaze swept the clearing, locking onto the mutilated remains of its child.

Its eyes burned.

It pawed at the ground, rage building, preparing to charge.

“Stand down.”

Korr froze.

The Foxkin’s voice cut through the tension with quiet authority.

“Did you not inspect it?” he snapped back. “You need to run before it’s too—”

“I said stand down.”

There was no force in her tone.

No raised voice.

And yet—

It carried weight. Final and absolute.

Korr hesitated. And in that moment, a realization settled over him with quiet certainty.

Ignoring her would be worse. Far worse than facing the boar.

He lowered his blade slowly.

“I suppose I will test that…” she murmured, her gaze fixed forward.

She stepped forward, and the world shifted.

At first, Korr thought it was dizziness. The ground beneath his feet felt wrong. Too firm, too still, as though the earth itself had braced for something it could not withstand. The wind, once threading lazily through the trees, died without warning. Leaves hung suspended in the air, caught mid-fall as if time itself had been asked to wait.

Something tightened in his chest.

Not from the front. Not from the boar.

From behind him.

The girl took another step.

Korr’s breath caught. Not painfully. It simply… stopped, as if his body had forgotten the purpose of breathing. His heart slammed once—heavy—then faltered, then resumed at a pace that felt wrong. Too fast. Too forceful. Each beat reverberated through his skull like a warning he could not comprehend.

Around him, the others broke. One stumbled, boots scraping uselessly against unmoving earth. Another’s weapon dipped, fingers loosening as strength slipped quietly away.

No one spoke. No one could.

The air was not heavy. It was dense.

As though sound itself had to struggle to exist.

The girl did not hurry. She walked, measured and unstoppable.

Korr’s knees bent. Not by choice. A subtle drop. A slight loss of height. His body lowered itself as if proximity to her required it. At the same time, his spine straightened, his posture aligning into something that was not readiness, but presentation.

His instincts screamed.

Not fear. Something deeper. Something older.

You are not the highest thing here.

It was not a thought.

It was truth.

Her white hair shifted gently as she moved, the only thing in the forest permitted motion without resistance. Everything else—people, air, sound, life itself—felt paused. Waiting.

Korr’s vision narrowed. The edges dimmed, shadows creeping inward until the forest itself fell away, leaving only one point of focus.

Her.

The realization struck.

If she decides to kill it… it will die.

No doubt. No calculation. No possibility of failure.

Only certainty.

One by one, the bandits collapsed, clutching their weapons close as their bodies gave in, breath shallow, barely present.

The boar lowered itself. Its body yielded against its will, rage burning just as fiercely as before.

Yielded to her.

The predator.

She raised her hand, unhurried. A blade of pure black settled into her grip, nearly as tall as she was.

Then—

She vanished.

There was no movement. No transition. Only absence.

The pressure shattered. Air returned. Sound followed soon after.

And a massive head struck the ground with a dull, final thud, the rest of the body collapsing a heartbeat later.

Korr lifted his head.

She stood atop the corpse, small and still, white hair drifting softly as the wind resumed its path through the forest.

One thought remained.

One truth.

He spoke it under his breath.

“…monster.”
