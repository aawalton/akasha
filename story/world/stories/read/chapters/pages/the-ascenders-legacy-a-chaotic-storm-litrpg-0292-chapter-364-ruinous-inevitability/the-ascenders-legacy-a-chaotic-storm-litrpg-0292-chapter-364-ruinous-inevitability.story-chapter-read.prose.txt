Aodhán was halfway across chaotic space when light suddenly split the heavens and shook the earth—a crackling bolt of amethyst lightning striking down so fast it crossed the distance in an instant.

It hadn’t even seemed to pass through time at all. It had simply struck, obliterating the Skitterling and all those around it before Aodhán could arrive back in reality.

Aodhán couldn’t believe it.

It took less than a second to teleport such a short distance. And yet, even already halfway there, the bolt had struck and vanished, leaving only obliteration in its wake.

Stunned, he stared down at Crixis, amazed by the speed and power of the lightning he had just summoned.

Perhaps speed was even the wrong word. That bolt hadn’t been fast.

It had been instantaneous.

Ruinously Inevitable.

Could he have dodged it? Could anyone have?

Glancing down once again, he fixed his gaze on Crixis as the remaining Skitterlings regrouped, fear gleaming in their crimson gazes.

Crixis swayed slightly, but his eyes were as hard as granite. He had just taken his first step on the path of Ruinous Inevitability, and if he was going to summon another bolt, Aodhán would be damned if he missed it.

Activating stealth once more, he settled in to watch as the Skitterlings selected a new leader, gathered behind it, and lunged again, ash rising and mandibles clicking furiously.

Crixis didn’t move. Didn’t speak. Didn’t smile. Didn’t do anything.

Yet in an instant, the heavens themselves—not the storm he had created—thundered so loudly the earth shook. Light split the sky once more, lighting up Aodhán’s intent gaze.

And yet, despite his intense focus, Aodhán missed it again.

He saw the light split the sky, felt the earth shake, but everything in between was lost to him despite how intently he watched.

The bolt was just too fast to follow.

Too fast to evade.

He glanced down at the earth then, eyes widening at the scale of destruction the bolt had wrought—obliterating not one, not a dozen—but hundreds of Skitterlings in an instant.

“By Raol,” he whispered, a wide smile pulling at his lips as pride bubbled within him.

Crixis had done it. He had stepped into a league of power far above his peers, threading a path even transmigrants would be proud to wield.

And the fact that he’d had a hand in it stretched his smile into a grin.

Deactivating his Stealth perk, he called out to Crixis, ready to reveal himself. However, just as he did so, Crixis slumped, falling face-first onto the ash-covered ground.

Almost immediately, the scattered remains of what had once been hundreds of Skitterlings shrieked, their crimson gazes burning with killing intent as they rushed at Crixis’s fallen body, mandibles and limbs clicking with excitement.

Before they could even draw close, however, Aodhán was there, a protective growl instinctively escaping his lips as he raised a palm and snapped.

Leech.

The Skitterlings froze instantly—hundreds of them—as energy was yanked mercilessly from their cores. All that energy poured into Aodhán’s spirit, and he channeled it straight into his class.

The creatures spasmed, dropping like flies as the energy they possessed was drained dry.

But the influx of energy only lasted a moment before drying up, and Aodhán grimaced when his class counter ticked up by barely a quarter of a percent.

Sighing in disappointment, he turned his attention to Crixis, who was almost as pale as the ash around him. Blood seeped from his neck and several other parts of his body where he had taken injuries, but it was his core that drew Aodhán’s attention.

It was weak, flickering like a candle in the wind.

His energy had bottomed out.

Quickly, Aodhán raised him into a sitting position, pulled a core from his spatial storage, and pressed it against his chest.

“Absorb it,” he commanded, and with the little strength he had left, Crixis obeyed.

Aodhán pressed another core against his chest as soon as the first crumbled, and a moment later, Crixis’s core surged back to life, blazing brightly with amethyst light as he advanced to the nineteenth tier.

“Ugh,” he groaned, coughing ash from his lungs as life surged back into his body.

“Easy,” Aodhán said, rubbing his chest. “You’re alright. Your energy bottomed out, but you still have willpower. You’ll be fine.”

Crixis inhaled deeply, heaving. “The Skitterlings—”

“Dead,” Aodhán smiled. “I killed them all.”

Crixis stilled for only a moment before letting out a breath of relief, his body shuddering.

“I hate you,” he gasped. “I thought you left me. I thought I was going to die.”

Aodhán chuckled. “Next time, I’ll make sure to swear on my Ascendant mother so you’ll believe me.”

Crixis rasped out a laugh, his breathing still ragged. “Don’t bother. I wouldn’t believe you anyway.”

“Wise,” Aodhán smiled. “But I did warn you. I told you I wouldn’t save you until it was obvious you couldn’t save yourself.”

Crixis groaned. “It’s my fault. I told you I learned better this way.”

“And you did,” Aodhán grinned, pride bubbling within him once more. “You took your first step on the path of inevitability today. You learned.”

“I did, didn’t I?” Crixis smiled brightly, his breathing finally steadying. “It was as if time slowed to a crawl and I realized I was focusing too much on my attacks rather than my target.”

Aodhán nodded. “You’re the lock mechanism.”

Crixis frowned in confusion, and Aodhán explained.

“It’s a term from my world. There are these machines called missiles. They lock onto a target and chase it until they hit. You lock onto the target. The storm does the rest.”

Crixis’s frown deepened in contemplation for a moment before he nodded slowly. “That’s a really good illustration.” He glanced at Aodhán. “I think you just gave me insight into a new skill.”

Aodhán smiled. “Your debt keeps piling up.”

“It’ll pay it all back very soon,” Crixis chuckled. “I’ll pay it back double.”

“Good. Now get up and let’s pack these cores.”

Crixis pushed himself upright, ash raining from his shoulders as he swayed. Aodhán caught him before he could fall, smiling.

“You can add that to your debt.”

Crixis shook his head. “Just kill me.”

They laughed, and for the next few minutes, they scoured the ash for the Skitterling cores they had gained. A new horde arrived while they worked, but another activation of Leech killed them all before they could even surface.

Once again, his class absorbed the energy in seconds, drinking it up before it could properly gather within his spirit. Aodhán couldn’t believe he had once considered evolved creatures powerful—not with how weak they all seemed to him now.

Then again, he was twenty-six tiers above them.

Minutes passed in this manner, and half an hour later, they were finally ready to exit the rift. They stepped up to the mouth of the swirling portal and, without hesitation, passed through, arriving back at the sect.

Only it wasn’t nearly as peaceful as they had left it hours ago.

Alarms blared loudly, deep and resonant in that familiar way that signaled imminent danger. In the sky, protective arrays shone gold, painting the sect in hues of orange and amber. Shouts and orders to stay behind closed doors echoed in the distance, and the usual hustle and bustle of the outer sect was nowhere to be found.

There were no wandering disciples. No idle chatter. No peace.

There was no doubt about it. Their enemies had returned.

The war was beginning again.

“By the storm,” Crixis cursed from beside him. “We’ve only had four days of rest.”

Aodhán swallowed. “Yeah, well. Apparently four days is enough time to mourn the deaths of loved ones.”

He had known this day would come. To be honest, he had expected it to come sooner. But now that it was here, he couldn’t help but feel unprepared.

The thought of another battle—or weeks of battle—caused his insides to twist with dread, but he pushed through it, whispering softly to himself.

“You wield the path of authority, Aodhán. You do not have the luxury of fear.”

Not even when it meant entering another viewing deck.

Exhaling deeply, he gestured forward. “Come on. Let’s return to the special sect. We’ll know more when we get there.”

Crixis nodded, expecting Aodhán to create a floating platform for them. However, after several seconds passed and he didn’t, Crixis finally realized just how much this war was affecting him.

He didn’t look scared. He didn’t even seem bothered. But Crixis had learned over the past few days how frighteningly good Aodhán was at hiding his emotions. He had also learned to recognize certain tells, like the subtle tick in his jaw and the constant twitching of his fingers.

He was terrified.

Crixis stared at him for another moment before taking it upon himself to create the platform. The construct appeared a second later, a rectangular slab of amethyst lightning far less refined than anything Aodhán would have created.

Aodhán didn’t seem to mind. He muttered a distracted thanks and stepped onto it, fingers twitching as he fixed his gaze on the special sect, looking somehow both impatient to get there and more than willing to waste time.

The platform zipped forward—a crawl compared to the speed Aodhán usually traveled at, though Crixis doubted he even noticed, his arms folded behind his back in a posture of false calm.

They rode in silence for a long moment, the only sound the blaring alarms, until Crixis finally broke it.

“Are you okay?” he asked, glancing sideways at Aodhán.

“I’m fine,” Aodhán responded without looking at him.

But he wasn’t fine.

He was putting up a brave front, but with the way Crixis kept staring at him, he doubted it was working.

Because the truth was that he was scared.

He was scared of stepping foot onto another battlefield after what had happened the last time.

He was scared of dying. Scared of failing.

But most importantly, he was scared of losing more friends.

There was so much more to lose now that he had grown closer to the remaining Specials. To Crixis. To Nymera.

He had already lost Mirelle and Seamus, and had nearly lost Darien too. But try as he might, he couldn’t shake the feeling that he was about to lose someone else.

They arrived at the special sect a few minutes later, and the moment they touched down, Aodhán felt his communication ring buzz with a message.

It was Nymera.

Meet us at the command tent. Elder Ghaltris has summoned us all.

Aodhán read the message with a blank expression before turning to Crixis. “I have to go now. There’s… there’s a meeting.”

Crixis nodded. “I understand.”

Aodhán regarded him for a moment, then sighed. “Be safe, Crixis. I don’t know what’s going to happen in the coming days, but it’s very likely I won’t see you for a while. Your training is up to you now.”

“I understand,” Crixis nodded again, though the fear in his gaze was obvious. “The next time you see me, you won’t be disappointed.”

Aodhán smiled faintly. “I hope so. For your own good.”

They said their goodbyes then, and a moment later, Aodhán took off toward the command tent, the world blurring into streaks of color as his platform tore through space like a lightning bolt.

He arrived barely a minute later, and after clearing himself, stepped inside, his gaze sweeping over those gathered within. There were over a dozen of them, their expressions hard as they stared at a flickering screen on the opposite wall.

Elder Ghaltris sat at the head of a large rectangular table, flanked by several other elders. Behind them stood Nymera, Ashscar, and Sophran, their expressions tight with worry as they watched the screen.

But they were not the only ones present.

The leaders of their allied forces were there as well, each clad in the distinct colors of their sects. Beside them stood the two Space Calamities, along with Master Gyatso and Meredith, both positioned behind their respective elders like bodyguards.

They all looked up at his entrance, and Aodhán bowed. “Greetings, elders. I apologize for my tardiness.”

“Take your position, Aodhán,” Elder Ghaltris said, waving off the apology as he continued speaking.

Aodhán obeyed, moving to stand silently beside Nymera, who watched him curiously, worry plain on her face. Aodhán gave her a small smile to reassure her he was alright before turning his attention back to Elder Ghaltris, who had now abandoned the screen to address them all directly.

“Like I said,” Elder Ghaltris spoke, fixing his gaze on each and every one of them, “we expected this and have put certain measures in place. There’s no need to panic. When our enemies come, we will meet them, and we will be victorious.”

The elders nodded, and the general mood within the tent shifted into something more relaxed. Aodhán had missed most of the speech, but the gist of it was simple.

The Storm Sect wasn’t unprepared for the war.

Elder Ghaltris clasped his hands atop the table and continued. “The former battlefield is still a mess of broken realities, spatial fractures, and abyssal essence. We’ll be using a different battlefield this time, just northeast of the previous one.”

He glanced toward the Space Elders—Elder Zerathis in particular—and wet his lips.

“The use of Icons has been banned from the war. On no account should anyone use their Icons.” His gaze sharpened. “We would like to avoid a repeat of the last incident.”

“And are our enemies aware of that?” Zerathis asked, his expression not remotely remorseful. Behind him, Master Gyatso shifted uncomfortably.

“Yes, they are,” Elder Ghaltris nodded. “In fact, this was their idea.”

Elder Vasilis, his right-hand man, nodded. “It works in all our favor. We’ve had too many unnecessary losses already. We would like to avoid increasing that number unless absolutely necessary.”

“Oh.” Zerathis grimaced, an expression that caused several people to scowl.

“Are you not pleased with this development, Elder Zerathis?” Elder Ghaltris asked.

But before Zerathis could respond, the other space calamity—Lordstrom Veylmont—did.

“We are, Elder Ghaltris. We are very pleased.”

Everyone glanced at him, then back at Zerathis, expecting him to say the same.

Instead, Zerathis smiled. “We are pleased. Very pleased.”

But though they were the same words the other elder had spoken, they carried very little reassurance coming from Zerathis. Aodhán wasn’t the only one to pick up on it, and the tension in the room rose slightly before Elder Ghaltris spoke again.

“Okay then. On to other matters.”

He glanced at the leaders of their allied forces and smiled. “The last battle cut our numbers severely. Fortunately, some of our allies have agreed to send more disciples to aid us.”

The leader of the Water Sect nodded. “Indeed. They should arrive within the hour.”

“Ours as well,” the Wind Sect leader intoned. “We believe strongly in your victory and are ready to see it through.”

Grateful smiles broke out across the faces of several elders, and everyone turned toward the next sect, expecting more good news.

Unfortunately, that was where the good news ended.

“We are very sorry,” the Lightning Sect leader bowed his head. “We would send more if we could spare them. We can’t.”

“Neither can we,” the Shadow Sect leader intoned. “But we have also lost the fewest members. Our efficiency will not be diminished.”

The mood dipped further, but Elder Ghaltris smiled regardless. “We understand. Your presence here alone is a great comfort in this trying time.” He glanced at the Space Elders. “Besides, the Space Sect has offset our losses greatly.”

Zerathis smiled proudly. “It is our pleasure.”

Aodhán glanced sideways at the man, sensing layers beneath those words. For some reason, they felt snide. Mocking.

This time, though, it seemed only he and Master Gyatso picked up on it.

Their gazes locked, but before either could read further meaning into it, Elder Ghaltris continued.

“Speaking of offsetting numbers, our sources report that our enemies have also gained a new member. The Solar Sect.”

Murmurs rose immediately, and one of the elders snorted. “The Solar Sect isn’t a threat. Our people can handle them with their eyes closed.”

Murmurs of agreement followed before another voice cut through them. It was the Water Sect leader, Elder Lenor.

“Isn’t that too arrogant?” he asked. “The Solar Sect is much more than solar flares and beams. I’ve seen Solar cultivators transform marshes into deserts. Their manipulation of heat and light shouldn’t be underestimated.”

“Nor their use of gravity,” the Wind Sect leader added worriedly. “The sun is a cosmic body and a god to many. It would be a mistake to underestimate them.”

An elder frowned. “Be that as it may, the truth remains that our storms can handle them. We can block their access to sunlight.”

“And they can burn away the clouds we create,” Elder Ghaltris countered with a slow shake of his head. “Lenor and Avery are right. It is never wise to underestimate any affinity. I have seen their suns dehydrate thousands of Mythics before, transforming perfectly healthy warriors into parched, half-dead weaklings in seconds.”

He glanced around the room.“We have to be prepared for them. We need measures in place.”

Minutes passed in this manner as Elder Ghaltris raised one matter after another on how best to handle the war. They discussed the layout of the new battlefield, the formation of Mythics, the viewing deck, and finally the transmigrants themselves.

Elder Ghaltris glanced at them. “We have no idea what plans our enemies have cooked up, but there’s no doubt we’ll need all of you.” He fixed his gaze on each person in turn. “Your missions haven’t changed. Draw attention. Vanish.”

“Unless their transmigrants show up,” Elder Vasilis inserted, and Elder Ghaltris nodded.

“At which point, you’ll need to run interference.” He glanced toward Master Gyatso and Meredith. “You’ll be fighting alongside our transmigrants. If you find yourselves locked in battle against any enemy transmigrants, you are either to kill them or extricate yourselves from the battle as quickly as possible.”

“They’ll kill them,” Calamity Zerathis replied calmly, his gaze cold. “Members of the Space Sect aren’t cowards.”

Lordstrom Veylmont glanced at his comrade with an unreadable expression, but said nothing, exacerbating the tension further.

“Well… we would be grateful for that,” Elder Ghaltris replied a moment later, seeming genuinely unsure how to respond to the Calamities.

Aodhán couldn’t blame him.

He was a Mythic trying to manage an Icon-happy Calamity.

The meeting came to an end half an hour later when another alarm blared, far more piercing than the previous ones. The sound sent a chill down Aodhán’s spine, and his breath hitched.

Their enemies had arrived.

He exchanged a glance with Nymera and Sophran, both of whom looked just as frightened as he felt.

“We’ll be fine,” Sophran whispered. “I’ll have Halo running interference. She won’t let any of us die.”

“Unless we’re already dead,” Ashscar muttered, then grimaced. “Sorry. That was inappropriate.”

“But true,” Nymera shook her head. “Our enemies have lost people. They’re definitely coming back stronger.”

“So have we,” Aodhán said, holding their gazes. “Sophran, you’ve gained a familiar. Ashscar, you’ve evolved your path. Nymera, you’ve gained two more tiers. And I”—he smiled faintly—“am now at the fiftieth tier. We’ve all grown stronger. We’ll be fine.”

They all nodded.

However, as Aodhán turned, he came face to face with Master Gyatso.

He hadn’t spoken a word to the man since finding out about his mother. He wasn’t quite sure whether he was waiting for the right time or simply avoiding the matter altogether.

But one thing was certain.

This wasn’t the right time.

They would be fighting another battle soon, and the last thing he needed was thoughts of his mother fighting an eternal war.

Instead, he gave the monk a polite smile and glanced back at his friends. “Let’s go to the viewing deck. We have a war to win.”
