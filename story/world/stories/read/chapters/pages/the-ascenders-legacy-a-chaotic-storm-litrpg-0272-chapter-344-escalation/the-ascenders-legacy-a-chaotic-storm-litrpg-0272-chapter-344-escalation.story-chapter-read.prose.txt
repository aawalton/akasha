Transmigrants were dangerous.

If there were any who hadn't known that before, the last two weeks of battle had corrected that ignorance in blood. It was the reason this war existed at all. Balance.

And the transmigrants of the Storm Sect had proven to be anything but balanced.

"It's ridiculous!" Elder Aspwick of the Blood Sect growled, his fist clenching as he watched scene after scene of Storm transmigrants carving desolation through their ranks. It was hard to watch. Harder still to sit through knowing that the people dying on those screens had been alive and breathing two weeks ago.

They were all dead now, their lives reaped by the hands of children.

A scene of Nymera Vaelthorn's ceaseless vengeance came up next, and Aspwick turned his gaze away, jaw tight.

Calamities were dangerous—the most dangerous entities on any battlefield. But these transmigrants were something else entirely. Their abilities seemed to transcend the ceiling of the advanced class entirely, bleeding into territory that should have been reserved for Mythics. For Calamities.

It wasn't even a matter of raw strength or bizarre skills.

It was the nature of their paths—so terrifyingly singular it was a wonder they'd stumbled upon them at all. And to make things worse, the Storm Sect had four of these terrifying bastards, each one seemingly more powerful than the last.

"No." Elder Aspwick shook his head, his resolve hardening into something cold and final. "We cannot let this continue. We must do something."

Elder Sang-Hoon of the Earth Sect nodded, his gaze hard and arms folded in silence.

Elder Thamior Mournwind of the Serenity Sect glanced at the both of them and exhaled slowly. "Our Calamities are hard at work and vigilant."

"Our Calamities are busy defending against enemy Calamities," Aspwick shot back, his gaze cutting across the room. "They cannot afford to spare transmigrants their full attention. You know this."

"Then what do you suggest?" Elder Clearfall of the Glass Sect asked, his expression darkening as a fresh scene flashed across the screen—thousands of glass Mythics reduced to nothing in the space of a single breath. His fury deepened, and with a scowl, he muttered, "I am open to ideas. Any idea. These storm transmigrants have proven themselves more powerful than any ordinary transmigrant, and they grow more powerful by the day."

"It's the expanding Origin Plane." Elder Aspwick's voice dripped with contempt. "Have you noticed the surge of Storm familiars filling their ranks? They are growing rapidly."

"Or stabilizing," Elder Malverin of the Illusion Sect spoke quietly from her corner, her expression troubled. "Their power is catching up to the Storm Origin's current expansion. We are losing time."

"We don't need the reminder, Malverin." Elder Clearfall's glare snapped toward her. "We are already hard-pressed enough. What do we do?"

For a heartbeat, silence reigned within the viewing deck—heavy and suffocating as scenes of relentless destruction flashed across the screens. Balance wars were always brutal, but this one had derailed far from all that they'd planned.

They had to do something.

Minutes passed as the silence stretched, then after what seemed like hours, Elder Sang-Hoon unclasped his arms, his voice gravelly from lack of use. "There is only one thing to do."

The elders waited for him to say more, but when he didn't, they frowned and turned to glance at Mournwind.

"What does he mean, now?" Elder Clearfall asked. "We do not speak rock."

Elder Mournwind glared at them, but before he could say anything, Sang-Hoon did, a cutting glare fixed on Clearfall.

"Perhaps if you applied yourself more to enlightenment rather than cheap power, I wouldn't have to spell out my every thought to you like I was speaking to a child."

"Easy, Sang-Hoon," Elder Mournwind whispered, his soft voice diffusing the tension as easily as water over stone. "Just tell us what you mean so we can move on."

Elder Sang-Hoon folded his arms again. "Like I said earlier, there is only one thing to do. With our Calamities busy against enemy Calamities, and Mythics against other Mythics, these transmigrants also must be kept busy… against other transmigrants."

Even still, it took a moment for the implications of his words to land. But when they did, it caused an uproar.

"Certainly not." Elder Malverin turned on him immediately, her voice sharp. "You cannot be serious."

Sang-Hoon ignored her. "It is the only option we have." He glanced at Aspwick, his gaze hard. "You know this."

Aspwick frowned contemplatively. "It is not our only option, but it's the only option that would produce any significant results."

Elder Malverin glanced at them all, eyes wide. "No. You all can't seriously be considering this."

"Why not?" Aspwick's frown deepened, his tone sharp. "The fact is that we are stretched thin, and these transmigrants are running circles around us. Do you not see how they leverage our own attacks against our people? How they lead us on chases only for their machines to rip our comrades apart? Sang-Hoon is right. Transmigrants are wildcards—and until we introduce our own wildcards into this war, we will never win. We must each deploy our transmigrants. All of us."

"Easy for you to say." Elder Malverin's gaze hardened. "You and Sang-Hoon have three transmigrants. We have one. Perhaps the next Balance War should be fought between us instead."

Elder Aspwick's eyes flashed. "You and your puny sect would be eradicated in a blink if you were ever stupid enough to try."

Malverin flared. "Is that a threat?"

"Enough." Elder Mournwind stepped between them, his voice carrying the flat authority of one weary of arguments and battles. He held their gazes until the tension ebbed, then stepped back. "We cannot afford to fight among ourselves. Not now. Our enemy is far greater."

He glanced at each of them in turn before continuing. "That being said—I find myself agreeing with Elder Sang-Hoon and Aspwick. Despite the risks, it is time our transmigrants entered this battle. Between the five of us, we have eleven transmigrants. The Blood Sect and Earth Sect have three. The Serenity Sect, two. The Glass Sect, two. The Illusion Sect, one." He paused. "That nearly triples their numbers. If we coordinate—"

"We can bring this war to a swift end," Elder Sang-Hoon finished, a satisfied smile on his face.

"Unless one of us loses a transmigrant in the process," Elder Malverin's voice cut through the optimism like a blade. "And our Origin Plane shrinks even further than it already has."

Elder Clearfall glared at her. "The Origin Planes will shrink if we lose the war."

"No." Elder Malverin shook her head resolutely, the anger in her eyes giving way to fear for a moment. "We cannot afford the risk. It's too dangerous. Our transmigrant… she's all we've got. If we lose her, then we have nothing."

"It'll be three against one for crimson's sake," Elder Aspwick growled. "Is your transmigrant so weak she can't hold?"

"What my transmigrant can or cannot hold is none of your business," Elder Malverin glared at the man, anger returning to set her gaze alight. "We've seen the strength of these transmigrants. How many of you can beat your chest in confidence of your own? How many of you can be certain your transmigrants won't lose their lives?"

Silence answered her.

She pressed on, quieter now. "We cannot afford to be rash about this. I've never been a fan of putting all my eggs in one basket, and I won't start now. The storm transmigrants have had two weeks to sharpen themselves against the chaos of war. Two weeks of real battle—of surviving things that should have killed them. Our transmigrants have had none of that. We'd be sending lambs to face wolves and calling it strategy."

"Speak for your sect," Elder Sang-Hoon's jaw tightened. "My transmigrants are no sheep. You underestimate your people."

"I don't have people," Malverin gritted out. "I have a person. And you're asking me to place all my cards on the table." She glanced at them all, seeing the lack of understanding in their gazes, and hers hardened. Slowly, she shook her head. "I'm not doing it."

"What do you mean you're not doing it?" Clearfall asked with a glare. "Sang-Hoon is right. You are underestimating your transmigrant."

But Malverin had already made up her mind. "Be that as it may—introducing our transmigrant into this war is not a risk the Sect of Illusion is willing to take." Her voice was quiet. Final. "You may proceed without us."

"And fight for you?" Elder Aspwick frowned.

"If that is what it requires, yes." She met his glare without flinching. "We are here, are we not? The Sect of Silence isn't—and yet we fight for them too."

What followed was neither quick nor clean.

Voices rose. Fists met tables. Accusations were leveled and grievances aired that had nothing to do with transmigrants and everything to do with alliances stretched too thin for too long.

But Elder Malverin refused to yield.

And in the end, they were forced toward the only conclusion all five could stomach. The Illusion Sect would contribute a dozen Mythics in lieu of their transmigrant. The remaining four sects would each deploy one.

It was a compromise that satisfied no one and solved nothing perfectly, but it was the best they could agree to. Perfect solutions were, after all, a luxury rarely found in wars.

"It is settled then," Elder Mournwind stated a moment later. "We shall deploy the transmigrants tomorrow. Give our Mythics some time to rest and recuperate."

Agreement passed between them in silence—but the silence was different now.

Glares lingered a moment too long. Knowing looks were shared. And in the spaces between words, lines had quietly been drawn.

But the enemy's viewing deck was not the only place where silence reigned.

Within the grand coliseum of the Silence Sect, a heavy quiet held dominion—so profound the very air seemed to tremble beneath its weight.

It had been a dozen days since they received the agent of the Storm Sect into this very coliseum. Yet the impact of his gift still resonated loudly among their ranks.

The pendant had worked.

Exactly five days ago, the slave they had entrusted with it gained his familiar—a faceless, alabaster-winged entity he had named The Null Seraph. A rare and formidable familiar only ever owned by one person.

The First Seat.

And it had been gained by a slave with no prior training or resources.

It was insolence, of course, to share a familiar with the First Seat. And the slave had been permanently silenced for it.

But the power of the pendant could no longer be denied.

Unfortunately, for five days straight, all efforts to replicate the pendant had failed woefully—an uncomfortable reminder that their expertise lay in ending things, not making them.

And after so many failures, they had come to a single conclusion.

They needed the Storm Sect to provide more pendants—or teach select architects among them how to reproduce it.

No doubt, the Storm Sect would require some persuasion to do either. But the First Seat had already foreseen this need.

At the center of the coliseum stood the androgynous figure, silent as ever, awaiting the First Seat's final decree.

The decision came just as dawn broke—sunlight streaming into the coliseum to bathe the marble floors in gold.

The androgynous figure bowed. "As you will, First Seat. We shall join the Storm Sect in battle. A thousand Mythic Silencers should suffice."

They walked out of the coliseum immediately after that, and an hour later, High-Elder Torvas, First Seat of the Storm Sect, received a missive that made him smile.

It seemed his plan had worked quite nicely after all.

Crixis awoke to a deep rumble of thunder that shook the walls of his chamber, a wash of familiar energy racing across his skin—his amethyst skin. The sight of it should have filled him with the same exhilaration it had for the past week.

Instead, dread coiled in his gut.

That thunder was no ordinary weather phenomenon. It was the sound of devastation unfolding on a battlefield hundreds of miles away.

A battlefield Aodhán was fighting in.

Shuddering, he rushed into the living room where a wide screen displayed live scenes from the war. He'd arrived too late to see the full attack, but he caught the tail end of it, and that alone was enough to send his blood pumping.

He recognized the attack. Who didn't?

It was the combo attack Aodhán and the other transmigrants had been spamming for the past week.

Ashscar's Herald's Toll. Nymera's Vengeance. Aodhán's Ruin's Obedience. And Sophran's Pressure Point.

The first time they'd used the combo had been accidental, but after seeing its devastating effect, the transmigrants had made it their aim to repeat it at every opportunity.

And the destruction it wrought was unprecedented.

Bloody craters. Torn bodies. Entire formations erased in seconds—the lives of thousands of Mythics cut short by a single, synchronized declaration of storm.

It was utterly insane what the transmigrants were capable of. The sheer magnitude of power they could bring to bear to unleash such devastation.

But beyond all that, Crixis was in awe of Aodhán, who was only at the 45th tier yet holding his own in a war so dangerous Mythics were dropping like flies.

And the war was indeed dangerous. After all, it had claimed the life of Mirelle Vaskoff, a double inheritor of immense power.

Despite the obvious danger, Crixis couldn't help but want to join. He too wanted to unleash destruction and inevitability on their enemies. To rip the world apart with skills so potent their echoes could be felt hundreds of miles away.

But at the seventeenth tier, Crixis knew he wouldn't survive a moment on that battlefield. The auras of the Calamities and Mythics alone would be enough to do him in.

But that only spurred him on, and with a resolved expression, he invited Rathis Cloudrend and Kairn Calcirra—Special Disciples Darien and Seamus's underlings—to train with him.

He would have invited Su Tianan, but the blond underling was still mourning Mirelle's death, just as they all should have been. But there was no time to mourn with the war escalating every moment.

He had to get stronger if he was ever going to stand by Aodhán's side.

Already, the gap between them was heaven and earth. Crixis knew he could never close the gap, but he could narrow it.

And he intended to begin immediately.

While all that happened, the Blood Sect, the Earth Sect, the Glass Sect, and the Serenity Sect had gathered their transmigrants within the viewing deck, each one specifically chosen to counter one of the storm's transmigrants.

The blood transmigrant, Kael Varros, was the very definition of cold, controlled brutality. The man was lean, almost gaunt in appearance, with ash-pale skin and eyes the color of blood itself.

Cultivating the path of Crimson Dominion, Kael was a master at battlefield control and precision wars—a perfect counter to the untamed volatility of Nymera's path.

The earth transmigrant, Volgrim Kaizen, on the other hand, was everything his element suggested—immovable, patient, and absolute in his endurance. Broad-shouldered and weathered, with skin the color of deep ochre and eyes like quarry stone, he had the stillness of someone who had long since decided that most things weren't worth reacting to.

Cultivating the Path of Silent Earth, Volgrim's power did not lie in destruction, but suppression—the earth's ancient talent for swallowing sound, absorbing impact, and burying the loudest declarations beneath miles of silent stone.

Where others sought to match the storm's fury, Volgrim simply refused to acknowledge it—making him the perfect answer to a transmigrant whose power depended entirely on declaring and heralding the storm.

Thunder, after all, only mattered when the earth yielded to it.

The glass transmigrant, Seraphine Ilex, had been chosen not just for her admirable strength, but her unique ability to sense faultlines and weaknesses in any attack, construct, or person. Cultivating the path of Fractured Reflection, her aim wasn't to fight the storm, but to find the flaw in the will that guided it—to target the seams in its authority.

Nothing was whole, after all. Not even authority.

The serenity transmigrant, Alaric Thorneveil, was the last of the four and in many ways the most insidious. He had dark bronze skin, long black hair tied low, and calm gray eyes that made him the perfect picture of moderation.

Cultivating the path of Absolute Equilibrium, Alaric was the antithesis of all extremities and imbalance. His power wasn't in overpowering his enemies, but in subduing them—flattening power spikes, neutralizing amplifications, and diminishing emotion-induced boosts.

He was the opposite of everything the storm stood for, and there was no doubt his presence in the war would enforce an unfavorable balance on the entire battle.

Fortunately for the Storm Sect, their enemies weren't the only ones preparing to enter the battlefield.

Hundreds of miles away, a thousand silence Mythics stepped out of their mountain and into a waiting teleporter.

After all, silence had no allies save itself.
