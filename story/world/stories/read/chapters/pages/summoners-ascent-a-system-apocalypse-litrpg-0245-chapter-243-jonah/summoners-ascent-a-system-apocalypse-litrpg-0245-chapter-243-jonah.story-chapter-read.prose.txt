The room was completely silent, eerily so. In this space where even the slightest sound would echo and feel even louder, not even a single breath could be heard.

Ashton's breathing was close to silent anyway, and Clementine was holding hers. As for Jonah... did he even need to breathe the way a normal human did anymore?

"Are you..." Clementine slowly started, seeing the man in front of her. This was the first time she had seen Jonah in a long time; it had been a while even before this apocalypse started. "Are you really my father?"

Jonah let out a loud, cheery laugh. His voice was booming, filling out the entire room in an instant.

"Of course I am! My dearest Clementine, I'm so glad that you have come to me," Jonah said, beaming from ear to ear. "Ashton, I am very grateful that you have brought my daughter to me after all, especially after how our last conversation went."

Ashton just stood there, leaning onto the summoner's staff. "Mm-hmm, whatever. Clementine, go ahead. This is him. I know he doesn't look it, but–"

"Ah! So that is what this is about! I know that my shedding of my weak body may be somewhat startling, but I promise that I am still the same, loving man that I have always been toward you."

Clementine shivered. Loving? Him? She snapped her head toward Ashton. "I, uhm... I don't really remember you like that, dad. But... okay, you say you're him, so... what are you doing here? People are saying that this is a cult, so–"

The moment Clementine said that word, Jonah's calm demeanour changed within an instant. He slammed his hand onto the altar next to him, and ground his teeth together, like pushing two plates of granite against each other.

"This is not a cult! What those people are saying are lies, Clementine! Can you not see what is going on here? Why must you be blind to these miracles that are happening before your eyes? Our god has forsaken us, and instead, the great divinity of the heavens has come to save us! They only call it a cult because they do not understand!"

Ashton couldn't help but scoff. 'Great divinity'? What a joke. Ashton had no idea what the hell the system was, or if it was a natural occurrence or if it was created, but it wasn't an 'entity'. If it was, then by now it would've attained an actual already.

Ashton had seen it many times before. This kind of cult that Jonah was leading wasn't the first, and it wasn't the last. In Lumia as well, Ashton had seen many cults rise and fall after the system came about, and he was positive that any world with a concept or capacity for religion was going to have the same type of thing happen.

So many beings across the multiverse having faith in the same entity would create a god, whether the target wanted it to or not. But in this case, there was no 'God of the System'. All of the power of faith that was generated by believers in the system just lingered in the air with no target. The system was a fundamental rule of the universe at this point. It was something rigid, not something that could be swayed or changed through one's belief. The same way that, no matter how hard you tried, it was impossible to counteract gravity and fly away just because you believed you could. Even if Peter Pan would have you believe otherwise.

Ashton could feel Clementine's gaze on him, like she wanted to get his help here, but he really didn't want to unnecessarily antagonize Jonah. He wanted Jonah to be clear-headed as he spouted those disgusting thoughts of his, so that Clementine would have no—or at least less—doubts about all of this.

"Fine, then it's not a cult, I guess. But what about that magic that you're using on the people during service?" Ashton asked instead, figuring it was better to guide that man in this way.

But Jonah seemed completely confused. "Magic? What kind of joke is that supposed to be?"

Ashton sighed and pulled his cloak to the side, revealing the lantern hanging by his hip. He tapped it a few times, and the coal briquette inside was quickly taken over by the flames of the lesser salamander. Ashton took some flames from inside of the lantern and guided them around with his hands, making them dance through the air.

"Seriously, why do people still refuse to acknowledge magic as real?" Ashton sighed.

"I don't know what kind of trick that is, but I do not use 'magic'. I guide the voices of my people through prayer, just as the apostles of the divine one have told me." Jonah clasped his hands together, smiling broadly. "And it is wonderful! With every sermon I hold, the sheep of my flock come closer together, they become even more true believers than they were before!"

Ashton clenched his fist. "Mm-hmm."

"What does... what does being a 'true believer' entail?" Clementine slowly let out. That seemed to be the thing she wanted to know the most right now. Were the people here safe, or were they not?

"How wonderful, you are already showing such enthusiasm!" Jonah smiled broadly. Some of his petrified skin crumbled off his cheeks. "A true believer, of course, dedicates him- or herself fully to the divine one. They have all attained holy classes that prove their devotion. Day in and day out, when they aren't doing work to keep our church afloat during these trying times, they spend their time in prayer. Even now, they should be gathered, proving their faith to the divine one alongside each other."

Ashton furrowed his brow. No wonder. Ashton felt that the energies flowing along the veil were stronger than they should be. He thought that it was just accumulated faith from before, but if they were doing nothing but praying all day, then it made sense.

And if it was this strict, then even all the other 'work' that they were doing would somehow count as a prayer, since it was all done to keep this place going to ensure that everyone could fully dedicate themselves to the 'divine one', as Jonah said.

"You're making them all do nothing but pray? For what, day, weeks? How long will this go on?" Clementine was startled. "What about food? Defence? What about the quests that are still coming? You won't survive here if there aren't any combatants here."

Jonah let out a hearty laugh, shaking his head. "You don't have to worry about that, my dear. Yes, it may have taken some a little more... convincing, but everyone is doing this happily and gladly. They voluntarily forgo much of the food that I do provide to them. And, believe it or not, but I am actually quite a powerful man at this point." Ashton could feel Jonah's gaze move over to him. "Stronger than any other in this town by now, I would assume."

Ashton held back a laugh. Yeah, sure. He could defeat Jonah without even breaking a sweat, no matter how much power he thought he had accumulated at this point.

"If you say so," Ashton yawned slightly.

"I do say so, indeed," Jonah nodded, slowly approaching Clementine. His steps were heavy, and now that he was approaching her, he must seem even more massive than he did before. He had easily grown a metre taller than he was before, and was far, far more imposing than he had been. It made Clementine freeze up. "I promise, my beloved daughter, that I will forever protect you. Just stand by my side, and I will prove it to you."

Something was wafting through the air. An unsettling, eery magic that made Ashton's skin crawl. It was that same magic that Jonah had used during the service earlier. He was trying to turn Clementine into one of his 'sheep'.

Ashton immediately stepped between them, interfering with the magic in the air. It was relatively easy to block something like this, especially with Jonah's meagre control.

"What do you think you're doing?" Jonah stared down at Ashton, who didn't pay that man any mind.

"Clementine, continue. There were more things that you wanted to ask." Ashton stood in front of her, and would make sure that Jonah couldn't try any more. He would protect her.

"Hm? Oh, yes... right, uhm..." Clementine, seemingly a little dazed, hurriedly nodded. "I think there's really just one thing that I need to ask at this point... Dad, how many? How many people did you kill during the first quest? And why, when there were so many other monsters here?"

Jonah stared past Ashton. "Excuse me? How dare you accuse me of doing something so abhorrent?! I would never–"

"Yes you would, and you did," Ashton interrupted. "You're a 'Marked'. You're not like us. You're a monster. You're just lucky that you're one smart enough to speak. So, answer her."

Jonah stared down at Ashton. He was holding something in his hand. Some kind of book, or stone slab, or something in-between. Ashton couldn't quite tell.

"If I took a life, then it was done to protect myself and my wife! I did not kill, I simple defended us the way I had to!" Jonah yelled, his voice echoing through the cathedral.

Poppy was shivering. This was a lie. A blatant lie that even Jonah didn't believe.

"How many?" Clementine asked again, staring at her father's face.

Jonah hesitated, but in the end, let out a long sigh. "I promise, I only did it because they were attacking us first. There were three. That gang of disgusting little street rats that would keep loitering around the church. The first chance they got, they ran at us, trying to rob or kill us. You know how they are... always so aggressive."

Clementine placed a hand over her mouth, startled. "You... killed three? Three people? For no reason at all?"

She took a few steps back.

"I told you! They were attacking us first, so–"

"Clementine," Ashton started, "did you decide?"

"I don't... I don't know, I can't..." Clementine stared up at the man that raised her. The man that beat her black and blue when she spoke back even once. The man that held her when she was a toddler, and told her he loved her. How was she supposed to make that choice in one night? "Please, Ashton, I don't know if I can choose, I just... Can't you do something else? Can't you stop him, lock him up? Anything, just... just give me more time!"

Ashton turned to Clementine, with a bitter smile. "So you're saying you're not making a choice at all?" He turned back to Jonah. "In that case, I'm sorry. But I'll have to make that choice for you."
