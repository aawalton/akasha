A faint bluish-purplish glow travelled in a straight path outside the cave.

Interesting. It wasn't as strong a light as [Flashlight], nor was it as bright. When she turned the light to illuminate the corners of the cave, something else happened.

An image flashed in her mind briefly, one that lacked color and depth. It was strange outlines, bumpy blacks with bright whites, and as she moved the light, the image changed too.

“What is that?” she wondered aloud as the spell time elapsed and the spell shut off. The orbs went in their different directions. The blue orb bobbed in the corner, euphoric after its first time in a spell in what was likely a long time.

It greeted the rest of the orbs that tiredly trekked its path.

"Hmm."

Arielle wrote down what she had seen: the blue orb plus [Flashlight] transformed into a ray of bluish-purple light and gave an image of something strange.

She wondered if it would matter where she placed the blue orb on the spell form? What if she placed it at the end of the structure?

She had to wait ten minutes for the orb to recharge before she could try again. When she did, it morphed once more into a slightly different structure***, a red light that wasn't quite as narrow, and it moved on its own. It swayed around the cave, stopping in different places.

What was it stopping for?

Arielle used [Sensate] to follow the movement of the light before it stopped on the cave wall. Her enhanced hearing picked up skittering and buzzing.

Insects.

It was stopping for the moving insects on the wall, identifying them. That was interesting. So in this orientation, the blue orb turned the spell into a movement tracker? Or just an insect identifier?

She tried a different configuration, placing the blue orb at the start of the spell. It twisted around, and this time, it gave her a yellow light that settled on a tuft of grass in the cave.

So maybe this light pointed out plants?

The fourth arrangement was a little harder to understand. It gave her little wisps that travelled around the space, before collecting at a given point. At first, she saw nothing special about that point. No insects or plants. And then she realized. Orbs. It was the cross-section of the ley-drift, where the orbs were thickest.

Reveal. It reveals things.

Arielle’s heart beat in excitement.

Luxa was already a revelatory orb, but it seemed that the blue orb deepened that or altered the type of revelation it was. This was good. Perhaps with the right combination of essences, she could reveal more about the soul and the abyss.

So far, she had only tried it out with light.

Time to try other spells.

Her next attempt was with [Wind Gust]. She placed the blue orb between the first Massa and Calor junction and waited to see what would happen.

Once again, the spell transformed slightly before it worked.

But it didn't reveal anything like with Luxa. Instead, a gentle, pleasant breeze floated around the cave, rustling her hair and soothing her.

She frowned. That was strange.

She tried it again, placing the Blue Orb at the very end of the spell, and what she got was a small whirlwind floating on the ground, picking up the debris before it disappeared.

Placing it at the start of the spell form caused a random particle in the air to move around the room until it landed in her palm, before disappearing.

Her fourth attempt was the only one that she found useful.

She cast the spell and then pointed at a rock. It located that rock, then pushed it back a few inches. The air did not carry anything else, just the rock.

So the last configuration had focused and isolated [Wind Gust]. That would be useful, except that it was also confusing.

She wrote down her findings and then moved on to the Vacu spells.

She created a [Air Slice] and placed the blue orb in the middle.

Suddenly the spell materialized into a slender black sword that shimmered and vibrated the air. She gaped as it swung around an axis, cutting through the air and making it ripple with the force before it disappeared entirely.

Arielle grinned. That was very useful.

She tried [Air Slice] again, and this time,  it sprouted black tentacles around an axis, whipping through the air, making it shimmer as well, before it disappeared.

She paused after that spell, noting it down and examining everything she had collected so far. Though she was more confused than ever, she was still excited.

The Blue Orbs seemed to amplify the magnitude of [Air Slice], but it had reduced the effect of [Wind Gust] and had transformed [Flashlight] into a different thing altogether.

And she couldn't really even blame it on different isotopes because this was the same isotope changing spells in different ways. What accounted for the changes? What was it actually doing?

She didn't have much time left, so finally, she combined the orb with [Sensate]. Her idea was that it might be one way to amplify her senses such that she could see through the atmosphere and into the abyss (or into a soul). It was a rough thought, and she expected it to come with some undesirable side effects.

But the explosion of sound and scent and everything else made her yelp before she quickly eliminated the spell.

It took her a second to catch her breath.

The blue orb had amplified every part of that spell, beyond tolerance. That much stimulus had nearly knocked her out.

She would have to find a way to turn it down in its next iteration, as the blue orb once again joined its compatriots at the side.

Arielle wanted to understand exactly what that last spell had become, wanted to see if she could isolate and eliminate different parts, but she didn't want to risk blasting her eardrums. After all, the spell tended to change slightly, even after she put it together, which might eliminate any modifications she made. She had to be careful here, because while the simple spells all had manageable effects, complex spells could easily hurt or overwhelm her.

She also had to remember that she didn't have the same anatomy or resistance as the Elves.

Some things might be too dangerous for her to try.

The orb drifted to her, bobbing happily. She balanced it on the edge of her finger, smiled, and petted it.

“You did great,” she said. "All of you."

The rest of the used orbs bounced tiredly as they retreated to their corners.

She had inadvertently presented her a better excuse than the truth. “Maybe.

You got back and have been nonstop at the library or at lessons, or in our room, reading. Your mind is always far away, so it’s hard to even carry on a conversation with you. You also didn’t tell me that your cousin was attacked by demons. I had to hear it through the grapevine like everyone else. Not to say that I blame you.” She hurried to add that last part. “I understand it's very traumatic to talk about, which is why I gave you space and everything. Maybe your secret classes with the dean are beyond anything we can understand. But you could at least give me a heads-up when you're going somewhere. Or tell me why you constantly look stressed these days, or just tell me what's going on in your mind."

Arielle had finished stacking the books and began to unstack them again. She didn't know how to respond to Lyra. She couldn't answer any of Lyra's questions, and it made her feel like a bad friend, but she also felt like anything she might say would be the wrong thing, and she would end up losing Lyra forever as a result.

She didn’t want Lyra to stop being her friend, and it was a lot easier to lose a friend than a sister.

"Yes."

"Yes."

Arielle didn't know what to say, so once again, she said nothing.
