Selina grew up primarily in the kingdom of Viridus, first on a small farm with human adoptive parents, and later in the great city of Corvale. She experienced life on Nexum as a relatively typical person, simply a child growing up in the country, and later as an academic, in her quest to become the Runemistress she now was. She’d studied and encountered much in that time, including the various demons that came through the occasional rifts.

For those reasons, Selina was certain that the creatures surrounding her were not native to Potentia.

The vines slackened as the eclectic group grew closer, looking down at her with a collection of faces slightly off from what Selina expected–provided these were what she believed. They were monsters, that vague term used to describe the generally hostile magical lifeforms everywhere on Nexum, yet each appeared somewhat different than those she was used to.

Of the roughly dozen or so surrounding her, more than half were the vineotto so common in Viridus. They were essentially lumbering plant creatures, looking a bit like camels from Earth–camels made entirely of moss and vines, that is. They weren’t the best fighters, but they were largely responsible for the abundant farming that her homeland was known for. They could grow crops in minutes, and were highly prized.

And yet…each one she laid eyes on had some kind of abnormality. Some were more pronounced–extra limbs, one even with two heads–and others just oddly proportioned. Looking at the other monsters silently inspecting her as she did them, she noticed the same.

There was a fulven, something like a yellow wolf, yet it had rigid bone spikes running along its spine. An ignarana, basically a giant frog that breathed fire, lacked any eyes, and had its head tilted away from her, as if to listen. Looking up into the trees, she spotted several rare dreadrachni, the spider-like monsters that Princess Ellevaro had Bonded. They were mostly lost among the branches, but she spotted oddities among them as well.

“You’ve all been twisted by this place, haven’t you?” she whispered. “But how did you even get here?”

The question seemed to break whatever spell had kept the monsters still, and they began shuffling around, looking to one another. They began making various sounds, clicks, scratches, and low howls, as if communicating. Selina didn’t understand it, but the unusual orchestra ended when one of the vineotto sent more vines around her arms, binding them tightly.

More sounds, combined with a jerking of her restraints, indicated they wanted her to move. Before Selina could even consider a protest, she was being pulled along at a brisk pace deeper into the forest.

Selina couldn’t account for the odd behavior, but turned her mind to making as much sense of the situation as she could. She began categorizing what she knew, while simultaneously considering different avenues of escape. This was fascinating, of course, and her inquisitive mind left her desperate to solve this new mystery, but she had to consider the importance of their mission.

The first bit of good news–aside from the monsters not immediately trying to kill her, was that they were all Dusk rank, the same as her. Obviously they’d been relying on their numbers and ambush tactics to hunt the Twilight Walter, and it meant that escape wasn’t beyond the realm of possibility.

The next major oddity that she couldn’t ignore, was that they were working together. There were very few monsters that mingled with anything not of their own kind, let alone an organised group of a half-dozen varieties, hunting in a pack and taking prisoners. Selina had to believe there was some kind of greater intelligence behind this, but she wasn’t sure that finding out the truth was in her best interest.

The reality was that quick escapes weren’t her strong suit. She was a spellcaster, a healer, and someone who prepared. She could draw out complex Rune Circles, prepare powerful magics, as well as protect and restore, but running a marathon back to the Fable Train was a doomed endeavor.

The bunker on the other hand… It may not be a place of safety, but she knew that Apollo could find it. If she could get away and find some place to hide, maybe she could buy the army the time it needed to catch up. She had no doubt that they’d come for her, and that was where the search would begin.

She continued stumbling after the vienotto, glancing warily at the circle of monsters shepherding her to some unknown fate, waiting for the right moment. It came when the trail dipped suddenly, the endless trees giving way to walls of earth on both sides. The path was still broad, and her entire entourage had no trouble fitting into the crevice, but it did force them to bunch up slightly.

That was Tibby, the light rabbit–now the size of a large dog–appeared above the group. Selina gave herself just enough time to cover her eyes before signalling her Familiar to unleash a blast of intense illumination. Even with an arm across her face and eyelids tightly shut, the sudden flash nearly blinded her, and the unprotected monsters howled their fury and surprise.

Selina didn’t hesitate. A disk of light sent her shooting up into the air, then back the way she’d come. She swatted branches from her face as she made a desperate escape, leaping from the disc as soon as she was out of sight of the odd group of monsters. At Dusk rank, the ability wasn’t much faster than a sprint, and in the darkness it revealed her like a glowing target.

She landed in the dry ground, nearly tripping over thick roots as she caught her balance, then rushed forward. Pepper the Pursi appeared behind her, letting out an adorable roar. The Familiar was smaller than a grizzly bear from Earth, but the brave creature could cast a few slowing afflictions before disappearing back into her soul. Selina would take every moment of time she could buy.

She could hear them behind her now, and saw vines slithering around, questing for her. Selina thought the monsters might need to see their target to guide those creeping wooden tentacles, as they seemed to thrash blindly for now. Her in-born Nator strength allowed her to snap a few that got too close with her bare hands, and after Pepper rejoined her, she cycled to light to burn away more with one of her only dedicated attack spells.

The beam of searing light was highly effective in this way, but it not only gave away her position, but also threatened to set the whole forest aflame.

She kept running, hoping she could get nearer to the bunker before finding a suitable hiding place, but her hopes soon shifted. Her spiritual senses felt…tickled. That was the only way she could think of it. It was as if she were walking through–

“Damn!” she swore, recognizing what that feeling was. She’d seen the dreadrachni, and remembered belatedly that they were capable of sending out thin webs that connected back to them. They didn’t need light to find her–she was leaving an unmissable trail.

Selina risked a glance upward, and the shifting tree-limbs showed that her pursuers were already right above her. She could just barely make out skittering legs, and disturbingly bulbous bodies.

“I hate bugs!” she shouted at them. Spiders weren’t insects, she knew, and these weren’t even truly spiders. She hoped the insult stung.

Selina kept running, despite knowing that she was being easily tracked. She wasn’t entirely out of tricks yet, and slowed to stamp the ground several times, her Soul Rune abilities allowing her to make basic Rune Circles instantly. These were simple traps, sending out vines of her own to reach up into the trees.

She didn’t stop to see if any succeeded, instead looking for anything that looked like a real hiding spot. Unfortunately, the forest was thick, and trees seemed to have grown over anything that could have suited. She also knew any such place she did find wouldn’t be any good so long as the dreadrachni knew her location.

When one dropped from a tree in front of her, she played another card. Before the spider could do more than snap its pincers at her, long, sinuous tentacles reached down from the tree, wrapping around the creature. The thin legs were no match for Grippy’s powerful arms, and the spider was pulled away in a heartbeat.

Selina’s healing Familiar had begun barely larger than the palm of her hand, but had turned out to be one of the rare monsters that grew exponentially with evolutions. Little Grippy was now the size of a car, with twenty arms, looking like a true seamonster.

Selina knew that he didn’t actually enjoy fighting, so she used him sparingly. But when he was needed, he never hesitated.

The brave monster did his best to snag several of the spiders, and Selina used the opportunity to try something new. She dodged around a particularly thick tree trunk, then leapt directly up it. She hoped that if she could get high enough, the monsters chasing her would race by. It was a longshot, and she couldn’t be sure she wouldn’t find more invisible webs waiting, but she had few options.

For a brief moment of peace, she perched on the highest limbs she could find, flattening her form against the trunks. Then a sharp crack told her that her plan had failed.

Her stomach protested as she was suddenly falling, the entire massive tree tipping over. She leapt free at the last moment–no Dusk rank truly feared falling from a minor height, and rolled to her feet, ready to run once more.

She slid to a halt an instant later, as a pack of mutated fulven appeared from the darkness. She was surrounded, and their bared teeth were a clear threat.

“Easy now,” she said reflexively, holding up her hands. She knew she wasn’t meant for this kind of fight, and feared what they might do to her now that she’d tried to escape–and proved more difficult to hold onto than they might have thought.

Her fears were realized as one of the wolves leapt at her. But before her fist could slam into the creature’s face, it was yanked back unexpectedly. Selina blinked in surprise as the same thing happened to every other fulven, ropes appearing seemingly from nowhere to bind them, and pull them away into the darkness.

Selina looked around, wondering what new madness she’d found. But no more monsters appeared. Instead, a woman strode out from the shadows.

Selina’s jaw dropped as she noticed tiny horns on the newcomer, and for an instant she was sure that she’d found another Nator. The truth was even more surprising.

The horned woman had a bow. When she came closer, and Selina’s night vision revealed her face, she nearly fainted.

“Cara?” she whispered disbelievingly.
