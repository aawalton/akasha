It had been a good long while since the last time Viktor visited the Adventurer’s Guild.

Yes, he hadn’t returned to this place ever since the day misfortune befell Claire. After all, what was the point? The person he used to bring lunch to wasn’t here to receive it anymore. So, he stayed away. For the first week after the incident, he had spent most of his time in the dungeon, and after that, in the Sanctuary.

Now, though. Now there was a reason to come back.

Information, of course.

The Guild was, once again, in an uproar. The discovery of the Folded Chamber had ensured as much. The missing pyromancers had been found, details of their grim fate revealed to the public. Everyone now knew about the masked men, shady figures who had been moving through the shadows near the town, kidnapping people with brazen audacity. And, of course, the monsters. The chamber was littered with their corpses, which more than a few adventurers had looked at and immediately recognized as the same creatures that had attacked Daelin’s outpost.

He had been staying at the Sanctuary, and yet he was not entirely cut off from the news, as Rhea brought it back with her every evening when she returned from work, sharing the day’s developments over dinner with the others. And so, she and Jeanne and Alycia had all sorts of speculations and theories about what was happening. He, on the other hand, just listened to their discussion without offering any input of his own.

His plan was to let the Guild do the investigation for him. A couple of days had passed, so it was time to check if they had found anything worthwhile. He held no illusions that they would simply walk up to him and lay out their findings in full detail, of course. But even a hint of something would be enough for a start. He could always send his sometimes-shiny, sometimes-invisible spy to fill in whatever remained.

He had told Rhea and the others that he would go to the Guild this morning. He gave them no explanation, but he didn’t have to. Because they could come up with one themselves easily enough. If the masked men had a hand in the attack on the outpost, then they might be connected to the adventurers from Iskora. Naturally, he would take an interest.

Jeanne then said she would go with him, and Rhea suggested the three of them have lunch at the mess hall together. Someone still needed to stay behind in the Sanctuary to look after Claire while the rest of them were out, though, so his esteemed master would have to eat alone this noon. Oh, well. He would cook something nice for her tomorrow.

And so here they were. He and Jeanne, standing side by side before the main doors of the Adventurer’s Guild.

“It’s been a while, huh?” she said.

“Less than three weeks, actually.”

“You’re right. But somehow, I feel like a year has passed.”

Same here.

Yes, a great deal had happened, not just to him but to everyone as well. He turned to look at Jeanne. She, too, had gone through a lot of changes. That day, when he had finally returned to the Sanctuary after a week away, he had barely recognized her. She looked as if she had been wrung out and left to dry, her hair tangled like a bird’s nest, her skin drawn and dull. But she was considerably better now. Not fully her former self yet, but better. Healthier. She smiled more often, and when she did, her freckles seemed to come alive on her heart-shaped face. And her eyes—

“You have blue eyes,” Viktor said.

“What?” Jeanne arched a brow. “...Is that a problem?”

No, but... It was strange. He had never cared about other people’s eye color, yet for some reason, he had a feeling her eyes hadn’t been blue before.

“Are your eyes always blue?”

“What kind of question is that?” Jeanne laughed. “If I didn’t know you, I’d think that was a very weird way to flirt with someone. Anyway, I... don’t know. I’ve never really paid attention to it myself. But if they’re blue now, then they must have always been blue, right?”

Viktor shrugged. Oh, well. Whatever. Not that it was important anyway.

“Never mind,” he said. “Forget it.”

Together, they went inside. The main hall hadn’t changed in the slightest. Still packed with adventurers tracking the message boards and Guild employees working the reception desks, still thick with the kind of conversation that happened when people hadn’t quite learned the difference between talking and shouting. Viktor let his gaze drift over the noisy crowd until it settled on a warrior who looked vaguely familiar.

“Quinn, Jeanne,” the young man raised his hand in a wave the moment he spotted them.

“Cedric?” Viktor blinked in surprise. After half a year, the boy—if he could still be called that—looked very different. He was taller, and... “Are you growing a beard now?”

“Well, I’ve reached the age when this sort of thing happens,” Cedric said with a shrug. “And you know, I hadn’t been in a good mood over the past few months, so I didn’t bother to shave it. Now I’m back, and... I’m in a dilemma about what to do with it. Lucian says leave it as it is. Fiora wants me to keep it but trim it a little. My mother, on the other hand, demands it gone entirely.”

Viktor wondered what Noi’ri’s opinion would be. “Braid it, then adorn it with teeth of the monsters you killed,” probably.

“The good thing is you’re back. I take it this means you’re continuing as an adventurer.”

“Yes, I am. We are. All of us.” Then, something shifted in Cedric’s expression, and he looked down for a moment before bringing his gaze back up to meet Viktor’s once more. “By the way... I’ve heard about your sister. I’m really sorry that happened.”

“I am alright,” Viktor said, “and she will be alright.”

I can’t believe I’m starting to sound like Rhea, he thought. But in this case, it was fine, he supposed.

Cedric nodded. “Yes. We will save her. My mother is still searching for a more capable Emerald Mage. Someone with greater skill and experience. And I’ve also heard that Overseer Rennald is doing the same. They both know a lot of people, so I’m sure that eventually, they will find someone who can help your sister.”

Viktor was not so optimistic, not after what he had heard from Galatea. Still, these people were doing their best for Claire’s sake. The least he could do was not discourage them. So, he nodded along.

“My party and I are planning to visit her,” Cedric said, “but we’re rather occupied at the moment, investigating that place... Perhaps this weekend, if it’s convenient for you.”

Viktor arched a brow. “You mean the secret lair everyone is talking about? You’ve actually been there?”

“Yes. The Guild has been sending a lot of adventurers into the place. We’re among them.”

“What’s it like in there?” Jeanne asked.

“You wouldn’t believe it,” Cedric said. “We didn’t, until we actually stepped through ourselves. From the outside, it’s just a tree in the forest. Speak the right phrase, though, and a portal appears right there in that spot. And then, on the other side, a whole new world opens before you.”

The young man began describing it: the vast expanse of water, the two islands, the corpses of the shark-men drifting across the surface, the crumbling building half-swallowed by vines. Of course, Viktor already knew all of it. He had seen everything with his own eyes, after all. In fact, he was personally responsible for much of how things looked right now.

“It reminded me of the third floor of the dungeon,” Cedric added. “It’s called a water biome, I think. I can’t believe that someone managed to recreate such a thing outside the dungeon.”

“This is some sort of spatial manipulation magic, right?” Jeanne asked.

“Yes.” Cedric nodded. “Which is why the Guild has brought Azran in to help with the investigation.”

“Azran?” Viktor asked, amused. “He actually agreed to take part in something like this?”

“Ah, right. The last time, when the Guild needed information about the portal those people from Iskora built, it was you who went to him on their behalf to ask for his help. I’ve heard about that from my mother. This time, with you not around, she was at a loss, since he was known to be difficult to work with. In the end, I volunteered to go and talk with him.”

“What did he say when he saw you?”

“He just snorted. ‘You still alive?’ That was all. Then he went back to his drink.”

Viktor chuckled to himself. The bald man had been visibly concerned during Cedric’s long absence, but now when the boy walked back through the door, he acted like he didn’t care at all.

Oh, well. Whatever.

He turned back to Cedric. “But he agreed to help in the end?”

“Yes. He said since he didn’t have anything particularly pressing at the moment, he didn’t mind lending a hand, provided the pay was good.”

Does the guy still ferry Pimm back and forth between here and Iskora? Viktor thought. He hadn’t checked on Pimm in a while. After all, he now had more than enough fire and wind gems. So long as that remained true, he didn’t really care what that man was doing. He would just let Celeste handle it.

“So,” he asked. “Did anything strike you as unusual when you were examining the stone building?”

“Well, we—” Cedric stopped mid-sentence, his attention suddenly on something over Viktor’s shoulder. “Chief Secretary!”

He and Jeanne followed his gaze, and sure enough, there was Calyssa, stepping out through the door at the back of the hall. When her eyes found them, she came over without breaking stride.

“Quinn,” she said. “You came.” A pause. “How’s Claire?”

“She’s... the same.”

“I see.” The bespectacled woman let out a sigh. “I suppose you’re here because of the monsters.”

Jeanne nodded. “Is it true? That they were the same ones that attacked our outpost?”

“Yes.”

“Then that means—”

“Let me be clear first. We have no proof that Iskora is behind any of this. So we cannot connect these masked individuals to the adventurers from Iskora based on this alone.”

“Quinn was just asking me whether we’d found anything yet in the building,” Cedric said. Then, he lowered his voice. “Can I tell him about that?”

Oh?

Calyssa frowned. She remained silent for several seconds, then she slowly nodded.

“Fine,” she said. “But I need both of you to promise me that you won’t speak of this to anyone. Anyone. Even many employees of the Guild, including Rhea, don’t know this yet, and there’s a reason for that.” She gazed at Viktor and Jeanne from behind her glasses. “If this information leaks, it could spark a war in the town immediately.”
