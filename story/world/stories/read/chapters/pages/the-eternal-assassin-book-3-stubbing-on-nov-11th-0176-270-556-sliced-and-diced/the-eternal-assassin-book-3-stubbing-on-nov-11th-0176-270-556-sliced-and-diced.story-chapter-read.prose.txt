Asher kneeled on the floor, his mind reeling as the sound of blood rushing through his ears lessened and his vision slowly returned to him. His consciousness flickered and grew, like a nearly extinguished fire someone had hastily thrown another log onto and stoked back to life. Slowly, sensation spread back down his arms and legs from his chest, every beat of his rapidly strengthening heart forcing feeling back into his extremities and reminding him that he was still alive. Unable to stop himself, Asher laughed. He laughed harder than he’d ever had in his life, pushing himself to his feet as he laughed like a man possessed, barely able to believe what had happened.

Complete Resilience had finally lived up to its name. He wasn’t resisting the Death Aura. He wasn’t purging the Death Aura.

He was completely and utterly immune to it.

“Impossible,” the King of Death said, the putrid brown fires burning in his eye sockets flaring as he stared from across the room. “My Death Aura… But complete elemental immunity is unachievable! Not even the scholars of Gostoria were able to accomplish such a feat!”

“Yeah, well, they’re all dead,” Asher said, taking in a deep breath and cracking his neck as he remembered what it felt like to no longer be in possession of a body that was actively dying. His entire body was still in horrific shape, but he could feel Eternal Regeneration already working overtime in tandem with his Amulet of Improved Healing to patch him up. “Just like you’re about to be.”

“Cute,” the King of Death said coldly, taking a step back and raising his hands. “This certainly wasn’t my original plan, but I suppose I can repair the body once we’re done here.”

Not willing to let the lich do whatever the hell it was he was planning, Asher summoned a dagger to his hand and hurled it directly at the wizard, compressing space to shorten the distance and limit his time to dodge. Asher’s aim was true and the dagger spun straight over the giant table in the center of the room, directly on track for the lich’s burning eyes.

Only to be caught as a giant, undead hand shot up from the table, snatching the dagger out of the air.

Asher cursed as the undead body that had been specially prepared for him slowly turned and shifted its feet off the table. Dropping the caught dagger, the corpse snatched up the large soulsteel blade lying beside it, the metal screeching as it was dragged unceremoniously along the table. Getting to its feet, the corpse lurched off the table, its bones popping and forcing its body into position as it straightened up. Even with the top of its head missing just above the eyebrows, no doubt in preparation for having his brain placed within, the corpse towered over Asher by nearly three feet. Its steel armor shone in the crimson light of the gemstones lighting up the workshop, and the brilliant white spikes of bone jutting out here and there already looked soaked with blood.

“I am a master of flesh and bone!” the King of Death shouted at him from across the room, not sounding the least bit worried as to who was going to come out on top. “You may be able to resist my Death Aura, but I am not above getting my hands dirty doing things the old-fashioned way.”

“And I’m not planning on fighting fair,” Asher muttered, attempting to slip into the astral and run straight through the hulkish meat puppet before him. But the moment he tried, the crimson gems lining the walls flashed a brilliant white, and Asher gasped as he felt himself ripped right back to the material realm. It was almost the identical sensation he felt when he’d tried to Recall back to his team earlier. As if his skill was being undone the split second after he activated it.

“Did you really think I wouldn’t lock down travel between realms within the heart of my own workshop knowing you were coming?” the lich scoffed. At the same time, the undead puppet tried to take advantage of Asher’s surprise, lunging forward with astonishing speed and swingingly wildly with its sword in an attempt to cleave him in two. Eyes widening at the unexpected agility of the recently animated corpse, Asher barely managed to make the swing fall short with a quick burst of Distortion, expanding space just enough that the tip of the soulsteel sword merely scraped a line across his chest armor rather than cut him in half.

He’s fast! Going to leap back with another use of Distortion to put space between them, Asher blinked as the muscles in his calves locked up and he fell to the ground. Before he could even figure out what had happened, the puppet capitalized on his position, jerking its sword down toward his chest.

Asher blocked the strike at the last possible second with a miniaturized Spatial Lock, gasping at the chunk of energy that was ripped out of him. Thankfully, despite the puppet’s impressive muscles and speed, it didn’t actually hit nearly as hard as he’d expected it to while being controlled by the lich. Of course, the soulsteel sword was sharp enough that if it hit him, it wouldn’t matter how much force was behind the blade. If it made contact, it would cleave through his flesh and bone as though he were made of butter.

Keeping that in mind, Asher took advantage of the undead monster’s blade bouncing off his Spatial Lock, reaching out from the ground and slicing straight through the monster’s neck with an elongated Crescent. He grinned as the corpse’s head fell to the ground, but his elation was short-lived. Unlike every other undead he’d fought up until now, rather than crumble to the floor, the corpse ignored losing its head completely as it kicked out at him, its powerful, steel-clad foot connecting directly with Asher’s chest.

Asher gasped as his ribs cracked and the wind was knocked out of him, and he was sent hurling back into the wall of the lich’s workroom. He hit the solid stone wall hard and the world went temporarily black as his consciousness flickered from the impact. He barely managed to stay on his feet, and by the time he managed to focus his eyes once more, they widened as they spotted the corpse’s soulsteel blade coming right for him.

Asher compressed space and threw himself to the side with Distortion, barely avoiding getting skewered as the headless corpse speared the wall with its sword right where his chest had been a second prior. Seeing his opening, Asher went to lunge toward the corpse before it could pull its sword out of the wall, but stumbled and fell yet again when the muscles in his legs locked up for the second time.

The King of Death’s Flesh element! Asher started, suddenly realizing what was happening. He’d assumed most of the lich’s elements were focused around creating and commanding his undead minions, but it made sense that he would have at least a few random other skills here or there to help him out. Currently, the lich’s Death element was useless, and his Life element undoubtedly wasn’t all that great for combat, which only left Flesh. A skill to lock up an opponent’s muscles and keep them from moving was definitely annoying, but not something Asher couldn’t deal with.

Flexing Complete Resilience’s purge effect, Asher ignored both the searing pain in his chest from his shattered ribs and his near-empty energy reserves as he leapt up and lashed out with Distortion, slicing straight through the corpse’s thick arm with Crescent. The masterwork mithral blade didn’t disappoint, cutting through thick cords of flesh and steel armor alike as easily as if they were paper.

Abandoning the soulsteel sword lodged deep within the workshop wall, the corpse snatched its own falling arm before it could hit the ground, turning and swinging it like a makeshift club directly at Asher. The move caught him by surprise, and Asher instinctively tried to flicker through the astral, only for his skill to fail yet again from whatever preparations the King of Death had made. Asher barely managed to bring up his own arms to block before the end of the arm slammed into him, sending him hurtling back again even as the sharpened bone spikes tore through his forearms.

Hitting the ground hard, Asher rolled with the momentum, coming up as fast as he could in order to keep the corpse within his sight. The move saved his life, as he righted himself just in time to witness his own spilled blood gather in the air, transforming into a dozen spearheads that rocketed directly at him.

Of course, wizards can use their tritessence to mimic other skills they’ve learned, Asher thought, flinching with each spearhead that blasted into his hastily erected Spatial Lock. I’m not surprised the undead-obsessed lich would have some Blood element skills available.

As the final spearhead crashed fruitlessly against his skill, Asher staggered and dropped to a knee as his Spatial Lock flickered and went out. He’d fought as best he could, but he’d been running low on energy before the start of this fight, and was now just about empty. Seeing the corpse rushing him yet again, still holding its severed arm in its good hand, Asher grimaced and used the final sliver of his energy to reach into his Personal Rift.

Bringing out the tritessence crystal shard he and the other realm wanderers had retrieved from the wild realm.

Shattering the crystal within his grip, Asher breathed in the pure tritessence that was released. He grinned as the energy was sucked directly into his soul, replenishing his reserves in an instant. The small crystal had only held enough tritessence to refill his energy by about a quarter, but that was a serious improvement over having none at all. As the hulking corpse reached him and swung its own severed arm at Asher yet again, Asher used Distortion to dart toward the monster, slipping within its reach as he lashed out with an elongated Crescent. The mithral longsword cleaved straight through the corpse’s waist, severing the undead abomination in two.

Which did absolutely nothing to stop the still-standing legs from lashing out and kicking him in the side, cracking something in his hip and sending him tumbling across the workshop floor once more.

Asher barely managed to hold onto his dagger as he rolled, and he gasped as his vision flickered black from the unexpected pain. Having an opponent who could shrug off being decapitated or its body being sliced in half was a new experience for him, and not one he enjoyed all that much if he were being honest. That was the third time now the corpse had sent him flying across the room, and Asher was already rather sick of the experience.

Forcing himself back to his feet, Asher nearly blacked out as his cracked hip and damaged ribs suddenly snapped completely, at least a half-dozen minor breaks in his bones fracturing all at once. One look at the grinning lich still puppeteering the corpse from across the room was all the confirmation Asher needed as to what had just happened, and he gritted his teeth as he realized the wizard had just used a Bone element skill of some kind to make his injuries worse.

He didn’t have too long to dwell on that, however, as his two opponents were coming right for him.

The corpse’s legs ran all by themselves on the right, sprinting right for Asher as though the lack of an upper body was no big deal. Immediately beside them was the corpse’s one-armed, headless torso, using its impressive muscles to drag and hurl itself toward him with a scrabbling of fingers on stone.

I can’t keep fighting like this, Asher realized, trying to put weight on his left leg and grimacing as his shattered hip refused to support him at all. As it was, he could barely breath through the bone soup that was his destroyed ribcage floating around within his chest. He could cut the corpse into a dozen pieces, and the lich would continue puppeting them all, occasionally throwing out the random skill to make him suffer. No, if he wanted to win this fight, there was only one thing he could do.

Preparing himself for a world of pain, Asher held up his daggers and shifted into position for his reckless plan. The two halves of the giant corpse reached him at the same time, lashing out just as he’d expected they would. The torso threw a powerful left hook aimed at his own chest, which Asher forced to fall short with Distortion by expanding the space between its fist and his body. Of course, that meant there was nothing to block the powerful kick from the solitary legs, which lashed out like they were kicking a soccer ball, smashing into his already heavily damaged side just as he’d predicted they would. Rather than even try to dodge, Asher simply shielded himself as best he could with his daggers, ignoring the sensation of one of his arms breaking as he moved with the kick and allowed the blow to send him hurtling across the room for a fourth time.

The lich had proven himself predictable, and even if the move dealt him some serious damage, Asher was more than willing to accept it in order to get himself into position. On top of thoroughly breaking an arm, the blow had finally stripped him of sensation in his lower body as it snapped a part of his spine, but that was okay.

Because more importantly, as Asher flew across the workshop, bouncing off the central stone slab and ragdolling across the ground, he realized his aim had been true.

He’d landed a mere fifteen feet away from the King of Death.

Through nothing but sheer willpower, Asher kept himself from blacking out from the horrendous pain, his eyes locked directly onto his target. The King of Death may have been a genius creator and skilled beyond belief when it came to corpses, but he clearly didn’t have much if any actual combat experience himself. He had been so focused on beating Asher into submission that he realized far too late it didn’t matter if Asher was little more than a bag of blood and broken bones bleeding out all over the floor.

Asher still had one last functional limb and plenty of daggers, and that was good enough for him.

With a roar, Asher created one final tunnel of compressed space stretching from him to the recoiling lich, summoning a dagger from his rift and lashing out with his still-functioning arm.

Driving the masterwork iron dagger straight through the King of Death’s neck and severing his head from his body.
