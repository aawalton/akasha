Chapter 113

The wolf came for him fast.

Thorne's sword was already coming across when it lunged, a clean sweep that would have opened the animal at the shoulder if the animal had been where it started. It wasn't. It leaned into the leap and turned in the air, faster than any Level One had ever turned, and the blade passed under its belly and bit only ground. Thorne felt the miss all the way up his arm. He tucked as the wolf came down over him, dropped his shoulder into the grass, and rolled clear of the body, and when he came up onto one knee the wolf had already landed and was already turning.

It did not pause. It did not measure. It came at him again with the ground still shifting under its paws.

He got his feet back under him with the sword low and his weight forward. He had time for one thought, no more, and the thought was the one he had spoken to Zelgra at the tree line. This one would not lunge the way the others did. It would be where he was going to be, not where he was.

**The wolf circled to his left, low, its shoulders working under the thick fur in a rolling gait that kept its weight centered. It did not rush. A Level One would have rushed. A Level One did not know how to hold the distance between itself and the thing it wanted to kill, and that ignorance was the weakness Thorne had used to kill three of them. This one held it. It kept a body's length of open ground between them and moved along the edge of it the way a man with a blade moves along the edge of his reach, testing, watching for the weight shift that would tell it which way Thorne would go.

Thorne kept it guessing. He kept his feet even and his sword low and turned with the animal, and the two of them made a slow quarter-circle in the flattened grass while the growl went on. He had circled wolves before. None of them had circled him back. The birds on this side of the cut had gone silent. On the far side they had not. The imbalance told him where the second wolf was without looking, and he did not look, because the one in front of him was waiting for exactly that.

On the far side of the cut a different sound.

Zelgra had her hammer in both hands by then. Thorne could not see her, but he could hear the second wolf's step change as it turned off him and put itself onto her, the quick shift of a big animal picking a new line. The growl went with it. Then the second wolf leapt.

It went for her throat, claws bared out to the sides. She saw it come. She let it come.

She turned into it, not away, and the hammer went with her, the long haft sweeping the full circle her body was making, and the head of it caught the wolf at the temple in the middle of the air. The sound was flat and short and very final. The wolf did not so much land as fall, its shape gone slack before it reached the ground, and it hit in a heap and lay there twitching, one hind leg kicking against nothing, alive still but not for much longer.

Thorne did not see it happen.

He was still with the first wolf.

It came at him again more aggressive than the first pass, the growl with a wet edge to it now, and Thorne read the leap coming and stepped to move outside its line. He had done this move a hundred times. It had always worked. This time the wolf came into the leap wider than it had come the first time, its forelegs spread far off its body with the claws bared to the sides, and the reach of it caught Thorne where he was moving instead of where he had been. One set of claws hooked at his side and took him off his feet and flat onto his back. The sword went out of his hand.

He knew where it had gone. He had felt it leave his grip. Somewhere to his left, close, in the grass.

The wolf landed past him. It came down on his far side, and for a breath there was open ground between him and it, and then the animal turned. It was between him and his sword now. It lowered its head and drew its lips back off its teeth and the growl was continuous and low.

Thorne was still and let his breath come down. The wolf was close enough that he could see the blood on its claws, his blood, a thin dark line down the inside curve of each one.

The wolf held.

Thorne showed it nothing. He lay in wait and thought about the distance. Three paces to the sword. Two to the wolf. The math was bad and the math would not change. His side burned where the claws had caught him and he could feel the warmth going down his ribs, not deep, but enough that the wolf's nostrils were working. It smelled the blood. Its growl changed. The muscles in its haunches gathered and its head dropped an inch lower and Thorne understood that the breath he had was the last one he was going to get.

Thorne pushed up on his elbow and reached across his body for the sword.

Something moved above him. He felt it before he saw it, a shadow that cut across the light. He did not have time to look. He heard the sound.

Metal met bone.

The wolf's growl stopped. The heavy body dropped, one full drop that he felt through the ground under his palm, and then the animal was on the grass three paces from him whimpering, thin and confused, and one hind leg kicked once and then again.

Thorne did not move. He stared at the wolf and did not move.

Zelgra stood over him with the hammer held low.

"It is not dead," she said. "Kill it."

He looked at her. He blinked once. Her voice had not gone up. She was already stepping past him, watching the wolf on the ground the way she watched a customer's face for the moment the price landed.

He nodded. Once. He shook the stunned feeling off him the way he shook rain off him and pushed up onto his knee, reached across, found the sword in the grass where it had gone, and closed his hand on the hilt.

He crossed to the wolf and put the edge under its throat and finished it.

"And the other," Zelgra said.

He looked at her again. Her face did not soften. The second wolf lay a body's length behind her, still twitching, one leg still moving. He had not known.

He scrambled up, sword in hand, and crossed to it, and brought the blade down across its neck in one clean cut, and the leg stopped.

His vision flickered and the HUD came in.

He stood over the second body and let his breath level out. The cut was quiet again. The birds had not come back yet.

"I figure you have to kill them," Zelgra said, "or it would be a waste?"

She had settled the hammer haft-down against her boot and stood the way she had stood at the ore table twelve years running, one hand loose on the shaft, waiting for him to price the transaction.

"Ah, yeah a waste." He was still starring at her. "There wouldn't be a chest."

"Do you think we could find a few more before we head back?"

He looked from her to the two bodies and then back to her. His breath was steady now, but his face was not.

"We should get these two back and processed. They are better fresh."

She considered the nearer body a moment, wide teeth showing in something that was not quite a smile.

"So. Dog for supper." She lifted her chin at it. "Sounds terrible."

He did not answer that.

The chest formed in the air between the two bodies, the familiar shimmer of it settling into a solid shape on the flattened grass. Thorne walked to it. He knelt and put both hands on the lid and opened it, and the HUD slid back in.

❖ Drop Available: 2 Day Building Speed Up

Two items rose into his palms. He closed his hands around them and stood, one in each hand, and turned back to her.

"What are these?" Zelgra asked.

"Another step closer to winning the coming war."
