Chapter 21

“The candidate will not come from us. This is by design. A guardian shaped by the order would guard the order. The Grid requires a guardian who guards the species.” - Codex of the Long Watch, Founding Tenet

They found a solicitor's office two streets from Temple Church. It was on the ground floor with windows on the street side. Donna cleared it in under a minute. Two rooms, a kitchenette, and a toilet that still flushed. Someone's working schedule sat preserved in a desk diary next to a cold cup of tea.

Arthur barricaded the door with a filing cabinet and sat down on the carpet, his body registering the respite with the same grateful collapse as the rooftop in Deptford. The Printworks had taken more out of him than he’d admitted to himself.

Donna drank water with her eyes closed and then passed the bottle to him. Steve sat with the guidebook on his knee but didn’t open it. Donna distributed the last of the food, protein bars and dried fruit from the corner shop.

"Archie." Arthur waited until the geometry rotated towards him. "The machines in the Printworks. The press didn't just have creatures nesting in it, the press was a creature. Metal fused with organic tissue. How?"

Archie was hovering near the ceiling in the low-power configuration he adopted when the party wasn't moving. At the question his geometry expanded.

"Ether does not mutate inorganic material in the biological sense. It transmutes it. When concentrated Ether flows through conductive materials, copper wiring, circuit boards, electromagnetic components. The molecular structure is rewritten over time."

"Rewritten into what?" Arthur asked.

"New material compositions with properties dependent on the original conductive medium, local Ether density, and duration of exposure."

Steve had started writing. "So the longer dead technology or materials sit in Ether, the more it changes. What's the progression?"

"Materials exposed to a Standard Zone for a few weeks will begin to harden and densify. Material in a Sanctum Zone develops novel properties within days depending on the Ether density. Material inside a Saturation Anomaly transforms fastest."

"So, the dungeon isn't just a combat zone. The transmuted machinery inside the Printworks, the conveyor belts, press frames and wiring, all of it is becoming materials that don't exist in normal metallurgy."

“And those materials are vessel-grade," Steve said. He'd stopped writing, his pencil hovering over the page. "Better base materials for binding. We made the Bullguard Shield from a bin lid. Imagine binding a core to a vessel made from transmuted steel that's been soaking in Sanctum-density Ether for weeks or months."

“The correct term is Saturation Anomaly, not dungeons.” Archie corrected.

“I doubt Saturation Anomaly will take off.” Steve said.

“So, higher-tier artifacts," Donna said, bring the conversation back on course. She'd been listening with her eyes closed. "Better materials, better kit. No magic swords waiting in treasure chests."

"Salvage, not loot," Steve said, wrote it in the guidebook and underlined it twice. "The post-System world doesn't have loot drops. It has transmuted industrial infrastructure that you extract with tools and knowledge. We could do with an engineer for that."

He looked at Arthur who was glancing at the Bullguard Shield leaning against the wall. A bin lid with a monster core that had saved his life four times. But it was essentially scrap metal underneath the Ether enhancement, and he'd known that since the first time a real impact tested the limits of the corrugated steel.

"When we find a dungeon that's been cooking longer than the Printworks," Arthur said, "we go in with tools as well as weapons."

"Lovely. Another reason to go into terrifying physics-breaking buildings." Donna sighed.

***

The party sat around for a while, grateful for the temporary lull. Finally, Donna decided that was enough faffing about.

"Right, allocation," Donna said, opening her eyes. "Six points each. Steve. Put two in Constitution."

"Two? Donna, that's—"

"You have a Constitution of seven and a Strength of six. A stiff breeze could knock you over. Two in Con."

"That is profoundly suboptimal. I need Acuity for—"

“You don’t get to do any magic shenanigans if you’re dead, Steve. Two in Con. Then you can put the other four wherever you want."

Steve looked at Arthur for support and found him studying the ceiling with sudden fascination.

"Fine," Steve said. "Two in Constitution."

"Good."

Arthur allocated first. Three in Strength and three in Constitution, using the same logic as before. He was the wall. Walls needed density and endurance. The restructuring hit in two waves of about a minute each and by the third he'd learned to breathe through it.

When it was done, his hands felt like they belonged to someone slightly denser. The grip strength was different and the sense of reserve beneath his ribs was deeper. He had always been strong, his profession required it but now he was close to crossing what was humanly possible.

Donna allocated with her usual efficiency, putting four in Dexterity, and two in Constitution. The restructurings ran through her in silence as she clenched her teeth and rode it through. She rolled her wrists afterward, mimicking a strike. Testing the new speed of the neural connection between thought and movement.

Then it was Steve’s turn, he closed his eyes and allocated. His restructurings took longer. The Acuity rewrites hitting harder than physical attribute changes. Each one was a recalibration of neural pathways, widening the Ether-processing channels in his brain. When he opened his eyes, they were watering, but his expression was the strained ecstasy of a man who could feel the bandwidth opening.

"Done," he said.

Donna looked at him. "Two in Constitution?"

"As promised."

Donna's eyes narrowed. Arthur had seen the look before; Steve was in trouble.

"Tell me the truth, Steve."

Steve held her gaze for approximately two seconds, which was approximately two seconds longer than most people managed. Then he deflated.

"One in Con. Five in Acuity."

"Steve!"

"I couldn't do two. It's too inefficient. I need the bandwidth. I'm so close to the threshold for actual combat spells, Donna. Every point matters. One in Constitution keeps me alive. The other five get me to fireballs."

"Fireballs."

"Thermodynamically mediated Ether excitation of ambient molecular..."

"You promised two."

“And I delivered half the promise. Which is still one more point in Constitution than I wanted to allocate.”

Donna stared at him for a very long time and then turned to Arthur.

"He's going to get himself killed."

"Probably," Arthur said.

"And when he does, I'm going to say I told you so."

"Also, probably."

Steve straightened. "When I throw my first fireball, you're both going to apologise."

"When you throw your first fireball," Arthur said, "I'll be standing well behind you."

***

The Templar history book was thick, dense, and exactly the kind of reference material that made Steve forget he was in a solicitor's office during an apocalypse. He'd been reading for over an hour, his pencil making occasional notes in the guidebook margin, when he stopped.

"William Marshal," he said.

Arthur looked up. Donna opened one eye.

"‘The boy king’s guardian.’ ‘Where the boy king’s guardian bears the weight.’ It's William Marshal." Steve was reading aloud from the book, his finger tracing the passage.

"Earl of Pembroke. He served five kings and made his name on the tournament circuit. John died in 1216, leaving a nine-year-old heir and amidst a civil war. Marshal took charge as regent, defeated the French invasion, reissued Magna Carta, and died in 1219. At his funeral the Archbishop called him—" he looked up. "The Greatest Knight who ever lived."

“"The nine-year-old heir was Henry III?" Donna asked.

"Quite, and Marshal’s effigy is in the Round at Temple Church. There are nine knight effigies there, stone figures lying in repose but Marshal's is the most famous.” Steve turned the book to a photograph: a knight in mail, one hand resting on the hilt of the sword at his side. "The riddle gives us a man and a place."

“Is there anything in there about the weight?” Arthur asked

"The book doesn't say definitively. He was keeping a child’s crown on his head while the country was in revolt and a French prince wanted the throne," Steve said, setting the book down. "That is one meaning. The node may mean something more literal."

"A man holding the kingdom together," Donna said. "That sounds like a protector."

"He was the state,” Steve nodded. "He took responsibility for a kingdom whose king could not yet rule. That is the weight I can explain."

"So, we're looking for Marshal's effigy specifically. Arthur does something there and then the node activates." Donna said.

"That's my reading," Steve said. "The riddle points us to the specific effigy in the specific church. That ought to be enough.”

"Fair enough. Let's get some sleep," he said. "We go in at dawn."

"Hang on. We've still got Techniques to try." Steve pleaded.

"We will, tomorrow," Arthur said.

Steve opened his mouth to protest before Arthur cut him off.

"Steve, I’m knackered. We cleared a dungeon, walked miles, and just put our bodies through six restructurings each. My joints feel like they've been packed with sand. We’ve been awake for twenty hours. You gasped during your third allocation, and your hands are still shaking."

Arthur leaned back against the wall. "Whatever they are, we try them when we’re fresh. When our heads are clear and our bodies aren't running on fumes. Not tonight."

Steve looked at his trembling fingers. The argument he'd been building collapsed.

"First thing in the morning," he said.

"First thing."

Steve nodded. He set the Templar book aside and opened the guidebook. Arthur saw him writing something long and elaborate and wondered when Steve had stopped taking notes and started keeping a record.
