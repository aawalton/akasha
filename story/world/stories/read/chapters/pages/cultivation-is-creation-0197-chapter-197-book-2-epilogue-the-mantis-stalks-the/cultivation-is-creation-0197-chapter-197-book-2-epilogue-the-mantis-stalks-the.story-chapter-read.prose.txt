There is always a type of silence that comes after a battle is over, a stillness that settles over everything as if the world itself needs a moment to process what has just happened before it can move forward again.

This was especially true of what remained of the shrine.

The stone platform was cracked and warped, showing exactly what kind of fight had taken place on it: deep furrows carved by the blood red division lines, sections crystallized into jade, patches melted to glass by the intense heat of Su Yue's flames, and the distinctive radial pattern left by stellar energy that had burned through the final moments of a Civilization Realm cultivator's existence.

Silence held over the space for a long stretch of time. And then, almost too small to notice, something moved.

A single speck of crimson, no larger than a grain of sand, pushed itself out of a narrow crack in the temple floor. While every other trace of the ancient spirit's essence had been drawn in and consumed by the Genesis Seed, this one minuscule fragment had somehow survived, shielded either by the angle of the stone above it or because it had already begun transforming itself into something more resilient the moment the stellar energy struck.

There was simply no way to know which one it was. Whatever the reason, it had made it through.

The blood droplet trembled in the air, as if it was taking stock of what it had left to work with, and then with an agonizing slowness it began to rise, lurching forward a few inches at a time, drifting sideways, occasionally dropping several feet before catching itself again, each motion costing it something it couldn’t easily afford.

But it kept going, gradually gaining enough height to orient itself away from both the ruined temple and the distant village where the Azure Peak disciples had retreated, and then it began making its way across the mountain range with stubborn persistence that had survived total destruction.

Hours passed while the speck moved through the wilderness, and occasionally it would drop down into the shelter of trees or rocks when its energy got too low to sustain the flight, drawing what thin nourishment it could from the ambient qi of the mountain before continuing on.

When it finally reached a secluded clearing several miles from the shrine, the droplet descended to hover just above the moss-covered ground and then pulsed once, twice, and expanded rapidly, stretching and shifting until it had taken on a vaguely humanoid shape.

The red substance twisting and writhing and releasing wisps of crimson vapor into the night air as features slowly formed out of it, first a torso, then trembling limbs, and finally a face contorted with the effort of what it was doing.

It was Ke Jun, he collapsed to his knees.

His semi-transparent form flickered like a flame that a breeze keeps almost extinguishing.

The once imposing figure that had floated above the shrine platform with complete confidence was now hunched and fragile, the edges of his body blurring and reforming in endless cycles, and the blood red eyes that had looked at his descendant with possessive certainty now struggled to maintain their glow, dimming and brightening in irregular patterns that made it clear how little was left.

"A humiliating setback," he rasped, his voice barely above a whisper. "Centuries of planning undone by a child."

He looked down at his own trembling hands and watched his fingers occasionally lose their definition and merge into a shapeless mass before painfully pulling themselves back into recognizable form.

The truth of what was happening wasn’t something he could avoid looking at because the Divine Spirit Reclamation technique that had preserved him through centuries was failing, and without a proper vessel to anchor his consciousness, his essence was slowly dissipating into nothing.

He turned his gaze back in the direction he had come from, and his expression held fury and something that was reluctantly close to admiration mixed together sat uncomfortably on his face.

"To think a descendant of mine would have that kind of talent," he murmured. "The World Tree Sutra and stellar energy at the Qi Condensation stage." He shook his head slowly. "That kind of potential is rare even across millennia."

A bitter sound that was almost a laugh came out of him.

"Perhaps I chose my vessel too well," he said, his shoulders dropping slightly. "The qualities that made him ideal, his spiritual sensitivity and his unusual constitution, were exactly what allowed him to defeat me."

He tried to stand up, and his legs lost cohesion beneath him and he went back down to his knees.

"It would be foolish to pursue that body again," he admitted to himself, and he seemed to be working through something difficult as he said it. "I recognize the signs, I have seen talents like his before, back in my own youth, and the ones who had that quality became cultivators who surpassed even the Civilization Realm."

His expression darkened as old memories moved through him.

"The World Tree Sutra," he whispered, and there was fear threaded through his voice now. "Of all the cultivation methods, why that one? That boy is walking a path that ends in one of only two ways, either the technique eventually consumes him, or he ascends to heights that only one person before him has ever reached."

A shiver moved through his unstable form.

"No, it would be better for me to avoid that descendant," Ke Jun said, nodding to himself as if confirming a decision he had just finished making. "The next time we meet, if we meet, he will have techniques that go well beyond stellar energy, and I..." he looked down at himself, "I am in no condition to challenge what he is going to become."

He refocused on recovery and his form stabilized a small amount as he drew ambient qi into himself. The outline of his body became marginally more defined than it had been, and while it was going to take years to regain even a fraction of what he had lost, that was not a problem for a consciousness that had already waited centuries. He would simply find a more suitable descendant, a talented one but not so monstrously so, and he would begin again with more caution.

He was still working through what that plan might look like when he froze.

A pressure in the air, subtle but unmistakable to someone who had spent as long as he had at the highest levels of cultivation, the distortion of natural qi flows that indicated something was happening nearby that shouldn’t be happening.

Four figures materialized around him at the cardinal points of a perfect square before he had any chance to react to their presence, and in his injured state he hadn’t felt them approaching at all until they were already in position.

Three of them radiated power at the peak of Qi Condensation, which under normal circumstances wouldn’t have been something Ke Jun needed to concern himself with, but the fourth figure was something else.

Ke Jun’s essence ran cold the moment he assessed what he was actually dealing with, because the masked individual standing before him wore robes of absolute black that seemed to absorb the light around them, and the aura coming off this person was Stellar Realm without any question.

In his prime Ke Jun wouldn’t have given this person a second thought, but in his current state, the gap between them was not crossable and he was experienced enough to know it immediately.

He arranged his expression into something pleasant anyway, relaxing his posture to appear less threatening than he felt, because centuries of operating at the highest levels of political and cultivation society had left him with resources that went beyond raw power.

"Well, well," he said, his eyes moving between his four visitors while his mind was already running through every possible route out of the clearing and calculating the viability of each one. "To what do I owe the pleasure of such distinguished company? I do not believe we have been formally introduced."

The masked figure tilted its head at an angle that was slightly wrong for a human neck, and the other three remained still with their faces hidden inside their hoods. When no response came, Ke Jun kept talking because talking was something he could do while he gathered what little energy remained to him.

"Silent types, I see, perhaps you are here by coincidence, this forest has excellent spiritual energy for cultivation this time of year, I was simply passing through myself when I stopped to rest..."

He launched himself toward the gap between two of the figures the moment he had gathered enough for a single desperate burst of speed, his body transforming into a crimson streak that flowed through the air toward the narrow opening between them.

He hit an invisible barrier, and the impact sent him reeling backward with his already unstable form rippling dangerously from the collision. Waves of pain moved through what remained of him as the reality of his situation became clear.

A formation, and not a simple one either. The four cultivators had established a complex containment structure the moment he had materialized in the clearing, and he hadn’t felt it because he hadn’t been in any condition to feel something that subtle.

Ke Jun turned slowly to face the masked figure.

"Well," he said, forcing a rueful smile onto his semi-transparent face as he straightened his robes, "cultivators these days really are opportunistic, I suppose we should discuss terms."

The masked figure raised one hand without saying anything and Ke Jun felt space beginning to warp around him and the false confidence he had been wearing dropped away.

***

The Masked One stood at the center of the formation with his breathing labored beneath the crystal mask.

At his feet knelt what remained of Ke Jun, the once mighty Civilization Realm cultivator reduced to something that flickered between being barely solid and being almost entirely mist, with one arm missing and the stump leaking crimson essence that evaporated the moment it touched the air around it.

"Remarkable," the Masked One said quietly. "Even after being nearly destroyed by Stellar Realm energy, you still managed to put up that much resistance, the Civilization Realm truly does produce fascinating specimens."

Ke Jun said nothing, because the energy he had managed to preserve after the battle with his descendant had been completely exhausted in this second fight, he had fought with the desperation of something that knows it is cornered and has nothing left to lose but it hadn’t mattered in the slightest.

The Masked One made an elaborate gesture with his right hand, drawing complex symbols in the air, and when the final sigil was completed, reality tore open beside him, forming a portal of absolute darkness ringed with what looked like shards of broken crystal.

"You have the honor of joining my collection," he told Ke Jun. "The first expert of such a high caliber that I have managed to acquire, your knowledge of ancient cultivation methods alone makes you extremely valuable."

Tendrils of darkness extended from the portal and wrapped around Ke Jun's kneeling form, and the former Civilization Realm cultivator made one last feeble attempt to pull himself free, his remaining hand clawing at the moss-covered ground as the tendrils dragged him toward the void.

"You will regret this," Ke Jun managed, his voice barely audible anymore. "My descendant will…"

"Yes, yes," the Masked One said, and he actually sounded bored about it. "The protagonist will avenge you and defeat me and save the world and so forth, I have heard all of this before." He leaned closer to the flickering figure. "But between you and me, I have collected quite a few protagonists in my time, and your descendant will simply be one more to add to the shelf."

The tendrils finished their work and Ke Jun disappeared into the portal.

The tear in reality sealed itself, leaving no trace of the ancient cultivator behind at all.

A brief silence followed before the three other figures moved toward the Masked One, their forms wavering slightly at the edges as if maintaining their current state required continuous effort.

The young man among them, who had proud features sitting above eyes that were hollow of anything that should have been behind them, spoke first.

"Master, shall we pursue the other one as well?" Li Yuan asked. "The Azure Peak disciple who used stellar energy?"

The Masked One turned slowly and looked in the direction where Ke Yin and the other survivors had gone, and for a long moment he seemed to be reading something invisible in the air with his fingers moving in small and precise gestures.

"An intriguing prospect," he said finally. "That one shows significant narrative potential."

He looked at his three servants, Li Yuan and Mo Qingyin and Mo Tian, and what he saw in them wasn’t encouraging because all three were showing signs of severe stress, their forms less stable than they had been even a few days before with the edges of their bodies occasionally blurring and spiritual energy leaking from poorly sealed cracks in their artificial vessels.

They would need maintenance before much longer or risk coming apart entirely.

He then looked at his own body, and the assessment there was also not what he would have preferred.

The fight with Ke Jun had been brief but it had not been without cost, and beneath his robes several stars had dimmed, and their light temporarily extinguished by the techniques Ke Jun had used in his final desperate moments.

More concerning than any of that was the crack in his mask, which had not come from today's fight at all but from his previous encounter with that troublesome sword spirit. A confrontation that had produced a strike which came through space itself without warning and hit him in the shoulder. It had not finished healing in the week since it happened, if anything it seemed to be spreading rather than closing.

"I have already underestimated a Qi Condensation cultivator once," the Masked One reflected, and there was bitterness in how he said it. "I did not account for the possibility that he was carrying an ancient expert capable of bypassing all of my defenses, and a single slash nearly severed my connection to this realm entirely."

He pulled his attention back to the present moment and the decision in front of him. "No," he said. "I will not make the same mistake again by moving too quickly, we will put him on the list and address him later when the situation is more favorable."

Before any of them could respond, a ripple passed through all three of his servants simultaneously.

"It appears my ability to maintain your physical manifestations has reached its limit for now," the Masked One sighed. "Return to the collection, we will resume once proper repairs have been completed."

He watched as his servants began to come apart, their bodies breaking down into particles of light that flowed into him and would reform within his inner world in their designated chambers until he had rebuilt enough strength to reconstruct their vessels properly.

He had pushed them further than their current limitations comfortably allowed and if he had kept them manifested much longer, the bonds of control might have weakened enough to create complications he had no interest in dealing with on top of everything else.

Alone in the clearing now, the Masked One looked up at the night sky with his collection grown by one for the day even if the true prize was still somewhere out of reach.

"The Azure Peak Sect," he said to no one. "So many interesting threads converging in one location."

He opened a new portal, smaller and more stable than the one that had taken Ke Jun, and before stepping through it, he looked one last time in the direction of Azure Peak Sect and the people who had no idea he had been watching any of what happened today.

"Enjoy your victory while it lasts, little protagonist," he whispered. "Our story is only just beginning."

Then he was gone.
