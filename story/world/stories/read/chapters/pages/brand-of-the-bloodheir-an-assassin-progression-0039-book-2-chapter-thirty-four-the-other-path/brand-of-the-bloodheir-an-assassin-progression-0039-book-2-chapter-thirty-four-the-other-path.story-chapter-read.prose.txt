Free from the shackling burdens of time, Risens delved into piles of information he had removed from the secret vault of the Gilded Cage. He scanned the contents of each folder, digesting the scattered details before arranging them in the cabinets he’d found waiting for him inside the Barren.

The general finances of the Lady Myrenas’s estate and holdings, though substantial, were of little concern to him. Her payroll, her tax burden owed to the crown, the paid invoices for this resource or that were immaterial to his present cause.

Amid a myriad of nothingness, he nearly missed a distinctive entry. If not for the happenings of the last few days, he would have likely overlooked it in its entirety.

Corvus.

It was a line item he found repeated page after page. Payments of varying degrees were chronicled, all bearing the same name, with an utter lack of explanation. The Hunt, the assassins sent to see to his demise, had mentioned the Magus by name. Lady Myrenas had been purchasing something from the mysterious organization, though whether it was information or goods, there were no immediately identifiable clues.

After what was likely hours of intense scrutiny, he could discern nothing tangible from the financial documents, though more questions had arisen. It was with a muted sense of excitement that he turned his focus to another section of her vast trove.

He did not doubt that, given time, desire, and the intentionality that it required, he could find some impropriety to tie back to her name. But effort would serve little good for any involved. His purpose was not to sully her name. Hers was now a reputation so stained and putrid, even the swine wouldn’t risk stymying themselves in the mud of her farms. The Lady was dead, and Halthome was better for it. Her house and holdings were forfeit, though he alone now controlled the deeds to all of her assets.

At last, eyes sore, he placed the final page in the folder before fitting it carefully on the shelf and pulling the next. Beyond the banal runnings of a sizeable estate, Lady Myrenas had volumes of information, one for each of the major Dukes, Barons, and nobles that held any tangible influence throughout Halthome. He smiled as he quickly thumbed through the pages for Faltrun Dutchy. Aside from the numerous rumors of philandering, the fictitious character Lord Marken Cortinerie remained a plausible mystery. He was surprised that it had escaped her sight. Perhaps, being so far from the paved streets and the courts of Windwake, the dealings of the country nobles were a trivial concern. More likely, not a threat to her or her power and control.

His legs were cramped from sitting, and his back needed frequent stretching to undo the kinks that formed from remaining stagnant. His life was one of movement; frantic activity was regular, though now he fought off rigor. At present, he was grateful for the thick layer of calluses that covered his fingers and hands as they warded him from the minutely serrated edges of the parchment. If not for his toughened skin, he no doubt would have suffered from thousands of paper cuts.

Filing away the last of the seemingly innocuous folders—this one detailing the graphic, albeit pointless communications of her failed stud farm—he returned his focus to the map. The visual representation of the scattered houses and properties called to him, luring him to the one piece of property that time seemed to have forgotten. Or, as is more likely the case, that for whatever reason, Halthome had erased.

His careful scouring of the deeds had given it a name.

Myrenest.

Gauging the distance using the cartographer’s legend, he estimated that it was less than half a day’s travel from the King’s holiday estate at Pearlview. It was even closer to whatever remained of the village that it once existed in its shadow.

Risens’s mind was set. If he survived the return to the King, he would have considerable time to himself; thus, he resolved that seeing these locations was a task worth pursuing. Perhaps he would unearth whatever answers were buried there. His focus for the immediate future was set, though the course ahead curved away into uncertainty. All sights of what lay before him were obscured by the looming spires of the palatial complex and the wicked shadow and deadly reach of King Lathrenon.

His return to His Majesty would be required within a matter of days. There were loose ends to tie off. Undoubtedly at the point of the blade. The King’s healer, his lips loosened by the exuberant ministrations of the fairer sex, had divulged far too much. He needed to be silenced once and for all.

The attempts on Risens’s life had grown in startling frequency, enough that they could no longer be explained away as merely the hazards of the job.

Far more than at any time he could recall, the uncertainty of his own life weighed on him. He would return to the King with his message and the completion of the task that he was assigned. Lathrenon would judge the ultimate success or failure of the true purpose of the quest. Risens had no expectations that the King would raise his own hand against his Rightmaker. He would merely issue the order from the comfort of his padded throne.

Risens had neither pity nor remorse for whoever was given the task. Even before the application of the Brands that now scarred his chest, his skills had been formidable. The new powers had made him deadly beyond any previous experience or training could provide.

He was not without options, though of all, only one made sense. He could easily forego giving his report and ignore the summons that would follow. He could vanish into the bowels of Windwake before disappearing into the lands beyond, yet there was a greater pull that bound him here—a greater desire beyond the fact that running wasn’t in his nature.

He doubted that the voice, the true power that commanded his obedience, would allow for truancy. The King could send all the assassins under his command, and Risens would fend them off. Whatever organizations hunted him could post their killers within the city, spoiling the sanctity and secrecy of his haunts, yet he would defend himself still. His blades would cut down those sent to end his life.

However, as talented and skilled as he was, he had no means of protection against the voice that commanded him.

If it could Brand him and grant unheralded powers with only words and intent, he was sure that it could end his life just as easily.

As his studies of the documents progressed, it was quickly made clear that Lady Myrenas’s wealth had come at a distinct cost. A tangible human cost in suffering and pain so that she and her familial holdings would be further elevated. She had the means and motivation, and apparently no qualms, as she ran a complex scheme to defraud those who lacked the power and influence to counter her wealth.

Her practices were tainted. She leveraged every ounce of her power to muscle out anyone in her way, those who couldn’t afford the means to defend themselves against the might of her deep wallet. Her vaulted position on the council enforced the fact that few were foolish enough to cross her. She didn’t kill with blades, but she did so with the might of her purse.

Lady Myrenas manipulated the strings of magistrates and soldiers with dual allegiances. The angry red skin of the Brand of the Forked-Tongue likely marred the skin of each as they pledged their fealty to the crown while allowing their wallets to sway their true fidelities. Coins determined the selective enforcement of the rules that benefited her while punishing others for similar offenses.

Whether or not the King was complicit in the knowledge, it mattered little. Lathrenon’s power was not challenged by the Lady Myrenas or any of the other councilors or nobles. His pockets were far deeper than the sum of their wealths combined. The might of the armies at his command far eclipsed any forces that they could muster. He could crush them at his will as he had already displayed with brutal efficiency.

His assumed blindness to the financial schemes she and all the nobles played was likely strategic. The more they earned by stealing from and squabbling amongst themselves, the more tax revenue they paid to the Kingdom.

To him.

As Lathrenon oft said, “I am Halthome.”

Unkinking his back, he massaged the cramps from his legs. His vision traced anxiously across the room, eager to be free from the black script on yellowing parchment. The view of the pair of doors set into the wall on either side of the hearth triggered a sudden jolt of energy. He could feel the call of power emanating from the sealed panels. He yearned for another taste of hidden powers.

Questions of what dwelled deep within the Under set his mind ablaze. The knowledge that he’d abdicated from the Gilded Cage, in the correct situation, could be leveraged for power or influence. The skills that lurked behind the doors were tangible.

Deadly.

Working his way through some dried rations to settle the rumble in his stomach, he considered the options.

His purpose now was set on more alluring propositions than crafty nobles. The gaming and positioning of the courts would be better left to others. Word craft would not be the skill that extended his perilous life. His power was in force. In the blade.

And now in magic.

Before him stood options, each enthralling and terrifying in its own regard.

To a point, he understood the power and possibilities that lurked within the sealed doors of the Roost. There were skills, evolutions of the powers, and items he’d been granted, though none would come without trial and cost. Failure had meant pain in most cases. Though he was lucky to have survived the battle in the ravine, the Conspiracy of Ravens had nearly cost him his life.

The secrets of the Under were an entirely different quandary. He’d quenched the blades in the Tears of the Under, forging and sharpening them with the power against the black, disfigured demons he knew as the Carrion. What other creatures, dangers, and opportunities lurked among the unknown levels or chambers?

It was the overpowering lure filling his mind like a river that threatened to overflow its banks that determined his next action. The sense of urgency that surrounded silencing the loose-lipped healer was dimmed here in the Barren. Time froze here just like in the Under and the Roost. He would only press on so far. His mind demanded to know what was next, what hidden secrets lingered around each corner.

He couldn’t help but feel that the revelations here were far more consequential than the newly surfaced questions about his past or the mounting political strife that aimed to light Halthome ablaze.

The King’s missive, the attempts on his life, demanded answers, but for now, he would press on. He would take advantage of what powers he could glean before moving on with the next steps in the realm of Windwake.

Whatever they may be.
