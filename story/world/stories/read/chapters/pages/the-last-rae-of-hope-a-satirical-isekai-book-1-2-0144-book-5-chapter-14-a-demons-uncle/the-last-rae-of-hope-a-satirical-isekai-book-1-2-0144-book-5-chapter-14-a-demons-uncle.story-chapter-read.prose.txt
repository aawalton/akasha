Anhinga’s anguished screeches heralded our summons back to the main tea room. Galenus and Nora had already gathered, trading looks of suspicion and annoyance, while Ambrose leaned over a map of Speranza, fully engrossed in its detail.

“We already searched there!” Nora exclaimed. “Twice! There is nothing there anymore.”

Galenus adjusted the gauntlet upon his wrist, his right eye staring at it through his monocle. “There was nothing,” he corrected. “I strongly recommend we inspect the ruins again.”

“Ruins? What ruins?” I asked as Olethros stepped through the mirror portal behind me.

“Castle Epiales,” Nora replied. “You know, where we first—” She paused, her right upper lip curling. “He made you cry?”

“No, I made myself cry,” I clarified, waving a hand as I pointedly glanced at each of our hosts. “You know that haircuts make me emotional.”

She blinked, realizing the blatant lie wasn’t aimed directly at her, then mumbled, “Oh. Well, it looks just fine to me.”

Galenus had a quizzical look on his face, but he didn’t pry. Ambrose didn’t bother to look over. Instead, he leaned forward and pressed his palm to the map. It glowed crimson for a moment.

“Short, fleeting bursts of animus… weak, but present,” he declared as he straightened up. “A single origin point.”

“That could be anything,” Nora snapped, then frowned. “Even something important, I suppose.” Her eyes flashed as they settled on me. “Even though you’re nerfed, you’re going to insist, aren’t you?”

I pulled my arms behind me, trying to look as innocent as possible. “I have full confidence in our team to keep me safe.”

It wasn’t a lie. Putting up with a possessed human for three months straight? Sure, it might have been an act of necessity for some, but that’s only because I had something long-term to offer in return.

Nora narrowed her gaze. “Don’t go off looking for trouble, okay?”

I nodded. “Your investigation, your rules!”

“I’ll be sure to keep watch over her,” Olethros added, leaning in over my shoulder from behind.

Too close!

“Not hardly,” he murmured. “But to Nora’s point, we should aim to land a bit away from the origin point—observe first, then engage, but only if necessary.”

His point was well taken. Galenus’s portal dumped us slightly southwest of what was once Castle Epiales. It looked like even less than what it had been before—a flat, uninterrupted wasteland of the dustiest complexion.

“Sure looks like a whole lot of nothing…” I remarked, a bit sourly.

And then I looked up.

Suspended in the sky, a strange island floated.

It was encased in a shimmering, alternating barrier of pastel and darkness that bent the visible spectrum around it. Somewhere within that distortion, the familiar spire of what had once been Euphridia’s High Temple caught the sun.

Paradise.

“And here I thought he was being a bit figurative about bringing it to Speranza…” Nora muttered, suppressing her awe at the spectacle. “But at least it’s clear now that he literally looks down on everything here.”

I nodded. “Tacky is definitely on brand for—”

The ground in front of us detonated.

Dust and grit blasted upward in a giant, choking wave. I flinched even as shadows surged from behind to intercept. Additional impacts erupted against the defensive darkness, but Olethros held it steady until their aftershocks were absorbed.

He lowered his hand.

A large, smoldering crater had blown open about two hundred paces ahead. From its epicenter, an unsettled, ever-shifting shape rose, blurry and writhing with dark animus.

Wings?

No. Claws.

The form glitched several times violently, snapping into something vaguely humanoid just as a massive metal angel plunged down on it from the sky above.

Bodhil!

Ambrose summoned a skull-topped staff and tore a wave of stone up from the earth, shielding us from the worst of the fray. “I don’t believe it’s one of ours,” he said quietly to Galenus. “But I’ll need you to confirm.”

“The demon is not a citizen of the Maze,” Galenus agreed with a shake of his head.

Olethros released a displeased sigh, his tail lashing. “Regardless, we stand to gain by intervening.”

Another blast shook the earth even as I turned. “You know that demon out there fighting the angel, don’t you?”

His scowl deepened. “Yes… and,” he added sourly, “so do you.”

“I do? Well, maybe—”

Olethros didn’t wait for me to finish my weak approval.

He translocated to the top of the rock wall in a ripple of shadow, fired several probing blasts at the angel’s back, then snapped back to my side before their echoes finished ringing.

“No effect,” he muttered. “As expected of Ereborite.” He glanced toward Nora. “How refined is your application of amity?”

She flinched. “Healing only. Why?”

“I believe Ereborite would yield to an attack similar to what Rachel and I used on the portal to Paradise,” he said calmly. “And on Epiales.”

I knew it! He did help with that final strike!

His gaze flicked to me.

“Well, I’m game if—”

Nora immediately stepped in between us. “Don’t you two dare.”

I drew Will anyway, the blade sliding free with an excited, familiar hum. “Nora—please. Believe in me!”

“It’s not that I don't—”

A shrill, ragged scream tore across the field.

The demon was losing ground.

“Your animus barriers are your greatest strength, Lady Nora,” Olethros admitted. “Let us both give Rachel the edge she needs.” He frowned somewhat as he gestured at me. “You’re going to have to get in close—I don’t think a ranged attack will suffice.”

I nodded as Nora stared at him for a moment, then swore viciously.

“Fine,” she snapped. “Galenus! Be ready to pull her back the instant before this all goes wrong!”

I guess I couldn’t blame her for thinking it would.

Galenus straightened, then bowed.

Ambrose lifted his staff, the skull atop it igniting crimson. “I will drop the wall and draw the angel’s attention.”

I inhaled and reached for my aura. It answered, not very brightly, but it was there.

Olethros’s mark then flared, and dark violet light crawled up my sword, resonating through my arm into my chest.

Nora’s staff flashed, weaving a thin green sheen over it all.

I shifted into a runner’s stance. “Ready!”

“Rachel,” Olethros barked. “Now.”

The wall collapsed, and I sprinted forward.

Pebbles screeched past my ears as Ambrose’s distraction hit, with shards of stone and shadow pelted the angel’s flank. Unfortunately, it didn’t flinch or retaliate, wholly focused on the glitchy demon it had been assaulting. Even as I closed the distance, it never even turned.

Your mistake for ignoring me!

I leapt, bringing Will down in a two-handed arc. The strike landed on its left upper arm with a screeching impact that staggered it sideways. A thin, ugly black line hissed across its armor. The angel recoiled, then backhanded me away almost absently.

I skidded across the gritty ground, triumphant nonetheless.

I can do damage!

Unfortunately, the other demon did not see me as its ally. Its blast hit me from behind with enough force to rattle my teeth.

“Damn you, Chosen One! Damn you and Relias to the void eternal!” he shrieked.

Lieutenant Beigaldi?!

“I agree with the second half of that assertion,” I snapped as I stood up. “But for right now, let’s focus on that metal monstrosity!”

He swore at me in the manner of demons, but his hisses were tinged with confusion. Instead, I deliberately turned my back on him to emphasize my point and reset my stance, driving Will into another strike.

A second thin, shallow scar screeched across Bodhil’s armor, but she still didn’t see me as a threat. With a precise sidestep that left her wide open, she turned, firing a blast from the palm of her metal hand straight at Beigaldi.

What a one-track mind… Fine!

I’ll aim for the gem in her head.

The instant Will’s edge tapped the jewel, however, everything changed.

She caught my sword in her hand as the jewel flared from black to gold, a pulse spreading throughout her armor. Her head snapped towards me, her eyeless gaze still somehow menacing.

All I saw was a silver blur.

Crunch!

Green and gold starlight burst in front of my eyes, and warm fullness filled my nose.

It was the painful reality punch I needed.

No matter how confident I am, I have to realize my limits.

Flat on my back, I saw her leap into the air above me, a foot kicked out to end me on the downward impact.

Damn it…!

Not strong enough yet…

But that works on a similar principle… right?

“Galenus! Portal!” I shouted hoarsely. “Far away—for her!”

Vines tore open reality itself as she descended, spiraling into a wide, unstable tear that hummed with a blend of animus and amity.

“Bye-bye for now, bitch!”

It probably sounded less cool than in my head, because by this point, I was sure my face was too swollen for me to enunciate clearly.

The portal swallowed her whole and snapped shut with a thunderous whump.

Hah…!

I started to sit up.

“Time to die a second time!” Beigaldi screeched from behind.

He never got the chance to attack.

Olethros dropped in from a few feet above, slamming Beigaldi into the ground. Shadowy coils erupted outward, snapping around the lieutenant’s limbs and hauling them taut in all cardinal directions. Olethros then seized a fistful of Beigaldi’s hair and forced his head back.

“Final choice,” he said coldly. “Declare who is your rightful sovereign evermore, or pass on to Naught.”

Beigaldi thrashed as the shadows constricted. “I—uncle!” he screamed, his voice full of panic. “I yield! I yield!”

Silence fell over all of us as the two of them froze, Beigaldi's declaration sealed by a shared flash of animus. Even the pounding in my face paused, as if it, too, needed a moment to process.

“You—” I started, then dissolved into hysterical laughter, blood dripping straight into my mouth. “You literally cried, "uncle?!" And—shit—that’s entirely true, isn’t it? Olethros is your uncle!”

Olethros blinked.

“…How profoundly undignified,” he murmured, hauling Beigaldi upright before shoving him aside. “Next time, nephew, try not to embarrass me in front of my partner.”
