Night fell as the four of them ventured deeper into the Valley of Silence. The air was cool and dry, but thick with the smell of rotted roots and dead earth. Before they went to sleep, they took one last campfire break to have dinner.

They had carefully stocked enough food rations to last a three-month trip. Things like meat were preserved with ice magic inside a closed crate. Veronica found it reminiscent of the life she had lived during the apocalypse. The only difference this time was that she could have freshly cooked food.

Gwen worked as a waitress, and as it turned out, she knew how to cook.

With some rough, earth-raised stumps serving as seats, the four of them sat around the campfire, set alight on the ashen, dry soil. Although the soil itself wasn’t flammable, having two mages who could generate fire made that irrelevant.

“You’re a pretty good cook, you know that?” Nasha said.

Gwen blushed and chuckled nervously. “Thank you. I do a lot of cooking for my aunt back home.”

“Mm. You’ll make a good wife one day,” Nasha said, taking a bite of some perfectly seasoned beef on a stick.

“Yeah, if I can ever find anybody,” Gwen replied, sullen. “I just don’t like anybody near my age. I didn’t go to school in Ronswick for… reasons.”

Veronica knew she was talking about her ordeal with Lorence. Since Nasha had overheard their conversation back at the restaurant in Ronswick, she might have known too. Though, she wasn’t privy to all of the details.

“I wish I could find a dashing knight or some handsome noble willing to part ways with their family ties and date a commoner,” Gwen said wistfully.

“Sounds like someone reads a bit of Sheryl Hobbs.”

Gwen’s eyes lit up, pinning onto Nasha. “You know Sheryl?! I’m such a big fan of her books!”

Nasha shrugged. “I’ve read a few of her novels. My favorite one was The Maiden’s Reverie.”

“Oh! That’s a great one! What book are you on? I’m on…”

Veronica drowned out their voices as they continued talking about romance books. Strangely enough, Veronica had also heard the name before, though she wouldn’t have ever guessed she was an author, let alone a romance one. Perhaps she’d heard the name from one of the noble ladies back in Annesheim.

As Veronica ate in silence, she couldn’t help but stare over at Dusk. While he was sitting with them at the campfire, his legs were turned away, as if he didn’t want to partake or have anything to do with them.

She couldn’t help but pity the child. Had he truly lost something important to him? Losing something so important at such a young age… she’d seen how that could change people. How it could change oneself.

Nasha, mid-conversation, noticed the frown on Veronica’s face and followed her gaze.

“One moment,” Nasha said to Gwen.

She stood up and walked a few steps over to Dusk. Then she leaned down and offered him a thin metal cup of water.

“Would you like a drink?”

Dusk turned to look up at Nasha. For a few moments, he was silent. Seeing that Nasha didn’t turn away, he spoke.

“Why… are you giving this to me?”

“Because you look thirsty?” Nasha replied, almost like a question.

Again, he went silent for a few moments before eventually reaching out and taking the cup.

Gwen, who had been watching their interaction, came over. “Now, what do you say, Dusk?”

“Say?” he repeated.

Gwen crossed her arms, leaning forward. “Mhm. What I taught you this morning.”

“I…” he said, hesitant. “Th-thank you,” he said, looking down at the cup of water.

Gwen and Nasha smiled. Nasha went ahead and rubbed the top of Dusk’s head. “Don’t be afraid to ask for anything you need.”

Veronica watched as the three of them talked. Dusk was an odd one, but he didn’t seem like a bad kid. Distant and socially awkward, perhaps. Though maybe Veronica had been like that in the past, too.

Nevertheless, it was apparent that both Gwen and Nasha were good with children.

As everyone sat down again and took their seats, Veronica changed the subject. “So, Dusk. What should we expect the deeper we get into the valley?”

Dusk, whose legs were now aimed toward the campfire, making him seem more willing to engage, looked over at her. “The valley… the center has ruins called the Old Sanctuary. It was a small temple where worshippers came to pray to the Goddess Selene.”

“A temple this far away?” Veronica asked.

He nodded. “Before then, we will reach a small village slightly north of here. It was where the people stayed after prayers. But the village has been destroyed for a very long time. It’s mostly dust now.”

“How long until we reach the sanctuary?” Gwen asked.

Dusk looked down, thinking. “Maybe… one more week. We’ll see the village a few days before then.”

“A week, huh?” Veronica said. “It’ll be my birthday soon, then.”

Nasha looked over at her. “Oh? Your birthday was that close?”

“Mm. I’ll be turning twenty in about eight days.”

“You should have told us sooner,” Nasha said. “Could’ve given us time to prepare you a present.”

Veronica waved her off. “It’s fine. Birthdays aren’t that special. Besides, we’re in the Valley of Silence right now. We’ll probably be too busy fighting more of the carrion beasts to worry about my birthday.”

She turned back toward Dusk. “Tell us more about the village and the people who lived there.”

“The village… was made by worshippers of Selene over a thousand years ago. She was the Goddess of Salvation, who saved people with miracles. One person told another, and eventually a group of people came to the valley and created a village. After a while, they limited the number of people who could come. They lived there in secret, so no one outside the Valley knew there was a goddess there.”

“What happened to the Goddess?” Nasha asked. “Is she still there?”

Dusk frowned. “No… she isn’t. After a while, something happened to her, and she went missing. Then a sudden drought hit the village. All the farmer’s plants and vegetables died. There wasn’t a single survivor. Ever since the last villager died, not one person has been able to reach the village. It’s been closed off for a very long time. Hundreds of years, I think. Everything is mostly gone.”

“A drought, huh…” Veronica repeated, humming. “So something happened to the Goddess, and that caused the land to corrode?”

“Isn’t that odd, though? For a goddess to just disappear?” Nasha asked.

“It is odd,” Veronica said. “Gods don’t disappear that easily. Unless they are killed.”

Dusk remained silent. “Perhaps she did die.”

“Can… gods die?” Gwen asked, looking between Veronica and Nasha.

Nasha glanced over at Veronica. “You are part dark elf, right?”

Veronica hummed, nodding. She understood what Nasha was getting at. “There are different types of gods. The ones people generally think of are the Reverent Deities. Those are the all-powerful gods. Ones that control entire concepts, or hold unimaginable power. Things we can’t fathom. Bending fate, causing miracles, all of that.”

She brushed back the side of her hair, making her ears more prominent. “Most dark elves, me included, worship Nidhogg. He is the god that lives beneath the world tree, Yggdrasil. However, he isn’t a Reverent Deity.”

“A god that isn’t a god?” Gwen asked, slightly confused. “I’ve heard the term Reverent Deities before. There’s a temple in Ronswick where people go to pray to one of the many gods there. Though, I’ve never been there myself.”

“Those are divine temples. An acolyte usually manages those places and allows people to connect and converse with one of the Reverent Deities,” Veronica answered. “But there are other powerful beings that aren’t Reverent Deities. Nidhogg is one of them. He is what is known as a mythical beast.”

Gwen looked from her over to Dusk. “Then… is this Goddess Selene a mythical beast?”

Dusk remained silent.

“Not every non-Reverent Deity is a mythical beast. Many of them are actually closer to primordial spirits, wielding immense influence within their sphere, yet little beyond it. Nidhogg is extremely powerful, but he’s not as strong as an actual Reverent Deity that can bend fate.”

“Then I’m guessing these lesser gods are ones that can be killed?” Gwen asked.

Veronica nodded.

“Then that would explain why the Valley is the way it is now,” Nasha added. “If a god was here managing the land, then I could see it blooming with beautiful flowers.”

“That reminds me…” Gwen said cautiously, looking over to Dusk. “The elder told us that everyone in that settlement was cursed, right? That all of you had lost something.”

Both Veronica and Nasha turned to look at Dusk.

“What… exactly did you lose, Dusk?”

For a long while, Dusk remained silent.

Gwen’s brows furrowed. “It’s alright if you don’t want to—”

“I lost someone important to me.”

Silence consumed their camp. The only sound that could be heard was the crackling of the dying fire.

“I see… I’m sorry, Dusk.”

Gwen came over and sat beside the uneven earthy stump. She placed her hand on his. Almost on cue, the fire finally died, the ashen dirt refusing to burn any longer.

“Alright. I think it’s time to sleep. We still have a few days ahead of us, and probably a lot more monsters to fight. It’ll only get tougher from here on,” Veronica said, standing up.

Nasha followed, and then finally Gwen and Dusk.

Everyone moved toward the wagon to sleep. Veronica sent another large burst of mana into the wagon’s mana cell, powering it enough to travel until morning.

Just eight more days, then she’d find out what exactly had caused the accident in the future.

Veronica’s ears twitched.

She slowly opened her eyes, sensing something was off. She looked beside her. Inside the wagon, there were two large seats, one on either side of the carriage. Gwen lay on one bench by herself, sleeping silently and nearly taking up the entire seat.

Veronica sat a little straighter. She had been sleeping while seated, using the back wall of the carriage to lean against. Looking beside her, she saw Nasha sleeping against her shoulder, using it as a pillow. Her fingers were wrapped tightly around Veronica’s own as she slept peacefully.

Veronica couldn’t describe it, but… seeing how serenely Nasha slept put her at ease.

However, there was still something troubling her.

There was someone missing from the carriage. She could tell that they were sitting at the coach, instead of sleeping.

Sage. Amplify my hearing.

[Very well. Amplifying auditory senses…]

Since the Valley of Silence was extremely barren, there was little to no noise in the area. Anything that fell, screeched, spoke, or fluttered would be noticeable.

This was especially true with her enhanced senses and Sage’s amplification. With the wagon wheels crunching along the dirt, it was extremely easy for Sage to isolate the noise and amplify everything else.

A dull, muted sensation enveloped her ears, like she had fallen into a pond. She could hear her own heartbeat loud and clear. Not only that, but she could hear Nasha’s and Gwen’s heartbeats at the same time. Gwen’s was… slow.

Veronica’s own heartbeat matched Nasha’s.

Strangely, she didn’t hear another heartbeat, yet she knew Dusk was sitting at the front coach. Was he perhaps too far? Hearing the heartbeats of others around her was already an astonishing ability. Having it be limited in range made sense.

She focused on Dusk outside. There were… murmurs.

Thank… you. Thank… you? Thanks…

He was… thanking someone? No, he was only repeating the phrase Gwen had taught him. But… why?

It seemed oddly endearing, but strange at the same time. He hadn’t had much trouble saying it earlier. So why wake up just to practice thanking someone?

“…Nasha. You are awake, aren’t you?” Veronica whispered, looking down at her.

Silence.

“How long has Dusk been out there?” she added.

Once again, nothing.

Veronica sighed. She could just ask Sage.

Sage. How long has—

“I thought I concealed myself pretty well. How’d you know?” Nasha whispered. She didn’t shift her body, but her fingers gripped Veronica’s hand tighter.

“Sage,” Veronica answered plainly.

“Mm. I figured,” she replied. “To answer your question, it’s been about twenty minutes.”

Twenty minutes? He’s just been practicing ‘thank you’ for twenty minutes?

“I know you’re probably suspicious of him. I’ll keep watch, don’t worry. Just go to sleep.” She raised the hand interlocked with Veronica’s fingers and placed a light kiss on the back of her palm.

Veronica blushed slightly, but then let out a weak sigh. “It’s not that I don’t trust him. It’s just… something feels weird about this trip.”

Nasha didn’t reply anymore. Still, Veronica could hear her heartbeat. She was still awake. Perhaps that was her cue to go back to sleep.

And so, she did, leaning to the left and lightly resting her head on Nasha’s.

Hopefully, by the time they found the demon, she would have her answers. If the demon attacked villagers who strayed too close to the sanctuary, then it was only a matter of time before it came to attack them.

They needed to be prepared. Especially if it was a manipulative type. Those kinds of demons required much more than raw strength to defeat. Gwen, with her healing and purification, would be the key to disrupting any of their tricks.
