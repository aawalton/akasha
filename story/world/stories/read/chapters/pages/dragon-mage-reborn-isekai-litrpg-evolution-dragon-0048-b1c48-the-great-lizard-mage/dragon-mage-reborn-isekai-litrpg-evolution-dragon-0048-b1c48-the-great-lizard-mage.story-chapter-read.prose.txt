Up the stairs we headed, in a section that looked like it was leading us to the main level. No more strange luminescent fungi. Instead, light fixtures lined the walls with proper crystal-powered lanterns, revealing the sheer opulence of this place.

Organ harvesting paid well for the hardcore criminal.

The mansion’s interior was a testament to obscene wealth—vaulted ceilings soared twenty feet overhead, engraved with depictions of fae being hunted through enchanted forests. Tall stained glass windows lined the walls, reflecting bold vivid colors along surfaces. Gold-veined marble pillars supported archways as we walked through the foyer, the walls covered in rich damask wallpaper in deep crimson red.

The floors were no longer rough stone but polished marble along the empty corridor. They were so shiny that I could see my reflection on them. This was definitely the main part of the mansion, a stark contrast to the dungeon of horrors below.

When we reached a hallway, Nolan turned right. We passed a study room—empty—then, a den—also empty. But then we heard soft grumbling in the next room over, the same room the Ulmah wanted us to go into. The voice inside, it sounded familiar. It wasn’t Zev; this voice was more nasally and higher pitched.

By the expression on Nolan’s face, he recognized the voice too. Without hesitation, he snatched the lever of the door and flung it open, the gunslinger holding his sword to his hip.

Lun squawked, the lizard boy so startled that he pushed himself away from the bag of knick knacks at his feet and into a row of tall wooden shelves, causing several vials to teeter dangerously close to the edge.

This was a collection room, and Lun was the stock boy.

“Don’t move,” Nolan warned, pointing the sword at the lizardkin as he kept his hands up.

Lun’s slit eyes widened, his scales taking on a paler shade of green.

“This has got to be the worst night of my fucking life!” he wailed.

“Join the club,” Nolan retorted. “What the hell are you doing here? The last time I checked, you were scheduled to be shipped over to a local enforcer camp.”

“Well, I escaped!” he sassed. “But I wouldn’t have bothered if I’d known I’d end up here!”

“Still smart-mouthed after getting your ass beat by a baby dragon,” Nolan taunted.

“Listen, asshole,” Lun hissed, lowering his voice. “I didn’t choose to be here. After I escaped those enforcers, I got caught by Zev’s men. They gave me a choice—work for him or become the next specimen in his collection.” His eyes darted to a row of preserved organs floating in glowing liquid on the shelves. “I chose to live.”

I scampered up to one of the glass containers, my reflection distorted in the curved surface. Inside floated what appeared to be a fae heart, still pulsing faintly in a viscous liquid.

“Well, knowing Zev, you won’t be alive for long,” Nolan commented.

“Oh I could say the same for you. At least I’m supposed to be here. You two just walked straight into the Lion’s Den. And not just any lion! Zev is a savage, and when he finds you, he’s going to go all ape-shit on your asses!”

Welcome to the jungle….

“I’m talking full-scale warfare! He doesn’t play fair with uninvited guests.”

“That piece of shit doesn’t play fair at all, or else you wouldn’t have been forced into being one of his slaves.”

‘I bet this is where he keeps all of the stuff he had stolen,’ I muttered to myself, my eyes lining the countless shelves of items. His collection was vast, ceiling to floor. Beyond the preserved organs, I spotted weapons, jewelry, and strange artifacts that pulsed with magical energy. In one corner, a glass case contained what looked like a human hand, perfectly preserved but transformed to solid gold.

“If it wasn’t for you two, I wouldn’t be in this mess! You!” he seethed, turning his head to me. “This is all your fault! If I had my scepter, I would have turned you into sheep and sliced you into lamb chops!”

I gave him a pair of sassy eyes. He had a chance to do that and he still failed.

“Your scepter? The one that my dragon friend here has?”

Lun’s eyes jumped. “What?!”

“Or had, until a klepto stole it. The same klepto who probably stole the rest of these items, too.”

“What the hell are you talking about?!”

“Do you have any idea where they came from?”

“How the heck am I supposed to know? They come here and give them to me to stock. And as long as I do what I’m told, I keep my head from being mounted on his wall!”

“You mean to tell me you have access to all of these items, and you have no idea where they came from? Better yet, you haven't put them to good use?”

“I don’t think you understand who you’re dealing with, here.”

Nolan shrugged. “Fair enough. But you should have some idea how they ended up here, right?”

“I said I don’t know! Why is that important anyway?!”

“Because,” Nolan said, lowering his sword slightly, “our stuff is somewhere in this place, and I need to find it before your boss finds us.”

Lun sucked his teeth. “Look, I just catalog what comes in. This is his general inventory. The real valuable stuff goes straight to Zev’s private collection upstairs—weapons, magical artifacts, anything that could be dangerous or particularly valuable.”

“Upstairs? Like, how far upstairs?”

Lun glared at Nolan. “What do you take me for? Some type of idiot?! I’m not going to tell you where it is! What makes you think for a second I’d help you two meddling asshats?! What I should do is scream at the top of my lungs and have Zev mount your corpses in the fields for the vultures!”

“You’re a dumbass, but even you wouldn’t do something so idiotic,” Nolan retorted, and then snatched Lun by the back of his collar and shoved him ahead of us.

“Hey, what are ya—”

“We are in a hall of magical items, after all.”

Nolan continued to pace forward. Along the row of wooden shelves was a doorway into a second section of the display room. In here was much smaller than out there, the collection in a darker area. That wasn’t to say that the space wasn’t lit up. The artifacts alone were giving off various shades of warm light, as well as the glass cases they were displayed in.

Before I could follow Nolan and the Ulmah to the far end of the room, I noticed something mounted on display that made my heart nearly leap out of my tiny chest.

‘MY EVOLUTION STONE!’ I tweeted, rushing over to it. I bounced up and down on my hind legs, Nolan immediately pulling a lever on the top of the podium to open the glass encasing.

When he handed it over to me, I snatched it up with my tiny claws, cradling the precious stone against my chest. The familiar warmth radiated through my body, like reuniting with a piece of myself.

‘I thought I’d never see this again!’ I kissed the precious stone and then froze. A realization dawned on me, my eyes turning to the Ulmah, and then to Nolan.

‘Wait… you used the Ulmah on me?’

‘That’s not how it works, remember?’

‘What do you mean that’s not how it works? Your deepest desire was to help me evolve, no?’

‘Call it what you want. I just followed the damn thing and this is where it led us.’

Why was he acting all weird about it now? It was so very obvious that what he really wanted involved me, seeing as the ladybug led him to my complete evolution stone….

I didn’t know whether to shed tears of joy or leap in there and give him a hug! I was beyond happy that Nolan’s deepest wants were in my best interest, seeing as he could have asked for anything in the world. And I mean anything!

‘Thank you for this, Nolan!’ I squeaked, giving his calf a tight hug.

‘Ugh, can we save the theatrics for another time? I have a slimy slit-eyed scumbag in my clutches right now.’

After I peeled back, I prepared to trigger my evolution, but Lun cawed at me immediately. Nolan followed suit, saying that I needed to be outside. The outward force from the transformation could cave them in. I rolled my eyes, forgetting about that part. After the Ulmah granted Nolan’s wish, it turned into stone, signifying its dormant state until it became usable again.

“Interesting….” Lun said. “I swear I overheard Zev searching for that particular special item you got there. But stealing it wouldn’t have worked, seeing that its last master needs to transfer ownership to the next.”

Oh right, that’s why Kauli marked Nolan so he could use it.

“The Ulmah isn’t just some magical compass. It bonds with its master. Even if someone stole it, they couldn’t use it properly without the previous owner willingly giving it up.”

Nolan’s expression turned serious. “How do you know so much about the Ulmah?”

“Because I have family in a small reptilian village where it is from, and seeing as you’re a common human thug, I bet someone from that village gave it to you to save their hide.”

“It was given to me as a gift for annihilating the Dark One.”

Lun’s eyes lit up, and he turned his head over his shoulder to Nolan. “Say what?”

“Yeah, that demon terrorizing Obayek is dead, all thanks to Freya, here.”

All of that rage and hostility from Lun suddenly melted away. His pent up shoulders rolled down, and there was a sadness in his eyes as he said, “My… sister was taken as a sacrifice for the Dark One, not too long ago….”

“Your sister? From Obayek?” Nolan raised an eyebrow. “I thought that you were the strongest mage from the Empora District? Trained under the guidance of nobles, mastering every arcane discipline there was.”

Lun glared at Nolan’s taunt. “I could migrate, can’t I?!” His face turned grim again as he sighed. “I wasn’t there, but when I heard the news, I was crushed. According to my family, I’d deserted them. I didn’t like how small-minded we were, stuck in our old ways and outdated customs. I wanted more from life, to become a mage, someone worth more than the crops I harvested. But... I never got to say goodbye to her.” Pain spread across his face. “So the Dark One is really gone? For good?”

“Yeah.” Nolan nodded. “Little one here made sure of it. Carried his head out of the cave of hell and everything.”

I felt a swell of pride at Nolan’s acknowledgment, though I wasn’t sure how to feel about Lun’s revelation. This was the same lizardkin who tried to capture me and sell me with his bounty hunter friend, Varg. The same one who attacked me with his magical scepter.

Nolan’s face softened slightly. “I’m sorry about your sister.”

“Don’t be,” Lun replied, his voice quieter now. “If what you’re saying is true, then I owe you one. The Dark One is dead and my family can sleep easier now. You’ve given them peace, one that I could only wish to give.”

“So what are you saying? That you’d help us?”

“Third floor, east wing. His private collection is behind a door with a golden serpent handle.”

Nolan was skeptical, but after Lun disclosed the possible location of the rest of our things, he released him.

“Thanks.”

Lun nodded. “Now, get out of here. As far as anyone else knows, you’ve never been here, and I haven’t seen you!”

“What the hell are you talking about? You’re coming with us.”

“What?! Are you insane? I can’t leave!” he retorted, and headed back to the main area. “I’ve told you what you needed to know. That's payment enough for what you did for my family.”

“You’d be signing your death bed if you stick around,” Nolan said as we followed him. “When Zev discovers these items missing, who do you think he'll blame first? The stock boy who has access to everything.”

Lun pretended he didn’t hear Nolan as he continued stocking the shelves.

“Hey, look, I know you’re scared of Zev. That man, much like all of the leech’s men, have a reputation on them that’d have anyone shitting their pants. But trust me when I say, you don’t want to be around when we leave.”

Lun’s hands trembled slightly as he placed a vial on the shelf. “You don’t understand. He has ways of finding people. Tracking spells, bounty hunters, connections in every district. There’s nowhere to hide.”

“And yet, here I am,” Nolan said. “I’ve been on his hit list for years.”

Lun scoffed, still arranging items. “Yeah, and look where that got you—breaking into his mansion, gunless, and out of your freakin’ mind. Quite the picture of success.”

“It’s not about never being found,” Nolan countered. “It’s about being able to handle yourself when you are. The universe has a funny way of giving you chances—this is yours.”

Lun paused, a small ornate box trembling in his grip. His scaled fingers tightened around it as he seemed to consider his options.

“Look, I know we’re not exactly friends, but we are in this together now, whether you like it or not. We have a common enemy, and I’m sure you want to get out of here and reunite with your… bunny friend.”

Lun tried to hide a faint blush before he gave up and said, “Fine,” he said as he dropped the box. “But if we’re caught, I’m telling Zev you held me at swordpoint!”

“Fair enough,” Nolan replied, and then proceeded to loot Zev’s vault like the vagabond he was.

Lun looked at him like he was nuts, but honestly, his hands needed to get dirty, too. Most of these things were actual collectibles, items valuable in currency and not in combat. Either way, Nolan wasn’t biased, stealing both the collectibles and the combat-useful items. Both men worked as quickly and quietly as they could while I stood look-out.

Nolan nodded. “All right, let’s move.”

Lun led the way. The hallway outside was still empty, but I could hear distant voices somewhere in the mansion.

“Quick, this way,” Lun hissed, veering toward a narrow servant’s passage hidden behind an ornate tapestry.

The corridor was dimly lit and smelled of dust and old polish. We moved in single file, Lun leading, with Nolan behind him and me perched on Nolan’s shoulder.

“The main staircase will be guarded,” Lun whispered over his shoulder. “But there’s a service stairwell the kitchen staff uses. It’ll take us straight to the third floor.”

As we crept through the hidden passage, I couldn’t help but notice how the walls narrowed, forcing Nolan to hunch slightly. The servant’s corridor was clearly not designed for someone of his height. Lun, on the other hand, moved easily, his scaled feet barely making a sound against the worn wooden floorboards.

We reached a sterile kitchen area. At this hour, it was surprisingly deserted—a small mercy. The only sound was the soft hiss of a pot left simmering on a low flame, filling the room with the rich scent of a meaty, savory stew.

“Through here,” Lun whispered, leading us to a door at the back.

The service stairs were steep, meant for servants to move quickly and unobtrusively between floors. We were stealthy throughout our ascent, our footsteps muffled against the worn wooden steps. The stairwell twisted upward in a tight spiral, forcing us to move single-file again. The walls were bare stone here, which was uncharacteristic with the rest of the mansion, minus the underground prison area.

When we reached the third floor, Lun raised a scaled hand, signaling us to stop. He pressed his ear against the door, listening intently for several long seconds before giving a cautious nod.

“Coast is clear,” he whispered, easing the door open just enough to peek through. “But we need to be quick. Guards patrol these halls every fifteen minutes.”

We slipped through the door into a hallway that was even more opulent than the floors below. The wallpaper here was a deep midnight blue, embossed with silver patterns. The carpet beneath our feet was thick and plush, muffling our footsteps as we crept along.

Lun nodded, pointing to our right. “This way. And keep your voices down. Sound carries in these halls.”

We didn’t travel long before we reached a room with grand double doors. Each door was adorned with intricate carvings of serpents twisting around each other, their eyes inlaid with small gemstones. The handles were exactly as Lun had described—golden serpents coiled around themselves, their open mouths forming the grips.

“This is it,” Lun whispered. “Zev’s treasured private collection.”

Nolan grabbed both grips and paused, like he was bracing himself. When he opened the doors, I gasped. Nothing could have prepared me for what we saw inside….
