She knew it was coming. Ash kept waiting for it. Like a coin tossed that refused to fall back down. Just waiting for her to look away just for a second before it clinked down. She believed there was even a saying about something like that. About how watched pots refused to boil or something in that vein.

So when Velistraine finally arrived in front of her and took a seat, leaving her fellow A-rank at the other end of the tavern to chat with the ship’s enchanters, Ash swallowed quietly. The captain shooed Fabian away, and the bastard just abandoned her and went to check on Henry and Maurice as the two worked their way through the tavern’s menu.

What should Ash say? Start with apologizing? She didn’t regret her actions, not really. But her captain did say not to cause trouble, and while Ash had been waiting for the A-rank to address her all night, it had taken a while, which could only mean Velistraine was pissed but looking at her, it didn’t look like it.

The captain leaned forward, lips tight as she stared down at her, and Ash could feel sweat percolating down her back. Ash opened her mouth to stammer out an apology, only for a snort to escape Velistraine.

“Relax. I’m not gonna eat you. It was obvious something was up ever since I said we were coming up here. At least no one’s dead yet. That’s always a plus.”

Ash felt some of the stress leak out of her chest, only for the captain’s next word to wind her right back as she casually flagged one of the servers for drinks. “What’s your plan for tomorrow? Are you going to kill him? And… if you don’t mind me asking, how did this all get started anyway?”

Ash looked down at her barely touched bowl of fried shrimp and reflexively, she felt herself backing away, thinking of a way to deflect the captain’s attention, only to catch herself a moment later.

She wasn’t surviving on the streets anymore. She didn’t need to hide.

Taking a deep breath, Ash reached for a piece of shrimp and just… talked. She told Velistraine everything she remembered. About her dad. About her nanny and her two tutors. She didn’t remember her mother as the latter had passed when she was young, so there wasn’t much to say there. There had been a couple of pictures, but they were likely lost in the takeover. She told Velistraine about her dad’s guard escorting her through the night, and about her inquiries later on about what had happened. Stealing and running with the gangs to have a roof over her head—if one could call it that—and to afford the informants and learn about what had happened to her family.

Finding out what had happened hadn’t been difficult. Her father and tutors had shielded her from the political tensions and intrigue, but from the outside, the move had been obvious. The smear campaigns. The sabotage of their last couple of stores and of course, who had ended up taking over everything her family had owned before.

Leaning back, Velistraine sighed, lips pursed. When Ash was done, Velistraine looked up at the tavern rafters, deep in thoughts, though there was denying the scowl on marring her features.

But there was still one part that Ash hadn’t answered.

Wringing her hand under the table, Ash muttered. “As for the duel… I’m not sure.”

Velistraine looked back down, but she didn’t speak. She just waited for Ash to continue.

“Lucian Clydemar was barely older than I was back then,” she said, looking around for some water. Her throat felt dry. “It didn’t look like he’d taken over. Cassius was the one who’d gotten my father accused of treason. Not alone, probably, and Lucian might not be an innocent child anymore, but he had no hand in what happened to my family. I’d still knock him down a peg or two, but I’m not sure about… doing what they did to me. I want those that are guilty. That’s it.”

“There will be bad blood if you knock the snot out of the heir. But killing Cassius will bring down a shitstorm on your head. And depending on who’s backing them, things might get… hairy. Hey Attal,” Velistraine suddenly turned, shouting at her fellow A-rank.

“What?” the man shouted back from across the busy tavern.

“Who’s backing the Skidmarks?”

“Clydemars,” Ash corrected.

“Clydemars!” Velistraine shouted, which immediately quieted some of the patrons, but not her own people, and certainly not Attal, Henry, or the giggling Maurice.

“No one. But they’re pretty close to the king, and the king’s daughter’s betrothed to Octavian.”

Velistraine grunted in annoyance as she turned to Ash. “So, yeah. Look Ash, I like you. And I’ll absolutely knock some heads down for you. I’m even tempted to just go and cut off this Cassius’s head myself, but I don’t think that’s what you want.”

Ash shook her head. She didn’t want the captain to take over her vendetta. She needed this herself. She needed Cassius to pay for what he did to her father and for everything he had caused.

“Right. This is… yours. This is your story. But do you trust me?”

That took Ash a little by surprise. Still, she nodded. “Good. So here’s what I’m thinking. And here’s what’s probably going to happen.”

Velistraine leaned over, a glint in her eyes, and began talking. And while Ash had been a little reticent at first, she had to admit, by the end of it, she was sold.

This would feel better than just breaking into the Clydemar’s home and dragging Cassius out by the balls.

It’d feel much better.

Eventually, the barkeep rang the last call, and the crew filtered out, most heading back to the inn, while a couple opted to stay on the ship. Even Maurice wanted to experience what sleeping at an inn in a human city was like. But Henry was far too awake, and he wanted to train some more. So once he made sure Maurice was settled in along with Fabian and Ash, he left him with a clone and transferred himself away.

Specifically, underwater.

He’d considered flying, but for this, he wanted to be comfortable, and he wanted to take his natural form for one reason and one reason only: he felt close to a breakthrough.

The clone he switched with was only a few yards out from dock. The water wasn’t too deep, but with a deft use of Control Water and his other passives, Henry took off, quickly navigating away from the island. The area was mostly sand, but while it was a little surprising to find a few shipwrecks on the way, it made sense, considering his location.

Once he was far and deep enough, Henry shapeshifted back into his normal form, scaring the heck out of a dark, shadowy shark that thought it was sneaking up on him, and landed near a large, shipwreck. Henry didn’t push himself to his maximum size. But he was already nearly twice as large as the sunken brig he was resting his back against.

Inhaling deeply through his gills, Henry stretched his limbs and sighed. He took a second to enjoy the cold fresh water, eying the curious, multicolored fish that peeked at him from the ship’s cracks, then he summoned a batch of clones and sent them scouting, just to make sure he wasn’t interrupted.

Thanks to the upgrade to his Aspect today, he’d gained around fifty other clones, bringing him to nearly 310 active clones, and as he had calculated, the number was growing fast.

Turning his attention back to why he was here, Henry hoped that what he had in mind wouldn’t take long. He felt he was close, and while this phenomenon had only happened a couple times for him, he felt it was about to take place a third time.

He might be getting a new skill.

Just like when he had acquired Mana Control and Toxic Resistance, Henry could feel some sort of pressure internally. Like part of him was holding its breath. It was close. And even if it wasn’t, he knew that sooner or later, something would click.

With his surroundings scouted, and his mind rested, Henry was about to get started when he paused and glanced around. Henry scanned the dark water for a few seconds, and if he had his human form right now, he’d be frowning.

For a brief moment, he felt like he was being watched, but no matter how much he looked around with his own eyes and with his clones, he couldn’t see much.

Maybe he was imagining it?

Henry gave it a few more minutes, to make sure he wasn’t missing anything important, but the sea continued to be as calm as it had been since he’d arrived. Even using Find the Path to try and pick up on any watchers just fizzled out.

Maybe the encounter with that weird creature was still messing with him.

Dismissing the worry for now, Henry closed his eyes and focused inwardly. First, he activated the first three instances of Control Water, creating the three skill nexuses along his first arm and taking control of a little patch of water above the limb. Once the skills were active enough, he gave control to that arm’s Octomind.

His subminds hadn’t recovered enough to take their manifested form, but they were perfectly functional for this.

Henry went around, activating multiple instances of the skill along his arms and giving partial control to their respective Octomind until, within a couple of minutes, 24 instances of Control Water were active, pressurizing a patch of water above every arm. It was only a little pressure, easy enough for both his mind and soul to handle. But it could be tuned up.

Then, Henry activated three skill nexuses himself, right above his head.

He could have used Telekinesis, but that Skill was a little more straining to use, and he wanted to slowly ramp up the pressure in his soul. Having more dials—here represented by the three skill nexus every Octomind controlled—meant he’d have a finer control, and most importantly, it would spread out the load more evenly compared to a single Telekinesis skill. Plus, pressurizing water was incredibly challenging. Much harder than air, so this would make for a good exercise.

Taking a moment to check his control over every arm and its skills, Henry nodded to himself. He was ready to move on to the next step.

One arm at a time, Henry began pressurizing the skill nexuses. Using the local Octomind to each arm, he only pressurized the skill instance, but did not apply any further pressure around the water it was controlling. Already, he could feel the strain begin, but of course at this stage, it was very manageable and once all skill nexuses were mildly compressed, he used the bolstered output. The increase in power was then used on the water orbs hovering above every arm and above his own, increasing the pressure on the spheres.

The strain on his soul instantly doubled, and Henry found himself a little light-headed, but it was nothing a few deep breaths couldn’t fix. He’d been doing similar exercises for days, now, so he was used to strain. Once he took stock of his general state, he smiled to himself. The stress was evenly spread all around his spirit, and in a way, the exercise was reinvigorating him. Just like a muscle grew a little stronger when the blood flow brought oxygen and energy to the fibers, something was happening to his soul. The exertion was “waking” up whatever the muscle equivalent to a soul was.

He was getting there. He knew it. So while everyone slept and dreamed, Henry worked on his soul as a few small fish nibbled at his head and swam around his arms.
