Don barely had time to think Oh shit, when Snarlak plummeted towards him at breakneck speed.

The boss was in the air, wings tucked, a goddamn missile made of muscle, claws, and malice.

Coming straight at him, whistling with speed in the air.

The platform wasn’t big enough for anything clever. Barely fifteen feet across. Slick with a thin crust of frost. Every footfall had to be placed perfectly. Don only had one option.

Run.

Behind him, Snarlak hit.

The whole platform detonated. A wall of force threw Don forward like a toy. He hit the slick bridge, rolled twice and fell off. With a gasp he flicked out his claws and slammed one into the bridge. His heart hammered like a hummingbird on caffeine overdose. His legs kicked nothing and below the endless abyss opened like a hungry maw waiting to swallow him.

Don wouldn’t let it. The ice where he had stuck his claws was cracking, so Don swung in the air and slammed the other claw into the bridge.

Snarlak screamed, and Don had to tank the hit. The world spun as he dangled above nothingness and death with a single claw.

[Debuff Inflicted: Disorientation]

Don swallowed bile and tried to breathe through the nausea and headache. He had to move. He had to push through.

With the help of his superior physique and Telekinesis, he managed to scramble back on the bridge.

Don looked back.

Snarlak stood in the smoking crater it had made. Massive, hunched, half-spread wings twitching. One eye gleamed in the darkness. It didn’t roar. It just stared at Don, black froth bubbling from its jaws. That little hit-and-run had failed, and the boss knew it. And it was pissed.

Don bared his teeth. “Yeah? Fuck you.”

No answer. Of course not. But Don swore, somehow, the bastard narrowed its eyes. Then it shifted again, wings rising. It wasn’t done.

Don used Telekinesis to throw a few blowpipe darts at the giant beast as it took to the air. He aimed for the face, and managed to deal some damage. Or at least piss the damn thing off just a bit more.

Snarlak screamed again, but this time Don was ready with the vacuum. The disorientation was nasty, but he could work through. It was slowly fading anyway.

Don sprinted upwards the bridge and didn’t stop at the platform. Snarlak was still gathering altitude.

The next bridge was thin, barely four feet wide. And it was ice. Of course it was. Crystal clear, slippery, and arched downward just enough to mess with your balance. Beneath it was nothing but pitch black void.

He didn’t care. No time to care.

Halfway across, Snarlak launched again.

This time it didn’t dive. It glided low and slow, hugging the opposite wall. A hunting arc, giant talons extended. It was trying to catch Don from the side.

Don cursed, pivoted mid-run, and ducked low. He jammed his claws into the ice bridge for balance, sliding on his feet like he was carving down a mountain slope. It worked.

Snarlak’s claws raked the air inches above his head. The bridge cracked from the wind pressure alone. Don slid under the worst of it and vaulted the last five feet to the next ledge.

He hit hard, rolled, came up gasping. No time to rest.

The beast was already circling. It slowed down. It wasn’t that badly hurt. Was it tired?

Whatever it was, Don pounced on the advantage.

Don launched a dart mid-roll. Pure Telekinesis. It spun through the air like a bullet, struck Snarlak dead center in the wing joint.

Don grinned. He wasn’t done.

Even from this distance, he could control the dart. He ripped it vertically along the leathery wing, cutting it like a branch to a kite.

[Racial Skill Increased: Telekinesis]

[Telekinesis Level 5]

The effect was immediate.

Snarlak howled—real pain this time—and tilted in the air. The wing stuttered, flapped once awkwardly, then drooped. The bat swerved wide, flapped hard to stabilize, and crash-landed on a lower platform. The one just above Don.

He grinned and sprinted over the bridge.

He charged again, leapt the gap, and landed just as the boss started to rise.

Don didn’t give it time.

He jammed a claw into the same wing joint, twisted. Something wet tore inside. Black blood sprayed in a wide arc across the ice. Snarlak shrieked, backhanded blindly. Don took the hit on the shoulder and was thrown aside. He slid on his back across the platform and slammed into the wall.

[Health at 73%]

He stood. Snarlak screeched. Don formed a vacuum just in time.

They just stared at each other, standing on the small icy platform. Both were winded and hurt. And both knew they would have to continue the fight. Only one of them would be standing after this.

Don’s ribs ached. His shoulder was already swelling. But the bastard couldn’t fly now.

Don used Drain Life.

The ability immediately formed a tether of red energy between him and Snarlak. That sweet life force was pouring into Don and his ripped shoulder was starting to heal.

Snarlak stood lopsided and looked at the red tether in shock and rage. One wing dragged on the floor. Its breathing came in ragged gulps. But the rage in its eyes hadn’t dimmed. It opened its jaws.

Don threw up the psychic vacuum.

It didn’t work.

Snarlak didn’t screech. It made a low, almost subsonic sound. Then the world pulsed. A deep, compressed burst of air—something between a concussive wave and a sonic cannon. Don’s vacuum did absolutely nothing. The force alone picked him up and slammed him backward like a kicked doll.

He hit the wall with a nasty crunch.

[Health at 59%] [Debuff: Fractured bones]

“Son of a—” Don muttered as he spat out blood.

Another pulse fired. This one went wide, striking the ice above him. Stalactites shattered. Don dodged left and as the ice spikes fell down, Don used Telekinesis to swerve them to the side.

Snarlak lunged forward, dragging its body like a broken gorilla, maw open, another blast charging in its chest. Don knew the timing now. He threw himself to the side again and rolled. He could feel the ripples of the sonic blast vibrate the air. Don activated the Drain Life again. His bones started slowly knit back together. But he wasn’t done. He needed to deal damage.

He slashed the beast’s thigh as it passed. It screamed again, this time in frustration, and fired another air burst directly into the floor. The explosion cratered the ice and sent cracks rippling across the platform. The enraged beast shot out another air blast. The platform cracked. It wouldn’t hold both their weight. The edge was already crumbling. Don jumped.

He landed on a steep slope of a broken bridge—somehow still attached—and ran up it like a wall. His feet slipped once, twice, but he forced every muscle to obey. Near the top he jumped again, clearing ten yards, and landed on a wide circular plateau halfway up the shaft.

His breath caught.

The bridge behind him snapped off completely. The platform quivered but held.

Don turned around.

Snarlak stood at the base of the platform. Not charging this time. It started climbing up the wall with its claws, dragging its battered body, wings twitching. One leg limped. Don had to move before he lost the initiative.

But instead of slinking away, Snarlak climbed a bit and then landed on a large platform in the middle of the spire. This one was two bridges away from Don, and he could see it above with his Night Vision. This platform was large. It was the original perch the boss had watched him from.

It stopped there. It looked at Don with a strange gleam in the eyes. Like glee.

Something was wrong. Don could sense it.

The bat’s chest expanded. Its posture changed. No more hunched lurching. It straightened. Bones shifted. The neck elongated. The shoulders bulged with new muscle. Don heard bones pop. The wings didn’t just twitch. They folded, then split.

“Oh, what the hell is this now…”

Snarlak tilted its head again, but this time the eyes were different. Clearer. Sharper. There was no more beast in them. Just intent.

The black scream was changing. It was going through some kind of metamorphosis. Its body was becoming slimmer. The limbs longer. The wings split, and suddenly there were four of them. The black skin hardened into some kind of a carapace. Suddenly Don wasn’t looking at a mutated bat. He was looking at a goddamn demon.

Snarlak, The Black Scream, spread its spindly arms as it rose. It was looking like a humanoid now. The face was still monstrous, but it stood straight, the four wings flapping rhytmically behind it. The muscular body shined with an exoskeleton and its claws dripped with some black venom.

With one finger, it beckoned Don.
