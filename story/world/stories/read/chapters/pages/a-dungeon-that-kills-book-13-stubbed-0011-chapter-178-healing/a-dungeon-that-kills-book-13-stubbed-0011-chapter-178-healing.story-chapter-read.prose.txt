The world shifted, and Viktor found himself in a cramped room, its air thick with the notorious odor of goblin cleanliness.

Beside him stood Galatea’s projection, filling the dim chamber with light and warmth, the elegant contours of her ethereal form a stark contrast to the filth and misery that clung to the place. One had to wonder if these creatures had thought they were seeing a collective hallucination when she suddenly manifested before their eyes a moment before.

There were six of them in total, sprawled across rough straw mattresses, their bodies twitching and groaning with pain as they craned their necks toward Viktor.

“I told them to remain where they were,” his luminous Guardian said.

He nodded. “Yes, there’s no need for anyone to get up.”

This room was one of the many designated havens where his injured minions could retreat to after a fight, positioned at the edges of the floor, far away from both the entrance and the staircase that led to the next layer of the dungeon’s depths. Here, they could rest, waiting for the regeneration provided by Celeste to slowly patch up their bodies.

This particular bunch, however, was about to find themselves unexpectedly lucky today.

“Show me your power,” Viktor said.

Without a word, Galatea drifted to the nearest goblin. A blade must have carved a deep furrow across its ribcage, slicing through leather armor and the green skin beneath like soaked parchment. The wound gaped with every breath, a grinning mouth rimmed with yellow fat and speckled grime.

“Be still,” she told the creature, hovering her hand above the gash.

And the Light came.

It emerged from the center of her palm and expanded outward, unfurling dozens of glowing threads that coursed through the stale air. They descended into the opening, vanishing into bone and flesh, and from somewhere deep within the injury, a radiance began to bloom, illuminating the damaged structures beneath. Viktor could see the fractured ribs realigning, the torn tissue reweaving, blood and muscle reorganizing as though they were recalling their former state. Then, the wound sealed entirely, leaving behind only a faint golden line that would soon fade away. The goblin took a deep breath as it looked up at Galatea, its expression a mix of fear and awe and gratitude.

“Very impressive,” Viktor said. “Strange that you didn’t mention any of this the day I summoned you.”

“I didn’t think it was important enough.” Galatea shrugged. “Anything inside the dungeon can regenerate using Celeste’s mana anyway, so this ability adds very little to what is already there.” She glanced at Viktor. “You thought the same, right, Master? I’m certain you learned about this aspect of the Light quite a while ago, but only now did you ask me to demonstrate it.”

Viktor nodded.

“That night, I saved Ekon from those masked men, but he was still half-dead afterward. Fortunately, I managed to find one of his potions, but if there hadn’t been any, we could’ve been deprived of an important asset. Also, I sometimes fight outside the dungeon. I haven’t suffered any injury so far, but I’m not so arrogant as to assume that will remain true forever. So, a reliable method of healing myself would be very useful.”

Yes, those were the only reasons why he had begun to take an interest in this type of magic.

“Anyway,” Viktor said. “I’m quite curious that light magic could heal at all. Tell me, what is the difference between your spells and those that belong to the domain of Life?”

“To put it simply, one works with the body to help it heal, while the other commands it to heal.”

“Care to elaborate?”

“Let’s start with life magic,” Galatea said. “Just keep in mind this is only what I’ve been told. I’m not a practitioner of this discipline myself, so take what I say with a grain of salt. Basically, it accelerates the body’s own capacity for repair, making the body remember what it was and rebuild itself bit by bit. Depending on the severity of the damage and the power of the mage, full recovery can take minutes, hours, days, weeks, months, or even years. Nevertheless, it tends to be slow, and the process might not be very pleasant, since the body has to reconstruct itself through effort. A more powerful mage could push it even further, achieving feats such as regrowing a missing limb. In that case, the body is asked to recall the blueprint of that limb and rebuild it from scratch.”

Viktor nodded. “How about light magic, then?”

“When I heal with the Light, I am imposing perfection upon imperfection. The wound is not ‘healed’ in the sense of a natural biological process. It is undone instead. So, while I can attach a limb back to a body, I cannot make it regrow. Most of the body’s missing parts must be present for it to be restored. I reshape them into their original form, reassemble them into coherence, and then seal the breach so perfectly that the body simply accepts it as if it were whole. The Light tends to heal faster than life magic, but there are certain problems it cannot resolve. Aside from dismemberment, as I mentioned earlier, illness is another example. Conditions that exist not as structural damage but as persistent states are things the Light struggles to address, because there is nothing cleanly ‘broken’ to undo.”

“Hypothetically,” Viktor said. “Let’s say there’s a... well, a goblin that has received a particularly hard blow to the head. Could you heal it?”

“That depends on the severity of the injury,” Galatea replied. “From my experience, though, a goblin taking a heavy blow to its head tends to die instantly.”

“It doesn’t die. But it’s rendered unconscious.”

“Then I would wait for it to wake up.”

“It remains unconscious for days.”

Galatea frowned. “If it doesn’t wake up even with the healing provided by the dungeon, then I’m not sure what exactly you expect me to accomplish.”

“In this hypothetical scenario, the goblin is not sustained by the dungeon’s mana.”

“Why?”

“Because it’s outside the dungeon.”

“Why is a goblin outside the dungeon?”

“As I said, this is a hypothetical scenario.”

“Alright,” Galatea said. “In this hypothetical and oddly specific scenario of yours, Master, I can attempt to cast my spell on it. I would impose the Light upon the body, undo the physical damage, and forcefully wake it up. It would help considerably if the goblin were brought into the dungeon so I could use my main body instead of this projected form. But even then, the real problem is that I cannot guarantee the success of such an action.”

“You can’t?”

“Because the brain is more than mere flesh, Master. It is where thought resides. Memory. Identity. The self. As a result, damage to the brain is also damage to the mind. I can fix the physical structure, but repairing the tissue does not guarantee the mind once inhabiting that tissue will also be restored. If I forcefully wake up your hypothetical goblin, there’s a possibility that the body would function while the mind would not. It could become nothing more than an empty shell animated by instinct and reflex, hollow in every way that actually matters.”

“Why are you making it sound like resurrecting the dead?”

“They are similar in principle,” Galatea said. “Attempting to restore a mind after catastrophic damage to the head is not unlike trying to resurrect a corpse after the soul is already gone. Of course, in this case the subject is merely unconscious, so its soul would still be present. No, wait. It’s a goblin. Goblins don’t have souls—”

“This one has.”

“This definitely fictional scenario is getting more and more bizarre with every passing minute,” Galatea muttered. “Still, my point stands. This is not a problem that could be resolved by the Light. I strongly advise against attempting such reckless treatment. In order to properly heal this injury, you will need not only life magic to repair the physical damage, but also mind magic to ensure the thoughts, memories, and sense of self are restored as well.”

“So,” Viktor asked, “you’re telling me I need two powerful mages of two different disciplines?”

“Preferably a single individual skilled in both disciplines simultaneously.”

Where the hell am I supposed to find someone like that? Does such a person even exist in this world?

Well, there was he himself. After all, he had his Thaumaturgy, which allowed him to wield different types of magic and accomplish feats that should be impossible for any ordinary mage. Without a doubt, eventually he would be able to craft a Thauma to solve this exact problem.

Yes... eventually.

The problem was, Mastery of Mind could only be unlocked at Level 40.

Far too long...

Viktor exhaled slowly, pushing the thought aside. There was no point staring at a locked door when he lacked the key to open it. He needed to focus on what could be done at the moment instead of obsessing over what could not.

“There’s another matter I want to discuss,” he said. “Heal the remaining goblins, then we will talk. I’ll wait in the Core Room. Celeste, teleport me there.”

[Understood.]

The stench of blood and festering wounds vanished instantly, replaced by the cool stillness of the Core Room as space folded and reassembled itself according to Celeste’s will. Viktor found himself once again standing beside the crystalline heart of the dungeon. Without saying a word, he moved to one of the nearby chairs and dropped into it.

[Master... we will definitely find a way... so please do not abandon hope...]

He didn’t answer.

What exactly was there to talk about anyway?

Hope?

People loved speaking of that word as though it possessed some mystical power that could alter reality itself, as though merely wishing hard enough would somehow make the world change in their favor.

No, of course not.

Reality didn’t give a shit about hope. The world turned as it pleased, crushing optimists and cynics alike under the same wheel.

In the end, hope was nothing but a pretty little lie people told themselves so they could keep crawling another day. It was invented by the weak, embraced by the miserable, clung to by those trapped in situations they lacked the strength to escape. They needed hope because the alternative was despair, and despair terrified them.

But he was different.

He had never known despair, so he had no need for hope.

Time passed quietly, measured only by the steady pulse of Celeste’s crystalline form, until golden illumination bloomed, flaring across the chamber. Galatea had arrived, stepping forward from the fading radiance.

“You done with those goblins?” Viktor asked.

“Yes, Master.”

“Good. Now we can move to today’s main topic,” Viktor said. “Celeste told me you’ve located those masked men’s secret hideout. Is that correct?”
