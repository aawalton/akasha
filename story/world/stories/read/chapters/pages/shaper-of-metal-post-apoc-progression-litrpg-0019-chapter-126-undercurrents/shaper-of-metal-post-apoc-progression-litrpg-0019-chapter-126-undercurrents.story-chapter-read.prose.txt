Chapter 126: Undercurrents

The team moseyed on over to the awkward party, held in a very nice, oversized event hall, filled with enough white-covered round tables that every team could leave multiple empty tables between groups. The team steeled themselves to keep neutral faces, agonized internally by having to keep things from their wronged teammate, Inkblot. He was an observant fellow, and his eyes said he could tell something was up, but he never asked or commented on it.

Jack almost thought the guy knew exactly what it was, already, being the streetwise devil he was. He’d even told them that what happened might very well happen. If he did know, his Mini-Mem would’ve told him to keep his mouth shut, in any case. It was apparently a pretty well-preserved secret, and clearly a right of hazing that the veterans wanted to preserve.

He understood, he supposed. One day, he’d likely pull the same shit on someone. If Inblot didn’t actually make ANP, he might be the one to haze and cake Augur in the face one day, for instance.

He saw a few others struggling likewise. Délight seemed to blend in with the already subdued attitudes of Hiccup and Chime, who were definitely avoiding even looking at the team that beat them.

Another team also avoided even looking at them, perhaps out of shame: Corroline’s. Quillote wasn’t even there, which was quite possibly enough to get a reprimand. Maybe it was an unspoken indication, but the ‘celebration’ party seemed pretty damned mandatory. Even if it wasn’t, who would chance some hidden black mark from the brass when you were fighting in the PACCs?

Of course, Snapper’s team was all good times and cheer. Jack wanted to think of them as ‘lucky bastards,’ but the truth was that they’d earned the right by being a stellar team.

Despite the issues with enjoying the party, it couldn’t help but be a little amusing to see newbies get confused by the lack of cake.

There will be no eating cake, kids. The real cake is the jacket, and most don’t get it.

Jack ate a lot, and not just for appearances. It was free food, and it was good food. He might’ve eaten a little too much bread pudding. It was incredible. Who needed cake, anyway?

And he was sipping coffee. Glorious, free coffee. It had run out not long after he got his second cup.

Jack caught the moment when Délight approached Lighthouse, while she was up, getting more food. Lighthouse barked something rude and tried to leave her hanging. And then Délight called, “I’m sorry!” loud enough to be a little publicly humiliating. In fact, the number of people who turned to her at that moment made her face get a little red. But when Lighthouse whipped around incredulously, Délight added, “I apologize for how I treated you, which was unfair and terribly. Please. Can we talk? I don’t want anything ulterior, I swear. There is nothing ulterior left between us.”

Perhaps the sheer embarrassment swayed her. Lighthouse looked around wild-eyed, and her eyes ended up locking on Jack's.

Jack gave her a slow nod.

She blinked, tore her eyes away, and nodded curtly to Délight, muttering, “Okay,” almost too faint to hear.

And so they went off and sat alone, Lighthouse stiff at first in listening, and then leaning forward and delivering emphatic accusations. Délight was really taking it on the chin. She was doing well: Light’s butt stayed parked. Her food went cold.

“Never thought I’d see the day,” Highfive said, shaking his head as he munched on fried pickles. His eyes were often locked over toward the scene.

“She’s acting odd, for sure,” Bo said, pointedly not looking as she sipped coffee and scrolled on her phone.

“Which?”

“Both.”

Jack wanted to stir a better answer, so he asked, “How is Délight acting odd, specifically?”

Bo gave him a withering look. “As if you don’t know? You’re the first to report it.”

“Maybe I wanted the superior observations of a bona fide woman, toward her fellow of womankind.”

Bo snorted. “Look, you’re right on some level. She’s different in a way she’s never been different before. Are you happy now?”

“Naturally. I’d be happier if you explained why I’m right, from your perspective.”

“You’re impossible.”

“Am not.” Jack savored what remained of his coffee with a sip, then flashed a grin. “Just improbable.”

“Well, isn’t that the truth? Me, too. Yet here we are.”

“Here we are.”

Bo sighed. “It’s a long, long road before I trust her, Jack.”

Jack shrugged in acceptance. “Sure.”

She squinted her eyes accusingly. “Don’t look so damned smug.”

“I’m not! You haven’t admitted enough for me to be.”

“Then we’ll just stop here.”

“I see.” He sipped his coffee, trying not to smile under Bo’s steely, analytical glare.

After a lengthy silence, Highfive breathed out in a way that vibrated his lips, and then said, “I didn’t get any of that conversation, other than that Délight is different. Which I guess I, uh, cautiously agree with. I mean, the match is over. We’re done, and yet she’s still pulling this crap.”

“Perhaps she’s wearing you down,” Inkblot offered, “in a time without any apparent motive, so she can manipulate you later on down the road, after the trust is finally earned. She might not even have anything in mind.” He sipped at his tea. “Yet.”

The other two nodded thoughtfully to that.

Jack winced briefly. Inkblot is the devil on their shoulder, damn it. “Or… she’s legitimately paving the way for a future we can all see, down that road where we’re cooperating as professionals. You want a motive? How about not getting someone killed in the line of duty due to old, irrelevant feuds? That’s just as pragmatic as it is idealistic. No one wants some frag-up on their record or conscience.”

Highfive whistled. “Damn. That’s some Big Picture shit, right there.” Frowning slightly, tapping his knuckle repeatedly on the table, he finally shrugged. “If Lighthouse can say ‘bygones,’ I don’t see how I can hold out. I’m game to move on.”

“She hasn’t done that yet,” Bo said, glancing over at the table in question, which triggered everyone else to do the same.

Lighthouse was sitting silently and staring at the middle of the table, a sort of half-hollow, half-perturbed expression on her face. Délight had moved closer, leaning over the table, speaking low and soft. Some tears had trailed down her face.

“Waterworks,” Bo muttered darkly. “Classic Délight industry.”

No one said anything further. Délight seemed to finish speaking, finally leaving a quiet Lighthouse, who sat alone a little while longer before picking up her tray. Her head half-turned toward their table, but absent-mindedly. She finally moved to dispose of her food and exit.

Bo, frowning, glanced at Jack and Highfive, then got up to leave herself.

“Girl stuff,” Highfive muttered as he scooped up the last of his food.

Jack huffed in amusement and nodded. “Yeah.” I guess that’s that. They’ll work through it together, one way or another. Jack downed the rest of his coffee and grinned over at Highfive. “Wanna go have a five-hour-long conversation cross-legged on a bed? Do some ugly crying together? Watch some romantic comedies?”

Highfive made a long, hiss-like ‘shooo’ noise as he shook his head. “I actually do not, bro, believe it or not.”

Jack threw his hands up and sagged, feigning crestfallen surprise and disappointment.

“If we had an issue with each other,” Inkblot declared, “we’d just hop around, hoot, beat our chests a little. Maybe an intimidating charge or two.”

“Fling poo,” Jack added, nodding sagely.

“Nah, I only fling poo for fun,” Highfive said as he rose. “On that note, time to make some more ammo, know what I’m saying?”

Jack nodded reluctantly as he rose with his tray, too. “I wish I didn’t, Blondy, but yes.”

Inkblot finished off his tea and nodded to them as he also rose. “Tomorrow might be a no-training day, if things follow as normal.”

Jack and Highfive exchanged a brief glance. Highfive shrugged and nodded agreement, “Yeah, probably so, bro.”

Arrrrgh. I hate this. Why do they do this to us?

Jack was slower than the other two on the way out. Happening to pass close to the table of the trio, X-Tender, Corroline, and Abundance, he saw Abundance ‘mess up’ and meet his eyes, but they quickly widened, and he looked away, embarrassed and shifting in his seat. It was a strong sign of absolute shame. Meanwhile, X-Tender and even Corroline were not looking at him so hard that he could almost feel the muscular tension in their bodies.

Délight already said it was all Quillote. If even Corroline isn’t acting smug… Well, we did win, anyway, so fat chance of that, regardless. But this looks awfully consistent with a group embarrassed by one member’s indiscretion.

Spontaneously, Jack walked over and pulled out a chair, sitting at their table and leaning forward with his elbows on the table, smiling over at three people who, each in their own way, clearly dreaded whatever he was coming to say. “I don’t think you three had anything to do with the leak. I don’t think Bo does, either.”

Abundance looked relieved and hopeful, and X-Tender remained thoughtful, eyes behind the mask never quite meeting his. Her gloved hand on the table rubbed two fingers and a thumb together continuously.

Corroline had a typical sour expression on her face as she did something just shy of glaring at him. “How noble of you to offer,” she said. “Now, if you’ll excuse us-”

Abundance leaned over the table and blurted in an emphatic whisper, “We kicked Quillote off the team! That’s why he isn’t here.”

The other two glared balefully at him, and he shrank backward, swallowing, but he refused to lower his eyes this time, meeting their eyes and Jack’s, though still with reddened cheeks.

“Are you sure that’s wise?” Jack asked seriously. “You’re fighting for a jacket, not a popularity contest. If you work well together-”

“We can do better,” Corroline said, eyes and voice hot. “Far better.”

X-Tender sighed and shook her head. “It doesn’t matter, Ferro. Reputation is important to us, and he broke our trust. Who helped him along in breaking it might be a specialist in doing so, but he was completely belligerent about it, not apologizing, not admitting wrong, doubling down, and even making demands. He’s been slowly growing poisonous even before this, but now? He’s become a disease. I’ve never been sure he even wanted this enough to help us carry through in the way leadership wants.” She took a deep breath and looked at Jack soberly. “I think we’ve made the right call.”

Jack nodded slowly. “Well, I can’t argue with that. A shame. I hope you find someone better for you, then. You’ve certainly got a good core to build on.” He glanced at Abundance. “Don’t squander having this healer here, eh? It’s wise to keep him happy.”

Corroline’s smile was half-sneer, as she reached a hand over to grasp Abundance’s shoulder. She patted it and squeezed. “Bun-bun is a good boy. There’s no concern there — not that it is even remotely your business, Ferro.”

Abundance blinked rapidly at the comment, unsure how to take it.

“Trust me,” X-Tender said significantly, glancing at the others and back to Jack. “I know what you mean. Abundance earns more of our respect with every passing day.”

Abundance took that comment quite a bit better, though his eyes centered on the table, embarrassed with the collective attention.

Jack smiled. Right answer. I think she does get it, after all. But man, do I not envy her having to manage Corroline. I guess I get it, though. She’s too good of a damage-dealer for a versatile stall-machine like Ten.

Abundance finally found his voice. “It was wrong what he did, and he couldn’t even see that after the fact. We made a deal. What good is any of this if we can’t trust each other? We have to draw the line somewhere. Expediency only gets you so far.”

Jack nodded. “I find I agree. Seems we both suffered for what he did, but my team did alright despite it. Hope the same for you lot.”

Corroline smiled in a suddenly pantherine way. “So much in common, you and us.” It was said with flippant sarcasm. “Care to defect to us, then? There’s an open slot, after all.”

To this, X-Tender dropped her head and shook it with long-suffering airs.

Jack blinked and regarded Corroline in surprise. Despite her flippant tone, it sounded like a serious offer. “After your impassioned tirade telling me off right in my face, you say this?”

Corroline shrugged with one shoulder. “You made an impression with your grip around my throat. I hated you a lot then; I hate you a little less now. I can see what’s plain in front of my eyes. You might be just the piece of shit we need to push through.” She somehow made ‘piece of shit’ sound like a term of endearment. “It’s worth a shot. Care to prove you’re man enough to win with anyone?”

Jack remained in disbelief. He cleared his throat. “Thank you, but I must politely decline.”

Corroline shrugged as if she couldn’t care less, picking up a drink with a straw in it and sipping, eyes shifting outward, ignoring him.

Meanwhile, X-Tender looked up at Jack with eyes that communicated mortification. “I’m sorry about her, Ferro. She’s weird.”

Jack smiled it off as he rose, waving a hand. “No worries. A Controller is a good flex spot, I admit.” After a pause, he added as casually as possible, “A Stalker is another that would be good with you three. Plenty of distracting members, and they’re good at fading away after wounds, which can be healed.”

X-Tender held his eyes. “I’ll keep an eye out. See you around. Thanks for clearing the air. Really.”

“No problem.” Jack suppressed a wince as he nodded and turned away. Shit. I shouldn’t have done that. She’s too sharp.

Ah, what am I even thinking, inflicting the curse of Corroline on Inky? Then again, if anyone could handle her nonsense like a champ, it might be him. She won’t rattle him no matter how hard she tries, and she might actually come to respect that. She claimed she wanted a rougher character. He’s among the roughest I’ve met.

Only time would tell if that was a possibility, so Jack cast it from his mind. He headed to his quarters, wondering how much longer he’d even be living there. He’d earned his jacket.

I’ve earned my smeggin’ jacket! This is incredible!

It was still just hitting him. He wanted to gush to Neex, but she was already asleep. Despite everything, after Jack settled down, watching tv, he passed out cold.

<Central Processing wishes to officially acknowledge your prowess, skill, and fitness to serve, to extend and award you the title of Agent Nonpareil.

Congratulations, Soldier, and welcome aboard the next stage of your career!

But you still have yet to attain the final proof. The official ceremony is slated for tomorrow morning at 0900 hours, where you’ll be personally jacketed by our two highest officials. These quarters will be permanent, though some choose to purchase separate, additional outside housing. Report to Central Processing 5-10 minutes early, with your full and dressed uniform impeccably presented, medals included.>

Jack smiled. Command. That was not the same as CPHQ, which was more like its own whole building in size, though lodged within the tower. Very few ever got to see Command, he was sure. It was something barely ever mentioned, but known as where the biggest of the Big Wigs operated. Certainly not up top in Nimrod. It was deep in the structure, like much of Central Processing. Blind without remote eyes. But there were plenty of those.

It was truly about to be a new era. And even that ‘home’ wasn't likely to be one he’d be parked at for long. The trip he was destined to take loomed closer each day.

Jack poked over at Neex through the bond and found her awake. He could resist gushing no longer. “Neex! I have imminent and fantastic news, my Suction-Cupped Wonder!”

Neex got preliminarily excited by these words. “What?! What is it, come on, tell me, Jack! Tell me now!”

“Yours Truly… has obtained… Agent Nonpareil status! I’m a bona fide Non! I’m getting a jacket tomorrow!”

I’m giving you a new title…” She became suddenly dead-serious and solemn as she declared, “Fast Obtainer of Jackets.” After a brief pause, she added, “You’ve already got two special ones!”

All the time, as Ira and she still hadn’t really changed — she was who she was inside.
