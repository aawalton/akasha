I nudged my bedroom door open with my toe and stepped inside, followed by Rex. His eyes were fixed on the plate in my hand, seemingly convinced whatever I was carrying was for him, and I didn’t have it in my heart to tell him otherwise. I’d just have to give him a bit, I decided. He couldn’t have much, though. I was pretty sure bread wasn’t good for dogs. Or was that just birds?

Hesitation washed over me, and I felt my eyebrows draw together as I tried to remember, but I couldn’t. I knew that he’d probably eaten worse before, so I doubted a little bit of bread would be that bad for him. It wasn’t chocolate or anything else that everyone knew was poisonous for dogs, so it would probably be okay, but I didn’t want to risk it.

Coming to a stop in the middle of my room, concern lapped at me. I couldn’t help but pull out my phone and Google it quickly, scanning the results as Rex continued to stare up at me. Apparently, it was fine. As long as he didn’t have too much, and he had plenty of water to wash it down, it was okay for him to have some bread. Luckily, he had a bowl in my room, and it was still full, so it should be fine.

A smile pulled at my lips as I sank down onto my bed again and waited for Rex to jump up next to me. He sniffed at my plate eagerly before gazing into my eyes, and I felt something in me break. The urge to give him an entire slice of bread, followed by the other two pieces, gripped me, and I started to reach towards it before catching myself.

The internet had said he could only have a little bit, I reminded myself. As much as I wanted to feed him everything on my plate, I couldn’t. It would be bad for him, and that was more important.

“I’m sorry, Rexie,” I told him softly as I ripped the corner off one of my slices of toast. “You can only have a little bit, okay?”

His expression seemed to grow sadder, and I felt a band tighten around my heart, but I forced myself not to give in to his wide eyes.

“It’s bad for you,” I said. “This is all you can have. Take it gently.”

Rex sniffed the piece of bread I held out to him before taking it so cautiously that his fur didn’t even touch my fingers.

“Good boy,” I crooned, stroking his head as he chewed.

I wiped my fingers on my leg before picking up the slice and taking a bite. Rex watched the toast, his gaze longing, before letting out a huff. He stood slowly before walking further along the bed and dropping down. A soft laugh slipped through my lips as he curled up at my feet with his back to me.

“Aww, I’m sorry,” I told him, but Rex did not respond in any way.

Torn between feeling guilty about not being able to give Rex more of my food and finding his sulking adorable, I continued eating. It didn’t take long for me to get bored, though. Within a few bites, I could feel the need for something more pulling at me.

I had nothing to entertain myself with. That was the problem. There was nothing for me to focus on, other than my food, and that wasn’t enough. Rex seemed to be drifting off to sleep already, and I didn’t want to disturb him, so instead, I found myself looking around my room.

My eyes landed on the book I’d left on my bed, and I started to reach for it before freezing. Although I knew it would probably distract me, I was reluctant to pick it up again. It wasn’t that interesting, and it would be hard to read with one hand. Normally, I had no problem using only one hand, but the book was too thick. It would be too heavy to lift, and I was tempted to just hold it on my lap, but it would end up covered in crumbs.

That was a bad idea, I decided as I pulled my phone out of my pocket again and tapped on the ebook app. I scrolled through my options, trying to work out what I wanted to read, but nothing was appealing to me. I clicked on one of the books I’d started reading in Scotland and got halfway through before moving on to another, but I only got through one page before boredom began to grow within me again, and I closed it.

Taking another bite of toast, I tapped on the Instagram app and started to scroll through. That wasn’t enough to keep my focus, though. I didn’t really care about the majority of the posts on my home page, and I needed something more.

The urge to text Phoebe or Duncan pulled at me, and I searched through my phone for the messages app before hesitating. They were both busy. Phoebe had texted me at some point that evening to say she had just started packing before her flight in the morning, and it wasn’t going well, so I knew she wouldn’t be able to reply, and I was pretty sure Duncan was busy cleaning his house. It had seemed pretty clean when I’d been in it earlier, but he said he planned on cleaning it more before his parents got home tomorrow, so he was probably doing that.

Either that, or he’d already fallen asleep. He hadn’t texted me for a few hours, so he might have just gotten an early night. I wasn’t sure, but I knew that I couldn’t text him. I felt like I already texted him a lot, and I didn’t want to bug him too much in case he got bored of me.

I dropped my head back against the headboard, continuing to eat. Even though I’d buttered it, the toast was dry and mostly flavourless. I felt as though I was eating cardboard, but I forced myself to keep going as restlessness churned within me, and my eyes scoured my room in search of something else to focus on.

It wouldn’t be long until I finished my dinner, I told myself. I was almost finished with the first slice, and I only had two more to go after that. The last one was pretty small too, so it wouldn’t take long. I just needed to find something to distract myself with until then because I couldn’t just sit there and do nothing.

Maybe it shouldn’t be that hard, I told myself. I just had to sit there quietly, doing nothing for five minutes. It probably wouldn’t even take that long, and that felt like it should be manageable for me. I should have been able to be alone with my thoughts for a few minutes without wanting to scream or tear my skin off, but the boredom was too much. The lack of anything to do was unbearable.

Perhaps Mom was right. She always said that my phone had rotted my brain, that spending all day glued to that thing destroyed my attention span and would make it impossible for me to be a normal, functioning human, and I hadn’t really believed her before. I didn’t spend that much time on my phone, not compared to her, but maybe she was right.

I had always found that difficult, though. I couldn’t really remember too much of my childhood, but I was pretty sure I’d never been able to just sit there in silence. Even in class, when I was meant to be listening to the teacher or waiting for everyone else to finish with something, I’d struggled. It was why I spent so much time daydreaming before I started going to the other worlds, or whatever it was that I was doing.

A sigh escaped me before I took another bite of the bland toast. Maybe I could just do that again. I was a little apprehensive of leaving my reality whilst eating, but perhaps it wouldn’t be that bad. I’d definitely eaten whilst in other worlds before, and I hadn’t choked or anything, so I’d probably be fine again. I knew that, but something still held me back. The fear of being sucked into yet another terrifying world, especially when I felt like I wasn’t fully recovered from the last, lingered in the back of my mind, but I pushed it away.

It would be fine, I decided as my restlessness grew more frantic. I would be fine. I just needed to pay more attention to what was happening, and if I felt myself being dragged out of the world again, I wouldn’t let it take me. As long as I spotted it early, I could fight back before the new place had a chance to fully sink its claws into me, and I’d keep myself at the Academy. Or I’d return to my reality. Either way, I wouldn’t let myself go anywhere that I didn’t want to go.

A hint of concern bubbled in the pit of my stomach even as the room began to spin around me. I was still slightly worried about what could happen, and I knew it was risky, but I didn’t care. I just couldn’t stay in my reality for any longer. Even if something bad happened, it had to be better than being bored.

“Alright, I think we’ll have to leave it there,” I heard someone say as the world began to come into focus around me. “Now, make sure you all read up on chunking before the next class because it can be a really useful technique. If you have any questions, I can answer them next week, but for now, have a good rest of your day!”

Rustling immediately sounded around me, and I blinked quickly, trying to clear my vision. People were moving, I realised. The handful of people in the classroom with me had shut their laptops and were getting to their feet, and I hurried to do the same, but I had to grip the desk as I stood. I still felt a little unsteady on my feet, and I was worried that I’d stumble or fall over and make a fool out of myself.

I glanced around, trying to work out where I was and what I’d missed, but my eyes landed on the teacher sitting at the front of the classroom, and confusion washed over me. Quickly, I searched my memories, but that just made me more confused. I was in my observation and memorisation class. That much was clear from the room and the teacher, but I only had that class twice a week. Surely, three days hadn’t passed since I’d been ripped out of the Academy.

But it hadn’t. I had no recollection of the other days that would have happened between my first observation and memorisation class of the week and my second, and I would have had some. Even when I wasn’t actively in the world, I could still access some of the memories. They were sometimes a little hazy and hard to recall, but they were always there. That time, I had nothing.

Because three days had not passed. It had only been an hour, I realised. A hint of happiness began to bloom tentatively in my chest, but I ignored it. It didn’t feel right. I’d been in the other world for so long. It felt like the sun had set countless times as I’d stared at the skies. Empires had risen, conquered half the world, and fallen whilst I’d been sitting there, keeping watch, and even in my reality, a few hours had passed, but at the Academy, it had somehow only been an hour.

I was glad, obviously. That meant I had a class with Seth next, and I had been looking forward to that. It was our first one together, and although I’d already had cooking a few times and knew we normally worked independently, we could share a workstation. That would be almost like we were cooking together, and I was excited for that. I knew it was going to be fun.

Unless I was late, and someone else had already claimed the space next to him by the time I got there, a small voice in the back of my head whispered, prompting me into motion.

“Bye,” I called to the teacher as I moved quickly towards the door.

“Have a good day, Grace,” Cara replied with a wave.

I smiled at her, speeding up as I slipped from the room. I had to get all the way down onto the floor below where I currently was, and that would take a while. It always did because the nearest stairs were back in the foyer, and there were too many people milling around the corridor.

Weaving around them, I began hurrying towards the foyer. It wasn’t too busy, compared to how bad it usually was, but it was still hard to move as quickly as I wanted to.

“Grace!” I heard someone shout.

Immediately, I came to a stop and turned, a smile already forming on my lips. A body almost slammed into mine, and I jumped back to avoid the person rushing past.

“Sorry,” I told them with an apologetic smile before looking around for Seth.

My eyes found him easily. He was leaning against the wall in an alcove, slightly further along the corridor. I wasn’t sure how long he’d been there, but it seemed like he was waiting for me, and that realisation made my stomach tighten.

It made sense. His last classroom was only a few doors down from mine, and he knew we had the same class next, so perhaps I should have expected him to be waiting for me, but I hadn’t. I just assumed he wouldn’t.

“Hey,” I said as he made his way over to me.

“Hey! How was class?” he asked.

I hesitated for just a moment before replying.

“Fine.”

Even I could tell that wasn’t convincing, and Seth threw his head back as a laugh spilled from his lips. I couldn’t help but join in. His laughter was too contagious. It was so free and filled with joy, and the sound of it just made me want to do the same.

“You sure about that?” he asked, still chuckling slightly.

“Yeah. It wasn’t too bad, just…” I trailed off, unsure how to finish the sentence.

I didn’t want it to seem like I was complaining. That might have made it seem like I wasn’t grateful that I had the opportunity to be in at the Academy, and I was. I was really grateful. I was just exhausted, and my brain was tired. It felt like it needed to shut down and reboot.

“I get it,” Seth said with an empathetic smile.

“Yeah… How was first aid, though?” I asked.

Seth’s smile turned more genuine, more filled with happiness.

“It was fine,” he said, sending me a grin before adding, “Actually fine, though. I don’t know. I wasn’t really sure at first, but I really like the class. Like, I find it really interesting, you know?”

“Yeah, I get it.”

“I mean, it’s kind of gross sometimes, but I don’t really mind that,” he explained, hesitating before continuing. “I keep thinking that maybe… I would have become a doctor if I hadn’t been brought here.”

I felt my lips pull up into a smile. I could imagine him being a doctor. He’d be a good one. I was sure of that. He was kind, and there was something calming about him. I wasn’t sure what it was exactly, but it just seemed like he didn’t really get stressed out or bothered by much, and I felt like that was a good trait for a doctor.

“You could still be one,” I suggested.

“Yeah, maybe,” he replied, his tone soft and thoughtful.

Seth didn’t speak as we emerged into the foyer. It seemed as though he was lost in his thoughts, and I didn’t want to disturb him, especially if he was actually considering that. I didn’t really know how it worked, but I knew some trainees were specialising in medicine. It was rare, but I had a vague memory of meeting someone who had said they were. Perhaps if I remembered who it was, we could always find them, and Seth could ask them a few questions. That might be kind of helpful.

“You’ve already had a cooking class since being out of the induction wing, right?” Seth asked.

His voice no longer had the thoughtful quality that it had held moments before, and I blinked quickly, drawing myself out of my own head.

“Uh, yeah. A couple.”

I hadn’t actually been in the world for any of them, as far as I could remember, but I knew I’d had some.

“Oh, cool,” Seth replied. “This is my first one. Are they any good?”

I cocked my head to the side, considering his question, as we stepped into the stairwell. Immediately, we were met with a blast of cold air that caused goosebumps to break out on my arms. For some reason, the staircase was always freezing, regardless of the temperature outside.

“Yeah, they’re pretty good,” I told Seth. “But I mean, I like cooking, so I think that helps.”

“Oh yeah? Do you do it much?” Seth asked, pausing before rephrasing his question. “I mean, did you? Like, before…”

He trailed off, looking uncomfortable, and it took me a moment to realise why. He was trying to avoid mentioning my life before the Academy. That was what we’d discussed before. We’d said we were going to pretend none of that had ever happened, but it was hard to ask about something like cooking without acknowledging it.

I wasn’t sure how to answer Seth, though. I had a smattering of memories of being in the kitchen at home, trying to make something edible whilst constantly peering over my shoulder to check whether my mom was coming in to yell at me, but I couldn’t figure out which world they were from. They seemed to be a blend of my reality and the world I was in, but there was no way to know for sure.

“Kind of,” I said carefully. “I mean, I’ve never cooked anything too… like, fancy, but I can manage, you know?”

“Yeah, I get that.”

“What about you?” I asked, trying to figure out a good way to phrase my question. “Have you… You’ve not had a cooking class here yet?”

He’d already said it was his first one, so I knew he hadn’t, but I couldn’t work out how to ask him whether he cooked a lot without breaking our agreement.

“No, not yet,” Seth replied, his smile telling me he appreciated my attempt. “I’m kind of excited, though.”

“Oh yeah?”

“Yeah. I mean, I’m looking forward to having a class with you, and I’ve not had too much experience in the kitchen before. Like I can boil an egg or make pasta or something super basic, but that’s about it, so it’ll be fun to have a chance to play around more, you know?” he explained quickly.

I had to fight not to grin when Seth said he was looking forward to being with me, but it was difficult. I pressed my lips together, trying desperately to hide the happiness that soared within me and caused my heart to skip a beat.

“Yeah, I get that,” I replied, relieved to hear that my voice sounded fairly normal.

Seth smiled, glancing at me out of the corner of his eye. I wasn’t certain, but it seemed like his cheeks were a little red.

“So what kind of thing do you do in the class?” he asked as we exited the stairwell and began walking down the brightly lit corridor. “Do you just focus on one dish each class, or is it more… techniques?”

“Uhh, mostly just a dish,” I told him, searching my memories to confirm. “I mean, last time, we were taught how to make a stir fry, which was pretty easy, but apparently it’s a pretty good dish for getting a bunch of vegetables and protein in, you know?”

“Oh, cool. That makes sense.”

“Yeah. The tutor said the whole point of the class is just to teach how to survive if we ever need to cook for ourselves for a week while out on assignment,” I recalled. “Like, they’re not trying to teach us how to become professional chefs or anything. That would be kind of suspicious.”

Seth cocked his head to the side.

“Huh, I guess so,” he replied. “I’d never really thought about that, but I guess I would be kind of confused if I saw some kid with flawless knife skills or something. It’s this way, right?”

I glanced down the corridor that Seth had pointed towards before nodding.

“Yeah, it’s just around the next corner,” I told him before returning to our conversation. “I’d probably want to know where they learnt that from if I saw a kid doing that, but I guess we could just lie and say we watched a video on YouTube or something?”

Seth grinned.

“For sure. I bet there are a bunch of tutorials on there,” he agreed. “I mean, I bet some are rubbish, but some have to be not too bad, right?”

“Probably,” I said. “I think Gillian did say there was an option to do a more advanced class at some point, though.”

I could vaguely remember hearing her say that, but I couldn’t recall any more details than that.

“Oh, really?” Seth asked, looking intrigued.

“Yeah, I don’t really know what we’d do in it, but I guess that would be more knife skills and complex dishes or something. I don’t know,” I said with a shrug.

Seth’s expression turned thoughtful again.

“Huh, that could be fun,” he mused. “I’ve always kind of wanted to be able to make something really impressive. Like a… soufflé or something.”

My eyebrows drew together.

“A soufflé?” I repeated.

“Yeah,” Seth said, laughing awkwardly and running a hand through his hair as a blush appeared on his cheeks. “I mean, I don’t really know what they are, but I feel like I heard or read about one somewhere. Apparently, they’re really hard and impressive to cook, so it would be cool to be able to just do it, you know?”

I had no idea what a soufflé was. I couldn’t remember if I’d ever had one, but the word brought the faint memory of a texture. It was fluffy and light, strangely eggy, but not necessarily in a bad way. That was all I recalled, and I wasn’t sure where that information had even come from, but I had to reply to Seth. His expression was unbothered, but he was still watching me out of the corner of his eye.

“Yeah, I get that,” I told him as we turned another corner. “It would be a cool skill to just have.”

Seth’s smile widened, the anxiety draining from his face.

“Wouldn’t it?” he asked before pointing towards the door that had just come into view. “That one’s the kitchen, right?”

“Yeah, that’s us.”
