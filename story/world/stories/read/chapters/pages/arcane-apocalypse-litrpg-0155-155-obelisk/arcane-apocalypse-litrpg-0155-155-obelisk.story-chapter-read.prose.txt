It was larger than she thought it would be, a towering chunk of black stone with all four sides polished to a shine. It appeared to be a single block of obsidian, reaching approximately 4 metres in height at its tip.

Of course, it wasn’t obsidian. Mia couldn’t feel the intricacies of the magic flowing through the structure, but she could feel the power it radiated. The Obelisk felt … regal, for lack of a better word. As if it were a king and all the ambient mana within kilometres bowed before it in supplication. It was a strange sensation.

Mia shook her head, quickly returning her focus to the last obstacle standing before her and the Obelisk: a cordon and a group of guards. They surrounded the Obelisk, looking — glaring, really — at anyone looking at it. And there were a lot of people looking at it; the entire courtyard of the Castle was filled with onlookers, though only a scant few of them were Level 10, thus actually able to make use of the thing.

Honestly, it looked like the Obelisk had turned into a tourist attraction overnight. Curious. Mia would have barred passage to the castle to everyone not on a short white list if she were in Zeigler’s shoes, but he probably knew better. Maybe he wanted to raise morale? Show the people that they really won? Hell, if she knew.

“Do you feel like brawling your way through that mob?” Gabriel asked, looking over the mass of bodies with a mild frown.

“Why?” Mia asked.

“I could do a Moses and open a way,” he said. “Or do you want to go get permission from this General, or whatever?”

“It would be a bit more proper,” Brent said. “It’s not like he can not give us permission, but undermining his authority for no reason other than convenience would be … just causing trouble for no reason.”

“My existence undermines his authority," Gabe said, then shrugged. “Oh well, alright. I have to talk to him anyway.”

“Why?” Mia asked curiously.

“I made a quick stop in Vienna before coming here,” Gabe said. “Met this Marshal of theirs who’s apparently taken over the Austrian armed forces, and he asked me to deliver a letter. Oh, and he is like me, though he is lagging a couple of dozen Levels behind me.”

“Like you?” Mark asked. “Another one the System kidnapped?”

“There are supposed to be hundreds from across the globe,” Gabe said. “Though most of them are probably only Rank 1s, maybe a few 2s and a handful of 3s like me. I was … very lucky. Advancement is just as much about luck, politics and such in the other Realms as it is about fighting monsters. No, fighting monsters, and especially delving into the Rifts, is a privilege that is controlled by those in power.”

“That’s … I will process that information later,” Mia said, shaking off the distracting thoughts trying to burrow into her mind, nudging her down another spiral. “Okay, let’s go bother the General a bit then.”

“That won’t be needed,” a familiar voice spoke up from behind them, sounding drier than the Sahara. “You all have permission by default. I will have the men open up a path for you. No need for anything more overt.”

Mia turned around, blinking at the man. Major … something, he was Zeigler’s aide, that she could remember, but his name escaped her. Stupid Memory attribute was slacking. She’d always been bad with names, but not this bad. Maybe it was all the chaos that was to blame, but that felt like an excuse even to her.

Whatever happened after was pretty much ignored. Mia kept herself in some semblance of calm, but she was bouncing off the walls in excitement. Finally, finally she could continue the climb. This Rank-Up was long overdue.

The aide’s men opened up a path, nudging the crowd aside and letting Mia’s group pass through, along with the other handful of Level 10s who’d gathered around them, all of whom looked familiar enough to Mia. Probably people she recalled from the fight against the Goblins or the cleanup afterwards.

“I’ll be back shortly,” Gabe said. “Take your time. You may spend as much time inside as you need, and barely any will pass outside.”

Mia nodded absently, and she was just about to ask what the hell he meant by ‘inside’ when one of the braver ones strode right up to the Obelisk and pressed his hand up to its polished surface. The outline of his palm glowed, and then he was … sucked inside with an undignified yelp.

“It’s safe,” Gabe said. “Yes, it’s supposed to do that.”

Well, alright then. Without wasting another second, Mia stepped forward too, alongside half a dozen other people, and touched the Obelisk.
