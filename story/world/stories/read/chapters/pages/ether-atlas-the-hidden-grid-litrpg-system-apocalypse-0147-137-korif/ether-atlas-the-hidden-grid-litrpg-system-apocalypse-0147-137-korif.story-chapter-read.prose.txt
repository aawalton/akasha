THE ETHER ATLAS: FIELD NOTES OF STEVEN PARR

The Temple of Aphaia, Aegina

AUTOPSIA

My discipline's founding word is autopsia. Seeing for oneself. Herodotus built the method in one scene: Candaules tells Gyges that men trust their ears less than their eyes, and the whole edifice of inquiry has stood on that ever since. The eyewitness at the top, the hearsay at the bottom, and what followed was twenty-five centuries of us grading every source by its distance from a working pair of eyes. I have enforced that ranking my entire career. I have written "the author cannot have seen this" in a margin and considered the argument closed.

The old stories rank it the other way, and I have taught that as irony. Tiresias is blinded and given prophecy, and I taught the blindness as a poet's compensation, a tragedian's symmetry. Phineus, the same. The three Graeae sisters had one eye between them and saw more than armies. Odin gives an eye at Mimir's well and considers found wisdom cheap for the price. Homer himself comes down to us blind, as though the tradition wanted its greatest seer to have no eyes on principle. A whole corpus insisting that true sight begins where the eye stops seeing, and I filed the entire thing as a literary device, because what else could it be.

This morning a creature the size of a fishing boat switched off my eyes as an instrument. It did not hide itself; instead it stopped emitting what eyes collect, and four sighted people stood in full morning light staring at an emptiness that was three metres away. The one person who found it was not using her eyes. She read the room with an older instrument, and everything that happened afterwards, including my continuing to be alive, followed from her reading without her eyes.

So here is the correction I owe the corpus. The eye is not the top of the hierarchy. The eye is the loudest instrument on the desk and it is cheapest to fool. Any surface that manages light manages the eye. The traditions that blinded their seers were not being poetic. They were explaining the procedure. Take the jammable instrument off the desk, and the quiet ones underneath it can finally be heard.

And this temple, of all temples, belonged to Aphaia. The one who vanished, worshipped on this hill for a thousand years by people who never once expected to lay eyes on her and did not consider that a defect in the arrangement. I have called that sort of cult a pious abstraction in print. It has today stopped looking like an abstraction and started looking like a specification.

The honest counter-case, before I convert on one morning's evidence: most blindness in the record is only blindness. The beggars at every ancient gate saw nothing and prophesied nothing. One should    not build a church on a single data point, even a data point that recently tried to eat one.
