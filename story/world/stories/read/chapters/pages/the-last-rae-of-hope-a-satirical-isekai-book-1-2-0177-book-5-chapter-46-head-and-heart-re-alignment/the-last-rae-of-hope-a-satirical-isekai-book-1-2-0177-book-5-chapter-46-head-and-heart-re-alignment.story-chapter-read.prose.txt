Nora was already working on it, her staff tracing patterns as the white crystal on it brightened. Her magical filtration helped. Not completely, mind you, but enough that breathing became something you could do without worrying about your stomach heaving with each inhale. Since Aurum was particularly irritated by the smell, I dismounted.

“I don't think we're going to charge in blind,” I said as I gathered up his reins and put a hand to his lead rope. “The least I can do is not make you carry me.”

A welter of demons fizzled in behind us, setting up as defense against the inevitable, though hopefully disoriented, charge from the Order of White still out on the plains. The main issue, however, was that we changed plans.

“What do you see?” I called to Olethros. “Any way we can use another exit for civilians?”

“The outer city seems devoid of them entirely,” Olethros said, floating from somewhere above street level. “Not entirely unexpected; however, I do believe we are in need of some additional information.”

I looked up and adopted a scowl. He had one arm extended, and a nearby tendril of dark magic held a very irritated Skreethi at arm’s length.

“What’s he saying, other than to put him down?”

“He keeps repeating that he’s only seen murderers.” Olethros tilted his head and added, defensively, “I’m holding him by the scruff up here to calm him down. He couldn’t even form words a few moments ago.”

A brass horn sounded from deeper into the city, and sure enough, the Skreethi in his grip screeched, spraying His Dark Majesty at point-blank range.

I should probably feel a little sorry for him, but I’ll settle for keeping my mouth shut—so I don’t breathe any of that in.

Tetora went rigid. “The Order of Blue’s call. No wonder they’re stirred up!”

Every skreethi in the vicinity, from the ones Nora had been slowly approaching to herd them away, to the ones who had been regarding us with professional curiosity, all went taut simultaneously. Then they started spraying again.

“The Blue hunted them,” I said, the pieces connecting. “For pelts!”

No wonder they went overboard!

Olethros slowly set the Skreethi down, tail pointed away, then shook himself out, glitching as he cleared his coating of stench. “I will remove the source of agitation.” He then extended his hand, clawed his fingers, and concentrated.

Nothing happened.

His eyes opened in momentary confusion.

“Over there,” he declared resolutely, pointing down an alleyway.

A lone holy knight of Blue shambled from the shadows, holding the brass horn. He took exactly ten slow, measured steps, then stopped dead, as if waiting for his next command. His eyes were hidden behind a solid visor decorated with a jewel of Ereborite, void of even the tiniest pinholes. His pale lips, however, were exposed. Just as he puckered them, Tetora sprang forward, stealing the horn from his hands with a swift swipe before retreating. Nora flung a gemstone at the soldier, but to our surprise, the Ereborite jewel disappeared into the surface of the visor, such that the gemstone was unable to latch on to anything to negate. As the gemstone bounced on cobblestones, the soldier continued the motion as if nothing had happened, blowing air between pursed lips.

“They’re evolving?” Nora asked with a hiss.

“Not very well, if that’s all they can do,” Tetora muttered, sniffing as shallowly as he could.

Aurum and I watched as the soldier continued to shuffle down the street, pausing every ten steps to blow his non-existent horn.

This is all stench and mirrors.

The real question is where are all the people?

“Perhaps the inner sanctum,” I murmured to myself, knowing there was no way it could house them all.

Another soldier stumbled towards us from a different alley, his sword sheathed. Nora frowned, then almost negligently tossed a gemstone at him. The ereborite jewel in his forehead retracted further into his head just as the other had, and he didn’t so much as even blink as the gemstone smacked him in the face.

Nora frowned. “This is going to make things harder—”

At the sound of her voice, the soldier stopped and inhaled sharply, pointing at her with an accusing forefinger.

“Demon Queen Nora!” it rasped loudly.

Nora’s face went through several descending layers of disgust. “Queen?!”

“Absolutely not!” Olethros declared with the same energy.

“Guys!” I shouted. “Focus! This is most definitely a trap—”

Thoom!

I cursed myself as a large silver magic circle manifested itself in front of us. Several runes gleamed into existence, their light flaring upwards as a silvery veil split apart, revealing none other than a full-plated Captain Corwin of the Holy Order of Silver and Mother Liora, gowned in a glowing robe of gold and white.

“As promised, Captain Corwin!” Liora crowed gleefully, religious fervor carved into her features. She pointed at us. “Your fated confrontation with the Demon Queen and her demonic retainers!”

Tetora gave Olethros a wicked side-glance as he adjusted his iron claws. “I guess you were overthrown?”

Olethros stiffened, pulling together precious metals for another staff. “Better than married off to the likes of her.”

Captain Corwin still stood in the dissipating light of the magic circle. To my astonishment, he raised his visor, his eyes alert but confused.

He’s still himself?

“Captain Corwin!” I shouted. “Mother Liora is not to be trusted! I don’t believe for a moment that Pravum and Procul’s murders weren’t premeditated by—”

Mother Liora stepped back as he started to advance, her hands clasped in front of her.

The others formed up around me. “You didn’t see? On the battlefield, the angels—”

Then he turned to Liora, but she was faster.

“What—”

The ereborite jewel was already in her hand, and she flung it between his eyes before he could complete his pivot.

“Fear not, mighty Captain Corwin,” she said, her voice flushed with warmth. “Fulfill your Purpose, and you will be reunited with Sonea in Paradise.”

She smiled openly at all of us as the inscribed circle swallowed her, the runes dimming as she escaped.

Corwin didn’t have time to react to any of it. His jewel didn’t settle into his brow so much as burned, black vapor igniting him. Just as it had done for Marcus back in the castle dungeons, the instant conversion to animus overload corroded his armor in chunks.

“Sonea!” he screamed, over and over.

Nora’s barrier went up the moment Corwin’s aura ignited.

To her credit, it held for at least three seconds. The mixed overload crashed against it, amity and animus blasting simultaneously. She had imbued her defense with amity as well, given that both crystals on her staff were blazing, but whatever amplitude mix Corwin was giving off hit its resonant frequency and was then destroyed.

“Nora!” I shouted upon seeing her stagger.

“I’m fine!” she snapped, even as Aurum shielded her. “Keep him busy!”

Olethros moved next. Tendrils shot out from the ground around the enraged knight and wrapped around his torso. For a few moments, Corwin struggled, slowing. Then his overloading aura simply burned through the grip, the tendrils dissolving at contact points.

Tetora rushed in before Corwin could resume his advance. His glowing iron claws raked across an exposed shoulder. The impact staggered him half a step, and for a moment he froze in a momentary electric blue haze, but then again, the mix of gold and midnight aura flared, reanimating Corwin. The Knight Captain shoved Tetora away with the flat of his sword and simultaneously kicked with inhumane strength, and then zeroed in on me.

“Captain recognizes Captain,” I noted, giving ground even as I watched him for any openings.

Nothing.

Compassionate Smackdown wouldn’t work on him without incapacitating him first.

Our first exchange of blows confirmed my worst suspicions. Even as he burned alive and dissolved, his technique was better than mine, and blocking it would demand my full attention.

Faith caught his first overhead strike and redirected it wide. The force still drove me back several steps across the wet cobblestones.

He came again with a diagonal slash from the right. I caught it with both Will and Faith, jarred again by the impact. His rhythm was erratic, almost as if he didn’t know what his next strike would be. Haymaker, feint—they were all rather poorly timed.

In my periphery, Nora glowed, muttering something I couldn’t make out.

Corwin pressed forward with a low sweep that forced me left, then immediately reversed with an upward cut. Another short step back taught me he was herding me toward an alley wall.

Nothing doing, Corwin!

Tetora moved closer to Nora.

Corwin’s unfocused gaze flickered toward them.

“Eyes on me, Corwin!” I shouted, striking purposefully at his sword to draw his attention, then sidestepping right to cut off his line of sight to them.

Whatever they’re up to, I’m sure it’s better than me having to stab my friend.
