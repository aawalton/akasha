I flew through the air, world whizzing by, the open tower window above my singular focus.

My Aero Shift started from basically standing still, so I'd come out of it with no momentum. The window had a small exterior landing behind a railing that became clearer as I neared.

I pulled out of the Shift perfectly, right in the window. My feet skidded to a stop. I readjusted my aim, pulling the bowstring back, and scanned the room rapidly.

She's here.

Pirhani stood in the small room looking out the far window.

Alone.

I loosed my arrow at her head. Then another. And another.

She didn't even budge.

The arrows vanished in thin air the moment they should have connected. The first. Then the second and third.

Pirhani turned to me, eyes bristling with a bizarre mix of rage and… entertainment? Her featureless face contorted as she laughed wickedly.

"Oh, Zephyr… You really thought that would hurt me?"

I cast Life Drain, but the smoke evaporated like the arrows. She laughed louder, almost maniacal. Ranged attack ineffective… next strategy. I Aero Shifted at her, fingers of my carapaced hand going for her chest.

Pirhani ripped me out of my Shift. It felt like I'd been clotheslined at the neck. My head jerked to a stop in her grasp, the rest of my body flailing like a rag doll. Her hand clenched around my throat, long pointed digits digging into my spine.

I kicked my legs as she lifted me into the air, still cackling. My fingers scratched at any part of her I could reach. My legs pounded against her torso. It all amounted to nothing. Not so much as a smudge on her impeccable white gown.

I gagged and coughed as she squeezed tighter around my trachea, impeding my airflow. I tried Aero Shifting to escape, but it just resulted in my legs flopping around, neck never leaving Pirhani's clutches.

What the hell was I thinking? I was no match for a god.

She wasn't supposed to be able to hurt me.

Even so, I'd known how this could end… But I hadn't really known. I hadn't thought it would go this way. Hubris… Main character syndrome. Whatever you wanted to call it, I was guilty. I'd tried everything. All my skills. Every attempt refuted. Every—

Wait. No. I haven't tried everything.

My armored arm tingled as I called forth my scythe. The shaft rippled into existence in my hand, the blade flicked out from my forearm and weaved up to its place at the head of the weapon.

Pirhani stopped laughing and raised a non-existence eyebrow.

I swung the scythe into her chest, screaming out the last breath of air from my lungs.

The blade twanged off her chest, the vibration of the impact reverberating up my arm. My fingers stung and reactively released the weapon, allowing it to clang to the stone floor.

Pirhani looked at me, pity in her glowing gaze, as my Health began to drop from the lack of oxygen. "You truly are a persistent little insect, aren't you?"

I grunted an acknowledgement as I attempted to summon items. Defense boosts, area-attacks, Health recovery, transports, even anything unappraised. Nothing appeared, like my inventory had been disconnected somehow.

"But you know," Pirhani purred. "The only thing more satisfying that squishing you right now, would be doing it in the face of your pathetic master." She winked at me, then we were zooming through the air. I reached back for my scythe, but it was already far behind. I dropped my hand in defeat, strung along by the neck.

It was nothing like moving through Aero Shift, more that reality was warping around us. We propelled into a tunnel of black like a subway train. Lines of light strobed along the walls. Were they walls? I didn't know. My mind was getting fuzzy, I hardly knew if I could trust my own eyes anymore.

We burst through the end of the tunnel and were suddenly standing in a torchlit stone room. Unfamiliar runes glowed in a circle on the floor beneath where Pirhani stood, still holding me effortlessly by the neck.

The runes dimmed and I glanced around the room. It looked like it could've been a throne room in a medieval castle. There were nine other inactive runic circles along the wall beside the one we were one.

A dozen or so people stared at us.

No, not people.

Skull faced beings in black robes.

The same monsters that had mutilated my body and orchestrated my transition to the Umbral Plane all that time ago. I wanted to jump from Pirhani's hold, tear them to shreds, crush their brittle heads to bone dust. But I couldn't move, too weak to even kick my legs anymore.

"Uh… Pirhani? What's—" one of the skulls started, but stopped himself. He stood by an unfamiliar map on the wall. Not Penumbra. Not Earth. A number of red circles commanded attention against the drawn gray landscape. Nine of them.

The goddess held a long finger to where her lips should've been and hushed the skull face.

Footsteps on stone sounded from an adjacent hall, like thunder against the silence of the room. A figure walked in, taller than the skulls. Faceless, but for the eyes. White vestment with gold ornamentation. A crown of thorns upon his head.

Another god.

He stopped dead in his tracks, looking up from a scroll as he saw Pirhani. Then he looked to me. His pupil-less, glowing eyes changed briefly to shock, then displayed an emotion I never thought I'd see in a god. Sadness? But it was gone in a fraction of a second, so fast I wasn't sure I'd truly interpreted it correctly, especially in my state.

The god looked back to my captor. "Pirhani, what have you done?"

Pirhani scoffed. "Only what you all are too weak minded to do."

The runes beneath us activated again and we were sucked away into the dark tunnel.

In seconds, we whooshed into in the center of the colosseum's arena, dust blowing up in a swirl around us. The same runes burned into the ground under Pirhani's feet before fading away as if they were never there.

She finally released me. I fell to my knees. Hacked and coughed, rubbing my sore neck. Precious air burned as it travelled through my throat for the first time in minutes. How could something as neutral and necessary as oxygen hurt so bad?

"Zephyr?" someone asked.

I recognized the voice. Forcing myself to look up, I saw it'd been Eugene. He stood with Sibyl who had a hand over her mouth. They both stepped toward me, but I held up a hand.

"Stay back…" I croaked, my voice hoarse. "She can—"

Pirhani stepped on my back, flattening me to the dirt. "Now can someone please tell me where Valciferous is?" She scanned the crowd of onlookers. There were more here than I'd realized. Several Reapers hovered around a table and it appeared they'd been deep in conversation, strategizing.

Lancelot was there, clearly organizing the defense. And Silas nearby. Even Zeus. He'd advanced to Amethyst. I wanted to scowl at him but didn't even have the energy for that.

At the edge of the arena in the stands something slammed hard against a door. Several Reapers braced it, using whatever skills they could to bar it shut. Another door elsewhere banged, the sound of wood cracking echoed off the bowl of the arena.

"It's holding for now," someone yelled.

"You all need to run!" I shouted, voice still rough, but slowly returning to normal.

"Oh, be quiet, will you?" Pirhani hissed, squeezing her fingers into my scalp. Blood trickled down my forehead and into my left eye.

No one moved.

They knew there was no where to run. I knew it too. Unchained at the doors. Goddess amongst them.

It was over.

Pirhani pointed up to the balcony where Valciferous often sat. "Oh, Valciferous," she sang. "Come out, come out. I've got a little present for you."

Footstep scuffled softly in the dirt. I heard several scythes drawn.

Pirhani whipped her head around. "Stay back or he's dead."

"Looks like he's dead regardless," Lancelot said, a prismatic sword forming in the air above him.

Silas was beside him, several items already in hand and ready.

"You can't hurt her," I said, tasting tears as they dripped down my face to the corners of my mouth.

"Listen to your boy here. None of you have to die."

"None but him?" Lancelot asked.

"Exactly."

"See… That's the part I have a problem with."

"And me," Eugene said.

Bang. The doors thundered again as Unchained continued their assault.

"Me too," Sibyl added.

Bang.

Several others echoed the sentiment.

"And me," Silas said.

Even Zeus nodded and winked at me.

Bang.

"Well, aren't you all just the happiest little family," Pirhani mocked. "So be it."

A door creaked, but not one of the ones I was expecting. Above, the door on Valciferous' balcony swung slowly open. All attention shifted. The god stepped out, followed closely by Trix, her Sapphire cloak blowing in the breeze.

"Ah, Valciferous. Nice of you to finally join us."

The god looked down at us, stoic as ever.

To my surprise, Jericho and Calliope joined them shortly after, Nuzzle on Jericho's shoulder. They'd been up there? Meeting with Valciferous? Why?

I locked my gaze with Calliope's. She didn't look away, so neither did I. Her eyes were warring with several emotions I couldn't place. I'd expected hatred for what my actions had done to Allium, but that wasn't it. I tried to read what she was feeling. What was going on, but was interrupted.

"Pirhani," Valciferous said sternly. "It didn't have to be this way."

"It did! I told you it did. But you won't listen. No. You never listen. None of you do. You just keep all your Reapers here, letting them lead happy little lives, when they should be coming to me, to your pal Zonicus, to everyone else. We need more who can fight the high level Sins. It's so obvious yet you continue to dally here in your pretend paradise.

"And then you go and bring this cancer here?" She shoved me forward, face scraping against the ground. "What am I supposed to think?"

"It was never about you."

"It's always about me!"

"See that's your problem. That's always been your problem. Vanity. What you've done here will have ramifications across all Penumbra. You've changed the paradigm."

"I am the fucking paradigm!"

"No, Pirhani," Valciferous said, fingers wrapping around the railing of the balcony. "You are nothing."

She spat. "That's where you're wrong. You've always thought so little of me. But look at me now. Look what I've accomplished. Look what I can do."

Pirhani held up a hand, fingers expanding, growing longer into five pointed blades. Then she plunged them into my stomach.

I gasped.

Around me people screamed and ran to my aid only to be brushed back by a swipe of her other clawed hand. But I could barely register any of it, my mind reeling in pain. In memories. In what could have been.

Avery.

The life I'd fought so hard to return to all this time.

It slipped away, like water through a sieve. Gone forever.

Even this stupid dream. To fight sin and purify humanity. It'd been foolish. I was a Death Reaper. I wasn't some white knight here to save the day. I had my own demons.

And now I would never face them.

Warm, sticky blood soaked my clothes.

Pirhani gouged me again.

I felt her fingers go through my lung. My heart.

My Will.

I felt it breaking.

The goddess slid her spear-like fingers from my gut and strode in a circle like a victorious gladiator, saying something mockingly. I couldn't comprehend it. Could barely hear it. Or anything at all.

My body slumped to a knee. It wanted to fall further, to curl onto the ground and bleed out. I couldn't let it. I couldn't…

I vaguely comprehended Lancelot charging at Pirhani, only for his prismatic blade to be deflected and his body to be launched twenty feet backwards, his amor clanging as he rolled through the dirt.

I had nothing left. Maybe I could just lay down. Sleep. Let the end come.

But eyes betrayed me. Unwilling to close. My vision darkened. I looked up at the balcony, seeing nothing more than a blur of shapes. Valciferous. Jericho. Calliope. Nuzzle. Each of them had accepted me in their own way. I just wished I could've done more for them. Not let them down.

But then there was something else. Something coming at me through the air, thrown down from the balcony. Something green. A ball? A—

A Will.

Not green, but Emerald.

I didn't even need to use my Second Sight to know what that meant. They'd found a Death Affinity Will for me.

I reached a hand up—my last ounce of strength—as the red bar atop my vision reduced to a sliver.

I reached, not for the Will, but for something else. Something more distant.

And I felt it answer my call.

My Health hit zero.
