Isolde entered the housing area with Lamonte, Hector, and Tricia on her heels. The destruction within was almost non-existent compared to the rest of the sanctuary, which was only mildly reassuring, and the four of them immediately began their investigation. With no signs of forced entry or clear violence in the immediate area, they proceeded quickly—taking note of everything while heading upstairs.

It was there that they found the corpses of Jorry and Lacy.

The first thing Isolde noticed was that there was every indication the two had been in the middle of armoring themselves when they’d been set upon. Half-fastened chainmail lay across their bodies, their weapons remained near the bed, and two arrows—one for each Squire—had evidently struck them from behind before either Squire could have defended themselves.

The first they spotted immediately, embedded in the wall at the correct height to have punched through Jorry’s neck—evidenced by the immense amount of blood pooled around his corpse’s upper torso—and the second they saw impaled still in Lacy’s side, through the open seam in her armor.

“They didn’t die from the arrows,” Lamonte said grimly, moving with Tricia to examine the bodies. “Stab wounds at the base of the skull. Uniform and consistent. They died to the knives, or else the killer wouldn’t have bothered with the follow-up.”

“No signs of feeding or odd blood patterns, either,” Tricia added.

“No signs of struggle,” Hector put in from the doorway, his face twisted faintly in distaste. “These young’uns were taken out before they could do more’n realize they were dead.”

Isolde nodded in agreement as her Wardens worked, and remained silent. Leadership, she had learned, was often the art of simply permitting more experienced subordinates to express their skills and knowledge in whatever manner they found most comfortable. Some leaders may have found such competence threatening—Isolde did not. She was younger than most of them, and despite her position, far less experienced. She respected the Wardens she had come to call hers—she did not feel jealousy over their capability.

“No signs of forced entry,” Lamonte continued while rising and turning to look around the room, settling his gaze on the open window and then glancing back at the corpses. “Attacker entered through the window, perhaps,” he added, walking toward the open shutters and taking up a mimicry of an archer’s position to sight the imagined forms of the Squires as they’d been likely to have stood. “Took aim and hit them both with an arrow before they knew what was happening.”

“The kills are also inconsistent with the conflict in evidence around Sir Roderick,” Tricia added, glancing from the bodies to Lamonte and then toward the arrows. “I don’t think this was the Vampire, Captain.”

“I concur,” Isolde said with grim calm and glanced down at the dead Squires. “Which means it had an accomplice.”

“At least one,” Lamonte agreed. “We can’t rule out more than one.”

“Thrall, maybe?” Hector ventured, growling a curse under his breath. “Bloody talented Thrall, if so.”

“Few people in Blackstone could kill two Hyperian Squires this swiftly,” Tricia added, pulling out an aetherglass instrument. “This looks almost professional, Captain. The person that did this is a veteran. I’m not sure if it was a Thrall or a different kind of ally, but I think it was definitely someone else. The Vampire’s combat implied, at least, the ability to keep pace with a Paladin of Hyperion—these kills are inconsistent with that skillset.”

“And, again, no signs of feeding,” Hector said, repeating Tricia’s words. “Whoever did this didn’t share the leech’s need fer it. Very probably an accomplice.”

“Which means this isn’t just an isolated Vampire, it’s potentially a coven or something more organized,” Lamonte said grimly.

“There’s also magical residue around the arrow marks and stab wounds,” Tricia added, moving the aetherglass near the wounds. The crystal at its center clouded immediately, blackening until Tricia removed it before it could shatter from the residual energy overwhelming it. “The magic suppressed their healing. Squire Jorry’s hand is near his throat, and Squire Lacy’s is near her side. The two were trying to heal and failed, is my assessment.”

Isolde’s eyes narrowed at the words, and Tricia lifted her gaze to meet the Captain’s.

“My lady, whoever did this, they weren’t an ordinary assassin. This isn’t normal magic. I have no idea what sort of Spellform could do this, but it isn’t one I’ve encountered before. Nature magic could, theoretically, produce an anti-healing toxin, but the aetherglass didn’t read a Nature residue. Whatever this is, Captain, it’s not like anything we’ve encountered before.”

“Bloody wonderful…” Hector growled.

“We will not reach for too many conclusions prior to investigating the communal hall and chapel proper,” Isolde said before her Wardens could decide on a more dramatic hypothesis, glancing once more at Lacy and Jorry. “Have the watch come and prepare them for a proper Hyperian pyre. There’s little we can learn from these two anymore, and they deserve that much.”

“Aye, Cap,” Hector said as the group turned to leave. “Though, has to be asked: What in the pits are we dealin’ with? A leech and an assassin, both with unseen sorcery working together is bad, bad news.”

“We’ll save imperatives until we investigate further, Hector,” Isolde said as she led the other three out of the room and down the stairs. “The chapel will give us the rest of the story.”

They left the housing complex ten minutes later after confirming no other breaches in the rest of the residences, and as four they crossed the courtyard toward the communal hall. Here, at least, the devastation was more blatant—though whether that was good for their investigation or simply more disturbing in what it implied remained to be seen. Hector passed the burial message on to a nearby watchman as they walked, and then cursed when they closed on the communal hall.

“Whole section of the bloody building is cut open…” Hector rumbled. “I saw it on arrival, but this is bloody nefarious.”

“It’s consistent with the blood from elsewhere,” Tricia added analytically. “I’ve seen Aquamancers do something similar with condensed water pressure, but this is something else entirely. The blood must have been wielded with extreme precision to obliterate the building’s blessed wards like this.”

The interior was little better when they entered. Timber supports, tables, ceramic vessels, and human bodies had been sliced apart at the same height, and yet there were no marks consistent with steel, flame, or any conventional Spellforms Tricia could name. Blood, as always, was the consistent marker—it coated the walls in a wide arc, and the wound patterning across the destroyed corpses suggested a Spellform which had continued rotating as it passed through the interior.

“Hyperion’s toes…” Hector breathed in disbelief, looking around the room. “This was one blood Spellform, wasn’t it?”

“That looks likely,” Tricia confirmed while Isolde glanced at them and Lamonte let out a low, aggrieved sigh. “The momentum pattern is consistent with a single, high-powered arcing manifestation with variable rotational properties. I’ve seen writings on Aquamancers doing similar, as already observed, but again, not with blood—not like this. The fight with Sir Roderick was largely martial, I’d wager, but this… this, Captain, was pure sorcery. Whoever this Vampire is, they can wield a tremendous amount of mana.”

Isolde took that in without response as she cast her eye around the interior, tracking the implicated progress of the Spellform, and then turned at the sound of approaching footsteps.

“There were two,” Kalaria said without preamble as she joined them, entering the building and wrinkling her nose faintly at the sight within. “I found the tracks near the wall, in the dirt. Two sets of footprints; one notably larger and heavier, another lighter and smaller. The large set has to belong to the Vampire, but the second is consistent with lighter attire, and the size marks it as likely female.”

“That’s consistent with what we found in the housing,” Lamonte agreed, glancing from Kalaria to Isolde. “That makes it two attackers, Captain, unless more entered through a route that left nothing for Kalaria to track. That means we’ve got a Vamp and an accomplice, potentially female—maybe a lover or bloodbag?”

“It’s possible the Vampire enthralled an operative from the heartland,” Tricia said speculatively while her eyes roamed the devastated interior of the communal hall, and she passed the aetherglass over the residual blood to read the magic carefully. “They’re known to take a liking to beautiful people, male or female, regardless of gender. Could be that the accomplice is as much a victim as the people here, in their own way.”

“Or it’s a leechfucker,” Hector said gruffly. “I’ve seen it before, Trish. There’s all sorts of nutters that go wild for them freaks. Silly chit could be in it for the thrill of fuckin’ the corpse.”

“That’s absolutely disgusting, Hector,” Lamonte said with a look of revulsion.

“Aye,” Hector agreed, “but people can be bloody disgusting, Lamonte. It wouldn’t be the first time I’ve seen some idjit get horny for a leech, mad as it is.”

“Postulating on it gives us nothing in the moment,” Isolde interrupted before Lamonte, Kalaria, or Tricia could respond. “What we know is that there were at minimum two attackers, one wielded a sword and some sort of blood-manipulating sorcery, and the other wielded a bow, knives, and some form of unknown sorcery of their own. Everything else is conjecture, including genders. I’ve been mistaken for a man by my bootprint before.”

“Aye, Cap’n,” Hector said with a nod. “As you say. Two attackers, no genders affirmed yet.”

“The Vampire entered here,” Kalaria noted, glancing down at the floor. “There’s signs of hesitation in its steps. It stopped to kill the three here—” she looked toward the dead adolescent the two with him “—and then moved on. I’m not sure why it hesitated. Perhaps it was gloating or perhaps it had some sort of doubt, but whatever happened, it spent enough time in that spot to leave definitive markings, and then it left the same way it came in.”

“Then that leaves us with the chapel,” Lamonte finished.

“And perhaps some answers,” Isolde agreed more calmly than she felt. “Let’s go see if we can find some.”

Together, the five of them—now including the newly-returned Kalaria—made their way out of the charnel house of the communal hall and crossed the courtyard, approaching the still-smouldering remnants of the destroyed chapel. They ascended the stairs as a pentent, with Kalaria taking the lead as their best tracker, and entered the damaged building carefully. Within, the entire chapel had been destroyed. Isolde had herself come to pray there more than once, and the sight of the chapel so thoroughly obliterated faintly pained her.

“No one else has entered?” Kalaria asked.

“No one,” Lamonte confirmed. “The first responders were smart enough not to risk shuffling any evidence around.”

“The blast will have done that well enough,” Hector grumbled, “but it’s somethin’.”

“It’s going to be hard to deduce anything from this, my lady,” Tricia warned Isolde as they picked their way through the shattered pews and destroyed interior. Her eyes scanned the corpses they could see amid the ruins, and she shook her head once in frustration. “I can already see evidence of arrows exchanged, but I’m certain the blast eliminated all the relevant evidence. These bodies are half-burned and blast-tossed. I couldn’t tell you where they actually died or whether the blast or their wounds killed them.”

“At least we know there were arrows involved,” Lamonte said with an attempt at positivity. “That tells us that the Vampire may not have been immediately involved. There’s no evidence of blood patterning consistent with their magic, at least.”

“The blast could have destroyed it,” Kalaria put in, “but I’m inclined to agree with Lamonte, Captain. There would be some residue if the Vampire had been involved—I’d wager this was done by the accomplice.”

“It’s a piss-poor bit’ve certainty, but I concur, Cap,” Hector added finally. “The bodies aren’t mangled and gutted like the other poor sods. They’re burned to shit, aye, but they aren’t dismembered. I’d wager the Vamp arrived after the fact, judging by the bootprints in the soot.”

“Probably to retrieve their companion, and potentially the Priestess,” Kalaria said grimly. “Squire Gareth has been moved, based on the ashes around his position, and there are signs of post-collapse excavational work. I think the Priestess may have been taken, my lady.”

“How can you be so sure?” Lamonte asked, not in doubt, but with open interest.

“The burn-markers and ash-formations,” Kalaria explained, walking over and gesturing indicatively. “Squire Gareth took the detonation directly against his body, based on the signs on his corpse, and there’s a clean section of floor roughly large enough for someone to have been lying amid the excavation. I’d bet a crown that Gareth shielded the Priestess and died for it, then someone took her after moving his body.”

“So Lightspeaker might be alive?”

“And in duress,” Tricia agreed. “Vampire Lore is quite clear on what they do to captives. She could be drained, but that wouldn’t explain why they took her. She could be being tortured, forcibly converted, raped, or gods knows what else.”

“Fuckin’ leeches…” Hector growled in disgust.

“We will not leap to assumption of her fate until we learn more,” Isolde cut in before the mood could shift too dangerously. Her own worry threatened to rear its head, but she crushed it beneath professionalism. Emotional rationales wouldn’t help Sara. “For all we know, the Vampire and its accomplice took her for someone else. There is also a chance, however unlikely, that she escaped on her own. Until we know anything, supposition remains exactly that.”

All four Wardens nodded at her words, though Hector muttered something dissatisfied while he did. Isolde couldn’t blame him, not really. They all knew what Vampires often did to their prisoners—debasing a Priestess of Hyperion would be precisely the sort of triumph such a creature would revel in. It was an ugly, disgusting thought; one that made her own stomach twist with fury and pain both, but reality often cared little for moral reservations.

Before anyone else could speak, a new voice cut through the chapel.

“Captain Argent!”

Isolde and the others turned as Warden Abigail Morris entered the chapel, her face a mask of discipline-constrained fury.

“Morris?” Isolde asked, raising a blonde brow in curiosity.

“Captain,” Abigail greeted her again, saluting once. “I’ve got some news.”

“On the attack? Or something else?” Isolde asked.

“Something potentially related,” Abigail said with a look of restrained anger. “A guardswoman named Bonnie Gray was spotted last night traveling away from the Sanctuary with a wheeled litter and two people in accompaniment—a man in armor and a woman in dark half-plate and leathers. She apparently used Warden Warren’s name repeatedly to clear two checkpoints, and the last reports have her headed for the heart of Blackstone, toward the citadel.”

Every Warden in the room froze, and Isolde narrowed her eyes.

“Are you certain of this?”

“Absolutely, my lady,” Abigail said angrily. “I know Gray. She’s a good guardswoman, if a little simple. She used Warren’s name repeatedly to disarm investigations and direct people here instead of questioning her, and there’s more—two of the watch reported seeing a person in the litter, Captain. A blonde woman.”

Hector swore under his breath, Lamonte sighed, Tricia clicked her tongue, and Kalaria grunted.

“Is there any chance she was compelled, Morris?” Isolde asked instead of reacting outwardly, despite the mix of shock, trepidation, and anger bubbling within her. The thought of Sara being carted away made her heart clench painfully in her chest. She didn’t want to think about the smiling Priestess, a woman Isolde’s own feelings for remained painfully complicated, being carted away for Hyperion-knew-what.

“Unknown, my lady. It’s definitely possible, though. Gray is loyal, so if she’s done this, it may not have been by her own volition.”

“Enthrallment,” Hector growled.

“Possibly, yes,” Tricia agreed grimly. “It would fit the evidence in some capacity.”

“But heading toward the citadel?” Lamonte asked skeptically. “Why would they go there?”

Romulus, Isolde thought silently, remembering the handsome adventurer and his easy smile. He entered the citadel already. Could something have happened to him? Could he have unleashed something by accident?

“We can’t jump to any assumptions,” Isolde said aloud. “All we now know is that we have a lead. That means we have to act on it.”

“But Captain, it’s the citadel,” Lamonte said, entirely unashamed of his own wariness, which was frankly commendable. “Everyone who enters tends to stay there permanently, and not because of the amenities, by all reports.”

“Aye, that’s true Lamonte, maybe, but something’s changed,” Hector said, his voice thoughtful, rough, and almost reluctant. “I’ve lived here longer than any of you whelps—no disrespect, Cap—and I know the bloody city. Something’s changed in that old keep. The pressure’s gone. Been feeling it for just over a day now; like the hand around m’throat finally released.”

“He’s right,” Tricia added after a moment, filling the wary silence that followed Hector’s words while Isolde looked between them. “My magical instruments have been less unreliable for the better part of twenty-four hours, now. Something has changed, my lady. The citadel doesn’t feel like it used to.”

Isolde glanced between them again, and then turned, facing toward where the citadel would have loomed had it not been for the chapel’s half-destroyed walls. The citadel had been a dark fixture of Blackstone since she’d been born—since before any of the settlers had even arrived. Now that she thought about it, her subordinates were correct, something had changed. She could feel it.

What did you do, Romulus?

“Send word to His Grace,” she said aloud, narrowing her eyes as she turned back to the Wardens. “We’ll report in, first. Then, we’re assembling an expedition force to investigate the Citadel. I want a full shift of the watch and half the day-shift Wardens assembled. We’ll set out in two hours, once the duke gives his blessing. I know you’re all scared—so am I, but Sara Lightspeaker is a Priestess of Hyperion, and a citizen of Blackstone.”

Left unspoken was the significance the woman held in Isolde’s own heart, even if she refused to confront it yet.

“We owe it to her, and to guardswoman Gray, to find out what happened,” the Captain finished without letting her conflict show. “We can evacuate if we encounter anything unexpected, but I won’t let old fear impede our duty.”

Silence greeted her words for several moments and her Wardens exchanged glances, trepidation blending with duty, until finally it was Lamonte who broke the silence with a sudden reversal.

“Hell, I always wanted to explore the citadel,” the Warden said, surprising Isolde when he spoke first. “Bugger it. Let’s do it.”

“I’ll prepare more aetherglass and instruments,” Tricia said afterward, nodding once. “We’ll need them for the exploration.”

“I’ll work on supplies,” Kalaria said immediately. “We’ll need rations, potions, and emergency provisions just in-case we get stuck.”

“I will inform His Grace, milady,” Abigail said with a bow, both in response and acceptance, and turned to hurry away.

“Shit,” Hector said succinctly, though there was no argument in his tone. “Now we’re bloody in for it.”

Isolde smiled despite herself at her Wardens’ loyalty and turned back toward the unseen citadel.

If you’ve hurt either of them, she promised silently while thinking of the grinning Traveler and Priestess both, I’ll make sure you burn for it.
