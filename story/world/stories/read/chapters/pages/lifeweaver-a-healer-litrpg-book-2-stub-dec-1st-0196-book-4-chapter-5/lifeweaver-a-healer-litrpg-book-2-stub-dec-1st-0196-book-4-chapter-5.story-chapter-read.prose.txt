The portal was massive, like a several-story-tall building. Why it needed to be that size, Luke didn't have a clue, but it didn't matter. Its appearance here in the physical world meant nothing when seen through the weave. One thread was all it was, and he was now close enough to not only reach out and grab it but also to see how it was anchored to Earth. Just as Lemira was demonkind's anchor, the Singers had found their own person. A human. Who, he couldn't say, but it wasn't too far-fetched to imagine it being someone from either Luminous Larks, the guild Integrated Solutions Group worked through to attack The Band, or someone from ISG themselves. Humans who had thrown their lot in with the Singers. Disgusting.

The way it worked was simple. A thread came from outside the system, having traveled across the vast emptiness somehow, and latched onto the anchor human before opening the portal. The human was in a room somewhere beneath the ramp. What Luke was about to do might kill whoever it was, but that person had made their choice. The coldness of that thought post-blockage removal was daunting, but he already knew he hadn't turned into Mr. Empathy and Love. Luke was no monster, but he was no sweetheart either.

Luom spoke to another set of officials while the large group of Singers flanking them moved up to the portal. It was soon time to pass through. Luke was close enough, and he might never get another chance this good, so he took it. Managing a decent enough state of meditative concentration, he reached out to the system weave and singled out the portal thread connecting the anchor to the Singer world. Rather than cut through it right away, he waited. Their escort stepped through the portal in pairs, lining up for their turn. Luke and Luom were right in the middle of the column and at first neither spoke as they approached the floating motes of light, but then the Singer Lifeweaver broke the silence.

"This is for the best. We'll learn so much from you and you from us. Once The Greater System opens up to us, you'll be free to go, but I believe you'll want to stay with us. Our world can become your new home."

Just two more pairs in front of them. Luom would be able to see the threads, so it was crucial to time this just right.

"If you say so," Luke said, taking a step forward.

Once the last pair in front of them stepped through, it was Luke's turn next. They turned to look at one another. Luom's face was blue from the light of the portal, and she smiled. "Ready for this?"

Luke stepped closer, invading her personal space as he looked into her eyes and nodded. "I'm ready."

Holding up a hand, he breathed in and pushed, shoving Luom through the portal. Her eyes widened just as she disappeared, but that was all he had time to see. Hoping this would work, Luke sliced through the thread and felt the human below die in that very same instant. The portal winked away, and the camp fell into darkness. Singers cried out in alarm as their main source of light, and their way home, disappeared. Those closest to Luke grabbed hold of him but didn't attack, as if they were unsure what had just happened and didn't want to blame him. At least not yet. Luke, however, had no intention of staying to see what would happen. The portal was gone, and his job was done. His gamble had worked, but there was more work to be done before the night was through.

The weave had remained in place even with the disappearance of the portal and the chaos that followed. All he had to do was find the right echo, reach out, and go.

A figure stood hunched over something, but straightened when Luke arrived at ISG headquarters. He held a dagger that fed on the souls of those it killed, and from the way it glowed, the weapon had done a lot of that lately. Ray's eyes glittered in the darkness as he nodded to the still form on the floor. "You're too late."

Panting, feeling overcome with the weakness of traveling through the weave, Luke leaned against a desk that looked a lot like the one in Alan's office back in Chicago. "Who's that?"

"Your pal. Their guildmaster."

"Peter?" Luke asked as he finished healing the many wounds brought on by using Weavestep on echo threads. "Didn't think you'd get him. Always struck me as the prepared type."

There was tension in the air, as if Ray felt he'd been caught doing something he shouldn't. In Luke's mind, the man had brought this upon himself. Like the anchor for the portal, this had been their doing. Luke and Ray were just the consequences of their choices.

"Is Curtis here?" Luke asked.

Ray breathed a slight sigh of relief and shook his head as he wiped the blood off his dagger. "No. He didn't want to come. He's all about the Singers, didn't you know? The guy is no fun anymore. Too serious."

"Yeah, I should probably talk to him," Luke said.

"Not every problem is yours to solve, Mr. Lifeweaver," Ray said with a shrug as he nodded to the ceiling. "More board members up there. Want to help with the scourge?" He started walking toward the door of the office, but then stopped and frowned. Turning to Luke, he asked, "You actually closed the portal? My guy watching the building just informed me that all the Singers left. That makes the rest of this a whole lot easier."

"I closed it, yeah. Are there any Integrated here?"

"Not anymore."

"Did they leave?" Luke asked.

Ray shook his head. "Not in the way you're thinking."

"Right," Luke said, clearing his throat. "Well, if it's okay with you, I think I'll take the rest of the night off if there's no resistance here. The Singers will want revenge starting tomorrow, I think, so it wouldn't hurt to be fresh for that fight."

"No stomach for the dirty work, eh?" Ray asked, using a mock-Canadian accent for some reason.

Luke looked down at the outline of Peter visible in the darkness. "Something like that."

"Just messing with you," Ray said, his eyes taking on a bit of a faraway quality. "I've got so much blood on my hands, a little more isn't going to make much of a difference."

"We don't have to kill all of them. You got the leader, and I'm guessing the lieutenants are dead?"

Ray nodded. "They are. Those here in New York, at least."

"And the board members who, for some reason, decided this was a great city to work in, aren't Integrated?"

"Not sure."

"Let's just leave them. Doubt they'll try anything more after this. Once things settle down here, we'll go after whoever's left back in Chicago."

"You sure?" Ray asked. His shoulder slumped. "I am pretty tired. Gone through three dungeons today."

Luke let out a tired laugh. "I'm sure. Let's get back to headquarters."

Ray's smile didn't reach his eyes, but he still seemed relieved. "Great. Fine. Race you there?"

Without Weavestep and Manipulate Thread, Luke wouldn't stand a chance. While he'd gained a lot of speed since becoming Integrated, and even more learning to use his threads for boosting, there was just no competing with Ray's Assassin class in the domain of sprinting. The bastard was as quick as he was deadly. This meant Luke lost the race, but he didn't mind much.

When he reached the new, hidden location for the plaque, he met a large group of Integrated coming back from somewhere. To his surprise, Milla was among them. They were ragged and looked like they'd seen their fair share of combat.

"Milla? Nash? What have you been up to?" Luke asked.

Milla nodded at the plaque. "Let's go inside first."

To Luke's even greater surprise, Lana, Milla's girlfriend, was among those in the group too, and she looked deep in shock, her eyes blank and her hands balled up into tight little fists. Once inside, Nash and his group, now with Priyah and their healer added back in thanks to Luke fetching them, went off to the corner of the main hall and began celebrating what sounded like a successful mission with beer. Ray skulked off, too, and a couple of guild members brought Lana to her room so she could rest. That left Luke with Milla.

"What was that all about?" Luke asked.

After downing a large glass of water, Milla gave a contented sigh before speaking. "You closed the gate. Good work."

"Yeah."

Luke waited a moment for her to continue, then lost his cool. "So? What's going on?"

"Lana wanted to help, so I sort of suggested a way she could. With Lara from ISG gone, a spot opened up for her class. Once the portal closed, most of the Singers guarding the orb left. We killed the rest and had Lana touch it."

"Your girlfriend is an Architect? That's what this is about?"

Milla nodded. "It was her idea to become Integrated so she could help. I just suggested a class that would help us a lot, where she wouldn't need to fight all that much. She's not big on fighting. And now she's in the guild and can use a communication gem. Win-win-win-win."

"Win?"

Milla nodded. "Win."

"Just don't have her mess with our minds," Luke said, leaning back into the couch and stretching his legs.

Her face darkened. "I would never, and she would never. Why do you think I offed Lara? Other than being a damn Quisling, that is."

"Fair enough," Luke said. After a moment, he added. "Heard anything about the Singers?"

"Nothing yet, but I'm guessing they'll be pissed off, and they're still all over the place. We're in for a harsh time now, I think. You'd better get some rest."

"I will," Luke said, getting to his feet. "I'm just going to make another attempt at finding Hannah first."

The sadness that flashed past Milla's eyes made Luke pissed off more than anything. She, along with a lot of the others, assumed Hannah had met her demise in the pink portal. While he couldn't prove it, Luke just knew they were wrong.

"Good luck," Milla said, closing her eyes and leaning back. "I'm going to sleep right here for five minutes before checking in on Lana."

Luke nodded. "Good luck yourself."
