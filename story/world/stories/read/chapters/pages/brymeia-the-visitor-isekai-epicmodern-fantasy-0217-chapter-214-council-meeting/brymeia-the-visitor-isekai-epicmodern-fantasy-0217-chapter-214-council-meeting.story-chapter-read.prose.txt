Council Meeting

“Three Nightmare Signs.”

Frein heard Void Mother Selfiya Lunasensia declare the number as soon as he entered the bridge. There he found more familiar faces. Aside from the Mother of the Void, there was Venry and his gang, Hal, Ral, and Dystro. Admiral Garm Militia, Katherine’s biological father, was also there, as well as Jaylene Atlas Morphinnel, her adopted mother.

Lynera Lunasensia was there, too. As far as Frein knew, she had arrived as an ambassador for Vyndival Kingdom, to declare peace. He also knew that she was a Void Mother in training, which justified her place in the crowd. Of course, Frill was also there, and lastly, Kristel Irista, the crowned Princess.

“We have quite the cast here,” Frein mentioned as he joined them in the circular table of the council room. “Care to add one more?”

“Where’s Katherine?” Kristel asked. Her light azure hair had slightly grown, since there hadn’t been enough time for Frill to trim it. She gestured for him to take a seat.

“Sleeping in,” he replied, taking the seat closest to him. “She’ll be alright.”

“They’re not attending? We’re discussing a complicated strategy here,” Lynera interjected. Everyone turned towards her, as if she had said something wrong. Her brown felintine ears folded as she withdrew. “Wait, why are you guys looking at me?”

“Ah, I suppose you don’t know,” Frein said, scratching his head. “It’s a little complicated, but I’m Tethered to a faunel named Elizzel. That means she and I are the same person. We practically can’t hide anything from one another, since trying to do so will show signs, and trying to peel those secrets forcibly will also show signs.”

The felintine raised an eyebrow. “That’s impressive, but what’s that got to do with Katherine?”

“Because she’s also Tethered to Eli,” Frein explained. “It makes Eli’s identities a little convoluted, but with her acting as a bridge, you can technically say that Katherine’s with you here now, as well.”

“Are you sure it’s alright to share this information?” Selfiya asked this time. “She’s my sister, and you can trust her, but you’re being a little loose with whoever you tell nowadays.”

Indeed, Frein, Elizzel, and Katherine had decided to no longer hide their complicated Tethered relationship. The faunel had been careful before, unsure of who she could fully trust. But now that they were strong enough, hiding no longer mattered.

Now that all three of them were Worldborn, due to their Tether, anyone who could look into Destiny could easily see them. Of course, he could sense them too. Not enough to perceive, but enough to be aware. And that was all he needed. Matters of secrecy with regards to another nation seemed to pale in comparison to his real problems.

“Yeah, I’m sure,” Frein said, finally, deciding not to tell more than what he had to share. “If you trust her, then I trust her.”

“If that’s the case, then we should proceed.” The Void Mother gestured over to Garm. The Admiral cleared his throat and began to speak.

“From what we’ve observed, the three remaining Nightmare Signs seem to be moving at crawl’s pace, unlike the others we’ve seen, which were stationary. These airborne obelisks are also verified to be the sources of the Incursions.

“However, as opposed to the original descriptions of what a Nightmare Incursion should be, the destruction these three combined have caused does not entail the expected result. Eastrise should’ve been fully corrupted by the Nightmare by now.”

“The Incursions were fake,” Frein and Selfiya said at the same time. It stirred everyone’s attention, as expected. Out of courtesy, the Visitor gestured for the Void Mother to continue.

“It was purposefully induced by a faunel named Alphazzel. He used some special containers to gather the Nightmare and unleashed them in rapid succession to manifest the Signs. Then these opened portals for the Nightmares to pass through. It’s more complicated than it sounds, but that’s the gist of what happened.

“A real Nightmare Incursion will just explode out of nowhere. Like a random freak of nature. There would be no Signs, not at first. The Incursion will forcibly open a portal and bring with it all manner of Deep Nightmares. The Nightmare Signs will then appear as the result, not as the cause.”

“And that’s why we can still save Eastrise, right?” Venry asked.

Now away from surviving in the Nightmare Lands, the Guard Knight had begun to look significantly younger. A complete opposite from Princess Kristel. The signs of his aging, caused by a healing from a fatal injury, were still present, just slightly alleviated by his well-trimmed hair, and shaved facial hair. He had decided to let go of his wolftail, much to everyone’s disappointment.

“Correct,” Mother Selfiya said. “Not to mention Katherine disrupted the Incursion’s expansion. Though it was only on one side, that was enough to keep the lands from becoming completely inhabitable. And with Atlas Sid’s help, we’ll be able to navigate quickly and destroy all three Nightmare Signs before they regenerate one another.”

“We’ll need to send a team for each Sign,” Jaylene said, taking the reins of the conversation now that her grand carrier was involved, though her blonde half-vork ears still lazily flopped and her yellow eyes were half opened.

“Discuss among yourselves who goes into which team,” she continued. “I’ll have my carriers bring you over. Admiral Garm will take care of the logistics.”

“I suppose that puts you and Katherine in one team,” Frill mentioned while passing by. She had been distributing coffee for everyone.

“Actually, I want to go solo,” Frein said, not just to the Aria, but to the entire room as well.

As expected, all heads turned to him. Lynera, the one who knew him the least, was on the verge of blurting out some harsh words, but her sister stopped her.

“Show me where the farthest Sign is, and I’ll take care of it.”

Selfiya gave a nod. “Good. That makes groupings a lot easier. Katherine will be with my team. However, we won’t be dealing with Nightmare Signs. Instead, we’ll be making sure the Nightmare stays where it is when you begin to aggravate it.”

“Hold on a second!” Lynera blurted out. “This doesn’t make sense! Why are we sending one person to deal with a Nightmare Sign?”

“‘Cuz I can handle it,” Frein said, letting a bit of overconfidence drip into his words. “And it’s a fake Nightmare Sign. And I have to deal with them on my own eventually. And there’s no better way to test myself other than plunging into danger head first. And because no one here will stop me.”

Lynera blinked, fully caught off guard. She looked around to find that everyone was giving her an apologetic smile. Frein didn’t really care for what they thought about his intentions, but he appreciated that they all knew that talking him out of it was impossible.

“That means we need two more teams.” Kristel pondered for a moment, involuntarily covering her mouth as she thoughtfully rubbed her chin.

Frein observed the weary Princess. She was tired, that was plain obvious. She had barely recovered from the Ritual of Peace. Though he hadn’t been present when she had offered her meiyal to all the dead, he could infer from studying the memorial that she had built behind the High Citadel. From that moment, Kristel had been spending every waking moment in the High Palace to gather a force capable of retrieving Eastrise Region, unable to completely rest.

Siffera can only do so much.

Frein couldn’t exactly stop her and tell her to relax. Time was of the essence after all.

“I can take Venry, Dystro, Hal, and Ral. Along with some troops, we should be able to handle one Sign. Frill, you can lead another force with Xiv.”

The Aria nodded. “Roger that.”

For Frein, two things were made clear after that small exchange. First, Frill was just as tired. She would’ve argued to not get separated from the Princess. But she ultimately knew she had to lead the other team, so she quickly gave up. Second, the Vyndivalians were now their reliable allies. Trusted enough that they would be allowed to work with the crowned Princess. Dystro was scratching his head.

“If I had known surviving the Battle of the Vanguard would’ve led me to working directly with you, Princess Kristel, I wouldn’t have said what I said back then. I sincerely apologize.”

“That’s all in the past, Dystro,” Kristel said, sparing a tired smile. “I’ve long since forgotten it.”

While the Vyndivalians tried to subtly interrogate Dystro to spill what he had said, Frein detected a rushing presence heading towards the bridge. He sighed.

“By the way,” he began, gaining everyone’s attention once again. “I’ll have Scuti join my team.

“What!?” Kristel exclaimed, standing suddenly, as if she had regained all her energy out of nowhere. “Why involve her?”

“Because she’s about to enter this room in ten seconds,” Frein explained. “And rather than have her sneak away to fight on her own and cause us problems, she can observe the Nightmare safely within my presence and learn.”

His answer didn’t cause an instant reply. Instead, people waited for his prophecy to come true. And when they heard the footsteps, they all sighed.

The door slammed open.

“Sister! Allow me to join the fight!” Scuti declared. “Eh? What’s wrong?”

Frein had his back turned on the door, but he imagined how confused the Second Princess would be in front of Kristel. The First Princess was clutching her forehead, like she had a migraine. He would be too, if he had such an energetic sister as Scuti. Technically, he did treat these two as his little sisters.

“Can you promise to follow Frein’s instructions?” Kristel asked, already too tired to argue. She had probably skipped an entire dialogue in her head. “He doesn’t intend you to fight, but to learn.”

Frein felt everyone’s gaze. He felt Scuti’s even more, even though he was deliberately facing away from her.

“I’m his student,” she declared. “Of course, I can follow him.”

“Can you promise to keep her safe, Frein?” Kristel asked him next.

“Yes. Of course.”

They locked eyes for a moment. Frein knew Kristel had considered her overprotectiveness. She knew she couldn’t keep Scuti locked inside the High Palace forever. They were sisters after all. So he gave her all his confidence.

The First Princess gave an exhausted sigh. She had broken all the decorum that a princess should display in front of her council. She sat back down.

“Fine. Just be safe, please.”

The discussion within the council room went smoothly after that. Admiral Garm gave a quick presentation of their approach. Though Kristel had some questions, they were more of clarifications than challenges.

“We’ll arrive by evening,” the Admiral finished. “I suggest everyone take their much-needed rest in preparation for our assault.”

He was obviously talking about Kristel.

“Thank you, Admiral,” said the Princess, standing up. “Yes. I need a break. This meeting is adjourned. Thank you, everyone.”

Frein watched as Frill helped Kristel out of the room. He continued to watch as everyone left. Soon, it was only him, Scuti, Lynera, and Selfiya left.

“I suppose you want some justification?” Frein guessed, directing the question towards the Void Mother in training felintine.

“No,” Lynera replied. “The others would’ve stopped you if what you’re doing is stupid.”

“Technically, it is stupid,” Selfiya interjected. “But it is justifiable.”

“It’s fine. I just hope your confidence won’t backfire, Frein.”

“You don’t have to worry about me,” Frein said.

“He’s right,” Selfiya followed. “You should be worried about Kristel instead.”

“Let’s hope she gets some rest.” With that, Frein stood to exit the room, but he didn’t forget his apprentice. “You’ll be sticking with me during the operation, Scuti. Make sure you’re well prepared.”

“I’ll be ready!” said the Second Princess, pumping herself up.

“Good. Now, if you’ll excuse me, ladies. I have two sleeping beauties to wake up.”
