“Oh, you’ve done it now,” the Archdragon said after Ryland had finished explaining everything to the ancient creature.

After his revelation that he intended to break into the Overrealm and take the fight to the Great Gods themselves, the enormous dragon had actually stopped. This probably left everyone upon it very concerned as to why their weirdly-shaped planet had stopped revolving around their local sun, but since that didn’t seem to concern Vorkorax, Ryland decided against worrying too.

“They hate your guts, Ryland,” Vorkorax said, as if Ryland was somehow unaware of that fact. “You’ve been a thorn in their side far too often. They will never allow you to reclaim anything that’s gotten into Overrealm.”

That was all quite true. The Great Gods had no notions of rightness and wrongness they would stick to. At least, nothing that most normal people understood. They operated based on their own fickle desires and what they felt about a certain event or person. Relying on human sensibilities wasn’t going to get them anywhere.

“Which is why their goodwill is the last thing I intend to rely on,” Ryland said. “Since it’s non-existent to begin with.”

“Ha.”

One of the reasons Ryland had come to Vorkorax was because the ancient Archdragon didn’t bear any goodwill towards the Great Gods either. Ryland could rely on that.

“Hold on,” Viren said. His eyes were going wide even as he spoke. “You’ve met and faced off against the Great Gods before, sir?”

“Indeed.”

“He has quite the rich history against the Great Gods,” Vorkorax said. “Does Ryland.”

“Like what?” Viren asked.

“Well.” Ryland took a deep breath. The act was so familiar, he almost felt nostalgic. Sadly, he was interrupted before he could expound further.

“He hasn’t told you?” the Archdragon said. Because of his gigantic head, he couldn’t actually turn it much to glare at Ryland, but the impression was distinctly present. “There was this one time a Great God tried absorbing a Calamity, but Ryland killed the monster too quickly.”

“No,” Ryland said. “I don’t remember killing the Great God.”

“What? No, I meant the Calamity was the monster!”

Ryland frowned. “How rude.”

The Archdragon’s maw glowed like it was about to breathe fire again, but no flames emerged, much to Viren’s palpable relief. Instead, the dragon addressed the boy. “Did you know that he also broke one of those divine pricks’ heart?”

Viren looked a little flabbergasted. “What?”

“That’s right! It was a romance of the ages. The most scandalous affair across both the cosmos and the Overrealm. A torrid tale that set hearts ablaze among every society and in every world. You will never know the number of songs that have been written, and how obnoxious it was to hear every bard on my back sing the same Calamity-cursed song for ages and ages!”

Viren appeared too flabbergasted by all that to say anything in response. Ryland briefly hoped that it was just how casually an ancient Archdragon was talking that had him stupefied. But no, it was probably the content of the juicy gossip that had him disbelieving everything.

“I will admit it,” Ryland said. “But only if we cease this line of conversation and focus on matters of actual import.”

“Well, go on, then. Explain to the boy what a heartthrob you once were.”

Ryland sighed. He would unfortunately need to humour the old Archdragon to get his help. So, he explained everything. How he met a Great God through one of the cracks into the Overrealm, how they had formed a close connection, how their conversations had unfortunately caused certain spikes in certain spectrums that others who looked out for such things were able to pick up.

The frequency of prophetic dreams rose among the clergy of the various faiths. New hymns and chants were constructed. Worshippers of that specific Great God claimed to possess more and more blessings.

Of course, when a Great God was in a particularly good mood, word of the divinity’s bountiful nature spread far and wide. That was what led to everything else the Archdragon had been talking about. All the minstrels and their songs, all the new stories and myths, all of the annoyances Ryland had been forced to shrug off.

“What happened in the end?” Viren asked in a hushed tone. “Did you… get together?”

“Viren,” Ryland said with a very deadpan voice. “That was a Great God. There is no getting together. Plus, the entire thing was very one sided.”

“Ha,” the Archdragon said. “He’s just too embarrassed to mention that even he can fall in love.”

Ryland rolled his eyes.

“I’m just surprised that sir was even capable of…” Viren didn’t finish what he had started saying out of embarrassment.

“What?” Vorkorax said. “You thought he was a eunuch?”

The Archdragon proceeded to laugh. It sent quaking ripples down his body, no doubt causing mass devastation in the civilization he carried upon his back, unless they had magical precautions against this sort of thing. The noise of the laughter would have forcefully crossed the intervening gap in the cosmos and exploded their bodies too, if not for Ryland’s barriers.

“I think we’ve entertained enough distractions for the time being,” Ryland said. “It’s time we focused on the important matter of breaching the Overrealm.”

Viren gulped, then nodded.

Vorkorax sobered before long as well. He still looked like he wanted to continue exacting a price from Ryland. “Fine, then. I will grant my assistance. My essence, as you no doubt want. But only if you do me a favour.”

“Making you laugh wasn’t enough?” Ryland asked with an arched eyebrow.

“Please. I laugh all the time from the antics of the dragonkin I host.” His maw and gullet glowed with the prospect of flames once more. “Move the sun for me, and I will grant you what you seek.”

“Move the sun, sure,” Viren said. His eyes started goggling. “Move the sun itself…”

“Gah. It’s nothing. All you need to do is shift its trajectory ever so slightly in the direction of the Astramor Galaxy. That will in turn allow me to catapult myself in a different trajectory compared to the last few centuries.”

“Catapult? I don’t understand.”

Ryland cleared his throat and went into teaching mode. “A Planetary Archdragon tends to use powerful gravity wells as catapulting mechanisms. By maintaining his momentum and using the gravity of the sun as a centripetal force, Vorkorax can propel himself in whichever direction he seeks to go with far less effort from himself.”

“Don’t call me lazy,” Archdragon Vorkorax said. “But yes, that is the truth of the matter, in your tongue and understanding, at least. Stars are powerful. It would make sense to make the most of them.”

“Then we have a deal,” Ryland said.

Viren looked at him sharply. “A deal, sir? Just like that? You’re… sure you can move a star?”

In truth, the prospect was technically beyond Ryland too. He could do a lot. The power he could channel was already incomparable and inconceivable for most people. But even then, it wasn’t an infinite power. Even he had limits, astronomical though they may be.

That impossibility was what the Vorkorax was testing. Acting against the Great Gods could put anyone in great jeopardy, even an ancient, all-powerful being like a Planetary Archdragon. That was why Vorkorax wanted to see just what Ryland would do when faced with odds even he couldn’t normally overcome with sheer power alone.

It was a good thing that Ryland had a plan to accomplish what the Archdragon sought.

“Would you like to remain here, Viren?” Ryland asked. “I’m certain you’ll be welcome upon Vorkorax’s back.”

“Uh…”

Viren didn’t finish his answer, and in all honesty, Ryland should have expected something like that. To expect him to be articulate in a situation such as this was a little bit much. No doubt, he was still trying to fully process getting a star-moving quest from a gargantuan dragon.

“Indeed,” the Archdragon said. “In fact, I will even bless you to ensure your safety.”

A tiny coil of white-hot mana emerged from the Archdragon’s maw, shooting towards Viren. The speed of the fiery bolt alarmed him, but Ryland placed a comforting hand on his shoulder. This wasn’t a bad thing. In fact, as far as Ryland saw it, this was a proof extended on purpose by Vorkorax.

A confirmation that he did indeed intend to help Ryland if his condition was met. There would be no trickery or treachery involved.

Viren gasped loudly as the bolt hit him. His whole form glowed unbearably hot, though he wasn’t burning at all. “Sir… my Soul Glyph is evolving! It’s—it’s a Soul Glyph of Inferno now. Unbelievable.”

Ryland let Viren acclimatize himself to his newfound power while turning to face the enormous dragon. They couldn’t quite look eye to eye because of the differences in their sizes. But he got the impression of sincerity from the old dragon. This really was his way of saying that he was in on this, if the endeavour proved true.

After a few minutes, when Viren had stopped glowing and casting some spells to check their enhanced effects with his new Soul Glyph, it was time to leave.

“I want to come too, sir,” Viren said.

“Alright, then,” Ryland said. “Let’s get going. [Translocate].”

They disappeared together. Ryland had of course layered a few more protective spells around Viren. These ones were specifically geared towards safeguarding him against the intense heat, light, and other radiation from the humongous ball of gas powering this entire solar system.

He had also ensured that Viren was quite far away from the sun. The boy didn’t need to get too close. Certainly, nowhere near as close as Ryland had been when he had staked Mavar.

“I can barely see you, sir,” Viren said.

By maintaining a rift between himself and Viren, Ryland had no issues communicating. The problem was that the noise of the star was actually uproarious, so it was sometimes difficult to make out what Viren was saying.

“You don’t need to see me,” Ryland said, raising his voice a little over the din of the flames. “Just keep an eye on the spell itself.”

Ryland focused on the work he needed. The star was so enormous, glowing with searing white and yellow plasma, that he could consider nothing else. Heat was liquid. Energy frothed with such intensity, it was all that existed in Ryland’s world for the time being.

He was right upon the face of a star. In this situation, where even his Incarnation form and all the powerful barriers such as [Absolute Timefort], [Awakened Avatar], [Animus Aegis], and [Eternal Bastion] layered on top of it were taking a beating. He wouldn’t be able to last long here.

So, he got started.

“[World Node],” Ryland incanted.

In this specific situation, the spell was an absolute misnomer. This was no singular world where Ryland was setting up a coordinate system where every point was the location of a tiny rift pocket. No, this was an entire star. Even all the Accorded Realms together wouldn’t fill up one hundredth of the entire burning astronomical edifice.

And yet, Ryland pushed through and brought it into being anyway. His Incarnation form turned incandescent. Every time his mana was brought to its limit and his form glowed, it had always felt starlike. It had always shone so bright.

Yet now, when he was against an actual star, he understood that it was nothing even close. Not against a cosmic being of this proportion.

The nodes spread out. They appeared in little dots all over the sun’s burning surface, quickly spreading out so far that Ryland could no longer note the mana with his naked eye. Thankfully, there was [Omniscience] to confirm that it was working. The sun was slowly but surely being overtaken by the [World Nodes].

But the strain was starting to make its presence felt. Ryland experienced it acutely. His Incarnation form was starting to waver. His barriers, assaulted endlessly by the endless stream of energy from the sun, were now starting to crack.

Even [Absolute Timefort]. It stopped anything from crossing the temporal line and kept him safe, but the sheer amount of energy being outputted by the sun was overcoming the barrier mathematically. There was only so much matter and energy that the barrier could stop at any given moment. When that threshold was crossed, Ryland would start suffering.

And the sun was very good at crossing that threshold.

But that sort of damage was something Ryland could bear and control. The real problem was how his manasoul was being stretched. Ryland almost never had issues of lacking enough mana to accomplish a given task. But now…

The sun was winning. The rate at which the [World Nodes] were establishing themselves was slowing down.

Ryland couldn’t allow it. He forced the spell to keep working, to keep going, and it started drawing on other sources of energy. One of the reasons that mages were warned against exhausting their entire supply of mana was because the spell would then consume other things to keep going. Such as their manasoul’s very essence, deleting the mage’s soul to power the spell.

In this case, that started deteriorating Ryland’s manasoul. However, he was loath to let that continue for any amount of time, so he switched up sources yet again.

He gritted his teeth and focused. His physiology was starting to feel and respond to the pain now. The heart of his current body had started hammering, and his muscles were tensing in an effort to get him to prepare to move from utter annihilation.

Ryland just focused on his spell. This time, golden cracks broke through reality itself. “[Gods’ Tangle].”

Divine energy spewed into the Plane that Ryland currently inhabited. Every mote of power from the Overrealm was worth a million bits of mana of the same size, so the energy output that Ryland could draw on was now starting to match the sun itself. The [World Nodes] gained a furious boost, quickly starting to blanket the entire sun.

Since the first step was succeeding, Ryland quickly moved on to the next part of the sun-moving procedure.

“[Gravity Well],” Ryland incanted

Where he had established every point with a rift pocket through the [World Nodes], now, he was calling forth the power of gravity within each of those nodes as well.

The good thing was that Ryland didn’t need to cover the entire sun for this procedure to work. Vorkorax had relayed the exact direction that the star needed to be moved, so Ryland only needed to cover enough of that direction with the nodes to create a gravitational pull that would  move the entire sun in one direction.

And it was working.

Ever so slightly, bit by bit, the sun was starting to move. Ryland was actually shifting the trajectory of an entire star. It was costing him, breaking apart his Incarnation form and starting to leave grievous injuries he’d need a good deal of time to recover from, and he hadn’t even dealt with the worst consequence of the entire operation. But it was working. Ryland was succeeding.

Even the Grand Frame agreed.

[ Achievement Recognized: Star Shifter

You’ve moved a star, despite being only Primordial-ranked.

Rank up from Primordial [70] to Empyrean [4].

Reward: Glyph of Star. Glyph of Star already discovered, so Glyph of Star has been evolved to Glyph of Supernova [Tier 29]. ]

Unfortunately, it was at that moment the real consequence of incorporating the Overrealm into Ryland’s spell showed up.

Reality cracked further. Before even Ryland himself could react, the world he was originally inhabiting disappeared completely, leaving them surrounded by a miasma of liquid gold and burning opalescence. So fast. The cracks and fractures had appeared and surrounded Ryland so quickly.

In another ridiculously fast moment, before even Viren could finish shouting out at the sudden transition, the presence appeared like an avalanche of ice crashing into a village. A storm of sharp crystals that would shred apart reality itself.

A Great God was among them. A Great God who wasn’t happy at all.
