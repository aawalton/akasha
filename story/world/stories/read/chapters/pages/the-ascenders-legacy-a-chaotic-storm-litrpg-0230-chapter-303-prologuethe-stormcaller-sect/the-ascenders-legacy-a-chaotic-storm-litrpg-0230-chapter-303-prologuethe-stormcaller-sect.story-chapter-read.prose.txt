Gordon is a continent riddled with hundreds of powerful sects. Each affinity known to man is represented. If there is a single wielder, a sect is created for it. That is the law. Unsurprisingly, sects with the largest number of members make up some of the most powerful sects—fire, water, earth, wind, light, and shadow. But numbers aren’t the only thing that makes up power in Gordon. The true mark of a sect’s power is the number of transmigrants they possess. Unfortunately, no sect will sit back and watch while another sect accumulates more power than them. That too is a law. Balance must be preserved.

Excerpt from “The Hierarchies of Power: A Study of Gordon’s Sects”—Gordon Grand Archives, Year 2201 After the First Ascension.

Gordon was a battle-torn realm where sects ruled instead of kingdoms, each one guarding their corners of the heavens jealously. Each affinity had its bastion—a place of power and inheritance where cultivators from all over the continent came to refine their paths to ascension.

Fire had its sect built within the Pyroscar Caldera—a super volcano created from the first Fire Origin Plane breach Gordon had ever experienced.

Water had its sect located deep within the Tidelands of Gordon.

Wind had a massive floating island the size of a country.

And in the storm-shrouded north, above jagged mountains and beneath clouds that never parted, the Storm Sect reigned. It was not the largest sect. Nor the most powerful. But it was alive—always rumbling, shifting, and hungering like the storms that birthed it.

And tonight, that hunger reached a fever pitch.

Lightning cascaded down the mountain range like veins of molten silver. Rain poured in torrents, and every thunderclap sent tremors rippling through the peaks. But the foul weather was a joy for storm cultivators, and they moved about their day without so much as a grimace.

Rain didn't always fall in the storm sect, but when it did, it was a blessing so potent that on the highest peak—a black mountain scarred by centuries of lightning and tribulation—the essence-saturated downpour could scour mortal flesh from bone as easily as a knife through butter.

On that peak, however, was where the High Elders of the storm sect usually held their councils, and today was no different. Five Calamity-class elders sat within a dome-shaped hall carved into the mountain's heart, each one seated upon elaborate thrones of raw, untamed electricity.

They were the sect's highest authorities—beings of such immense power and authority that reality bent to their every whim. They were all above the 180th tier of cultivation, with the least of them at the 185th tier and the highest at the 194th.

Their familiars—beasts of immense power and size—stood behind each throne, each one capable of wreaking untold destruction all on its own. Together with the High-Elders themselves, their auras pressed down on the mountain so heavily it groaned, the differences between them nearly indistinguishable save for intensity and path—a distinction only noticeable to the most sensitive of observers.

However, the moment High-Elder Zarxan Al'ruhn walked into the chamber, that distinction became as clear as day. At the 189th tier, Zarxan was by no means the most advanced of the elders, but there was no doubt he was the strongest for his tier.

He smiled as he walked to the only vacant throne and settled into it calmly, his familiar—a colossal panther of living storm and crawling lightning—padding soundlessly at his side.

A stray bolt of lightning struck him in that moment—not a rare occurrence considering their position in the mountain—but High-Elder Zarxan barely reacted, flicking the bolt away like one would a pebble.

No one raised an eyebrow at the display. They were neither surprised nor impressed. At their stage of power, deflecting or absorbing lightning was as easy as breathing.

One thing, however, did catch their attention, and as soon as Zarxan settled, one of them spoke.

"Is that a smile I see upon your face, Al'ruhn?" High-Elder Mystral, the second seat, asked with a frown, her locks of golden lightning flowing around her as if she were underwater. "By the storm, your sojourn to Lutia must have been eventful indeed."

"And swift," High Elder Kaelthorn, the fourth seat, added, radiating a crushing sense of power even while seated. "When you spoke of seeking enlightenment in Lutia, I expected you would tarry there for several decades at the least."

Zarxan snorted. "I doubt even a few dozen years would have been enough to overcome this roadblock I've encountered in my path."

"You will surmount it in time," High-Elder Torvas, the first seat, said with an encouraging chuckle. "The 190th tier is treacherous ground. I recall it took me some years to overcome, and with your talent, Al'ruhn, I doubt you shall require as long."

Zarxan bowed his head in deference. "Your words bring comfort, Torvas. I'll hold tightly to them when I begin my next round of meditation."

Lightning struck again, this time bathing the entire chamber in a wave of plasma so hot it would have fried an advanced awakened to ash in seconds. It washed over the six elders, but none of them paid it any mind, their bodies far too refined to be affected by it.

The wash of lightning almost seemed to punctuate his statement, and with a sagely nod, High-Elder Velora, the third seat, spoke, her voice like the rumble of thunder itself. "So, if you didn't find enlightenment in Lutia, what then did you encounter?"

Zarxan settled more comfortably into his throne and sighed. "A war—crude and needless, like the land itself." Lightning sparked around him, and Zarxan shook his head. "The truth is, I didn't go to Lutia in search of enlightenment. I went out of curiosity."

"Curiosity?" Mystral scowled, as if the word left a bad taste on her tongue. "What manner of curiosity could compel you to break a three-year meditation?"

"A boy," Zarxan replied soberly. "I broke my meditation for a boy. But this boy is no mere child. He is a transmigrant."

Lightning struck again, and the elders glanced sharply at each other.

Then the First Seat spoke. "Is this the same boy you returned with?"

"Yes," Zarxan nodded. "I found his will imprint floating in the origin plane during my meditation—"

"Forgive me, but I fear I must cut you off there." High Elder Tharn, the fifth seat, suddenly spoke, his face dark with shock. "This boy… this transmigrant—did you just say he left a will imprint? What tier of advancement is he?"

This time, Zarxan smiled. "It’s outstanding, isn’t it? He was at the 35th tier when I found him—far too weak to have come up with such an idea on his own, but he isn’t alone, is he? He's a transmigrant with the perfected path of his patron running through his pathways."

"And who might this Patron be?" the First Seat asked, his voice dropping to a quiet rumble. "Is it a name we know?"

Zarxan nodded. "Yes. The boy is not just any transmigrant—he's the legacy seed of Az'marthon Ranok himself."

"What?" Mystral blurted out in quiet shock, her throne losing its form for a moment before snapping back together. "Isn't that your previous master? The one you told us refused to leave you, his legacy?”

“The very same,” Zarxan muttered, Mystral’s words bringing a grimace to his lips. "But it seemed he changed his mind after his ascension and handed his legacy to this boy.”

Silence reigned for a heartbeat, and then Kaelthorn asked, "What is his name… this boy?"

"Aodhán," Zarxan whispered, feeling a slight thread of resentment coil within him.

"And does he deserve the power?" the First Seat asked, his voice cutting easily into Zarxan's thoughts. "Have you tested him by any means?"

The memory of Aodhán’s icon flashed into his mind, and Zarxan grimaced. "Yes, I have. I watched him fight, and I must say the boy is far more impressive than I was at the 70th tier."

"Surely, you exaggerate," Kaelthorn scoffed. "I remember you at the 70th tier, Zarxan, and you were a great talent. A boy half your tier, transmigrant or not, could not have been more impressive."

Zarxan shook his head. "I wish I were exaggerating, Kaelthorn, but I'm not. Now, I'm not saying the boy could have defeated me with a thirty-five-tier gap, but his understanding and potential would have far outstripped mine at the 70th tier."

"You are serious?" Mystral asked in shock. What could he possibly wield to be so formidable?"

"Too much," Zarxan replied grimly. "The boy has a familiar and has evolved his relationship with the origin plane all the way to a force. But as if that weren't enough, he has unlocked the aspect of water, utilizing it in a way I've never seen before. And then, there's his icon…"

A few seconds passed as Zarxan described the sheer scale and power of Aodhán's icon. When he finished, the silence in the chamber was absolute. Even the booms of thunder had died down.

With such a communal path to cultivation, a storm transmigrant was not an obscurity. Even now, they had three currently living within the sect. Yet, the power Zarxan had just described seemed far more than even they possessed.

A familiar was one thing. But an Aspect at the 35th tier?! That was… that should have been impossible. And yet, Zarxan insisted that it was. That this child was somehow stronger than any transmigrant they had ever seen before.

"We will have to see this boy in action," the First Seat finally said, echoing all their thoughts. He glanced at Zarxan. "It is not that I do not trust you, High Elder Al'ruhn, but things like this are best seen with one's own eyes."

Zarxan didn't object. "I expect nothing less. Though I would ask that he be given some time to deal with his mortal emotions. He did just leave his family and friends behind. A day or two should suffice."

"Two days, then," the First Seat agreed, his lips curving into a smile. "I fear the shock of the boy's prowess has caused us to overlook a critical matter. Zarxan has brought us a transmigrant, making us the only sect with four transmigrants in all of Gordon. And if he’s as powerful as Zarxan says…”

“Then the origin plane has truly blessed us,” High-Elder Velora whispered, a wide smile on her face. “Not only will our political standing rise to the heavens, but our abilities too.”

A low rumble echoed through the hall—not from thunder this time, but laughter. Deep, crackling laughter that rippled through the chamber as they all realized the gift they’d just been given.

"Four transmigrants," Mystral repeated, her voice awed. "I can almost feel the origin plane expanding.”

“And others shrinking,” Elder Tharn quipped with a wide smile. “I fear we’ve just entered a new age without realizing it.”

“Oh, we realize it,” the First Seat chuckled, his snow-white mane vibrating with laughter. “The other sects won’t just sit back and let us have this victory, not when it skews the balance so. But this is an opportunity for us to enter a golden age, and we cannot let it pass us by.”

Zarxan smiled sadly. He had thought about the repercussions of bringing the boy here as opposed to leaving him to die in Lutia and had decided that bringing him here was the better option, despite only trading one war for the other.

In Gordon, transmigrants were a sign of power and heavenly favor. Every powerful sect had at least one. With the three previously under their belt, they had been neck-and-neck with nearly a dozen other sects. Aodhán’s addition to the storm sect would jeopardize that balance.

But it wasn’t just a matter of transmigrants. It was the effect their presence usually had on the Origin plane. The expansion it usually caused. An expansion that would no doubt cause others to shrink, thus reducing their corner of the heavens and potentially wiping them out.

Aodhán’s addition to the sect might benefit them, but it would no doubt affect others.

And that single reason was the root cause of nearly all the wars Gordon had experienced.

The balance of power between the sects was always a delicate matter. Simply put, the more transmigrants a sect had, the more powerful they became, and the more powerful they became, the more powerful the Origin planes became, and no sect wanted their enemies having that.

Even the most peaceful sects.

The war between the Sect of Serenity and the Sect of Entropy had only just ended a few months ago—with Entropy losing one of its most powerful members in the process, thus restoring the tenuous balance.

And though the Storm Sect had very few natural enemies, news of a fourth transmigrant would certainly not go uncontested. But that also was to be expected.

“Things are about to change for us then,” the First Seat smiled at them. “The moment the boy’s initiation into the sect is complete, we shall have a war of balance on our hands. A war we cannot afford to lose.”

“Then we must prepare,” High-Elder Mystral grinned. “It has been too long since we fought a war.”

“And for good reason,” High-Elder Tharn grunted. “Wars are a waste of time and resources.”

“But not this one,” High-Elder Mystral argued. “If the boy is even half as powerful as Zarxan said, then we can expect an expansion so great it’ll double our population in the next ten years. That is worth fighting for.”

“It most certainly is,” The First Seat nodded, his voice a rumble of thunder as he continued. “We must prepare the sect for war. We must prepare ourselves, but most importantly,” he glanced at Zarxan. “We must prepare the boy.”

Zarxan nodded. “I will do my best, First Seat. After his initiation, we can then proclaim his status to the rest of the sect.”

"Do that," the first seat spoke, and then shifted the conversation to another topic. However, while the others began deliberating on the new subject, Zarxan couldn't help but be troubled.

He had saved Aodhán from one war only to shove him into another. It hadn’t been intentional or strategic on his part, but he had known this would happen and had believed—still believed—that Aodhán would be safer here, despite the irony of it all.

But Aodhán was powerful, and with the sect’s resources backing him, he could become much stronger in very little time.

This war was the price of that power, and he hoped Aodhán would be willing to pay it because a storm was gathering, and he was once more at the eye of it.
