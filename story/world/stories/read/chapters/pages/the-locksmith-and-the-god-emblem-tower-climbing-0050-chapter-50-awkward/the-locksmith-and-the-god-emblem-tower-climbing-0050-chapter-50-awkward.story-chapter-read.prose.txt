God, this is awkward. He refused to look at Madeline, who also seemed embarrassed. It shouldn't be like this, should it...? I mean, it wasn't anything serious. And he continued to exchange glances between the sky and the center of the now-empty arena. To make matters worse, they were almost completely alone; a good part of the audience had disappeared while they prepared for the next loop, and consequently, the next fight.

In a grandstand reserved only for competitors, Locksmith and Monet waited anxiously for the next loop and Daniel's fight. They tried to talk to Goldman and Brun, but were prevented, the guards telling them that they could somehow "influence" the pair. How could I influence Brun!? She's not even going to participate. He recalled his attempts to argue with the guards.

Not long after, he remembered Madeline's kiss again and closed his eyes, not knowing how to act. Fuckin' hell... I'm not 16 years old.

"Let's act like adults." Then he verbalized it. As if his thought had come to life, it emerged from the depths of his mind and exited through his mouth. Realizing his mistake, he continued staring ahead, mortified.

"...What?" Madeline said with a slight smile, confused. "Who are you talking to?—"

"Um, nobody. Nothing." He replied without looking at her. "I... like you."

Am I brain damaged? Harry added inwardly. Shit. I should've thought this through. The young thief felt his heart thumping as he so desperately wanted to disappear.

"Are you serious right now?" Madeline's smile faded as her eyebrows were raised.

"W-Why? Is it a bad thing? Look, I—"

"No!" She blurted out and then looked around, now lowering her voice. "I mean, um... No."

"Then what?" Why is this so uncomfortable!?

"I just didn't think you'd be so direct about it."

"I learned at a young age that concealing emotions like that isn't practical." The Locksmith replied, their shoulders touching.

"Is that how you talk to your brother, then?" Madeline felt the pressure change as she said it.

"Oh, that's different..."

"How?" She asked, slightly twisting her head to the right in a way so adorable it made Harry unable to refuse the question.

After the kiss, the two young thieves had been in this awkward position for a while. It was rather obvious that Harry had feelings for the woman, but would it be reciprocated? Maybe. That's not a good sign, though... He thought to himself before answering. She didn't even say it back. Does she really want to know about that right now? And it was easy to see how Harry could be insecure. Either way, more than wanting to feel her lips again, Harry had a lot of time to think about his brother. It was nice to have someone to talk to about it.

"I don't even think he loves me, you know?" Harry began. "I mean, yeah, we're brothers, and we care about each other, but love? That's an intense emotion that I haven't felt, specially from him, for years. Brotherly love was supposed to be this tender connection coated through dozens of layers of conflicts, like all brothers had. With him, though, I feel there are so many layers that the tenderness is as stiff as a stone tablet."

"You were close with him as a kid?"

"Yeah, he used to look up at me." The Locksmith gave a faint smile. "I was the big brother. The confident and strong kid who'd help him out with the other kids at the village. My jokes always got up to him. But then... mother died and I left."

"...You left?" She asked with a sincere curiosity. "Where?"

"To New Cavebrook. I couldn't stand being in that same old village. Being surrounded by the same people. Oh, we are all so sorry! They 'd say, but who was there to take care of us when she died? No one. We were strangers in our own lives, until our uncle came along. He worked for the nobles in the military, and I could see him. I could see through him. He'd make us just like him. And then... I left."

"You left Matthews." She concluded. "Don't you think he was also afraid of him? Afraid of the world by the way it treated him?"

"I..." Harry paused. "Either way, it's been a long time. He should've gotten over it."

"Have you?" She asked calmly.

And this time the Locksmith found himself without words. Yes, Matthews was throwing all of this onto Harry and his friends backs, and the young thief felt justified in his hate for the general. But what if all of that could be resolved if Harry had taken Matthews along with him to the capital? He wouldn't make it... He would say to himself, a feeble reasoning of his brother's tenacity. Now, Harry could see how tough his brother really was. Could things have been different? And maybe they could've been. Maybe it wasn't his mother's death that unleashed all of this, but the way he dealt with it.

"Fuck." Harry closed his eyes and shook his head.

"Oh, I..." Madeline snapped back to herself and looked away, messing with the curly tips of her hair. "I didn't mean to say all that! I-I'm sorry, Harry. I, um... I'm sure it is all very personal stuff, and I—"

The young woman was silenced by the warmth of Harry's lips. This time, oblivious to the world around them, they shared a passionate kiss that could last an eternity. It was a sweet entanglement of particles and also a perfect harmony of strings. It was as fragile as a puddle in the middle of summer, yet as resilient as a volcano whose heat still didn't represent the fervor that raged between their lips.

It was love.

And it was over.

"Oh..." She stared at him with her messy hair.

"I gotta go."

"Are you—"

"You were right. I need to talk to my brother." Harry then looked at the empty arena. "And I need to find us a way to get out of here, fast."

And just like that, he left with one of his doors.

"I like you too..."

The corridors were all the same, but yet, somehow different. How can anyone find themselves in this place? Harry thought to himself as he met his third dead end. Shit, maybe I should've stayed with Madeline... And a part of him would've loved to follow that reasoning. There were, however, more important things to take care.

The Locksmith found a room upstairs after having unknowingly ascended through the corridors. The window led to a vast and irregular desert, with dozens of dunes that overlapped with the supposed flat nature of the floor. On top of one of them, a piece of wood could be seen, exactly the same color and texture as the hatches that led to another floor. Is this...! But before announcing his discovery to the whole Tower, he saw it move. Wait. And before he could finish his thought, the hatch was devoured by the sand as a dozen more appeared in the same spot. It's the fucking boat again... But why does its body has the same properties as the hatch? Is the creature copying it or is this just a coincidence?

"Locksmith!" A rough voice made Harry turn around, ready to jump off of the Colosseum.

"Who...!" Harry then sighed deeply as took a step forward and looked behind the viking. "I thought it was a guard, man... You can't keep yelling anytime you want."

"Of course I can." He declared. "What are they going to do to me? Throw me into the games? HAHAHA!"

"...They could kill you."

"They could try."

An uncomfortable silence then took over the space as Harry glanced back at the outside. "What are you even doing here, in the first place? Weren't you supposed to be, like, tied up and ready for the fight?"

"Like I said; all they could do is try." Daniel grinned. "The real question is why are you here? Shouldn't you be with your lover while you watch all of us?"

"She's not—" The young thief stopped himself. "Actually, yeah. I was supposed to, but this view beats the one from the arena."

"I see..." The viking walked until he was standing next to the Locksmith. "I've seen your fight. With the ball kid and the fake pirate."

"Oh, you did...?" Harry didn't know where this was going, but he kept listening.

"Yes. You fought well, Locksmith. Time and time again you prove yourself to have tenacity and resilience I can only find in the most glorious of fighters. And you did all of that only having this scrubby shape."

Of course... Harry chuckled at Daniel's mention of his stature. "Thank you, really." The young thief was sincere. This was also surprising coming from Daniel, but also curiously in line with his way of thinking.

"...You said he was a fake pirate?" The Locksmith probed. "What do you mean?"

"Have you seen his teeth!?" Daniel raised his voice. "And his hair? Even his clothes were awfully put. It is a disgrace to all pirates and it appears more to me as a shameful costume more than anything else!"

"He was strong, though..." Harry turned to the majestic view of the desert. "His Emblem had such a big range... What I didn't like, though, was how he was treating that kid—"

"Oh, so that was a kid? I though it was a baron! Maybe my sight isn't what it used to be... Either way, it actually gets worse. A pirate is meant to lead, to trust. That... That man isn't a pirate, he's a mimic."

"...So Goldman is a pirate?"

"Positive." Daniel said with a hint of a smile. "See, Locksmith, that's what I mean when I say lead and trust. Being a pirate is all about the adventure. That's the difference between him and the other ones; Goldman can see that. He can see the world is an illusion, so he defines his own reality. It just so happens that the way he does it is absurdly annoying."

I didn't know you could articulate yourself so well like that. Harry added inwardly, afraid of angering the giant viking.

"I—" The young thief then saw a masked figure standing in the middle of the desert. Even though he was possibly miles away from Harry, the Locksmith could see him perfectly. Fuckin' hell... The figure then pointed at an imaginary clock on his wrist and disappeared with a surge of sand. The waves of the boat could be briefly seen as he faded away.

"I'm sorry, Daniel, but I have to go..." He cracked his knuckles. "It's been a long... series of loops."

"...Do you want to talk about it?" Harry didn't realize it at the time, but this was totally out of character for Daniel. Asking to help? Offering to indulge Harry and his mental problems? That was something. And Harry almost waved it off.

"Yeah, you know what? I'm fine, Daniel, really. But thanks for the—"

"Look, I'm not stupid, okay?" He grabbed his shoulder, which made Harry stop and turn around with a frown. "I know that type of brotherly hate, and I know how it goes. You don't want to lose yourself to that hate. It will consume you like a fire consumes a candle."

"Did you get that from Vela?"

"No. You may have not realized it, but that man too is ready to explode." Daniel warned. "And you don't want to be near him when it happens."

He's serious... Harry added inwardly. "What do you want me to do, then?"

"I am afraid it is all falling into this floor. If nothing happens soon, this situation is going to implode, and it's going to get worse. Promise me you will keep your... shit... together."

"I..."

"Promise me!"

"Yes! I promise." And then Harry slipped out of his grasp and went through the corridors.

In the distance, the sound of armor from the guards could be heard. Daniel simply looked at the desert as he sighed to himself. Fuckin' hell...
