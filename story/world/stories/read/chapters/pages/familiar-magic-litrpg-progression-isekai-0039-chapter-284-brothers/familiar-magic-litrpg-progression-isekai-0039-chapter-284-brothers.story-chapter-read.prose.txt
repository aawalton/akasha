Valanor’s shield crashed into the bullish demon, the sound echoing through the dusty red canyon. His hammer followed, catching the beast in the base of its misshapen skull, and it fell with a groan, joining the rest. Valanor had to stop himself from throwing his weapons away, and digging his teeth right into the demon the moment it stopped moving.

He contented himself with a roar of triumph instead. Not far off, the roar was echoed by Savilar, as the twisted knight finished off his own target.

Valanor shook his head furiously to banish the hot fury that lanced through him. He didn’t know how long he’d been on Potentia at this point, but the corruption could no longer be ignored. Every day he fell a little further, raged a little harder, lost himself a little more. It didn’t help that he was close to starving, and these beasts resembled herd animals from back home.

They’d eat well tonight.

Savilar was already hard at work carving his own dinner away. By contrast to Valanor, the white demon seemingly became more human every moment. He spoke easily, and confidently, the deep gravelly voice unhindered by his transformation. His mind appeared to be almost as clear as it ever had been, as well.

At first, Valanor had been grateful for the change. The more of himself that his brother regained, the more Valanor had been able to communicate. He had increasingly been able to tell his wayward sibling the truth of the Church, and the evil acts perpetrated by Lathander, its former leader. Valanor had been certain that if his brother knew everything, that he would understand how he’d been used.

Things had not gone as expected.

“You were wrong!” Valanor roared, never looking away from the demon he was butchering. He even hurled a rib bone at Savilar for emphasis. It made a pleasing sound as it cracked against the man’s horns.

“I was right!” Savilar howled back, hurling an entire demon corpse back at Valanor.

Valanor caught it on his shield, then glared back. “I’m going to eat this!” he called back, already preparing a fire. He swore as he worked. It was getting so damned hard to just think.

Sometimes he didn’t care what Savilar believed. Sometimes it was all he cared about. He wanted his brother to understand, to know that he’d been manipulated, to repent, and join him. He wanted to believe it wasn’t the stubborn bastard’s fault, that he’d only betrayed him because he’d been used.

After many weeks together, maybe months, it was becoming clear that the knight had few regrets, if any. His brother had changed so much physically, even lost his Familiars, and much of his old power, yet his conviction hadn’t slipped an inch. When he joined Valanor by the fire, dumping a crudely butchered demon for cooking, those perfectly confident eyes stared into Valanor’s, with the same challenge as always.

“You were wrong,” Valanor repeated. He was rarely even aware of saying that, it just slipped out, perhaps a hundred times a day. Even if it meant nothing to his brother, it served to keep Valanor’s mind focused, and helped him ignore the slippery, filthy feeling of Potentia’s corruption moving through him.

“I protected our world from this,” Savilar said, gesturing around them at the barren canyon. “We made terrible sacrifices, but we’d make them again.”

Valanor growled. “This place is only as bad as it is, because of what you did. The Union should have started thousands of years ago. This corruption is the work of selfish fools.”

Savilar grunted a laugh. “Oh, and our home is so much better now? You’ve told me what has happened since the Union finally began. Forget that it will end with all this world’s filth and ruin joined with the other two, Nexum is already being torn apart, piece by piece.”

Valanor almost regretted speaking of the Dimensional Bleeds, and the wars that had come with the fall of the Church. The Nator revolution was only a fraction of the madness affecting his world now. Valanor feared what might have happened since he’d left, but he couldn’t regret his decision, even with the losses.

Savilar was right that Nexum couldn’t survive what was happening to it. It was difficult to imagine the terror and anxiety that came with the Bleeds. Trying to live your life, to raise a family, cook a meal, all while knowing you could be ripped away at any moment, with no way of knowing where you’d end up. Whole cities had disappeared, entire cultures lost.

It may have been an inevitability of the Union, but that didn’t change that Valanor felt compelled to help end it as quickly as possible. He still couldn’t be sure how to do that, but he knew where, and would fight until the corruption had scoured away his mind to make it happen.

“This had to happen,” he muttered. “The Union had to happen. Lathander was interfering with the work of the gods, and we’re all paying the price for his hubris now.”

Savilar growled, but didn’t respond. Lathander was the only part of the discussion that could silence the knight. He didn’t seem to believe that the Church had truly been built on the man’s crimes, but those details had been the only thing Savilar didn’t appear to already know.

“Even if you’re right,” the white demon said, “you never deny the greater truth. Whoever’s fault this is, however we’ve gotten here, this is a planet of death, and horrors. The Union will bring those horrors to whatever new world it might create. It damns us to a terrible fate.”

“I don’t believe that,” Valanor said. “There have to be solutions to such things, Ethan wouldn’t have begun the Union if he didn’t believe that.”

Savilar spat. “Well if the great Ethan Bishop thought so, who am I to judge?”

Valanor smirked. “You’re still angry that he saw through your betrayal, and knew you for the liar you are. Then he killed you. A Dawn rank, freshly Bonded, managed to kill you, and you’re bitter. You should be. It’s embarrassing.”

Savilar growled. “I’d like to see how well you fight after a necklace explodes around your throat. Besides, it was the Twilight beast he drew me to that did most of the work. Bishop was devious, and I was hasty.”

“And because he beat you, you refuse to believe in a better future.”

Savilar scoffed. “I don’t care about Bishop anymore. I’ve died a dozen times since he killed me, and I have no room for a dozen such grudges. I simply lack your faith in the man.”

“He earned it,” Valanor insisted.

“So you say,” Savilar said. He was watching as Valanor turned a spit of meat over the now-raging fire. “But I grow tired of discussing the boy. I grow tired of discussing myself. I did what I had to do, to save lives, to save worlds. You will not turn my mind from this simple truth.”

“You were wrong,” Valanor whispered mechanically.

Savilar threw clawed hands into the air with exasperation. “Enough of this! We have said all there is to say on these tired subjects. Yet still a mystery hangs between us, and you refuse to speak.”

Valanor looked up from the fire, considering the demon that was somehow his brother. “If you’re convinced you’re right, then I still can’t trust you. You taught me that, Savilar. You showed me that you were never worthy of my trust. You think now that you’re barely a man, it should change?”

Savilar looked down at his twisted, demonic form, then sagged. “I won’t betray you again, little brother,” he said in a small voice. “My Church is gone, and the Union has begun. There’s nothing left to betray you for. There’s only you, and Potentia. Tell me why you’re here. Let me help you.”

Valanor shrugged. It wasn’t like he was holding much back, not really. He didn’t truly know enough to hold back anything of importance. And he’d fought beside Savilar long enough, trusted him long enough that perhaps it was getting silly to withhold the meager plan he clung to. Fine.

“You truly wish to know, brother?” he asked, and Sav’s eyes locked on his.

“I truly wish to help,” he replied.

Valanor turned back to the meat, turning it on the spit. “Fine then. I’m here to hunt a god.”

Savilar laughed.
