“Because of that,” Thorne replies, nodding at the gates which have just opened. Out of it comes another beast of similar shape though slightly smaller size. Its coat is also a different shade – golden instead of white, though with the same black markings.

Gulnis watches with incredulity as the second beast approaches the first and…rubs its cheek against the Beast King’s? Gulnis’ sharp hearing picks up a rumble even from this distance, but it doesn’t sound threatening in the slightest.

Movement at the gate catches his attention, and he turns his head to see a black form run out and down to join the other two, almost skidding to a halt near them. This one is much smaller than the previous two – about equivalent to a horse in size – but a similar shape. It pauses, then, though seeming more tentative than the golden beast about it, steps forwards and reaches up to the white beast.

Under Gulnis’ disbelieving gaze, the Beast King bows its head to rub its cheek against the black one’s. A moment later, the black beast turns and runs back to the settlement gates, while the golden beast and white Beast King turn and amble off to the forest edge together.

Gulnis blinks, then turns his head and exchanges a glance with Oisella, who seems similarly perplexed. What have they just seen?

The predatory aura that had been blanketing them fades and then disappears entirely shortly after the Beast King vanishes from view. Captain Thorne begins stepping towards the settlement gate again and the rest of them follow almost automatically. Gulnis’ heart still takes a bit of time to calm down from its frantic pace – encounters like this are not good for his health.

As they step through the gate, Gulnis automatically notes the signs of powerful enchantments – the settlement must have several high-level Enchanters – as well as the marks of the manastorm’s recent presence. He’s just about aware of Thorne speaking with those on guard, explaining their presence.

He wakes up from his uncharacteristic daze as they step into a massive cavern that is more testament to the power of their Earth Mages. And if Gulnis’ Assessment Skill is as accurate as normal, there are even more enchantments around them, though he cannot tell exactly what they are due to them being buried beneath a thick layer of rock.

Yet not even powerful enchantments explain the interaction he saw outside, and suddenly, he can’t stand not knowing.

“Captain Thorne,” he starts, hurrying to walk next to the man who must have the knowledge he seeks. “The beasts outside. What…?” Even now, he finds he doesn’t have the words for them.

Thorne gives a small chuckle, incredulity more than humour audible in it.

“I know. Stuff of legends, isn’t it?” Then his smile drops and he looks ahead to where the houses begin – most stone on their lower level and wood for the second floor. Solid for a beginning settlement, but with less variation than Gulnis would have expected. Curious – has one organisation gained a monopoly over the building works already?

Captain Thorne continues speaking and Gulnis pays close attention to what he says – and what he doesn’t. “The golden nunda is one of Lord Titanbend’s companions, as is the black one. The white nunda is a Beast King who, if rumour is correct, is the golden nunda’s mate. I heard from my colleagues on duty yesterday that things almost came to blows, but the lord managed to pull out a miracle and create peace.”

Then, tightening his lips as if worried they’ve revealed too much, Captain Thorne glares at the route ahead.

Gulnis falls behind, all other thoughts knocked from his mind as he processes what he’s just heard. Heir Titanbend…talked down a Tier four? And stopped it from eating them all? And Gulnis thought the rumours Rithan had related that morning were incredible enough.

Oisella nudges him and he flinches, increasing his pace again to keep up with the rest of them. But, as he looks up at his partner’s face, he realises that she hadn’t just been trying to bring his focus back to where it should be. Instead, there is a faraway look on her face.

“A nunda,” she murmurs. “Didn’t Heir Rockfell mention a nunda Beast King that killed the whole expedition he sent up-river?”

“You’re right,” Gulnis realises as he recalls that conversation. The heir had promised them an exclusive trading contract for ore if he could work out a way to get them past the Tier four without having to kill the beast. Apparently the scouts had identified it as far too strong to cull without major casualties. “And I’m sure he said it was white.”

His eyes turn towards the settlement again. If their conclusion is right…this changes things considerably. Though he will have to consider which angle to approach this from. Speaking to Heir Titanbend will cost them…but offering knowledge of the Beast King’s weakness in its mate might set them on course to offending Titanbend, which would most likely cost them more.

No need to make the decision right now – the more desperate Rockfell becomes, the more generous he will be in the negotiations for the merchants’ profit.

Walking at a fast pace, they soon enter the settlement itself. Gulnis’ sharp gaze flickers across the carefully planned streets – wide enough for at least two carriages to pass, even on the side roads – and the well-made houses. Now closer, he sees that they are a testament to mages and non-Classers working together – the stone lower parts are too uniform to be built with anything but magic, yet the wooden upper levels are just as clearly built without it.

He also notes the people – their thinness is clear evidence of the starvation Rithan was speaking about, but their eyes are fierce and their movements are full of unexpected vigor. There is none of the sickness he was expecting based on Rithan’s intelligence. This is a healthier settlement than some he’s seen, though the challenges they’ve faced are written all over them.

Yet there are elements that are even more odd. The first time Gulnis saw a slave standing and chatting with a free-man, he had thought it an exception. A favoured slave, perhaps, with ideas above her station. And then he’d seen slave and free working together in several other places – not free-men directing slaves, but cooperating together – and realised that it was more than that.

By the time a shieldwall of guards walked past with their eyes sharp and weapons bared, that the collars on the necks of three of them were the only difference between them and the other soldiers caused only a flicker of surprise in Gulnis’ breast.

I had heard that something occurred at the palace between Heir Titanbend and another lord over a slave, Gulnis mused. A duel of some sort. Perhaps he just has odd ideas about them.

Still, he had to admit that there was a surprisingly pleasant atmosphere of camaraderie. There were few angry shouts, and no sounds of punishment, even in the busiest squares.

As he sees the regular patrols and the wide streets of the city, Gulnis finds himself wishing that Goldport was as defensible, as disloyal as it feels.

That said, it’s not as adapted to trade – the harbour is well-made and protected, but it’s further away from the settlement than is ideal, and the only storehouses he’s seen are obviously situated for ease of access by the residents, not by merchants. However, that could be built later – if this settlement truly has something to offer.

As they continue walking through the sprawling city, he turns his mind to what the local people might wish to buy. Not wheat, if the fields outside are truly edible, but perhaps seeds for a wider harvest. Metal too – there is little of it on display. If there is still sickness lingering, they might want herbs. At least they have plenty of all of those – the Lady’s wisdom in filling their Inventories with such wares before leaving Moriax has already been proven during the previous stops, and might easily prove itself again.

At the same time, he considers what he might be able to trade for. The dyes in the clothes even the commoners are wearing are rich and attractive, and a surprising number of people are wearing mana-imbued cloth.

Gulnis nods. If he can come to a trade agreement for some of those goods, it won’t have been a wasted trip.

After walking through what seems to be most of the settlement, the party approaches an imposing manor that sits directly across their path. It’s relatively small for a noble, let alone one from a Great House, but clearly well-designed. Care has been taken in its construction.

Captain Thorne shows them into a large dining room where the five lords are already present, grouped around one end of the oval-shaped table with Heir Titanbend at the head.

Gulnis falters for a moment as he sees the black beast sitting near Heir Titanbend, but quickly bows, greeting the lords with a loud, but respectful voice. Oisella and Rithan do the same on either side of him. Their accompanying guards bow but remain silent.

“My lord, the merchants insisted that they bring some of their retinue with them,” Thorne says in a ringing tone. Gulnis clenches his jaw and glares at the flagstones. He wouldn’t have used that particular turn of phrase – but he hadn’t been given the chance to explain himself.

Now, he doesn’t dare speak out of turn – there is something unsettling about Heir Titanbend’s presence. He feels it now – that gaze on the crown of his head that is all too similar to the predatory regard of some great beast. Gulnis wonders whether that’s how he tamed the Tier four – because it recognised he is as much of a predator as itself. That the black beast sits so docilely next to him is more evidence of his monstrous Class.

“It is a dangerous land,” Heir Titanbend replies eventually. “I don’t fault them for wanting extra security. Please take the guards to the kitchens for a bite to eat – I’m sure Ser Gulnis will feel secure enough here, in the safest part of the settlement. Is that well with you, Ser Gulnis?”

Gulnis is tempted to laugh incredulously at the idea that any room including the Heir of House Titanbend could be considered the ‘safest part’, never mind the other four lords who, due to their powerful Classes, are dangerous in their own right. Yet there is only one answer he can give.

“Of course, my lord,” he agrees, standing upright smoothly. His partners rise after him, Oisella far more gracefully than Rithan. Gulnis prays to Prosperity that his younger cousin will continue showing the rare good sense he did yesterday.

With a discreet gesture, Gulnis sends his guards off with Captain Thorne, and takes his place at the table along with his partners. Gulnis steels himself to sit closest to Heir Titanbend – and his beast companion. It rumbles at him and he has to steel himself not to flinch. He notices Heir Titanbend cast an amused glance at the beast, but at least it stops making noise.

They make small talk while waiting for the first dishes to arrive at the table, mostly comments about happenings on Moriax before they set off – innocuous topics that are most likely out of date anyway.

The dishes don’t take very long to appear – twisted wheat flour rolls packed with slices of cold, roasted meat. Gulnis discreetly Assesses the rolls and identifies the meat as from a local beast. It’s surprisingly savoury – well-cooked and seasoned. The roll is made with fresh flour – ground only yesterday, according to Assessment.

“Excuse me, my lords,” Gulnis begins as soon as the conversation dips sufficiently for a shift in the topic to feel natural. “These rolls…are they made with locally grown wheat?” he asks delicately.

Lord Markus smiles, the expression not reducing the intimidation of his presence even the slightest.

“They are. This wheat was harvested yesterday, in fact.”

“Impressive,” Gulnis compliments with a nod, his mind racing. He doesn’t detect any hint of a lie. Yet there’s no way grains harvested yesterday would have been ready for a meal today without mages. Does that mean mages are necessary for the whole process? Then what is the trick? How have the mages here discovered it when all the mages in the settlements he’s visited haven’t?

Either way, the wheat flour roll tastes just as it should – perhaps a little better, even. Assuming none of them have any ill effects later, it serves as proof of the lords’ claim about having found a method to grow Moriaxar crops on the new continent.

After that, the dishes just keep coming. Gulnis has to struggle not to allow his eyebrows to rise too far up his forehead – for a settlement that was apparently starving not that long ago, this is an unexpected feast.

He reaches for a platter of greens and hesitates as the results of Assessment return. Local plants? Are they trying to poison them, or something else?

Heir Forestheart has sharp eyes and catches his moment of hesitation.

“Fear not,” he tells Gulnis languidly. “We have found a method to render local plants edible even to non-Classers.”

Gulnis bows towards him in his seat.

“No offence intended,” he hurries to assure the noble. “I simply had not heard that such a treatment had been discovered.” To avoid giving offence, he takes a spoonful of the greens and places them on his plate.

He then contemplates them with a moment of apprehension. If he does not eat them after expressing such confidence, he will insult his hosts, yet he really doesn’t want to have to take that pill in his storage space.

A subtle glance upwards shows Lord Elarion watching. Oisella sends him a meaningful glance.

Gulnis swallows. No choice. There is too much to be gained – or lost – to give offence now.

Proud that his hand isn’t shaking, he lifts a spoon of strips of green leaf to his mouth. He barely tastes the mouthful, registering crunching, and a vinegary bite before he swallows. He waits tensely for the first symptoms of poisoning – for his vision to waver, or his stomach to cramp.

Yet none come.

Lord Elarion reaches for the same tureen of greens, taking a large portion for himself. Apparently losing interest in the merchants, he begins spooning up the dark green leaves while turning to speak to Lord Softrain.

Gulnis exchanges a look with Oisella.

“Have they really–” A sharp nudge to his ribs cuts off Rithan’s unwisely verbal question – the same one both Gulnis and Oisella were asking. Have the lords here really found a way to make the local produce edible? That would be just as valuable as the method they’re clearly using to grow wheat.

Finally, the meal comes to an end. Gulnis watches the servants clear the table with some dissatisfaction – though several of the dishes were unusual, and almost all the local flora present had a vinegary flavour, it was truly a breakfast fit for nobles. And so far, his digestion has been quiescent.

As the final dishes are removed, Gulniss feels a frisson of nerves go through him. There are clearly three pieces of information they need to seek – how to process the local produce so it is edible, how to grow Moriaxar crops, and how to get past the Beast King currently blocking House Rockfall from mining the valuable ore deposits in the mountains.

He had thought he understood the value of this settlement; now, he understood that he had been underestimating it considerably.
