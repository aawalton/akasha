Dryadil and I arrive at the restaurant shortly after Lauren and Chad do. Since it was a late booking, they didn’t get a private room, and they’re just out at a random table on the floor, but I sense Dryadil cast something around us, and neither of them even glance our way.

The host at the door guides us inside without a word, apparently having recognized Dryadil. He doesn’t look nervous, so either he’s known who she is for a while, or he only knows her as Illiana.

Our table is on the opposite side of the restaurant from Lauren and Chad’s, but we have a completely clear view, and with my Sensitivity as high as it is, I have no trouble seeing everything going on. The two of them still look awkward, but Lauren is laughing at something Chad said, so that’s a good sign, I guess. I can’t hear what they’re saying, though, since while they didn’t get a private room, the tables still have basic privacy wards.

When we sit down, we are immediately brought glasses and a bottle of something that might be Japanese sake, which pours itself into the glass before settling down into a small ice bucket. Within seconds after that, we are brought bread and butter, and disappointingly, no menu.

“I ordered a special preset menu,” says Dryadil. “You can order a la carte at the end if you’re still hungry.”

“Ah. Thanks.”

She smiles, then looks over at Lauren and Chad. Lauren is unusually animated, and it looks like she’s ranting about something. Hopefully not the food. Chad is listening and nodding along and barely speaking, but he is smiling a little bit. They look happy.

“She’s probably talking about you.”

Shut up.

“So…” I say, turning back to Dryadil. “Are we really just people-watching?”

“What else would we be doing?” she replies. “Were you hoping for something more?”

“No, I’m just… I dunno, now that I know that you’re Dryadil, it feels kinda weird to just have a casual meal with you.”

“We were both planning on doing this anyway. Might as well make it a social event.” She shrugs. “Besides, I don’t think we’ve had a chance at a proper conversation. Not when either of us were being honest about our identities.”

“Then is there something you wanted to talk about?”

“Nothing in particular. But Ari and Cece both got to talk with you, and I haven’t, and I thought that was unfair.”

It takes me a second to place those people from their nicknames.

“Arianne and Cecilia?” I ask. “Wait, are you in that social club thingy?”

“I founded that social club thingy, long before either of them were even born.”

“Oh.”

“It’s called the Tea Society. You’ll be eligible to join when you reach 6th or 7th Tier.”

“Really?”

“If you’re interested.”

“Is it really just a social club? Like you just sit around and gossip while drinking tea?”

“More or less. There’s usually food too. We had the former True Gluttony with us, and he made sure that the food was always good, and it’s a tradition we’ve kept around.”

“I’m interested,” I say. “Also, what happened to the former True Gluttony? Did he ascend to godhood or something, or…?”

“Ah, no,” she says with a sad expression. “He died. True Patience killed him.”

“Oh. Uhhh, sorry for your loss?”

“Oh, no, he deserved it. He ate a country. Set up a grand ritual and just gobbled them all up.”

“Oh.”

“He was a funny guy, though. I wish he hadn’t done that.”

“Yeah… Wait, when was this?”

Dryadil furrows her brows. “Maybe four naps ago? It was in the middle of the most recent Immortal War. Maybe 1500 years ago? It’s hard to keep track of years.”

“What are the Immortal Wars anyway? Jane mentioned them too.”

“An Immortal War is just what they call it when a bunch of people like the True Sins and Virtues fight and a lot of regular people die. They’re called “immortal” because a lot of us who reach those levels are usually immortal in one way or another. Usually not completely immortal—unless you’re Arianne—but immortal enough that it doesn’t matter to most people. We try not to do them if possible, though. The last one sunk half a continent.”

“That’s not good.”

“No, not particularly.”

“Which continent got half of it sunk?”

“Essir, I believe.”

“Where’s that?”

“North of what you call the mainland.”

“There’s more continents?”

“Of course. Elyria and the mainland are just the ones safe for ordinary people to live on. They don’t even make up a tenth of the world’s total landmass, though.”

“Are you serious?”

“Why would I lie?”

“It just seems pretty crazy, and–”

I stop as the waiter arrives with the first dish. It’s some kind of cold salad thing with crab in it. The plating is gorgeous, and the smell, while subtle, makes my mouth water. I immediately grab my fork and take a bite, closing my eyes as I take in the flavor.

Like the scent, it’s subtle. It’s definitely meant to be a light starter dish, and it does its job perfectly. There’s some kind of citrus I don’t completely recognize in the dressing, and the crab itself is rich and buttery, while the leaves add a faint bitter overtone that enhances the other flavors.

I haven’t seen any crabs in the swamp personally, but apparently there’s a microbiome not too far away where they spawn. I assume that’s where this meat comes from. And as I think of that, I have a sudden thought about what Dryadil just said.

“Wait, you said that those continents aren’t safe for ordinary people. Does that mean they’re full of powerful monsters?”

“Yes, among other things,” she says. “That’s something that could be handled, though. The real issue is the mana concentration. Even here in my swamps, they have to be careful with pregnant mothers and young children. Over there, it’s more or less impossible to start a proper colony without some fairly extensive wards and formations to keep the mana concentration manageable, and then the monsters are so strong that those wards and formations can be easily broken if they’re not careful. It’s just not worth the risk. I know there are some small communities of more powerful Adventurers living out there, but nothing grand.”

“So, like, a bunch of 6th Tier monsters everywhere?”

“6th Tiers are near the bottom of the food chain on most of those other continents.”

“Are there any that have powerful fish? Or cows?”

“The former True Gluttony wrote a guide, actually,” says Dryadil with a smirk.

“Can I have it?”

“I’m afraid it contains some Tea Society-exclusive information, so not at the moment. But in the future, I’ll happily show it to you.”

“Aww…”

“But I can tell you about a few of the things he brought back for the meetings.”

Over the next half hour, I spend most of my time eating and listening to Dryadil talk about some of the things that the former True Gluttony brought back from the other continents. From almost ordinary cattle, to eldritch chimeras that seemed to combine a dozen different kinds of meat, there seems to be at least one of everything over there.

Occasionally, we both look over to see what Lauren and Chad are up to. The awkwardness that existed at the beginning seems to be completely gone, and they’re both fully involved in conversation. Sometimes Chad is talking, sometimes Lauren is talking, and sometimes they’re both dying laughing over something one of them said. The date is definitely going well, but it doesn’t feel quite as good to see as I thought it would.

“Not quite what you were expecting?” asks Dryadil.

“Hmm?” I say.

“You were sighing.”

“I was?”

“You were. Disappointed? People-watching isn’t always the most exciting hobby.”

“No, it’s not that…” I look over at the happy couple again. “I dunno. It just feels weird.”

“Weird how?”

“They look happier now than I ever saw them while we were traveling and working together. And not too long ago, I kinda realized that we wouldn’t be able to be a Party forever. They’re great… but I think they’re already starting to fall behind. I’m growing faster than them, and I think the twins were always a step above, and now after all those levels I just got… I knew we would have to split up eventually, but if they’re together now, and they’re already falling behind, I think that eventually might be really soon.”

“Ah, I see.”

“Like, what if they decide to leave right now? They don’t have to come with me. Well, I guess Lauren kinda does, since she’s my official supervisor, but I won’t need one forever, right? And what if she becomes just my supervisor. I don’t really want that. Traveling with her is fun, but I don’t think it would be as fun if we weren’t in a Party anymore.

“Plus, I keep getting involved in crazier stuff. I mean, I don’t actually care about the stupid Chosen One Competition, but those insane bastards keep ending up near me, and if they keep getting stronger, while Lauren and Chad keep falling behind…”

“I understand how you feel,” says Dryadil, leaning back as she swirls a glass of red wine. “Goodbyes are never easy, even after all this time.”

“It’s not even that…” I say. “It’s… I don’t know what it is. It just feels weird.”

“Well, I can’t say I know what it is either, but can I make a suggestion?”

“Sure.”

“Don’t skimp on the goodbye. I’ve watched many people come and go over the years. Some of them I was very close with. And that comes with a lot of regret. Things I never did with them, or never told them, or that I never asked about… You’re lucky that you can see the end coming. You can prepare. So my suggestion is, whatever you do, make sure you don’t have any regrets. Do everything you can with them before you can’t anymore, and don’t leave anything hanging.”

I take a bite of steak, chewing silently as I think over her words, then not.

“Yeah, I think I will.”

“Great!”

“There’s another Delicacy in Ocula. We were gonna go do that Dungeon together, and I’m pretty sure after that—or maybe even before—I’m gonna have my next Evolution, and after that, I’ll definitely get a leg up on them. Unless they can upgrade their Class and Species to Tier 5 to keep up, but I don’t think they’ll be able to. Not as quickly as I can, at least.”

“So that will be your last adventure together?”

“Maybe.”

“Then you’d better make it count.”

“I was planning on it.”

“Though be careful about it. There was this one time I watched an Adventurer party try have a last hurrah.” She shakes her head with a chuckle. “Let’s just say there are ways to do it wrong.”

The rest of the meal passes pleasantly, as we switch back to lighter topics. Dryadil tells funny stories about people she’s watched, and I share a few as well, filtered for Earth details. Lauren and Chad’s date progresses perfectly, with both of them having a lot of fun. Seeing them like that feels a little painful for some reason, but I ignore it.

Dryadil and I leave before Lauren and Chad do, having done enough people-watching, and also wanting to give them a bit of privacy. She’s back to her usual, cheerful, laid-back self as she says goodbye and melts into the root the street is built on. No one seems bothered by this, if they saw it at all.

Once she’s gone, I walk back to the inn, taking the scenic route and enjoying some of the gardens.

“You feeling okay?”

I’m fine. Why?

“You’re just in kind of a weird mood.”

Yeah… Hey Abby, wanna hear something sad?

I don’t think I’ve ever properly said goodbye to anyone in my life. Everyone always dies or disappears or leaves before I get the chance.

“That’s… Yeah, that’s pretty sad.”

Should I just kidnap them?

I have a Dungeon in my Stomach. Lauren and Chad could live there.

Yeah… you’re probably right.

Probably not. But then how am I supposed to fill my missing positions?

Handmaid and Knight! If Lauren and Chad leave, I’ll be short two subjects! I guess I could use the zombie siblings, but none of them really give Handmaid or Knight vibes.

You can’t be a princess without a proper Handmaid, Abby. Maybe I should change your role. You’d look good in a maid uniform.

Well, after talking with Dryadil, I kinda realized I might have been a little lazy.
