“This is a terrible idea,” Apollo said.

“He’s not wrong,” Strategus agreed.

“I think it’s great!” Zakiel added, hopping up and down with excitement.

“It’s necessary,” Ethan said, shaking his head.

“Do these…yous really need to be here?” Steelbinder asked from a nearby drone.

Ethan chuckled. They were gathered in the Fable Train, waiting, and the tension was understandable, to say the least. He’d managed to convince Selina to stay behind in Bastion with her people, and Cara had needed no convincing, but that left Ethan and his Dimensional duplicates, Steelbinder, and Fable.

“If this goes as badly as–let’s face it–it’s likely to go, then they won’t exist after today. The least I can do is let them be a part of the craziest plan I’ve ever had.”

“Is this the craziest?” Strategus asked. As usual, he was leaning against a wall, arms crossed, looking bored and defeated.

Leaning back from the many displays showing the outside world, Ethan had to think for a moment. He thought back on a few of his more questionable choices over the last few years.

Bonding with a demon. The duel with the Prince of Sands. Luring Savilar to Potentia. Diving into the ocean to save Richard. Betraying both sets of Champion rank Chosen. Healing a Goddess with the stolen power of souls. Not blowing up the Eiffel tower. Venturing into alien ruins. Breaking into Ulric’s tower two ranks early. Then breaking into a city of Masters in the Astral. Healing another god. Making two walking cities battle. Even his mad, cross-country dash with the Basilisk in tow had only been a few weeks earlier.

“I’ve done some wild shit,” he admitted. “But yes, this is the worst.”

“I think it’s great!” Zakiel repeated.

“Do you even remember what we’re doing?” Apollo demanded.

Zakiel gave him an indignant expression, then paused. “No,” he said after a moment, “but I stand by my opinion.”

Apollo just shook his head, then moved back to the monitors. There still wasn’t much to see. They were perched on a high cliff overlooking the ocean, and waiting. As always, the sea churned with unseen life, the grey waters murky and unwelcoming.

There’d be more to see, very soon.

“How’s Allyara doing?” Ethan asked. Steelbinder’s containment unit was integrated into the center of the small chamber, her Champion rank Techwork Bond required to give the Fable Train any hope of surviving the next few hours. The added advantage was that she was able to maintain communications with Allyara, despite the corruption interfering with most subtle magics.

“The same,” her drone said. “She’s on her way with the package, and is not currently in immediate danger.”

“Stupid silver lady,” Zakiel muttered. She’d failed to save him in his reality, and seemed to feel it was reasonable to blame this version. Not where she could hear, of course.

“Any sense of the timing?” Ethan asked. He found it nearly impossible to look away from the monitors, despite having waited half a day already. At this point, he just wanted to get it over with.

“She’ll make it work,” Steelbinder assured him, and not for the first time. “We’re not the ones in control here, after all.”

Ethan couldn’t argue with that. It was a miracle that things had worked out as well as they had already. Now it was largely out of their hands.

The minutes ticked by after that, just as they had all day, until finally, blessedly, Fable’s metallic voice rang out from all around them.

“Target approaching, Doctor Bishop.”

They were all crowded around the monitors a heartbeat later, even Strategus, four sets of eyes searching desperately.

Ethan felt a sense of excitement mixed with dread as he noticed the water swelling in the distance. It was like a mountain was slowly rising from the depths, and the ocean curved around its peak.

Waves were breaking and rushing toward shore as the living mass rocketed toward them, and finally, Ethan saw their target.

The Champion rank demon breached the water, gleaming vertical fins first. Their size fooled the eye, and Ethan’s mind went to the dorsal fin of a shark, but that was a mistake. These were merely ridges on the creature’s head, made clear when two black eyes became visible, almost lost beneath the water rushing down, and the thick brow ridges.

But there was no mistaking the cavernous mouth that next, nor the rows of sharp teeth, long enough to pierce through a three-story building. It wasn’t until the entire head was visible that Ethan made an alarming discovery.

“I’ve seen one of these before,” he whispered. “Hell, I think I Bonded one of these before.”

Apollo looked at him in open disbelief. “You could have told us this before we went to so much trouble.”

Ethan laughed, ignoring the manic pitch to the sound. “The one I found was Dusk rank, and I eventually had to give it up for Prime. I suspected that there were rifts lost somewhere in the oceans of the Nexum, letting demons through, but I could never be sure.”

The demon’s gargantuan head had risen even higher above the water now, and its serpentine body was more recognizable. It didn’t seem to have changed much, compared to its Dusk rank counterpart, at least from what Ethan could see. He remembered snaring the smaller version with [Karmic Threads], and riding it to shore, before Bonding it.

Hard to believe that that comparatively miniscule creature could grow into…this.

“I suppose it makes sense,” he said, watching more and more of the colossal beast rise out of the water. “There can only be so many demons that fit the description I gave Steelbinder, and an enormous serpent certainly works.”

“I was worried it would be a shark,” Apollo said.

Ethan looked at him with a raised eyebrow. “A shark? How would it even get to The Throne?”

Apollo shrugged, then mimed a sort of “shimmy” motion. Zakiel laughed, while Ethan and Strategus shared an eyeroll.

“You did well, Steelbinder, this is perfect,” Ethan said.

The nearest drone groaned in response, before the Champion spoke. “You’re likely to kill me with this. I could have lived forever.”

Ethan shrugged. “It’s worth the risk, unless you have a better plan you’ve yet to mention.”

“We could wait,” she said, “heal.”

Ethan shook his head. “You know that won’t work. Putting aside that you basically need to regenerate your entire, Champion rank body, the two of you would just keep having to fight more demons like our friend out there.”

Apollo sighed. “And that’s if the Followers don’t take The Throne first.”

“The time for debate is over,” Strategus said, gesturing to the monitors with a nod. “It’s nearly ready to make landfall.”

Ethan let out a long breath, centering himself. “We’ve done all that we can to prepare,” he said, knowing he was trying to reassure himself.

They really had done everything they could possibly try to make this attempt possible. The Fable Train had been upgraded and restructured, and they’d reworked the runes in every way they could think of.

His animus was entirely channeled into this one attempt, as was his solitary temporary Bond, the one he’d earned upon reaching Twilight. He was even channeling every last drop of his Spirit’s accumulated power for a single moment, and that one hurt–he’d wanted to save it for the confrontation with the Emperor, but this was too important.

They really were throwing the kitchen sink at this demon, and that meant every last card he could think of. Ethan took King Rothavaro’s gift from his inventory.

Glistening Razor, Epic

Masterwork Twilight Greatsword

Special Effect: [Damage increases with larger targets]

King Rothavaro created this sword himself. Forged from thousands of independent shards of Twilight glass, the blade was designed to slay giants. Each swing will extend the sword’s reach as desired, allowing the user to hunt the greatest of game.

Ethan hoped the enchantment would work as well as they hoped. He moved to the open slot in the floor, designed exactly for this purpose, and carefully slid the blade home. The swirling, glittering shards of glass obediently disappeared into the Fable Train, and Ethan felt a swell of power as his Spirit was drained. The complex web of runes was coming to life.

He moved back to the monitors. The Demon was alarmingly close.

“Well?” he asked.

In answer, another monitor flickered, showing the sea from another angle. Steelbinder’s voice echoed from the nearby drone.

“She comes.”

The group watched expectantly. They really were throwing everything they had into this, but they needed something more.

Abruptly, Allyara’s silver form burst from the water, flying toward them on a stream of light. A short way behind her, a fin burst from beneath the waves, and an enormous form began to rise from the depths.

“Oh, there is a shark,” Apollo said.

Ethan smiled. It takes a Champion to fight a Champion, after all.
