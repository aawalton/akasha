And after all of that…

There was still the cleanup to consider. There were the bodies themselves, there was my still snoozing away captive Savoy, and, of course…

There was Humphrey Vanclay.

In the aftermath of my little ad-hoc self-therapy session in regards to my once captors, I felt…light. Free, in a way. I was nearly whistling when I approached the still caged gravemender, sitting upon the floor of his own home, here beneath the surface of Vereden. I was self-aware enough to realize why he was looking at me with such a wary gaze, as I came to stop before his shoddy prison. After all, I had been acting a bit…odd, ever since I’d suddenly appeared down here.

Erratic, even.

But that was fine to me. It was all part of the reputation.

Humphrey cringed back a little from the smile I was directing at him, but rallied admirably moments later. “Ah…thank you for dealing with the Elves, Sir Hart. Now, do you mind-”

Almost nonchalantly, I activated Grinding Crimson Sunder upon my still drawn dagger and swiped up with the suddenly whirring blade. It sliced through the iron lock upon the cage like a hot knife through butter, falling to the stone and clattering loudly against it.

“-open…the…cage…” Humphrey finished lamely as I opened the door and stepped inside. I could tell he was a little nervous as I approached him, but he was visibly surprised when I knelt down to his level. He blinked at me in confusion, still sitting on the floor.

“Hmm…” I said, looking him over with a critical eye I had learned in my Healing classes. The Dwarf looked a bit…rough, to be frank. Thin, dirty, and with copious amounts of visible bruising all over his body. “Do I have your permission to heal you?”

The Dwarf blinked at me again. “You’re a Healer? You?” He rapidly corrected his tone at my raised eyebrow. “I mean, of course, Sir Hart. Please.”

I held out my hands and reached for both my Mana and the Spell form for a diagnostic. When I interpreted the results fed back to me, I carefully kept my dismay off my face.

Instead, I pulled out a potion from my pouch and handed it to him with a more serious expression. “Please, drink that.”

As the gravemender did, I mentally rolled up my sleeves and got ready for a more serious Healing session.

It wouldn’t do for my new friend here to die of the impending kidney failure he was apparently only moments away from. No wonder he hadn’t stood up yet. The guy had to be in massive amounts of pain, and yet hadn’t said a word.

It…probably had to do with just how visibly afraid of me he was.

For the first time since I’d set foot down here, I felt a small pang of guilt.

I’d…make it up to him. Primarily by saving his life for the second time.

I got to work.

………………………………………..

I wasn’t the most skilled Healer, and I was definitely going to want Renauld to check Humphrey over for my later…

But I was still able to stabilize the Dwarf enough that he could actually stand up without vomiting from pain. Once he did, the look he gave me was a tad more sincere in his gratitude. “Thank you, Sir Hart. I am, once again, in your debt.”

I waved the thanks off, my eyes wandering through the wreckage of his home. I winced at the copious amount of blood coating the surfaces of it. “Don’t worry about it. Rather, I have a few questions for you, if you don’t mind.”

Humphrey followed my gaze, wincing himself at the destruction. “As you say. I’m more than willing to assist my rescuer.”

“How long were you held captive by these creatures and their…minder, Humphrey Vanclay?” I asked, my eyes lingering on the still snoozing form of the Savoy over near the fireplace. As if on cue, the Dwarf let out a particularly deafening snore, only amplified by the acoustics of the literal stone room we were in.

“Ah…” Humphrey blinked. “It’s…hard to recall the time, in truth. Perhaps two weeks, at the most?”

So, just a bit before the saboteurs had begun to trouble. I suppose that fit.

“And this was all of them?” I asked leadingly. “There are no other Elves in this group currently out making…mischief?”

A nod was my response, as I began to lead the Dwarf from his cage, letting him hang off my shoulder. “Yes, it was just these five. The…Savoy…” He trailed off for a moment, glowering at my erstwhile captive. “Never left this room after they had ambushed me. Good job on getting him so early, milord. That bastard is particularly cruel. He had absolutely no problem taunting me just as much as the knife-ears did.”

I set him on one of his own stools around the fireplace, and mentally organized what I had to do next. I’d have to disarm the traps in the tunnel leading back up to the surface if I wanted to haul Humphrey up there. Once I was back in the open air, I’d have to signal the expedition forces to come to my position. I wasn’t even going to try to haul both Dwarves back over to the tannery district, former captive and current captive both. An overcharged light Spell should do the trick, directed high up enough above the roof tops and flared bright enough.

I’m absolutely positive that Maria and her team would come running at the sight of it, if only to investigate.

However…

I was curious about something. The thought of tunnels reminded me of something, and I needed to let Humphrey catch his breath anyway. I let my gaze drift over to the other tunnel.

The one that seemed to lead downward.

“Humphrey…” I said slowly, turning back to the Dwarf. I blinked at what I found.

He blinked back, a spoonful of the same soup the Elf had tried to drown him with in his mouth.

I…whatever. Sometimes, I just didn’t get Dwarves, man. I shook my head. “Where does the other tunnel lead?” I asked, nodding towards it. “The one downward.”

Humphrey removed the spoon from his mouth with a pop. “Oh, that?” He shrugged. “That’s just one of the old entrances into the Long Road. It’s pretty traditional to set graveyards over them. Something about descending into the underworld, blah blah blah. I mostly don’t bother with it, except for a spot of hunting every now and again.” He waggled his entirely hairless eyebrows at me. “The moles are free, you know. You can just take them.”

I blinked slowly at him. “What the Hell is the Long Road?”

………………………………

Humphrey had some interesting things to tell me, as we cleared the traps from the tunnel leading upward. I guess all he needed was a bit of food that actually went into his stomach, and he was perfectly fine to get back to work.

I can’t believe the Dwarves had something like the Long Road. It was, apparently, a mostly abandoned set of underground tunnels that snaked its way through nearly the entire breadth of the Principality. According to him, it had once upon a time been the main way that goods and services were transported in the time of the gods. Most of them connected to hubs that originated from the Mountain Holds leading north. But according to Humphrey, there were branches that led to every major city within Velancia.

That sounded incredibly useful to me, but apparently, they had either been deliberately collapsed a long time ago or simply fallen into disrepair. It was part of a campaign of neglect to close off the Mountain Dwarves from the broader Velancia they had split from.

Now, it was nothing more than a historical relic.

What I found most interesting, though, was that it had snaked all the way into Herztal, once upon a time.

But that was neither here nor there.

For now…

Humphrey and I had cleared the traps laid down by the Elves, and then, as a kindness to him, I had helped him haul the corpses of the Elves I had slain up as well. He assured me he knew what to do with them from there and I…just rolled with it. Then I’d carried the still knocked out Savoy Dwarf up to the surface like a sack of potatoes…

And launched my overcharged light Spell high into the sky of Rhoscara to signal our position. It flared brightly over the rooftops, likely drawing quite a bit of attention from the city as it did. After all, these days, we had neither sun nor moon capable of drowning out the artificial light.

As expected…

It didn’t take long for us to be discovered.

One moment, I was chatting idly with Humphrey on the lawn of the graveyard, sitting upon the stools we’d hauled up with us and sharing the leftover stew from the Elves, the next there was an entire squad standing before us. Humphrey jumped from their appearance, spilling half of his bowl with a curse, but I just sipped on mine, eyeing the new arrivals.

You know, for a bunch of murderous animals, I was surprised at how well the Elves could cook.

I’d felt them coming, of course. The only person I’d ever met able to hide themselves from my blood sense was Liora, and she was not with this group.

But plenty of other former Nocturne members were.

Including Maria…and Sylvia.

Neither looked all that pleased to see me. Probably had something to do with the very obvious stealth gear I was wearing and the dead Elves lying on the grass behind me. More than most, those two knew just what it had taken to get me out of this line of work. At least I wasn’t wearing the mask anymore. I probably would have gotten more than a few narrow-eyed glares if I were.

Thankfully, there was someone among the group who was happy to see me, who knew nothing of my past in the shadows. Captain Gastone of the Rhoscaran Rangers stepped forward from the group with an unusually wide smile on his face. “Good work, Marshal. I see you managed to track down our pack of miscreants on your own. I…wasn’t aware you would be assisting us with this hunt, though?”

I shifted my eyes over to the stoic forms of Maria and Sylvia, standing with arms crossed behind him, and had the sense of mind to at least wince. “I took the initiative to track them down on my own, Captain, and merely got lucky.”

Gastone blinked at me curiously, but seemed to sense it was a sensitive topic. He dropped it in favor of shifting his gaze over to Humphrey, who straightened up under the Ranger’s gaze. “Good afternoon, citizen. Were you involved in this matter at all?”

Humphrey nodded vigorously at the Captain, setting down his bowl and standing up straight. “Yes, sir! I was a captive of the knife-ears for several weeks while they hid in my home below. Them and their Savoy minder. Sir Hart here rescued me and took care of them at the same time. A regular hero he is.”

Sylvia’s eyes in particular narrowed further at me, but she remained silent.

Ah…

The Ranger Captain circled around Humphrey, followed by several of his direct subordinates in the group, and came to stand directly over the form of the Savoy. There was a distinctly predatory glint in his eye as he stared down at him and his staff. “A Savoy, eh?” He nearly purred. One of his men even laughed in outright malice at the word. “We’re going to have some questions for this one.”

As if on cue, the sleeping potion seemed to wear off on the Savoy, and he groaned as he slowly woke from his induced sleep. Almost stereotypically, he stretched out his arms over his head with a sleepy sigh before opening his eyes.

To find an entire squad of Rangers standing over him, staring down with a mix of amusement and ill will.

The Savoy froze, the blood draining from his face.

“Good morning, sunshine,” Gastone said faux pleasantly. He then promptly bent down and slammed a fist into the face of the Savoy minder hard enough that his head created a crater in the turf below. He was immediately knocked right back out. “Good night.” With a deep breath, Gastone straightened back up and turned to me. “We’ll be taking custody of this one, Marshal.”

I nodded at the not quite demand. “Of course, Captain. I surrender him into your…care. I’m sure he’ll have plenty to tell us about his activities within your city.”

A round of malicious chuckles sounded from the Rangers, and one of them picked up the Savoy to sling him over his shoulder like a sack of potatoes. With their prisoner secured, Gastone nodded to me once more. “We’ll keep you informed of what we discover, Marshal.”

With that, the Rangers faded from view at some unseen signal. With my blood sense, I could feel it as the invisible soldiers rapidly retreated from the area. When they were gone, I turned back to look at my soldiers, watching me with varying degrees of disgruntlement.

I sighed. Yeah.

It was time to face the music.
