It was still early afternoon by the time Vin and Alka reached the fragment border separating them from the final, unexplored fragment of the third ring. Vin hadn’t really understood exactly what Drintus had meant when he’d stated this one was difficult to traverse, but a single look into the waiting fragment answered the question for him. In all his time wandering across Edregon, Vin had encountered any number of tricky fragments he’d had to pass through. Radiation that killed anything that set foot within its borders. Magical darkness that not even Alka’s eyes could pierce through. A complete lack of gravity and floating spheres of water containing hidden monsters seemingly at random. All that to say, he wouldn’t say he’d seen it all, but he’d certainly seen a lot.

Though sometimes Edregon just loved to remind him that there was always more, just beyond the horizon.

“Is that…” Alka began. Her eyes flared in shock while she stared at the distant town that was propped up high enough they could make it out from miles away.

“For our sakes, let’s hope not,” Vin muttered, in just as much awe as she was as he took in the unforgettable sight. The fragment itself seemed straightforward enough. Grass and trees. A scattering of hills and fields. Meadows here and there.

Along with a colossal creature that had to be almost a half-mile tall standing near the center of the fragment. With what clearly looked like a large town nestled safely on its back.

“Whatever it is, it’s not an epic monster,” Vin said, letting out a slight sigh of relief when his Epic Monster Slayer title failed to activate. While that was certainly good news, the fact that this fragment had a creature of some sort that could quite literally step on Terra if it were ever led out of this fragment was more than a little concerning.

Beyond its ridiculous size, the creature reminded him of a blend between an elephant and a crocodile. It had a large, long snout that was currently bent down toward the ground, and six round legs that had to be immensely powerful in order to support the creature’s weight. He didn’t have the faintest idea how something like this could even exist if it wasn’t a monster, but seeing was certainly believing, and he shook his head as he laughed.

“We have got to check that place out!”

With that, Vin walked through the fragment border, his grin only widening at the long-awaited notifications.

Third fragment discovered! 1,500 exp gained.

Third ring fully discovered! 15,000 exp gained.

And just like that, he was less than ten thousand experience away from reaching level 57. His quest to complete the third ring and all the spells he was picking up along the way were doing wonders for his level, and he was beyond excited to hit that third prestige and see what else Edregon had in store for him.

Though first, the colossal creature with a literal town nestled on its back.

Vin ran toward the creature, with Alka trailing along right behind him. Despite the fact that the creature’s insane size allowed them to see it all the way from the edge of the fragment, they did still have a number of miles to cover, and it took them a few minutes to reach the center of the fragment. To Vin’s shock, as they got closer, he managed to make out a few other details about the creature that he hadn’t noticed previously.

Such as the fact that it had a monstrous chain wrapped around its neck, connected to what looked like a giant, rune-covered spike of metal half the size of Terra driven into the ground.

Along with the curious leash, he was also able to note exactly what the creature was doing with its head pointed toward the ground. Almost like an anteater, a long, winding tongue was reaching out from its mouth, wrapping around fully grown trees and ripping them out of the ground. The whole tree would then be pulled back into the creature’s mouth, where it would take a minute to chew and swallow them, before going for another one.

“This thing is insane,” Vin laughed to Alka, slowly coming to a stop beside the giant metal spike. While they’d passed the occasional animal here and there while running, he couldn’t have told anyone exactly what they’d looked like even if his life depended on it. He hadn’t taken his eyes off the mountain-sized creature since entering the fragment, and he absolutely had to know more about it.

“No people as far as I can tell,” Alka said, coming to a stop beside him as she peered up at it. “Think that thing’s an herbivore? That’d be my guess from the tree eating, but who knows what poor critters are tucked away within those branches.”

“Whatever it is doesn’t really matter. It’s big enough that it could do some serious damage if it came to it,” Vin said, finally tearing his eyes off the creature to look at the spike instead. While he wasn’t nearly as good at parsing through runic objects as he was dealing with artifacts, he was still able to make out most of what the magic did. “Unsurprisingly, this thing is enchanted to hell and back, with what looks like exclusively weight and durability. Whoever put this here clearly didn’t want that creature going anywhere.”

“The sheer amount of effort it must have taken to chain this thing here…” Alka said, sounding like she was at a rare loss for words while she watched the creature continue to slurp up one tree after another. “...I don’t even know what to think. You think the Gods did this?”

“No, this doesn’t feel like something they would do,” Vin said. Turning back toward the creature, he peered up at the town on its back. “I think the creature was like this long before Edregon. This isn’t something that just happens overnight.”

“Whatever the case, I’m just going to bite the bolt and assume we’re going up there, right?” Alka asked, giving him a knowing look. “Redirect Gravity?”

“Always one step ahead,” he grinned, motioning for her to prepare herself. Getting a nod from her, Vin did just that, flipping the world on its head as he caused the two of them to flip around and shoot up into the sky. The creature didn’t even seem to notice them as they shot up, following along the chain before shooting past the creature’s massive back. He waited another few seconds just to get higher before cancelling the spell and catching the two of them in midair with Create Cloud.

The large town had clearly been constructed carefully, without a single inch of space going to waste. There were shops, apartments, and even a tiny little park located near the very center of the creature. Throughout it all, Vin spotted people going about their days, wandering to and from as though they weren’t living on the back of a mountain-sized creature currently eating its way through an entire forest. The population appeared to be entirely beastkin, most of them wearing the usual loose robes to ensure that their fur didn’t get caught up in anything. Already a few people were pointing up at them on their cloud, their magical entrance not going unnoticed.

“Guess we’re not quite as sneaky as Scule is,” Alka said, waving at a small beastkin child who was jumping up and down and pointing excitedly at them.

“Not like we were trying to be,” Vin shrugged. Trying to decide how to introduce themselves, he recoiled backward when a grappling hook of all things shot up out of nowhere, snagging onto the side of his cloud and going taut. A simple glance down showed a beastkin armed to the teeth in weapons and leather armor running up the side of the rope at a complete ninety degree angle, as though they had their own enchanted boots that allowed them to defy gravity. Before Vin could even decide what to do, the beastkin leapt the last few feet, swinging up onto the cloud and pointing twin swords directly at the two of them.

“Who are you and what is your business with us?” the beastkin demanded, his eyes narrow and teeth barred as he looked between the two of them. The fact that he was actually growling at the two of them showed that he was quite serious, as Vin didn’t need Common Ally to tell him what that meant.

“Just two people who spotted this impossible creature from afar and wanted to get a closer look,” Alka shrugged, her own sword already in hand and pointed right back at the unexpected beastkin. “Seriously, this thing eats trees? How do you not run out of them?”

The beastkin seemed a bit surprised to find that neither of them were concerned in the slightest at his arrival, but he didn’t lower his swords. Instead, his knuckles whitened as he tightened his grip, and he lowered himself as if preparing to lunge. Before they could cause some sort of incident or be forced to knock the beastkin unconscious, Vin stepped in front of Alka and raised his hands placatingly.

“Sorry for the scare! I’m an Explorer trying to create a map of Edregon, and this is my friend. In all the fragments I’ve visited, I’ve never seen anything like this creature before, and I just had to get a closer look. We’d be happy to drop the cloud and speak within your town if you would prefer! We’re friends with Drintus if that means anything. Big, tall minotaur with a giant axe? Did I mention he was big?”

The beastkin continued staring at him for a few seconds longer before he finally stopped growling. Returning his swords to their sheaths, his angry stare turned into one that was more measuring as he crossed his arms.

“You know Drintus? How?”

“We ended up fighting alongside one another for a bit a few months back, and only just met back up the other day,” Vin admitted. “Spent the last day over in Harmstead meeting his people before continuing on our way. …How do you know him?”

“I stopped the idiot from trying to climb his way up here along the Binding Chain,” the beastkin snorted, shaking his head and grinning at the memory. “More brawn than brains on that one, that’s for sure. Though the scary thing is I’m pretty certain he would have made it if I hadn’t intervened.”

“That honestly sounds kind of fun,” Alka said, turning to look down at the chain. “Any chance I could give that a shot? Then I can rub it in Drintus’ face that I actually pulled it off when he couldn’t.”

“No you may not,” the beastkin said, his frown snapping back onto his face just as quickly as it had left. “The Binding Chain is a sacred object from the time before even our oldest of elders. It is forbidden to touch the chain for any reason, and doing so will result in death.”

“So… why didn’t Drintus get killed?” Vin asked.

“Because he’s an eight-foot-tall wall of muscle who is really good at using that battle axe of his,” the beastkin said bluntly. “After he bested me and apologized, I decided to make an exception. Though that was only because he didn’t know any better. You two are now aware of our customs, which means no exceptions for either of you.”

“We won’t touch the Sacred Chain,” Vin said, turning to stare pointedly at Alka. “Right?”

“Right, right, no touching the special chain,” Alka grumbled, sheathing her own sword. “Sheesh, I was just asking.”

Rolling his eyes, Vin turned to smile at their still-unnamed guest. “By the way, I’m Vin, and this is Alka. Is there any chance we’d be allowed to set foot within your town? We would love to get the chance to meet your people and potentially share information. I’m a wandering mage, so if you happen to have any magic users, I’d be happy to trade spells with them.”

“We have a number of Druids who might be willing to meet with you,” the beastkin said, hesitating for a moment before sighing. “And I’m Fritz. I have to admit, we don’t really have rules in place for allowing visitors to come up onto God’s back, so I suppose I’ll have to take it upon myself to watch over you both until you leave.”

“God?” Vin asked, glancing down at the giant beast peacefully eating below them. “That’s what you call the creature? Do you worship it?”

“It is solely because of God that any of us yet live,” Fritz shrugged. “I’d be happy to give you a lesson once we’re back on God’s back if you would like. I will admit, standing up here on a cloud is a tad unnerving, and I would very much like to get down now, if you wouldn’t mind.”
