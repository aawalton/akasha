Soft rays of evening light streamed through the colored windows of the special sect library, painting Aodhán’s visage in hues of gold, crimson, and blue as he sat hunched over a pile of books, his gaze fixed on the text before him.

A heavy silence hung over the room. The only sounds were his steady breathing and the occasional rustle of paper as he turned a page.

Overhead, a corona flickered to life, casting artificial light across the library to aid his reading, but Aodhán barely noticed, his eyes adjusting instinctively to the shift in brightness.

Minutes passed in silence, frustration gradually creeping across his face as he neared the end of the book.

Another page turned.

Then the final one.

A scowl tugged at his lips as his eyes landed on the author's note, irritation surfacing once more when he realized he had wasted another three hours of his time.

This was the ninth book he had read on the nature and forms of energy today alone, yet not one had mentioned pure energy—or anything remotely close to it. He had even asked the librarian herself, hoping she could at least point him in the right direction, but she had been just as clueless.

It was as though the term simply did not exist.

But Aodhán knew that wasn't true. If it didn't exist, the system would never have mentioned it.

He simply hadn't found it yet.

Glancing at the pile of completed books beside him, he pinched the bridge of his nose and let out a slow breath.

Alright. What do I actually know?

Energy existed naturally throughout the world—dense in some places, sparse in others. This free-flowing, unfiltered energy was known as essence. Yet even in its rawest state, essence could hardly be called pure. It always carried attributes.

Light essence. Wind essence. Water essence. Earth essence.

And here in the Storm Sect, lightning, storm, force, shadow, and sound essence saturated the atmosphere itself.

Every form of essence carried identity, which meant none of it was truly pure.

Pure energy, then, had to exist beneath essence itself—raw magic unshaped by affinity, identity, or personality.

But how could such an energy exist in a world where essence developed attributes almost instantly? If no one had ever observed it in its natural state, then how was he supposed to access it?

Much less create it?

His gaze shifted to the last book on the table—a worn tome so old it seemed to predate half the library itself. Its plain gray leather cover gleamed faintly beneath preservation runes that looked ready to crumble into dust at any moment.

You'd better give me something, he thought as he carefully pulled the tome closer, his gaze settling on a faded insignia near the bottom of the cover. Beneath it were two names Aodhán could barely make out.

Duchess Selena Vaust. Lorene Vaust.

A slight frown crossed his face at the familiarity of the surname, and he found himself wondering where he'd heard it before. When nothing came to mind, he glanced back at the names, idly wondering if the two were mother and daughter or perhaps sisters.

The thought lingered in his mind, unnecessary as it was until he finally dismissed it. Waving the dust away from his face, he opened the tome to reveal a line of handwritten text across the first page.

“Transmutation is not the act of changing one thing into another. It is the revelation that they were never truly different to begin with.”

Aodhán paused, his frown deepening as he read the line again.

So the authors were transmutation cultivators.

That alone sparked a thread of curiosity. He had always wondered how their abilities truly worked, but he had never been close enough to one to ask. And Borevin—the one person he could have asked—would probably have cursed him in the name of God just for speaking to her.

That was, assuming she would have answered him at all.

Shaking his head in amusement, he returned his attention to the book. His gaze lingered on the text for another moment before he flipped the page and began reading.

The opening chapters were insightful, but ordinary enough to be considered general knowledge. Things quickly became more complicated after the first few pages, however, as the authors began delving into the principles behind transmutation terms like conversion ratios, affinity degradation, and a dozen other concepts Aodhán had never imagined seeing paired together started appearing with increasing frequency.

Still, he read on with growing interest, absorbing what he could and setting aside the rest. The first chapter alone answered several questions he had wanted to ask for years, and though it wasn't quite what he had been searching for, it made for an interesting read nonetheless.

Time passed quietly in this manner. Seconds. Minutes. Then hours. Yet Aodhán barely noticed.

He was nearly halfway through the book when his eyes caught on a paragraph midway down the page.

"There exists no such thing as elemental or conceptual energy. There is only energy assuming different forms, much like a man putting on different robes.

Beneath the robes, there remains only a man."

Aodhán stilled. He read the paragraph again.

Then slowly sat up straighter.

If he was understanding this correctly, then his assumption that different types of energy fundamentally existed was wrong.

Or at least... incomplete.

His eyes began moving faster.

Fire does not emerge from the heavens as fire. Lightning does not descend already bearing the name lightning. It comes down as primal energy, takes on a form, and begins to act in that form. Transmutation, at its core, is the manipulation of these forms.

Aodhán's eyes widened as understanding dawned, and he quickly jotted the unfamiliar term down.

Primal energy.

It obviously referred to the initial state of energy before it took on affinity, identity, or personality. It was a different name from what the system called it, but the meaning was the same.

Pure energy.

He read the passage again before underlining the word form.

The idea that energy could assume different forms wasn't exactly new to him, but after everything he had read so far, he suddenly found himself viewing the concept through an entirely different lens.

Essence could be likened to a blank canvas—highly impressionable, yet deeply loyal to whatever form it assumed. The moment energy entered an environment, it aligned itself with the affinity of its surroundings and remained that way unless something actively forced it to change.

And the thing responsible for forcing that change was—

Aodhán's gaze dropped to his stomach, his eyes widening as one realization rapidly gave way to the next. A storm cultivator couldn't manipulate fire essence. Nor could a fire cultivator manipulate storm essence.

But they could absorb it.

He could absorb fire cores, water cores, earth cores, and every other elemental core in existence, yet all that energy changed the moment it entered his core.

His storm core.

And then it was stripped down—transmuted, if you will—into storm energy.

The realization struck him like lightning and his hands flew across his notepad as he hastily sketched a rough diagram of a core absorbing streams of different affinities before converting them all into a single form.

Storm.

The core wasn't simply storing energy. It was filtering it.

No. More than that… It was transforming it

The core wasn’t a container. It was an engine.

A transmutation engine that converted all absorbed energy into its own affinity.

A fire core converted absorbed energy into fire essence. A water core did the same with water essence. Every core instinctively rewrote incoming energy according to its own affinity.

But if that was true, then—

Aodhán's senses immediately turned inward, his mind's eye focusing on the blazing core within his spirit. It shone brilliantly, far brighter than he had ever imagined possible.

But it wasn't flawless.

Tiny impurities marred its surface—too small to hinder his advancement, but flaws nonetheless. And for the first time, Aodhán understood they weren't metaphorical. They weren't some abstract system concept for magic scholars or theorists.

They were real.

And he finally understood what caused them.

It wasn't simply because he had absorbed conceptual cores or broken some kind of system law. It was because his core had failed to fully process the concepts embedded within the energy he absorbed.

His gaze snapped back to his notes, and he circled the words primal energy three times.

His core was a storm core. But more importantly, it was an elemental core.

Whenever he absorbed elemental essence, its elemental structure was stripped away, leaving behind primal energy that immediately aligned itself with the affinity of its new surroundings.

But conceptual energy was different.

The core could not fully dismantle the conceptual structure embedded within it because it simply wasn't designed to process something so abstract. The energy that reached the core therefore wasn't fully stripped of its affinity.

It remained impure, causing impurities to accumulate within the core itself.

Impurities that would eventually build until they gave rise to the Limit.

Aodhán stilled on the word, his thoughts grinding to a halt as it echoed through his mind.

The Limit.

These were all things he had, technically, known already.

Yet somehow, seeing them through this lens made it feel as though he were looking at the entire cultivation system for the first time.

He turned the page and swallowed as the words before him confirmed his thoughts.

Affinities are patterns imposed upon energy. Elemental patterns are stable, grounded, physically intuitive. Conceptual patterns are abstract, information-dense, and self-referential.

The reckless absorption of unaligned patterns will cause impurities to build up in the core, thus leading to the stagnation of advancement.

Aodhán stared at the page in stunned silence, and it wasn't until several moments later that he realized he had stopped breathing altogether.

This was the first time he had ever seen the Limit openly discussed in a book.

The very thing that held Lutia in chains...

Printed plainly on paper for anyone to read.

A slow breath escaped him as he glanced over the mess of notes scattered across his notepad, making certain he had recorded everything important before returning his attention to the tome.

Page after page disappeared beneath his fingertips. Notes accumulated in the margins of his notepad, and every few pages, he paused to sketch another crude diagram before pressing onward.

Overhead, the corona dimmed and brightened twice as the library adjusted to the deepening night, yet Aodhán never once looked up.

The cycle continued in silence. Pages turned. His pen scratched against paper. And by the time he reached the final page, nearly two hours had passed.

He inhaled deeply, then lowered his gaze to the page.

At the very bottom was a short-handwritten dedication..

"This book is dedicated to my grandson, Benevolence Axilas Vaust, who proved that storms are capable not only of destruction, but purification."

"May the path he threads one day reach the silent state between all forms."

Aodhán stared at the final page for a long moment before finally shutting the tome with a quiet sigh.

In the span of a few hours, he had learned more about energy than he had in years.

Transmutation. Affinity structures. Impurities. The true nature of pure energy.

Yet despite everything he had uncovered, one frustrating truth remained unchanged. He still had no idea how to access it himself.

There were no skills, no techniques, or abilities recorded in the book. Only the revelation that transmuters apparently brushed against pure energy every single time they performed transmutation—breaking essence down into its primal state before reconstructing it into something else entirely.

But he wasn’t a transmuter.

And if he wanted to use the technique Geneva had left behind for him, then he first needed to learn how to access pure energy himself.

Which meant, as far as he could tell, he only had two options.

Visit the Transmutation Sect and somehow convince them to teach him, if that was even possible.

Or find the storm cultivator this book had been dedicated to.

His gaze drifted back toward the final page, narrowing slightly on the surname written there.

Vaust.

Once more, the name stirred something faintly familiar in his memory, and his frown deepened. It wasn’t just familiar. It was recent.

Something he had heard here in the Storm Sect.

His eyes lingered on the name, his thoughts racing as he searched through his memories, trying to grasp where exactly he had heard it before—

And then, like a bubble rising to the surface of a pond, a memory emerged.

It was the morning after Calamity Ghaltris’s ascension. He had just opened his eyes to find a nervous Mythic staring down at him.

“Vaust,” she had introduced herself with a small smile. “Lilian Vaust.”

Aodhán blinked in shock.

The null guard.

Whatever did Lilian have to do with transmutation?

***

Lilian Vaust sat cross-legged within her cultivation chamber, reading a trashy novel and completely ignoring the meditation she was supposed to be doing.

Storm essence swirled densely around her as her core pulsed leisurely with every breath she took. It was perhaps the laziest method of cultivation imaginable, but Lilian had never been the sort to sit cross-legged for hours, contemplating the mysteries of the universe.

So she had devised this breathing technique that allowed her to multitask—occupying her mind with lackluster novels about impossibly powerful cultivators rescuing helpless damsels while simultaneously exercising her core.

It was an unorthodox arrangement that had worked for nearly a decade now, and Lilian saw no need to change it.

She flipped a page and settled deeper into her cultivation chair with a content sigh.

It had been two weeks since Calamity Ghaltris's ascension, and with no other ascensions expected anytime soon, she was relatively free.

That was one of the perks of being a null guard.

Granted, the position made it nearly impossible to benefit from ascensions like other Mythics did, but the flexibility more than compensated for the slower path to advancement. Despite the hidden hunger for strength the Crown of Ruining Storms had stirred within her, Lilian had never truly chased power or glory.

She had clawed her way into the Mythic class through persistence, stubbornness, and an embarrassing number of cores.

She was average at best.

And she was perfectly fine with that. Probably.

Exhaling softly, she flipped another page as storm essence drifted lazily around her arms and legs like strands of living mist. Absentmindedly, she waved a hand through the air, feeling the dense essence obey her touch without resistance.

Even without conscious effort, the storm within this room belonged to her. It responded to her emotions, her breathing, even the smallest fluctuations in her thoughts.

Nothing happened within her chamber without her sensing it. Which was why she noticed the moment that control shifted.

The feeling was unmistakable.

One moment, the storm curled around her like a loyal servant. The next, it stilled—alert and attentive—like a soldier standing at attention before a king.

What??

She barely had time to process the sensation before her communication chip suddenly pinged with a message. Her eyes widened when she realized it was from the Crown of Ruining Storms himself, and she hurriedly opened it.

A line of text crawled across her vision.

Good evening, Elder Vaust. I need to speak with you.

Lilian shot to her feet so quickly that her novel went flying from her lap. Her fingers blurred across the chip.

Whatever for? And how did you get my contact?

His reply came immediately.

I have my ways.

A pause.

Also, I’m at your doorstep.

Lilian stared at the message in horror.

What?!

The Crown of Ruining Storms was at her doorstep.

Right now.

She rushed toward the stairs before abruptly slowing halfway down.

Wait.

No.

No rushing, Lilian. You are a Mythic. You are composed. You are dignified.

Inhaling deeply, she straightened her posture, smoothed out her robes, and calmly walked toward the entrance with every ounce of authority she could muster.

Then she opened the door.

“Good evening, Elder Vaust,” Aodhán greeted politely.

“Good evening, Crown of Ruining—”

“Call me Aodhán, please,” he interjected with a light smile. “And I’m sorry for disturbing you this late, but I was reading a book on the transmutation of energy by Duchess Selena Vaust and Lorene Vaust, and I was hoping you could answer a few questions.”

Lilian blinked, first in shock, then in confusion. “I’m sorry. You read… a book?”

Aodhán frowned right back, clearly confused by the question. “Yes?”

“You?”

“…Yes?”

“The Crown of Ruining Storms sat down and read a scholarly text?”

Aodhán stared at her in bewilderment. “I can read, Elder Vaust. I’m not some barbarian.”

Lilian opened her mouth. Paused. Then coughed awkwardly into her fist. “Apologies. Do continue.”

Aodhán narrowed his eyes suspiciously before speaking again. "It was written by two women from the Vaust family. I assumed you might know something about transmutation."

Lilian frowned. "I don't know anything about transmutation, I…" Her eyes widened as realization dawned. "Oh, you mean those Vausts."

"Yes," Aodhán replied, now frowning in confusion. "Have I made an error?"

"A fairly large one, yes." She leaned against the doorway with an amused smile. "I am a Vaust, but not that kind of Vaust. We are not related. You're looking for Grand Alchemist Axilas Vaust."

"Yes—" his eyes went wide. "He's still alive?"

"Very much so," Lilian replied. "Though I believe he's currently in seclusion." Her expression dimmed slightly. "His son died during the war, unfortunately."

Aodhán grimaced. That was another life the war had taken.

"But his granddaughters are still around," Lilian added.

Aodhán looked up immediately. "They're storm cultivators?"

"All three of them." Lilian nodded. "You must have met them during your initiation."

Aodhán frowned. The only sisters he had met during his initiation were the triplets, and they—

He looked up at Lilian. "You don't mean the triplets."

Lilian frowned. "They are the Vaust sisters. One of them cultivates the same exact path as her grandfather. They should be able to help you with your questions."

A memory of the triplets came to mind, and Aodhán smiled.

It seemed he would be paying the core sect a visit sooner than he'd thought.
