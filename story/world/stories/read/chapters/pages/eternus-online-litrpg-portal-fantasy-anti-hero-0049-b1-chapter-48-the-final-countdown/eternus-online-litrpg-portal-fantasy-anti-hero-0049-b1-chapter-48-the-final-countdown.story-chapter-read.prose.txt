Three Eternian hours later, after an exhausting protocol lesson from Mortarius that had involved three dynastic lectures, fourteen different ways to greet a petitioner, multiple recounts of fomented rebellion, a spoken dissertation on realm politics in the Dark Faith, and every acceptable form of address from “Sire” to “My Liege,” “Your Majesty”, “Your Holiness”, “Autarch”, and “Dark Sovereign”, Romulus observed the three black stone coffins standing in the center of his throne room while Kate kept her customary place on the left arm of his throne.

Mortarius stood to his right, the ancient King’s expression markedly more serene after impressing upon his friends that calling him “Dee” within hearing of any Eternian was likely to cause not merely severe confusion, but to invite a familiarity or undermining of his authority that could prove to be the fuel for future rebellion. The former Autarch had explained that the Dark Faith functioned entirely on Might, and that while powerful servants could be wielded through their oaths of fealty, eventually some would seek to challenge Romulus for his Autarchy if given enough belief that a window existed.

The list of those permitted to do so was extremely narrow; that didn't mean they were nonexistent.

“I still don’t really see what the point of Siring a Sanguine outside of my immediate circle is if they can just backstab me and steal my place,” Romulus said while resting his hands on Lightsbane’s pommel with the blade tip-down against the dais before him, and his chin resting on said hands. “It seems like all I’m doing is exposing myself for minimal gain.”

Kate glanced between them with faint amusement as he raised the gripe, but chose silence instead of answering with a witticism, which Romulus loved her for.

“The point of the warning is that you are selective,” Mortarius replied patiently. “The vulnerability exists, that much is true, and no Sireling is guaranteed to remain forever loyal—but there is nothing in this world that is attained without risk. Selecting a proper Sireling is your obligation. The broader benefit of the Midnight Court goes far beyond the Attribute bonus a Sireling offers.”

“Immortal bodyguards?” Romulus asked skeptically, glancing sidelong at the specter.

“In the most basic consideration, certainly,” Mortarius allowed. “However, you are thinking too narrowly—or being willfully obstinate. An immortal court means possessing subordinates who can administer your Empire without the disharmony of managing new vassals each time a designated feudal subordinate succumbs to age. It means a network of loyalists who grow endlessly in power, and whose eternal existence is tied to your original goodwill. That is not to say that they will never manifest their own ambitions; but that is why the Faith informs its adherents to pursue Might. The Autarch is not merely included; they are expected to be the premier example.”

Romulus weighed Mortarius’s words in silence, turning back to the coffins surrounding Nate, Maya, and Cora. He could see the logic within his mentor’s words, and he couldn’t deny that the theoretical benefits of having a group of ridiculously attractive immortal retainers were appealing. However, none of that meant that he was in a position to go creating that kind of collective any time soon.

Besides, there was just too much to do.

Having his friends with him was incredibly beneficial, and it represented an immediate boost in his strategic and tactical capability, but that didn’t stop the rest of what had to be done from existing; he still needed to deal with Isolde, he still needed to recruit fifty thousand Faithful for Lilith, he still needed to raise his level enough to not get bodied by the first angry Paladin that found him, and he still needed to evolve both his Armor and Runeblade enough to make them formidable beyond their basic designations.

There was a lot he needed to do. The time in which to achieve it remained far less forgiving.

“You have the thinking face,” Kate noted from where she sat. “The grumpy one.”

“I don’t have a grumpy thinking fa—” Romulus sighed mid-protest. “Fine. I was grumpy-thinking.”

“I know,” his best friend said with a glimmer of jade-green-eyed amusement. “Thinking about your own Revenant-cult?”

“Kind of,” he admitted, leaning back in the throne as Mortarius observed the coffins. “Mostly just thinking about how much more there is to do, as well as how unlikely it is that I’ll be able to just snap my fingers and make new Sanguine Revenants. My current limit is two with my present Charisma Attribute, and even if my [Aura of Darkness] and level-ups improve that capacity, it’s going to be a hot minute before I can justify Siring anyone.”

Mortarius glanced back at him with faint approval, while Kate frowned faintly and turned to the coffins. “I know you have to wait,” she conceded after a moment. “However, I don’t think you need to put it off forever. I know you probably immediately feel guilty about not being able to Sire all four of us at once”—he sighed dramatically at her accurate deduction, and Kate smiled when she glanced at him afterward—“but that doesn’t mean you shouldn’t still think about the benefits. The Light and Fire weakness is a pretty big bummer given what we’re up against, I’ll grant you that, but the benefits super outweigh the negatives.”

“I’d prefer to wait until I can Sire you all with the extra perks of the Skill’s future growth,” Romulus admitted after she was done. “There are some nice bonuses that come with waiting until [Sanguine Kiss] evolves, but it paradoxically requires an existing Sireling to matter.”

Mortarius chose that moment to join in, his voice stern without being impatient. “Waiting until a later tier of the Skill to Sire your closest companions is not a mistake,” the former Revenant-King began, “but neither is indefinite procrastination wisdom. You will need at least one Sanguine Revenant to begin properly leveling the Skill. It would be wiser to view the early Siring as an investment. The Sired Sanguine may not possess the immediate benefits later ones will, but that will not impede their long-term growth.”

Kate nodded along at the Specter’s words, and Romulus reached up to run an armored hand through his platinum hair. “You make it sound so reasonable,” he accused Mortarius.

“I am usually quite a bit more reasonable than you entirely deserve, Romulus,” his mentor said serenely.

“Thanks for the reminder,” Romulus groused while Kate let out another pleased laugh.

The sudden flash of white along the first of the coffins halted the conversation, and Romulus sat up straight immediately. The leftmost coffin, from his viewpoint, began to split—expelling silver-white light through its top as it sliced itself open.

“Nate,” Romulus breathed as the light cut its way down to the upright coffin’s base, and the black stone abruptly burst into midnight flames. The inferno spiraled, twisting around the heart of where the coffin had been, then drew inward toward a rapidly revealed silhouette at the center of the conflagration.

Kate’s soft curse of disbelief punctuated the reveal perfectly.

Nathanius stood tall where the coffin had vanished, his body no longer covered by haphazard equipment, but instead embraced by jet-black warplate cut through with silver striations. Runic enchantment glyphs shimmered along his gauntlets, heavy pauldrons, and the edges of his warplate, which itself covered him from head to toe. Each piece was bespoke, and when Nate—his face covered by a sealed black helmet with two back-swept draconic wings—looked down at himself in disbelief, his arms and legs shifted without even the hint of difficulty.

Armor was usually sectioned or at least created in such a way as to permit chainmail to cover what plate could not—this harness showed no such concessions.

Each movement rippled through black metal that seemed to shift effortlessly to accommodate the wearer, with shimmers of platinum chainmail visible only at the joints where metal could not be bent, no matter its craft. A crimson tabard cloth hung from Nate’s belt on the front and back, emblazoned with a crowned-skull eclipse on the blood-colored material. An immense tower shield bearing the same marking sat on his left arm, and a thick broadsword ensconced in a bespoke black sheath hung at his left hip, perfectly positioned for a smooth cross-body draw.

All in all, he looked fucking outstanding.

“Holy shit, Nate,” Romulus said aloud, his lips spreading into a grin. “You look terrifying.”

“Says the man with skulls on his armor,” Katherine replied lightly while hopping from the armrest and descending to approach Nate, who looked between both of them and set down his immense shield, letting it rest against him while reaching up to remove his helmet.

“Dude, this is unreal, and Lilith…” Nate’s slate-gray eyes grew distant at the words, and he shook his head. “You weren’t exaggerating, man. Lilith is… something else.”

“That’s one way of putting it,” Kate quipped while Romulus grinned wordlessly in response, the redhead’s steps halting before their taller friend as she craned her head to look up at him. At 6’4, Nate was just shy of a foot taller than Kate even in her Eternus Eidos, and she seemed both amused and faintly aggrieved by the added inch the armor evidently gave him. “You’re big enough to tank, now. That’s promising, as long as you don’t act like you’re immortal.”

“Believe me, Kay, I’m not going to make that mistake,” Nate assured her.

Romulus, however, snorted. “Technically, Kay, we’re all immortal as Travelers.”

“Not functionally,” Mortarius corrected as Kate turned toward Romulus with an eyeroll, and all three Travelers turned their attention to the specter. “You are granted considerable leeway with death compared to others, but immortality is not a true description. Romulus may return when destroyed via Lightsbane’s anchor, allowing the Runeblade to tether his soul to Eternus—not within the sword; it is not a phylactery—while his Dark Mana reconstitutes his body through his soulbond with the Runeblade. The rest of you lack that particular indefinite anchor-based return.”

“So we’ve got, what, bonus lives?” Kate asked curiously. “That was never explained in the [Codex],” she said, summoning hers and flipping through the System-created book’s pages as Nate leaned in to look over her shoulder. “It just says we returned at Shrines.”

“Travelers existed during my reign, and I was able to observe their disconnection from regular demise through my Dominion,” Mortarius explained steadily. “The System anchors your Eidos through a form of reserve energy. Each time you die, this reserve diminishes. It recovers with time and advancement, but it is not infinite. To put it in more empirical terms; you are granted approximately three reconstitutions upon actualizing your Eidos in Eternus and connecting to the System. Each Tier you advance through—Initiate, Adept, Specialist, Expert, and so on—increases that allotment by one reconstitution.”

Romulus leaned back while considering the words carefully.

“So I only have unlimited exceptions because of Lightsbane, as long as it remains whole and isn’t destroyed”—the sword pulsed indignantly, and Romulus patted it reassuringly while he spoke—“but Kate, Nate, Maya, and Cora would only come back three times?”

“Until they reached higher Tiers, yes,” Mortarius confirmed.

“Then why did the System tell us permanent death was rare when we made our Eidos?” Nate asked with a frown. “That seems like kind of a relevant detail.”

“How often have you been near death without putting yourself up against an opponent that no sensible person of your level would seek to challenge, Nathanius?”

Nate hesitated at Mortarius’s words, and Romulus saw Kate’s expression light up with understanding at the same time.

“Basically never,” Nate admitted after a brief time of thought. “Everything we’ve fought within three levels of us has required concentration but hasn’t really threatened our lives. We still need healing and stuff if we don’t level, but life-threatening injuries are pretty rare unless we’re being idiots.”

“Unless you’re me, in which case you’re gucci unless some asshole goes full pyromaniac on you,” Romulus added in a disgruntled mutter.

“Yes, Lulu, we all know about your bullshit super-healing,” Kate said without ire, her green eyes glimmering with amusement. “Morty is talking about us mere mortals right now.”

Mortarius observed the exchange without disapproval—largely, Romulus assumed, because they’d taken his earlier decorum lessons seriously and because they were firmly alone at present—before nodding in agreement to their joint observations.

“You are all correct. As Travelers, the System is far more invested in guiding you directly than regular Eternians. That was not always the case, but that is another discussion. The fact remains that your Eidos are collectively more durable and inherently more predisposed toward bonuses like Traits, Titles, and other bonuses that regular Eternians cannot match. This makes you highly durable, and thereby, makes permanent demise extremely rare. Not impossible, but rare. The System did not, therefore, lie—it spoke from a position of logical presumption.”

All three of them considered the specter’s words, and Kate closed her [Codex] and let it dissipate into black motes matching her Dark Mana Affinity while looking mildly dissatisfied still.

“That sounds like some retroactive ass-covering, Morty,” she said with a sigh.

“Your perceived plot holes are not my concern, Katherine,” Mortarius noted mildly.

Romulus laughed despite himself at the reference, and Nate joined in, drawing Kate’s light-hearted glare toward both of them before a sudden crack of light arrested their joint mirth. Romulus’s eyes snapped to the two remaining coffins, and his lips spread into a grin for an entirely different reason.

“Finally,” he breathed, satisfied. “They’re done.”

Nate, Mortarius, and Kate turned as one, and the coffins exploded into flames.
