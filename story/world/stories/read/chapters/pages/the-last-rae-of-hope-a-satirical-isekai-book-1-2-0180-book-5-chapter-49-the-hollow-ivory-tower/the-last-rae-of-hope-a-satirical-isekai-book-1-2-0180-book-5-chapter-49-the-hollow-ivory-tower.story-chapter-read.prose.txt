Despite his best efforts, Ragnerus did not, in fact, blow a hole through the High Temple’s front doors. After Aleph and Tetora were safely out of the way, he tried his best, gathering up animus in his crimson-coated greatsword. As the blast he released arced up the stairs, the crystalline statues blazed white in stark opposition. His waning burst dispersed on contact, splashing harmlessly.

Ragnerus inhaled deeply, his size growing. “I will attempt a second maneuver.”

“You might want to take a moment,” I replied, noting that he was turning translucent. “Maybe we can combine efforts?”

The suggestion made him glitch. “I have no intention of connecting with you or whatever energy you are using.”

“Amity?”

He shook his head, steadfast in his refusal. “Amity is not pink!”

I spread my hands at Nora, scowling as I silently demanded backup.

She just shrugged in return.

“Together?” Olethros suggested as he settled in next to him.

Ragnerus narrowed his eyes, grunted, and replied, “Together but separate.”

“As you wish, brother.”

Olethros produced another throwaway staff in his hand. He added his own parallel blast to Ragnerus’s next attempt, but the statues glowed brighter. The doors held against their onslaught.

I stretched my arms, cracking my neck and shoulders. “Okay, third time’s a—”

“No.” Nora’s hand came up, almost making it into my face. “Save up for the battle inside. It’s my turn now.”

Ragnerus and Olethros exchanged a look before parting to the sides.

“I’m helping,” she instructed, waving her hands. “And I’m the bridge between you. None of this separate stuff, got it? We strike as one! On three!”

Both grumbled under their breath, including her confusing use of numbers, but eventually nodded.

“One,” she said as she gripped her staff in both hands. “Two!” she shouted as both black and white crystals ignited.

The statues began to pulse.

“Three!”

All of them fired simultaneously. Nora swung her staff almost as if casting a fishing line, her arms somewhat rigid with just enough give to ride the blowback from her dual-barreled blast. Her electric blue aura extended along with it, pulling both Olethros’s and Ragnerus’s beams into a single, focused line of magical destruction.

When their blast hit, however, Nora pulled up on her staff, righting and tapping it on the stone floor to ground it.

There was no explosion. No debris, not even a smolder. The doors simply didn’t exist anymore. Even the metal hinges had been vaporized. Yet nothing touching them had been harmed.

Even Aleph’s attention had been ensnared, his jaw slack with astonishment as he stared into the entrance.

“Whew!” Nora said, blowing the blazing white lock of hair out of her face. “Well, shall we?”

I turned back to look at Aurum, but Ragnerus was already on it.

“Beigaldi.”

He and Bodhil both formed with a loud buzz, appearing quite excited to be tagged into the party.

“See to the Captain’s horse.”

Their simultaneous salute was crisp, but the glare they gave me was full of silent epithets.

***

Once inside, Aleph insisted on taking point. His reasoning, mostly directed at Vernie, was quite blunt.

“I know of no traps, but that does not mean there aren’t any.”

“I am most aware of the layout here.”

Aleph turned away. “You would do well to follow.”

He moved through the main entrance hall with long, swift strides, not even bothering to stay within Nora’s generated barrier.

“There is nothing left inside,” was all he would say when we scolded him for his recklessness.

The High Temple’s interior had not fared well. Past its foyer, the gold had long since been stripped from the fixtures, as we had recommended back during our first stay. Tapestries hung askew. Candles, even if lit, were mostly just stubs, with nary a replacement in sight. Books and parchments were scattered along the edges of the hallways, mixed with a layer of dirt and leaves.

A cloak was abandoned on a side bench in a dark hallway. Most doors were left open, cracking on hinges as we passed. One crossing path was full of footprints.

“What happened to housekeeping?” Nora finally asked, kicking a broken clay cup out of the way.

“The staff were sent to Paradise, to prepare a house for the Faithful,” Aleph said quietly. “Or so I was told.”

“And yet you stayed?” I blurted. “Here? In this?”

Aleph’s shoulders sagged, though he did not look back. “I should be the last to flee.”

We reached the sanctuary doors, sealed with a golden glow. Nora glanced about before asking, “No statues to inhibit us, right?”

Aleph nodded. “Priests… Priests were not crystallized—inside the temple.”

Tetora inhaled sharply at that, and I stifled a gasp.

“Any private denunciations within the Temple were open invitations to Paradise,” he continued quietly. “None came back.”

“Aleph?”

“Outside, the judgment was swift,” he continued despite my interruption. “If dissenters came to me, I could send them out of the city through hidden exits. But if they were caught by Liora or her puppet Corwin…”

Nora struck the ground with the butt of her staff. “Is that what happened to Arlena?!”

“I could not cast her away without removing her from duty,” he replied morosely. “For all my pathetic attempts at subterfuge, I was never punished. Only those I tried to help.”

Meaning he was too useful to get rid of.

Oh Aleph…

Nora, Olethros, and Ragnerus repeated their destructive performance on the sanctuary's doors, which proved considerably less intimidating the second time around. Nora didn’t even need to count to three.

Yet there was nothing of any value to us inside. Even Vernie didn’t bother pocketing any trinkets.

“I thought…” I trailed off. “I thought we’d find them in here—Sparkles and Aziza!”

“So did I, er, at least one of them, anyway,” Nora admitted. She lit up her staff, casting a bright glow throughout. The altar, wide and broad, was devoid of any religious artifacts.

General Ragnerus nudged me. “Please instruct me.”

“On what?”

He grunted. “How to pray to Euphridia.”

“Oh, uh, you… Well—”

“First, go up front,” Nora commanded, pointing with a resolute finger. “You can kneel or stand—either is acceptable.”

Ragnerus turned his attention to her, putting his hands stiffly at his sides. “And then?’

“How about I just walk you through it?” she replied, heading towards the altar. “That way you can be assured you did it right.”

Nora… Teaching someone how to pray to Euphridia.

Now I’ve seen everything.

Actually, maybe I shouldn’t be seeing this.

As I started to turn my back to give a bit of privacy, Ragnerus called out, “I want witnesses.”

With Nora’s instructions, he kept it simple. She taught him about praying silently, but he would have none of it. Though kneeling, he was as loud as he could be when he spoke.

“Goddess Euphridia. I have come to pray for Epiales’s, my father’s, soul. I do this on behalf of my father, as requested, over 2700 years ago.”

How old is this demon?!

“Epiales seeks not your forgiveness. He wishes only to be unmade. To return all existence that does not belong to him, and to have taken from him what he cannot rid himself of.”

With that, he stood up. I glanced at Olethros, who had covered himself in shadow.

“End the prayer with an amen,” Nora muttered, appearing quite flustered. “It’s customary.”

“Amen.”

Nothing changed. No glow, yet no smiting either; nothing in the room reacted. And so, left to silently contemplate the words Ragnerus shared, we moved on.

***

“Maybe we should just go to the top of the main tower and work our way down?” Vernie suggested after searching the previously sealed assembly hall, stone bleachers and all. Chester mewled his agreement from somewhere behind her.

“Perhaps.... Yet there is one other sealed door that I know of,” Aleph replied. “The tower in which Relias resided during his penitence.”

Nora and I exchanged a glance.

“Of course…” Tetora huffed. “Of course they’re there! Why didn’t you suggest that first?!”

Aleph stiffened. “We have to be thorough," he replied quietly.

Tetora paused, then looked down. “The words are right, but the tone is still off,” he murmured sadly.

***

The tower stairs were narrow and winding, designed for isolation rather than convenience. The walls leading up were just off-white, simple and unadorned.

Aleph led the way once more, speaking more now.

“The first ones were protestors,” he recalled two flights up. “They spoke out against the angels. Some said we were turning into demons ourselves.” He paused. “They were easy to frame as the enemy of the faithful.”

Nora’s barrier hummed around us as we climbed. We passed by a rare window that held only a cloudy darkness beyond it.

“Demon sympathizer was another term brought forth as a crime,” Aleph continued, his footsteps slow and heavy.

Another landing. I turned, noting Olethros and Ragnerus looked more solid now, farther away, I supposed, from the crystalline statues.

“How high is this thing?" Nora complained. “We’ve been climbing for hours!”

Aleph paused. “It is just a little shorter than the main tower.”

“And, to clarify, it has only been a few minutes,” Olethros added, glancing at Aleph. “Does she not remind you of anyone else who tends to bluster and exaggerate?”

Aleph refused to look at him, choosing to go back to confessing as the easier interaction. “I began removing the Order of Silver when I saw some of the knights acting strangely. I demoted those I could, giving them reason to be cast out of the city. Stripping rank made it easier, but now… I am not sure if anyone truly made it out as I had surmised.”

None of us wanted to tell him that none of them had reached us.

The door at the top was unlike anything else in the temple. It was a thick alloy of copper, gold, silver, and, according to Olethros, platinum. Both halves of it seemed to connect like a puzzle.

And it opened on our approach without a single sound.

The room beyond was just as ornate: wide, round, and low-ceilinged. Everything was ordered in concentric circles, golden runes carefully inlaid in the white marble floor. A large banker’s desk was built into one curved wall. A bed in another. Books, trinkets, even a crude telescope of sorts.

Basically, Relias’s prison had all the amenities Speranza could possibly offer.

And in the middle, a raised, glowing dais, with a giant orb floating above it. Crimson ink flowed within it like liquid lava, coiling and stretching as it swirled in a pool of… iridescent sparkles.

“He imprisoned them together?!” Nora gasped.

“Zizi!” Ragnerus bellowed as Olethros braced himself. “Zizi! A truce!”

"That goes for you too, Sparkles!" Nora added.

The thick crimson liquid swirled, webbing across the orb’s surface, only to be swallowed into the depths of the sparkle around it. Then the two mixed almost frantically, each fighting for dominance. Something whirred loudly from the dais, and both Olethros and Ragnerus turned translucent once more.

"Rae!" Nora shouted.

Her barrier flickered as both crystals on her staff dimmed. Her blue aura faded with them.

“You’re up!” she cried as her barrier poofed.

I set myself. There was a low and persistent pull at my aura.

Oh, I don’t think so!

Whatever energy-collection device this was, I didn’t consent to being part of it. With an audible growl, I reached out to my companions, asking and receiving all of the Faith and Trust they could muster.

Aleph was the last to respond. Hesitancy masked his guilt and anger, with more than a little pointed at me. With a simple but heartfelt murmur of "please meet me where I need you now,” I was able to secure his trust at least temporarily. Then I started to push back against the invisible pull.

The hum stalled, skipping as the dais began to crack. With my pink aura flaring, I shielded with Faith.

“Brace yourselves—both of you!”

I swung Will in a wide arc and struck the orb with everything I had.
