The third-floor desert quadrant was just how Jade remembered it. Sweltering, sandy, and mercilessly sunny.

"Ugh, why did we choose this node?" Naomi groaned, holding up a hand to shield her face from the sunlight. "We should've picked the one in the flatlands. You know, the place that won't burn me in twenty minutes.""We shouldn't go back there so soon. There could still be demons prowling about." Jade replied.

"Since when were you the careful one?"

"Since we almost died fighting them." Jade said with uncharacteristic solemnity. "I want to protect our home, Naomi. But I also want to protect us."

"Thanks…" Naomi sighed, trudging after Luis as he led the way towards the road that ran along the edge of the quadrant towards Illysport. She gave Jade a weak smile. "I'm glad you're thinking like that."

"It's easy to think these powers make us invincible. But we aren't."

Jade squinted against the brightness of the day. The light was affecting her bad eye more than her good one, and it was giving her another headache. When she'd been human, she had been just as susceptible to burning as Naomi. Growing up in the Detroit suburbs didn't give many opportunities for a girl to get a tan. While she couldn't fix her eye, the latter issue was one she now had a solution for.

"No fair." Naomi grumbled, watching enviously as Jade's skin darkened to a deep tan.

"You made it through this place once, you'll get through it again.""I burned that time, too. It only just went away.""Just check in Jade's bag!" Luis called back from his position in front of them. "I put some sunscreen in while we were on Earth!"

"You did?" Jade asked. "When?"

She fished around in the dimensional pouch and, sure enough, felt her hand close around a bottle. Pulling it free, she examined it.

"That day I went shopping. I think I dumped half the drug store in there, just in case."

"Where did you think we'd be going, the sun?" Jade asked skeptically. The bottle's SPF rating was '100'. "I'm as pale as they come. Err… at least I was. And even I don't wear this."

"I'll take what I can get. Thanks, Luis! You're the best."

"Never know what you'll need." He said cheerfully, giving her a thumbs up.

"Did you get one of those ten-foot poles you kept telling us about in the early days?"

"Sure did! A normal one wouldn't fit, but I found a collapsible one in the hardware store."

True to his words, Jade pulled a segmented pole from the bag next. She grinned.

"Remind me about this the next time we're in a dungeon.""Sure thing, amiga. Like Uncle Marco always said, 'the classics are classic for a reason.'"

The three of them reached the road less than an hour later. The journey was uneventful, interrupted only by a handful of scorpion monsters that burst up out of the sand to attack them. The level 19 beasts possessed a strength that once would have terrified them. Now, with their combined power and combat experience, they were little more than a nuisance. From there they turned spinward, following the path that led towards the city of Illysport. There were few travelers on the road; Over the course of the next hour the only group they encountered was a caravan of wagons pulled by strix. The large lizards wore harnesses, and the heavily laden carts were drawn by four beasts each.

Jade and her companions stepped off the road to allow the group to pass. She looked over them curiously, noting the mix of humans, elves, and frog-like rhyssits sitting in the wagons alongside crates of goods. Their group caught more than a few wary looks, but once it was clear they would keep to themselves, no one bothered them.

"Pretty heavily armed for a trade caravan." Luis commented after the last of the wagons passed by, glancing over his shoulder at them as he stepped back onto the road. Most of the passengers held large crossbows or had blades sheathed at their hips.

"What did you expect?" Jade asked with a shrug. "The monsters on this floor are too tough for any normal person to take one in a fair fight. A group that size will draw attention, and they need to deal with it."

"Remind me why people live here, again?" Naomi asked.

"I don't think all of them had a choice."

"You remember all the loot we got in the stronghold? These folks must make a killing buying and selling to wayfarers like us."

"I bet food goes for a lot, too." Jade added. "The area around Estelhelm had some farms, but it can't be enough to feed everyone in the city. I remember Fenrin mentioning that most towns and cities in the Labyrinth import their food."

"That's crazy." Naomi looked surprised. "I wonder what the cost of interdimensional shipping is."

"Probably depends on how strong the monsters are between the transport nodes."

"Dimensional bags have to help." Luis said, pointing at Jade's hip. "A cart filled with those could carry anything."

"Not anything. These do have a weight limit, you know."

Luis blinked.

"They do?"

"Yes. We were told that when we bought it." Jade gave her friend a sidelong glance. "Also, the bag's interface is telling me we're almost at the limit. Just how much did you put in there when you went on your shopping trip?"

"Oh, a little of this and that. Tools, medicine, hardware, food-"

"How many places did you have to go for all that? I only gave you the bag for, like, an hour!"

"Oh!" Luis snapped his fingers, ignoring the question. "I stocked us back up on the explosives we used, too."

"Oh, well, in that case… all is forgiven."

"Wow." Naomi said, pointing ahead. "Look!"

The three of them reached the top of a gently sloping hill, revealing a stunning vista laid out before them. The desert scrubland gave way to a patch of vibrant green wetlands that stretched to the shore of a distant lake so vast, Jade would've called it an ocean if Fenrin hadn't described it to her. Rolling swells broke against a sandy beach, sunlight sparkling off the crashing waves like millions of diamonds. The road continued, winding down to a cluster of buildings surrounding a pier that stretched from the beach to a pair of waiting ships.

"Damn, now that's a view." Luis said, whistling his appreciation as Jade nodded in agreement.

"I thought the city was on an island in the lake." Naomi asked, shielding her eyes against the sun's glare and sweeping her gaze across the azure expanse."I'm sure it is, we just can't see it from here."

"Lakes get this big? I thought we'd be able to see across it."

"As a Great Lakes girl, I promise you that they do." Jade laughed.

"Let's see if we can get passage on one of those ships." Luis suggested. "It won't be dark for a few hours. If we hurry, we should be at the city before night."

Jade and Naomi nodded in agreement and the three of them set off down the hill, making their way to the dock. The lake's size made distance difficult to gauge, and the trip took longer than Jade expected. It was only thanks to their advanced stats that none of them were more than mildly winded after such a long day on the road.

"Remember our first day together?" Jade asked as they entered the small lakeside town. "We only made it an hour before we had to take a break, and that was with goblins chasing us."

"Oh God, my legs were sore for days. The only thing that kept me going was that bath in Juniper's grove."

"That's why we put points in fortitude." Luis grinned."Easy for the tank to say!" Naomi pouted. "Some of us need other stats to be effective."

Jade grinned, leading the way to the pier. The small town wasn't bordered by a wall, although it did have several watch towers stationed around its perimeter. A pair of bored looking human guards stood next to the road, but they seemed accustomed to travelers. Jade merely had to present her wayfarer mark, and they were all waved through without being challenged. She found her way to a ticket stand built into one of the dock facilities and purchased passage to Illysport for the three of them. Even after donating some of their crescent coins and magical materials to L-Ref, Jade's team had no shortage of funds. Multiple quests had showered each of them with lavish rewards since the last time they'd had a chance to spend any money. Compared to their shared treasury, the cost of the tickets wasn't even a rounding error.

That settled, Jade, Luis, and Naomi followed the elven attendant's directions and moved to board the nearer of the two ships. As soon as she set foot on the dock, a notification popped up.

System Notice

Now entering 'Wavecrest Lake' Quadrant, 'Tempest' Zone, Area #254,187

"Tempest zone?" She wondered aloud, stopping near the gangway.

"First time on the lake, miss?" A rhyssit crewman asked her. The squat, amphibious creature spoke with a raspy, squelching voice that sounded masculine to her ear… although it was hard to tell.

"That's right." She nodded, trying not to stare. "Something we should know?"

Elves and tek'kalla were one thing, but rhyssits were truly alien. This one wore a similar blue jacket to the other men and women aboard the vessel. Unlike them, though, he wore… nothing else. The man looked back at her with his bulbous eyes, blinking slowly.

"Gets stormy out there. Have one rolling in now." He pointed with a webbed hand towards the horizon, where a bank of dark clouds was gathering. Jade could have sworn they hadn't been there earlier. "Get ready for a bumpy ride, miss!"

Jade staggered onto one of the many piers stretching from the island city of Illysport, heedless of the rain that continued to fall from storm grey skies. Falling to her knees, the succubus clutched a wooden post and retched, leaning off the side of the boardwalk. Nothing came out, of course. There was nothing left. Unfortunately, her body hadn't yet gotten the memo.

"That was awful…" Naomi groaned next to her. She plucked a few notes on her guitar, but the magic fizzled out. Muttering a curse, the bard repeated the tune. It took three tries before the spell took hold, soft light emanating from the instrument and sinking into her skin. Color slowly returned to her cheeks and she sat a little straighter, breathing hard.

"Me next, please…" Jade moaned.

Another few notes, another glow, and Jade felt warmth spread through her body. The nausea eased, and she drew in a deep breath, letting the lake breeze gust against her face. Beside her, Naomi rubbed her temples.

"Ugh. Low mana headaches are the worst."

"C'mon you two, you'll feel better once we get inside and find somewhere warm." Luis said, helping them up. "Let's get out of the rain."

"I've changed my mind." Naomi said, the three of them walking along the pier towards the myriad lights that marked the tall buildings of the island city. "I'm putting my next level ups into fortitude."

Braston Brent had few regrets when it came to his current employment. Yes, the Ebon Eye was ostensibly a criminal organization, but as far as such groups went, it really wasn't that bad. What businesses didn't skirt along the edges of the law every now and again, after all? That the group he belonged to was more open about their position than most made them more moral, not less. That aside, his assignment for the past month was an especially boring one. Standing watch by the main gate of the city and recording descriptions of the foreign wayfarers coming and going was neither lucrative nor engaging. At least on the expedition with lord Ledkin there'd been opportunities to loot monsters, as insufferable as the man was.

But no, he was spending another day in the incessant pouring rain watching people come and go all because he had been the one to identify the demon adventurer. Now, the Matron was intrigued and was content to waste his time on the off chance she showed up with the other "Earth" wayfarers that had been filtering in lately.

"Who is she kidding." Braston grunted to himself, the words lost to the pounding of rain on cobblestone. "Even if they do come here, they won't be on the third floor for another-"

His words trailed off as another group entered the gate, waved through by the guards. Through the rain and gloom, he caught sight of a streak of blonde hair that stood out among the darker tones that were more common in these parts. He peered closer, trying to get a better look. Could it be her? Yes… she was walking with the large, tan-skinned man and the bard. The smaller woman was even carrying the same kind of strange instrument she had been on the first floor. It had to be them.

With a murmured prayer of thanks to the lost gods Braston slipped into the street, making his way through the cramped, winding roads of Illysport as he trailed the odd group. First, he had to find out where they were going. Then, he could bring word back to headquarters and finally be free of this damned assignment. Get back to doing jobs that didn't involve spoiled nobles or mysterious demon adventurers.

How nice that would be.
