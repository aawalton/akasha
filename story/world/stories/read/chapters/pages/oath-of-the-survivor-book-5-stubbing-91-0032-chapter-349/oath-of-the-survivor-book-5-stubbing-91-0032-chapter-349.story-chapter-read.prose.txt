“For the dozenth time, I didn’t know anything about Ger’Sinh, the egg, or his backer prior to the Practicum.  And honestly, I’d almost entirely forgotten them until they showed back up to try and kill me.”

“I apologize for the redundancy.  Sometimes such is necessary for a thorough investigation, after all.  Do you care for more tea?”

By now, Kyle’s resistance to the offered treats was nil, and he gladly accepted the cup of warm liquid that was teleported over to him.  And, in fairness, the tea was delightful.  Kyle should know, as it was his third cup.  He and Iberious had been talking for nearly four hours now, with the orc asking detailed questions about most aspects of his life since arriving at the Collective, always seeming to bring the conversation back to different pieces of his meeting with Ger’Sinh.

“Wasn’t Ger’Sinh captured?” Kyle asked after taking a sip.  “I’d imagine you could learn quite a bit by interrogating him.”

“Bwahaha!” Iberious exclaimed, slapping his stomach as he let out a belly laugh, made more disconcerting by the orc’s plates of food and cups of drink floating where he’d left them.  “Oh, that was funny, my boy.  Quite amusing indeed.  No, the Archdrake Supremacy have been quite slow in granting access to Ger’Sinh and his co-conspirator, that goblin scholar you met.  It seems as though they’re still working quite hard on gathering the information they’re looking for as well.  I’ll get my time with them though, soon enough.  And rest assured, I’ve also read all the reports the Supremacy has put together.”

“Oh?” Kyle asked, his curiosity piqued.  “What did they have to say?”

“Not my news to share, Mr. Mayhew.  Although, you’re asking the right types of questions.  May I be blunt with you, Kyle?”  The orc asked the question as he tilted his head forward, eye behind the monocle twinkling slightly.

This was the first time Iberious used Kyle’s first name, and he leaned forward, nodding for the orc to continue.

“You’ve a good habit of asking the right questions, which is to be commended.  However, you continually ask them to the wrong people.  How common are mid-tier D Grades across the Collective, Kyle?”“I’m not entirely sure,” Kyle said.  “One in a few thousand or so?”

“Ah, you’re letting your lived experience cloud your answer.  Roughly one in three hundred and fifty thousand.  And how common are C Grades?”

This one Kyle knew.  “We’re about one in a billion,” he responded.

“Nominally, that number is close,” Iberious agreed.  “Realistically, nearly ninety percent of C Grades get bottlenecked in early C Grade, however.  Of those that make it to mid-tier C Grade, even fewer will make it to high-tier, and only a scant few among C Grade will make it to B Grade.  Now, with that said, why do you think I bring this up?”

“I’m not entirely sure,” Kyle admitted.

“Of course you aren’t!  Because, for some reason, you still see yourself as the same E Grade you were back on Earth!  You struggle to recognize the impact you should rightfully have, and are altogether too passive.  You are a personal existence that can, and should, be compared to corporate leaders across the Gray Conglomerate.  While that doesn’t mean making threats, you can rightfully be more demanding, particularly in matters that involve you personally.”

“What are you trying to say?” Kyle said, bristling at the words.

“I’m saying that you would be well-served to start thinking like a C Grade!  When you have been wronged, you are well-within your rights to demand answers.  All of the Collective member nations have policies and procedures in place for just such events.  Demanding a seat at the table is something expected of somebody with your station.  While it is unlikely that the Archdrake Supremacy will simply grant you access, I would be shocked if there was even the slightest hesitation in releasing transcripts of their interviews to you.”

Kyle nodded along with Iberious’ words.  Marcus had given him similar advice, not long ago.  The problem was, Kyle didn’t know how far he could push.  Since his world had been turned upside down by the cataclysm on Earth, he’d been largely on the back foot, trying not to cross one power or another.  It was an odd feeling knowing that he was now in a position to make demands and be taken seriously.  He’d come a long way from being a simple frontier doctor trying to make his way.  Of course, alongside the advice, Iberious had also played a bit of his hand.

“So if you think I’ve been wronged, I take it that means you don’t think I’m guilty of planning the slaughter of countless innocents to pursue my own path to power?”

Iberious snorted.  “Of course not, my boy.  I largely had that pegged before I ever arrived here for your interview.  If I’d truly believed you to be a danger to the Collective, or society at large, I would have had you taken in and sealed away before we met.  No, I wanted to get your measure.  To better understand the upstart who caused so many waves.”

“And?” Kyle pressed, meeting Iberious’ eyes.

“And what?” the orc shot back.

“Did you find what you were looking for?”

“As a point of fact, I wasn’t looking for anything.  Rather, I found deeper insights about who you are, which is sufficient for my purposes.  You are stubborn, foolhardy, and idealistic to a fault.  At the same time, you’ve been thrust into situations well beyond both your power and experience, which has given you a skewed perspective on the world.  Your pursuit of improvement is bordering on recklessness, yet you are timid as a mouse in your dealings with powerful organizations.”

“After everything that’s happened, wouldn’t you be?” Kyle asked, feeling a bit of familiar heat in his chest.

“Of course I would!”  Iberious exclaimed, the bombastic response dousing the building fire before it even formed.  “I don’t fault you for your response in the slightest!  However, it is crucial for you to recognize the bias, and stay mindful of it.  You’ve had terrible dealings, yes, but that doesn’t mean that every negative interaction has to do with you personally.  Remember, what you see of yourself, and what other organizations see of you can be two entirely different things.”

“It’s hard not to take it personally when they’re literally trying to strip away my livelihood and my citizenship all at once,” Kyle grumbled.

“And that’s a perfectly valid reaction,” Iberious affirmed.  “At the same time, your opposition sees a budding powerhouse with a stack of favors growing by the day, just waiting to be called in.  And if such an organization had anything to hide, say, on a planet they’d hoped was lifeless, they would be most surprised to find such an individual not only thriving on their planet, but with a strong claim of ownership to it.  Why, in such a case, their best interests would be served by trying to remove that piece from play before it gets too much time on the board.”

Kyle frowned, hearing some of Marcus’ words echoed by the orc.  “When you say I have a claim to the planet, what do you mean?”

Iberious held up his porcine hands in a gesture of surrender.  “Now, I never said that you did.  But topics like residency get quite complicated, particularly when meaningful, qualitative improvements are at play.  Coupled with a catastrophic event all but forcing an occupant to settle the world, and some courts would be quite sympathetic to the person who was left behind.”

“And you think there’s something on Kertan Six that they don’t want to be exposed?”  Kyle said.

“Mr. Mayhew, does the word ‘subtlety’ mean anything to you?”  Iberious asked with an exasperated sigh.  “But yest, if you chose to interpret my words that way, it wouldn’t be wholly incorrect.”

“What is it?” Kyle asked, continuing to ignore the orc’s desire for a subtext-filled conversation.

“I have little more than speculation.  However, throughout all of this mess, there is a question that I’ve yet to receive a strong answer for.”  Iberious shuffled a bit in his chair as his expression grew more serious.  “What happened to the C Grade that killed Malcolm Corthus?”

Kyle opened his mouth, then closed it again.  He assumed that the Collective handled it if Valentine was teleported away, or that he was killed by the attack that scoured the planet should they have reneged on their deal to send him off-world.  But he didn’t know.  Nobody save Tillienne had really talked about it at all.

“I see you understand the conundrum.  Across all my investigation thus far, I have yet to get a firm answer as to his fate.  That only leaves me with the assumption that his disposition or incarceration is largely overstated.  Which makes sense, given his power.  Who wouldn’t want to fold a powerhouse like him into their military might?” Iberious asked before taking a sip of tea, then continued.

“It’s a classic misdirection, really.  Your accusers turn the spotlight toward you in order to hide what they’re doing behind the scenes.  Ideally, you are taken off the board by an old enemy, with causes and motivations that are easy enough to explain.  When that fails, they turn litigious to avoid attention on the one thing they don’t want discovered.”

“But then the planet gets sealed off,” Kyle realized, the pieces coming together.  “So they can’t get to whatever it is they don’t want found.  And now I’m a problem, because I may have a stronger claim than they would at the auction.  They assumed I understood that, and acted to try and prevent me from making a move for the planet.  Because evidence of what they did with Valentine could still be there.”

“It certainly appears that could be the case.  Now the question is, what will you do about it?”

Kyle thought about his options for just a moment, but his decision was clear as day.  “I’m going to get access to the planet again, investigate the ruins to see what I can learn, and then I’m going to chase down Valentine and put an end to him.”

A strong sense of agreement came from both C.H.A.D.D. and Phaegos, echoing Kyle’s own conviction.  If an organization out there pulled strings to save Valentine, he was going to expose them, then finish what he’d started back on Earth.  There was also an unspoken question raised about Victor DeRosa, and Kyle fully intended to investigate that, too.  If there was even a chance that they both survived, Kyle was the only survivor from Earth with the power to put them down.  And they needed to be put down, to answer for what they’d done.

“Strong words,” Iberious said with a nod of approval, standing and straightening out his clothing.  “Well, with that, I believe this portion of the investigation is complete.  Oh, and work on that identification skill of yours.  The Collector’s Guild has jobs from time to time that you may be suited for, and with your skills you’d be welcome to subcontract here and there.  Enhancing your powers of perception will only make collaboration more likely.”

“I’ll keep that in mind,” Kyle said, standing.

“Good hunting, Kyle Mayhew,” Iberious said with a smile, then stepped out the door, leaving Kyle alone with his thoughts.  There was a load of pressure off his shoulders, knowing the Tribunal would end in his favor.  There was also quite a bit to set up before anything was formally announced.  Iberious had given him an opportunity to gain his footing, and Kyle intended to take advantage of it.

Pulling out his nav bracelet, he sent a message to Suierrillax and Jarberry.

How soon can you meet?
