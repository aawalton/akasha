The Rhellians were a lot easier to manage now that there were no more Juren folk to influence them. It also probably helped that Ryland was truthful about his intentions. He explained how Acrypha had been taken by the Flourinus Order and had chained her to the Overrealm, to breach which Ryland would need a powerful Inscription.

This Inscription needed a base to act, and as such, Ryland had chosen Rhel as the closest site. After all, it was deserted and ruined already. If there were further altercations or other issues, no one would be too negatively impacted by it.

The Rhellians weren’t exactly happy that their home was being used as a base from which Ryland would launch himself into the Overrealm. But they understood.

Just as Rhel was precious to them, Acrypha was precious to Ryland as well.

“You are free to remain on Rhel if you wish,” Ryland said. “I certainly shan’t be chasing you out and I can even help you a little with things like finding shelter and whatnot. However, I won’t be able to pay proper attention nor give you the time you deserve until my business is done.”

“We understand,” the first dragonkin said. He had mellowed out a bit now that there was no Juren mage to influence him. “We are all suffering from the same unfortunate consequence. From acts beyond our control. And we’re all trying to cope as best as we can.”

“Indeed. We are. We’re doing the best we can. I know someone who will be very happy to help you however you need, so I’ll introduce you to him. [Translocate].”

A rift opened up before him, mildly startling all the Rhellians, before Viren appeared.

“Hello,” he said warmly. Ryland had sent him a message about what was going on, and unsurprisingly, Viren had agreed to deal with the issue personally and with great gusto. “I’m Viren and I’m here to help.”

Satisfied that the Rhellians were in good hands, Ryland left them to it. Of course, Viren wouldn’t be capable of giving the Rhellians everything they needed, but with this little move, Ryland had now struck two birds with one stone. Viren was occupied instead of being bored out of his mind and the Rhellians were dealt with as well. For the time being, at least.

Which meant Ryland could now deal with the third stone.

“Are you ready?” the Oracle asked, appearing before him as though she had stepped out of thin air.

Ryland nodded. “Let’s go meet our guests.”

“I believe they’ve been terrified for a bit too long.”

“Terrified? I’ve only kept them trapped in [Void Seal] for about two hours. Probably less.”

“Only.”

Ryland shook his head, unwilling to see much of a problem with this. For now, they entered the [Void Seal]’s boundary. All the Juren mages jumped as the veil parted, allowing Ryland and the Oracle entry into the enclosed region. [Void Seal] quickly snapped closed behind them.

“You!” The first Juren mage had summoned up the courage to confront Ryland somehow. “Release us this instant! How dare you keep innocent mages trapped inside like this?”

A few hours inside [Void Seal], which should have shown him that there was no point in getting into an altercation against Ryland, should have mellowed him out just as the Rhellians had done. After all, he had failed to get out despite his best efforts. But despite that, he was valiantly trying to use outrage as a weapon instead of magic.

Smart and bold. Ryland had to give him that much, at least.

“I will allow you to leave,” Ryland said. “But only after we’ve had a nice, long conversation.”

“We will not agree to any of your demands!” the Juren mage said.

His continued aggressive stance was injecting his companions with bravado too. They were quickly discarding their earlier apprehension at seeing Ryland effortlessly enter a space they had failed to escape. Now, they appeared almost as fired up as their apparent leader. This was looking like it might pose Ryland a bit of a headache.

Thankfully, he had brought his own form of reinforcements.

“Even when you know you are in the wrong?” the Oracle asked.

All the Juren mages turned to her. Her voice had struck with such gravity, they weren’t even able to summon up a pathetic protest that it wasn’t true.

“What gave you the right to think that your claim to Rhel supersedes anyone else’s?” another Juren mage asked, tall and richly dressed.

“You are mistaken,” Ryland said. “This is not at all about determining legitimacy of claims or anything of the sort. You are working under the mistaken assumption that I’m here to negotiate or convince you of anything. Let me make this clear—there is only one person who decides what happens next, and that is me.”

If the Oracle was able to summon a great deal of gravity, the Ryland was capable of undeniable finality as well. The Juren mages understood they had zero room to negotiate here. They were at Ryland’s mercy.

“What do you want?” the first Juren mage asked, finally cowed.

His sudden submission depressed the rest of the Juren mages even further. Where they had seemed like they were about to rekindle their hopes, now they deflated as though a needle had poked holes in their flesh suit.

Ryland didn’t answer. Instead, he allowed the Oracle to take the floor.

“You’ve come from Jure with an agenda,” she said, her words a statement, not a question. “And this agenda is not one about who decides what happens with Rhel or anything of the sort. It may be in part, but it is far from the only or even the most significant truth.”

The Juren contingent didn’t answer. Once again, the sheer gravity of the Oracle’s words didn’t allow them to come up with silly protestations. And of course, they surely had an Oracle or two on Jure as well.

“You were bidden to come here,” Oracle Vertha said. “By entities who are motivated beyond simple politics.”

“I… cannot answer that,” the first Juren mage said with a sigh. He was starting to look rather uncomfortable. “But you are correct. Even I suspect that my superiors didn’t simply want to gain control of Rhel. They want something more.”

Of course, Ryland suspected that the Juren mage might just be stringing them along so he and his party could leave in one piece. But Ryland had a Glyph of Truth actively confirming what were lies and what was the truth.

“And I suppose you have suspicions of what this something more might be?” the Oracle said. “You must have. I know you came here knowing full well that the simple desires you came to satisfy are not at all things that interest your superiors, who have it all and more.” Then she twisted the knife. “Your actions are… religiously motivated. By the faith in the Great Gods.”

They didn’t deny it. Not a one of them. Maybe they had figured out that Ryland and Vertha would be able to catch lies rather easily.

The first Juren mage sighed. “Alright, I’ll tell you what I know. As much as I can. But in return, I want a promise of our safety once this is over.”

“You have it,” Ryland said. “But you will only be allowed to leave with a few conditions. I will reveal what they are in due time. For now, answer the kind Oracle’s questions, will you?”

Ophelia wasn’t quite appreciating the fact that she had to spend time with this… nutcase.

She wasn’t sure how else to think of the fellow. This was supposed to be a Turnalian. She had read the old stories, had heard the old legends spoken to her, had immersed herself in the fanciful tales that children all across the Accorded Realms enjoyed when they were young.

Now, she was seeing one in the flesh, when there weren’t supposed to be any of them alive anywhere.

And what a far cry from the legends this Turnalian was turning out to be.

“Are you certain you don’t wish to bask in the Master’s light?” Devas asked for the hundredth time. “It’s quite bright, I’ll have you know. Also, if you were not aware, constantly refusing the light reflects poorly upon you. For you are refusing all that is great and holy in this world.”

“I will pass, thank you,” Ophelia said with as much grace as she was capable of summoning just then.

“Suit yourself.”

That was a lie. He was going to pop back up with that question sooner rather than later. The man was relentlessly devoted.

He was also insane, but that went without saying. In part because it was insane to think that Ryland had a real, live Turnalian in his employ. But then again, who else except Ryland would have Turnalians slaving after them?

“What made you follow him?” Ophelia asked. She had been too spooked and taken aback by Devas to speak frankly with him, but over the last day or so, she had somehow gotten used to his antics.

“I am a Turnalian,” Devas said simply. “We follow where power leads. And the Great Master is the ultimate power in all of existence.”

“Is that so? Is that why he’s so desperate to fight the Great Gods themselves? To prove that he’s even stronger than the very gods?”

In truth, Ophelia knew full well what Ryland intended against the Great Gods. For reasons she hadn’t been able to discover, the Great Gods hated Ryland. They’d tear him apart if they could cross between the Planes keeping them locked away. But confronting wasn’t at all Ryland’s goal.

All he wanted was to reclaim Acrypha from wherever she had fallen. All he wanted to do was get his student back safe and sound.

That was why Ophelia was still here, after all. She wasn’t here to fight off Great Gods and whatnot. None of that mattered. But a missing student from Arcoryx? One whose whereabouts she now knew? Well, she couldn’t exactly rest until the student was back, now could she?

“What is there to prove?” Devas asked. “I have full faith that there is no being that can eclipse Marionys Ryland. He will best the Great Gods again as he has done so before. Of that, I have no doubt.”

Before? Now, that was new to Ophelia. “When did he best the Great Gods?” Forget when, there was a bigger question at hand. “How did he best them?”

Devas’s eyes glinted like he had just been asked a question about a topic he was intensely passionate about. Which… was the case, now that Ophelia thought about it. But just before he could answer, they both straightened as magic flared.

In the distance, where one of Ryland’s fortification spells was protecting one of his Inscription sites, a burst of sorcery fell like downward fireworks, their little explosions spreading across the entire spell array protecting the etching on the ground. The detonating impacts went off like a series of bombs, air pulsing at the sudden pressurized blasts.

Another attack. After they had just dealt with one yesterday. What was going on here?

“Looks like we have another fool to deal with on our hands,” Devas said with relish. “Come, Mage Knight. Let us prove our devotion to the Great Master together!”

“I’m not here to prove my devotion to anything,” Ophelia said, but she followed quickly anyway.

They didn’t see the actual mage causing trouble. Odd. But it made sense since neither of them had detected a foreign presence tampering with Ryland’s Incarnation. They were well-hidden.

“It’s an Inscription,” Ophelia said after a moment of observation. It was hard to look through the magic that seemed ceaseless, but she was able to make it out eventually. “On top of the fortification spell. I suspect it will keep channelling mana into energy until its carrier breaks apart entirely.”

“Quite an ingenious little Inscription then,” Devas said. His wood armour was starting to flourish, growing harder and leafier at the same time. “I wonder—”

They were attacked a moment later. Well, Ophelia was.

A thumping impact sent her sprawling to the ground, and before she could get over her alarm and get back to her feet, more impacts pressed her down. It was like she was being stomped on by a stampede of overheated feet.

It stopped as suddenly as it had arrived. Ophelia had managed to manoeuvre herself to protect her face from getting smashed in, so the pain was mostly in her limbs and torso. Those were armoured effectively, so she had no trouble getting back to her feet, her manasoul flaring as her Soul Glyph of Vanguard came to life.

“[Relentless Sentinel],” she cried.

Her golden aura solidified around her, encasing her in the power of her surging manasoul. Now, she was ready to fight back.

The assaulter had been counterattacked by Devas, who was flinging spears made of rippling space. His spells hurt to even look at. But credit to their infiltrator, the spells from Devas never properly found their mark. They were either defended well, or simply evaded, though the latter occurred more often.

In other words, the infiltrator was able to mostly resist Devas’s devastating spells.

Ophelia let loose a war cry and rushed to join the battle physically. “[Saviour’s Bulwark]. [Unrivalled Call].”

Both spells worked better when Ophelia was asserting herself as her Soul Glyph of Vanguard properly demanded of her. When she was defending, when she was protecting. When she was being a proper Mage Knight.

But in certain cases, offence was the best defence, and in this case, Ophelia intended to take advantage of the opportunity.

The attacking mage dashed away with frightening speed before she could reach them. She cursed.

“Hmm,” Devas said, momentarily pausing his casts. “A tricky opponent. Probably Primordial-ranked at the very least.”

Ophelia was hardly listening. There were Inscriptions floating in the air itself before them, which was part of what had made even Devas pause. Because it just didn’t make sense. Inscriptions could only be applied to something solid. The ones before them were supposed to be spell circles, but those activated instantly. This floating collection of Glyphs was unprecedented.

She charged again, mostly because more Inscriptions were taking shape all around her. Glyphic etchings quickly tried to cage her and Devas in, but she dashed out of the offending area.

Then rushed straight at the attacker.

They stayed still this time. Another Glyphic array bloomed in front of their hand, and thankfully, it was merely a spell circle this time, leading to a sabre of sorcery formed in the infiltrator’s hand.

What followed was a quick exchange of blows. Ophelia had no idea who or what her adversary was, but they were skilled with that blade. Her golden broadsword clanged with furious sparks against the blur blade. She couldn’t find a way through. Despite being rather proud of her swordsmanship, despite weaving her shield into the combat, she was being pressed back.

Impossible. Just because she was Titan-ranked and her opponent was Primordial-ranked didn’t mean she’d be beaten in swordplay this easily, did it?

And yes, she was being beaten. The defence granted by [Saviour’s Bulwark] was all that saved her from the retaliatory blows her opponent managed to sneak in. [Unrivalled Call]’s glints and sparks helped her create some leeway against her adversary, but it wasn’t anything amazing. She was barely surviving.

It frustrated her to no end that she was on the back foot, but battlefields weren’t where frustrations won. Not usually. Cold, calm calculation. That was what granted victory more often than not.

And it was that which allowed them to overcome their adversary.

Devas had waited all this while till he had a clear-cut chance. One that was unmistakeably opportune. Much like how a cage of Inscriptions had tried to trap Ophelia, a cage of ruptured space itself enclosed around her and her adversary.

“[Broken Realm],” Devas said from farther behind, his voice almost echoing how Ryland’s did. Come to think of it, wasn’t that one of Ryland’s spells too? Great Master, in truth…

More importantly, their adversary was trapped. Ophelia and the infiltrator mage disengaged from their close-quarters fight as more cracks in reality fractured around them. She’d have been quite fine with trapping the arrogant opposing mage so long as it meant she didn’t get trapped too. Unfortunately, Devas wasn’t very discriminate.

“There,” Devas said, sauntering forward like he had just defeated a marauding Calamity. “Now you can stop flopping around like a fish on land. It’s over.”

Hopefully, he meant the unknown mage from who knew where, and not Ophelia. Nevertheless, even she found the tone aggravating at the very least.

“Hmph,” their adversary finally said. A man. A gruff, older one at that. “Hardly.”

Ophelia’s alarm came in second to the new set of adversaries deciding to launch an assault upon their location.
