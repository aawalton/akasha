“Where is Omar?” asked a scarred man in a wary tone of voice.

“We were supposed to meet up,” agreed a Latino who spoke in a much more relaxed tone.

“You know Omar,” replied a balding man, smiling faintly. “He tends to get distracted. That man loves fighting more than any of us.”

Three Holy Kings were gathered in one spot, having met up in the melee as they had discussed in advance.

The distressing thing was that, somehow, the other ten of their number hadn’t made it to the planned meetup where they had signaled it should be—the signal being a small fireworks display in the shape of their holy symbol.

“I don’t really like it,” said Eisel in his soft, paper-thin voice. “It feels like I can smell something on the breeze. An ominous odor… death?”

The three Holy Kings exchanged looks, uncertain how to react to Eisel’s disturbing words.

The old man was their strongest non-Ruler comrade, the only non-Ruler within the Holy Kingdom who had made it to this stage as far as they knew. It was impossible to be sure with their millions of subjects, but no others had come forward and announced they would be there, at least, though the Rulers had beseeched the public to join hands with them to plan and prepare for the competition.

It was a simple fact that most of the faithful weren’t nearly as strong as their leaders, except old Eisel. He was odd in many ways. Wise, though. Prescient.

And, though he was not a Holy King, he was undoubtedly a holy man. He had been even before the System appeared. He had seen things, been a participant in miracles.

“What are you thinking, Eisel?” asked the balding man in a tone of curiosity.

“I’m thinking that perhaps going to war is not the wisest choice…” the old man said in a humble voice.

“If the report we received is correct, he’s brought the war here,” said the Latino.

The balding man nodded. “We had to send the Assassin King early when we heard that he was targeting us in the melee. It is, perhaps, a shame. Something probably could not have been worked out, but there was hope. Theoretically. If he saw the light and chose to embrace accountability…”

“I’m just an old man with a weak heart, blessed to have had a second chance at robust life, not holding any grudges,” said Eisel. “I’m hoping to reach the end of this life with my friends in one piece, our new country intact, and my soul as pure as I can make it. Not my friends in many pieces, our new country torn apart by war, and my soul destroyed.”

That was the report they had received: someone had seen one of the Holy Kings meet a horrifying and potentially permanent end at the Fisher Emperor’s hands early in the melee. Assuming that was true, he had already fired the first shots in the war that was to begin.

“Eisel, you’re very kind, and your pacific approach might have been better if we could go back and rethink our decisionmaking from the ground level,” said the scarred man in a gentle tone.

“That said, we already have a commitment against the Fisher Emperor,” added the Latino. “And he isn’t backing down anymore than we are.”

“How can a man back down when you’re attacking him?” Eisel asked, raising an eyebrow. “The way you all recounted the meeting at the Summit, it sounded like you almost stepped into the territory of threatening his family. But I know you wouldn’t do that.” His words were almost scolding, if not for the fact that he kept his tone calm and gentle.

Eisel was the sort of person who could make you scold yourself in your head.

“What about the Assassin King?” asked the balding man, trying to defuse the situation. The other two Holy Kings looked like they were about ready to gang up on the old man otherwise. “Do we think he’s engaged with the Fisher Emperor right now? Perhaps he’s going to render this whole discussion moot.”

“I doubt it,” muttered the scarred man. A look of comprehension dawned on his face. “Hey, wait, is you asking that your way of trying to get me to use my Skill?”

“Well, now that you mention it, I know it gives you a headache, but…” The balding man let his voice trail off.

“Not your fault, but you’re the best at reconnaissance,” agreed the Latino.

“Very well,” said the scarred man. He ran a thumb over one of his scars. “Some sacrifices must always be made for the greater good, and looked at in that light, this is obviously fairly minor.”

His face took on the look of absolute focus that always characterized it when he used the Scry Skill. The others knew he was looking through an invisible window they couldn’t see. They knew that any distraction could be harmful and delay them getting an update. So all three of the scarred man’s nearby comrades remained silent.

“No sign of the Assassin King still existing at all,” said the scarred man after a moment. “I can’t find any trace of him.”

“How can you tell?” asked the Latino. He hadn’t been there for the other times the scarred man had used his divine gift.

“Scry is usually pretty infallible,” said the scarred man. “I can’t find the Assassin King at all. I’m not even seeing an empty field or something, which might happen if he’d rendered himself invisible. Hiding completely from Scry is, as far as I know, impossible, no matter how good you are at stealth.”

“So, you think he’s dead,” said Eisel somberly.

“That’s not truly that surprising to you, I suppose,” said the balding man grimly.

“There was a reason I was suggesting this was a bad fight to be in,” the old man quietly agreed.

“It’s not the worst thing that could have happened,” said the balding man slowly.

“It is not,” said the scarred man.

“Not if he injured the Fisher Emperor badly,” finished the balding man.

“The Assassin King said he’d made special provisions for a situation like this, too,” said the Latino, shaking his head. “The man underestimated what he was in for.”

“That assumes it was even the Fisher Emperor who killed him,” said the balding man, his mouth a stiff line. “The truth is, we’ve come to a place where everyone is devouring everyone else. The violence of sudden, random, unexpected deaths is to be expected in this accursed melee. The true worst case scenario for us with the Assassin King is that he was killed randomly, and the Fisher Emperor wasn’t harmed at all… It’s the sort of thing that’s almost inevitable, the way this melee has been structured. I wish the System had selected a more orderly process.”

“What, like a tea party?” asked the Latino, amused.

“Like skipping this nonsense and going to individual duels,” said the balding man. “Or team battles. This Wild West crap doesn’t prove anything about who’s strongest. The winner here might just end up being whoever’s best at hiding! Some of the losers might be people who got caught in the crossfire between two people slightly weaker than them.”

“I doubt that the winner will be the world’s hide and seek champion,” said the scarred man dryly. “If it wasn’t sinful to gamble, I’d bet it will be a big, violent brute of a man who also has some facility with magic. Please be silent for a moment, friends. I’m going to try and Scry the Fisher Emperor himself. If he killed the Assassin King, perhaps he lies wounded somewhere.”

The others quieted down, and the scarred man’s staring into the apparently empty air resumed.

“I don’t believe this,” he said after a moment.

“What?” asked Eisel.

“Yeah, don’t leave us in suspense,” said the Latino.

“He’s flying through the air with two other allies. One of his creatures and a member of the Taiwan delegation.”

“They were the ones who seemed to be canceling everyone’s Skills, right?” said the balding man.

The Latino nodded.

“The shocking thing,” said the scarred man, “is that he appears totally uninjured.”

“Are you gentlemen ready to contemplate making peace?” asked Eisel. “Before more unnecessary losses?”

“Check on Minjun,” said the balding man.

The scarred man refocused for a few seconds, then shook his head ominously.

“Look for Omar!” demanded the balding man.

“I tried him, too,” the scarred man said uncomfortably. “It’s not showing me anything.”

“It’s not possible!” exclaimed the balding man. “He was too strong. There’s no way the Fisher Emperor has eliminated that many of us!”

“He has a number of subjects here,” said the Latino quietly. “The creature apparently flying around with him now is just one of many.”

“The mad fool even has his mother here, pitching in,” said the scarred man, quietly scoffing.

“Is any of this mad, when he seems to be winning?” asked Eisel.

“Should we be preparing for a fight, you think?” asked the balding man, turning to look Eisel in the eyes.

“I think you’ve been asking for one, and you’re going to get it,” Eisel replied bluntly. “Whether we can prepare for it in time, I don’t know. If we survive this place, then sure.”

“He only has two with him,” said the Latino, annoyed. “We can take that many.”

“Assuming he doesn’t bring in the others as backup,” said the balding man.

“Yes, even if we could measure up to his quality, which is frankly doubtful,” said the scarred man, “he has far more Rulers on his side than we do, especially after everything that’s happened. Almost all of the creatures I’ve identified as belonging to the Fisher Emperor appear to still be alive in various areas of the melee.”

“Well, then what do you propose we do?” asked the Latino in a tone of frustration. “Make peace? Surrender before we’ve even fought?”

“We’re at a disadvantage here,” said the balding man slowly. “It’s foolish not to acknowledge that and play this game accordingly.”

“What does that mean, as far as your solution?” asked the scarred man.

The balding man drew a dagger from his belt that gleamed brightly in the sunlight.

“We take ourselves out before he can,” said the balding man. “If the report was true about him destroying the soul of one of our comrades, then the worst possible scenario is that he captures more of us and subjects us to the same fate. We’re in a setting where we lack the power to inflict similar damage to him, since none of us dabble with his brand of dark magic.”

“Isn’t that just running away?” asked the Latino.

“Do you have a better idea?” asked the balding man.

“I do,” said a voice from overhead. “Just die.”

“We might have waited a little too long to decide what to do,” said the scarred man dryly. “Guess I should have kept tracking him. I knew the background in Scry looked familiar… We passed that area an hour ago.”

“You fellows figure out what you’re going to do quickly,” said Eisel, drawing a mace from his inventory. “I’ll distract the Fisher Emperor.”

“I’d wait before approaching, Eisel,” said the balding man slowly. “Just look at him.”

There was a massive halo of Mana around the enemy Ruler, as if he was preparing a big attack. His two allies, the alligator and the Taiwanese, stood to either side like bodyguards, ready to act if anyone moved in on the Fisher Emperor—or perhaps ready to join in an attack that he was about to launch.

“That might mean this is my last chance,” said Eisel decisively. He rushed forward, surging up toward their opponent. Then the energy around the Fisher Emperor just disappeared.

A flurry of new presences presented themselves in the seconds following.

Bird Rulers, a rattlesnake with gray and brown spotted scales, a Werewolf, the Fisher Emperor’s mother flanked by a bunch of undead…

In their minds, all the Holy Kingdom members cursed in unison.
