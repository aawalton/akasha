The town felt odd for the rest of the day. The king was not visible to most of Glenfall’s citizens, as he hardly could be. Between all the meetings and arrangements in place for him and his family, whatever wandering he could have accomplished would have been minimal, at best. Still, every inhabitant of the town seemed to walk with a certain enforced nobility, as if the king was going to come around any corner at any minute and judge them in a kingly manner.

With nothing much to do, Bella and Jane rounded up their friends and whisked them away to Jane’s bakery. They piled into the upstairs room to while away the hours before whatever big event was coming next.

"And then the king just stepped right up to Jane like it was nothing," Bella was saying, her hands moving dramatically through the air as she narrated. "Like he does this sort of thing every day. And Jane just puts her hand on his arm, and suddenly his voice is coming from everywhere at once. The whole crowd went silent."

"We were there, Bella." Brit's voice was dry, but he was smiling. "We saw it happen."

"Yes, but you didn't see it from my angle. I was right there. I could have touched the queen if I wanted to."

"Why would you want to touch the queen?" Emily asked.

"I don't know. To say I did?"

Jane navigated carefully around the room, distributing cups of tea to her assembled friends. The space above her bakery wasn't large, but it was large enough to sit a group of four or five people if they didn’t mind being close together. Right now, it was filled with the people she cared about most in Glenfall.

Bella had claimed the most comfortable chair. Brit sat on the floor next to it, his back against the wall and his legs stretched out in front of him. Emily and Sadie had taken seats on Jane’s bed, leaving room for Jane. Allen was leaning against a wall, apparently fine with standing.

"The best part," Bella continued, “was when she told the king she'd heard good things about him too. I thought I was going to die."

"I didn't say it like that," Jane protested, handing Bella her tea.

"You absolutely did. The king laughed. The actual king laughed at something you said."

"He seemed nice," Emily offered. "From what I could see, anyway. Friendlier than I would have expected."

"He was nice," Jane admitted. "I always thought kings would be more… I don't know. Formal. I thought I’d be terrified."

"You didn't look terrified," Brit said. "You looked like you were concentrating very hard on not making a mistake."

“I was a little bit of both. But still, it wasn’t what I expected. I’ve been in the same room with him before, but always in a crowd, and always for official ceremonies. He really was formal then, every time. It’s not like he wasn’t real, either, but this was different. This was a whole new thing.”

The conversation drifted after that, moving from topic to blessedly non-king topic. Jane let it wash over her, content to listen more than talk. It had been a long morning. She really had been nervous. But now, the tea was warm, and she was surrounded by friends.

It was turning out to be a very good day.

"Are any of you showcasing?" she asked eventually, during a lull in the conversation. "Besides Allen, I mean. I know about his presentation."

Emily shook her head. "The king will probably visit the library at some point. If he does, that will be that. Anyone can look at the books I stacked. There’s no showcase needed."

"I'm not showcasing either," Brit said. "Too much trouble. I make normal things. I wouldn’t want to stop doing that for how long it would take to do something different.”

Jane sighed. "You're all lucky. Or smart. Why did I want to cook for the king? I hardly remember now."

"When is the showcase, anyway?" Bella asked. "I know they mentioned it in the announcement, but I had other things taking away my attention."

"Not today," Sadie replied.

She would know, Jane realized. As Xand’s niece and assistant, Sadie had probably been involved in all the planning for this momentous royal visit.

"Tomorrow at the earliest,” Sadie went on, “but more likely the day after. The royal family needs time to settle in. Then the word will go out. These things take coordination."

"So we have at least a day," Jane said. "Maybe two."

"Probably two," Sadie confirmed. "Enjoy it while you can."

Jane intended to. This was the first time in weeks that all six of them had been able to gather like this, with no pressing obligations demanding their attention. They’d met in twos or threes nearly every week, of course, yet there was something special about a complete assembly.

Between everyone’s various responsibilities, finding time when the whole group was free had become increasingly difficult. Though rest days helped, even those weren't guaranteed stocks of truly free time. Someone always seemed to have something that couldn't wait.

But today, somehow, they had all managed to be in the same place at the same time. Jane suspected the king's arrival had something to do with it. This holiday was a little more official. The king’s announcement truly demanded everyone’s time for just a while, before setting them all free to other things. She didn’t know when she and her friends would be so lucky again in the near future, so she was determined to enjoy it for as long as she could.

A knock at the door downstairs interrupted her peace.

Jane set down her tea, skipped down the stairs, and went to answer it. She expected perhaps a late customer who hadn't seen the ‘Closed’ sign. What she found instead was a young man in royal livery, standing at attention with the air of someone delivering an important message.

"Miss Jane?" he asked.

"That's me."

"I bear an invitation from His Majesty the King. His Majesty requests the pleasure of your company for dinner this evening. He asks that you bring along whatever companions you might wish, claiming that the party can be as large as you like." The messenger straightened even further. "He especially requested that you bring along someone called ‘the breakfast girl.’ His Majesty made it clear that he finds her amusing. The queen concurred.”

Jane stared at the man for a moment, her brain struggling to process the words. The king wanted to have dinner with her. And Bella, for apparently no reason other than the fact that she was friendly.

Things were getting odder and odder, and it was still only the first day of the royal visit. Jane shuddered to think how odd life would become if things continued in this way.

"I… Yes. Of course. Tell him we'll be there."

The messenger nodded sharply and turned to leave, but Jane stopped him.

"Wait. Where? Where should we come?"

"The inn where His Majesty is staying, Miss Jane. He will meet you there."

"Right. Yes. We'll be there shortly."

The messenger departed at a trot. Jane closed the door and turned to find that everyone in her upstairs room had come downstairs. They were all staring at her.

"Did the king just invite you to dinner?" Bella asked.

"Apparently."

"And he asked you to bring companions?"

"That's what the man said."

Bella's face split into an enormous grin. "This really is the best day of my life."

Brit, on the other hand, was slowly edging towards the door. "Well, this has been great, but I should probably…”

"Stop right there," Jane warned.

"I really should be going,” he protested. “I have things to do!"

Bella’s voice brooked no argument. “Brit. You’re staying.”

Emily was inching towards the door as well. "I just remembered… I left something at the library that I need to see to."

“No you didn’t.” Sadie grabbed Emily’s arm and pulled her back. “You were just saying there’s nothing special to prepare for.”

"You're both coming." Jane said firmly. "The king said to bring companions. You're my companions, so you are now in this too. I will turn you both into toads if you try to leave."

Brit had been staring at Bella, trying to plead silently, but now his head snapped around to look at Jane. "You can't actually do that."

"Can't I?”

Brit looked doubtful. He knew she couldn’t, on some level. On another level, he wasn’t sure.

Jane held his gaze. "Nobody ever really wants to take a chance on the toad thing. Do you want to take a chance on the toad thing? You could be the first."

Brit glanced at Allen, but the tinker held up his hands.

“I’m going, regardless. And I wouldn’t risk the toad thing, if I were you. Just my personal opinion.”

A long moment passed. Brit looked at Emily. Emily looked at Brit. They both looked at Jane, who maintained her stern expression with an effort that was more strenuous than any magic she'd performed that day.

"Fine," Brit said. "But I'm blaming you if this goes terribly wrong."

The walk was shorter than Jane would have liked. Glenfall wasn't a large town, so the inn where the royal family was staying was only a fifteen-minute journey from her bakery. She would have liked some more time to settle her nerves.

When they arrived, the royal family was easy to spot. They were standing just outside the front door, dressed in what Jane assumed was casual clothing for royalty. It was an approximation of everyday wear she suspected was still the finest of the fine, upon closer inspection.

The king spotted them approaching and raised a hand in greeting.

"Miss Jane! You came. Wonderful."

"Your Majesty." Jane curtsied to him, then to the queen and prince. "Thank you for the invitation. These are my friends."

She introduced them one by one. The king acknowledged each with a nod and a few words of greeting. The queen smiled politely. The prince looked like he wished he were somewhere else, though he managed a passable impression of interest when Allen was introduced as a craftsman.

"I hope you'll forgive the informal nature of this invitation," the king said. "We had planned something more elaborate, but it turns out that planning elaborate dinners on short notice is rather difficult."

"We're honored regardless, Your Majesty," Jane said. "Are we eating here? At the inn?"

"That was the original thought, but I've eaten at the inn twice now.” The king glanced at his family. “We’d like a change. Unfortunately, I have no idea where that might be found."

"We should take them to my mom's restaurant,” Allen said, before Jane could respond.

Jane turned to look at him. "Would she like that?"

"No." Allen's expression was entirely too cheerful for someone suggesting they surprise his mother with royalty. "She'd hate it. Way too much pressure. And with no warning? She’d kill me."

"I can't imagine your mother flustered."

"It can happen. It's rare, but it can happen."

The queen, who had been silent until now, spoke up. Her voice was pleasant and measured, the voice of someone accustomed to diplomatic conversations.

"I wouldn't want to put the poor woman out. Unexpected guests are difficult enough without them being, well…"

"Don't ruin the fun, Mother." The prince's tone was dry, but there was a hint of genuine amusement in it. It was the most animated Jane had seen him since his arrival. "When was the last time we ate somewhere that didn't know we were coming?"

The queen looked to the king, clearly hoping for support in her reasonable position. The king, however, seemed more interested in another question.

He looked gravely at the assembled group of Glenfall residents.

"Is the food good?" he asked, giving each word its due weight.

The friends immediately turned to look at Brit, the acknowledged connoisseur in such matters. Out of all of them, he was the one who appreciated huge portions of filling food the most.

Brit spoke with not a trace of hesitation.

"The best."

The king's face broke into a grin. "Well then. Lead the way."
