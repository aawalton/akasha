Broken teeth lined the walls, each one the size of Cade’s torso—a cathedral of ivory and decay where gods might have once chewed the marrow of creation.

Cade Stormhollow admitted to himself in that dark underbelly of the world that he might be just a dash melodramatic.

The skeletal maw stretched before him, ancient fangs forming a grotesque corridor that stank of time-worn magic and forgotten divinity. Every instinct screamed to retreat, but the rumbling chamber behind them offered no sanctuary—not with the stone golem’s shattered remains still twitching with residual enchantment, fingers of arcane energy skittering across broken granite like dying spiders.

“Well,” Cade whispered, his voice barely disturbing the dust that hung suspended in the still air, “this is cozy.”

Nora stood beside him, her plated armor pockmarked with fresh dents and scrapes. Her eyes caught a sliver of light as she surveyed the cavernous mouth they now stood in. Cade wondered yet again what really went on behind that stormy gaze of hers.

“You have an interesting definition of ‘cozy,’” she said, but her voice carried none of the sharpness it reserved for others.

Cade’s lips curved upward, his blue eyes reflecting fragments of bioluminescence that clung to the tooth pillars. “Enclosed space, mood lighting, just the two of us... some might call it romantic.”

“Some might need their head examined,” Nora countered, but the smile that ghosted across her face betrayed her. She stepped forward, massive greatsword ready, the obsidian blade drinking what little light existed as if perpetually starved. “At least we’re moving deeper.”

The jaws slammed shut behind them, sealing them inside this bizarre anatomical corridor.

“Well, that makes our decision easy. Down we go. Again,” Cade commented dryly, brushing a hand across the calcified ivory walls. Nora scoffed at their rotten luck but took point as they descended further.

Cade followed, silver core humming beneath his skin, raw magic coiling through his veins like liquid lightning. In these close quarters, another destructive outburst would likely bring the entire structure down upon them, turning this ossified corridor into their shared sarcophagus.

“Do you think the others made it this far?” he asked, the casual tone a mask worn to cover the dark spiral his thoughts kept subjecting him to. His mind conjured images of Rayka’s smile, of Orro’s silent strength, of Jer’s laughter, of Chubbs’s tiny hands reaching for impossible wonders. It was far too easy in this place to imagine those precious things shattered and torn apart. And he hated it for that.

Nora’s shoulders tensed slightly—the only indication of her own worry, a crack in otherwise perfect armor. “They’re resourceful,” she said, the words carrying the weight of a prayer. “From what I’ve been told, Elena’s escaped more death traps than most people even encounter in nightmares. Whoever’s with her is bound to be strolling through this place like they own it.”

Cade nodded, his fingers brushing against the wall, feeling the subtle vibrations that ran through the structure. The connection to his friends felt tenuous here, stretched gossamer-thin across chambers of ancient malice. Each step forward was both progress and separation—what if they were walking away from the others rather than toward them?

“I just—” he began, but Nora silenced him with a raised hand, her entire body suddenly alert.

“Listen,” she whispered, head tilted toward some distant sound.

Cade strained his silver-ranked senses, filtering through the ambient groans of settling bone and the omnipresent heartbeat. Then it came to him—voices. Human voices, echoing from somewhere ahead, carried on stale air through the skeletal passageway.

“—completely innocent until I touched it—”

“—said the exact same thing in the guardian’s chamber—”

“—scientifically impossible for every single artifact to be trapped—”

Relief crashed through him, a tidal wave sweeping away the debris of fear that had collected in the corners of his mind. Those bickering tones could only belong to two people who had elevated disagreement to an art form.

“Gav and Elena,” he breathed, exchanging a look of pure hope with Nora. The tension that had been building between them—that delicate, dangerous thing neither dared name—vanished like morning mist, replaced by urgent purpose. His pace quickened, boots clacking against unnervingly smooth bone. “They’re alive.”

The passage curved sharply before them, twisting like the spine of some impossible beast, then opened without warning into a cavernous chamber that defied comprehension.

Above them, a domed ceiling disappeared into shadows, while luminescent fungi clung to what might have been ribs, casting everything in an eerie blue-green glow that made shadows dance like half-formed spirits performing ancient rituals. Massive vertebrae formed archways around the perimeter, supporting a structure that seemed to mock conventional architecture.

And in the center of it all stood three figures, unmistakable despite the phantasmal lighting.

“—not saying you’re actively trying to kill us,” Elena was stating, hands planted firmly on her hips as she glared up at Gav. Her red hair caught the bioluminescent light, turning it to living flame around a face carved from pure stubbornness. “I’m just saying disaster follows your curiosity like it’s on a leash made of pure catastrophe.”

Gav, his normally immaculate appearance reduced to tattered finery and mud-caked silk, stared back with wounded dignity. Even in ruin, he maintained the bearing of Elysian nobility—shoulders squared, chin lifted, as if the universe itself should apologize for daring to soil his attire.

“The fire-statue room was hardly my fault,” he insisted, brushing futilely at a particularly stubborn stain on his sleeve. “Those puzzle blocks were clearly already moving before I approached. They practically invited interaction.”

“They weren’t until you said—and I quote—‘I bet these connect to something important,’” Elena fired back, finger jabbing the air between them. “The moment those words left your mouth, everything lit up like festival lanterns, and suddenly we’re fighting for our lives against impenetrable stone goddesses with a clear grudge against the living.”

“Children, please,” Evie interjected, her melodious voice carrying a rare edge of exasperation. Her curly brown hair formed a wild halo around her face, with portions of her hair singed across the edges. “We’ve been through this three times already. Perhaps we could focus on—”

She paused, head tilting like a bird catching a distant call. Her eyes widened, recognition blooming across her features like sunrise breaking through storm clouds.

“Nor-Nor!”

The siren launched herself across the chamber, curls bouncing wildly as she bounded over debris with ethereal grace that defied the treacherous terrain. She collided with Nora in a flurry of delighted laughter and flailing limbs, the impact staggering even the stoic paladin.

“Birdie,” Nora murmured, the single word carrying more emotion than most people managed in entire speeches. Her hands moved over Evie’s arms and shoulders with practiced tenderness, checking for injuries even as relief softened her stern features into something approaching vulnerability. “Are you hurt?”

“Only my dignity,” Evie replied cheerfully, practically vibrating with joy. “Apparently fighting an undead guardian, solving ancient riddles, and parting a supernatural lake doesn’t earn you immunity from those two.” She flicked her wrist dismissively toward Gav and Elena, who had momentarily paused their disagreement to acknowledge the newcomers with mirrored chagrin.

Cade blinked, momentarily distracted from their reunion by this casual bombshell dropped between breaths. “You parted a what now?”

“Cade!”

Elena abandoned her argument with Gav to approach, relief breaking through her perpetual mask of annoyed competence. Her whip-blade was coiled at her hip and fresh burns marked her forearms where something had clearly attempted to melt through her leather bracers. Scorch patterns traced intricate paths across the weathered material, telling tales of narrowly avoided death.

“Tell me you’ve figured out what this nightmare dollhouse wants from us,” she demanded, eyes scanning him for injuries even as her tone maintained its usual edge. “Because I swear it’s getting more personal with every chamber we enter.”

“Nightmare dollhouse,” Cade repeated, lips curving into an appreciative grin. “That’s good. I was going with ‘architectural digestive tract’ myself.” His eyes swept over her, noting yet more scratches on her armor, the way she favored her right leg, the subtle tremble in hands that never shook. “Looks like you three had your own adventures while we were busy getting intimately acquainted with animated stonework.”

“That’s one word for it,” Elena snorted, brushing matted hair from her face with a gesture that couldn’t hide the exhaustion etched into every line. “Another might be ‘extended torture session designed by deranged ancient beings with too much time and magic on their hands.’”

Gav joined them, amber eyes flickering with concern as he studied their equally battered appearances. His gaze darted between them, counting, recounting, his frown deepening with each tally.

“Where are the others?” he asked, voice tight with carefully controlled fear. “Rayka? Jer? Orro?” His gaze sharpened, focused with sudden intensity. “Chubbs?”

The unspoken question hung between them, and a bit of the warmth fled the chamber. Cade exchanged a glance with Nora.

“We were hoping they’d found you,” he admitted, the lightness dropping from his tone like a discarded mask. “We got separated almost immediately after entering this place. Death paid me a personal visit to explain that this crypt is testing us all separately.”

“Death?” Elena’s eyebrows shot skyward, disappearing beneath tangled bangs. “As in, the final toll collector? The ultimate party crasher?”

“The very same,” Cade confirmed, the memory of that impossible encounter still raising goosebumps along his arms. “Tailored suit, perfect hair, talks like he swallowed a dictionary and enjoys making you feel like you failed the exam.” He shrugged as if discussing an unremarkable acquaintance rather than an ancient cosmic force. “Mentioned that all paths eventually lead to the central chamber at the crypt’s heart—assuming we survive these idiotic trials.”

“We believe that the crypt divided us deliberately—different tests for different people,” Nora added with a nod as she held Evie’s hand. “According to Death, we each face challenges designed specifically for us.”

“Like personalized torture,” Gav muttered, unconsciously rubbing his wrist where a bruise was forming, dark even against his tanned skin. “How considerate.”

Evie’s hands clasped together excitedly, her earlier distress apparently forgotten in the face of new discoveries. “Oh! That explains why that chamber was all about water and wisdom.” She dug into a pouch at her belt, movements quick with barely contained enthusiasm, and produced a crystalline orb that pulsed with hypnotic blue light. “We retrieved this.”

The orb’s light cast Evie’s features in stark relief, turning her usually gentle face into something otherworldly—a creature of threshold spaces and twilight hours rather than simple flesh.

“Unpleasant doesn’t even begin to cover it,” Elena interjected, a strange note of pride entering her voice as she glanced at Evie. Her expression transformed, harsh lines softening into something almost reverential. “This girl—this absolute wonder—she did the impossible when it mattered most.”

She gestured dramatically, mimicking a parting motion with both arms sweeping outward. “The lake was closing over my head, shadow-things circling for the kill—I could feel them brushing against my legs, hundreds of them, hungry for something more than flesh.” A shudder ran through her, the memory clearly still raw. “Then Evie... she just stood there at the shore, arms raised, and commanded it to part. Like a goddess from the old stories, power radiating from her in waves I could feel like physical pressure.”

Cade watched Elena’s animated retelling with quiet amazement.

The sharp-tongued thief, who typically maintained emotional walls thicker than the Temple of Scorn, was practically glowing with admiration for the siren. Something had happened down there in the darkness—something that had transformed their dynamic completely.

It warmed something in his chest to see this shift. Elena, who trusted so rarely and so reluctantly, had clearly found something worthy in Evie’s gentle soul.

Good, Cade thought, a genuine smile alighting across his lips. Maybe we can make this all work after all.

“It was nothing,” Evie demurred, though her cheeks flushed pink at the praise, roses blooming in snow. “The words just came to me, and the water listened. I merely asked, and it answered.”

“Nothing?” Elena scoffed, her hand unconsciously moving to Evie’s shoulder in a protective gesture so unlike her usual standoffishness that Cade nearly did a double-take. “You parted a supernatural lake, saved my life, and then collapsed bleeding from your nose and ears. We had to carry you for the next hour because of what you endured to save me.”

Nora’s eyes narrowed with concern, storm clouds gathering. “Bleeding? Birdie, you didn’t mention that.”

“It wasn’t serious,” Evie assured her, though she avoided direct eye contact—a sure tell that she was downplaying things. “Just a small reaction to channeling more power than usual. Like a nosebleed from thinking too hard!” She offered a brilliant smile that didn’t quite reach her eyes.

“That doesn’t happen,” Cade countered.

“Whatever it was, she saved us,” Gav concluded, his tone brooking no more room for argument or further questions. And that look in his eyes.

Maybe Elena wasn’t the only one smitten with the siren.

Cade studied the orb in Evie’s hands with newfound caution, noting how the blue light seemed to pulse in rhythm with her heartbeat rather than the crypt’s. “And this was worth nearly killing yourself for, then?”

“The guardian called it ‘The Heart,’” Elena explained when Evie became distracted by the orb’s pulsing glow. “Said it reveals hidden truths, whatever cryptic nonsense that means. Typical ancient guardian gobbledygook—big on ominous warnings, short on useful specifics.”

Gav cleared his throat, drawing their attention from the mysterious artifact. “Not to interrupt this fascinating discussion, but should we be concerned about the fact that this chamber is starting to, um, digest us?”

Cade followed his gaze upward and felt his stomach drop. The vertebrae that formed the room’s architectural support were shifting subtly, the spaces between them narrowing like a ribcage drawing a breath. The bioluminescent fungi pulsed in rhythm with the crypt’s heartbeat, their light intensifying with each cycle from blue-green to something approaching daylight, then fading back to submarine gloom.

“That’s probably not ideal,” Cade agreed, scanning their surroundings more carefully.

Six passages branched from the chamber, each framed by massive vertebrae that curved overhead like gothic arches. One in particular drew his attention—a wider corridor descending at a steeper angle than the others, its entrance adorned with carvings unlike anything he’d seen before.

The symbols reminded him of the arrays in the Lifekeeper’s book he’d taken, but warped and twisted into shapes that hurt the eyes if viewed too directly. It was as if someone had tried to map something cosmic in scale but lacked the proper frame of reference for mortal perspective—a child trying to draw an adult’s face without understanding proper proportions.

“That way,” he decided, pointing toward the unsettling passage. “Those markings are different from anything else we’ve seen. If this place is testing us, that’s likely our next challenge.”

“Deeper is good,” Elena agreed, already checking her weapons with swift, practiced movements. “Anything to get away from what we left upstairs.”

Gav coughed to cover his chuckle. Cade gave him a flat stare and the lycanthrope rubbed the back of his neck and shrugged.

They moved toward the chosen passage without delay, the echo of their boots reverberating through the hollow room. The chamber’s narrowing continued, vertebrae creaking overhead like ancient ships straining against storm winds. As they reached the archway, Cade noticed something embedded in the bone forming the arch—a strange crystalline formation that protruded from the ivory like frozen sap or perhaps a tumor. It pulsed with a faint green glow, symbols scrolling across its surface in an endless loop of incomprehensible communication.

“Hold up,” he warned, raising a hand to halt their progress. The hairs on his arms stood on end, silver core responding to whatever energy emanated from the crystal. “There’s something—”

Gav leaned in, scholarly curiosity overriding caution as he squinted at the formation. “Fascinating. These markings aren’t like anything I’ve seen in known magical traditions.” His fingers hovered near the surface, tracing the pattern in the air without touching. “It almost looks like a mathematical equation, but gods above and below if I can read it.”

“Progenitor script,” Nora murmured, her expression guarded as stone. “Or something similar. The ancients were said to understand reality differently than we do—perceiving dimensions beyond our comprehension. I once read that they perceived time and space as malleable substances rather than absolute laws.”

“You know about them?” Gav asked, surprise evident in his voice as he glanced away from the mesmerizing crystal.

Nora shook her head, the movement tight with caution. “Only fragments. Whispers heard in forbidden archives. The Fateweavers guarded what little knowledge survived the Culling Wars.” Her voice dropped lower, as if the walls themselves might judge her for speaking such things. “They say the gods created the Progenitors first, before mortal beings. But they were too powerful, too independent. So the gods wiped them out to make way for more obedient children.”

“Heartwarming family dynamics,” Cade muttered, unease crawling up his spine like a centipede. “Let’s try not to activate whatever this is. Given our luck so far—”

His warning came too late.

Gav’s fingers, hovering millimeters from the crystal’s surface, suddenly jerked forward as the room around them rumbled. His entire body went rigid, a strangled cry escaping his throat as tendrils of green light raced up his arm with vines of fire, wrapping around muscle and bone in elaborate patterns.

“I—I can’t move,” he gasped, amber eyes wide with panic as the light spread across his chest, crawling toward his throat. “It’s—”

The crystal flared blindingly bright, the symbols accelerating their scroll until they blurred into continuous lines of light. Sound erupted from it—not noise but pure mathematical concepts translated into auditory form, equations given voice that human ears were never meant to comprehend.

They tore into Cade’s mind, seeking purchase even as they ripped him apart from within. He forced his thoughts to calm, attempting to use the meditative technique Nora had taught him. It worked, but only enough to let him take in a single breath. He pushed harder. He would not let yet another force take over his body. Not again. Not ever.

He activated his core, yanking on the red cords that snaked throughout it. They responded instantly, red energy thrashing violently even as the Destruction magic started to course through his magical pathways. The twin forces purged the alien energy, and Cade refused to let any of his own magicks out. Instead, he let the pressure build, removing any space for the green light to burrow into him a second time.

The vertebrae around them shuddered violently, bone groaning against bone. Dust rained from every surface, and for one terrifying moment, Cade thought the entire structure would collapse inward, crushing them inside this ossified heart.

Instead, the bone floor beneath their feet simply ceased to exist, reality editing itself to exclude the concept of “ground.”

They fell.

The world became a blur of shadows and flashing bioluminescence, punctuated by the sounds of startled cursing and the rush of displaced air. Through the chaos, Cade glimpsed Elena twisting mid-fall into a controlled dive, her body moving with the fluid precision that came from years of escaping through windows and trapdoors, turning potentially deadly descents into merely uncomfortable landings.

Gav, still partially paralyzed from the crystal’s effect, tumbled helplessly like a rag doll thrown by a petulant child. His limbs jerked in aborted attempts at control until Evie somehow maneuvered through the air—a feat that seemed to defy physics—to grab his wrist, steadying his descent with surprising strength.

Nora reached for Cade as they plummeted, her fingers closing around his forearm with iron certainty. With a grunt of effort, she pulled him closer, angling them both toward what appeared to be a less jagged section of whatever awaited below. Even falling to possible death, she remained a guardian, a protector—it was woven into the very fiber of her being.

They crashed through layers of fungal growth and accumulated debris before hitting solid stone, the landing knocking the breath from Cade’s lungs despite Nora’s intervention. Pain lanced through his shoulder, bright constellations of agony blooming across his vision as reality reasserted itself with all its harsh solidity.

“Everyone alive?” he wheezed, trying to orient himself in the absolute darkness that engulfed them. His voice echoed strangely, suggesting a space far larger than the chamber they’d fallen from. “Sound off before I assume the worst and start composing tragic ballads about your heroic demises.”

“If we were dead, we couldn’t exactly respond,” Elena groaned from somewhere to his left, pain lacing her usual sarcasm.

“Taking that as ‘still breathing,’” Cade replied, pulling himself into a sitting position. Fresh pain stabbed through his ribs with each movement, turning his breath into shallow gasps. He pressed a hand against his side, feeling the hot wetness that suggested at least one cut had reopened. “Nora? Evie? Gav?”

“Here,” Nora confirmed, her voice steady despite their unceremonious descent. Even in absolute darkness, she remained an anchor of certainty. “Evie’s with me.”

“Just a bit... discombobulated,” Evie added, though her typically melodious voice sounded strained, the syllables frayed at the edges. “Everything’s spinning in weird patterns. Is that just me?”

“Yes,” came a chorus of responses.

“Oh, goody,” Evie said in a dazed voice.

“Gav?” Cade called again, concern mounting at the telepath’s silence. The green light that had ensnared him had looked anything but benevolent.

A muffled groan answered, followed by distinctly irritated muttering about silk being “completely impractical for adventuring” and how he should have “listened to that tailor in Elysia before it burned to the ground.”

Relief flooded through Cade—if Gav could complain about fashion, he was at least not at death’s door.

As his eyes adjusted to the gloom, he realized they weren’t in complete darkness after all. Faint phosphorescence clung to the cavern walls around them, providing just enough illumination to make out basic shapes and suggest a vastness beyond.

They’d landed in an underground chamber, far larger than any they’d encountered thus far. The ceiling—the floor they’d fallen through—loomed impossibly high above, now sealed once more as if their dramatic entrance had never occurred.

A skittering sound echoed from the darkness ahead—something with too many legs moving across the stone.

“We’re not alone,” Cade warned, his fingers finding a dagger’s hilt.

The blade came free with a whisper of steel against leather, the familiar weight an anchor in this sea of unknown terrors. Silver-gold energy gathered at his fingertips, casting small pools of light that only emphasized the darkness beyond.

“Wonderful,” Gav muttered, finally climbing to his feet. The paralysis from the crystal seemed to have worn off, but he moved with the stiffness of someone expecting pain at any moment. “Just when I thought this place couldn’t get more—”

A strange sound cut through the cavern—not the chittering they’d heard moments before, but something completely unexpected. A snort. Followed by the unmistakable sound of hooves pawing at stone, the impact leaving small sparks in their wake.

“Is that...?” Elena left the question unfinished, disbelief rendering words inadequate.

“A horse?” Cade supplied, his voice colored by equal parts confusion and suspicion. “Underground? In a crypt filled with death traps and undead? That doesn’t seem—”

The flapping of massive wings interrupted him, the sudden downdraft powerful enough to stir dust into miniature cyclones around their feet. More snorts echoed through the chamber, coming from all directions now—a chorus of equine communication that carried an unsettling intelligence.

“Not just one,” Nora observed, her sword already drawn. Its obsidian edge seemed to drink the meager phosphorescent light rather than reflect it, a sliver of absolute night made solid. “Many.”

Red eyes blinked open in the darkness—first one pair, then another, then dozens more, forming a circle around their position. The eyes hovered at about waist height, moving with fluid grace as they tightened their formation like predators that understood strategy.

“Whatever they are,” Cade whispered, silver-gold energy gathering more intensely at his fingertips, forming dancing motes that illuminated their immediate vicinity, “they’ve surrounded us.”

Hooves clacked against stone as one of the creatures moved forward, emerging into the faint phosphorescent light supplemented by Cade’s magic. His breath caught, reality momentarily refusing to align with what his eyes reported.

It was a pegasus—but unlike any depicted in storybooks or temple murals. This creature stood only about waist-high, its body sleek and muscular beneath a coat so black it seemed to absorb what light existed.

Leathery wings extended from powerful shoulders, and curved talons tipped each hoof, leaving small gouges in solid stone with every step. But its face seemed almost...

Cute.

Large eyes, velvety muzzle, and small ears that flicked attentively as it studied them with predatory intelligence.

“Oh!” Evie breathed, her expression softening with delight, previous pain apparently forgotten in the face of this impossible creature. “They’re beautiful!”

The miniature pegasus tossed its head, releasing another snort that sounded almost inquisitive. Behind it, more red eyes blinked, dozens of them, as the herd shifted with synchronized movements that spoke of a shared mind or perfect communication.

“Are those... pegasi?” Gav asked, his voice barely above a whisper, academic interest temporarily overwhelming his well-earned caution.

“Tiny ones,” Elena added, her whip-blade still coiled tight in her fist despite the apparent harmlessness of the creatures. “Why would they be down here in the dark? What do they eat? There’s nothing growing...”

Her question trailed off, the implications suddenly apparent in her widening eyes.

The lead pegasus pawed at the ground, leaving gouges in solid stone with its taloned hooves. It approached Cade and the others slowly, neck extended in curiosity, movements graceful despite the ominous soundtrack of wings rustling in anticipation.

“They seem friendly enough,” Evie said, stepping forward with her hand outstretched before anyone could stop her. Innocence radiated from her in near-tangible waves. “Hello there, little one.”

“Evie, wait,” Nora cautioned, alarm evident in her voice as she reached for her ward.

Too late. The siren had already moved within reach of the creature, fingers extended in greeting.

Cade watched as the pegasus sniffed Evie’s outstretched fingers, its velvety muzzle tickling her palm. The siren giggled, enchanted by the unexpected tenderness of the moment, her face lit with childlike wonder.

Maybe they’re just stuck here too, like us? The young thief posited.

“See?” she said, turning to smile at her companions, trust shining in her eyes like a beacon. “They’re sweet—”

The pegasus’s head snapped forward with viper quickness, jaws opening to reveal rows of razor-sharp teeth where herbivore flatness should have been. It bit down on Evie’s hand with savage force, the crunch of bone audible in the sudden silence that fell across the chamber.

Evie’s scream tore through the darkness, primal and wounded, as blood spurted from between the creature’s teeth—bright arterial red that seemed to glow with its own light in the phosphorescent gloom.

“EVIE!” Nora’s voice shattered the moment, her sword already arcing through the air toward the attacker, darkness cutting darkness.

But it was too late. The scent of blood triggered a frenzy. All around them, leathery wings unfurled and serrated teeth flashed as the herd moved in for the feast.

The hunt was on.
