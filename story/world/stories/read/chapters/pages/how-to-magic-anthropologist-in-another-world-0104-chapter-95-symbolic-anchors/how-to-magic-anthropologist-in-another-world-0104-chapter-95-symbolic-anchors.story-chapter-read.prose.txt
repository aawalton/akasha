If these objects were going to form the foundation of a miracle meant to protect me, then they shouldn't simply represent where I came from. They should represent who I was.

I pulled out my phone and opened my calculator.

Three months, with each month having thirty-six days.

3 × 36 = 108 Hano days.

With one Hano day having forty-two hours:

108 × 42 = 4,536 hours.

And, converting that into Earth days:

4,536 ÷ 24 = 189 Earth days.

That's six months and nine days.

I checked my phone calendar, and sure enough, my calculation seemed to match. I had gone missing and left Earth in mid-March 2025, and now my phone was telling me it was nearing the end of October.

I wondered if I had missed anything important. It would be the height of irony if a certain video game with a demigod bug as its protagonist had been released only after I got myself isekai'd.

I wondered if I could play video games in my Dream Sanctuary's Moon Room.

Wait!? Would that count as piracy?

Accessing everything cost me Inspiration, but I doubted the original creators would accept that as a form of payment.

I rounded that up and cut seven fourteen-meter lengths of rope to give us some leeway for the knots.

Using the first eight-meter rope, Vena and I placed the first fence post at the back of the tower, directly opposite the collapsed doorway. We then used the side rope to extend the line to the left, making sure the new point was exactly eight meters from the tower.

Once we had the correct position, we placed the next post and knotted the rope around it.

A few minutes turned into nearly an hour.

I placed the key over the first pole, stretching the metal ring of the keychain wide enough to fit around the wood before fastening it in place.

"In the name of Hestia, safeguard my hearth and home."

For the first invocation, I had chosen Hestia.

The connection was obvious. Hestia was associated with the home, the hearth, and the safety of the household. More importantly, she was not completely random to me. My friendship with Mark had given me a personal connection to the goddess, even if that connection was rather tenuous.

The key was also the perfect starting point. It had once protected the door to my home on Earth. Now I was asking it to protect the door to my new home.

The key began to glow. A faint golden light emerged from the metal before sinking into the wooden pole. From there, it spread outward, racing along the rope toward the next pole.

One by one, the other six poles briefly illuminated before the light faded. Just like last time.

I smiled. It was working.

I moved clockwise to the second pole. I attached the picture frame using the silk ribbon, allowing it to hang against the wooden post.

I looked at the photograph for a moment.

Zuri. Mark. Jenny. People I hadn't seen in half a year. More in the case of Jenny. People who probably had no idea where I was.

"In the name of Earth, let me never forget those I left behind."

"In the name of the Holy, I witness and confirm."

The photograph glowed.

I had briefly considered whether Earth itself could qualify as a Holy place.

Someone could certainly argue that it didn't. They could point toward its wars, corporate greed, environmental destruction, and every other horrible thing humans had done and conclude that the planet itself was unworthy of the title.

I disagreed.

Earth was also a place where mothers desperately protected their children. Where people sacrificed themselves for strangers. Where friends stayed together through hardship. Where billions of people spent their entire lives caring for one another.

There was enough unconditional love on Earth to outweigh the evil of ten thousand genocides.

The light traveled through the rope again. The second pole joined the first, and the faintly glowing line continued onward.

I moved to the third pole. I took the silver necklace and wrapped it twice around the wooden post before fastening it tightly.

The Pisces symbol rested against the wood.

"In the name of Hecate, intertwine my fate with magic."

"In the name of the Holy, I witness and confirm."

The necklace glowed.

Hecate was a harder sell than Hestia. She wasn't exactly a goddess designed around the concept of good. Her association with witchcraft had also made her a convenient target for centuries of fictional villainy and modern misconceptions.

But the Holy religion didn't seem to judge morality on a simple good-versus-evil scale.

Its distinction was closer to society versus selfishness. And by that standard, Hecate had plenty going for her.

She had never been defined by a desire for personal power at the expense of others. More importantly, she was connected to magic, crossroads, and things that existed between boundaries.

There was another connection too. I had chosen her name. The name Hecate was part of the identity I had built for myself in this world, and the goddess I had encountered in my dreams had been far more Holy than anything I had read about in Earth's modern media.

That was more than enough of a connection for me.

The light traveled onward. Three poles were now glowing faintly.

I moved to the fourth. I carefully attached the meteorite to the pole. Just like me, the fist-sized piece of rock had crossed worlds.

"In the name of the stars, let this miracle guide my path."

"In the name of the Holy, I witness and confirm."

The meteorite flashed.

This one was almost self-explanatory. The stars were the source of my powers. This tower stood in the Sunless Reach, where the stars remained visible throughout the eternal night. The meteorite was a piece of the heavens that had fallen into a world below.

And, in a way, it represented me. Something from another world that had somehow fallen into this one.

Besides, nobody was going to argue that the stars weren't Holy. They guided travelers, marked the passage of time, inspired countless discoveries, and gave people something to wonder about.

The light spread from the fourth pole. The rope connecting the poles became brighter. I could feel the pressure building.

I moved to the fifth. I used the leather strap attached to the astrolabe to fasten it securely to the pole.

"In the name of Copernicus, let this place help me understand the world around me."

"In the name of the Holy, I witness and confirm."

The astrolabe began to glow.

I smiled.

An observatory tower with a heliocentric model at its center and a telescope at its top. If Copernicus had been a god, this place would have been his temple, and the astrolabe would have been his perfect totem.

More importantly, it represented the part of me that hadn't changed since arriving here. I still wanted to know. I still wanted to understand. There was an entire world around me that I knew almost nothing about, and I intended to learn as much of it as I possibly could.

The light surged through the rope once more. Five poles were now activated.

I moved to the sixth. I placed the taser on top of the pole, applying a small amount of adhesive to its wooden base before securing it in place.

The Sky Mana core glimmered faintly. I pressed the activation mechanism. A pair of sparks jumped between the metal prongs.

"In the name of Tesla, grant me power and understanding."

"In the name of the Holy, I witness and confirm."

The taser flashed brightly.

I had considered several possible figures for this one. But I wasn't about to invoke a pervert like Zeus or an idiot like Thor in a world where gods could actually manifest.

At least if Tesla showed up, the worst thing that could happen was everyone suddenly having electricity. Besides, I genuinely admired the scientist.

The taser represented more than just lightning.

Nina had created it because I had shared my knowledge with her. My knowledge had become her creation, and that creation had become something I could use.

It was the perfect representation of the two sides of me. The scholar who changed the world by introducing new knowledge, and the freelancer who had learned how to use that knowledge to become dangerous.

I looked toward the final pole. Six objects. Six prayers. Six totems. Only one remained.

I picked up Nina's feather. It looked almost impossibly delicate inside its hardened resin casing.

I walked toward the seventh pole. For a moment, I simply held it in my hand. This one was different. The key belonged to my old home. The photograph belonged to my old life. The necklace represented the person I had been born as. The meteorite represented how I had arrived. The astrolabe represented what I had come here to do. The taser represented what I had become.

And this… this represented what I had found.

I attached Nina's feather to the final pole. Then I placed my hand against it.

"In the name of friendship, let this place become my home."

"In the name of the Holy, I witness and confirm."

For a moment, nothing happened. Then the feather began to glow. The light wasn't merely brighter than before.

It exploded. A brilliant pulse erupted from the seventh pole, racing along the ropes in both directions. The entire heptagon illuminated at once, the light jumping from pole to pole in rapid circles.

Once. Twice. And again.

The seven poles flashed repeatedly as the light traveled around the perimeter faster and faster, until the entire heptagon seemed to have become a single glowing ring.

I felt the pressure in the air intensify. Then the light sank. It flowed down through the poles and into the earth beneath them, spreading inward beneath the entire Sanctified Ground. The glow disappeared, but the pressure remained.

For several seconds, neither of us moved.

I could feel something surrounding the tower now.

An invisible boundary drawn around my home by seven objects, seven prayers, and seven pieces of my life.

I looked at Nina's feather. I had lived here for half a year. I wasn't just Alice Abbott anymore. I was also Alice Hecate. I had friends here. Friends I fought alongside. Friends I studied with. Friends I experimented with. Friends who had shared their dreams with me.

Friends I belonged with.
