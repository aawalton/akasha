The ceremony was relatively small. It played host to just a few dozen folk.

A very rare and eclectic mix—there was a section in the back with an older fellow in a big fluffy hat, and smaller fellows in smaller fluffy hats. They were situated next to a former dragon-prince, who was situated next to a stocky fellow in spectacles.

It included, inexplicably, a rhinoceros in a tuxedo in the back-right, just chilling there. It also included three Faction Heads—friends of the bride—and also a certain Evan and Avery, who could be seen at the front, cheering, as the World Tree priest said the words.

There was a woman at the altar, dressed in a white gown that suited her nicely, and a man in a well-tailored suit.

And with two ‘I do’s’, they kissed.

***

Zane’s life settled down a little after that.

He spent quite a bit of quality time with his friends. It felt like all of Dragonspire kind of needed a breath.

It was the first time, really since the start of his journey—that first notification he’d gotten as his car pulled off the side of that middle-of-nowhere road in Washington—that there was truly nothing immediate in his future, at least that he had to worry about.

He could simply rest.

And he felt he’d earned it.

Though that didn’t mean he’d stopped training altogether.

***

Noughtfire had returned to his mountaintop study, and fairly often Zane would visit the old fellow.

He had some time to think and to study.

They’d chat a bit—about news regarding folks they knew, such as the fact that Striker was already back in the End, hunting void-dragons.

But they’d mostly research Zane’s other Concepts, and speculate.

Zane’s next Concept of study would likely be the ‘Reality-Breaking Flame.’ It was a topic of some fascination for the Sage, since it’d been established that Zane’s Tier 7 Concepts could hold aspects of higher-tiered Laws.

They considered if they could infuse more space into this Concept, or maybe even Time.

But the Galaxy had very few resources covering Laws at that level—“These are all speculative, as of now,” the Sage warned.

Then the Sage had smiled slightly. “I assume you’ve seen the Scryer’s Guild’s report.”

He nodded. “I have.”

“It seems vast swathes of the Universe are opening up,” the Sage mused. “And if certain stretches are now open to void-walking, paths to the beyond once considered unworkable… perhaps no longer carry that restriction.”

The Sage paused.

“I imagine all you’d need to do is to procure a treasure which enables you to void-walk. Difficult, but possible.”

“It may be some time before that happens,” Zane told him. He expected he’d spend the next little bit just chilling.

They chatted a little more, drinking tea as they did. And then he thanked the Sage, and made his exit.

He truly meant what he’d said to the Sage. He felt pretty much at peace.

He was sure he’d feel the desire to get out there again, but for now—he simply had other things he wished to attend to.

He had plenty to look forward to.

***

And one more thing—

On the night of the wedding, Reina had decided to take off her birth control.

Nine months later, she gave birth to twins.

He supposed all that Leveling paid off.

***

6 years later…

“Take that!”

A girl thrust out her staff, and a maelstrom of frost elementals descended on a black-haired boy, who cried out and vanished. It was a maelstrom far more potent than any Core Formation cultivator had any business summoning. And the ice-block they formed, similarly, should’ve been impossible for any Core Formation cultivator to break.

But in seconds seams showed on the block. The boy quickly broke out, panting.

“That’s enough, Celeste!” called Reina, acting as referee. She beamed. “Excellent work.”

The boy seemed slightly discouraged. “You did excellently too,” she reassured him. “It’s no easy task, breaking a block like that. It just takes a little time to get used to your new body—let’s call it a day, alright?”

“Alright,” said the boy, reluctantly.

When Reina said ‘excellent work,’ at times, she knew it sounded a bit generic. But there truly wasn’t any other way to describe them.

Two seamless Cores, at age six…

She should’ve been used to it by now. She supposed it wasn’t unexpected, with their heritage.

Still—if Dragonspire hadn’t been ready for their father…

“When’s uncle Hax coming over!” cried the boy.

“He’s got a duel this weekend, sweetie,” she said patiently. “Remember?”

“Is he gonna be okay?” Asked the girl.

“He’s very strong,” Reina assured her.

The girl looked slightly dubious. Reina decided it wasn’t worth saying more.

Their standards were... slightly skewed.

“I want to go train with Grandpa Jogo,” the boy announced.

“You may not,” she said sharply. And then, at the look on his face, she softened a bit.

“At least until you’re thirteen,” she offered, after some hesitation.

It was a promise she quickly hoped he’d forget sometime before then. Though something told her it wasn’t likely.

She considered the skies.

She’d had to wrap up because she had a Council meeting in the next hour—a meeting that’d finalize the new Lord Commander-led expedition to a promising new pocket dimension just outside the Galaxy… but first—

She pursed her lips.

It should be here any time now.

It’d be a few more minutes until it arrived.

A postcard, hand-delivered by her handmaiden Aelin.

Core of the Galaxy

The Dreamcore Grand Knight fell, sliced in half in a single blow.

A slight disappointment, since it’d been quite some time since Zane had faced something that could resist him.

He’d been hopeful there.

He touched the dreamsteel door, and felt a scanning sensation wash over his soul—he blinked.

And then it gave way.

He headed on in.

Inside was a box of pure white, with a system-grid—a tiny pocket dimension.

At its center was an altar. On that altar lay a letter.

He opened it, heart pounding.

I think it’s fair to say, long time no see.

He could feel the amusement in the words.

If you’re reading this—Let me offer you my congratulations. The Universe must be open for transversal.

And your journey in Dragonspire is now complete.

Included here is a token granting you the highest level of citizenship within the Celestial Imperium. Please feel free to visit me, in the Imperial Palace, at your leisure. I look forward to catching up.

Your friend,

Aiwë.

P.S.—I do hope you liked what I did with your pipes.

He smiled. Included in the letter was a badge.

Icon of the Celestial Imperium (T)

T?

That was new.

There was also a map in there, detailing the journey. A journey that he estimated would take 6 months to a year—an initial expedition, maybe.

He wouldn’t be the first in Dragonspire to leave the Galaxy—Lyxandor had done it, but plenty had afterward as well, in the past six years. Including several of his friends.

He’d wanted to wait a bit before he undertook the journey—just spend some time in the World Tree. It’d felt only right.

But now…

There was quite a bit left for him to do.

His second Supreme, for one, hadn’t been unlocked yet. He’d only scratched the surface of that ‘System Administrator’ title—although, come to think of it—was that what’d just let him open that door?

There was his Title too, whose true potential was supposed to unlock at Empyrean. A level he hadn’t yet reached.

And to get there he’d need to attain several new Concepts too.

His journey was nowhere close to ending... it was simply that this chapter—in Dragonspire—had concluded.

He had mixed feelings about that.

Not long after he found himself at the edge of the Galaxy, staring out into shattered space.

There he sent his final post-cards.

Then he stepped out on his next adventure.
