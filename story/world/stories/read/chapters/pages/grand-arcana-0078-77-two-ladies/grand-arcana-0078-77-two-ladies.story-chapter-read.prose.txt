Waking up during a trial always came with fear. Back in my previous run, sleep was a luxury. People would mostly wake up upon sensing danger. They’d draw their weapons and look around, trying to determine if anyone had gone missing. The last few days had been different. I still woke up afraid, only this time my fears were that all I had gone through could turn out to be a dream.

Three days and the war was no closer to starting than before. There were always rumblings, things always seemed to be escalating, yet at the same time nothing had changed. I had managed to find a few more participants in the city. Words had been exchanged, plans made, but neither side agreed to any commitment. Everyone was looking for an angle, navigating the space between helpful and too helpful.

“Same dream again?” Sparta asked beside me, then turned to the other side.

Pup? I thought, looking at her with a stern expression. When my dream beast barked in my mind, I allowed myself to smile.

Something good had come out of this run. In part due to my strength, in part due to my specials, I had pulled it off. One couldn’t say we were as close as before; last run it had taken months for either of us considered calling ourselves a couple, but we were sleeping together and that was a start.

“No dreams,” I replied, but she had dozed off to sleep.

Thanks to the Observer card, I could always tell whether she was pretending. For several minutes I lay in bed, looking at the shuttered window. Light wasn’t an issue for either of us, so we never bothered to let it in. This tended to confuse people, especially the pair of thieves who had attempted to visit our room.

Gently, I slipped out of bed.

Hunting time? Serp asked.

I just sighed. There was a time when I would have considered him and the dream pup intrusive. Until I found the appropriate card, they got to see everything I did, just like all the deities and rule-masters. Privacy had long stopped being an issue. After years of living on the verge of death, one tended to grab any opportunity without caring what others thought.

A half-full mug of juice lay on the table. Enduring the inn’s piss-poor alcohol for a day was enough to get me to switch to anything else. The local water had a strong chalky taste, so fruit juice was the best way to mask it without making me want to vomit my guts out.

I’m so close to getting a new skill, Serp continued.

It was always the same with him. So far, we had gone on two more hunts with Sparta. In one case the creature had proved strong enough to drop a reward, in the other, it hadn’t. The reward was effectively split between Sparta and Serp: she got a few Minor cards, and the saber got the booster sphere. I still wasn’t sure how I felt about this, but both of them had their own way of making up for it.

“We’ll see,” I muttered.

I took a gulp from the jug, then went to the window. Just as I reached to open the shutters, there was a knock on the door.

Serp, get ready, I tensed up.

Thanks to the Listener card, I’d hear anyone arriving long before they reached the door. The fact that I didn’t could only mean two things.

Quietly, I went up to the door and waited. Once there was another knock, I briskly opened it, ready to leap to the side. As I expected, a participant stood there. To my surprise, it was Eleonor.

“Nice to see you, too,” she said with her usual snobby smirk. It didn’t escape my attention that she looked over me a bit too intently before focusing on my face again. “I see you’ve been having fun.”

“What do you want?” I whispered.

“Maybe just to join in?” She tiptoed, looking over my shoulder in an effort to force a reaction.

I remained silent.

“Your dog has been guarding your dreams again, so this was the only way to talk to you,” she said, visibly disappointed that her efforts hadn’t paid off. “I want you to help me out with something.”

“Get Marcus,” I said without hesitation.

“Things between me and Marcus remain a bit tense.”

I tried not to laugh. Tense would be an understatement, considering what she had put him through during the cruise trial. If I were in her shoes, I’d be worried about a lot more.

“Besides, he’s not right for this job. You are.”

When she said that, I already knew that she needed someone without a pact. Even severed from Hypnos, she had the ability to enter people’s dreams and convince them to follow her will. Frankly, I was surprised she hadn’t. The trial restrictions put in place by Harlequin meant that no deities could protect their chosen. She could easily entangle them with dream threads, making them puppets to her will. Of course, once the trial was over, she’d have to answer to a lot of pissed-up deities.

“Not for free, of course.” A bronze card appeared in her hand. “There will be payment. We can even have fun after this is over.”

I narrowed my eyes.

“She can join in, of course. I wouldn’t mind.”

“What’s the card?” I asked.

“Does it matter?” She kept it hidden for a few more seconds before turning it around.

It was a Drummer—possibly the least valuable card of the Major Arcana. It took special skill to offer me something so insignificant, which was why I suspected she had done it on purpose.

“It’ll take a lot more than that.”

“And you’re not even curious what job I need you for?” She kept trying to pique my interest. “Things worked quite well last time, you must admit. Especially for you.”

She wasn’t wrong, but something in the way she said it made me want to slam the door in her face. I wouldn’t go as far as kill her, not yet in any event, but she could definitely do with some pain.

“Join me on this, and you might learn more about your sister.”

The serpent blade appeared in my hand.

From this distance all I needed was one strike to kill her. No matter what cards she had, Serp’s quick draw would pierce her before she had a chance to act, and once inside, nothing short of a Phoenix card would save her.

“That’s what the minion said, wasn’t it?” she pressed on. Clearly, she was confident in herself. Even without special cards, she could see the reaction this had gotten and instead of backing off, kept poking even further. “What if I told you that there’s a way not only to get information on our target but also learn a thing or two about the world outside?”

“I’ll say that you’re lying.” I moved the tip of the blade closer to her throat.

“You know I’m not,” she said. “To be more specific, you want to believe I’m telling you the truth. You’ve still got questions you need answered.”

“I warned you not to mess with my dreams.” It was taking great effort not to kill her, and yet she was right. After all this time, three things made me act irrationally despite years of experience: talk of my family, information about the world outside, and anything related to my betrayal.

“Do you think everything revolves around you?”

There didn’t seem to be any fear in her eyes, almost as if she knew exactly what I was going to do.

You arrogant bitch! I thought. Even now she kept on acting as if she owned the game. True, she had backing and was more manipulative than most, but that reign had ended once we had formed the new group. Everyone here had some sort of backing, and her little tricks no longer had any effect. I had seen plenty of people like her in my previous run. Most of them didn’t tend to last long. Sooner rather than later, she’d end up facing something she wasn’t ready for and rid us of her presence. The question was who she would drag along.

“Ten seconds,” she said. “That’s all it’ll take to convince you.”

“You have ten seconds, then.” I tightened my grip around the saber.

“No.” The bronze card disappeared from her hand. “You’ll need to show me you’re serious. I’ll be waiting outside. If you want to hear those ten seconds, get dressed and meet me there.”

I was just about to follow up with a demand of my own, but by the time I blinked, she was no longer there.

Damn it! I took a step back, ready for any attack.

That was one of the annoying things about the Thief card. Like Sparta, she must have maxed its level to be able to vanish without a trace.

Next sphere I’m getting goes for the Observer, I thought.

I closed the door and looked at Sparta. She was still sleeping, half-covered on the bed, seemingly without a care in the world. Just watching her made me want to forget my conversation with Eleonor and join her. Sadly, it was impossible. The goth seemed to know precisely how to push me into action, which made her more dangerous than I had given her credit for. Up until now I had viewed her as a spoiled bitch with more ego than skill, yet I was starting to think I was wrong.

“Whatever I do, shit always pulls me back,” I muttered and went to get dressed.

While I did, I considered leaving Sparta a note. The thought had never crossed my mind ever since I had met her for the first time. After all, everything in the Grand Arcana was temporary; every day could be one’s last, and all that everyone—even the best—could do was fight to delay it for as long as possible.

Gaining a bit of recognition from Harlequin had changed things. It had given me a glimmer of hope that maybe there were good sides to the Grand Arcana. For starters, we hadn’t been thrust into a fight during this trial.

Just forget her, Serp grumbled, making it unclear whether he was supporting or criticizing me.

“Which one?” I asked as I pulled my boots on.

Both! They’re just slowing you down and stealing your rewards.

“Look who’s talking.”

I unsummoned the weapon. Despite himself, Serp sometimes had the uncanny ability to make me smile. Of course, I had to be mindful that he was also highly dangerous, and would get even more lethal as he grew stronger.

Quietly, I left the room.

It was early morning, so the inn was mostly empty. The only person awake was the innkeeper, who seemed to hate sleep almost as much as people who didn’t pay their tab.

“One more night.” I tossed him two silver coins as I walked by.

The man gave me his usual that’s-what-you-always-say look, but grabbed the coins without a word. I had no idea to what extent Harlequin had gone when it came to the economy of the trial, but it was clear that coins were in short supply.

The goth was waiting for me outside, just as she had said she would. She was standing by a pair of thoroughbred horses, which indicated that she had both means and connections. The aether within them made them glow as if their coats were outright bioluminescent. I could only assume how many dreams she had roamed through in order to achieve so much favor in the city. In different circumstances, I might have even asked her about it.

I walked up to her and crossed my arms. It took a special level of cockiness to assume that I’d join her. If nothing else, she was definitely confident, I had to give her that.

“You have ten seconds,” I said.

“I’m the Delphi Oracle,” she stated.

Ten seconds? She had convinced me in less than one.

There wasn’t a participant who had survived their first year that didn’t know about oracles. There was the card, naturally—a Grand Arcana that was said to grant powers surpassing the gods themselves. It was said that the future was the only restriction placed upon deities. They were close to omnipotent, omnivoyant, with powers surpassing the imagination, yet when it came to looking into the future, they remained no different from any of us.

Only participants were blessed with the power to see ahead: clairvoyants, soothsayers, diviners… Among them, the oracles were said to hold the greatest power. As the rumors went, they were the only ones whose visions came true.

“Prove it,” I said, ready to summon Serp again.

“Do you think I’m lying?” She looked me straight in the eye. “What’s the Observer card telling you?”

I didn’t say a word.

“I won’t prove it for something as stupid as this.” She turned back to the nearest horse and mounted it. “Join in or not. It’s entirely your choice.”

Now she was behaving like a deity. The added layer of arrogance gave some weight to her words. The real question was whether I was willing to risk letting such an opportunity slip away.

“I can’t ride that,” I said after a few seconds of thought.

The goth tossed me a bronze card. The moment I grabbed it, I saw that she had given me the Rider.

“You can now.”

Effectively, that was it. A transaction had been made, and I had accepted. Now it was only a matter of seeing whether I had made the right choice.

Using the skills of the new card, I climbed up the creature. It didn’t resist, accepting me as its rider.

“She’s bad for you, by the way.” Eleonor turned at me.

“The horse?” I looked down instinctively.

“The woman you’re sleeping with.”

Sparta? “Is that a prediction?”

“No.” Eleonor kicked her horse into a gallop. “Just a personal opinion.”
