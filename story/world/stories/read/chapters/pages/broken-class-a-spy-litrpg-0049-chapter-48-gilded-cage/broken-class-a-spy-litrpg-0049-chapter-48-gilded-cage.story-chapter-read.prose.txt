Ysara dismissed the Chamberlain with a nod, then turned to her two guards. "You are dismissed as well. Refresh yourselves. Be ready to accompany me in an hour."

Having given her orders, she swept past, heading towards her personal wing of the palace. The moment the heavy, soundproofed doors of her chambers closed behind her, the mask of the princess shattered. The strength that had carried her through the forest, the city, and the portal finally gave way. A sob tore from her throat, and she collapsed to the floor, the grief for her fallen friends a physical weight that crushed the air from her lungs.

A pair of soft hands touched her shoulder. "Your Highness," a gentle voice murmured. It was Seren, her personal maid, who had been preparing the chamber for her return. The maid knelt, wrapping a comforting arm around her shaking shoulders.

"Braska... Thorne... they're dead," Ysara choked out, the words raw and broken. She was about to say Kaelin's name, but the lie caught in her throat. The man outside wore his face, a constant, painful reminder of her loss and her impossible secret.

The maid's eyes widened in shock. She had known something was wrong, but not this. "Oh, my lady," she whispered, her voice thick with sympathy. "Their spirits will be at peace, knowing their sacrifice brought you home safely."

"I know," Ysara sobbed, burying her face in her hands. "And that is why it hurts so much. They were more than guards. They were like my siblings."

The maid held her for a long moment, then her tone shifted, becoming firm but gentle. "And now you must be strong for them, Your Highness. You have a meeting with the King. To show this sorrow now... your enemies will see it as weakness." She helped the princess to her feet. "The bath is ready. You must prepare."

Ysara allowed herself to be led into the bathroom. She disrobed, letting the filthy, travel-stained clothes fall to the floor, and sank into the steaming water. She drew her knees to her chest, wrapping her arms around them for comfort as the warmth seeped into her chilled bones. The maid began to gently scrub her back, her presence a silent, steady comfort in the quiet room.

An hour later, Ysara was a different person. Dressed in a simple but elegant gown of deep blue, her face was a masterpiece of regal beauty, her makeup expertly applied to hide any trace of her earlier tears. "Are they back?" she asked, her voice calm and steady.

The maid nodded. "I will check, Your Highness." She opened the chamber door just a crack and peeked out. She saw two figures standing guard, motionless and silent on either side of the entrance. She closed the door. "They are on station, my lady."

Ysara waited, her hands clasped in her lap, as the maid brushed her long, silver hair until it shone. When the time came, she rose, the perfect image of a princess.

She stepped out of her chambers. "Yudursh. Kaelin. Accompany me."

Yudursh gave a quiet nod, which was copied a moment later by Kaelin. They fell into step behind her, their footsteps silent on the marble floors. As they walked, a new wave of anxiety washed over Ysara. What if her father saw through the deception? He was a D-grade, his senses far beyond those of a normal person. Would Arthur's magic fool him? If he discovered the imposter, what would he do? If Arthur was killed, she had no doubt his powerful clan would seek revenge. The destruction of her entire world hung on this fragile lie.

Lost in her thoughts, she didn't realize they had arrived until the guards outside the King's personal dining area bowed. She turned to her guards. "Wait here." Bracing herself, she entered the room.

Her father, King Ysender, sat at the head of a long, polished table. He was a man in his prime, his dark hair streaked with silver, his face lined with the burdens of rule. But his eyes were sharp and clear, and the sheer presence of his mana was a heavy, oppressive force in the room.

He looked up as she entered, and for a brief moment, the mask of the king slipped. A flicker of genuine relief softened his features. "You are unharmed," he said, his voice holding a note of fatherly concern. "That is what matters most." Then, just as quickly, the mask returned, his expression hardening as he shifted back to his duty. "Join me, Ysara."

She curtsied low. "Father. Long may you live and prosperously rule."

She nodded and took her seat, a servant pulling the chair out for her. She waited in silence. Court etiquette was strict. Matters of state were never discussed in front of servants.

A multi-course dinner was brought in, each dish a masterpiece of culinary art, rich with mana that hummed in the air. There was roasted sun-finch, glowing moon-melons, and wine that tasted of starlight. But Ysara had no appetite. She nibbled at her food, stealing glances at her father, who ate with a focused, deliberate silence. When they were both finished, the servants cleared the table with practiced efficiency. The King waved a hand, and they bowed, leaving the room and closing the heavy doors behind them. They were alone.

The King leaned forward, resting his elbows on the table, his fingers interlaced. "Report," he said, his voice calm but carrying the weight of absolute authority.

"Father," she began, her voice steady. "It was all going well until the tenth day. We had just taken down an Earthen Dreadspine. Before we could even recover, Garran and his men attacked. They had been following us, waiting for us to be weakened."

"Are you certain it was Garran?" the King asked, his eyes narrowing.

"Yes. His blood skills are unmistakable."

"That changes things," the King said, his voice grim. "Proceed."

"Braska and Thorne died protecting me," she said, her voice catching for a moment. The King paused, his gaze softening for a fraction of a second. "They were good men. Their sacrifice will be honored." He then gestured for her to continue, the moment of sentiment passed.

"As we fought, a pack of Stoneclaw Howlers attacked Garran's party from the rear. It was chaos. Yudursh and Kaelin used the distraction to get me away from the ambush." She hated the lie, but it was the only one she had. "We abandoned the Hunt then. We hid in the forest until it was over and made our way to Copperpine. We didn't know who else might be involved."

The King listened, his expression unreadable. "And this victor... 'Arthur.' Unaffiliated. His performance was unprecedented. Did you encounter him?"

Ysara kept her composure, the lie ready on her tongue. "No, Father. After the ambush, we remained in hiding. We saw no other participants. I know nothing of him."

"A ghost who appears, shatters all records, and vanishes," the King mused, his gaze distant. "For such a powerful unknown to emerge at the exact same time the Water Clan makes their move... it reveals a vulnerability I had not anticipated."

He refocused on her, his expression hardening. "Whether this 'Arthur' is their hidden ace or simply another shark in the water, the conclusion is the same. The situation is more serious than I had assumed. The Water Clan has made a move against the crown, and we are clearly not as secure as I believed."

His gaze became unwavering as he delivered his verdict. "We need new allies. Stronger alliances. You will be married before the year is out."

The words hit her like a physical blow. "Father, a marriage alliance is not our only option," she countered, her voice sharp with a desperate strategic plea. "Allow me to open diplomatic channels with the Earth Clan. An alliance built on mutual interest would be stronger than one forged in a marriage chapel."

"I have had enough of this," the King said, his voice rising, the calm authority replaced by a flash of iron will. "Your mother's greatest wish was for you to be safe. I will not fail her in that. This alliance will secure your future, and my word is final."

Ysara stared at her father, her heart sinking. He had used her mother's memory as a shield, a final, unassailable move in a game she was not allowed to win. She had survived an assassination attempt only to be thrown into a gilded cage. She rose from her chair, her own determination hardening into a cold resolve. She would not accept this fate. She would find a way out.
