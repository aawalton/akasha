“We agree that the Eternal Shard kicked the bucket just two years ago, right?” My words were dry, dull and mechanical.

“That’s what Cuilcis said.” Steve agreed. “And so far, we’re pretty certain he can’t actually lie.”

“And whatever gentleman’s agreement the powers that be in this world came up with regarding this area, the…Torn Divide. It can’t have been cobbled together in just a few days, right?”

“Stands to reason. Politics being politics, it should’ve taken a while for them to agree.”

“Okay. Let’s assume that’s all true, and on top of that, that it should take all minor players a while to get their asses into gear and move here in order to abuse the local possibilities… can you explain to me why the everloving fuck we’re looking at a fully-fledged fortress city?”

The day and a half since our near-clash with the Menial Servant, our progress had been fast, unimpeded and, dare I say it, boring. The climate had grown drier, the ground rockier and the local vegetation increasingly sparse. Yet, we hadn’t experienced any clashes, ambushes or anything to relieve the monotony of our travels. The most important detail was that I’d gained an additional five points to Poison Resistance and hit Level 25.

Your skill Poison Resistance has reached the first evolution threshold at Level 25. This earns you a choice between two different evolution options.

Option one:

Level 25 evolution:

From now on, your Poison Resistance skill will be 30% more effective against natural venoms.

Option two:

Level 25 evolution:

From now on, your Poison Resistance skill will be 30% more effective against crafted poisons.

We’d swiftly agreed that crafted poisons was the way to go, out of the idea that sentient beings were more likely to think up the deadliest concoctions. Of course, the dangerous beasts of this world would probably line up to prove us wrong. But the choice was made.

A short while after picking my evolution, I got an upgrade. A little late, of course, but better late than never.

Congratulations. You have unlocked the higher rarity of a skill:

You have been afflicted with a total of 5000 health points of poison damage over your life.

Poison Resistance evolves to Higher Poison Resistance.

Higher Poison Resistance

Passive skill

Rare

Whether ingested or entered into the bloodstream through a bite or a weapon, the result is the same. You have been poisoned. Poison is an insidious, deadly force that lingers, often resulting in an ugly, protracted death. Every level in Poison Resistance will moderately decrease the damage received from poison and increase the speed at which the poison is metabolized.

In addition, once a week you gain the ability to downgrade the severity of the deadliest poison afflicting you.

There was still quite a way to go for the next evolution, but damn. At this speed, I was going to hit the Level 50 one in a week. Once I managed that, I’d be chugging concentrated poison just for kicks!

That didn’t change our current confusion. We were staring down upon a crater, shaped like what looked for all the world like my old foster family’s inherited pudding mold. The deep depression went down, down and down in a pockmarked mess that looked optimized for tripping runners and breaking legs, and then, right at the center, it rose again, until almost reaching the same height as the edge of the crater.

And in that center dwelled a fortress city. Tall, colored walls painted with a wide range of jolly colors rose up into the sky, like a merry statement to their surroundings. “Here we are.” It said. “We are not afraid to announce our presence, loudly.” Unfortunately, apart from any number of happily wafting banners and a single, taller building within that looked like a castle, we were unable to see past the walls.

“Really? I have to admit, this is pretty much exactly what I was expecting.” Victoria shrugged. “You told me that these were supposed to be mages, and not just that, but mages who liked fighting defensively. What else were you expecting? I mean, back in the Ever Steady, we’ve got a single mage who knows how to work earth, and he’s putting up dozens of meters of solidly crafted wall each damn day. How many mages do you think they’ve got working on this?”

I blinked. Then I slapped myself. “Yeah, that’s on me. Obviously this isn’t crafted normally. Looks efficient, though.”

Steve nodded. “I must admit, I didn’t expect them to be under siege either.”

It was hard to debate against that. I gave it my best try, though. “You know, I might argue that you need more attackers for a siege… but those Ceaseless really do keep on coming, don’t they?”

They absolutely did. The crater itself was enormous. Probably seven or eight kilometers across. And while the attackers looked like ants, and disappeared when you started looking at the full scope of the scene, there were constantly new attackers throwing themselves into the crater, mostly from the western sides.

“A que si. They are absolutely getting themselves slaughtered, though.”

Steve whistled softly, as one beast in the distance went sky-high. “Look at that one blow.”

I snorted. “They’ve got some effective traps down there, that’s for sure. I can’t tell which level the attackers are from this distance, though.”

“I can.” Steve smiled. “Remember my Identify Evolution? They’re all Ceaseless Fighters, Level 25, like those we, erm, fought. Oh. There’s a couple Level 15 scouts too. Everything looks the same as what we fought.”

“Sure does.” I considered. “So. If this place is totally booby-trapped, and their traps literally explode… how are we supposed to get in? Just chance it?”

Victoria snorted. “I’d say we take the one super obvious path they’ve clearly prepared for this exact purpose.” She pointed.

I slapped my forehead. Amidst the dull earthen colors of the crater, a section had been framed in bright yellow, indicating a long, indirect path that wound its way all the way to a small gate in the wall way, way up. “Yeah. That’ll do it. Okay. Here are my thoughts. With the way their fortress is laid out, we’re not learning anything from out here, except for the fact that they have excellent defenses. Agreed?”

“Yup.” Steve nodded.

“So, the question is – do we say hello from a distance and check if they’ll try to murder us? Or do we just go back?”

Victoria scoffed. “Seriously? You’d go all this way, only to turn back? I want to meet the aliens!”

“Technically, we’re the aliens here.” Steve tapped his nose with a smile. “Also, I would much rather have ‘to boldly go where no man has gone before’ apply to greeting new species than, you know, to stepping on magic land mines.”

I grunted in agreement. Honestly, it would feel like a huge waste of time if we’d turn back before even trying to greet the neighbors. “Anybody needs a break before we start climbing, this is the time. This looks like it’s going to be a hike and a half.”

It was. Not only was the full route likely five kilometers. The climb wasn’t made any easier by how the soil was every bit as broken as it looked from up top. My money was on the Hiscariat actively doing something to ruin any proper footing.

The trip wasn’t safe. Twice, some of the Ceaseless came close enough that they spotted us and deviated, charging right in our direction. That, of course, allowed us to confirm beyond a doubt that these were indeed the same Ceaseless Fighters we’d clashed with before. Same looks, like a rage elemental had a kid with a werewolf and decided to peel all its fur off with a cheese grater. Oh yeah. Same horribly scary looks, same behavior, even their damn levels were the same.

Ceaseless Fighter

Level 25

Health: 380/380

We had changed since our last clash with the beasts, though. One fighter bumped into a trap halfway across, half its body turning to ice, while the other half kept scrambling for us. The other made it all the way, and committed suicide by shield in the very first charge. I didn’t even have to take a step back. Hell. With the way its neck cracked backwards from my Deflect, I wasn’t even sure I’d needed the Wave of Reflection.

Now that we started the climb, we started actually seeing shapes on the battlements above. Steve had spotted them way earlier and tried to point them out, but there wasn’t a whole lot we could do to change how he had twice our Perception. But the Hiscariat were up there.

At least, I was way ahead of him in Fortitude. He huffed as the degree of the incline grew, and small rocks made the footing treacherous. Meanwhile, my own steps were steady and my breath even. I could do this all day.

I went a bit ahead of the others. We figured it was the best approach. Not that we expected anything to go wrong. They had gone to the effort to prepare what I assumed was a safe approach to the city.

Above us, shapes started to appear and disappear. The wall, even discounting the added height from the hill we were climbing, was tall. Tall enough that their heads were miniscule from this distance. I did spot enough to reassure myself they were humanoids of some sort and not… shapeless blobs or something. I don’t know why that helped, but it did.

One quick reaction allowed me to identify one of them for myself. Not that it helped.

Hiscariat

Further information locked

Well. At least we hadn’t taken a wrong turn somewhere.

The walls were, indeed, brightly colored. Large sections of wall were painted with a specific color, with little rhyme or reason that I could make sense of. The tiny entrance ahead and above us was right at the junction of a bright red and a sunflower yellow clashed. I tried, and failed, to find any significance in that. Still, just a few more minutes, and we’d learn for ourselves.

I couldn’t help but feel a rush of excitement at this. Last Chance had been… something different. I’d still been exposed to an entirely foreign place, filled with species that were both weird and amazing to me. Only, Last Chance had been a Mos Eisley-kinda place. A temporary refuge for all sorts of misfits. Not a real home. This, meanwhile would be an actual home for the Hiscariat. A true window into how one species native to Ademia lived. I wasn’t even into anthropology – I sure as hell couldn’t spell it – but I still couldn’t help but feel goosebumps at the idea. This was a true Neil Armstrong-moment.

“Erm, Liam?” Steve asked.

“What’s that, mate?” I looked over my shoulder, and immediately perked up at the alarmed look on his face.

He slowed, then stopped entirely, reaching out to grab Victoria’s shoulder. “Those people up on the wall. They’re moving a lot now, looking rather agitated.”

I looked back up and instantly paused in my steps. Steve was, indeed, right. More tiny heads appeared and disappeared in quick succession. No sound, weirdly. I figured we were close enough that we’d have been able to hear it if they were panicked enough to be yelling. I figured I might as well reach out, get a chat going. “Ahoy the walls. How are we on this fine day?”

Silence. Not a damn thing. Seconds ticked by. Then, finally, a head appeared above the walls. And death rained down on me.
