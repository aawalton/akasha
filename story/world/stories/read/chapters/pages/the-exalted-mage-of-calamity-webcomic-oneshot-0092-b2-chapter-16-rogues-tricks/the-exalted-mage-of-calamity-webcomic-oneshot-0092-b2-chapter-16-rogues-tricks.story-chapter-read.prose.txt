“My… life?” Dusk whispered.

Gwen’s hand tightened around his shoulder. “Dusk, no. It’s trying to trick you. He’ll kill you.”

Despite her words, Dusk didn’t seem to hear her. His eyes were fixed on the rift beside Hevestra, staring at the half-ethereal body imprisoned in shadow, drained so thoroughly that her outline looked ready to scatter.

Hevestra didn’t miss it. Instead, its smile widened at the sight.

“Yes,” the demon said softly. “Your life for hers. A generous offer from a demon such as myself, wouldn’t you agree? She is nearly spent, but not quite dead. A cracked vessel, one I am willing to trade.” Its gaze dragged over Dusk’s body with open greed. “You are far better suited to her position. Perhaps with your spiritual energy, I’ll finally achieve my goal.”

“Your goal?” Veronica asked.

Hevestra chuckled, its gaze briefly flicking toward her. “Demon matters. Nothing you insignificant mortals need to worry about.”

“How about you fuck off,” Gwen snapped. She turned around, glaring at Hevestra. “Don’t act like we’re stupid. You probably want to take Dusk and then kill the rest of us. There is no trade here. You think after killing that girl, he’s going to take your offer?”

“Well said,” Veronica chimed.

While they took the opportunity to speak with the demon, Veronica used the pause to refill her reserves. Her mana regeneration was impressive, but casting several Tier-3 spells in a row, along with maintaining barriers, would drain her quickly.

“Even demons have honor, you know,” Hevestra said. “I’ll even spare the others if you wish.” It laid out its clawed hand, pointing toward Dusk. “So? Is it a deal?”

Dusk opened his mouth, but no words came out. His body trembled, hesitant to make a decision.

“Enough,” Veronica said.

She raised a hand, gathering mana within her body.

“We won’t listen to any of your deals. We know what kind of demon you are. Either you kill all of us by force and take him, or we kill you. There isn’t much else to it.”

Hevestra’s eyes slid back toward her.

With a wave of its hand, the rift closed.

“Very well. This scenario works as well. Just a minor hindrance.”

Veronica launched a large ball of fire at the demon as she kicked off the ground, rushing forward. She prepared another flurry of spells, launching them as she moved.

Hevestra moved at the same time, blurring across the rising steam that had already begun to dissipate. The flames exploded around it, dispersing into the air above. However, the damage was insignificant, and Hevestra flew out from the fire moments later.

“Let’s see how long you can entertain me, mage.”

As the two of them resumed their fight, Gwen tried her best to console Dusk.

“Dawn…” he said softly.

“Dusk. Who is that girl? Why does that demon have her?” Gwen asked.

“She’s… my sister.” His eyes longed for her, as if he couldn’t even see Gwen standing in front of him. “I need to save her.”

“Dusk. You can’t,” Gwen said, looking him in the eyes. “If you do that, you’ll be falling straight into its trap. I don’t know why it needs you, but it won’t leave us alone if it gets you. And with your sister like that… I don’t know if she can be saved. You need to understand that.”

“She’s right,” Nasha added, steadying her breathing. “We can’t trust that demon. Everything it’s saying could be a lie to trick us.”

Nasha’s gaze shifted toward the shadows within the rising mist. She watched them fight. More specifically, she watched how Hevestra moved.

There was something off about its movements whenever the steam was thick. But now, as the mist dispersed, that strangeness was gone. Almost as if the demon’s eyes weren’t as sharp as a human’s. And yet, it could still detect Veronica through the steam.

“She’s… not dead,” Dusk said softly.

Gwen’s brows furrowed. “Dusk. She was only half a body in there. I couldn’t even see her legs. How could she still be alive like that?”

He didn’t say anything else. He simply kept staring ahead, toward the spot where the rift had been before Hevestra closed it.

“I can still save her…” Dusk said.

“It’s too risky,” Nasha said. “Even if you want to save her, we need to kill that demon first. But right now, things aren’t looking too good. I’m not sure Veronica can defeat it on her own.”

Nasha pulled the last arrow from her quiver, holding it in her palm. “I only have one shot left.” She frowned, looking toward Gwen. “And I’m afraid of what’ll happen to me once I use it, considering I’m only conscious right now because of your magic.”

Dusk clenched his fingers. “Is… is she strong?” he asked.

Gwen stared at him. “Veronica? Yes. She’s strong. But this fight isn’t going in our favor—”

“No. Is she… going to become strong? One of the strongest humans?” His voice trembled slightly. “She… promised me she would look for Selene in the future.”

Gwen’s eyes widened. She briefly looked toward Nasha, then back at him.

“Yes,” she replied. “Veronica is going to be one of the strongest humans in the future. If… we don’t all die here, that is.”

Dusk opened his mouth to speak, but the words froze in his throat. His fingers clenched tighter, and his expression grew complicated. “If… if we can save Dawn… I think you can beat that demon.”

Nasha narrowed her eyes at him. “What do you mean? Is she that strong?”

He shook his head. “No. Dawn is… not like that anymore.” There was a deep sadness in his eyes as the words left his mouth. “But… if you bring her to me… I can give you what you’re looking for.”

“Looking for?” Gwen asked.

“…Selene’s Tear.”

Both Gwen and Nasha froze.

That was the one thing they had come into the valley searching for. The treasure Veronica wanted to obtain. The artifact that, according to Veronica, could greatly increase a person’s power.

And yet, Dusk was the one who had it all along?

“Just making sure…” Gwen said slowly. “Are you saying we need Dawn for it, or that you’ll only give it to us if we save her?”

“I… I need Dawn to give it to you.”

Something about his expression didn’t sit right with them. But since he was offering a way to beat the demon, there wasn’t much else they could argue over.

“Okay,” Gwen said. “But… how are we going to rescue her? That demon closed the portal. We’d have to sacrifice Dusk.”

“…Maybe not,” Nasha said slowly.

Her eyes remained fixed on the fight ahead. Despite the explosions and shockwaves, she kept her focus.

Each time Dusk’s aura shuddered, protecting them from the impacts, Nasha noticed Hevestra would briefly glance toward them.

Something was drawing its attention.

Perhaps…

“I have a plan,” she said.

She turned toward Dusk, her face serious.

“Do you still have that thing from the village?”

Back near the center ruins of the sanctuary, Veronica and Hevestra clashed again and again.

Shadow hands were pierced by spears of light. Corroding miasma was scattered by sharp gusts of wind. Direct frontal attacks were stopped by a barrier of mana.

“This is truly odd,” Hevestra said. “The spells you are using are all quite weak. And yet, your mana doesn’t seem to be running out.”

It raised one clawed hand, swirling a mass of darkness above it. With a swipe, it sent the mass barreling down toward Veronica.

“Weak?” Veronica repeated.

She raised her own palm, summoning a wall of light in front of her.

The demonic energy crashed against the light barrier, different from her regular mana shield. The light grew brighter, absorbing the darkness as it tried to smother it. Then, with a clench of Veronica’s palm, the barrier broke apart, sending giant shards of light flying straight back toward the demon.

Hevestra reverted into its shadow form, narrowly dodging the large chunks of mana that shot back toward it. Unfortunately, most of the steam had dissipated, restoring its movements to normal.

“How is it that you have so much mana?” Hevestra asked, reaching out from the shadows above. “Perhaps I should leave you alive as well.”

Veronica let out a heavy breath, her chest rising and falling.

She was already using some Tier-4 spells, and yet she still couldn’t deal enough damage for it to matter. Its physical abilities were enhanced by aura. Its demonic energy was nearly limitless. And on top of that, it had mana barriers to block or weaken her more powerful spells.

Casting a Tier-5 spell was also impossible. She needed concentration, focus, and time to form one. There was also the need to conserve enough mana for a barrier in case it attacked suddenly.

Unlike aura-users, mages could be killed by a single unguarded strike.

“Veronica!”

Veronica flinched, looking to her left.

It was Nasha.

“Veronica! Can you do that steam explosion again?” she shouted.

She certainly had enough mana for it. However, it would leave her nearly drained.

“I can! But—”

“I need you to do it! We can defeat the demon! Trust me!” Nasha shouted.

“Hooh?” Hevestra said, clearly listening to their battle plan. “I do wonder how that same attack will defeat me.”

It pointed along its rib cage.

“That wound from earlier has already healed.”

With another raise of its claws, it summoned another wave of demonic miasma, this time sending it toward Nasha.

Before the wave could reach her, a massive torrent of flames intercepted it, smothering the darkness entirely.

Hevestra stared back at Veronica.

“Don’t drag the others into this,” Veronica shouted. “It’s just you and me!”

Sweat dripped down her face. Only half of her hair was still levitating, and the glow in her eyes had grown dim. Even so, she continued fighting with her dwindling reserves.

The torrent of flames surged forward, spiraling back toward Hevestra, followed by the channel of wind Veronica summoned. In the next moment, the air flashed as a large block of ice appeared within the eye of the storm.

Another explosion of steam shook the sanctuary, blasting loose rubble and debris straight off the platform to crash near the base below. The air filled again with dense mist, completely covered by the steam from the blast.

Hevestra crashed through the air deeper into the steam. It slowed, shaking its head and waving away the mist in front of it. It growled, looking left and right for the mage.

After a moment, it spotted her despite the poor visibility.

Veronica wasn’t moving. Instead, she stood there heaving with exhaustion.

“Heh. Looks like you’re all out of energy.”

Hevestra clasped both clawed hands together in front of itself, forming a compressed ball of demonic energy. With a thrust, it sent the attack flying through the steam, straight toward where Veronica stood.

A moment later, an explosion occurred.

In Hevestra’s eyes, Veronica’s body flew across the ground, stopping only when she crashed abruptly into what was likely a pile of stone rubble.

“Now, with one problem gone…” it rasped.

“Dusk!”

Hevestra turned its head sharply.

It was the voice of the other mage. The one that had used magic to dispel its nightmares.

“Dusk! Don’t! Come back!”

Through the steam, Hevestra noticed it. The boy was running forward without the others.

“Kekehehe!” Hevestra chuckled.

It flew down rapidly, summoning a dome of shadows to encase the boy before it could run any further.

With a raise of its other hand, the rift containing Dawn opened. “You made a good choice, boy,” Hevestra said, slowing down as they approached the dome. Reaching forward, Hevestra dispelled the dome, hoping to grab the boy and toss him into the rift.

However—

“What?”

As the dome disappeared, something fell to the ground. A doll in a blue dress.

Strangely, it was one imbued with spiritual energy. But behind it…

An arrow—one with condensed aura.

“You puny in—”

An explosion sounded as the arrow finally detonated. Hevestra was thrown far away from the blast.

With the opportunity open, Nasha dashed through the steam, now partially cleared by the arrow’s explosion. She covered her eyes against the smoke and ran toward the dark speck. As she reached the open rift, she quickly drew a spare dagger and slashed apart the shadows chaining Dawn within.

The shadows began reforming, so Nasha simply slashed faster.

She reached deeper into the rift and grabbed Dawn’s wrist. The void burned her skin the moment she crossed its threshold, cold and searing all at once, but she didn’t pull back. Gwen’s magic was focused almost entirely on keeping her stamina from collapsing. Injuries could be handled later.

Though she hadn’t expected demonic energy to hurt so much.

The shadows inside were alive. The moment Nasha’s arm entered, they lunged for her. One wrapped around her dagger and tried to wrench it from her grip. Another crawled up her forearm, tightening like a black serpent.

Nasha gritted her teeth and cut through it.

A third shadow snapped around the edge of her leather chestguard and pulled. Her body lurched, her head nearly dragged past the point of no return.

“D-damn it…” she growled.

“Nasha!” Gwen shouted from far away. The steam was too thick, so neither side could see each other. “Are you okay?”

Rather than fight the pull, Nasha drove her dagger beneath the strap and cut it loose. The damaged outer guard tore away, vanishing into the rift as the shadows dragged it into the dark. This left her only in her cropped undershirt, shoulders and sides exposed.

Nasha didn’t even bother looking down. She reached in farther, finally grabbing hold of Dawn’s arms.

The shadows shrieked without sound, lashing across her arms, ribs, and back as she dragged Dawn out of the prison. Each strike left another blackened burn across her skin, but Nasha kept moving, teeth bared.

Then, with one final pull, Dawn came free.

Nasha tumbled backward, holding Dawn in her arms.

She fell onto her back, but a shadow was still wrapped around her leg.

Nasha grunted, took her nearly decayed dagger, and slashed at the fastening strap of her shinguard. Her leg came free as the shadows dragged the armor into the abyss.

With a huff, Nasha sat up and looked at Dawn.

She felt like ice that had turned to air. She was too light; her body was like that of cotton.

Nasha couldn’t feel a pulse or heartbeat. Not even the subtle movement of breathing.

But none of that mattered.

Nasha forced her legs to stand, hoisting Dawn over her shoulder. She turned away and began heading back toward Dusk and Gwen.

However, it didn’t take long for Hevestra to recover.

“You… you filthy ingrates…” its voice echoed through the steam.
