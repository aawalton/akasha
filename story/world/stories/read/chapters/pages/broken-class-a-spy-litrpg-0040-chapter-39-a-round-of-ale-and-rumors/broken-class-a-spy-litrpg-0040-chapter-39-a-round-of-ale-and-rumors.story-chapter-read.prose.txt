The heavy scent of dried herbs and tanned leather from The Beast's Bounty clung to them as they stepped back out into the bustling street. The team’s mood was light, buoyed by the satisfying clink of coin and the successful offloading of their hard-won spoils. Gunnar, their brawling frontman, pocketed the earnings with a broad grin.

"Alright, lads," Gunnar declared, his mood instantly improved by the clink of coin. "To The Rusty Flagon! First round of ale is on me. We earned it."

The others cheered. Arthur, wearing the face and name of Zane, simply nodded, his mind already moving past the shop, the coins, and onto the next phase of his plan. The tavern would be loud, full of loose tongues and valuable information.

The Rusty Flagon was exactly as advertised: a sprawling, low-ceilinged room thick with the smell of stale ale, roasting meat, and the sweat of tired hunters. Smoke from cheap tallow candles and the occasional enchanted pipe curled in the air, casting a hazy gloom over the rough-hewn wooden tables. It was the kind of place where stories were embellished, and blades were kept close. Gunnar navigated the throng with the ease of a regular, slapping backs and trading gruff greetings as he led them to a secluded booth in the back.

Anya, the barmaid, spotted them from across the room and began to weave her way through the chaos. She moved with a quick gait, her sturdy frame navigating the crowded floor without a wasted step. She was a woman of average height, with strong shoulders and arms developed from years of hauling heavy trays and casks of ale. As she approached, the wry smirk that seemed permanently etched on her face deepened. Her eyes, sharp and observant, took in the state of their gear and the weariness in their postures.

"The usual, Gunnar? Or did you finally kill something worth celebrating?" she asked, her voice carrying easily over the din.

"The usual, Anya. And five bowls of your best stew," Gunnar shot back, tossing a silver coin onto her tray, which she caught with hands noticeably calloused from her work. "And keep the ale coming."

As the team settled in, their conversation turning to the day's hunt and the meager profits, Arthur remained silent. He took a long drink of the bitter ale when it arrived, letting the noise of the tavern wash over him. But he wasn't just listening; he was filtering. His mana-enhanced hearing allowed him to pick out individual threads of conversation from the chaotic tapestry of sound.

He caught his own name, his real one, muttered at a nearby table. "...some ghost named Arthur. Came out of nowhere, topped the leaderboard, and vanished. Bet he was a cheat." The gossip was fleeting, already old news in a city that moved on quickly.

More interesting was the conversation from a table of minor nobles a few feet away, their voices low and laced with disdain.

"...the princess is a fool," one of them sneered. "Ysara plays at being a royal but has no real backing. She rejects powerful alliances, surrounds herself with common-born guards... I even heard a rumor she wastes her mana healing slaves down in the lower districts."

Another noble scoffed. "Compassion is a weakness. She'll learn that soon enough. Without the great houses behind her, she's a queen with no kingdom."

Arthur processed the information, a flicker of something unexpected stirring within him. He had seen Ysara as just another piece of the oppressive machinery, a royal born into a system built on suffering. But this painted a different picture. A rebellious princess, isolated from her own class, wasting her precious power on the powerless? It didn't fit his neat assessment. A seed of doubt was planted, a question mark next to her name in his mental ledger. He still didn't trust her, but his perception of her had fractured, just slightly.

The stew arrived, thick and hearty, and Arthur ate with a hunger that was more than just physical. It was the first warm, real meal he’d had since his rebirth, and the simple act felt like a victory in itself.

When the last of the ale was gone and the bowls were scraped clean, Gunnar pushed himself up from the table with a satisfied groan. "Well, lads, as much as I enjoy your ugly mugs, I've got business of a more... delicate nature." He shot them a lecherous grin. "The Silk Pillow calls to me."

Torvin grunted in amusement while Elara rolled her eyes. "Don't bring back any diseases this time, Gunnar."

"No promises!" he laughed.

Arthur saw his opening. "I've got an errand of my own to run," he said, his tone flat and noncommittal.

Gunnar’s grin widened, and he nudged Arthur with his elbow. "An 'errand,' is it? Good on you, Zane! A man has needs." The others chuckled, a knowing smirk passing between them. The assumption was clear, and it was the perfect cover.

Arthur simply shrugged and stood, parting ways with the team as they spilled out into the night. He watched them go their separate ways before turning down a side street, the sounds of the tavern fading behind him. He saw a lone slave hurrying through the shadows, carrying a bundle of laundry. Arthur stepped into his path, his face a mask of weary indifference. The slave flinched, eyes wide with fear, but Arthur kept his voice low and calm. "Where can a man get a hot bath around here?"

The slave, trembling, pointed a shaky finger down the street. "Two blocks down, then left at the blacksmith's," he whispered, before scurrying away without another word. Following the directions, Arthur soon found himself standing before The Stone Basin. He paid the tired-looking slave at the entrance and stepped into the men’s section. The steam was a welcome embrace. He found a secluded corner, undressed quickly, and sank into the hot, mineral-rich water with a sigh that was almost genuine.

As he soaked, letting the heat seep into his bones, he kept his ears open. The bathhouse was another hub of gossip, and the conversations here were much the same as in the tavern.

"...heard the princess was ambushed in the woods. Serves her right for trusting commoners over proper knights."

"She's too soft. My cousin is a palace guard, says she sneaks out to heal sick slaves. Wasting mana on the worthless."

Hearing the same rumors from two different circles, first the disdainful nobles and then the gossiping hunters in the bathhouse, solidified something in Arthur's mind. The nobles scorned her for her compassion, and the common folk whispered about it with a mix of awe and disbelief. She was an anomaly, a disruption to the city's cruel logic. She was either a naive fool or something far more dangerous to the established order.

He decided then and there. It was time to find out which.

He would give the princess a chance. He would talk to her, truly talk to her, and see what lay beneath the title and the rumors. If she was genuine, she could be a powerful, if unpredictable, asset.

And if she wasn't? If it was all a political game? He could always vanish. With the Mask of Infinite Faces, he could become a ghost in the wind, leaving Zane and this entire chapter of his life behind forever. The thought was a comforting safety net. He had nothing to lose but time.
