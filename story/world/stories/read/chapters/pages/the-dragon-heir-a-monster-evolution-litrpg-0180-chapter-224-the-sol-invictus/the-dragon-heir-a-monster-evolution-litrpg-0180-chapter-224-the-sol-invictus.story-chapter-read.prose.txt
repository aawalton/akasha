Serath flew.

His wings pumped hard against the ash-choked air, every beat carrying him and the remnants of his command staff back toward the Fold's inner layers. Around him, the surviving Kovrath forces streamed in disciplined retreat, the companies were collapsing into squads, squads funneling through the layered defensive checkpoints, each barrier sealing behind them with a hiss of sacred energy. It was a maneuver they had drilled for centuries and never once expected to use. Retreat was not in their nature. It had never needed to be.

And yet not a single attack pursued them.

His precognition screamed with every wingbeat. Every instinct honed over decades of combat told him he was about to die, that a spatial blade would materialize in his spine, that a metal spear would punch through his ribs, that the creeping rot of the Nature demigod's domain would finally catch up to him. Turning your back on an enemy like the Depth Dwellers was suicide.

But his precognition didn't account for her.

His hand drifted to his chest as he flew, claws pressing against the scales above his heart. The phantom sensation still lingered. That terrible, intimate grip, fingers of pure magical force closing around the core of him like a child squeezing a piece of fruit. He had been seconds from death. And not the honorable death of a warrior falling to a superior foe, but just… execution.

She could have killed him. She could have killed all of them. And the terrifying thing was how little effort it would have cost her.

They passed through the final barrier and into the first civilian layer. The chaos of the outer Fold gave way to something taut with a different kind of tension. The air here still carried the faint sweetness of the shaderoot trees, though it was undercut now by the acrid bite of fear. The civilians had been preparing for breach for days. Everyone within the Fold trained from childhood, there were no true noncombatants among the Kovraths, only those who had been selected for the army and those who hadn't. The distinction was one of specialization, not capability. Every able body was a warrior when the situation demanded it.

And the situation was demanding it.

The secondary defensive line stretched across the inner courtyard in a living wall of dark scales. These were the reserves, the too-young, the too-old, the ones whose classes had been deemed auxiliary rather than frontline. Healers with combat staves. Craftsmen with reinforced tools. Hunters whose prey had been beasts rather than soldiers, but whose claws were sharp regardless. They had formed up in disciplined ranks, ready to die defending their homes, their families, the sacred shrines that had sustained their people for millennia.

They were staring outside now.

Through the barrier, that strange, one-way membrane of shadow that looked like impenetrable darkness from outside but resolved into something like smoked glass from within, the entire inner courtyard could see what was happening on the battlefield they had just fled.

And what they saw had turned every face to pale stone.

Serath landed on the command platform and turned to look back. He already knew what he would see. His precognition had stopped screaming the moment they crossed the barrier because whatever was happening out there no longer posed a threat to him.

But knowing and seeing were different things. He looked. And he understood why his people were silent.

The battlefield where thousands of Depth Dwellers had raged moments ago was gone. In its place was something that resembled a butcher's floor more than a war zone.

The golden dragon's three replicas moved through what remained of the army like a hurricane of blades. That was the only comparison Serath's mind could conjure. They were a force of nature, reducing everything in its path to component pieces. The replicas weren't casting spells or channeling mana. Their claws had elongated into grotesque, beautiful arcs of gold, longer than their bodies, and the sharp bones at the edges of their tentacles had extended into massive scythe-like appendages that swept through the Depth Dwellers' ranks with no more resistance than a scythe through wheat.

It was pure physical force and nothing else. Just mass and velocity and edges honed to an impossible sharpness, wielded with a speed that made Serath's eyes ache to track. The replicas laughed as they worked with a manic, joyful sound that carried across the distance and through the barrier like a song.

The Depth Dwellers had no answer. Their coordination, that perfect, hive-minded synchronization that had made them so deadly, meant nothing against opponents that moved faster than thought. Their weapons shattered on golden scales. Their spells fizzled and died before they could form. And the replicas never stopped moving and never stopped killing.

The demigods tried to respond. Serath could see them struggling against the tide, their immense power lashing out in increasingly desperate attempts to stem the slaughter.

The Space demigod wove spatial compression around one of the replicas, attempting to shunt her into oblivion. The golden dragon's main body, still standing motionless in the center of the battlefield, still watching with those swirling violet eyes, didn't even gesture. The compression collapsed. The Space demigod's trident trembled in his grip as he tried again. Collapsed again. Every attempt, every distortion, every rift he tried to open simply... failed. Like the universe itself had stopped listening to him.

The Metal demigod lunged with all his Hunt-infused might, a thousand blades screaming toward the replicas from a thousand angles. The main body tilted her head, just slightly, and the blades dissolved. They came apart into their constituent motes of mana and then into nothing at all, as if the concept of "blade" had been revoked from the reality around her. The Metal demigod's entire body was wreathed in his Hunt aspect, his warform at full power, and it didn't matter. The aura guttered and died the moment it touched the sphere of influence radiating from the golden dragon at the center.

The Nature demigod's flora couldn't even encroach upon the land where she stood. His vines withered at the boundary. His spores turned to ash. His domain, that creeping, rotting garden that had spread across the battlefield like a cancer, stopped at an invisible line and could advance no further. The golden dragon hadn't moved. She hadn't cast anything visible. She was simply... denying them. Canceling whatever they attempted, the moment they attempted it. Like their magic was a child's drawing and she was the tide, washing it clean without effort and acknowledgment, without even seeming to notice it had been there.

Her eyes were just fixed on the three demigods with an expression that Serath could now clearly identify.

Hunger.

She was waiting. Letting the replicas handle the chaff and letting the demigods exhaust themselves against her passive denial, while making them understand, slowly and then all at once, exactly how outmatched they were.

It took less than a few minutes.

Serath watched, as he was unable to look away, as the last of the Depth Dweller army was reduced to strips of meat. Thousands of warriors. Thousands of mind-controlled, suicidally aggressive, perfectly coordinated killing machines that had pushed his people to the brink of extinction.

And now there was only flesh and scattered across the shattered stone. Steam rising from the warmth of bodies that had been whole seconds ago. The replicas stood amidst the carnage, chests heaving with something that looked more like exhilaration than exertion, their elongated claws dripping gold and red in equal measure.

The three demigods remained. Arrayed before the golden dragon's main body, their tridents raised with defiance. But their coordination had fractured and their confidence had cracked. Even from this distance, through the barrier, Serath could see the hesitation in their stances.

They had been hunters their entire existence. Predators at the apex of a brutal food chain. They had never been prey before so they didn't know how to stand like prey.

But their bodies were now learning.

The main body finally moved. Her head tilted to one side, and Serath watched her lips curl back from terrible fangs.

Serath couldn't look away.

The replicas vanished. Only the main body remained, hovering above the scorched stone, her four wings spread wide.

And then the dead began to bleed.

It started slowly. First a trickle here and a rivulet there. Thousands of Depth Dweller corpses, along with dead Kovraths, suddenly started weeping crimson in perfect unison. The blood moved, crawling upward against gravity, tracing paths that formed angles and curves and intersections, spreading across the battlefield like ink through veins.

A rune was forming. Then two, until an entire spell circle was taking shape.

Serath's honed mind tried desperately to parse what he was seeing. The scale of it defied comprehension. The scale of it defied comprehension. This was a working painted across an entire battlefield, fueled by the lifeblood of an army, its outermost edges stretching into dimensions even his sharpened eyes couldn’t follow.

His precognition howled.

"Raise the barriers!" The command tore from his throat before conscious thought could catch up. "Every shield! Every ward! Every scrap of sacred energy you possess— USE IT NOW!"

His people moved. They had been staring in frozen horror, but his voice cut through that paralysis. Across the inner courtyard, mages and warriors alike channeled their sacred energy into the layered defenses. Barrier after barrier flickered to life, overlapping and reinforcing, the accumulated protection of an entire civilization started pouring into a single desperate bulwark.

He tried to divine what the spell circle was. Tried to trace its purpose through the threads of fate. The answer came back as a single, blinding impression: Solar. Purification. Serath severed the connection instantly. Too late.

Pure white flames erupted across his eyes, burning through the very channels he had used to glimpse the spell's purpose. He forced his regeneration into overdrive, healing the damage before it could spread any further.

The golden dragon rose higher, ascending through the ash-choked air until she hung above the battlefield like a second sun. The blood-rune beneath her pulsed once and then began to glow in the colour of sunrise. The colour of the moment before something vast and terrible crests the horizon.

When she spoke, her voice was not loud. But it still carried across the battlefield and through the barriers and into the bones of every living creature present, like a resonance that bypassed ears entirely and spoke directly to the oldest parts of the soul.

"I have been called many things across my rather small life." Her wings stretched wider, and the air around her began to shimmer. "The Doomwing. The Sol Invictus. The Overlord. I am the one who once ascended beyond the Firmament itself and found the Sun waiting. I faced it. I challenged it. And when the struggle ended, when the solar fires had burned away everything I was and reforged me into something new, I devoured its Authority and made it my own."

The temperature spiked. Serath felt the heat through layers of sacred barriers, felt sweat bead and evaporate on his scales in the same instant. The corpses on the battlefield were burning. Each body igniting like a candle, flames rising from the scattered flesh in perfect geometric patterns that fed the growing spell circle with their incineration.

She raised one claw, and a spark kindled at its tip.

"I am the embodiment of burning solar light. I am the flare that turns worlds to glass. When I once walked among the stars, my coming was announced by storms of fire that reshaped continents and boiled oceans to steam. The mortals simply fled— and even that was not enough."

The spark became a flame. The flame became a conflagration. And the conflagration began to spin.

"Let me give you a glimpse."

The winds answered her.

It started as a whisper, like a stirring of heated air. Then it built. Hot wind screamed across the scorched battlefield, whipping ash and embers into spiraling vortices that caught the light and blazed like captive stars. The spell circle below her blazed to full illumination, runes and sigils igniting in sequence, and Serath had to avert his gaze because looking at them directly hurt his very core. The runes were not written in any script he knew.

The wind became a gale. The gale became a tempest. And the tempest became something that Serath could only describe as the breath of an angry star.

The three demigods stood at the edge of the spell circle, their tridents raised with defiance. But they were trembling. Serath could see the fine vibration in their limbs, the way their grips kept adjusting on their weapons. They were demigods. They were mind-controlled extensions of the Water Leviathan's will, and that will was absolute.

But even absolute will had limits. Even mind control couldn't fully suppress the primal instinct that was now screaming through every cell of their bodies.

The golden dragon looked down at them.

"Your next action should not require conscious deliberation," she said. "Listen to what your primal brain is telling you. Listen to the part of you that remembers what it was like to be prey, before you grew claws and forgot your place. That voice? That screaming, desperate, animal terror clawing at the inside of your skull?"

She smiled.

"Listen to it."

Her wings folded.

"RUN!"

And the storm fully descended.

It was the pure concept of heat given motion and intent. A spiraling cyclone of solar fury that screamed across the battlefield with a sound like reality itself being torn apart. The corpses were vaporized in an instant, their component matter sublimating directly from solid to nothing, and the demigods caught the full brunt of it before they could even process the command they'd been given.

The Space demigod moved first. His trident blazed as he tried to open a rift, tried to teleport himself and his companions out of the kill zone. The magic formed and then died, snuffed out like a candle in a hurricane. A massive golden claw materialized from the heart of the storm and tore his hand off at the wrist. The trident spun away into the inferno. The Space demigod screamed a sound of pain and primal, overwhelming terror, and then a fist caught him in the face with enough force to crater the stone beneath him.

"Teleportation?" The dragon's voice was a laughing snarl. "I told you to use your legs, insect. Did I stutter? Did my pronunciation lack clarity? RUN! On those pathetic, underdeveloped, entirely superfluous appendages you call legs! RUN!"

The Space demigod ran.

The Hunt demigod was already moving, his lithe silver form blurring across the scorched ground with the speed of pure survival instinct. Whatever mind control bound him, whatever leash the Water Leviathan had wrapped around his will, it didn't matter anymore.

He didn't make it far.

The dragon caught him mid-stride, golden claws longer than his entire body shearing through his warform's armored plates like they were wet paper. She tore into him with blurring speed with a flurry of strikes too fast to track, each one removing a piece of him. A forearm. A shoulder. The jagged metallic fins along his spine. She dismantled him in motion, piece by piece, her laughter ringing through the storm.

His head came free last. She held it up, claws digging into his skull, and for one terrible moment his eyes met hers. He was aware, comprehending and utterly broken.

And then she tore his face off. The storm cooked what remained. Yet she still left him alive.

The Nature demigod got it worst. Serath understood why immediately: where the others were beings of metal and space, of concepts that could theoretically escape or endure, the Nature demigod was life. He was growth and vitality and the eternal cycle of decay and renewal. But solar fire was the endpoint of all growth. The terminus of all vitality.

His own mana was his undoing. The spores and vines and blooming rot that had made him so dangerous, that had spread across the battlefield and nearly choked the life from the Kovrath army as they fed the flames. They were fuel. Every scrap of nature magic he channeled to defend himself only made the inferno around him burn hotter, and he couldn't stop channeling because the alternative was dying, and the channeling was also killing him, and he was screaming—

The dragon caught him. Pinned him to the scorched stone with one claw. The storm raged around them, but in that small space, there was almost stillness.

"Ah." Her voice was soft again. "You drew the short straw, didn't you? Nature against Sol. Growth against the furnace. You never had a chance."

She crouched down. Her claws glowed with a gentle light that was nothing like the killing fire around them.

And she healed him.

The Nature demigod's wounds closed. His burned flesh knitted. His ruptured channels mended. His body, which had been seconds from death, was suddenly whole again. The relief in his eyes was almost heartbreaking and bewildered hope.

"RUN!" she screamed, and her voice was joy and rage and something genuinely unhinged. "RUN! WHY AREN'T YOU RUNNING!"

She punched him. Gutted him. Ate pieces of him while he was still alive and cooked and screaming, and when he was on the verge of death again, she healed him. Again. Let him run. Caught him. Burned him. Healed him. Screamed at him to run faster, to try harder, to make it interesting.

She did the same to all of them. The three demigods who had terrorized the Fold for a week, those who had pushed a Gold Core diviner and his entire army to the brink of extinction. They were being hunted like prey by something that wasn't even trying to kill them quickly but instead was sadistically savoring every moment of their terror.

Serath looked away. He couldn't watch anymore. Pity for the Depth Dwellers was the last thing he wanted to feel, and yet it rose in his throat like bile anyway. No creature deserved this. No matter what they had done. No matter what they would have done.

The barriers held against the storm, but barely. Every mage and warrior in the inner layer was pouring their sacred energy into the shields, and even then, the heat that bled through was almost unbearable. Serath could feel his people trembling from terror around him.

This was not the same dragon that had been sealed a week ago. There was something fundamentally different about her now.

The solar winds hammered against their shields. The screaming continued. Serath stopped keeping notes on what was happening outside. Some knowledge was not worth the cost of witnessing it.

He didn't know how long it lasted. Time had stopped meaning anything.

And then, there was silence.

The winds died. The heat ebbed. The terrible golden light that had turned the world outside into an approximation of hell slowly, gradually subsided. Serath raised his head. Around him, his kin were doing the same, their expressions uniformly haunted, their scales still radiating residual terror that would probably never fully fade.

The battlefield was gone. What remained was a landscape of fused glass and cooling slag, still glowing faintly at the edges and radiating heat that shimmered in the air like a mirage. The corpses were gone. The spell circle was gone. The demigods—

Serath didn't look for the demigods. He didn't want to know.

A figure stood at the edge of the barrier. She was not a dragon. A Vrathi— or the dragon wearing the shape of one. Golden hair cascaded down her back. Her skin was vibrant, almost luminous, and the clothes she wore were woven from fading ash and dying embers. The garment shifted and crumbled even as he watched, perpetually on the verge of dissolving. Her eyes were the same swirling violet, and they were looking directly at him.

She raised a hand and knocked politely on the barrier. The sound was absurdly mundane.

She seemed to be muttering to herself. Serath caught fragments of it, something about "WHAT THE ACTUAL FUCK DO YOU MEAN YOU OVERCOOKED IT?" something about "WHAT DO YOU MEAN GETTING CARRIED AWAY YOU SOLAR WHORE?!?" something that sounded suspiciously like “YEAH BECAUSE I'M THE ONE LEFT DEALING WITH THIS MESS NOW!”

She knocked again. Politely.

And she waited.
