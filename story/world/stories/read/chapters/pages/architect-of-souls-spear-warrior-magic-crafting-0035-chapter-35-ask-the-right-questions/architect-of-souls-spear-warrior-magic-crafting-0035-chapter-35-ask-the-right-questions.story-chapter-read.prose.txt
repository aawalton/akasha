Back at Lily House, Ferris had about an hour to wait before dinner. He sat in the common room, sipping a cup of tea and watching Jade patrol the room. Mother Lily had pressed the tea on him after she noticed him, and it had a pleasant, soothing floral aroma. He was excited to cultivate, yet he needed a break and tried to simply enjoy it.

Sitting in the common room, Ferris had started to become accustomed to living at Lily House. It wasn’t home, but it was less strange. The people weren’t his family, but they were nice… well, except for Karl.

The thoughts made him homesick. It’d been almost two weeks since he had arrived at Scalefort Bastion. Before, he’d spent every day of his life with his family. The only good news was that it was almost time for his clan’s delvers to make a supply trip. Uncle Winton said he would volunteer for it, and Ferris would get to see him at least.

Dinner was served from a giant platter of rice, Mother Lily scooping each resident a heaping bowl. Mixed in were a variety of brightly colored chopped vegetables and strips of sautéed bird meat; apparently, it was back in stock. The whole dish was flavored with a salty, oily sauce.

It was filling, although yet another dish that was difficult to feed to Jade. Although that is mostly Jade’s fault, Ferris thought. The slime had started requesting he give them singular grains of rice to rate.

After the meal, Ferris and Jade settled into their room to cultivate. Ferris checked Jade’s memory to find the slime had forgotten the numbers. He noted the timing, which was in the middle of the recorded durations, with about ten hours.

“Jade,” Ferris said over the bond, closing his notebook, “do you remember my family name?”

“Charla,” the slime said, rippling happily at being able to answer. They hadn’t enjoyed admitting they had forgotten the numbers.

“Yep, like the pepper,” Ferris smiled at the slime.

“What’s a pepper?” Jade asked.

Ferris’ smile froze, turning to a frown.

“Charla is a pepper,” he said slowly. Jade swirled a bit, mirroring Ferris’ confusion.

With a few follow-up questions, he determined that Jade remembered answering the question, but not originally hearing the explanation. They just knew the word, but nothing about what it meant.

While it wasn’t great, at least it was a new piece of information. Ferris renewed the safety instructions, then applied more of the burn cream to his hand. The pain hadn’t bothered him as much that day. At this point, he didn’t even need the bandage.

That night, Ferris found it uncommonly easy to enter his soulspace, perhaps because he’d already done so four times that day. He called out for Jade to follow his mind, like they had for the gate, and entered his soul. Happily, the slime appeared with Ferris next to his Heartfire.

Stretching his mental body, Ferris considered the night’s goals. He wanted to harvest qi, construct new weapons, and check on the oath statue. The Air Passage likely had some qi, but if he collected from the river first, he could make a new spear beforehand. The slimespace wasn’t safe—especially whatever moved out in the ocean—but it had been safer than his soulspace so far.

Ferris filled the bucket half full of qi, and the other half with a length of demon chain, then created a new fishing chain. It was the same length as yesterday, which couldn’t reach the center of the river. Unfortunately, the bucket wasn’t large enough to make anything longer.

After a moment of thought, Ferris decided to make two chains and tie them together. There was a chance the connection would unravel, losing half his length in the process, but he decided to risk it. He made the second chain with a hook to make it a bit easier to connect, then added a couple awkward knots for good measure. Chains do not knot well.

Ferris and Jade took the chain—and the axe for protection—to the slimespace.

After climbing down the Heartfire tower, and then back up the tower that had the Core Tree and dark green crystal on top, Ferris inspected the black rock ground. The material looked and felt exactly like the pollution he had spent all day cleaning. This black rock was leveled out to form a ground floor in the slimespace, where in the spirit gems it was smaller chunks fused to the crystals. Ferris wanted to remove it, but he wasn’t sure if it was safe… and he had no tools to do so. This might be a project he did later, as he didn’t know the benefits. The risks of messing with the tree were too great.

Checking the tree, Ferris was excited to find that the last fruit had fully ripened. It was now a vibrant blue, plump and glowing.

Looking at Jade, Ferris thought about the last time he’d eaten a Core Tree fruit in front of the slime. In retrospect, Jade’s reaction was partially due to Ferris’ lack of communication. He was just used to the slime going along with anything he said or did.

Jade had shown more understanding of language since then. Ferris wasn’t quite ready to trust the slime with long-term planning, but he could explain himself more…

“Jade,” Ferris said, “the fruit from this tree is special.”

“Yes! Eat fruit!” Jade started bouncing around as usual.

“Do you know why it is special?” Ferris asked.

“I can feel it, feels delicious!” The slime continued to bounce.

“This fruit is something I need,” Ferris tried to imagine a piece of himself missing, and the fruit filling that hole. It wasn’t a perfect explanation, but it fit if you removed a hundred steps between now and the ability that would heal his leg.

Jade stopped bouncing at that, rolling back over to Ferris.

“Ferris is hurt?” The slime swirled and bubbled, rolling around him. Jade had no basis for long-term injury, as the slime could fully heal from anything short of death.

“Yes,” Ferris said, sitting down next to Jade and looking at his foot, which was fully present on his mental body.

“I lost my leg,” he pictured the day it happened. The monsters breaking through into the bastion from the Wild, everyone trying to run away, the grifwolf dragging him down.

Pain.

Confusion.

Then Uncle Winton’s face.

Being carried away. The healers, unable to save his leg. The months that followed, wallowing in grief and regret. The lowest point of his life… followed by his breakthrough.

“This fruit, it is a way to fix me,” he thought of the excitement when he became a cultivator, tempered by his loss. The praise of his family, and the recognition of his uncle, versus the scorn of jealous people asking why it happened to someone like him.

Uncle Winton had warned him the path would be hard, but that didn’t scare him. The man was right, it was hard: leaving home, facing monsters of the rifts, living surrounded by strangers. Yet, he had faced the hopelessness of no way forward, and lived through that. How could anything else stop him?

“It won’t help immediately, but it will help.” Ferris pulled Jade into his lap and held the slime close. He thought about his plans for the future: healing his leg, becoming a strong delver, feeding Jade lots of food, and then taking the slime home to Bluereef. Introducing Jade to his family. Being part of the community of people that raised him, jealous or not. Protecting and providing for them.

“Ferris needs fruit,” Jade rippled in his arms, “even if Ferris greedy and steal first fruit.”

Ferris chuckled at the memory—and Jade’s new use of the concept of ‘stealing’ from today’s lecture on the ownership of spirit gems.

Then, he looked at Jade in shock. Ferris had eaten the fruit days ago—five days, to be exact.

“You remember that?” he asked in disbelief.

Jade swirled a bit, but then responded. “Ferris greedy, important memory, decided to keep safe.”

“You can decide what to remember?” Ferris’ jaw dropped.

“Not everything,” Jade swirled a bit harder, but then stopped, “just important things.”

Ferris was about to ask why Jade had never mentioned this before… but he had never asked. He had treated it as a problem to solve himself, a mystery to unravel.

Instead of just… talking.

“Can you… tell me what you remember?”

Jade began with the slime ranch’s pit. They had eaten a magical berry and wanted to remember where they found it. Ferris had no idea how long ago it was. The memory was like a single moment in time, with a myriad of connected ideas and impressions that were hard for Ferris to follow.

Next was the memory of swallowing Ferris’ hand. Apparently, that had shocked Jade just as much as Ferris. Perhaps more.

Slimes naturally have no concept of self-control. Jade was used to eating anything and everything, but now Ferris wanted them to constantly not eat. The pain and shock of something Jade tried to eat suddenly being Ferris had deeply affected the slime.

The safety lecture wasn’t remembered at all, although the concept of Ferris’ body being a part of him was solidly part of the memory of eating his hand. Jade said the lecture was too complicated, with lots of unrelated rules.

The food rating game wasn’t a stored memory—apparently Jade just practiced it every chance they could, which kept it from ever being forgotten. Curiously, Jade also had no memory of eating the magical berry, yet still used it as the top of the scale.

When they got to the memory of eating Ferris’ shoe—apparently important enough to remember forever—he asked why Jade broke the rules.

“Greedy only have one foot, not need two shoes,” Jade answered, a bit sheepishly.

Ferris groaned, but couldn’t help chuckling a bit, deciding not to argue that Jade had eaten his right shoe, which he still had a foot for.

Jade remembered Mother Lily fondly, as well as both Brix and Cyrus. Karl was forgotten, as was Hemon, Serena, and Elder Harper. People who don’t feed Jade aren’t important, apparently.

The slime didn’t remember fighting the chain demons, or any of the tactical plans they had practiced.

Ferris’ tests with the numbers never came up—not important enough. He couldn’t help but agree. Jade did preserve a fond memory of listening to Ferris tell stories, but no details of the stories themselves.

Jade had no memory of dying, either to the fish or the chain demons. Either it was too painful to remember, too common to bother with, or impossible for some unknown reason. Ferris could never forget either of his deaths in the soulspace… the experience was seared into his mind. Briefly, he wondered if he would choose to remember them, given the chance to forget?

“If something is painful, do you remember it?” Ferris asked.

“Only if important,” Jade answered immediately.

Ferris smiled. Death wasn’t important, but Mother Lily’s cooking was… and eating his shoe.

“Jade,” Ferris asked, still holding the slime in his arms, “do you know why you forget things? I don’t choose what to remember, I remember everything.”

“Thoughts are used up unless protected,” Jade bubbled in displeasure, but then deflated with a little swirl, “but no idea why.”

“Well, just as we will fix my leg, we can work on fixing your memory.”

Ferris set Jade to the side and stood back up. He walked up to the ripe fruit, and carefully grasped it. The fruit came off the tree easily, as if it were just about to fall off.

Ferris looked down at the fruit, then back at Jade.

“We’ll do it together,” he said, then took a bite.
