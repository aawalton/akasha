Darian opened his eyes against the muted red light within the immersion coffin with a blink, reaching up to pull away the mask as the liquid within continued to drain, leaving residue across his skin. The lid remained transparent enough for him to see the room beyond it, and he gave himself time to flex his fingers to refamiliarize himself with his actual body. The neural band around his forehead was cool against his skin, and Darian reached up to remove it after several moments of readjustment, pushing it away as the coffin’s seal hissed and its surface slid away.

He braced his hands on the padded edge, took a breath, and pulled himself up a moment later.

“Welcome back, Young Master!” Alfred greeted him warmly, the household A.I.’s voice entering through the room’s hidden speakers. “Miss Katherine has already asked after you, owing to your promise to call her, based on her message. Shall I inform her that you have disconnected successfully?”

“Yeah, Alfred, thanks,” Darian said with a faint smile as he rolled his neck and shoulders and took a waiting towel from nearby, cleaning off his feet, legs, and torso before stepping naked from the pod and wrapping the towel around himself. “Tell Kay I’ll call her after I have a shower; this liquid is still on my skin.”

“Of course, sir,” Alfred said warmly. “Shall I prepare a meal as well?”

“Please,” Darian agreed. “Eggs Benny, if you can. Nothing too elaborate, though. I promised I’d be back in an hour of real time, and I don’t want to piss off Mortarius.”

“But of course, sir. I shall set an alarm. Enjoy your ablutions.”

Darian gave the room a thumbs-up and headed through his bedroom toward the adjoining bathroom, feeling strangely well-rested and revitalized despite the time he’d spent in Eternus. Between the electrical pulses designed to stimulate muscle movement and the nutrient infusion the gel was supposed to deliver through his pores, alongside hydration, he felt as though he’d been in a comfortable sleep rather than mentally detached from reality.

When the hot water hit him two minutes later, he sighed in relief under the showerhead and scrubbed himself thoroughly. He’d be going back under soon enough, true, but he wanted to feel cleansed before he did—perhaps a silly need, but one he refused to relent on. It only took him five minutes, but he felt markedly better after the shower and brushing his teeth.

Darian left the bathroom after he was done, wearing a simple white tee with black sweatpants, and made his way to his desk. Food was already waiting for him, courtesy of Alfred, along with a pitcher of chilled mango juice, and he scarfed it down quickly while signing into his terminal and navigating to the flashing red icon informing him of a waiting message.

A call invitation appeared on the screen when his read receipt went through, and Darian accepted it while finishing his meal, wiping his mouth and grinning at Kate when she appeared on the screen—wearing a loose-fitting black shirt and a simple skirt, its details hidden below the thighs by the camera’s limited frame. Her red hair was left loose, and she smiled at him warmly while Darian idly reached out to increase the size of her call window before settling back into his chair.

“Welcome back to Earth, Revenant-King,” his best friend greeted him slyly, brushing a stray lock of hair from her green eyes. “Feeling refreshed?”

“Hilarious, Kay,” Darian said with a warm smile. “I feel good. These pods are nuts, I feel like I had a workout and a good sleep all in one.”

“I know what you mean,” she said, stretching as if his words brought her own physical state to mind. “I feel weirdly energized. This tech would be crazy for physical therapy, I bet.”

“Not a bad idea,” Darian agreed while reaching out to take a glass of juice he’d poured and sip it. “Did you contact Cora, Nate, and Maya?”

“They’re waiting in a separate convo,” Kate confirmed. “I thought I told you to call me when you got out?”

“I wanted a shower,” Darian said, “and then I called you, without looking like I swam in slime.”

“I’ll forgive you this time,” Kay replied with a grin. “Ready to face the music? I think they’re annoyed at us.”

“We didn’t even do anything bad yet!” Darian objected immediately. “Well, not really…”

“Cora has complaints, Dee. I think she may have them in triplicate. I told her you’d naturally be more than happy to hear them and answer, of course.”

“You’re a menace, Katherine Dragomirov,” Darian said dryly in response. “Alright, no point hiding from Corey and her lists. Link us in.”

Kate gave a grinning two-fingered salute and tapped her screen, transferring their call into a larger group meeting a moment later. When she did, three new faces appeared on the screen; Corey Lee first, the half-Korean, half-English finance major dressed in a simple blue robe belted at her waist, her amber eyes framed beneath black hair settling on him immediately with a look of determined interrogation.

With her were two others: Nathaniel Mercer, Darian’s male best friend since high school, a fellow former footballer, and his usual righteous partner in most games they played. Tall, burly, and unmistakably African American; Nate’s dark face shifted with a grin at his appearance, and his slate-gray eyes twinkled with amusement while he waved in greeting.

The last of the new arrivals was the smallest of their group in terms of height; a Dominican med-student named Maya Harrera, Nate’s girlfriend and the ostensible central peacemaker of their little clique of five, whose brown eyes moved between Kate and Darian’s windows with eager interest as they arrived while her curly brown hair sat tied behind her.

“So,” Corey began sternly. “Katherine”—Kate groaned at the use of her government name—“has told us some of what you’ve been up to, Dee. Care to fill in your abandoned friends?”

“Abandoned? That doesn’t seem fair,” Darian said immediately, while kicking his legs up on his desk. “I prefer the term ‘involuntary tension-building’.”

“Fuck off,” Nate said with a warm laugh. “You totally ditched us, dude. What’s this Kay’s been saying about a castle? Did you already manage to get a damn guild hall?”

“And something about a hot ghost assistant, too!” Maya put in eagerly. “Kay mentioned that, as well.”

“I didn’t say he was an assistant, technically…” Kate said slowly, while Darian gave her a look.

“Before any of that,” Corey interrupted. “All we know, like Nate said, is that you have a castle, Kay has new boots—which seems like an incredibly unfair comment to make without screenshots—and that you’re in some special zone. Neither of you thought to send directions, an invite, or even a Social Link request!”

“We’ve been busy, Cora,” Darian said guiltily. “Time got away from us.”

“Yeah, busy having an epic private adventure while Cora’s been testing whether or not being bitten interrupts her spellcasting.”

“That was one time, Nate!” Corey objected immediately.

“You stood still while a giant rat took a chunk out of your ankle, Cora,” Maya said with a laugh.

“It was for science!”

“Did you like the scientific experience of the pain simulation?” Kate asked in amusement.

“Shut up, Kay, it hurt!”

“Being bitten hurts?” Kate replied. “Consider me thoroughly enlightened, professor.”

“Seriously, Dee,” Nate cut in, ignoring Kate and Corey as they started arguing with old familiarity. “What are you doing, man? We’ve been completely in the dark.”

Darian felt his guilt override his instinctive smart-aleck reply and grimaced. “I know. Sorry, Nate. I got a bit carried away with what was going on. I’d planned on telling you guys everything, but I ended up heavily distracted by what’s been happening.”

“There was a little fighting involved,” Kate added, breaking off from her laughing argument with Corey. “Some property damage, an explosion, and a city going crazy.”

“Wait, what?! Start from the beginning!” Maya commanded.

“Okay, okay,” Darian said in acquiescence. “So, you guys know I won the raffle for the custom pod, right? Well, turns out that the raffle edition of Eternus has this insane questline. So, I logged in, and…”

Darian recounted the first few hours of his time in Eternus for them quickly as he spoke, detailing meeting Lilith, finding Lightsbane, being transported to Blackstone, meeting Isolde and the Duke, dealing with the Wayfinder Ziggy Stardust—that name caused multiple roars of laughter—and eventually entering the Necropolis to meet Mortarius. By the time he finished, all three of his and Kate’s mutual friends were sitting back in their chairs—or on her bed, in Corey’s case—and staring at him in disbelief.

“So let me get this straight,” Nate said slowly. “You got a unique quest, freed a goddess—”

“Divinarch,” Darian corrected.

“—Sure, a ‘Divinarch’, from captivity, pissed off the other g—Divinarchs, met a hot NPC, got a quest from that NPC’s dad, entered the creepy old fortress, got murdered, and now you’re its King?”

“Uh, sort of,” Darian said slowly. “A bit more to it, but that’s the basic gist.”

“I haven’t read anything about a Divinarch of Darkness or Death,” Maya said, glancing at another screen. “There’s nothing about her on the message boards, either. I see stuff on Hyperion for Light, Sylvaine for Nature, Pyranthos for Fire, Aethyra for Air, Kharadun for Earth, and Thalassara for Water, but nothing on a seventh Divinarch for Darkness.”

“Lilith’s Faith and knowledge of her were suppressed centuries ago,” Darian said in immediate explanation. “It’s why the quest line is so difficult, and why we’ve had to make so many rapid gains. We’re fighting an uphill battle, basically.”

“Between that and exploding chapels,” Kate added, “it’s not exactly been a cakewalk.”

“The Experience has been nice, but Kay’s right; it hasn’t been easy, no,” Darian agreed.

“And what level are you now, Dee?” Corey asked with narrowed amber eyes.

“Twenty-four…” Darian said with a wince.

“TWENTY-FOUR?!” Kay’s female best friend demanded. “DARIAN KING!”

“Hey, I’m level ten!” Kate interjected with wounded dignity.

“Well done, Kay!” Maya said with warm sincerity, which caused Kate to preen while Corey continued glaring at the screen.

“And now you’re a Vampire, too?” Nate asked before Corey could rant again.

“A Sanguine Revenant,” Darian corrected quickly, grateful for the change of subject. “Not a Vampire, man. Vampires are undead, I’m not. My heart beats, my blood flows, and I’m not weak to sunlight or silver or anything. Lilith is pretty strict about the cycle of Life and Death.”

“Lilith being the Divinarch of Sin, Darkness, Entropy, Order, and Death?” Maya clarified.

“Yep,” Kate confirmed brightly. “She’s super hot, too, Maya. Like really hot. She’s got this whole Deathmommy schtick that is just—”

“And how,” Corey cut in while interrupting Kate’s gushing admiration, “does this whole thing work? Are you, like, her slave or something?”

“What?” Darian asked with a blink. “No. I’m not. Lilith gave me a lot of agency, Cora. I can do basically whatever the hell I want within the limits of her precepts, and those precepts are pretty simple: Don’t put other Divinarchs ahead of her in worship, enforce the Cycle outside of dire necessity, and always remain loyal to her Faith. It sounds complex, but it really isn’t. She’s got some hard and fast rules about the whole enslaving souls thing, like I said, but otherwise she’s pretty hands-off.”

“Okay, sure,” Nate said, taking control of the conversation again while Corey leaned toward the screen in preparation for another outraged exclamation, and Kate grinned at her. “So how does this all work? You’re playing for the bad guy now?”

“It isn’t that simple,” Darian said with an immediate shake of the head. “I know it might look that way, but Lilith isn’t the storybook villainess. She’s not exactly benevolent, no, but she’s not needlessly cruel or cartoon-evil, either. She’s all about cause and effect.”

“How does that tie into Sin, Entropy, and Darkness, though?” Maya asked next. “I can see Death and Order, because of what you said about the Cycle, but the other three?”

“Temptation isn’t compulsion,” Kate said instead of Darian, jumping in to help. “It’s all about that idea, really. Lilith tempts people, but she doesn’t force them to listen. She creates the circumstances, and she can probably be a capricious bitch if she wants to be, but she’s been really fair with me and Dee. She asks little, she gives a lot. It’s not really a deal with the devil like you’re thinking.”

“Okay,” Maya accepted while Corey looked thoughtful and Nate frowned, “what about the whole Entropy and Darkness thing, then?”

“Everything has to end at some point, right?” Darian said rhetorically. “That’s the idea behind her Faith. Everything that is created has to cease, that’s the essence of Entropy—as for Darkness, it’s just an element. Darkness, Light, Fire, Earth, Water, Air, and Nature are just elements of existence. Lilith embodies one, but it doesn’t mean she’s inherently bad. Without shadows, the world would die from overexposure to sunlight, and Kate would fry like bacon in the summer.”

“Rude!” Kate put in, while the others laughed.

“And an Autarch is basically her avatar, right?” Nate asked afterward, looking thoughtful.

“Basically, man, yeah,” Darian agreed with relief. “My job is to protect her Faith and build it back up. I’m not just her stooge taking quests as she doles them out, either. Even her High Priestess would answer to the Autarch. I’m supposed to rebuild her Faith, create a proper Dark Faction—including territory, laws, armies, all of it—and then expand from there. It’s not like working for some NPC king that makes all the important decisions.”

“So, what, you’re running a base building and civilization simulator as well?” Corey asked with interest.

“Sort of?” Darian answered slowly. “I’ve got to conquer Blackstone, but there’s a lot that goes into that. Way more than I can cover right now.”

“Sounds like Civ XVI,” Nate said thoughtfully.

“Not quite, but you’re not entirely wrong,” Darian agreed.

“And how do we come in?” Maya asked next. “Do we have to play walking simulator to find you?”

“Nope!” Kate answered before Darian could. “He has this amazing thing called a Mana Nexus, under the Necropolis, which gives him crazy cool abilities. He can summon all of you there!”

“And you didn’t do this earlier why exactly?” Corey demanded immediately.

“…I got caught up,” Darian admitted. “I only summoned Kay because she harangued me.”

“You summoned me because you would be lost without me, Dee,” Kate corrected haughtily.

Corey and Maya glanced between Darian and Kate knowingly, but thankfully said nothing.

“So let me get this straight, then,” Nate interrupted before the conversation could derail, saving Darian from any awkward jokes about him and Kate being a married couple. “First, you entered the game and your portal got hijacked by this special event. Then, you wound up in a space-throne-room and freed a chained Divinarch with a sentient sword that wants to stab everything, after which you were awarded a bucket-load of experience and entered Blackstone?”

Darian nodded along as his friend spoke, while Kate grinned, Corey checked something on her other screen, and Maya quietly made notes.

“Then, after that, you got your Traveler license, took a quest from the city’s NPC ruler, met an ancient demi-god-turned-wight-lich, got turned into a Sanguine, became the mortal representative of one of the game’s seven absolute powers, became a full-on faction leader, and now you have to find a way to stop Hyperion’s followers from crusading you out of existence?”

“…more or less,” Darian said wryly.

“And after all of this, you only now remembered the fact that you’re missing the rest of your party?”

Darian deflated at Nate’s observation and lifted a hand defensively. “In my defense—”

“No defense!” Corey cut in. “Nuh uh. We’ll forgive you for being immersed, Dee, but no defenses. You’re gonna summon us the moment we all go back in.”

“You’re going to need the help, Dee,” Maya said warmly. “We’re stronger together.”

“And it gives Nate an excuse to be a masochist again,” Kate added slyly.

“I don’t see you complaining when you hide behind my shield every time you draw aggro, Kay,” Nate observed with good humor.

“I never said it wasn’t appreciated!” Kate replied with a laugh.

“So we have a plan, then,” Corey interrupted, pulling her knees up under her. “We’ll all log in to Eternus in the next twenty, and Dee will summon us. Is there anything we should know?”

“Yeah,” Darian said, grateful for Corey’s decisive nature before things could tangent again. “Mortarius is a ghost, but he’s also old, like really old, and he used to be the next-best thing to a Divinarch. He’s a really knowledgeable and insightful guide, but he can be very blunt and less-than-impressed by empty bravado. Be polite when you talk to him. If he likes you, he can help you massively with your build—but he’s just as likely to ignore you completely if he doesn’t. I don’t control him, he’s bound to me, but he’s not my servant, he’s my mentor.”

“Don’t piss off the ancient King’s ghost,” Nate summarized. “Got it. Anything else?”

“Blackstone is probably going to be a shitshow in the next few days in Eternus,” Kate supplied. “We kicked a hornet’s nest when we raided the Hyperian compound, and we ended up in something like a two-phase boss fight. By time you guys arrive, things might already have escalated.”

“Are there bonuses we can get to handle that, then?” Maya asked immediately. “I’m level six, Nate’s level seven, and Corey’s level five. We aren’t exactly the Avengers.”

“Let me worry about that when we’re together,” Darian said in response. “Worst case, we can ask Mortarius. You guys will want his advice for builds and stuff anyway.”

The other three nodded, and Nate finally spoke again.

“I’m not against joining you in a renegade playthrough, brother, but I’ve got my reservations about Lilith. I don’t like game factions where my only choices are to kill NPCs or terrorize them. Does summoning us force us to join her faction?”

“No,” Kate replied instead of Darian. “It’s all about choice, Nate. Lilith doesn’t really do compulsion in that sense. She wants willing followers, not slaves. Darian could theoretically have slaves, and he sort of has one already”—her hand went up when Maya and Corey both opened their mouths—“but that’s also technically inaccurate. We’ll explain more when we’re there. You can meet Bonnie that way, too. Point is this: the choice is yours, but you can’t align with another Faith faction and stay with Dee long-term. That, I’m afraid, is the truth.”

“Then the choice is between sticking with Dee or fighting him?” Nate asked skeptically.

“Not much of a choice, Kay,” Corey agreed.

“Nobody’s gonna be forcefully converting your characters through a travel invite, guys,” Kate said firmly before the concerns could grow. “Come over, ask Morty what you need to ask, and then decide. I’m not going to bullshit you and pretend that, like, joining the other side is something we can handwave, though. It’s just not in the cards for how the game works.”

“Then we’ll figure it out when we arrive,” Maya said with finality. “Our names are Mayarella, Corva, and Nathanius, Dee. We’ll send you Social Link invites when we’re in-game.”

“Romulus, right?” Nate asked. “Lucky going getting that one.”

“Thanks, man,” Darian said with a smile. “And for the record, I’m sorry I didn’t get you guys involved sooner. Things really have been crazy. We really could have used your help in our last fight. I promise I won’t make that mistake again.”

His friends softened at his words, and Corey sighed in an aggrieved way.

“Okay, okay, apology accepted. I’m also sorry for shouting at you. Now, are we ready to get into this? I wanna meet the hot ghost and see Kay’s new stompers.”

“Oh my god, Cora, they’re so—”

“Yeah,” Darian cut in before Kate could ramble about her new attire, “we’re ready. I’ll see you guys in Eternus.”

Nate saluted with two fingers, Maya waved warmly, and Corey gave him a grin, showing her anger had largely been performative, before all three of his friends disconnected to get ready, leaving Darian alone with Kate.

“Well,” she said after a minute, “that wasn’t as bad as I feared. We didn’t really get to give them much about Dark Mana, but Morty can cover that.”

“He can,” Darian agreed, though he felt a little worried. “You really think they’ll be okay with what we’re doing, Kay?”

Kate blinked at him when he spoke, and then her face softened into a warm, reassuring smile. “Relax, Dee. They might complain a bit or ask uncomfortable questions, but it’s them. They’d rather punch themselves in the face with a spike than abandon us. It’ll be okay.”

Darian exhaled at her reassurance and smiled at her in turn. “Thanks, Kay.”

“Always and forever, Dee,” Kay said back to him warmly, blew him a kiss, and disconnected.

Darian chuckled at her teasing antics, reassured that if nothing else he’d always have Kate with him, turned from his desk, and looked toward where the pod awaited while Alfred’s fifteen-minute-warning alarm sounded once from the terminal before he clicked it off to acknowledge it.

Time to go back in, he thought to himself quietly. Let’s hope nothing insane has happened in the time we were away.
