“An Apostle... and the Forbidden Words?” Ekon asked.

“Yes,” said the beautiful woman carved out of radiance, her voice soft and clear as still water. “My lord asks whether your friend knows anything about those terms, and if she does happen to possess such knowledge, what price she would want in return for sharing it.”

Ekon slowly nodded. “Understood. I’ll tell her if I have the chance, though I should warn you that it’s always she who contacts me whenever she wants to talk. I, on the other hand, don’t have any way to contact her myself.”

“If you’re unable to reach her, then it can’t be helped. Still, there are only four days left before the competition begins, so it would be best if we could learn the answer as quickly as possible. I’ll come back tomorrow morning to see if there’s any news.”

Then she disappeared, without so much as a ripple in the air to mark her passing.

And, not even three seconds later, the familiar voice echoed in his head, sounding entirely too pleased with itself.

“Finally. I was begining to think he’d forgotten about me. But here he came after all, sending someone to ask for a deal. So, the very first transaction we might make together, the first real exchange between us, is this, huh?”

“You were here the whole time?” Ekon frowned. “Then why didn’t you show yourself immediately, while she was still standing in front of me?”

“Why should I do that? Deals are serious matters. They require careful consideration before anything is agreed upon. We will discuss this properly, just the two of us, first. I’ll give the information to you, along with the price I want for it, and only then will you carry it back to them.”

“Fine,” Ekon said with a sigh. “I take it that means you do know what those words mean, then?”

“Yes. But I’m not going to tell you here. Let’s go to the Realm Beyond instead.”

“What? Why?”

“Because our shiny friend might not have left at all. She could still be standing right there, invisible, listening to every word that leaves your mouth. And you might let something slip during our conversation, something I’d rather the other side not know. Information is valuable, after all. I see no reason to hand it over for free, least of all by accident.”

Ekon let out another sigh, long and heavy. He really did not want to go to that creepy world for a second time. But it seemed, as always, he didn’t have a choice in the matter.

“Fine then,” he muttered. “But how do I get there anyway?”

Anastrasza chuckled. “Sleep, of course.”

“I’m not sleepy right now.”

“Just lie down on your bed and close your eyes. I'll handle the rest.”

Ekon didn’t quite know how to feel about it, but there was little point in arguing any further, so he did as he was told. He just lay there, waiting for something, anything, to happen. But nothing came. Silence stretched on and on as he saw only the black surrounding him, an absence so absolute it felt like there was a real weight pressing against his closed eyelids.

Then, he heard a giggle.

“Hey, how long were you planning to lie there like that?”

That was Anastrasza’s voice. Yes, it was unmistakably hers. But it did not come from inside his head the way it usually did. Instead, it seemed to come from someone who was in the same room as him.

He snapped his eyes open, only to find the surroundings had changed into the familiar interior of Mandra’s hut. But of course, this wasn’t real. After all, it was not her sitting in the chair by the window, but a woman dressed in a white gown, her long red hair cascading like a fall of fire around her shoulders. Her eyes were fixed on him, pale and blue and cold as a winter pond. Amusement glittered there, the kind that always laughed at a joke only she understood.

“This is a dream?” he asked.

“Yes.”

“So you can make me sleep anytime you want?”

“Only if you cooperate,” Anastrasza said as her lips curved into a smile.

Ekon sighed. “Let’s talk about what the Sovereign wants to know. I can’t stay here for long, so we shouldn’t waste any more time than we already have.”

True, the last time he was here, their conversation had been abruptly cut short. But the real reason he wanted to get this over as quickly as possible was that he had no desire to linger in this world any longer than necessary.

“Let’s talk about what I don’t know first. I haven’t heard anything about the Forbidden Words, not in any context that stuck with me. At least, I haven’t heard of it as a common phrase referring to anything specific and widely recognized. It might be some internal label the Children of the Abyss use among themselves, a term that means absolutely nothing to anyone else.”

“I see. How about the Apostle, then?”

“That,” Anastrasza said, the smile never leaving her lips, “I know.”

“What is it?”

Instead of answering, Anastrasza leisurely leaned back against her chair with the unhurried ease of someone who had all the time in the world.

“What do you think the greatest weakness of a Reliquary is?”

Huh?

Why did she suddenly ask about something that has nothing to do with the matter at hand?

“Isn’t that something that depends on each Reliquary individually? Surely each one has its own particular flaws, its own particular limits.”

“No.” She said it the way one would correct a child. “It’s a weakness that every single Reliquary has, without exception, no matter how powerful or how ancient or how unique it might be.”

“Is there such a weakness?” Ekon asked, frowning. He had never heard of anything like that.

“There is,” Anastrasza said, laughing softly. “Reliquaries are powerful, undeniably so. Each one different, each one its own little miracle. And they are hard to destroy. Not indestructible, of course, but destroying a Reliquary is something very difficult to achieve. However...” She glanced at him. “The same thing cannot be said of the ones who wield them. They are just human, after all. There are countless ways to kill a person, no matter how strong or how skilled they are. And, even if you can’t kill them in combat, you can always do so outside of it. Because humans are not only fragile, but also full of need. They need to rest, they need to eat, they need to sleep. And in each of those moments lies an opportunity. You can poison their food. You can lay a trap and wait. You can wear them down until exhaustion does the work for you. And sometimes, you don’t even need to kill them. You can just deceive them or steal their Reliquaries.”

“So, you’re saying the weakness lies not in the artifact itself, but in the human behind it?”

“Yes.”

“Well... you’re correct, I suppose. But I fail to see how any of this has to do with what we are here to discuss.”

“How would you solve that problem? How would you remove that inherent weakness?”

“I don’t know if that’s even solvable. Add more protection to the wielder, maybe?”

“Add more protection? Ah, yes. You’re right, technically. If only we could somehow make the user every bit as durable as the Reliquary they carry. Make them immune to poison and disease. Strip away their need for food, for sleep, for rest. Further still, make the human and the Reliquary inseparable from one another, joined so completely that nothing could pull them apart.”

“What? How?”

Anastrasza smiled. “By turning the human themselves into the vessel that holds the power. A living Reliquary.”

Ekon was aghast. “Is that even possible?”

“The Abyss makes it possible.”

He swallowed as realization dawned upon him.

“And that... that’s an Apostle, isn’t it?”

“Yes.”

Ekon was completely taken aback, his mind scrambling to catch up with what he had just learned about. Such a thing was unfathomable to him, an idea so bizarre and monstrous it seemed to belong to nightmares rather than to the waking world.

“Of course it’s not easy to accomplish,” Anastrasza continued. “There are few humans suitable to become such vessels in the first place, and there’s also the matter of the original souls that inhabited those bodies resisting the intrusion of the new souls—the essence holding the power—that seek to claim the hosts for themselves.” She giggled. “Annoying, isn’t it, that resistance? If only there were a way to make the original souls go away, to make the vessels empty so that something else could fill them...”

Wait.

Ekon recalled what he had once heard during the conversation with the Sovereign.

“When you talked about those masked men, you said once their souls were completely consumed by the Abyssal Armaments they wielded, what was left behind was nothing more than empty shells, most of which were simply discarded, but some could be used for other purposes...”

“Yes. That’s precisely how an Apostle is made. A soulless. A human Reliquary.”

What an abomination, Ekon thought. But also, he had to admit, a fearsome weapon.

“Then it’s something without any weakness,” he said. “How are we supposed to deal with it?”

“As I said, Reliquaries are durable, but not indestructible.” Anastrasza gave a shrug. “So hit it harder, I guess.”

That doesn’t sound helpful at all, Ekon thought. But he supposed someone of the Sovereign’s caliber would have little difficulty handling such a problem.

“Anyway, you can share that information with our shiny friend. In return, let’s see... after the Sovereign defeats the Apostle, assuming he manages to do so, I want the power held within that vessel.”

“What do you mean?”

“If the Apostle is destroyed inside the dungeon, then the Dungeon Core will acquire the essence sealed within them. It can then transfer that power into another vessel, creating a new Reliquary...”

That’s also possible?

“...A human who can serve as a vessel is rare, so I doubt they would waste one on an E-rank power. It should be an A-rank at the very least, if not higher.”

E-rank? A-rank? What on earth was she even talking about?

“Why do you want that power?”

“Why wouldn’t I want it? Even if I can’t use it myself, I can always let you or someone wield that artifact in my stead. Or I can trade it for something else later.”

Fair enough, Ekon thought. Then, he let out a heavy sigh.

“I’m learning more and more unbelievable things every time I talk to you. A human Reliquary, huh? What a terrifying concept. It must be one of the most powerful weapons in the Children’s entire arsenal, then.”

“Actually,” Anastrasza said, “the greatest weapons they’ve ever possessed are the Divine Instruments. But they don’t have those anymore, so I suppose you’re right.”

Divine Instruments? That name certainly evoked a sense of awe all on its own.

“Are they also something made by the Abyss?”

“Yes.”

“Then even if they are lost, what exactly stops the Abyss from making new ones?”

Anastrasza chuckled. “Because the conditions that allowed him to forge them in the first place are no longer there.”

“What conditions?”

“You may not know this, but there was once a time when the entity now referred to as the Abyss had been blessed by the Gods. All twelve of them, every single one, vested their power in him, and it was that combined blessing which allowed him to create the twelve Divine Instruments. That fool. If he had played his cards right back then, he could have ascended to godhood already. But instead, he went out of his way to walk the path of defiance. So he lost their support. That’s why he can no longer create those artifacts.”

Ekon sighed again. Another cosmic secret, dropped in his lap like a brick he hadn’t asked to catch, one he would now have to carry around forever. He really should learn to keep his mouth shut and stop asking questions.

That was when he noticed Anastrasza’s expression had shifted, her usual playfulness fading into something more withdrawn. She turned away, gaze fixed on something far beyond the walls of the hut.

“What’s the matter?”

“I’m thinking about the other term... the Forbidden Words.”

“You’ve figured out what it is?”

She shook her head. “No. I’m just pondering, turning over the possibilities. Since it was mentioned together with the Apostle, then it should be something just as dangerous, or perhaps even more so. Could it be...” But then, she waved a dismissive hand. “No... can’t be it.”

“Just say it,” Ekon said with a frown, “whatever it is. Better to hear something unlikely than miss something true. If the Sovereign knows what he might be facing, he can prepare for it.”

Anastrasza laughed.

“This is not something you can prepare for, my dear Ekon. If it’s what I’m thinking, then we are done. There’s no defense against it. Game over. The only thing you can do now is pray the crazy bunch we’re dealing with are not that crazy.”
