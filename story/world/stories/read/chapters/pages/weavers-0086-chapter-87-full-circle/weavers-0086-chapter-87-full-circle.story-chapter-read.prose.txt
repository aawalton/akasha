Lena’s body hit the ground.

For a moment, the world narrowed to that single point. Her vision tunnelled, the edges of the courtyard fading into a blur of motion and colour. The roar of the wind beside them, the frantic movement of dust and debris, the crack and hiss of stone tearing through apart—it all dulled, as if she had been pulled underwater.

Her breath caught somewhere in her chest.

“No…”

The word barely formed, little more than air escaping her lungs.

Lena didn’t speak.

Didn’t move.

Didn’t breathe.

Kaelin’s mind tried—desperately—to deny it. To rewind, to correct, to undo the last few seconds. But time, for once, refused to bend for her.

And Zephyr saw.

The shift in him was immediate and catastrophic.

“You said—!”

His voice shattered the fragile stillness, raw and jagged, ripping through the chamber with a force that rivalled the magic itself. He turned on Aric like something unleashed, whatever restraint he had been clinging to finally breaking under the weight of reality.

“You said she’d be safe!”

His magic flared up, summoning his weapons from the earth once more.

The ground beneath his feet convulsed violently, jagged slabs of stone erupting upward in uneven, chaotic spikes. Cracks split through the chamber floor like fractures in glass, spreading outward in sharp, branching lines. From those fractures, vines surged—thick, thorned, and wild—snapping through the air with brutal force, lashing toward Aric again and again.

There was no rhythm to it. No control.

Nothing but rage fuelled him.

Zephyr attacked relentlessly, each strike heavier, faster, more reckless than the last. He didn’t pause to think, didn’t dodge any hits aimed for him.

He took them on the chin, and doled out blow after blow.

Shards of rock and glass flew around them, rocketing across the battlefield with no regard for friend, nor foe.

“Zephyr!” Kana’s voice cut through the chaos, sharp with urgency.

“Stop!” Aria added, already moving, redirecting debris and intercepting stray attacks before they could cause further damage.

But Zephyr didn’t hear them.

He didn’t see them.

His entire world had narrowed to a single point.

Aric.

And the body lying behind him.

Kaelin’s stomach twisted as she watched.

He’s not going to stop.

Worse.

This isn’t working.

Aric moved through the storm of magic with unsettling ease. Each attack that should have crushed him, pierced him, overwhelmed him—he avoided or deflected with minimal effort. His movements were precise, economical, almost detached, as though the chaos around him wasn’t something he needed to engage with fully.

He wasn’t struggling.

He wasn’t even close.

Kaelin forced herself to breathe.

Forced herself to move.

“ZEPHYR!”

Her voice cut through everything. Sharp and commanding, the one person Lena cared about more than him.

For a fraction of a second—

He looked past Aric, and stared her in the eyes.

Kaelin acted instantly.

She made a hand gesture to him, cupping them both and putting them together as if imitating some sort of enclosure. Zephyr understood what she meant, and dropped to the ground, forcing his hands into the earth for better control. Threads surged outward, not wild like before, far more controlled. Stone responded to his will, rising in a tight, compressed arc around Aric. It curved inward, thickening as it formed, sealing him inside a dense, reinforced barrier.

A prison.

Zephyr staggered slightly, breath ragged, magic still crackling around him in unstable bursts. His eyes snapped toward Kaelin, wide and unfocused.

“He killed her,” he said, his voice hoarse, almost unrecognisable.

“I know,” Kaelin replied, her own voice tightening despite her effort to keep it steady. “I know.”

The words felt insufficient.

Meaningless.

But she forced herself to continue.

“But if you keep going like that,” she added, stepping closer, “you’re going to get everyone else killed too.”

The statement hung between them.

Heavy.

Zephyr didn’t respond immediately. His chest rose and fell in uneven bursts, his gaze flickering—just briefly—toward Lena before snapping back to Kaelin.

“I have a way to stop him,” she said.

That got their attention.

Aria turned fully now, her posture sharpening. Kana stilled behind them, her usual composure replaced by something more alert, more focused.

“What kind of way?” Aria asked.

Kaelin hesitated.

“A forbidden spell.”

The words settled like weight in the air.

“It can break through his time barrier,” she continued, forcing clarity into her explanation. “It doesn’t fight his control—it bypasses it. It lets me accelerate the aging of a target… from a distance. Instantly.”

Kana’s eyes narrowed.

“That’s—”

Kaelin met her gaze.

“Yea, it’s what I did to Daniel. But it’s stronger, I don’t have to get near enough to touch him even, I can do it all in a second from here.”

She looked to Guy as she spoke.

“If I have enough power,” she said quietly, “I can reduce him to nothing before he can react.”

Zephyr spoke up instantly.

“Then do it.”

There was no hesitation left in him or his actions.

The only thoughts he had in his mind were grief, and rage.

Kaelin shook her head. “I can’t. Not alone.”

She stepped closer, her gaze locking onto Guy’s.

“I need everything you can give me,” she said. “Life energy, ambient magic—everything in the area. Use your Essence threads to transfer it directly into me.”

Guy looked at her, shocked.

His gaze flicked just once, toward Aric’s cocoon.

Then he nodded.

“Understood.”

Essence threads ignited around him, brighter and more unstable than Kaelin had ever seen. The air shifted immediately, magic bending inward, drawn from every corner of the chamber.

Plants withered. Weeds died. The faint, ever-present hum of ambient magic thinned as it was pulled into a single, concentrated flow.

Then he shot one out to Kaelin, stabbing her in the thigh.

He pushed all the energy he could into her.

The force of it hit like a tidal wave.

Kaelin gasped as the power flooded through her, overwhelming her senses, her body, her mind. It burned—too much, too fast—threatening to tear her apart from the inside out.

Her vision blurred.

Her knees buckled.

Still not enough.

The realization struck immediately, cold and unyielding.

Even with all of this, it wasn’t sufficient.

Her breath came in sharp, uneven bursts.

I need more.

“Kaelin!”

Kana’s voice cut through the haze.

Something struck her hand.

A bracelet.

Small. Somewhat familiar.

“Use that!” Kana shouted. “It was Daniel’s—it stores mana! I’ve been using it this whole time. It’s the only reason I’ve ever been able to keep up with you.”

Kaelin stared at it for half a second, recognition flickering.

Then she nodded.

“Thank you.”

She drew from it.

The bracelet responded instantly, releasing its stored energy in a controlled surge that merged with the chaotic flood already pouring into her.

More.

Closer.

But still—

Not enough.

Her body screamed under the strain, but in doing so, it answered.

That same orange glow as each other time. The ability to influence outcomes, even if it goes against the eternal cycle of life and death, power over the threads of fate.

They coiled around her mind, subtle and dangerous.

Kaelin clenched her jaw.

Fine.

She reached for them.

Not to control Aric.

Not to manipulate the fight directly.

But to alter the conditions.

To bend the requirement itself.

To make this, what she had available, enough.

Reality resisted.

The strain hit her instantly, a sharp, searing pressure behind her eyes as though she were forcing something fundamental to shift. Her thoughts wavered, her focus threatening to slip—

Then it gave.

It’s enough.

Kaelin exhaled.

And stepped forward.

The barrier around Aric started to peel away as she approached. Inside, he stood still, watching her with an expression that was almost… understanding.

As if he knew exactly what she had done.

She stood only a few steps in front of the others, far from Aric himself. He wouldn’t have time to react.

It was already too late.

Time threads ignited around her  in a continuous, overwhelming flow. She reached beyond what she had learnt. Reached into her subconscious, feeling for the rage she’d felt before. Understanding the spells which she had found so long ago.

She reached into herself.

Into the inevitable.

She raised her hand.

And she pulled.

Time collapsed.

The space around Aric warped violently as centuries compressed into seconds. The air thickened, breaking down under the strain. Zephyr’s barrier shattered instantly, unable to withstand the weight of time.

Aric.

Flesh withered, darkened, collapsed inward. Skin cracked and dissolved. Bone followed, structure failing as time devoured it completely.

Marcel.

Where they had stood, there was nothing left but a wet, collapsing mass of rotted matter, already breaking down further under the residual strain.

Silence remained, in their place.

Kaelin stood there, unmoving, her breath uneven as the power drained from her all at once. The energy that had filled her vanished just as violently, leaving behind exhaustion so deep it felt like her bones might give out beneath her.

She looked around slowly.

The academy above was silent. Students were long gone, their panicked screams hadn’t been heard for some time, though the academy was in ruins. Scattered with debris and bodies, it looked like a post-apocalyptic wasteland. No help from the pile of mulch which flowed out from one spot.

Kana stood frozen.

Aria’s expression was unreadable.

Zephyr didn’t move.

His gaze was fixed on Lena.

Kaelin followed it.

Her chest tightened painfully.

We won.

But it didn’t feel like it.

Her hands trembled at her sides.

Was it worth it?

The question settled heavily in her chest, unanswered and unanswerable.

If she had gone with Aric…

If she had let him use her…

Would this have been avoided?

Or would something far worse have followed?

She didn’t know.

She would never know.

Kaelin closed her eyes briefly, her breath unsteady.

She had stopped him.

But standing in the wreckage of everything it had cost—she couldn’t tell how much had actually been saved.
