Finding Doctor Cecilia's house was surprisingly easy. Oswald asked a few people for directions, and they all knew exactly where the doctor's house was. It apparently also doubled as a clinic, one that everyone in town knew about, as it was the one of the few places where people could reliably get treatment for their illnesses.

As Oswald talked more with the people of Volstarn, he learned that there were some apothecaries, and there was a minor temple dedicated to a foreign god which accepted the ill. Yet, the apothecaries frequently ran out of medicines to sell, and the temple barely managed to alleviate the people's suffering.

When Oswald stopped in front of Doctor Cecilia’s house, he couldn't help but stare for a while. The place looked positively majestic compared to the other buildings he's seen so far. It was roughly as wide as four houses if they were placed right next to each other, built with dark colored wood.

The shutters that were painted a deep charcoal color, showing no sign of rot or neglect. Even the iron lantern hanging by the door appeared freshly oiled and unclouded by rust. That doc must be rollin’ in coin.

A crowd had already gathered in the streets, waiting to get into the house. Some leaned against the building, looking very pale. Others sat slumped along the roadside, wrapped in tattered blankets or cloaks worn too thin to fight off the cold. A few were lying against the ground, unmoving except for the slow, shallow rise of their chests.

Yet, just as Oswald was about to enter, he spotted an old man hunched against the wall, knees drawn close to his chest. He looked barely more than skin and bone under the ragged shawl draped over his shoulders. Every few seconds, his body shook with a violent cough, rattling in his chest like a loose stone in an empty jar.

Out of pure curiosity, Oswald crouched beside him, tilting his head. “Hey, old man. How long you been waitin’ to get in?”

The old man let out another wet, hacking cough before swiping at his mouth with the back of a trembling hand. “’Bout an hour now, boy. Ain’t surprised if I gotta wait longer, though. There's no shortage of folk worse off than me.”

"You really think the doc's gonna be able to help you?"

“If anyone can, it’s her. Ain’t no one else left in this town worth a damn when it comes to healin’ folk. But if she can’t fix me up… might finally give that stupid cave a go.”

Oswald blinked. “What cave?”

The old man shifted against the wall, looking more tired than before. He waved a hand weakly. “Ain’t in the mood to jaw about it, buzz off, boy.”

Oswald held up his hands in surrender as he stood up. "Alright, alright. Don’t go coughin’ up a lung over it.”

The old man grunted, already closing his eyes as if to rest. Oswald, meanwhile, decided to finally make his way in.

The people standing closest to the entrance looked worse than the ones sitting, some barely had the strength to keep themselves upright. Oswald tried his best to look away and step around them, eyeing the door.

He reached for the handle. Let’s see what all the fuss is about.

When he got in, Oswald saw more sick people. The clinic’s interior was cramped, packed with people in various states of distress. Wooden benches lined the walls, filled with slumped figures, some coughing weakly into rags, others cradling bandaged limbs. A child whimpered in his mother’s arms, flushed with fever, while an old man sat hunched in the corner, wheezing loudly.

Nurses in simple white uniforms wove through the crowd, their arms laden with rolls of bandages and trays of tinctures. They all seemed too busy to pay any attention to Oswald.

"Oi, if you’re here for treatment, you’ll need to wait in line," a voice snapped.

Oswald turned and found himself face-to-face with a young nurse, her red hair hastily pulled into a loose bun. A fine sheen of sweat clung to her forehead, and though her uniform was clean, the creases and faint wrinkles made it clear she worked long hours and got little rest.

Her sharp green eyes swept over him like she was deciding whether he was worth her time. The nurse's frustration was clear as she jerked a thumb toward the queue. “Line’s back there.”

“Ain’t here for that. I’m from the Astral Wing Adventurer’s Guild, got a quest from Doctor Cecilia.” Oswald explained.

The shift was instant. Her eyes widened, not in shock, but in that calculating way that came when someone had just reassessed a situation. She sighed, rubbing her forehead as if warding off an incoming headache. “Oh. Right. Apologies, I assumed… never mind. Follow me, then.”

She turned on her heel and weaved through the crowd of people inside. Oswald trailed behind, sidestepping a man who groaned as a nurse tightened the bandages around his leg.

"Name’s Lily, by the way," she called over her shoulder.

"Oswald," he replied.

Lily gave a small nod of acknowledgment but didn’t slow her pace. "Doctor Cecilia’s office is just down the hall. Try not to touch anything on the way."

Oswald huffed, stuffing his hands in his pockets. Wasn’t plannin’ on it.

Lily led Oswald through a narrow corridor lined with numbered doors, the air thick with the scent of antiseptics and herbs. Patients sat slumped on benches along the walls, some coughing weakly, others lost in exhausted silence.

At the end of the hall, instead of stopping at a door, Lily went up a spiraling staircase. The wooden steps creaked underfoot as they climbed. Guess the doc works upstairs. Probably don’t want sick folk crowdin’ her space.

At the top, Lily strode down a short hallway to a heavy wooden door. Unlike the ones below, this one bore no number, just a worn brass plate engraved with Cecilia Silversong, Physician.

She knocked twice before pushing it open without waiting. “Doctor, got someone here for you,” she called, stepping aside to let Oswald through.

Oswald entered, the door clicking shut behind him. The office was cramped but organized. Against one wall stood a simple bed with crisp white sheets. A sturdy wooden desk occupied most of the space, its surface buried under stacks of parchment, half-empty ink bottles, and a single flickering oil lamp. Strange-looking equipment lined a nearby shelf: glass tubes, metal instruments, things Oswald didn’t have names for.

Medical stuff, probably. He wasn’t about to start guessing.

At the desk, a woman sat hunched over a piece of paper, scrawling hastily. Long black hair fell over her shoulders, stray strands clinging to her face. Her deep blue eyes, sharp and focused, skimmed the page, though the dark rings beneath them betrayed her exhaustion. Every movement was precise yet heavy with fatigue. A white doctor’s coat hung loosely on her frame, its sleeves rolled up just enough to reveal ink-stained fingers.

She finished writing with a final flourish and tore the parchment from the stack, handing it to the mother and daughter seated before her desk. "Take this to the pharmacist on the first floor, follow the dosage exactly. If the fever worsens, come back immediately."

The mother’s hands trembled slightly as she bowed her head. "Thank you, Doctor. Thank you so much."

Doctor Cecilia gave a tired nod but didn’t watch them leave. Instead, she rubbed her temple with ink-stained fingers and let out a quiet sigh.

Oswald stood silently, taking it all in. Workin’ herself half to death, huh?

Cecilia lifted her gaze. "Lily, who’s the boy?"

Lily stepped aside, motioning to Oswald. "This is Oswald. He’s here for a quest."

Cecilia blinked once, then reached for a stack of parchment at the edge of her desk, flipping through the pages. "Which quest, exactly?"

"Duskbloom gathering." Oswald said.

Cecilia’s fingers hovered mid-turn over the paper before she stifled a yawn, covering her mouth with the back of her ink-stained hand. She muttered something under her breath before flicking a weary glance toward Lily and waving her off. “Close the door, will you?"

Her gaze shifted to Oswald as she gestured toward the chair opposite her desk, “And you, sit down.”

Oswald sank into the chair, the wood creaking slightly under his weight. The seat felt stiff, uncomfortable, but he didn’t complain. Across from him, Doctor Cecilia exhaled quietly before stretching out a hand, palm up. "Your guild card please, Just to confirm you are, in fact, the adventurer assigned to this request."

Oswald reached into his cloak, fishing the card from an inner pocket. He slid it across the desk without a word, watching as Cecilia picked it up and glanced over the details.

After a moment, she gave a small nod and handed the card back. "Thank you."

Oswald tucked it away and leaned back slightly. "So, what can you tell me about this quest?"

Cecilia rubbed her temple, then sat up straighter, shifting her focus. "Volstarn is getting sicker by the day. Basic remedies are no longer enough. I need stronger medicine, which means I require stronger ingredients."

She turned her head toward Lily. "Fetch the map."

"Understood, Doctor." Lily said, walking over to a nearby shelf, running over the spines of several rolled-up scrolls before plucking one free. Then, she returned to the desk and unfurled the parchment, smoothing it out so the details became visible.

Cecilia tapped a spot on the map. "Duskbloom grows deep in the forest north of Volstarn. It thrives in damp, shaded areas, typically near old ruins or caves. But, wherever you find Duskbloom, you are likely to find Duskwings as well."

Oswald leaned forward slightly, resting an arm on the desk. “Duskwings?”

Cecilia sighed, then pressed her fingers against her temple before she spoke. “Odd creatures. Bird-like monsters, roughly human-sized. Think of a deformed harpy, if you have ever seen one.”

“I don’t even know what a harpy is.”

“Never mind, then. More importantly, you should know that individually, Duskwings are not particularly dangerous. However, they are rarely alone. In flocks, they become a serious threat.”

“What’s the danger?”

“They are not mindless. Duskwings often follow an Alpha, a stronger, more intelligent variant. Under its command, they move with surprising coordination. Underestimating them would be unwise, so be careful.”

“Don’t sound too bad. I'm sure I can get the Duskbloom back… but it might make things easier if I know what I’m lookin’ for, so could you show me an example?"

Cecilia gave him a small nod. Shifting forward, she pulled open a drawer, retrieving a thick almanac. Flipping it open, she found a dried, pressed sprig of Duskbloom tucked neatly between the pages. The petals, a deep violet, had lost some of their luster but still held their distinct shape: five slightly curved petals surrounding a darkened center.

“This is Duskbloom. It thrives in shadowed areas, particularly around damp soil. It is a vital ingredient in several remedies, primarily for fever and blood infections. Without it, many of the patients downstairs will not last very long.”

Oswald studied the flower for a moment, committing its shape and color to memory. “Got it. I’ll be back soon.”

“Good.” Cecilia said. Turning to Lily, she motioned toward the corner of the room. “Grab a backpack and hand it over to the boy."

Lily stepped over to a small storage area, pulling a worn but sturdy backpack from a hook. She tested the straps briefly, then handed it to Oswald.

"Put all the herbs you find in here, try not to crush them in transit." Cecilia instructed.

Oswald slung the bag over one shoulder. "No worries, Doc, I’ll be careful."

"Then that is all. Good luck, Oswald."
