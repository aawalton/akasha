The corridors were emptier now. The light from the high windows was softer -more red than yellow. My legs ached with every step. They had been aching since I had first stood after the forging class. They had wobbled and forced me to brace against a table, just for a moment. From then, I had adjusted my stride and kept walking as I always did. I did not limp. A Queen did not limp. I simply walked with marginally more deliberation than usual.

In my hands was a small pouch, heavy with raw meat. I held it carefully. It was the sustenance for my familiar, after all. "Will these be enough for her?" I asked.

Ash smiled beside me. "More than enough for a few days. I'm fairly certain you just took all of the spare meat the mess hall had. You do remember that she's still tiny, right?"

"It was not all of it. There was a portion they claimed was reserved for the morning. I allowed them to keep that."

"How generous of you." It sounded like the Hero was teasing. She let none of it show on her face.

The detour had been my idea. Hours had passed, and by now Solara would be hungry and waiting. She would be hungry and waiting and I was not there. So, we needed to bring her more food. Fresh food. There was only one way to acquire that.

Ash, who had somehow learned the secrets of navigating this Academy, had steered us to the mess hall in mere minutes. How she had found her way was a mystery. We had eaten after that. The same food we had eaten the last time we had gone there together. Then I had approached the broad woman behind the counter and demanded that she give me all of the raw meat they possessed. It had been a simple request. One I expected fulfilled in mere moments.

The woman had looked at me as if I had grown a third horn. She had thought I was joking, even after I had made the same demand twice. By the third time, she had realized that I was not joking at all. She had been about to refuse. I was sure of it. But then Ash had stepped forward, taken her aside, and spoken to her for several seconds. What Ash had said was a mystery, but it had made the woman's expression change from a frown into a smile. Then she had nodded. When they came back, she had handed me what I had asked for without another word.

"What did you say to her?" I asked now, as we turned a corner.

"I can't tell you. Call it a Hero's secret," Ash said. There was a faint smirk on her face, visible from the corner of my eye. My retainer was growing bold.

"It must be a great secret indeed. I was sure she was about to say no."

"It's nothing so great as that." Ash's voice was light. "People just have a way of...talking to me and trusting me."

I looked at her. I knew what this was now, for she did it so very often. She was being modest about something that did not warrant modesty. I had seen it everywhere.

In Hamel, she had become part of the village so surely one might think her born there. On the road, even some of the surly guards had spoken to her, though they never had a word for me. At this Academy, she seemed to leave an impression on everyone we met. I suspected the only reason she did not have her own court was because she did not desire one.

I nodded. "I suppose it is a valuable skill for my retainer to possess." I paused. The next words were more difficult to say than they should have been. "Especially given my own shortcomings in that area."

Ash looked at me. "I'm surprised you can admit that. You really wouldn't have been able to, once."

We kept walking, and I did not answer her. My retainer really was growing bold.

We rounded the final corner. The Beast Hall's entrance was ahead. We heard the sounds even from a distance. Nothing should have been able to leak out past those heavy doors.

I heard grunts and animalistic cries. Something that sounded almost like a roar. I stopped and went still. I looked at Ash. Ash looked at me. We ran.

My shoulders threw the doors open, and we were in the Beast Hall. My legs screamed. I did not care. The sounds of all of these beasts washed over us. I was already channeling Essence into the Requiem. If anything in this hall had so much as ruffled a single feather on-

Twelve feet ahead of us, Sol waddled back and forth on the floor. I stopped and stared at her. The tiny phoenix took three steps to the left, paused, and took three steps to the right. Then she took three steps to the left, and to the right again. Sol was pacing, and it was a Queen's pacing. I had done it myself. There could be no other word for it.

Her pillow sat abandoned several feet away. The sounds we had heard outside came from all of the beasts around her.

The horse-sized spider in the far corner pressed itself as far back into its cage as it could. The crystal serpent had coiled so tightly it was nearly a sphere.

There were other reactions. One from every beast I could see. The Inker was nowhere to be seen.

I crossed the distance. My legs ached as I knelt and the Ember Core ground beneath my ribs. Neither was enough to stop me. Sol's back was to me. I leaned down until my face was level with the floor. Until I was level with her. "Hello," I murmured. The word was almost swallowed by the many sounds around me. I considered repeating myself, or perhaps reaching out to touch her.

Solara stopped her waddling at once. She slowly turned and her enormous golden eyes met my own. She regarded me for one full second without moving at all. Then she let out a long and slow chirp.

In a Beast Hall with a dozen creatures making their own noise, the sound of her chirping should have been small. It was not. Her tiny body trembled with the force of it. I had received many greetings in my time, both reverent and fearful. This was closer to an accusation. One that I deserved. "I apologize," I said. "It seems I was away for too long."

Sol chirped again, louder. Then again. Then again. Each one was more pointed than the last. Sol's four wings flared wide as she chirped, and the effect was of something very small trying to be very large.

In my very long life, I had been dressed down like this only a few times. Most of them had been before I'd become Queen, back when I still had equals, and even the rare superior. After I had ascended, only the Gods themselves had tried, and I had broken two of them for their trouble. This was worse than all of them.

"I apologize," I said again. I opened the pouch and held it so she could see inside. I had already bathed all the meat in the Cradle. Sol's chirping paused. She regarded the offering. Her head tilted and she let out a single trill. I still felt the accusation, but it seemed she had spared my execution in favor of a warning. I would have to teach her manners, in time. For now, I would bear with this.

She hopped forward. I had expected her to lunge for the meat. She did not. She bypassed the pouch entirely, pressed her small body against my outstretched hand, and stayed there. Her feathers were impossibly soft against my palm.

I smiled. I could not have stopped it even had I tried, and I did not try. Ash was kneeling beside me now.

I remembered something. Edda, reaching toward Sol's wing. The way Sol had permitted the touch when she had refused Ren's. Edda had scratched beneath one of the black wings, and Sol had trilled.

I reached, slowly, toward the nearest of Sol's black wings. My fingers were uncertain. I had held this creature since before she was born, and yet I did not know how to do this.

I scratched beneath the wing just like I had seen that girl do. The motion was clumsy. Too firm, perhaps, or in the wrong direction. Sol let out a soft trill. She raised herself slightly, lifting the wing higher so I could reach better.

"See?" Ash said quietly. "She was fine." I nodded. She was fine.

The Inker stepped in from somewhere around the back of the hall. Inker Hesta was wiping her hands on a thick cloth that was stained with things I wished not to know. She stopped when she saw us. The sigh that left her could have filled a sail. "Thank the Divine you're back for your bird," she said.

I rose and ignored the ache that came. "You were not at your post," I said. "You left her unattended, for however short a time. What do you have to say for yourself?"

The Inker regarded me. "One of the beasts was having a fit," she said. "Had to go and calm the thing down. It was having a fit because of your bird. Hit its head trying to back away in the pen too hard."

"Away from Sol?" I pointed. Sol had currently folded herself on my right foot…so I could not raise my leg. I hadn't even noticed her weight until I'd looked.

"They've all been at it, ever since you left her here this morning." Hesta's gaze swept the hall. "Strangest thing I've seen in twenty years. It doesn't make any sense. She's the size of a mouse. They act like she's a dragon."

"It makes perfect sense," I said. "Even a beast can recognize royalty when it is plain enough." Hesta stared at me as if I'd grown a second head. "Did you follow all of my standing instructions?" I asked. "To the letter?"

"I followed most of them, Your Highness." The words were respectful, but they certainly sounded like an insult. And why had she said them at all? I ignored them.

"Most," I repeated.

"Most. You did give me one impossible one." What was she talking about? Hesta's gaze moved between Ash and me.

"Stop being vague," I said.

The Inker sighed again. She moved to the pouch I had left that morning and picked out a strip of Cradle-infused meat. I was sure it would have been empty by now. She stepped towards me and leaned down.

She held the meat in front of Sol. Sol chirped once, leaning forward. Hesta snatched the strip away before the small beak could close on it, and flung it through the air. It landed in the nearest empty pen. It was on a slightly raised platform, raised high enough that Sol could not simply walk in.

"What are you doing?" I demanded. The Requiem stirred on my right arm. "That is her food, not a-"

"Just watch," Hesta said.

Sol chirped. The sound was furious. She hopped off my foot and toward the pen. She was as furious as a Queen ought to be. She reached the edge. The small platform was low by any standard, but Sol was small.

She spread all four wings and leapt. She made it two inches. She hit the edge face-first and slid back to the floor in a tangle of gold and black feathers. A muffled chirp came from beneath the pile. Sol rose and shook herself. She spread her wings again and launched. Her second attempt was no more successful than the first.

I stepped forward. Hesta's hand caught my arm. "Just watch."

I did not want to watch. Every part of me wanted to scoop Sol from the floor, place the meat before her, and heal any injury she might have gotten with the Cradle. I forced myself still. Hesta's grip loosened.

Sol's wings fluttered. She rose again. She went higher and higher, until she crested the platform. I stared. Her wings flapped faster. She sank. Rose. Sank, and rose again. She fell entirely, but she'd moved forward enough to fall inside the pen. She picked herself up, hopped over to the strip of meat, and chirped in triumph.

"Did she just," Ash said, very quietly. "She just flew."

Hesta shrugged. "She did. Been trying to do that since you left, and after a couple hours she started making progress. Doesn't seem to have much control over it but she can stay in the air for a little bit. Damn near put herself into the serpent's pen at one point. Would've given us both a real fright that."

Sol swayed on her tiny feet. She sat down as heavily as she could manage, her four wings drooping, and let out a soft chirp. She had not even eaten the meat. I felt something through the frail bond we shared. She was…tired. This thing she had done had cost her.

Something moved through my chest. Pride, most of all. My familiar could already imitate flight, however poorly. She would be extraordinary. Alongside the pride was something else. If she could do this, what else could she do? It had only been a single day, and she had learned this. She had breathed fire in her first hour of life. What else could she learn to do?

A man had stolen her when she had only been an egg. Now she could somewhat fly, and could breathe fire. What would happen when someone saw that? I would protect her. This, I swore to myself.

I reached into the pen, cupped Sol in both hands, and lifted her. She was very warm, and very light, and she pressed herself against my chest with a chirp that was barely more than a breath. I retrieved the strip of meat from where she had been sitting. She took it with a small, tired sound. I held her tighter than the feeding required.

"That's a strange bird," Inker Hesta said. She was watching us with an expression I could not read. "Essence beasts tend to grow faster than unbonded beasts, but don't think I've ever seen any of 'em grow this fast. Makes no sense at all."

"She will one day be the greatest steed in the world," I said. "It is no wonder at all."

The Inker shook her head and went back to her duties, muttering something under her breath.

It took a few minutes to return to our room, and during those minutes, I had not felt Sol make even the tiniest of adjustments on my head. Nor had she let out any of her chirps. The corridors were nearly empty by now. "Did she fall asleep?" I whispered.

"She fell asleep the moment we stepped outside," Ash said. Her voice was a whisper too.

Ash led us back to our room, and it was as we had left it. Ash stepped forward, placing the pillow in front of the hearth. Her placement was adequate. It was where I had put it yesterday.

I knelt and slowly lifted Sol from my horns with both hands. The tiny bird was asleep, and I did not wish to wake her. Gently, I put her on the pillow. Her beak opened in a tiny yawn that thankfully this time was not accompanied by a burst of flame.

I lit the fire. I leaned close and blew, and the flame spread across the wood, crackling softly. I knelt there and reached out for Solara. She was almost impossibly soft, and warm besides. I kept my touch light enough not to wake her. There were small footsteps. Ash knelt and reached for Sol too. I brushed the line of Solara's back. Ash brushed one of her golden wings. Our fingers touched and neither of us pulled away.

We stayed like that for a time. Today had been an eventful day. All of my days had been eventful since waking up in this new world, but today somehow stood out even amongst them.

"So she's started to fly…or float, at least. Might not be that long before she can actually fly, rather than just fall forward," Ash said.

I nodded. She was right. It was difficult to believe, even now, and I had seen it with my own two eyes. Ash continued brushing Sol's golden feathers. "Wonder what else this little breadloaf can do, if she can already do this much."

"Why are you so certain there is more?" I asked.

Ash looked at me. Her hand stopped its tracing. "Because she's yours. Why else?"

It was a good answer. A better answer than I had expected from a Hero. I smiled. Another flare of pain came from my legs, and I winced.

"It seems your legs are finally catching up to you," Ash said.

"I am perfectly fine," I said.

"Lys, I saw you wince. It just happened."

"There was no wince. What you saw simply came from me adjusting the way I sat." I straightened my back. It put me almost a head above the Hero.

Ash looked at me and raised an eyebrow. "You might regret this in the morning, Lys. It takes a little while to really hurt, but when it does, it hurts."

"We shall see."

Slowly, I rose. I moved over to the bed. Ash joined me. We sat at its edge and watched Solara. She truly was in some deep sleep. She let out a small yawn and tucked her four wings against herself.

The Hero was looking elsewhere. She was looking at her own hands, slowly wringing them against one another. I knew what this was. I knew Ash well enough by now to recognize this. There was something she was readying herself to say. Or perhaps ask. I almost asked her first when she finally spoke.

"I saw that look. When Alisa asked Edda about wanting to follow her. Edda looked at you first. And then there was what Corin said, after you healed him," Ash said slowly. "Honestly, I thought it was just…If you keep helping people, they will follow you."

Of all the things I had expected her to say, this had not been among them. "I tried to say this before too. I am not trying to become what I was," I said. "If somebody does follow me, it will not be because I asked."

"They might do it anyway, though," Ash said. "There aren't as many people laughing anymore. At your name, I mean. I don't think any of the demonbloods I've seen are anymore. How much more will it take to go from not laughing at it to revering it? And how long will it take to go from revering it to-" She did not need to say the word 'worship' for me to hear it. I had nothing to say to what she had just said. I could not control the minds of all of these people.

"So what will you do," Ash asked, "when people really do start following you just like they did before? It might happen. Even if you're not trying to make it happen."

I did not need long to consider my answer. For I had been considering it since I had seen that look on the blue-haired girl's face. "In the unlikely event that something like that truly does start to happen, I will turn them all away. It went poorly the first time," I whispered. "It will go worse the second."

Ash's look was searching. She looked away from me, and at a journal that still lay on a table. The musings of a fool. She looked at it for some time. Her eyes were unreadable. Then, Ash reached out. She put her arms around me.

I froze. Ash's arms were around me, and she was very warm. I felt her breath against my neck, and it was warm too. "I did not ask for this," I said. My voice only came out in a whisper.

"It felt like you might need it anyway," Ash said.

Slowly, I embraced her back. My embrace was far more awkward than hers. I did not know where my hands were supposed to go. One landed too high, and the other landed too low.

We stayed like that for a time.
