A bitter wind howled across the Barren Highlands, whipping up small cyclones of dust around their makeshift training ground.

Ace surveyed the setup. He’d done his best, but the sight was still hard to take in. He'd spent hours arranging targets fashioned from thorny bramble bushes at the edge of the woodland. Though he wished they had some practice dummies, the best he could do was align some of the larger loose rocks in the hillside into rows. Towering boulders jutted out from the Highlands, too massive to move, but they would serve well as terrain tests.

Not ideal, but workable.

His team gathered in a loose circle, their expressions ranging from eager to skeptical to downright terrified. The gentle half-light of the violet twilight cast long shadows behind them, stretching toward the dark line of Gloomvale Woods in the distance.

"Alright people," Ace said, falling easily into the cadence he'd used with his Marines back on Earth. "Today's about mastering your Skills, building teamwork, and learning to play off each other in the heat of battle. We've been fighting as individuals thrown together. That ends now."

Marcus crossed his arms over his chest and chuckled. "Vampire boot camp, huh? Do we get fancy t-shirts? Maybe some jackets?"

Rachel nervously picked at her nails. "I've drafted some potential fighting mechanics based on observed Skill interactions, though the sample size remains inadequate for—"

"Spreadsheets won't save us in combat," Ace interrupted, though he kept his voice gentle and calm. "But your analysis might. That's why we're here."

Tara leaned her weight onto one hip, her bow slung across her back. "I've been working on extending my Trauma Conversion range, but without actual combat..."

“We’re going to do our best,” Ace said with a shrug. “We work with what we’ve got. That’s all we can do.”

He strode to the center of their makeshift training ground and pointed to various stations he'd prepared. "Each area focuses on different skills. Tara, you'll practice healing from range there. Marcus, that's your zone for psychic control. Rachel, I've set up pattern recognition challenges over there. Olivia, balance practice and drills are going to take place on top of the taller boulders."

"And you?" Tara asked, her eyes lingering on him a moment longer than necessary.

"I'll be rotating between stations. My job is to push each of you beyond what you think you can do."

Rachel raised her hand like a student. "Based on what metrics?"

"Based on what we all need in order to stay alive," Ace replied flatly. "The System isn't grading on a curve."

As if summoned by her name, the air wavered and the small, childlike form of the System appeared, sitting cross-legged atop one of the nearby stones. Rachel gasped at the sudden poof that came with the System’s arrival, and her breath quickened.

Ace watched their resident analyst out of the corner of his eye.

She was hiding something.

"Oh, how utterly adorable!" the System exclaimed as she clapped her hands together. "Playing soldier with your little vampire friends! Do you make them call you 'sir' too?"

She giggled.

Ace deliberately turned his back on her. "Ignore her and focus on the environment instead. Don’t let her distract you. That's lesson one."

The System's face fell in an exaggerated pout. "So rude!"

Ace shrugged. "We're busy… unless you've got something useful to contribute?"

"Fine, fine. Enjoy your little drills." The System waved dismissively. “Spoil sport.”

With that, she dissolved into mist that blew away on the Highland winds.

Silence fell over the group, broken only by Rachel's rapid breathing as she tried to compose herself.

"Does she always have to do that?" Tara asked quietly.

"She feeds on fear and chaos," Ace said. "Another reason we need to work together. She wants us at each other's throats."

"That girl needs a hobby,” Marcus muttered.

“Does murder and mayhem count?” Tara asked with a scoff

Ace chuckled.

"What exactly is the endgame here?" Olivia asked. "Besides not dying, of course."

"Becoming a team that can handle whatever this world throws at us," Ace replied. "The stronger we are individually, the stronger we are together."

"So... what's next?" Marcus asked. "Are we going to run drills or something?"

"Yep," Ace said. "First, practice your Skills. You need to be able to reliably use them in combat. Second, we’ll work in tandem."

"What do you mean?" Rachel asked.

"I mean we practice in coordination with each other," Ace explained. "Rachel and Marcus, you need to learn how to control those fields of influence you used against the Carnage Fiend. Figure out how to complement each other. Tara, maybe you can work on long-range healing. Olivia, you and I should probably practice some CQC drills."

"Deal," Olivia said.

"Alright," Tara added, a hint of something unreadable crossing her features.

Marcus and Rachel glanced at each other.

"Yeah," their Mind Drinker said with a shrug. "You're probably right."

Rachel simply nodded.

Good.

It was time to shift gears—and become the hunters they needed to be in order to survive this place.

Ace clapped his hands together, dismissing everyone to their practice zones. Everyone pivoted on their heels, each heading off in a different direction as they fanned out across the Highlands.

All, that was, except for Marcus.

"Well, wait a minute." Marcus held up one hand. "Ace, you didn't talk about your Skills. Are you sure you're comfortable enough with them to focus on us instead of practicing yourself?"

The others paused, and one by one, they shifted their attention to him. A heavy weight settled on Ace's shoulders as four sets of eyes locked onto him—four souls looking for something he wasn't sure he could provide.

"Fair question,” he said.

“Yeah, Marcus, good point.” Tara headed back to the two of them. "He's right. You've seen all of our abilities, but we've only caught glimpses of yours in combat." Her voice softened. "Show us what you can do."

Ace surveyed the training ground, weighing their request against his instinct to keep parts of himself hidden. They had a point. Trust flowed both ways. If they were going to follow him into hell, they deserved to see the weapons he carried.

"Fine," he said. "But step back. This can get messy."

The four of them retreated as Ace stretched out his shoulders. A familiar cold emptiness bloomed in his chest—the killing calm he'd cultivated in war zones where hesitation meant death. His body remembered this feeling from before his transformation, a ghost of his humanity haunting what he was quickly becoming.

"As we discussed, I have three primary Skills," he explained, voice carrying on the bitter highland wind. "Duskblade Manifestation, Shadow Realm Immersion, and Lifeforce Siphon. Still working on mastering a fourth—Abyssal Armory."

He squared his stance toward the row of rocks that served as practice dummies.

"You’ve seen my Duskblades already," he said. “Any questions about those?”

“Can you control them?” Olivia asked as her eyes raked over his body. “Summon them at will?”

In answer, he simply raised his hand. In a flash of smoke, he called upon the shadow energy that coursed through his veins, now. The darkness within him responded, and shadows poured from the palm of his hand. The darkness hardened into something deadly—a wicked dagger born from the void between worlds.

“Show off,” Marcus said with a grin.

With a snap of his wrist, Ace took aim and unleashed the Duskblade. The weapon sank deep into the stone, and small cracks splintered along the rock from the point of impact. Where it struck, darkness bloomed like blood in water before the weapon disappeared in a rush of smoke.

“Alright, alright.” Olivia smirked. “Point made, Sergeant.”

“And Lifeforce Siphon?” Tara asked. “Can you control that? Or is it passive?”

“Passive.” Ace shrugged.

“And that thing you do, where you disappear…” Rachel said with an audible gulp of fear. “What’s that called again?”

“Shadow Realm Immersion.”

“You can control that, too?” Tara asked.

He nodded.

“Can we see?” Rachel asked with a tentative smile.

He debated for a moment, not entirely sure he wanted to spend the SM, but he ultimately shrugged. “Sure. I’ll need some space, though.”

As they backed away, Ace inhaled deeply in order to clear his mind and center his thoughts. He reached for the Shadow Realm—that dark mirror pressed against reality—extending his awareness beyond the veil that separated worlds.

The darkness answered his call.

Shadows climbed across his body, consuming him from feet to head. For one suspended moment, he existed in two places—half here, half elsewhere—his form translucent, the edges dissolving into nothingness.

Then, he surrendered to the void.

The world around him transformed. Colors inverted and faded to muted grays and blacks. The sky above became a swirling vortex of deep purple and midnight blue. The ground beneath his feet no longer felt solid but pliable, like walking on the memory of earth rather than earth itself. Sound dampened to a hollow whisper, as if the world had been wrapped in thick velvet.

Ten seconds.

It was the first time he’d been in the Shadow Realm without some sort of pending crisis happening in the real world, and this time, he took a moment to study his surroundings.

Most disturbing were the figures—tall, faceless entities that stood motionless at the periphery of his vision. They watched him without eyes, their elongated forms swaying slightly like underwater plants caught in an unfelt current. He tried to ignore them, but the weight of their attention never fully disappeared.

Six seconds.

Before he was forcibly kicked from the darkness, Ace wanted to test something.

He closed the distance between him and his team. In the real world, they stood in a semicircle, their expressions ranging from curiosity to apprehension. They glanced around, the turns of their heads leaving after images on the air as they searched for him. In the Shadow Realm, they appeared as dull silhouettes, though a small light radiated from each of their chests.

In the Shadow Realm, they couldn't see him, but Ace saw them—not just their bodies but glimpses of their true selves, illuminated against the darkness like stained glass catching sunlight.

Interesting.

Determined to uncover the limitations of Shadow Realm Immersion, he headed straight for Tara. He paused as he reached her, wondering what would happen if he walked clear through her, but he doubted it would affect her. There hadn’t been any mention of dangers like that in the Skill description.

Worth a shot.

Ace reached out with one hand, and it passed through her body with no resistance. In the real world, she shivered, and her brows furrowed as she glanced around the Highlands.

Interesting.

He walked through her, this time, and her essence wrapped around him like a physical embrace—warm, stubborn, and radiating a healer's instinct to mend what was broken. Something deeper pulsed between them, but he couldn’t put a name to it.

Ace would have to figure that out later.

Three seconds.

Now standing behind his team, he paused and turned on his heel. In the final seconds before he left the Shadow Realm, he studied them all.

They were haunted, every one of them—broken in different ways by the same terrible transition—but they were survivors. Each of them had fought, bled, and clawed their way here. Each of them had done what they needed to do to see another day.

That was what bonded them—just like Ace, they were all too stubborn to die.

They weren't soldiers like he had been, but they were still dangerous in their own unique ways. The System had chosen them for reasons he couldn't fathom, but in doing so, she had created fighters. Even Rachel had earned her place because, for all the System’s cruel comments, she was still here. At any point, she could’ve succumbed to her fear—but she didn’t. She fought through it every time.

They all had what it took to survive. They just needed to learn how to work together.

In a rush of color and sound, he returned to reality. The others scanned the space where he’d been, and Tara was the first to look over her shoulder. She gasped in surprise and set her hand on her undead heart, closing her eyes as she fought back a surge of panic.

“Don’t do that,” she chided.

Ace grinned. “Didn’t realize you were so jumpy.”

Her eyes narrowed, but she couldn't hide the playful smile tugging at the edge of her lips.

“Can you use it all together?” Rachel asked. “Combine it into multiple attacks?”

Ah.

They wanted a show.

Alright, then.

Without a word, Ace unleashed everything. The hunger within him stirred—not just for blood, but for the power that came with surrendering to the shadows. His body dissolved into wisps of darkness as he tapped into Shadow Realm Immersion, and the world shifted once more to its inverted state. He darted through the void with inhuman speed, heading for a tall rock formation jutting from the barren landscape that roughly resembled a broad-shouldered figure.

He summoned a Duskblade, and it instantly obeyed. Shadow energy coalesced in both hands and formed the now-familiar dagger of concentrated void. He took aim, channeling Shadow Mark, and a target appeared in the center of the stone.

He slipped instantly out of the Shadow Realm, before his ten seconds were even up, and he flung the first blade with a grunt of effort. The weapon pierced the stone, and where it struck, darkness spread outward like living ink. The stone cracked from the sheer force of the blow, fissures spreading along its surface as the shadow energy wormed its way deeper into the heart of the rock.

With a deafening crack, the stone exploded outward, reduced to dust and fragments that rained down across the training ground. Ace stood motionless even as bits of stone rained down on him, surveying the carnage he’d wrought on the makeshift practice dummy.

Though the display had cost him more than he'd intended, the stunned expressions on his teammates' faces told him it had served its purpose. They needed to see what was possible. What they could become.

The monster inside him, the one he had fought to control since coming here, had shown them a glimpse of power they would need to survive this world.

"Holy shit." Marcus finally breathed, awe and fear tangled in his voice.

"Your control is remarkable," Rachel said, her lips parting in awe.

Olivia's gaze lingered on him with newfound appreciation, her eyes tracing the shadow energy still dissipating around his hands like smoke.

“Now,” he said, his voice breaking the dumbfounded silence. He looked at each of them in turn, seeing not just what they were, but what they could become. "Let's get to work."
