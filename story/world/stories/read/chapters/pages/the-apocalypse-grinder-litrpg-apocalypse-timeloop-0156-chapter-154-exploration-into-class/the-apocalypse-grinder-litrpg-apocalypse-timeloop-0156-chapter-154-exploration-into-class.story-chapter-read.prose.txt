Whirling to face the new arrival, Ronan conjured a shortsword in his right hand and formed a mana bolt in his left. The panic was overkill, as there was only a solitary goblin rogue staring back at him, hatred burning in its gaze.

Without waiting for another moment, the rogue rushed towards him. Seeing its slaughtered kin had clearly driven it wild, as goblins were wont to do. That worked fine for Ronan.

Unleashing the mana bolt into its face, he jumped forwards and drove the shortsword through its heart a moment after the explosion dissipated. A twin chime followed.

Pulling up that kill notification, he was satisfied to see that he’d gained yet another level. Not only that, but the second message informed him that he had actually completed stage 2.

First he did want to have a look at some of the interesting classes he’d unlocked access to. The full list was exhaustive already, but most of them weren’t worth pursuing—either they were like one he’d already used but worse, or simply not beneficial whatsoever.

While there were more classes than he was noting, the six Ronan picked out were either interesting from a theoretical point of view or were classes he wanted to explore in future iterations. The first of those was rather basic, but demonstrated a pattern that may continue into higher ranked classes.

Lord of War (Rare)

With overwhelming might and strategic insight, the lord of war dominates his foes. Possessed of a mastery over all weapons, there is no tactic or scenario that can possibly catch him off guard. If the field is battle, make sure you stand on his side, for those arrayed against the lord will find themselves filled with terror as he begins his work.

A while back Ronan had unlocked the weapons master class, which seemed to be an inferior version of the lord of war class. Both required him to possess a certain number of weapon-based masteries, along with a slew of other requirements, while also boosting those particular attributes.

In a future iteration he would definitely be picking lord of war. Spending some time to develop all of his weapon masteries to higher tiers and unlocking the bonuses they offered was well worth the time investment.

This class also showed that there were likely higher rarity classes he could unlock for either discovering more weapon masteries, or bringing the ones he already possessed to higher tiers at level 10. He wondered if there was an elite version of this class for having 10 masteries at tier I, II, or even III.

The next class on the list was similar, in that it was also seemingly an evolution of a weaker class. However, it was an evolution of one of the original seven classes as far as Ronan could tell.

Wizard (Rare)

While it wasn’t possible to see the descriptions of class skills before choosing any given class, Ronan could guess what the wizard skills did without much difficulty. Mana bolt he’d already encountered, while mana barrier was clearly a magical shield. As for elemental aspecting, that was trickier, but still relatively self-explanatory; he suspected it would allow shifting other magic skills to a specific element.

Creating a fireball from mana bolt or a barrier of water with the mana barrier skill sounded like the sort of thing that would be possible. The wizard class was interesting, but more for what it showed him about magic, than as a path he actually wished to follow.

Sorcery was his chosen route to magical mastery, so he would no longer be letting the system corrupt his understanding. Not to say he wouldn’t take the lessons that simply knowing of the class offered, but he doubted it was worth his time leveling up. Especially as it was only rare.

The third was the last rare class on his list of noteworthy classes. The name had seemed rather boring, but the class description piqued Ronan’s interest.

Perfectionist (Rare)

There are many who chase higher rarity skills, higher realms of cultivation, or ever greater levels in their pursuit of power. You shun those imperfect, hasty paths in favour of true refinement; the mastery of oneself, the world, and all that falls within those bounds.

The threshold for unlocking new masteries is minutely lower.

Gain progress in all masteries infinitesimally faster.

Challenger has at least 4 masteries at tier II or higher at level 10

Challenger has at least 1 mastery at tier III or higher at level 10

Of all the classes that Ronan had unlocked until now, perfectionist was the most interesting by far. To begin with, it gave him the most stat points per level of any rare class. More than that, all of those stat points were his to do with as he pleased, without any forced assignment.

That wasn’t mentioning the debuff it had, in that the class did not grant him a single skill upon selection. No class skills, no regular skills.

The tradeoff for that was the increased stat point count, and he suspected the fact that it gave a flat boost to every single mastery in existence—an absolutely absurd boon—played a large part in that as well. If nothing else, Ronan would certainly try this class a few times simply to see what random masteries he might gain, that he hadn’t quite breached the threshold for in the past.

Moving onto the first epic class, things got a lot spicier. While Ronan had possessed an epic class before—unrelenting juggernaut—he’d never had the option to do so from level 10. The 40 levels of stat gain before first evolution and the better benefits would make all the difference.

Energy Manipulator (Epic)

Challenger has Mystic Energy cultivation at the first threshold (sixth realm) or higher

Weirdly, this class was less stringent in its requirements than something like perfectionist, as there were no level limits in the requirements. However, Ronan suspected the increased rarity was down to the fact that achieving the requirements was ridiculously hard for anyone who didn’t have his heritage, even if they were level 50 or perhaps 100.

Gaining triple mystic energy cultivation talent for a few iterations might help him push it even further than he’d already reached. Having hit the first threshold, moving the progress forwards had become excessively difficult.

With only one class skill, he had to assume it was a powerful one, but the lack of any free stat points did put him off the class a bit. Even so, all the investment going to his resources wasn’t bad, he just enjoyed having some freedom of choice in how he developed.

While the first four noteworthy classes were all hard to unlock and provided interesting benefits or unlocked new avenues of thought, things began to get truly groundbreaking when it came to the final two on his list.
