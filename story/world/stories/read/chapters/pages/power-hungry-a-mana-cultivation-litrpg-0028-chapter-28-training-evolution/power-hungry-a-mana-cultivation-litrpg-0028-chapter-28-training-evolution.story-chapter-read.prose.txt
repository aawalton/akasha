It was hard. Even though Cale was not doing more than leaning with his body weight and use his backfoot to control the speed, even minimal movement made controlling the energy manifold more difficult. With each shift of his foot, or nudge to throw Sintra higher up his back, the violent mana of the murderbots he was draining from the cores fought Cale. It was like trying to dribble a soccer ball. Requiring high control and awareness. Hard enough on its own? Great, let’s do it on solid ice!

The biting wind of the Blacksteel Citadel certainly didn’t make things easier. But at least the terrain was flat, as it seemed the convoy a mile ahead was traveling through a makeshift path. It was surrounded by jagged structures of red and black, twenty feet high. They glowed with a faint red light, a fusion of metal and crystal, with stray filaments waving in the wind.

But slowly Cale mustered the required focus and grit. It didn’t get any easier, but Cale was getting better. All the while Aura guided him as patiently as she could. Which was not very patiently, but Cale appreciated it none the less.

“Be pre-emptive with your control!” Aura snapped. “If you know you are going to move and you know the mana will fight, then why are you repeating the same mistake over and over again?!”

Aura was right, no matter how annoying she was. Cale nodded to himself. Around the swirling energy circling in his solar plexus he imagined a round cage with high walls of solid steel. He braced them with his mind. Then he pushed the speed pedal gently on his backfoot. The energy inside him lashed out, but Cale focused on the wall. He pressed the violence of the mana back into the smooth circling mass he was creating.

“Good!” Aura said. “You are not entirely hopeless at this.”

Cale allowed himself a mirthless smile. The convoy ahead of him was still over half a mile. His pace with Sintra on his back was slow, but Cale wasn’t in a rush. He still had nine murderbot manacores left to suck dry.

If they get attacked again, I’ll be able to react. But I have to leave Sintra here, which puts her at risk. Not a choice I’m a fan of, but I know the difference between soft and stupid.

This went on for an hour. Cale knew the other recruits must be wondering why he was advancing so slow. They had to have seen him. He could always say it was because he didn’t want Sintra to start bleeding again. If they were annoying, and by that Cale meant smart, they would ask why didn’t Cale zip Sintra to the convoy, so she could be put on the escort platform. Cale didn’t have an answer for that yet.

But he was starting to get a handle of the cultivation. During that hour, he managed to absorb four mana cores. And more importantly, his understanding was increasing. Aura stopped yelling at him, and even gave him compliments. That must have meant he was doing something dramatically right.

“You are still not attuning the mana enough, but considering the circumstances, this has to do,” she muttered.

Cale agreed. To get the most squeeze out of this mana, he would have to work completely still, condensing the mana during the attunement process to the point it was almost solid. Ideally, crystallized, but Cale had never managed to do that yet, even though he instinctively knew that was the end game. This was wasteful, but life doesn’t always give you ideal circumstances.

In the immortal words of my great master Darius, “you have to play the hand you’re dealt.”

Or something like that…

It wasn’t all for naught though. Cale had made an important discovery. He had understood consciously how to work with mana while moving. That was an absolutely central step in achieving combat proficiency with techniques. He would use the first chance he got to practice Ghost Step while moving. Trying it right now would have been awkward, as Sintra would have dropped like a rock off him.

Another hour passed. The convoy was a quarter mile away from him. He saw Ragnak wave at him. They weren’t going to slow down though, and that suited Cale just fine.

He had two more cores left. While he had acquired more mastery over the process, he was also getting tired. The mental and physical strain were starting to mount up. Fighting the boiling, storming, surging energy was becoming a real struggle. But he was so close. So close to advancement…

Cale gritted his teeth, his foot slipping slightly on the hoverboard. His control was slipping.

Sintra stirred weakly on his back, and for half a second, his knee wobbled. The mana inside him seized on that moment of weakness, surging wildly like a feral beast sensing blood.

Damn it—hold it!

He tried to push it back down. Tried to force it into submission like he always did.

But this wasn’t the dojo. This wasn’t a neat, refined process. He wasn’t working with clean Nevani mana—he was fighting the raw essence of the Citadel itself.

The old method wasn’t going to cut it. Then he remembered how Aura had instructed her when he had his first breakthrough to upper Body Tempering. Darius had told him to dominate the mana, to conquer it under his will. That hadn’t worked. But that was how he was still doing things.

He needed a new approach. One with less effort.

"Don’t fight it. Don’t try to crush it. Aura told me to breathe with it, to synchronize. Not to dominate, but to rule. A ruler doesn’t strangle his people into submission—he guides them, channels them, gives them purpose."

A flicker of understanding clicked into place. He had been treating it like a battle of wills, like he had to conquer the energy.

But energy didn’t need to be conquered. It needed direction.

So he stopped resisting. Let it move. It surged up from the circling mass of energy Cale was creating, like a jolt of fat lightning. Cale’s instinct was to crush it down. He overrode it. He met the surge with his will and guided it in an arc back to the circle.

And suddenly—it clicked. A pulse ran through his core, like a taut wire snapping into perfect tune.

The speed at which the mana was circling around his solar plexus increased with the momentum of the surge. Another violent surge arose. Cale directed it back towards the center, and the momentum sped up. It became harder and harder to control, but something else was happening too.

The mana was solidifying.

Cale hadn’t managed to attune mana so refined yet. And now that he was using the momentum of the struggling mana against itself, he made a huge leap of progress.

Cale solidified his gains and absorbed the mana.

The violent red mana sank into his body, not in rebellion, but in agreement. It flowed, smooth and natural, winding into his solar plexus like a river finding its course. From there the mana flowed into his body, strengthening his muscles, bones, tendons, even his skin. He could feel the flush on his cheeks, and suddenly the sharp wind of the Citadel felt less oppressive.

Cale exhaled sharply. His balance steadied. His frame solidified. Sintra felt lighter on his back. He tossed the spent mana core on the ground.

Something shifted inside him. Cale felt more solid. More alert.

More powerful.

“Aura—”

“You did it,” she said with a gentle grace. “Body Tempering Five."

A pulse of strength surged through Cale’s limbs. He clenched his fingers, feeling the new sharpness to his instincts, the deeper well of energy now under his control. His body had changed. Stronger. Faster. His breath came easier. The cold bit him less. The world felt just a fraction slower.

A grin spread across his face.

Hell yeah.

The hoverboard glided smoothly beneath him as the convoy loomed ahead. He had leveled up.
