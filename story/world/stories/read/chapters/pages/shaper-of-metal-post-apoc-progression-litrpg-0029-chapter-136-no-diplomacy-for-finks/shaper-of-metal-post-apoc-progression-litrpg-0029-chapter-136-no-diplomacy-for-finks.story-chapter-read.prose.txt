Well, that was surprising. It appears diplomacy has broken down. At least I’ve got him on an assault charge, I guess?

Jack’s heavy jacket was ripped up, but protected him from the glass edges; his legs weren’t so lucky, his pants getting cut through and his skin raked. Fortunately, his skin was tough enough to take it and only got superficially cut. Enough to bleed for a little bit, but nothing worrisome for a Non. He could ignore it.

He soared over a hedgerow to hit pavement and roll. The first thing he did, while he had no real capacity to get his bearings anyway, was get a communication to Neex. “Send it! Send the cavalry, send them now!”

An anxious reply immediately returned. “Roger, Jack — doing it! Hold on!”

Almost simultaneously, Jack channeled memorite immediately to himself.

He heard lots of people screaming, but over it was Mr. Heavenward’s commanding voice, calling, “It’s a ratfinkin’ fed — take him down!”

“That’s my son!” came Freddy’s agonized voice.

“No, he isn’t, Freddy — it’s a power-infused facade! They replaced him.” His voice quickly faded with distance.

Freddy’s response was garbled, but it could only be described as a loud reaction of horror.

When the world stopped spinning, and Jack came to a stop, he flashed his eyes around to assess things.

A guard who appeared to have already been outside was just pulling out an electric stunner to train on him. Jack quickly transmuted his raw memorite into liquid-like material even as he moved it directly toward the weapon; it swept toward him, and it was going to be close…

The memorite formed right before the shot went off, positioned right in front of the barrel. The metal was zapped, electrified, and was close enough to reach and touch the man’s hand, even as the metal was still electrified and getting zapped.

The man plopped immediately and violently to the ground as he took the voltage through his body, and his weapon went flying from his grip.

In the distance, Jack could hear the cacophony of many sonic booms going off, which was most certainly extensive cavalry on the way. He was also starting to hear some sort of buzzing noise without a definitive source, as if coming from everywhere.

What the hell is that?

Jack pushed himself up just in time to see another guard point a weapon at him from inside the broken window. He couldn’t see his dad through the reflective surface of the rest of the windows.

Damn it! That plastic weirdo is making a run for it. Where the hell is my dad?

Jack took a dive that was sideways and a bit forward, to utilize the cover of the nearby bushes. He saw the bright bolt of lightning shoot right over the top of the hedge and hit the ground, expending itself uselessly.

“You’re under arrest in the name of Memoria and the Agentus!” Jack called. “Stop resisting!”

“Frag yourself, fink!” came the reply. Another zap could be heard, but it lost itself in the hedge, and smoke was the only result.

Fink — that’s kind of an old word, isn’t it? I guess the old became the new yet again.

Under cover, Jack took the time to pop off his tungsten necklace, rings, and his mostly steel keys to collapse memorite into and make a fully functional metal base to work with, quickly forming a cord length. He kept the mass of memorite he already formed as a separate glob, and added a little more to it, while making it more solid.

Forming the vague shape of a head out of it, he peeked it above the hedge, and it promptly got zapped. Jack popped up to get a visual and zipped the cord across the space, to which the man only managed to flinch slightly before it was wrapped around his neck. Jack put immediate pressure on his carotid. The man dropped his weapon to grab at his neck in alarm, throwing himself away from the window.

Just as Jack moved to awkwardly get over the hedges, another shooter moved into view. Before he could even aim, Jack flung the blob of memorite but flattened and slowed it along the way, deliberately ‘pushing’ with around half of his strength, which was about 400 kilograms of moving force, at least across a short distance.

It barreled into the man like a charging linebacker, knocking him and carrying him into a collision with yet another person. Jack followed up rather chaotically and blindly, stretching the blob and erratically moving it around to keep knocking any other assholes waiting inside. Finally, he came through, and decided to make use of some of his training to do an acrobatic dive and roll through the broken window — particularly as his current gear state was pretty damn light.

When he came up, four guards were down, one rising with his hand on a table, and missing a weapon. All the ‘new age nobility’ types that were in the parlor before had vanished like ghosts, as had Mr. Heavenward and his father.

Shit.

He saw the bartender turning away from a panel in the wall behind the bar, which promptly disappeared by way of some sort of hologram, as the man began running away from it…

As Jack moved toward him, the windows exploded inward as a storm of ANPs and weapon-brandishing Exemplars crashed in and began shouting for everyone to get down and stay down.

Jack’s metal moved much faster than he could — he pulled his blob of memorite away and sped it with full power for the fleeing bartender. Just before the bartender rounded a corner, the memorite wrapped around his ankle and tripped him.

One enemy guard, now freed of Jack’s disrupting memorite swarm, began pulling up his weapon, hatred in his eyes for Jack. In his peripheral vision, Jack saw two Exemplars screaming and pointing rifles at the fanatical idiot.

Frag me, that’s suicidal!

Green ooze suddenly shlorped over the man, encasing him and hardening, freezing him in position even as his arm still strained.

“Ah!” came the familiar voice of Agent Ooze as his creepy floating helmet and jacket came into view. “No need for that, Citizen. A shame you added another charge onto your record for nothing, hmm?”

Jack, able to keep his focus on the bartender, quickly had him totally wrapped up in semi-liquid metal. As the trapped man screamed obscenities and struggled, Jack floated him over.

“I see your handiwork around this man’s neck, Agent Steel,” Wall-of-Ooze called, probably using the right moniker considering Jack was currently ‘blown cover’ undercover. “You might want to relieve the pressure before brain damage begins.”

“Oh shit!” Jack cried, turning briefly to regard the unconscious, purple-faced man on the floor and pulling off the cord from his neck. “Thanks… I’m a bit, ah… hyperfocused right now.”

“Of course, my friend. Don’t mention it!”

“Where are they?!” came a modulated voice suddenly in front of Jack. He blinked and saw a man standing there in a weird, vaguely ANP-style outfit — a golden helmet with a prominent angular crest out of the back, and wearing an old-school aviator bomber jacket with fur on it. Jetset — the flying speedster powerhouse. His head swiveled around like a fast-forwarded bird. “Damn this buzzing! They’re jamming everything. Even the radio is nothing but static. We need fraggin’ intel!”

At that commentary, Jack tried to ping Neex… and found even his connection was also disturbed and muted. That rather spooked him, as it shouldn’t be possible. However, he was sure there was something communicated across. He felt alarm — for him — from Neex. He tried to send ‘safe’ across, but it was unclear what was translated.

In any case, he couldn’t do much about it, and there was no time, either. He had to move.

Jack nodded in the direction of the restrained bartender he was floating over. “Question this guy, someone!” Meanwhile, Jack ran behind the bar and over to the spot the bartender had been manipulating. “I saw him screwing around over here right before he ran. There’s a hologram.”

It looked like a generic bit of wall behind alcohol bottles. He put his hand slowly up and watched it pass through. Feeling around with his hand, he found a simple switch. Flipping it caused the hologram to fade, revealing a circular panel apparently made of steel, and next to it, a keypad.

“There’s a keypad!” Jack called, but momentarily, he collapsed memorite into the panel, feeling around through it. He identified quickly that the panel was like a safe door with hidden hinges on the inside, and a flagrant hollow. The metal abruptly ended. It didn’t feel like there was anything in it.

Strange.

Jack manipulated the metal to simply unbolt itself from the material of the wall, pulling the whole safe door out. Behind it was a continuation of the stone hollow no bigger than a porthole that could fit a man’s head. It looked to curl down on the inside. Jack’s sharp eyes caught bits of fabric fuzz sitting just inside the lip, before the descent.

What the h- oh. I think I get it. Holy shit.

“Mr. Heavenward went down this thing alone!” Jack called, already turning around and dropping the metal safe to the side.

Almost as if he’d teleported, Jetset appeared next to him to peer into the hole. “No fraggin’ way! Nobody can fit…” He trailed off. “Ugh. Seriously? That’s creepy.”

“Yes. He’s made of plastic or something.” Jack cast his gaze over where the bartender was getting questioned, but the man’s countenance alone showed how little he was willing to cooperate.

Agent Ooze shlorped across the space, cascading himself over the bar toward the hole. “I believe I’m the goo for the job, here, wouldn’t you say, gentlemen?”

“I guess,” Jetset said. “I damn sure can’t fit.”

“Could you start looking for my dad, then? He should be a high-priority target.” Jack asked. “Maybe down to the left from the bar. I’ll take right?”

“Freddy? Sure, why not?” Hovering up into the air briefly, Jetset made wind as he blasted away, headed in the direction the bartender had been going.

Jack started jogging in the opposite direction, feeling an immediate sinking feeling in his gut that he’d lost his dad already. Just beyond the parlor area inside the main hall, he paused, remembering how he’d noted his dad’s ‘metal bling’ and such. The smartglasses, the watch, the two rings. Perhaps that wand thing, if he still had it.

Can I track the unique impression of those things together? Frag me, I have to try it.

He stopped and closed his eyes, sending out memorite high speed in every direction and extremely diffused, much as he’d practiced and successfully executed for the zone areas of the fortress in the last PACCs match.

Immediately, he was getting informational noise from a hundred things — household items, door hinges, bolts, tools, kitchen stuff, and electronic device ‘spam’ in excess. Obviously, quite a few people had the right to use phones in the place, and then there were Agentus running around on top of it.

Jack wrapped his memorite around everything he found, ‘tagging’ it with tiny amounts of his channeled substance and then blotting it out of his perception after he’d concluded it was junk info. It worked well enough because the non-moving, effectively ‘anchored’ memorite acted as flags, and was continually different from that which he kept in motion, still hunting and seeking more items.

It was a process of elimination, and not exactly ideal for presumed time constraints.

In his search, he came across the elevators, which naturally were full of metal. On a hunch, Jack moved his operation and focus over and inspected them. He soon found they were not moving, but an elevator cab was way down into a deep basement. It was many stories down, which made it more like it was into the structure of the platform itself.

Makes sense. The stone tube went down that far, too.

Deciding he had best head down, Jack collapsed memorite into the elevator door and used his powers to cut out a section to act as a platform. He stepped into the elevator shaft, a prominent, long series of cables stretching down and down from the middle. Stepping onto his platform and crouching, Jack started lowering himself to the bottom, pulling out the handy keychain light to see by in the dark. He eventually made out the top of the elevator cab below.

It’s a damned shame I can’t communicate with anyone right now. Maybe I should’ve chased down an Exemplar before doing this, but I’m betting I need to hurry.

Nonetheless, as he went, he tried pinging Neex his location, somehow, and ‘sent alarm’ toward Memoria. He was pretty sure neither thing would work, but it was worth a shot.

Arriving at the bottom, he forged a hole out of the cab ceiling. In the elevator compartment, he once again used his powers to cleanly carve out a section through the elevator compartment doors, not bothering to open anything conventionally. In fact, he was harvesting material to bring along with him, and in quite satisfactory masses.

Not sure how much he’d be committing his powers to other things, Jack turned one of the panels into a carryable shield and reinforced it, getting it nice and braced on his arm so he could lead with it going forward.

Stepping out, he found himself in a rough stone compartment lit dimly with red emergency lights, and corridors going three ways. The buzzing was worse than above, a continuous, baleful sound reverberating off the stone. He intrinsically didn’t like it.

Rather than pick a direction, Jack started his process again of flooding memorite in every direction, seeking out his dad’s patterns of metal on his person. The ‘noise’ was awful from the general superstructure of the area, because it was saturated with the metal framework of Memoria’s levitational structure, like veins of metal inside the meat that was the stony bedrock. Any other day, it would be kind of fascinating, but he had no time to appreciate it.

By simply discounting the structure and focusing on the hollows, he was able to tune out the noise of the environment. Doing this, he quickly found evidence of metal material carried by people, moving around a central location, most of them apparently bunched up within. A few were still moving rapidly, in what he guessed was to get to that place.

If there are no signs of Agent Ooze down here, it must mean that the place Mr. Heavenward went is different from this one.

Checking over the outside people carefully, in sequence, he quickly found the last straggler had on a gold watch, two metal rings, and smart goggles on his head.

Operating through Interpret, Jack collapsed memorite into all those pieces and then pulled down, to pin who was hopefully his dad down on the floor. He also manipulated the metal to form tighter seals on the target’s skin, to avoid him being able to slip it off.

Anchoring the metal, effectively, he sensed the target struggling futilely to resist and get up, but the anchored pieces at his hands and head weren’t moving. At best, he could twist his body around.

Jack rushed down the central stone corridor to get to the target. He had to take numerous twists and turns in what looked to be a maze. He heard voices as if in the distance somewhere. He also passed another elevator.

My fellow Mems are mostly rounding up people above, likely dealing with guards and servants aplenty, but this place is where I’d guess the VIPs went. An escape plan?

He could hear his dad screaming just before he got to him, not far from a door that led into a square, central, and separated room that looked heavily reinforced. In getting to his dad, he passed one corner and saw another heavy door into it on the far side. Everyone in the area he’d detected with metal bits was inside that room, other than Freddy.

“Let me go, you bastards!” his dad yelled, caught in shrill hysterics as he awkwardly tried to jerk his body toward the door, despite his jewelry pinned down and holding him. He was bleeding at the wrist from his efforts. “I won’t let you do to me what you did to my son! I won’t, damn you! I won’t let you ruin-” He trailed off, sobbing hysterically.

Jack sighed as he walked up. “Dad, you’re confused.” He used some of the spare metal he was toting to form rough bands and wrap his dad up around the torso, pinning his arms. “You have no clue what is going on here, and you’re blind to something pure fraggin’ evil you’re working for. Basically, what they claim of Memoria is what they are.” He loosened the bonds from the jewelry on his dad and turned him around, floating him up so his feet were just above the floor. “If I told you the truth of things, it would sound more realistic than the fantasies they’ve filled your head with. Memoria isn’t perfect, but we literally need her. Just trust me, please.”

Freddy’s face was disgusted and horrified at seeing metal wrap around and levitate him. It only intensified as he met Jack’s eyes. “You… you’re doing this…” His face seemed to twist in pain. “What are you? Why my son? Why?! Just to hurt us?!”

“I don’t have time for this, Dad. Why are those people in that room?”

“To escape your chains!”

Jack frowned and collapsed memorite into the room, feeling through the structure. There was metal in the floor, in an endless series of panels, with hollows inside. He detected a network of various wiring, too, linking them all. It went down and down, deep into the structure.

I don’t understand exactly what I’m looking at, but I don’t particularly like it. Aside from that, if I go in there, I may get attacked by dozens of fanatical people at once.

Without another word, Jack began moving his dad rapidly back the way he came, with Jack following right behind, intending to evac him and report to the first person he could find. He rounded a corner…

The detonation and the fireball almost took him in the back. Thankfully, he felt the first rumblings through feedback from Interpret, through the memorite still touching the room, which was suddenly and violently scoured.

Jack took the panel he was still toting by levitation and braced it with all he had, squishing it into the floor, ceiling, and one wall. He knew it was too strong to totally block off the corridor.

Even as the wall got slammed and pushed, and a fireball propagated around it, Jack was curving and stretching the metal into a partial bubble around him and his dad. He flung his shield into the gap to add more needed material, in a race that should’ve been impossible.

But his speed was superhuman, his perceptions enhanced, and his instincts were well-honed by rigorous and grueling training. Furthermore, it was an act of survival, and not just for him. He acted without thinking.

Heat battered him, and one lick of flames pushed around the sealing metal bubble, searing into his dad’s cheek, before it was enclosed completely.

Pouring the extremes of his output into the effort, far above 100%, Jack actively held the metal in place against a terrible impact pressing in from all sides, so strong it was squeezing the confines ever tighter. He was raked with a vibration and a painful sound seemingly fit to shake the world apart, quickly driving him deaf.

Gritting his teeth, Jack held on for dear life, knowing he could not keep up this output for long, energy-wise.
