Gavin’s finger swept the blue screen aside. Eyes of solid emerald with not a speck of white turned up, capturing the cryptic numbers which hung on the edge of his ‘HUD’. The ‘gamers’, as they often referred to themselves, used these numerals to tell time.

He had learned to do the same.

“I’ve already lost half the morning,” the goblin grumbled.

The forums were too addictive. Shaking his head, Gavin hopped off his bed before descending the narrow stairs of their apartment. A good, diligent goblin needed to put such things aside. Thinking this, he entered the living room. As usual, everything was too big.

Th-th-thud.

Gavin stopped to hammer on a barrel occupying a corner of the room. The dull beat was half-swallowed by the liquid inside.

“Elroy, you lazy bastard, isn’t it about time you woke up?” he roared.

The werewolf gave no answer.

Damn mutt, always taking his time. As usual, Gavin had to do everything himself. It’d been like this for the last two months. Grumbling under his breath, the goblin jerked a gray cloak off its hook. Then he snagged the heavy battleaxe left beside the door.

After checking his soul to make sure everything was in order, he peered at the fate compass he’d hung off the wall. It was a cheap piece he’d finagled from a witch a year back. There was an error in the enchantment. It was easy to see why. The marble wobbled around danger, flavored with opportunity and discord.

The goblin tapped the glass twice. As expected, the ball settled closer to neutral.

“Worthless,” he complained. “One of these days, I’m going to have real money. And when I do, I’ll buy something better than this knockoff.”

Gavin opened the door.

It was late morning. Here in Pyrkagiás, that meant the smoldering clouds which filled the fourth layer’s cavernous ceilings glowed with a yellow-red hue. This dim twilight radiance couldn’t compare with the stark light of day, so the city had seen fit to augment the light with a chain of street lamps set along the roads and hanging from every building.

The dark-skinned goblin locked the door behind him. Magic shimmered, weak wards sealing the room.

“Oy there, Gavin,” a cheerful voice called. A minotaur leaned over a metal rail, waving her hand. “I haven’t seen Elroy out and about. He isn’t in a bit of trouble, is he?”

“You don’t need to worry about the old wolf, my dear Elpis,” Gavin said suavely after entering the courtyard. He gave the lady a bow. “No trouble at all. He’s just busy with a breakthrough in his cultivation. But, I’ll be sure to let him know you’ve been calling for him.”

“You do that for me, love,” Elpis replied, looking pleased. “He’s a good man, that Elroy. I don’t know why you quit your jobs with the guard, but I won’t be poking my head where it doesn’t belong. Do take care of yourselves out there.”

Gavin flashed a toothy grin. “You know I will.”

Without another word, he entered the main street.

Pyrkagiás was bustling as usual, a burning dry heat clinging to the air. The goblin drank the scents of the city. Merchants hawked their wares, eyes glinting with greedy desire. A red carriage rolled by, the occupant radiating arrogant disdain. Lesser demons stepped aside, gazing at their betters with resentful envy.

Delicious.

Lords and ladies might cavort in walled-off palaces, but life here in the dregs was the truest home for any redcap. The stew of misery was like a fine wine. With one heady whiff, Gavin could feel the emotions curling in his gut. A beverage of psychic energy which helped dissolve the essences of blood and wood lingering in his stomach.

With a light step, Gavin followed along the road. Being a wee little goblin, he knew better than to stray into the heavy traffic near the center. Better to stick near the walls, otherwise his six and seven foot companions would trample him underfoot.

A pair of garudas flashed overhead.

“Lucky bastards.”

A good goblin had to keep his feet on the ground. Wing tools? Posh. If he used them, people would think he was an imp or a faerie. Brooms? Forget about it. May as well throw his rep in the gutter. If anyone saw him on one, Gavin would never live it down.

No. What he needed was a good pair of boots. He had his eye on an enchanted set. Shadow magic, which would let him slip in and out of the dark. Now, there was a stylish way to travel.

Without missing a step, Gavin suddenly slipped into a side alley. Out of view, the goblin called on his magic.

Natural magic existed in a space between spells and ki. Mana was lighter and closer to the psyche. It was more easily contained by concepts and ideas than the energy of life. It bent eagerly to the will. Yet, though natural magic was intuitive rather than schooled, it still required skill.

Just like ki had realms of mastery, so too was natural magic divided into the domains. These domains were: shaping, alteration, mysticism, imbuement, authority, emancipation, and sovereignty.

What he used was shaping, alteration, and mysticism.

Gavin melted. His skin flowed like water, muscles thickening as he filled his body with figments of mass. His dark skin paled to a deep tan. His long, sharp nose transformed, becoming short and bulbous. Wisps of dark brown hair poured from his chin. Eyes of solid emerald turned blue, gaining whites and pupils. Fine adjustments followed, smoothing fingers before tweaking his face.

Once all the transformations were done, Gavin dissolved his fancy shirt, replacing it with plated leather. The battleaxe was projected from his soul, the heavy weight clipped to his belt. Finally, the dwarf tossed on his gray cloak over the top, hiding his face and body.

“Goblins?” he sneered, his voice a deep rumble. “How dare you. What business would an honorable dwarf have with such vile creatures?”

A wicked grin spread on the dwarf’s lips. The sharp points of his teeth had been replaced by a more conventional expression.

Flesh was the aspect magic wielded by redcaps. Its natural expression was violence. Alteration softened this destructive tendency into a nurturing support, while shaping guided the structure of his transformation. Mysticism was necessary to merge magic into ki. This let Gavin project an authentic aura and mimic simple arts.

He’d always been adept at transformation. In part, this was thanks to the trait: malleable flesh, which he picked up during his first consolidation. The rest, however, was skill and talent.

Smoothing his smile, the grumpy-looking dwarf marched out of the alley and onto the main road.

A few minutes later, he pushed through a pair of heavy doors to enter a dingy tavern. Gavin tugged at the hood of his cloak, making sure that it hid his false face. Smoke curled in a poorly lit space, intoxicating fumes stinging his nose. Blue eyes swept the interior. A salaman nursed his drink while a tall Satyr stood behind the counter.

The tavern was dead, which was normal this time of the morning.

Knock. Knock.

“The usual,” Gavin said gruffly after drumming the bar twice.

His thick hand uncurled, leaving behind a roll of silver. The barkeep swept it up silently. His head jerked to his right.

“Room’s in the back.”

Gavin entered the private space.

The room was small, a single ether light lamp providing weak illumination and an occasional flicker. The building was ill-conceived. Ether stagnated at points, creating bad flows. The lamp was starved as a result. Gavin paid it no mind. Instead, he took one of three chairs, his face toward the door.

Onto the table, he set three items. First was a metal cylinder. Second was a bowl of brass. The vessel had a hole-filled cover, three tines rising from the center. Last was a crystal cup. Gavin put it face down on the table, feeding it with a touch of mana.

The air reverberated, the room deathly quiet.

He waited.

It didn’t take long.

Andreas was a hogmin. The thickly built demon had a face like a pig’s. His skin was a dark peach, verging on brown. Hogmin weren’t tall, as warrior-class demons went, but they were thickly built. Andreas was more so than usual, giving him a visage akin to an ogre’s.

“Shut the door and take a seat,” Gavin ordered roughly.

The hogmin stared at the dwarf, brown eyes judging. Then, quietly, he did as he was told, sliding the bolt into place.

“I’ve heard you sell… potential.”

“You’ve done more than just hear it, Andreas,” Gavin asserted. The dwarf looked over his bulbous nose, blue eyes sharp under the hood of his gray cloak. “You’ve seen it.”

The hogmin glowered. “You’ve been spying on me.”

“We don’t deal with anyone without a background check,” Gavin returned. “In your first life, you were owned by Sir Alwan of the Flóga Pétra mining company. Due to your service, he allowed you to reach the first consolidation. In your second life, you worked under Sir Alwan as an overseer of his slaves. The devil promised you a chance to advance: a bloodline key to unlock your nether code. But you were smart. You brought it to a coven to have it checked first.”

Andreas glowered.

“It’s hard for a hogmin to reach the second consolidation, much less Awakening,” the dwarf continued, blue eyes hard. “The slavers bred it out of you.”

Hogmin had been bred for millennia to eliminate their potential. It made them easier to control. Without gaining access to the original bloodline code, it was almost impossible to achieve a breakthrough. Worse, if Andreas did manage a breakthrough, he’d be second consolidation without a third of the advantages such demons should possess.

The most important thing lacking wouldn’t be the trait. It’d be the improved talent.

“And you can make it happen?” Andreas challenged, brown eyes burning.

“You came here because you know we can,” Gavin returned. “The true hogmin lineage might be rare in Hell, but not… everywhere.”

He left the implication hanging. He wanted his associates to believe that the dwarf along with his other faces worked for the Fey Federation.

Bitter, Andreas fell back into his chair. “What’s your price?”

Free was a word only a fool would believe in. Free things were bait. That was what Gavin believed. It was a mantra anyone who’d lived on the underside knew by heart. That naive and lovely lady, Sylvia Swallows, didn’t understand the muck. When she’d offered her System for free, he’d almost balked.

Free things were often the most expensive.

And, as expected, the System was the most expensive thing Gavin had ever bought.

But even so, it was very much worth it.

He, however, knew the darkness lurking in men’s hearts. He’d walked through the rotted crevices of society. He knew the malice of the predator. The envy. The rancor. He’d lived it. He’d breathed it. Malice was his bread and butter. Gavin knew better than to offer the System for free, lest his customers turn tail and run.

No, he wanted their eyes fixed on the obvious price and not the hidden one.

“We know you’re desperate, but we aren’t cheats,” he said. The dwarf unrolled a length of parchment onto the table. “Our price is the same for everyone.”

Gavin could feel the hogmin’s fear, his suspicion. But under it was a more powerful ambition. Andreas lusted for power. He dreamed of being the lord instead of the servant or the slave. He wanted respect. More than that, he wanted revenge.

But most of all, he wanted to be special.

Everyone wanted to be special.

From across the table, the hogmin studied the contract.

“Read the terms. Out-loud,” the dwarf demanded. “I don’t want any misunderstandings.”

With a snort, Andreas picked up the parchment.

“I, the signer, agree to keep the details of this transaction secret,” the hogmin read stiffly. “And to hold in confidence anything I learn about any organizations involved. Nor will I reveal the information of any associated persons or tasks which I render, excepting when granted permission by my handler.

“This contract will stand until three favors have been paid. Favors may only be asked by my handler, unless those favors have been transferred to another. No favor may require more than a year of labor, or a sacrifice valued at five years of labor, in cash.”

“Break those terms and the contract will rot your soul essence and the outer membrane with it,” Gavin warned.

The hogmin dropped the parchment onto the table before shoving it toward the dwarf.

“I want a time limit on those favors,” Andreas demanded. He pointed at the metal cylinder. “I don’t want that thing sitting in my soul for centuries.”

Soul contracts were a heavy-handed way of securing negotiations. They were, in short, bombs stored in the soul. These bombs, naturally, had spiritual mass. Expensive contracts were light. The cheap ones were heavy. Gavin’s little operation wasn’t all that well-funded, so the contracts he was offering were reasonably cheap.

“Fair,” Gavin allowed. His blue eyes flashed. “But it’ll cost you another month when we call in a favor.”

In fact, he had neglected to include a time limit on purpose. It was important to give his mark small victories. It didn’t matter much to him either way. Once Andreas knew the value of the System, temptation would be a tighter chain than any threat. And Gavin never had any intention of holding onto those favors for years on end. Contract shells could be recycled. He wanted them back, sooner rather than later.

“How will I know when I’m about to break a term?” the hogmin asserted next. “From what you wrote, it’d take no more than a slip of the tongue and I’d spend two lives as a gutter rat.”

“Contracts are based on intent,” Gavin grunted, revealing just the right amount of professional disdain. “You know what we meant when we wrote those words. And if you forget yourself, the contract will give fair warning. Nobody wants to lose karma here. But play games – ”

Bang! The dwarf’s fist pounded the table, making the instruments jump.

“ – and you’ll get what’s coming for you.”

Andreas stared at the parchment. Gavin didn’t need a redcap’s sense of malice to know the hesitation was fake. The hogmin’s eyes were pure avarice.

“Take it or leave it, boy,” he growled.

The hogmin paused, then looked up. “I heard I can pay off one of the favors with cash.”

He did allow as much. His little organization always needed more cash. Especially right now, when it was expanding.

“Ten years of wages. Up front,” Gavin answered. “This is a one-time courtesy, understand. We like to keep our cells independent. Moving money leaves a trail.”

“The contract says five,” Andreas challenged.

The dwarf stared, his blue eyes unwavering. An open-ended favor was worth more than simple cash. Both men understood that.

“Fine,” Andreas accepted bitterly. “Amend the contract and I’ll sign your damn paper, dwarf.”

Gavin spun the parchment. His quill danced, adding lines to the contract. Once done, he pushed it in the hogmin’s direction. While Andreas reviewed the terms, Gavin cut himself on the tines of the brass offering bowl. The dwarf hissed as the magic sucked from his body three essences: blood, soul, and psychic.

The liquids sizzled in the container.

After a moment, Andreas signed the paper. The dwarf pushed the bowl in the hogmin’s direction.

“Soul contracts are witnessed by the Heavenly Will,” Gavin explained.

Andreas hesitated, eyeing the instrument warily. There were all sorts of nasty magics which made use of blood and other essences. Spells that a demon couldn’t hide from unless they switched layers or hopped a ship to another plane. Sometimes, not even then.

Finally, with gritted teeth, the hogmin cut his meaty hand with the brass bowl’s tines.

Blood and essence poured in through the holes of the lid. Gavin rolled the parchment before slipping it into the metal cylinder. With the item sealed, the dwarf took the lid off the offering bowl. Liquid bubbled within, wisps escaping as it popped. Lifting the vessel, Gavin poured half over the grayish metal of the contract. Blood sank into the cylinder as though drawn through veins.

The liquids in the brass bowl burst into purple fire.

A great eye turned toward them. Both men shuddered. The gaze of the Heavenly Will pierced through the roof, Tartarus, and the cosmos alike, leaving their souls naked in its sight.

Then it was gone.

“And there you are, sealed and accepted by up high,” Gavin said, pointing at the sky.

He let a spark of mana play over the tube. Magic crackled in response. Metal twisted into a cord before winding itself into a ball of barbed, wire mesh. With a ting-a-ling it rolled on the table to stop in front of Andreas. Only when the contract was secure in the hogmin’s soul did Gavin smile.

The dwarf placed two items in front of him. First was a vial containing a red drop. The second was a small, key stone which gave access to a vault.

“The key to your future and the key to the account,” he said gruffly. “Put your money there and then leave the stone with it.”

Andreas’s brown eyes glinted with desire. With thick hands, he gripped the tiny vial.

“So small,” he muttered.

“Small things can change the world,” Gavin replied. “You have to use it here. Take it out the door and it’ll self-destruct. And don’t try to fake it. I’ll know.”

There was a flash of suspicion in the hogmin’s eyes. Then greed won out. Eagerly, Andreas took the vial into his soul. With blue eyes, Gavin watched. Only when the System dinged did he know the deed was done.

Ten more merit points landed in his lap.

Andreas’s brown eyes opened. He gazed at the nothing in confusion.

“Welcome to Bluelight, Andreas,” Gavin whispered. “GrimQuill will be your handler. Everything you need to know, you’ll hear from him. Now go. We won’t cross paths again.”

The hogmin took an uneasy breath then stood. “The money will be in your account before the week’s out, dwarf.”

Andreas walked out of the room. Gavin didn’t move even after the door shut. Instead, he gathered his things, hiding them in his soul. The dwarf checked the clock. Three more minutes. It only took one before a fist sounded against the door.

Thunk. Thunk.

“This is a private room,” Gavin growled, jerking the door open.

Outside was a salaman. The lizard had scales instead of skin. His eyes were slit, a forked tongue flicking between sharp teeth. His face was rounded and long, akin to a salamander’s.

“And I have private busssinesss,” the salaman hissed. He turned his head, scanning behind before adding. “I hear you sssell potential.”

“You’ve heard wrong,” Gavin replied.

The dwarf slammed the door. With a taloned hand, the salaman caught it.

“That anti-divination tool of yoursss isssn’t asss good asss you think, Thiefcatcher,” the salaman asserted, a clawed finger pointing at the ring on Gavin’s right hand. “The feathered onesss are looking for you.”

The dwarf’s blue eyes narrowed. Gavin gave no hint of recognizing his name.

“Is that so?” he sounded. “Who are you then?”

The salaman blinked, eyelids passing along the vertical. “Firessstorm.”

Gavin’s head leaned, looking past the salaman.

“And them?” he asked. “They with you too?”

In the smoky tavern, a kelpie and a selkie had risen from their seats. Dorchadais Farraige, Gavin recognized them on sight. They were a gang of sea demons from the Aos-Si Fuath genera. They’d come down from the third layer a few years back after Archduke Asmodeus threw a fit and drove out their competitors.

A cat’s paw for the Inquisition? Surely they weren’t Heaven’s agents while merely at the top of the first consolidation. If they were working for the angels, it’d explain why Dorchadais Farraige had shown up in Pyrkagiás of all places. While the fire and the brimstone wouldn’t kill them, the fourth layer wasn’t exactly comfortable for their kind.

But Gavin wasn’t about to neglect the possibility that the salaman was working for Heaven and that the sea demons were friends of the cults.

“It ssseemsss I’m a bit late,” the salaman sounded. His head tilted so his eyes caught the pair. A sword hissed from the lizard demon’s sheath. “It’d be bessst if you made yourssself ssscarccce.”

The kelpie drew a mace from his soul while the selkie summoned a spear. Soul lamps dangled from their belts. These fellows meant business. Luckily, Gavin had a slip ring, but for all he knew it was shit given where he’d picked it up. After all, Lady Swallows hadn’t had too much trouble when catching the bastard’s soul.

The salaman tilted.

Ki exploded. The lizard demon shot across the room like a bullet. The kelpie jumped forward in response, a shield of water appearing over his left arm. Wrong move. The salaman’s falcata caught fire. The blade flickered. Water exploded into a wall of steam, steel slicing straight through the kelpie’s arm.

The limb went flying, bicep and forearm waggling around the loose joint.

The selkie lunged, his spear a flashing light. Clang! Metal rang, haft deflected by the salaman’s parry. The lizard spun, tail swishing as he suddenly pivoted to the gray-skinned seal demon’s back. Mace held high, the kelpie chased.

“HA!”

With a roar, Gavin threw his axe. The weapon whipped through the air, crashing into the selkie’s spear. The man took a half step back, only to turn green an instant later. A slip of magic had been riding on the projectile, and now it was wreaking havoc in the selkie’s gut.

Rather than rush in, Gavin turned and ran right back into the private room he’d just left. He’d be a fool of a goblin if he put his fate in the hands of a demon he’d just met.

Bang!

The door slammed behind him. Gavin took a second to throw the bolt before kicking the chairs and table aside. The dwarf reared back. Magic surged, flooding his right arm. Flesh swelled. Bones stretched. Muscles grew into mighty thews even a cyclops would envy.

Then – BOOM! – he punched straight through the tavern’s wall.

Someone was already outside waiting for him.

“He’s running out the back!”

A shellycoat stood in the main street, overlooking the tavern’s backdoor and alley. Gavin glanced in his direction. The sea demon charged. The dwarf jumped, legs stretching as though they were springs. He flew three stories into the air, his two arms growing longer even as they split into four. With long, sharp fingers, the distorted horror of flesh gripped the wall before climbing it like a spider.

The shellycoat lunged after him, claws digging into the stone.

Fool.

Leaning back, Gavin gestured. Flesh aspect magic was squeezed into a series of needles. Invisible threads shot across the distance. The shellycoat was sensitive enough to detect the spell in context. But, with his body clinging to the side of a building, there was no chance to evade.

Like a living thing, the curse slithered through the fey’s shell-crusted skin before burying itself in the sea demon’s bones.

C-crack!

The shellycoat’s arms twisted, white shards of bone tearing through his skin. The sea demon screamed before crashing down onto the alley’s stone surface. With a mocking smile, Gavin threw himself onto the roof. Low-level fey should know better than to mess with a redcap’s magic.

“He’s on the roofs!”

A partial defeat didn’t stop the shellycoat from giving away Gavin’s position.

The goblin tore across the roof tiles before diving into an alley beyond. A monkey, not a dwarf or a distorted horror, descended into the narrow street. His gray cloak and armor were dismissed, replaced by a red, silk shirt. Calm and steady, the golden-furred vanara merged into the crowd.

And not a moment too soon.

Whoosh. A kelpie flew over the roofs on leathery wings. Doubling back, the sea demon circled in confusion. But it was too late. Gavin was indistinguishable from the throng of hundreds of demons traveling across Pyrkagiás.

“Elroy, you damned mutt, you better be alright,” Gavin muttered under his breath.

The salaman had dropped his name. If Heaven knew who he was, Elroy would be in danger. Not just him. Gavin’s entire network was at risk of being plucked. Quickly, the vanara called up a blue screen. With a chain of PM’s, he sent warnings to all his recruits.

Then he checked his inbox. Nothing.

Strange.

Gavin’s eyes narrowed. “We have a rat.”

Andreas, that bastard, was it him? Or…, Gavin skimmed over the list. Most of those he’d invited into the ‘Bluelight’ had never met the dwarf. Instead, they had traded with the muki or the elf. An elaborate illusion which forged an organization larger than a lone goblin and a werewolf.

No doubt, some of the older hands had already met. But there were also a few greedy parties who’d started recruiting gamers of their own.

“What business does a goblin have with divinations and secrets?” Gavin murmured. “We need a specialist in this business. Which means Elroy and I will need to make friends with the fire.”

Their apartment was just ahead.

Gavin studied the courtyard from the road. A nuckelavee was waiting outside their building. The giant fey had a horse body with a human-like torso, much like a centaur. The similarities ended there. The sea demon’s face formed a muzzle like a horse, his arms so monstrously long that his fingers dangled near his knees.

In his right hand, the nuckelavee clutched a lance.

Lowering his head, Gavin approached as though he were a nervous resident returning. The nuckelavee gave him a glance only to be distracted when a minotaur in a yellow dress stormed over. It was their landlady, Elpis.

“Oy!” she called loudly. “You there. Put that weapon away. You’ll make my – ”

BOOM!

Dark stone exploded out in a shower of blocks and pebbles. A ball of white fur flew from Gavin’s apartment, a gray-skinned selkie clutched in the giant wolf’s jaw. With inhuman grace, Elroy’s paws landed on the building opposite. With the twitch of his neck, Elroy threw the fey girl aside.

The nuckelavee turned, then thrust up with his lance.

Elroy jumped. The huge wolf bent in midair. Though the giant beast was fast, Elroy wasn’t yet used to his new, massive body. The sea demon’s lance sliced into Elroy’s fur, a haze of pestilence hanging near the tip. Where it touched, foul rot spread, white hairs turning dark.

The wolf landed, twisting to face this new foe. He pattered back. The nuckelavee charged. At the same time, a shellycoat threw himself through the broken wall, two short-bladed swords plunging into Elroy’s back. The wolf howled.

Lance raised, the nuckelavee made ready to thrust through Elroy’s throat.

Only for his front legs to splinter.

“You bastards,” Elpis roared. “How dare you touch my man!”

Giant club in hand, a raging minotaur crashed into the fray. Her first blow sent the nuckelavee reeling into the building opposite. Still screaming, Elpis charged. Glaring, the horse demon raised his head then breathed out a cloud of putrid mist.

Gavin’s finger twisted. The shellycoat’s limbs bent. His timing was perfect, matching Elroy’s furious buck. Distracted by the curse, the shellycoat was thrown high into the air. The giant wolf didn’t miss his chance. Crunch. Elroy snatched the falling fey with his massive maw.

“Anyone – ” Thud! “ – who messes with my – ” Thud! Thud! Thud! “– residents messes with me!”

Thud!

With one final blow, Elpis crushed the nuckelavee’s skull. Breathing heavily, the minotaur woman escaped the pestilent mist. Her red-brown fur was matted and worming with rot.

“Miss Elpis, I dare say you are looking especially lovely today,” Gavin said cheerfully.

Fur melting, Gavin was restored to the handsome shape of a dark-skinned goblin. The redcap twirled his fingers, forming a magic that purged the nuckelavee’s rot. Elpis’s red-brown fur immediately looked better.

“Gavin, my dear,” Elpis replied, expression still pained. “You are a charmer. And Elroy, love, I never imagined you had Awakened.”

The giant wolf stepped closer. Gavin detected a faint tremble in Elroy’s legs.

“Miss Elpis, we are thankful for your assistance in this matter,” Elroy rumbled. The wolf’s golden eyes swept the courtyard. “And I apologize for damaging the room.”

“No need, love. No need,” Elpis waved off happily. Storing her massive club, the woman straightened her dress. “It's only right that I look after my residents.”

Gavin showed a wide, toothy grin. “It’s about time you woke up, old mutt.”

“Good fortune, nothing more,” Elroy replied, voice strained. “The outward danger forced a resolution to my contradiction.”

Shuddering, the great white beast slumped. Elroy wilted, fur falling away to reveal a humanoid pup. The childlike body collapsed onto the ground.

Their landlady let out a gasp.

“Miss Elpis, I’m afraid we must cut this meeting short,” Gavin asserted.

Stooping, the goblin picked his friend up off the pavement. Luckily, Elroy’s unfinished resurrection left his size manageable.

“Just see him somewhere safe,” Elpis said worriedly. Then she straightened before asserting. “And don’t worry about your things. I’ll make sure they’re protected.”

“Thank you, Miss Elpis,” Gavin offered. “Now, we really must be on our way.”

Luckily, they already had a safe house planned out.

Aos-Si Fuath

The Fuath genera is one of three major branches in the Aos-Si lineage. Whereas the Tuath mostly consist of human-like fey, the Fuath are a broad category of sea and water demons. As members of the Aos-Si lineage, the Fuath are sometimes called fey even if they are residents of Hell. Like most non-human bloodlines, those of the Fuath genera are very rare in Heaven.

Bloodline: Kelpie

Type: Mixed

Potential: Medium

Commonality: Uncommon

Formal Name: Aos-Si Fuath Ceffyl

Kelpie are bipedal sea creatures with a horse-like face and hooves instead of feet. Though they have natural magic which allows them to manipulate water, their attributes lean heavily toward the physical. For this reason, they are more akin to magically oriented warriors even though their species is technically mixed.

The Kelpie bloodline is the most common of the Fuath genera, with their numbers found everywhere outside the 33 Heavens. Like most of the Fuath, they prefer terrain with abundant water. Some subspecies of Kelpie prefer salt water. Others prefer fresh water.

Before Awakening, Kelpie have three-fingered hands which undermine their success in the crafts. After Awakening, they humanize, gaining two additional digits.

Bloodline: Selkie

Type: Warrior

Potential: Medium

Commonality: Rare

Formal Name: Aos-Si Fuath Seula

Selkie are gray-skinned sea demons with seal-like features. All selkie can partially transform while underwater, making themselves more effective swimmers. After Awakening, this transformation becomes greater, allowing them to freely switch between a fully human form and that of a seal.

Many mistakenly believe Selkies have a natural magical affinity for water and ice. This is false. Rather, they have an innate ki resonance with any concept related to water. This allows them to easily form manifestation or even transformation arts of this nature. This combined with their high physical toughness and strength has led them to not only be successful warriors, but also skilled craftsmen of water-based martial equipment.

As a universal rule, Selkie like salt water and disdain fresh water. They can, however, swim in either source. Like most Fuath, they are rare in Heaven, though typically more welcome than their brethren.

Bloodline: Nuckelavee

Type: Mixed

Potential: High

Commonality: Very Rare

Formal Name: Aos-Si Fuath Plàigh

Similar to a centaur in shape, nuckelavee are quadrupedal horse demons. They have unnaturally long arms which reach their knees when dangling. Despite being a mixed species, nuckelavee can often overpower warrior-class bloodlines by using their physical size as an advantage. A consequence of this inhuman bloodline, however, is that newborn nuckelavee suffer from a greater strain on their soul.

Nuckelavee are typically shunned by most demons, even those of the Fuath genera. This is not just for their grotesque appearance, but also because of their innate power. All nuckelavee have natural magic that lets them conjure and manipulate plague and pestilence. Young nuckelavee often struggle to control this vile magic, inflicting it on everyone around them.

Despite their horse body, nuckelavee are better at swimming than they are at running. This doesn’t mean, however, that they won’t defeat most two-legged fey in a race. They have a fondness for salt water. Fresh water that emphasizes aspects of purity, however, is hated as it stifles their magic and saps their mana.

Bloodline: Shellycoat

Type: Warrior

Potential: Medium

Commonality: Uncommon

Formal Name: Aos-Si Fuath Slige

Shellycoats are ugly, squat, long-armed fey. Despite their appearance, their bloodline is surprisingly successful. This is owed to their traits. All shellycoats sport a natural crust of shells for protection along with an innate high-speed regeneration. Both of these traits have a strong ki resonance, meaning fey of this species quickly learn manifestation arts of a similar type. This has led shellycoats into a rich subculture of brewing healing potions and manufacturing armor.

Regardless, shellycoats cannot escape the disgust of society. Being ugly and inhuman, shellycoats find it difficult to ever rise above the lower class.
