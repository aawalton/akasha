Alex, in the sky…

Bright light burst over the horizon, as the sky itself changed. A massive sun appeared, then disappeared as clouds formed in the air.

A cold gust blasted into the side of me, throwing me off course.

The green light surrounding the crack turned off, leaving behind the gaping hole.

Must move faster!

I yanked on every bit of energy inside me, and directed it to my wings. I was so close, so freaking close.

A strange burst of energy pushed at me from behind, causing me to slip end over end a few times. I caught snatches of Noseen, in his terrifying natural form, far behind me, then nothing as I passed through the gaping hole in the sky.

Freezing cold washed over me, then my armor thickened.

[SYSTEM WARNING: You are outside the system.]

[SYSTEM WARNING: Return to system space as soon as possible.]

[SYSTEM WARNING: Your levels are not compatible…]

The gap lingered open for a single second, then snapped shut. The last notification froze and just hung there, incomplete.

My hands shook at the sight, and I tried to bring up my stat sheet. It flickered into view, but when I tried to allocate a free stat point, it didn’t work. I closed it, and tried to not think about what that might mean.

I continued to be pushed back from where the hole had been before I steadied myself with my wings. In front of me, a massive sphere rippled with energy.

Darkness surrounded me, and I quickly took note of my surroundings.

My jaw dropped as I spotted what stood off to one side. A literal sun glowed in the far distance, unbelievably bright, but not visibly large. It was in a field of stars so vivid and dense that it reminded me of when I’d been on the orbital station, or in the ship before entering cold sleep. I spun about, trying to calm my beating heart, but failing.

This was space.

Outer. Fucking. Space.

Stars glittered in the distance, and something shimmered off to one side. Much like the sphere which I had left. A shimmering curtain covered most of what was behind me.

Somehow, I was breathing. Well, not really breathing, but at least not dying? At least, it didn’t feel like I was dying.

The shimmering curtain continued as far as I could see, but weirdly curved around a moon off to the far side of the sphere. Like it didn’t dare touch it.

I flew up to get a better look, then everything inside me shuddered to a stop.

A strange metal ring, with a few missing pieces, curved around the rock.

Ever so slowly, I started flying toward the thing. It grew bigger, very slowly, as I approached. Bit-by-bit, more of the ring came into view. Large sections were completely broken off and floating nearby.

The spine of what had to be the beginnings of a colony ship was docked in one of the bays, but it remained incomplete.

The horror dawned on me as I glanced over my shoulder at the sphere behind me.

I couldn’t even think it.

This couldn’t be possible.

Still, I kept flying forward, something pulling me to the moon. This moon, Earth’s moon, that I recognized. The last place I truly remembered before going into a deep sleep. We were the last ship ready to go, but it was clear they had still tried to keep building.

Something held the shimmering curtain back. What was it about the moon that was doing that? The planet behind me had been consumed… My brain stopped, but my body kept going. Time ticked by slower than ever, but I eventually made it to the edges of the broken ring.

It wasn’t broken.

Clean cuts sheared the metal off everywhere I looked. Someone had cannibalized it. Taking parts for something.

Maybe that was how they built the last piece of ship.

Or at least tried to.

The base on the moon didn’t appear touched. Well, not from here. I continued in that direction, trying to not think about the time spent on the ring trying to prove I belonged on the colony ship.

Whatever the First had done, it had definitely unlocked some of my memories.

Was that on purpose?

I shivered even as I landed on the docking platform. The sealed doors were closed and I approached. A small light blinked, catching me off guard, and the doors snapped open to the massive airlock.

It’d fit one of the shuttles inside without a problem, I knew. Two would be a tight fit because of the wings, but I’d seen it done, during that last frantic loading of our ship.

Once I stepped inside, the doors shut behind me and a hissing noise filled the large space. Lights flicked on, making everything flash white for a second, and a strip of light above a small door on the far side turned on.

Stillness hung in the air. A disassembled shuttle lay scattered across the floor, with many pieces missing. Next to it stood a cart-like object.

The door snapped open when I pressed the activation button on the side.

I stepped through quickly as my training took over. The second door snapped open once the first closed, then I stood inside the base.

We’d only been here for a few tests and briefings. Otherwise, everything took place in the ring.

Being here felt surreal.

Lights flared to full brightness along the corridor, just like I remembered. The entire place ran on solar power, which clearly still worked. I quietly continued down the hall. Each of the signs had thick scribbles above them in some language I didn’t recognize.

They also didn’t translate.

Something or someone had found this place. I stared at the symbols, trying to figure out where I’d seen them before. My eyes widened as I darted back through the airlock to the shuttle bay. Within seconds I stood next to the cart, then touched the front panel.

It hummed to life, lifting off the ground.

A hovercart.

A freaking hovercart. Just like the one the Forgers had used.

Somehow, they had reached this place from the sphere, and looted it?

This time I returned to the hallway on edge. I wanted my spear, and my fingers twitched. Everywhere I glanced, I was searching for signs of Forgers.

Then the first change appeared. A door stood where one hadn’t before, or at least it hadn’t been built when I’d been here.

Processing Chamber.

The label next to it sent shivers down my spine. But it stood there, with nothing written above it in the Forgers’ language.

There wasn’t a window, and I almost continued on without looking inside, but just couldn’t.

Instead of a normal button to open the door, this one required a handprint. I pressed my hand to it, and it flashed green, then a message popped up.

‘Colonist detected.’

The door slid open, revealing a room with a row of five beds. Crumbling skeletons rested on each. Large helmets covered each of the skulls.

I didn’t want to enter the room, but did anyway, ignoring the bones and heading toward the closed door at the far end.

Upload Construct, read the label next to the door.

I pressed the button and it slid open. Instead of more beds, a massive dark screen took over most of the room. As I stepped closer, dark green writing flickered across the surface.

Uploads complete.

It blinked on and off. Again and again.

Nothing else happened, and I turned away, trying to piece the puzzle together. The doors remained open behind me as I left.

I relaxed a little as I left the room with the bones, and continued on my way. The main command station would be the place to find any information about what happened.

I paid closer attention to the rooms I passed. Most were the same.

Some of these were broken into. Metal had been peeled back and systems ransacked. Others were completely empty, when in the past they hadn’t been. Nothing was in any of the medical rooms, for example, or the cafeteria.

I didn’t linger in any area. Finally, I reached the door that I’d been heading for. It was not the same.

Instead of the normal door for the command center, this door was reinforced.

Someone had painted a black cross and bright red eyes next to it. Then an X again over the door.

The warning was clear, even without a translator. Whatever they’d found inside was too dangerous even for the Forgers. They didn’t dare touch it.

This was the source of whatever held back the shimmering curtain. The curtain that must be the edge of System space.

Taking a deep breath, I pushed the button, and the door slid open silently. Inside, the two rows of command stations all remained in place. Dark screens and empty chairs.

But the main screen wasn’t there anymore. It had been replaced by a window that had broken at some point.

Light drifted up from below, highlighting massive cables that hung from the ceiling. The rest of the room was covered with metal paneling.

I made my way closer to the window. The light inside didn’t change.

The cables hung down to a podium floor, and connected to a pulsing orb.

I leaned closer, and the floor creaked.

The orb suddenly flinched and flew up in the air, still connected to the cables. The form shifted, taking on the appearance of legs and arms, until a massive humanoid form had constructed itself, with the cables coming out of its shoulder blades.

“Become the seed.” The voice boomed out from the orb, like a physical force.

“Save our species!” The answering call ripped from the depths of my memories, as my mind flooded with images of the posters and ads recruiting people to apply for the colony ship initiative.

The orb floated closer to the window, and I stepped back.

It flew through the opening and landed in front of me on the floor.

“Leave the cost of living behind!” The words hit me like a brick. The slogan had been picking up steam right before we left. A way to survive, even if Earth didn’t.

I took a step back away from the glowing thing. If this was what I thought it was, I didn’t want to go near it. People had gotten into massive fights, between those who wanted to sign up for it, and those who thought the very idea was crazy.

“Your Story, Saved Forever!” This time the voice came out weird, all echo-ey, like more than one person said it at the same time.

I took another step away from the thing.

Then it lunged.

I dodged to one side, and its arms passed right by, formed into a blade of light.

“I am not fighting you,” I said, as I rolled across the room. I let the armor fall from my face, and immediately gagged. A rotting smell filled the air.

“Lies!”

Suddenly, it rushed me as I gagged, then I was airborne. Wings sprouted from my back, keeping me up. Then I saw it.

Beneath the podium. A mound of death. Arms, legs, and bones.

None of it appeared human.

I darted back as the glowing being jumped at me.

Now I knew what had happened to the Forgers who made it into this room. The warning made more sense now.

“Preserve Your Mind! Protect Your Legacy!” That was my only warning, before the figure of light leaped again, glowing blades flashing right by me.

I darted past without a problem, and through the window.

Something had crushed the button on this side of the door. I didn’t even hesitate before I slashed with my claws. Black lines cleanly sliced through the metal, then I burst through into the hallway.

I needed to get out of this place.

“Flesh Fails!” Echoed behind me, as I flew down the corridor. “The Core Endures!”

Metal crashed to the ground behind me, as the glowing creature stepped into the hallway after me. It didn’t dash, just kept putting one foot in front of the other, following.

Something had gone wrong. Very wrong.
