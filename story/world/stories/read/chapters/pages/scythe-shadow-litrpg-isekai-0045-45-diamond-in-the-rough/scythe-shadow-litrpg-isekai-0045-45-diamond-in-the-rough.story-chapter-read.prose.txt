The inside of the Meridian was the same as I remembered. The detailed map upon the ceiling had new meaning since I'd last been here though. Places I recognized stood out. Locations of the Meadowvale entrances, icons denoting seaside lighthouses, the Jungle zone that I'd briefly peered into. The central spire of a mountain that held a potential escape from all this.

The four walls of different themed bars remained the same as well. Judith tended to the patrons at the jungle bar like before. Today, only one mattered though. The volcanic bar along the left wall and the single patron sitting alone at the end.

I retracted my scythe and strode over to Lancelot, the orange glow of the faux lava flows reflecting off his resplendent armor. He didn't react as I sat on the empty stool beside him and lay Nuzzle on the bar top. HE simply took a sip from his smoking shot glass.

I waited.

"Haven't gotten yourself killed yet, then?" he finally said, voice gravelly.

"Not for lack of trying."

"Why am I not surprised?"

"I know you're not exactly fond of me after out last encounter, but I'm not here for me."

"No? Here for Valciferous, then?"

He raised his glass for another sip, but I plucked it from his hand and slid it down the bar.

"I'm here for all of the Meadow. Maybe more. I don't know… I don't have a full grasp of what's going on. But I think—I think you and your…" I cleared my throat, unsure what to call the Shepherds and their cultish worship of the Sins. "Your friends might be interested."

Lancelot didn't face me, but his lip quirked up ever so slightly. "How d'you figure?"

"You were at the lighthouse when those Unchained attacked me. Almost as if... as if you were expecting them. Hunting them. And perhaps, seeking their source? Well, I found it. There's an Iron Reaper who made a deal with the god Pirhani to control the Unchained from the forest zone and bring them to the Meadow. They've already breached Meadowvale."

Lancelot exploded from his stool, armor clanking. He slammed a hand on the counter, cracking the stone. Nuzzle jumped up like a scared cat and hissed at the burly knight.

The entire tavern quieted and looked at them.

"They attacked? How the devil have I not heard?"

"So you were looking into them."

"Of course I was damn well looking into them."

"They struck without warning. Left defense at strategic locations to intercept anyone seeking aid. I had to fight three Reapers and an Unchained just to get in here. There are… a lot of them."

He gaped at me, anger still seemed onto his face. Then he seemed to look deeper, as if he were analyzing me, my soul. His expression softened, but only slightly.

I stood, joining him. "Like I said. Meadowvale is already under siege. I need to return with help… You're the only one strong enough I could think of."

Lancelot scowled, but not at me. "I'm not in the Meadow. It'll take some time to rally some friends and get there."

"How much time?"

"I'll do my best."

"That's not an answer."

"It'll have to do."

Lancelot pushed away from the bar and dropped a handful of black coins in the crater he'd left behind. "You lot," he announced to the bar. Everyone froze, most likely hearing him speak for the first time after fearing or idolizing him for years. "Listen to Zephyr here. The gods have brought their war to Penumbra. While we sit and eat and drink in here it's already begun. I'll see you on the battlefield."

With that, the Diamond Knight stormed out of the tavern.

All eyes turned to me.

I breathed in deep, not ready for this role. Yet having no choice but to seize it.

"Some of you may have seen me in the arena a few days back. If not, perhaps you've heard of me. I am the Death Reaper, and I'm here requesting aid." I tried to sound authoritative, like a leader, but couldn't tell from the expressions of those around me if I quite accomplished it. They mostly looked afraid or angry.

I explained the situation to the best of my knowledge once again. At the end, several Reapers stood, scythes drawn, eager to fight. At least, I'd inspired some of them to action. "Anyone farther inland, spread the word. Those of you in the Meadow, meet me outside. We make for Meadowvale, and pray on your Vitrial lives that it still stands."

The tavern cleared out fast, all but a few stragglers and the Penumbrans who'd been dining there left behind. Another Amethyst Reaper hovered awkwardly nearby. He kept tapping his fingers on his scythe. I waved him over as I made my way toward the jungle bar.

"What's your name?" I asked.

The man was maybe a few years older than I, skinny in an athletic sort of way, with fierce red hair. "Felix."

"Nice to meet you. I'm Zephyr. You nervous, Felix?"

"I've never fought another Reaper. Arena's not really my thing. Been lucky enough to avoid the Unchained too until recently."

"They go through your village?"

"Demolished it while I was phased out. Lost everything."

"You're not alone there." I placed a hand on the man's shoulder. "We'll come out on the other side of this."

"I hope you're right."

I waved to Judith as I slid onto a stool at her bar. Nuzzle hopped up and licked some unknown spilled liquid off the surface.

"I see you've been busy," Judith said, examining me up and down. "How can I help?"

"Willing to take a couple IOU's for a meal? My Health is a bit precarious at the moment."

She rolled her eyes. "I thought I told you IOU's won't cut it. You've advanced to Amethyst, signed a pact with a god, revealed yourself as a Death Reaper and still you have no money?"

"Well, when you lay it all out like that it makes me sound incompetent…"

Her expression was deadpan.

"C'mon. Even for an old friend who's trying to save the entire Umbral plane?"

She seemed to consider it when Nuzzle hacked and vomitted up whatever mystery liquid he'd ingested.

Judith sighed and threw a bar towel over Nuzzle and the regurgitated mess. "I don't know who's the bigger pain in my ass. You or your pet."

I smiled sweetly. "That's why we make such a great team!"

"Don't flatter yourself. The gods play these games. They all hate each other, so they jockey for dominion over the others, each thinking they know how best to run the other's realms. It's endless and tiresome. Though if what you said to the crowd is true, this is the largest scale conflict I've been around for."

"See! Just one little taste. A pick-me-up to get me back on my feet. What do you say?"

"Ugh. Fine. But you owe me double. And if you die you owe me triple."

I saluted her as she ventured down the bar to gather a meal.

Felix stared at me, incredulous.

"She adores me," I said.

He only raised an eyebrow.

"In a curmudgeonly grandmother sort of way."

"You know, I could've loaned you some coins…"

"Eh, it's fine."

I exited the tavern, Felix in tow, after a cold bland meal. It had done the trick, restoring my Health, but I got the distinct feeling Judith had chosen the least appetizing option she could find for me.

A dozen or so Reapers waited for me outside—mostly Ambers—deep in discussion with Jericho. One other Amethyst beside Felix and myself had joined. It wouldn't make much of a difference against the force we were dealing with, but at least it was something. We just needed to distract the enemy until Lancelot arrived with reinforcements from the deeper realms.

I left Felix with Jericho and the others, instructing them to get preparations for departure under way. While they handled that, I went around the back of the tavern.

The pillar waited for me there, it's signature circle of frost fogging into the air. I glanced at the back wall of the tavern, where Calliope had leaned me down to recover after my first harrowing experience relinquishing a Will. She wasn't here now. Even Jericho didn't know where she was.

He'd been right. My apology wasn't for his ears. If I survived this, I owed it to Calliope to find her. The face the pain and grief I'd caused her. To atone for my mistake somehow.

In the meantime, all I could do was get stronger. Try to stay alive. Keep striving toward the goal of leaving this place—the lure at the end of the line Valciferous had cast for me.

I stepped into the frozen circle. Nuzzle remained outside, watching curiously as always. As I approached the pillar, I called forth my scythe. The dim Emerald glow of the Will trapped within tinted the icy blades of grass a fluorescent green. I slide the scythe into its designated slot in the pillar.

[Will Relinquish Commencing.

Rank: Emerald.

Affinity: Glacial.]

I once again felt surges of vibration through my mind and the pillar pulsed. Foreign emotions flooded my heart, constricting it, releasing it, building up to a deluge. This was the strongest Will I'd ever relinquished and I could feel it, almost as if it was taking hold over me, trying to possess me instead of the other way around.

I pushed back against the pressure, trying to Strengthen my own Will against its presence.

Then I felt myself being sucked away to the starry void.

Upon arrival, the pressure vanished. I held the Emerald orb, snowflake emblazoned upon its surface. How could something so small, so passive hold so much power?

I found the matching symbol in the sky and released the Will, its essence breaking and filling the stars above with a brighter glow.

[Will Successfully Relinquished

Rank: Emerald

Affinity: Glacial]

[Initializing corresponding augmentation to:

Defense

Health]

The power burned through my veins, rivers of green light visible on my arms and legs. I could almost feel my muscles growing, my skin tightening as it grew thicker and more protective.

Relief washed over me.

[Augmentation complete.]

I returned to Penumbra, gasping. It felt like I hadn't taken a breath in several minutes. Once I recovered, I could feel the difference. This had been no small bump in Defense and Health like I'd been experiencing with the Amber Wills I relinquished. It felt exponentially more potent, as if my body were now indestructible.

I knew all too well that it wasn't though.

And that fact was about to be tested to its limit.

I rounded the corner of the tavern, Nuzzle back on my shoulder. He puffed out a little triumphant cloud of smoke as we came into view of the others. I wondered if they could see any visible difference in me after that augmentation. Regardless, they straightened. All looking to me. Waiting for my instruction.

"Let's go save the Meadow," I said.
