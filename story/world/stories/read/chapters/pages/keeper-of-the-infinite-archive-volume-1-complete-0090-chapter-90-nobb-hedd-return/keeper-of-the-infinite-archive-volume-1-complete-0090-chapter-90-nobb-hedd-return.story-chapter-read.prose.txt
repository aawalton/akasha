The morning after their latest investigation into Keeper #4,091, Bart stood behind the Patron Services counter. He was attempting to read the daily tasks list, but his attention kept returning to the five recorded steps in his predecessor’s final sequence.

Mabel stepped into the entrance hall and announced.

“Keeper,” the Sphinx said. “Nobb and Hedd have returned.”

Before Bart could say a word, a loud voice echoed down the dim stone corridor that led to the Staff Restroom.

“WOOD PRIEST!”

Bart closed his eyes and let out a long breath.

At the other end of the counter, Gregor immediately stopped working on the Route 3 ledger. He looked up, grinning.

“Nobb!” Gregor called out.

Hedd marched out from behind Nobb and pointed a green finger directly at the human.

“THREE PIG MAN!”

Gregor froze. On their previous visit to the Archive, the cave goblins had called him Two Pig Man, and he had corrected them to three.

Gregor puffed out his chest slightly, looking weirdly proud. “See?”

“I see,” Bart said.

“Progress.”

“Yeah technically ” and even had a little smile on his face.

To Bart's mild surprise, Nobb and Hedd marched directly to the counter without investigating anything else.

Nobb reached into a rough leather pouch and produced a heavily folded sheet of paper. He placed it proudly on the counter and pushed it toward Bart.

The handwriting was still jagged, written in black charcoal, but it was substantially better than the last time Bart had seen their work.

NOBB

HEDD

NEED BOOK FOR CAVE

WATER COME IN

SUPPORT WOOD SOFT

They had come back because there was a problem at home, and Gregor had taught them enough to ask a library for help.

Gregor leaned his heavy arms on the counter and looked immensely smug. “Taught them that.”

“Gregor teach words,” Nobb confirmed, nodding.

“And bad words,” Hedd added happily.

Bart raised an eyebrow.

“Those were optional, more extracurricular," Gregor muttered.

Ada Kett, who had been inspecting the load bearing capacity of the nearby arches, heard the words water, support and wood soft. She walked over to the desk, her ledger tucked under one arm.

Gregor noticed her approaching and gestured toward the two arrivals.

“Ada. More fellow goblins.”

Ada stopped and looked at him. “Yes.”

Gregor waited for a moment.

“Was there a second part to that statement?” Ada asked.

Gregor gestured between them again. “They're goblins.”

“I had noticed.”

Nobb and Hedd were completely ignoring Gregor. They were staring intently at Ada's orange coat and the licence badges on her coat.

“Fancy goblin,” Nobb declared.

“Municipal engineer,” Ada corrected smoothly.

“Very fancy goblin,” Hedd agreed.

Ada considered the evaluation. “That's more acceptable than a lot more I have been called.”

Bart stepped in to manage the introductions before the cultural exchange derailed. “Nobb, Hedd, this is Ada Kett. She is conducting our facilities assessment.”

Nobb concentrated hard on the unfamiliar syllables.

“Adequate?”

Gregor made a sudden, strangled sound and turned around, staring fixedly at the wall.

“Ada. Kett,” she repeated clearly.

Hedd sounded it out carefully. “A-da Kett.”

“Adequate,” Nobb concluded, very sure of himself.

Silence fell over the desk. Gregor's shoulders were shaking. Bart said absolutely nothing. This was not his problem.

Ada looked at Nobb and sighed.

“Close enough for provisional use,” Ada said.

Bart pulled up the Archive’s patron registry on his interface. Since they were borrowing material properly this time, he needed to formalise their patron records.

“I need you to confirm your full names for the ledger,” Bart said.

“Nobb,” said Nobb.

“Hedd,” said Hedd.

Gregor turned back around, picking up a piece of scrap paper and a pencil. He quickly wrote down the names for Bart to input and slid it across the counter.

NOBB HEDD

Bart immediately picked up a pen and drew a heavy slash right between the names. “Two patrons. The slash is important right Gregor!.”

Ada glanced over at the paper. “Those are two people.”

“Yes,” Gregor said.

“Then why did you write them on one line?”

“Tradition,” Gregor said simply and smirked.

Bart got them into the system correctly.

PATRON 1: NOBB

PATRON 2: HEDD

With the administrative hurdle cleared, Ada turned her attention to the folded charcoal note on the counter.

“Explain the structural failure,” Ada said.

Nobb pointed at the paper. “Rain come in. Wood go soft. Cave mouth go down.”

“How far down?” Ada asked.

Nobb threw his arms down in a dramatic gesture toward the stone floor.

“That is not a measurement,” Ada informed him.

Bart and Gregor exchanged a look.

Ada pulled a blank sheet of paper from her ledger and slid it forward. “Draw it.”

Hedd took a charcoal stick from his pouch and drew something terrible, jagged, but fundamentally understandable. It showed a cave entrance with a crude timber overhang, and water pooling heavily against the support posts.

Ada leaned over the drawing. “You have inadequate drainage around the entrance. If those timber supports are standing in saturated ground, they may be losing bearing capacity.”

Both goblins stared at her, their mouths slightly open.

Gregor held up a hand. “They can read. They can't understand engineering speak.”

Ada looked at the drawing again and took a deep breath.

“Water should go away from the wood,” Ada said.

Nobb nodded slowly.

“Wood holding cave mouth should not be soft.”

Hedd nodded vigorously.

“If the wood is soft, do not stand underneath it.”

Gregor snorted.

Bart gave him a warning look.

“Sound engineering advice,” Gregor said quickly.

“Yes,” Ada replied.

Gregor looked slightly disappointed that she had not even noticed.

“Good,” both goblins said in unison, completely oblivious to the exchange.

Bart was already searching the catalogue. He skipped the advanced magical architecture texts and the theoretical masonry volumes. They needed instructions, not professional certification.

He brought up a few options on the screen. Ada leaned over and pointed to one.

“That one,” she said. “Illustrated Drainage, Shoring & Timber Supports.”

Albert glided away and returned a few minutes later, placing a wide, durable book bound in treated leather on the counter.

Nobb flipped the cover open. He stared at the first page, which was dense with text, and looked mildly panicked.

Hedd leaned over and pointed at the opposite page, which featured a large, clearly labelled diagram of a water diversion trench.

“Pictures good,” Hedd decided.

Ada watched Nobb tracing his dirty finger under the heading on the diagram. He was sounding it out slowly. Not perfectly, but he was genuinely reading.

She turned to look at the goblins. “Who taught you?”

Both of them pointed immediately at the man behind the counter.

“Three Pig Man.”

“You taught them to read?” Ada asked.

Gregor crossed his arms, getting instantly defensive. “Somewhat, I was kinda forced.”

“Letters,” Nobb said.

“Numbers,” Hedd added.

“Bad words,” Nobb finished proudly.

“We can stop listing the curriculum,” Gregor muttered.

Ada did not mock him but simply looked at the guy who was learning to be a technician.

“Useful work,” Ada said quietly gave him an acknowledging nod.

Gregor opened his mouth, apparently found nothing useful to say, and settled for a nod back.

He leaned back over the counter and helped Nobb sound out the next heading.

Bart watched Gregor guide Nobb's finger beneath the printed words. The Archive contained considerably older and more dangerous knowledge. At the moment though, a book about drainage seemed the most important.

Bart initiated the loan through the Archive's circulation system. The interface chimed softly.

“Do you understand the return terms?” Bart asked.

Nobb nodded seriously. “Bring back.”

“Yes,” Bart said.

“Not fire,” Hedd added.

“Correct.”

“Not eat,” Nobb confirmed.

Bart paused. “Also correct.”

“They've learned” Gregor noted approvingly.

Mabel stepped closer to the counter, her golden eyes fixing on the two goblins.

“Material damage will result in suspension of borrowing privileges,” the Sphinx stated flatly.

Both goblins froze, staring at her in silence.

Gregor leaned over the counter. “She means don't hurt the book.”

Nobb grabbed the heavy book and hugged it fiercely against his chest.

“Book safe,” Nobb promised.

Before they turned to leave, Nobb pointed a finger at the goblin engineer in the orange coat.

“Adequate help.”

Gregor finally lost his fight and let out a sharp, loud laugh that echoed off the ceiling.

Ada looked at Nobb then she looked at the jagged charcoal diagram they were carrying away.

“For a preliminary consultation, yes,” Ada said.

Hedd nodded respectfully. “Thank Adequate.”

“Just go away,” Ada said, her voice resigned.

Nobb and Hedd turned and marched happily back toward the dim corridor leading to the Staff Restroom, holding their book like a sacred relic.

Gregor was still grinning.

Bart looked at his provisional technician. “You taught them well.”

Gregor shrugged, watching the corridor. “They still think A is a mountain.”

From the echoing depths of the restroom corridor, a voice shouted happily.

“MOUNTAIN!”

Gregor sighed. “Mostly well.”
