At the gates, a gruff guard barely glanced at Gelken.

Maus was tucked beneath the Administrator’s clothes, entirely out of sight. With Gelken’s help, they were manipulating their mana and spiritual energies, masking their identifiable signatures while blending their presence to appear as a single entity.

Currently, they should read as a high-level Tier 0. Strong, but not alarming.

“Reason for entry?”

The burly woman looked to be the canine equivalent of the Vulpian species. Maus had noticed that despite the settlement being mostly made up of Vulpians, very few of the people with the biggest stores, the biggest swords, or the biggest jobs were actually Vulpian.

Maus wondered if that had something to do with the rule requiring all Vulpians who reached Tier 1 to leave within forty-eight hours, lest they be forcibly relocated.

“I’ve heard there’s a new Portal World that’s good for beginners,” Gelken said.

“Got a pass?”

“I do.” Gelken opened her palm, a very convincing illusion of a hunter’s badge appearing there.

For a tense moment Maus wondered if they’d try to examine the thing. He didn’t think he could trick someone’s senses like that. Though the thought did give him ideas he wanted to speak to Boss about.

The guard nodded and let them pass. Each step they took, each street they passed ratcheted the tension higher until they reached the far gate. Heart beating fast they waited for another guard to examine the badge.

Gelken didn’t relax until they were nearly a kilometre away from the settlement. Then she looked beneath her collar, where Maus was clinging on. In his time with the Administrator Maus had developed a newfound appreciation for the comfortable perch Scarlet usually provided.

“How did you know that would work?” Gelken asked.

“Why wouldn’t it? How many people do you think he sees? All of ’em go and come back with that same badge. If you’re not doing anything weird, why would he look at you funny?” he asked. “No funny business means no funny looks.” Maus nodded sagely.

The Administrator paused, considering the words before continuing towards the distant Portal World.

“So, what can you tell me about the people you don’t want me to hand you to?”

“That’s a strange way to phrase that.” Receiving no response, he continued. “They want my authority.”

“Your authority?”

“My Portal World authority.”

“Oh,” Maus said. There was a pause. “What does that authority do?”

“Well, there are a lot of things, actually—”

Maus stopped her before she could exhaust herself listing everything. “I mean, why does it matter? What do they want it for?”

“Oh. Access,” Gelken said. "My authority grants me the ability to keep people out and, conversely, to lock people in, depending on the permissions I set for the Portal World.”

“Okay,” Maus didn’t really get it, but he figured Boss would understand when he explained this conversation to her. What Maus did understand was that this lady had a key to a door, she could lock it from either side, and everyone wanted that key.

Maus understood that. Locked doors could be super safe, or super scary.

“I can see why they all want you,” Maus said.

Gelken did not find those words reassuring.

There was a brief lull in conversation as Maus thought and Gelken increased the distance between herself and that accursed prison.

“Okay. I've got a plan.”

The quest had given Maus two potential opportunities for rewards, but Maus had no intention of leaving any prizes behind.

The Portal World entrance was only a short distance from the settlement, by Tier 1 travel standards. As they travelled, Maus hashed out his plan with Gelken.

Surprisingly, the vulpian found herself amenable to most of it. Maus was glad he hadn’t needed to resort to other means to secure her cooperation.

Once the details were filled in, Maus pulled out another mana document.

By the time they reached the final tree line, both Gelken and Maus had signed.

It was good timing too, Maus thought, looking out into the clearing.

The Portal platform was easy to spot, glowing with moderate intensity near its centre. Obviously, it was delved enough that there was no risk of a Portal Break, though Maus was curious about what it might look like to witness one. All that wild, destabilising mana exploding outwards in a corona of energy, like a rainbow bomb.

No, the portal itself was good and steady. The problem was everything around it.

Haphazard camps of individual delvers dotted the clearing alongside the much more orderly, much more intimidating rows belonging to both the Independents and the FelisCanis Faction.

Maus had heard the Historian talk about the history of Vixia. He wondered how closely this Portal World resembled its past, and how exactly the Matriarch had managed to secure the Administrator all that time ago.

The Historian had never mentioned that.

That thought gave Maus pause. Why hadn’t he mentioned that?

For some reason, the thought reminded Maus of something Mother had once said. The memory was fuzzy, buried in the time before he was him. Still, the quote had stuck.

She’d said “There are no true heroes. Only the sheltered, the martyred, and those successfully self-interested.”

The spatial mouse refocused on the task ahead.

A very low-output Echo gave Maus a glimpse of the impressions of each person within the FelisCanis and Matriarch’s camps, as well as the unaffiliated individuals and groups.

“We’re gonna have to sneak,” he whispered. Gelken looked down at him, incredulous.

Maus understood. There was no hiding in plain sight here.

Every entrance was watched. Every camp had patrols, every group kept a lookout, and individual delvers watched everyone, and were watched in turn.

People were watching for betrayal, for enemies, for an escaped Administrator, and whoever helped her out.

Here, everyone was suspect.

There were also other Tier 1s here. Maus knew they likely weren’t too much stronger than him. The Boss had made sure his Stats were full up to the brim. She’d also told him never to mistake confidence for arrogance.

There was no reason to fight when he didn’t have to.

“We’ll sneak better,” Maus said. “I’ll get you to the Portal World, then we’ll do it like we agreed.”

Gelken just continued to stare at Maus before sighing and nodding.

So, they snuck, but not like before.

Maus decided to try something new.

Tweaking the patterns he had been weaving, Maus worked to construct a layer over Gelken’s illusion focused on completely suppressing their energetic signatures.

He reached for that energy he’d been using, the one that showed him the bright and dark and frayed places inside people and pulled it tight around himself and Gelken.

He wasn’t trying to simply hide their signatures. That would leave a void in the world for anyone sensitive enough to notice. Instead, he was trying to hide the impression that there was anything there to notice at all.

It took an agonising amount of concentration, but he managed a rudimentary form of it.

Maus strained as they crossed the clearing. His aptitude with the weave increasing every time another gaze slid past them without stopping.

They passed first through one gap in the flow of people, then another. All along, subtle pulses of Echo gave him a solid map of every being’s movements.

Closer, they crept. Closer still. Every frantic pause, every mistimed dodge of every moving group was a potential blown cover.

Finally, they reached the 100-metre stretch surrounding the Portal World itself. It was, ironically, the least secure area of the entire clearing. It felt like the groups had all come to some sort of mutual agreement not to settle too close to a place where vulnerable groups might exit.

It also happened to be the place the Administrator was most likely to be discovered, what with all the open space and the lack of competing signatures.

Just as Gelken began to move, Maus felt his obscuration construct settle, something in his mind shifting into place.

A notification nearly broke his concentration.

[Skill Available – Superior Sneak (Tier 2)]

[Yes | No]

Oh, Maus thought.

Boss would like this.

All his attention on the notification, and maintaining the sneaking construct Maus almost missed the Administrator making it to the Portal World.

Maus could think about Skills later, he told himself, ushering the Administrator through the portal alone.

Nearly the moment she went inside it was as though Maus had set off a flare.

What had been the iridescent, shimmering light of an active portal suddenly flickered, then dimmed to a dull, red glow that rose barely fifteen centimetres from the platform.

The camp went silent in an instant.

Gelken had changed the Portal World entry to ‘Invitation Only’. Now nobody could get in unless they were on a pre-approved list of people, or they stepped on the platform and were invited in after petitioning the portal’s Administrator.

Maus hadn't known the resulting change would be so visible, but he wasn't worried. He’d needed to attract the attention of the FelisCanis and Independents anyway.

Maus had even dropped his concealment when Gelken had entered the portal.

Yet even when people began converging on the neutral zone directly outside the portal, they nearly missed him, eyes searching for a figure everywhere he wasn’t.

He was, he supposed, rather small.

"Hi, hi. Hello!" he said.

Searching gazes that had been darting around the clearing suddenly turned towards him. Anger and frustration boiled through the gathered crowd, their spiritual signatures flickering with intense colours and raging patterns.
