Sakura did not have time to take a proper breath or step back in advance. Pampli lunged at her so fast that for one brief instant the world seemed to fold in on itself. She jerked sideways, her body moving before thought. Her feet slipped on the wet clay, her shoulder snapped back, and the beast shot past so close that she was hit with the hot, heavy smell of blood.

Pampli missed her by pure luck. One huge paw slammed into the mud where Sakura had been standing a second earlier, spraying black sludge and bits of straw everywhere.

“Shit!” burst out of her.

The crowd roared, disappointed and hungry at the same time. They were already counting down the minutes to her death.

Sakura nearly fell. She managed to throw out a hand, catch her balance, straighten sharply, and jump back another couple of steps because Pampli was already turning around. He was not clumsy, not for all his mass. Quite the opposite. That entire huge body worked with terrifying precision, like a real predator’s.

The crimson stripes along his body flared a little brighter, the beast lowered itself and came for her again, this time not in a leap, but in a slow, dangerous, animal-like advance.

“You’re not as cute as your name, Pampli,” Sakura hissed. For one brief moment she focused on him, trying to see his level.

PAMPLILevel: 10

Holy hell, Level 10. And she was only Level 2.

Sakura felt her heart pound in her chest, hard and sickening.

The knife. She had a knife. She pulled it from her inventory almost automatically, and the cool grip settled into her palm, and for one moment it felt easier just because there was metal in her hand. But the relief did not last long. Even at a glance it was obvious this blade was too small for a creature like that. She could not face it head-on with a weapon like this. She would only be able to strike from very close, far too close. And letting Pampli get that close was almost the same as laying herself down under his fangs on purpose.

The beast lunged again.

This time Sakura was already waiting for the movement and did not step backward. Instead, she cut sharply across at an angle, almost under him, then immediately sprang aside because Pampli’s paw tore up a chunk of clay where her foot had just been. Mud slapped against her boot and splashed her shin.

The beast shot past, skidded with a scrape so harsh it sounded as if the stone beneath him groaned, then turned his head and growled now not just in rage, but with obvious irritation.

He was wounded, covered in scars, clearly used to fighting. Maybe that was Sakura’s chance. Her chance to survive.

Sakura took a step back and forced herself not to look at the stands, not to search for Merren, not to listen to the crowd. There was nothing useful there. She caught her breath quickly, trying not to choke on it, and only then, through that roar and the pounding of her own heart, heard something else.

A whisper. The whisper of the wind. It had followed her here. It had not stayed back in the ruins the way she had expected.

The whisper moved across the arena, between the posts, then drifted closer to her, cutting through the roar of the crowd.

“Listen,” the whisper said.

Sakura clenched her teeth. Now? Seriously? At the exact moment an obsidian predator the size of a tiger was charging her, the wind wanted her to listen to something?

Pampli leapt again.

Sakura threw herself aside, lower this time, almost dropping to the ground, and the beast swept over her. Apparently, she was not that bad at dodging after all. That was good. Or maybe that was Agility 6 finally doing something useful. Sakura did not care which. If the System wanted to make her a little harder to hit, she was not going to complain.

The ground slipped out from under her. She crashed into the mud, her palm slamming into the wet clay, but Sakura forced herself to push off before Pampli could come crashing down on top of her. She rolled aside, came up on one knee, then yanked herself back to her feet, and only then realized the knife was still in her hand, her trembling fingers still locked around the grip.

“Trust yourself. Listen to the power,” the wind kept whispering.

Sakura could barely make it out.

“Listen to the power.”

Sakura inhaled heavily. Her whole body was under so much strain it felt almost wooden. Her legs were shaking, and her chest burned from breathing too fast.

She could not use the knife. Too risky. For Pampli it might mean one more scratch. For her, it could mean death.

“Listen to the power, listen to the power,” Sakura mocked back between breaths. “Not exactly that easy.”

She kept her eyes fixed on Pampli.

The beast was fast, but not invisible. Every time he moved, it started with the same microscopic signal: the body tightening, the crimson lines along his ribs twitching, the shoulders dropping lower.

Sakura quickly figured out how to anticipate his movements. But she would not be able to keep dodging forever. She could already feel herself growing horribly drained, exhausted.

And then she finally understood what the wind had been whispering about. The power, flickering in her chest, calling to her. It was becoming harder and harder to ignore. But letting it out was still dangerous. And yet if she did not let it out, she might die under Pampli’s claws or fangs.

Pampli came at her again, no longer with the same furious, straightforward rush as in the first seconds, but with a new, far more dangerous confidence, the confidence of a predator that had learned the rhythm of its prey, and Sakura suddenly understood with sickening clarity that one more charge like that could be the last. She had already grown too weak even from simple evasion.

The whisper of the wind slid back to her, but now there was no vagueness left in it. It sounded almost insistent.

“Listen to the power.”

Sakura dragged in a sharp breath.

The same power that had answered her before. The one that lived somewhere beneath her ribs, as if trying to rise to the surface on its own. All this time she had done nothing but suppress it, hide it, fear letting even the smallest part of it out. Because to let it out meant revealing it. To reveal it meant exposing herself. And exposing herself in front of Merren, his guards, and this crowd hungry for spectacle could cost her not just her freedom, but her head.

But dying to Pampli now was no better.

The beast lunged.

The crowd roared louder, as if they sensed the end drawing closer.

Sakura jumped back and dropped the knife, as if she had already decided she would not be needing it anymore. From the outside, it might have looked like surrender, like she was accepting death. But...

Pampli was already preparing to leap.

And in that brief instant, when there was no time left to think, she suddenly understood what to do. No, not understood. Felt it.

Sakura felt the power in her chest and, for the first time, did not shove it away. Instead she reached for it herself, like the last chance she had left.

Sakura threw one hand forward, straight in front of the beast’s muzzle, as if that could somehow protect her, and in the same instant something inside her seemed to break open.

“[Scarlet Ash Tamer]!”

A sharp, painful release of alien will, as if something in her blood had opened its eyes.

A small scarlet light flared above her palm, like a clump of light and ash at the same time, a brief flash of something ancient and hungry. It shivered, stretched into thin crimson threads, almost too fine for the eye to catch, and dissolved right in front of Pampli.

The beast stopped dead.

It happened so suddenly that for one terrible moment Sakura thought he would still crush into her just from momentum. But no. Pampli froze only a few steps away, so abruptly it was as if he had run into an invisible wall. Mud slid under his paws, clumps of clay sprayed outward.

The stands quieted. Not completely, of course. Somewhere an incredulous curse still rolled through, someone sucked in a sharp breath, someone gasped.

Sakura froze too.

Her hand was still stretched out in front of her. Her fingers tingled. She did not fully understand what she had just done. She only knew one thing: that had not been a Devourer strike. Not destruction. It had been the zero-damage skill: [Scarlet Ash Tamer].

Pampli jerked his head. Sakura tensed all over again, sure that now the beast would snap into even greater fury, but instead a sound came from his throat that sent chills down her spine. A whine. Hurt and confused.

The enormous obsidian beast blinked, shook his head, then slowly, as if he did not understand what he was doing, lowered himself down. At last he lay directly at her feet, huge, all crimson cracks and old scars. Sakura’s head spun for a second.

He no longer wanted to kill her. The scarlet in his eyes remained, but it had softened.

The crowd shifted. Even the announcer above had fallen silent.

Pampli let out a low, rough breath and edged a little closer, as if searching for her hand. It was so absurd, so alien to everything the arena had been a moment ago, that something inside Sakura went blank. She could not believe what she was seeing.

“What...” she breathed.

At last a voice came down from the stands. Merren’s voice. It tore across the arena in a sharp, almost shrill shout, stripped of every trace of his earlier swagger:

“Seize the damned mage!”
