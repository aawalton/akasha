James drew his Windblade against the four woodmen spearmen. First, he tested the waters, swinging his sword blindly and activating his Windblade. The slash aimed for their heads, but it was blocked by their shields.

James dashed toward one knight. His parallel thought activated ice spears at the others while he breathed fire on the remaining knight. Shields were his most hated weapon because they rendered weapon skills ineffective. But James had learned how to cope with them. If shields were all they had, James did not fear them. He was a jack of all trades; he could rely on his magic, pure stats, or his lightning horn. The knights shrieked as the fire breath assaulted them.

“Despite being a tree, you guys can scream, huh.” James smirked when he saw his plan had worked, then broke into a sprint toward the treeman. For half a heartbeat, everything felt perfect—the distance closing, victory within reach—

Then, the ground ripped away from him.

Vines coiled around his ankle and yanked him hard. His face slammed into the dirt, the impact knocking the breath from his lungs.

“What the heck?” James was stupefied. He set himself ablaze, burning the vines that tried to coil around him. A golden glow flashed at the corner of his eye, sending a shudder down his spine. Flame rushed out from his new wing as he leaped away from the golden spear that went for his heart. It pierced the ground where he had been moments before.

James regained his breath as he landed on top of a house. If one were to think about it, a tree soldier controlling plants was normal, but he had never seen anyone control them directly before; that was why he was caught off guard.

“Shit. Healing magic too? I am going to get that,” James muttered as green light glowed from one of the wounded knights, restoring its bark-like skin from its burnt state back to verdant green. James’ healing mostly came from Health Potions, and recently he could recover by using his biomass as well, but those healing methods couldn’t be used until the battle was over.

Healing magic was different. It could be used anytime as long as one had enough mana. With healing magic, one did not need to fear being wounded during a fight.

Unfortunately, James had yet to see someone with a Life Magic mana core. Andrew and Hou Yi had it, but none of his enemies could use it yet. If he could kill it, that meant he could use healing magic as well.

“I need to kill them quickly.” James leaped from one house to another as he swung his sword, casting Windblade. He thought he wouldn’t need a bow since his sword could do ranged attacks, but he was sorely mistaken. Weapon skills still reigned supreme. The enemy didn’t use a shield skill, but their shields still repelled all of James’ attacks. If it had been a bow skill, the arrow would have penetrated the shield and killed them.

“Can’t they use shield skill? Then this will be easy.” James smirked. A golden glow shone from his sword, and he lunged. The tree knights seemed to smirk at his action. That was when a chill ran down James’ spine. He knew he had been duped when the knights’ shields glowed.

“Fuck!” James’ skill was repelled by the shield skill, tossing his arm back. His flank was open, and the knights took advantage. They activated their spear skill, aiming for James’ heart.

Anticipating the knight to have a shield skill, he channeled his mana into his wing glider. With a short burst, he evaded a golden strike from the knight. But he didn’t expect the other three knights to use a different spear skill. They threw their spears like javelins, each aiming for James’ life. It was the same as Andrew’s skill. James felt dread; he had no more cards up his sleeve to dodge the three spears coming for his life.

James spun and raised the shield in his hand. The beetle shield blocked the spears that aimed for his head and chest. The shield cracked but held on. However, the last golden meteor pierced through his stomach. Like a bullet, the spear tore through James’ body, exited from his back, and exploded into a stone house behind him.

“Fuck!” James cursed as he felt a throbbing pain in his gut. Blood gushed out like a fountain. James used his ice magic to seal the wound. It was a desperate attempt, but it worked.

That last attack had surprised him, but he saw the silver lining. Now that those three knights had lost their spears, James thought it should be easy. James gaped as new spears grew from their arms, and when they took a throwing stance, they shone golden.

“Fuck! That’s cheating!” James leaped away, his eyes keeping a close watch on the incoming projectiles. Luckily, the spears couldn’t home in on their target. James dodged three of them and blocked the last one with his beetle shield. The strenuous activity sent a pulsing pain through his hollowed flank.

The knights spawned another set of spears in their hands. This time, James knew he couldn’t win. He called his raptor and dashed toward it. He tucked his pride away and drove the raptor away from the great tree. For the first time in the dungeon, James fled a battle.

The knights shot their spears once more, but they couldn’t hit the fleeing raptor. Glancing behind his back, James noticed the knights were hot on his heels. Fortunately, the raptor was still faster.

His vision grew hazy from the wound, and his raptor was encumbered with weapons and loot James had plundered from the city. The knights shot out more spear skills, aiming for his life, but they couldn’t lock onto his galloping raptor.

“No. Buy a Health Potion.” James sighed as he collapsed outside the city. The knights didn’t follow him anymore. Biomass was precious. He needed it to survive until the crimson moon.

Outside, the land was desolate—devoid of life. It was barely a week ago when it had been teeming with life. No grass, no trees, not even skeletons.

All was consumed by the cockroaches.

“Should I go back? Or should I challenge those knights again?” James muttered. Now that he knew their tricks, he could prepare. He had foolishly come without preparation. James was certain he could tilt the scale in his favor with some well-placed grenades when he engaged those knights.

Just as he was about to rechallenge the knights, black clouds approached from the horizon. James channeled his magic and erected an ice dome for himself and his mount. Those cockroaches were back, and they were more numerous than ever.

James wondered for a bit how Marus and the others survived the cockroaches. The answer was simple: they never went past a certain boundary.

Luckily, the cockroaches didn’t spare James a single glance. They hadn’t seen James now, so they only saw James’ ice dome as non-edible.

“So those cockroaches nested inside the trees,” James muttered. “Well. I guess I have to challenge it again next month.”

There was no way James could challenge the knights when the cockroaches were around. For a moment, he wondered how the knights survived when the bugs supposedly ate everything on sight. James hesitated.

Should he go inside the city to scout, or should he leave and come back next month?

Scouting inside the city was risky. If the knights were alive and worked together with the cockroaches, that would spell the end for James. But if the knights were also eaten or became non-active because of the cockroaches, James could enter the great tree unimpeded. He could light himself on fire or use ice magic to thwart the cockroaches.

“Let’s watch from here for a few days.” James made a compromise. He scouted the city from different angles, driving his raptor around the perimeter. He also went closer to the gate, sneaking up to the wall and scouting the tree from afar. As he feared, the knights could somehow control the cockroaches. The pests didn’t attack the knights, nor did the knights attack the pests. They acted as if the bugs were pets and the great tree was their home. This took five days and 500 biomass.

James decided to call it quits and left, leaving the ruin as the cockroaches enveloped the tree with their black carapace. The verdant tree soon turned black with the exception of its branches and leaves. The cockroaches didn’t eat the tree; they merely nested there, unmoving.

James wondered how that giant tree had survived the crimson moon that had transformed all life, and he found his answer. There was no other tree as tall and massive as that giant tree. The crimson moon should have transformed all life. That tree should have been transformed into shrubs or other creatures. But it didn’t. James was sure that those cockroaches were protecting the tree somehow.

“That’s a question for after the next red moon,” James said as he drove his raptor back.

Despite the lack of monsters and vegetation, the return journey took longer than the departure. The raptor had enough strength to carry all of James’ loot. The problem was the space. Just tying everything with a steel cord made the load bulky and disrupted the raptor’s balance. Hence the slower return journey.

It took him another five days to return. When he arrived, the scenery was desolate. Only withered, rotting grass and occasional dead trees were left around the town. These men were like locusts, devouring anything and everything in sight.

James could understand the logic, but he couldn’t help but frown at the practice. Since there was no sun, farming was futile. All they could do to survive was kill and harvest every plant and animal so they could store and eat it later until the next moon came.

James sighed as he entered the city, wondering whether Kouki was available.

He would need his help now to contact a local blacksmith to repair the rusted weapons. He wondered what those rusted things would turn into. Would they become weapons with skills similar to his Windblade? Or would they become mundane weapons like his iron spear?

It was time to find out.
