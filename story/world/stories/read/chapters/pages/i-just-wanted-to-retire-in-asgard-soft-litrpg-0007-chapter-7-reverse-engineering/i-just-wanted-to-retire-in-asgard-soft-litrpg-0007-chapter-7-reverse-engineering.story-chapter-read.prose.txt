Outside the workshop, the lava flats ran black and red under a sky the color of slag. Barry stood at the edge of a melted ridge with the new prototype portal gun in his hands.

The housing was dragon-horn, dark and dense, with lava-forged crystal channels running through it that pulsed blue. It looked more like a weapon than a way to travel.

Beside him stood Brokkr's younger cousin, the only dwarf who had ever walked on the leveled floor and come back with his beard and his mind intact. Everyone called him Surface now. He was holding a crate of calibrated crystal rods, confused about whether what they were doing was allowed.

"Experiment number eight," Barry said.

He adjusted the output, and the gears locked with a click. The heat off the flats pressed against his palm and face, but the housing stayed cool. That was the point of the material. Dragon-horn didn't conduct like divine alloy. It absorbed, held, and redirected, and it didn't bleed energy off along the way.

He pointed at open air and pulled the trigger.

A beam of blue light hit the air and spread into a disc full of small blue stars.

Barry came close to the disc, wearing his goggles, and the details followed, showing all green.

"Let's see." He muttered, placing a hand inside the portal, then slowly pushing his head. A whole new world behind it.

"Haha... hahahaha... HAHAHAHA! I did it!"

Yes, finally, after so long, he had fixed his portal travel. The Bifrost wasted so much energy every time it opened. The gun needed only coordinates and a tenth of the power.

He turned to the dwarf, Surface. "You know your part."

Surface swallowed and nodded. He hadn't agreed to this, exactly, but somehow Barry had talked him into it. He walked toward the portal with his legs shaking, shut his eyes, and jumped.

The portal closed a heartbeat before he reached it. He landed face-first on the basalt.

Barry checked the gun. The charge was gone. "The crystals are a crutch," he said. "We're not using it."

Surface spat out grit and threw the crystals he was holding. "Then what are we using?"

"Kinetic energy, properly converted and held." Barry turned the gun over and checked the alignment of the crystal throat. He thought about the dragon. He'd hit it with everything he had and hadn't put a dent in it. The horn hadn't resisted the blow. It had absorbed it.

"I have an idea."

"I hope it doesn't involve dangerous fields and thundering electricity."

"Thor hits it super hard. We catch the strike, shunt it into the gun, and open a resonance window. No magic. Just plain physics, surface."

Surface set the crate down on the basalt, took out a rod, inspected the cut, and put it aside. "What if the conversion fails?"

"Thor hits a rock and we try again."

"And what about me?"

"You stand behind the rock, of course."

Surface only nodded, but his heart was beating hard, as Thor's full power had previously vaporized anvils and whole civilizations.

Barry looked up and said Thor's name once, quietly. It wasn't a prayer but a call.

The sky cracked, and Thor came down, shrinking from his usual giant size, Mjolnir in his hand. He looked at the device, then at Barry's face, and nodded once. "I'm ready."

"I haven't explained it yet."

"You'll tell me where. I'll hit it."

Barry braced the resonator against a basalt outcrop he'd chosen for its density and angle. He aimed the crystal throat at open air, checked the charge lattice one more time, and stepped back. "Use full power and don't hold back, not even a little."

"I am the god of thunder. I never hold back."

Mjolnir came down, raw and heavy.

Doom!

The strike hit with a flash and a pressure wave that cracked the ridge. The housing didn't crack. Its crystal channels flared, the hum climbed, and then everything locked. Three meters ahead, a circle tore open in the air, edged with the same thin blue light as the Bifrost.

For a second it showed Midgard: a dense forest, rolling hills beyond it.

"Experiment nine," Barry said, with a small, pleased smile. "Independent transit. No All-Father required." He glanced at Thor. "Thor is required about once a month. Which is a chore."

Surface let out a breath. "It worked. It really worked. Sir Barry!"

Thor lowered Mjolnir and studied the cracked ridge. He touched it, his hand passing right through. Yes, it felt like Bifrost but not at the same time.

[Source Code]

No warning. No lock. He didn't trust that.

Click!

The remote's antenna went hot in his hand. The screen held out for a long second and then gave.

What came back wasn't the code he'd expected. It was older.

Layers of it. Code that predated the Aesir's runes, that predated the first filtering of Yggdrasil's output, that predated the idea of "gods" as anything more than early users.

Barry read it, and kept reading. The remote slipped out of his fingers and hit the floor. He didn't look at it.

It was recursive, compact, and utterly complete. Nested functions mapped force to effect, belief to yield, observation to persistence. Leveling up. The interface didn't just explain things. It showed its whole architecture, and for Barry, who could digest anything, in this one moment he felt burdened by the sheer quantity and quality of the data he had just seen. So much so that for a second he forgot to even breathe. His eyes blurred, and it took him a moment to realize why.

"Sir Barry?" Surface had stopped packing. "What happened?"

"No, nothing." Barry's voice was almost quiet, even respectful.

"Nothing at all."

He closed the interface. The afterimage stayed behind his eyes. The remote lay on the floor with its antenna bent and its casing blackened. It wouldn't be working again. Next to it, the old notification was still hanging in the air.

Science and magic had never been separate. Everything he'd worked from was a subset of this. "The System isn't managing us," he said, mostly to himself. "It's what everything runs on."
