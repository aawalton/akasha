Plummeting through the sky, Harry didn't even have the strength to open his eyes. He only felt his body surrendering to the wind. The only thing he could deduce from this floor was that it was daylight.

In a semi-awake state, he was aware of his exhaustion and, to a certain extent, of his surroundings, but nothing more. His mind constantly told him to go back to sleep, but Harry knew that if he gave in to comfort, he wouldn't wake up again.

Aware that he was about to die, he stayed awake. Harry then opened his eyes, the shock of what he saw fully waking him.

"Fuckin' hell...!" he said to himself as his eyes widened.

Below him was what appeared to be the top of a tower on a small island. It was tall and emitted a bright light that circled the island. Is it a lighthouse? Harry thought, but he didn't have enough information. Around the island was an ocean that stretched to the edge of the floor.

Like an eye in the center of the ocean, the lighthouse's light illuminated the surface, almost as if the sea was searching for itself. Entangled in the rocks near the island and on the walls of the lighthouse, yellowish vines climbed and took over the surroundings, a botanical virus.

Harry, while observing the island, also noticed a figure falling below him, one with broad shoulders and pieces of metal in the palms of his hands. Goldman! Of course it would still be falling; he descended just seconds before me...

Goldman, for his part, specially having withstood the weight of a mountain, didn't seem to be in much better shape than Harry. The young thief might not have been able to see it, given the distance between them, but pockets of fatigue were soon forming under Goldman's eyes, which could only mean one thing: if he didn't get some rest now, he would be in serious trouble.

The man then noticed Harry's presence and gradually tried to pull him. The force of the pull, however, wasn't strong enough due to the distance between them. If they had more time, it would work, but at the moment, they had to think fast.

Before he could expend any more energy on it, however, he felt a solid, yet somewhat liquid, presence around his body. He slid along this form, which seemed to gradually decrease his speed as a path was procedurally generated to the shore of the flying island.

From the Locksmith's perspective, it was possible to see Goldman being carried by a... paint slide? Its striking texture and colors could never have gone unnoticed by Harry, who, perhaps unconsciously, immediately thought of Madeline. But of course, they fell before... If they're here, they'd be waiting for us. And the group had no idea what Goldman and the Locksmith had just done.

Vela, Daniel, and the others were also nearby, and looks of shock were visible on their faces as soon as they saw Goldman's condition. Following the pirate, Harry fell onto the slide that was losing its mass.

With some effort, the two then threw themselves onto the sand when they reached the bottom. Harry, not quite understanding what had happened, instinctively pulled a C-Coin from his pocket and immediately activated it. He didn't know when this loop might reset or even if this specific location existed at the beginning of it, but he did it anyway. After that, he turned his face down, feeling thousands of tiny bits itching his face as he rested on the sand.

Goldman, for his part, lay on the sand with one arm over his eyes. Inadvertently, he smiled at everything he had been through so far. His shoulder throbbed with pain, and he could no longer close the muscles in his left hand. How were they still alive? Some would say it was luck, or even fate, but Goldman knew; it's the human spirit. A will that transcends all barriers, barriers he never thought he would overcome amidst unwavering danger. He chuckled softly and sighed again, taking a C-Coin from his pocket and using it immediately.

Before the others could come to their aid, Harry and Goldman embraced their unconscious states.

"He's dead, okay!?" A woman screamed, her hands trembling. "I saw him on the ground before I went down. The candle-wielding madman and the little professor wouldn't have had time to get there before, and even if they did, some rock from the mountain would have hit them."

Inside the lighthouse it was damp, especially in the basement. You could feel the effects of the sea all around, like the walls sweating and the ceiling dripping.

Inside the room, a tall and elegant man sat on the stairs. His features exuded perplexity, his emotions hid in a coffin inside his mind as he listened to the girl's retelling of the story. Did it matter? If the kid was dead or alive? If they could reach the last floor, all of this trouble would be swept away as the clock ran its twenty four hours or so they thought. They already waited too long waiting for her to come back, and despite her best attempts, he could sense a warmth on her voice that wasn't there before. This was going to be a trouble.

"But what about the others?" Another man, with the proportions of a barrel, said. "Me and Gale here don't want any trouble like last time. Y'all remember last time, right?"

Leaning on a door to his right, a scrubby guy with an ostensive hat simply nodded. The cowboy-wanna-be pointed to him in a snap-like fashion, a sharp crack coming from the tip of his fingers.

"About that, um..." She looked at her feet. "They can be dealt with, okay? But it's going to be a bit harder. The monk is a fucking menace with his candles and the other thief girl can mess with ink and brushes alike... and there are two literal giants on the team."

"Fuck me..." The big guy said. "And here I was thinking you were up for the job—"

"Oh shut the fuck up, Cal." Prism raised her voice. "You and Gale wouldn't last a day with those guys. Tweedledee and Tweedledum would get themselves caught the second they opened their mouths. You know what, since we're already fucked, I'm saying it; that's why Alisa dropped on us. You couldn't keep your damn mouth closed and scared her, you pervert fat fuc—"

"Enough!" The man on the stairs said, not taking his eyes from the water drops on the wall. "She did her job. She caught the journal; that's what we needed. Tomorrow, after we deal with them, he'll show us access to the last floor."

"Why did he want it? Didn't Prism say it only had some symbols and stuff? Why can't it be today?" The scrubby guy answered. "Are we even sure he's trustworthy? I'm getting impatient just sitting on my ass all day."

"We can see that, Gale." The man addressed him, his stare piercing. "He is not a trustworthy ally. But, we don't need a trustworthy ally; we need to have a common enemy. Now listen closely. We are not fucked."

"How come?" Cal said, still red from the argument with Prism. "You heard what she said, right? A man who can create and manipulate polarity fields, one that can summon arrows and a guy who fights with a fucking candelabrum. They defeated Dracno, for gods sake!"

"That was mostly Harry..." She replied succinctly while her hands now shook less. "And he's Conrad, now. Not Dracno."

"Nobody fucking cares." Gale answered for his friend. "Cal is right; are we equipped to deal with this shit? Wouldn't it be better if we just laid low?"

"Are you a child, Gale?" The man's words seemed to shut him up. "Are you scared? Is that what this is? I can tolerate fear, but not cowardice. You and Cal can outmatch that Daniel man and Gold-whatever-his-name-is tenfold without a scratch. Emblems on the Triangular form are normally pretty inflexible."

Like a defeated dog, Gale just stared at the floor, afraid to argue with the group's leader. Cal and Prism also fell silent, listening only to what the tall man had to say. Standing up, he stared at a drop falling on the wall in front of him and slightly turned his head. At that same moment, the wall began to vibrate, causing the group members to move away.

The wall then cracked open as water poured from the hole, quickly filling the room. That's much better. He thought to himself. I hate leaks.

"They won't leave it if I don't allow it." He then gave a fake smile and patted Cal and Gale on the back. "Let's go home, boys."
