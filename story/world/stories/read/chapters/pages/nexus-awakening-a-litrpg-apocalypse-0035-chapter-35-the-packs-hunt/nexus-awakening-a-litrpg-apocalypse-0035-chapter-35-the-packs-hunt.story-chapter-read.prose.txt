Chapter 35: The Pack's Hunt

Hours passed before I decided on a course of action. The plan formed slowly as I sat perched on a thick tree branch, my senses extending outward through the alien forest like invisible fingers testing the air. The twisted bark beneath me pulsed with warmth, and I could feel the Flux flowing through everything around me, connecting the scattered pieces of this impossible ecosystem.

I was learning that Perception carried more value than I had initially considered. Back when I was first learning the basics of the Flux system, I had dismissed it as secondary to the raw power of Resonance and Projection. Seemed logical enough at the time. Why worry about sensing things when you could just hit them harder? But perception was the foundation that everything else was built on. You couldn't crush what you couldn't sense. You couldn't command what you couldn't reach.

The realization annoyed me. All those attribute points I'd poured into Resonance and Projection, building my offensive capabilities while neglecting the very thing that made those abilities useful. Like building a house with a perfect foundation but forgetting to install windows. You could live in it, but you'd be blind to everything happening outside.

My bloodline helped with detection, granted me a natural authority over the aspects that other initiates struggled to even recognize. But that advantage could be enhanced dramatically with proper Perception training, and mine remained embarrassingly low. Four points. Pathetic for someone of my level, a glaring weakness in my development that had gone unaddressed while I focused on offensive capabilities.

The shortcoming hardly mattered in direct combat, when enemies stood directly in front of me or circled close enough to touch. When violence became personal and immediate, raw power could overcome subtle deficiencies. A resonance blade to the throat worked the same whether you had four Perception or forty. But for what I was planning, the limitation forced me to work harder than necessary.

I could have compensated by investing in better equipment, maybe. Found some kind of Flux-enhanced scrying device or perception amplifier. But I didn't have access to that kind of gear, and even if I did, I probably wouldn't know how to use it properly. My essence crafting skills were basic at best, focused on keeping myself alive rather than creating sophisticated tools.

If my Perception had been higher, perhaps I could have completed the first steps of my plan from the comfortable distance of my tree branch. As it was, I had to abandon my elevated position and venture closer to the creatures I needed. I left the backpack behind, wanting mobility over supplies. The canvas pack would only slow me down, and what I was about to attempt required speed and precision.

Flux beasts roamed these twisted woodlands in abundance. Wolves with eyes like burning coals and boars whose metallic hide clinked softly as they moved through the undergrowth. At this point in my development, they presented little challenge to most initiates. These creatures had been designed as obstacles for newcomers, not tests for those who had survived long enough to matter.

They tried to run from me when I approached, their primitive minds recognizing the predator that walked among them. Smart beasts. Smarter than some of the initiates I'd killed, at least. They understood the food chain, knew when something higher up was hunting in their territory. But understanding didn't matter when that something could reach directly into their consciousness and rewrite their priorities.

I commanded their aspects with the casual authority of my awakened bloodline, reaching out through the Flux and snatching their consciousness like the fist of an angry god closing around helpless insects. The resistance was minimal, their wills too simple to offer any real defense against someone with my capabilities. Like trying to stop a sledgehammer with tissue paper.

The sensation was intoxicating. Pure dominance flowing through me as their wills bent to mine, their instincts overridden by power they couldn't comprehend or resist. One by one, they abandoned their flight and turned toward me with eyes that reflected my own amber glow.

They came to me willingly then, the Flux beasts of this alien forest. Wolves whose shadows seemed too deep for their bodies, and boars that sparked when they moved. Crawling things that defied easy description, their forms shifting between states of matter as if they couldn't decide whether to be solid or gas. Before long, I had assembled a pack of roughly fifteen creatures, their combined presence creating a low harmonic that vibrated through the ground beneath my feet.

Their levels were low compared to mine, which allowed me to maintain firm control over their aspects. Any more than this number, and I would struggle to keep the mental threads untangled, the connections clear. Commanding multiple minds simultaneously required focus and strength, both of which had limits even with my enhanced capabilities.

I had them follow me as I made my way back toward the camp outside the cave, a small army of nightmare creatures padding silently through the twisted trees. Their eyes glowed in the eternal twilight of the dungeon, and their breathing created a rhythm that almost matched my own mechanical respiration. We moved like a hunting pack with a purpose, predators united under a single will.

When the camp came into sight through the alien foliage, I gathered my bloodline's power and surged it outward, commanding the beasts to attack with all the fury their primitive natures could muster.

The response was immediate and overwhelming. With growling roars that shook dust from the cave mouth, the creatures stampeded across the open ground between forest and camp. The earth trembled under their charge, and the air filled with the sound of claws against stone and the otherworldly cries of beings that had never known fear until this moment.

There was no stealth to the assault. Fifteen large Flux beasts moving together created a thunder that echoed off the dungeon walls, drawing attention from every corner of the camp. Even if they had been moving silently, creatures of their size grouped in such numbers would have been impossible to miss. The initiates noticed immediately, their shouts of alarm echoing across the black stone as they scrambled to respond to the sudden threat.

Resonance weapons blazed into existence across the camp, white and blue and crimson light cutting through the artificial atmosphere as defenders prepared to meet the charge. I watched from the forest edge as battle erupted in a chaos of violence and terror, exactly as I had planned.

I didn't expect the beasts to kill many of the initiates, and that wasn't the point of this exercise. They were a distraction, a way to scatter attention and create the kind of confusion that opportunities were born from. While the defenders focused on the immediate threat of tooth and claw, I would strike where they least expected it.

I strode out from the tree line behind my creatures, my own resonance blade igniting with its familiar hum. The white light touched with crimson cut through the smoke and dust raised by the stampede, marking my presence for any who cared to look. But the initiates were too busy fighting for their lives to notice one more threat approaching from an unexpected direction.

As the defenders engaged the Flux beasts in desperate combat, I used the chaos to devastating advantage. Reaching out with my power, I began systematically destroying their infrastructure. Tents collapsed as I crushed their support poles, canvas and rope tangling around the legs of anyone unfortunate enough to be standing nearby. Wooden tables exploded into splinters that became improvised shrapnel, cutting down initiates who thought they were safely behind cover.

My Flux Crush ability worked overtime, bursting apart anything that lacked sufficient Containment to resist. Weapons racks toppled, scattering blades across the ground where running feet could trip over them. Water barrels split open, their contents mixing with blood to create treacherous footing. Supply crates detonated like grenades, sending their contents flying in all directions.

The camp transformed from an organized defensive position into a nightmare of debris and obstacles, every surface becoming a potential weapon turned against its former owners. Broken wood became spears. Scattered metal became caltrops. Even the ground itself became treacherous as water mixed with blood to create slick patches that sent running feet sliding into walls or each other.

I moved through the destruction like a conductor directing a symphony of violence, my mechanical breathing providing a steady rhythm beneath the chaos. The respirator's filters worked overtime, processing smoke and dust and the metallic tang of spilled blood. Each breath reminded me how far I'd come from the man who used to worry about sawdust in his lungs at the end of a long day's work.

My blade claimed any life that crossed my path. A young man with ritual scars covering his arms turned toward me just in time to lose his head, the resonance energy cauterizing the wound before his body understood it was dead. An older woman tried to gather Flux for some kind of binding technique, but I consumed her gathered aspects and drove my weapon through her chest before she could complete it.

I froze others with my Command Presence, not even bothering to maintain control of the Flux beasts at this point. They were in the thick of combat now, their hunting instincts fully engaged. They would continue fighting until they were killed or there was nothing left to fight, requiring no further guidance from me.

The sounds of battle filled the air around me. Shouts of command and cries of pain echoed off the cave mouth, mixing with the roars of dying beasts and the distinctive chimes of resonance weapons meeting in deadly conflict. It was the music of warfare, harsh and discordant but carrying its own terrible rhythm. The kind of symphony you learned to appreciate when you'd heard it enough times.

Some part of me catalogued the sounds automatically, sorting them by threat level and priority. A scream of pain meant someone was down but not necessarily dead. A sudden silence meant either victory or death, and either could be dangerous depending on who had achieved it. The wet sounds of Flux beasts feeding meant the distraction was still working, still drawing attention away from my own activities.

It didn't take long for some of the initiates to notice my presence among them. Two dark-haired women broke away from their own fights, abandoning the Flux beasts they had been battling to converge on what they correctly identified as the greater threat. Their whip-like resonance weapons snapped through the air toward me, seeking to bind or lacerate whatever they could reach.

The weapons moved with practiced precision, suggesting these weren't complete novices. They'd survived long enough to develop actual skill with their chosen forms, long enough to understand that sometimes you had to abandon one fight to prevent a worse one from developing. Smart tactical thinking. It wouldn't save them, but I could respect the competence.

I crushed the throat of the first woman while simultaneously freezing the second with my aura, my divided attention proving more than sufficient for opponents of their caliber. The first one dropped without a sound, hands clawing at her ruined windpipe as her brain slowly registered that it wasn't getting any more oxygen. The frozen initiate could only watch in horror as my blade thrust forward, emerging from her chest in a spray of blood and the deadly hum of disrupted energy fields. Her body crumpled to the ground like a marionette with severed strings.

My mechanical breathing continued its methodical rhythm as I went about my work, the respirator's filters processing the smoke and dust without missing a beat. Each inhalation and exhalation marked another moment of violence, another step toward the goal that had brought me to this place.

The plan was working perfectly. The camp's organized defense had dissolved into scattered pockets of resistance, their numbers whittled down by both beast and blade. Soon there would be few enough survivors that I could finish them off without risk, claim whatever points they carried, and move on to the real prize waiting in the cave's depths.

I told myself it was tactical brilliance, using the beasts as a distraction while I systematically dismantled their defenses. Smart planning. Efficient execution.

No. It was just necessity. The killing itself held no appeal, but the victory did. Proving I was stronger, faster, better prepared than anyone who stood against me. The Flux beasts had given me an advantage and I'd used it, simple as that.

My mechanical breathing continued its steady rhythm as bodies fell around me. This was what I was good at now. Not building things that lasted, but overcoming obstacles that stood in my way. Each death was just another problem solved, another step forward.

I wanted that key in the cave. Wanted the power it represented. Everything else was just work that needed doing.
