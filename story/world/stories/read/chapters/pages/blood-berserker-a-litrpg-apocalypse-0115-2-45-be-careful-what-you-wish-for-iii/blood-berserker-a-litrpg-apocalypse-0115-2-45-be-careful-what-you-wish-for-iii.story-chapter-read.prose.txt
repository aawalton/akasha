Tristan couldn’t help but feel annoyed with the creature's tough hide, but he refused to concede defeat as he continued to fire arrows at the hellhound even as he increased the distance between them.

His arrows bounced off the head, throat, and sides of the hellhound, falling to the dirt, without so much as making a cut in the demon's hide.

Tristan took a couple of shots at their eyes but ended up without any success as the heads of the hellhounds had now learned to close their eyes, so the arrows just bounced off harmlessly.

The demon might just be hell's dog, but it was smart enough to learn from the mistakes of the other heads. So the ranger was forced with no option but to retreat, at least until he could create enough space to make sure that the hellhound couldn’t immediately attack him if it got out of the mud.

He completely moved away, abandoning any notion of firing arrows at the hellhound wading through the mud; he’d rather just use the time to run and think.

That was what separated him from these beasts. The ranger knew that his most important weapon was his brain. His bow and arrow were just a means to an end if he could not rely on his brain to get him out of a pinch.

As nice as that sounded, nothing was really working out for Tristan. He knew that he had to think up a plan faster, but at the moment, he might as well have been smacking the heads of the hellhound with a twig, as none of his arrows were doing any damage. So, he turned and ran again.

His recently seared back sent waves of fresh pain down his spine as he ran, his muscles stretching and contracting with each step he took, but Tristan didn't pause to catch his breath. That aside, he had one more use of [Nature’s embrace II] and his last remaining skill [Take root II], which he was saving for the demon prince.

[Take root II] was very brutal, even for Tristan. But the ranger couldn’t imagine a creature more deserving of having its body turned into a tree than the demon prince. But that wasn’t the only reason he avoided using the skill.

[Take root II] would temporarily give him a 25% debuff for thirty minutes. And the skill only had a thirty percent chance of actually working, as well as the fact that if his target was to remove the arrow before the six seconds required for [Take root II] to activate, the process would immediately end.

The skill was so volatile and unreliable that Tristan preferred to keep it as a last resort, and even then, he wasn’t even sure he’d use it, because the skill did far more harm than it was worth.

But in this case, with nothing working against the hellhound, then it was up to him to use [Take root II], and if he was going to actually have a shot at pulling this off, then everything had to be perfect; he couldn’t afford to make a small mistake, or else he risked everything collapsing

I should maximize my chances by preparing the spot with [Quick fire II]. Tristan still needed to wait for the skill to cool down completely, but that was besides the point.

Of course, it all depended on him gambling on the fact that he’d be able to injure the hellhound by using [Quick fire II] to fire at a singular spot on the hellhound.

Considering what he had seen, he was going to have to rule out the eyes of the hellhound as a target because their eyelids seemed to be even stronger than their normal hide. On the other hand, there was no telling what would happen if he could actually use a [Take root II] prepared arrow to take out one of the heads through the eyes.

It was very much possible that only the head of the hellhound hit with the skill that would be affected, and the others would survive as that head crumpled into itself. Or maybe the other heads would tear out the infected head themselves.

He couldn’t risk it.

To give himself the best shot at success, he'd have to pick a spot running at the spine of the hellhound where the monster could not reach, nor easily remove by throwing itself on the ground.

The spine it is.

A plan had been fully formed, and all that Tristan had to do was try his best to stay alive long enough to actually execute it.

And the best way he could do that was by running, so he pushed all his strength into his legs. Pumping his feet faster and faster, gritting his teeth as his burnt skin at his flexed, with each bead of sweat that rolled down the nape of his neck and onto his burnt back, causing a stinging sensation.

The ranger cursed his luck as he pressed on.

[Quick fire II] only needed about a second before he’d be ready to try out his plan. And as much as he’d like to deny it, the plan wasn’t reliable, but it was the only chance he had at defeating the hellhound, so he stuck by it.

The ranger did not believe in luck; he’d always played the hand that he was dealt unfazed, and this time wouldn’t be any different. He would hold on as long as he could, and when the time came to fire, he would go through with his plan. And if he failed, then so be it.
