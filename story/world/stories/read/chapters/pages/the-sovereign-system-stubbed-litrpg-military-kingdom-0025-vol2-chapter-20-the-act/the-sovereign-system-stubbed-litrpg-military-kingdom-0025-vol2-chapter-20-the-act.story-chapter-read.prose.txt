After a restful sleep in the palace guest room, Luke and Victoria returned to the alchemist’s workshop to perform a few final checks on the product.

“It’s time to load the Thunder Bombs,” Luke said with a faint smile.

“Yes, wonderful!” Blake exclaimed, clapping his hands. He turned to his two assistants. “Retrieve the gunpowder and prepare the wax and rope.”

Luke’s eyes glinted ever so slightly as the assistants brought out the clay jars, now filled with gunpowder. A hint of pity mixed with amusement touched his thoughts as he watched the pride on their faces.

My poor youngsters… I’m sorry in advance, he thought.

As the two men placed the jars on the workbench, Luke stepped forward and inspected the contents. Just as he’d expected, the mix held traces of moisture—evidence of the damp air and the porous material of the jars.

“Is everything in order, Viscount?” Blake asked, his voice slightly hoarse.

Luke let out a heavy sigh and wore a disappointed expression.

“Which one of you stored the gunpowder?” he asked calmly, glancing at the two assistants.

The young men visibly stiffened. Their gazes darted toward Blake, their faces painted with dread.

“Well?!” Luke’s voice shot up, his calm veneer cracking.

“Viscount, is there a problem with the gunpowder?” Blake stepped between Luke and the assistants, trying to mediate the situation.

“Is there a problem?!” Luke scoffed, grabbing one of the jars. “This is fucking ruined!” he shouted, hurling the container to the ground.

It shattered on impact, shards scattering across the workshop floor.

“V-Viscount! What is the meaning of this?” Blake trembled, his anger rising.

But Luke didn’t answer. Instead, he snatched a piece of wood, stuck it into the nearby furnace, and waited until the end caught fire. Without hesitation, he brought the flame to the pile of spilled gunpowder.

Blake and the assistants jumped back in alarm, their panic justified. They’d seen firsthand what even a small ignition had done to the last assistant.

But instead of an explosion, there was only a weak sputter—followed by a thick plume of smoke and the sharp scent of sulfur. The flame fizzled out almost immediately.

“Still think this gunpowder isn’t ruined?” Luke spat, his gaze searing into them.

“I-I don’t understand… Yesterday, when we tested the mixture, it ignited just fine,” Blake stammered, clearly shaken.

Luke tossed the smoldering torch aside and pointed at the remaining clay jar on the bench.

“Who the hell told you to store it in that? Of course moisture is going to seep in, you imbeciles!”

“But… the Thunder Bombs are made of clay too. What’s the difference?” one of the assistants asked, defiance creeping into his voice.

Luke’s nostrils flared.

In truth, the apprentice wasn’t entirely wrong. His assumption had merit—but he’d failed to consider a critical detail.

Luke turned his eyes toward Blake, waiting to see if the man would discipline the boy for speaking out of turn.

But the head alchemist stood frozen, too stunned by the outburst to react.

“First of all, the Thunder Bombs are not only fired and glazed, but also sealed tightly. There’s no chance of moisture making its way into the mixture,” Luke said, his tone dangerously low.

“Secondly—who the hell do you think you are to question me?” he snapped, stepping forward until he was right in the assistant’s face. “You’ve done nothing but ruin everything… and you have the nerve to question me? Me?!”

“Viscount, please—calm yourself,” Blake said quickly, stepping in between them once again.

“I made this mixture from scratch during a siege, with less than half the resources you have here,” Luke growled, each word sharpened by fury. “And yet you lot can’t follow basic instructions to complete a recipe that’s already been perfected?”

The assistant shrank back, clearly shaken by Luke’s presence and rising anger.

“I-I’m terribly sorry, Viscount. Please forgive me—and my inept apprentices,” Blake said hastily, bowing his head so low it nearly touched the floor.

Luke’s eyes scanned between the shaken apprentices and the humbled alchemist. His expression twisted with disdain.

“I cannot participate in this farce any longer,” he said, his voice filled with quiet disappointment.

“This is the third day I’ve been here, and somehow we’re even further from completion. I gave you the knowledge—handed it to you—and your buffoonery has done nothing but set us back.”

He turned to Victoria. “We’re leaving.”

With that, Luke pivoted and strode toward the workshop doors.

“V-Viscount! Please wait!” Blake called out desperately.

Luke stopped, glancing over his shoulder at the royal alchemist.

“I’ve fulfilled my duty to the king. What happens now is in your hands. I want no further part in this.”

Without waiting for a response, Luke left the workshop. Victoria followed a step behind, silent the entire way.

Only when they stepped out of the palace and onto the main street did she finally speak.

“I haven’t seen you that angry before,” she said matter-of-factly.

“I wasn’t angry,” Luke replied coolly. “That was all part of the plan.”

He couldn’t hide the smugness in his voice. Now, he no longer had any obligation to return to the alchemist workshop—and no one could complain.

Of course, if the king insisted or made a formal request, Luke wouldn’t have much choice. But he doubted that would happen. The recipe had already been delivered. His part was done.

“So… you planned to storm off like a spoiled noble child?” Victoria asked, her lips curling into a teasing smile.

“Exactly. We’re free now. No more delays. We can focus on what matters.”

Victoria’s eyes gleamed. “You’re talking about the perfume, right?”

“Yes. There are a few steps left before we can launch it. I’m confident in the formula itself—but I’ll need a distributor who won’t steal half my profits,” Luke replied, already shifting into business mode.

“Why not speak to some of your classmates?” Victoria suggested, her tone casual. “I’m sure some of them have merchant connections in the city.”

Luke froze mid-step, casting her a side glance. “You do realize most of the academy probably sees me as a threat, right? I killed the son of a Marquess… and instead of being punished, I was rewarded with a Viscount title.”

“I still don’t think it’s a bad idea,” Victoria said, shrugging.

However, Luke shook his head. “No, I want to remain anonymous. I don’t want people knowing I’m the creator of the perfume, and I definitely don’t want our enemies catching wind that I’m making money.”

“That makes sense,” Victoria agreed. “But if anonymity is what you want, then let me handle the groundwork. I’ll find a merchant for you.”

Luke considered it for a moment before nodding. It made perfect sense to entrust such a delicate task to Victoria—especially given her track record of moving unseen through both noble and underworld circles.

“Alright, sounds good. But first, I need something to sell. There’s no point securing distribution if we have no product.”

The two made their way back to the Royal Academy in relative silence. Luke was lost in thought, mentally refining the basic process of making perfume—despite his limited experience with it.

I’m pretty sure all I need is a solvent and a fragrance. I can use either oil or alcohol as the base... and for the scent, a mix of floral and citrus should be good enough for now, he mused, rubbing his chin thoughtfully.

After passing through the academy gates, Luke turned to Victoria. “See if you can get some rice wine and oil from the kitchens. I’ll head to the gardens and gather some materials.”

“Shall I meet you back at the room?”

Luke nodded, and they went their separate ways.

He made his way toward one of the academy’s many gardens, and it didn’t take long to find what he needed—jasmine, rose, and lavender. The scents of the flowers lingered on the breeze, calming yet vibrant. Perfect for what he had in mind.

This’ll do for the first batch. I can expand the fragrance selection later.

With his gathered ingredients, Luke returned to his room, where Victoria was already waiting, practically bouncing with excitement. In her hands were two large bottles—one filled with rice wine, the other with sesame oil.

“Alright, let’s get started,” Luke said with a grin.

He pulled out two of the glass jars he had quietly taken from the alchemist workshop in the palace and set them on the table. Into one, he poured the oil until it reached three-quarters full. He did the same with the rice wine in the second jar.

Then, grabbing the flowers, Luke crushed them gently in his hands, releasing their natural fragrance. The scent was delicate but distinct, instantly filling the room with a fresh, floral aroma.

He dropped the crushed petals into each jar, sealing the containers tightly before moving them to a shaded corner of the room—one that never received direct sunlight. A perfect spot for steeping.

“What do we do now?” Victoria asked, barely able to contain her excitement.

“Nothing—for now. We just wait a few days before straining it and pouring it into smaller vials for sale,” Luke said, wiping imaginary sweat from his brow.

“Wait... that’s it?” Victoria looked a little let down. She had clearly expected a more complex process.

Luke chuckled. “It might not seem like much now, but trust me—you’ll love the final product.”

He gave her a confident nod. “Do you think you can go out and source some ceramic vials? Make sure they look elegant and expensive. We want the brand to feel luxurious.”

“Brand?” Victoria looked momentarily confused by the term but nodded regardless.

Once she left, Luke found himself with little else to do until the lectures started.

Kayson and Sebastian weren’t expecting him back until the afternoon—they were probably off training somewhere. That left Luke with something he rarely had: free time.

Shall we go to the library then?

Without hesitation, he left the dormitory and made his way to the central building of the academy grounds. This was where most lectures were held and, as such, served as a hub for student activity.

Luke stepped into the library and smiled.

The rows of towering bookshelves, the faint scent of parchment and dust, the silence—he welcomed it all. It didn’t take him long to make his way up to the fifth floor, where he had left off last time.

Leaning casually against a shelf, he picked up a book and began flipping through it slowly, his gaze occasionally skimming the spines of the other tomes.

Not even ten minutes passed before he’d absorbed all the remaining engineering knowledge from the shelf. That section was now fully committed to memory, leaving only the astronomy and medical texts.

I’ve still got some capacity left today. Maybe I should keep going… he thought.

But just as he was about to reach for another book, footsteps echoed behind him.

Luke froze.

“Come to pass the time, young one?” a voice asked.

He turned slowly toward the source of the voice, surprised. It had sounded like an elderly man—but the person standing before him was younger than expected. Mid-forties, perhaps.

The man had thick, raven-black hair peppered with streaks of gray, and thick, bushy eyebrows to match. His face was clean-shaven except for a modest goatee, and a series of faint wrinkles framed the forced smile on his lips.

“I came to read, sir,” Luke replied respectfully.

He’d never seen this man before—not in any class or gathering—and his clothing bore no insignia or markers of rank. But Luke could guess: he was either an instructor or someone important within the library’s administration.

The man laughed lightly and scratched his head. “Forgive me, young one. I’ve been watching you for a while. It didn’t exactly look like you were reading.”

Though the words were casual, Luke detected an edge to them. The man wasn’t pleased. Perhaps he thought Luke was slacking off—or worse, disrespecting the sanctity of the library.

“I can assure you I was reading,” Luke said calmly, bowing once again.

“Oho? I gave you the chance to admit your mistake, and yet you choose to lie to my face?” the man said, his smile thinning. “You’ve got nerve, boy.”

For a moment, the middle-aged man reminded Luke of a wrathful buddha—serene yet dangerous, ready to dispense divine punishment.

“Please, sir,” Luke said, keeping his tone even. “Allow me to prove it.”

He reached for the book he’d just been browsing and handed it to the man.

“Turn to any page,” Luke said with quiet confidence. “I’ll tell you exactly what’s written there.”
