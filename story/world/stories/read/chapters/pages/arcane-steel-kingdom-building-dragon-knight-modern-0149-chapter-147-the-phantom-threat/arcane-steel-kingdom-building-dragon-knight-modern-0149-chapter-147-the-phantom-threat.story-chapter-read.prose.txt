When we returned to the Mobile Command and Information Center Patricia welcomed me and Kaelus.

Kaelus didn't even acknowledge the greeting. He immediately hopped off my shoulder, marched straight up to Patricia, and began scratching aggressively at the hem of her pristine maid uniform. The sharp little claws snagged the fabric, leaving visible, white marks on the dark material.

Brother, his mental voice rang in my head, bitter and thick with genuine draconic despair. Tell the shadow lady to give me some food. I need something sweet to drown out my sorrow.

He let out a pathetic, ozone-scented sniffle. I am a failure as a predator. As a hunter. I am no better than a domesticated house pet.

Patricia didn't need to be told what needed to be done. She possessed an uncanny ability to read the mood of the room, or in this case, the mood of a cosmic entity having an existential crisis. She disappeared into the mess hall of the MCIC. Kaelus trailed after her, turning his sad, weaponized puppy-dog eyes on her retreating back, begging her to prepare a huge plate of sweets.

A moment later he returned empty-handed and began rolling on the floor in the center of the command room, throwing a full-blown temper tantrum. He writhed on the cold steel plates, his tail slapping the deck in a staccato rhythm of pure, unadulterated frustration.

I sat on the floor next to him and gave him a belly rub, trying to soothe the wounded pride of a Prince.

"It's okay," I murmured, scratching the soft spot right behind his front leg. "You will get them next time. This is just the start."

He stopped thrashing, crawled up into my lap, and let out a long, tragic sigh. I carried him to the main holographic table. It was time to tune into my favorite show: Cinderfall Command.

...

The Cinderfall nobles and generals were all silent.

They sat around the heavy weirwood table, waiting for the King to arrive. None of them looked at each other, keeping their eyes lowered to the maps and ledgers in front of them.

The heavy oak doors swung open. King Theron entered the room.

Everyone stood up from their seats instantly, the scraping of heavy chairs echoing off the stone walls, and dropped down to one knee.

This was exactly why I replaced my army's greeting with a modern salute. They just wasted a full five minutes on formalities. Medieval armies really need to make things more efficient; they are wasting my time here too.

But it's not like I don't know why they do this. It enforces the supremacy of the monarchy. A visual, physical reminder of who holds the power over life and death.

The King took a seat at the head of the table and waved a hand, dismissing the formality. The generals scrambled back into their chairs and began delivering the reports.

They started with the so-called good news.

A general overseeing the frontline operations stood up, clearing his throat. "The sacrifice of those valiant knights was not for naught, Your Majesty. For now, the enemy has a gaping hole in their defenses. As long as we keep up the attack, the enemy cannot keep up."

The lords all nodded in agreement, murmuring their approval as if they had achieved something massive. It was the first perceived break for them in the war.

Why are they acting like this is a win for them? I thought, watching the feed from the ceiling drone. Wait, am I missing something here, or are they all delusional?

The King nodded, a rare, genuine smile touching his lips. "Yes. I have already heard of your great achievement. We shall commence the attack again tomorrow morning to ensure they cannot repair their defenses in time."

Wait. Do they think it will take me weeks to repair or replace the grid? Every unit is modular and adaptive. My automata will have it all operational by tomorrow morning. Are they really not planning on a night raid to stop my automata from replacing the damaged turrets and cannons?

Tes chimed in smoothly. [Master, it appears they believe the engineering units crawling over the damaged turrets means we are clearing the debris because we do not have replacements, and are instead attempting to repair the broken components. We can assume they do not understand that our turrets are designed with plug-and-play modularity, meaning one can simply be removed and placed directly in its spot. You need to think of it from their perspective. To them, a defensive line cannot be repaired and the cannons replaced in a single night.]

Well, it will be funny to see their reaction tomorrow morning. We have ample replacements.

We watched as Cinderfall planned around the gaping hole they believed would still exist at dawn.

The King offered reparations and great honors for the families of the knights slain in battle today, a strategic move to showcase how vital their role was in giving them this "win."

The lords all rejoiced with the King. Even celebratory drinks were served from the royal cellars, goblets of rich, dark wine sloshing as toasts were made to the ingenuity of the Hegemony.

The generals were clearly buttering the King up for something. I had no idea what that could be. I thought they were all so nervous because they too saw this engagement as a loss for their side.

My questions were answered a few moments later.

Some of the generals near the back of the room pushed a man forward. The man held a large, polished crystal ball in his trembling hands.

"My... my King," the man stammered, his face pale, sweat beading on his forehead despite the drafty stone room. "There is a slight problem. You were in your quarters during the engagement, and..."

The King interrupted him, waving a hand dismissively, clearly still in a celebratory mood. "Just spit it out."

The general swallowed hard, his Adam's apple bobbing. "The enemy... I mean, during the siege... We... we... we..."

King Theron was visibly losing his patience. He stood up, intending to take the crystal ball from the stammering man to see what all this fuss was about himself.

I was also intrigued. I zoomed the drone's optics into the crystal ball to see what was going on.

I was completely and utterly disappointed.

It was a playback. The crystal was functioning as a crude, magical mirror, replaying footage from my fight with the Cinderfall knights. It looked like they had recorded a lot of it, but the quality was terrible—like an over-zoomed, blurry image seen through a dirty windowpane.

But there was no mistaking the crimson and black plating of the Reaper armor.

The King, on the other hand, had a completely different reaction upon seeing the playback.

"What is this..." Theron breathed, his amber eyes widening. "Who is this..."

He snatched the crystal ball from the trembling general and smashed it on the stone floor. It shattered into a thousand glittering pieces.

"Is this some kind of practical joke?" the King roared, spittle flying from his lips. "Who is this mage?"

The King kept yelling "How... how can one man have a talent for lightning and space magic? And how, in the name of the First Flame, did he end up on the enemy side? How did a man of such talent slip through our fingers? The only spatial magic user to appear in the last three hundred years is among the elves, and that man is dead! How did we let such a man slip through our fingers!"

He looked frantically around the room for an answer, expecting an explanation from his generals.

"My King..." another lord ventured, his voice barely a whisper. "He... he... he is no mage. He... he is a Dragon Knight."

The King just looked at the man, blinking a few times, unable to process the words. He stepped over the shattered crystal, grabbed the man who had answered by the scruff of his ornate collar, and lifted him bodily into the air.

"Say that again," Theron growled, his face inches from the lord's. "I may have misheard you."

The man, terrified, dared not speak.

Everyone stayed silent. The festive environment had completely shifted. The laughter died in their throats. A lot of the generals put their drinks back on the table discreetly.

The King looked around, his chest heaving. "Bring me another crystal with that recording. I need to see it carefully. Make certain it's clear."

The King dropped the man he was holding onto the ground. The lord collapsed in a heap of tangled silk and armor. Theron sat back down in his heavy chair, resting his chin on his hand, drumming his armored fingers impatiently against the wood.

Tes, isn't this a bit of an overreaction?

[No, Master. Space and Time are considered one of the most versatile and useful elements. Combined with lightning magic, which is one of the most destructive, it is a skillset unheard of in this world. The combination of Blue Dragons were already considered the second strongest due to their mastery of wind, water, and lightning. But now, adding space magic puts them in a different class entirely. The only other entity that is comparable now is the Gold Dragons, with their mastery of two primary magical disciplines: Light Magic—encompassing offense, healing, and defense—and Time Magic.]

Kaelus, who was still rolling in my lap, puffed up his chest and raised his front paws high into the air. You may now praise my greatness, brother.

I patted his head. No wonder the Gold Dragons want Kaelus dead. He is a direct challenge to their supremacy.

A mage finally hurried into the room, handing King Theron a fresh crystal ball. "My King, this is the best our magic could allow."

The King placed the crystal ball on the table, leaning in close, analyzing every moment of the blurry combat footage.

"This fighting style," Theron muttered, his eyes tracking the swift, precise movements of the crimson armor. "And this spell at the end, especially. It is as if I am seeing Kaelen Wight descend onto the battlefield again."

A lord stammered quickly from the back of the room. "But... but Duke Kaelen Wight is dead. It can't be him."

The King snapped his head up, annoyed. "I know that, you imbecile. I am saying the fighting style is the same. But Kaelen Wight has way more destructive power. That swordsmanship is unmistakable though It is taught to members and knights of House Wight. There is also the fact of using spatial magic."

The clues all pointed to one person. But Cinderfall flatly refused to accept the notion.

King Theron tapped the crystal, freezing the playback on the image of Kaelus swooping up into the sky to engage the phoenixes.

"That is the same dragon we saw during the Dragon Dance," Theron said, his voice dropping an octave. "That is the Blue Dragon Prince."

Murmurs erupted around the table.

One general chimed in, grasping at straws. "That means... he received the Blue Dragon Clan's magic and had an innate talent for spatial magic?"

The King was hesitant, but he nodded slowly. "That is most likely the case."

Well, they are all wrong. I have zero innate magical talents. It's my little brother who is the magical one.

Another general added a point that shattered whatever fragile reasoning they had built. "But that can't be. The boy is no more than twenty years old. How can he be Tier 8? Are you saying he beat Kaelen Wight and became the youngest to achieve Tier 8 status???"

King Theron gritted his teeth, a muscle feathering rapidly in his jaw. "If that man's blood flows through that child, then yes, it is possible. He is mocking me from his grave yet again."

The generals all settled into a state of profound gloom.

"My King," one lord ventured, his voice thick with dread. "We must deal with the boy now. Lest in a few hundred years we will have a Tier 9 Blue Dragon contractor who uses spatial magic."

Another general chimed in eagerly. "Yes, my King! Killing him would also end the war."

The King sighed, running a hand over his face. "During the Dragon Dance ceremony, we found out his sister lives. They would just rally behind her. Killing the boy won't end the war."

The King looked directly into the eyes of the man speaking. "Do you understand how hard it is to trap a spatial mage? He can just teleport to Dragon Valley when he thinks he is in danger. It is beyond difficult to lock them down in one place. That is the innate problem with spatial mages. The recent rise in power of Sylvanheim was due to their spatial mage."

The heavy blast door to the MCIC slid open.

Patricia entered carrying an abnormally large plate of tarts. Her maid uniform was pristine again.

Kaelus jumped off my lap instantly. He made a dead sprint toward the snacks, his existential crisis forgotten in the face of sugar.

I watched the Cinderfall generals just talking in circles on the feed, making no headway. But one thing seemed odd to me.

Why would the King skip the battle and hide away in his room? What was so important?

I looked at Patricia, who was setting the tray down carefully. "Do we not have any monitoring devices in the King's bedchambers? Why not?"

Patricia bowed slightly, her expression perfectly composed. "Apologies, Young Master. But no one but the King is allowed to enter that room. Plus, we found out the room uses a special alloy and a combination of runes that constantly isolate it. Nothing can enter or exit. It is completely isolated."

She smoothed the front of her apron. "Even if we smuggle a monitoring device inside, we can't smuggle it out. And no signals exit. Whether magical, infrared, or even radio waves. It is completely isolated. We have ruled it out as a target due to the King himself being affected by the same limitations, but our attempts to infiltrate that space are ongoing."
