Chapter 28: The Weight of Choice

Zoey attacked first. Her whips materialized from her resonance weapon, living vines of pure energy that lashed toward me with desperate fury. I knocked them away with ease, my blade moving in perfect arcs that intercepted each strike before it could find its mark. Despite the fire burning in my lungs with each labored breath, I was at a skill level I hadn't quite understood I had reached before now.

The weeks of training under Vash, the constant pain, the endless repetition of forms until they became as natural as breathing. All of it had forged me into something far beyond what these initiates could handle. They were still thinking like students, like people who had learned techniques from books and instructors. I had been beaten into understanding every principle through blood and agony.

But more than that, something new had awakened within me. I could feel their aspects, the way they accessed the Flux within themselves. Their connections to the universal energy called to me like subordinates hailing their master. Each pulse of power they drew upon resonated through my awakened bloodline, and I could sense the pathways they used, the methods by which they channeled their abilities.

It was intoxicating. Like suddenly being able to see colors that had always existed but remained invisible until this moment.

I reached out with my own Flux, drawing on the Command Presence that had manifested with my bloodline's awakening. Through my ruined lungs, I whispered a single word, a command that carried the weight of absolute authority. The sound was barely audible, more breath than voice, but it carried through the Flux itself rather than the air.

"Stop."

The plant woman froze mid-attack, her whips dissolving as her connection to the Flux came under my control. Her alien eyes went wide with confusion and terror as she realized she could no longer move her own body. Her bark-like skin shifted to darker hues, the colors of fear and panic rippling across her surface like oil on water.

Then Kari was there, moving in the familiar patterns I had seen from her in our first duel back in the initiate training area. Her twin blades sang through the air, seeking my blood with the desperation of someone who understood that hesitation meant death.

"Why are you doing this?" she growled, her voice raw with betrayal and rage. Her two blades were a blur of white light that I wove around like they were moving through thick honey.

Speaking hurt worse than the constant burning in my chest, each word a rasp of agony through scarred airways. So I didn't bother answering. Her aspects spoke to me instead, the Flux connections she relied upon laying themselves bare to my enhanced perception. I could see the intent of her movements almost before she made them, her body telegraphing every strike through the energy patterns that preceded physical action.

It became trivially easy to wait for the perfect opening, then drive my boot into her chest with Flux-enhanced force. The impact sent her crashing into the stone wall of our narrow refuge, her body striking the rock with a sickening crack that echoed through the confined space. She slumped to the ground, gasping for breath, her weapons clattering across the stone floor.

I regarded Zoey, still frozen in place by my command, and once again, this was one of those moments I would like to tell myself I hesitated. That I considered at all sparing her life, finding some alternative to the violence that was about to unfold. That some remnant of the man who had once helped neighbors and coached children still existed somewhere in the thing I had become.

Sweet little lies. Always with the comfortable fictions.

Her alien eyes were wide, filled with a terror so pure it was almost beautiful. She struggled against my control, her bark-like skin rippling with the effort of trying to regain command of her own body. But my command of her aspects was absolute, my bloodline's authority over the Flux brooking no resistance from someone of her limited power.

She knew she was going to die. I could see it in those strange, inhuman eyes. The acceptance that came when all hope fled, when the universe revealed its true nature as a place where the strong devoured the weak without ceremony or justification.

Vathwin brought his resonance blade down in a powerful ten strike, the heavenly cut descending toward my head with mechanical precision. But I simply held out my hand, drawing on an ability I had never used before, and for the first time activated Flux Crush.

The sensation was unlike anything I had experienced. Power flowed out from my core, carried by rage and will in equal measure, seeking out my target with hungry intent. I was targeting his lower armor, the chest piece that protected his organs, carefully avoiding the respirator that I so desperately needed. The Flux obeyed my command, pressing down on Vathwin's armored form with invisible force.

He was brought crashing to his knees as the Flux attempted to crush him like an empty can. His armor creaked and groaned under the pressure, stress fractures appearing in the metal as forces beyond normal physics worked to compress his body into a much smaller space. Steam hissed from the joints of his suit as internal systems failed under the strain.

His own Flux fought me, of course. I could feel his desperate attempts to break free, to reassert control over the energy that kept him alive and functional. But my Resonance was far higher than his, my bloodline connection too great for him to combat effectively. His aspects were mine to command now, so the fight he put up simply wasn't enough to matter.

I kept the ability going as I walked toward Zoey, savoring the sensation of absolute control over forces that had once seemed beyond my reach. The plant woman couldn't even whimper as I approached, her voice as frozen as the rest of her body. Only her eyes could move, tracking my approach with the kind of desperate hope that made what came next all the more satisfying.

With a powerful chi strike, I cut her in half without ceremony. The earth cut sliced through her torso at an upward angle, separating her upper body from her lower in a spray of sap that looked disturbingly like blood. Her alien anatomy revealed itself in the moment of death, organs and systems unlike anything found in human physiology spilling across the stone floor.

I felt a rush of power flow from her as she died, Flux energy transferring from the defeated to the victor. It was like drinking lightning, pure strength flooding through my veins and settling into my bones. Behind me, Kari let out a horrified cry that echoed off the narrow walls of our refuge.

"Stop! I have a key!"

I turned to her slowly, eyes narrowing as I processed this new information. A key to the cathedral, one of the four I needed to complete the games and claim whatever prize waited in the heart of the dungeon. It changed the tactical situation considerably.

"I have a key," she repeated, desperation creeping into her voice as she struggled to sit up against the wall where I had thrown her. "I'll give it to you. Just let us live, okay?"

Her eyes held resolve beneath the fear, the kind of determination that came from having nothing left to lose. She was bargaining with the only currency she had, hoping that greed would overcome bloodlust in the thing that had once been her ally.

Elisa's face flashed through my mind, unbidden and unwelcome. My daughter's smile, her laugh, the way she used to curl up in my lap when thunderstorms scared her. The man she thought I was, the father she expected to return from this place. The lie I told myself about everything I did being for her protection.

Sweet little lies indeed.

I closed my hand into a fist, pouring more power into the Flux Crush ability. Vathwin's chest armor imploded with the sound of shrieking metal, his ribcage collapsing as invisible forces compressed his torso like a can under a hydraulic press. Blood and metallic fluid spurted with violent intensity as his internal systems ruptured, the cyborg man's augmentations failing in a cascade of sparks and hissing steam.

He fell to the ground with a crash that shook dust from the stone walls, his mechanical breathing finally, blessedly silent. The respirator that had kept him alive lay undamaged on his crushed chest, black metal gleaming in the dim light of our refuge.

Kari screamed her fury as she came at me, drawing on reserves of strength I hadn't known she possessed. But I turned my newfound ability toward her blades, the crushing force that had killed Vathwin now focused on her weapons. The resonance crystals that powered her twin swords cracked under the pressure, their light flickering and dying as the metal housings crumpled into useless slag.

I swept her legs out from under her a moment later, my blade coming to rest at her throat before she could recover from the fall. She lay there beneath me, chest heaving, staring up with eyes that held no recognition of the man she had once considered a friend.

Flux energy had flowed into me from the cyborg's death, another surge of power that made my bloodline sing with satisfaction. I knew I had gained initiate points as well, advancement that brought me closer to whatever I was destined to become. Kari represented more points, more power, more strength for the challenges ahead.

In her, I had seen a kindred soul. She too had scores to settle, losses of her own to avenge. Her brother, dead on a world far from here, killed by creatures that shouldn't exist. We were alike in our pain, our rage, our willingness to do whatever it took to become strong enough that loss could never touch us again.

Call it a whim, or perhaps it was the last vestiges of the man I had been. Maybe I hadn't fully succumbed to the Nyxen way. They wouldn't show mercy, wouldn't spare an enemy who could come back stronger, more dangerous. True Nyxen would see weakness in my hesitation, would kill without thought or regret.

I wouldn't call it goodness, choosing to spare her. In all honesty, it was probably stupid. She knew what I was now, what I was capable of. Letting her live was a tactical error that might come back to haunt me later.

But I did it anyway.

I moved my Resonance Blade away from her throat and deactivated the weapon, the white light fading to leave only the simple crystal hilt in my hand. Without a word, I turned my back on her and stalked toward Vathwin's corpse, my breathing harsh and labored in the sudden silence.

The respirator came free with a soft hiss of released seals, the straps and connections designed for quick removal in emergency situations. It was heavier than I had expected, the black metal housing containing filters and processors that would scrub the air I breathed, making life bearable again.

I held it up to the dim light, examining the device that would become as much a part of me as my own lungs. The technology was elegant in its simplicity, form following function with no wasted material or unnecessary ornamentation. It would cover the lower half of my face, transforming my appearance into something more machine than man.

Behind me, Kari was struggling to her feet, using the stone wall for support. I could hear her labored breathing, could sense her watching me through the Flux connection that linked all living things. She was afraid of me now, and rightly so. I was no longer the ally who had fought beside her, shared meals with her, pretended to be something I was not.

I was something else entirely. Something that took what it needed and justified the taking afterward. Something that wore the face of a loving father while harboring the soul of a killer.

I fitted the respirator over my face, feeling the seals engage against my skin. The first breath through the filtered air was like being reborn, clean oxygen filling my lungs without the constant burn of damaged tissue. My voice, when it came, carried the mechanical undertones of the device's speakers.

"The key," I said, the words emerging as a digitized rasp that barely sounded human. "Give it to me."

From behind me came the soft sound of crystal hitting stone as Kari tossed her cathedral key across the floor. It skittered to a stop near my feet, gleaming with captured light.

I picked it up without looking at her, adding it to the one I had earned from the essence trial. Two keys now. Halfway to my goal.

When I finally turned around, Kari was gone, vanished into the shadows of the dungeon beyond our refuge. Smart girl. She understood that mercy was a limited resource, and she had used up her allotment.

I stood alone among the bodies of my former allies, breathing freely for the first time in days, listening to the mechanical rhythm of my new voice. The respirator's speakers gave every exhalation a sound like distant thunder, like the breathing of something vast and terrible.

I smiled behind the mask, though no one was left alive to see it. Soon, very soon, I would be ready to face whatever waited in the cathedral's depths.

But first, I had two more keys to find.
