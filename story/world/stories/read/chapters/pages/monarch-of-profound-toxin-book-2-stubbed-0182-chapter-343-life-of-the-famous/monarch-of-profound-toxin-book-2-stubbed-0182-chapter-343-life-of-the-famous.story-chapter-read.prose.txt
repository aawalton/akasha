“Uncle Mikey!” Bin squealed as she barreled into Michael’s arms, the healer laughing as he caught her and spun her around a few times.

“Wow, didn’t I just see you two days ago, cutie?” he gasped. “Have you Awakened in the meantime or something like that? That felt like an E-ranker slamming into me!”

The little green girl giggled mirthfully. “You’re so silly. I haven’t Awakened yet. Not until I’m older.”

“Then maybe you’re just super duper strong,” Michael praised. Truthfully, that might well be the case. She was a member of an alien species, after all. “I bet you’re going to be a strong Awakened when you grow up.”

There was also little doubt about that. Even if she turned out to not be interested in combat, which Eik kind of hoped for the sake of his worrying heart, her father would make sure to fill her up to the brim with empowering pills and elixir. Whether she tried to or not, she would be the most powerful warrior in her age group throughout the Unified Mass.

“Are you coming to shop with us today?”

“That’s right, I am,” he said and set her down on her feet where she wobbled around for a few moments, dizzy from the spin. “Since I have a few things I need to buy as well, your dad suggested we all go together.”

“Yay!” she exclaimed.

“Me…” came a much smaller voice from somewhere, and then, a second later, a small figure dove out and past a smiling Eik and rocketed toward Michael at a speed his low-ranked senses were completely incapable of reacting to. “too!”

“Nope, you stay here with me, kiddo,” Eik broke in and snatched Goo out of the air by the back of his shirt collar as easily as if he had been crawling along the ground on all fours. “You get to fly into Uncle Mikey’s embrace once you realize that you’re monstrously strong for your size and learn to control it so you don’t accidentally kill your friends and family. Here, go on. Give him a normal, gentle hug.”

With his round, malleable face warped into a pout full of dissatisfaction, the little boy did as he had been told and walked up to hug Uncle Michael around the waist. The healer ran his finger’s through the boy’s hair. “Nice to see you, Goo. Let’s have fun today, eh?”

The boy nodded before unceremoniously climbing up Michael’s body and taking a seat on his shoulders. Short, little fingers grasping a couple of handfuls of shoulder-length locks, Goo rocked back and forth on his steed, urging him forward.

“I guess we’re ready to go,” the healer chuckled. “Should we wait here for Ihasu?”

“Nah, she’s not coming today, unfortunately. She’s busy as all hell, what with her new role at work and all that. And I guess it’s kind of my fault that Earth has so much fracture traffic these days,” Eik winced with a smile.

“New role?” Michael asked as they took the first steps from the clinic where he worked. On the way in, they greeted a few patients who were on their way in. There was no longer any of the generally grim atmosphere that had been an everyday thing for the first decade plus of Earth’s existence in the Unified Mass.

Cut off from both modern medicinal manufacture and access to the sterile, machine-assisted surgery environment that had allowed humanity to conquer both disease and injury, it had felt like a hard reset.

Despite the theoretical possibility of healers filling the role of skilled doctors, the reality had been that healers were few and far between, and for the first many years, none had possessed the ability to do as great work as modern medical professionals.

And any of the few healing-specialized Awakened there had been, had been forced to undertake dangerous endeavors in order to become strong enough to make a meaningful difference in the survivability of their fellow humans—endeavors that often ended with their deaths before they could reach such a point.

Sickness and injury that were trivial in the old world had become threats to one’s life.

Now, however, that was a horror of the past. With high-ranked healing abilities, magically empowered medicine, and the combined knowledge of multiple civilizations, the ailments of mankind were even more effectively fought.

“Hell yeah, she’s got a new role! Didn’t you hear she got promoted recently? She runs the Earth connection now!”

“No, I didn’t! That’s amazing! I know she’s been studying like crazy for it!”

“Oh, man, We’re all so proud of her, aren’t we, kiddos?”

Both children raised their little fists to the sky, Bin screaming her approval at full lung while Goo laid his head back in a silent roar of familial support.

“Are we meeting Heath there then?”

“Yeah, I think he’s pretty busy with his exam work, so he’s basically at the workshop from early morning to late night every day these days, as far as I know. I was honestly surprised when he agreed to come with us,” Eik said and threw his daughter onto his shoulders.

“Resting is a crucial part of proper work.”

Eik arched an eyebrow at that. “Maybe you should try to remind yourself of that once in a while.”

“Yeah, you’re not wrong. Not that the same can’t be said for you,” Michael said with a grin. “Do you know what he’s making for his exam? It’s gotta be something special to pass with that slave driver of a master smith he’s working under.”

Eik huffed a laugh “I’ll give you one guess. Hint: it is literally exactly what you would expect our big friend to make.”

“Sword?”

“Dude, it’s absolutely enormous too. You’ve seen the default size of Andihar’s sword, right?”

Michael’s eyes widened as he frowned. “Oh no, it’s not even bigger than that, is it?”

“By a good bit too, yeah. I saw a couple of his practice pieces a few days ago and the big guy can barely lift his own project,” Eik chuckled.

“That aside, isn’t it a little too… I don’t know, standard, for something like a test? A sword, I mean. Don’t they want to see something more impressive?”

“Oh, dude, if he succeeds with what he’s set out to make, it will be plenty impressive. At least if I’ve understood how difficult it is.”

“Enchantments?”

“Lots of them, yeah. Our friend has really become a smith worth a smith’s title.”

Once they made it to the streets of Gimleh, they sped up a bit as they headed for Heath’s workshop. It was bright out and folks flocked in the streets.

It was the perfect day to do a bit of shopping.

Ever since his rather recent reception of the title of Elixir King granted by the Alchemist Guild of Gimleh, a lot more of the population of Gimleh had become familiar with his face. It wasn’t the first time he had been a part of a public spectacle, of course, but it was the first time the title of genuine X-ranker could be used to draw people’s attention. People had flocked to witness it after that.

Yeah, there was no doubt about it.

People were looking at him. A lot of people.

Did they know what was happening behind the scenes? How the Lord of the Moon was trying to regain his power?

No, hardly anybody were privy to that knowledge.

He looked around, momentarily absorbed in the sight of life. It was more or less the same sight as it always was. People falling into lively conversation after meeting coincidentally on the street. Kids weaving between people as they played and got chased, laughing, from stores and stalls for disturbing the peace and business. Families and friends enjoying a meal together in the sun, sat around small tables or walking shoulder to shoulder with Eik.

No, they wouldn’t know anything more than that something was going on. That he and the others were working to protect them. As one, they were standing against adversity.

Good.

He would do the worrying for them. Their faces were like hands of support on his back.

“By the way, did I hear something about a bit of progress with regards to the Lord of the Moon?” Michael suddenly asked.

“Kind of,” Eik told him, erecting a precise sound barrier around just the two of them. “We found some storage devices that absorb miasma. I managed to get a Living Manifestation into a servant when it came to take one of them away.”

“Wait, seriously? So you know where the Lord of the Moon is right now? That’s huge!”

“Not quite yet, no, unfortunately. The damned thing is still traveling, but hopefully some time soon.”

The healer pursed his lips in thought. “What are you going to do when it reaches the Lord of the Moon?”

“Honestly? I’m not sure yet,” Eik admitted. “There are many different opinions coming from different directions.”

Michael rolled his eyes. “I can imagine.”

Someone whispered Eik’s name, drawing his attention. Quietly enough that most people wouldn’t hear it, but he wasn’t most people.

Before he could pretend to not notice, he turned his head to look. It was two women, slightly younger than he was, both of them boring holes in the back of his head with their eyes until he turned to regard them.

The moment their eyes met, the two of them gasped and froze. He was always careful to subdue his aura completely when he went out in public, but they must have seen something in his eyes, because they appeared to be paralyzed.

Briefly, the memory of first meeting Atla flashed through his mind, when she had appeared suddenly out of a fracture in the middle of the city center in Forest. The depths of power he had witnessed in her eyes for the first time back then had broadened his view of the Unified Mass then and there.

Was that what those two women now saw in him? A bottomless pit of might that could cause untold ruin at the snap of a finger?

He hoped not.

“It is him!” one of them breathed, elbowing the other to break her out of her stupor.

Confused, Eik stopped and watched them as they approached him cautiously. And before he had a chance to say anything to either of them, they took one of his hands each and bowed deeply to him, pressing their foreheads into the knuckles of his hands in performance of some kind of cultural gesture.

“Wh-What are you…? I don’t understand!” he spluttered, flustered.

“Thank you,” they spoke in unison as Eik became embarrassingly aware of the multitude of eyes now turning to stare at him.

As if a dam had been broken, more and more people swarmed around them then and seemingly endless words of praise and gratitude washed over their little group as he and Michael were pushed against each other with the kids on their shoulders.

Oh, boy, this was going to be a long day.
