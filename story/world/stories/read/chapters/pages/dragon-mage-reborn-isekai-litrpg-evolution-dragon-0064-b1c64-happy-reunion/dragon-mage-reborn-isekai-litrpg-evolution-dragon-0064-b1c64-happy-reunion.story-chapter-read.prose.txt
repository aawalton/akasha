We did it… we actually did it!

I panted, feeling totally drained from that complex binding ritual. After catching my breath, I rushed to Zaiden’s side. He had collapsed onto the cathedral floor, his body limp but intact. I could sense his life force, weak but steady. The arm that had been restored by Theron’s power remained, the shadow energy still flowing through his veins, but barely.

‘Zaiden?’ I nudged him gently with my snout. ‘Can you hear me?’

His eyelids fluttered, and when they opened, I was relieved to see his normal eyes staring back at me—not the pitch-black voids of Theron.

“Freya?” His voice was hoarse, but completely his own. He winced as he tried to sit up, the confused expression on his face asking me about a billion questions. “What... what happened? I remember my arm growing back, and then...” He shuddered. “I faintly remember….”

‘Theron possessed you,’ I explained, helping him steady himself. ‘But he’s contained now. Trapped in the altar.’

Zaiden looked past me to where the altar now stood, its surface carved with glowing binding sigils. “You bound him? How did you—” He stopped, his eyes widening as he looked at me more closely. “Your scales... they’re different.”

I glanced down at myself, noticing for the first time that some of my scales had taken on a darker hue, shot through with trails of deep purple that matched the sigils on the altar. The necromantic energy I’d channeled had left its mark.

‘The binding required me to use his own power against him,’ I admitted as I noticed the color gradually fading. ‘I couldn’t have done it without you, or Nolan.’

“Nolan?” he repeated skeptically. But as soon as I powered down my domain, I was able to feel their essence more strongly. We were back in the prince’s domain, which I now noticed was actually a throne room without the pitch blackness covering every square inch of the place. It was also a lot smaller than I expected, allowing me to pinpoint where our friends were.

I flew over to the east wall where I realized their faces were carved in stone. Lun, Typha, and Nolan. A small pinpoint gold light shone on their foreheads, the intensity growing stronger with each passing second. I watched in awe as the stone began to crack around them, golden light seeping through the fissures like molten sunlight.

“They’re breaking free,” Zaiden whispered, coming to stand beside me.

The light intensified until I had to shield my eyes. When the brightness finally subsided, my friends stood before us, disoriented but whole.

Nolan stumbled forward first, his usually messy hair an even bigger mess and his clothes disheveled.

“What in the seven hells...” Lun muttered, blinking rapidly.

Typha dropped to her knees, breathing heavily as she regained her bearings. She looked at her hands, flexing her fingers as if to confirm they were real.

“Typha?!” Zaiden cried out, and then rushed to his sister.

Typha’s head snapped up at the sound of her brother’s voice. Zaiden tackled her with a tight hug as soon as she got up to her feet, my half-fae half-human conduit leaving his sister completely speechless. She couldn’t even hug him back, Typha beyond herself. I think a part of her truly believed that she wouldn’t reunite with him again, this dungeon whipping our collective asses in more ways than one. Finally, the realization of everything fell on her, and Typha bawled. She clung onto her brother tightly, her weeping face buried in his chest.

“I’m sorry, Zai!” she cried. “I should have listened to you! I should have—“

“Shh,” he whispered, stroking her hair. “You’re safe now. That’s all that matters.”

I felt my heart swell watching the siblings reunite. After everything we’d been through—the terror of losing them, the desperate fight against Theron, the exhausting binding ritual—seeing them together again made it all worth it.

‘Freya,’ Nolan called out to me, and I immediately jumped. I couldn’t keep the excitement off my feet as I ran circles around him, making sure all of his parts were there. Then I stood in front of him and wagged my tail.

‘You’re really here, aren’t you?’ I beamed. ‘Boy, hasn’t this been one hell of an emotional rollerco—’ I squeaked, the hard-shelled gunslinger giving me a tight hug. The unexpected dose of affection caught me completely off guard, and for a moment I forgot how to breathe. “Thank you,” he whispered.

I nuzzled against his shoulder, savoring the warmth of having him solid and real in my arms again. ‘I couldn’t leave you behind, Nolan.’

Lun approached us, paused for a moment, and then leaped into us with his arms wide open. “Haha! Dungeon defeated! Body back! For a second there, I didn’t think we were gonna make it! What an adventure!” he cheered, nearly knocking us both over with his enthusiasm. “I knew you'd come for us, Freya! Though I gotta say, being a stone statue was murder between the ass cheeks.”

‘TMI!’ I squeaked.

I laughed despite myself, the sound coming out as more of a draconic purr. Having all my friends back together felt like a weight I hadn’t realized I was carrying had finally lifted from my shoulders.

After we had our heartfelt moment, Typha, Lun, and Nolan realized that Zaiden was not only doing much better, but he was also sporting a brand new arm. I filled them in on the fight, and already, Typha was looking at Zaiden and me funny. Nolan was surprisingly quiet, or should I say, nerve-rackingly quiet. I knew he had his own opinion about everything that had happened, but he kept it to himself for now.

“So let me get this straight,” Lun said, scratching his head as he processed the information. “Theron possessed Zaiden, you absorbed shadow magic, bound the prince's soul to an altar using necromancy, and somehow I missed all the fun while being a lair ornament!?”

‘That’s... actually a pretty accurate summary,’ I admitted inwardly.

“And now Zaiden has a dark magic arm as a parting gift from the prince himself?” he added as he examined Zaiden. “Hmm, separating the soul from the aura is some pretty intense dark magic business. All while at 20% necro proficiency? Well, now it’s 50%. What a jump!” Lun said as he [Inspect] me. Since he was still in my party, he was allowed to do a deep dive inside my codex.

I ducked my head, feeling oddly self-conscious about the proficiency boost. ‘I think I was running on pure adrenaline and desperation. Plus, having Nolan’s energy there at the end….’

“Don’t downplay your success,” Nolan said. “All of this wasn’t easy.”

“Nolan is right,” Typha said. “It’s incredible what you managed to do.” She turned to me with respect in her gaze. “Thank you for this.”

Zaiden flexed his new shadow-infused arm, watching as wisps of darkness curled around his fingers. “This is going to take some getting used to, but it doesn’t feel foreign anymore. The connection to Theron is severed—I can tell the difference.” He clenched his fist, and the shadows responded smoothly to his will. “It’s just raw power now. Mine to control.”

“We should probably discuss what this means for your magic compatibility,” Lun said, his analytical mind at work. “Shadow magic isn’t typically compatible with most fae, let alone hybrids. You’ll need specialized training.”

“One crisis at a time,” Typha interjected, though I could see the worry in her eyes as she watched her brother experiment with his new limb. “I’m just happy he’s alive. Don’t ruin the moment.”

“All thanks to the big girl right here!” Zaiden cheered, and then swooped me right off my feet! I yelped as he swung me over his head. ‘Put me down this instant!’ I demanded, though there was no real heat behind it. The relief of having everyone safe made it hard to be truly irritated.

‘No way! Not before I show our savior some proper appreciation!’

‘Hey, are you sure you’re my thrall? Because you like to back-talk a lot!’

I felt foolish for being afraid of falling, seeing as I had wings now. Even so, I made a real mess out of his blond hair with my claws, before I trusted him enough as he hoisted me on a seat across his broad shoulders.

Zaiden led our party to the treasure chest on the other side of the lair. Our prizes for beating a tier five dungeon awaited us, and I was allowed to snatch everything. But I wasn’t selfish. This was a team effort. So instead, I suggested I get first dibs on a prize, and my options were very appealing: 16 gold coins, Elemental War Hammer (ultra rare), Salzer’s Skill Maximizer (ultra rare), Salzer’s Eternal Bread Basket (ultra rare), and the Elementium Stone.

It was interesting how the final boss was Prince Theron but these dungeon items came from his father’s arsenal.

‘I’m leaning on the Skill Maximizer,’ I told the parties of my private link as Zaiden placed me down.

‘Then I’ll take the Bread Basket,’ Nolan replied. ‘A bottomless source of not only sandwiches, but also the added benefit of attribute boosting after eating those sandwiches.’

“I’ll grab the Elemental War Hammer,” Typha said, and then turned to the mage. “Unless you want it, Lun?”

He shook his head. “Unnecessary! I will never betray my darling side-kick!”

“I’m surprised you didn’t take the stone,” Nolan said to Typha.

“Well, I don’t see why someone like me would need it. Even though I jumped up a few levels from reaching the finish line of this god-forsaken dungeon, I’m not a true dungeon crawler. Someone else could benefit from the Elementium Stone. I know it’s priceless. It’s one of the top three items he wanted me to steal for him.”

“Oh, I’ll take it!” Lun said excitedly, chuckling to himself. “I have plans for this stone!”

“I hope nothing devious,” Nolan teased.

“Not at all! I’ll save it for a special day.” With his warm voice, I could only imagine that he had plans to give it to that bunnykin Brava.

“Well, that leaves the gold split in five ways, and Zaiden could get the excess since he didn’t get an item,” Nolan suggested.

“Nah, I want Freya here to have it,” Zaiden offered.

I smiled, or at least as close to smiling as a dragon could manage, at Zaiden’s generosity.

‘You don’t have to do that,’ I followed, though I was touched by the gesture.

“I insist,” he said firmly, then grinned. “Besides, I didn’t do anything, not until the very end. And let’s not forget, I got a new arm out of the ordeal—that’s worth more than a few gold pieces and then some.”

'“Fair enough,” Nolan replied, and then went on to distribute the items.

He then turned to me and asked, ‘Any idea which skill you’re going to max out?’

‘No. I consulted with my codex, and unfortunately, I can’t max out my necromancy proficiency, seeing as it’s not considering it as a traditional skill. So I’m waiting until I get a really good one to boost.’

He smirked. ‘Sounds good. Now, let’s get out of here. I’m tired, hungry, and in desperate need of a fucking smoke.’

Thankfully, the dungeon core couldn’t sense its boss in its lair, and deemed us completing it. It caused a chain reaction that broke Zev’s magic keeping us here. We were finally allowed to leave, poor Lun rushing out and kissing the ground as we emerged into the night’s radiant moonlight. “Sweet, beautiful, non-cursed earth!” he declared dramatically, pressing his face into the dirt. “Oh, how I missed you so!”

“Don’t get too excited,” Nolan said as he eyed the place. “We’re still on Zev’s property.”

“Then we’re leaving,” Typha said as she marched right up to him, hugging her arms in her hands desperately. “Right now.”

I get it, she didn’t want to spend not one more second in this place, and neither did I. We were dropped off at his front yard, stranded there until I suggested using my rainbow feather. Lun mentioned that the feather wasn’t the best teleportation device, since the colors on it, according to the colors on the rainbow, each signified the last seven major locations I’d been in within a certain radius.

Well, at least the cool down was relatively low.

My codex informed me of those recent places, the forest right before Zev’s mansion being one of them. I thought about the fae prisoners we’d left before we were sucked into the dungeon, and figured they might be around that area.

My party agreed to go there, and sure enough, signs of someone being here were present. Foot prints on the dirt, crushed twigs, and carved paths along the foliage. Every sign except actual fae.

But my nose, as well as Lun’s, were sharp.
