The cavern was silent but for the strange man panting to catch his breath after the sudden outburst. Lee Jia stared in stunned silence as he took a deep breath, swept a hand through his hair, and schooled his expression into a carefree smile.

“Ah! I feel much better now. Thank you all for freeing me. I, Jianmo, am in your debt.”

Jianmo began to stretch and crack his joints as though he’d just woken up from a long nap. The first one to come to their senses was Yan Zhihao.

“Demon! I hold the demon-sealing divine artifact that trapped you here! I demand that you recognize me as your new master and obey my commands!”

Lee Jia’s head jerked towards Yan, her face a mask of incredulity. Was he serious? What was wrong with his head? Jianmo threw his head back and laughed.

“Hahaha! I don’t know what you’re talking about, but you’ve got guts, kid. Alright, I’m in a good mood and I don’t hate cheeky brats like you. Go ahead—what shall I do to reward you for freeing me?”

Yan hesitated, glancing down at the sword and then back at his companions—or rather, his lack of them. Yan’s entire team had died fighting the fire elemental and he was now alone. If not for Eui’s timely intervention and Jianmo’s sudden appearance, he would have certainly died by now. As Yan Zhihao’s gaze swept across everyone present, his eyes met Lee Jia’s and his gaze hardened. A cruel smile grew on his face as he pointed the sword at her.

“Kill her and retrieve what is rightfully mine—no, wait! I can’t have any witnesses, if I am to conceal this new power. Kill them all!”

There was a collective gasp of shock from the group at Yan’s betrayal—even Jianmo seemed surprised. Jia and Eui were the only exceptions as they just shook their heads with resignation. They had known something like this might happen, they had only hoped it wouldn’t be quite so well timed.

“Huh!? Are you sure about that? I saw the whole battle, you know. They saved your life twice, and you never would have freed me without them.”

Yan Zhihao straightened his back and adopted a smug posture as he responded to Jianmo.

“On the path to heaven, one must be as ruthless as the heavens themselves. When winter comes, every leaf falls—no matter how beautiful the autumn colors. I bear no grudge against most of them, but if I must tread on them on my way to ascension then I will not hesitate.”

“Hahahaha! Oh, you are definitely one of Qin’s brats! Ah, I wonder what that little bastard’s been up to these ten thousand years? Very well! I don’t hate ungrateful brats like you, so with this I’ll consider our debt absolved.”

Jianmo turned towards Lee Jia and her team as they scrambled into defensive positions—not that she thought it would do them any good against someone who so effortlessly destroyed the elemental that had caused them so much trouble. Jianmo still had the same easygoing smile, but his glowing red eyes seemed to radiate malice.

“Now then, how would you like to be rewarded? After all, you all contributed to my freedom!”

His words gave Lee Jia pause. She blinked a few times in confusion, but before she could respond Yan started shouting.

“What are you talking about, Demon? Reward? What’s the point if you’re just going to be killing them!?”

Jianmo turned his head without quite looking at Yan Zhihao.

“Boy, I spared you once because I was in a good mood, and a second time because I was indebted. There won’t be a third.”

Yan was momentarily flabbergasted before he raised the demon-sealing sword towards Jianmo with a furious gaze.

“I’ve had enough of this! If you won’t obey my commands then I’ll just seal you back away using—”

Suddenly, Jianmo’s face dropped into an ice-cold expression that made Jia tremble involuntarily. Before Yan could finish speaking, the sword in his hand twisted itself out of his grip and swung in a wide arc so quickly it left an afterimage. Lee Jia screamed in shock as the unexpected attack sent Yan Zhihao’s head tumbling to the ground, his body collapsing with a spray of blood.

It wasn’t the first time Lee Jia had seen death, but the sudden, visceral brutality had shocked her in a way that the other deaths hadn’t. Her vision was swimming and she felt like she was going to be sick. Only the feeling of Eui’s hand gripping hers tightly kept Lee Jia from losing her composure further.

The hovering blade floated over to Jianmo and was absorbed into his body as he walked up to Yan Zhihao’s corpse with a frown on his face. He began kicking it as he shouted.

“Demon-sealing divine artifact!? As if something that convenient exists! If that was all it took, then why would that idiot Qin need to contract those damn overgrown lizards to seal me away, huh!? Why would they use a formation, huh!? Idiot! Stupid fucking moron! I don’t hate brainless shits like you, you know!”

When Jianmo turned back toward Lee Jia’s party, his carefree smile had returned, though the effect was ruined a bit by the blood splatters marring his face.

“Ah! I feel much better now. So, where were we?”

Nobody answered, the fear written plainly on their faces. Jianmo heaved a sigh and shook his head.

“What’s the matter? Shouldn’t you all be happy he’s dead? He was trying to kill you, you know!”

Lee Jia grimaced at that. It was true that Yan had tried to kill them, and he’d been nothing but trouble from the moment she met him. Still, she had never hated him so much that she wished him dead. Oblivious or uncaring towards their discomfort, Jianmo approached the group and regarded them with a curious expression.

“This is quite the group! I had assumed more of Qin’s worthless progeny, but that one—” he pointed at Takeda “—looks like a member of the southern barbarian tribes, and I don’t even know what the rest of you are.”

He reached forward and tugged on one of Lee Jia’s ears and she jerked back reflexively, holding a hand up to the ear he had touched.

“Spiritual essence fused with mortal flesh? You spirits have gotten creative since I’ve been gone.”

Lee Jia’s skin prickled at the comment and against her better judgement she protested.

“We’re not spirits! We are humans, thank you very much!”

Jianmo started, blinking a few times in surprise before breaking into a wide grin.

“Hah! So you are! Forgive me my ignorance, although—” his eyes flickered towards Eui for a moment “—well, never mind. It figures those spirits would botch up their attempts at embodiment. Still, little Qin must have been furious!”

Lee Jia didn’t know what he was talking about, but it sounded ominous and she was growing increasingly uncomfortable in the ancient immortal’s presence. Xin had a slightly pained expression as he raised a trembling hand.

“U-um, venerable elder—”

“Just call me Jianmo.”

“...Jianmo, when you say ‘Qin’, could you by chance be referring to the Qin? The God-Emperor himself?”

Jianmo raised an eyebrow at Xin Wei.

“God-Emperor!? That cheeky little brat! I’m as close to a god as has ever existed in this world—or I was, ten thousand years ago. He certainly has some nerve. I don’t hate that arrogant attitude of his.”

Xin Wei couldn’t hide his shock, and even Guan Yi’s eyes widened visibly with surprise.

“B-but, the God-Emperor is the greatest cultivator in the world! He returned after his ascension to lead the Empire to—”

“Pfuahaha! The world!? That little Qin? Not even after ten thousand years! Not after a hundred thousand! He may love lording it up over this little island of his, but the world is much larger than you realize. And let me tell you, before I was sealed away ten thousand years ago, not a single person had ascended to the heavens in the entire history of the world!”

Once again, the entire group could only stare at Jianmo in stunned silence. Just who was this ancient immortal?

“Alright, since you children are obviously too timid to ask anything of me, I suppose I’ll have to choose your rewards myself. Let’s see...you’ve got some odd cultivation—like a bunch of Qin’s idiots got tutored by some fiends and elementals. Still, closer than I’ve ever seen humans get to doing it right, so how about I give you a little nudge?”

Lee Jia had a terrible premonition and was about to ask what he meant when a sudden invisible pressure pressed down on her, knocking the wind out of her and nearly causing her to collapse on the ground.

“Oops, overdid it a little bit, there...”

As Jianmo spoke, the pressure lifted slightly and Lee Jia could breathe again.

“That pressure you’re feeling is caused by something a little bit different from the spiritual energy you’re used to. It’ll get worse as time goes on and even leaving this cave won’t put a stop to it. If at least one of you manages to comprehend it before dying, you can free the others. Good luck!”

Jianmo turned and began to saunter his way out of the cave before he stopped as if he’d just realized he’d forgotten something.

“Oh, I nearly forgot!”

He returned to stand before Eui, raising a hand and poking her in the head. Lee Jia had a moment of panic as she sensed a powerful strand of destruction ki pass from him into Eui, but it was quickly absorbed into her core without causing any harm.

“If you’re ever lost trying to control that unruly element of yours, seek me out. Though you’ll have to survive first, you adorable little baby demon!”

Lee Jia’s face paled as Jianmo left them with a revelation that confirmed her worst fears. She didn’t have time to ruminate on it, though. The oppressive feeling of Jianmo’s ‘reward’ was already steadily growing worse.

Closing her eyes, Lee Jia tried to focus on the pressure in order to discover what was causing it. To her shock, she couldn’t sense anything at all through either her mana sense or her soul. It wasn’t a physical pressure either—it was as if she was being crushed by nothing. Lee Jia was grinding her teeth in frustration when she felt a tug on her sleeve.

Looking over, she met Eui’s now-unfamiliar red eyes—the same crimson color as Jianmo’s. Eui struggled to speak as the mysterious pressure made it difficult to breath properly.

“Need to...meditate...”

Looking around, Jia saw that everyone else had already sat down and began meditating, their faces bunched up as they tried to concentrate through the slowly increasing pain. Jia nodded and sat down in the lotus position to—Eui pulled on her sleeve again. Looking up, Eui was staring at her with an expression that said ‘Are you stupid?’.

“Together...dumbass.”

Jia covered her blushing face with her hands. That really should have been obvious. Eui sat down in front of her and they joined hands before Jia paused for a moment. She was about to ask Eui which meditation technique they should start with, but as she tried to gather enough breath to speak, the choice became obvious.

“Mana.”

The one word was enough to get her thoughts across to Eui, who quickly nodded in agreement. It took them a few attempts to get their breathing techniques under control while fighting against the oppressive force of Jianmo’s dubious gift, but once they got started they were able to join their auras—and their minds—together with practiced ease.

She’s in pain. Dying. Xin is trying his best, but she doesn’t have long left. Jia is crying—she whispers an apology and Eui just wants to tell her it’s not her fault. Jia leans forward until Eui can feel the warmth of her breath, and then—her lips are so soft...

There was an awkward pause as they woke from the dreamlike state of shared memories.

“Why was that what we remembered!?”

“I don’t know! It was the last time we meditated together and it was a pretty intense experience. I guess it’s just a natural thing to think about.”

With their minds linked, it was impossible to distinguish their thoughts from each other’s, but they could still have a sort of dialogue. Though it felt a bit like talking to herself, this was the reason Jia had suggested starting with mana meditation.

“Did we both remember the same thing?”

“I think so, yes. It was definitely a traumatic experience, but why did it have to focus so much on the—”

The kiss. Damn it! She hadn’t meant to complete that thought, but it was almost impossible to hide thoughts like this.

“Nevermind, it’s not important right now. Let’s focus on the present.”

“Right. This pressure, what is it? It’s completely overwhelming, but I can’t sense it at all.”

“I don’t know, but there has to be some kind of hint, right? Surely he wouldn’t just casually doom us all.”

“Tell that to Yan...”

“...”

The sword separates Yan Zhihao’s smug face from his stupid shoulders. Jia screams in horror—ah, that’s right, she’s never seen someone die like that. Wow, that is a lot of blood. Eui gives her friend’s hand an encouraging squeeze. She’ll get used to it one day.

Another memory. That usually didn’t happen after they’d begun meditating, but they didn’t usually try to carry on conversations like this either.

“I don’t know if I want to get that comfortable with death.”

“Neither do I. I think Eui sees it as a necessity, though. It’s kill or be killed.”

“Yeah...”

They tried to shift their mind away from that topic for now, but the memories were distracting.

“Hints...he said that the pressure was from something that wasn’t the same as mana, but he seemed to imply it wasn’t exactly different either.”

“Maybe he meant divine energy, but I don’t see how he could possibly expect any of us to comprehend divine energy when we aren’t even in the third stage yet.”

Divine energy was what separated the houtian and xiantian stages. It was expressly the ability to create and sense divine energy from mana that defined a xiantian cultivator. They had only vaguely touched upon it in their lessons, as it wasn’t relevant to them at such a low level of cultivation.

“Ok, forget that for now, what else did he say?”

“Hmm, something about how our cultivation was strange? Like we’d been tutored by ‘fiends and elementals’ he said.”

“What did he even mean by fiend?”

“Well if ‘Qin’s idiots’ means spiritual cultivation, and elementals implies mind cultivation then...”

“You think they are some kind of body cultivators?”

They’d never heard of ‘fiends’ but it made a certain amount of sense. So he was talking about the three disciplines, but what was odd about that?

“He said we were close to doing it right. Does that mean that there’s some kind of fundamental flaw to the three disciplines?”

“Maybe...don’t you think it’s weird that they are separate in the first place? It’s always felt like they were meant to be used together. Most of the incredible things we’ve accomplished happened when we crossed one discipline into another.”

That was true. The combat meditation technique used natural mana to replenish the ki within the body, when ki meditation was normally fueled by the aura. The incredible lightning bolt that had won Jia her duel with Yan Zhihao had been powered by her lightning-attuned ki. Even Eui’s ability to recover from the backlash of her martial arts was a result of wood-element qi techniques.

Certainly, there were a lot of synergies, but they were still fundamentally separate disciplines. When Jia and Eui meditated on mana, they cultivated their mind. For ki and qi, their body and soul, respectively. As each became more refined, a part of their being was improved—three parts of the whole. How, then, could they truly be separate?

Eui trembles in fear as Jianmo approaches. Why is he looking at her like that? He calls up a mote of familiar dark energy and pokes her in the head. The destruction mana invades her aura before travelling from her head, down to her heart, and then finally being absorbed by the core of crystalized mana inside her chest. Eui is startled by the words that follow after that. Did he just call her a demon?

It happened again, it seemed like there were side effects to trying to communicate mentally like this. She was almost frustrated by the new distraction when a realization snapped into place.

“From Eui’s aura into her body!?”

“Is it that strange? Jia’s combat meditation does the same, doesn’t it?”

“That’s just drawing mana into the lungs and absorbing it into the body directly. That went through Eui’s awareness, and then somehow ended up in her body.”

“They’re connected!”

The revelation was exciting, and they nearly lost their concentration. They had been cultivating their mind, their bodies, and their souls but what if that was wrong? If they were all connected, then shouldn’t it be possible to cultivate all of them at the same time? Why refine parts of themselves, when they could just refine their entire being?

They circulated the mana in their aura and calmed their breathing. Jianmo had given Eui an enormous hint compared to the others. Was he playing favorites because she was a demon? He seemed to have a strange affinity for her. Setting that aside for now, they focused on their awareness—the source of their mana sense.

The ‘awareness’ was composed of mana, like the rest of their aura. The only difference was that it was an intrinsic part of them, rather than the rest of the aura which they simply had access to. The goal of the mana gathering stage was to expand the awareness until it filled the entire aura.

When meditating—or if they focused on it—they could move that awareness freely, altering the origin of their mana sense. Normally, however, where did it reside? Naturally, in the same place as all their other senses—the head! They tried to recall their awareness back, and immediately ran into a problem.

They currently had only one awareness, but two heads! They couldn’t return their awareness to their bodies without separating it. After some consideration, they decided to forget the physical aspect for now and focus on the metaphysical. Whether it was in their head or not, their awareness should maintain some kind of connection to their bodies. It was, after all, a part of them.

They concentrated on the mana that made up their awareness, trying to retrace the path that Jianmo’s mana had taken to reach Eui’s core. It was a strange feeling, like their awareness was trying to turn itself inside-out. A thread of their awareness collapsed in on itself and wound its way through their mind until it suddenly met an odd obstruction.

Stagnant, static mana that ignored all attempts to control it blocked the path of their thread of awareness. They mentally started as they realized they recognized it. Corruption! This was the same corruption that blocked the meridians within their soul! Hadn’t they been practicing how to clear this for months?

With practiced ease, they began to open this unusual meridian within their mind—drilling down slowly as they wondered where it would lead. The meridian seemed to be somehow thicker than what they were used to. If they were to compare meridians to blood vessels, then this one was like a main artery compared to the smaller veins. When at last they reached the end, they broke through to a familiar opening.

The dantian—no, not quite. The thread of awareness was drawn in and circulated on its own before being scattered throughout their bodies. Suddenly, they could acutely feel the blood rushing through their veins, the beating of their hearts, and the synchronized rhythm of their breathing. The opening they had reached was the heart—or rather, hearts.

“I can feel our ki! It’s like we’re meditating on mana and ki at the same time!”

“We opened the connection to both bodies at the same time. I can feel two of everything, it’s so weird.”

“It worked! We were right! It’s all connected.”

“That’s great, but we only figured out this much because of Jianmo’s little cheat. How will we find the last connection?”

They considered the question as they basked in the warmth of their combined ki. They realized that their heart had been like another dantian, connected directly to their mind by that large meridian. Was their awareness—or perhaps their aura—like a dantian for the mind? If so, then maybe they could find another connection from the heart to their soul.

It was difficult to search the space of their hearts for a blocked connection. The heart seemed to want to constantly circulate any energy that passed near it throughout the body. With a concerted effort, they were able to maintain control over a new thread of awareness and began to probe the metaphysical space surrounding their hearts.

Sure enough, they soon found what they were looking for. An enormous meridian—even more so than the first one—which was blocked by corruption. They wasted no time in beginning to clear it—the pressure had been growing significantly while they were meditating, and they could now feel blood trickling down from their noses. They had no choice but to commit to this path and hope that it was the right one.

After countless hours of intense concentration, they slowly managed to open the giant meridian. The rush of emotions that flooded them when they broke through was unmistakable—they had reached the dantian and connected their souls.

For the first time, Jia and Eui had truly become connected to each other—body, mind, and soul. She had been two people sharing experiences—connected, but distinct. Now, she was something else. The emotions didn’t overwhelm her anymore—they were completely her own. For the moment, she had ceased to be Jia and Eui and become something greater.

Her first unique feeling as a nascent being was confusion. Who was she? Did she have no name, or two names? She knew she only had one mind and one soul, but what about the body? They were still distinct, so was she one body with two of everything, or was it still two bodies? What had become of Jia and Eui? Were they gone forever? If not, then what happened when they came back? Would she be gone forever?

She shook her heads and calmed her breathing. As confusing and terrifying as those questions were, she had to focus. If she didn’t comprehend Jianmo’s oppressive pressure, then she would definitely die along with Jia, Eui, and all her—their?— friends. She expanded her senses and nearly cried out in surprise at what she felt.

Her mana sense and her aura had evolved into something else entirely. She could sense not just mana, but everything within her aura with a vivid clarity that she had never experienced before. Every piece of rock, every invisible mote of water vapor floating restlessly in the air, every last speck of energy was clear to her.

On a deep, intuitive level, she understood that everything that existed within her aura was hers. The rocks, air, and water were as much a part of her as her own flesh and blood. And of course, so was the mana. Everything except for a strange, foreign energy. An invader—someone else’s influence intruding into what should have been her domain.

The foreign influence was just a remnant, she realized. It somehow continued to exist without an active will behind it, but it put up no resistance as she exerted her own influence upon it. As the foreign energy was added to her own domain, it began to vibrate strangely. Instantly, she knew she couldn’t control this energy, it was too far beyond even her current level.

Just as she was beginning to suspect it had been divine energy after all, it exploded. Unable to keep its form within her aura, what she was now certain had been divine energy collapsed into raw mana. Somehow, the small amount of divine energy broke apart into a volume of mana several orders of magnitude greater than it had begun.

She strained as the mana flooded her aura. It couldn’t escape her domain—it was hers now, after all—but there was too much for her to contain. Without anywhere else to go, the mana flooded into her bodies and soul. In mere moments, both her bodies and her dantian had been filled to capacity—she was at the peak of the first stage!

Yet still, there was too much mana! As it continued to flood into her, it threatened to burst her meridians and rampage through her bodies. She had no choice but to break through here and now or her cultivation would be irreparably damaged—or worse, she would die.

Luckily, she had been prepared for what came after the gathering stage. Unluckily, it involved a qualitative change that was different for each discipline! After qi gathering, came qi condensation—the semi-gaseous energy would be compressed on itself until it began to behave more like a liquid. After ki gathering was ki infusion—instead of circulating through her body like blood, the ki would be infused into her flesh and bones, strengthening them dramatically. After mana gathering was mana comprehension—but with her newly evolved mana sense she hadn’t the slightest clue how that might change.

So two completely different tasks, plus one entirely unknown challenge. How hard could it be? She couldn’t help but cry out in her mind.

“Jianmo, isn’t this a bit too hard for a bunch of teenagers? Actually, since I’ve only just begun my existence, aren’t I a newborn? This is far too much to ask!”

With an exertion of will, she found that she could split her attention into two things at once. She had originally been two people, after all—she should be able to do this much. With one will focusing on ki infusion and the other on qi condensation, she could only hope that the aura part would sort itself out.

She didn’t know how long she sat there, with blood streaming out of her ears, eyes, and noses. Sweat poured off her bodies as she focused on compressing the qi in her dantian over and over, while the other will worked on the bodies. After what felt like an eternity, there was finally a breakthrough. At the same time, both wills accomplished their task, and the mana that had been flooding into her finally reached a manageable level.

With a pair of shuddering breaths, she finally allowed herself to relax. Much to her displeasure, both her bodies were once again covered in that bizarre sludgy corruption from her ki awakenings, but the pressure had finally stopped—she had succeeded.

She wanted to check on her friends and make sure they were ok, but hesitated. A feeling of existential dread came over her. What would happen to her when she stopped meditating? No! She couldn’t think like that. What was she going to do, meditate forever? Besides, she didn’t want Jia and Eui to disappear any more than she wanted to disappear herself. Without any appealing options, she could only resort to cold pragmatism.

With a deep breath, she ended the meditation and opened her eyes. She was met with the sight of Eui staring at her in a way that reminded her of their first awakening. Jia was certain that the confusion and uncertainty on Eui’s face was a match for her own.

“What...the fuck was that!?”

Jia started to giggle at the nostalgic exclamation before freezing. She’d heard Eui’s voice, but Eui’s lips hadn’t moved at all. She stared wide-eyed at Eui, who was also wearing a shocked expression.

Before they had a chance to figure out what had just happened, they were interrupted by the sound of clapping. Jianmo appeared seemingly from out of nowhere, applauding them with a wide grin.

“Congratulations, you two! That was quite the show!”
