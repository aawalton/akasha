Five days into their hike through the forest, they managed to reach the heart. Ever since first hearing about the supposed heart of the forest, Asher had been curious if they would be able to notice when they hit the zone that Sedrick had warned them about.

While it wasn’t quite as distinct a cut as the Deadlands had been, they were still able to tell the difference soon enough.

“I think we made it,” Rulfar muttered, glancing at the dying plants all around them as they continued walking. None of the trees appeared completely dead, which made the sickened forest all the more eerie. The plants were drooping and trees were peeling bark. Mushrooms were growing all over the place, and the decaying grass crunched beneath their feet as they walked.

“What’s with all the mushrooms?” Moxy asked, flicking one off a nearby tree.

“While it's not always the case, they’re often an indication that a tree is sick,” Samantha said, frowning as she gently ran a hand along the tree. “It normally means the interior of the tree is decaying. Though this is so strange… I should be able to repair the tree with Tree Mender, but it’s almost as if there’s nothing to repair. Like the forest somehow sees this current state as its new normal.”

“I mean, despite how the place looks, I doubt we just happened to show up right as everything started dying,” Asher pointed out. “The lich’s diseases must keep all the plant life on the brink of death for some reason. Now we should use Polly’s ointments.”

Rulfar and Samantha both nodded, and despite Moxy’s grunt of displeasure, even she caught the bottle Asher threw her way. The four of them quickly rubbed the mixture onto their exposed skin, taking advantage of Asher’s Spatial Lock to keep the bugs at bay for the moment.

Seeing as they were going up against a lich that specialized in diseases, Asher had been on the lookout for any and all methods he was personally aware of that one could use to transmit diseases. Their helmets should cover the issue of airborne diseases, but he hadn’t missed just how many bugs were constantly buzzing around in the forest. They were little more than annoying out in the main forest, but here in the heart where everything was clearly diseased, he was all but certain letting the bugs bite them would be a horrible mistake. Malaria was carried by mosquitoes back on Earth, and he figured a lich with a few centuries of free time on his hands could easily create a disease that made malaria look like a common cold.

Luckily, Polly ended up having just the thing for them. As always, the team used Recall to pop back to the royal palace during the night, so swinging by Dormaul and checking in to see if this world did in fact have magical bug spray was a simple enough task. The alchemist had been more than happy to whip some up for him, but she’d warned him about applying too much of it. While it actually worked far better than bug spray did back on Earth, that was because it was technically a mild poison. More than enough to kill any bugs that got close, but too much of it would start harming the wearer.

“For the first time, I’m glad we have these helmets on,” Rulfar admitted as he finished applying his bottle. “I have a feeling this stuff smells absolutely horrible.”

“Polly warned me it would,” Asher chuckled. “So, that covers bugs and airborne vectors. We’re not going to be eating or drinking anything found in the forest obviously, which really only leaves injury. Sedrick said the monsters in this portion of the forest are far deadlier than what we’ve encountered getting here, so everyone be careful.”

“Damn, I was planning to let a monster rip my arm off, but now I’m having second thoughts,” Moxy drawled, tossing the empty bottle back at him before pushing onward. “And let’s hope the monsters get more interesting. I was starting to get bored of slapping them down without a real fight!”

Moxy ended up getting her wish far sooner than any of them expected.

Barely ten minutes after they’d hit the heart of the forest, there was a gentle whistling sound from off to the side. Asher had just enough time to turn toward it before two quills the size of javelins punched straight through his chest and stomach, ripping out the other side and causing him to stumble back and fall to a knee. Samantha managed to move her glowing wings fast enough to block the three quills that had been sent her way, and Moxy deflected hers with a reflexive Air Nova. Rulfar wasn’t quite so lucky, and the large man took two quills to the gut and one to his leg, forcing him to stumble back as well. Either his Strength or his Bear element made his body far tougher than Asher’s, however, and the quills didn’t manage to punch straight through him.

“What’s attacking us?!” Samantha asked, her eyes going wide as she took in the holes in Asher’s torso.

“It has to be a monster,” Rulfar grunted as he ripped the quills out of his gut and grimaced at the tips. “If these things carry disease…”

“We’ll worry about that once it's dead,” Asher said, triggering his purge function from Complete Resilience just to be safe. Already the holes in his chest were slowly closing up before his very eyes thanks to Eternal Regeneration and his Amulet of Improved Healing. He’d thrown up a Spatial Lock immediately after being struck, just to make sure any further quills didn’t tear into them. “It’s too far out for my Shard Detection skill to pick it up. Moxy, do you have a bead on it?”

While it wasn’t a skill she talked about much, Moxy’s fifth skill from her Air element was Whispering Wind, which allowed her to hear far better than the average person.

“I think so,” the warrior said, her eyes narrowed as she tried to track the monster in the woods. “The helmet makes it harder to hear, but I’m pretty certain it’s circling around behind us, using the trees as cover. Drop your freaky bubble thing, I want to take a crack at it!”

“You’re good to run through it, just don’t let it injure you!” Asher said, still watching Rulfar as the bloodhound finished ripping the quills out of his body. Part of Asher was terrified the large man would simply seize up and fall over, but so far, he seemed alright. Though seeing as they were dealing with diseases here, there was a high possibility he’d been infected and they wouldn’t know until symptoms started popping up.

Hearing she was free to leave his barrier was all Moxy needed, and she sprinted off into the forest to go after their assailant. Just as Asher turned to ask Samantha to start hosing down Rulfar with her Southing Spring skill to be safe, five more massive quills smashed into his Spatial Lock from the complete other direction that Moxy had run, causing Asher to stagger as his energy reserves took a significant hit.

“There’s another one!” he shouted, gritting his teeth as he tried and failed to locate the monster among the dense trees. “Samantha—”

“I got it!” she said, already nocking another arrow as her wings flared and she shot forward. Unlike Moxy who had been forced to run in a zigzag, Samantha flew straight through the heavy treeline, utilizing her Woodland Stride skill to the extreme as she phased directly through a tree that was large enough Asher couldn’t even have done so with Astral Dip.

“Asher…”

Turning toward Rulfar’s strained voice, Asher’s heart dropped as he took in the pained look on the bloodhound’s face. Already, there were dark lines of purple and black radiating out from the areas the quills had struck his flesh. Rulfar looked like he was concentrating, and Asher blinked as he realized the dark lines were actually growing and shrinking every few seconds, as if caught in a battle of tug of war.

“It’s almost like a poison,” Rulfar managed, breathing heavily as he grimaced down toward his chest. “I’m using Alter Flow to keep the tainted blood away from the rest of my body, but it’s difficult. It’s as if the disease has a mind of its own and is trying to spread as fast as possible. I can probably keep this up for an hour or two if I can keep concentrating, but not much longer.”

“Force the diseased blood out through the open wounds,” Kroult instructed, stepping forward and peering more closely at Rulfar’s condition. “Asher, you must stab him. The disease is somehow healing the wound that was created, presumably to prevent Rulfar from doing just that. Your Sever the Thread will keep it open.”

“Right!” Asher said, thankful that Kroult was keeping a cool head. The only thing he’d been able to think of was using Permanence on Rulfar to freeze the disease in its path and buy time, but if Samantha or Moxy had gotten infected as well…

Deciding not to worry about that just yet, Asher did as instructed, stabbing as gently as he could into Rulfar’s already wounded skin to ensure the blood would continue to flow. Straining, Rulfar began to sweat as the diseased blood poured out of his open wounds, and it was Asher’s turn to grimace at the sight of the almost black blood that poured out. The disease had only been in Rulfar’s body for a couple of seconds, and it had already managed to do some serious damage. This latest lich was no joke, and Asher now understood how Sedrick had almost died every time he’d tried to push into the heart of the forest. If even just breathing the air did something similar, this place was a complete death trap.

“I think that’s all of it,” Rulfar grunted, giving Asher a tired smile. “If you could stop Sever the Thread, I’d appreciate it.”

“Sorry!” Asher said, doing so and watching as Rulfar’s wounds stopped bleeding almost instantly.

“Well that’s nasty!” Moxy said, grinning as she rejoined them. “Take a look at this thing.”

It was pretty clear Moxy had managed to find and take down their initial assailant, because the monster she was dragging behind her was covered from head to toe in massive quills. It was like a wolf combined with a porcupine, and larger than any dog Asher had ever seen. Its quills and body were the same withered brown and green of the dying forest, allowing it to blend in easily enough.

“The other one was the same,” Samantha said as she walked straight through a tree to rejoin them. “Their quill attack is pretty telegraphed once you know it’s coming, so they’re really only a danger for that first ambush.”

“But what a danger they are,” Asher muttered, glancing back at the black blood that was seeping into the forest ground. “We’ve been in here for all of ten minutes and already Rulfar nearly died. If he hadn’t had a Blood element with the perfect skill, he’d be down for the count.”

“Then you would have frozen the disease with Permanence,” Moxy said, waving away his concern. “We got cocky, and we paid the price. Now that we know what to expect, it won’t happen again. Wings, can you sense these things coming now that you know they exist?”

“Yes, Nature Sense will help with that,” Samantha nodded, looking just as conflicted as Asher felt as she looked at the condition Rulfar was in. Despite his size and impressive fortitude, his face was pale and covered in sweat from the near-death experience and strain of keeping his own blood at bay. “Moxy… I know you aren’t going to like this pitch, but I think it might be best if Asher and I go after this lich alone. We thought we were prepared, but if the diseases plaguing this forest are capable of killing people that quickly… If it’s just the two of us, I can keep an eye out for monsters and help track the lich down with my Forest skills, and if I get infected, Asher can use Permanence on me. But if we stick with a group—”

“Asher might have to decide between which of us he’s going to save, and it sure as hell ain’t gonna be me,” Moxy said, rolling her eyes as she shook the monster she’d killed by the leg. “I’ll admit these aren’t exactly the most fun things to fight anyway, but I don’t like the idea of running away from any fight.”

“Why don’t we compromise?” Asher offered, just happy that he hadn’t been forced to be the one to make the pitch that Moxy sat this one out. “Samantha and I can make better time moving through the forest with just the two of us thanks to Astral Dip and Woodland Stride. We can take care of the boring work of combing the heart of the forest to find the lich, and then if we need you, we can grab you guys for the big fight at the end. Sound fair?”

Moxy looked like she still wasn’t the biggest fan of the idea. Yet before she could argue, Rulfar stepped forward and gave her a serious look.

“Does the thought of dying to a disease you can’t do anything about sound like a warrior’s death to you? You’ve made it clear you want to go out in a blaze of bloody glory. There’s nothing exciting about dying from the inside out.”

“That’s fair,” Moxy grumbled, frowning at the puddle of black blood on the ground. “Alright, fine. You’re lucky monsters are boring to fight against. But if you come across any that are more fun than these, you better grab me.”

“Will do,” Asher said, letting out a sigh of relief as he gave Samantha a thankful smile. Like she said, the two of them would be able to move far quicker on their own, and only having to worry about one person would be a huge weight off his back. “Alright, let me drop the rest of you guys off back at the palace for now, but don’t get too comfortable. There’s no telling when we’ll find the lich, so we might need you at a moment’s notice.”

“I’m always ready to punch things,” Moxy snorted. “Though I suppose it’s only fair I let you take the lead on this lich seeing as I basically took down the last one myself!”
