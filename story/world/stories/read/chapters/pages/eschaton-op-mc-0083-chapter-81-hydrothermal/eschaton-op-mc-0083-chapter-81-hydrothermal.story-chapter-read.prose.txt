After taking several hours to pull out as many of the crystals as they reasonably can, the Cerulean Guard and Machina leave the cave. As a group, they march towards a skyscraper sized hydrothermal vent off in the distance. They often come across much smaller ones. When they do they take more samples of their plumes and as well of the more docile, or at least less aggressive creatures. Over time the rocks underneath their boots shift from a dark, craggy rock into a more blueish one. Small specks of metal shine in Machina’s light.

They set up the core driller again. Machina rip a chunk of the seabed out and crushes it with his hand. He peers at the dust, probably scanning it. The driller continues to growl in the background. It’ll pull up a much deeper sample far more useful for academia.

Infused Cobalt ore, extremely pure.

What’s that mean for us? Tiffany asks.

Nothing, really. For humanity? Don’t know, it depends if it’s even possible to extract.

The field of blueish, shiny rock stretches on as far as they can see in every direction.

If this deposit is as large as I think it is, it could be worth billions of dollars. Assuming it could ever be extracted, which right now, it can’t.

Danny shares a shocked look with his teammates.

What? Tiffany frantically signs back.

Mundane Cobalt is used in high grade aerospace components. And batteries, among other things. It’s extremely rare, normally only produced as a byproduct of copper mining. Pure deposits like this only exist near hydrothermal vents. Africa used to produce about half of the world’s supply of cobalt via copper mining, but the Empire of the Sun was pretty quick to snap up those mines.

How could you possibly know that?

Machina points at his head. Perfect memory and an ultra high-speed internet connection. I saw global logistics breaking down and wondered if I could help. So I studied all of it. The answer is no, by the way.

Fractures could be a source for minerals? John asks.

Apparently. Machina replies. Better yet, mining it would have no environmental impacts on the real world. Unfortunately, just to get here meant diving two miles straight down in the ocean, and would also require constant vigilance against monsters. You’re all experienced monster slayers. I’m sure you can tell me exactly how practical trying to mine down here would be.

Not a fucking chance, Danny signs to him. My dreams of wealth are smashed once again.

I guess you’ll have to source the cash for your swimming pool somewhere else.

All four of them leave the mineral field behind, though Danny does throw a half-hearted despairing look at all the mineral wealth left behind.

Finally they arrive at the closest monolithic skyscraper-sized hydrothermal vent. A huge tube worm pokes out of a massive white shell. It’s easily the size of a bus, and everyone except John immediately braces for a fight. It doesn’t even notice their presence, and continues to gently wave in the flowing water. John waves them all down.

Tube worms don’t even have eyes, he explains. They have bacteria living in them that turn sulfide compounds into energy for them both. Even if it knew we were here it couldn’t eat us. They literally don’t have the parts for it. Well, mundane ones at least. Hard to say how that changes for magic ones.

Danny rolls his eyes. The biologist in you is coming out once again.

Hey, gotta use that degree for something.

Tiffany brushes noxious, foul smelling black snow off her armor.

What a horrible smell.

It’s heavy in sulfur and iron compounds, with traces of other metals. Cobalt, gold, and copper, Machina replies.

Toxic?

Yes. How it’ll affect you, I don’t know. We can retreat if you want.

We’ll be fine. Let’s get to work. Best not to linger if we can.

Machina, John directs. Can you carve out a small chunk of the tube worm for study?

I could just rip one out.

Machina, the worm itself is probably a good thirty feet long, and that’s not counting the fifty feet of shell.

That’s doable. I can chop it down and pull the worm out.

Sure, why not.

As Machina begins to cut down the worm and its tube shell, the rest of them continue to set up the various instruments for the fourth time.

Boss, we’re running low on floaties. Got maybe a quarter of them left.

Hopefully we tackle the boss soon. Thankfully we’ve got Machina to carry extra stuff if we really need to.

Why do we need the floaties anyway? Danny complains. He put the crates in his little pocket dimension anyway.

A click gets their attention, and Machina signs to them.

I’d rather not have to pull out a thousand individual items. Not to mention lots of these things are sealed in the containers. If I pulled them out they’d be open to the air. It’s better for the labs if the samples are as pristine as possible.

He hauls on his sword, and the tube worm is cut free. It slowly falls to the seabed, landing with an earth-shaking thud they can feel in their chests. Dust jumps up, and all three of the Cerulean Guard are left coughing.

I think this won’t fit in a crate. I’ll have to carve it up just to store it away.

No, John signs after recovering. I don’t think it will.

Best get to work.

Machina quickly starts butchering the worm, and clouds of neon red blood start to fill the water. John scoops up a few gallons of it. It’s hard to say what it could be used for, but it is at the very least, infused material. Even if it’s extremely toxic, there’s a chance it can be reduced into something less toxic or maybe converted into poisons used to kill monsters.

Despite the size of the massive worm, it doesn’t take Machina very long to chop it all up and store it away. What must have been sixty tons of monster meat and shell vanishes in less than ten minutes.

The Academy is going to be— Danny is interrupted by something the size of a freight train slamming into Machina. The shockwave of whatever just blasted past them throws them and all of their equipment spiraling off into the dark. Only Tiffany’s quick reaction time keeps them somewhat together, though the instruments are lost to the darkness.

A massive transparent eel, jaws open wide bites down on Machina. He holds the mouth open, vicious pointed teeth shattering on his gauntlets. A second, inner jaw that reminds Danny of a xenomorph tries to snap for Machina, but can’t quite reach. The clack-clack-clack of bone on bone is a violent promise of a bloody end. They both slide backwards, and Machina’s boots grind a deep trench into the seabed. His cloak flutters and flows around his arms, leaving him free to fight.

He throws the eel back, and it rolls to the side with a hiss. Its tiny, beady eyes focus on the Cerulean Guard, realizing there’s much easier and tastier prey. It lunges forward at them, both sets of jaws open wide.

“Oh no you don’t.”

Machina grabs the eel’s tail and pulls hard.

The whiplash snaps the jaws shut and a dozen jagged teeth shatter. He swings the train sized eel into the hydrothermal vent, crushing half a dozen worms and even cracking the stone mountain. Black smoke pours from brand new vents. A deep orange glow backlights the noxious plume.

Machina stomps towards the eel as it shakes off the stunning strike. It dashes away, and the shockwave nearly rips the Cerulean Guard away.

“I got this, just stay in cover!”

The eel charges back in. Its bottom jaw scrapes along the seabed, shattering boulders and digging a trench. Machina leaps up, and slams his fist into the eel’s head.

Thoom!

The impact is so powerful it causes a shockwave that Danny feels rather than hears. It hammers the eel’s head even deeper into the stone, and bunches up. It’s tail goes up and over, flipping onto its back.

Machina’s sword forms in his hand, and another shockwave punches Danny in the chest. He hurls the blade and it zips through the water, slamming into the Eel’s head. The hilt stops the sword just over the eel’s skull.

“Brace yourselves!”

Tiffany forms a bubble of water around them as a shield and freezes it solid. John adds his power to the mix, throwing every bit of mana he has left into it.

For a brief moment, Machina floats above them all, gravity yet to pull him down. His cloak roils around him like a living thing. It pulls back, revealing the hard angles and edges of his armor. No longer is Machina a friendly giant, but a vengeful titan. His Aura blasts out, and everything freezes. In fear, in awe, perhaps both.

Let the hammer fall. It says. It promises the end.

Machina kicks against the water so hard he blasts a visible pressure wave that blows the top hundred feet of the hydrothermal vent away. He rockets forward like a bullet from a gun, just a blur even to Danny’s superior, magically enhanced vision.

Machina slams into his sword and hammers it straight through the eel’s skull like a bullet. Pink brain matter and blood bursts out from the hole. The sword disappears entirely, blasting a crater in the seabed, completely burying itself.

The pressure wave that blew the hydrothermal vent in half reverberates off the top of the fracture and back down. Machina weathers it without notice. The Cerulean Guard isn’t so lucky. The ice cracks and then shatters. Even reinforced by nature of being magically conjured and John’s assistance, it’s still not strong enough to withstand such crushing force. Danny, Tiffany and John are all slammed into the black, rocky seabed hard enough to rip their breath from them.

The shield, despite its failure, still did its job.

“Ooooooow,” Danny groans. “Fuck, that hurt.”

“Everyone still alive?” Tiffany asks.

“I’m pretty sure being dead would hurt less,” John grouses.

“Sorry,” Machina rumbles. “Are you hurt?”

“We’re fine. What…what was that?” Tiffany gasps while struggling to her feet.

“That was me killing the monster. Here, take these. Three 3.6 Amp Fractal Shards.”

Tiffany looks at the three rainbow quartz crystals in her hand with wide eyes.

“Hey, Danny?”

“Yeah?” he asks, brushing the metal-rich dust off his armor.

“I think we can fill your swimming pool now.”
