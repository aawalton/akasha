Chapter 47: Mulch of the Mole Men

The good news, Ambrose supposed, was that the spider venom was apparently not as bad as expected. No one dropped dead. Not even Luthor, which meant that the rest of them were probably safe…albeit in a great deal of constant burning via their bites and cuts. It was a little like having several patches of fire ants stinging relentlessly every other second, and moods were low.

Except for their guides.

“It was real lucky that y’all came by. We were hanging up’n those webs for the better part of a day, right Roy?”

The bat let out a screech, and the meaning forced its way into Ambrose’s brain, “Near about.” He walked on all fours, low to the ground, his clawed wings pulling him forward with a surprising dexterity.

Only the last of their trio didn’t speak. The walking corpse, as he thought of her, was actually the sapient and carnivorous mushroom colony puppeting the body. ‘Mychele’ was apparently intelligent and a friend of theirs, and no one quite knew what to make of her. Or them, or whatever.

“Why were you three even down there?” Ambrose asked as they made their way through the spider queen’s tunnel out. It would, apparently, lead them back to whatever passed for civilization around these parts. “I thought the different species here didn’t get along.”

SNAC had told them a little about Stonskarr, but it had been hard to retain it all with everything going on and the pride bordering on propaganda coming from the golem. The empire successfully used local racial tensions to blame a natural disaster on them mixing and stepped in to pick up the pieces and rebuild. It was apparently a common tactic to get a foot in the world before a full invasion.

Mulch looked over his shoulder as he trudged along. “We, uh…Well, it’s a bit embarrassing, you see, but the three of us are old buddies. We’re not supposed to be seen talking to each other, so we meet up in different places, you know?”

Roy screeched his agreement and scampered ahead. The tunnel went uphill, and the bat was even better at climbing than he was at crawling.

“This time, we got unlucky. We watched them eat four other people, and I know they were getting hungry again. We all owe you a mighty big debt. Which kind of leads us into something awkward.” He and Mychele stopped and turned, right where Roy had jumped ahead.

“What’s that?” Luthor asked. Despite taking the worst of the last fight, he insisted on taking the lead, and did so with the help of a long bone he used as a walking stick. “We really don’t want any surprises.”

“Well, it’s about which of us you go home with. You can’t go with Roy, since I don’t think none of you can fly and he’s not in any condition to bring anyone with him. So that leaves either coming to Crag with me, or Morelton with Mychele here.”

The body turned, and the orange cap poking through the eye socket glowed.

We would be happy to have you among us. We have plenty of food and drink for outsiders, and could make you very comfortable before you travel on your way.

Everyone but their prisoners jumped in surprise. The words had wormed their way into Ambrose’s consciousness, along with flashes of emotion and images. The end result had been something honest, vulnerable, and disgustingly intimate. He hated it at once, and wasn’t the only one.

“For the love of everything sacred, warn us next time,” Raeva snapped, hands out like she was warding off the voice in our heads.

Sorrow followed, an acknowledgment without words, the psychic equivalent of a nod.

“They get that a lot,” said Mulch, nose twitching wildly. “They’re good people, despite using corpses to get around and their words feel like your brain is itchin’. It’s kind of why so many of my kind don’t trust ‘em.”

Jessica understood why before everyone else. “Does that mean they can read minds too?” Her eyes were wide, either with excitement or anxiety.

“Uhh, yeah, kinda,” said Mulch apologetically. “But only if they get close. That’s why Mychele’s been keeping their distance, you know? Better to not stomp all over your privacy.” His dark eyes shifted uneasily for a second, but the worry was replaced with a big, broad smile, showing big buck teeth.

Ambrose had already been suspicious, but now his senses screamed at him that they weren’t safe, and that Mulch was hiding a lot. How much had Mychele managed to scan from them? There were reasons, many of them, why all forms of telepathy were considered deceptively dangerous.

“What did they find out about us?” he demanded, ignoring the cacophony of pain and deteriorating clothes to push past Raeleq and Luthor to jab a finger into Mulch’s furry chest. He was too tired and sore to have any tact. “And are we going to have a problem? Is that why you sent Roy ahead?”

“No no, friend, of course not,” Said Mulch, backing up. It was actually kind of comical. He may have been shorter than Ambrose, but he was also twice as wide and probably as heavy as Raeleq. “It’s just…”

“It’s just what?” asked Luthor, so exhausted it came out as outright irritable.

We know you are not of the Empire, Mychele spoke again, dimmer this time, less obtrusive.

Ambrose sucked in a breath, and Mulch jumped in to ward off any potential violence. “But that’s okay! I kinda figgered it out myself, and to tell you the truth, we’re not too fond of them bastards neither. But we gotta keep quiet too, right? Ain’t a good idea to be talkin’ dangerously in mixed company.”

“Ahhh,” said Raeva, “and thus meeting outside the spider’s den with people who are supposed to be kept separate? Risky, but I get it. Our home is in the process of being taken over as well. We left, and ended up here.”

Maybe being so free with that information wasn’t a good idea, but they were all more than tired. It was enough, perhaps, to know that their guide wasn’t their enemy.

My people are of one mind regarding the Empire, Mychele projected into their minds, along with the cold anger of an old and ongoing outrage. If you come with me, it will be no effort to keep up the lie.

“If we come with you, we have to deal with you getting in our heads,” said Ambrose. “And I for one refuse. How about the rest of you? You want to be surrounded by a bunch of smelly moles, or mushroom zombies who rummage around in your brain?”

Mulch’s brow furrowed, and he gave his armpit a sniff before shrugging. “For what it’s worth, so long as y’all act like you’re Empire, nobody’s gonna give you trouble in Crag. But, uh, Mychele says that there’s some friction…”

Everyone looked at Jack and Dexi. Jack scowled, but Dexi shrugged and said, “You already got my silence so long as I get to look at that beauty of a ship. But we might want to leave the boy behind. Sorry Jack.”

That got a lot of people talking very fast, and ended with Jessica putting two fingers in her mouth and letting out a sharp whistle. “That’s not happening. We’re not leaving anyone behind, and we’re not going to murder some kid just because he’s too stupid to cooperate. We’re going to go to this Crag, get some rest and food, and then figure things out. Because if we don’t, I’m actually going to lose it and take people with me.”

Silence at first, and then Ambrose laughed. “Well, we can’t have that. Alright Mulch and Mychele. Lead the way. It’s not like we’ve got much of a choice at this point. Jessica will kill us all if we don’t get some damned sleep soon.”

Raeleq laughed as well, then Raeva, Dexi, and soon everyone, including Jessica, vented out their exhaustion and pain for just a few relieved seconds. The sound echoed throughout the tunnel, growing warped the further it went. Their path was uphill, but didn’t look as daunting as before.

“Let’s get a move on,” said Mulch, waving for them to follow while he grunted his way up the steep incline.

Ambrose had some trouble, but Raeleq wordlessly helped him by staying behind and making sure he didn’t stumble or fall. Neither of them said anything, but he gave a respectful nod after the first time.

The walk, or climb, or however one wanted to describe the experience wasn’t fun, but it was only another ten minutes the ground leveled out. Then the tunnel narrowed and spat them out into a new area, one that already felt safer.

It was almost like the area the ghouls had chased them through, with herds of beetles marching through, winged insects flying overhead and what looked like a real, paved road not too far off in the distance. All of it lit up by the same kind of magelights used in Rentan Academy and, Ambrose assumed, everywhere in the Empire.

“Here we are,” Mulch said, panting a little from the effort. He wiped some sweat from his brow with the back of his hand, flashing big claws. “The Ring Network. From here, the road leads between all the towns in a big circle. We even got trains that go from each town to the city and beyond, like a bunch of spokes in a wheel. We might not like the empire much, but the roads and railways are pretty nice.”

Unfortunately, this is where I must leave. We will see each other again, before you leave this world. Mychele bowed their head before trudging off to the left, leaving everyone behind.

“It’s not too much further to Crag. Just let me do the talking, okay?” The molekin grinned, and set off once more.

It was only another fifteen minutes, and the moment Ambrose stepped onto the firm, smooth road, he nearly cried from relief. Blisters lined his feet and a couple had scraped open and raw from constantly stepping over jagged rocks. The relief was enough to just let go and finish the rest of the walk with minimal complaining while Luthor and Mulch conversed.

This new, open area was so much more pleasant. While there were far more creatures in the open, it seemed mostly safe and secure. The area around the road looked manmade, as if someone had intentionally gutted a part of a giant mountain just for people to get around easier. It remained that way all the way to the outskirts of Crag, where it opened up again.

They were first spotted by a watchtower built into the rocky wall above. “Mulch?” A deep voice called out. “Is that you?”

Mulch stopped and waved his shovel-like hands wildly. “Hey Mitch, it sure is! I nearly got myself et by a spider.”

Pause. “You’d have it coming, you lazy, useless, son of a worm! Get yer ass home before Elder Marphy buries you alive.”

Their guide laughed weakly. “Yeah, about that. You’re gonna meet my him, and he’s a bit…yeah.”

They followed him past the watchtower, between a tall, narrow gap in the stone, down a paved path leading to what was, Ambrose had to admit, a charming little place. It was an open but narrow cavern, like much of this world so far, lined with homes built directly into the rock walls, with grand murals and sculptures carved into the stone everywhere. Crag continued on for a ways, long after his eyes failed him and only a dim glow could be seen.

Hundreds of molekin of all shapes and sizes wandered around. The children were short, pudgy, and undeniably cute, while the largest of them gave Raeleq a run for his money. Several stood around a giant cauldron. The smell of cooking wafted their way, torturing them with the promise of respite and relief.

“Mulch! It’s Mulch!” a voice called out.

“And others.”

“Empire folks?”

“Mulch!”

The mole in question turned around and grinned sheepishly. “Welcome to Crag, folks. Brace yourselves for hospitality.”
