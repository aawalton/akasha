James sensed how strong the Holy Kings were as he and the other two approached, and he made a quick decision.

Yes, he might be able to defeat them on his own. He probably could. He would certainly defeat them with the help of a giant alligator and a man who could nullify all Skills.

But the victory condition here wasn’t just winning the fight. He needed to kill them in a specific way, and he had to do it personally. Soul Eater Roscuro was bound to James’s soul specifically. If Han Jianguo or Samuel killed any of these men, James would end up seeing them again, and he emphatically did not want that to happen.

That decided it. He would try the overwhelming force approach and hopefully suppress them enough that no one could escape or avoid being killed by James specifically.

He quickly gathered up all the Mana he needed and didn’t bother concealing it this time. Let the aura around him make the Holy Kings question what they should do for a second or two.

Their hesitation bought him enough time to summon up all the energy he needed.

When he used Summon Divine Helper, he grabbed up the two birds—who should be healed up enough by now—along with his mother, Damien Rousseau, and the Dusky Pygmy Rattlesnake King. The roster of allies he still had available here had thinned out considerably in the pursuit of the Holy Kings.

But it would be enough.

“Keep them from fleeing or killing themselves,” James ordered.

He would have spoken the words telepathically, but he had summoned his mother and Damien here along with the nonhumans. Though mother and son were close, they didn’t have that particular link, let alone James and Damien.

Something to try to figure out a fix for after this is over, maybe. I shouldn’t need to speak for everyone to know their orders. A major flaw in a situation like this.

His subordinates charged as one, hurling themselves down at the enemy. Zora’s undead scattered all around, trying to force the Holy Kings to scatter their attention even further than the number of Ruler-caliber individuals otherwise would.

For their part, the Holy Kings displayed a certain indecisiveness, but one—the scarred man—recognized the situation he was in rapidly. He drew a dagger from his waist and plunged it into his own heart in a single smooth motion before anyone could react to stop him.

James moved at almost the same instant, but he knew it was too late before he’d made it.

Warp Step!

He lifted his foot, set it down, and appeared in the midst of the Holy Kings. The group of men, postured to face the enemies all around them, practically jumped out of their skins and shifted to deal with James. The Fisher Emperor had arrived a second after the scarred man had stabbed himself, but he couldn’t afford to try to attack that man just then. The other Holy Kings all threw themselves at him, wielding weapons and magic.

James had to resist the urge to laugh. It was exactly what he had wanted them to do: decide they had a chance to win if they all attacked together, and forget about the idea the scarred man had embraced of killing themselves. He only had these few Holy Kings left to dispose of. There were already ten down, unless he had mistaken his count somehow.

Wait, then how are there four here? he questioned. I guess one of these or one of the others wasn’t a true Holy King? They’re supposed to be thirteen.

He sidestepped an attack and swung ax-form Roscuro up in a brutal, violent arc. The attack connected with an old man who had tried to strike James with a mace. It took him in the neck, and he screamed horrendously as his head was separated from his shoulders and his soul was severed from his body and destroyed forever in an instant.

In the distance, constellations moved as some other Ruler launched a complicated attack against whoever they were fighting.

James barely managed to keep almost all of his focus on where he was. That constellation attack wasn’t for him.

He had to pivot and step back to get the old man’s body off of him and pull his sword free from the collarbone above the stump neck. That left him a limited range of motion with his next swing, but he managed to use the ax haft to block the sword that aimed for his heart, at least.

What had begun almost as a dance turned instantly into a pushing contest, back and forth, until James managed to shove hard enough that his ax head embedded itself into his opponent’s chest. The balding Holy King managed to keep the head low enough that it wasn’t near the heart—and therefore would not be an instantly fatal kill wound.

But James thought the blow would take the balding man out of the fight until the Fisher Emperor was ready to deal with him.

As the balding Ruler slumped toward the ground, Roscuro reshaped to pull free. At the same moment, a blade found its way into James’s trapezius, cutting its way from the shoulder up to the neck.

James’s body jerked involuntarily before he managed to punch this attacker backward. The enemy staggered, still holding the dagger in a firm grip, and stumbled into Samuel’s grasp. The giant alligator and the birds all grabbed for the Holy King’s limbs, and the man found himself struggling to break free.

The undead dogpiled onto the balding man, and Zora placed a foot on his chest with a smile at her son.

The situation was almost fully under control, with James’s allies all having closed the distance and the enemies mostly disarmed.

The Fisher Emperor first kicked away the blade that the balding man still held loosely in one hand, then turned back to the first Holy King who had stabbed himself in the heart.

James had hoped there would still be some ember left there for him to extinguish, some chance for Roscuro to be the final cause of death.

But he saw that the light had already gone out of that man’s eyes. It was too late for James to deal the finishing blow.

He scowled and let out a low growl. There would be at least one survivor. There would not be chaos in the Holy Kingdom. They had at least one man to rally the troops no matter what else James did today. The scarred man would probably act with resolve. Rather than heading off a war, the Fisher Emperor had fired the first shots of one. He looked back down at the balding man and considered what the best next move would be.

One logical choice would be to simply slaughter the remaining two Holy Kings.

Another possibility was to use the fact that there were still two alive as a negotiating opportunity. If James showed leniency here—No, they’re not the types to take my generosity as a sign of good faith. How frustrating. They’ll say it was God saving them by staying my hand or some stupid shit like that.

The Fisher Emperor raised the ax for a blow to sever head from neck, the easiest way to be certain of death. The man below him opened his mouth to speak.

“Have you heard the good news?” asked the balding man. His lips twitched in a strange smile. “Our Lord has returned. Joy will—”

James swung the ax down.

He never saw or heard the explosion. He didn’t directly witness the blinding flash.

But at the moment when he was about to make contact with the balding man’s flesh, bright light flooded his entire field of vision. The source was from outside his field of view, somewhere above.

He didn’t know what a disaster it was for a split-second. He thought, to the extent he was able to think in the tiny amount of time that passed, that it was some last ditch effort by one of the Holy Kings to survive. A light manipulation Skill similar to what King Omar had used.

He pressed on with Roscuro.

James felt the head of the ax begin to bite into the man’s skin, and then he felt an intense heat all over that came almost simultaneously with the light. The sensation scorched him. It felt like he’d suddenly decided to take a dip inside the sun. He sensed his body attempting to resist the fury for a tiny fraction of a second, and then he felt his resistances all burn away in the impossibly intense fire.

Then the Fisher Emperor knew nothing.

All was darkness, stillness.

Finally, there was light again.

Did I… die?

James blinked and found himself in a large room. It resembled the location where the Summit had been held, but he could see instantly that it wasn’t only Rulers and their entourages here—though all the entourages were there, in addition to the fighters. For one thing, Damien Rousseau, who hadn’t been included in James’s Summit crew but had been a participant in the melee, stood just a few feet from James. Next to the Werewolf stood Luna.

Everyone else linked, darted their eyes and swiveled their heads around uncertainly, and generally looked as confused as he felt.

An automated-sounding voice filled the air.

[This is the waiting area. All violence in this area is prohibited, under penalty of death.]

Now James understood.

So, the melee is really over. But how? What the hell happened there?

“We failed,” James breathed. “Damn it!” The second sentence was growled through clenched teeth.

“My apologies, Your Majesty,” said Samuel from just behind him.

“Our apologies,” said Luna’s voice from a bit further off.

“We should’ve moved faster,” muttered Damien.

James waved his hand, his emotions cooling almost instantly.

“No, you don’t need to all go apologizing,” he said. “Any failure here is a failure on my part. A failure to plan all the way to the end.”

I don’t know how I could have won the day, though. Even if I just captured them and didn’t kill them until later, that would have kept them from knowing what I intended to do, but it would have had its own risks. Maybe I would have kept any of the Holy Kings from killing themselves, or maybe the failure would have been even worse.

There was no foolproof way to destroy a group of fellow Rulers, not in a place where death was not supposed to be permanent.

“We still have the chance to finish what we start with them, son.” His mother’s voice cut into his thoughts.

He turned and gave her a nod and a smile.

Mina stepped into James’s peripheral vision, quietly took his hand in hers, and squeezed.

That reminds me, I need to see if they actually did die…

If they hadn’t, all the effort he had put in during the melee and before it would be for nothing.

James started looking around carefully, trying to see if he spotted any of the Holy Kings—whether ones he had killed already or ones that had simply been annihilated at the end alongside him by whatever that white hot fire was.

“Even the ones who survived, if I can encounter them again in the Victors Tournament, maybe these subsequent fights are supposed to be to the death,” James said quietly, thinking aloud. “Or at least they might leave the possibility of death open. Whoever won the melee, they influence the rules, right…?”

His eyes, in addition to seeking out the Holy Kings, tried to spot what the winner of the melee might look like. If he could get to that person and make some sort of deal before they gave their input on the tournament rules, maybe he could obtain an advantage.

A voice filled the air once again, silencing the low rumble of chatter that had settled over the room. This time, the sound wasn’t automated. It was one of the System’s many human-sounding representatives, a masked figure James swiftly spotted in a small gap in the large crowd of fighters.

[All participants in the melee, congratulations if you made it here. We have reconstructed those who were killed or damaged during the melee and restored all damaged or destroyed equipment to its prior functioning. Unfortunately, due to unforeseen events involving the use of Soul Magic, some of your colleagues did not survive the reconstruction process.]

Yes! Suck it, Holy Kings! I lost absolutely no one permanently during the melee, despite the best efforts of your hired gun, and you lost three-quarters of your leadership!

James allowed himself a small, triumphant grin. As long as the System did not specifically call him out for being the one using Soul Magic, he couldn’t even see any negatives to this. Even if he did end up having to accept the blame for what he had done, it was still a massive, important victory. He and his family were not out of the woods yet, of course.

But I still have the main part of the Victors Tournament itself. If I get the opportunity, I might be able to improve my position there, too.
