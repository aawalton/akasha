The newlyweds collapsed onto their suite's bed as Clay moved to the balcony to look over the city. While their day hadn’t been very physically straining outside of the morning before they took the Full Night’s Rest, it had been mentally taxing. The competition was fun, however.

While Adabelle hadn’t picked up on Ultimate Fighter Rushdown as well as she had hoped, Robot Laser Tag turned out to be her saving grace. She was a pro when it came to combat and was familiar with firearms thanks to Wayne. Once the demonkin got used to the controls, she was a force to be reckoned with and always ended up in the top three.

Trevor was especially happy because Boel turned out to be a good challenge when they returned to the fighting game. They went back and forth a lot, the demon seeming to soak in his opponent’s tactics like a sponge. He was great at adapting, and their bouts ended up being a ton of fun.

There had been other events, but they were overshadowed by the first two. Puzzles that had only been built with one solution in mind, electronic equivalents of air hockey and foosball, and a few carnival games. Adabelle had dominated those, too, but that was the perk of having high stats.

The demonkin’s tail snuck over Trevor’s leg and wrapped around it as she rested her head on his chest. She sighed contentedly. “I’m glad we came here. I think I understand you a little better, son of Ander.”

“Oh, yeah?” he asked, curious.

“It’s one thing to be told about the technology of your world, and another to actually see it,” Adabelle explained, draping an arm across his stomach. “There’s a clear vision in my head of devices that can produce the Quest Master’s text box, except that they can also display pictures. I can see that much. Then I’m told that those screens can show moving images that rival that of real life. Or perhaps exceed it, depending on the art style. That’s when it gets a little harder to process because producing moving pictures is something that takes a high level of magic proficiency, but almost everyone from Earth says they’re everywhere.”

“Usually at least one in every pocket where I came from, but not every part of the world was so reliant on technology like that,” Trevor said.

The noblewoman nodded. “Right. Video games were harder to imagine. Moving pictures that you could control directly, doing whatever it was you wanted them to do. The thought was appealing, but I didn’t realize just how much until I tried it today.”

“It was a lot of fun, wasn’t it?” he asked as he began idly stroking her hair.

“Yes, it was,” she confirmed. “I can see how you could lose yourself in them, especially the competitive aspect. Which I have to admit is a little strange.”

Trevor arched an eyebrow. “In what way? You’ve seen me compete in things before.”

“Yes, but not like this. You’ve always been too gentle to strike me as someone with a fierce competitive spirit, but I saw a side of you today that you’ve rarely shown,” Adabelle answered, trailing her fingers across his chest. “You were soft on the youngest Sammerson, but you only dialed back your abilities enough to give the others a fighting chance when they weren’t being rude.”

“I don’t particularly like dominating people when they’re not as skilled at games,” the courier said with a shrug. “Jedfry was just a kid and he wasn’t too bad. That first match with Hedry was fun, though. Seeing the shock on his face was super satisfying after I won. I’m glad he toned things down and became more friendly afterwards because it made the whole atmosphere better.”

Shifting, Trevor reached over and grabbed the remote for the bed. Their heads elevated at the touch of a button, moving to a comfortable spot. “Wouldn’t really call what I have a fierce competitive spirit. More of a, I dunno, mild training mindset? I guess? I remember going oh and two at tournaments, no wins but getting knocked out, and it was always so disheartening. Once I got good enough, I usually let myself lose one game in a best of three so the other player got to, you know, play more. So long as I wasn’t too at risk, of course.”

“Of course,” Adabelle giggled “Yes, and that’s where your gentleness comes in. You did give out a lot of advice after the challenge. But, no, I would definitely categorize it as a fierce competitive spirit.”

“Okay,” he replied, not really sure if he agreed.

She patted him on the chest. “It came out a bit against Stammy and Lilsian, but only a little. Boel was where you really started to shine.”

“Ah,” was all the courier could say. He nodded at that. Their sets had been great, with him pulling ahead by a few games. If it weren’t for the fact that there were so many people and only one system, he could have played against the demon for hours.

“Ah, he says,” Adabelle laughed. “The only time I’ve seen such an intense look on your face as you concentrated and played was… I’m struggling to think of one, actually. Perhaps it can only be matched by the indignation you feel whenever you think someone slighted me in particular.”

“I do get a little heated when that happens, don’t I?” he asked sheepishly.

“Yes, though I find it endearing,” she replied, smiling. “This was intense in a good way, though. Once someone of your general skill level showed up, you dropped that so-called training mindset and let loose. I recognized it right away, that’s why I let you take some of my turns for me. Stammy, too, gave up his chance to play to Boel if it meant he played you again.”

Trevor nodded as he thought back. That was exactly what had happened. He hadn’t noticed it at the time.

“Well, thanks,” he said, kissing the top of her head. “Dude just ate up the strategies I was sending his way, and I didn’t really get the chance to use them twice without mix ups. He would have thrived in the competitive scene on Earth, I think. It was a lot of fun to let loose and go full tilt, not just physically but mentally, as well.”

“I could tell. It was a good look on you, son of Ander.”

Trevor laughed. “Because of the confidence, right?”

“The confidence, the intensity, the way you nodded when they did something unexpected,” Adabelle listed, her hand moving upwards to stroke his neck and chin. “How your eyes lit up with every victory, both small and large, and how it was clear you were thinking of what you could do better in your next match. This was the side of you that I don’t get to see very often.”

“I didn’t realize you were paying that much attention to me,” he replied as she shifted onto her side.

“I’m always paying attention to you, Trevor. I can’t help it,” the demonkin whispered. Her tail untangled from his leg and reached up towards his hand, which was still stroking her hair, and wrapped around his wrist. “Especially when I get to see something like your fierce side.”

“You liked that, did you?”

“You already know the answer to that.”

Trevor smiled as his fingers left Adabelle's hair and began stroking her tail instead. The attention sent a shiver down her spine, especially as he got closer to her back. “Well, if you like my fierce side, I think I have a little more of that in me today. What do you say, be my partner one more time?”

Adabelle's answer was not one that needed words.

Trevor was performing his usual final checks as Lora and Adabelle chatted on the balcony. Clay laid across his shoulder, oozing somewhat, and enjoyed the proximity of their master. The earth elemental wanted some one on one time after both getting passed around so much yesterday and their absence while working on the engine before that.

Not that they had minded; it was just clear they had favorites and Trevor was at the top.

“All clear,” the courier announced as he left the bedroom. The doors from the suite’s main room to the balcony were open, and he walked out to where the ladies were having their conversation. He wrapped his arms around Adabelle’s waist from behind and rested his chin on her shoulder. “We’re all good to go.”

“Glad to hear it,” she replied with a smile, patting his hand gently.

“Only thing we have left is to get the goods that are going to your next destination and beyond, and that’s just downstairs,” Lora explained. “Then, we can be on our way! I’d say buckle up, buttercup, but unfortunately we’re not going anywhere else with cars.”

“That’s fine. It was fun while it lasted,” he replied, reluctantly pulling away from the demonkin. “Next time you see Klar, tell him that we’re really happy with this honeymoon so far. It’s been perfect, from the privacy of the Wildlands all the way to now.”

Adabelle nodded. “Yes. I hadn’t thought I’d see the wonders of Aletander for myself given how long it would normally take to get here, but the inventions they’ve made and the magical technology passed down from Sammie the Gamer was a great joy to see.”

“He’ll be thrilled to hear it,” Lora said with a grin before heading inside. “Lady Griffin, too. While the boss man was up for sending you two just about anywhere he’d thought was cool and fun, she tempered this trip into something very special for the both of you. Well, for you at least, Lady Adabelle; the boss had a pretty good idea of what Trevor liked.”

“I’ve got no complaints, he nailed it,” the courier chuckled. He scanned the area one more time as the women left the suite, and then nodded to himself once he was satisfied. “So, Lora.”

“So, Trevor,” she replied expectantly as they entered the stairwell.

“Little pixie, huh?”

“Ugh! I want to be mad at him so bad, but he did win fair and square, so I’m merely slightly livid instead,” the teleporter joked, throwing her hands in the air as if she were really upset. “Boel and his big mouth. He just can’t help but tease me.”

“It seems as though he enjoys your company,” Adabelle stated, betraying nothing of what Stammy told them.

“Of course he does, I’m awesome,” Lora replied, turning around to grin even though she was heading down the stairs. She didn’t so much as stumble before she jumped down to the next landing and resumed her descent normally. “But, yes. I was a pixie back in my original life, if that’s what you wanted to know.”

“Yup. I was interested in that,” Trevor confirmed as he trailed behind his wife. “I’ll be honest, neither of us thought you were human, what with the naturally colorful hair and your pupils having stars in them.”

“I’m a little customized, yes,” the teleporter giggled. “The Quest Master was gracious enough to grant me some requests on the way here after I got my bearings enough to know what I wanted.”

“Rashie was like that, too,” he said, remembering one of his first conversations with the shark girl. “Customized, I mean. Some of us weren’t so lucky.”

“Oh, no?” Lora asked as she led the way out of the stairwell.

“Trevor here didn’t realize it was an offer,” Adabelle said, her tone teasing. “When the Quest Master asked about race, he answered human like it was natural.”

“It’s true, I did,” he agreed with a chuckle.

“Well, I came to grips with things a little quicker than that. Sorry though, Trevor.”

“No worries. I’m pretty happy with how I turned out.”

“As well you should be,” Lora said with a nod. She pulled a key out of her pocket as they approached the room they had used as storage before. “I’d always loved humans. Me and my sister both. We’d leave Avalon and explore the outside world as much as possible. Sneaking into movie theaters was one of our favorite pastimes, and we spent hours watching whatever new flick came out.”

“Avalon, yeah, that does sound familiar. You’re right, that would have given me a big clue to work things out with,” Trevor replied. Adabelle gave him a questioning look as the teleporter unlocked the door. “I don’t really remember a lot about it, but there’s a legend about a king named Arthur in England, which is near where Lailah came from. Avalon is some magical realm, or island? Or something. I didn’t actually know that there were fae there, but I probably could have made the leap.”

“Yup! My Earth was a place where all that was real,” the slender woman chirped. “Magic was mostly gone after the Arthurian age though, sad to say. We pixies as well as a few others managed to stick around thanks to people’s belief that we were real, which allowed us to see some really neat stuff. Aletander is a bit like a homecoming for that reason. Was it the same for you?”

“Yeah. I wouldn’t give Tosa up for anything, but this was a refreshing destination,” he responded as she opened the door. The courier was first to enter this time, and he immediately began pulling the crates into his inventory.

[[New Quests! Here we go again.

You know what we’re doing here. Take some crates, put them somewhere else, do it all over again and piggyback off of your previous quests. An efficient system, this, but I’m still allowing it because I can’t get you anything physical as a wedding gift.

Anyway, you know the drill. This cluster has five components. Lora will give you the details when you need them.]]

“Five components this time,” Trevor stated as he dismissed the text box and kept working on the crates. “It feels like we’re counting down.”

“That’s exactly what it feels like, doesn’t it?” Lora laughed. She put her hands on her hips as she watched him work, fingers idly tugging at the vibrant green ribbon she wore on her belt. “Now, I do have a warning for you two.”

“What kind of warning?” Adabelle asked.

“This next teleport might be a little, uh, rough.”

The noblewoman gave her a patient look. “Lora, I don’t think either of us knows what that means.”

“I have the destination, it’s just going to be a little bit in-motion, is all,” the teleporter said before holding up a hand, then letting it descend about a foot. “There will be a slight fall. Due to circumstances.”

“Circumstances?” she asked, arching an eyebrow.

“Circumstances,” Lora repeated, not elaborating. “But, we’ll be fine. We’re Summoned! A short fall won’t hurt us.”

“And we’ve got these Feathers of the Graceful,” Trevor said, tapping the magical token affixed to his chest. It didn’t activate when touched because he wasn’t airborne. “We’ve only really had a chance to use them from a few feet off the ground, but we’ve been looking for a reason.”

Lora took a moment to learn what they did as the courier took in the rest of the cargo, and she nodded. “Okay, not quite that big of a fall, but you will get a chance to use them at your next destination.” She cringed slightly. “Well, I mean, your next next destination. Promise!”

“Alright. Well, it should be fine regardless,” he replied with a shrug. Trevor wasn’t sure why she made that face, but the once-pixie was fairly eccentric so he didn’t think much of it. Clay jumped into his arms and their husk went into his inventory, leaving a black sphere in his hand. “I’m all set.”

“As am I,” Adabelle said as she looked out of the window at the city below and the mountains in the distance. “I’ll miss this place, but I think I’m going to be saying that after everywhere we visit.”

“So long as the boss and Lady Griffin did their job, I’d say that’s accurate,” Lora agreed. She offered them her hands. “Anyhoo, let’s get going.”

The teleporter scrunched up her face in concentration. It took her a few seconds longer than usual to activate her Power, enough that Adabelle started to show concern, but it worked before she could voice it.
