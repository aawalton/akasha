The square erupted into motion.

Bella moved like lightning. She had Jane loading keln onto one section of the griddle while she worked the other sections, laying eggs down in perfect circles, browning strips of meat and setting vegetables to sizzle. The heat coming off the griddle was intense. Jane was sweating like a pig before she knew it.

"Flip those," Bella called, gesturing at the keln. "Then start assembling. Keln on the bottom, then eggs, vegetables, meat, cheese. Roll it tight. Stack them on the warm section."

Jane did as she was told, working as fast as she could. The process was repetitive, but required attention. She found a rhythm quickly. Soon, she had a growing stack of completed wraps on the edge of the griddle where they would stay warm without overcooking.

Bella was managing three different processes at once. Jane had no idea how she was keeping track of it all. Eggs were going down on one section, vegetables on another, meat on a third. The breakfast cook was constantly moving, flipping, and checking temperatures with a practiced eye.

"Keep it moving, Jane. We need at least a hundred of these before the crowd hits."

Jane kept working, but she couldn't help glancing at the other stations. Most of them seemed to be managing well enough, but one cook in particular was clearly struggling. He was attempting to make individual omelettes, each one filled with multiple ingredients and folded with perfect precision. It was beautiful work, but he was falling behind.

"Bella." Jane pointed. “That guy is drowning."

Bella looked where Jane was indicating and nodded. "Go help him. I've got this for now."

"You sure?"

"Positive. Go."

Jane wiped her hands on her apron and hurried over to the struggling cook. He was maybe in his thirties. Despite the relatively cool morning, sweat was already dripping down his face.

"Need a hand?" Jane asked.

"God, yes." He gestured at his setup. "I can make perfect omelettes. I just can't make them fast enough."

"Show me what you're doing."

He walked her through his process. Each omelette required perfectly beaten eggs, carefully placed fillings, a precise folding technique, and exact timing. It was impressive work. Jane identified a few places in the process where she couldn’t fail to help, namely in prepping ingredients, and applied herself to them.

Her pressure on the process was just enough to tip the scales. Soon, the prepped ingredients filled bowls to the brim, and the cook had a healthy backlog of completed omelettes keeping warm on his griddle.

"I've got it from here," he said. "Thank you."

Jane moved to the next station in trouble, then the next. One cook had underestimated how much oil she needed and was about to run out. Jane spotted the problem and ran to grab extra from one of the supply stations set up at the edge of the square. Each new cook she approached had similar problems, though just unique enough to keep Jane’s mind active.

The other cooks were grateful for the help, and Jane found herself enjoying the challenge of solving their various crises. Soon enough, there were no more to solve. The entire line was functioning as it should.

When she finally made it back to Bella's station, she saw that her friend had pulled significantly ahead. There was a massive stack of wraps ready to go, and Bella was working at a steady, sustainable pace rather than the frantic sprint from earlier.

"Welcome back," Bella said without looking up from the griddle. "The royal family already ate, by the way."

Jane blinked. "They did?"

"About ten minutes ago. They were very nice about it. The queen asked where you were, and I told her you were helping the other cooks. She seemed to like that."

"I missed it entirely."

"You were busy being useful." Bella flipped a set of eggs. "Now get back to work. The crowd's about to hit."

She was right. The royal family had finished their circuit, and now the general population was approaching without fear or reluctance. Suddenly, every cook was working at maximum capacity.

Jane fell back into her rhythm at Bella's station, assembling wraps as fast as Bella could cook the components. The work was repetitive and exhausting, but every wrap they completed meant another person fed. It was a good feeling. Jane saw what Bella liked about it.

Other stations were managing as well, though Jane noticed several had run out of ingredients and closed early. The omelette cook she had helped was doing much better with time management now. He caught her eye at one point and nodded his thanks.

Finally, mercifully, the crowds began to thin. People had eaten their fill, and the available food was running low. Bella cooked the last of her ingredients and distributed the final wraps, then stepped back from the griddle.

"That's it. We're done."

"We did it,” Jane said, barely able to believe it.

"We did." Bella wiped sweat from her forehead with the back of her hand. "Now I just have to wait to find out if it was good enough."

They didn't have to wait long. Xand was already climbing onto the platform, amplification device in hand. The crowd quieted as he prepared to speak.

"Citizens of Glenfall, the royal family has made their selections for the breakfast cook showcase. It is my pleasure to announce the winners."

Jane felt Bella tense beside her.

"In first place, for her exceptional keln wraps and impressive volume of service, Bella."

Jane screamed. She didn't mean to. It just came out. Then she was hugging Bella, squeezing so hard that Jane was worried she would hurt her friend.

"You won," she said into Bella's shoulder. "You won first place."

Bella stood frozen for a moment, then seemed to process what had happened. "I won."

"First place. You beat everyone."

The crowd was cheering. Xand was announcing second and third place, but Jane barely heard the names. She was too busy celebrating with Bella, who looked absolutely shellshocked.

"I won," Bella said again, as if trying to convince herself it was real.

"You did. You're the best breakfast cook in Glenfall. Officially."

Bella's expression slowly shifted from shock to something else. Something that looked almost like panic.

"Oh no."

Jane pulled back from the hug. "What? What's wrong?"

"The shop." Bella's voice was faint. "Jane, the shop is already so busy. I'm at capacity most mornings. This is going to make it so much worse."

"That's good, isn't it? More business?"

"More than I can handle. I'm going to be absolutely slammed for days. Maybe weeks. I'll have to hire help. I'll have to expand. I'll have to..." She trailed off, looking genuinely overwhelmed. "I wasn't ready for this."

Jane squeezed her friend's hand. "You'll figure it out. You always do."

"I don't know about this time." Despite her worry, though, Bella was starting to smile. "But I guess I'll have to, won't I?"

"You will. And you'll do great."

Bella looked at her cooling army griddle, at the empty supply crates, at the certificate Xand had pressed into her hands at some point during the celebration.

"I won," she said one more time. And this time, she sounded like she believed it. "I actually won."

"You did. Now let's get this stuff cleaned up and back to your shop before you have a complete breakdown."

"Too late for that. But yes, let's go."

They began the process of packing up Bella's station. Jane found herself smiling as she worked. Her friend had won. First place, beating out experienced cooks who had been doing this for decades. It was exactly what Bella deserved. Jane couldn't have been happier for her, even if it did mean Bella's breakfast shop was about to become the busiest place in Glenfall.

Once everything was loaded onto a Dave-supplied cart, Jane heard a gentle cough from above and behind her. She turned to see the king there, standing tall and looking very, very full.

“Jane, could I have a word, before you leave? I believe there’s an open room in the meeting hall behind us, if you don’t mind walking with me.”

Jane took a subtle but deep breath. It had been a long morning already. Adding a private conversation with royalty felt like a lot. It felt like an especially large amount if it meant what she thought it meant, and the conversation was going to stray to princely concerns.

There was no way out of it, though.

“Of course.” She hugged Bella one last time and tried to answer her friend’s questioning glance with a reassuring one. “I’ll be right along.”

The king nodded and walked ahead of her as she followed him into the meeting hall. The big table was ridiculously oversized for the two of them, but the king pulled out a chair for her and took one just two seats away, functionally carving down the conversation space to several feet.

“I’m sorry to pull you in like this. I know you must be tired.”

“A bit.” Jane shifted her feet beneath her. “But it’s fine. What’s on your mind?”

“Oh, lots of things. But relevant to you: my son. I have to admit something. When I found you were otherwise involved with another young man, I found it hard to accept. Here was a young lady who was perfect for my son, making what I considered to be a mistake.”

Jane bristled. It must have been obvious, as the king noticed immediately.

“Now, now. I’ve changed, I promise. Not least of all because I made the tremendous mistake of contacting your aunt to see how best to remedy what I saw as a problem.”

“Oh, no.” Jane almost felt bad for him. “She must not have taken that well.”

“She said, and I quote, that I should keep my meddling to matters of state.” The king reddened slightly, as if remembering more words than he was sharing. “She also threatened to turn me into a toad.”

“She can’t do that, you know.”

He grimaced. “I know. But there are a great many things she could do, and she was merely using the toad thing to politely avoid mentioning them. Suffice it to say, she impressed upon me that a king can’t have everything he wants, and neither can a father. I brought you here to apologize, Jane.”

“Ah.” Jane did not know how to take an apology from a king. She decided to move through it as quickly as possible. “Accepted, then. No further words needed.”

The king seemed as relieved to avoid making more elaborate apologies as Jane was to avoid hearing them.

“Good.”

“If I can ask, why did it ever seem like a good idea?” Jane leaned forward a bit. Though she didn’t want more apologies, she did feel like she was owed that much explanation. “Brand’s a good man, but there was never any reason to think we would hit it off.”

“Oh. I suppose I’m just starting to think about that.”

The king pondered for a while. Jane gave him plenty of time.

Finally, he sighed. “I suppose it’s that Brand seems to have… not lost his way, but never to have found it. He doesn’t want to be king, you know.”

Jane hadn’t known. She wondered how she could have.

“He rejected the throne?”

“Yes, but it’s not as big an affair as it sounds. It’s happened several times throughout our nation’s history. The real disasters were those times it should have happened and didn’t. I can accept Brand’s decision, but I am uneasy about his future, nonetheless. He hardly knows what he wants to do.”

“He’s a fine mage.”

“Through tutoring. But he’s never loved it.” The king stood with another sigh. “I supposed I thought a family of his own would help move things along. To the extent I thought at all, that is. Which was never very much. Again, Jane, I apologize for overstepping my bounds. I’m sure it wasn’t pleasant to hear the rumors of it. I owe you a favor, at whatever time and in whatever form that might take.”

Jane stood and offered the king a small curtsy, which felt appropriate.

"Thank you for telling me all of this," she said. "And for the apology. I know that’s not easy."

"Easier than my conversation with your aunt, I promise you." The king smiled, wry and tinged with lingering regret about provoking the world’s strongest aunt. "Go on, then. Enjoy the rest of your day. You've earned it."
