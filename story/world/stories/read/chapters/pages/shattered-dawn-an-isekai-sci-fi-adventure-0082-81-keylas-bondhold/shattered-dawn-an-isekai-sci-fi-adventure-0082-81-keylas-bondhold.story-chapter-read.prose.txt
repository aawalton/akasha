A burning light glowed near the front gate of New Kairn Tol. Black smoke boiled into the night sky, blocking out twinkling stars refracted through shards of the Celestial Sphere. Elion watched the flames for a moment, lights flashing in the night as guards ran to fight against the attack.

“They’re making a move tonight,” Hol said. “The chaos will help us.”

Elion nodded, then continued, leading his squad down the road toward the warlock camp.

“We need speed,” Elion said. “Get in, get out fast, before they know what’s happening.” He scanned his little squad. They had four rifles, including his and Yaret’s, and a shotgun. The other members of the group held melee weapons of some type.

“Here,” Elion said, handing his rifle to Ryfer. “I’m going to be able to tank hits better than anyone else here. I’ll take your sledge.”

Ryfer shrugged, accepting the rifle and handing Elion his sledge hammer. The long shafted tool was heavier than Elion expected. He leaned it over his shoulder.

“Four rifles,” Elion said, “I need you spread out along the perimeter. Pick off any warlocks who challenge us.”

“I’m going with you,” Yaret said. “That’s my job.”

“But you have a rifle,” Elion protested. He pointed to a few potential locations, a window in a ruined building, an outcropping of rocks. “We need people covering us from vantage points like those.”

“I’m going with you,” Yaret said again. He handed his rifle to a nearby woman, exchanging it for a long metal rake that she carried. “Now I don’t have a rifle.”

“Fine,” Elion said. “First priority is to find my knife. Then we’ll look for whoever has Keyla’s bondhold. Hol, you’re in charge of the rifles. Go set up.”

“Got it,” Hol said, taking the other three riflemen with him; Ryfer, Remy, and the woman with Yaret’s gun.

Elion scanned the six people with him. Dirty, worn down, and brimming with enthusiasm for the fight. They wanted to hit back at the people who’d done this to them. “We’ll sneak in close, and find a defensive position. Then I’ll slip into the camp.”

They crept through the darkness, staying off the road. An arachnatronic full of warlocks scrambled past, heading toward the fighting at the main gate.

They reached the edge of the warlock’s camp.

What had been a bustling scene earlier in the night had now quieted. A few figures moved in the darkness between the black tents, but most of the warlocks in the camp seemed to be out where the fighting was happening.

Elion crouched behind a ridge of earth that might have once been part of a foundation of a skyscraper, watching.

Behind them, a path ran along the edge of the acid pit toward the refinery, and then to the steps leading down into the pit. Elion scanned this first, but couldn’t see down into the pit. He hoped Keyla was still down there, just trying to do her job fixing pumps.

Tael could be a bit hotheaded, but he had gone with her. Hopefully that was a good thing.

Elion turned his attention back to the camp. He spotted Dave, the man who seemed to be in charge of the sword capturing machine. The man cursed and kicked one leg of a crouching arachnatronic, then cursed again, holding his toe.

The spider-mech had taken more damage during Elion’s attack than Elion had realized. Dave was trying to fix one of the legs. It twitched and spasmed randomly.

The sword machine still sat in the cargo area of the arachnatronic.

A single warlock stood sentry near the main road. Elion mapped out a path that would lead him around to where Dave worked, keeping him out of sight of the sentry.

Could it be that easy? The warlocks couldn’t be expecting an attack on their camp. They had sent most of their forces to go defend the gate.

Dave didn’t seem like the type who’d put up much of a fight. He’d probably help Elion get his sword back, if Elion promised not to hurt him. That line of thinking made Elion feel like a bully, but then again, the man had stolen his weapon.

Elion looked for Hol and the other riflemen. He saw a movement of shadows in one of the windows he’d pointed out, but couldn’t spot the other team. *We should have put together a better way to signal each other.*

Too late now. Elion gathered his team and whispered his plan to them. “Watch this path,” he gestured to the track leading to the refinery. “I’m going to sneak in and out. If someone comes this way, stop them.”

They crouched in the shadows, wielding their weapons. Elion slipped into the camp, Yaret on his heels.

*What if I can’t deactivate the machine? I wish Keyla were here; she’d be able to figure it out. Maybe even glitch it out with her ability.*

Staying low to the ground and creeping from shadow to shadow, Elion and Yaret moved across the warlocks’ camp. The camp was silent, only interrupted by sounds of fighting in the distance at the main gate.

A volley of rockets fired from one of the battle arachnatronics cast a harsh red glare over the camp. The ground rumbled as the explosives detonated.

They reached the clearing where Dave worked.

He grumbled, coughing as he spoke to himself. “I can’t believe—*cough cough*—stupid warlocks, unprotected—*cough*—they should have known…”

Dave had totally removed the damaged leg from the Arachnatronic. The sword machine’s control panel glowed softly in the darkness, a dial with a needle. The dial currently pointed straight at Elion.

Hammering angrily on the arachnatronic leg, Dave cursed again. “No more spare parts, eh? Get your machine working you stupid hack… You think I’m a stupid hack, do you? You’re the one who let him smash the skyskimmer up, not me. Without me, you’d have no idea where he was!” His rant was cut off by a fit of coughing.

Elion hefted the sledge hammer in his hands. A single blow on the back of the man’s head would probably kill him. *Feels wrong to kill a defenseless man, though.*

And what if he needed Dave’s help to deactivate the machine?

Elion carefully set the sledge down on the ground, and left cover, creeping forward behind Dave.

Dave continued working, totally oblivious.

When he was within arms reach, Dave whirled around, turning to face Elion. His eyes widened, and he tumbled backwards off his stool. Elion dived forward, grabbing the man as he fell on top of him.

Clapping a hand over Dave’s mouth, Elion pinned the mechanic to the ground. Dave reached for a piece of arachnatronic, trying to swing it around and strike Elion in the head.

Elion blocked the blow, knocking the arachnatronic leg to the ground. Dave whimpered.

“I need my knife back,” Elion hissed.

Dave grunted, eyes flicking about as he evaluated his options.

“I won’t hurt you if you deactivate that machine,” Elion said.

Dave nodded slightly, as best he could while pinned to the ground.

“Okay,” Elion said. “I’m going to let you go now. Don’t make a sound.”

He carefully released Dave’s mouth, and Dave started coughing again. “Horrible acid pit,” Dave grumbled. “It’s still burning.”

“Get up.” Elion stood, pulling Dave to his feet. “Turn off the machine. I want my knife back.”

Dave shuffled over to the machine, working his jaw and rubbing his arm where Elion had struck him. “Didn’t have to hit me that hard, you know.”

He reached the machine and examined the instrument panel. “Oh, this is what I need!” he exclaimed. “Would you move a bit to the right?”

“Are you deactivating it?” Elion asked.

“Yes,” Dave said. “Hurry, please.”

Elion shuffled a bit to the side. The needle on the panel rotated slightly, pointing directly at him.

“A little to the left now?” Dave asked.

Elion did as requested, and the needle continued pointing at him.

“A small jump perhaps?”

“How is that going to help you deactivate that machine?” Elion growled.

“It’s not,” Dave said. “I just want to check one thing and then I’ll turn it off. It’s so hard to calibrate stuff like this when you don’t have someone you can easily test it on.”

Elion sighed and gave a little hop.

“You’re kidding me!” Dave exclaimed. “I can’t believe it. It went down!”

“Just turn it off already,” Elion said, looking around uncomfortably.

“I’ve got the inputs inverted somewhere! No wonder the elevation setting was giving such weird results. Argh, I can’t believe that I overlooked something so simple!”

Yaret moved out of his hiding place and handed Elion his sledge hammer. “I say we just smash it,” he said.

“No!” Dave whirled on them, protecting the machine with his body. “Please, don’t hurt it. I’ve worked so hard to make this!”

“Are you going to turn it off?”

“I’ll be in so much trouble if I do that,” Dave whimpered, wringing his hands. “She’ll kill me.”

“Okay then we’re going to smash it,” Elion said, pushing forward.

Shouts rang out from the edge of the camp. A flare of purple light fountained into the air nearby, lighting up the area where Elion had left his team.

Dave screeched.
