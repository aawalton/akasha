I still felt like I was in a state of shock as Mom pulled onto the drive of our house. It felt too good to be true. There was no way we could just adopt Rex and take him home like that, and I could feel myself waiting for something to go wrong. It seemed so inevitable, and I’d spent the entire journey glancing at my phone. I was expecting the animal centre to call me and say there had been a mistake, and Rex wasn’t actually ours. I was sure they’d insist that we return him immediately, but no call came.

We’d filled out the paperwork for him, though. Mom had paid the fee to adopt him, so he should have been ours, but I was still scared. I had just wanted him for so long; it felt like my whole life had been spent waiting to find him, and the fact that I finally had seemed wrong. It had to be.

My vision spun again as I reached out for the dizzinesses floating at the back of my mind, searching to make sure I hadn’t accidentally slipped into another world without realising. That would be one explanation for why things were going so well. I could have gone into some other place where everything was different, and Mom was nicer to me, but I couldn’t feel my reality there. A lot of the worlds felt somewhat familiar, but they weren’t my home.

A snuffling noise broke through my concentration, and I blinked quickly, looking around and quickly spotting Rex’s face. It was so close to my own, and his nose twitched as he sniffed, blowing the hair away from my cheek.

“Good boy,” I muttered, stroking him carefully.

His back was mostly healed, but the cuts were still visible around the tufty black fur that had been shaved so he could be stitched up. The vet had said he didn’t seem to be in any pain, but I wanted to avoid going anywhere near his injuries, just in case.

“Alright,” Mom said, interrupting my thoughts. “I’ll bring all of the things we bought for Rex in, so take him into the garden for now. I’ll have the front door open whilst I’m unloading the car, so make sure you keep him on the lead until I say to release him, okay?”

“Okay,” I replied.

Mom immediately climbed out of the car, and I unbuckled my seat belt before turning towards Rex. He stared at me as I reached for the clip that connected him to the seat belt, but the angle was awkward, and I fumbled with it a couple of times before I managed to get it open.

“Oh crap,” I murmured as I grabbed his lead from the space between us. “Wait a second, Rex.”

I’d meant to clip it on before detaching him from the seat belt, just in case he’d gotten excited or tried to run before I could, but I’d completely forgotten that I’d even taken it off in the first place. I hadn’t at first. There was enough space on the loop on his harness for both his lead and the clip for the car seat to be hooked on, but it had looked so uncomfortable for him. I couldn’t find a way to rest his lead without it pressing against his fur, and that couldn’t have been comfortable.

The boot opened behind me as I clipped the lead on and stroked him one last time before getting out of the car. My hands gripped the plastic handle tightly as Rex jumped down onto the gravel, painfully aware of how close we were to the road. Even though Lily had told us he didn’t seem to have any problems with going on walks and never really pulled at the lead, with the exception of when she first collected him to come see us, I was terrified that he’d make a run for it, and I wouldn’t be able to pull him back in time.

I shouldn’t have been, though. We weren’t that close to the road, and Rex had barely even glanced in that direction. He just looked around briefly, seemingly to survey the world around him, before starting to walk towards the door, and I hurried after him.

“Careful,” Mom snapped.

My head turned quickly, and I saw Mom moving towards us, carrying a giant bag of dog food.

“Here, Rex,” I said, tugging the lead slightly to pull Rex out of the way.

Mom dropped the bag heavily by the front door before reaching into her handbag for the keys. It would have made so much more sense for her to unlock the door first before getting anything out of the car, but I resisted the urge to point that out. There was no need, and it would just annoy Mom.

“Did you not bring your keys?” she asked, her tone snappy as she withdrew the keys from her bag.

My eyebrows twitched together as I racked my brain, trying to recall whether I’d grabbed them before we left. I wasn’t sure, though. I’d been too distracted by thoughts of the dog, who was standing in front of me and sniffing the bag of dog food intently.

“I did,” I replied.

Mom glanced at me, her expression irritated.

“Then why were you waiting for me? You’re capable of doing it yourself, aren’t you?”

“They’re in my bag,” I said hesitantly. “And I… left it in the car. I’ll grab it now.”

A loud sigh came from Mom as she bent down to pick up the sack again with a small grunt.

“Don’t bother,” she told me. “I’ll get it for you.”

I couldn’t help but shoot her a surprised look as she strode into the house ahead of me.

“Are you sure?” I asked, hovering in the doorway. “I can—”

“Just take Rex out into the garden,” she said, cutting me off as she dropped the bag in the hallway and turned towards me again. “You’re in the way.”

Hesitating, I scanned her face quickly. I wasn’t sure what to do. Even though she’d said she’d get my bag for me, I was worried that it would annoy her, but I didn’t want to get in her way either. That would probably be worse than her having to grab my backpack out of the back seat. It would make her even angrier.

“Sorry,” I apologised quickly as I rushed into the house and edged around her, Rex following closely behind me. “Thanks.”

“It’s fine,” Mom said with another heavy sigh.

She marched through the door again without even looking back at me, and I watched her for a few seconds, still debating what to do. Following her instructions felt safer, I decided. If I went to grab my bag after she told me it was fine, she might get angry or suspicious. Either she’d think I was ignoring her or she’d want to know why I didn’t want to leave her alone with my bag. She might assume I was hiding something in there.

I wasn’t, though. My bag was basically empty. It just held my purse and some other random bits that had been floating around at the bottom for a while, but at least I’d been smart enough to slip my phone into my pocket whilst at the animal centre earlier. I wouldn’t have minded Mom searching my bag, but I didn’t want her to look through it.

There wasn’t anything too bad on my phone. I was cautious to delete all of my texts with Duncan and any messages to Phoebe about him, but I still didn’t want Mom looking through them. It felt invasive, but there was no way I could refuse. She wouldn’t accept that.

“Okay, boy,” I said to Rex quietly, making my decision. “Shall I show you the garden then?”

Rex looked up at me, his tail wagging excitedly, and a smile pulled at my lips.

“It’s just through here,” I told him as we started to walk along the hall.

I wasn’t sure why I was talking to him. Rex couldn’t exactly reply to me, and I wasn’t sure if he even understood me, but it felt weird to just lead him into the house without saying anything.

Rex sniffed constantly as we moved, his nose staying close to the carpet, but he glanced up often, peering around the house. I watched him closely, trying to work out what he was feeling, but I had no clue. I had no real experience with dogs, but over the past week, I’d watched what felt like countless videos and clips about dogs. There was so much information online, and a bunch of the videos seemed to contradict other ones I’d watched, so it was hard to know what was true.

I’d done some research in the other world too, though. I didn’t have any classes on animal psychology at the Academy; they came later, but there were books on animal behaviour in the library, and I’d read a couple.

That had confused Seth. I could tell he’d noticed that I’d suddenly taken an interest in animals, specifically dogs. It would have been hard to miss, and he’d asked a couple of questions about it, but I think I covered it pretty well.

One of the field agents had run into a guard dog whilst on assignment the other day. I’d been in the GSOD at the time, and that was the first thing I thought of when Seth had asked me about it. I’d pretended the analyst had been really good, so knowledgeable of how to deal with the dog, but I knew nothing. I told him that I wanted to learn more so I’d be able to help out if I were ever in that situation, and luckily, he had seemed to accept that.

“Wait a second, Rex,” I said to Rex as we stopped by the French windows, and I reached for the key. “Just gotta unlock it, and…”

Rex stayed close to me as I slid the door open and stepped through. His sniffing grew even louder, and I glanced down at him nervously. The last time he’d been in our garden, it hadn’t gone well for him. He’d been hurt, and I was worried he could remember that experience, that he was scared of it because of that.

His posture hadn’t changed too much, though. His ears were a little perked, but they had been ever since we picked him up from the animal rescue, so it seemed more like he was curious about where we were rather than afraid or anxious. Still, the urge to reassure him pulled at me.

“It’s okay, Rex,” I muttered in the same low, soothing tone that I’d been using for a while. “It’s just the garden, and nothing here can hurt you. Me and Dad fixed the fence, so you won’t be able to get under it again, and it won’t cut you. I promise.”

Rex stared up at me, his expression unreadable. His tail was still wagging, though. It hadn’t stopped since he got out of the car, and that had to be a good sign. Dogs normally wagged their tails when they were happy, didn’t they?

Although… one of the videos I’d watched had said it wasn’t necessarily a sign the dog was excited. Apparently, sometimes, it could indicate that they were nervous or scared. I was sure they said it was something to do with the speed, but I couldn’t remember whether they said faster was better or if that was a sign of anxiety?

Was Rex’s tail moving fast? What counted as fast? Concern grew within me as I watched Rex quickly, but I couldn’t work it out. I’d seen too many videos. They were all blurring into one, and I couldn’t recall exactly what had been shown in the one I was thinking of.

My hand twitched towards my pocket as the urge to pull out my phone and hunt down the video again rose within me, but I froze before I reached it. It would probably help my anxiety to know for sure, but, at the same time, it didn’t feel necessary. Rex appeared fine. He didn’t look nervous.

In fact, he was still standing beside me and staring up at me. It seemed almost like he was waiting for me to tell him what to do, and I hesitated before looking around the garden again, checking one last time to make sure that there was nothing that could hurt him there.

“It’s okay, Rex,” I said before pushing a button on the retractable lead to remove the lock. “Why don’t you… explore a bit, huh? Have a sniff around?”

Rex didn’t move. I wasn’t sure whether he didn’t understand what I’d said, which would have made sense, or if he was waiting for something else, some other sign that it was okay to go explore. Lily had said that he’d want to. She’d said it was normal for him to be a bit nervous, but that he’d probably spend the next few days, if not longer, sniffing everything and probably peeing all over the garden. That was how dogs normally reacted to new places, but Rex hadn’t left my side.

It probably didn’t help that I was so close to the door, I realised. Maybe he thought I would wait until he’d walked away a bit, and then I’d run back inside and lock him in the garden. Perhaps his old owner used to do that to him all the time, but I would never. Plus, he was still on the lead. I wouldn’t be able to leave him out there unless I let go of the lead or unclipped him, but he might not have realised that.

“It’s okay, boy,” I repeated, taking a few steps away from the door. “I’m staying out here with you. You’ve got to stay on the lead for now, and I know it’s probably kind of boring, but we’ll go out for a walk soon, yeah? Once Mom is finished unpacking the car, I’ll take you out for a walk.”

Rex’s ears seemed to perk up even more at that, and he cocked his head to the side, staring at me intently. A giggle slipped out of my mouth.

“Oh, you liked the sound of that, huh? Well, you’ve got to wait just a little longer, and then we can go out, but go on. Have a look around, okay?” I said, gesturing towards the rest of the garden in the hopes that Rex would understand that.

He continued to look at me for a moment longer before slowly starting to walk away from me, his nose buried in the ground. He seemed to follow the scent of something on a winding path towards the back fence before lifting his head and gazing through the gaps, and I couldn’t help but wonder what it was that he’d smelt.

I wished there was a way for me to communicate with him and to understand what he was saying to me. It was a childish wish, and I knew that. Only young kids wished they could talk to animals, but it would have been so interesting, and I longed to know what Rex was thinking.

My phone buzzed, distracting me from my thoughts, and I looked at Rex before peering over my shoulder to check whether I could see Mom through the windows behind me. She wasn’t there, though, and I glanced at Rex again before reaching for my phone.

Hey! How’d the appointment go??

A smile pulled at my lips as I read the message from Duncan. I’d managed to sneak away from Mom when we were shopping earlier to text him, and I’d told him about Rex. I’d already mentioned him when I’d seen Duncan a few days ago, and I’d said I’d let him know what happened, but I hadn’t had a chance to whilst we were at the animal centre, and I was too nervous on the drive home. My attention had been fixed on Rex the whole time.

Glancing at the window again, I lifted my phone and took a photo of Rex as he wandered around the garden. I sent it to Duncan quickly before also sending it to Phoebe and Dad. I’d meant to call Dad earlier to let him know about Rex too. He’d asked me to keep him updated and tell him as soon as I heard anything, but I’d been too nervous to do it before we went to get Rex. I didn’t want to get his hopes up in case anything went wrong during the appointment.

He’s here, my dad replied, the text coming through almost immediately. Oh, look at him.

The absolute lack of excitement in his texts made me smile. I knew he didn’t mean it to sound so unenthusiastic. He just never really used exclamation marks or anything in his messages, but I could tell he was happy.

I know! He’s so cute, isn’t he? I replied.

Yes. Has he been okay with the fence? Hasn’t tried to get under it again? Dad asked.

No, no, he seems okay! I sent back as a message from Duncan appeared at the top of my screen.

Rex!!! Duncan wrote back. I assume the appointment went well then?

Yeah, they let us adopt him!! I told him.

He’d probably assumed that, I realised the moment I sent the message. Otherwise, why would Rex be in my garden? My thumb hovered over the screen as I debated editing the text, but the three dots, showing that Duncan was typing, appeared before I could.

I’m so glad. I can’t wait to meet him! Duncan had said.

My eyes darted towards the window behind me to check that Mom wasn’t there before finding Rex again. I bit my lip, trying to hold back the grin that had appeared as I replied.

Well, I’m going to take him out for a walk in a few minutes, I think. You could always come with, if you’re not busy? I typed, hesitating before hitting send.

It felt risky. We’d only hung out at Duncan’s house for the last week or so, and too many people could see us if we went out for a walk together. Mom or people from our school could find out, and that would be difficult. I didn’t want Mom to know there was something going on between Duncan and me, and we hadn’t discussed what we were yet, so I didn’t want rumours to start just yet, but I wanted to see him. Even though it had only been a couple of days since I’d seen him last, I missed him.

That sounds great! I’ll throw some clothes on now. Let me know when you’re almost here, and I’ll come out and meet you? his text read.

Sure! I replied.

I looked up at Rex again, watching him as he sniffed the square of mud that used to be filled with flowers. A shadow of shame flickered in the pit of my stomach, but it was chased away quickly as Rex lifted his leg and peed. Maybe it was a good thing there were no flowers there. I wasn’t sure whether dog pee helped them grow or killed them.

My phone vibrated again, and I glanced down at it as two more texts came through.

Oh my god, read the first text from Phoebe. OH MY GODDDDD. I love him!!! What’s his name???

Rex, I replied with a smile, realising that I hadn’t told her that yet. The people at the shelter named him, but I think it kind of suits him, right?

Yes, absolutely, she wrote without hesitation. Oh my god. I would die for Rex. I love him.

I snorted, beginning to type another message to Phoebe before hearing footsteps behind me and locking my phone quickly.

“What are you doing?” Mom barked as I turned towards her.

“Oh, I was just letting Dad know we got Rex,” I said.

That wasn’t entirely a lie. After all, I had also told Dad, but Mom didn’t seem too happy about that. Her face twisted slightly, and anxiety nibbled at me.

“You shouldn’t be on your phone. You’re meant to be keeping an eye on the dog.”

“I am—”

“Don’t argue with me,” she snapped, cutting me off. “You can’t watch him and stare at your phone at the same time.”

My grip on Rex’s lead tightened as emotions swirled with me. I didn’t like the way she was talking to me, and that made me angry, but at the same time, I hated being angry. Especially at Mom. It always made me feel so guilty, and I knew she was right. I was keeping an eye on Rex, but I hadn’t been focusing on him fully, and that was wrong of me.

“You’re right,” I forced myself to say as I slipped my phone into my pocket. “Sorry.”

Mom sniffed loudly.

“As you should be,” she said, looking away pointedly. “Did he poo yet?”

“No.”

I was certain he hadn’t. Although I’d been on my phone, I’d checked on him a lot, and I would have noticed if he’d pooped at all.

“He didn’t, or you didn’t see it?” Mom asked, her eyebrows rising challengingly.

“He didn’t.”

Mom pursed her lips.

“Mmm,” she replied. “Well, you should probably check the garden to make sure you didn’t miss anything when we get back from his walk. You’re the one who has to clean up after him, remember? I’m not doing it.”

I stared at her in confusion.

“When we get back from his walk?” I repeated.

Mom’s eyebrows rose even higher.

“Yes?”

“You’re coming too?” I clarified.

“Obviously.”

“Why?” I asked, the question slipping out before I could stop it.

I didn’t expect her to. She’d made it very clear that walking Rex would be my responsibility, and I was happy about that. I was looking forward to it, in fact. I felt like I hadn’t spent enough time outside since getting back from Scotland, and I longed to be able to go on runs again, but it didn’t feel safe enough at home.

It was probably fine, and I knew that. I lived in a small village, and there was hardly any crime, but Mom had warned me that there could be murderers or rapists hiding around any corner. I wasn’t sure that I believed her, even though being at the Academy was teaching me that she might be correct, but it still made me nervous whenever I went out.

Plus, I’d planned to meet up with Duncan. I’d already texted him about it, and I didn’t want to have to cancel on him. I wanted to see him again, and I knew I would probably have a much better time going for a walk with him than I would with my mom, but I couldn’t exactly say that to her.

“What do you mean ‘why’?” Mom demanded, her tone becoming dangerous.

I needed to be careful. If I didn’t say the right thing, she would get even more angry than she already was, and I didn’t want that to happen. She’d been in such a good mood before.

“I just assumed I’d go alone because it’s my job to walk him, right?” I explained quickly. “But it would be great for you to join us if you want!”

My tone was enthusiastic, but I was worried it was too much. I’d laid it on thick, and there was a chance she’d see straight through me, but she didn’t seem to. Instead, she just let out a sigh.

“It is your job to walk him. I’m only coming with for the first walk to make sure you can control him and that he doesn’t maul you or drag you into oncoming traffic,” she told me in a haughty tone.

Anxiety shot through me. I hadn’t really considered that, and I glanced at Rex, eyeing him nervously, but he wasn’t looking at me. His eyes were fixed on my mom, and it was hard to tell because he was a dog, but his expression appeared almost affronted.
