The creature’s red eyes burned with something between hunger and raw, seething hatred. It barely blinked. Every movement it made carried an eerie fluidity, too smooth for something looking barely more than a set of bones.

Then it lunged. Oswald barely had time to react before it was on him.

A blur of pale limbs and flashing claws shot toward his chest. He twisted sharply, sidestepping the rush, but the damn thing didn’t just miss. Its body bent mid-motion, spine snapping backward as if the bone inside wasn’t solid.

Oswald’s sword cut through air, the strike wasted. That ain’t right.

The creature twisted with him, moving like a snake in human skin. Claws lashed toward his ribs, and Oswald threw himself back, but he wasn’t fast enough.

Pain tore across his side as the creature made a cut across his shoulder. It was a sharp, biting wound, cutting deep enough to leave a burn in their wake. Not fatal, but it stung quite a bit. Fast little fucker.

Despite this, Oswald feinted high, flicking his sword toward the creature’s face, but at the last second, he dropped the strike low, slashing for its midsection. The thing reacted instantly, snapping sideways in that same impossible way.

But Oswald wasn’t done. He pressed forward, forcing it to give ground.

The creature hissed, a sharp, grating sound that sent a chill down his spine. Then, it scuttled back on all fours, just for its legs to coil beneath it. All before it jumped straight at him.

The impact hit like a hammer. Clawed hands crashed into his shoulders, driving him back with bone-rattling force. His boots skidded across the soil, heels digging deep, but the creature didn’t stop. It pushed harder, an unnatural strength hiding beneath that thin frame. Mud sucked at his soles, but it wasn’t enough to hold him. How is it this strong?

Teeth gritted, Oswald braced his stance as he shoved back. A sharp exhale left his lungs as he forced space between them, prying the thing off bit by bit.

And then, just for an instant, the creature's grip wavered. That was all Oswald needed.

Steel met flesh and bone with a brutal crack. A jolt ran up his arm as his sword cleaved clean through the creature’s limb, severing it at the elbow. The pale, spindly arm hit the ground with a wet thud, fingers still twitching, as if trying to grasp something that wasn’t there.

Thick, black blood oozed onto the dirt as a piercing wail split the air, rattling the surrounding trees. Oswald winced at the sheer volume, but he held his ground, watching as the creature staggered back. Its remaining arm clutched at the bleeding stump, black blood dribbling between its fingers.

Oswald recognized that look, that desperate, frantic glint of survival taking over instinct. Oh no, you don’t.

The creature twisted, its thin body snapping forward like a whip as it ran away. It was surprisingly fast considering how injured it was.

“Get back here!” Oswald snarled, already chasing after it.

Oswald's boots thudded against the forest floor as he tore through the dark. The cold night air scraped down his throat like broken glass, but he didn’t slow down in the slightest.

Further ahead, the creature weaved between the trees at an inhuman pace. Its pale, wiry frame twisted unnaturally, too thin to carry the strength it had shown. The stump of its severed arm flailed with every movement, while the remaining one clawed at the earth to pull it forward in jerking bursts. Its spine bent in ways it shouldn't, snapping into crooked, impossible shapes as it moved.

Oswald grit his teeth. You ain’t gettin’ away that easy.

Yet, despite Oswald's enhanced physique, he just wasn't as used to moving through the forest as the creature. He constantly brushed against roots and various plants, while the ground constantly sucked at his boots with a wet, sucking pull. Branches lashed at his face, some drawing thin lines of blood across his skin, but he powered through, ducking and weaving, leaping when he had to.

Every few seconds, he caught sight of a red glow, just ahead, just out of reach. The creature was incredibly fast.

Oswald pushed harder. His vision blurred as the forest smeared into streaks of silver and black, moonlight catching on wet bark and whipping past him in bursts.

Then, the creature jerked sharply to the right. One blink, it was there, the next, it had vanished behind a wall of thick trunks and brush.

Oswald threw himself after it, pivoting hard. Mud sprayed in all directions as he barreled toward the trees. His eyes locked on the gap where the thing had vanished.

But when he crashed through the thicket, there was nothing. No footprints. No snapped twigs. No broken undergrowth. It was like the thing had never been there at all.

Oswald slowed, chest heaving. His gaze darted from shadow to shadow, nerves sparking hot beneath his skin. Where the fuck did it go?

He took a step forward, scanning the underbrush, but there was just the sound of the wind rustling through the leaves. The forest, so alive with movement just moments ago, had returned to its eerie stillness.

Oswald rubbed a hand across his brow. He didn’t like this. Something about the way the creature vanished felt wrong. No blood trail, no snapped twigs, no nothin’. Just gone.

Frustration simmered beneath his skin, but there was no point in standing around. He’d lost it. For now.

He tightened his grip on his sword, staring into the empty darkness where the thing had disappeared. Fine. Run while you can.

And as much as he wanted to keep chasing after the creature, Oswald still had a quest to finish. Doctor Cecilia was waiting, and the last thing he wanted was to explain why he’d gotten sidetracked chasing after some twisted, half-human creature. But this wasn’t over.

Oswald turned as he started to make his way back towards town. Once he reached Volstarn, however, he planned on asking a lot of questions.

***

By the time Oswald reached town, the first light of dawn had already stretched across the horizon, painting the sky in pale gold and soft streaks of pink. He had slipped his eyepatch back on long before the town gates came into view, no need to give folks something to gawk at.

Doctor Cecilia’s house wasn’t far, but as he rounded the final corner, the sight before him had him clicking his tongue. Of course.

A crowd had already gathered outside her door. Men and women huddled in clusters, some clutched bandaged limbs, others held fevered children against their chests. Oswald knew Doctor Cecilia was the only proper healer in town, but damn, it looked like half of Volstarn had shown up overnight.

With a sigh, he adjusted his grip on the strap of his pack and pushed forward through the crowd. A few folks shot him irritated glances as he nudged past, but no one said anything, probably too tired or too focused on their own pain to care much about one more person squeezing through.

Inside, the waiting area was packed, just like outside. People sat on wooden benches, some hunched over with feverish shivers, others clutching wounds wrapped in old, stained bandages.

Oswald barely took two steps before he saw Lily with her arms crossed over her chest. "Do you have the Duskbloom?"

Oswald pulled the strap of his pack tighter. "Yeah. Got ‘em right here."

"Then you should see the Doctor, follow me."

Oswald trailed a step behind her as they moved through the corridor. Soon, they reached the end of the hall, where a narrow staircase curved upward into the quiet heart of the infirmary. At the top, nestled between tall shelves and shuttered windows, sat Doctor Cecilia’s office.

The door was already open. Inside, Doctor Cecilia was hunched over a patient as she wrapped a fresh bandage around a man’s forearm. White linen pulled snug against dark red flesh. The man flinched but didn’t move, jaw clenched tight. His other hand rested gently on the shoulder of a little girl seated beside him. She couldn't have been more than eight, tiny legs swinging above the floor.

"You’ll be alright," Cecilia said as she tied off the bandage with a final tug. "Keep it clean, change the dressing twice a day. And make sure you actually rest this time."

The man gave a tired nod. “Got it. Thanks, doc.”

Cecilia took a step back, and that was when her eyes found Oswald. She didn’t speak right away. Her gaze drifted to the pack slung over his shoulder, then back to his face, reading him like a worn page.

"You have the Duskbloom?" she asked, already walking toward him.

Oswald slipped the pack off and let it drop by his side with a dull thud. "Yeah. Got as much as I could carry."

"Good, wait there for a moment ."

The doctor turned back to the man and his daughter. "Lily’ll get you the medicine. Take it with food, twice a day."

The little girl looked up at her father, then to the doctor. “Thank you!"

Doctor Cecilia gave a small nod, then motioned to the door. "You may go now. I have another patient to attend to."

The man eased the girl down from the table, murmuring another quiet thanks as he took her hand. They walked past Oswald on their way out, the girl’s wide eyes flicked up at him for just a second, but she quickly looked away.

Once the door shut behind them, Cecilia exhaled and ran a hand through her loose, dark hair. She turned to Oswald, motioning toward the pack. "Let's see what you brought."

Oswald pulled the pack off his shoulder and set it onto the desk with a dull thud. He sat down with a sigh as he unbuckled the flap and peeled it open. The Duskbloom were mostly still intact, though quite a few were noticeably squished together.

Doctor Cecilia leaned in, scrutinizing the bundle with a critical eye. Her fingers brushed over the leaves, testing their texture, before she gave a slow nod of approval. "This is good enough, you did well."

She turned toward Lily. "Collect all of it and store them properly."

"Yes, Doctor." Lily said, stepping forward and taking the bundle of herbs from the pack. She transferred them into a wooden tray, then carried them to the storage cabinet against the wall and set about sealing them in glass containers.

"Outta curiosity, doc... how long have you been stuck in Volstarn?" Oswald asked.

Doctor Cecilia leaned back against the edge of her desk, arms folded tight across her chest. Her eyes drifted upward, not in thought but in weariness, like she was counting the cracks in the ceiling just to keep herself from snapping. "One year, though it feels like ten. This town tends to drain you more thoroughly than any illness I’ve treated."

"Then why come here at all, Doc? Folks in this place don’t exactly strike me as the type who can afford a doctor."

"You don’t need to know my reasons. and in any case, our business is nearly concluded."

She turned without waiting for a reply, opened a drawer, and pulled out a leather pouch. Without ceremony, she tossed it toward him. "Count it."

Oswald caught it with ease. The weight in his palm told him what he already knew, but he loosened the drawstring anyway. Silver coins spilled into his hand, clinking softly under his thumb as he counted. Fifty silver exactly.

He tied the pouch shut and tucked it into his coat. "’Preciate it."

"You are free to go. I will send word to the guild confirming the request has been fulfilled." Doctor Cecilia said.

Oswald turned toward the door. He made it halfway before he stopped. "One last thing. You know anything about weird pale creatures lurkin’ in the forest north of here?"

Cecilia didn’t answer right away. Instead, she closed her eyes and let out a long, drawn-out sigh. "The locals call them ghouls, they're just another type of predator. Dangerous, yes. But no mystery to them. Monsters, nothing more."

Oswald frowned. Just another monster? That thing he fought didn't move like any animal he'd ever seen. It had been too... wrong.

He was about to press the issue, but before he could get a word out, Lily cut in. "With all due respect, Doctor Cecilia has more patients to see."

Oswald resisted the urge to argue. He wasn't gonna get any more out of her, not with how busy she was. No point in making a scene.

"Fine. Thanks for the work," he said, tipping his head slightly before turning on his heel.

Lily didn’t say anything as he passed. The nurse was already moving to prepare for the next patient.

Oswald stepped out into the hall, shutting the door behind him. Even as he made his way back down the stairs, his thoughts swirled. Ghouls, huh?

Something about that name didn’t sit right with him. Oswald had a feeling that there had to be more to those creatures than what he was told.
