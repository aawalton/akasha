“Thank you both for coming,” Rosslyn said as the mystic beasts shuffled into the High Command meeting room.

The rest of High Command was already seated around a large table that was familiar to both Goldie and Adon. It was carved with a carefully scaled map of Claustria marked with every fortification and major town and city in the Kingdom.

“Did you actually want me, as well?” Goldie asked.

Adon had been in a number of meetings in the last week, while Goldie had mostly roamed free, as if there was a tacit understanding that nothing could be asked of the spider.

But the Queen looked surprised at the question.

“Well, of course,” she said after a moment. “If you are willing, we would enjoy both of you becoming more involved in the reconquest plans as we move them forward.”

“What’s happened since we last discussed them?” Adon asked.

“Only continued forward motion,” said one of the knights, Sir Haldayn, energetically.

“Our force continues to grow,” Rosslyn said in a voice that mingled satisfaction with slight unease.

Adon could read her concerns on the surface of her mind, of course. More and more people showed up every day, but feeding and sheltering them was a challenge that grew whenever there was any significant increase in their human footprint.

It was a difficult situation, where the Queen needed as many soldiers as could be brought into action, but she also needed a logistical network that no longer existed to properly supply them.

“We had another handful of knights ride in today,” Sir Angus said, the Sergeant having been knighted by the Queen several days previously. “At this rate, we Claustrians will have more knights than the Parmonians by next year.”

It was a joke, but it landed flatly. The Parmonians in the room didn’t like what sounded like their contribution being trivialized, and those aligned with the Queen were concerned about the practicalities of feeding and housing the knights and their horses.

Just providing hydration for the fort town had already required a group of magical knights to reroute a river two days prior, to ensure a consistent supply of fresh water that would not require them to travel far from their base of operations every time someone needed to bathe.

“Well, as long as we have enough to fight the Empire, I will count myself happy,” Rosslyn said, forcing a smile.

“That still seems out of reach, Your Majesty,” said King Janus in a tone of regret. “While they are fortified in Wayn, they have a position of relative strength. We cannot properly bring the power of our cavalry to bear unless we face them in an open field battle, which they have little incentive to willingly do.”

“What are our numbers actually looking like right now?” the Queen asked straightforwardly. “That is one of the main reasons I wanted to have this meeting. An assessment of our current position, how sustainable it is, and whether we can or should move against the Empire soon.”

Adon felt the undercurrents in the room: Rosslyn’s eagerness for a single battle that would hopefully settle everything, avenge her father, and drive the Empire from her homeland forever; Janus’s caution and wish to only fight if he could be fairly certain of eventual victory; Haldayn’s excitement at the gradual increase in their number of knights and soldiers; Angus’s logistical concern that the rising number of inhabitants of the fort town and the surrounding area would eat the woods bare and starve.

The officers began discussing numbers of new arrivals, how much food was being eaten, and other logistical questions.

“It sounds as if this is unsustainable over the long term,” Adon said bluntly, voicing directly what several people were quietly thinking.

“Well…”

“Oh, no…”

“We will find a way…”

The same people who had been agreeing with what Adon said in their minds hemmed and hawed at the words spoken out loud.

“What is the implication meant to be, Prince Adon?” asked King Janus.

Are you suggesting that we should go and throw ourselves at the enemy now, unprepared? the Parmonian ruler thought.

“There is no implication,” Adon replied. “I’m just listening to the words coming out of everyone’s mouths, and forgive me for being unpolished, but it sounds as if we have to be careful about how long we wait, just as we have to be careful about when we attack. I keep hearing things that sound like starvation.”

Goldie gave Adon a surprised look.

Is this what you’re like in these meetings? she asked telepathically. I would have attended them much sooner to see you act like this!

Adon ignored her words as best he could and managed not to smile, a facial expression that could only be taken negatively in the current situation.

This was only his second time speaking up in one of these meetings. The butterfly felt that he somewhat knew everyone now, and that gave him a bit of confidence. But inside, he wished he had returned to butterfly form before he said anything. His heart was pounding, and he didn’t trust his face not to betray him in some way if the conversation went on long.

“Of course, Adon is right,” Rosslyn said. “This is the balance we all want to strike, whether we say it in such explicit terms or not. We have to wait until our strength is ripe, but not so long that the fruit falls from the tree and begins to rot.”

She half-smiled at Adon, and he could hear her think, Someone has to say these things outright. Thank you.

I’ll support you however I can, Adon sent telepathically.

The only trace of a reaction to that, physically, was the Queen’s ears turning slightly pink. As she and Adon took their engagement more seriously as a real thing, the situation seemed to be setting in for her more and more with each passing day.

“We have dispatched scouts and spies to report on the condition of the Demon Army and its movements,” said King Janus, frowning slightly. “Hopefully they will give us some good news.” From his tone, he sounded almost ready to throw his hands up. “Right now, I do not know that we can responsibly do more.”

“Is it just a matter of insufficient numbers of men or limited materiel?” asked Frederick, sitting in a shady corner of the room. He had mostly recovered by now, but everyone was still treating him like an invalid, including giving him a seat outside of direct sunlight—as if he might boil away if exposed to too much heat.

“As much as we appreciate your offer of support, it is doubtful whether we could feed and house a regiment of Dessian troops at this juncture,” King Janus said before Rosslyn could speak.

Is he actively trying to avoid fighting? Rosslyn thought. It was a question clearly directed at Adon.

The King’s motives are complicated, Adon sent back. He would never think of himself as avoiding fighting. He wants to wait until circumstances are right, but at the same time, he would hate for more powers to involve themselves and dilute the glory he expects to be his when he helps you save the capital.

Rosslyn gave Adon the faintest of nods and then spoke as if he hadn’t said anything.

“I am uncertain whether you are correct, King Janus,” she said slowly. “After all, we are managing to feed this ever-ballooning collection of knights and trainees. If the number of additional men would be enough to swing the balance of force in favor of an immediate strike, the burden on the countryside might be less as a result.”

“Hmm… I suppose so.” His face was the picture of reluctance.

“I will write to my father, then,” Frederick said, very deliberately ignoring the Parmonian King’s feelings.

“Better safe than sorry,” said Adon. “Although King Janus’s knights have clearly created a fairly secure situation here, if we want to go on the offensive, maybe we do need more soldiers.”

Put that way, even the Parmonian ruler had to nod in a grudging agreement.

“Hopefully we will also have word back from our spies and scouts by then,” Janus said. “That is the most essential factor for me. If we got word from them that there was some weakness, a break in the Empire’s supply lines, something that would enable us to assail their fortified position without being at a total disadvantage, then I would not even wish to wait to see how the Duke responds to the request.”

Adon could feel King Janus’s face-saving intentions—and, oddly enough, his sincerity—in the words the King spoke, even without Telepathy, though the butterfly still used the power to confirm what his intuition told him.

Does he really mean what he is saying? Rosslyn thought. He is not just stalling until logistical issues compel him to withdraw his forces from the Kingdom without fighting?

The butterfly told her that the King did indeed mean it. The innermost mind did not lie.

And that seemed to make a difference with the Queen.

“I look forward to the reports of the scouts and spies as well,” Rosslyn said, smiling at King Janus.

Adon had the sense that he might possibly have prevented an argument between the allies.

The conversation went on from there. There was discussion of siege engines for attacking Wayn, additional fortifications that could be built if the reconquest force continued to grow at its present pace, and the possibility of rerouting another river to have a second reliable source of water. They ordered the siege engines constructed but tabled the latter two topics until such time as they became more pressing. The combined military force only had limited manpower at any given moment, after all, and it wasn’t as if they were short of tasks to perform.

The discussion went on into the night, and the officers and nobles ordered wine brought up from the stores that King Janus had brought with him. They drank to fuel their continued cognitive work, until their minds weakened from the strain of such demanding efforts, and they passed out, or had to excuse themselves so that they would not pass out, in front of their fellow leaders.

The next day was similar. The spider and butterfly hunting, followed by Adon training with Goldie in the use of Transformation for combat, and an ensuing meeting.

The days kept going like this, each one recognizably like the one before, with very little apparently being accomplished by the various leadership council meetings other than using up time and making the officer and noble class tired of each other’s faces.

Adon didn’t mind them terribly. Each day left him stronger than the last, since he was still hunting and training. The meetings broke up what would have been a monotony of killing beasts in the wild, and they brought him close to his fiancee, who might otherwise have been too busy to spend time with him.

The Queen seemed to find the meetings much more maddening than he did, but then, she was hoping to get her country back, and none of the long, meandering conversations ever seemed to bring that meaningfully closer.

Frederick had written to his father, so that was accomplished, and the scouts and spies had certainly been dispatched.

But the discussions otherwise seemed to be tedious.

You are so good in these meetings, Rosslyn thought in Adon’s direction, smiling slightly, after one such long conversation ended. The moon was rising, and he was walking her to her bedchamber in the central fort. You know, I would go crazy if I had to do all of them without you here. I would start to doubt everything, starting with myself, just because every day seems to be the same. Progress is meant to be happening, but… I do not see it. I am glad I have you there, at least.

Adon was confused for a moment, certain she must be thinking about someone, anyone else, until the two locked eyes.

Then he realized that the only person in the world she saw at that moment was him. The emotion in her eyes was one he couldn’t identify, but whatever it was, it gave him a beautiful, surreal feeling to be the object of it.

That look got him to answer her.

I guess I am decent in the meetings, Adon sent back modestly. Thank you. I’m glad to be helpful. I mean, glad to help you. Maybe I’m becoming a little bit of a… social butterfly.

Rosslyn smiled more broadly at him, and they kept walking.
