Weaving through the trees, the group quickly approached the lighthouse. Daniel, Vela, and Goldman had been outside checking the perimeter to see if any of the other possible members were coming. As far as they knew, there could be several other opponents in the bushes around the island.

They soon regrouped and left the shelter made just for Harry. Brun, shortly after, also warned Harry not to overexert himself again. What the Locksmith heard, however, was "Don't try to put the top of a mountain through one of your doors and you'll be fine.". Despite this, Harry was being careful with his Emblem, and certainly didn't want to feel as fragile and dependent as before. Even though he wanted to jump several meters with his doors, he held on and continued walking alongside Brun and Madeline. Leading were Goldman and Daniel, who occasionally gave orders to each other.

The group had interrogated Gale and Cal, who quickly told everything. The hatch was in the center, and the center of the place was the lighthouse. It was just a matter of following the path and going to the next floor.

Vela was still without his candelabrum, and the difference was noticeable when he was without his candles. All the man's menacing presence had vanished. However, Harry had seen Vela in combat and knew that the man was strong even before his Emblem. From time to time he still wondered about his past and what had brought him back to the Tower and how he acquired his Emblem. He was usually caught talking to himself with his candelabrum, always with a smile on his face. Now, however, Vela only stared in the direction they were walking with a stoic but still gentle look.

Approaching the lighthouse, a man could be seen at the top. Before Goldman could warn them, the man shouted from above, "Come, I invite you all."

Unable to discern much of his features, Harry could only see that the man had slicked-back hair and appeared to be in an elegant pose, like a butler.

Goldman, putting a hand behind his back to prevent anyone from reacting immediately, frowned. He waited a few seconds for the man to continue, but remained silent.

"Who are you?" Goldman shouted with the same intensity, ready to deflect any projectiles that came from above. "Are you with Prism?"

The mysterious man gave a dry laugh. "No. It is Prism who is with me. Now come on, I know you have defeated Gale and Cal. I presume it wasn't as easy as you thought, right? Still... I just want to talk. Me and Prism."

Thinking to himself, Goldman waited a second before replying. Who guaranteed their safety if they were on the top? Who knew what this man was capable of? And the most pressing issue; how could they know if Prism was really there?

"If Prism is in there, say something." Goldman said, still careful.

"...I'm here, guys." Harry told himself he wasn't going to crack so easily, but hearing her voice for the first time since the last floor shook him more than he'd like to admit. That voice came with the memory of Petrikov, of the distress she caused Goldman and everyone else.

Inwardly, the Locksmith growled and muttered to himself, breathing rapidly. He then closed his eyes and reassured himself he wasn't the vindictive kind. Sure, he'd love to see her being left on her own and getting what she deserved, but what'd happen after it? The price of vengeance was harsher than the satisfaction it could give.

Goldman looked behind him and questioned everyone about the situation.

"Fuck it." Harry said, with a strange calmness. "Let's go. I want to see her face one last time."

"Harry..." Madeline sighed. "I don't think its worth it. We both know—"

"He probably has my fucking journal, and I need it." No, he didn't.

Seeing how Harry was choosing to handle this, Madeline gave up and replied with a weak "Okay..."

After a few more seconds of discussing, they agreed to meet the man, but Goldman and Daniel went up first. Soon, Goldman descended from the lighthouse and created a negative polar area on the ground and another one on each of their shoes, making them slowly fly to the air. Then, individually, he helped each one to reach the top safely.

In front of everyone, a man stood still. White gloves. Polished shoes. Everything about him was immaculate, except his face. Straight, brown hair matched his entire outfit, but his gaze deceived him; one of his eyebrows seemed to jump off his face while his mouth trembled. And I thought Dracno would be the last crazy person we'd find... Harry thought to himself, the butler's eyes falling on him.

The main part of the lighthouse, however, was missing; the light.

Beside the man, a girl with curly and short brown hair stood. She grinned as she amused herself with the confused looks of the group that came by. Harry noticed she was fiddling with something in her hands, and as soon as he saw what it was, he clenched his fists and gave the woman a cold stare.

"...That's not yours, is it?" Harry said, breaking the silence. "Come on, Estela. Give it back to its owner."

"A thief blabbering about property?" She gave a dry laugh. "Are you serious?"

"Is this just to spite me? You think holding a piece of Petrikov's clothes would be funny?" He tried to reason with Prism. "That's just pathetic—"

"Said the man with brother issues. Yeah, Raven told me all about your... escape."

Alexander then took a step back and stared at Prism, attentive.

"You know what?" Harry said. "Fuck you, Prism. Nobody here cares if you die. You're just a sad, broken girl. You stole my journal. You manipulated the whole group, and for what? Just for you to end up here with us. Why do that? We could've climbed together to help your fat—"

"Don't!" Prism yelled as she glanced around, scratching her head. "Just... Don't. He told us he'd help us, okay? T-That if we, um, caught your journal we would skip the 5th floor. Not even Goldman or Daniel could survive the 5th. Besides, we don't even know if the God Emblem allows for more wishes."

God Emblem? The group narrowed their eyes, beggining to understand Prism's motivations.

"So you decided to turn on us?" Harry said, taking a step forward.

"There was never us, Harry! Can't you see that? It was only me and them." She took a step back as well, standing beside Alexander. "We're in on this way before I met you guys. Come on, it isn't personal. It was fun while it lasted, you know?"

"...Fun?" Harry muttered to himself, doubting if he heard her right.

"What's on the 5th floor?" Goldman interrupted, frowning at Alexander.

"...It's a game." The tall man answered simply, not bothering to explain further.

"What game?" Goldman insisted.

"A game of death, of course." He crooked his head and grinned. " What else could it be?"

"Spit it out." Daniel said, also frowning and ready to summon an arrow at any given time. "What death game are we talking about?"

Alexander sighed and returned to his apathetic look. "He told us it's a coliseum. Most of the inmates stay there. Apparently, putting dozens of the most dangerous people in one spot, with reduced chance of death, is quite profitable. To pass, though, you need to win it."

"Ha..." Daniel gave a genuine laugh, relaxing his muscles. "So that's why you went with the easy way. You're scared of hurting those hands of yours, hum, princess? I suppose the weird big fucking guy and the whip freak were your only shot at this."

"...I suppose a savage ogre like you could do well on the 5th floor." He somewhat agreed with Daniel, who now growled silently. "I, on the other hand, am not such a fan of... violence, you know?"

Before Daniel could reply and maybe start a chain reaction, Goldman intervened and asked. "Why'd you call us here?"

"To propose a deal." He took a step forward, meeting the gentle giant's eyes.

"Wait, that's not what—" Prism was interrupted by Alexander.

"One that would benefit both of us."

As Goldman kept quiet, the whole group felt suddenly tense. They were all already on edge since meeting the mysterious man, but now the anxiety was almost palpable. Vela, even without his candelabrum, clenched his left fist as Madeline fiddled with her paint-brush. Harry, however, wasn't in the moment.

They waited for Alexander to expand on the proposition. "You are all wondering where the exit is, yes? I can tell you. In return, all I ask, is for a truce. In case we meet again in the future, there shall not be any bad blood between us. This would only apply, of course, if you made it out of the 5th floor."

"We already know that the hatch is in the lighthouse. The two knuckleheads told us." Brun talked for the first time, already analyzing the lighthouse with her Emblem.

"Here!?" Alexander have a chuckle. "Now that's funny..."

What does this mean...? Suspicious, Goldman glanced at his teammates, not yet ready to accept such a proposal. Alexander seemed meticulous and cunning; If he could simply let two of his members behind, what would he do if he encountered another obstacle? In hindsight, if we took the deal and honored it, we'd all be fine. If we didn't, however, we wouldn't know the exit...

"Fun?" Harry said.

Amidst the silence, everyone was confused to what Harry was referring, even Alexander. The Locksmith took a step forward and continued to stare at Prism.

"Fun, Prism?" Harry repeated. "That's what it was to you?"

"...W-What are you talking about?" Prism replied, her voice cracking. "Of course it was. We had a couple laughs and—"

"Petrikov died because of you!" He yelled, his blood boiling. "Did you know that? Because of your murder attempt, he came back and rescued me. He couldn't keep up, you know? Every step was a chore to him."

"I wasn't trying to kill him—"

"To hell you weren't!" He yelled once again, each word heavier than the last. "I-I know we share a cause. But I can't just let it slide, because... I don't give a shit."

The woman, as if she took a hit, glanced around nervously at the scrutinizing gaze of Goldman's group and Alexander. Each word from Harry making her shrink even more. "...Don't say it."

"Do you know what it's like to have an entire world of hope being ripped off of someone?" He asked. "His eyes glimmered with the idea that he could finally leave, that he'd find his place back in the world. And you know what the best part is? He didn't even remembered their names. Their faces were nothing but a blur, but still, in that moment, he saw a chance. An idea stronger than the temptation of the nothing. That's what they were to him."

"Please, stop..." She pleaded, tears in her eyes.

"He figured all that loneliness and that battle would be worth it. He'd find himself surrounded by an infinite hug that would cover all scars. But he didn't. He didn't because of you. You fucking, selfish, piece of shit..."

"I-I didn't, it wasn't me."

"...I was blaming myself, but of course it wasn't me. It was you. You killed Petrikov and I hope you die in blood and tears, you devil."

"Stop... Stop! Stop! I-I can't..." Prism cried as she fell to the ground, mumbling to herself.

On the other side, a pressure inside Harry was finally released. He relaxed his fists as he thought about all the problems the woman caused to him. Robbery and betrayal were common on the criminal world; that's why he didn't had any companions. "Too much of a hassle." He'd say to himself, but that wasn't his heart talking. Harry actually feared betrayal, and in so, like many lonely individuals, shut himself from the world.

That's what I've been missing? His mind wandered as he stared at Estela. For a second, he really wanted to kill Prism with his own hands. Throw her off the light house and watch her being plunged downwards. It would server her right, wouldn't it? He thought about it, but soon regained his senses. That wasn't Harry talking, but rather his anger; his resentment.

Harry never met the original Prism, only a fraction of her. He sighed deeply as everyone wasn't sure what to say next. They all stood as she wept, the water in her eyes clashing against the stone.

Harry gave her one last pity look as Alexander stretched his gloves and silently placed his hands on her shoulder.

The Locksmith stared Prism in the eye. What he saw, however, shook him to the core. He could already imagine the nightmares his mind would create to repeat this moment. In her eyes, Harry saw himself. He saw a person consumed by grief and the past. A person who didn't trust anyone. Prism is what Harry would've become if he hadn't found Goldman and the group. She just wants her father back. A wave of empathy shoveled his anger away.

"Please..." Estela whimpered as she stared at the Locksmith. "It wasn't meant to go like that... I don't wanna die, Harry."

In a swift movement, Alexander, who wore a grin, snapped his fingers right on her head. As if by magic, her eyes rolled back as a bullet seemed to pass through her skull; an energy far greater than that of regular weapons. The woman then fell onto the cold ground, her tears falling between the cracks.
