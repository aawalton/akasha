The morning light was still pale when Elynara stirred, the faint gold of dawn barely brushing across the dormitory’s wooden floor. Her limbs felt heavy in the soft quilt, and the faint ache behind her eyes reminded her of just how much mana she had pushed last night.

With a soft grunt, she sat up.

The small crystal rested where she had left it on the windowsill, faintly pulsing with a dim glow. Not yet full, though most of the way there. And her own reserves? She brushed her senses inward and frowned, then checked with her status to make sure.

Only nine mana regenerated overnight. Not nearly enough.

“Well… better get to it.”

Mumbling so to herself, she stretched her arms overhead until her spine gave a satisfying pop. A little shiver ran through her at that.

Starpuff emerged from the folds of her pillow with a disgruntled hum, flipping once in midair before plopping down at her side with all the gravitas of a sleep-deprived cat. The sound was suspiciously close to a yawn, and it almost seemed like it cracked one eye open, if it had eyes at all.

“Good morning to you too.”

Amused at its antics, Elynara pulled her notebook close, flipped it open to the margin beside her latest entries, and settled back against the headboard. The crystal was carefully brought to rest on her lap. With a long breath in and out, she began.

At first, the mana in her core moved slowly, sluggishly, like stretching limbs after a long rest. She didn’t force it. Just let it gather, then pushed it gently toward the crystal, shaping it into the steady stream she had been practicing.

The battery pushed back, just a little. Like a door not quite oiled enough. She slowed her breathing, focused on the flow. Alicia’s words floated back to her:

Steeping tea, not pouring wine.

Starpuff curled beside the notebook, mimicking her calm posture with an exaggerated stillness. Then it blinked once and began lazily orbiting the crystal, casting faint spirals of green light across her blanket.

The rhythm settled in: gather, shape, release.

Minutes passed. Then more.

Outside, the first sounds of the church's awakening started to come in: soft voices greeting each other, distant kitchen clatter, bare feet on the floorboards.

She tuned it all out, focusing.

By the time the crystal gave a soft pulse and its glow brightened to a steady, rich hue, her breath had grown shallow, her limbs tingling. She leaned back, resting her head against the wall, letting the drain sink in.

“Whew…”

Her fingers flexed a little, and with a lopsided grin, she gathered the last scraps of her mana into three small spheres. She held them above the crystal – only for Starpuff to pounce on them before she could even think.

The sprite zipped forward with sudden energy, devouring the motes mid-air with a delighted hum. Then it flopped belly-up on her notebook like it had just completed some grand act of heroism.

She smiled wryly at its antics, a weary mutter escaping her lips.

"Yeah, you earned it."

The battery was full. Her core was empty, leaving her feeling extremely lethargic. However, the entire day was still ahead, and she was already somewhat pleased with how it had started.

Now to restock mana the old-fashioned way: breakfast, errands… and maybe a little bit of people-watching if time allowed.

She rose slowly, Starpuff curling into the hood of her tunic like a lump of smug satisfaction, before going ahead with what now was becoming her daily routine.

The sun had climbed just a little higher by the time Elynara made her way across the town square, the last traces of morning chill giving way to the warmth of a clear day. Standing where it always had, the Adventurer's Guild was lively and bustling with people.

She stepped inside quietly, pausing to let the familiar hum of activity wash over her.

The interior wasn’t packed, but there was a steady rhythm to it: boots across floorboards, laughter near the tables, and Alicia’s clear voice as she handled a trio of adventurers wrapped in mismatched gear.

Elynara held her notebook close, the carefully copied map tucked between its pages. The mana battery was nestled beside it, wrapped to prevent any jostling. She took a small breath, and slipped into a seat along the side wall to wait.

Starpuff, ever curious, peeked out from beneath her collar and zipped up to the tabletop, bouncing once before plopping beside the notebook. It tilted, stared at the covered map for all of two seconds, then tried nudging it open with a tiny, glowing poke.

Elynara rolled her eyes.

“Impatient, are we?”

The sprite gave what might’ve been a chirp of agreement and began hovering in slow circles above the notebook while she turned her attention inward. With time to spare and the quiet bustle of the hall around her, she focused on the gentle draw of ambient mana. Even though it was barely a trickle, it was still better than nothing. She pulled slowly, letting it settle into her core like a second breath.

Eventually, Alicia’s voice rose in farewell to the departing group, and a soft “next” followed with far less formality.

Elynara stood and approached, holding out the mana battery first.

“Finished filling it up this morning.”

Alicia took the crystal with practiced hands, turning it once in the light. The faint glow within pulsed evenly, no flickering or overstrain.

“Looks like you did. No issues, full to the brim. Nicely done.”

She reached under the counter, retrieved a small leather pouch, and handed it over.

“Here is your payment, just don’t spend it all at once.”

Elynara accepted it with a smile, quickly checking that it was about half as much as she got from the herb request, and set the notebook down gently.

“I actually wanted to ask something else. I copied this map from a book in the church library. It looked pretty old, so I figured it might not be quite… up to date.”

That earned a raised brow. Alicia opened the notebook, her expression shifting quickly from vague curiosity to focused interest.

“You copied this yourself?”

Elynara nodded.

“Hmm.”

Alicia leaned over it, pencil in hand, scrutinizing it with the full force of her professional weight Elynara had come to know and respect.

“Good linework. And yes, it’s rather old. These villages here? They’ve grown, renamed, or moved since. Some aren’t even marked.”

She began annotating, lightly sketching around the edges of the map as she spoke.

“This ridge path here? Don’t use it. Washout and erosion from two years ago. Dangerously unstable now.”

She circled another patch to the west.

“This glade’s corrupted, or some weird monstrosity found its home there. People who go too deep tend to come back sick. If they come back.”

Elynara’s eyes followed every mark.

“And here, right at the edge of the desert and deeper into the forest, there have been some reports of uncovered artifacts, though all I have seen confirmed were really just scrap. The entire area is very unstable, with ground cracked, with crevices and sudden rises. Not fatal, but not worth stumbling into blindly.”

By now the page looked fuller, touched with warning marks and updated names. Elynara bit the inside of her cheek.

“…I didn’t realize how much could change.”

A familiar voice cut in before Alicia could answer.

“Still not the worst old map I’ve seen.”

Marcus had wandered over, drink in hand, his usual slouch in full effect.

“But there’s a few bigger things worth knowing. This bit here Alicia mentioned?”

He tapped the large patch of desert making up the west side of the map.

“That desert? Don’t go there. Used to be forest, or so they say. Now it’s just dry, cursed dust with dangerous monsters that don’t know how to die properly.”

His finger moved again, tapping a jagged marking near a mountain.

“This one’s worse. That whole mountain’s rotting. Corrupted top to bottom, oozes some black sludge. Runs right down into this bog here to the north of the land sea. Undead all over. And they wander.”

Elynara stared down at the marked additions, her throat dry.

Marcus’s gaze lifted briefly to hers.

“And don’t get close to the mountain range at all, really. Dreadwings prowl near the foothills. Big bastards, got an average wingspan ‘round three meters. Smart, aggressive, and real fond of snatching things that scream.”

Alicia sighed.

“Language.”

Marcus just smirked.

“But yeah. Stay clear.”

He gave Elynara a short nod before turning to leave.

“You’ve got a good head. Keep using it. It'll take you further than muscle alone ever will.”

After making one last correction to a river's course in the corner, Alicia closed the notebook and handed it back.

“That should save you a lot of walking. And maybe a twisted ankle or two.”

Elynara took it with both hands, the extra weight of caution and knowledge settling into her grip.

“Thank you. Really.”

“Just remember the map isn’t the world.”

Despite already turning back to her stack of forms, Alicia threw a few last words of encouragement.

“But it’s a good place to start.”

After saying her goodbyes Elynara made her way towards the one place she was very excited about, to hopefully pick up her first ever proper mage staff.

She reached her destination quickly, and as usual, the workshop smelled of wood shavings, resin, and a faint hint of smoke.

With a slight creak, the door opened, and Elynara's skin hummed beneath with anticipation as she stepped into the warm space.

The wolf-eared craftsman didn’t look up right away. He sat behind the counter, turning a small length of carved wood in his hands with a file, each pass slow and even.

He flicked a glance her way as the door clicked shut behind her.

“You’re early.”

Elynara hesitated.

“…Am I?”

Tillian grunted once, as if it was all the explanation he needed to give.

“Doesn’t matter. It’s done.”

He set aside the carving and reached under the counter. From a rack hidden behind, he retrieved a long cloth-wrapped bundle and laid it down with a soft thump.

Without ceremony, Tillian untied the ties and drew back the cloth to reveal smooth wood that had been slightly darkened by oil, reinforced or altered in some areas, and curved cleanly and subtly near the top.

Elynara stepped forward, eyes drawn to the piece instantly.

The staff was… simple. Just smooth, sturdy wood that shimmered faintly when she reached for it with her mana sense.

She reached out with both hands and lifted it carefully. It was lighter than she expected, balanced, and the grain along the length seemed to guide her fingers into place. The moment she let a trickle of mana run down its length, it hummed softly.

The fact it barely resembled the stick she had left with the man spoke volumes about his skills, or perhaps even Skill? She wanted to ask, but remembered what he told her last time, opting for honest praise instead.

“It’s good,” she murmured, unable to keep the smile off her lips.

The craftsman gave another noncommittal grunt.

“Should be.”

Elynara looked up at him then. Bright-eyed, expectant, her posture practically radiating unspoken questions. The kind of look children gave when they believed every grown-up had some secret knowledge just waiting to be shared.

He noticed.

“…Don’t give me that look.”

She blinked.

“What look?”

“That one. The hopeful one. The ‘please tell me all about the magic’ one. I ain’t no teacher or anything.”

She tried to look apologetic, but her curiosity wasn’t something she could fully hide. He saw right through it, of course.

Letting out a long-suffering sigh, he leaned back against the counter, and crossed his arms.

“I’m not a mage. Never was. But you work with the stuff long enough, you start to feel it. I learned my trade from my old pa, trial and error, and a few hermits who didn’t like to talk much. You want real secrets? You’ll have to find those on your own.”

Pausing, he added in a softer tone:

“But if the staff hums for you, you listen. That’s all I’ve got.”

She nodded slowly, absorbing every word. Then bowed her head slightly.

“Thank you.”

He waved a hand dismissively and returned to his carving.

“Just make sure to treat it well. As good as my work is, whacking rocks with the thing will still destroy it fast.”

Elynara gave a quiet laugh, paid her due and turned to go, her first ever staff held lightly at her side.

Once outside, she took her sweet time returning to the church. Happy to have accomplished all she set out to do that day before noon even hit.

Now that she had the staff to hold too, she quickly decided to hide her notebook in her bag of holding, before settling into a light walk around the market.

To get accustomed to holding her staff of course.

And oh, those stalls with snacks sure looked great!

By the time she returned to the church with her tummy full and in a happy mood, it was already early afternoon. She didn’t even mind that it used up the last of her money, the treats well-worth it!

Plus, she could just earn more!

Not long after her return, Elynara was once again caught in the chaos of chores and helping out the nuns with keeping an eye on the kids.

Her good mood made the time fly fast, and soon she was all done and with some more free time on her hands.

Elynara stepped outside, stretching her arms above her head with a soft sigh. Helping with afternoon chores at the orphanage had left her a little flushed, her sleeves damp where they had caught spilled water. Still, there was a nice rhythm to it.

One she really didn’t mind.

Looking around, the courtyard behind the church appeared as peaceful as it usually was on warm summer afternoons, with dust motes floating languidly in the golden light as sunlight filtered through the narrow lines of wooden fence slats.

Perfect to enjoy a little break in.

She tucked a loose strand of hair behind her ear and turned toward the low wall that edged the yard.

There, she unexpectedly met Mina, the little tamer girl she had known about since her first day at church.

The quiet girl sat beneath the narrow shade of the building, knees drawn up, her arms looped around a familiar white fluffball that lay curled in her lap like a discarded pillow. Its little ears flicked occasionally, tail twitching with each passing breeze.

Pufflet, she remembered. The same strange little creature, looking like it had been sewn together from cloud and fur.

She had seen Mina around often enough. Always nearby, but never in the way. They had exchanged a few words here and there during her chores work or when wrangling the younger children, but nothing that stuck.

Elynara hesitated for a moment, then stepped forward.

“Hey. Mind if I sit?”

Mina looked up, brushing her bangs back from her face. She blinked once, like she hadn’t quite expected the question, then nodded and patted the grass beside her.

“Sure.”

Elynara lowered herself to the ground, smoothing her skirt out before crossing her legs. She rested her hands on her knees and tilted her head slightly, watching the little lump of fur shift in Mina’s lap.

“It’s fluffier than I thought up close.”

Mina hummed faintly, brushing a hand along Pufflet’s side.

“He’s a pain sometimes, but I love him all the same.”

She paused, gently pressing her fingers behind one of its ears.

“And he listens. Usually.”

That earned a faint laugh from Elynara. Starpuff, not one to be left out, drifted lazily down from wherever it had been hovering – she had stopped trying to track it long ago – and settled just behind her, lightly thumping against her shoulder before bobbing in the air nearby.

They sat in silence for a moment, the warm air wrapping around them like a blanket.

Elynara shifted, her fingers brushing idly against a blade of grass.

“You always look calm with him. Like it’s easy.”

Mina’s lips tugged up in something halfway between a smile and a shrug.

“He knows me. That helps.”

A beat passed before Elynara curiously asked again.

“What’s it like? Raising one of those?”

Mina glanced down at Pufflet, then gave a tiny shrug.

“I just feed him. Let him nap. Don’t let the kids pull his tail too much.”

Elynara snorted quietly, and Mina’s mouth twitched again, continuing after a pause.

“You know, I’ve got a little spirit companion. No idea what it is, really. It’s not like yours. It’s a bit smaller, maybe. Glows green a bit. Loves flying around and causing trouble.”

Mina tilted her head slightly, a little confused.

“Spirit?”

Elynara nodded.

“It followed me from the start. I don’t think anyone else can see it properly. Most can’t, anyway.”

Mina looked at her for a longer while, her expression unreadable.

“It sounds like it likes you. And will listen to you if it trusts you.”

Elynara snorted at that.

“Not sure mine even knows what listening is.”

That earned her a small, quiet laugh. Pufflet shifted again in Mina’s lap, letting out a soft whuffle before curling tighter.

They fell into silence again, this time a little more comfortable. Starpuff hovered nearby, doing lazy circles in the air before plopping down beside her in the grass with a contented flicker.

Evening sunlight spilled through the narrow windows of the church hall, catching on the motes of dust that danced through the air. The walls, warmed by years of quiet conversation and long afternoons, glowed faintly in the warmth of the slow-fading light.

Father Alaric stood just outside the side corridor, a hand braced on the old stone sill as he adjusted the angle of a candleholder mounted to the wall. The iron base creaked slightly under his grip – more from age than weight.

Or perhaps it was him that creaked. Hard to tell these days.

The sounds of soft chatter drifted through the space: children murmuring to one another in the side room, a quiet laugh from the kitchens, the shuffle of someone moving furniture that didn’t quite need moving.

He let his hand fall back to his side, watching.

Out in the courtyard, Elynara headed through the last of the fading light while holding a small bundle of something in her arms, perhaps a basket of kindling or laundry. It looked just a little too heavy for her, but she carried it anyway, her brow furrowed with quiet focus.

She passed Mina on the way, exchanging a few parting words. The other girl nodded, her pale puff curled comfortably around her neck like a lazy scarf. Just a few quiet words, and then they went their separate ways.

Alaric watched it all unfold in silence.

Unusual child, that one.

But then again, when were children ever ordinary?

He had watched dozens, maybe hundreds, pass through these halls. Some loud, some sullen, some curious enough to drive the sisters to distraction. Most stayed a year or two, sometimes less, before life pulled them onward.

The names faded first. The voices lingered longer.

The faces… he remembered forever.

He thought back to her first day. To how stiffly she moved, how her eyes took in everything at once, like she didn’t trust any of it to stay the same if she blinked.

Now she moved fluidly and without worry, her initial awkwardness almost gone. Like she was finding herself a place here, growing up almost day by day.

He couldn’t help the small smile that tugged at the corners of his mouth.

“Watching them grow never does get old.”

There was no one nearby to hear him, but he said it anyway.

He reached down, adjusted the edge of his sleeve, and turned from the light, his steps measured and even as he made his way back down the hall.

There was still work to do. There always was.

But for now, he let himself carry that quiet warmth with him.

The next couple days passed with a slow, steady rhythm.

Mornings often began with sweeping the church floors or helping Sister Elara with firewood.

One day included errands to the nearby quartermaster or walking children back from lessons. The afternoons blended into light guild duties, such as making deliveries, compiling reports, and delivering messages throughout the town. Nothing exciting, and rather poorly paid. But then, not everything needed to be.

The work left her time. Time to practice, rest, and think.

Each evening, she set aside some time for mana training. Seated cross-legged in her small room, or tucked beside the low stone wall behind the church, she focused on drawing in the ambient threads, expanding her capacity to 34 and improving her control. Her staff warm to the touch and rested against her knees during each session.

It didn’t amplify her strength. Not really. But it gave her focus. Shaped the flows that once spilled like a tipped bucket into streams she could better guide. Although it took her some time and practice to adjust, it was like someone had placed a handle on her power, finally giving her a way to carry it.

Her control improved quickly. Just a week ago, even holding a small orb steady had taken most of her focus. Now she could form and hold several, though not yet effortlessly.

She was making progress!

Elynara also found herself drawn more and more to Mina.

They didn’t become fast friends. But the words came easier. Sometimes, they would talk while watching the kids play. Other times, they would just sit together.

She had questions about Pufflet. How much it ate. Whether it got cold. What it liked. Mina didn’t always have full answers. But she answered what she could, and Elynara was just happy to listen and learn.

She found herself smiling more during those calm moments.

And just as a bit over a week of her new life passed, it was time for the first public church event she would be part of.

It was Worship Day.

She woke early to the rustle of cloth being laid, the soft clink of candlesticks being arranged across the chapel space. The air smelled faintly of cut flowers and wax. The altar cloth had been replaced, fresh thread and needlework gleaming slightly in the morning sun.

One of the nuns, who invited her to join the day prior, swung by her room soon after, delivering her meal and following up with a quick:

“Don’t be late!”

Really, was this the same nun always so happy to throw all her chores Elynara’s way? Shaking her head in mild amusement, she quickly ate and prepared to go downstairs.

She entered the church hall with the others, wearing her usual clean and simple clothes.

Her staff tapped lightly against the stone as she walked, and several children near the rear pews looked up. One whispered something behind their hand. Another grinned, tugged at a friend’s sleeve.

“She looks like a proper mage now!”

“I bet she would be a good monster boss!”

“We should invite her to our knight game!”

As she went by, Elynara stifled a smile, her thoughts momentarily straying to the infamous Flower Knight, painted on the orphanage wall.

She slipped into a quiet corner near the side, settling in just as the first chanted lines began to echo softly through the room.

The service wasn’t long. Spoken readings gave way to murmured prayers. Some were kneeling, others standing. With an occasional song, if you could call the slow, melodic hum that, but it fit the space.

She watched more than she joined at first. Curious how people bowed their heads, how they moved with practiced familiarity. Whether out of obligation, tradition, hope, or comfort. She didn’t know.

Eventually, the time for offerings came.

People stepped up the aisle one by one. A coin or two. Some paper or item. A candle placed gently into its holder. People gave what they could, but all were welcome.

Elynara rose quietly, awaiting her turn.

When she reached the altar, pausing before it. One hand on the staff, one across her chest.

And once again gave her thanks to the only goddess she knew:

Thank you. For the second chance. For the strange little puff of a companion. For the days where nothing tried to eat me.

She wasn't sure if Celestia had even heard her prayer, but a sensation of lightness settled in her chest, as though someone had gently tousled her hair.

Leaving some coins as an offering, she turned and made her way back down the aisle.

Near the middle row, a familiar voice of a certain snarky guard murmured as she passed.

“Didn’t peg you for the religious type.”

She glanced sideways to find Josh making his way the other direction, posture too casual for the setting.

Arching a brow, she replied in an equally low voice:

“Didn’t peg you for punctual.”

He grinned, raised two fingers in a half-salute, and didn’t stop walking.

Outside, after the service, the courtyard filled slowly with townsfolk. A few people offered her nods of greeting. She recognized one as the vegetable seller from her errand run. Another had signed off one of her delivery notes earlier in the week.

“Good day, miss.”

“Ah, the little miss mage, so good to see you!”

Greeting them all politely, she made her way over to the side, watching people coming and going.

Elynara stepped away from the door, settling on the low stone edge that lined the building’s side. Her staff rested against her shoulder, and she leaned her cheek to it, eyes tracking the lazy dance of a bird flitting between rooftop tiles.

Invisible to everyone except herself, Starpuff drifted nearby in its slow, circular float.

She smiled, relaxing, letting her thoughts wander.

Things were looking up.
