I blinked, utterly confused. Where walls should have been, there was nothing but endless blue, dotted with a line of thick clouds that stretched to infinity. We stood on what looked like a floating stone platform, suspended in midair with no visible means of support.

“What in the name of the Thirteen Realms?” Lun muttered, cautiously approaching the edge of the very small platform.

The platform we stood on had runes etched along its perimeter. Beyond it was nothing but open sky in all directions. Seeing the big drop made my stomach lurch. When I turned my head around my shoulder, I noticed that the door leading to this trial had disappeared. Okay, nothing new. The same thing happened with the earth trial, but unlike the earth trial, we weren’t standing on the literal brink of death!

“I’m not seeing a way out of this,” Typha said, irritated. “Unless that stupid stone wants us to hop on clouds to get across.”

“Well, they are positioned in a path for a reason,” Nolan added, the left and right diagonal pattern before us.

“The gaps between the clouds are too wide,” Typha rejected. “We need to find another way.”

“There is no other way, unless you’re seeing something we aren’t,” Nolan bit back. “The distance isn’t really the issue here, it’s the potential false support within the clouds.”

“I’m finding it hard to believe these clouds could support us in the first place,” Typha hissed.

“More than likely, some of them could, others could be false platforms.”

“I can confirm this,” Lun said. “I’ve heard one too many stories of mechanisms within dungeons that work similarly.”

‘So how will we differentiate which clouds are safe to step on and which ones aren’t?’ I asked.

‘I bet it has something to do with these runes on the stone,’ Nolan said as he looked down by his feet. “Lun, can you make sense out of these runes?” Nolan asked, crouching to examine the intricate markings.

Lun knelt beside him, running his fingers along the etched symbols. “Ancient Aeromantic script. Very old, but I recognize some patterns.” His eyes narrowed as he concentrated. “It speaks of... forces that both guide and deceive. The trial of air isn’t just about finding solid footing,” Lun continued, “It’s about trusting instinct and tuning into the song of the wind.”

“Total bullshit,” Typha grunted under her breath.

“So we have two clouds closest to us,” Nolan said. “How do we know which one to take?”

“Oh no, bad news, gang,” Lun said. “My codex just sent me a notification—all wind magic and teleportation magic has been suppressed within this trial. That includes items.”

“Well, the teleportation magic being null isn’t news to me,” Nolan said. “So much for levitating across. This trial stumps anything that’d help us cheat our way across.”

“This just keeps getting better and better,” Typha muttered, peering over the edge again. “So we’re supposed to jump onto clouds based on what... the song of the wind?” As if responding to her question, a gentle breeze began to stir around us. It was a particularly unique breeze, honestly, one that carried notes. It was faint at first, then more distinct. A melody wove through the air, ethereal yet haunting.

Almost sounded like ‘Never Gonna Give You Up’ by the king of internet troll jingles.

‘Wait just one second,’ I said as I noticed the runes on the stone faintly light up. ‘The tune of the wind, and these symbols…’

I had a sudden realization as I listened more carefully to the melody. ‘Guys, the notes in the song correspond to the runes! Look how they light up in sequence with the tune!’

“You’re right!” Lun chimed. “Each note activates a different rune.” He studied the pattern more intently. “And I think... yes, we must repeat the sequence exactly, to reveal the correct path?”

“Is that a question or a statement?” Nolan asked.

“I am pretty sure,” Lun said as the last soft note in the wind tapered off, and all of a sudden, the symbols began to elevate slightly off the surface.

The symbols hovered just above the platform, glowing with a soft blue light. With careful movements and instructions decoded by Nolan from me, Lun reached out and touched one of them. It chimed, producing the same note we’d heard in the wind’s melody.

“I think I understand,” he said, excitement building in his voice. “We need to play the sequence correctly, like a musical puzzle.”

The rest of my party missed the first few notes, but I had been paying attention to the runes. The perks of being easily distracted. With my help, Lun began pressing the runes in order, each one producing a clear, resonant tone that echoed across the empty sky. As the final note rang out, a shimmering path of light appeared, connecting our platform to the first cloud.

‘Lun, you’re a genius!’ I cheered inwardly.

‘Hey, you did good too, short fry.’

“Oh my god, it actually worked,” Typha gasped.

Lun chuckled to himself. “You all are lucky the dragon here and I have a sharp memory.”

Nolan was the first to test if the path of green light was solid enough to walk on. I was ready to reel him in with my vines if it wasn’t. Once he made it to the cloud, the path of light turned into stone, similar to the one we were standing on. The rest of us followed him, and that was where I noticed this new stone contained similar runes like the last one.

“Once we break the next puzzle, all three of you move ahead,” Nolan suggested.

‘What? Why?’

“I’ll stay behind a cloud or two, just in case bullshit happens. I’ll have a good view of what’s ahead. This way, if there are any problems, I can help from a distance.”

‘That makes sense, I guess,’ I admitted, though I didn’t like the idea of splitting up even slightly.

As we progressed from cloud to cloud, each platform presented a new melody to decipher. The patterns grew increasingly complex—some notes lingering longer, others in rapid succession. Lun’s forehead began to sweat as he worked through each musical puzzle. Then annoying Typha started giving the lizardkin suggestions when he paused on a few notes, throwing off his concentration.

It didn’t help that some of the notes were being blurred between harsh winds without notes. By the twenty-fourth cloud, the wind began to intensify. What had been a gentle breeze now whipped around us, threatening to tip us over. The melodies became harder to hear, notes scattering in the howling gusts. Typha began panicking more than Lun, which made him panic even more.

“I need all of you to shut up!” Lun shouted over the wind. “I demand absolute silence!”

We pressed forward, each step more treacherous than the last. The paths to the next clouds tilted in higher elevations, making it easier for the winds to rock us.

I didn’t realize I was bouncing up and down impatiently as Lun worked. The anxiety of this trial was through the roof. Then suddenly, Nolan called out to us at the sound of faint rumbling. “You need to hurry over there,” he shouted urgently. “The platforms are beginning to crumble!”

What? Crumble?!

As we completed the umpteenth musical sequence, the platform beneath us began to rumble. Without warning, it started to disintegrate at the edges!

What type of Super Mario falling platform mechanic was this?!?!

“It’s timing us now!” Typha shouted. “Hurry!”

‘Nolan, come on!’ I called out to him, realizing the platforms we left were falling in large chunks of stone as he ran toward us. He was cutting it dangerously close, my heart leaping out of my chest. The next tune was ringing, and Lun was kneeling on the ground with his fingers tapping the air, trying to make mental notes off memory. But our luck with this trial was thinning. Everyone was on edge, especially Typha, who threw herself into me when the next gust of harsh wind blew into us hard.

I lost my footing, and my awareness. The fear of falling delayed my reaction time, my head turning over my shoulder to see my body plummeting south. I used [Vine Slash] to grab the edge of the platform, but its length was too short! I lost my breath as my claws scrambled uselessly against the air. Time seemed to slow as I fell, the platform receding above me. Wind rushed past me, drowning out the panicked shouts from above. This couldn’t be happening—not after everything we’d survived!
