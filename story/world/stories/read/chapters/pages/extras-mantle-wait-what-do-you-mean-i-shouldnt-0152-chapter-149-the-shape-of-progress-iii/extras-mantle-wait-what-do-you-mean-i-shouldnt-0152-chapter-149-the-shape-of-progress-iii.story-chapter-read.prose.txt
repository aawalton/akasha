Reyana now found herself in Joe's quarters because her dad outright denied telling her more about what she needed to do with her path… her mantle. And as much as she hated letting Joe have the upper hand, it was undeniable that he was indeed an expert and had all the knowledge to give her solid advice.

“Ahem.” Joe cleared his throat, breaking Reyana out of her thoughts, and leaned back in his chair, his signature eccentric grin softening. "So. I’m assuming you're finally giving your future some serious thought?"

Reyana crossed her arms. Her jaw tightened, a sign of restrained stress she couldn't quite hide.

"I'm serious, Joe," she said, forcing the words past the lingering tightness in her throat. "Before we came to Vienna, my entire plan was just... suppressing the damn thing." She looked away, staring at the walls. "Suppress my mantle so I didn't wither everyone I touched. But now? After watching Jin and the others? Playing it safe feels like a good way to die. I don't want to do that anymore."

"That is good to hear," Joe said. He stretched his arms overhead, joints popping loudly in the quiet room. He swept a thick stack of vellum aside, making space on the cluttered workbench, and gestured toward the small living area.

Reyana took a seat on the edge of the sofa. She eyed the scattered papers he’d just shoved away. Intricate arrays and complex mana-circuitry spilled across the pages. "What’s with the diagrams? They look like weapon blueprints."

"Because they are," Joe said. He reached and procured a ceramic plate holding a thick sandwich and a glass of juice out of nowhere and handed them to her.

Reyana stared at the food. "You always have an absurd amount of food on you, and not just the same type. When do you even prep this?"

"Cooking," Joe said, resting his elbows on his knees, "is one of the few passions that keep my sanity intact when I'm knee-deep in severed limbs and lords and cultists and gods and, well, the usual bunch.

He smiled. “I'll tell you the same thing I tell your father: pick a hobby. Do anything to get your mind to relax."

Reyana’s nose wrinkled in blatant distaste. A hobby. Right. Because knitting was exactly what she needed while her mantle demanded constant attention.

But she looked down at her gloved hands and her clothes. For years, she had made sure to cover every part of her from the neck down, but now, ever since meeting Jin and Trish… that suffocating weight was slowly turning into a new source of strength. Maybe she would try cooking. Assuming her bare hands didn't immediately turn the vegetables into grey sludge.

She picked up the sandwich and took a bite. The roasted meat was perfectly spiced. She chewed, annoyed by how much better she instantly felt.

Bzzzt.

A sharp vibration from Joe's tempad broke the quiet.

Reyana swallowed her bite. "Something important?"

"Nope." Joe silenced the device without breaking eye contact. "So, what exactly do you need help with? Make it quick. I need to finish those schematics and start crafting. Almost all the gear of you... wonder trio is hanging by a single thread."

"Let me guess: Rudy’s the worst?" Reyana guessed.

"All of his gear is on its deathbed. Jin’s armor took a beating, but it’ll recover. His gun, however, is a different beast entirely." Joe exhaled a long, tired breath.

Reyana set the sandwich down.

"I won’t take much of your time. I need to know what skills to mark and what progression path to take," she said, her violet eyes locking onto his. "Dad told me he trusts your judgment. He said you are far more versed in my archetype than he is."

"That is true," Joe agreed, offering a grin that managed to be both entirely pleasant and utterly lacking in modesty.

Reyana rolled her eyes, but she leaned forward, resting her elbows on her knees to give him her full attention.

"The foundational decision—the very first of many you have to make—is defining your archetype," Joe said. He picked up a stylus from the workbench, tapping it rhythmically against a blank sheet of drafting parchment. "Take me, for example. At my core, I am a mage—an arcanist, to be precise—but I hybridize into a martial path, using the spear. Jin, on the other hand, is..."

Joe paused. He blinked, a look of profound, tired exasperation passing over his features.

Reyana knew exactly what he was picturing. They were both trapped with the same mental image of Jin violently taping chaotic, conflicting sorceries to bullets and blowing his own arms off to harvest the energies he shouldn’t.

"Right," Joe sighed, shaking his head. "We won't use Jin as a baseline for anything sane. But theoretically, he's a Mage hybridizing into firearms. Rudy is the easiest to tell. Like your father, he is a pure martial archetype. He hits things until they stop moving."

Joe pointed the stylus directly at her. "You, on the other hand, are a mess."

Reyana stiffened.

"You’ve spent the past few years violently suppressing your own path," Joe continued, raising his arm before she could mutter a retort. "And I agree with you. Your situation was on the extreme side, and there’s nothing that could be done. But this doesn’t change the fact that you have indeed starved your path. You rely on a sword. And you are exceptional with it, no doubt. But if you keep doing what you are doing, you will stay exactly that—exceptional. You will never reach Master rank."

Reyana’s jaw tightened. She kept her mouth shut, but the sting of the assessment burned. She had Elite mastery. She was barely out of her teens and fought against Order IV abominations. Calling her 'pretty good' felt like a slap, but she knew better than to interrupt him.

"Elite is the absolute ceiling for how far raw technique can take you," Joe explained. "Forms, arts, combat manuals—they can only do so much. To break into the Master ranks, you have to manifest your own conceptual understanding of the weapon. The most famous example is sword intent."

Reyana nodded slowly, piecing it together. "Like Aura manifestation? I have heard Dad once being able to project hundreds of swords."

"A sub-branch of it, yes, and Sal at his peak did hundreds of thousands of swords. It was a nightmare." Joe said with a sad smile. "Anyway. It's a fancy, metaphysical way of asking a simple question: Why do you wield the blade? What does it represent to you? As you climb the orders or ranks, power stops being about raw accumulation and starts being more metaphysical."

He tossed the stylus onto the table. It landed with a sharp clack that echoed in the quiet room.

"But let's shelve the metaphysics for a moment and look at your actual build. Correct me if I'm wrong: You have a formed second circle. That's solid. But you only know three, maybe four actual spells. You haven't bothered to learn more. You haven't sought out a grimoire or studied spellcraft theory."

Reyana bit the inside of her cheek. The metallic taste of blood grounded her, but she couldn't refute the assessment. It was entirely accurate.

"You have Elite mastery in swords, which is genuinely phenomenal," Joe pressed on, holding up a single finger. "It is your primary weapon. But—and this is a massive 'but'—why is your Mantle still your primary source of damage? If you intend to forever keep it shackled?"

Reyana frowned, her violet eyes narrowing.

"Why?" Joe asked, his crimson eyes locking onto hers. "You hide it behind artifacts and seals. Yet the moment a fight turns bad, you release those seals and let the death essence eat you alive from the inside out just to secure the kill. Why?"

Reyana didn't have an answer.

She crossed her arms tightly over her chest, burying her hands deep in the fabric of her sleeves.

"Your point?" she asked.

Joe’s smile lost its mocking edge, turning into something softer and far more annoying. "That is my point, Reyana. You are at war with yourself. You are confused, and until you untangle that knot—until you figure out exactly why you fight—I'm afraid you won't ever break into the Master ranks, much less spark the underlord flames."

He let the silence stretch. Reyana stared at the clutter on the workbench.

"But," Joe said, tapping the table, "introspection and meditation are problems for tomorrow. Let's look at the basics for today."

Reyana nodded tightly, grateful for the pivot away from her own psychology. "Yeah."

"Your archetype is strictly skill-ceiling based," Joe explained. "Your entire kit relies on meeting highly specific conditions for optimal output. You either play the long game—stacking afflictions like those death petals of yours and letting the enemy rot from the inside out—or you detonate those afflictions early for massive burst damage."

He leaned back in his chair. "Both methods require precision. If you want an example of a raw-damage build, look at your father or Rudy—”

“—hitting things with a large piece of metal until the problem stops moving.” Reyana completed. “I know, I know.”

“Good,” Joe nodded. “Which brings us to your empty skill slots."

"Jin mentioned I should get a mental enhancement core," Reyana said.

"He is right. Your current skillset demands a brain capable of processing a dozen lethal variables simultaneously without dropping concentration," Joe said. "Right now, you're doing that manually because you are up against one or a few enemies. If you want to specialize as a duelist, keeping your focus on one-on-one fighting, you can forsake getting this core, but I’d still suggest you get it. You're good, but good gets you killed when you are outnumbered, outmuscled, and outwitted.”

Reyana nodded slowly. The logic was sound. "So, the obvious first move is a mental enhancement type skill. Something that lets my processing speed keep pace with my afflictions."

"Yeah, they come in all flavors, like observation, weak point analysis—"

A sharp buzz vibrated against the wooden table. Joe glanced down at his tempad, thumbing the screen open with one hand.

"Who is it?" Reyana asked.

Joe hit send and flipped the device face-down. "Rudy. Wants advice."

"About?"

A teasing smirk touched the corners of Joe's mouth. "Most likely the exact same thing as you. Give him a minute."

Reyana scowled. "Well, while you have him on the line, tell him to mark a proper healing one. One of these days, that's going to stop being annoying and start being a regular at the clinic.”

"Noted," Joe laughed, the sound genuine this time. "But right now, we are here for you."

Reyana exhaled a sharp breath through her nose—the absolute closest she was willing to come to conceding the point out loud. "Fine. You mentioned earlier you needed my status? How do I do the system friend thing again?"

"Just accept the prompt," Joe said.

"What prom—" Reyana started, but the words died in her throat as a translucent blue screen bloomed directly in her field of vision. "Oh. Done."

Half a second later, a matching holographic panel projected into the air in front of Joe.

« CONNECTION ESTABLISHED TO USER (JOE) »

« ADDED JOE AS A FRIEND. »

Reyana’s violet eyes widened as the interface updated. "Wow. Okay, I've got—there are more tabs now. Wait."

She looked up at him, her gaze suddenly sharpening with the profound disbelief of someone who had just noticed a blatant detail everyone else had apparently accepted for weeks.

"Your actual verified name is just Joe?"

Joe only offered an unapologetic smile.

Reyana swiped at the interface hovering in front of her. "What does this whole friend feature actually do? Can I add Jin and Rudy?"

Joe leaned back against the faded upholstery of the sofa. "You can. Fair warning, though—having someone on your list means you can track their location, and they can track yours. It is not a one-way street."

"And what else?"

"Status requests," Joe listed, his crimson eyes tracking the air as if reading his own invisible menu. "Quest sharing, assuming both parties consent and the quest allows for it. Some passive notifications. For example, you will know immediately if someone on your list dies or ranks up."

He delivered the last sentence with the usual casual tone, but something in the pause afterward made Reyana think he'd learned that fact the hard way at some point.

"Oh," Reyana said, quieter.

She didn't press the issue, and Joe didn't offer any elaboration. Some silences did plenty of explaining on their own.

"Alright," Joe said. “Tell me about the last few days. What exactly have you been doing, and where do you think you are falling short?"

That got her talking. Reyana despised small talk, but handing her an actual tactical problem to dissect engaged her coping mechanisms. She walked him through the outpost raids. She described the visceral toll of pressing her bare hands against the dome's intake rune, violently pouring her death-essence into the array for two uninterrupted minutes. She explained the lingering aftermath.

Then, she told him all about Trish.

"She just... grabbed me," Reyana said. She crossed her arms tightly over her chest, burying her fingers deep in her sleeves. "She guided my hands through the motion. She used my mother's voice to do it, Joe, and for a moment I was sure it was her."

"The very same daughter of the Darkened One that our resident boss decided to make a soul pact with," Joe said, shaking his head.

"Yeah."

"Jin is Jin, I suppose," Joe sighed. "I'm just establishing that we are talking about the exact same unhinged individual. But she taught you something real, didn't she? You are channeling much cleaner today than you were a week ago."

She nodded. "She showed me how to let the death-essence move through my channels instead of constantly fighting to hold it back. She called me a conduit instead of a cup." A heavy pause stretched in the quiet room. "It worked. I absolutely hate that it worked."

"Welcome to the metaphysical aspect of the higher Orders," Joe said dryly. "Sometimes enlightenment comes wrapped in something that makes you want to violently throw up."

He held out a hand, his fingers tapping the air. "I'm going to request your status. Accept it."

Reyana eyed his outstretched hand. "Do I get to see yours in exchange, or is this strictly a one-way mirror arrangement?"

"You are far too young to see these numbers," Joe teased, his signature grin returning. "Get to Underlord rank first."

Reyana snorted, rolling her eyes as she tapped the glowing acceptance prompt in her vision.

Reyana finished reading her status. She had spent so much time mulling over it, and yet nothing much had come out of it apart from the pride of seeing her ranks and efforts.

Joe's expression didn't change.

That was the tell, really. Normally, the man's face was doing three things at once—a lazy smirk, a raised eyebrow, a twitch of amusement—even when he was just sitting back in a chair. Now? Total stillness. His crimson eyes were locked on the glowing text, his features freezing into the clinical, deadpan mask of a veteran ranker staring at a fatal wound.

Reyana shifted her weight on the sofa, burying her hands deeper into her long sleeves. She knew exactly which line he was reading.

[Master] Undying Heart (201).

Master rank. Already. She would be happy if it wasn’t the bane of her existence.

She bit the inside of her cheek. That wasn't a normal racial ability, and they both knew it. It was a slow-motion catastrophe that was very slowly turning her into an undead.

She waited for Joe to crack a joke, to deflect the tension, to do something. But the silence in the safe room simply stretched, thick and suffocating. He knew exactly what that number meant for her life expectancy.

"Okay," Joe said, breaking the silence. “You definitely need a neural enhancement. Ask Jin for specifics, but you ideally want something that’s closer to his version. You need some to keep your racial ability in check.”

“I’ll talk to him," Reyana said. “Next?”

Joe held up a hand, a fraction of his teasing smirk creeping back onto his face. "Before we map out the rest, we need to address the elephant in the room. You are going to ask about skill combinations eventually, so let's handle it now before you spend the next three days stewing in silence and glaring at the back of Jin's head."

"I was going to ask that," Reyana said flatly.

"Skill combinations have been around since the dawn of the empires," Joe explained. "But here is the ugly truth nobody outside the old bloodlines and houses likes to admit, much like cultivation manuals. Almost none of the rankers even dare to touch their essence heart, much less thier mantles. So the only way to achieve skill combinations is through legacies. They get passed down and hoarded like family silver because that is exactly what they are." He spread his hands. "I know maybe ten combinations in total. Every single one is built around my specific domain, developed over more years than I care to admit."

"So these are just secrets," Reyana said, her violet eyes narrowing. "Sitting in vaults."

"Every noble house on this side of the continent guards its secrets like crown jewels," Joe laughed, the sound short and dry. "Well. They did. Right up until Jin showed up and essentially made a video guide for dummies."

Reyana's frown sharpened into something dangerous. "When exactly was I getting let in on this secret? If you and Dad knew this the entire time—"

"You weren't ready for it until today," Joe cut in, his tone brokering no argument. "That isn't me being condescending, Reyana. It is a fact."

He tapped his knuckles against the table, forcing her to look at him. "Combination theory only becomes something you can physically touch once one of your core skills hits Master rank; at least that’s the known way of doing it. How would I or anyone even expect Jin to go and reinvent the wheel.”

Reyana rolled her eyes.

“Also, we weren't gatekeeping you for the sake of politics. Reaching Master elevates your internal essence channels, reinforcing them to the point where you can actually hold the volatile structure of a combined mark without it collapsing your essence heart. Try to force a synergy before that threshold, and you are just setting your own essence heart on fire for absolutely zero reward."

The anger drained out of Reyana, replaced by a sudden, heavy clarity.

"And Dad knew," she murmured.

"Sal has been hunting down combination legacies specifically for you the day your manifestation took hold," Joe said, watching the words land. "Why do you think we actually took the Silver Spire contract? The contractor had a hint of a legacy, and we all traded our protection for that information. You were the only reason that deal got made."

Reyana went perfectly still. She stared at the scattered blueprints on the workbench, but the intricate ink lines blurred. Something deep behind her ribs shifted—a heavy, locked door cracking open just a fraction. Her father communicated entirely in grunts and brutal training regimens. He had never once mentioned the expedition was for her sake.

"He never said a word," she said, her voice tight.

"He's not big on talking," Joe noted gently. "You might have noticed."

"I've noticed." She swallowed hard, blinking away the sudden, frustrating sting in her eyes.

Joe let the silence stretch for exactly one beat. “If you want him to talk, take tips from Rudy… how in hell he got the old fu—I mean old bat to teach him is beyond me.”

Reyana laughed.

"Anyway. Focus. We have a build to finish," he said. "Right now, your mobility is handled. [Shadow Shift] and [Shadows Link] give you plenty of battlefield coverage, and your sword mastery is already Elite. That leaves three open slots to cover what you are lacking."

He held up three fingers. "Slot one goes to a neural accelerator. Non-negotiable. That leaves two."

Reyana pulled herself together, grateful for the pivot to raw math. "And those go to?"

"One sustain-type," Joe explained. "Healing, raw defense, or damage mitigation. Something that keeps you breathing when an engagement runs longer than you planned. The final slot goes to a damage amplifier. Or, if you want to live dangerously, you skip the sustenance and take two amplifiers."

He shrugged, leaning back in his chair. "Personally? Given the slow-motion catastrophe sitting in your racial abilities, I would strongly lean toward the sustain. You are already carrying a body that actively fights you. Give it a skill that fights back on your side."

Reyana looked down at her hands. These were the same hands she had spent months hiding, terrified of the rot they could inflict, right up until Trish had grabbed her wrists and violently demonstrated a different way to channel the decay. A conduit, not a cup.

"Sustain," Reyana decided, her tone flat and resolute. "Fine, any idea on what types?"

He was scrolling through skill cores when a sharp buzz vibrated the tempad.

Both of their tempads chimed at the exact same time.

Joe flipped his screen over. Reyana checked hers a half-second later.

JIN: Core training floors. Bring Reyana if she’s with you.

That was the entire message. No context. Just the demand.

Reyana looked up from the screen, meeting Joe's crimson eyes.

"That isn't a 'come see something interesting' message," Reyana said slowly, her brain already calculating threat vectors. "That's a different kind."

"Yeah." Joe was already on his feet. "I remember telling him not to do anything stupid. You wanna bet if he is doing stupid.”

“No,” Reyana shook her head. “I know I’ll lose that bet. He is definitely up to no good.

“Then let’s find out. I needed to run these designs by him anyway.” Joe snapped his fingers, and a door manifested in front of them.
