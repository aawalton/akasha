For what feels like nearly an hour, I sit perched on my branch like a smug woodland gremlin, staring up at the night sky. The stars chatter endlessly, loudly debating the philosophy of bikini armor, casually discussing cosmic horrors that could erase planets by sneezing, and then—deeply—arguing whether turkey or ham is superior on a sandwich.

The answer is obviously both. Some things require neither debate nor hesitation.

Their voices overlap in chaotic harmony, each one alien yet… familiar. Family, even if they aren’t mine—not the old ones, not the ones I left behind.

The thought stings.

My ears soften downward for a heartbeat, then snap back up, just a bit too late. The sky explodes with panic.

“She’s sad! Emergency plan! Somebody do something cute!”

“Sparkle formation alpha—GO!!”

“Does anyone know jokes? Tell her a joke! QUICK!”

A constellation rearranges itself into what might be a starry velociraptor wearing sunglasses and flexing.

A tiny voice tries very hard to sound intimidating:

“I’ll headbutt reality on your behalf.”

Despite myself, a small laugh slips out, quiet and uneven, but real.

That only encourages them.

It takes another solid fifteen minutes to calm them down, soothe their nonsense, and promise I am not dying, broken, or emotionally collapsing again. I have long since become immune to such imperfect things, after all. Only once the sky settles back into its usual level of chaos do I finally open my status window.

My eyes widen slightly.

Mana: gone.

Wisdom and Intelligence: gone.

Trait: fully visible.

I read.

Also, it’s nighttime.

Better than daylight, actually. Every shadow is sharp, every leaf etched in silver light. Starlight clings to everything like dew.

There may never again be a reason to hunt during the day.

“No no no, ride a deer into battle.”

“Useless. Every single one of you.”

Their collective amusement is palpable, like laughter made of light.

So I ignore them.

Temporarily. Out of principle.

Humming a cheerful little tune, I skip down the grassy path—purely because walking normally is boring—and delight sparks through the sky at the sight, even if I am not listening to them.

Obviously.

Then—

A slime.

I brighten.

Before thought can catch up to instinct, I’m already moving, feet silent, breath steady, body low. I dart through the trees, closing the distance in seconds.

And without slowing, without hesitation—

I attack.

“Slash!”

Because dying to a slime one level higher than expected wouldn’t just be tragic—it would be humiliating enough that I’d probably perish again in the afterlife from residual shame.

Eight hours have passed.

So now there are three working theories.

One: level scaling happens at midnight.

Two: monsters scale along with me.

Three: I've wandered into a higher level section of the forest.

No way to test yet, so they remain acceptable guesses.

The leaves are cool. The night is calm. The sky hums above me, distant and unfamiliar.

For the first time in a long, long while…

I’m actually looking forward to waking up.

With a small, content smile, I close my eyes, tail curling around me—

—and drift into sleep beneath the watching stars.

Jack stalked through the forest with the swagger of someone who believed the world—old or new—now belonged to him. Leaves and branches crunched quietly beneath the boots of his group, but stealth hardly mattered. Monsters were weak, people were terrified, and the forest was already theirs.

Tyler’s grating voice chimed beside him.

“Got that right, bunch of losers who exist just to farm points before dying for someone useful.”

Jack’s jaw twitched, having been half-listening to the conversation behind him. Tyler—now an elf, courtesy of absurd cosmic lottery—had become even more insufferable than usual. The pointed ears and extra stats hadn’t improved his personality, only inflated it. Technically, he was the second strongest of their group by virtue of elves getting a ridiculous ten points per race level, half of which were free points. Too bad he invested all of them into Vitality, making himself nearly useless in a battle due to a lack of Dexterity.

Jack fantasized briefly about attacking him anyway, race advantage or not, but forced the thought aside. Not worth the argument. Yet.

Instead, he shifted his gaze to Damian, a thickset warrior with brawler shoulders and a grin that showed too many teeth.

“So,” Jack asked, voice lazy and edged with anticipation, “what do you think we’ll kill next? Monster or person?”

Damian considered, tapping the hilt of his sword with rhythmic excitement before that same feral grin stretched across his face.

“I’m hoping it’s a person.”

Jack chuckled, sharp and humorless.

People gave more points. People begged. People broke.

And nothing in his entire life—Earth or Asteria— felt as good as the moment when someone realized screaming wouldn’t help.

Power. Absolute power. This world was paradise.

His thoughts broke when Ryan raised a hand, a silent command the group instantly obeyed. Ryan didn’t need to be loud. He didn’t need to posture. His perception skill told him everything before anyone else even noticed something was wrong.

“There’s someone ahead,” Ryan murmured. “Not moving. Could be dead. Could be asleep.”

Jack grinned.

Perfect.

He motioned the group forward, and they slipped into the undergrowth. After only a short walk, they saw her, and all four of them went still.

A small girl lay quietly on a wide branch overlooking the center of a clearing, sleeping as though the forest itself were a bed and the world beyond it simply didn’t exist.

The clearing was small and unnatural, grass worn thin in places, moss clinging stubbornly near the roots of surrounding trees. Shrubs and low branches ringed it tightly, forming a green wall.

The girl herself looked almost… unreal.

For a moment, none of them spoke.

She slept curled loosely on her side, her posture soft and almost childlike. One hand rested near the sword she’d brought to bed, fingers barely touching the hilt as if she had forgotten it was even there. The blade wasn’t gripped or guarded.

It simply existed within reach.

Her hair was the first thing Jack truly registered. It was long, absurdly long, spilling down the branch like a white waterfall. Sunlight filtered through the canopy and caught on it, scattering into soft highlights that made it seem faintly luminous, like it didn’t quite belong to the same world as everything else.

Her skin was pale—not sickly or fragile—but smooth and even, like porcelain warmed just enough to seem real. Untouched. Unmarred.

A pair of fox ears sat atop her head, covered in plush white fur. They twitched faintly every few breaths, small, unconscious movements that made it clear she wasn’t fully still, even in sleep. Curled loosely against her was a matching white tail, drawn close and half-wrapped around her frame, her fingers lightly buried in the fur as if it were something she reached for without thinking.

Like a comforting habit rather than a conscious pose.

Her face—

That was where the silence lingered.

Delicate, but not fragile. Soft lines, perfectly balanced, as if every feature had been placed with deliberate care. Her lips rested in the faintest hint of a neutral expression, not quite a smile, not quite empty. Long lashes cast subtle shadows across her cheeks, and her breathing was slow, even, undisturbed.

There was no tension in her brow. No strain. No sign of dreams or discomfort.

She looked…

Complete.

Like a finished piece.

Her clothes were the same as every other player from the tutorial—plain, fitted travel gear—

Except hers were clean.

Impossibly clean.

No dirt pressed into the seams. No tears from brush or bramble. No blood.

Either she moved like a ghost—

Or she was careful in a way Jack didn’t want to think about.

She looked young. Sixteen, maybe seventeen. Small. Light. Delicate.

Not threatening.

And yet—

Beautiful enough that all four of them stared.

Not the kind of beauty that invited admiration.

The kind that made something in the back of your mind go quiet.

Still.

Untouchable.

Jack recovered first, and immediately cast Inspect.
