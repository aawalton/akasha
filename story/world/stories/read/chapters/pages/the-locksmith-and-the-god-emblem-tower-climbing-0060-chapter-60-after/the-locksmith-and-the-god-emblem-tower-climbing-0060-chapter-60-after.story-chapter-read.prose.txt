How fragile we are. Harry thought as he watched the ashes of Vela and Ogien drift away outside of the Colosseum. They mingled in the wind and clashed as if even in death they kept fighting. No. The Locksmith looked more thoroughly. One of them is just trying to get away. He then grabbed a clump of ashes, the one belonging to Liam, and threw it into the middle of the arena, near the boat carcass so it couldn't bother Vela anymore.

Since arriving at the Logos Tower, he had experienced a few constants: danger, adrenaline, and Vela. He was a curious man, perhaps even too strange for the young thief's tastes, but he was his friend. He was someone who had given him advice during his worst moments. He was also someone who harbored a great deal of resentment. How could I not have seen this? Harry added inwardly. He felt that if he had cared more, perhaps things would have been different. Perhaps yes. Perhaps no. It was a guilt that lodged in his heart, just like all the others. This time, however, the feeling wasn't as strong.

"You okay?" Matthews came to his side, and now the two of them were watching the boat in the middle of the arena. "This was a really bad plan. You know that, right?"

"...What?" Harry looked confused to his brother. "Oh, right... Yeah, but it worked, didn't it?"

"So you think the hatch is somewhere in between those..."

"Metas."

"Yes." He cleared his throat. In this case, bones. "Metas. They used the hatch to attract the prisoners?"

"Probably a lot of them, tired of waiting, took their chances in trying to find the real hatch in the creature, only to be swallowed alive." Harry felt his voice weaken.

"So they're dead, right?" Matthews kept staring.

The young thief gave a chuckle.

"What?" Matthews asked, visibly irritated. "What is it?

"Nothing, it's just..." The Locksmith grinned. "Didn't know you were such a pussy. You're scared of a few pieces of wood?—"

"I... You...!" Harry's brother stomped his feet in the cold ground. "These creatures are vicious, okay? They'll devour you whole and not even spit it out. Terrifying."

Harry, his eyes lost in thought, embraced a dangerous yet poetic idea. One that clung to his mind like honey, except for its golden properties.

"...What do you think happens when they die?" The young thief watched as Vela's ashes flew away.

"The prisoners or..."

"Anyone. Everyone. What if some of the molecules that make up the Metas accidentally ended up stuck to one of Vela's ashes? What if Vela is forced to live eternally inside this loop, this Tower, because of something totally beyond his control? What if—"

"There's no point in dwelling on it, Harry," Matthews said, his voice hoarse. "We can't control everything. We can't save everyone."

"But we can!" Harry exclaimed and turned to his brother. "Otherwise; what's the point of this Tower? What are these Emblems for if not vehicles for our salvation?"

Matthews opened his mouth but found himself speechless. He sighed deeply and suddenly sat down in one of the seats in the stands, one that wasn't destroyed, at least...

"Have I told you what it was like when I used my second Emblem?" he asked.

"N-No..." Confused, the young thief sat down too, the two of them observing the arena.

"I died," the skeleton declared. "And I died. And I died again. I felt dozens of deaths at once. I thought that if I used this Emblem, since I already had bones, it would only reinforce the ones I already possessed. Unfortunately, Emblems don't always get along with their users..."

"..." Harry listened attentively.

"...They burst out of my body and rearranged my organs countless times. There's no agony like it, Harry. There's no reason for such agony. My skin was stretched and torn, only to snap back into place in a desperate attempt by my body to return to its former state. My heart seemed to liquefy as it passed through my ribcage. You can't hear or see it, courtesy of the Emblem and its warped sense of privacy, but I hear it. My heart pounding and my organs moving. Even the vibrations of my vocal cords."

"Then why did you do it?" Harry asked with a worried expression.

"I don't know. To prove something, maybe..." His voice was now trembling. "I was about to get married, you know? Yeah. She was beautiful, man. Really beautiful. She was empathetic, intelligent, and courageous. She understood me, you know? She understood me in a way that only Mom understood."

"I'm sorry, Matthews," Harry said, his tone heavy. "I didn't know."

"Of course you didn't know. Nobody knew. And it was my fault. I put myself in that position."

"And she still loves you?"

"She says she does." Matthews looked away. "I can still see the same qualities I saw before, but our relationship has changed; of course it was going to change... How do you fuck a skeleton?"

The young thief let out a laugh that he tried to disguise as a cough.

"Seriously!" He said, raising his hands.

"Well, at least she can't, you know..." Harry tried to find the best way to say it. "At least she can't accuse you of never being hard."

A second of silence passed until Matthews gave a genuine laugh. Before he knew it, Harry had joined his brother in his deep laughter.

"Fuckin' hell..." Matthews said. "I thought you were supposed to be my older brother."

"I never said I was responsible."

"On that we can agree..." He chuckled.

Before they could continue, though, a woman with red hair appeared in the distance while holding a brush.

"Harry!" She yelled. "Finally, I found you!"

Her worried expression made Harry get up. What is it now...

His melancholy face, now pale from blood loss, fought against his own will to live, as if the Viking wasn't ready to die yet. He held the criminal's jaw to his stomach, trying to remove it, even against the advice of his daughter, who seemed to be crying. He shouldn't have been surprised, as of course a daughter would cry at the loss of her father, but this wasn't just anyone; it was Brun. The woman had endured so much and didn't seem to be very close to her father, perhaps due to the age difference. That said, tears slid down her cheek as she desperately wiped them away.

"Argh!" Daniel finally removed the piece just as Harry arrived. "...Finally. Imagine dying with a piece of that imbecile still inside me. I don't think I'd go to Valhalla like this..." He gave a weak laugh.

"Daniel." Harry knelt beside Brun and Manes, who also seemed to be crying more intensely. "You... What happened!?"

"What do you think, Locksmith?" He said with an uncharacteristic smile, "I ended up putting too much food in my mouth and couldn't chew. I think this was... it was going to happen for a... while." He coughed violently between sentences, his voice with a third of the presence it had before.

"Isn't there anything we can do?" Harry asked Brun, who seemed to straighten her back at the sound. "Maybe you could use your Emblem to—"

"No," she said dryly. "There's nothing that can be done. My father will die."

"...!" Harry looked horrified at the older woman. "How can you say that in front of—"

"It's alright, young man..." Daniel held Harry's hand, a rather strange gesture coming from the Viking. "I prefer direct answers to lies. That just means she got at least one thing from me... hahaha..."

Harry squeezed Daniel's hand tightly, which seemed to elicit a reaction from the man. He certainly didn't do it to feel stronger, since he wouldn't take any pleasure in taking advantage of a man on his deathbed. He did it to show Daniel that he respected his strength; he knew he could endure it.

Manes stood up and went into the darkness of one of the nearby tunnels. Brun followed him shortly after.

"Remember what you said to me?" Harry said, now that they were alone. "When you intercepted me between the games?"

"...Yes." He replied. God... his voice is so weak.

"Thank you." Harry smiled at him. "Really. I always thought you hated me, and maybe you do, but I appreciate the reality check; I needed it."

"I don't hate you, Locksmith." The Viking said, removing his hand from his wound. "In my tribe, we'd call you 'Anchor'. You are our anchor. You're what keeps us together... through all this... madness."

"Call me Harry." The young thief said. "Only my enemies call me Locksmith."

"Certainly, Harry." The Viking looked at the sky, his eyes — which were now losing their opacity as if a weight were being released from his body — reflecting an endless blue sky.

"How do you think he'll handle it?" Harry nodded toward the tunnel. "I still find it strange to see you two together..."

"He reminds me a lot of myself at that... age." Daniel coughed up blood again. "Rebellious. Out of control. Frustrated. Erratic. All the qualities of a great warrior. If they are honed, of course... But he will survive. I would hope he could apply that discipline somewhere... unfortunately, we're all stuck here, aren't we? But it's not him I'm... worried about, but rather—"

"Daniel!" Goldman floated to the body with a worried look. "I can't believe it!"

"I can't believe I'm still going to die before you..."

"If I could, I would give my life for you without a single complaint." He said, now kneeling as well. "You know that, don't you?"

"Of course I know..." The Viking coughed. "And I still think it's one of your worst qualities."

"So now it's no longer a weakness!?" Daniel let out a laugh that seemed to warm Daniel's heart. "It's good that we agree now!"

"A quality can also be a weakness, you know?" But Daniel gave in to the few laughs his body could produce.

"...Are you in pain, friend?" Goldman asked, scanning around to see if he could find any way to ease Daniel's pain.

"No." The Viking's smile seemed to fade. What took over was not an expression of discontent or anger as he usually had, but one that Harry had never seen on the Viking's face; terror.

"I'm afraid. I'm afraid of what comes next. I have my... beliefs, but I don't know what the Tower did to me. I don't know if I'm worthy of glory. Give me strength, Goldman. Give me your strength."

Daniel squeezed Goldman's hands tightly as he writhed to keep from shedding a tear.

"Have I told you about my crew?" Goldman comforted him with one of his characteristic smiles. "Or about my beloved? Or perhaps about what happened when we were captured!?"

"...Never." He said.

"I'm sure they weren't as absurd or violent as yours, since you've always been the stronger of the two of us, but they can also bring you entertainment!"

And even though Harry thought Goldman had lied about their strength, he thought it best to withdraw. He stood up and left the two to have their last moments while leaning against the wall, next to a charming young woman with red hair.

"Do you think we can still make it?" she asked, holding Harry's hand. "I mean, with everything that's happened now..."

"I..." Harry thought about lying to her, but he couldn't find the strength. "I don't know. I hope everything works out, but you know how this always ends, don't you? What I can promise you is that, even in death, I will be with you."

Madeline turned and smiled at the Locksmith, a young man with long, black hair. "That's enough for me."

Suffice to say that talking was no longer in their minds.
