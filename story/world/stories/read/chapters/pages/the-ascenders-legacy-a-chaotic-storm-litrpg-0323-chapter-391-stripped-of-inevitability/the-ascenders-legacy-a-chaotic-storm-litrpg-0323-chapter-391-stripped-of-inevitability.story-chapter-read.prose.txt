Lysera's words echoed heavily in the silence that followed, and for a long moment, Aodhán simply stared at her in confusion.

Then he blinked. "What?"

The triplets exchanged glances, and Lysera inhaled deeply, the trepidation in her features obvious as she repeated herself.

"I said I want you to make me your underling."

But Aodhán's confusion hadn't stemmed from hearing troubles. He had heard her perfectly the first time.

His confusion stemmed entirely from the shock of the request.

Being an underling—or choosing one—was not a trivial matter. Neither did people simply go around offering themselves as underlings to someone else.

Despite the obvious benefits attached to the position, being an underling was a lifetime commitment. An eternal vow to stand beneath a Special, placing their needs above your own. Their wants. Their protection. Their very existence.

It was a bond deeper than that of a brother. More binding than that of a friend.

And though, thankfully, Crixis had never been forced to experience the darker aspects of the life he had chosen, Nymera's and Ashscar's underlings had not been so fortunate.

That was the true reason for Aodhán's shock, alongside the simple fact that it was the very last thing he had expected her to ask for.

Still reeling from the request, he glanced at Lilian and Crixis—both of whom looked just as surprised as he felt—before glancing back at Lysera.

He shook his head a moment later. "I'm sorry, but I do not need another underling. Is there nothing else you want?"

"No," Lysera shook her head, arms folded stoically behind her back. "That is what I want."

Aodhán hesitated, but before he could respond, Lysera continued. "We were all initiated together. At the same time. And while you were extraordinary even then—" her gaze drifted toward Crixis. "He wasn't."

Aodhán followed her gaze to find Crixis scowling. Lysera continued regardless.

"I am an inheritor," she said plainly. "I should be stronger than him. Yet standing here, I feel lesser."

Crixis's scowl deepened.

"Not only has his path changed, but he exudes an aura that rivals my own." She looked back at Aodhán. "That is not normal. It is your doing.”

“And you want the same.” Aodhán stated.

“I want the chance,” Lysera replied immediately. "I want access to treasures I can only reach in my dreams. Opportunities I can barely glimpse from afar." She took in a steadying breath. "I want to witness ascensions with my own eyes. To stand where history is being made instead of hearing about it afterwards."

She took an instinctive step forward, her expression urging. "I have potential that is wasting away here in the core sect." She glanced at Crixis, and for the first time, bitterness tinged her words. "I don't know what he did during the initiation that made you choose him, but I know I can do better if given the same chance—"

"I think you've made your point," Aodhán cut in with a glare. "Underlings have access to nice things. You want in."

"No." Lysera shook her head immediately.

"Your underling has access to nice things." She gestured toward Crixis. "He is thriving. And it's because of you."

"Stop pointing at me," Crixis growled at her, his expression twisted in irritation.

Aodhán sighed, glaring at Lysera in agreement. "Stop pointing at him."

A beat of silence passed as Lysera's gaze darted between the two of them before coming to rest on Aodhán with a small smirk.

“You know, I see you a lot,” she said quietly. “In the outer sect, making your way to the Rift verge to train him.” Her gaze drifted to Crixis, and though she didn’t point, she didn’t have to "I've seen the state he comes back in afterwards. Each time stronger and better than the last."

She paused. "How many Specials do you know who spend their time personally training their underlings?"

"It isn't their job," Aodhán corrected.

"And yet you do it." She held Aodhán's gaze. "He's thriving because of you. I want the chance to thrive too."

For a long moment, Aodhán simply stared at her, unsure how to respond.

On one hand, her words were undeniably flattering. There was obviously admiration there, and to be honest, Aodhán liked that. It was also far more restrained than Crixis's own admiration, which occasionally bordered on reverence.

He doubted Lysera would ever call him Senior Brother.

But on the other hand, her words had revealed exactly what she wanted from him. The true reason behind her request, and it was selfish.

She didn't care for him like Crixis did. She simply wanted the opportunities that association with him could get her.

And that, Aodhán did not like.

Still, if he hoped to obtain the knowledge he'd come for, then he would have to refuse her carefully.

Exhaling slowly, he turned toward Lilian. “What do you suggest, Elder Vaust?”

Lilian stilled, as though the question itself had caught her off guard. But she recovered quickly, squaring her shoulders as her gaze settled on Lysera. Silence stretched between them for a moment before Lilian spoke.

"Let Lysera fight for it." Her words were firm. Decisive. "If she wants to become an underling so badly, then she'll have to fight Crixis for the position."

Crixis whipped around to stare at her, and Aodhán blinked at the absurd suggestion.

Despite all Crixis had gained these past few months, he was still a normal cultivator while Lysera was an inheritor. Not only that, he was also still in the evolved class while Lysera had broken past it into the advanced class.

Should Crixis lose the duel, he would effectively be saddled with Lysera as an underling.

Crixis reached the same conclusion then, and his eyes widened. "I… I don't think that's a good idea."

"Me neither," Lysera added quickly, her gaze snapping toward Lilian. "No offense, Elder Vaust, but my path isn't particularly suited for combat."

Aodhán twisted to look at her immediately, his eyes narrowing as he realized the game Lysera was playing.

Cunning.

True, her path wasn't a combat-oriented one. But nobody who was truly weak would ever have made it this high into the ranks of the core sect.

Nobody.

Lysera was playing a weakness card. She wanted them to underestimate her.

Lilian’s gaze narrowed knowingly. “You will not be fighting for his position, Lysera. You will simply be proving yourself worthy to stand beside him as a second underling.”

Aodhán glanced at Lilian, her explanation easing his concerns somewhat. Then he looked back at Lysera, a decision forming in his mind.

"This duel," he began carefully, "will not determine whether I take you on as an underling. It will simply give me a chance to see what kind of cultivator you are, how you fight, and whether there's anything I can actually teach you."

His expression hardened. "If there isn't, then I won't be taking you on, and you'll have to ask for something else. If you can't accept that, then I'll find my answers elsewhere—even if I have to leave the sect to do it."

He met her gaze. "What do you say?"

Lysera stared at him for a long moment, seemingly weighing her options. Then her gaze drifted to Crixis's boots.

Aodhán saw the exact moment resolve crystallized in her eyes.

She nodded. "Let's do it."

"Alright," Aodhán waved a hand and crafted a dueling ring right there in the middle of the core sect, liquid ruin settling within his spirit as he severed the tendrils tethering it faintly to his fingers. "Whenever you're ready."

They flew up to the ring a moment later, and it didn't take long for a crowd of spectators to form, lightning platform crackling across the heavens as hundreds of core sect disciples surrounded them, eager excitement on their faces.

Aodhán and Lilian stood at the edge of the ring, now surrounded by some of the strongest individuals in the sect. Cultivators so strong and confident in their strength that Varéc's massive form hadn't scared them away.

One such person was a man called Orpheus Al'ruhn, an apparent descendant of High Elder Zarxan himself. The man was at the 76th tier and cultivating a path that had to do with resonance and electromagnetism, judging by the faint thrum of his poorly leashed aura.

The man stood beside him now, having appeared seemingly out of nowhere, but from how quickly the space had been vacated for him, Aodhán knew he wasn't a man of little influence in the core sect.

"What a spectacular show you've put up, special disciple Aodhán." he said as Crixis and Lysera finally entered the ring. "Who could have predicted such excitement on a day like this?"

"No one, I'm sure," Aodhán replied dryly and the man laughed.

"Indeed, it is a pleasure to meet with you. I've always wanted to. Ever since the initiation." He grinned. "Did you know that High Elder Al'ruhn is my great-great-great—"

"Grandfather. Yes." Aodhán sighed. "I believe you've mentioned that thrice now. But if you don't mind I'd like to concentrate on the duel now."

He turned away immediately and looked to Lilian, who nodded. She moved to stand at the center of the duel, and in that moment, she looked nothing like the friend he'd come to know, but a Mythic through and through.

She raised a hand and silence descended immediately.

"My name is Elder Lilian Vaust and I shall be overseeing this duel between Core sect disciple Lysera Axilas-Vaust and Core sect disciple Crixis Marcellan."

She paused, her gaze roaming the crowd before continuing. "This is not a battle to the death. A person loses if they are thrown out of the ring or sustain any grievous injuries. Surrender is also an option." She glanced down at Lysera and Crixis then. "Any use of excessive, deadly force will not be tolerated. Do you understand?"

Crixis nodded easily, and after a beat of hesitation, Lysera did the same.

"Good," Lilian raised a hand again and brought it down in a swift arc. "Fight."

Two minutes earlier.

Crixis stared at Lysera Vaust with a frown, not really sure how to feel about this whole thing.

No, that was a lie. He knew exactly how he felt, but was too possessive to actually admit it.

Crixis knew how lucky he was to have been chosen by Aodhán. How amazingly privileged he was to have someone like Aodhán as a mentor.

Even the other underlings said so—not because they weren't just as lucky—but because Aodhán was different.

To him, having underlings wasn't just a right of passage or an act of goodness to help others. It was companionship, friendship, loyalty.

And Crixis had made sure to live up to it all.

And so far, he couldn't deny that it had been a pretty great ride. He wouldn't be where he was today if not for him—figuratively and literally. And as weird as Aodhán was, Crixis wanted to keep things that way. Intended to keep things that way.

Selfish or not, he wasn't quite ready to share Aodhán with anyone else yet.

Squaring his shoulders, he glanced at Elder Vaust as she raised a hand for the second time and brought it down in a swift arc.

"Fight!"

Crixis dashed forward immediately, amethyst lightning erupting around his body as the anglewalkers carried him across the distance in an instant.

He didn't need to get so close. But he wanted to end this battle as quickly as possible.

Lysera reacted at once, a glass-like barrier of condensed storm energy springing into existence between them.

Crixis was unfazed. He simply stretched a hand toward it and whispered: Beam of Inevitability.

Amethyst lightning burst from his palms immediately, too fast for Lysera or anyone else for that matter to react. But it wouldn't even have mattered whether she could.

The beam passed right through her barrier as though it wasn't even there and slammed into her with a force that hurled her completely off her feet and into the air.

Shocked gasps filled the air as Lysera's body hit the ground hard, confusion and disbelief plain on their faces.

Crixis barely gave them the chance to even process it.

He attacked again, half a dozen spears of amethyst lightning rushing down to strike at her.

Lysera raised another barrier, willpower gushing out of her body at the intensity she was channeling. She even conjured her own spears in return, a dozen of them slicing through the air to skewer him to pieces.

But once again, the anglewalkers came in handy.

A twist sent him into the air, racing sideways as though he were running up a wall.

Half the spears exploded against the dueling ring. The other half chased after him, but a quick activation of Inevitable Shield—Maxim halted them all in their tracks.

Lysera’s own shield, however, failed against his own spears, inevitable as they were.

They stabbed, pierced and sliced into Lysera, whose shocked expression was as comical as his had been in the early days of his training with Aodhán.

At this point, the shocked whispers from their growing audience had transformed into full-blown shouts of confusion. But Crixis didn't smile until he saw the first sign of blood—red against Lysera's shin.

He—an evolved cultivator—had hurt an advanced class inheritor, despite their passive resistance to evolved skills.

His smile widened into a grin. Perhaps it was time to take things seriously.

Sweeping both arms forward, he opened himself up to the chaos of the Origin Plane and activated Beam of Inevitability—Maxim.

Twin beams of crackling amethyst burst out of his palms immediately, far brighter, faster, and more powerful than before.

This time, however, Lysera abandoned her barriers entirely. She retreated instead—not backwards. Sideways—then upward, then backward again, a sequence of movements designed to throw off prediction.

But she might as well have been rooted to the spot.

It didn't matter how she moved or how many attacks she threw up. The beams followed, unrelenting and unstoppable, until inevitably they struck, blasting her backwards with so much force her bones shook.

And that was when Lysera finally understood what she was dealing with.

Her eyes widened as Crixis appeared before her once more, cloaked entirely in amethyst, his eyes aglow with smug excitement.

He stretched his arms towards her once more, lightning blooming.

But now that Lysera knew what she was dealing with, she didn't bother running anymore.

For a moment, she simply stood there, breathing hard, something calculating flickering behind her eyes even as blood dripped from her shin. It wasn't fear Crixis saw in her expression. It was the look of someone rapidly running out of easy answers—and deciding to reveal more of herself than she wanted to.

Determination flashing in her gaze, she stretched a hand directly toward the attack. And something very surprising happened.

The beam struck. Yet none of its power reached her—only a pure white beam that splashed harmlessly against her skin like mist.

The amethyst lightning had been bleached white in an instant, its crackling fury stripped away as though washed clean.

Crixis stumbled to a halt, his eyes widening as the implications of what Lysera had just done settled over him.

He glanced at the evaporating essence, then back at Lysera, whose expression had suddenly widened into a grin.

She hadn't blocked his attack. She had purified it, stripping it entirely of everything that made it inevitable.

Crixis had no idea how that was even possible, but when Lysera lifted her gaze to him, eyes aglow with pure white light, a chill ran down his spine.

It seemed he had greatly underestimated his opponent.

And he was only now about to witness what an inheritor of the Path of Purification could truly do.
