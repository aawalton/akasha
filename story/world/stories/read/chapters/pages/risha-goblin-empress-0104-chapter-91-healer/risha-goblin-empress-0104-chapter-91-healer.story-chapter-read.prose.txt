Risha spent her next morning with Sun-Chaser in the council hall. Even if Risha didn’t like politics, there were so many things to handle before she left.

Sun-Chaser told Risha that it would be best if no one knew she was gone until she was back, and Risha agreed with the idea. Officially, she’d be spending time with the Under Goblins while Sun-Chaser managed her affairs on the surface.

Unofficially, an escort was being prepared and chosen to accompany her through the depths.

Risha lounged in her chair, watching Sun-Chaser talk with a group of Orcs from the new city on the river. The establishment of “standardized currencies” and “finances” were being handled by the humans, and the nearby Orc city was the newest to be inundated. Risha still didn’t understand beyond enjoying the shiny look of the coins that had been chosen to represent the trade of goods.

They also printed her image on the coins, which gave her an immense amount of indescribable happiness. Really, if they had her image, they should all belong to her. But, she would be nice and let her people use them. For now.

Rilok, the Mystic’s apprentice, stood with the orcs, although he didn’t speak much. Instead, he walked up next to Risha as the politicians talked.

“He knows you’re avoiding him.” Rilok said.

Risha froze, then relaxed her body as she looked at the ceiling and continued to lounge, “Risha doesn’t know what you’re talking about.”

“He doesn’t blame you, Mother. He chose to fight for you.”

Risha looked at the young orc from the corner of her eyes. He still stood strong, the same orc who had bled as he begged her to save his people. She sat up straight in her chair, her eyes meeting the young orc’s fully. “If Risha told you that she had the power to heal him and Shasha, but she chose not to. What would you say?”

The orc was caught off-guard by the words, but the expression quickly passed as he turned serious. “I would trust that you have your reasons, Mother. He would too.”

Risha looked to the side where Sun-Chaser and the other orcs were still deep in conversation.

She stood, and their conversation quieted. She waved them to continue, then motioned for Rilok to follow her.

The orc fell into step behind her as she made her way towards one of the private rooms in the back.

“Risha’s life is full of hard choices, Rilok. Who to protect, who to save, who to let die.” They stopped in front of a door, a pair of guards in front of it. Risha opened the door, and motioned for the orc to step inside.

Rilok stepped inside, and she slipped in behind him, before closing the door.

“I understand your choices, Mother, all too well.” The Orc said.

The room was a simple storage, barrels full of mysterious liquids and chests of items lined the walls. Risha sat on top of one of the chests then laid down fully on top of it, her legs hanging off the edge. “I suspect you do.”

Risha stared at the ceiling, debating how much she should share.

“Do you know how rare the ability to restore limbs is?” Risha asked.

“The humans say there is a Saint with the ability.”

Risha paused, then sat up, staring at the orc, “What did you say?”

The Orc paused, then bowed his head, “I have spoken with some of the Riders, they told me of a saint that could restore limbs.”

Risha stood up, walking back and forth. She had a powerful healer at her disposal, but even Tya could not restore limbs the way that the Lin child could. But Tya wasn’t as powerful as she could be.

The Saints were Shyanne’s, The Human Mother’s, chosen. Risha had her own chosen, and if it was already known that chosen could perform that kind of miracles…

“Rilok, fetch Tya for me.”

The Orc bowed low, then left the room.

Risha looked at the wall, then called upon the number that she had gained with her latest levels.

Spirit Evolutions:

5 / 6

It had been a hard choice for her, but if there was even the possibility, then Risha would not refuse it.

She paced back and forth in the room until Rilok returned minutes later. Tya, the old High Goblin Arch-Healer, hobbled in behind him, her staff clunking against the ground with every step. Despite the image she portrayed, her two evolutions had turned the Goblin into a rather beautiful goblin, with long healthy hair showing only the smallest bit of gray, and strong limbs capable of wrestling an orc.

Risha waited until the door was closed, then turned to Tya.

The Old goblin straightened her back under Risha’s direct gaze, then bowed low, “Tya greets Mother Risha.”

Risha frowned, “That’s unlike you, Tya.”

The goblin cackled as she straightened, “You were looking so important, Tya got lost in the moment. Why have you called for me youngling?”

Risha smiled, happy at the lack of respect in the Elder’s words.

“I need you, Tya, as my daughter.” Risha called upon her spirit, the world around her stilling as power rushed through her. The flows of the world entered her as an ancient and powerful gaze moved to them.

Tya’s eyes widened, then any illusion of old age left her as she straightened, “Tya is a loyal daughter to the Mother Of Monsters.”

Risha stepped forward, and Tya knelt before her, her wooden staff falling to the ground. She lowered her head as Risha gazed upon her.

Risha placed her hand on Tya’s chin, then gently lifted her head. Risha leaned down and gently kissed Tya on her forehead.

[Blessing Of Mother’s Spirit]

Purple spirit flowed through Risha into the older goblin, filling her with Risha’s spirit. Reality cracked as the gaze reached through reality, and Risha’s Spirit extended behind her. A great monstrous beast, Risha’s spirit held the canines of the Kobolds, the bat-ears of the Goblins, the scaled wings of the Drakes, the red eyes of the Orcs, the soft skin of the Humans, and the chitinous legs of the spiders. The monstrous Spirit held the power of the Grand Mothers, the power of Risha’s Soul, and something beyond reality.

Mist gathered, then surged through Tya once more, filling her. The gray in her hair turned silver, and her skin flushed with life as any wrinkles on her face disappeared.

The power finished it’s surge, Risha’s Spirit stepped back through reality, and the world resumed its turning.

Risha pulled back, then stumbled her legs giving out beneath her.

Rilok was there in a second, catching her as she gasped for air.

Spirit Evolutions:

6 / 6

Tya did not move, her gaze lost to the messages the great system sent her.

Risha slowly managed to push herself back to standing, but kept a hand on Rilok so that she wouldn’t fall.

“Tell me Tya, what did the spirits grant you?”

Tya lifted her head, meeting Risha’s eyes. She cleared her throat, then picked her staff off the floor. She leaned on it as she stood up. Tya was still shorter than Risha, but her hunch was no longer there at all.

“Let me show you, Mother.”

Name: Tya

Race: High Goblin Spirit Healer

Lvl: 42

Skills:

[Spiritual Healing] Mend the spiritual, and the physical will follow. Heal the spirits of even the most corrupted.

[Alchemy of Spirit] Infuse your spirit into your alchemy, enhancing the effects of any potions that you create.

[Spiritual Restoration]: Follow the path of spirit to restore that which has been lost in the physical realm.

[Greater Spiritual Heal] Create a circle of mass healing, restoring monster and mortal alike to their true spiritual and physical forms.

[Hold Spirit] Grab, pull, and hold the spirit of a creature to their mortal form, so that healing can be done.

[Grand Mother’s Spirit Magic] allows you to commune with the spirits, and control the spirit that resides within you.

Risha smiled, then laughed in pure joy. If she understood the words correctly, then she had succeeded in her goal. A healer capable of restoring that which has been lost in the physical realm.

Risha lept onto Tya, pulling her close for a hug, then stumbling as she almost fell again. Thankfully, Tya was able to hold the both of them up.

Risha pulled back, and Tya’s smile matched her own. Risha took a deep breath, trying to pull back her excitement. She glanced over to Rilok, before looking at the healer again, “Can you…?”

“I will see both Shasha and Klu, right away Mother. The others will follow.” Tya gently led Risha to one of the chests and helped her sit. “Rest easy, Risha.”

Risha’s smile bloomed across her face, and tears dripped down her face. The fact that she could set right some of her wrongs, it filled her with such joy.

Tya pulled the younger goblin into her arms as Risha started crying in earnest. She hadn’t even realized how much it had affected her.

She still remembered the names of those who rested with incurable injuries in the healing tents. Burnt by the fire of Risha’s enemies, tortured by their former masters, scarred beyond recovery. Perhaps they too, could be healed. Maybe she could walk by those beds tomorrow, and find them empty.

Risha sobbed with relief, the guilt that haunted her growing ever so slightly lighter.

The smallest piece of anger filled her, that it had taken her so long to realize this solution, but she stuffed that anger down. It was a moment of relief, and she would hold onto that.

Tya held her, rocking her until Risha calmed down with heavy breaths.

Finally Risha stood and smiled wide. “We win, Tya.”

“We win, Risha.”

It took a little while longer before Risha was ready to leave the room, at which point Tya slipped away.

Risha walked out the door with purpose, and nodded to the two guards who saluted her. Rilok followed her with quiet support as they made their way through the halls.

Risha bid him goodbye when they reached Sun-Chaser and the orcs, then made her way to where the Scaled councilors chatted quietly.

“Tell her that I’m ready to make my choice.” Risha spoke with confidence. Her release of emotions had given her an inner strength that she was going to use.

The Scaled shared a look, then one of the few with white scales stood and motioned for Risha to follow him.

It was time to fulfill her end of the deal with Elderoa.
