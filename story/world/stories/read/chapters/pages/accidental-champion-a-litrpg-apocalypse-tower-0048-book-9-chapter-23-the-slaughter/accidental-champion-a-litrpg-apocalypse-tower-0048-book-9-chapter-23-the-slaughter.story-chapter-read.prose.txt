Queen Aecia: [How in the Holy Worlds of Atherian did you manage that?]

The holy worlds of what? Xavier wondered but didn’t ask. [I told you, I’m not—]

Queen Aecia: [Not an ordinary time mage, yes. I did hear you the first time. However, that does not tell me much, Xavier Collins.]

Xavier didn’t need to tell this woman anything about the powers he possessed. But really, what was the harm? These people were from another universe. The chances of them ever being able to contact anyone from his own universe were… astronomically small.

He hadn’t gotten to share this with many people, and he had to say he was proud of his new ability.

Xavier: [I have the power to manipulate energy outside myself directly]

Queen Aecia blinked. The others stared at him. It was a while before any of them responded.

Brarri: [Directly manipulate energy… but that would mean…] The tall mage shook his head, eyes wide. [No. Such a thing can’t be possible.]

Queen Aecia narrowed her eyes. [Who… are you?]

Xavier shrugged. [I told you, I’m Xavier Collins.]

Xavier flapped his wings and was propelled through space toward the hull of the lead ship. He’d been careful not to allow his time dilation field to touch the ship itself, so though the force field was down no one on board would be aware of that.

The others mutely followed, still clearly digesting the revelation he’d revealed.

When Xavier reached the hull, he couldn’t help but pause and look down the length of the ship. The hull was made from smooth metal. The ship stretched onward for a long while, giving it that spear-like appearance. Though from up close, it looked far too large to be a spear.

His gaze moved from the lead ship to the others close by, staring at them with his natural sight. None of the ships had windows. There was no large, glass observation deck. No windshield in the main cockpit.

They were beginning to look more like massive steel coffins than a spear this close up.

Three million…

That number still hadn’t sunk in.

He turned his attention back to the ship, touching the hull with his bare hands. He could phase through the steel easily enough. He could even teleport, though there were more runes in the length of the ship that would likely cause some kind of alert to go off. Even inside the time dilation field, he didn’t want to risk that.

Xavier was going to break through the old-fashioned way.

Just as there were no windows, the spacecraft also lacked airlocks. Xavier drew Bones, shifting its form into a shorter sword, then thrust the blade into the steel. It was softer than he’d imagined, and he was able to cut a large hole through it with ease.

I guess they never expected anyone to get through the main shield.

He cut a rectangular, door shape into the hull then kicked the metal when he was done. The “door” slammed onto the floor on the other side.

The second he moved through into the ship, gravity seized him. It didn’t surprise him that the ships were functioning under artificial gravity. He’d had access to spells like Untethered for some time, and he’d felt a similar kind of energy cycling around the ship. He supposed it was more comfortable than travelling in freefall.

The room he’d come through was deserted. He’d chosen it intentionally, sending his spiritual sense through the hull once he’d edited the protective runes. It looked to be some kind of magical engine room, though it was like nothing he’d ever seen before. He hadn’t found a storage room on the ship, no cargo bay doors to slip through. That had confused him only momentarily, but only because whenever he thought of spaceships he thought of ones from sci-fi media back on Earth.

Of course there wouldn’t be a cargo bay when everyone has spatial devices. An A Grade’s spatial device was likely as large as the ship itself—what could they possibly need a cargo bay for?

The ships were so large because of how many people they packed in on them, and the fact that each of those Denizens had their own personal quarters.

Quite the luxury for a military ship.

The others streamed through the hole after him, their gazes shifting, weapons in hand, as they scanned the area.

Queen Aecia stood across from Xavier. [You are more than meets the eye, Xavier Collins. That powerful time dilation field is just the beginning of what you can do, isn’t it? Is that how you’ve managed to solo floors of the tower, with all these special skills?]

Xavier smiled. [They’ve certainly helped.]

Brarri: [What happens if someone cancels that time dilation field of yours? There are only five of us. Can you fight without it?]

[We’ll cross that bridge when we come to it.]

Though he’d been wondering along similar lines. What would happen if his time dilation field was countered? There were several ways it had been deactivated in the past. If he got hit by Locked Down again, like what had happened with Arrack, or if there were enemy time mages with their own fields in here…

But only the captains are A Grades, and I’ll only need to face one of them at a time.

Still, Xavier supposed it was time to take a few precautionary measures. He manifested several dozen spells inside himself, one after another. In moments, fifty drones were standing around the room.

The others merely stared at the drones, then gave Xavier an odd look that seemed to say, What else do you have in your bag of tricks?

Inside all fifty drones, Xavier manifested another Time Alteration spell. Now, fifty-one separate time dilation fields were active. The drones’ fields weren’t as powerful as Xavier’s, and they were currently inside his time stream, but if his own became compromised they would be a powerful back up.

Xavier was about to head out of the room and into the ship’s hall when he paused to really look around. He couldn’t help it.

He was standing on a spaceship!

The strange devices in this engine room—if that was indeed what it was—looked like nothing he’d ever seen before. In the centre of the room was a crystal ball twice as tall as him. Inside the ball, energy of all different colours swirled in intricate, looping patterns. There were no runes he could discern, so he wasn’t sure how this device functioned, but the way the energy swirled was familiar—it reminded him of aspects of his Untethered spell.

The ships, their flying through space… is that all simply a manipulation of gravity?

He hadn’t seen any kind of engine burn at the rear of the ships. Whatever drive made these things function was nothing like what was imagined in any of the books he’d read. It was intriguing, but he supposed he shouldn’t stand around examining it for too long.

He’d never end up finishing this floor if he did.

Xavier had the drones lead the way, looking through their eyes as they made it into the hall. The halls were clear of enemies. In fact, as far as his Immortal Soul lens could discern, the enemies were all clustered around the same place in the ship. The largest room on the entire spaceship. A vast hall that managed to hold every single one of them. He imagined that was where they would be transported to the ground from.

The hallways were made from the same boring, smooth steel as the hull, and he had to say he was a little disappointed. He was hoping for an environment that was more… novel.

He was on a spaceship, and yet each hall looked exactly like the one before it.

The hallways had atmosphere, and as Xavier had used a time dilation field when breaking into the ship, that atmosphere hadn’t become depressurised—with the way the antigravity worked, he wasn’t even sure if it could become depressurised. Still, Xavier kept his Force Armour active, just in case. The others didn’t talk as they walked down the hall. No one would have heard them, but there was something about sneaking around an enemy spacecraft that made silence a natural response.

When they reached that vast hall that contained every enemy on the ship, Xavier paused at the large doorway.

All one-hundred-thousand-and-one Denizens were frozen outside his field. Every single one of them looked utterly defenceless—in that moment, they were defenceless.

There were Denizens of all the different races that he’d encountered in his journeys, and several that he hadn’t. A great many Denizens were elves, like the people of Galadsea. There were demonkin, dwarves, and oxmen—those he’d encountered on the four-hundred-and-fiftieth floor. Unsurprisingly, they had been a stubborn folk. There were also scaled men and women he didn’t recognise.

There was even a small cluster of dragonkin at the back.

Mostly, however, the Denizens were human like him—well, like he used to be.

Xavier was prepared to kill every single one of them, on all three million ships…

But something about how he was about to do this didn’t feel right. Yes, he would do anything to save his own universe—he’d already pushed his moral qualms about causing these people’s deaths out of his mind. This was no different to what he’d done to the Denizens that had comprised the Endless Horde.

However, those Denizens had been conscious of what was happening.

They had been given an opportunity to fight back.

Xavier had the sudden urge to give every Denizen in that room that same chance. To give them an awareness of what was happening. If Roln were functioning, the dead Wanderer would have whispered to him how foolish that would be. To give any enemy a chance at defeating you when there was another option was… arrogant.

With his ability to use time dilation fields, he could likely neutralise every B Grade in this room with incredible ease. As for the single A Grade, he spotted the woman easily enough. The captain of the lead ship didn’t look terribly distinguishable from the A Grades around her. She didn’t wear any particular uniform. It was the energy radiating off her that told him what she was.

She was a human mage wearing purple robes, with a steel staff with a red, glowing crystal at its head.

Something told him this wasn’t the fleet’s leader. There was probably a general somewhere, way back on the three millionth ship, who didn’t even intend to fight in this war because it was “beneath him.”

Xavier cracked his neck and faced Queen Aecia, Brarri, Yalia, and Evali. They each looked out at the enemies with evident vicious intent. They would certainly had no qualms killing these Denizens, even as defenceless as they appeared.

And why should they? Billions of people on their planet are depending on them.

Why do you hesitate? Bones asked. Your plan has worked. The enemy is before you.

It was a good question. But something about all this was beginning to feel too easy. He’d proved he could infiltrate one of these ships, and here all the enemies were, standing before him, ready to be killed.

He didn’t even feel like he needed the others here.

This is… too easy.

It reminded him of the moment before he’d reached the Memory Room in Arrack’s base. Everything had gone so smoothly. He’d steamrolled through all the obstacles, and he would surely steamroll through the next.

And he hadn’t.

Then Palini had died.

Yet he had steamrolled through the last couple hundred Tower of Champion floors. Any time something had seemed like it might be an obstacle, he’d overcome it with ease. It was all beginning to feel disappointingly easy.

That was why he’d skipped to the seven-hundred-and-fiftieth floor in the first place.

Perhaps he should have felt glad that he hadn’t struggled through those floors, but he knew that struggling was the only way he was going to achieve anything.

Struggling was how he’d gotten so far.

Xavier didn’t want to manufacture a struggle. He didn’t want to handicap himself so that this entire floor would be harder. That would be foolish—an action that could lead to his death, having ripple effects that he couldn’t even contemplate.

No. He needed to take this floor seriously.

Xavier turned back toward the enemy.

He shifted Bones back into a sword.

Then he sent his drones out, each wielding an almost identical sword in their hands.

Being cautious, Xavier did not begin with the A Grade—he would leave the captain of the ship until last.

And thus, the slaughter of the B Grades began.

He didn’t allow the kill notifications to flood his vision. Instead, he displayed his Mastery Points counter in the top right corner of his vision. The number ticked up as the B Grades died. Less than a minute passed for him by the time every single B Grade in the room lay dead on the floor. None of them had been strong enough to fight back against one of his drones. Most of them had died after one or two strikes. Some of them had taken a little longer to kill, but even they hadn’t been able to fight back.

When all the B Grades were dead, Xavier looked at the number of Mastery Points he’d gained, tilting his head to one side, doing the math.

Xavier was C Grade, yet even killing a hundred thousand B Grades had barely left a dent.

But if he did that three million times…

There’s no way I won’t reach B Grade.

The queen and her people exchanged a look as Xavier gazed out at all those corpses. He felt cold and empty inside.

But that wasn’t all he felt in that emptiness—he also felt hunger.

A deep, abiding hunger.

More. I need more.

Xavier’s grip on Bones’s hilt tightened as he looked to the final living enemy soul on the ship.

The A Grade captain.
