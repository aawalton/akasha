I glanced at my phone, checking to make sure my dad hadn’t texted me yet, but luckily, my lock screen was blank. We should have enough time to finish the game, but it had been a while since he’d texted me to say he was just leaving work and wanted to know if I needed a lift home, so he was probably close.

Part of me kind of wanted to tell him that I was okay, that I’d be staying at Duncan’s, but I wasn’t sure how he’d react to that. He knew I was kind of on a date, and I knew what he’d assume if I said I planned on staying over. Despite his sweet but embarrassing offer, I doubted he’d be okay with it.

That wasn’t why I didn’t want to leave, though. It wasn’t that I necessarily wanted anything to happen between Duncan and me. I was just having fun, and I didn’t want the day to end just yet. If I stayed, we could spend the entire night playing games and talking, and I would have loved that.

Although… I wouldn’t have minded too much if something else had happened too. I would have been quite happy about that, and I think that was partially why I wanted to stay too. I almost hoped that if I spent more time there, it would give Duncan more of a chance to make a move.

He’d been tempted to do it a couple of times. It seemed like he had been, at least. I’d seen his eyes flick towards my lips when I’d teased him earlier, and there was another time too. I’d turned to see him looking at me with a smile that made it feel like all of the air had been sucked out of the room, but when I asked him what he was looking at, he just shook his head and changed the subject.

Perhaps if I spent the night at Duncan’s, he’d finally just lean forward and kiss me or tell me he was into me. That was what I hoped would have happened, but I still would have enjoyed my time if it hadn’t. We would have still had fun.

“Alright, you’re up,” Duncan said, breaking me out of my thoughts. “You just need a seven to get to the star, but if not… the will is mine.”

I glanced at him over my shoulder, raising an eyebrow.

“We’ll see about that,” I told him. “Seven shouldn’t be too hard.”

My tone was light. It was strangely confident, and for once, I actually felt that way. I wasn’t sure if it was just the amount of alcohol we’d drunk; we’d almost gotten through the whole bottle of blue mystery drink, but I knew I’d be able to roll a seven. I didn’t even need to bother with using an item to ensure I hit it.

“Mmmm, I don’t know,” Duncan said, his voice teasing. “Didn’t you say something like that earlier, before I crushed you?”

“No, I don’t think so,” I lied. “I’m pretty sure I’ve destroyed you in every single game.”

A smile tugged at Duncan’s lips, but he tried to hide it as he cocked his head to the side.

“Oh, is that right?”

“Yeah. It is, actually,” I continued. “I mean, I’m just as surprised as you are. I expected better from you.”

Duncan couldn’t stop his grin from showing. Playfulness sparkled in his eyes, making my heart clench with happiness and pride. I loved being able to tease Duncan. I always teased him a bit, but I was being more bold than I usually was, and it seemed like he was enjoying it. I was too.

“Damn, sorry to disappoint,” he said with a heavy sigh. “I’ll try harder next time.”

“Good,” I replied. “Give me a real challenge.”

A snort slipped out of Duncan as I leaned forward, my eyes fixed on the screen as determination grew within me. Although I’d won the first two games, Duncan had also won a few. I’d lost count of who was in the lead, though. Some of the games were a bit of a blur, thanks to the mystery drink, but I was pretty sure it was him, and I wanted to emerge victorious.

I was so close too. I was only one star behind Duncan, and as long as I had a good roll, I’d get to the star. I had enough coins for it; more than enough, actually. So all I needed to do was reach it, and then I could win. He could still take more bonus stars than me, but I’d have a better chance of winning.

Letting out a slow exhale, I punched up with the controller to roll the dice, watching as the number came to a sudden stop. The confidence drained out of me in an instant, leaving me numb as I stared at the number on the screen.

“What?” I muttered as Duncan burst out laughing.

Six.

I needed a minimum of a seven, and I’d rolled a six. My character ran forward, jumping from space to space, before coming to a stop just before the star. It was so close. If I could just reach out, I’d be able to claim it. It would have been mine, but I couldn’t. My character was frozen, unable to run, as coins were torn away from her.

Dizziness slammed into me, threatening to knock me off the sofa. Another world vied for my attention, and I almost gave in. The excitement, the eagerness with which it pulled at me was intoxicating, and I was almost certain it was trying to whisk me away to a world where I’d won. Either that or I was about to be pulled into a world that was just like the game, but there, I’d be able to reach out and grab the star that should have been mine.

Temptation lapped at me. I felt my reality slipping away. My grip was growing weaker and weaker, driven by my competitiveness. My desire to win was so strong that I almost couldn’t resist it, and I barely noticed it happened. I didn’t feel my reality growing more distant until Duncan’s voice dragged me home.

“Oh, so close!” he cried.

“Urgh!” I exclaimed as I fell back on the sofa, the room still spinning around me.

My hands tightened around the controller as I tried to stave off the dizziness that made my stomach want to rebel, and fear gripped me. I’d drunk too much. If I were sick because of the other worlds, Duncan would assume the mystery drink was the cause, and that would be so embarrassing.

I’d thrown up around him before, obviously. That had been bad enough, but it was my fault that time. I knew I was drinking too much, but I didn’t want to stop. I was younger then, and stupider too. If I were sick during our date…

Heat began to build in my cheeks as I tried to breathe as deeply and steadily as I could, but my mind continued to race. Would he think it was his fault? Duncan had been the one who made the mystery drink, after all. We’d been egging each other on all afternoon, making the other one drink more and more, so would he blame himself if I got sick? And what if that made him think the only reason I was flirting with him was because I was drunk?

I wasn’t drunk. I was kind of tipsy, but it wasn’t that bad. Sure, it was making it easier to say the things I wanted to say to Duncan, but the alcohol wasn’t the only reason I was saying them. It was because I wanted to.

“That was cruel,” Duncan said as he leaned back to join me, his arm resting against mine.

Vertigo was still pulling at me distractedly, but I forced myself to ignore it and send the television a glare. It swam in and out of focus, but that didn’t matter. I could tell where it was, and that was enough.

“Right?” I sighed. “I just needed one more space, and I would have got it.”

I didn’t actually feel as dejected as I was making myself sound, and I was pretty sure Duncan had noticed the slight smile on my face because he was playing along. Slowly, he shook his head, his expression sombre.

“Don’t worry. I’m sure you’ll have better luck next time,” Duncan said, his tone overly reassuring as he lifted his arm and wrapped it around my shoulders to comfort me.

The touch instantly made my heart race. I hadn’t expected it. His arm had been touching mine, and I thought that was all he would do. I assumed he’d just lean against me slightly, not that he’d put his arm around me.

I liked it, though. The weight was strangely comfortable and grounding, and I could feel heat radiating from him. Before I could stop myself, I leaned into the touch and rested my head on his shoulder.

“You think so?” I asked, my pulse thumping in my ears.

My eyes had remained fixed on the screen as I spoke. I wanted to turn and look up at him, but I was too scared. His face would be so close to mine if I were to do that, and even just the thought of that was intimidating. I’d never been that close to someone, but at the same time, I wanted to do it.

“Of course!” Duncan told me, his arm tightening around me ever so slightly. “I mean, you won so many games today. More than me, I think.”

That made me pause. As much as I wanted it to be true, I was pretty sure he was wrong. I started to count in my head, but I couldn’t keep track of the numbers, and my memories were already a little hazy.

“I don’t think so,” I said as I lifted my head from his shoulder and turned to look at him, intentionally leaning away slightly to give myself more space. “I mean, I won the first two, but then you won the next one, and the one after that?”

“Yeah, but then you crushed me in the western one,” Duncan added.

“And then you destroyed me on that train level, and in the shopping one!”

That had gone particularly badly for me. Duncan had absolutely decimated me. Somehow, he’d come out with four stars, and I’d gotten none. Well, not exactly. I had a star at one point, but it had been stolen from me.

It wasn’t even Duncan who had stolen it, which made things even worse. It was Birdo. And she didn’t even come second in the end. She came third.

“Oh yeah, I forgot about that,” Duncan said with a chuckle. “But you won on the train board!”

His body shook slightly as he laughed, and I found myself joining in.

“I liked that level.”

“Me too,” Duncan agreed, smiling down at me.

“So, we’re probably drawing at the moment, but when you win this one…” I paused for dramatic effect, “You’ll have won more games than me.”

Duncan laughed again, the sound softer that time, and I felt it dance across my lips. When had I gotten close enough to feel his breath on my face? I hadn’t noticed that I’d been leaning towards him. It felt like I hadn’t moved, but I hadn’t seen him lean at all either.

“Maybe,” Duncan allowed. “But maybe we should just call this one a draw and play again sometime. We’ll actually keep track of the winners next time, so we’ll know once and for all who is better at Mario Party.”

I bit my lip as I pretended to deliberate that, and my heart felt as though it skipped a beat as Duncan’s eyes flicked downward, watching the movement for a moment before meeting my gaze again.

“I guess we have to. How else are we meant to know who’s better?”

“Exactly,” Duncan agreed with lazy smile.

“But…” I started to say, trailing off before continuing. “Draws are no fun.”

A grin pulled at Duncan’s lips, and I found my gaze dropping towards them, unable to help myself. He just had such a nice smile, and I’d always loved it. It lit up his face and made him radiate pure joy. No matter how I felt, it was hard not to return the smile.

I’d never noticed how soft his lips looked, though. Perhaps it was just because I’d never studied them quite so closely, but they were so smooth. I wasn’t sure whether he used lip balm, or if that was just how they always looked, but I couldn’t stop myself from wondering what they’d feel like when they were pressed against mine. I’d felt it in other worlds, but I wanted to know if it was the same in my reality.

“Normally, I’d agree with you,” Duncan said, his voice low and almost husky. “But I think you might be the only person I don’t mind drawing with.”

My gaze flicked back to his eyes, and I swallowed as I stared into them. He was moving closer; I was certain of it. Or maybe we both were. Either way, the gap between us had definitely gotten smaller. I was so close that I could just lift my chin, and our lips would be touching.

Subconsciously, my tongue darted out to wet my lips.

“Oh yeah?” I asked, my tone matching his. “Why’s that?”

A shaky breath skittered across my lips, and I wasn’t sure whether it had come from Duncan or me. Anxiety and anticipation had built within me, approaching a crescendo, and I could feel my body trembling. I hoped Duncan couldn’t feel it, though. His arm was still around my shoulders, but it had dropped slightly lower when I’d turned towards him, so his hand was resting on my back.

“Because I—” Duncan started to say, but a loud noise came from the speakers.

My heart thundered in my ears as Duncan and I sprang apart instinctively, and he snatched his arm back. Fear ricocheted through me as my mind jumped to the worst-case scenario, and my head snapped around to stare at the television. I almost expected to see a threatening message on it, addressed to me, or to see an image of someone sitting on the screen and staring directly at me, but I did not.

A giggle slipped out of my mouth, the sound high-pitched from relief as adrenaline drained out of me, and I sagged back against the sofa. My body felt suddenly exhausted, and I was pretty sure Duncan felt the same way because he flopped back, running a hand through his hair.

“Final mini game!” another cheer announced.

That was the noise, I realised, fighting the urge to giggle again as blood rushed into my cheeks. It was just cheering coming from the game or something. It wasn’t anything scary or dangerous.

“Oh, sandwich dash,” Duncan read out as the title of the mini game appeared. “Have you played this one before?”

My gaze flicked between Duncan and the screen as I tried to steady my breathing, my heart still beating far too quickly. Duncan looked irritatingly relaxed and unflustered. If it wasn’t for the slight pink hue to his cheeks and how pointedly he was keeping his eyes on the screen, I would have assumed he hadn’t been affected by what had just happened and how close we’d come to kissing at all.

“I don’t think so,” I said, the words coming out slightly too fast. “Was it on any of the previous games?”

It had been hard to keep the disappointment out of my voice, but I was pretty sure I’d hidden it well. My tone had been fairly normal, but I couldn’t help glancing at Duncan again as my mind continued to spin.

Was he disappointed too? He didn’t seem it, but maybe he was just better at hiding it than I was. The smile on his face was a little sheepish, and he was still blushing. He hadn’t looked at me once since we’d jumped apart, and maybe that was because he was trying to conceal how he was feeling, just like I was. Perhaps he was wondering about what would have happened if the game hadn’t interrupted us too.

I hoped he was.

“Mmmm, no,” Duncan replied, pushing his hair back from his face again. “I’m pretty sure it wasn’t on the last game, anyway?”

“Oh, okay. I don’t think I’ve played it before then,” I said as I tore my eyes away from him to look at the tutorial on the screen. “So you just have to dodge the falling sandwiches?”

“Yeah, but it’s pretty easy to get trapped. They build up around you as they fall, so you have to climb or jump between them,” Duncan explained, watching as my character got squished on the screen. “See?”

He finally looked away from the television, his eyes finding me again. I couldn’t hold his gaze, though. I had to look away quickly as my cheeks grew even hotter, and I stared at the screen, feeling his eyes remain on my face.

Despite the nerves flickering in my heart, my lips ticked up into a smile as I saw my character reappear. I was glad we were still in the tutorial because I’d made a stupid mistake and died embarrassingly quickly. I could respawn in the tutorial, but generally, if that happened in the mini game, I’d be out.

There wasn’t much pressure on me to win it, though. Even if I won the mini game, I probably wouldn’t be enough to impact the rest of the game. I’d just get a few more coins, but they didn’t matter anymore. I couldn’t use them on anything, and I’d definitely not done well enough to get a bonus star for winning the most mini games.

“Oh yeah, I get it,” I told Duncan, my eyes flicking towards him as I hit the button to ready my character. “Let’s go.”

Duncan hesitated for just a second before doing the same.

“You’re on.”

A countdown appeared on the screen, and I chewed on my bottom lip as the number grew smaller. Part of me wanted to ignore the game, to purposefully throw it and toss my controller to the side so I could grab Duncan’s shirt and pull him towards me, but I batted that mental image aside, trying desperately to ignore it. There was no way I had the confidence to do that, and it was just distracting me. I wanted to win, so I had to focus.

Sandwiches began raining from the skies, dropping into neat towers, and my hands gripped the controller as I directed my character to dodge the falling bread. It wasn’t too hard at first. Only a couple of sandwiches fell at a time, making it easy to avoid them, but they quickly began to speed up.

“Yes!” I cried as a perfectly square, crustless sandwich narrowly missed me and crushed one of the NPCs.

“Take that, Daisy!” Duncan cheered. “Next up… Birdo!”

My eyes darted around the screen, flitting between watching out for appearing shadows that heralded the incoming bread and watching Duncan’s character and the other computer. I was determined to survive until the end. I wanted to win, but if I couldn’t do that, I just wanted to beat Birdo. I wasn’t sure why I hated the character so much, but I did.

“Come on,” I muttered under my breath as Birdo managed to squeeze out through a gap that should have been too small for it. “That was so close!”

“Next time, it’ll be trapped for sure,” Duncan told me before laughing triumphantly. “Yes!”

The pink bird disappeared beneath a piece of bread, and I beamed happily. I’d defeated it, I told myself as my gaze returned to my own character. I’d managed to avoid getting crushed to death before Birdo.

“Oh no,” I breathed as my fingers tapped the buttons on my controller repeatedly.

“What?” Duncan asked, not looking away from the screen as his character lunged to the side.

“I’m trapped!”

My character spun in a circle, trying desperately to get out of the hole it had somehow found itself in. I’d been too focused on watching Birdo die that I hadn’t paid attention to what I was doing or how high the stacks of bread around me were getting. My desire to witness her death had doomed me, and all I could do was watch in horror as a shadow appeared on the screen, slowly casting my character into darkness.

“No!” Duncan cried dramatically.

“Game over!” the game announced cheerfully.

“Well done, I guess,” I told him, barely able to keep the pout on my face until I’d finished speaking. “Good game.”

Duncan laughed, and my restraint shattered, forcing a grin onto my lips.

“Good game,” he repeated. “But hey, there’s still the bonus stars. You still have a chance.”

“I don’t know about that. I think you might have played too well,” I said, bumping him gently with my shoulder before freezing.

Was that too much? We’d not really touched since we’d almost kissed. We’d gotten distracted by the game, and it had felt so natural to nudge him like that. I’d barely even thought about it, but suddenly, I was gripped by the worry that he’d find it weird. I almost expected him to move away from me, but instead, he shot me a cheeky grin.

“I guess I’m just better at the game than you,” he teased. “And all mini games. Maybe we don’t need to have a rematch after all.”

My eyes narrowed.

“I wouldn’t go that far.”

“Hmmm,” Duncan said as the prize ceremony began, his expression thoughtful. “No, I think you’re right. We need to have a proper tournament. When are you free next?”
