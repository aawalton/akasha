It took [Draconic Wings] nearly five minutes to activate once more, even though the worm hadn’t given chase. Being forcefully dispelled might have triggered some sort of cooldown, he guessed, because he was pretty sure he wasn’t being followed. Ethan had been sprinting non-stop ever since he’d gotten to running and by the looks of it, the worm had neither chased him nor the girl. It had hovered in its place, barely burnt by his fireball, and after leaning left and right for a few minutes for their scent, it dipped back under the sand. He kept expecting the rumble to reach his ears at any moment, but it didn’t.

Whatever it was it had used to find them, he didn’t think it was their movement or smell. Magic had played a part, most likely, but he’d soon have time to confirm that.

Once the wings were back, he snapped them open and this time, he flew high up in the sky. High enough that no bow or arrow could reach him. By the time he stopped his ascent, his fellow competitors were barely visible dots on the sand. Some were easier to see as they clashed, and the flashes of light and magic were easy to pick, but the rest blended easily enough with the sand and now that he had a better view of the rift, a few things were beginning to click in place.

First of all, the rift was circular. At its center was a massive temple-like structure, and in every cardinal direction, a much smaller structure was visible. If this was a puzzle of some sorts, then he’d bet they’d need to visit those side structure first before heading to the center, but just in case he was wrong, he’d check the central one first.

Aside from those structures though, there was nothing but sand. Dunes as far as the eye could see and clearly enough, he wasn’t the only who’d come upon the mysterious worms. Already, he could pick up three sites of fighting with the giant creature, one of which were on the retreat but considering they were still slinging spells, the worm had no issue following after them, which told him that that might be how they were orienting themselves.

“Come to think of it, it showed up right after we started throwing spells at each other,” he muttered as he eyed the terrain below him. He could head to an empty stretch and try to bait one toward him. But before that, he needed to prepare a little. Run a couple of tests. That fight had not gone well and now would be a great time for a little post-match analysis.

First of all, his Prime Manifestation hadn’t been easy to summon. It always required some focus and a specific mindset, but in general, it was barely harder than casting any of his spells. But earlier? He just knew he couldn’t swing it. He’d barely been able to keep a hold of his remaining spellforms, much less spare the attention to activate the Zenith state.

But why was that? Had it been the worm? Or was it something else?

Closing his eyes, Ethan reached inward and let out a slow breath as he relaxed his shoulders. Then a frown pulled on his brows, and he cracked his eyes open.

“Huh.”

He did manage to tap into it, but it definitely was much harder than he was used to.

“And if there were no worms nearby,” he muttered, his gaze drifting down toward the magic-restricting bracelets. Was it because of the restraining magic?

“That could be it… or maybe it has to do with the Prime spellforms?”

It would make sense. The Prime spellforms were an expression of his Prime abilities, and he was pretty sure than whenever he used the Prime Manifestation, he had at least one of the Prime forms active. Either the Lance, the Horns, or both. Unfortunately, if that was the case, he wouldn’t be able to confirm this theory until the bracelets came off, which meant that he was going to have to put a pin in that.

Turning his attention to the next point, Ethan eyed his claws and recalled the disturbing feeling the worm had provoked. All his spellforms had failed. All but two. He wasn’t sure if he had been hit by a spell. Those often had either a visual indicator or could be perceived otherwise but then again, Ethan was no expert on magic. It could have been a spell. It could have been a Zenith State. It could even have been some sort of innate magic to the species. But one thing was for sure, the effect was not absolute.

Two of his spellforms had resisted the disabling effect, and it wasn’t lost on him that both were his oldest and most familiar spellforms. They were nearly an extension of his own self. So was that why he managed to hold on to them?

Ethan glanced down at the ground in the distance and spreading his wings wide, he began descending as he continuously scanned the sand for another worm. There were plenty of questions, and not that many ways of getting answers aside from getting back down there and testing things out. Ideally, it’d be against a weaker worm so in case things don’t work out he’d be able to get away and for that, he needed to keep his wings.

So Ethan pulled a pair of shorts out of his [Soul Space], put them on, then dismissed the scales, keeping only the claws and the wings. He knew he couldn’t keep more than his claws and scales, but he needed the mobility of his wings. In the hopes of lessening the strain enough to maintain his wings, he chose to sacrifice the scales. If they still failed, then he’d get the scales back, but it was worth a shot. Now, he just needed to find—or lure—a target.

***

It didn’t take long for Ethan to find a decent target. In fact, it wasn’t even paying attention to him as he dove lower some distance behind it and trailed after, partially hidden by the cloud of sand and dust it was kicking off in its pursuit.

[Pale Maw (II) - Level 97]

Ahead of it, someone was running, sprinting as fast as they could with some sort of gazelle-like legs. He kept hopping above the dunes and zig-zagging all over the place, desperately trying to lose the massive creature and reach the fast approaching small structure ahead. But with all the magic he was sending behind him, there was no hope of that.

[Human - Level 83 (II)]

If he’d just stopped casting spells, Ethan was pretty sure the worm would have lost the scent, but it seemed the runner wasn’t aware of that fact just yet. Then again, for being dropped in the middle of the desert without any prior knowledge, it was an easy mistake to make, and the last few minutes of observation made that clear.

Flying high, Ethan had been able to observe some of the fights taking place and one thing was sure, the worms were definitely resistant to spells and highly sensitive to magic. As soon as two participants began clashing, they had a minute at most before one of the pale creatures popped out of the ground and crashed the party and as far as he could tell, no one had managed to dispatch any of the monsters yet.

Ethan grinned as he caught up with the worm, floating silently in its wake, just above the trail of kicked up sand. Maybe he’d be the first. Because seemingly, the ability to disable spellforms wasn’t something that the creature could just continuously maintain. He was only a few yards above it now, and he could feel no strain though, just to be safe, he kept the scales off. And he definitely kept his mind focused on his target.

The spot right behind its neck, where the worm’s head curved to target its prey.

He just had to get the timing right, and until he was ready, he should restrain from casting anything so without a word, Ethan let the hot air carry him a little higher, approximated his trajectory, then angled himself sharply down once he was a hundred yards or so above it. Wind howled in his ears as he held his clawed hands in front of him, like an eagle’s talons as it swooped down. With great effort, he activated his Prime Manifestation, narrowing his attention on the obstacle ahead of him. While it was certainly more difficult than usual, he still managed to activate it, and once he was half a second away from impact, he activated [Extended Enhancement] as well.

The dark talons of [Claws of the Ossyrath Dragon] surged in length, each at least ten inches long. Ethan had barely half a second to see the surging flames about them. He even believed the worm had noticed his approach. But it was too late for it.

Ethan hit the creature like a bullet through the back of its head, tearing into its pale flesh and crunching into its vertebra before both of them hit the sand below in geyser of blood and viscera.
