Interlude V.I

Anata doodled as her teacher, Ms. Reads, prattled on about basic Universal Script characters and their alternate meanings. While not as talented as Aoi, Anata felt like her drawing skills had improved quite a lot in the last few weeks.

“Is that a fat, furry vole?” Rui asked, leaning in to see her sketch of Dog the capybara. Rui, who was strictly forbidden from accessing Owl’s Respite and the nearby cove, hadn’t ever seen Dog and likely didn’t even know what a capybara was.

Anata shrugged. She supposed a fat vole was a pretty close approximation to a capybara. She flipped the scrap of parchment and started on the other side. Now warmed up, she felt ready for today’s magnum opus. Next she wanted to capture the image of the newest addition to her life. Comet.

The hippogriff was gorgeous. She couldn’t believe it when Kizu came back from a date with Ione and revealed the eagle-horse-lion creature. At first she thought it might be a summoned monster created by Ione, but the summoner had nothing to do with it! Comet belonged to Kizu! And, if Comet was Kizu’s daughter, then that obviously made Anata her cousin!

It was all she could think about these last few days. Kizu had been hard at work trying to clear out a home for Comet while Anata kept the hippogriff company.

Anata frowned at the wing she’d drawn. Comet’s coloring was hard to get right with just a quill and parchment. A mono colored image didn’t do the beast’s beauty justice. The feathers looked wrong as well. Maybe she needed to adjust the direction they pointed in?

“Oh! Is that a new card?” Rui once again interrupted her thoughts. “What’s the blood cost going to be? What sort of effects will it have? Since it has wings can it fly over land bound creatures? How does that play into your sea monsters though?”

With Mae out of town, Mitsuko had turned to Rui to play her usual card game. She had a complete deck of Ione’s bestiary cards copied that they used. The thing about Rui though was that he always thought up weird ways the monsters should interact with one another. It added a lot of additional layers onto the game.

A hand darted out and snatched the parchment from in front of her.

“Teacher! Anata is playing her stupid card game again! She’s not listening to your lesson!” Dean shouted. He waved the crumpled piece of parchment like a flag over his head.

Beside her Rui stiffened. Anata knew he wanted to deck Dean in the face again. They got in a scuffle at least every other week. Usually Dean only cornered him while outnumbered with his friends after class, but even still Rui never left the exchanges without getting a few licks of his own in.

Anata glanced at Rui and silently willed him not to start fighting Dean in the middle of the classroom with Ms. Reads watching. While it would certainly be entertaining, if Rui got suspended it would leave Anata to suffer alone.

Thankfully Rui seemed to understand her silent plea and he slumped back in his seat, grumbling quietly to himself as an exasperated Ms. Reads confiscated the drawing and gave Anata a quick, half-hearted lecture about paying attention during lessons.

Anata spent the rest of the class silently glaring at a smug Dean as the boy preened from his victory.

It was a relief when the school bell finally tolled, releasing her from the prison of imbeciles.

Kon, as per usual, lounged on the schoolhouse’s porch, watching with suspicion as people passed in the street. He’d been even more irritable than usual ever since Mae’s departure, but he still followed Anata to school every morning and walked her home at the end of the day.

At night, when he thought her asleep, Kon would climb into her bed and press up against her. He’d woken her up several times now, shaking and crying. But he was always back on his own small bed whenever the sun came up.

He never bluntly spoke the words, but Kon desperately missed his sister. Anata understood a bit how he felt. When she first left the World Dungeon, she’d clung to Kizu. But, unlike her where she slowly met more people and adjusted to the world, Kon’s personality had closed himself off. Which worked against him as he slowly had people important to him plucked out of his life. Anata could only imagine how she might have felt if Kizu had disappeared in her first months on the surface.

Rui hummed as they walked the familiar streets in the direction of Taroe’s home. They were considering returning to Rui’s hideout at the abandoned observatory, but it was usually better to go to Taroe’s place first. There they could get a snack and head to the hideout after. A precaution just in case one of the other school children tried following them.

One additional nice part of Kon walking home with her everyday was that the other kids were all terrified of him. They never approached her or Rui when Kon was nearby. It kept their walks through town peaceful.

As Anata opened the front door, a black and red object slammed into her and tackled her to the ground.

“I missed you so much!” Mae shouted. She shoved her face into Anata’s shirt and rubbed her snotty nose all over the cloth. Anata noticed there was a wide brimmed hat, similar to the one Stina wore, fallen on the ground nearby.

Beside them, Kon climbed up on their prone forms. He sat back on his haunches, as if their tangled bodies were a mountain he’d proudly summited.

Mae shifted her attention from Anata, over to her brother, wrestling him off her and pinning the white fox to the ground in a big hug.

Anata rose back to her feet and noted that Kon was only giving a token effort of resisting as Mae clung to him.

“Mae!” Rui called out. “You’re back from your trip? How was it? You got new clothes? They look so cool! Is that your hat? Can I try it on?”

Untangling herself from her brother and sitting up, Mae nodded eagerly at Rui. Anata noticed her friend’s cheeks were wet but Mae quickly brushed the tears away with a long black sleeve.

“It makes me double my height!” Rui proclaimed proudly as he plopped the hat over his tangled hair. His declaration was an exaggeration. It give him maybe two handspans more of height. It wasn’t nearly as tall as Kizu’s headmaster’s hat.

“It’s a witch hat!” Mae said proudly. “I earned it. I’m a witch now!”

“No way!” Rui’s eyes went wide. He quickly looked from Mae to Anata to Mae again, as if expecting them to be pulling a prank. “You actually went on vacation and came back a witch?”

Mae puffed up her chest. “Yes!”

“Nice! Now when Dean tries being a doofus you can hex him. Turn him into a frog. Oh! I know! A bullfrog because they’re super loud and obnoxious just like him. They make soooo much noise at night.”

Anata nodded her head in agreement. Rui did come up with some excellent solutions to problems sometimes. She wondered if Mae could also hex Dean’s mother.

Mae giggled. “I can’t hex anyone yet! Stina needs to teach me first. Right now she’s still teaching me special astronomy stuff. Like when other planets go into retrograde and help create holes in fate.”

“Woah. Now that you’re an apprentice, does this mean you won’t be coming to the schoolhouse anymore?”

Mae deflated. “No. Stina says normal school is still important. I have to learn arithmetic and study with everyone.”

Stepping forward, Rui punched her in the shoulder. “Nice. It’s been quiet without you around. Anata never talks back to the teacher and I need some help putting Dean and his crew back into their place.”

Anata and Mae both grinned. Kon growled his agreement as well.

“Oh!” Mae’s face lit up. “I did learn one witch secret.” She glanced over her shoulder as if someone might overhear her. “It’s super powerful. An old witch told me about it when Stina was busy staring at some star charts.”

All of them leaned in.

“A forbidden magic?” Rui asked.

“Not forbidden exactly. But secret!”

“Let’s go to the hideout,” Rui suggested. He handed her hat back and she plopped it on her head. “We can talk safely there.”

They were all in agreement and scrambled to get to the other side of town. Kon stayed at Mae’s feet as they dashed away. A few familiar people waved hello to Rui as they ran through the cobblestone streets but nobody stopped them.

Once successfully hidden away in the room with the massive, abandoned telescope, they all turned their undivided attention to an out of breath Mae.

She took a moment to straighten her witch hat and robes. Her bushy fox tail swished the dusty ground and she stood tall.

“I’ve been told the secret… of how to bond a familiar.”
