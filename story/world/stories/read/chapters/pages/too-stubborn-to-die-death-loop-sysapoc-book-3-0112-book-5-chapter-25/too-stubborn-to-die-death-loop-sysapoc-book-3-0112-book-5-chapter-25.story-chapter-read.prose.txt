Spotting ominous clouds creeping across the horizon, Aaron had taken to the sky for a better look.

“And just when I thought I was going to go on a relaxing cruise.”

The crude goblins did and said many questionable things, but Aaron had enjoyed sitting back and letting them do all the work. After all, most of the time he was nonstop trying to get stronger. And now, his chest sank. Thanks to his inhuman perception, he could see the lurching undead even as they were far on the horizon, hundreds of kilometers away.

Bloody fantastic. I’m not getting a break any time soon, am I?

It wasn’t just undead, though. There were many constructs among them, including the biggest he’d ever seen.

“That is one big bastard.”

That said, despite the massive size of the colossal construct, none of Darius’ minions had proven too difficult for him so far. Scanning the army, he could tell that while it was stronger than the previous one he had seen them fight, it was only marginally so, and with their new suits of armor, they would face such a threat much more easily than before.

However, the undead would still be good experience for the defenders of the city, and Aaron was starting to feel like their world needed stronger combatants after the dungeon.

Also, he knew there were more stages of the multiverse tournament that pitted the greatest against one another. And seeing what Darius had achieved while he and the other top rankers had been gone made him realize they needed to help lesser talents improve.

I better go check in with those goblins.

“Yes, like Mork says! The breeders will find us irresistible with all the credits we got!"

“I thought you weren’t coming to Dober to look for breeders,” Aaron said as he narrowed his gaze on the two lying goblins.

Mork and Gork glanced at each other as they traced circles in the ground with their feet.

“Oh, whatever,” Aaron groaned. “Just give me a moment.”

He wondered how many credits the goblins had earned off of the Alliance for making that armor, considering how much he had helped. Perhaps he should have asked more questions before helping them.

Not that Aaron was particularly driven by wealth. He could seize an entire city if he wanted to, after all.

The armor should balance the two sides. But that undead force does look menacingly powerful. Best make sure that they’ve got everything they need to overcome it.

To be extra sure that his decisions wouldn’t lead to unnecessary deaths, he also checked in with Ernest, who seemed to think they were more than capable of doing it themselves.

“What, the guards? You’re worried that they’re going soft? Heh. Don’t worry too much about that. They’re stronger than they look. Besides, field tests have verified that the new armor works well.”

If Ernest was happy, so was Aaron. But he still wanted to help from the sidelines if at all possible.

Aaron wanted to make sure that the Alliance could defend themselves, which meant coming up with a way to help them without doing it all himself. One day, the barriers would come down, and Superearth needed to defend itself without elite rankers like himself hanging around.

Well, I could cook, I suppose… But what?

As his mind spun with ideas, Aaron shot out from the city. Knowing that the army was quickly approaching, he went out into the destroyed lands where they had been fighting previously. The ground was absolutely soaked with the corrupted aether, and while he had already cleansed it through the obelisks, anything that had been growing in the foul soil had been well and truly infected by the corruption, and it gave Aaron an idea.

Speeding across the war-battered landscape, Aaron snatched up any vegetation he could find. Weeds, skeletal remains of trees, and random roots. Whatever, it didn’t matter. None of it looked edible, but that just inspired his creativity further.

“Where are you going?” The headmaster asked as Aaron marched through the front gates.

“I thought I could use the academy?”

“You can. But what have you got planned?”

“A cook-up.”

“A cook-up?” The headmaster said, following Aaron through the courtyard.

“Yeah. I’m probably going to be releasing all kinds of mana and aether while I do it.

“You will?”

“Yeah, mate. Pretty much sure of it.”

“Well, why didn’t you say so? Go right ahead. Mi casa tu casa, or however you say it.”

Reaching the kitchen, Aaron started pulling out all of his ingredients and going over them.

Even from a glance, he could tell that the undead horde would be great for leveling. He just needed to give them a bit of a hand so that opportunity wouldn’t turn into body bags.

“Okay, something to help the troops. Something to warm their souls, and fight back the corruption.”

First, he got to work cleaning and cleansing the tainted veg. This meal was supposed to help the troops, not hurt them, and he couldn’t risk making them sick, even if the ingredients had been cleansed already.

As he got to work, he sent out a call for ingredients. Most people felt indebted to him, and so, all kinds of meat and vegetables were forthcoming. People were eagerly arriving at the academy with all kinds of food to donate. Especially since news of the new horde had already reached the city, and no doubt the denizens were hoping Aaron would help them once again.

The barely edible vegetation he had looted was fit for normal use in the kitchen. But with some bones, vegetables, additional herbs, and spices, it could make a decent stock, he reasoned.

Meanwhile, as the stock bubbled away, he started to barbecue several different kinds of meat over a smoky flame, pasting it with a sweet and tangy marinade as it cooked.

Aaron sniffed it in and groaned. The smell was a delight, and watching over the academy courtyard, he was fairly certain he wasn’t the only one feeling it, as several students were clutching at their stomachs.

But all wasn’t perfect. The moment he tasted the stock, he knew something was wrong. His taste buds were not full ogre anymore, and as he evolved, they were becoming a mix of human and ogre, and he knew this wasn’t right.

It was too rich, and too earthy. It was almost chalk-like in his mouth, and quite unpleasant. The stock wasn’t everything, but the corrupted taint was what would help protect and heal the souls of the defenders, and so he wanted to incorporate it without ruining the food. After all, he had turned a new leaf, and rather enjoyed people enjoying his food.

Also, he could always make some grotesque ogre feast when nobody was around.

Roasting a wide array of vegetables and spices over the flame, he then took them and blended them up with a destructive whirlwind of aether, infusing the power into the soup.

“Okay, now that tastes good. Hopefully the stock doesn’t ruin you.”

Aaron added the stock and blended again. But still, the chalky, earthy flavor came through, and he knew he’d have to take it a bit further to mask that unpleasantness.

“Okay, let’s push this up a notch.”

He fiddled with the flavor a bit, but it was already too rich, and watering it down would weaken the power of the corrupted ingredients, lessening their effects.

Instead, he decided to create a green sauce. It was a simple blend of herbs, oil, and a bit of salt and lemon. He used the sole of his foot to grind up the herbs, pulsing mana through his anchors as he did it, and adding aether with his spirit hands. Aaron took his time balancing it and tasting it in between.

“Now that is the flavor of fresh. Delicious.”

Plating it up, he poured the soup base down, and layered meat atop it, then drizzled the green sauce over the top.

First serve was for himself, and Aaron took a bit of everything as he tried the layered dish.

“Now that is good stuff. Smoky rich meat, strange, but nice soup, and that fresh sauce just brings it all together. I’m getting pretty damn good at this if I do say so myself."
