Samuel’s hands were shaking.

As the adrenaline wore off and his body returned to normal, the fight played out in his mind over and over again. If it could even be called that. It was a curb stomp, and the creature did not stand a chance. He didn’t even get to see if it had any way of attacking that would have made up for its lack of weapons.

The revenant was far from the first thing he had killed, of course, but there was a big difference between killing a venomous snake and bashing in the skull of something that looked so similar to a human.

‘That was no human,’  he reminded himself.

It was an undead creature that was very willing to kill him. Besides, he did not have the luxury of choice with his quest.

He took a deep breath and shook himself before taking one last look at the smashed skull on the ground. It was disgusting, yet he forced himself to look. The gory sight was a reminder of the fact that somehow, the world he was living in had changed, and he would have to change with it if he wanted to survive.

Thankfully, if there was anything his grandmother had taught them well, it was how to adapt.

He did not find any more undead for a few minutes as he continued walking forward, which made him frown.

What was a single undead doing so far from the others? He had thought it was doing patrols or something similar, but that did not seem to be the case. Or else why would it be the only one so far out?

Perhaps he was simply overthinking it. He would not have questioned seeing a single small snake in the previous domain, after all. The fact that these undead were humanoid made him expect a certain level of intelligence. There was also the presence of a lich in charge, He would have expected a lich to at least be smart enough to properly plan defences, if his experience with fantasy had thought him anything.

The thought made him pause. His experience with fantasy could certainly not be relied on, and he would have to do well to remember that. So far he found himself doing a pretty terrible job of that.

Still, he supposed there was no harm in being paranoid and expecting intelligence when his life was at stake.

He was about to shrug and move on when something finally struck him.

The undead’s scream.

Hr had thought it was just a normal, if loud, monster scream, but what if he was wrong. What if it was a signal to tell everyone his location. It seemed like a reach to him, but who knew what was possible now?

Wariness and a sense of foreboding filled him as he walked on, hoping his theory was wrong and he would not get chased by an army of undead. It did not take long after that before he finally found signs of movement once more.

Unlike before, there were five undead, all walking around a small area with seemingly no pattern. They looked… mindless, enough that he was wondering if his earlier conjunction was just pure nonsense. These did not seem like they were capable of any kind of tactics. Certainly not advanced one’s like he suspected. The way they moved made him wonder if they had even registered the revenant’s scream. Maybe he had just been worried for nothing.

He approached them stealthily once more, thinking about his next steps. He could not use all his psi at once, given how little he had and how long it took to recover., so he would have to defeat them in a way that used his reserves sparingly. Not an easy task given his weapon.

Once more, they only noticed him when he came within a few metres of them. He actually suspected it would be the same way even if he did  not bother being quiet.

This time, he decided to reduce the amount of mental energy he put into the stick. He did not need to smash their skulls in to kill them, so there was no reason to waste so much power.

Or at least so he hoped. There was always the chance that he was wrong and the revenants would not die unless he smashed open their heads.

When his stick smashed into the head of the first one, its head snapped back. Just as he expected, there was more resistance to his swing, and there were no smashed brains. He did, however, hear something crack.

Unfortunately for him, that single strike was not enough to actually kill the thing, so he swung his stick again. Though he used the same amount of mental energy, the skull split open this time, filling the air with the pungent smell of rot as the creature died.

You have slain an [Undead Revenant - level 2].

He pursed his lips at that.

That took more mental energy than what he expected, though it was still noticeably less than what he used the first time, which made it a win.

The other revenants did not just stand there waiting. Mindless as they seemed, they were not so clueless as to ignore the very blatant threat in front of them. Thankfully for Samuel, they were merely faster than he expected, not fast enough to actually overwhelm him.  Avoiding their attacks was very doable, though not particularly easy. He would have to kill them quickly or risk getting into genuine trouble if the fight continued for much longer.

He jumped back to create some space between him and the undead. When the first one predictably came at him, he was swinging his stick up in an uppercut,  a quick blast of telekinesis dislocating its jaw. That did not kill it of course, but another quick strike tore off its head.

Another kill notification came in. This time the  revenant was level three, something he was actually grateful for.  Higher levels meant he would level up faster, after all. At least as long as it was something he could handle.

The next two were not difficult to kill, being level three and he found himself face to face with the last remaining undead soon enough. When he hit it with his stick, though, he found that its reaction was completely different from the previous ones. Instead of the cracking sound he was expecting. The head of the revenant was merely pushed back a bit, its head otherwise unharmed.

Samuel frowned. That did not bode well for him. This last revenant was probably at a higher level. Which was strange because it did not seem to move faster than others. And there was not really anything that would indicate its level was higher. He quickly realized that his previous tactic would not work. He would have to increase the amount of telekinesis he was using if he wanted to hit it.

He was about to send a fresh blast of telekinesis when something happened. For the first time since he had met the revenants, this one finally did something other than merely stumbling around and trying to hit him blindly. It moved with speed instead, much faster than anything he had expected, and grabbed him by the neck, fingers digging into his throat.

Samuel didn’t want to know what would happen if he let it continue. Perhaps it would eat his brain. Or maybe it would turn him into an undead like it was. Either way, it did not matter, because he panicked at that moment and emptied a fourth of his remaining mental energy reserves straight into the head of the creature.

The blast of telekinesis was not quite as compressed as what he used with his stick. With the amount he was using, it did not matter. The head burst open like a watermelon, spraying bits and pieces of rotten, revenant flesh on his body.

Samuel shuddered in disgust. Perhaps he should have pushed it away from him first before blasting its head open.

You have slain an [Undead Revenant - Level 5].

He looked at the message, unsurprised. He had already suspected that it was a higher level than the others. It was simply two different not to be. He was, however, fairly impressed by how quickly he was able to end it. Especially compared to his previous fight against the level 5 snake.

Though he was not sure if that was because he had gotten much stronger since that fight or that the snake was just a worse matchup than the revenant? He was leaning towards a mixture of both because while he would rather fight the Revenant, he could definitely feel himself getting more powerful. His telekinesis was becoming more fluid and easier to use than before.

You have gained a level. You are now level 4.

Telekinesis is now Level 3.

Samuel looked at the new levels with satisfaction. Despite the risk involved, the sight of numbers going up was starting to grow on him. He couldn’t resist a good rush of dopamine.

Once more, he put two points in psi immediately. He’d decided a two thirds split was what he should aim for. Every two points would go to psi while the last point could rotate between his other important stats.

His psi rose to 13 points, up from 11. As for the last point, he spent a few minutes thinking about where he should put it. After weighing his options for a bit, he eventually decided on agility. During the fight with the last revenant, he had noticed how fast they were. He could keep up with them for now, but perhaps there would come a time where he had no psi remaining. Or when, his enemy would overcome him with speed. It was better to invest in the stat now.

One stat point brought his agility to eleven.

He did not waste time after that, moving forward a few moments later. Now that he knew he was capable of killing the revenants, he intended to push forward as much as he could. Gaining levels and increasing his skills. He did not wish to repeat what happened with the snakes in the previous domain. Thankfully, it seemed like these undead were too stupid to come up with an elaborate plan? Or at least he hoped they were. He was still wary about the presence of the lich.

Speaking of which, he actually wondered about these snakes. He was not sure if the way they were all gathered in one place was natural or they had somehow set a trap for him? He desperately hoped it was the former, but something told him that was unlikely. He had already seen some signs. But if his suspicions were correct, then it would prove that monsters were actually intelligent.

The night went on, the light of the moon illuminating the world around. Samuel killed two more groups of revenants. Each group had one level 5.

He managed to gain one more level, taking him to level five.

Two stats went into psi, as usual, and the third one to vitality. After spending some time deliberating, he was not sure whether the stat could actually heal him or how exactly it would work. What he doubted was his ability to stay completely free from injuries forever. In the event that he ended up talking one, he would want to increase his recovery speed as much as he could at least.

It was shortly after that when something finally changed. Because ahead of him, a small group of undead walked around randomly just like the others he’s faced before.

Unlike the last few groups he had come across, though, none of them were revenants.
