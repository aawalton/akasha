Kalsin woke drenched in sweat.

He was lying on a hard straw bed stuffed in the corner of the dilapidated cell. Vines had broken through the cracks in the floor, creeping up and around the iron bars separating his small space from a torch-lit corridor, with several more cells yawning empty on the other side. Silence was absolute, save for a faint whistle of wind rearing its head up from somewhere far down the hall.

Kalsin sat up straight with his head throbbing, and though the pain was retreating by the second, he had a sinking feeling that nothing would be the same for him going forward. The images from his vision blinked before his eyes, and yet again he looked back on the bottled up dread Jack felt bursting out that day, from a time years and years ago before they had met.

Each insult, every word, gesture and glance that dehumanized her adding up to something terrible that eventually broke her spirit. Though he knew he'd just lived through the story of her scar—he could still hardly believe that brittle, self-doubting girl was the same, confident bodyguard he'd come to know through their travels.

But then he remembered another memory next to it. One he'd seen as a child in a dark alleyway, after a thief had forced him to touch the repulsive rotten line marring her face. After which he had woken up no longer Clenn.

Kalsin shivered once.

He desperately pleaded as he slowly reached up. Please, don't let it be real, chanting it over and over again like a creed, though he knew it wouldn't make a difference.

And it didn't. His fingers searched only for a moment until they brushed against a scar sitting squarely on his jawline. A scar that was the exact mirror of Jack's.

He let his arm drop, getting it out of the way just before foul things pushed up his throat and out his mouth. He doubled over, retching until there was nothing left in his stomach.

The spasms slowly died down. He spit to rid himself of the bitter taste, but laughter came along with it, the crazed sound breaking out of Kalsin abruptly. Somehow, having drawn a bad card from fate's deck at birth, or through some twisted fucking joke at his expense by the gods, he didn't get scars like an ordinary human.

No, turns out, things couldn't be that simple for him. It wasn't enough to be born surgeless, it wasn't enough growing up without his real parents, but apparently—scars would take residence on his body upon a glancing touch. Infecting him on contact, like the plague.

Funny, he said in his mind, and maybe out loud too. Funny how his inherent disgust of scars had kept this tidbit of detail in the dark throughout his life. But the real punchline? He'd been desperately looking for a way to heal himself, and now he was farther away from the prospect of being Clenn than anyone else in the Republic.

He laughed harder. His voice slowly turned into small whimpers like deformed sounds of an injured animal.

Footsteps echoed from the hallway in chorus with the metallic rattling of keys. Strangely enough, for a fleeting second Kalsin was embarrassed about the mess he'd made next to his bed, but it was gone quickly. He was in prison. No sane chap's going to expect him to present a welcoming committee with bouquets of roses and tulips. Likely, they wouldn't care if he soiled himself for a week straight, considering the fact that the cell lacked even as much as a bucket.

It was a soldier who came for him, not an inconspicuous agent of the law. Things were looking up, eh? Except the soldier was Clenn, and he'd made that blindingly clear as he opened the iron door, barged in, and roughly pushed Kalsin against the cold wall of his cell.

Kalsin silently endured as he had his wrists tied behind him, wondering if the guard was currently standing in the spent contents of his belly. Probably not the wisest direction for his bleary wits. Kalsin hoped there wasn't an insane smile plastered on his face as the guard pushed him along the hall.

He expected to be led through a long series of connecting tunnels, but they had only ascended a single step of stairs before arriving at a level considerably more civilized. For one, it was bright with sunlight streaming through the narrow windows, and closed doors had taken the place of iron bars. This prison wasn't a large one.

They stopped before a room at the end of the hallway as the guard paused to knock. The affirmative to enter came immediately, and they shuffled inside a tidy little office with a nice view across the valley.

The first thing Kalsin noticed was a young woman standing at the far side of the room, wearing a summer dress with lily patterns, deep red hair reaching down below her waist. Kalsin couldn’t see her face through the red locks, so absorbed she was looking down at something at her feet, facing him, eerily still.

"Ough, good gods," exclaimed the man behind the desk as he spotted Kalsin, then waved away the guard without a word. Wearing a formal military uniform with shiny markings sewn around his collar, he was probably more than a lieutenant but less than a major. Or maybe fulfilling an entirely different role. Kalsin didn't know much about the army's inner workings.

The officer scratched his neatly trimmed beard, probably not due to fleas since it was properly maintained. "Well, that's a complication I certainly didn't need right now," he muttered to himself, then spoke louder. "You feeling antsy, hm? Got anything to get off your chest? Just a moment ago I would've sworn there was a tissue somewhere around here." He started rummaging around his desk with a complete air of gravity.

"Oh, this?" Kalsin asked, making an attempt to point a trapped finger upwards at his face. "Naw, you know how it is. It'll probably take a few hours until shock wears off. You can't tell, but I'm screaming on the inside," he deadpanned.

The officer glanced down at the poster laying before him, then back up at his prisoner. The wanted poster depicted a half-assed drawing of Kalsin's face except for the main determining factor of identification, his scar. Or now scars, plural. While the paper-Kalsin had a single black mark between his eyes, the real deal standing before the officer sported another one on his jaw.

So, obviously, the officer thought getting imprisoned traumatized Kalsin to a point where he developed another scar.

Unironically, that was fucking hilarious, but Kalsin managed to reduce his mirth to a twitch of a muscle.

The officer made a noncommittal sound. "Right then. Standby-Colonel Atlas Nevor, at your service. Or I would be, were you not the focal point of a razored shitstorm, with the Civil House's agents acting out the role of metals, and me shacked in the middle of bumfuck fieldwhere to witness it."

"Sorry. Next time when I get the cravings to get clapped in chains, I'll ask my captors to leave you out of it."

"You say that now, but..." Atlas trailed off, absorbed in a wrinkled letter that looked like it had been rotting on his desk for a decade.

Kalsin surveyed the man's features, head tilted. He'd only met the general once when he was a kid, but everyone across the Republic knew the name. "Nevor?"

"No I'm not his son, yes we're related, no I did not receive my rank as a result of favoritism," Atlas rattled off in an almost bored tone. He rubbed an eye with his thumb. "I'm General Silas' nephew, though frankly, I fail to see how that's relevant to your situation. Figures you're the curious type."

Kalsin let that hang in the air before getting to the question he'd been holding back. "I'd like to ask, what exactly happened at the gate? Things get a little hazy."

"This notice here," Atlas said, tapping the poster and ignoring the question, "Says you're wanted for murder, conspiracy against the Republic and," he leaned closer, squinting as he read. "Tergiversation of sleuth perlustration."

"I can attest to the latter. I'm quite handy with a pen, but I've always been terribly shaken from sloth illustration."

Atlas cracked just the faintest of smiles. "I swear they make legal gibberish overly convoluted on purpose." He leaned back in his chair, watching Kalsin thoughtfully. "But, in any case. I have a situation on my hands. The Civil House is knocking on my door to hand you over so you can be tried and prosecuted for your crimes. Now I don't have to comply, but they expect me to, and the military as a rule hates to deal with government fallout. Except—and this is where legality comes to deliver a bite in the ass—you made your intentions to join the army very clear before the agents could identify you."

"What about my companions?"

"We'll get to that later." He paused. "Thing is, immunity for a soldier is no joke. It's probably the largest driving factor to get people to join, and, though the rest of the Republic likes to pretend otherwise, we desperately need new blood to maintain defense along the Eastern Expanse. You've seen all our propaganda outside, yes? All that work is blown right off the hinges if townsfolk see an aspiring soldier dragged through the streets. So while I really don't want to piss off powerful people, throwing you to the wolves isn't quite a winning situation either."

Kalsin decided it was time to keep his mouth shut. He hadn't been taking the situation with the severity it deserved. He'd been too distracted by… well, by suddenly becoming whatever kind of human-adjacent abomination that he was. But now he needed to make an effort to focus. If not for himself, then for the sake of those close to him.

Atlas, quite uncolonel-like, slowly tipped his chair back and forth, though he never took his eyes off Kalsin. "You see, people don't look kindly upon people killing other people, just as a general principle. How harshly they punish murder depends on the given region. In Araz, you'll probably get away with a quick beheading. Seven Fallter? A month in the deepest cells to marinate in your hopelessness, then the slow, agonizing noose around your neck. But here in Újtavak, well. Are you familiar with the right to duel?"

"Heard about it. If both parties accept, they can settle their differences in a duel to the death?"

"To the death, yes. That's not murder, mind you, but it does create a certain open mindedness about the matter of life-short-cuttage. Elsewhere, people would cringe at the thought of being friends with a murderer. But here, if you can put someone in an early grave, people figure you won't be entirely useless if the Veiled invade."

Atlas kept up his intense gaze for long seconds, and Kalsin matched his stare. At around half a minute it was getting a little uncomfortable, so Kalsin finally cleared his throat. "So where does all this leave us?"

"If they buried you today. What do you think people would say at your funeral?"

The bizarre question threw Kalsin off his game for a moment. It was a test, though not an obvious one. Surely, Atlas was trying to see what kind of man he was, and Kalsin had a feeling the colonel would sense if he didn't answer truthfully. So no matter how ridiculous he found the question at first, he carefully considered it before giving his reply.

"They would curse me for disappointing them. They would proudly call me an unethical bastard. They would mourn the loss of a friend."

Atlas rubbed his eye again, head turned down in thought. Long seconds passed. He then righted his chair with a thunk. "Hmph, to hells with it. Can you follow orders?"

He went on before Kalsin could answer.

"Never mind that, because if you can't, I'm signing your death warrant just the same."

With that, Atlas launched a seemingly endless barrage of questions at Kalsin, from inquiring about his birth place to asking him to state any property he owned. Previous professions, family ties, combat experience were all documented. Kalsin's answers weren't entirely honest, which is to say, he made most of them the fuck up. When asked about his surge, he gave the usual lie—weak rot. A low amount of mental surge, and a practically worthless amount of dirt's physical surge. With his newfound double-scars, he looked the part too.

Eventually, Atlas stopped writing to look through his handiwork, absently scratching at his beard. Kalsin stood there waiting, patient.

Until he wasn't. Now that his own future was set, he had other concerns.

"The people who traveled alongside me," Kalsin asked. "I hope your generosity extends to them, as well."

"Your Scarrd friends? Well, my men did hear you say 'we', and one of them did carry a letter of reference. But things aren't certain just yet," Atlas said, still reading the document.

Kalsin stiffened. "Why's that? Our warrant was the same. It stands to reason that if I get my immunity, then so should they."

Atlas looked up, blinking. "Oh. Sorry, you misunderstand. They're now fresh recruits of the army, just like you—nice and out of the Civil House's reach. But they weren't as lucky as you were during the confrontation at the gate. The man caught a nasty bolt to his thigh, and the woman's injuries are much worse. She might not live through the night."

"The woman?" Kalsin asked with a desperate voice, suddenly not giving a damn about looking collected anymore. "Who was it?"

A small frown appeared on Atlas's face. "The Scarrd one, of course. Why, are you claiming to cavort with a Clenn?"

Wondering if he'd heard wrong, Kalsin tried and failed to process what the colonel was saying. The hells did he mean by the Scarrd one when that description applied to both Jack and Erazza? "I don't understand," he managed to get out.

"I honestly can't blame you." Atlas said with an exhausted sigh. "It's a proper mess. Let me paint you the story I received from my men. People approach the gate like they always do, nothing out of the ordinary. The agent of the House asks them to reveal themselves so they can be identified via scars. Annoying, but eh, we've been putting up with it for the past few days. That's when you," he pointed at Kalsin, "declare your intention to join. The agent is frothing at the mouth to capture you. My men are fucking confused, and they're on edge, which is never a good combination."

Atlas shook his head, exasperated. "The moment you fled, panic reared. My men opened fire. While you were lucky enough to be tackled by another traveler—probably trying to help the guard capture you, bless the moron's heart—your friends didn't fare so well. After we had you secured, the agent confirmed the identity of three wanted persons, two unharmed bahten, and a Clenn bystander who, despite the shield on her back, ended up with a bolt in the foot. Hopefully a reminder to let guards do their jobs in the future."

He might've said more, but Kalsin didn't hear any of it. He felt dizzy, his vision tunneled as if the world itself was constricting around him. He tried making sense of it all, but his mind kept circling around a single thought; Jack was Clenn.

Jack.

Was.

Clenn.

His lifelong friend, bodyguard and companion was cured. And Kalsin was the one who suffered for it.

When he'd touched her scar by accident, somehow, for some indecipherable reason, the scar ceased to exist on her body and instead became a part of him. Did the same thing happen with his first scar, in the alleyway? It had been so dark that night, happened so long ago that he couldn't remember. He couldn't remember, but even if he did, would he care?

Because for him, this made one thing clear; he would never be Clenn.

If people found out about this ability—this curse—that Kalsin had, they would swarm him until he was a blackened husk, a ruin made of a hundred-thousand scars. He'd be used without remorse, used for the greater good.

Was he… was his own body the legend he’d been chasing for all those years?

Kalsin shuddered. He wanted to laugh, and to scream, and to curl up until the world that played this cruel joke on him disappeared. Not now. He… he needed a clear head to think.

For the present moment, he was just a freshly pressed soldier—and the greater good could go fuck itself.

The colonel was watching him with a bewildered expression, probably wondering whether his newest recruit was mentally sound.

Kalsin raised his bound wrists. "This still necessary? It would seem I'm no longer a criminal, uh, sir. Do I call you sir moving forward?"

He nodded. "Yes, but that's assuming you live to see another one of our meetings again. You're going far from here."

"Then, sir, I have an observation to report. This entire mess of a situation on your hands, and the reason you decided to help us? It has nothing to do with propaganda, or army reputation, or even what kind of person you think I am."

"Really. How come?"

"You caught the Civil House bent over a chasm, and you took the opportunity to deliver a crunchy kick in the balls."

"I'm a simple man, who likes to indulge in simple pleasures. But that's not something I can either confirm or deny, soldier," said Atlas with a stern face, only his eyes betraying his mirth.

Kalsin looked to the side, and his smile suddenly froze on his face. "Where did your daughter go?"

"Daughter?" Atlas asked, following Kalsin’s gaze toward the corner of the room, then back at him. "I don’t have children. And, Gods, even if I did, I’d never let them step foot in this office."

"But I saw..."

"Are you feeling fine?" Atlas watched him, his tone answering his own question.

"Fine. Yeah."

Kalsin was not fine.

Because the red-haired girl he’d seen when he entered the room was nowhere to be seen.

No trace of red hair.

No lily patterns.

No obscured face.

A shiver ran across Kalsin’s neck, and he swore he heard the faintest echoes of a distant giggle.
