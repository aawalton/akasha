I knew exactly which world I was in before I even opened my eyes. I didn’t need to. The world had become so familiar to me that I recognised the feel of it, the touch of the dizziness that swept over me. I was back at the Academy.

Restless energy filled me. It felt so similar to the anxiety that had coursed through my body in the other world, but I wasn’t terrified as I lay in my bed at the Academy. The sensation was different. I was excited.

It was more than that, though. I’d been excited before, but I’d never felt quite so teeming with anticipation, and I wasn’t quite sure why or what to do about it. I wanted to leap out of bed, to rush out into the halls, and…

I didn’t know what I wanted to do, but I wanted to do something. I had to. It felt like if I stayed in bed for any long, I’d explode or scream, and I didn’t want to do either. It would have been better than just lying there, but it was still too early. I was sure of it. When I’d checked the time what felt like moments ago, it had been just before five, so I didn’t need to get up for another couple of hours.

A shrill noise split the air, as if having heard my thoughts, and a smile appeared on my lips. I sat up immediately, reaching for my alarm clock and stared at it in surprise. Somehow, more than two hours had passed. I must have dozed off at some point without even realising, but I was glad. I’d spent most of the night waking up every hour or so, and it was so frustrating. I just wanted time to move quicker, but it had ticked on at a stubborn crawl until that moment.

Throwing my duvet back, I leapt out of bed, barely able to stop myself from sprinting into the bathroom. For once, I was glad Katie and Abbie hadn’t made it out of the induction wing yet. I knew they would have noticed the fact that I practically skipped across the room or that I couldn’t keep the smile from my face, and I didn’t want them to know how happy I was.

They wouldn’t get it. Not properly. They would assume that Seth was the source of my happiness, that I was so excited and eager to hurry up and get downstairs because it meant I would see him again, but it wasn’t that. Obviously, I was looking forward to seeing him, but I would have been just as excited if it was one of the others too.

It would have been slightly different if it was Katie or Abbie; they were probably going to move into the dorm with me, so they would have been there from the moment I woke up, and I wouldn’t have to rush out to see them.

I was just glad to have a friend in the Academy with me. That was the real reason I couldn’t stop myself from bouncing on the balls on my feet as I brushed my teeth. It felt like I’d been alone there for an eternity, but that was done. It was over. I could go back to living half in the Academy and half in my reality without being worried about the anxiety and loneliness that would slam into me whenever I returned.

That was a relief. I was genuinely relieved that I had a safe world I could visit whenever I wanted. I didn’t have to find new ones and risk being murdered or hunted down or handed far too much responsibility anymore. I could just go to the Academy whenever I was bored or needed to get away from home.

I spat the mouthwash out and splashed water on my face quickly before peering up at my reflection. It was clear I’d barely slept the night before because the bags under my eyes were even darker than they normally were. They stood out clearly against my pale skin.

My hand twitched towards the mirrored cabinet above the sink. I had some makeup in there. I vaguely remembered requesting it in class the other day. I’d been encouraged to. They said good makeup skills came in useful surprisingly often, but I’d not touched the pieces since then.

Indecision washed over me. Part of me longed to pull the doors open and layer some concealer under my eyes to cover up the dark bruises, but I was conflicted. I normally wore makeup to school, both in that world and in my reality. I didn’t wear much, just some concealer and mascara mostly, but I hadn’t since I entered the induction wing.

There was no point. Not really. My days in the Academy were so much more active than in my reality or the school I went to before I was recruited. I had more PE classes, and even my regular classes were more physical than I was used to. If I did take the time to put makeup on, I’d probably just sweat it off within a couple of hours. Then I’d need to keep reapplying it, and I didn’t have the time or patience for that.

Although some people did, I thought. My hand moved slightly closer to the cabinet where I kept the makeup again. Most people didn’t, but I’d definitely noticed some people wearing it. They were mostly the people who worked in the GSOD or the research labs, though. The trainees who only had a PE classes a few times a week rather than every day. That made sense, but a couple of the field ops did too.

I stared at my reflection in the mirror for a few more seconds, trying to work out what I wanted to do before shaking my head. No. It wasn’t worth it. I had swimming just before lunch and climbing in the afternoon, and I looked fine. Not great, obviously. I was too tired for that, but I looked fine.

Plus, it would have been weird if I suddenly started wearing makeup. I wasn’t sure how many people would notice, but some would. They were trained to pick up on things like that; most of the trainees had observation classes multiple times a week, after all. Even if I only wore a little, not enough to really be noticeable, they’d see it, and they might jump to the wrong explanation.

A voice floated through the world, making me jump so hard I almost hit my hand on the cabinet. I couldn’t hear the exact words being said, but there was a sharpness to them that simultaneously made me want to return to my reality and never go back. Dizziness pulled at my mind, and I stood frozen as I grappled with indecision.

Mom didn’t say anything else, though. I strained my ears, trying to make sure I wasn’t missing anything, but I was pretty sure she didn’t speak again. It was just a one-off. A single snappy comment that probably wasn’t even directed at me.

Someone probably cut her off again, or maybe someone braked suddenly in front of her. She always reacted pretty loudly when that happened, and if I’d somehow done something to annoy her, it was unlikely that she’d just let it go without saying anything more.

She did sometimes, though. It depended on her mood. Sometimes, she liked to sit with her irritation, let it fester and become more and more furious until she finally exploded. Then, she’d confront me about it.

I chewed my lip, my eyes finding my own in the mirror. I could see the reluctance etched into my face, the longing to never return to my reality so that I didn’t have to face my mom again. It would have been so much easier, in a way. I would have been happier if I never went back, but I knew I couldn’t do it. I couldn’t leave Phoebe and my dad.

Still, I was glad I’d found somewhere. Having the Academy to escape to made everything so much more manageable. The seemingly endless car journey with my mom felt like it was passing quickly, and I was barely restless, which was a huge change from how I normally felt by that time.

I didn’t need to go back, I told myself. It was unlikely that I’d done anything to annoy Mom whilst running on autopilot, and if I had, she’d either forget about it or explode later. If I went back, there was nothing that I’d be able to do to fix it, so it was fine to stay in the world with the Academy for a little longer. I could always check in on my reality in a little bit, if I wanted to.

Even as I thought that I knew I wouldn’t do it. I wouldn’t return to my reality until I needed to. If something else happened, something more urgent, or I no longer felt the need to stay in the Academy, I’d do it, but not until then.

The smile returned to my face, and I spun around, bounding out through the open bathroom door. I really needed to stop doing that, I realised. At some point, I’d gotten into the habit of just leaving the bathroom door open as I showered or got ready for the day, and I wasn’t sure why or even when it had happened.

It just made more sense to me, for some reason. My bedroom door was closed and locked, so no one could just barge in, and I liked being able to see into the room easier. Plus, it meant I could hear if anyone knocked on my door. That didn’t happen often, but someone had stopped by a couple of times whilst I was in the bathroom.

But I was going to have roommates soon. Hopefully. Katie and Abbie had to get out of the induction wing at some point, and I was still clinging to the hope that they’d be moving into the room with me. If they did, I couldn’t exactly leave the door open whilst I showered or used the bathroom.

Even the thought of that made me blush, and I wasn’t sure why. We’d gotten changed together when we’d shared the room in the induction wing, but something about showering or peeing with the door open felt different.

I’d remember not to do it when they were there, I decided as I pulled the wardrobe doors open and peered inside. It would be much easier, I reassured myself. When it was just me, there was nothing really stopping me from doing it, but once they were out, it would be fine.

My head cocked to the side as I examined the row of uniforms, trying to work out what to wear. Normally, it wasn’t too hard to decide. It had been at first, obviously. For the first few days after I moved to the main building, I’d struggled to choose what I wanted to wear to class. I’d been so worried about standing out in a bad way, and I thought that anxiety was gone, but I could feel it pulling at me again.

I was being silly, I told myself as I reached out towards one of the dresses. That was what I normally wore. It was the most comfortable option, and there was no reason for me to pick something else just because…

There was no reason.

Still, worry churned in my stomach as I got dressed quickly and brushed my hair before pulling it back into a ponytail. I tried to practice the deep breathing techniques I’d been taught in one of my classes, but it did little to quell the sensation that continued to swirl within me.

Glancing at the clock one last time, I grabbed my backpack and unzipped it. My eyes scanned the contents inside, making sure I hadn’t forgotten anything. It wasn’t really a big deal if I did. I could just return to my room at any point throughout the day and grab it, but the thought of having to admit to any of the teachers that I’d forgotten my tablet or something made me grimace. They wouldn’t care too much, but I still hated it.

I had everything, though. I’d packed my bag the night before, so there was nothing else I needed to do. I grinned as I zipped it up again and swung it onto my shoulders before racing towards my door.

The corridor outside was quieter than I expected. There were a handful of people milling about, either heading down for breakfast or heading up to bed after a night shift in the GSOD, but there were usually more. Or at least, it felt like there were. It had always been a little intimidating to leave my room. I could hear voices drifting through the door as I tried to summon the courage to do it, but I hadn’t even hesitated. It wasn’t until I was halfway along the corridor that I even realised that anxiety had been missing, for some strange reason.

A realisation washed over me as I reached the top of the stairs, and I froze. Before we parted, Seth and I had agreed to meet in the morning. We’d said that we’d have breakfast together because we both had international relations first thing, so it made sense. Plus, we normally ate together when we’d been in the induction wing.

But we hadn’t said where we’d meet. We just decided that we would.

“Oh, sorry,” I said, quickly stepping out of the way, my cheeks flushing.

“No worries,” the boy who’d been coming up the stairs towards me said, flashing me a smile.

I barely noticed it, though. My attention was on the thoughts racing through my mind. Should I go to Seth’s room? Perhaps he was in there, waiting for me to knock and show him the way down to the canteen. It wasn’t hard to find it. We’d been to the canteen yesterday, but he still could have been worried about getting lost.

He didn’t seem like the type, though. Unlike me, he barely seemed to worry or ruminate on things. Seth was much more confident than I was, and he’d most likely gone down to the canteen without even considering waiting in his room for me to come and collect him. Even if he wasn’t already down there, he’d probably be confused if I did go and knock on his door. He’d be polite about it; he always was, but it would seem weird to him.

That was enough to spur me into action. I blinked quickly and shook my head slightly to clear it before moving towards the stairs again. My eyes darted around, checking to make sure no one had noticed my hesitation, but no one was even looking at me.

Still, my thoughts raced as I started to make my way down the stairs. I couldn’t help but picture Seth sitting alone in his room and staring at the door. The thought of him picking nervously at the skin around his nails as he waited for me to knock, trying to gather enough confidence to stand up and leave the safety of his room, broke my heart. My stomach sank, and I almost turned around and raced back upstairs again.

Keep going, I told myself, forcing my legs to continue moving. If Seth wasn’t in the canteen when I got there, I’d go back up. Or I’d give it a few minutes. It was still really early, and he might still be waking up and getting ready for class. If he was, I didn’t want to interrupt that or make him feel like he had to hurry.

The temptation still pulled at me as I continued down the stairs, and each step was a fight. My mind flitted between wanting to make sure he was okay and didn’t feel too anxious about being in the Academy and not wanting to seem weird or overbearing. I wasn’t. I didn’t think I was, anyway. I’d just been so scared and nervous when I first got there, and I didn’t want him to feel the same way. It had been so horrible, and if I could stop anyone from feeling that way, I wanted to.

I was still filled with indecision as I reached the ground floor and started to cross the foyer towards the canteen. I couldn’t help but stare into the room, scrutinising the tables in search of Seth. It was hard, though. Despite the different uniform options, everyone was dressed in basically the same thing, and that made them all blend together. It was hard to pick out anyone who looked like Seth, but as I entered the room, I found him.

Seth was on the far side of the room, and his head was down as he placed his tray on the table, but as he began to sit down, he looked up. I watched as his eyes scanned the room before landing on me. Visible relief washed over his face, and a smile pulled at his lips as he waved at me before gesturing towards the empty seat beside him.

My heart thumped at the sight, and I returned the smile before nodding. I started to move towards the buffet, feeling the anxiety drain from my body. He’d already found his way down to the canteen, I told myself. There was no need to worry about that at all. I knew he’d be fine.

He seemed happy to see me, a small voice in the back of my head whispered as I grabbed a plate and began to load it. I wanted to dismiss the thought, to not really think about it, but I couldn’t. It was impossible not to think about his smile as he’d spotted me.

And he’d saved me a seat. We’d agreed to eat together, and there were plenty of free spots in the canteen. It wasn’t even half full, but he’d still saved it for me. He wanted me to sit with him, and that made me happier than it should have.

Stop it, I snapped at myself, trying to stop my runaway thoughts. I was overthinking it and reading too much into everything, and I needed to stop. Seth was acting completely normally. He hadn’t done anything that unusual, and he was just happy to see me because I was his friend. That was all, and I had to stop letting myself get carried away before it ended badly.

“Morning,” he said cheerfully as I approached.

“Morning,” I replied, matching his tone.

“How did you sleep?”

“Really well,” I lied as I slipped into the chair next to his. “How about you?”

Seth’s nose scrunched up as he took a bite of sausage and chewed it before replying.

“Eh, not too badly,” he replied. “It was weird, though. I think I’ve gotten too used to sharing a room. It was hard to fall asleep without Scott snoring in the other bed.”

A laugh spilled from my lips as I picked up a slice of toast.

“I know what you mean. I found it really weird at first too. I kept waking up and expecting to see Abbie and Katie in the other beds…” I trailed off, worried that I’d said too much.

Seth didn’t need to know about that, and I didn’t want him to know just how difficult I’d found it, but the sight of those empty beds broke my heart a little more every morning. They were a symbol of my loneliness, and more than once, that had brought me to tears.

“I did the same!” Seth cried with a smile. “I fully expected Scott to be waiting for me when I got out of the shower this morning, and it was so strange that he wasn’t!”

“Hopefully, he’ll pass the induction soon. And Katie and Abbie too.”

Seth’s expression turned thoughtful as he continued eating.

“That would be nice.”

“Yeah.”

Silence settled over the table. For a few minutes, neither of us spoke. We just ate. I could feel the urge to say something rising within me. It was growing stronger and stronger, but I couldn’t work out what to say. Half-formed thoughts and questions flitted through my mind, but none felt right.

“So, you have empty beds in your room?” Seth asked.

“What?”

The question had come out of nowhere, and I was taken aback by it. Of all of the things that I thought he’d say, that was not on the list.

“You, uh… you said you kept expecting to see Abbie and Katie in the other beds,” Seth explained, his cheeks turning pink.

“Oh, yeah,” I replied, feeling blood rushing into my own cheeks too. “Yeah, there’s two.”

“Oh, cool. And there’s no, like, nameplates on the door or anything?”

“Nope. Just mine.”

Seth nodded to himself, his expression relieved.

“That’s good.”

“Yeah. I mean, I know they might not be for Katie and Abbie, but it’s promising, right?” I asked, wanting to hear someone else say it.

“I think so,” Seth confirmed. “I mean, they both seemed to be doing really well in the induction wing, so I doubt they’ll get kicked out now. I reckon they’ll all be out soon.”
