“Are you ready?” Andihar asked Eik.

They were sitting together in the front room of the absolutely massive structure that the Dayarunar family had the gall to refer to as their house.

It was ludicrous, to be frank.

Eik as well could have bought something like that, but since he had only very recently become wealthy, he had a strong preference for the cozy and relatively small home he shared with his family now. It just felt a little too could and empty if you couldn’t see from one end of the house to the other without a high-ranker’s powerful senses or at least an expensive pair of binoculars.

He certainly had the money to afford, well, whatever he wanted—quite literally. There was probably not anything in this world that he could not go out and get his grabby little mittens on if he so desired. So long as it existed and was for sale.

To be precise, he did not ever have that much dough on his credit slab. Most of his personal wealth was donated, mostly to serve the development, maintenance, and comfort of Forest along with some of the other settlement around the world.

He had also found investing to be quite fun, although the kind of returns he tended to see cast… some doubt on his insight and wisdom when it came to that side of the market.

No, his true economic fortune lay in the vaults of Eik’s Excellent Elixir Emporium, not to mention the belt pouch of holding that he always wore on his waist. Considering the Olympic swimming pool-sized piled of Eik’s Legendary Mystery Medicine contained within, it was doubtlessly the single most valuable item in the Unified Mass.

And as the owner of that company, it placed him solidly as one of the wealthiest individuals in the universe.

“Am I ready?” Eik repeated with a sigh. “No, I don’t really think I am. Andi, today I potentially sentence an entire civilization of people to their doom. In fact, ask me in a thousand years and I will still not be ready.”

The elf nodded understandingly. “And you still want to go through with it?”

Eik leaned his forehead on his knuckles, elbows on his knees. “The only thing worse than what I’m about to do would be to let all of those atrocities go unpunished and practically forgotten. Too much has been lost for that. This is inevitable.”

“I agree.”

“Wait, really?” There was genuine surprise, both on Eik’s face and in his voice.

The elf offered a smile. “I wish I could say that what has happened to the Earthlings was unprecedented, but that is unfortunately not the case. The Unified Mass, and in extension the Nidafjeld Alliance, is a brutal and often unforgiving place. If this can serve as an example, that some things are punished properly, then how could I stand against this?”

“Right,” Eik muttered, head hanging low.

“Not to forget,” the Colossal Royal Guard continued, Eik’s eyes seeking his again. “You all more than deserve a taste of retribution.”

At that, the Supreme Divinity chuckled. “And here I thought it was just me.”

“I would like to thank everybody for coming on such short notice. Unfortunately, due to the immensity of the situation at hand, we were forced to push forward with urgency,” the speaker said. His name was Imkoh and he was a middle aged man from… whatever bureau or government office was in charge of this kind of thing.

Eik couldn’t keep track of the many minutely different authorities and acronyms, and nor did he particularly care to try.

The stage wasn’t nearly as grand as the one they had used when he had been named Elixir King but he still felt like there had been put quite a bit of work into it by the organizers.

Given time constraints and a need for a certain amount of space, the best place they had been able to find was one of the larger parks of one of the central districts of Gimleh. Initially, they had considered doing it up in headquarters, but given the pain it would have been to admit so many journalists through security screenings, they had quickly discarded the idea.

Sat in a chair off to the side, Eik watched the man commence what would be a very tense event, no doubt. Something in the area of a thousand people had gathered in front of the raised platform, most of them reporters for this or that newspaper, magazine, or whatever other periodical they published in the Nidafjeld Alliance.

They all sat attentively, ready to record everything that happened.

In addition, several high-ranking government figures or politically adjacent figures were in attendance, their mere presence adding a weight to the day that couldn’t be denied. This was serious.

And lastly but probably most importantly were the Gohkamorian representatives. Most of them wore expression that mixed anger and nervousness in more or less equal parts.

“Nonetheless, I am glad to see that so many of you have shown up. Many of you are probably wondering why we are doing it this way—out here under the open sky—but this is simply how Mr. Magnasen wished to proceed,” the man said, his own expression revealing the same trepidation that was reflected in the audience. An X-ranker didn’t just call for a public thing for no reason. This was big. “In just a moment, I will give the stage to Mr. Eik Magnasen who will deliver his demands.”

From the first notice that Eik Magnasen had an issue he wished to address, it had been made clear that the issue was with the Gohkamorians. And it had long since become public knowledge that he, as a representative of Earth, had accused Gohkamoria of being responsible for a continuous monster invasion of Earth since its inception into the Unified Mass.

But Eik had not revealed exactly what he was going to say today.

“This is absurd!” one of the Gohkamorians seated in the front row growled.

He was enormous. Possibly the largest Gohkamorian Eik had ever seen, and he’d seen quite a few at this point.

Tightly, the huge man’s fingers curled around the links of a knee-length chain mail skirt, anger flashing across his face. Lightly dressed in armor, he looked half ready to start a fight then and there. Besides the chain mail skirt, he wore a pair of greaves, bracers, a leather breast plate, and large pauldrons.

“This farce can’t be allowed to continue!” he yelled, getting up from his seat as uncontained fury got a hold of him. “We came here hoping for this matter to be handled properly, but this is outrageou—!”

“Enough!” the judicial representative of the Alliance suddenly barked, cutting the much larger man off mid-sentence. “With a case of this magnitude, it has been decided that handling it openly and publicly like this is the best way to prevent unwanted rumors from spreading,” he said, the restless tapping of his fingers on the wooden lectern and the beads of sweat quickly forming on his forehead revealing exactly how thrilled he himself was with what was happening.

More than anything, he looked like he wanted to yell at the top of his lungs that he didn’t have a choice in the matter and would rather be at home, relaxing on the couch. “We will first hear what Mr. Magnasen has to say, and then we can take it from there.”

Considering his central seat, he must be a high-ranking member of Gohkamorian society. Despite the ongoing feud with them, Eik actually barely knew anything about the societal structure of Gohkamoria, so the guy could be the president for all he knew.

And frankly, he didn’t care. He didn’t just want one. He wanted them all. Any of them with a hand in the suffering of his people would be brought to justice.

“Please, Mr. Eik Magnasen, if you would come up here so everybody can see you,” the judicial representative requested as he took a few steps back to yield his spot to Eik.

He stood from his seat with a face devoid of the usual humor he would plaster on in a situation like this. It could both throw off an opponent, but more importantly calm his own nerves.

None of that was necessary today, however.

Eik still couldn’t quite believe that this was actually happening. Here they all were—some of the most important people of the Alliance—all because he had called them together. And none of them even knew what he had to say yet.

The fate of the Nidafjeld Alliance and the Unified Mass as a whole was truly in the palm of his hand, and they all knew it.

The Lord of the Moon was undeniably growing his power at a frightening pace, having recently escalated to both direct acts of war against the population of Gimleh, as well as the colonization of multiple worlds just to fill them with a empowering miasma. Eik’s help was absolutely indispensable in order to deal with it.

The only other X-ranker whose whereabouts was even known was Chop the Blade, and he mostly stayed by Eik’s side for reasons that had never quite become clear. Even the powerful upper-rank servants of the Lord of the Moon were too strong to be handled by Alliance forces alone.

Was this a misuse of power? An abuse?

Maybe. But if that was what it took to right the wrong that had been committed against Earth and the humans living there, he would gladly take that on his shoulders.

With a somber expression, he looked out over the gathered audience. It wasn’t as intentionally broadcast as the Elixir King event, but he did have one large display screen behind him and something that functioned like a microphone to pick up his voice.

In addition to the many seated attendees, countless spectators had shown up to witness the unusual gathering, standing anywhere they could see, including rooftops and treetops.

Eik’s gaze touched briefly on each of the Gohkamorians. Each and everyone of them had tension and fear painted in their eyes, although some of them managed to hide it well. They knew they wouldn’t like what was coming.

Eik had to strain himself to keep a smile filled with malice from forming on his lips.

He cleared his throat.

“Yes, thank you for coming. As the presider already mentioned, I apologize for the rather short notice. As you all know, we are going through a challenging time and I’m personally knee-deep in it. That means I only have so much time. But what I have to say really can’t wait any longer,” he said, deliberately keeping his voice calm.

“As a matter of fact, it has already been put off for far too long. We of Earth have been suffering—no, made to suffer—and we had enough long ago. Such suffering has consequences, and what I have come to tell you here today is exactly what those consequences will be.”
