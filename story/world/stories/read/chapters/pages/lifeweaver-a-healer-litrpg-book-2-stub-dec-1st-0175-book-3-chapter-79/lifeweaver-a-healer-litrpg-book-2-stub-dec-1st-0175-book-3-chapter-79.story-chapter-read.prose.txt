"You created a portal?" Luom asked, her expression conveying just what she thought of that revelation. Crazy, she thought he was crazy, and perhaps he was.

"Almost," Luke said. He'd considered lying to her, but since he might need her to deal with the Singers later, she would find out at some point, anyway. If it worked, that was. "Thanks for the mana."

"Of course."

As it turned out, Singer mana potions worked just as well on humans. Good thing that, or he might not have kept his consciousness. Luke had figured it would take a lot of mana to open a gateway, but not that much. He wasn't even sure that was what had happened, since it all became kind of a blur, but his gut told him he was on the right track. If they wanted to leave this place, they would need to make their own exit.

"What now?" Luom asked, eyeing the still-blocked door.

"Now we make friends with everyone and convince them to give all their mana to me. Easy peasy."

"I like peas."

Luke couldn't help but grin. Having a goal to work toward made all the difference. "Singers have peas? The round green veggies?"

She nodded. "We do."

In that moment, a shriek of anguish filled the room, and a mass of undulating shadows flickered into existence in the corner, tentacles of shadow lashing out. Luom cried out, but Luke pulled her out of harm’s way. Another shriek, and this time her face appeared.

"Hannah!" Luke shouted, but she didn't hear him. Her eyes were wide open in shock, horror, or both, and every inch of exposed skin showed intricate tattoo-like dark patterns, far more advanced than the last time he had seen her. She was hurt, but not dying, as far as he could tell.

Then, her darkness flickered, and the shadows, with Hannah among them, were gone. If not for the tears in the floor and a couple of broken cabinets, he might have thought the whole thing a bizarre delusion. Unfortunately, that wasn't the case. Hannah was in trouble somewhere out there. All the more reason to hurry.

"You know that girl?"

"Yeah," Luke said. "Let's go."

"She looked possessed."

Pushing the stuff out of the way so they could open the door, Luke shook his head. "She'll be fine."

Dot's echo returned as Luke and Luom traversed the corridor of infinity, somewhere far below again, but he ignored it. She had made her choice. Soon, they found some monsters gathered around a wheelchair. An injured Singer sat in it, but it took Luke a moment to realize he was still in the process of getting injured. The damn hospital staff was stabbing the poor bastard to death.

Luke dispatched three doctors and two nurses with cold efficiency, then allowed Luom to heal the victim. To their surprise, the Singer didn't disappear.

Integrated Cage. Lifeweaver. Level 21.

"It's one of yours," Luke said, unsure what to do with that.

Luom stopped healing a deep gash across Cage's chest and looked up at Luke as if asking if she was allowed to continue.

He nodded. "Go on. We're going to need him."

While she worked, he asked, "You said you didn't know any Lifeweavers other than Trypten?"

Her concentration wavered, but she still answered the question. "I don't know this Singer. Wasn't allowed out much, and every party I went into dungeons with consisted of my father's guards purposefully kept at a low level."

"You a princess or something?"

"No," she said, finishing up the worst of the lacerations.

Luke pointed at Cage's chest. "You need to do something about his heart. It's failing."

"I don't know how," she said, the hand she held out to use Threads of Mana wavering.

"Here, I'll show you," Luke said, extending a thread of mana to deal with a collapsed wall inside the male Singer's heart. Luom watched, looking as if she was taking mental notes.

Once the procedure was done, it was only a matter of time before Cage woke up. Luom explained the situation to him while he sat there, glaring at Luke. In the end, Cage gave his name in some sort of formal greeting ritual, and Luke gave his in return. It was clear the new addition to their little group didn't believe what they were saying about Luke summoning a portal, but he went along with it anyway due to knowledge of the list of human Integrated who were to be gathered and offered passage to whatever the Singers called their planet. Luke also detected hints of recognition and subservience to Luom in Cage. That, along with her comments, made him think she was the daughter of some higher-up. Not that it mattered.

Cage had reached a decent level but expressed a lot of regret in having picked Lifeweaver as his class. Most Singers didn't get to pick whatever archetype they wanted, and he had been selected for a healing role. This was before they knew much about the system and the different classes, and he'd picked one at random. His complaints pretty much boiled down to having terrible range and constant issues with mana. He'd tried using the manual method but gave up before even reaching Level 10, and now just used burst healing.

Having met quite a few Lifeweavers at this point, Luke found it interesting how so many of them were stuck in the mindset he'd pushed through. The healing ability of a Lifeweaver was far better than what they thought, than what he'd thought when he started out. Even if Cage was more than a little pitiful, Luke elected not to share much about his own style of healing. Maybe he would in the future, if the Singers turned into actual allies and not just assholes trying to take over Earth.

One point of interest after Cage joined them was how Luom grew more reserved. She didn't speak as much and even flinched a few times when the new addition looked at her. What was that about?

While they walked the corridors and stairs, still heading down, Luke drained mana from not-nurses and not-doctors, giving everything to the Singers after topping himself up. He did so without them noticing, since he walked up front and just shrugged when Cage asked how he was regenerating so much mana. Luom knew, of course, since he'd taken mana from her, but he saw no reason to share the knowledge beyond that.

He was considering bringing the fake hospital staff with them to use as mana batteries when they found another Lifeweaver. It was of a species he hadn't seen before.

Integrated Splinter. Lifeweaver. Level 18.

It was a half-rat, half-human hybrid that reached up to around hip level on Luke, and the little bastard even walked with a cane. Rather than run or attack, Splinter just stood there, watching them, eyes gleaming with interest.

"Your name is really Splinter?" Luke asked. "Any green ninjas around?"

"What are ninjas?" Splinter asked, his voice far deeper and more mature than expected.

"Never mind," Luke said. "Want to get out of here?"

Splinter raised an eyebrow, a very human-looking expression in his little rat face. "You have found a way to complete the quest?"

Before Luke could answer that question, a familiar face materialized on the floor between them. Even covered in blood and with numerous injuries all over his body, there was no mistaking Elijah.

Splinter stepped forward and hunched down, holding out a hand over Elijah's trembling, groaning form. "Heart, stomach, an organ filtering blood, in some manner." He looked up at Luke. "One of yours, I take it."

Luke was on the floor a moment after Splinter and set to healing his friend, the former ISG member, who had a claw through his entire skull the first time they met. "One of mine. Don't hurt him."

"I'm a healer," Splinter scoffed. "I only hurt those who annoy me."

Glancing up, Luke saw a glimmer of mirth in the rat's eyes.

Once healed, Elijah disappeared into glittering motes of light without ever having woken up. Again, Luke wondered what had happened to those he had healed. Was Ray alive out there? Had he ever really been injured and ended up here, or was it just some trick? There was no way of knowing before he made it out, so he'd better get to it.

It didn't take much to convince Splinter that joining them was in his best interest, even if it was clear he didn't put much stock in Luke's self-proclaimed ability to get them all out. Even so, safety in numbers was usually a good bet.

Rounding up hospital staff as they went after Luke explained he needed them for what he was planning, they continued down the corridor while keeping an eye on their monster prisoners. They'd attacked several times without a clear pattern to their assholeness, at least none that Luke recognized, so it would be a bad idea to keep them on a long leash. For the moment, they followed along without arguing, almost looking happy to do so.

Now that they'd gathered a ratkin, which was what Splinter called himself, they had three races represented. Not a bad start. The four of them kept going, and little by little their group grew as more Lifeweavers joined them. It was still a way to go before Luke would be satisfied with their combined mana pools, but they'd get there.

Luke was getting home soon. He just knew it.
