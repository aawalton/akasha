There were many whispers, and they started the second Aldric's mouth finished closing.

"…another half-demon? Are you serious?"

"Look at those horns. They're even bigger than Ilyasviel's."

"Shit, don't tell me that's her."

There had been many whispers since I had come to this Academy. Most of those whispers had been in crowds. Barely audible. In this otherwise quiet room, these whispers were more than loud enough to hear. I let the words go. Even without the [System], some things were universal. The strong whispered about those they feared. The weak whispered about those they hated.

The Inker slowly composed himself, and the composure did not come without effort. He took a deep breath, and then crossed the chamber to us in four long strides. He sighed, "You are both very, very late. Come, let us not waste more time." He was already walking away. Ash and I looked at each other, then followed. The whispers only grew louder as we moved effortlessly through the room.

The Inker led us to the far corner of the room, and then turned towards us. He looked past us, and I followed the gaze. He was looking at Ren, who hovered between two students. One was…Edda. I had not seen her immediately, which was a surprise. I should have. She was sitting against one of the walls with her back straight and her eyes closed. Her mouth was slightly open, and she breathed steadily. The other who Ren hovered over was a boy I had never seen before.

The boy was thin, and looked young. Younger than Ren or Edda did. He had dark hair that fell over his eyes, and almost hid the tiniest of nubs that rose from his temple. The horns -if they could be called that- were so small and thin they could have been mistaken for a trick of the light. His hands rested on his knees and his eyes were shut. I saw no glowing mark on either of his arms.

"You," Aldric called, pointing at Ren. "Ren Eryn! Since you've decided to come in here, you will make yourself useful. Walk the students through their breathing exercises. Some of them are near their final stage. I am sure you can tell which."

Ren's mouth dropped open. "I'm not a-"

"Do as you are told," the Inker said, not breaking stride. I saw Ren grind her teeth. Then she shot me an angry look that certainly carried with it some amount of accusation. I had done nothing. Ren clicked her tongue, turned, and stalked towards the closest students, muttering something to herself all the while.

The Inker sat on a low stone and gestured for us to sit in front of him.

The floor was cool beneath me. Ash sat next to me, closer than was necessary. We stared at the Inker. He sighed before he began. The man seemed to do a great deal of sighing.

"It seems that the Archon has left the particulars of your instruction to me. I am to explain what I can and see you through your core formation." He paused. "I will start with some rather…unfortunate news." He looked at me, and for once, there was no sneer behind his eyes.

He began with the basics we had already heard on the road -Essence, its importance, and why a core mattered at all. Neither Ash nor I had asked him to repeat any of it. I suspected he was stretching his explanation to make Ren work longer. "An Essence Core," he said, "is carved directly out of…or rather inside, a mana core. I said before that it is a painful process. This is why." Both Ash and I looked at each other, then back at the Inker. "Think of your mana core as your birthright. I will try to explain this simply. Your mana core is the land you were born to." He glanced between us. "To make an Essence Core is to build a foundation on that land, so that one day that foundation may be something great."

"Then one's potential is limited from birth," Ash said what I was thinking.

"Yes. One can build a better foundation on suitable land. This much is obvious," Aldric said easily.

Ash's core, from what I had felt of it, was no small thing. Especially for a human. Mine had let me wage battles that might never end. This body was different, and my capacity was not quite that extreme, but still. I saw the same understanding in Ash's blue eyes, followed by the smallest of smirks.

"It is not quite as simple as you seem to think," Aldric said dryly. "The bigger your Mana Core, the harder it is to carve into an Essence Core. The less of it you probably will. The smaller the Mana Core, the more of it you can reshape for Essence. There are some exceptions every now and then, but most end up being in the same range as far as capacity is concerned." It was almost difficult not to smirk. The greatest exception in history sat before him, and he did not know it.

"Then there must be a way to measure this," I said.

Aldric nodded. "There is a way. It is called efficiency. Efficiency is the ratio of your Essence Core to your mana core. An average person achieves perhaps forty to fifty percent. Meaning the Essence Core they make ends up being half the size of the mana core they started with. This is a respectable result, and is sufficient for most." He raised a finger. "Of course, being respectable is not enough. A talented cultivator on the other hand, one with both good instruction and access to resonance chambers should reach sixty to sixty five percent instead. This is considered excellent." He raised a second finger. "An incredible core is around seventy five percent efficient. These are rare enough to be noteworthy. Of course, as I said, some cores are easier to reshape than others, so a number might be technically impressive, but practically not worth comment."

The Inker stopped and waited. I could admit that this was rather interesting. There was nothing like this in the old System at all. How strange, that this new world works the way it does. "How does the formation itself work?" Ash asked. She had leaned toward me without quite seeming to notice. Her side pressed against mine.

The Inker answered Ash's question, or rather, he had been answering it for some time. I only heard him now. "-agonizing in practice but very simple in concept. One must fill their mana core to their fullest capacity. They must draw in as much power as they possibly can. That is why we are in this chamber. Draw in mana until you feel like your core might burst from the inside, and hold it there."

He looked between us. "After that, you produce Essence through your mark. You force it into the core, replacing some of that mana. This is where the pain begins. Holding Essence is normally impossible, but when a core is full to bursting, you can. With enough focus." His eyes went distant. "This is perhaps one of the more painful things most people will ever go through, but you endure."

This strange roundabout instruction served only to vex me. Pain? I had felt pain. And more of it than this small man could fathom. "And what happens then?" I pressed.

The Inker stared at me. He frowned, but still answered, "You do not have to do anything at all. Everything is done by the mark. You see, as Essence is held inside the core, the mark begins to impose a…kind of structure on the surrounding core. This new structure that the mark creates can hold Essence without it leaking like it would from a normal mana core. The process is…painful. For some, it feels like a thing burning alive inside you. For others, it feels like being a twig in the center of a great storm. Either way, there will be pain and much of it. You must hold out against the pain while the mark does the carving. That is all." The Inker shook himself.

"That is all," Ash said.

"I did say it was straightforward in concept." The ghost of a smile crossed his face and died. "If you are thinking you need only learn to ignore the pain, it is not quite so simple. The pain gets worse the more of your core gets carved. There is no technique that dulls this. No 'cheat' one can use to bypass this. There is pain, and one must face it honestly." He looked between us. "Many people pass out during formation. Most pass out, in fact. That is when most stop. When consciousness is lost, naturally, the process stops. Others lose focus and let their mana leak from their core. That is another way to halt the process, and the more shameful one."

"And it cannot be resumed once it stops?" I asked, though I already suspected the answer well enough from how the Inker had described all of this.

"Yes. There is no second attempt. There is no correction. Once the process properly starts, you must see it through." His emphasis there was not subtle. "This is why will matters more than talent, and more than technique." He looked between us. "There are two reasons this process takes weeks. The first is the time needed to make Essence at all. The second is so you can practice with the pain. Hold your core to bursting and get used to the feeling. Channel the tiniest bit of Essence into your core, such that some of the pain can come, but the process itself won't start. That way, when the time comes, it is easier." He frowned. "Though, truth be told, it is still painful in the end."

I examined the process in my mind. Fill the core to bursting, produce Essence and force it in and then finally endure the pain that follows. It was brutal, but not complex. I suspected it was the System doing the carving, not the marks. Not that it changed anything. Ash was warm against my side. I adjusted the satchel.

"Now." Aldric clasped his hands together. "For the difficult part." He looked at me. "You carry two marks of the same order. Two…" He glanced at the distant students. "...marks of the same order. This is a problem."

I frowned. Ash spoke before I could. "Explain." The word was sharper than I had expected.

Aldric eyed her, then turned back to me. "If you have two marks of different orders, the process is more complex and slower, but that's all. A Primal and a Cypher could, for instance, carve the same core without any conflict at all. Their Essences would carve the Core into two different structures, but they would both work. The issue is that they would add inefficiency in the boundary between them."

He turned to Ash. "For you, this boundary is an added limitation, besides the pain. This boundary normally takes up ten percent of your core. So, if you had an Essence Core that was seventy percent efficient, it would have been eighty percent, had you only one mark."

"We'll see," Ash said. Her voice was perfectly even.

Aldric shook his head faintly and turned to me. "There…there is a problem. One almost nobody faces, as most everyone with two marks received marks of different orders. For those of the same order…."

"Explain," I said, loud enough that it carried. I did not appreciate his theatrics.

The Inker sighed. "Two marks of the same order cannot carve the same core," he said. "They impose the same type of structure in the exact same places, and the two carvings destroy each other. It is like two sculptors attacking the same block of marble from opposite sides. Other…strange things might happen. It is a rarity among rarities, you see." He spread his hands. "There is one, and only one solution to this. You choose one mark to carve your core, and the other operates on raw mana alone. What mana is left from the inefficiency, that is."

He looked at my arms. At the marks that were the wrong color. "Every mark is valuable," he said. "Yours especially." The whisper was almost gentle. "But you will have to abandon one."

"No," I said.

"This is not a matter for discussion."

Ash tensed beside me. Something rose in my chest. The egg pulsed faster against my skin. "I am not asking for your advice," I said. "I see the depth of your ignorance plain enough."

Aldric stared at me for a long moment. He sighed, rubbed the bridge of his nose, and rose from the bench. "I have done my duty," he said. "Do as you will. You will accept it yourself, in time. For today, I would suggest you merely try and get used to the pain, as I have said. More would be..." He shook his head.

He led us back. The nearest students cleared space before we reached them. There were fewer whispers now. Ash sat closer to me than before. I had not asked her to.

The first step was to fill the core completely. Past completely. In the open air, with this world's thin mana, filling my core completely would have taken hours. Perhaps longer. My core was absurdly large. I had not relished the idea of sitting somewhere for hours or perhaps even days just to fill it.

I observed the others. They drew mana with what I could only loosely call techniques, such that I disliked using the term 'technique' at all. I could feel them doing it. They simply breathed it in, opened themselves, let the mana flow, and guided it toward their marks with whatever clumsy method they had been taught. It was as basic as a breathing technique could be. One might as well call it not breathing at all. It was pathetic.

I almost pitied them. Almost. I had actual techniques, twenty of them. Now, I chose the seventh. The method had once been called [The Breath].

This was a method that compressed mana through controlled cycles. To use it here, it needed only two simple modifications. Simple in theory, at least. The first: instead of compressing that mana in my core, I would route the flow through both marks, letting them convert the mana to Essence and compress it on the way in.

The second: I needed to do this compression twice, for two marks. I needed to do it equally for both. It would have been difficult if the two marks drew in mana the same way. They did not. The Requiem pulled mana with a deep and feral hunger. The Cradle drew it in gently and slowly. Matching the two marks was like steadying a scale with a boulder on one side and a feather on the other. I closed my eyes and tried anyway. It took longer than I would have liked. Far longer. Someone else might not have managed this at all. Eventually, I found it.

A rhythm between the two marks that both compressed the two flows where I wanted, and in equal measure. I was making Requiem and Cradle Essence now, and quite a bit of it. It leaked through my core, just as the Inker had said. That was fine. This had just been a test, after all.

"Ash," I said, eyes still closed. "Do you need assistance? I can teach you, if you wish. It should be simple for you."

"I know that one." Her voice was calm. "You don't need to teach me."

I opened one eye. She met my gaze, and there was the faintest smile. I smirked and closed it.

We began in earnest. The pressure of the air around me dropped first as mana rushed inward through my skin, and towards my core. The flow was faster than I had expected. Beside me, I felt Ash match my rhythm.

For now, I was using the same technique inside my core, and it opened the world to me, such that I could almost see the world around me as clearly as I might have with my eyes. It was from the thousand tiny vibrations in the air itself. I felt the moment the nearest students noticed. Their own mana draw faltered as the mana thinned around them. A girl three seats to my left gasped. A boy further back opened his eyes and stared openly at us. I did not care for them.

It took half an hour for my mana core to fill. My capacity was massive, and it would have taken hours upon hours to do it outside these chambers. It seemed this place was even more generous than I had assumed, and combined with my technique, that was enough.

Ash finished half a breath after me. I felt her exhale slowly as the mana draw around her ended. Her core was full too, though hers should have happened earlier.

I opened my eyes and saw almost a dozen pairs of eyes staring back at me. Many of them had been staring for some time. A few of them had not tried to draw in any mana at all for some minutes. Whether that was so they could stare more effectively, or if there was too little mana left for them to draw in, was hard to say. Even Ren stared at us, and her eyes were wide, and for once, she was not talking.

Edda had opened one eye. The girl had the same bored expression as always. Ash was right. I was beginning to prefer her. Aldric stood at the edge of the chamber with his arms folded. His eyes were a fraction too wide. It was a smaller reaction than all of the children. He deserved some credit for that, at least.

Drawing this much mana in was the easy part. I could feel the faint taste of Essence from Ash. She had the same thought. My lips almost twitched. I breathed, steadied myself, and reached for both marks at once.

Requiem Essence formed in my right arm. It was cold and tasted of endings. Cradle Essence formed on my left. It was warm and tasted of beginnings. I guided both streams inward, toward my core.

Replacing some of the mana in my core with the two types of Essence was simple. Then the pain came, and I could feel the two marks beginning to carve their structures onto the walls of my mana core. It was not that painful. It was-

The two streams of Essence found each other. The Requiem's carving tore through the Cradle's, and the Cradle's tore through the Requiem's. What I was left with was nothing save for a pain far sharper than it had been just moments ago.

Slowly, I opened my eyes. There was light and darkness. White light leaked from my left, and the darkness leaked from my right. Where the two powers met, the air itself was warping. My hands struck the stone before I could even begin to compose myself. Something inside me was tearing itself apart. Ash was screaming and-

There was a hand on my shoulder. The Inker had moved towards me, though I had not seen the motion. Something cold moved from him to me. It ran through all of me at once, and the spiralling powers died, and the warping stopped. Then the pain receded, slowly. I gasped. My vision was a blur for a moment before it finally steadied. The chamber was silent. I raised my head. Sweat ran down my temples. Students had scrambled back. Two were pressed against the far wall. Most of their faces held shock. Most. The rest were smirking at me.

There was a choked sound from a girl on the far side, and a quickly stifled laugh from a girl on the other. Then there were a handful of snickers, gone before I could see who they came from. Petty reactions, all of them. Ones I would not have noticed at all once. I noticed them all now.

Aldric spoke, and his voice carried throughout the chamber: "Out. All of you, out. Those of you who have achieved an Essence Core, come to the measuring chamber tomorrow morning. The rest of you come back here tomorrow and try again, but for today, everyone leave."

Slowly, the students filed out of the room. A few of them lingered and cast glances back. Ren shepherded the strangely dim boy toward the door. Edda followed behind. When the last of them had gone, Aldric stood in front of me with his hands clasped behind his back. The chamber was empty.

"I warned you," Aldric said. "And yet you tried anyway. I should not be surprised." He took a breath. "You said you produced Essence in half an hour when my best students take weeks. That was unreasonable, and I accepted it. You advanced from Spark to Line in a matter of days. That was unreasonable, and I accepted it. You used a breathing technique, one I have never even seen before and one you should not know. That was unreasonable, and I accepted it. You filled your core just now with enough mana that half of the other students couldn't even draw any more in. That was unreasonable." He paused. "And I accepted it."

His gaze landed on me. "But you cannot be unreasonable with the laws of our world," he said. "Half-demons are unreasonable, but they see sense in the end too. You will choose one mark and carve a core for it that functions. You will still be extraordinary. That should be enough."

I said nothing. My hands were fists at my sides.

Aldric studied my face for a long moment. Whatever he found there made him sigh. "The chamber is yours for the night. Consider it a personal favor from me. The Archon expects results. Given your…absurdity, it may not take long." He moved toward the doors. "You may even be ready for the measuring tomorrow, but it will be with a core carved for one mark."

The Inker took a step past the threshold. He paused and turned back towards us, his hand resting on the frame. His mouth opened and then closed. Then he turned around, and was gone. A second later, the heavy doors closed behind him. Ash and I were alone.

"Lys." Ash reached for me. "That was dangerous. Maybe we shouldn't have tried on the first day." She paused. "It's fine. I couldn't get it either. We can always-"

I pulled away. Ash's hand hung in the air between us. Something in her face shifted. "What-"

"You lied to me," I said.
