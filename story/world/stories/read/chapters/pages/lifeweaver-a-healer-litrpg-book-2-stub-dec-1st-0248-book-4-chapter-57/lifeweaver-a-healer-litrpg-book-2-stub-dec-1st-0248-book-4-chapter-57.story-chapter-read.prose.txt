It wasn't that Luke sensed Earth's weave in the dungeon, but the 'vibrations,' for lack of a better word, were strong enough that they affected the area right inside the portal. Rather than climb the last bit out, Luke weavestepped back to Milla, ignored her yelp, and then ran off to a pile of rubble where he could be by himself. There, he plopped down and entered meditation, thinking he needed to learn how to move through the weave without a person to anchor the destination. Luke reached out for the weave. His consciousness stood right next to Dimitrios no more than thirty seconds after the alarm started blaring, but it was still a mess.

No sign of Falzyxi. That was something, at least. Void creatures, though, were in no short supply.

"There are even more of them this time," Dimitrios said, making a broad gesture with his hand to encompass perhaps thirty mini-Deep Dwellers.

"You can't go out there," Luke said, taking it all in.

"But they need to die!"

"You're right," Luke agreed.

Dimitrios turned with a shocked expression on his face. "I am?" He gathered himself. "I know I am, but I thought you disagreed."

"You're not going soft on me just because I healed your brother, right?" Luke said as he did his best to strengthen the end seam of Earth's weave.

Dimitrios drew his blades. "Never!"

"Good. What I mean is, there will be more and more attacks, and the big bad is coming. If we don't start thinning out the herd, they're guaranteed to overwhelm us at some point."

"True."

"So how do we kill them?" Luke asked.

"You're asking me?"

Luke sent a mental shrug. "It's your thing, isn't it?"

"We could blast them, like last time?"

After considering for a moment, Luke decided against it. "Let's try something different this time."

"What?"

"Let's let them in."

"Did your sense fly out when that system went dark?" Dimitrios asked.

Luke drew in a deep breath before answering, unsure of his plan even as he explained it. By the time he had finished, Dimitrios smiled so wide his cheeks had to be hurting.

"You got the gist of it?" Luke asked.

"Of course," Dimitrios said with a nod. "It's simple enough, but full of risk."

"We have to understand more if we want to beat them. This is the only way I know."

"Oh, I'm with you all the way on this one."

"Ready?" Luke asked.

"Hell yeah!"

They both moved to the edge of the weave. This time, the void creatures gnawed in several places rather than one, meaning a pure defense by strengthening the end seam would be that much harder. Luke hoped what he was about to do would draw their attention and focus on the specific spot of his choosing. Offering them a way into the weave would be irresistible, wouldn't it?

With that in mind, Luke sliced through the end seam, creating a gap just wide enough for one of them to wriggle through. The effect was immediate, and the closest one surged forward while all others clustered behind it, pushing to get in, the gnashing teeth on their long tentacles latching onto their fellow void creatures in anticipation. Dimitrios stood before the opening with blades still drawn. The being at the front pushed, but the Mythos blocked its advance, slicing at its undulating nothingness with blades that drew white lines in its bulk, severing pieces and tentacles alike. This time, Luke pitched in. Two threads in Dimitrios's mana channels meant he could heal whatever damage he sustained, and another couple of threads extended into the void creature, allowing him to see what they were fighting against.

At first, there was nothing, just like with Smudge. If he hadn't looked deeper into his friend's void, perhaps he would have given up then, but he knew what he'd seen. There was something there, deep inside creatures of the void. Deep was perhaps not the right word, but like with most things Luke uncovered, it defied description to an extent. Difficult to perceive was a more apt descriptor, maybe. Either way, he found what he was looking for. Creatures of the void were not just aspects of the void itself, but beings of nothingness. The vast majority of their makeup was like that, yes, but not all of it. If you knew where to look, you could, in fact, sense faint lines of power running through their bodies. It acted like mana, in a way, but it reminded him of something else. A moment of searching his experiences was all it took for him to identify it. The energy running through the void creature in front of him was the same power found in monster cores. It was so obvious once he got it. Siphoning power from one of these things and funneling it into a dungeon meant taking that power and showing it into cores, then using those to bring back whatever or whoever had once lived in that place. Was that also why void beings reacted to monster breaks? Did they search to reclaim the power taken from them? That seemed to be the case.

With some answers and knowledge gained, Luke did what any self-respecting human would do with that information. He used it to hurt his enemies. Even this lesser void creature put up an impressive amount of defenses against Luke's threads, but, armed with a way to hurt these things, he wasn't about to give up. Threads snaked through the bulk emptiness of his and Dimitrios's opponent and reached the inner sanctum. Once there, he started cutting. There was no core, meta-heart, or equivalent. Just whispers of power running throughout. In cutting one, he found himself able to siphon it. Rather than risk it, Luke let it bleed away, and bleed it did. The nothingness inside the void was pulling its own power out, as if devouring itself, emptying the creature in an instant.

When Luke opened his eyes, the creature was dead.

"What happened?" Dimitrios asked.

Another one took the first monster's place.

"Keep going," Luke said. "We're far from done."

The void creatures appeared similar to the naked eye, but they differed once you got a closer look at what was inside. Some held more power than others, and the way that power moved and acted within each opponent different. A few put up far more resistance than others. One or two wielded the power within them in strange ways, almost as if attempting to cast spells. Despite their similar outward appearance, that of small copies of The Deep Dweller, and their internal differences, they all had one thing in common: before Luke's focused will, they all died. One by one, he turned them from raging masses of mouths and eyes into nothing. Once their internal power drained away, they lost their form, and the void itself took them, their own nothingness joining all that surrounded them. That said something about what sort of creatures they were, Luke figured, but in that moment of exhaustion, it was beyond him to grasp for the answer to that question.

Once all void creatures were dead, Luke closed the opening he had created in the end seam. With that put together and no enemies outside, the weave was still and quiet.

"That was something," Dimitrios said. After Luke explained what he had found and how he had exploited it, the Mythos nodded in appreciation. "And so you popped them like water balloons! I must also find a way of doing this."

Luke nodded, tired but satisfied. "I doubt it will work on the bigger ones, like Falzyxi, but it's a start."

"Why not?"

"These things," Luke said, making a mental gesture to where they had just fought. "They are like void babies. There is some power in them, but not much, and their will is weak. If they worked together, things would be different, but they don't. Guide said they get smarter when they get stronger, so they'll have defenses."

That made the triumphant expression on Dimitrios's face drop a little, but only a little. The man was so like Mateo in some ways. It would be interesting to put the two of them in a room together.

"Next time. I will cut these lines of power inside them, and not their dark flesh," Dimitrios declared.

"Let's try it," Luke said, since they were about to be needed elsewhere in the weave.

Losing two whole systems meant a lot more focus on Earth, and the void was relentless in its assault. That day alone, Luke and Dimitrios dealt with six separate incursions. A few of them even happened simultaneously, but by then Dimitrios had made good on his promise. After a few tries, he found a way to direct his attacks to where it hurt them, and he sliced through power tendrils with almost as much expertise as Luke. Splitting up was dangerous, but they didn't have much of a choice. More Integrated would join them as soon as Popov taught them the basics of their emerging talents. Luke just hoped it wouldn't take them too long.

As luck would have it, one such Integrated even joined them in the weave that very day. It was the second-to-last attack. Luke and Dimitrios dealt with two groups of void creatures at different points in the weave.

Dimitrios: "There's someone else here."

Luke: "Who?"

Dimitrios: "Bjorn, he says his name is. He's some sort of philosopher, I think. It's not going well."

Dimitrios: "Can you come?"

Luke: "Hold on."

Rather than let the void creatures into the weave one by one this time, Luke had entered the void proper, choosing to take them all on at the same time. It was a small group of four, and they charged him without hesitation. Tentacles full of mouths latched onto him as they crowded around Luke. The pain was very real but fleeting, and he ignored it, counting on Weaver's Bulwark and the healing mana stored in his meta-heart to keep him going for the brief moments he needed to deal with the enemy. Like those he had faced before, the four arrayed around him all consisted of almost ninety-nine percent void, with even their features like mouths and eyes being made up of the stuff. That last percent or so was pure power. It was the only way to describe it. Not mana, nor stamina, or anything else he recognized. Undiluted power. As Luke pushed their will until their resistance crumbled, he wondered where that power originated. It had to come from somewhere. Also, did it go somewhere when he....

Dimitrios: "Come now!"

Luke killed the void creatures and willed himself to Dimitrios. The Mythos was fighting against two void creatures that both looked like more advanced versions of The Deep Dweller. They were larger with longer reach, and their many eyes held an intelligence the smaller ones lacked. Spellweaves formed around their wriggling masses, producing bolts of pure power that cut into Dimitrios and sliced off pieces of him, as if he was made out of clay. At first, Luke didn't see the other guy, the philosopher. When he noticed slight movement outside the weave and found it wasn't another void creature, his eyes widened.

"Can you hold those two off?" he projected to Dimitrios, already charging toward the fallen Integrated.

Dimitrios let out a squeak as he dodged another bolt. "Maybe!"

To say the Integrated was in poor condition would be an understatement. The void had almost claimed him. Despite the few and faint signs of life in the man, inspect didn't even work. When Luke attempted to get his name, all he got was a bunch of gibberish.

Intxgrs Xuhicx. Xijh8??? Lvp 3=?

The reason for it, Luke thought, was that very little of what had once been a human Integrated remained. A faint outline of a human body drifted in the void, but when Luke attempted to pull it back into the weave using threads, they just went right through him, like with a ghost. Weaver's Perception picked up a heartbeat, but little else, and Luke had to strain and focus to get more information.

"Whoa," he breathed.

The poor guy was almost entirely part of the void. Very little of his physical body remained, and the mana channels withered as the void closed in on a flickering, seizing meta-heart. It pumped mana at a trickling rate, but even that was consumed by the void closing in.

Weaver's Mercy didn't work. It wouldn't recognize the patient as a person. Just as tendrils of void snaked into the meta-heart like veins of poisoned blood, Luke pushed forward and used Weaver's Renewal, straining to make it latch onto his patient's meta-heart. When it did, Luke felt a surge of hope but found that hope squashed when nothing happened. The meta-heart didn't remember the Integrated's previous form. There was nothing to restore. The void closed in on its victim, and soon there was nothing but drifting mana channels pulling away into the void. Before it disappeared, Luke thought he saw that familiar power filling them, a faint outline of a different creature taking shape.

It was turning the Integrated into one of them. Luke couldn't believe it.

"A little help?!" Dimitrios shouted.
