Lori and I walked into the large hall of the Kamatouu mansion. We were late— Lori’s fault, for the Zoom meeting with Felix and John Smith. All the other ‘officers’ were already there: Juliette, Hyun-woo, Samar, Naeem, Santiago, Duchess, and Shooya.

I took the phone. After a few minutes of precise tablet tweaking—I was actually really good at this—I got John Smith on the line. I handed the phone to Juliette.

“Hi,” Juliette said, “I’m here with the rest of the Stormcrows officers.”

“Hi,” came John Smith’s voice. “It’s only me and Felix on this side. How have you all been doing?”

“Bad,” Juliette answered flatly. We didn’t know all the details, but we knew more or less what this call was about. We were losing, and it was time for Plan B. Which was... ah... fleeing.

“I know,” said John Smith. “We are losing the war.”

A pause, the silence hang heavy.

“Our enemy has reached some World War I levels of technology,” John Smith said, “while we are still stuck in the medieval era. Even if we got better mages or high-level players, it’s not enough to compensate for the gap. We don’t have an answer for sleeping spore arrows or explosive gas bird-bombings, and that’s after we barely managed to survive everything else they threw at us.

If that wasn’t enough, they keep increasing in resources, innovation, and even money. Every battle, they get some new toys from the bamboo.

As you know, we have been working too. We even had a breakthrough in electrolysis magic recently; in a few more months, we could have had bombs, too. But we simply ran out of time.”

He paused for a long moment before continued, his voice somber.

“But I won't try to make excuses. In the beginning, the enemy had few resources—nothing but a few agents with calm and control spores, maybe a scorpion-cat beast army or two. We should have struck harder then and won. We missed that opportunity. That is why I am personally taking responsibility for losing the war. I have decided to resign as the leader of the Order of the Phoenix. Felix here will take my place.”

That, we didn’t know. It was a bit of a shock. I understood why he took responsibility, but I also knew that John Smith was a brilliant and extremely capable leader. Even with things turning out how they did, I didn’t think any other man could do a better job.

“I already gave my people a very long farewell speech,” he continued. “I will spare you that. My last order was to build ships and leave, which Felix will carry out. I simply… I don’t see any other way.”

John Smith stopped talking, and the line went quiet until Felix’s voice cut in.

“As John Smith said,” Felix started, “we are leaving. We used much of the Order’s resources in the last few months to build three airships. Each can hold one hundred people plus supplies for the journey. They will be complete in two weeks.”

We knew about it, more or less, though I had somewhat thought we could take more people.

“Who will go?” Juliette asked. Three hundred spots were less than half of the players on the continent. And that didn’t count the NPCs and goblins.

“I know there are a lot more players,” Felix said, “but that number covers the groups that actively participated on our side in the war. Almost all the Naked Nula groups, three Ire-Ish Birdfolk groups, two small Bugfolk groups from Rishuan, and the Goblin tribe near Kamatouu. The Bugfolk from Moeveth were mostly acting against us, helping the Agents for money. And the Birdfolk group in Sin-Irsh... we obviously couldn't help them, as they are under enemy control.”

“And NPCs?” I asked.

“We will still have one or two dozen spaces,” Felix said. “But we talked to many NPCs about it. A rare few want to come, even after we explained that the Bamboo will probably find a way to control them.”

“They have their NPC duties here,” I said. “It’s not easy for them to leave.”

I looked at Lori. There was something cold and angry in her eyes. I knew what it was about. Lori’s father wouldn’t want to go. I hoped we could convince him; it would hurt Lori deeply to separate from him.

“Two weeks,” Juliette said. “Is that even enough time? To our latest knowledge, their airship completion is imminent—a matter of days.”

Felix was quiet for a long moment, and then we heard John Smith’s voice again.

“We made a deal,” John Smith said. “With the Agents. With Zylil.”

What?

“They will give us thousands of square meters of cloth,” he continued. “That will send their airship construction timeline back by weeks, in exchange for... for the book.”

Now we were all quiet.

The ISO book with answers… It was a real treasure. They should have consulted with us first—especially since this book came mostly from Hyun-woo’s effort—but I understood the rationale of the exchange.

This book was probably the only thing we had to actually bargain with against the Bamboo.

But still... “Why would they agree to it?” I asked.

“I honestly didn’t think they would,” admitted John Smith. “I guess they are confident in the war and don’t see the harm in a delay. They probably also believe it’s a risk to have that knowledge lost forever. Apparently, the Agents knew about the book early on from a spy, and their former leader, Nurr, applied many resources to try and get it.”

"I understand.” Said Hyun-woo, “I'm just glad my hard work served a diplomatic purpose."

“This journey to another continent,” Duchess asked. “Is it safe? It doesn’t sound safe. If the ship stops working, we land in a desert and we'll all be Sock-wobblefuck and cooked alive! Do you even know where you're going?”

“We are going to Avaravush,” John Smith replied. “A player-less continent. This is what the god Han told Geshem, along with its direction and distance. We also confirmed it.”

“Confirmed how?” Hyun-woo asked. I was curious, too.

“One of our members, Rose, took the God Dream Summoning Skill,” he replied. “She was contacted by some alligator-looking god, Urilish. For many hours, she recited the whole first Harry Potter book to him, which she remembered quite well. In exchange, he gave her some bits of knowledge, as well as confirming what Han said about the Avaravush continent.”

Yes, the God Dream Summoning Skill was annoying, but actually useful. It made sense the Order made use of it too.

“So it’s all settled then,” said Juliette in a sad, low voice. “We lost. And we flee.”

“Yes,” said John Smith. “This is the best course of action at this point.”

We said nothing for a moment.

Then, a violent CRACK shattered the silence, right beside my head.

I jumped, muscles tensing as I expected an attack, only to see the heavy wooden table ruined, a fist driven straight through the center. I looked up. It was just Lori. Her face was a mask of fury, her hand still buried in the splintered wood.

“A coward lets the wind push him from his own hearth!” she said.

“We are weak!” I countered, suddenly angry at her. “We are easily killed. We are not like you!”

Lori looked at me, rage in her eyes, then she turned and walked out of the room.

“Maybe Lori is right,” John Smith sighed. “Throughout history, ruthless tacticians who weren’t afraid to lose men often had the advantage. Unfortunately, I wasn’t one of them.”

“We could still do it,” said Juliette, her voice cold. “Use the time we earned from the deal you made and conduct one last brutal attack. With me, Lori, Geshem, and the strongest from the Order.”

“No,” said Felix. “Despite what Lori said, I don’t think our home is worth dozens of lives. If we can start anew in a different place, we should. Then maybe one day, when we are strong enough, we can come back.”

There were nods around the table.

I thought Felix was right, too. But I also knew it was different for Lori; her attachment to this place was far stronger than the rest of us.

“The continent we are going to,” said Naeem, “it’s without players, and so, I guess without NPCs and shops. We would need to survive on our own, make everything from nature. Would there even be monsters for us to hunt? And even if there are, wouldn’t they disappear when dead?”

This made us think. We could always count on Naeem to give the survivalist point of view.

“We don’t know if there’ll be monsters,” admitted Felix. “If there are, we could capture them and have the few NPCs that come with us make the kill. If there aren’t monsters, we will make do with this planet’s original flora and fauna. Insects and some fruits are edible; we already tested it. We have some members thinking on farming options—admittedly, this is only in the early stages. We will bring many tools and magic gadgets with us that will make our lives easier, but if there isn’t any game to hunt, our life will be more difficult.”

“There are small edible fish monsters here,” Naeem said. “Some fisher NPCs love to catch them. If we have enough, we could establish a fish pond. There... there is also the beehive. I have some more ideas, I need to check...”

Then Naeem left the room in a hurry.

“He is right,” Hyun-woo said. “Survival in unknown natural conditions requires much planning. I need to meet with Camila.”

And with that, he left too.

“What’s with him and Camila?” I asked Juliette.

“Did you miss it?” Juliette said. “They are totally an item.”

“Shut up!” I said. “They are like the most opposite people on the continent!”

“Totally,” said Juliette.

“Well,” said Felix, “we all have many duties to attend to. Please remember that it all depends on a deal with a merciless, psychopathic enemy. They could have many surprises for us. Please stay vigilant.”

“Yes,” said Juliette. “We won't let our guard down.”

They hung up, and we were left to ourselves.

If we were going to a wild continent, Naeem and Hyun-woo were right—there were many things to think about.

“I won't be going,” said Santiago.

“Not going where?” I asked him. But he didn’t answer; he just left the room.

“He meant he wants to stay when the Bamboo arrive,” explained Juliette.

“What? No way!” I said. I started to go after him, but Juliette stopped me.

“Wait,” she said. “Think before talking to him. He’s been miserable since he lost Charlotte. He didn’t see any point in leaving. He’s even thinking about whether it would be better to be a happy slave under Bamboo control.”

“I...” I said. “I probably would feel despair if I lost Lori, too. But he must come with us. He is one of us. I will talk to him.”

“Yes,” said Juliette, “I don’t want to lose him either.”

“I don’t like!” said Samar. “When Felix talked about who can or cannot join us, I guess it was obvious that giant Armadillo Bears and Horned Snakes weren't exactly on the guest list.”

“The Scorpion Cats are small enough,” I offered. “Maybe we can get Dubadillo, too. But a two-ton snake on an airship... that is obviously a problem. But... aren't they controlled by Kim Jong Un and Stalin?”

“Of course not!” snapped Samar. “Dubadillo and Dubadilla haven't been controlled for over half a year. They are loyal and used to us, and there is no need for the Scorpion Cat’s poison anymore. Thubana, too, for over a month. Though Thuban... he needs some more time. Has a temper on him.”

Wait, so all this time Miriam just played with a wild, uncontrolled two-ton poisonous snake?!

“I don’t like this at all!” Samar snapped and left the room.

This was hard for a lot of us. This had been our home for more than two years now. Leaving really sucked.

“I don’t mind, chu!” said Duchess. “This continent annoys me anyway. And I'm not going to be a brain slave!”

“I probably need to go talk to Lori,” I said, “then to Santiago, and Samar. And then do a million other things.”

“Yes,” said Juliette. “I will arrange an assembly with all the Stormcrows to give them the news. It probably won't be easy for many of them...”

I went to the hunter cabin—my home—and heard shouting long before I reached the door.

“A hunter doesn't leave his blind just because the wind turns,” Elric scolded. “I stay right here.”

“Father,” Lori pressed, “The hearth won’t protect us when they come to reap your very soul.”

“A hunter doesn't leave his blind just because the wind turns.”

“You say this is your home,” Lori said, “but stay much longer and it will be your tomb... and your spirit their prize.”

“A hunter doesn't leave his blind just because the wind turns.”

And it went on like this.

My poor Lori. It was heartbreaking to see her struggle so hard, but she couldn't change her stubborn father's mind.

I personally thought the odds were that the Bamboo wouldn't bother controlling normal working NPCs. Even if they could, they really had no reason to. NPCs were loyal to the Monarch; if the Bamboo took control of the Kingdom—which shouldn’t be hard for them—the simple worker NPCs would serve them anyway.

I told as much to Lori, but she didn’t seem to listen, or agree.

Of course, even if she were convinced, she would still need to separate from her father.

“Lori, we won't be going forever,” I added. “Someday, somehow, we get enough power, we will be back to our continent, and we will get out home back from the Bamboo.”

“Promises are like the wind through the pines,” Lori said, looking far into the sky. “They howl loud enough to shake the needles, but you can’t catch them in your hand when the storm breaks.”

Ah, what? I didn’t get that one at all. If Shooya was here, she could actually translate it. I guess she meant it was a promise I couldn’t be certain of keeping. And of course, she was right.

It was simple logic. The Bamboo will be on a continent with resources, NPCs, and magic shops, while we will be in some forest collecting twigs to build a home and hoping to actually grow food.

As war technology goes, if the Bamboo reached WW1 levels like John Smith said, in the new continent we will be downgraded from medieval technology to the fucking Stone Age.

I really hoped to find a way to gain strength, but the truth was, I hadn’t the faintest idea how.

I wanted to see Miriam. I wasn’t always 100% at ease when she was left alone with Elric. I trusted him, but I could definitely see him reaching the conclusion that nine months old is the perfect age to take a toddler hunting.

“Elric, did you see Miriam?” I asked him.

“The girl belongs with her mother,” he said. “Tucked away from the wild and the wind.”

“But Lori is her mother,” I said. Wait... shit! Vel’nari.

I didn’t really think Vel’nari would take Miriam back to the goblin camp or something, so if she didn’t, they probably headed to town. Lori and I hurried there quickly.

In the past, the goblins couldn’t go to town, as the NPCs saw them as monsters and attacked them. They were only allowed at the hunter cabin, and we made town purchases for them.

But with time, more and more goblins came, and some curious goblins got to sniffing around other NPC shops. Little by little, the NPCs in Kamatouu more or less got used to them, even if they weren't really comfortable around them.

Still, it wasn’t uncommon for some guards to snap and chase a goblin away.

So Vel’nari could be at town, and if she was, the weapon shop is where I would probably find her. And sure enough, there she was, holding hands with Miriam while haggling, bargaining, and shouting with the NPC shopkeeper.

Looking at his facial expression, he was minutes from actually attacking her. I guess she knew NPC "pissed-off boundaries" better than me from lots of practicing.

“Hi, Vel’nari, Miriam!” I shouted at them.

“Hi Geshem, Lori,” Vel’nari said. “This silly NPC human try to swindle me, but we almost done.”

Miriam saw Lori and looked like she really wanted to go to her, but then looked at Vel’nari, and then down.

It wasn’t the first time Velnari had come, but she didn’t come a lot—maybe three times since she first brought Miriam here. And when she came, it was little talk and mostly training. So Miriam knew her to be her mother too, but was also somewhat respectful and afraid of her.

After each visit, I got an earful from Velnari for neglecting the child's training, which I mostly suffered stoically. No one will ever convince me that training a toddler in knife fighting is good parenting!

After Vel’nari shouted at the shopkeeper some more, they seemed to come to an agreement. She handed the shopkeeper something, and I saw it was gold coins. Actual gold coins.

I didn’t know what she could buy from this small town shop that would cost so much.

The shopkeeper went to the back and returned with something small wrapped in paper, handing it to Vel’nari. Then she kissed Miriam on the head and handed her the wrapped paper.

Miriam looked confused—she obviously didn’t expect kisses and gifts from this specific mother of hers—but opened the paper excitedly nonetheless. When she saw what was inside, her toddler eyes went wide.

A toddler-sized, beautiful, and vicious-looking knife.

It gleamed as she turned it. It was clearly custom-made, with a jagged edge designed for maiming. And if there was something Miriam loved—well, except meat—it was knives. It was a great gift, and my daughter couldn’t take her eyes off it, turning it and jumping excitedly. Oh god, please don’t cut yourself!

“It’s a really cool gift,” I said to Vel’nari. “Good job.”

“It’s a farewell gift,” Vel’nari said. “Something for her to remember me.”

Remember her... It took me a moment to get her meaning. So she knew we were leaving and...

“You aren't coming with us?” I asked, rattled. “In the airships? But why!?”

“This is my new home. My territory,” she said. “To run away from your home is a coward act.”

“We wouldn’t if we thought we had a chance,” I said. “Sometimes the enemy is just too powerful. You... you will become a mind slave!”

“Some of the goblins in tribe think so too. Some will join. But not me,” she said. “You also don’t know what will happen. Sometime, when rulers replaced, it is just new Chief. For simple goblin, life is same.”

“But... but Miriam?”

“I want her to go with you,” she said. “For her, I want what is the safest, so she surely grow and become mighty warrior. But I’ll miss her.”

“We will find a way to come back!” I gave the empty promise for the second time today, but I meant it this time too. “Somehow, we will come back. This is our home too.”

“If you won't,” she smiled at me, showing sharp teeth, “I’ll get to your continent and stab you in hundred different places.”

I smiled at her too.

I then saw a gathering of people around the mansion. Ah, that was the Stormcrow gathering Juliette had called.

“We probably need to go,” I said to Lori.

We said goodbye to Velnari and Miriam, and left them to have some quality mother-daughter knife training time together.

We got to the mansion hall, where all thirty of our Stormcrow members sat, and found a place beside Shooya and Samar.

Juliette then started talking, explaining what happened and what the plan was.

I saw that people were agitated, nervously talking one to another. They all probably knew even before what it was about—it wasn’t a secret we were losing. But now, with Juliette formally admitting defeat and a date of departure, it felt more real, and harder too.

Some Stormcrows argued about staying, whispering that maybe it's just new masters—like royalty changing—and wouldn't affect their lives. Juliette heard the whispers too, and had something to say.

“The Bamboo are evil as fuck,” she said. “Think about the Agents. We hate them, but they were a peaceful player species on their continent. The Bamboo didn’t have a problem sacrificing something like 80% of them in order to get them here. They promised peace, and then found new ways to kill us. They are liars. They use death gas and mind control. This is not your typical war; this is some Nazi shit. Don’t stay here only to find yourself becoming a hollow, drooling, smiling mind slave!”

“Nazi means bad, right?” Shooya asked.

“Yes,” I confirmed.

Juliette had her way with the crowd, and she gave some valid points, but the journey itself was a risk too. We all knew it. Some of us would stay. I just hoped Santiago would change his mind!

“On another note,” she continued, “John Smith is resigning, taking responsibility for the loss in the war or what not, and being replaced by Felix.”

She stopped for a moment, surveying the crowd.

“I don’t feel responsible for losing the war,” she said. “I even think I was an okay leader...”

Oh, not that shit again!

“But I decided I will resign as a leader,” she said. “Like, for real. Don’t make such a big deal of it; I’m just bored with it, is all. Someone else will do it.”

And like last time she tried to resign, the crowd looked at her in silence. No volunteers.

“I thought you would do that,” she said. “But this time, it will go differently: I will choose a nominee. If no one else presents themselves as a candidate, the nominee I choose will become the leader. If someone else comes forward, we will simply vote between them. Simple, right?”

Then Juliette walked into the crowd, grabbed Duchess, and pulled her back to the stage.

“Chu? What... what is it?” Duchess looked very much confused.

“I nominate Duchess as the new leader of the Stormcrows,” said Juliette ceremoniously.

Then Juliette gave me a quick look, and I saw a small smile there.

Oh, I see what her game was.

She wanted me to take leadership, and was threatening me with Duchess. We could all fairly imagine what life with Duchess as leader would look like.

This was a game of chicken.

But I still really didn’t want to be leader. I would play along. And besides, someone else could be pressed to be leader. So I smiled back at Juliette, and didn’t very much nominate myself.

I also saw Duchess follow Juliette’s gaze to me, then squint her eyes suspiciously. She was probably slowly figuring things out.

As time passed and still no one volunteer, Juliette’s gaze wandered to Hyun-woo, and to Naeem. Yes, she would become desperate and call her bluff soon.

“Hey! Why didn't Juliette look at me?” angered Samar. “I could be leader too.”

“Well, go on the stage,” I prompted her. “You’ll have my vote.”

Samar shrugged, but didn’t go to the stage.

“Shooya,” I ask, “What about you?”

“Hell no!” she said.

Then Lori got up from her seat and slowly stepped onto the stage.

What? What was she doing?

Did she... really want to lead the Stormcrows? She never spoke or showed interest in something like that.

Lori was a legend around the entire continent. She was also cute, and probably liked by all the Stormcrows. But... Lori was an NPC. She spoke only in NPC-like silly sentences, and... her mind worked differently.

She was capable in all things she set her mind to. But... I didn’t know if a leader role would fit her. What was she thinking anyway?

When she got on stage we all stared, Duchess and Juliette surprised as well.

“You... want to be the leader?” Juliette stammered.

She literally couldn’t say ‘yes’. Should I... like, help her somehow? I wasn’t sure what to do.

Then Lori spoke.

“When we flee, the forest will grow silent and this land will be forgotten by all but the wind. I will lead you away for now, but we shall grow powerful in the shadows and win back this hearth by blood and vow.”

Lori took this seriously, I understood. She didn't just want to be leader; she offered the Stormcrows a clear objective: we leave, yes, but this is just a strategic retreat. This is still part of the war. We go, we get stronger, we come back, and we win.

If the Stormcrows chose her as Leader, they would also choose this mission for the Stormcrows.

“But you can’t speak like people,” Duchess inquired.

Lori then suddenly looked very serious. Her face tightened, and every muscle seemed to twitch with effort.

Then she said something that left me in shock.

“Y…Yes,” she uttered. It sounded heavy, like it took a massive physical effort just to force the sound from her throat. “Canspeak... Itjust... stupid...”

My mouth remained open. I was in total shock.

“Oh, okay then,” Duchess said, then looked at Juliette. “Screw this! I’m not competing against fricking Lori.”

Then Duchess went back to her seat.

“So...” said Juliette, still in shock. “Anyone else?”

Quiet.

Oh, here I could help. NPC or not, I needed to support my girlfriend.

So I started clapping, showing my support.

Shooya immediately joined with her weird insect hand glag-sound clap. Then Samar, then everyone else. Juliette too.

We all loved Lori, after all.

And with that, our merry band of alien players had a new leader: my cute NPC girlfriend, who was for some unknown reason also stupidly strong.

And also, we got a clear objective ahead.

I just hoped things wouldn't get too weird.
