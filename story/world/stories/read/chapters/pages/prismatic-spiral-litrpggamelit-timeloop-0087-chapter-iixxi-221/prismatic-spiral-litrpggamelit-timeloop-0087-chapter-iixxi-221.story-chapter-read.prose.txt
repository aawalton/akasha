Chapter II.XXI (2.21)

This time when the sea monster struck, Mitsuko was prepared.

She attempted to Mend the monster and send it back into the depths of the sea where it belonged. Unfortunately, like the creatures commanded by the guardians, this monster held a natural resistance to her spell. Her attempt slid off it. It also didn’t help that the thing’s size was massive. That was likely a factor in her failure.

However, that immunity to her magic couldn’t be claimed by the crusts that attempted to board their ship. A few swift casts of Mend sent them tumbling back into the water where they belonged. While it was well within her power to kill the weak opponents, she deemed it a waste of time. Better to toss them out of the way to give her space to challenge the true enemy. It also would keep the sailors safe. So far her precautions had let them all survive this long. No one fell overboard. No one was killed by a harpoon. This was a trend she intended to keep up.

She stood balanced on the gunwales, sword in hand.

The whale of many eyes blinked at her. Her heart beat with anticipation as she examined her enemy. This would be a battle unlike any she’d ever faced. Before the timeloops, Mitsuko had always done her best to approach each fight with care. To carefully pick her battles and curate her opponents whenever possible.

Not so any longer. Her life weighed very little when placed on a scale alongside victory.

The eyes conjoined just as they had in the previous loop. Weirdly, this time it didn’t glow yellow, rather a light blue.

Without even a chance to consider what this might mean, Mitsuko threw herself at it, a blade of ice between her and her foe.

Midair, halfway across the gap from the ship and monster, the eye pulsed. Like a winter gale, the sheer power of the attack knocked her backwards and she slammed into the side of The Selcouth Sable’s hull. But instead of falling into the sea with a sploosh, Mitsuko slammed into a hard surface with a crunch.

After swiftly Mending her body to remove the new bruises, Mitsuko pressed a shaking hand down and felt the cool crater of ice under her. It would seem the eyes on the leviathan had the ability to switch elements. Last time had been electricity, this time ice. Mitsuko had no interest in suffering through another attack to learn the monster’s limits. It had been generous enough to create a platform on the water for her. Mitsuko decided to use it.

Hopping to her feet, Mitsuko created a new blade and charged forward. Her sudden movement provoked the eye to dart to her. The glow returned. But this time Mitsuko was already on the beast.

Her sword sliced in an upwards arc, cutting into the bottom of the massive eye. Goo rained down on her. She narrowly dodged it as it splattered on the ice.

BUUUUUHHHHHH, the beast cried out in pain and fury.

More eyes melded together. Green. Yellow. Gray. Black. A spectrum of colors from half a dozen points on the leviathan’s flesh. All focused on her. Mitsuko immediately knew she was outclassed. The sea monster wasn’t retreating as she’d hoped. Instead, it double-downed on its intent to annihilate her.

Mitsuko glanced up at the sliced open eyeball, now a milky white rather than the previous ominous shade of blue. All around her the different eyes glowed, centered on her small figure. She needed to duck for cover

Without stopping to consider how horrible of an idea this was, Mitsuko threw herself into the eye socket. Clumps of mushy eye goo clung to her as she climbed into the cavity. She carved her way forward with her blade. The optic nerve at the back of the eye socket traced along the lower layers of the beast’s skin. Rather than wiggle and cut her way through that, Mitsuko hacked through the beast’s fat. It screamed again in pain and threw its form at The Selcouth Sable, likely in an attempt to knock her out of her burrow. It might have worked too, if Mitsuko didn’t simply cast Mend on her clothing to pull her back in.

Blade after blade snapped as she continued to hack her way through the fat and muscle. She cast Deteriorate on the removed flesh to let it shrivel up into tiny, easily tossed out, piles. Blood was a harder substance for her to control, likely due to the unique bond between it and one's soul, but thankfully, for its size, this creature had far lower percent of blood compared to a human. However, her entire body was still stained red from exposure and her mouth tasted metallic. Still, she wasn’t drowning in the fluid.

The sea monster attempted to plunge into the sea to flush her out, which was an annoying, but manageable obstacle. It simply meant Mitsuko had to continuously cast Mend on the flood of water seeping in. In a way, it actually helped her goals as it flushed out the tunnel and removed shriveled flesh. Very little light entered her cavity as the sea monster sank deeper into the ocean’s depths. Once she carved her way upwards and created an air pocket in the flesh, she finally let the water wash crash in behind her.

Mitsuko had to navigate between bones but the leviathan was so big that there were gaps large enough that she could squeeze between them once cleared of flesh.

Whatever the monster’s magic was, it didn’t seem to protect its internal organs. Mitsuko stabbed into a new, harder texture. While she was practically blind, she had no doubt this substance was different from the muscle and fat she’d been carving her way through on the outer levels. She plunged her sword into it.

The sea monster wailed and everything shuddered around her. That was a good sign. She pressed her weight against the blade and tore down. The carved out crevice tensed and compressed, as if flexed by the monster. The walls closed in and crushed her. Spikes of pain shot through her body as the pressure popped several bones.

Mitsuko cast Mend on her sword. The blade attempted to retrace its recent history and pressed back against the tensed muscle. That bit of room allowed Mitsuko enough of a gap of space to create another sword and plunge it into the hard surface once again.

After several minutes of ripping through the organ, the monster’s body started pumping less and less blood. Her tunnel through the beast relaxed. No further beams of magic or crushing assaults.

“I think...” Mitsuko paused and licked her lips. They tasted of iron and salt. “I think the monster is dead.”

No one answered her declaration.

Suddenly her attack felt a lot more foolish. So what if she slew the creature? She was now deep underwater and her lackluster swimming abilities offered her no chance of an escape.

She leaned back against the fleshy wall and sighed. “Well, it was certainly an experience. I suppose I can spend the rest of the loop down here.” But, unlike on The Selcouth Sable, Mitsuko had very little to actually entertain herself or train with. For a bit she toyed with the idea of Mending her aging again, just to see how far back she could press her spell. But she discarded the idea. Maybe at the end of her loop. No point in limiting her options now. Not that she would have any clue when the loop ended while trapped in the dark down here.

If there was a time to take a nap and mentally rest, now would be it. But Mitsuko felt no weariness.

She flicked her wrist to create another blade. “I could keep burrowing into the monster,” she mused, swinging the sword. “But what would be the point?”

As she sat there in the dark, she considered what came next for her. The sea monster drifted in the middle of the ocean. She tapped the flesh with her sword.

“It’s dead,” she mused. She recalled in a previous loop pulling the dead bodies of sailors around with Mend. It had restored their bodies without restoring their lives. And since she could cast her spells on chunks of monster once separated from them, it made sense that the might the life and will of the creature was a factor into what held her magic back. Or it could be the blood of it, which still existed all around her. Regardless, she deemed it worth an attempt.

Mitsuko cast Mend.

She felt bits of flesh move past her. The chunks she’d shriveled up with Deteriorate struggled to reconnect to the mass, but otherwise the rest reknit itself into the main body.

More than that though, the monster moved once again. It jerked and Mitsuko felt it ascending upwards. The effort to Mend a creature this size was comparable to when she’d reversed the clocktower. She felt it draining her energy. Minutes passed and she was just considering taking a break and letting her body recover when the sea monster burst through the placid sea’s surface.

Light poured in at the end of her tunnel, once again allowing Mitsuko to see. The sight was as grim as she’d expected. A mangled burrow of flesh, bits of it Mended and requiring her to hack through again for her to exit.

She wormed her way out of the tunnel. Very few moments in her life equaled her disgust as she emerged from the popped eye socket.

But she took a deep breath of the fresh sea air and tried to relax. The top half of her body dangled from the opening while her lower body remained within the flesh.

Open sea stretched out before her. The Selcouth Sable nowhere to be seen.

“Now what?” she wondered out loud.
