Chapter II.XXVI (2.26)

Juniper, predictably, refused to tell Mitsuko about Eustace Hrim without the use of a question. There was no guarantee this was the guardian on Porcelain Island, but it felt right. A powerful ice mage who’d combined his magic with the art of golem making. Somehow though, he’d found a way to imbue a soul and life itself into the golems. Not only did they have personalities and feelings, but there were even historical records of necromancers latching onto sculpts souls the same as any other living creature. And yet, while the book mentioned these details, it didn’t say how the wizard had done it. Hrim’s achievements were widely known, but the means to get there were a complete mystery.

There was a two page biographical section about the creator of the sculpt race. After an accident removed the left half of his flesh, he became reclusive and little was known about his social life. If he’d had friends or hobbies, there were no records of them. His work, on the other hand, were widely documented. His prosthetics were famous worldwide, though they weren’t without flaws. After some unknown social slight by the wizards of Amber, he retreated to the abandoned, inhospitable Porcelain Island. Five years later, the sculpts appeared.

“The voice I heard definitely matches his face,” Mitsuko said to herself while staring down at the stern portrait of the man. While half of his face was clearly ice, the flesh half of his face was not any warmer.

“My ancestors never welcomed him on Verdant,” Juniper said. The bird was perched on the table, looking down at the tome’s open pages. “While we always had compassion for the sculpts themselves, their creator was an abomination to life.”

“That’s not very helpful information,” Mitsuko replied.

“I can’t give you helpful information. But I will say that he was a horrible father to his creations.”

“Can’t be worse than Anemone.”

“Yes, he can and was.”

It was bait. Mitsuko was sorely tempted to bite and ask about the man. But, again, it might not actually be him that was the guardian. And that would mean wasting a question on something potentially irrelevant. She still had a few questions to spare, but she didn’t want to waste anything on something potentially useless.

When she finished with The Origins of Ice, she replaced it on the bookshelf and browsed the shelves for another book. She was hoping to find information about the ice caves in particular, but after hours of searching, she found nothing useful.

“This is exhausting.” Mitsuko tossed another book into the growing pile of rejects at the corner of the room. At first she’d been gentle with them, carefully stacking the books. Then she realized that it was a waste of time to treat the books with respect. Everything would be reset in a few days, regardless of how she handled them. And besides, she could repair any damage if she needed to with a quick cast of Mend.

“I believe you,” Juniper replied. She used a talon to turn a page on the book she was reading. A storybook about a snowman from the northern reaches of Tross who came to life with magic who traveled across the world to Porcelain Island to be friends with the sculpts.

Mitsuko looked back to The Origins of Ice. There was a question somewhere in that book that Juniper could answer, Mitsuko just needed to figure out what it was. The right question could make everything so much easier. But the wrong question would gamble away one of her limited resources.

“I’m going for a walk,” she announced. Kicking back the chair, she stood and stretched. “I need to clear my head.”

“Are you certain that’s wise?” Juniper asked “The more you interact with the outside world, the more you risk exposing yourself.”

Mitsuko flicked her wrist and a blade of ice appeared in her hand. She raised the blade and pressed the sharp edge against her neck. Her warm blood clashed with the cool ice as it trailed down the blue blade. Weirdly, Mitsuko felt negligible pain from the wound. More like pressure than true pain.

“I can extinguish my life at a moment’s notice,” she said. “The loop ends when I command it to.”

“So you say,” Juniper said.

Tossing aside the sword, Mitsuko Mended her neck, repairing the surface wound with ease. “Besides, I made it all the way over here. A short walk shouldn’t be much in comparison.”

Mitsuko walked to the door and then hesitated. She turned back to Juniper.

“I have another question for you. Can you tell me Amber Island’s schedule?” Mitsuko asked. “The island roams across the Prismatic Archipelago but it should be on a set schedule for me while in the timeloops. Do you know where it goes?”

“I do not. However, I can gather that information for you,” Juniper offered. “It should be accessible in the Amber library vault.”

Mitsuko nodded. Unlike the riskier questions she’d considered earlier, discovering the island’s schedule was definitely worth the use of one of her limited questions. It would help advance her progress and save time in future loops. “That would be great. Thanks.”

“It will give me something to do while you wander around. I request you formally ask the question on your return.”

When Mitsuko opened the white door, the gull hopped off in the direction of the yellow library.

The hallway hadn’t changed much since Mitsuko’s arrival. She looked at each of the doors. Purple, green, gray, yellow. After some deliberation, she turned away from all of them with the intent to stroll on the nearby streets outside and get a few minutes of fresh air. She really wanted to go explore the recent disaster site with the newly grown yellow foliage. But that would be too risky. No, a simple jaunt was all she needed. Something to clear her head and help her sort through what her next objective should be for the loop.

As she turned away from the library doors she took a step forward and paused. When she’d entered, she’d stepped down a few stairs. But as she looked now, there was a slight wooden door that led to a storage room under the steps. A blue door.

“Azure,” she muttered. She approached the small, unassuming door and tried the doorknob. Locked. Mitsuko cast Mend on the door to send it back into an open state.

Instead of opening, the door exploded.

Mitsuko tumbled down the stone hallway and slammed into a wall. Her hand that had gripped the knob was little more than a stump of smoldering flesh. Her vision had blurred in one eye while her other went completely blind. She touched at her cheek with her relatively intact hand and winced as her exposed flesh tore away on the tips of her fingers. Her blind eye, it seemed, had melted in its socket. That explained the horrific pain bouncing around in her skull. The cut to her neck earlier was like a massage in comparison to this.

She cast Mend on herself and the pain evaporated. Her hand grew back into place, skin wrapping itself over the stump and bones retaking their form beneath.

Taking in a deep breath, she pushed herself up into a sitting position, marveling at both the power of her spell as well as the safety precaution on that door.

“What’s this?” The wizard she’d met when entering the library stomped down the steps. “Someone trying to access the azure vault! What idiotic nincompoop would ever—” His words cut off as he spotted Mitsuko.

She raised a newly skin covered hand and waved. The wizard averted his eyes and started mumbling into his beard.

Mitsuko tilted her head at the wizard’s odd response. She’d expected an earful based on the start of his tirade. Then a smell caught her attention. At first she assumed it to be her hair, but no, she’d unconsciously Mended it along with her body. Then she glanced down. At the smoking remains of her wizard robe and the body underneath. The very exposed body underneath. Far more than just her scarred stomach was on display. No longer suffering from third degree burns, the skin was vulnerable to all mortal gazes.

“Fuck!” She swiftly cast Mend on the robe and the fabric stitched itself back together with the slightest prompting. In a second, her nakedness was once again sealed behind a layer of warm fabric.

“What was that?” another voice asked.

“A self repairing robe?”

“Fascinating, what caused the delay in the enchantment?”

“Did it also protect her from the initial blast?”

“How did one such as her trigger such a reaction? She’s only a three-striped. She shouldn’t have that amount of power.”

Slowly, Mitsuko turned her head. A pack of wizards had emerged from the yellow library room and stood in the doorway, craning their necks to examine her as if she was an interesting fungus that might have poisonous spores.

“Hello,” she said weakly. “Um. I don’t suppose you all would be willing to forget seeing that?”

Realizing Mitsuko was once again decent, the first wizard finished stomping down the stairs and pointed a finger at her. “I told you to stay in line and only interact with the library vaults of your station. I don’t know how you caused such a reaction, but I promise you this, I will appeal to every authority in my contacts to make certain all of your stripes are stripped from that fancy robe of yours. Once I’m through with you, you will never be allowed access to even Amber's public chamber pots.”

Mitsuko rolled her eyes. “Fine. Fine. Whatever. It’s all a wash anyway.”

“A thief?” one of the other wizards asked. “Before our very eyes, do we see the final act of a grand heist's failure?”

“Sure.” Mitsuko sighed and leaned back on the wall. She tapped her head against the stone. “I just wanted to learn about Azure Island. I got greedy.”

“Stand aside, Sados,” one of the wizards demanded as he shoved aside his peers. “That robe lacks any unusual enchantments. Something is amiss here.”

The short wizard who’d yelled at her, Sados, glared up at the new wizard who spoke to him. But the newcomer paid him no mind as he had eyes only for Mitsuko. His crooked nose twitched as he sneered. Still not bothering to look at Sados, he set a hand on the short man’s arm and shoved him to the stone floor. There was a crack as a bone snapped on impact. Sados cried out in pain as his form crumpled.

“You’re her, aren’t you?” he asked Mitsuko. “The one my master is looking for. Unassuming and pathetic.”

This was bad. This wizard was almost certainly an acolyte.

“Who is this woman to you, Ruurin?” Sados demanded, clutching his side and struggling to sit up. “What power did she use to attempt access to Azure Island’s vault? And what is this talk of a master? You overreach, treating me so. You haven’t declared support for anyone as far as I’ve heard. I don’t know what new fixation has you in its grasp, but no master on this island will save you after this treatment of—”

“Stop your prattling, Sados. What will you do to me? Enchant a book and throw it at me? I have orders to carve out this woman’s heart. Only then will we all be free” Ruurin raised his hands, palms open in her direction. Orbs of light formed on each of his fingertips with beams of light converging on the center of his palms.

Mitsuko, not eager to have her heart pierced by whatever he was planning, cast Mend on the wizard. The spell slipped off him, as if his existence in time was as greasy as his hair. Mitsuko needed to change targets.

The wizard clapped his glowing hands together at the same time that a short wizard bounded up from the floor and slammed into Ruurin’s side. The beam of light went wildly off-target, disintegrating Sados’s arm and piercing beyond it.

On her feet, Mitsuko flicked her wrist and a sword of ice rested in her hand. She needed to end this now, before Ruurin could charge up another attack. A stab through the throat might not be swift enough at killing her so instead she repositioned the sword’s point under her chin.

She let herself fall on the blade, casting Deteriorate on herself to grant her additional speed and force in an effort to make certain she pierced through her brain. As she fell, for a brief second, she spotted where Ruurin’s spell had impacted. Azure Island’s library vault door had a clean circle of space cleared from its center. Within, a blue statue of a massive mermaid was proudly perched on a rock with stone waves caught mid splash around her. Rows of books surrounded the statue. A wealth of knowledge.

For a different loop.
